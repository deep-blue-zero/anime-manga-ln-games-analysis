# DJFW_CASE_0010 — Typehatena / Kodomo Idol... — Continuous Sequential Categorical Reading V2

## 0. Governing judgment

This case is intentionally different from the other continuous-sequential readings.

The source uses explicit minor/child-coded Idolmaster characters, so the canonical DJFW reading is **categorical-only**. It does not OCR, transcribe, translate, or describe explicit acts. The analytically relevant evidence is structural: character interchangeability, repeated scenario grammar, adult-control framing, subjecthood reduction, and the difference between visual character presence and character authorship.

The corrected governing formulation is:

> **This is not a Hiro-character work, despite a closing-page Hiro depiction. Hiro can be present visually while Hiro's authorship is absent.**

## 1. Source lock and safety boundary

Locked source: 20 pages, SHA-256 `b133824041038ca150a06e04fcf0eaeab0042de068e05ad1408e668a00438796`.

Metadata identifies Arisu Tachibana, Momoka Sakurai, Haru Yuuki, Kaoru Ryuzaki, and Mai Fukuyama. A closing-page image also contains Hiro Shinosawa, which corrects the earlier metadata-only assumption that the source contained no Hiro at all.

Because the package is explicit and centered on child/minor-coded characters:

- no explicit page OCR is part of this artifact;
- no explicit dialogue is quoted;
- no sexual scene mechanics are described;
- voice fidelity is not inferred from material that would require explicit transcription.

This is an analytical boundary, not a source-completeness claim.

## 2. Sequential structural pattern

At categorical level, the book establishes a roster of recognizable idol designs and then repeatedly subjects them to the same dominant authorial/scenario system. Character-specific narrative goals, professional worlds, relationships, and individual speech grammars do not become the organizing principle of the sequence.

The sequence therefore behaves less like several character stories and more like a **single Typehatena machine with multiple visual inputs**.

The closing Hiro depiction does not materially change that grammar. It adds Hiro's recognizable design to the roster but does not supply:

- Hiro's chosen-difficulty logic;
- her low-force deadpan evaluative voice;
- her ordinary longing to become cute;
- her Producer relationship;
- accountable attachment;
- a Hiro-specific decision structure.

Visual identity is present. Character authorship is not.

## 3. Subjecthood

Subjecthood outcome: **subject-erasing / subject-annihilating as function**.

The distinction “as function” matters. This is not a reflective work about the horror of losing personhood. Subjecthood loss is not the theme under examination by the characters; it is the operational condition that allows the scenario to proceed with high interchangeability.

That places the work near the lower bound of DJFW's subjecthood scale.

## 4. Character specificity

Character specificity is extremely low.

The appropriate test is counterfactual replacement: if one small/child-coded idol design is replaced by another while the dominant mechanism remains essentially unchanged, the work is not generating events from character grammar.

That does not mean visual design is irrelevant. Recognition still matters to audience consumption. But recognition and authorship are analytically different variables.

This case therefore provides a clean example of:

> **recognizable design + negligible voice + negligible relational grammar + high scenario invariance.**

## 5. Arisu as a particularly useful negative-control comparator

Arisu Tachibana is valuable here precisely because her canonical appeal depends heavily on resisting reductive treatment. She wants to be taken seriously as a competent, thinking person rather than collapsed into “small,” “cute,” or “childish.”

The source does not need that resistance to function. It needs her recognizable design category.

Accordingly, this is not best described as “Arisu is written out of character.” The stronger formulation is:

> **the mechanism does not require enough character for ordinary out-of-character analysis to become the main question.**

That is why the Re:Creators-style self-audit previously modeled the distinction as “this isn't out of character; it isn't character.” That modeled reaction is interpretive illustration only, not primary evidence.

## 6. Hiro closing depiction

The later discovery of Hiro in the closing material creates a useful methodological correction.

Earlier first pass: “not a Hiro work.”

Current formulation:

> **not a Hiro-character work, despite Hiro being depicted.**

The revision is important because DJFW must not equate absence of authorship with absence of image. A character can be fully recognizable at the level of silhouette, hair, costume, or face and still contribute almost nothing causal to the work.

This is one of the clearest cases yet for separating **presence** from **authorship**.

## 7. Voice fidelity

Voice evidence is **none / unusable for detailed assessment under the safety boundary**.

No line-level voice score should be reverse-engineered from the fact that character designs are present. Behavioral and relational grammar are instead judged structurally, and both are extremely weak.

Primary pressure: **interchangeable fetish/scenario engine**.

Secondary pressure: **adult-control/coercive framing at categorical level**.

The work is valuable not as a transformation of any one canon character but as a control for the point where transformation becomes **replacement**.
