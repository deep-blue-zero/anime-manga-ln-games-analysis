## 2. Chisato baseline

Chisato's baseline is not simple cheerfulness. She is a state-trained combatant who has interpreted her assigned capacities against their original purpose.

Key baseline traits for this case:

- elastic role performance;
- service voice, teasing, mock aggression, and sudden sincerity;
- refusal to let violence or mission context dictate a single true self;
- moral seriousness that often contracts into simple, plain language;
- social fearlessness paired with difficulty receiving stillness, vulnerability, and reciprocal care;
- intense capacity to perform roles without being wholly consumed by them.

This matters because `Junketsu no Sainou` pressures precisely the part of Chisato that usually gives her freedom: her ability to turn situations into playful roles. The fanwork asks what happens when the assigned role involves an intimacy/service domain where performance no longer fully protects her from embarrassment.

## 3. Takina baseline

Takina's baseline is containment, not emotional absence.

Key baseline traits for this case:

- literalness;
- procedural thought;
- complete explanations;
- competence anxiety;
- controlled speech and action;
- directness under pressure;
- difficulty with implicit social meaning;
- capacity for intense violence or unilateral action when Chisato is threatened;
- emotional development as expanded bandwidth rather than personality replacement.

The fanwork is strongest when it preserves this structure. Takina does not simply become a generic seducer or passive partner. She often converts the premise into a problem of instruction, competence, direct preference, and Chisato-specific attachment.

## 4. Chisato/Takina dyad baseline

The canon relationship is best summarized as:

> influence without ownership.

Chisato expands Takina's life without requiring Takina to imitate her. Takina later protects Chisato's chosen place and future without fully adopting Chisato's nonlethality. Their intimacy is tactical, linguistic, domestic, embodied, and emotionally central, while canon names it primarily as `相棒` rather than explicit romance.

The dyad's key grammar:

- Chisato expands conversation; Takina compresses it.
- Chisato performs; Takina specifies.
- Chisato invites; Takina verifies.
- Chisato plays with roles; Takina turns feeling into procedure.
- Their relationship grows through coordination, not identity merger.

## 5. Fanwork pressure against baseline

`Junketsu no Sainou` applies professional/service/intimacy pressure through an absurd DA-style mission frame.

The fanwork's premise is structurally apt for Lycoris because DA already treats girls, bodies, uniforms, talents, secrecy, and social presentation as operational resources. The doujin exaggerates this into a training premise where inexperience itself becomes an institutional category.

This is the core baseline test:

> Does the work merely use Chisato and Takina as bodies inside a scenario, or does it transform their known role/skill/relationship grammar into the scenario?

First-pass answer: the main dyad does preserve enough grammar to be meaningful, though not without genre flattening.

## 11. Second-pass bonus/afterword comparison

The bonus/afterword pass strengthens the baseline comparison because it gives both authorial and in-story evidence for the same dyadic structure identified in the first pass.

### 11.1 Authorial statement as method evidence

4why's afterword explicitly identifies the original Chisato/Takina relationship as interesting and frames the book's method as emphasizing each character's strong field in order to create stronger gap and exchange. This is unusually useful for DJFW because it makes the fanwork's implicit character strategy explicit: Chisato and Takina are not simply assigned to a generic yuri R18 frame; their respective domains are amplified to generate interaction.

This confirms the baseline comparison's central claim. Chisato's side of the dyad is role flexibility, enthusiasm, improvisation, and social performance. Takina's side is directness, suspicion, procedural control, correction, and Chisato-specific focus. The bonus material repeats this pattern rather than abandoning it.

### 11.2 ChickeIII's IF-route comment

ChickeIII's afterword comments on disliking IF routes, especially when character survival/death is involved, because deaths affect the larger scenario and accumulated human relationships. This does not make the doujin canon-compatible, but it does mean the work is conscious of canon consequence. It helps explain why the supporting-character material is framed as an IF-adjacent continuity branch rather than pure continuity erasure.

For baseline comparison, this matters because Lycoris Recoil is heavily relationship-consequence driven. The afterword shows that the authors understand at least at a meta-level that characters are embedded in relational histories, not interchangeable dolls.

### 11.3 Bonus comic: Takina as brake, not rejection

The p068-p075 bonus comic contains boundary-testing material that remains outside detailed analysis. Its usable character evidence is Takina's role as moral/procedural interrupter. Chisato expands the training/service premise recklessly; Takina stops the expansion, names it as wrong, and reasserts the boundary.

The important dyadic detail is that Takina's boundary-setting does not erase desire or attachment. She rejects the public/service extension but preserves the private Chisato/Takina training frame. This is very Takina-shaped: she can say no absolutely to a category while remaining intensely focused on Chisato within the acceptable dyadic frame.
