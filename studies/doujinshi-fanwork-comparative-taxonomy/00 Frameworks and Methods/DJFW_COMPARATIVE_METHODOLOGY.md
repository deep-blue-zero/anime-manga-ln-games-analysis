# DJFW_COMPARATIVE_METHODOLOGY.md

## Purpose

This document governs how DJFW compares fanworks across franchises, genres, and audience traditions.

The core method is:

`baseline character grammar -> fanwork transformation pressure -> observed alteration -> taxonomy placement -> ledger update`.

## Non-collapse rule

Do not collapse these judgments into one score:

- character-writing value;
- relationship-writing value;
- cultural-anthropological value;
- symbolic/literary value;
- baseline representativeness;
- personal taste;
- moral comfort or discomfort.

A work may be weak as character writing and strong as cultural evidence. A work may be ethically ugly but structurally revealing. A work may be all-ages and still flatten character voice.

## Method layers

1. **Source boundary** — what exact work, edition, translation, pages, or package is being analyzed?
2. **Baseline readiness** — how well do we know the character or pairing before judging fidelity?
3. **Transformation pressure** — what does the fanwork do to the character: jealousy, name intimacy, domesticity, R18 intensification, coercion, parody, AU displacement, post-canon repair, game-mechanic literalization, etc.?
4. **Voice component analysis** — surface voice, behavioral grammar, relational grammar, and subjecthood/interiority.
5. **Outcome classification** — voice-preserving, voice-intensifying, voice-flattening, voice-erasing, or voice-annihilating as theme.
6. **Comparative placement** — how does the case update or fit existing DJFW taxonomy?

## Evidence rule

A prior handoff can seed hypotheses and metadata. It is not page-level evidence. Strong claims about a work require the source package, an existing case artifact, or a precise evidence pointer.

## Baseline rule

Canon baselines remain in their original series roots. DJFW creates brief comparison surfaces and crosswalks, not duplicate source corpora.

## Scope rule

DJFW analysis is high-level and non-graphic. It focuses on structure, voice, subjecthood, genre logic, canon relation, audience orientation, and symbolic function.
