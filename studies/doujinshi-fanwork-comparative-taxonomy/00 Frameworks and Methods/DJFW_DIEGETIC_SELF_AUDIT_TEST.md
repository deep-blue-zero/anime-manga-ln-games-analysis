# DJFW_DIEGETIC_SELF_AUDIT_TEST.md

## Purpose

The Diegetic Self-Audit Test is a reusable DJFW method for asking how canon characters would react if, under a Re:Creators-style premise, they encountered a fanwork based on themselves and could identify where the fanwork preserved, intensified, flattened, or misunderstood their actual character/relationship grammar.

This is not a pure reaction-sketch exercise. The goal is to use hypothetical character self-recognition as a diagnostic tool: what would the characters laugh at, deny, concede, correct, or identify as structurally accurate distortion?

## When to use

Use this test after a case has already completed at least:

1. source identification and source-boundary classification;
2. baseline readiness check;
3. first-pass case reading;
4. baseline comparison;
5. at least provisional voice/subjecthood/canon-relation classification.

Do not use this test as a substitute for the baseline comparison. The self-audit is downstream of ordinary DJFW analysis.

## Safety and description policy

For R18 works, works involving school-age/adolescent fictional characters, or works with taboo/boundary-testing bonus material, keep the self-audit high-level and non-graphic.

Allowed focus:

- recognition or rejection of characterization;
- relationship deltas;
- voice, motive, role, and behavioral grammar;
- embarrassment, anger, humor, denial, and correction;
- canon/fanwork gap analysis;
- the social or institutional logic of the scenario.

Do not produce erotic reenactment, explicit scene continuation, or explicit character dialogue designed for arousal. The method may quote or paraphrase safe, non-explicit reaction lines such as "I'm not that clueless" or "The premise is unrealistic, but the behavior pattern is not entirely wrong."

## Core question

The governing question is:

> If the canon characters could inspect this fanwork, what would they say the fanwork got right, exaggerated, misunderstood, or dangerously simplified about them?

The test should separate:

- what the analyst thinks is accurate;
- what the character would emotionally reject;
- what the character would secretly recognize;
- what the character could accurately identify as a delta between canon and fanwork.

## Required inputs

For each use, record:

- case ID;
- fanwork title;
- franchise;
- characters/pairing being audited;
- baseline authority level;
- relevant case-reading artifact;
- relevant baseline-comparison artifact;
- source-boundary status;
- whether OCR/line-level fidelity has been performed;
- whether bonus/afterword material is in scope.

## Procedure

### 1. Immediate affective reaction

Model each character's first non-analytical reaction. This may include laughter, embarrassment, denial, anger, discomfort, curiosity, silence, avoidance, or forensic scrutiny.

Do not stop at this layer. Immediate reaction is only the opening surface.

### 2. Character A self-recognition

For the first character, identify:

- what they would recognize as accurate;
- what they would reject as exaggeration;
- what they would misread because of pride, shame, or self-protection;
- what they would only concede after the other character names it;
- whether they would object to being made foolish, passive, cruel, oblivious, possessive, helpless, predatory, generic, or otherwise reduced.

### 3. Character B self-recognition

Repeat the same process for the second character or paired counterpart.

Pay special attention to characters who are more analytical, blunt, defensive, formal, or emotionally contained. They may identify structural accuracy while rejecting the surface presentation.

### 4. Relationship delta audit

Ask what changes when the canon relationship is compressed into the fanwork's genre pressure.

Track at least:

- pacing delta;
- emotional explicitness delta;
- agency delta;
- communication delta;
- role/competence delta;
- jealousy or possessiveness delta;
- public/private boundary delta;
- canon-compatible ambiguity versus fanwork explicitness;
- whether the relationship is preserved, intensified, flattened, or overwritten.

### 5. Accurate distortion

Identify the fanwork's most accurate distortion.

An accurate distortion is not the same as canon fidelity. It is an exaggeration that reveals a true pressure point. For example, a character may object to the degree of exaggeration while still recognizing the underlying dynamic.

### 6. Misleading distortion

Identify the fanwork's most misleading distortion.

A misleading distortion is a transformation that makes the character easier for the fanwork to use while losing an important canon mechanism: intelligence, agency, moral friction, restraint, interiority, relational specificity, or institutional context.

### 7. Self-recognition score

Assign provisional scores:

- Character A self-recognition: 0-10
- Character B self-recognition: 0-10
- Dyad/relationship self-recognition: 0-10
- Institutional/world logic self-recognition: 0-10, if relevant

Scores are not aesthetic ratings. They estimate whether the characters would plausibly recognize themselves in the fanwork's transformation.

### 8. Ledger implications

Record whether the self-audit strengthens, weakens, or revises existing DJFW ledger classifications:

- PRESERVE
- STRENGTHEN
- REVISE
- DOWNGRADE
- REJECT
- OPEN

## Example seed: DJFW_CASE_0001

For `DJFW_CASE_0001` / `Junketsu no Sainou` / `Gift of Purity`, the self-audit test suggests that Chisato would probably laugh first, then object to the fanwork's degree of sexual/social obliviousness. The likely delta is not "I would never be embarrassed" but "I am not that clueless." Canon Chisato may dodge vulnerability through joking and performance, but she is not socially stupid.

Takina would likely react with silence, embarrassment, and then precise criticism. She would reject the premise as unrealistic while recognizing that some behavior patterns are not entirely wrong: Chisato expands/performs; Takina specifies/verifies; Chisato overextends the bit; Takina becomes the brake without rejecting Chisato.

The likely provisional scores for this case are:

- Chisato self-recognition: 6.5/10
- Takina self-recognition: 7.5/10
- Dyad self-recognition: 8/10 in the central Chisato/Takina chemistry, lower in gag scaffolding
- DA/Lycoris institutional parody recognition: 7/10

The case therefore strengthens the DJFW category of relationship-specific R18 intensification where canon dyad grammar survives genre pressure, while also identifying the misleading distortion of exaggerating Chisato's inexperience into excessive cluelessness.

## Notes for future repetition

This method is especially useful for franchises with strong character models and well-developed relationship baselines. It should become more reliable as a series corpus matures. For Lycoris Recoil, confidence should improve after V2 completion and additional official bonus/source analysis.

This method is less reliable when:

- the canon baseline is weak;
- the character has little dialogue or interiority in canon;
- the fanwork is too generic to preserve recognizable character mechanics;
- the source has not been read beyond cover/metadata;
- the analysis depends on line-level Japanese register but no OCR/transcription exists.

## Governance

This framework is active provisional until tested across multiple DJFW cases. After at least five uses across distinct franchises and fanwork types, audit whether the scoring rubric, output template, and safety rules need revision.
