# DJFW_CHARACTER_VOICE_AND_TRANSFORMATION_TAXONOMY.md

## Core question

What does the fanwork preserve, intensify, flatten, erase, or thematize about authored character voice under romantic, erotic, comic, domestic, parodic, grotesque, coercive, AU, or genre-specific pressure?

## Character voice components

### Surface voice

Speech register, rhythm, honorifics, pronouns, names, catchphrases, silence, hesitation, comic timing, and language-switching habits.

### Behavioral grammar

How the character reacts under pressure: embarrassment, anger, jealousy, praise, sincerity, correction, vulnerability, desire, fear, rejection, failure, or public exposure.

### Relational grammar

How this character treats this specific other person: rivalry, trust, protectiveness, possessiveness, admiration, guilt, dependency, teasing, private ritual, old wound, power asymmetry, or name usage.

### Subjecthood and interiority

Whether the character remains emotionally legible, internally meaningful, agentic, contradictory, and capable of interpreting what is happening.

## Voice outcomes

- **voice-preserving**: the work applies new pressure while preserving deeper character grammar.
- **voice-intensifying**: one true trait is amplified but remains grounded.
- **voice-flattening**: one trait, meme, role, or archetype replaces the fuller character.
- **voice-erasing**: the character becomes a body, role, commodity, scenario vessel, roster entry, or trope function.
- **voice-annihilating as theme**: loss of voice/subjecthood is the work's argument, critique, or horror.

## Baseline readiness states

- **A**: established baseline.
- **B**: strong but domain-limited baseline.
- **C**: partial baseline.
- **D**: insufficient baseline.

Do not make stronger fidelity claims than the baseline supports.

## Transformation pressure examples

Confession, jealousy, reassurance, domesticity, name intimacy, public embarrassment, romantic exclusivity, R18 intensification, service role, professional access, role reversal, coercive leverage, hypnosis, corruption, NTR/betrayal, body transformation, magical accident, AU relocation, parody exaggeration, post-canon grief repair, gacha bond/oath/producer/commander mechanics, roster/catalog completion, and parasocial collapse.

## Subjecthood outcomes

- **subject-preserving**: the character remains a recognizable person.
- **subject-reducing**: the character is narrowed into a role or function.
- **subject-erasing**: the work treats the character almost entirely as usable material.
- **subject-annihilating as theme**: the work thematizes the destruction of subjecthood.

## Use rule

The summary label comes after component analysis. A work can imitate surface voice while flattening behavioral grammar. A parody can distort surface voice while preserving deeper relational logic. A horror work can erase voice deliberately and still be analytically meaningful.
