## 1. Baseline used for comparison

The governing Hiro baseline is not generic "frail idol" memory. The GKM baseline frames Hiro as an idol whose mismatch becomes authored form: weak body, low-force/deadpan speech, startling sincerity, unusual appetite for difficulty, bodily constraint, and a relationship to Producer that turns private self-experiment into accountable attachment.

For DJFW purposes, the main preservation tests are:

1. Does the work preserve Hiro's **low-force, deadpan, internally coherent oddness**, or merely make her helpless?
2. Does bodily frailty remain **concrete but authored**, or is it consumed as a fetish mechanism?
3. Does Producer function as **witness / handler / co-traveler / accountable partner**, or merely as access device?
4. Does the premise convert Hiro's mismatch into story form, or simply use her design/body as a vessel?
5. Does the work allow Hiro any agency through analysis, preference, curiosity, boundary, or chosen weirdness?

## 3. DJFW_CASE_0006 — `Ichijou 10-man Yen`

### Classification

**Pharmacological bodily-risk scenario / Hiro frailty-collapse intensification / low subjecthood preservation.**

### Evidence anchors

The readable visual setup indicates that Producer has given Hiro a capsule and is surprised by the strength of its effect. A late caption says that after the effect was used up, Hiro spent three days and nights wandering between life and death. This is not treated as a medically serious recovery arc; it functions as dark gag escalation.

### Hiro fidelity

This is the weakest Hiro case in the trio. It is not character-empty, because it knowingly targets a real Hiro pressure point: her small physical reserve and the dangerous comic boundary between "weak body" and "chosen difficulty." But the work mostly converts that pressure point into pharmacological overdrive.

Hiro's distinctive qualities are present as surface ingredients:

- frailty;
- bodily extremity;
- willingness to endure impossible states;
- strange affective intensity;
- Producer as person who creates or permits the situation.

But the work does not preserve enough of Hiro's analytical oddness, deliberate attraction to impossibility, deadpan reasoning, or accountable attachment to keep the scenario strongly subject-preserving. The mechanism is closer to **collapse-risk illustration** than full Hiro reconstruction.

### Producer relation

Producer is structurally important but ethically degraded. Rather than serving as responsible witness/co-traveler, he becomes the person whose action introduces the risky premise and then reacts anxiously to its consequences. This is useful for DJFW as a **negative Producer-grammar test**: a Hiro fanwork can retain her fragility while losing the relational accountability that makes the GKM Hiro/Producer relationship meaningful.

### Verdict

- Voice fidelity: **low to medium-low**
- Behavioral grammar: **medium-low**
- Subjecthood: **reduced**
- Taxonomic value: **high as collapse-risk example**
- Best use in DJFW: benchmark for **frail-body overdrive / pharmacological vulnerability / Producer irresponsibility**.

## 4. DJFW_CASE_0007 — `Shirayoi no Yado`

### Classification

**Occult mystery / folklore-horror fanwork / Hiro spirit-medium AU-extension / Producer-as-handler-and-witness / R18 exorcism metaphor.**

### OCR-supported frame

The opening warning states that the pairing is Hiro/P and that P has a face. It also says the work uses folk belief and historical background as fictional themes.

The character/setup page is the strongest OCR evidence. It frames Hiro as an idol with an extreme spirit-medium constitution who is repeatedly affected when visiting haunted locations. Her resulting "cult" popularity has grown enough that she is now shoulder-to-shoulder with top idols. The same page frames Producer as someone still being dragged around by Hiro after Hatsuboshi graduation, physically depleted by repeated exorcism-related incidents, and privately wishing he could reject all occult-related requests.

The inn premise is also story-specific: an old island inn associated with a zashiki-warashi-like presence, elite/political repeat use, missing Hiro fans, and an investigation requested by the inn.

The afterword/explanatory page strengthens the folklore machinery. It discusses Oshira-sama, worship obligations, curse logic, liminal boundary between visible/hidden worlds, a ritual timing at sunset, and the author's interest in drawing a cool exorcist Hiro.

### Hiro fidelity

This is the strongest work in the trio.

It does not merely put Hiro into a random R18 scenario. It turns Hiro's baseline weirdness into story grammar:

- her body is fragile but also metaphysically responsive;
- her unusual calm becomes occult competence;
- her mismatch with ordinary idol expectations becomes cultic/public charisma;
- her tendency toward impossible experiences becomes literal investigation into haunted/liminal spaces;
- her relationship with Producer becomes operational companionship under repeated abnormal strain.

This is an unusually strong example of **fanwork transformation without total flattening**. The premise is not canon, but the transformation pressure is Hiro-shaped.

### Producer relation

Producer is not a faceless self-insert. The work explicitly warns that P has a face, gives him a role in the occult setup, and frames him as exhausted, protective, and reluctantly entangled. That makes him closer to a story character than a pure access device.

This P is not simply the canonical route Producer either. The exorcism/occult premise drastically retools the relationship. But the work preserves one important GKM-like relation: Producer is the person whose life becomes bound to Hiro's impossible vocation, even when he would prefer a safer life.

### Other characters / ecology

This is mostly a Hiro/P work. Other social actors exist through the inn, fan community, missing-person premise, and authorial occult background. Their function is worldbuilding rather than character ensemble.

### Verdict

- Voice fidelity: **medium-high structurally; line-level medium until manual OCR correction**
- Behavioral grammar: **high**
- Subjecthood: **partially to strongly preserved within AU/R18 frame**
- Taxonomic value: **very high**
- Best use in DJFW: benchmark for **Hiro-specific AU extension** and **folklore premise as character grammar rather than costume**.

## 5. DJFW_CASE_0008 — `GakuMas Dosukebe Goudoushi - Gakuen Ikisugi Master 4`

### Classification

**R18 anthology / roster-fantasy / multi-character premise catalog / short Hiro body-modification segment.**

### Character scope

Metadata lists multiple Gakuen Idolmaster characters, including:

- Ume Hanami
- Hiro Shinosawa
- Rinami Himesaki
- Misuzu Hataya
- Saki Hanami
- Mao Arimura
- Lilja Katsuragi
- Temari Tsukimura
- Kaya Rinha
- Tsubame Amaya

The anthology is not a unified Hiro work. It should be analyzed as a **goudoushi roster object**, with individual segments treated separately.

### Hiro segment

The visible Hiro material appears around p028-p035. It is a short body-modification/piercing-oriented scenario. The speech that remains readable gives Hiro a surprisingly useful micro-voice pattern: she comments on placement, concealability, aesthetics, pain, and cuteness in a matter-of-fact, evaluative way.

This is not deep Hiro writing, but it is not wholly generic either. The strongest preservation point is that Hiro's reaction is not only passive distress. She is written as someone who can evaluate an unusual, painful, body-focused situation with strangely calm aesthetic reasoning. That is closer to Hiro than a pure helpless-object depiction would be.

However, the segment is still heavily visual/premise-driven and too short for rich character reconstruction. It preserves a sliver of Hiro's weird composure while allowing the anthology format to dominate.

### Other-character analysis

The anthology's broader character value is comparative rather than deep. It shows how a multi-character Gakumas R18 roster handles different idol bodies, archetypes, and gimmicks. This is useful for DJFW because it can reveal which characters collapse into a single visual premise and which retain individualized speech or situational logic.

At this pass, the other characters are not analyzed deeply enough for individual fidelity rankings. The correct next step would be segmenting the anthology by page ranges and identifying the character/pairing/artist for each segment before making character-specific claims.

### Verdict

- Voice fidelity for Hiro segment: **medium-low to medium**
- Behavioral grammar: **medium in micro-form; low for full character coverage**
- Subjecthood: **reduced but not erased**
- Taxonomic value: **high as roster/anthology object**
- Best use in DJFW: benchmark for **anthology micro-preservation vs roster flattening**.
