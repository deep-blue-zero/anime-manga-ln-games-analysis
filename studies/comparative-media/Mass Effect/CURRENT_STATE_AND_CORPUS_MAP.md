# Mass Effect — Current State and Corpus Map

## Purpose

This is the canonical first-read entrypoint for the **Mass Effect comparative analytical corpus**.

The project is housed with the wider analytical archive as **comparative media**, not because *Mass Effect* is anime or manga, but because its treatment of military professionalism, exceptional authority, coalition command, emergency sovereignty, civilizational survival, political legitimacy, and dirty-hands ethics provides a high-value reference point for analysis of military- and politics-themed works elsewhere in the corpus.

The current project is intentionally compact. It is **not yet a full game-by-game or mission-by-mission deep-reading project**, and no primary-source or sequential-reading architecture should be inferred from the existence of these character models.

## Canonical analytical responsibility

Current authority is concentrated in two character monographs:

- `01 Character Monographs/MASS_EFFECT_PARAGON_SHEPARD_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md`
- `01 Character Monographs/MASS_EFFECT_RENEGADE_SHEPARD_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md`

These define two controlled interpretive archetypes extracted from the trilogy's player-choice space. They are intended for repeatable comparative use rather than as claims that BioWare authored one perfectly fixed Paragon personality and one perfectly fixed Renegade personality.

## Current canonical formulations

### Paragon Shepard

**Key identifier:** `Power held in trust.`

Paragon Shepard is the Alliance/N7/Spectre commander whose extraordinary discretion remains bounded by duty, proportionality, truth, coalition responsibility, respect for persons, and internal ethical constraint. The governing comparative question is whether overwhelming discretionary power can remain morally disciplined without collapsing into passivity.

### Renegade Shepard

**Key identifier:** `Power accepted as burdened necessity.`

Renegade Shepard is the Alliance/N7/Spectre commander who treats survival as the precondition of other political and moral goods, accepts coercion, deterrence, secrecy, intimidation, and dirty hands as legitimate tools under existential pressure, and fears that moral purity can become a form of command failure. The governing comparative question is when necessary ruthlessness remains command and when it becomes tyranny.

## Relationship between the two authorities

The models share the same institutional foundation and differ primarily in their hierarchy of values under emergency conditions.

- Paragon Shepard fears becoming the thing being fought.
- Renegade Shepard fears losing because the commander was unwilling to become hard enough for the crisis.
- Paragon centers legitimacy, stewardship, trust, and preservation of the moral community.
- Renegade centers survival, mission success, deterrence, responsibility for outcomes, and willingness to bear guilt.

Neither archetype should be reduced to a good/evil binary or to mechanically choosing every color-coded dialogue option.

## Authority and retrieval order

For questions about Shepard as a comparative character model:

1. read this corpus map;
2. read the relevant Shepard monograph;
3. consult the companion Shepard monograph when contrast is analytically material;
4. escalate to game transcripts, mission dialogue, audiovisual evidence, or external documentation only when an exact in-game claim requires verification.

The monographs are **canonical interpretive authority inside this corpus**, but they are not substitutes for primary-source verification when exact dialogue, mission-state behavior, chronology, or authored game mechanics are at issue.

## Comparative use

The corpus is especially suited to comparisons involving:

- professional military ethics;
- civil-military legitimacy;
- exceptional or emergency authority;
- small-unit leadership scaled into political command;
- coalition-building across hostile groups;
- deterrence and coercion;
- dirty-hands ethics;
- survival versus legitimacy;
- humanitarian restraint versus military necessity;
- sovereignty and the temptation of benevolent domination;
- institutional failure under existential crisis.

This makes Shepard useful beside characters and works such as Yang Wen-li, Reinhard von Lohengramm, Paul von Oberstein, *86*, *Attack on Titan*, *Mobile Suit Gundam*, *Saga of Tanya the Evil*, and other works where military command intersects with political or moral legitimacy. Such comparisons remain cross-corpus analysis; they do not make Mass Effect part of the anime/manga source canon.
