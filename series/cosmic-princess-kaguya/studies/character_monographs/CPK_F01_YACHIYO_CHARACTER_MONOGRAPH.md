# Yachiyo

*Cosmic Princess Kaguya!* · Character monograph · Film F01

Yachiyo (月見ヤチヨ, read Runami Yachiyo) knows how to give people a way into a scene. She can welcome a newcomer, turn an interruption into part of a show, or tell a painful history as a story someone else might want to hear. Those are skills she keeps when the film discloses her history as Kaguya. The disclosure gives those skills a history and shows what it costs her to use them with Iroha. The clearest test comes on the mirror-sea with Iroha. Yachiyo begins with a song they share and ends by saying what she wants to feel and what she wants to eat with Iroha. The person accustomed to managing an encounter finally gives Iroha a wish she can answer. (00:00:26–00:01:46 · 01:25:30–01:26:14 · 02:05:56–02:06:50.) [Prologue and transition][prologue] · [Interruption and hosting][disrupt] · [Mirror-sea exchange][sea]

## Hosting is a way of acting

When Kaguya enters Tsukuyomi, Yachiyo helps set up an avatar and sends her on her way. At the concert, Yachiyo moves from exuberant thanks to the announcement of an event whose eligibility she can later explain. She reminds Kaguya that the Cup is for streamers. Kaguya decides to become one. Yachiyo’s authority in Tsukuyomi appears through that combination of welcome and rules. The public invitation creates an opportunity, and the rule makes the newcomer do something before she can use it. The Cup also ties audience response to consequential fan counts. Helping someone enter a system and knowing how to run it are related parts of Yachiyo’s character. (00:24:29–00:24:55, JA#0517–0525 · 00:31:01–00:31:35, JA#0629 onward · 00:34:26–00:34:41, JA#0724–0731.) [First entrance into Tsukuyomi][entrance] · [Concert and Cup rules][concert]

When Mami faints and Iroha and Kaguya need a third player, Yachiyo arrives onstage as if she had been cued. Her joking entrance and mention that she sided with Asahi’s team on an earlier occasion give her a part in the entertainment while filling the actual vacancy. She also runs out of lives in the match. In a later performance, a lunar interruption demands a different use of the same skill. Yachiyo rebukes the intruder, asks the audience to stay tuned, and tells her companions she will look into what happened. Read together, these scenes give a model of competent, public-facing improvisation with practical limits. The readiness to intervene is visible. The outcome remains contingent on what Yachiyo and the others can do. (00:56:18–00:56:43, JA#1239–1248 · 01:07:15–01:07:20, JA#1475–1477 · 01:25:30–01:26:14, JA#1868–1882.) [KASSEN entry and play][match] · [Lunar interruption][disrupt]

The bridge after the first concert is smaller in scale. Yachiyo calls Iroha back and thanks her for always coming, then says her given name. The thank-you singles out one person after a performance for the crowd. It initially works as an idol recognizing a regular. Once Iroha and the viewer have heard Yachiyo’s history, the same recognition also carries the possibility of long-awaited personal contact. The film gives the second reading weight without showing what Yachiyo thought through each second of the encounter. The chamber cue under the farewell is a distinct melody from the song that later connects Iroha to Kaguya’s survival. (00:34:48–00:35:12, JA#0733–0739.) ## What Yachiyo tells Iroha and what she holds back

Iroha reaches Yachiyo through Tsukuyomi infrastructure and asks whether she is Kaguya. Yachiyo begins again with the formula of a tale. In her telling, Iroha’s song reaches Kaguya after the return to the Moon. Kaguya completes her work and handover, takes a time vessel, and crashes on Earth roughly eight thousand years before the film’s main events. The story makes explicit that only the accompanying Doge obtains a body from the vessel’s remaining power. Kaguya interacts with the world through a sea slug. Later scenes mark encounters with people across eras and the emergence of a network presence as Yachiyo. The broad sequence matters to personality because it gives the practiced host a remembered life of seeking contact where the means to make it are limited. The film presents selected encounters and Yachiyo’s account, leaving the intervening routine largely open. (01:56:36–01:59:46, JA#2392–2435 · 02:03:19–02:04:54, JA#2492–2520.) [Journey and network history][journey] · [Memory sequence][memories]

Yachiyo tells Iroha to celebrate that they have met again, calls the history a fairy tale, and points out a view she loves. Iroha asks why she keeps smiling. Yachiyo says that is who she is, then apologizes for failing to bring Iroha along and calls herself an old woman. The Japanese SDH marks the voice as on the verge of tears there. The change takes place inside the same conversation as the jokes. The memories later include a woman who tells Kaguya to smile through pain. Iroha sees the connection and says she understands why Yachiyo looked so happy. That is Iroha’s recognition and a persuasive reading of the pattern. Yachiyo’s enjoyment of hosting, making music, and sharing a view can also be genuine. A smile alone gives no reliable guide to which feeling is foremost. (01:59:44–02:00:53, JA#2434–2453 · 02:03:54–02:04:38, JA#2501–2516.) [Disclosure and apology][apology] · [Memory and recognition][memories]

Yachiyo offers Iroha the option of forgetting if she wishes she had never learned the truth. When Iroha asks to hear everything, Yachiyo brings her to a familiar room and tells stories. Both responses give Iroha a choice about how close to come. There is a limit to what Yachiyo volunteers. Iroha eventually asks FUSHI to reveal what Yachiyo has left out. FUSHI defends the withholding, warns Iroha about the physical strain, and ultimately agrees. Yachiyo’s willingness to tell stories and FUSHI’s eventual cooperation are separate decisions. The film leaves room to read Yachiyo’s offer of forgetting as care for Iroha, reluctance to be seen, or both. It does not show her expressly consenting to the further disclosure. (02:00:53–02:03:14, JA#2453–2491.) [Reunion and withholding][reunion] · [FUSHI’s decision][gate]

## A wish she lets someone else answer

Yachiyo’s response on the mirror-sea differs from a story that tidies up painful material for a listener. Iroha says she wants to be with Kaguya. Yachiyo begins to sing alone, says she kept remembering, and Iroha joins in the subsequent phrases. The owner heard the voices without instrumental accompaniment. Yachiyo says the song kept her alive, then imagines the warmth of touch and says she would like to eat pancakes with Iroha again. That is a report about survival in her own words and a present desire addressed to a particular person. The mundane specificity matters. Public recognition, a remembered song, and a shared meal do different work in her life. The first two are already available to her when she asks for the third. Iroha answers by saying that the story still has more to go. (02:05:41–02:07:14, JA#2526–2541.) [Japanese expression ledger][sea] · [Owner’s reprise observation][reprise]

In the epilogue, Yachiyo speaks from a lab speaker, teases Iroha about becoming cheekier, and appears with Iroha and the physically embodied Kaguya at the final show. Yachiyo’s greeting says it has been only a week, and the three laugh. The figure Iroha met on the mirror-sea has a continuing place in their life alongside the one who wakes in the lab. The late pancake coda even lets Yachiyo return to the ordinary desire in her own playful register. The film supports shared history, present co-participation, and distinct appearances and lines. The operation that makes their co-presence possible and the extent of any shared present experience remain open. (02:08:46–02:09:55, JA#2568–2591 · 02:19:55–02:20:10, JA#2710–2716.) ## Japanese voice and delivery

- **Storyteller.** The first narration starts with 今は昔, catches itself with あれれ？, and goes on to describe a world a little ahead of the viewer’s own. Classical endings such as ありけり sit beside a modern description of a girl playing a game. In the later private account Yachiyo uses the same opening and describes the princess working on the Moon with the contemporary バリバリ社畜. The move from the old formula to present-day language makes the audience’s distance from the story adjustable. The later use can make a painful first-person history easier to tell in the third person. That effect is a reading of the scene. (00:00:26–00:01:46, JA#0001–0021 · 01:57:34–01:57:51, JA#2398–2403.) [Narration registers][prologue] · [Return account][journey]
- **Host and administrator.** As she closes the concert, Yachiyo uses the light ほいでは and the borrowed クローズ. When she explains who can enter the Cup, she follows a teasing question with ライバー限定なのです. The rule is precise inside an easy delivery frame. In the final lift, the Japanese SDH tags ヤッチョは先週ぶり～ to Yachiyo. The self-nickname and playful expression of recency suit a reunion whose participants have experienced very different waiting times. (00:34:05–00:34:36, JA#0716–0729 · 02:09:33–02:09:55, JA#2583–2591.) [Concert and Cup rules][concert] · [Tagged lift line][lift]
- **Addressing Iroha privately.** The bridge isolates a personal thank-you and the given name 彩葉. At the disclosure the SDH describes Yachiyo’s line about being an old woman as nearly tearful. On the mirror-sea, いつも思い出してた (“I kept remembering”) sits between singing turns. The wish to have pancakes ends in the conversational 食べたいなぁ. In these scenes Yachiyo can recognize a visitor, apologize for the terms of a reunion, and express a wish. Cadence and intensity are best carried by the particular performance sample. The closing pre-credit narration includes untagged material with unresolved speaker attribution, so the tagged prologue, bridge, mirror-sea, and lift furnish the firmer comparisons. (00:35:06–00:35:12, JA#0738–0739 · 02:00:21–02:00:38, JA#2445–2449 · 02:06:05–02:06:50, JA#2530–2537.) ## Behavioral reconstruction

Set the audience and the amount of knowledge each participant has. Yachiyo can remember experiences that the young Kaguya has yet to reach. Before the reveal, Iroha can understand her as an idol who knows her name. After the reveal, the same two people can discuss loss and remembered experience directly. The epilogue adds a collaborator on familiar terms. These are differences in who can say what to whom. The long past supplies memories and learned practices, with few observations of an ordinary day outside a host or intimate role. The already seen scenes provide a consistency check. A purely cheerful host would have difficulty accounting for the apology and the request to eat together. A purely guarded host would have difficulty accounting for the help she gives in public and the openness she attempts in private. A host who knows and controls every outcome would make the loss of a match and the missed return hard to explain. Test the next scene first on whom Yachiyo is trying to help, what she can change, and what she can bear to reveal. The form of the joke or address follows from that choice.
