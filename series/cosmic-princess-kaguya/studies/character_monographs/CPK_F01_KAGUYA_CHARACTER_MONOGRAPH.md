# Kaguya

*Cosmic Princess Kaguya!* · Character monograph · Film F01

Kaguya (かぐや) hears the ending of the princess story and objects on the princess’s behalf. Going back to the Moon may finish the tale, but she asks how the person being sent back could possibly be happy. She applies a similar test to her own life. A rule, schedule, or ending interests her less than what someone can still do inside it. Her early response is to make something happen immediately. As her life with Iroha acquires costs and commitments, the important question becomes whose happiness she can account for when she acts. The girl who declares that she will make her own happy ending eventually chooses the form of her farewell and, after hearing Iroha again, completes a handover before trying to return. (00:13:15–00:14:36, JA#0263–0286 · 01:33:01–01:34:53, JA#2031–2061 · 01:57:34–01:58:10, JA#2398–2409.) ## Appetite, learning, and the cost of initiative

Kaguya learns through use. The fast-growing girl watches and listens to media, then tries phrases in conversation. When Iroha rushes away from her friends and promises to make it up to them, Kaguya promptly repeats the phrase. When Iroha later explains what she risked by asking to live alone, Kaguya echoes her wording again. What Kaguya can reproduce and what she has understood can be quite far apart. She learns that going outside can expose her identity at the same time that she learns how much she resents staying in. This produces a character who can sound familiar in Japanese before she understands why Iroha is speaking that way. (00:09:35–00:10:30, JA#0177–0193 · 00:19:44–00:20:03, JA#0407–0416 · 00:21:47–00:22:56, JA#0455–0484.) [Arrival, media, and household evidence][early]

Cooking is a useful ordinary-life test of that pattern. Kaguya buys and improvises freely, makes food Iroha enjoys, and asks what she wants to eat next. Kaguya also spends from Iroha’s funds without agreement and entertains an illicit shortcut when money comes up. Iroha’s pleasure in what they eat and her objection to the spending belong to the same encounter. The two responses explain why Kaguya’s company can transform the apartment and strain the household at once. Later, Iroha receives a transfer labeled as repayment for what Kaguya spent, with an apology. The record of repayment makes a specific repair visible. Kaguya can remember and address an obligation even when the two can no longer share the room. (00:20:01–00:22:17, JA#0415–0467 · 01:48:31–01:50:00, FRM-11.) [Early spending and cooking][spending] · [Household funds and repayment][household]

The fever scene supplies an intermediate step. Before KASSEN, Kaguya sees how hot Iroha is, contacts school and work about her absence, and makes ginger-and-egg porridge. Her first response to illness combines useful logistics with the fear that Iroha might die, partly because humans die so readily in the movies she has seen. She acknowledges having asked Iroha to do too much. The lines about making the bed soft, the recipe she recites, and the worry she blurts out all take place in the same visit. Kaguya has learned something about Iroha’s particular burden even as she is still calibrating how human illness works. The scene earns both her practical attentiveness and the disproportion in her reaction. (00:47:45–00:51:04, JA#1057–1121.) [Fever sequence][fever] · [Chronology and household reciprocity][household]

## A partner in making things

Kaguya makes a failed first attempt at a jingle and turns to Iroha for help. In asking Iroha to produce an original song with her, she uses the happy ending she wants as a persuasive appeal. Iroha calls the appeal unfair and joins her. The encouragement is effective because Kaguya can hear and want something in Iroha’s compositions that Iroha herself has put aside. The sequence also shows how Kaguya’s enthusiasm can make someone else feel responsible for fulfilling it. During KASSEN she puts her creativity to a more reciprocal use. She and Iroha share a plan, exchange weapons and roles, argue playfully during it, and still have to live with losing the match. The crowd’s pleasure in their teamwork matters to the wider Cup outcome. (00:35:13–00:40:08, JA#0740–0835 · 01:03:39–01:12:10, especially JA#1483–1537.) [Jingle and producer scenes][creator] · [Match and fan outcome][match]

When Iroha tells Kaguya about the unfinished song she began with her father, Kaguya offers to make lyrics and immediately tries a line that sets 壁ドン alongside a reference to their daily life. She also says she is sure Iroha’s father would be pleased. That confidence is Kaguya’s way of helping and her own guess about someone she did not know. What she can give Iroha is a line suited to the shared life they have now and an invitation to proceed. Iroha answers with tentative agreement, then takes responsibility for finishing what can be ready for the show. Kaguya’s contribution is specific enough to account for her impact without granting her sole credit for the completed song. (01:37:22–01:38:50, JA#2110–2136.) [Shared composition scene][composition] · [One-chorus decision][one]

## Leaving on her own terms

When Kaguya says that the Moon will come for her, Iroha suggests running again. Kaguya proposes a different ending. They will have as much fun as they can, she will give a show, and the audience can see her off. She says at the same time that she wanted to sing with Iroha and make more songs. The festival fireworks in this exchange register in concrete terms for Kaguya, through the impact in her body and the smell of smoke. Her plan preserves the value of experiences like this while she expects to lose them. It can be read as a way of protecting Iroha and the audience from a farewell organized solely around grief. It can also be read as a way of giving herself an action she can control when departure appears inevitable. The scene gives both readings room. (01:33:01–01:34:53, JA#2031–2061.) [Return and farewell decision][parting]

Her staged gratitude and her address to Iroha in the room are two parts of that decision. Onstage, after taking a breath, she asks fans to send her off happily. Much later the battle gives way to a private interval. The owner’s playback records only her tearful voice as she says Iroha’s name and 大好き. The film then returns to the spectacle and the departure. Kaguya can state her feeling in a few words when the audience changes. A reconstruction should preserve both that quiet directness and her ability to give an audience a cue for how to go on together. (01:41:14–01:41:31, JA#2181–2186 · 01:47:34–01:47:58, JA#2264–2268.) [Graduation Live][show] · [Farewell playback][whisper]

Kaguya’s return attempt gives her promise a second form. Iroha sings from the balcony. By Yachiyo’s later account, Kaguya finishes her work and formally hands it over before leaving the Moon with Doge. She takes a time vessel, collides with a rock, and reaches Earth roughly eight thousand years earlier. An early declaration of freedom and this later effort both support her willingness to seek an alternative. The handover gives the later choice an additional constraint that she meets. In the ten-years-later reunion, she reacts to pancakes almost immediately, while Iroha explains that taste still needs work. She also expresses impatience at the delay. The familiar appetite has survived a great deal. How the newly embodied Kaguya will handle more sustained delays belongs to the future the film opens. (01:53:18–01:54:49 · 01:57:34–01:58:27, JA#2398–2413 · 02:09:04–02:09:30, JA#2573–2582.) [Balcony and journey][journey] · [New body and taste][lab]

## Japanese voice and delivery

- **Play and protest.** The tablet objections build with the borrowed English expression バッドエンド, the intensifier 超～, and the conversational じゃん. In the household negotiation Kaguya also uses her name about herself (JA#0478), and in the sickroom she says 私も when she acknowledges adding to Iroha’s strain (JA#1087). Her name is one available self-reference. First person and quick imitation belong to the same repertoire. At the festival, the coined 楽しキングダム extends excitement into wordplay. (00:13:21–00:14:04, JA#0263–0275 · 00:22:31–00:22:56, JA#0472–0484 · 00:49:01–00:49:29, JA#1085–1091 · 01:29:45–01:30:28, JA#1971–1981.) [Tablet wording][tablet] · [Domestic and festival circumstances][daily]
- **Care and embarrassment.** In the sickroom, ふかふか describes the comfort Kaguya has laid out and becomes a verb-like invitation for Iroha to get comfortable. The SDH marks a rapid stretch of talk while Kaguya gives the recipe. She can follow a concrete report about calling off work with exaggerated fear and a comic explanation of where the fear came from. A useful care scene lets her take a real step and allows her wording to remain playful. (00:48:09–00:50:57, JA#1063–1120.) [Sickroom evidence][fever]
- **The person addressed matters.** With Iroha after the concert, the direct 彩葉 好き leads to a playful mention of marriage. With fans at the farewell, she thanks them and asks for a happy send-off. Alone with Iroha she says 彩葉… 大好き in a tearful whisper. Those are three different uses of direct address, at different levels of exposure. The tagged closing question at 02:10:03 also gives her a playful way to reopen the story for an audience. The later untagged narration has less secure speaker attribution and contributes no additional voice sample here. (01:24:04–01:24:27, JA#1849–1857 · 01:41:14–01:41:31, JA#2181–2186 · 01:47:40–01:47:43, JA#2266 · 02:10:03, JA#2592.) ## Behavioral reconstruction

Track the early guest, the collaborator who has seen Iroha’s overwork, the performer facing a deadline for departure, and the reunited Kaguya with a new body. Yachiyo’s account adds evidence about the journey after the Moon’s retrieval. The earlier Kaguya who admires Yachiyo lacks the later knowledge about their relationship. At the final show both figures appear and have distinguishable lines. Any account of what new memories or sensations they share exceeds what the film resolves. The film gives a retrospective check on the model. A rule that makes her repeat the early unauthorized purchase whenever she wants to help would miss her sickroom intervention and the care she takes to repay Iroha. A rule that makes public bravado her sole way of handling fear fails in the room at the farewell. A rule that has her obey every outside obligation quietly misses the work she puts into finding a way back. Those failures are more informative than how many youthful endings appear in a line of invented dialogue. [Household sequence][household] · [Private farewell][whisper] · [Lunar journey][journey]
