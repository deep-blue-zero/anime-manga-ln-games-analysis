# Iroha Sakayori

*Cosmic Princess Kaguya!* · Character monograph · Film F01

Iroha Sakayori (酒寄彩葉) begins with a workable answer to most problems. She decides what the problem will cost, takes responsibility for it, and fits it into a schedule that was already full. That answer lets her live in Tokyo, protect Kaguya, and make music under pressure. It also lets her describe a fever in terms of the shifts and study time she is about to lose. Iroha’s development changes what she counts as a responsibility and who she lets share it. By the end, staying with Kaguya can determine what work she takes on, and rest can count as part of that work. The film shows both changes through decisions about money, time, and help. (00:48:06–00:51:40 · 02:05:14–02:07:14 · 02:08:46–02:09:04.) [Fever and household evidence][household] · [Adult Iroha at work][lab]

## Independence and its price

The opening gives Iroha a social life before it gives her a crisis. She celebrates a good shot with Roka and Mami, answers a teacher’s grammar question in polite, technically precise Japanese, then lets out an unguarded あぶな～ (“that was close”). In a later school scene, Roka and Mami repay her for notes with food and urge her to rest. Their exchange shows an existing circuit of ordinary help. Iroha is good at the things people rely on her for, and other people already notice when maintaining that standard costs her too much. (00:01:15–00:02:09, JA#0010–0030 · 00:17:53–00:19:49, JA#0357–0409.) [Opening evidence][opening] · [School and friendship scene][early]

In the sickroom, Iroha tells Kaguya that she insisted on moving out and was allowed to do so if she covered tuition and living expenses herself. She remembers waking in an empty room and feeling a surge of resolve at the idea of making a life by her own effort. When Kaguya tells her to rest, Iroha explains that she has planned everything so tightly that several days off could put her behind and jeopardize her scholarship. Both accounts matter. Iroha’s pride in her decision is explicit, as is the fragility of the bargain she accepted. A model that treats her worry as simple perfectionism would miss what lateness, illness, or lost income can put at risk. (00:48:06–00:50:27, JA#1062–1110.) [Sickroom dialogue][sick] · [Conditional agreement with Momiji][terms]

Iroha carries Momiji’s expectations into how she accounts for her time, yet exercises judgment well before their late conversation. Finding the infant Kaguya, Iroha considers the police and worries that she looks like a kidnapper, then takes the baby out of what she judges to be an unsafe situation. Later, she allows the older-looking Kaguya to stay until someone comes for her and specifies three conditions about visibility, going outside, and interrupting her work. The conditions make room for an obligation Iroha has already chosen to take seriously. At the festival she exercises a different form of restraint. Kaguya asks whether Iroha wanted to tell her that she could never understand. Iroha recalls Momiji putting that barrier between them and says she did not want to say it. Her conduct can be guarded and independently chosen at the same time. (00:04:50–00:06:52 · 00:22:29–00:22:56, JA#0471–0484 · 01:32:02–01:32:58, JA#2016–2030.) [Rescue and house rules][early] · [Festival exchange][festival]

## What she learns to ask for

During KASSEN, Asahi reads Iroha through the childhood pattern he remembers. He expects her to handle the danger herself. Iroha and Kaguya swap weapons and roles, and the two sustain the exchange with joking asides even as they execute the maneuver. They lose the match to Black onyX and win the Cup on fan acquisition. Taken together, the outcomes show where Iroha has changed without making the tactic proof of general superiority. Afterward she asks Asahi to guarantee a lease and turns down his joking offer of an entire house. The request identifies a specific barrier to moving in with Kaguya. Iroha accepts the help that clears it. (01:03:39–01:13:20, JA#1416–1601.) [KASSEN outcome and delegation][match] · [Lease request][lease]

Iroha sets a similar limit in music. When the deadline for Kaguya’s Graduation Live leaves a second verse beyond reach, she settles on one chorus. She has already reopened the unfinished piano file she made with her father, Tomohisa. Only later, when she hears Yachiyo’s bridge, does she voice recognition of the shared melody. Her decision about the chorus concerns what the two collaborators can make in the time remaining. The subsequent recital on the balcony comes after a different decision. Iroha rejects the ending she has been offered, calls Momiji, and finishes the song. The call withholds Iroha’s words and lets Momiji’s permission be heard. The disclosure establishes that Iroha spoke and that Momiji made room for her request. The specific request remains unavailable. (01:17:20–01:17:42 · 01:38:44–01:39:14, JA#2136–2147 · 01:51:33–01:53:20, JA#2312–2341.) [Music and its deadlines][music] · [Phone call and completion][loss]

The same insistence that helps Iroha reopen the future can make her push past another person’s limits. She supports the farewell Kaguya asks for while preparing an intervention with their friends. Much later she asks Yachiyo to tell her what happened across the intervening years. When she suspects omissions, she presses FUSHI to show her the memories despite a warning about what her body might withstand. Yachiyo welcomes her interest and tells her stories. The film supplies no express permission from Yachiyo for the additional access FUSHI grants. Iroha’s urgency is part of the portrait. In a reconstruction, the fact that a decision follows from her devotion and the question of whether it respects someone’s boundary have to be assessed on their own evidence. (01:33:01–01:37:20 · 01:41:14–01:42:00 · 02:00:53–02:04:25, JA#2453–2516.) [Farewell and resistance][resistance] · [Access to Yachiyo’s memories][memory]

On the mirror-sea, Iroha first names achievements that would let her live after the loss. She has grown and can speak with her mother. Then a long interval of crying opens onto かぐやと いたい (“I want to be with Kaguya”). She tells Yachiyo the thing she wants in the fewest words the scene affords. The academic and family gains remain credible because she can name them separately from what she wants with Kaguya. The epilogue gives the wish a material consequence. Iroha takes support from others, works on a physical body, and calls it a day when her shift ends. That last choice is a small, visible answer to the girl who expected to go straight from a fever to work. (02:05:14–02:07:14, JA#2521–2541 · 02:07:50–02:09:30, especially JA#2571–2572.) ## Japanese voice and delivery

- **School and unfamiliar demands.** Iroha moves from an excited オッケー and イェーイ among peers to the explanatory です of a classroom answer. At home, when the fast-growing Kaguya asks for milk, Iroha delivers the service formulas 承知です… (“Understood”) and 少々お待ちくださいませ (“Please wait a moment”). The mismatch between a formal service frame and a girl who has grown overnight carries the joke. Treat that formality as a response to a particular situation. (00:01:15–00:02:09, JA#0010–0030 · 00:10:18–00:10:31, JA#0188–0193.) [Opening witness][opening] · [Arrival and early household scenes][early]
- **A finite task or an awkward offer.** Iroha shortens a production problem to ワンコーラスでいくか (“Let's go with one chorus”). When Kaguya raises marriage, Iroha starts with まあ and proposes splitting living costs as a condition for staying together. In the lease scene she frames the request with the tentative 保証人になってもらえないかと… and follows Asahi’s excessive counteroffer with a crisp 結構です. These are different speech acts. The recurring move is to make a proposal concrete enough to decide. The reply about the household also retains the affection in the prospect of living together. (01:13:00–01:13:15, JA#1596–1601 · 01:24:08–01:24:27, JA#1850–1857 · 01:38:44–01:38:50, JA#2136.) [Lease request][lease] · [Household reply][household] · [One-chorus decision][one]
- **With Kaguya and under loss.** At the festival the SDH marks Iroha’s voice as close to tears. Her 言いたくなかったの gives the crucial difference in meaning. The negative volitional says she did not want to say the hurtful thing. Later, ないっしょ！ uses a colloquial ending to refuse the story’s apparent end. The mirror-sea line has a simpler form again. In the balcony performance, the owner’s direct playback records initially exposed singing, an atmospheric layer, and then the full arrangement. The musical arrangement gives Iroha a way to address Kaguya across a separation that ordinary speech cannot cross in the film. (01:32:43–01:32:58, JA#2026–2030 · 01:51:39–01:51:47, JA#2313–2314 · 01:53:18–01:54:17 · 02:05:41, JA#2526.) [Japanese expression ledger][voice] · [Balcony playback][balcony]
- **Ten years later.** Work vocabulary persists. Iroha evaluates how well the new body fits, teases Yachiyo about having overtaken her, and announces her departure from work with a drawn-out polite ending. 休むも仕事 (“Rest is part of the job”) puts a newly legitimate activity in a familiar language of duty. The scene provides one concrete adult routine and a model for playful speech with someone whose status no longer intimidates her. (02:08:46–02:09:24, JA#2568–2581.) [Laboratory and reunion][lab]

## Behavioral reconstruction

Use the scene’s version of Iroha. In the early Tokyo household she knows what another shift or unplanned expense costs and has yet to rely on Kaguya as a proven collaborator. During KASSEN, Iroha has seen Kaguya detect and respond to an immediate problem. After the farewell, loss and the discovery of Yachiyo’s history change what Iroha is willing to risk. The ten-years-later epilogue supplies an adult with established collaborators and a workday endpoint. Each later state can carry an earlier habit without inheriting all of its constraints. The following extrapolations give likely choices and response families. They remain untested on new film material.

A retrospective check helps locate the model’s limits. A rule that sends Iroha to work whenever she is needed fits her first response to waking with a fever and fails at the adult lab. A rule that makes her reluctant to seek all help fails when she asks for a guarantor. A version that reads every monetary reply as rejection fails to account for her proposed terms for living together. Ask whether the projected decision makes sense under Iroha’s current constraints before polishing the Japanese phrasing. A spontaneous confession to a new acquaintance or the exact words she used with Momiji would go beyond these samples.
