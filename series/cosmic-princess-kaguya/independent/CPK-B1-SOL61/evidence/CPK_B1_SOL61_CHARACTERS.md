## Iroha: changes in permission, not discovery of a hidden competent self

**I-S1, opening–23:31: competent and constrained.** She already works, studies, plays successfully, feeds someone vulnerable, considers outside help and maintains friendships. Her self-support condition and ideal of physical health make complaint feel illegitimate. Public competence hides costs; it is not fake competence. Her formal politeness toward an impossible infant and bureaucratic housing terms reveal ways of managing conflict through rules. The home budget and school timetable are real constraints. O0001–O0011 support this state.

**I-S2,23:31–54:11: invited creator under continuing pressure.** Fandom becomes shared access and production. She downplays old compositions, calls herself an inadequate producer and yields to Kaguya's happy-ending appeal. Friends notice brightening but also challenge her logic that leisure is an elite duty worth sacrificing sleep for. Collapse is contrary evidence to a simple upward trajectory. A partner's care exposes the cost of trying to meet every obligation. O0012–O0023 distinguish changes in activity from a complete change of self-evaluation.

**I-S3,54:11–75:14: recognition and selected reliance.** She hides, is called into visibility by her brother, recognizes his practiced skill, delegates and participates in a plan that defeats his self-sacrifice prediction. Losing the game limits triumph; winning fans expands recognition. The request for a guarantor rather than a whole house is a precise behavioral change: accepting bounded support. O0024–O0029 supply both pressure and counterexample.

**I-S4,75:14–108:31: an ordinary shared life becomes worth defending.** Scheduling, cooking and cohabitation coexist with fatigue. Iroha initiates an outing. Her refusal to repeat her mother's “you cannot understand” is already an ethical act before the later phone call. She supports the farewell Kaguya requests while arranging resistance to the removal. This combination can be care and an unwillingness to accept Kaguya's stated settlement; the record should retain both. O0030–O0040.

**I-S5,108:31–127:15: capable of surviving loss, unwilling to equate survival with sufficiency.** She returns to school/work, receives care, and can imagine law and memory. She then refuses narrative closure, speaks to her mother and completes the song. When she meets older Kaguya, she wants the whole history rather than forgetting. This insistence also overrides privacy and bodily warnings. Her culminating statement separates growth from desire for the person. O0041–O0048.

**I-S6,127:15–end: chosen sustained work with local limits.** Graduation, experimentation and a ten-year marker precede restoration. She asks for investment, accepts collaborators and stops work at the day's end. Rest becomes legitimate. One line and one workday support a change in practice, not a permanent absence of overwork. Taste remains unfinished; future quarrels remain possible. O0049–O0052.

Iroha's recurring tendency is to translate desire into defensible obligation: school above life, leisure as elite duty, housing as terms, affection as shared expenses. The film gradually permits more direct wanting without abolishing practical language. A rival reading treats that language mainly as comedy. Its recurrence across cost, play and intimacy makes it useful characterization, but exact vocal sarcasm cannot be recovered from subtitles alone.

## Kaguya and Yachiyo: one disclosed identity, distinct experienced states

**K-S1, arrival–23:31: appetite and uncalibrated intrusion.** Rapid learning includes borrowed media phrases. Enjoyment of food and resistance to a dull Moon do not make her morally infallible. She spends another's savings, offers an illicit shortcut, cooks and asks for shared happiness. These actions show effective generosity and poor calibration of another's resources. O0006–O0011.

**K-S2,23:31–75:14: learning a collaborator's limits.** She is not automatically a good broadcaster or musician. She asks for instruction, credits Iroha, and makes public demands that Iroha resists. Sick care is a meaningful reversal, including practical arrangements and admission of having asked too much. Her hesitation before the duel preserves that lesson, though rationalization and exclusivity persist. Her strategy reads an opponent, showing more than impulsive appetite. O0015–O0028.

**K-S3,75:14–108:31: belonging and negotiated resignation.** Domestic life, affection and wanting music with Iroha become specific. She recognizes that humans curb desires for others, then adopts a joyful farewell under return pressure. It is possible to read this as moral maturation; it is also possible to see newly learned self-restraint becoming capitulation. Her farewell cannot be reduced to a secretly unanimous wish for Iroha's intervention. O0030–O0040.

**K-S4, narrated lunar return and ancient arrival: desire persists through duty and error.** The received song prompts another trip after work and handoff. Collision, displaced arrival and restricted bodily interaction produce the long interval. These are Yachiyo's retrospective account, visually illustrated. Younger Kaguya's appeal to Yachiyo in historical memory precedes recognition that she must become that figure. O0045–O0047.

**Y-S1, first present-day appearance through farewell: experienced host with concealed stake.** She thanks Iroha, recognizes situations, moderates threats, jokes, tires, plays imperfectly and speaks evasively about climaxes. Retrospective knowledge makes these details intelligible but does not make her an all-controlling author. She can uphold others' joy while withholding painful information. O0013–O0014,O0025,O0029,O0031–O0032,O0039.

**Y-S2, identity disclosure–127:15: reassurance exposed as effort.** She offers celebration, a familiar room, amusing history and even forgetting if unwanted. Iroha identifies an acquired resemblance to her own masking. The memory route reveals learned endurance; the song is said to have sustained survival. Warmth and pancakes reveal desires not satisfied by public success or virtual reunion. O0045–O0048.

**Y/K-S3, final project: continuity through multiple presentations.** The new body and simultaneous real/virtual performance support continued participation by the disclosed person. They do not fully specify the technology of consciousness, avatar multiplicity or younger/older interfaces. The story does not warrant declaring that an unwanted older self has been replaced by a fresh copy. O0049–O0050.

Two symmetric errors must be avoided: turning early Kaguya into an omniscient designer because the ending identifies Yachiyo, and treating older Yachiyo as a separate obstacle whom the youthful heroine must erase. The monograph's interpretation depends on accepting the older history, not merely recognizing a disguise.

## Other persons with causal weight

**Mother.** The evidence supports control through evaluation and conditional support, with a painful exclusionary phrase. It also includes a demand that Iroha assert a desired role, a permissive later response, and shared late imagery. She is not adequately described by “wants obedience” alone. Nor does the late permission absolve all earlier behavior. Her exact reasons, awareness of harm and terms of reconciliation remain underdescribed. No diagnosis or heard emotional trajectory is asserted. O0002,O0008,O0021–O0022,O0026,O0035,O0043,O0049.

**Father.** His remembered invitation to enjoy music and the jointly begun piece provide a usable inheritance. The unfinished work holds grief without proving all subsequent music has been a substitute for him. Iroha states that she wrote other pieces. Kaguya's help enables continuation in a new relationship, not erasure of the father's contribution. His independent interiority and wider family conduct are not available in these brief memories. O0015,O0038.

**Asahi/Mikado.** He is teasing, competitive, skilled and professionally invested in giving an audience spectacle. As a child he defends a capacity in Iroha but leaves her feeling alone. As an adult he can minimize her maternal difficulty, challenge her, revise his reading, give bounded support and later invest. His “mother waits for rebellion” is not neutral exposition. Ordinary assistance prevents the competitive avatar from exhausting his character; the financial boundary prevents the brother from becoming an infinite resource. O0024–O0028,O0049.

**Roka and Mami.** They repay, invite, notice, praise, arrange and help. Their concern precedes the protagonist's new romance and survives her reduced performance. They have their own fandom responses. Their claim that she once seemed liable to disappear is concern, not a diagnosis or independently verified intent. Little evidence supports elaborate private arcs for either; ordinary specificity is sufficient. O0009,O0018,O0023,O0033,O0037,O0042,O0049.

**Rai and Noi.** Rai's trap matters to the protagonists' loss; his later insistence on helping others matters to practical support. Noi's reluctance and wish for an avatar body retain a self-regarding element. Assistance does not require everyone to be equally altruistic. Their depth is limited by screen opportunity, not demonstrated shallowness of personality. O0028,O0037,O0049.

**FUSHI.** He provides access and technical mediation while recognizing that others may not want to disclose. His eventual cooperation raises a real tension between protection and the protagonist's demand to know. His warning about Iroha's body supplies a limit the scene's emotional urgency does not remove. The film is not a general ethical warrant for overriding privacy. O0012,O0044,O0046.

## Reconstruction limits and disconfirmation

These records describe supported tendencies and scene-conditioned changes, not clinical profiles. Ordinary contexts include bills, meals, scheduling, sick care, moving, requests for help and stopping work. Contrary opportunities are recorded: Kaguya can help yet demand exclusivity; Iroha can assert herself yet overreach; Yachiyo can reassure yet conceal; Asahi can recognize potential yet minimize difficulty; mother can permit yet remain incompletely understood.

A continuous return that contradicts speaker attribution or changes the force of a caption should produce an attributable revision, not a silent rewrite. Missing continuous audio particularly limits voice/persona comparisons. Limited screen opportunity constrains accounts of father, friends, Rai, Noi, FUSHI and historical acquaintances. Final relationships remain evidence-bounded; no global reconstruction capability is certified.
