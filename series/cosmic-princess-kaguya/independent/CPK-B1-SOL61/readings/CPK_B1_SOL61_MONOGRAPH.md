# A life that does not finish with growth

## Scope and argument

This is an independent full-film interpretation of *Cosmic Princess Kaguya!* from the supplied Japanese-source bundle. It precedes the neutral verification questions and the other analyst's interpretation. It covers the entire presentation, including the apparent ending, the historical disclosure, the future montage, the credits and the coda. Its evidence consists of all 2,716 Japanese subtitle cues, a documented survey of 127 contact sheets, 29 individually opened clean frames, and selected English crosschecks. It does not claim to have heard the audio or watched continuous motion. Scene descriptions combine textual events with sampled compositions; statements about their significance are arguments, not additional perceptual facts. The [observation record](../evidence/CPK_B1_SOL61_OBSERVATIONS.md), [character record](../evidence/CPK_B1_SOL61_CHARACTERS.md), [claim register](../claims/CPK_B1_SOL61_CLAIMS.md), and [execution profile](../provenance/CPK_B1_SOL61_EXECUTION_PROFILE.md) make those distinctions recoverable.

The film's most consequential question is not simply whether Iroha can become less obedient, or whether a tragic folktale can be given a happy ending. It asks what gives someone permission to want a particular shared future after the story of her personal improvement could already be considered complete. Kaguya first interrupts a life governed by adequacy, economy and endurance. Later, Iroha interrupts Kaguya's attempt to make endurance and a grateful farewell sufficient. The direction of rescue reverses without making either woman wholly independent of other people. Finally, the older Kaguya must be wanted with the history that a youthful fantasy of liberation would rather skip.

This reading rests most strongly on the exchange at 125:14–127:15. Iroha acknowledges that she has grown, that she can speak to her mother, and that her own life could count as a happy ending without Kaguya. Then she says she wants to be with Kaguya. The film itself distinguishes developmental benefit from the continuing value of the other person. Yachiyo's wish for warmth and pancakes gives that distinction a material object. A relationship cannot be replaced by the lesson it has taught. The subsequent ten-year interval and unfinished taste function make the continuation a project, while the final possibility of a future quarrel prevents a project from becoming a guarantee. [O0048–O0052; C0024–C0028]

There are important challenges to this preferred reading. One can organize the same film as an emancipation narrative: affection and music teach Iroha to express wishes suppressed by her mother. One can also read it as wish-fulfillment entertainment that translates loss into spectacular technical recovery, with an extraordinarily capable protagonist and a commercially successful support network. A third account emphasizes grief: Kaguya's changing forms let Iroha recover a relationship to loss without literally recovering the first period of their life. These are developed below rather than treated as errors. The preferred account should survive them by explaining details they leave insufficiently motivated, not by denying the pleasures they describe.

The competition is not a detachable action interlude. It tests whether Iroha can revise her brother's prediction that she will absorb the costs herself. Similarly, the historical disclosure is not only a solution to an identity puzzle. It transfers concealed endurance from Iroha to the person she has treated as an inexhaustible source of reassurance.

## 1. Hospitality begins with a reasonable refusal

The opening narration calls Iroha ordinary while listing demanding accomplishments. She studies, earns money, plays games successfully and appears competent. Her private description of functioning without really being awake undercuts that appearance. Six hours of hoped-for sleep is itself framed as holiday relief. Competence is real; its sustainability is the problem. Nothing here warrants a clinical diagnosis or a claim that all her relationships are false. [O0001; C0001]

The maternal conflict is compressed into a cute, flattened illustration between text panels. A clean frame at 01:37.665 shows the mother's hand on Iroha's face and an impact convention. It supports an uncomfortable conjunction of comic narration and coercive family imagery. A static frame cannot reconstruct a slap's movement or force. More importantly, the film later supplies the conflict's terms: independent living depends on Iroha paying her own way. The apparent escape is an arrangement still conditioned by maternal expectations. [O0002, O0021–O0022]

Her first wish on seeing a falling light is money. That wish should be taken seriously before being made ironic by the later gift. Money means tuition, housing, food and time. When she discovers a baby inside the luminous utility pole, she initially refuses because she is already at capacity. She changes course when she judges the street unsafe; she also considers the police and the risk of being mistaken for a kidnapper. Her reluctance is neither evidence of a cold essence nor a mere obstacle the story can morally ridicule. It is an accurate judgment by someone with no spare resources who nevertheless responds to vulnerability. [O0003–O0004; C0002]

The calming song offers an early model of possibility. Iroha cannot recall a lullaby, then uses a passage associated with Yachiyo. The baby sleeps. Recurrence is verified in Japanese lyric text; exact melody and performance were not heard. What can be established is the change of use: something acquired as a fan becomes an act of care. The image of the Yachiyo display surrounded by books and cans places that act within cramped daily life. Cultural consumption supplies a small resource for an unforeseen relationship. [O0005; M02; C0003]

That resource does not cancel the burden. Rapid growth, food costs, lost sleep and disorder follow. The child acquires phrases from devices, so her language is not an unmediated expression of a naturally liberated self. Kaguya spends Iroha's savings and offers to alter bank data; Iroha explicitly prohibits the latter. Excellent cooking gives real pleasure while violating consent over money. Liberation arrives mixed with intrusion. The film can enjoy that mixture without requiring every demand to be called benevolent. [O0006, O0010; C0004]

Iroha's housing conditions are correspondingly double-edged. They protect study, finances and safety, but risk reproducing the joyless structure she left. The comparison with her mother is a tension, not an equivalence. Iroha is accountable for a stranger whom she cannot support indefinitely; she also listens, cooks and revises arrangements. Her later statement that she did not want to tell Kaguya she could never understand confirms an ethical difference more precise than a division between a bad parent and a good caregiver. [O0011, O0035; C0006]

## 2. The happy ending begins as an argument about desire

The film supplies its own version of the older Kaguya story during the meal. That telling, rather than externally imported mythology, is the appropriate comparison. Iroha recounts arrival, upbringing, suitors, lunar return, a robe and forgetting. Kaguya objects not only to unhappiness but to a story presenting unhappiness as a satisfactory conclusion. Iroha replies that an established outcome cannot be changed by struggling or singing. Kaguya promises a shared happy ending; Iroha says an ordinary one will do. [O0007]

Iroha's position is understandable: she is maintaining a precarious ordinary life. Kaguya's insistence can be affectionate and presumptuous at once. The story redistributes their positions instead of proving one speaker always right. Near the farewell, Kaguya accepts a joyful last stretch before mandated return, while Iroha insists she is not confined to the old princess's role. Kaguya has learned to restrain desires for others; that can be maturity, but it can become an elegant language for resignation. Iroha has learned to want more; that can be agency, but it can become pressure on someone trying to leave well. [O0034–O0037]

This reversal is essential. The work is not a contest between a permanently pessimistic realist and an eternally free alien. Each learns from the other, and each is endangered by turning the learned position into a final rule. Their disagreements concern what care permits, including whether care should accept another person's announced ending.

## 3. Music moves between use, inheritance and public labor

Music is a narrative practice before it is an abstract symbol of freedom. Iroha learned under conflicting accounts of its value. Her father associated it with enjoyment and freedom; her mother insisted on winning. A school performance is praised for exactness. That establishes an evaluative environment, not audible evidence that her playing is mechanical or emotionally empty. Later teacher concern about excessive perfection also complicates a simple division between oppressive adults and liberating peers. [O0008, O0015, O0042]

Kaguya's first broadcast is not magically accomplished. Japanese text marks an unsuccessful jingle; she needs instruction and a plan. Technological fluency does not become musicianship automatically. She asks for repetition when Iroha plays, requests an original song, and proposes that Iroha produce her. Iroha calls the appeal to a happy ending unfair. The invitation makes old creative material usable, but works partly by pressing a known vulnerability. Mutual creation and emotional pressure are both present. [O0015–O0016]

The development passage combines lyrics of curiosity and imperfection with the work of becoming popular. Screens, rankings, streams, games and audience acquisition remain visible. The film does not abolish measurement. It distinguishes kinds of recognition and reasons for labor within a system that rewards successful performance. Tsukuyomi's currency explicitly depends on moving other people's hearts. That connects feeling and livelihood, yet also makes attachment measurable and exchangeable. A reading that calls the virtual world simply free from real-world pressure misses this continuity. [O0012, O0017–O0019; C0007–C0009]

The unfinished composition makes inheritance more specific. Iroha describes a first joint piece with her father before his death. She has written other music but cannot continue this one in a way that feels right. Kaguya supplies lyrics from their ordinary life and accepts one chorus. The solution begins by allowing an incomplete, particular use rather than demanding a definitive monument. At 99:09 Iroha recognizes a relation to Yachiyo's debut song. The source establishes her recognition; independent melodic identity remains an audio task. [O0038; M12; C0018]

The song connects father and daughter, young Kaguya, older Yachiyo, and Iroha as fan and caregiver. The disclosed loop changes who has sustained whom. It does not prove that the father learned his composition from Yachiyo or that the melody has no origin. “A loop” is not a license to assign an unshown causal link. The strongest established chain is interpersonal transmission, completion, receipt and reuse. [O0005, O0038, O0043, O0047–O0048; C0022]

The music record separates consequences from unavailable acoustics. It can explain why the farewell, the song reaching the Moon and the remembered refrain change relationships. It cannot establish instrumental color, singing timbre, strain, acoustic depth, precise leitmotivic transformation or beat-level synchronization. Those are substantial responsibilities for continuous-AV inspection, not extras that fluent prose may invent.

## 4. Competence becomes shareable in the contest

Iroha initially hides in a mascot form. Mikado's challenge brings her into visibility, and his identification as her brother Asahi changes the significance of her refusal to engage. His avatar name is Mikado Akira; the SDH calls the physical brother Sakayori Asahi. These are names at different interfaces, not two brothers or a mistranslation. [O0024, O0053]

Asahi's claim that their mother waits for rebellion is his interpretation, not verified knowledge of her intent. The childhood memory is more difficult: the mother scolds Iroha for yielding a desired role, Asahi says his sister has things she will not yield, and he announces his departure. The mother accepts; Iroha experiences being left alone. Maternal pressure demands assertion as well as compliance. Asahi recognizes a capacity in his sister while underestimating the conditions that make it hard to exercise. [O0026; C0010]

The matches deny effortless liberation. Yachiyo can be immobilized; Iroha's faith in her supreme ability exceeds the game evidence. Kaguya's tactic is not pure chaos: it exploits vertical space and predicts Mikado will retain a formation because he, too, hates losing. Later Iroha accepts help and makes another person's strategy usable. Mikado predicts self-sacrifice, but the weapon exchange and decoy plan defeat that reading. Precise physical timing needs motion inspection; the tactical and interpersonal revision is explicit in dialogue and consistent with sampled weapons and arena states. [O0025, O0027; C0011]

They nevertheless lose the game to Rai's trap. They win the Cup because it counts new fans, the criterion announced earlier. The film does not retroactively decree that emotional authenticity won the same contest whose rules it lost. Skilled rivals remain skilled while the protagonists achieve something different by appearing before others. Audience responses register Iroha's music, aim and smile; she is no longer only a hidden supplier of another performer's work. [O0028; C0012]

Asahi does not enforce the marriage premise. Iroha requests a guarantor and refuses an entire house. The larger home follows negotiated support. Her independence changes from doing everything unaided to choosing which help to accept. This makes the late investment request a development of an established relationship, not an unexplained resource. It does not make those resources equally available to everyone. [O0028, O0049]

## 5. The relationship exists outside exceptional scenes

Friends matter before and after Kaguya. Roka urges rest; Roka and Mami repay notes with food and time, notice changes, support the fireworks outing and help the farewell intervention. They admire more than one performer despite Kaguya's demand for exclusive loyalty. They do not merely certify that romance has finally made an isolated girl human. Iroha already has a reciprocal social world that she has difficulty allowing to relieve her burden. [O0009, O0018, O0023, O0033, O0037, O0042; C0005]

Kaguya's sickroom conduct is an ordinary test. She contacts work, obtains leave, cooks, supplies comfort and proposes a hospital. She acknowledges demanding too much. The person who consumed Iroha's resources now protects her capacity. Her hesitation over the duel shows the lesson persists, although she rationalizes gaming as relief. Change is conditional, not complete. [O0021–O0023; C0013]

The new home adds shopping, cooking, small requests, schedules and shared sleeping arrangements. These support continuing intimacy beyond confession and crisis. Unpacking still includes a weary, comic Iroha: inconvenience has not disappeared. Kaguya's affection, marriage language, handholding and wish to remain together make a romantic reading strong. Yet Iroha's answer takes the practical form of shared expenses, not an explicit legal wedding agreement. A fan marriage joke, a post-collaboration declaration and reciprocal domestic attachment are different evidential situations. [O0030–O0031, O0036, O0051; C0014]

At the festival, Japanese negation changes characterization. Kaguya supposes Iroha wanted to tell her she could not understand. Iroha says she did not want to say that; the mother's exclusionary phrase appears in between. This is more than an inability finally overcome by speaking. Iroha is already choosing not to reproduce an injury in her treatment of another person. Later assertiveness grows from this capacity rather than replacing a wholly passive former self. English preserves the distinction in the checked passage, but Japanese syntax supplies the primary warrant. [O0035, O0052; C0015]

## 6. Spectacle does not make the loss unreal

Tsukuyomi is neither a fraudulent substitute for reality nor a complete solution to embodiment. The tutorial distinguishes cheap virtual activity from real food delivery and states the absence of taste and smell. Kaguya later praises reality, and Iroha still values Tsukuyomi. The division is between affordances and limits, not authentic and inauthentic feeling. [O0012, O0034]

Yachiyo's concert surrounds participants with water, gates, multiplied figures and an expansive sky. Its scale makes an idol encounter a shared environment. Later lunar intrusion reaches commuters' devices and news reports outside the virtual venue. Public interfaces can carry connection and coercion. Yachiyo's ability to rebuke the arrivals and present the event as a show reveals privileged involvement, but does not establish control over every outcome. [O0013, O0032; C0016]

The farewell stages a conflict between positions that can both look caring. Kaguya wants joyful final days and a good performance; Iroha and the others stage a counteraction while helping realize that performance. Lyrics about facing fate coexist with Iroha's refusal of a bad ending. It would be a mistake to assign every lyric, character and the film as a whole the same proposition. The event is productive because positions differ. [O0036–O0040; C0017]

The intervention fails to prevent departure. Spectators can read what happens as remarkable production while the relationship is actually being severed. That does not prove every spectator is deceived identically, nor require an unheard crescendo or an invented audiovisual rupture. The evidence establishes divergent stakes in a shared spectacle. Kaguya's thanks and declaration coexist with an outcome she cannot make harmless. [O0040; C0019]

The empty home returns the film to material objects. Iroha recalls intrusions and inconveniences, then experiences their absence as the greater injury. A money transfer cannot replace Kaguya. This answers the opening wish without disproving the original need for money. The guarantor, moving costs and later laboratory still matter. The point is the nonexchangeability of a person, not the worthlessness of resources. [O0041; C0020]

## 7. The false ending presents a credible life, then refuses its sufficiency

After departure, Iroha continues school and work and names a law-school direction. Her teacher is reassured that she is less excessively perfect. Her friends value her being alive rather than useful. The narration can offer a plausible completed growth story: she has loved, lost, become more humanly imperfect and can move forward with memory. That is not an absurd or contemptible life. [O0042]

The film renders this potential conclusion as a streaming interface. At 111:36, credits, a return-to-menu option, a recommended Black Onyx live program and a countdown are visible. Closure becomes something a platform can process into the next consumable event. Iroha's refusal interrupts both narrative settlement and presentation as finished entertainment. This specificity supports a reading about who decides that a life has yielded enough meaning. A rival explanation remains that the interface is primarily a comic fakeout; continuous timing would help weigh its disruptive force. [O0043, O0051; C0021]

Her response is practical as well as emotional. She asks for more time over her future, answers her mother's call and completes the song. The decisive words to her mother are designated silent dialogue in Japanese SDH. The reply permits trying; Iroha says she managed to say it. That establishes a changed act of address. It does not reveal the exact request, prove a comprehensive apology or settle their relationship. Later shared images strengthen evidence of renewed contact while leaving its terms undescribed. [O0043, O0049, O0052]

The completed song reaches the Moon. Yachiyo's account confirms receipt and links it to Kaguya's decision to return after finishing and handing off work. That revises an opposition between irresponsible desire and duty: the return is not narrated simply as abandoning everyone else's needs. Music has a consequence because someone receives it and acts. A claim that sound physically shatters every barrier would exceed the evidence. [O0043–O0045; C0022]

## 8. Recognizing Yachiyo means encountering a history

The search moves through both worlds. An echo of Kaguya's playful gesture gives Iroha a clue; FUSHI becomes a gatekeeper; a physical apartment contains the infrastructure of the supposedly disembodied idol. The discovery includes a tank, damaged vessel and sea-slug route of interaction. Iroha does not simply enter a room and find a human-bodied Yachiyo. [O0044–O0045, O0051]

Yachiyo recounts a return through time that went wrong. Kaguya reached Earth roughly eight thousand years early; the damaged ship could provide a body for the accompanying 犬DOGE, while she could interact through a sea slug. Human technologies eventually made it possible for a soul without an ordinary body to participate through a virtual persona. These are narrated and illustrated events. The account of recurrence supplies a causal structure, but brief language about a wheel does not establish an infinite number of iterations, a multiverse, or every rule of identity. [O0045; C0023]

Knowledge must be indexed to each life stage. Older Yachiyo recognizes Iroha before Iroha recognizes her. Younger Kaguya admires Yachiyo and later calls for help without yet knowing whom she will become. The historical memory at 124:22 marks realization rather than revealing that the younger girl knowingly staged every earlier encounter. The first private thanks by name is retrospectively meaningful, but its earlier ambiguity remains part of disclosure. [O0014, O0047]

Yachiyo's smile becomes a complicated achievement. It conceals loneliness and upholds a hospitable public role. Historical encounters give her ways to endure, laugh, wait and understand age. Her long life is not solely an empty interval between meetings with Iroha, although the montage gives many relationships little space. SDH labels for historical figures are not independent historical evidence; these are the film's attributed encounters. [O0047]

Iroha notices that Yachiyo has become like her. The person who taught her to resist cheerful self-erasure now performs reassurance while hiding the cost. This reverses care. Yachiyo offers forgetting if the truth is unwanted; Iroha asks to know the whole history. That supports recognition of the older person rather than replacement by remembered youth. It is not ethically untroubled: FUSHI initially protects Yachiyo's withholding, and the memory route proceeds under Iroha's pressure. Clear prior consent for every disclosure is absent. Compassion and entitlement are not automatically separable. [O0046; C0024]

The private room and the vast reflective water and sky offer different scales of encounter. At 125:08 the pair is tiny within a tremendous field; at 126:26.450 they sit facing each other at equal height. Sampled images develop from darkness toward a lighter sky, but duration and transition technique are unverified. The compositions support a contrast between immense elapsed time and a particular encounter. They do not prove that the virtual scene repairs every wound. The private finger gesture recurs, making recognition bodily in form before restored ordinary embodiment. [O0051; C0025]

## 9. Continuation takes time and remains unfinished

Iroha could now count as successfully grown; she still wants Kaguya. This rejects the instrumental use of a beloved as a temporary means by which a protagonist becomes complete. Yachiyo's wish for warmth and pancakes shows that being remembered, understood or virtually present does not fulfill every desire. Iroha responds by finding something she wants to do. [O0048; C0026]

The montage is not instantaneous salvation. The visible “10 years later” marker follows graduation and experimentation. Iroha seeks investment from Asahi; he resists being treated as a boundlessly wealthy benefactor, then agrees. Rai and Noi contribute, with Noi retaining reluctance and vanity. The collective project does not erase differences. Skill becomes useful through resources, time and assistance rather than by replacing dependence with omnipotence. [O0049–O0052; C0027]

A brief ordinary line says rest is work. That is stronger behavioral evidence of change than a general declaration of liberation, although one workday cannot guarantee lifelong balance. It qualifies her earlier demand to remain awake through all of Yachiyo's history. The story permits local excess and subsequent adjustment rather than giving her a perfectly cured personality. [O0046, O0049]

The new body has weight, but taste is still being developed. The return performance occurs in reality and Tsukuyomi together. These facts favor continued coexistence of mediated and embodied life over final abandonment of the virtual. Simultaneous presentations of Kaguya and Yachiyo require caution: the source does not warrant inventing independent souls or a replacement younger Kaguya. Dramatically, renewed participation is possible and still incomplete. [O0049; C0027]

The final narrator asks whether the story counts as happy and allows a future quarrel. It ends for now. Photographs gather ordinary and virtual relationships; credit lyrics preserve pain alongside life; the pancake coda returns desire to something small and bodily. Neither credits nor coda establish that all waiting has been repaid or taste completed offscreen. Happiness remains compatible with unfinished life. [O0050–O0052; C0028]

## 10. What the sampled form establishes, and what it leaves open

The sampled visual design repeatedly puts familiar domestic needs inside extravagant technical or mythic spaces. A utility pole contains the infant; a fan display becomes part of a caregiving scene; gates, water and sky hold a concert; a cluttered apartment houses an ancient survivor's interface. These are not interchangeable symbols. Their common contribution is to prevent the extraordinary from becoming wholly detached from everyday maintenance. The later laboratory extends the same conjunction rather than replacing domestic life with abstract transcendence. [O0004–O0005, O0013, O0044–O0045, O0049]

The difference between visible performance and private knowledge is also spatial. A hidden producer becomes a publicly visible player. A performer can appear in multiple forms while her actual infrastructure occupies one room. An empty couch after departure carries loss differently from a crowded virtual stage. The enormous reflective plane at reunion diminishes the figures without making their exchange insignificant. These are arguments from composition and distribution of settings, not claims about camera movement or editing speed. [O0024, O0041, O0044, O0051]

Several apparent discontinuities are intentional parts of the story's account of mediation: illustrated family exposition, in-world interfaces, a next-content screen, historical images, photographs and production drawings in the credits. The film does not use one visual register for all truths. Yet a register change alone does not prove unreliability. The historical montage is narrated by Yachiyo and then encountered through a memory route; its epistemic status differs from an objective technical demonstration even when the film gives it visual presence. A later viewer should examine transitions and duration before making stronger claims about how these layers address or interrupt the audience.

The audio gap is most serious at precisely the points where form might discriminate among the readings: the first public concert, Iroha's playing, the farewell battles, the false ending and the remembered melody. Japanese SDH can identify a named sound or designate silence; it cannot provide the full dynamic field. The film's deliberate withholding of the mother's words is itself evidenced by the subtitle designation, but this run cannot judge how surrounding sound shapes it. The credit texts support persistence of pain, but cannot establish whether the music sustains, complicates or ironizes that proposition. This monograph's formal account is therefore substantial but explicitly incomplete in temporal and sonic dimensions.

## Rival readings and pressure tests

**The emancipation reading** explains maternal evaluation, play, music, speaking and choosing a project. The call and investment request are strong support. Its weakness appears when Kaguya becomes Yachiyo. Treating the older beloved merely as one last test of Iroha's independence cannot explain why Iroha separates having grown from still wanting her. The preferred reading includes emancipation, then argues that the film refuses to let it consume the relationship's meaning. Evidence that older Kaguya is discarded, or the ending concerns only Iroha's success, would weaken that preference.

**The technological wish-fulfillment reading** takes the solution's convenience seriously. A remarkable student, successful performers, an emotionally monetized platform and a laboratory make restoration possible. The narrative may reward refusal with a solution rather than confront irreversible loss. Its strongest challenge is that material limits become tractable because the characters are unusually capable and connected. The ten-year interval, investment negotiation, unfinished taste and uncertain future qualify rescue; they do not create social realism. Production or market context might illuminate the platform's ideology, but no such context was admitted here and it cannot substitute for film evidence.

**The grief-and-continuity reading** explains the father, unfinished piece, empty home, older survivor and retained pain. One can recover a relationship without recovering its original time. The preferred reading agrees. It diverges if bodily restoration becomes merely symbolic acceptance: the film presents concrete labor and performance after marked elapsed time. Conversely, literal recovery does not erase grief; the older person retains history and the final address remains provisional. A fantasy of repair can still distinguish repair from undoing.

Two tensions should remain active. Accepting help does not mean accepting all intrusion: Kaguya's spending and Iroha's demand for private history prevent a simple ethic of unconditional togetherness. Public visibility is not identical with being known: fans, friends, siblings and the beloved have different evidence about a performer. The contest and Yachiyo's smile explore this without making performance inherently false.

Potential findings that would revise the account include spoken Japanese contradicting SDH negation at 92:43–54, continuous AV that changes the formal significance of a recurrence or interruption, or an overlooked identity rule constraining the older/younger relation. These are real tests, not predictions that missing evidence must confirm the thesis. The claim register identifies further counterevidence and disconfirmation conditions.

## Final state of the independent interpretation

Iroha can more explicitly want, ask, share work and rest, but remains talented, forceful and capable of overreaching. Kaguya learns consideration and resignation; as Yachiyo she accumulates a history irreducible to youthful spontaneity. Attachment is sustained by music, ordinary care, public collaboration, family help and technical work. It is asymmetrical in knowledge and experience at particular moments and becomes reciprocal through actions rather than by declaring asymmetry absent.

The ending is best understood as permission to continue a particular relationship after growth has ceased to be a sufficient explanation for wanting it. It does not abolish loss, duty, money, embodiment or time. It reorganizes how the characters answer those limits together. That is the independent thesis offered for testing, with rival accounts and uninspected audiovisual responsibilities preserved alongside it.
