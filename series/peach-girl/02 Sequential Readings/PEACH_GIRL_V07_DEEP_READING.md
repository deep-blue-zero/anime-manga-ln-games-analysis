# Volume 7 — rescue, refusal and the coerced breakup

Momo interrupts the revenge she helped arrange: she rescues Sae because she recognizes the terror and the absence of support, while still refusing forgiveness. Her declared farewell briefly frees her. Sae then makes the photographs a means of controlling Tōji, whose effort to protect Momo becomes concealment from her and ultimately a breakup presented as loss of affection. Momo does not remain merely deceived: she asks for shared problem-solving and later infers the blackmail accurately. Tōji counters that insight by manufacturing a painful display of preference. The concluding kiss is real contact used to communicate a false causal account; it does not erase either his earlier explicit rejection of Sae or the injury he chooses to impose on Momo.

## 4. Close reading: agency and relations without moral compression

### 4.1 Recognition revises revenge; rescue does not retroactively purify it

Kairi's initial dismissal11 makes the error a test of responsibility, not only logistics. His scheme's professed limit was no actual assault. Once the actual situation exceeds his arrangement, he can still call it deserved punishment. Momo initially tries to accept that explanation21, but the cut back to Sae's struggle refuses the comfort of an abstract lesson. Momo asks whether she wanted this and then says she knows how such harm feels. Her decisive addition25 is relational: she had Tōji, whereas Sae has nobody. This inference is not an exhaustive survey of Sae's social network. It identifies the absence of help in this danger as a reason to act. Momo's anger remains explicit. Rescue is neither forgiveness nor proof she was unwilling in V06; it is an observable revision of a choice she had made (EV0003–0006).

Sae's defense12–20 matters independently. She tries resistance, then apologetic/cute performance to obtain one free hand, then attacks and tries escape. Earlier manipulation does not make this performance consent or present danger deserved. The source grants her tactical agency while depicting its failure; an account that reduces her to a passive punished villain misses that sequence. Conversely, her distress is not repentance. Her later accusation that the trio endangered her has force on those established facts even though she uses it to evade her own responsibility (EV0004/0009).

Momo's response is physically specific. Tōji attacks the door, Kairi supplies an extinguisher, but Momo takes it and breaks through. She sprays and kicks the man, then uses a vase when he rises threateningly. Writing only “the boys rescue Sae” would erase her conversion from uneasy coauthor to active protective force. The man collapses; exact injury severity is unshown. Tōji then offers the intended moral, that Sae now understands Momo. Sae's palm slap, demand for accountability and later destructive vow prevent the analyst from adopting his statement as completed education (EV0007–0010/0014).

Momo's answer is finally a boundary rather than another attempt to educate: she cannot forgive, will stop responding, and says goodbye46–48. Her tears outside55 register release from the relation, not healing from every consequence. Kairi articulates that the couple's happiness has been built by two people; taking Momo's place cannot reproduce it. This is a strong relational explanation within the work, but Kairi also admits the wrong delivery53 and protects Momo from Sae's resentment54. The protective speech does not erase her earlier participation or his earlier readiness to abandon Sae. His eventual explanation and accountability are changes in response, not a newly proven pure motive (EV0011–0013).

### 4.2 Aftercare has duration; a new threat uses that fact

Tōji's birthday-redo invitation restores an ordinary desired future: early start, bento and concern about spending. Yet Jigoro's magazine presence makes Momo's body lock63–64; she wants to forget and leaves. This is represented continuing distress, not a diagnosis and not fresh proof of the missing act. The bruises on the model can meanwhile become a fashionable wild look to classmates. The juxtaposition distributes consequences unequally: a public image attractive to others is a private cue of fear for Momo (EV0015–0016).

Sae observes and then directs the photographs at Tōji. They show a room composition capable of supporting a damaging story, while not establishing intercourse. She threatens the press event and predicts the model will benefit while Momo's future suffers. Those predictions become coercive because Tōji knows Momo's fear and because the source has shown it; they are not evidence that actual media already circulated or acted on the images. His initial response is an explicit refusal69/74, along with physically threatening Sae. Protection is thus neither flawless gentleness nor immediate surrender (EV0017–0019).

The date's failure is not simply that Momo is too fragile to enjoy it. Tōji bears information he withholds while she reads his distraction. She asks him to speak and proposes solving things together85. That offered alternative matters: the later solitary sacrifice is a choice under pressure, not the only response the text permits us to imagine. It is also not proven that disclosure would certainly defeat Sae. The source tests an asymmetry of agency: Momo wants participation in a problem affecting her; Tōji seeks to control her exposure without letting her understand the controlling condition (EV0020–0023).

The unattended basket, the spill and Sae's “present” claim connect covert placement to a deliberately public trigger. Actual bystanders notice and a print is picked up88, but their conclusions and further reach remain unknown. Burning the retrieved prints95 is a concrete local action. “All”98 cannot legitimately mean every copy/negative Sae owns. Momo's memories interrupt the date while the park continues cheering around the couple.
