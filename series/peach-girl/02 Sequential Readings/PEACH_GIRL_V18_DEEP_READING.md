## 2. Continuous reading and analytical distinctions

### 2.1 An actual refusal, an abduction complaint and an exhausted battery: i0005–i0044

Momo's continuation of the V17 choice is verbal as well as physical: she cannot be with Okayasu6 and says yes to dating Tōji7. Kairi then pushes Tōji9, grabs Momo's hand10 and trips the pursuing Tōji11 before taking her into a taxi. The backward foot and fall were rechecked at single-page resolution. The parents who arrive12–14 are Sae's, identified14; they are not Kairi's family intervening in his romance. Their complaints about education and parental blame introduce a domestic social context without giving a complete explanation of Sae's conduct.

Momo asks to return to the hospital; Kairi refuses. She names his action as kidnapping15 and insists he consider her feelings. Her order to the driver to stop conflicts with his order to continue16. His love and her later change of choice cannot retroactively authorize this removal. The taxi carries them to a waterfront where the driver waits. There Momo gives a detailed account of relational insecurity17–18: hopes are repeatedly disappointed, whereas Tōji cherishes her and makes her feel safe. Her earlier claim that casual sexual companionship would be enough did not describe an internally accepted arrangement. She wants to be cherished as the only one. This is a present correction of her self-presentation, not proof that every earlier statement was knowingly deceitful.

Kairi promises care and denies any current romantic feeling for Misao21. Momo actually hears “only Momo”; this is not merely a private ranking left inaccessible to her. But receiving true words is different from trusting them. She answers with the dead-phone-battery comparison22: her feeling for him cannot simply be recharged after recurrent disappointment. The phone has already failed20–21, after she manages to tell Tōji the coastal-road plaza location. This is not a wholly unsuccessful call. Her departure24 and private desire for Tōji25 precede his arrival26. At this frontier the volume supports a genuine Tōji choice; the ending must not be projected backward to make her refusal empty.

School and future planning27–30 make that choice socially inhabitable: examinations, Tōji's K-dai ambition, joint study and a holiday paid for from wages. The reference to entrance examinations in about a year and a half does not warrant a precise date or age calculation. Sae returns asking for school notes31–32. She says the loss of the imagined child has left her feeling lighter33; she no longer loves Ryo, values having loved intensely and describes complete emotional expenditure. This is her account, not independent proof of permanent recovery or Ryo's absolution. Momo envies a smile that can survive loss34. Her question whether being hurt necessarily means being unhappy opens the final volume's ethical problem without making suffering beneficial in itself.

Sae's comic encounter with the inert Kairi35–39 repeats the battery language. She urges recharging and interprets Momo's refusal as something to pursue past. Her gender generalization about women's words is character speech, not an analytical permission to ignore refusal. Kairi's later restraint of his own pursuit56–57/86–87 complicates that advice. When Sae provides a pickup alibi for Momo's trip40–44, her intervention first looks like familiar mischief. She then discloses that Kairi went to Shiranami during the earlier typhoon. Momo's surprised question44 marks new receipt, not prior knowledge.

### 2.2 Information arrives through other people, unevenly: i0046–i0085

Sae reports the stalled train, taxi, landslide and roughly20km walk47, Kairi's wish to be beside Momo if she was crying48, and his seeing Momo with Tōji the following morning49. She explicitly says this does not oblige Momo to restart the romance50. That sentence matters beside Sae's earlier encouragement of persistence: her interventions are not ethically uniform. Momo now reinterprets Kairi's cheerful next-day apology and Tōji's relief51. Her sense that he knew “everything” is her emotional inference, not certification that he heard every exchange or understood every motive. Concealment makes his cheerfulness newly legible as effort, while leaving its practical cost intact.

Misao encounters the crying Momo55 and asks why Kairi did not explain his lateness60. She tells her about Ryo's imitation-sword injury, the hospital accompaniment, the shock and difficulty making the hospital call61. Momo says she would have waited if told and cannot know what he does not tell62. This is a direct indictment of the communication failure; it does not imply that knowledge would have compelled a romantic choice. Misao then relays Kairi's red rose and statement that Momo was now the person he loved most63. Floral meaning here is attributed to the conversation, not an externally verified universal code. Momo finally receives a report of the V15 first-choice declaration; the old V15 prediction still failed in its original window.

Misao also recalls Tōji crying with regret about yielding under Sae's blackmail64. Her counsel65–66 allows regret with either choice and happiness with either: the person's own heart and conduct matter. Those lines recur over Tōji's image81–82 as Momo's recollection of Misao, not a new speech by Tōji. Misao's kimono outing is an arranged meeting requested by her parents67, explicitly not a wedding. She says she cannot date Ryo, did love him and firmly said goodbye68. Continued care therefore has not silently reversed her renunciation.

Ryo looks at parenting/family images on a computer69–70 and admits raising a child might have mattered to him. The images are imagined/site material, not a newborn or renewed pregnancy. The long-haired nearby woman is not securely named here; there is no warrant to invent a new Morika identification. A joke about his injured head is not a diagnosis. The scene gives his belated desire a shape while leaving responsibility for coercion and hired violence unresolved.

Momo's new lateness71–82 echoes Kairi's. Her phone is out of service, rather than established battery-dead; Tōji waits, starts to leave and later calls. Kairi takes Sae's phone to stop her telling Momo that Tōji has gone75. Momo sees the resulting phone struggle as apparent intimacy76, without hearing its purpose. No romantic Kairi–Sae embrace has occurred. She then remembers an earlier Tōji visit78, reaches him79–80 and hears his confidence that she would not cancel without reason81. Their departure83–84 and Kairi's actual closeup85 preserve the pain of a Tōji reunion. Trust is not simply absent with one man and secure with the other under all conditions: the construction repeats failures of contact while changing who waits and who interprets.

### 2.3 A metaphorical funeral, an actual stop and a retrospective correction: i0086–i0125

Kairi refuses Sae's proposed call to Momo86–87, then runs to the station88–91. He boards the Shiranami express from a different platform from Momo and Tōji. His destination is not proof of a plan to intercept their trip. Sae follows and asks why he is going. His “my own funeral”93–94 is immediately qualified: he does not mean literal death and wants to bury his unresolved feelings in the sea95. Despair and death rhetoric are real; a settled suicide plan is not shown. Sae describes sinking fully and then floating96. His tears97 and rage at Tōji99 are legible without treating his rhetorical threat as a performed murder plan.

He raises Momo's handmade phone case to throw100, but does not throw it. The phone is separately visible; later110 identifies the object as her handmade case. The stopped gesture is central: he can say he must let go while being unable to discard the material tie. Sae sees the ensuing collapse101. When she later borrows his phone110 because her own battery has died, she acquires the means of impersonation. Kairi notices the case is missing and searches111; he says he did not throw it. The reader now has a reason to question any message from his address.

Meanwhile Momo tells Tōji Kairi went to Shiranami104. He asks whether making herself unhappy as punishment would help Kairi105, invokes his own experience of seeing her with Kairi and asks whether she resents Sae's happiness106. Her answer distinguishes annoyance from a wish that Sae remain unhappy. Tōji's comfort107 and the mutual wish to spend more time together108 are actual care. They do not constitute permission for every later bodily act.

In the hotel room111–115, Tōji approaches, embraces and kisses; Momo says wait114. He stops115. That first stop is explicit and must remain in the account of the later coercion. The discussion produces a V15 flashback116–117: Momo wanted to settle matters with Okayasu first, and Tōji agreed to do nothing until morning. Single-image reinspection of these pages and V15_149–156 requires a dated correction to the new Codex V15 inference of a strongly implied sexual night. The intimate night image did not depict a completed act. V18 explicitly supplies agreed abstention until morning. V15's responsive morning kiss and affection155–156 remain actual; any later unshown act remains UNKNOWN. The old V15 reading and PG-FR-V15 stay unchanged as historical records; the successor ledgers carry the correction.

Tōji asks whether Jigoro-related trauma explains the current difficulty118. Momo says she does not know but probably not that. Neither speaker supplies a clinical diagnosis. She also says she does love Tōji117. The analysis should preserve that affirmative feeling beside her inability to continue, instead of converting reluctance into either proof of no affection or defective recovery. Tōji goes to cool his head. Only then does the alarming email arrive119–120.

### 2.4 A counterfeit crisis and an escalation from care to constraint: i0120–i0146

The message from Kairi's address says farewell and imagines becoming fish food at Shiranami120. At this exact reader frontier authorship is open: Sae has the phone and Kairi has not been shown composing it. Momo believes the danger, calls and reports no successful contact121. Her earlier experience of Kairi's sea distress makes her fear intelligible. Tōji doubts Kairi would choose such a pointed message and notes it does not explicitly say die or identify a present location122–123. These objections are materially reasonable; they do not entitle him to prevent her leaving.

He blocks the door123, pulls her onto the bed124 and pins both wrists125. He says he will not let her go. His argument127–128 warns that a rescue motivated by pity will renew Kairi's hopes and tells her to break up with him before going. It articulates a real problem of wavering commitment inside an encounter that physically constrains her answer. Treating it solely as a lucid demand for honesty loses that staging. Treating all his earlier care as a disguise loses the first stop and sustained patient acts. His tears129 and admission that he has feared her return to Kairi explain the pressure without excusing it.

Renewed intimacy follows130–132, without a newly depicted affirmative assent after the restraint. Momo's tears and withdrawn response are visible. Tōji notices and stops133, then covers her with clothing and tells her to go135–136. No completed sexual act is depicted. The ordered sequence matters: first stop115; door block/pull/pin123–125; intimacy after restraint130–132; second stop133; release135–136. Later release does not erase earlier force, and earlier force does not make the release fictitious.

Sae answers Momo's call on Kairi's borrowed phone137. Momo reports the message and her fear138. Sae asks which man she loves139 and tells her she already knows; she also challenges the desire to be universally good140. Momo's statement that her body is honest is her interpretation of her own experience, not a general theory of consent or a rule that bodily response always determines love. She acts on an articulated choice: she tells Tōji she is going142, explicitly says she loves Kairi143 and apologizes. Tōji acknowledges that she has decided for herself, urges her to catch the train144 and turns away145. Her grateful narration146 remembers his help then and now. That gratitude is evidence of her relationship experience, not the narrator's ethical erasure of his restraint.

### 2.5 Retrieval becomes a real emergency; the deception is revealed: i0147–i0178

Kairi continues searching147–148, unable to lose the case even after deciding to bury his feeling. Momo travels by train and finally taxi149–160, reflecting that fear of hurt made her flee and that facing him may hurt again. This is a decision to remain engaged despite risk, not proof that risk itself validates a partner. The threatening message propels her journey, but the later actual emergency has another immediate cause.

Three surfers and a woman arrive151. One wears the case at his neck. Kairi asks for it152, bows and explains that the person he loves made it153. They demand money; his wallet is missing154, with theft not established. He grabs the case, the cap-wearing man restrains his neck and Kairi pulls his ear155. Three men beat and kick him156. The woman's objection includes the fact that people are watching; her later request to return the case does not turn the group into protective companions. The cap man throws it into the sea158. Kairi never carries out his earlier intended discard.

He enters the water to retrieve it159, finds and raises it162, then is overwhelmed by waves and a leg cramp163–165. His thought is fear of dying without seeing Momo, not fulfilment of the forged message. Momo sees him, discards bag and shoes164, swims him ashore166–167 and gives rescue breaths168–169. He coughs and revives170. This is actual rescue breathing, unlike V12's conscious return without CPR; chest compressions and a full clinical protocol are not depicted. Her plea that she cannot live without him is crisis rhetoric within the fiction, not an endorsed definition of healthy attachment.

His death joke171 meets her rebuke172. Mutual love173–174 and her initiated kiss175 make the romantic reunion actual. Only afterward does she ask about the email176. Kairi denies sending it and explains he left his phone with Sae. Sae admits she sent it and takes the commemorative couple photograph. Both now learn authorship. The final comic chase177 and three-person walk178 place Sae beside the romantic Momo–Kairi couple, whose hands are joined. This is continuing social attachment, not a three-person romance. The explicit end marker closes the original series. The sea returns as meeting place and place of renewed connection, but the vow secures no depicted marriage, lifelong stability or comprehensive accountability.

The ending combines actual reciprocal choice with a deception that worked, rescue after third-party assault, and unresolved repair. A teleological reading can see the sea, case and returning love as formal convergence. A competing ethical reading asks why a forged self-destruction threat receives comic celebration and whether danger substitutes for the slower work of trust. Both have source support. The full-series synthesis must hold their tension instead of declaring that the chosen partner's past conduct was thereby right.

## 4. Forecast, self-correction and competing ending readings

PR0022 is DISCONFIRMED under the exact original BOTH condition. Misao supplies Ryo injury and hospital delay61, but police questioning never demonstrably reaches Momo in the complete172-main window. Do not change the test after partial disclosure. Misao's late first-choice relay63 cannot rescue failed PR0019; Kairi's eventual relationship knowledge cannot rescue PR0020's direct-words-in-V16 requirement. No new source forecast is meaningful beyond the terminal original-series boundary.

The dated inn correction is material to agency and event history. PG-EV-V15-0025 and PG-FR-V15 described a strong sexual implication. V18_116–117 now supplies an agreement to do nothing until morning. CS0214@2/IP0198@2/CM0146@2 and current CL0013/0015/0017 narrow the earlier inference; actual morning affection stays. No old reading/freeze is edited. Neither the earlier inference nor the later absence of a depicted act warrants a definitive unshown sexual biography.

Three ending readings remain distinguishable: formal convergence through sea/case/reciprocal choice; ethical concern about a forged self-destruction threat and crisis-driven reconciliation; Momo's active recognition through new information and departure from Tōji. The first does not refute the second; the third is supported before the rescue by her explicit declaration143. Her action is neither a pure reward for Kairi's effort nor a passive reflex to the near-drowning.

The dead battery, out-of-service message, borrowed phone and handmade case distribute failure across infrastructure, concealment and intervention. Kairi's metaphorical funeral94–95, counterfeit message120, surfers' actual throw158 and retrieval accident163 are not a single suicidal event. Three stops—an abandoned case throw100 and Tōji's stops115/133—also differ in object and ethical stakes. Repeated hands shift from forced removal to pinning, rescue and final voluntary handholding. Manga staging is essential to those differences; dialogue alone would flatten them.
