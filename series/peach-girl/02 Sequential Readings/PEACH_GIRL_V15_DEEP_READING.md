## 2. Sequential reading and close analysis

### A missed call, a declined answer and two recoveries

The opening fireworks reprise4–6 resumes Tōji's offer. Momo says she could not hear, but her later private reflection13 explicitly admits pretending. Literal nonreceipt would misread her evasive action as an acoustic accident. She neither accepts nor dismisses her renewed feeling. Her missed-call display7 newly confirms Kairi attempted to contact her ten minutes earlier. This is later evidence of a call, not permission to rewrite V14's contact-screen frontier as already established communication.

She calls back; the phone rings beside spilled water at the shop while the owner carries Kairi toward hospital8. The doctor9 describes incipient/developing pneumonia, hospital care and reassurance. V14 diagnosed influenza with pneumonia risk; the new progression does not alter what was known then. Neither Momo nor Tōji receives the hospital explanation. Tōji's suggestion that an important caller will try again10 is reasonable conjecture within ignorance, yet comfort does not supply the missing information.

Sae's next-day claim to have seen the fireworks15 and Tōji's exaggerated comic handling of her16 retain their familiar tonal conflict. His teasing17 invokes Momo's old extreme reactions; it is not a present diagnosis or new crisis. Shared work, old trials and a smile18–20 give Momo renewed bodily feeling21–22. She thinks continuing like this may make her unable to restrain her feelings for Tōji. This precedes Kairi's missed appointment: the return cannot be compressed into an affectionless replacement chosen only after rejection. Her image of Tōji always sacrificing for her23 remains her grateful generalization, qualified by his earlier hurtful method and actual V14 apology.

The owner brings Kairi a charger24. Kairi's fear of dying clarifies whom he wanted beside him25, but Momo is absent. The jigsaw counsel26–28 revises the earlier idealization problem: two imperfect people may add what is missing through effort. It is not a promise that one predestined piece will fit without labor, or that Momo must repair him. Momo meanwhile asks for different shifts to escape renewed attraction. Tōji volunteers the change, and she retracts rather than make him bear its cost28–30. Her attempted management of proximity shows agency and concern, without solving the desire that prompted it.

Kairi's email33–34 tells her about the soba job, travel money and wanting to see her soon. Momo actually reads it, cries with relief35 and privately reaffirms love37. A small generic assurance carries enormous weight because waiting lacked any support. It does not contain the first-choice comparison PR0019 requires, and it omits the hospitalization. At the store farewell38–42 Momo finally gives Tōji the exact noon/five-minute/give-up terms39; this new receipt cannot be backdated to his V14 partial knowledge. His assurance she can be happy40 does not guarantee Kairi's attendance.

### A blank puzzle, a single rose and the wrong recipient

Kairi receives100000yen for a month's work44–45, with the couple framing generosity as a parting gift. His return gift is a blank500-piece puzzle46: the owner and Sanae can draw or write their own shared work. His promised future masterpiece with Momo48 is spoken to the couple, not an achieved relationship or a statement Momo hears. The blank surface turns fitting a given picture into making one together.

He arrives at Yamate early50, but is not yet at the agreed plaza at the agreed time. His imagined hug is not an actual reunion. The florist cannot supply out-of-season peach blossoms51. Misao appears52, and Kairi directly tells her Momo is now his most loved person53–54; he retrospectively calls his old attachment closer to admiration. This is a consequential new self-account, not proof every earlier feeling was unreal. It is also the central recipient error: the reader and Misao receive precisely what Momo needs, while she receives only the generic email.

Misao's praise55 is not a reversal of her sibling-only refusal. She plans to express love to Ryo despite his current relationship with Sae and possible rejection56. Flower language offers a route where direct words feel difficult. Kairi chooses a single red rose57–58; the asserted meanings belong to the conversation, not independently verified botanical fact. He privately worries Ryo will hurt her, distinguishing concern from continuing romantic jealousy without certifying his self-interpretation eternally.

### Violence, actual obligations and the choice inside an emergency

Ryo's arrival brings confirmation that he brought Kairi's insurance card and helped at hospital59, then a demand for repayment60. This bounded brotherly assistance complicates him without absolving the prior coercion and exploitation. An unnamed man alleges guaranteed profits and bankruptcy61–62. Ryo's reply about risk and personal inadequacy is his self-serving explanation, not an adjudicated account of the business. The man's weapon is a sword/katana, later identified as imitation74/109; early private bat labels were corrected before canonical writing.

Misao interposes64 as the man swings. The impact is elided, and later dialogue establishes Ryo suffered contusions74. Misao is shaken, not securely shown physically wounded. Momo reaches the crowd65, sees Kairi tending Misao and helping her into a taxi66–67, and hears a bystander's sword/one-injured account68. Her repeated calls of Kairi's name do not securely reach him. She has partial true observation and an emotionally compelling incomplete explanation.

Kairi answers her phone call71–72, apologizes for an urgent delay and says he will call again, but does not name Ryo, the injury, Misao or police. Momo asks whether it cannot wait; she does not tell him she saw the taxi. Both withhold the detail that might connect their interpretations. At hospital75 Kairi also has police questioning pending, and Ryo is being questioned. This is a real external constraint, not merely a frivolous romantic preference. Yet Misao offers to speak for him and urges him to go; he initially says he cannot leave her frightened and assumes endless future time with Momo. Her reply76 insists each day and date is singular. Constraint, available alternative and chosen priority must all remain in the causal account.

He recognizes that feeling already together with Momo internally has not told her anything76. His next call fails at12:30_77. Momo remains waiting at12:50_78 and eventually with Tōji at1:55_84: she does not leave at12:05. The log79 shows8/30 11:50. She manually switches off her phone80; the intercut does not fix the exact second relative to his failed call. Her interpretation that Misao still comes first is wrong about his privately declared preference but understandable from his conduct and incomplete explanation. Kairi reaches the empty plaza at2:15_88 and calls her mother at2:55_91. Being early somewhere else50 did not fulfill the appointment.

### Tōji follows; Momo asks for solitude

Momo travels toward Shiranami; Tōji is on the same train90. His later apology102 explicitly says he followed because her departure troubled him. No prior Momo invitation to a joint romantic trip is established. She kneels crying on sand97; the foam portrait98 and old rescue recollection95 do not depict current drowning. Her question103 whether she looked suicidal, and his denial, are not evidence of an actual plan.

The persistent beach stranger ignores refusals and grabs her wrist99–101. Tōji intervenes without a shown strike; the man leaves102. Momo says she wanted to be alone to arrange her thoughts103. Tōji offers to wait and return with her104, then actually sits at a distance105–106. The quiet sequence matters: spatially restrained presence gives her room after an uninvited following. Help, intrusion, patience and romantic investment need not collapse into one moral label.

Kairi travels with Sae107. She correctly criticizes the broken promise but wrongly assumes Momo is alone; she lacks Tōji's movements. Kairi finally tells Sae of Ryo's injury109. Her alarm110 and desire to see him show continuing attachment beyond comic matchmaking. The train stops because typhoon landslide disrupts service111; her shouting is not a depicted mechanical cause. She announces going home for Ryo114, without an actual hospital arrival being shown.

### Interrupted routes, received intimacy

Momo wanted to return home that day111, but suspended services, a closing station and full rooms112 strand her with Tōji. They use her existing inn reservation under Adachi/Okayasu115. The companion is Tōji; a reservation name does not change his identity or authorize intimacy. Separate bathing116 and adjacent futons117 create awkward proximity, followed by a blackout118–119.

Tōji initiates the tight embrace120 and asks whether she could choose him121. Prior affirmative permission for the initial hug is unshown. Actual kissing129 and Momo's arms around his neck130 subsequently establish responsive participation; that response is not blanket consent for every possible act. His ringing phone belongs to him132, with caller unidentified. His choice not to answer133 recalls the birthday interruption134 and his desire not to lose her again135. The parallel with Kairi's emergency is deliberate, but an unknown call cannot make ignoring all competing duties an exceptionless moral rule.

He recognizes her earring137, the same one worn that day; it is not Kairi's necklace or cross. Momo's pleased response gives bodily memory and material detail a shared language. His request to restart from that day138 is enacted through further kissing. The ellipsis, intimate undressed embrace150 and shared morning bed152–156 strongly imply a sexual encounter. A faithful non-graphic reading must retain that implication without inventing a specifically depicted act or medical proof. Momo turns toward him155, participates in renewed affection and thinks happiness with him possible156. She remains a desiring subject.

At157 services resume and they leave holding hands. Her private questions158–159 ask whether she is using Tōji to escape Kairi's pain, whether one night can reverse her feeling and whether she is being swept along. These questions do not invalidate affection already present22, nor can affection prove the questions baseless. Tōji does not hear the complete uncertainty.

### The road does not deliver its message

Kairi spends money and effort toward Shiranami125–151. The driver warns roads are closed; the single rose wilts126. Kairi plans to give it with his feelings127, but planning is not delivery. When the driver assumes another day would not matter after long waiting139, Kairi identifies that exact assumption as his own arrogance140. His accountability is explicit; fate alone is insufficient.

A fallen tree blocks the mountain road140–142. With20km still remaining143, he pays the fare, climbs onward144, falls and scrapes himself145, then remembers the inn telephone146. His call147–148 reaches staff, who say Momo and her companion arrived under the Okayasu booking two hours earlier. The mistaken registration yields a true companion presence with a false name. His battery dies before he gives his own identity or reaches Momo. Her request for a flashlight after spilling tea149 is not receipt of his call. He learns someone accompanies her, without secure identification as Tōji.

At160 he is muddy and injured at Shiranami station. Adjacent161 already places Momo/Tōji on the return train: montage is a missed crossing, not their meeting. Momo privately wonders whether the absence of a clear breakup leaves hope. Tōji later calls her back162 and raises their old L-shaped love sign163–164. She runs toward him165 and embraces166, while Kairi is separately shown seaside with the wilted rose. Her wish to be happy is a present chosen return, not proof she has permanently extinguished Kairi or informed him.

The volume gives Kairi a clear internal first choice and Momo a different actual partner. The loss is neither a simple punishment for divided love nor an unmotivated accident: declared boundaries, incomplete explanations, specific choices, actual emergencies, broken channels and delayed travel each contribute. Reader knowledge makes the separation painful without authorizing an omniscient verdict against the people who lack it.

## 4. Counterreadings and prospective accountability

An accident-only reading preserves the hospital emergency and storm but loses Kairi's own admission140 and his earlier assumption75. A wholly culpable preference reading loses his actual M-first words53–54, pending police questioning and Misao's offered alternative. The source supports an interaction: real obligations constrain him, his chosen incomplete speech leaves Momo without their meaning, and his assumption of renewable time exposes a limit in his care.

A purely loveless rebound reading loses Momo's present Tōji attraction22 before the missed meeting, her responsive kisses and final returning embrace. A wholly resolved true-love reading loses her actual private doubts158–161 and the incomplete ending with Kairi. Tōji's distance105–106 is material counterevidence to an exclusively opportunistic account; his uninvited following102 and initial unasked hug120 prevent automatic idealization. His unknown caller132 is not a known emergency he virtuously ignores.

The visual forms carry these distinctions: generic email versus internal declarations; clocks extending five minutes into hours; flourishing bouquet versus fallen petals and Kairi's single wilted rose; a blank puzzle versus imposed perfect matching; the earring's shared birthday memory; silent beach distance; adjacent trains and missed crossing; the old L sign actually received versus the rose never delivered. Sound, gesture and object can communicate, but their recipients must be shown.

PR0019 is DISCONFIRMED under the exact original whole-V15 test. Misao receives the comparison; Momo does not. No rescue of the forecast through insight, effort or generic love. PR0020 instead asks whether Momo directly discloses the actual renewed Tōji involvement to Kairi in V16. It forecasts a new information problem, not permanent partner choice, model readiness or a repeated chance for the failed test.
