# Volume 8 — protection's injury and the terms of staying

Tōji's attempt to protect Momo continues as conduct that injures her: rejected communication, exposure to a claimed replacement, a closed door and the destruction of a valued gift. His private explanation to Misao confirms the protective reasoning without turning that reasoning into the only possible choice or its harmful result into success. Kairi helps Momo materially, awkwardly and sometimes intrusively; relief, gratitude, particular affection and persistent love for Tōji do not arrive as a neatly ordered replacement romance. Momo's central work is to distinguish what she wants from what comfort permits her to endure. Her direct admission that she still loves Tōji reaches Kairi before he offers to stay on those terms. The concluding kisses intensify their intimacy, but no explicit reciprocal dating agreement resolves that declared uncertainty.

## 4. Close reading: love, injury and choices

### 4.1 Momo believes the cause while suffering the conduct

Sae supplies a retrospective story10 in which she cared for Momo and Tōji overrode that care through passion. It fits the visible coupling while contradicting the reader's witnessed V07 refusals and pressure. This distinction matters: Momo's continuing belief in photograph coercion18/20 is not simply inability to accept an evident truth. She has already inferred the central cause correctly. Yet correct causal belief cannot prevent the daily injury of Tōji's actual behavior. He discards her note14 without depicted reading, refuses restart and invokes Sae's displeasure15. His conduct is not made unreal because the offered reason is misleading (EV0002–0005).

Kairi's comic enactment of her hurt17 makes space for anger she does not freely direct at Tōji; she immediately protects Tōji from his criticism. The source does not thereby make Kairi the authorized voice of Momo's hidden complete truth. Sae's argument21 goes further: if love existed, Tōji could violently retrieve the negatives and return. This is a strategically narrowed counterfactual, not proof that violence is the sole meaningful test of love. V07 already showed resistance, demands and grabs without complete recovery. Momo's choice to ask again24 for shared problem-solving offers a different response. The text does not show that response attempted or guarantee it would defeat Sae (EV0004–0006).

At the house, Tōji's private pained watching25 contrasts with Momo's hope that visible endurance can demonstrate her strength26. When he comes out, she treats approach as possibility; he makes it another rejection. The returned birthday-party photographs28 are ordinary couple history given back for disposal, not the blackmail material. Confusing those objects would turn emotional repudiation into a nonexistent evidence handover. The scent bottle concentrates the same injury: Momo reaches, his hand opens beyond her reach, glass breaks29–30. His assertion that his hand slipped is recorded, while staging strongly supports purposeful cruelty. The analyst need not invent an explicit confession of intent to register that discrepancy (EV0007–0008).

Momo names it the precious first present and puns on his love31. The V06 correction still matters: Sae steered its selection, yet Tōji gave it and Momo invested it with shared meaning. It was never either a wholly independent aesthetic discovery or a meaningless fake object. Destroying it attacks a relationship Momo still believes can be saved. The transparent door34 brings reaching and turned back into one composition; it is not literal reentry. The later taped bottle64 materializes preservation after attempted repudiation. Momo's attachment is not reduced to an abstract inability to move on; it survives in things she handles (EV0008–0009/0018).

### 4.2 Tōji explains himself to someone other than Momo

Kairi confronts Tōji35 about concrete consequences: illness after hours crying in snow. His attack and Tōji's retaliation make Momo's hurt produce another bodily cost. Misao treats both; Kairi leaves and Tōji stays38. It is Tōji who speaks in39–44. He calls repeated trust dog-like, then covers his face and says he did not want to hurt her in this way. The abandoned-dog simile belongs to his defensive description, not an analytical definition of Momo. His distress and his injuring action remain simultaneous facts (EV0010–0012).

He now explicitly tells Misao the photograph/media ultimatum. His explanation distinguishes heartbreak that may fade from a public label he fears might last a lifetime43. The imagined tabloid image is his anticipated harm, not a publication event. “Only way I could think”44 defines his constrained reasoning, not a narrator certification that no alternative existed. Momo offered collaboration repeatedly. A reading that respects his fear can still identify how that fear licensed exclusion of the person most affected. Misao's comforting hand makes his suffering visible; it does not undo Momo's suffering or endorse every decision (EV0012–0013; CL0023@2).

Misao then acts on what she has learned. Sae feigns headache56; she is the patient addressed as Kashiwagi57, not Kairi. Misao asks for negatives and challenges disregard for Tōji's feelings. Sae denies the media threat58, cries accusation of partiality59, then drops that performance when challenged60 and asks for proof. Here “both sides” cannot erase source knowledge: V07 directly depicted her threat, so the denial is false. What Misao can establish institutionally remains a separate problem. Sae's complaint61 about a home visit is reported evidence of that visit, not its actual depiction. Her chin-grip/property assertion62 shows how a preferred-girl claim can coexist with failure to obtain affection (EV0016–0017).

### 4.3 Repetition distinguishes returned objects from returned people

Tōji returns the phone by express post49–50, avoiding the encounter Momo hoped its return might permit. Her recalled no-need words51 extend an object statement into a felt account of her own rejection; they are not a new phone conversation. The dog bark52 triggers a remembered earlier visit53, then a present barefoot rush54 that finds only animals. The source distinguishes memory, expectation and actual arrival.
