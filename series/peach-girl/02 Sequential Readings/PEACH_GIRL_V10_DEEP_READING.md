# Volume 10 — credibility, competing accounts, and the limits of permission

A loving act becomes visible before a persuasive speaker becomes trustworthy. Kairi opens Momo's handmade case, wears it, and protects it at substantial bodily cost; Momo revises her fear that his affection is empty. That repair does not settle their knowledge of each other or his treatment of her refusals. Ryo supplies recognition Momo wants: he takes her anger at Sae seriously, praises her effect on Kairi, presents himself as the brother who absorbs blame to protect him, and defends her against an inverted account of the hotel attack. Some advice is sensible and some help real. The reader nevertheless sees him receive a call meant for Kairi and sustain the mistaken identity. The final assault exposes a contradiction already present beneath his reassuring account, rather than changing a verified good man into a bad one without preparation.

Momo's decisions remain differentiated. She feels attraction to Ryo's polished attention, permits one compassionate hug, and continues to name Kairi as the person she loves. She refuses the week of work, agrees only to a questionnaire, refuses the necklace, and tries to leave when Ryo advances. These choices cannot be compressed into total passivity or permission for everything that follows. Kairi's concern about his brother gains direct evidential support while his attempted violence and restriction of her social contact remain harmful. The cutoff places Momo under force and Kairi in the corridor: the need for help is established, successful delivery is not.

## 4. Close reading

### 4.1 The handmade object changes what the lover can know

Momo's anger begins with something she actually saw: Kairi accepted Morika's invitation and gift. Her own labor had remained secret. Opening the package9 gives him knowledge he lacked when accepting the commercial case. His thanks12 and report of its return answer part of that injury; wearing her case14 makes the response material. She cuts the call short, privately creates a deadline, then treats his absence as a decision against her. This deadline measures fear and hope, not a promise he broke: she never communicates it. Street attack and hospital scenes make the gap visible to the reader while withholding it from Momo.

The reservation supplies a surname, not Kairi's identity. Her friend's childhood anecdote offers an emotional script, and her imagined embrace27 gives it an image before she reaches507. Neither is evidence of Kairi's hotel conduct. Dismissing her as simply irrational would suppress his actual prior assent and the ambiguity he left her. The volume provides a mistaken conclusion with intelligible premises and a missing identity, rather than a rule that jealousy has no evidence.

Injury reaches Momo39 only after Kairi's mediation explanation38. His protected case gives her a costly sign of attachment, answered with a chosen embrace and explicit private recognition42. Their comic face-pinch and her earlier punch do not make all force harmless. This particular care is established; bodily sacrifice does not prove innocence in every scene. V09 restraint after refusal remains independently actual. Even the real gift repair is incomplete knowledge, not a moral certificate.

### 4.2 Desire, pressure and permission remain local

Momo's love-hotel refusal45–47 does not mean absence of desire. Her private reflection says she has begun considering intimacy; the setting and Kairi's deception violate what she wants for a first memory. The church is imagined, making her criteria legible without inventing an encounter. Kairi quickly translates the wish into a weekend plan before her flustered response settles its terms. The brochures later prove effort, not permission. Her hope that new memories ease the old hotel image does not guarantee recovery through sex.

Sae tries to make lack of sex evidence of inferior love58–62. Her daily-sex boast about Tōji is uncorroborated by their depicted monitoring conflict. The trauma taunt weaponizes Momo's incapacitation and uncertain missing interval; it neither independently establishes the act there nor clinically explains her present choices. Momo rejects the status comparison and angrily grabs Sae's clothing. That anger recognizes a real injury being converted into a joke; her aggression is still an act to record, not automatically justified by victimhood.

The hug125–127 has a precise sequence: request, shock, withdrawal/apology, then specific permission. Momo returns the embrace and gives it a compassionate meaning. Her choice remains actual even if the bereavement story proves false. It cannot authorize the refused necklace165, unsolicited kiss or intrusive touch166–167, or blocked exit168–169. She pushes away and declares she is leaving. Ryo's subsequent force makes the violation explicit without requiring a graphic account or a completed further act. Analysis follows changed conditions rather than infer one permanent permission from an earlier affectionate image.

### 4.3 Credibility recruits trust without establishing truth

Ryo's anti-revenge advice67 addresses a conflict Momo has lived. Praising her effect on Kairi74–75 acknowledges the relationship she has worked to sustain. His Morika account places a different cause under Kairi's resentment: she was originally Ryo's girlfriend and used tutoring to play the younger brother. Illustrated recollections make it vivid but remain inside his telling. No independent admitted witness establishes the whole chronology or exclusively protective secrecy.

Accepting the confidence73 causes Momo to relay only his care-Kairi request79. Kairi hears her accept a surface image; she thinks he cannot recognize his brother's quietly borne hurt. His every-girlfriend warning is broad and attributed. Phone reveal87 supplies narrower direct proof of a real reason for distrust: Ryo sustains her mistaken identity, then gives false proxy story93. This is actual deception, not merely Kairi's jealousy. His partly accurate suggestion that Morika forced the ride97 shows how a truthful detail can coexist with a false frame.

Ryo later defends Momo against Sae's reversed hotel accusation148–152. She hears someone refuse slander she has struggled to answer. That help has emotional consequence without proving integrity. His Sae explanation161–163 names behavior she recognizes, offers relief from feeling uniquely targeted, and promises advocacy. Recognition becomes a source of trust. The contradiction is a speaker condemning treatment of people as tools after impersonation has already redirected Momo. The final assault strengthens an exploitative trust-building interpretation; it does not retrospectively prove every historical story false or every helpful word consciously fabricated.

### 4.4 Attraction does not erase priority or refusal

The party gives Momo an unfamiliar escort, grand setting, public admiration of her companion and attention to comfort. Her flutter and thought that she might fall117 are actual attraction. Calling the encounter wholly brotherly would compress her; declaring completed new love would outrun her words. At124 she names Kairi's support as the route through grief. At146 she privately can think of no one else. The attraction and commitment coexist under pressure.

Her decision to disclose146–147 initiates repair. Asking Ryo permission153 shows the confidence still has moral weight for her. The volume interrupts before Kairi receives it; that is a temporal limit, not a permanent refusal to explain. Declining the research week153 explicitly prioritizes Kairi. The smaller questionnaire154 recruits a different, limited assent after her refusal. Income, loans and business-interest questions159 do not match teenage-romance research102. Completion164 is actual cooperation, not knowing organizational membership, financial commitment or physical permission.

The repeated crosses165 begin to challenge specialness before the assault. They link Momo to Misao and Morika while weakening unique romantic identification. They do not prove identical relationships or sexual histories for all recipients. Her alarm and gift refusal are her own judgments. Remembering Kairi's warning166 revises her view at a new frontier; she lacked this confirmation when defending Ryo142. The reader's earlier knowledge must not be used to ridicule her as knowingly consenting to deception or harm.

### 4.5 Accurate concern and harmful response coexist

Kairi sees the hug129 without its preceding account. His swing133 is dodged; the collar-grab and threat134 remain intimidation. The whisper135 is withheld from reader and Momo; assigning exact contents would invent the explanation the form denies. Outside, forbidding contact/approach139 makes concern controlling. Momo's objection140—that dating does not transfer choice of social contacts—is not refuted by the later assault.

Their trust argument distinguishes her intentions from Ryo's conduct. Kairi correctly infers interception141; she hears another unsupported attack on his brother. She can truthfully intend no romance and misjudge what he may do. Kairi can correctly identify risk and fail to communicate assessable evidence. Neither valid autonomy nor one true inference settles the whole conflict. His exit145 is not an explicit breakup. His run158 follows Morika's disclosure of Ryo's executive role157; corridor170 does not establish completed rescue or even that he heard the distressed text there.

### 4.6 Sae and Morika exceed their plot functions

Modeling transition48–51 threatens Sae's status and hoped-for entertainment route. Her complaint about constant following52 is real despite Tōji's harm-prevention purpose and her earlier harms. His dating claim does not prove reciprocal love. She escapes, considers breakup and regrets her choice55; no completed breakup appears. Her party claim to be free110 is further self-presentation, not verified new status.

Actual tears115 oppose a reading in which every emotion is tactical performance. Status, personal attraction, exclusion by the brother connection and mixed motives remain alternatives. Spying and slander follow; real distress does not cancel harmful action. Momo's wish that she not mistreat Tōji116 complicates enemy-only feeling without creating forgiveness or renewed romantic choice. Neither character becomes a single immutable function because the conflict recurs.

Morika's knife attack33–37 is dangerous although the guidebook blocks penetration. Pushing Kairi into a deceptive ride90 and pressing seminar attendance155–157 differ from his freely choosing reunion. The recruiter/executive disclosure connects their offers in one setting, but full coordination of every diversion remains unproved. Her apology relay request98 and withheld job details101 coexist. Debts, organization and her full knowledge remain open; a recognizable seminar pattern is not permission to invent a business mechanism.

## 5. Japanese expression and manga form

岡安 supports two mistakes: surname reservation25 and assumed phone recipient85–87. Morika'sりょう33, Kairi's older-brother identification38 and full岡安涼 self-introduction111 establish identity in stages. Cropped wet-haired face/mouth prolong ambiguity until the wider87 reveal exposes Ryo beside showering Kairi. The reader gains correction Momo lacks. This is a visual information operation, not a claim about acoustic performance in unvoiced manga.

The page37 book revises apparent wound33–34. Once shown, blood-like ink cannot establish penetrating injury or a fake prop. Momo's imagined embrace27, desired church47, old hotel memory76 and Ryo's illustrated histories71/121 have different evidential status.
