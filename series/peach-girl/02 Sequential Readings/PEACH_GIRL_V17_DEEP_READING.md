## 2. Continuous reading and analytical distinctions

### 2.1 A refusal in the gondola and a lie about tears: i0004–i0042

The opening returns to the tear and lingering apology, while Momo alone thinks about Kairi crying and his handbook portrait4–5. The reader carries his private V16 Momo attachment into a scene where she still lacks its explanation. Sae and Ryo visit Takarayama Familyland6–17. His initial kindness answers her fear that leaving without speaking yesterday meant hatred. Saying he will not hate her is not agreeing to resume their relationship. The closing park gives his next comparison a cruel practicality: other, bigger parks will replace this one, and she should find another man9. He says he cannot date her and wants to cherish only Aki/Misao10.

Sae's pregnancy question produces a drug box11, a refusal of fatherhood12 and an envelope described as treatment money13. Ryo's statements about an oral abortifacient, approval and safety are fictional character assertions. They are neither verified medical guidance nor an adequate account of Sae's options. No ingestion, completed clinical examination or termination occurs here. His claim that she cannot raise a child alone narrows her future for her; the analysis must not adopt it as social fact. Sae actually slaps him13. Her violence matters without converting his reproductive pressure into justified conduct. She clings outside the gondola14–16, asking to do anything he wants; his single request is that she break up with him. His right to refuse a romance remains distinct from the coercive means and prior harm.

At school Momo privately asks whether Kairi saw the kiss18. This is a plausible question, not a received explanation. When she finds Sae miserable, her response joins concern with a warning not to harm Misao19–20. She says she saw yesterday's public fight, not that she knows the entire hospital story. Asked whether the pregnancy is really true, Sae nods21. Her thanks22 for Momo's care is actually received and locally sincere in form; it cannot be erased merely because later information changes the medical claim. Sae then says the conversation with Ryo has reached no conclusion23, withholding the actual refusal. Momo offers to listen and says she is happy with Tōji24. Her private reflection25 insists that this happiness is not a lie while admitting that Kairi's crying face is unforgettable. The volume gives neither a wholly false Tōji attachment nor a settled absence of Kairi feeling.

Misao narrowly avoids a falling plant pot26. Kairi suspects Sae, invoking earlier raw garbage in a shoe locker and a near push on stairs27. These additional incidents are reported or recalled, not independent depictions of a securely identified attacker; Misao says she did not see who did this. Do not promote the pot's authorship from suspicion to fact. Misao says she should stop seeing Ryo but cannot switch off feeling simply because she knows this28. Kairi embraces her29 in recognition of emotional disorder. Momo and classmates see the embrace. The unnamed short supportive friend can be recognized as recurring, but the page does not supply a secure new personal name.

Kairi calls after Momo, retreats into “never mind,” then gives her a false explanation32: he came upset about Misao and Ryo and cried because only Momo knew his Misao attachment. The existence of that old confidence makes the lie plausible; it does not make its stated causal explanation true. Momo accepts Misao as the reason33–34, undoing her own hope. Misao is not shown receiving this whole exchange. The later true account to Misao54–57 supplies an unusually clear within-volume check against Kairi's self-presentation.

Tōji catches Momo's arm before a red-light traffic near miss35. The page does not establish intentional self-harm. He brings her to the same closing park on a separate visit36–42. Free admission is part of the closure service; he has chosen the quiet venue with her tastes in mind. In the gondola he names their earlier breakup there39 and wants to replace the last memory with new happy ones. That is specific restorative care and a future pledge, not proof the future is already secured. Momo initiates the embrace40 and he holds her. Her responsive affection is actual. No sexual encounter is depicted. The recurring gondola makes emotional departure and an attempt at renewed attachment occupy the same physical form, without making either pair identical.

### 2.2 Stopped violence, explicit renunciation and an unwanted embrace: i0044–i0084

Misao is alone after school when Sae raises a claw hammer42/44. Kairi stops the swinging wrist before impact45–46. The attempted attack is now actually attributed to Sae, unlike the unobserved pot incident. Sae threatens Misao and connects Ryo's refusal and drug box to a belief that removing Misao would bring him back47–49. Her assertion of a right to happiness does not create a right to another person's body or affection. Misao's warning about the drug remains dialogue within the fiction, not independent pharmacological verification. Misao pours water on Sae's head50, an actual forceful calming intervention. Sae's own assault does not make every intervention ethically weightless.

Misao says she will never date Ryo51 and confirms the promise when Sae asks52. This is a received renunciation, not merely private hesitation. Misao escorts her away; the exhausted taxi return53 strongly implies the home escort, but does not show the interior arrival. Kairi resists making Misao solely responsible for Ryo and Sae. He then tells Misao the true doorstep story54–57: he wanted to tell Momo he still loved her, saw her kissing Tōji, tried to leave unseen, was caught, cried, and regretted confusing her. His current willingness to let Tōji make her happy is a withdrawal intention. It has not yet become stable conduct. The truthful recipient is Misao. Momo still has the substitute explanation32.

Misao meets Ryo outside work58–66, wearing glasses he finds unusual. She confronts the drug and money. Ryo says he did talk, will not date Sae or become a father, and presents abortion as the only choice59–61. Misao answers with Sae's stated wish to give birth, refusing to let his claim of acting for her best settle the matter. When he removes her glasses62, the familiar romantic gesture becomes an interpretive indictment: she had removed glasses to look good with him, and had removed the “glasses” of her heart that might have let her see him63. Her goodbye64 and explicit never-date-you response65 are actually heard; she walks away66. His claim that he was deliberately cruel to prevent lingering is an excuse she rejects. His shock does not establish reform, and a present emphatic refusal cannot verify every future encounter.

The next school day Momo's attempt to return the handbook becomes an encounter with Kairi, not Tōji67–71. Kairi picks it up, sees the portrait and learns she had it. This is the actual material return and finder discovery deferred from V16. Momo explains she found it under his desk during PE75. She could not casually return a book whose owner might be mistaken for Tōji because it contained her picture. The object exposes a contradiction no public apology had resolved: why keep her photograph if Misao is the one he likes76? Kairi again says his home visit concerned Misao77. Momo explicitly asks him to stop stirring her feelings78 and cries79.

Kairi embraces her80. Momo says stop and release her; he tightens after the refusal81. His “I love you”82 is an actual confession heard by Momo, and the continued bodily contact is an actual boundary violation. Neither fact cancels the other. The manga uses the embrace's apparent romantic intensity while giving Momo speech that refuses its physical terms. A reading that keeps only the sincere confession loses her resistance; one that calls the feeling entirely false loses later explicit and private evidence. Tōji arrives and witnesses the embrace83–84. These are narrative pages and remain part of continuous coverage.

### 2.3 Choice recovered and constrained; a hired attack changes direction: i0086–i0124

Kairi grabs and tightens again when Momo tries to move toward Tōji87. Both tell him to release her; he refuses88. His plea to Tōji to “give her back”89 treats Momo as transferable between men. Tōji answers that she is not a dog90. That objection has ethical force without making Tōji's ensuing rivalry entirely free from pressure. Kairi denies that she is a fallback after Ryo took Misao91 and distinguishes the person who first recognized him, still important, from the Momo he loves now92. Momo and Tōji actually hear this distinction. It supplies a new present comparison; it cannot rescue the exact failed disclosure windows in V15 or V16. Tōji pulls her away95 and Kairi's hand finally opens. Momo has not consented to resume a relationship with Kairi.

Ryo hires a cap-wearing man to assault Sae and render her unconscious, after which he would arrange a hospital intervention97. His disclaimer that he is not asking them to kill the child now does not make the coercive plan nonviolent. No paid amount or receipt is shown. Sae arrives delighted and kisses him98. A returning woman appears with men99; bounded visual comparison with V10_33–34 supports Morika's identity. V17 does not newly speak her name to Momo. The comparison concerns identity continuity, not an unshown character introduction.

At the park Sae asks if she may give birth103. Ryo says he does not want to leave his genes and imagines himself as a harmful father104. This is his self-devaluation, not genetic truth. His account of Kairi joins envy, resentment and love105–106; sometimes wishing his brother absent is not a demonstrated plan to kill him. Sae recognizes a resemblance107–108: she envies Momo's strength and ability to have people understand her, feels people leave her, and says this is why Ryo attracts her. Those are her generalizations. They do not erase Momo's actual ostracism, vulnerability or labor of repair. Sae places his hand on her belly and promises a family and lifelong companionship109. His affected expression110 is a real change in the immediate scene. It cannot retrospectively delete the hired plan.

When the men arrive, Ryo quietly cancels it112 and leaves with an arm around Sae. Cancellation is performed, not just imagined. One man then clubs Ryo113 and announces a change of plan114. Ryo urges Sae to run; she does115. The attack on him remains actual harm despite his earlier commission. In the classroom Tōji asks Momo to choose between the two men now, saying neither will resent her choice115–116. This restores a choice displaced by the men's earlier contest, while imposing an immediate binary timetable. Momo starts to answer, but Sae's phone call interrupts117. She reports Ryo being beaten in a park, says she escaped and asks for help118. Momo tells the others119. The call transfers an emergency, not Ryo's private family talk or the hiring facts.

Morika holds Sae and explains that Ryo hired the men to attack her120. Their KIMASSE connections and Morika acquaintance give her a route into the plan; Ryo did not know that connection. This is a new account heard by Sae, not automatically by Momo, Tōji or Kairi. Morika urges Sae to forget him121, invoking pregnancy as an accepted claim rather than a new examination. Sae instead shields Ryo122; the cap-wearing man strikes her back123 and she falls124. The bodily intervention supplies costly attachment without proving that attachment was always benign or that a pregnancy actually existed.

### 2.4 What was lost, who learns it, and a choice made: i0126–i0164

The classroom trio does not arrive at the park as heroic rescuers127. An ambulance comes128. Morika answers Ryo's phone130 and reports an ambulance transfer and Sae shielding him, describing an abdominal hit; the depicted club impact123 is to Sae's back. Preserve the difference between report and panel rather than silently “repairing” either. She hangs up without identifying herself or giving a hospital131. Later Ryo answers Kairi and provides the route133; the trio actually arrives at emergency134.

In the ambulance Ryo asks attendants to prioritize Sae132. At the hospital his “my fault”136 is heard by the trio, but is not a complete confession of hiring. He identifies himself to the doctor as the child's father137, a new public acceptance of responsibility under the pregnancy belief, not independent proof of paternity. Momo, Tōji and Kairi seek Sae's family by telephone138. A police slip in conversation does not depict an actual report being filed. Momo privately feels some relief that the emergency postponed her answer138–140, then criticizes her own inability to choose despite having no complaint about Tōji. Relief and concern coexist; this is not pleasure at Sae's injury.

Sae returns to bed141. A private doctor–Ryo conversation143 withholds its object while Sae asks questions. She says a baby could not be confirmed by ultrasound and that she lost consciousness before hearing the rest146. She concludes the baby is gone147–148. The apparent miscarriage is initially Sae's interpretation of incomplete speech. Ryo then says there was no baby from the start: 想像妊娠149. He distinguishes delayed bleeding from miscarriage150. Sae reports a positive test and nausea; the doctor flashback offers a mechanism for the result. That mechanism is a fictional clinical assertion, not externally validated medicine. The story's disclosed outcome is no pregnancy; the character's sincere mistaken belief is not thereby deliberate fraud.

Sae herself tells Momo, Kairi and Tōji “想像妊娠だって”152. This explicit acknowledgment satisfies PR0021's original retraction test. The supporting result concerns the words and recipients, not the V16 suspicion that she was bluffing. She laughs, derides herself and says she never needed a family; the actual promise109 and current tears prevent that defensive account from cancelling her earlier desire. Her grief concerns an imagined future that had organized real commitments. No actual fetal death is established. No actual injury, reproductive coercion or distress is erased because there was no pregnancy. She asks to be left alone153; Tōji urges respect for the request154.

Tōji raises the romantic decision again155. Misao arrives because Kairi contacted her156; the trio sees her caring for Ryo158. Kairi explicitly tells Momo and Tōji he called and emailed her. Misao's question still assumes pregnancy156; she is not shown learning the correction here. Her care is actual contact, not proof that her explicit rejection has been permanently reversed. Momo's renewed anxiety at the tableau belongs to Momo; it does not establish Kairi's renewed Misao preference.

Kairi's fear becomes bodily comic distress159–161. He runs to the bathroom, rinses and coughs; a secure diagnosis or actual vomiting is not required by the image. Privately he is terrified161. Tōji tells Momo she owes him no decision from guilt161–162: hearts change, she should not blame herself if she chooses someone else, and should choose her own feeling. These are received words that loosen indebtedness after his earlier immediate demand. Neither episode should replace the other. Kairi returns163. Momo looks at both, takes Tōji's hand and apologizes to Okayasu164. The action and addressed apology make a present Tōji choice. They do not prove that Kairi feeling never existed, has permanently vanished, or that the series' final outcome is already known.

## 4. Counterreadings, form and forecast accountability

The strongest wholly deceptive-Sae reading points to prior schemes, current concealment23 and the actual hammer attack. It cannot account for mistaken clinical interpretation146–148, distress at correction, her family pledge109 and shielding122 simply by calling every emotion performed. The strongest fully redeemed-Sae reading keeps gratitude and bodily risk but loses attempted violence45 and the absence of durable repair. The nonpregnancy correction is factual within the story; an intentional fabricated pregnancy is not established. Her defensive claim that no family was needed152 receives counterevidence from her own pictured promise, not from a purity test.

Kairi's explicit present Momo love82/92 corrects a reading that he still merely wants Misao romantically. It does not excuse the false tear explanation or the refusal to release Momo. Tōji's protection and no-guilt words are substantial counterevidence to a single opportunist model, while the imposed immediate choice remains a counterexample to perfect patience. Momo's final gesture164 is an actual choice; the volume makes her divided feeling intelligible without declaring her incapable of choice or retrospectively making Tōji affection counterfeit.

The two gondola visits give the same enclosing structure opposite proposals: Ryo asks Sae to leave him, while Tōji asks Momo to build new memories. Glasses turn a routine attractive gesture into Misao's figure for chosen misrecognition. The portrait forces withheld feeling into a material object, but cannot itself supply a causal explanation. Repeated arms and hands shift between catching a traffic danger, stopping a weapon, unwanted romantic restraint, protective shielding and Momo's voluntary hand-taking. These physical differences make “holding” an inadequate single moral category.

Crosscutting repeatedly postpones Momo's answer while moving between a classroom and a park; it does not put the classroom cast at the assault. The hospital initially hides the object of the doctor's sentence143, allowing Sae's apparent-loss account before correcting it149. This is controlled disclosure, not permission to record a miscarriage as established. Comic bodily fear near the final decision coexists with serious vulnerability. Publication notes, posed breaks and a promotional collage frame the serial experience without supplying future mainline evidence.

PR0021 is SUPPORTED under its original test by Sae's own acknowledgment152 heard by Momo, Kairi and Tōji. The tested proposition was explicit retraction, not intentional invention. Its basis included V16 bluff suspicion; the outcome does not validate that suspicion's causal theory. PR0022 freezes a different question before V18 exposure: whether Momo actually receives BOTH Ryo injury and Kairi's police detention as the V15 late-arrival cause. Its narrow failure condition remains binding. Earlier failed windows stay failed despite the new confession/comparison.
