# Volume 9 — being chosen, being heard, and knowing the other

Momo and Kairi become publicly recognized as a couple before either their knowledge of each other or the reader's account of their motives is complete. Momo's own romantic feeling becomes explicit; it cannot be compressed into gratitude or rebound alone. Kairi's rescue and affectionate responses remain actual, while his repeated advance after her refusals makes a major qualification unavoidable: helping someone and saying she is special do not establish that he respects her will in another scene. Misao protects Momo bodily yet withholds Tōji's explanation and privately takes his side. Her own history with Kairi and his brother places her inside the conflict she counsels. The worn photograph and repeated necklace make identities appear legible, then expose how a plausible identification can go wrong. The final paired gifts turn an unseen labor of care into a rupture before any further explanation or proposed hotel visit occurs.

## 4. Close reading: reciprocal curiosity under unequal access

### 4.1 A couple category is not complete self-knowledge

The opening performs a real change from V08. Congratulations9 and Momo's later two-month account151 make current dating a source-supported category rather than the analyst's inference from a kiss. That does not rewrite V08's honest uncertainty. Her15–16 account still places the start before an explicit considered decision and treats forgetting Tōji as a duty to Kairi. The forged invitation exposes a continuing response to Tōji's name; it is not proof that her new affection is absent. She admits the lure to Kairi21 rather than protecting herself with a false story. Her poolside declaration37, wish to know him30/43, secret crafting49 and later145 self-recognition make her attachment particular and active. A rebound-only account cannot explain away these direct disclosures.

She also does harm. Kicking Kairi into the water40 follows his rescue even though she knows his fear and has just heard his drowning account. His immediate gasping at the edge41 prevents the comic panel from functioning as proof of harmless mutual play. The scene can stage irritation at his knowledgeable performance and still depict a risky response. Her resistance to the fans' coercive no-approach pledge is agency; her capacity for force does not make the later bed restraint consensual or easily escapable. Her83 statement that she could not move him is precisely a distinction between familiar comic retaliation and this frightening loss of control.

### 4.2 Rescue, desire and refusal need separate records

Kairi intervenes at the lighter37–39, says Momo special, then laughs/embraces with her44. Those are substantial acts, not narrative decoration to discard once he behaves badly. But the nurse-room sequence76–80 is explicit: Momo blocks the first approach, objects to place and timing, says no repeatedly, names fear/anger and calls out; his grip tightens. Misao's intervention changes the outcome. There is no source basis for calling the advance mutual or for treating her previous kisses as permission. Nor is a completed sexual act shown. Both limits are necessary to describe the actual harm accurately.

The threatened repetition89 is directed to Misao as leverage after she announces leaving. This strengthens the hypothesis that the approach also uses Momo to hold the teacher's attention. The cut to heard footsteps75, the necklace outburst and withheld8pm message supply a causal pattern. Nevertheless Momo's112 reconstruction remains an inference, not a confession from Kairi that every touch was staged solely for Misao. The proper result is competing attachment and instrumental use strongly supported at this event, while exclusive replacement motive stays unresolved. The later apology91 is prompted by Misao but actually spoken, including a promise to heed Momo's feeling. It creates a commitment to test; it does not certify reform. His public innuendo104 about yesterday being firmly joined turns the interrupted coercion into misleading intimacy before peers. Momo calls it a lie. A comic double punch cannot erase the preceding refusal or supply an unseen consensual completion.

### 4.3 Misao acts inside several loyalties

Misao is neither an all-knowing neutral counselor nor a false caregiver. Her interruption14 protects Momo from the gathered girls; her emergency departure69, nurse care and decisive intervention80 are real. She also remembers Tōji's confidence17 and chooses silence despite questioning whether this is right18. Her private Tōji allegiance20 and opposition to the new couple belong beside her professional demand that Kairi take Momo seriously. Tōji's secret is thus not simply missing because nobody could communicate it: an informed adult is making an actual choice under a confidence request and personal loyalty.

The tutor history62–68 makes Misao an affected participant. Her portrait of the accomplished considerate brother is affectionate testimony, not an objective biography, and her tenderness toward him need not imply a formally reciprocated prior relationship. Kairi's anger at the necklace can express jealousy, resentment at comparison and concern about her lingering hope at once. His criticism of Momo's lack of interest64 lands because she has already recognized her one-sided talk30, but that recognition does not license punishing or coercing her. The withheld party message links the old family network to the present plot. Kairi receives it71; Momo only hears its content108; Misao's three-hour wait103 reports its consequence. Collapsing these frontiers would remove the very process by which Momo reaches her suspicion.

### 4.4 A repeated necklace cannot uniquely identify its wearer

The photograph83–87 is material evidence of a past attachment, not an image of any sexual act. Momo's initial Misao inference has visual and biographical support. Her false94 denial of looking temporarily protects her from an awkward question while keeping Kairi unaware of her knowledge. She then watches his face98–101 without calling him. By116 he infers she looked and destroys the actual photograph, but disposal is a present gesture rather than proof that feeling has ended. Her refusal to accept a kiss as the whole answer119–120 distinguishes a demand for truth from demand for a prettier reassurance.

Morika's arrival adds a different woman, not confirmation that Misao was a hallucinated rival. Matching pearl crosses initially make her fit Momo's photograph question, but Kairi's141 expectation explicitly concerns Misao. The brother's gift habit137 explains why the same necklace is non-unique. Kairi's account143–144 addresses Morika: he reports that his brother took her, says the lingering feeling was resentment toward him, and reassures Momo that she is the one he wants. That can be sincere about Morika while leaving the Misao question unanswered. His failure to make the identity distinction explicit to Momo is consequential omission, not a complete revelation merely because he has said something autobiographical.

The dating history is attributed carefully. Morika says she dumped him three years ago135; he says his brother took her144. Those accounts can coexist but are not independent exhaustive verification of chronology or all motives. His account of brother resentment gives a new reason for the present sharpness without turning a family comparison into a deterministic excuse. The brother remains unnamed and unseen in the present admitted pages, although his actual calls71/157 establish more than the V08 spending report. Momo's positive reaction to the similar voice158 is a new impression, not a neutral confirmation of Misao's entire favorable portrait.

### 4.5 Momo's love and renewed trust are real but fallible

Momo's145 internal question about liking Kairi this much is strong evidence of romantic attachment now. Her anger at Morika, wanting to know him and labor on a personal gift are consonant with it; the direct statement carries more weight than a generic jealous-girlfriend label. It does not backdate completed certainty into V08 or prove Tōji feeling never persisted. Likewise her choice to trust146 is an action on partial information, not authorial certification that Kairi has always been harmless. Her thought that he will not hurt her is contradicted by76–83 if promoted into a global history. Keep that dissonance rather than silently changing either scene.

Morika tests that trust by claiming intimate bodily knowledge150–151 and provoking Momo through skin and age comparisons149. Her burn/ashtray story is a disturbing attributed claim about a past mark, not a verified act newly witnessed here. Momo's not-having-seen response and two-month dating account151 do not prove that reserve equals how much he values her; her tiny probably152 exposes insecurity beneath the public assertion that she is treasured. There is also no basis for accepting Morika's implicit hierarchy in which access to a body establishes superior entitlement. Kairi's public rejection of Morika153 and taking Momo's hand154 give actual evidence for her desired partner position. That evidence later meets an actual conflicting response, not merely another person's rumor.

### 4.6 Two cases and an interrupted gift

The old mobile case is damaged47–48; the phone works. Momo's labor begins in private49, proceeds through mistakes/yarn shortages72/96 and finishes155. It is not compensation she has been told she owes. Imagined pleasure in giving joins attraction with care for Kairi's ordinary needs. Her late delivery attempt156–159 involves active desire to see him and a new exchange with his brother, rather than passive waiting to be chosen.

In the bookshop, the composition first gives her a hopeful concealed approach, then a sequence of watching. Morika asks for one time at a specified hotel tomorrow161; Kairi agrees162. The reading must not supply a later explanation or actual visit. It also must not soften the visible assent into a refusal just because earlier scenes supported his attachment to Momo. Her hope that he will sharply reject the woman is drawn before his answer, making the reversal legible without omniscient narration.
