## 2. Narrative form and causal development

### A: Momo leaves the room; Kairi receives the aftermath

The V10 corridor cliffhanger invites a rescuer expectation, but the actual solution belongs to Momo. Her repeated no, first defensive kick and dash for the locked door are followed by recapture; the return to the door does not mean consent changed. Ryo's surname joke appropriates the name of the person she loves, while his inability-to-restrain-himself claim relocates responsibility onto attraction. The forced kiss16 and her writing-implement strike18 complete a sequence of rejected boundaries, self-defense and escape20. The page cut21 places Kairi outside, receiving her frightened embrace. He does not enter and interrupt the assault. The difference matters to agency and to the original PR0015 test, which is DISCONFIRMED rather than rescued by later comfort.

Momo fears she killed him22; the reader learns he is conscious24 before she verifies his condition. Sae enters the same room after Momo has left and receives the inverse story: Ryo says Momo pursued him and injured him when rejected. His account is directly false against the visible sequence. Sae's willingness to help and apology for her own earlier slander can be real acts without making her a reliable witness to the assault. The story creates two audiences with different explanations before closing the injury question through the hospital43. No wider school circulation is established.

Kairi's contribution is nevertheless substantive. He accompanies Momo back when she chooses to check the injury, embraces her after the empty-room discovery and explicitly rejects her attacker-blaming-herself account32. His reassurance counters her idea that distrust earned punishment. It does not turn the attack into harmlessness or constitute a clinical cure. The visually expansive embrace31 follows actual distress and actual care, rather than acting as a generic romance reset. The subsequent hotel/disinfection pitch42 and Momo's punch preserve a limit within relief: consent to a kiss is not an all-body invitation, and warmth does not require her to welcome every joke.

The cross becomes a device for transferring withheld histories. Momo asks Kairi to return it and tells the dead-lover premise33. He supplies a new report of V10's unheard whisper35: Ryo had asked for access to Momo and her breakup with Kairi, claiming her readiness and wanting Kairi's resentment. The present assault corroborates predatory access, while the claimed Momo agreement is contradicted by her actual choices. Only now does Momo relay Ryo's Morika-first history36. Kairi's competing account cannot be turned into a complete independently verified genealogy. Momo's reflection38 also supplies a neglected motive for trust: Ryo seemed like a possible future Kairi, formed in the same home. Her attraction, kinship confidence, compassion and misjudgment coexist; reducing the episode to naive infatuation loses its specific relation to Kairi.

### B: Ryo loses credibility while retaining power to set the question

Ryo's reported call47 reframes coercion as a test of Momo's judgment and encourages Kairi to keep trying with her. That is an assailant's self-serving reinterpretation, not the narrative's endorsement of an educational experiment. Kairi's spray gift49 has a practical protective meaning; his acceptance of Momo's refusal to revisit the company51 supplies a concrete contrast with the forced exit prevention. Her next choice to go60 arises partly from concern for Tōji after Sae's two-boyfriend boast. A person can care about an old partner without romantically re-choosing him. Kairi's account of feeling for hurt women, while tolerating women hurting his brother62, is an exposed partial loyalty rather than a universal ethical creed.

Momo's private comparison67 is ethically uncomfortable and therefore worth retaining: Kairi forgave her susceptibility to Ryo, whereas she could not forgive Tōji's susceptibility to Sae. It is her self-revision under a new experience. It neither removes Tōji's independent failures nor creates a duty to forgive a past boyfriend. The accidental drink spill gives her a practical reason to go to the washroom; it is not a secretly intentional separation.

The washroom scene changes Momo's tactics without making danger disappear. She raises the spray, holds a distance, demands that Ryo stop seeing Sae and actually calls Kairi79. Ryo offers a minimizing apology72 and claims one sexual encounter with Sae73; that claim and Sae's insinuation support an attributed encounter account, not independently depicted sex. His good-at-writing-scenarios boast74 strongly undermines the authority of his constructed histories. It is still not an explicit independent finding that every Aki detail is false or that Aki never existed. His broad acknowledgment of stealing girlfriends similarly adds testimony without supplying an exhaustive chronology.

Ryo's next tactic works because it is tailored to an existing uncertainty: a truly loved person cannot be touched, so perhaps Momo is not Kairi's beloved80. The first half is his proposition, not a medical or psychological law. The identity after his unfinished he-still remark81 remains unspoken. Feigned head pain then gets Momo close enough for him to knock the spray down82; the can falls beside the toilet, not into it. She has it again when Kairi arrives84 after passing his departing brother. Ryo leaves a card and a way back to him. His physical departure does not end the interpretive leverage of the question he planted.

### C: A chosen yes and a valid stop do not settle attachment

Momo asks Kairi whether he loves her91, and he answers affirmatively. She admits Ryo said something but does not tell him the precise beloved allegation. Generic reassurance against a known liar therefore coexists with an unanswered specific fear. Misao's tea and concern93–97 offer another potential support surface, but Momo cannot tell her the actual assault. Misao defends Ryo and explains the cross as a tutoring farewell gift. Momo privately guesses that Misao might be Ryo's real love. That guess must remain separate from the later disclosure about Kairi. Misao102 is startled by Sae's apparent relationship with Ryo/Okayasu, not discovering Momo/Kairi dating for the first time. Tōji's refusal to break up merely over Sae's conduct104 shows a continued decision; Momo's thought105 that their couple has its own time remains a fallible projection, not proof of reciprocal contentment.

The failed seminar meeting and rain move Momo and Kairi into his home. Her nervousness, changing clothes and sitting at the bed edge are not advance consent. Kairi's insecurity116–117 gives a distinct interior problem: women choosing his brother, and the things he truly wants seeming unavailable. It does not yet name Misao. Momo's desire119–121 is nevertheless explicit and current. She revises her own setting requirement and says she loves him in words, choosing this encounter rather than merely paying him back for rescue. The untailored love words122 should not be inflated into a separate exclusive-love pledge by Kairi.

Kairi then stops124–126 and says the problem is not Momo128. His withdrawal is valid despite earlier pursuit and her now being willing. Her humiliation and hurt are equally real. Neither the dignity of refusal nor the legitimacy of hurt establishes the unstated cause. At night in her own room130 she attacks a pillow, not Kairi; embarrassment language is figurative, not a self-harm plan. The torn card131 sustains two incompatible uses of Ryo's premise: maybe he does not truly love her, or maybe he cannot precisely because he treasures her. The form exposes a question with no independently established governing rule, rather than giving the reader a bodily truth detector.

### D: Avoidance, advice and an album turn doubt into a direct question

Repeated greetings and departures133–135 make Kairi's avoidance more than Momo imagining rejection. His denial137, head pat and errand excuse138 postpone the conversation. Momo is active: she confronts him, chooses swimming, consults an older student and later seeks Misao privately. The unnamed club couple140–143 are not Tōji/Sae. The girl's advice that men sometimes cannot proceed and need space supplies a consoling theory, not a diagnosis of Kairi. Momo is willing to imagine his embarrassment rather than insisting on rejection. His next deferral144 leaves the theory untested.

The blooming quince146 joins current loneliness to the earlier peach-seed wish without changing botanical identity. Momo now imagines her heart fully flowering; this is her present attachment, not evidence that Kairi's feeling has reached the same state. The window view of Misao helping students147 gives a credible reason to seek her as a health adviser. Once invited home, however, Momo learns through an object rather than through successful counseling. She asks permission to open the album150, finds college Misao/Ryo and younger Kairi photographs, then recognizes the smiling Kairi/Misao picture152–154 as the one Kairi carried. A shared cross and prior-girlfriend assumption had made Morika a plausible mistaken identity; the actual matching photograph now corrects it.

Kairi's unexpected arrival156 follows Momo's experience of being denied his company. Misao calls their closeness family-like158, but Momo takes him outside and asks specific questions. He nods that he comes often159. Momo asks why he let the Morika misconception stand161–162; convenient concealment is her accusation, not an explicit admission of a long plan. The final still-love-Misao question162, demanded answer163, affirmative164 and apology165 turn one part of doubt into a received fact: Kairi still loves Misao. This is not merely Ryo's allegation, and it is not information Momo had already securely possessed in V09. It does not prove Misao reciprocates, that a sexual affair occurred, that every care for Momo was false, or the exact psychological mechanism of Kairi's stop. No explicit breakup is uttered before the cutoff.

## 4. Claims, counterreadings and prospective discipline

The strongest competing account is that Ryo accurately names Kairi's unresolved attachment despite lying and assaulting. By the ending, part of that account gains independent direct support: Kairi himself admits continuing love for Misao. That does not validate Ryo's cannot-touch rule, his claim to understand every motive, or the idea that Momo's relationship was only a conscious fraud. Kairi's prior costly care, present no-fault reassurance, explicit love affirmation91 and choice to stop all remain evidence. The proper revision is unresolved competing attachments with demonstrated avoidance, not a total replacement of one simple character by another.

Momo's affection is not still only comfort dependency. Her declaration121 and specific willingness119–120, after explicit Kairi priority58, establish present chosen romantic attachment. Her post-stop hurt and energetic investigation do not retrospectively make that willingness passive or permanently binding. Conversely, the ending should not be used to pretend she already knew Misao's identity when trying to trust Kairi. The album and answer have actual acquisition frontiers.

Sae is not only a mechanism for harm here. She gives practical aid to an injured Ryo while believing a false counterstory, then accepts the cross and claims a new romantic status. Her two-boyfriend claim has no demonstrated Tōji agreement. Momo's care for Tōji, Sae's canceled-date report and Tōji's continued choice must stay distinct. Misao's support also has conditions: she can be warm while defending a brother whose attack Momo has not told her about. No omniscient diagnosis of either woman's complete motives follows.

PR0015 is DISCONFIRMED under its original whole-V11-mainline test. Momo's self-escape20 and Kairi's outside meeting21/empty room27 are decisive. The separate washroom arrival84 also follows Ryo's departure and cannot substitute for intervention at the conference-room encounter. Comfort and protective intention are real but were not the frozen success condition. Earlier supports, mixed results, failure and NOT_TESTED windows remain unchanged. New PR0016 below uses a narrow actual-disclosure test; no reconciliation or exclusive-choice forecast is smuggled into it.
