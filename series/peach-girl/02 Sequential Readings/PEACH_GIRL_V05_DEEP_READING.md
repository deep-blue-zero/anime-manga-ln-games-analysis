# Volume 5 — affection, copies and stolen agency

V05 tests the renewed Momo–Tōji relationship by separating recognizable signs of love from the person and choices those signs should identify. Sae gains a celebrity boyfriend, public admiration and luxury gifts yet cannot obtain the happiness she sees in Momo. Her explicit wish for Tōji does not vanish inside status rivalry; rivalry, attraction and frustration coexist. When Tōji repeatedly refuses her, she first stages a kiss spectacle, then arranges two simultaneous deceptions: Momo is isolated and becomes incapacitated, while Sae copies her clothes, hair and perfume to approach Tōji in darkness. He eventually recognizes the substitution, rejects Sae's story that Momo chose Jigoro, and starts searching. Momo wakes in a hotel uncertain what happened while unconscious. Jigoro's final claim that he fulfilled her wish is the cliffhanger, not the analyst's answer.

## 4. Close reading: satisfaction that a copy cannot supply

### 4.1 Sae gets the heroine position and still feels defeated

The opening temporarily moves interior access to Sae. The public rescue is experienced as a heroine scene, with a beautiful protector and an envious audience (EV0001–0003). Her fear that it might be another humiliation links her pleasure to the recent cruelty she endured. Jigoro's reassurance responds to that fear, even though its premise is distorted. Calling every kindness fake would flatten the scene as much as treating him as an informed judge. Sae's comic disappearance and revival make public recognition into a bodily/persona transformation; they do not establish a psychological diagnosis.

The reader-vote feature and followers then give the transformation a social apparatus (EV0004–0007). Peers who knew the earlier exposure can reward prestige again. Kairi's abandoned classroom punctures their praise of him without proving that they permanently cease liking him. Sae also understands the audience's opportunism and resents it while accepting its benefits. She is not merely passively deceived by popularity. The wig is first openly acknowledged as a shoot accessory; its later covert use changes an innocent-looking prop's function.

Her ride offer excludes Momo/Tōji in the form of hospitality, while luxury boasts invite a comparison Momo declines. Momo's account of inexpensive dates answers with chosen company rather than objects (EV0008). The argument about belief is more consequential: Momo would not love a man simply for believing only her and abandoning Sae. She names Tōji's faults, his recognition of error and his current kindness, preferring the present person to the earlier ideal (EV0009). This independently strengthens the audit's joint-repair account. It is not a denial of the earlier harm and not a rule that love requires tolerating any future injury.

Sae copies the L sign after learning its meaning directly, but Jigoro offers daily words instead. Purchases and reassurance accumulate without ending her feeling of defeat. The source explicitly makes her ask why Momo seems happier while she possesses more (EV0010). This supports rivalry/status as a causal force more strongly than a reader's generic villain label. Yet the breakup then names Tōji as the wish Jigoro cannot satisfy (EV0011), and her attention to Jigoro's wounded allure persists (EV0015). The adequate revision of CL0002 therefore retains specific desire alongside comparative acquisition. An exclusively romantic interpretation cannot explain why Momo's happiness triggers the comparison; an exclusively status interpretation cannot simply discard Sae's repeated, partly private desire statements.

### 4.2 Refusal, acquiescence and the cost of proving love

Tōji's new account of V01 is not a replacement for its panel order. He says he could have resisted seriously and wanted to seem experienced to Momo (EV0017). This supplies an attributed reason for later acquiescence, while two initial refusals and Sae's override remain depicted facts. His guilt may weight the self-account; the analyst cannot reconstruct every unshown motion from it. Conversely, retaining UNKNOWN as if he had never spoken would now omit material evidence. CM0008@3 preserves both phases.

The garden sequence is a fresh boundary, not an opportunity to universalize the retrospective explanation. Tōji explicitly loves only Adachi, repeatedly refuses a farewell kiss, turns his head and pushes Sae away (EV0017–0018). Momo's arrival is timed by Sae. Her slap and initiated kiss are not proof that she believed a mutual affair: she later produces the summons and calls out the staging (EV0019–0020). What she contests includes the contact, possession and comparison, rather than merely failing to understand the trick.

Momo becomes an active initiator after earlier hesitation. Tōji's surprise changes to responding embrace, then affectionate kissing after her demand to exceed Sae's count. Their pleasure is source-supported; so are territorial language, jealousy and the slap. The playful frame does not make every demand an ethical ideal, but neither does an analyst's discomfort establish unwillingness in his later response. The record distinguishes initiative, response and the forgiveness bargain instead of assigning permanent consenting/nonconsenting identities to whole characters.

Birthday comedy repeats the problem of anticipation. Momo imagines royal romance, then fears a different kind of private encounter; the actual darkness initially reveals a surprise party (EV0012,0026). The finger-care withdrawal and her absurd bodily anxieties demonstrate how limited cues generate private explanations (EV0016). On the birthday, Tōji's independent gift choice, food preparation and attention support care in an ordinary context, beyond declarations in crisis (EV0025,0028). When the interaction develops physically, he checks after her startled response and receives hesitant immediate assent (EV0029). That exchange does not license everything afterward, and no completed intercourse is established. It matters that the scene makes room for a question and answer before Sae exploits the interruption.

### 4.3 Helping someone is not choosing what they arrange

Sae's persuasion works differently on each target. With Jigoro she extends the earlier bully account: Momo is a fan who takes what belongs to Sae, and a direct confrontation would expose Sae to punishment (EV0022–0023). He first proposes verification, then explicitly refuses intimacy with someone he does not like. These moments prevent reducing him to a machine executing her initial command. Sae uses love, exhaustion and fear of rejection to redirect him; his later agreement and acts are changes for which he remains an agent.

With Momo she invokes an emergency and privileged confidence, rejecting Momo's offer to bring Tōji too (EV0030–0032). Momo knowingly chooses to help Sae alone, with a plan to return/call. She does not knowingly choose a night with Jigoro. Sudden weakness after the drink, attempted objection and limp removal establish the absence of the willing choice later alleged. An intentionally incapacitating drink is strongly supported by sequence and scheme; the particular substance and administration are not directly identified in V05. Neither medical specification nor a final answer about subsequent sexual activity is necessary to recognize the deprivation of agency already shown.

Jigoro's sympathy for Sae explains the misinformation route but cannot absorb all responsibility. He sees Momo's condition, asks whether this is okay, carries her away and later prevents access to her phone (EV0032,0036–0037,0041). He knows she remains asleep. His assertion that it is her wish is contradicted by the reader's access to her choice process, not validated by his confidence. At EV0045 Sae explicitly wants the fact of their sleeping together regardless of whether Momo wakes, and he agrees prospectively. Her aim has become manufacturing a relational fact through denied agency, rather than reporting one that happened. No completed act is thereby established. The moral criterion used here is meaningful, informed and voluntary participation, with particular attention to incapacity and identity; it is stated separately from the causal account of how the scheme works.

### 4.4 Private signs become tools of identity theft
