## 2. Close reading

### A. Hurt does not make every earlier kindness false

The opening continues the outside confrontation, not a newly established affair. Kairi supplies apologies, Momo asks whether his love and kisses were lies, and she hits him with the album8. His catching her fists and kissing10 does not answer the substantive question; her withdrawal11 and flight13 remain part of the encounter. Momo then tears the travel brochures14, not the album. Destroying a shared future plan has a different object and meaning from destroying the evidence that identified Misao. Her private claim to have been fooled all along15 expresses the collapse of her interpretation; it is not an omniscient finding that Kairi never cared.

Misao returns a forgotten bag16 and explains that Kairi would not tell her why Momo left17. That is new evidence of her limited access. Her tutor/family description17–18 may feel evasive to Momo, but the source does not establish that she has heard Kairi's outside confession or knows the bedroom incident. Momo's jealousy even turns on Misao's ordinary appearance/age20, followed by self-reproach21. The scene does not authorize the analyst to rank women's attractiveness or reduce attachment to a beauty contest. Momo's shame at having such thoughts is as material as the thoughts themselves.

The school/phone sequence separates channels from receipt. Momo refuses Kairi's approach22–23 and turns off her phone24; his sent-success screen25 establishes outgoing effort, not a reason she has actually read. Tōji's presence near the classroom23 and his later return of her cap29 give him contact with visible hurt without automatically giving him the reader's entire account. Momo swims to exhaust thought, not under an established intention to die. That distinction matters when water soon becomes physically dangerous for Kairi.

Tōji asks for Ryo's contact30, reports that Sae recently asked to break up31, and says he refused. The volume therefore does not begin by freeing him for Momo through an agreed Tōji/Sae separation. His counsel32–33 distinguishes forgiving what happened from deciding what a person means to him and what he himself wants. He cannot erase the hurt, and he does not claim to forgive effortlessly. Momo applies the counsel to Kairi34. Her ability to learn from her former lover is not already a romantic return to him, nor does his account impose an obligation on her to remain with Kairi.

At the pool, Momo dives to evade Kairi's apology35–37. He enters despite known water fear38 and actually struggles/sinks40–41. The danger is drawn before it is interpreted; nothing establishes a safely staged trick. Momo's startled pause is not a stated decision to let him die. She retrieves him42, brings him up, and holds him as he gasps/apologizes43–44. No resuscitation kiss occurs. Her bodily care enables listening, but neither care nor danger settles which relationship she will choose.

### B. A remembered history explains attachment without certifying every memory

Kairi's poolside account is substantial. He denies dating Momo for amusement46, says her acceptance made him happy, but learning the seriousness of her feelings suddenly cost him confidence47. He likes her and thought a relationship with her would work, yet cannot discard the feeling to which he returns48. This revises the all-fraud reading without reducing the hurt to Momo's misunderstanding. He supplies reasons for the pull toward Misao; he does not distinctly identify what caused the particular V11 bedroom stop or why he repeatedly avoided Momo afterward. The narrow forecast remains accountable to that missing link.

The embedded past49–78 is framed as Kairi telling Momo a history. Misao's first self-introduction52 gives 安芸操, Aki Misao, the same nurse/tutor already CHAR0005. It does not establish that the separately reported dead アキ is this woman or that every detail of Ryo's dead-beloved story has been resolved. Kairi describes being treated as an inferior replacement for his brother50. His all-girls language is his generalized account, not a verified census. His early sexual provocation53 and Misao's physical rebuke54 do not become evidence of her sexual biography. Her decisive answer is that Kairi is Kairi56: recognition of a separate person, not a romantic promise. The near tears57 mark the emotional scarcity that gives the phrase weight.

Kairi first believed Misao indifferent to Ryo58, then explicitly revises that belief59. The summer camp recollection gives the two-person photo its origin61; the picture's tenderness is real evidence of shared time, not by itself reciprocal romance. Misao slips62, Ryo catches63, then makes intrusive unrequested contact64 while Kairi protests. Her stillness and blush cannot supply affirmative permission. Ryo's casual surname address and necklace comment65, and Kairi's interpretation of her blush66, explain what Kairi thinks he discovered. They remain distinct from a present confession by Misao.

Misao says she is ending tutoring because his grades have improved and graduation makes her busy67. Kairi feels abandoned68. Keeping both formulations prevents his pain from becoming an objective verdict that she betrayed him. The typhoon disappearance/search69–70 and offshore episode71–72 deepen the earlier V09 remark. Kairi recalls wondering whether he could die offshore, qualifies that he thinks he was not serious, and imagines Ryo/Misao guilt if he died71. These are ambivalent death and retaliatory-guilt thoughts. The qualification does not erase them; the thoughts do not establish a settled plan or a clinical diagnosis. V09's uncertainty was appropriate at its earlier frontier. V12 supplies later recontextualization, not permission to rewrite V09 as though this disclosure were already known.

On waking73, Kairi initially assumes Misao rescued him; her angry relief and embrace74 make that belief understandable. Ryo instead attributes the rescue to the already-departed same-age Adachi Momo74. Momo's depicted presence on the beach72 supports the attribution. The reader receives a nested account: Ryo told Kairi, Kairi now tells Momo. No actual old mouth-to-mouth is depicted. This strengthens identification of Momo as a rescuer without resolving every CPR step or proving no other rescuer participated.

Ryo's later report of Misao's four-day search75–77 gives Kairi another basis for attachment. Kairi did not witness the whole search; the illustrated phone appeal76 does not securely name its recipient. Misao's request not to blame him/not to tell him she searched implies care without establishing romantic preference. Kairi's declaration that nobody cares as much78 is his emotional comparison, not the analyst's erasure of Momo, who has just rescued him in the present as well.

Momo asks for a kiss79–80; Kairi gives it81. She then asks the question that mere affection cannot answer: whether he loves Misao more82. His yes83 establishes comparative priority explicitly. The tearful coda84–85 retains both the tenderness of the requested kiss and its insufficiency. Full-color85 is an actual continuation with remembered counsel, not a removable poster. The sequence refuses a simple inference from physical intimacy to first place.

### C. A chosen continuation can still be refused

On the night walk, Kairi expects that confessing to Misao would fail87 and admits fearing damage to that relationship89. Expected rejection is not an actual result. Momo recognizes her own earlier attachment to Tōji when she began with Kairi92; her reflection creates an analogy, not a declaration of present Tōji love or a duty to excuse everything. She asks what she wants now. At her home the goodbye kiss94 is a customary farewell within a proposed continuation, not yet the breakup itself. She offers to enjoy the relationship while he still loves Misao96 and says she wants to remain beside him97. This is informed agency under painful terms, not passive ignorance.

Kairi's answering embrace98 must not be turned into agreement. He explicitly proposes breaking up99. His account101 distinguishes liking Momo, wanting kisses/more, and fearing he may hold her while thinking of Misao. The casual-sex comparison is hypothetical; no intercourse is established. Momo says she would accept it. He still declines102–103, citing the hurt/tears that continuing with Misao foremost would bring. She disputes that protection by saying separation will make her cry, but her hurt cannot require his continuing participation. He withdraws105 and leaves106; she is alone107. This is an enacted ending, not an idle thought erased by the later reconnection.

Tōji finds her crying108, returns the wallet she forgets109–110, and asks what happened. The scene initially withholds an exact spoken disclosure111–113. Momo's later private statement124 confirms she told him the previous day that Kairi dumped her. That later confirmation closes the breakup-receipt gap without inventing a full account of assault, bedroom intimacy or every Misao detail. At school Tōji punches Kairi117 after the dictionary encounter114–116. His because-it-was-you-I-yielded statement119 gives him a continuing personal stake; it does not confer ownership of Momo. His care, resentment, romantic investment and violence are simultaneous evidence. A pure wise-helper summary would omit the punch; a pure possessive-rival summary would omit his practical care and later advice.

### D. Rejecting one return and pressing for another

At the peach-seed site121–124, Tōji pulls Momo into a hug125, says he has reached his waiting limit126, and actually asks her to date again127–129. The approach is not requested in advance. Momo's remembered old romance131–132 and momentary bodily response do not establish assent. The Kairi image133 is mental, not a man spying nearby. She pushes Tōji away134, says she cannot, and names Kairi as the person she now loves most136. Tōji already knows the Misao attachment by135, although the exact earlier relay is unshown. Momo's account of having been abandoned for Sae135 remains her understanding; it does not overwrite the reader's knowledge of Tōji's coercive blackmail context or his independent hurtful choices.

Tōji tells her to speak honestly to Kairi136. His smile and hypothetical what-if-I-had-actually-kissed-you137 also confirm no completed lip kiss. Momo guesses that he staged the approach to strengthen her138. That interpretation explains her gratitude; it is not an admission from Tōji that the whole proposal was false. His earlier anger119 and explicit waiting/proposal126–127 are counterevidence to a wholly disinterested performance. The account preserves actual assistance without compressing away his stake. Kairi's dictionary-collision excuse140 is similarly a character's cover story, not the true cause of his bandage.

Momo returns to Kairi and accepts the breakup143–145. Only then does she announce that she still loves him146 and propose pursuing him anew147–148. She herself says the old relationship is over148. That chronological distinction matters: an ending was accepted, and a new claim is now being made. Kairi says no/cannot149. Momo grabs his collar150 and challenges his fear of rejection151 and distance152. Her accusation has some basis in his own earlier fear89, but it is not a universal diagnosis or a right to compel involvement. Her cannot-live-without-you language147 registers dependence; no enacted death plan follows. Her pressure must remain visible alongside her active refusal to reduce herself to a discarded partner.

Momo touches his cheek153 and kisses154 after his no and the collar grab. His stillness and lowered gaze afterward do not establish prior affirmative permission. The preceding no is to renewed dating, not a separately spoken no to this kiss; the contact's full internal reception remains uncertain. The narrative's romantic momentum does not erase this boundary problem. He says her words make him happy but he may not stop155; her casual-sex challenge156 is rhetorical acceptance of that category, not proof of completed sex or permanent consent. He then laughs157, initiates a hug, says he likes her second most, qualifies for now158, and initiates the later kiss. She closes her eyes/embraces him158–159. There is a later responsive reconnection. It must not be backdated to validate the earlier initiation; equally, the earlier refusal must not be used to deny the later response. Momo's resolve to become first159 remains a project, not an achieved exclusive choice.

The last school/street sequence displaces attention to Sae. Momo asks peers for a shortcut to redirect Kairi160–161. Their self-improvement/common-interest advice is ordinary counsel, not a demonstrated law. Kairi shows the magazine162; its page identifies Sae under 安西かりん, Anzai Karin163. Publication within the fiction, the image and Momo/Kairi recognition are depicted. Momo's composite question and Kairi's probably-not answer leave production/authenticity limited. The analysis need not reproduce the revealing image or infer sexual acts, contracts, consent or coercion from it.

Kairi reports rumors and that he borrowed the issue from another class164. This is bounded circulation, unlike earlier threatened but unproved nationwide media release. Sae's phone recipient165 is unshown. Momo/Kairi do not know whether she is dating Ryo166. They then see her meet an unnamed heavier adult man167 and walk away with his arm around her waist168. The man is not Ryo, but no secure Honda identity is supplied. The cliffhanger establishes contact and their shock, not payment, sex, compulsion or a fully explained fall. Sae must not be reduced to a final cautionary image before she has spoken about it.
