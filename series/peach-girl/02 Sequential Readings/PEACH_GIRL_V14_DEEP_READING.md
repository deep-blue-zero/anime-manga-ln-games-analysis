## 2. Sequential reading and close analysis

### After the doorway: tolerance as speech and self-erasure

Momo leaves while Kairi remains with Misao6. Her collision with the manager and spilled goods7–8 let ordinary work carry affect before a confrontation. Her private realization9 is not a second witnessed kiss. Misao wakes10 without demonstrated knowledge of Kairi's sleeping approach. Two hours of silent work11 make a dropped cup and apology12 the prelude to Momo finally naming what she saw13. She says a wish to kiss Misao follows naturally from accepting the attachment, yet covertly approaching someone asleep does not fit her image of Kairi. His answer that he would be hit if Misao were awake is an actual verbal response. It meets PR0018 without resolving completed contact, reciprocal permission or exclusive choice.

Momo's second-place arrangement becomes an argument against her own right to complain14. That is her self-suppressing interpretation, not the analysis's normative endorsement. Similarly her private thought that she should have ended things15 is not a breakup communicated to Kairi. Sae's arrival at Momo's home, questions about Jigoro and interception of Kairi's call18–19 shift the available channel. Sae identifies herself; she does not impersonate Momo. Her boast that she heard everything is not proof that she knows every detail. Momo has disclosed the unequal relationship by20 but refuses the first person's identity21. A later accusation-memory naming Misao cannot be assigned as Sae's secure receipt merely because it appears adjacent to their exchange.

Sae recognizes the pattern of suppressing grievance to avoid rejection23–25 and supplies encouragement. Her forehead tap24 is a flat palm, not a punch. Momo's restored smile at work26 shows a local effect; it does not settle what she should accept. Sae then confronts Kairi27–30. He acknowledges hurting Momo and agrees that the unconfessed first attachment keeps him stuck. That present causal admission matters, but cannot retroactively make the narrower failed PR0016 successful. It is not an exact explanation of every earlier physical withdrawal.

### What Kairi hears, and what he later omits

Momo directly tells Kairi the Jigoro outcome33–35, so the prior volume's reader/Momo knowledge now becomes his. Their embrace and relief are real. Momo's qualification that she was nevertheless undressed37 and Kairi's remaining anger prevent the no-act correction from becoming no-harm absolution. The bar scene38–44 turns relieved celebration into a different disclosure. Intoxicated Momo asks what is wrong with her and what she should fix40; Kairi says nothing. She asks why he cannot love her41 and how long she must keep waiting42. Those questions are heard, not merely reader-only thoughts.

After carrying her home46–47, Kairi answers her question about having said anything strange with a denial48. He protects her immediate embarrassment while withholding the very pain that motivates his next action. A generous reading sees care for a vulnerable person; a critical reading sees another selective omission inside a relationship already impaired by withheld information. The pages support both functions. Her later lack of recollection does not erase his receipt.

Kairi's visit to Misao's apartment52–63 defeats a simple inference that knowing her domestic, unpolished self necessarily destroys his attraction. Face pack, braids, nightclothes and familiar warmth precede the actual confession57/59. Misao initially takes it as a joke58, then says she has only regarded him as a younger brother and will continue to do so62. This is actual rejection, not an assumed answer to a confession never made. Kairi thanks her and reports clarity63; that cannot be promoted to instantaneous eradication of feeling.

He actually phones Momo65 and tells her the confession/rejection. He also says he cannot immediately redirect himself to her67, wants time apart68 and asks whether she can wait69 while permitting refusal. Momo's loving willingness70 is largely private reflection. It is therefore different from the precise terms she gives him later. She books the one-night/two-day trip and hands him the ticket: last day of summer, station plaza at12 noon, five-minute wait76. He understands. This deadline is communicated, unlike Momo's earlier private V10 waiting deadline. It remains a future condition at V14's exit.

### Sae's mixed repair, and the shop as an accidental social world

Sae hears the rejection outcome71 without securely receiving Misao's identity. Kairi quits the store job74 and leaves on foot. Sae separately arranges Tōji's employment by calling him the previous day and saying she herself is quitting88, although the actual vacancy is Kairi's. Tōji starts without knowing Momo works there87. Momo's direct call to Sae90 establishes deliberate interference, not an accidental meeting or a claim that Tōji independently sought the job to pursue her. Later the manager's story140 reveals Sae described a lovers' quarrel and a helpful reconciliation plan. That tender public account conceals the actual situation.

This renewed manipulation does not prove that the V13 apology and Jigoro correction were always fraudulent. Nor does that repair excuse the new interference. Sae can perceive emotional damage accurately, make a consequential apology, and still enjoy directing other people's relationships. Momo can accept useful words and later tell Sae to leave129–130. Their relationship has changed without becoming uncomplicated friendship.

Tōji receives a bounded account of the distance, confession/rejection and trip98–99; Momo does not recite every noon/five-minute term to him there. Her startled response to brief finger contact93 is not proof of his deliberate seduction. She explicitly thinks of Tōji as past and Kairi as most important94 despite bodily memory. The phone habit106 and refusal to call because Kairi must supply an answer107 give waiting an active discipline, though one whose costs remain visible.

Kairi's walking journey has heat, thirst and uncertainty95–97, then a missing wallet100. He infers skateboarders stole it101; theft itself is not shown. Ryo's call102 conveys parental worry and the actual note Kairi left. The battery fails before a complete request for money/location. This limited concern does not reform Ryo's V13 conduct or produce actual assistance. Kairi tries running from the unpaid meal104; the owner throws a sandal105. Later meals, lodging and counsel do not cancel that aggression.

Dishwashing108–109 and food after work109 give Kairi a world beyond the triangle: debt, competence, bodily need, hospitality and a couple's imperfect care. He says he loves Momo very much but would certainly rank her first if he had never met Misao111. The counterfactual exposes the obstacle rather than completing a current first-choice pledge. The wife says their youth resembled his situation112 and maps herself onto Momo and her husband onto Okayasu113. The Japanese construction is a role analogy, not a literal claim that their names are Momo and Okayasu. The owner later addresses her as Sanae153; his own name remains unestablished.

Their jigsaw quarrel139 complicates their apparent mentor authority. Finishing the sleeping husband's nearly completed puzzle may look helpful while removing the pleasure he wanted. The wife describes couples sharing tears and unattractive feelings151; the owner argues that Kairi compares a real girlfriend with a partial ideal149–151. His expired supermarket soba148 illustrates projected prestige. He mentions an annual soba award146, lets Kairi infer special status, then denies having said this dish won149. This is strategic priming, not a securely quoted explicit claim that the actual noodles won. Neither the analogy nor the food metaphor establishes a universal rule that one must date or sexually experience someone before evaluating love. Misao's domestic scene already showed familiarity and attraction can coexist.

### Blackmail becomes Momo's knowledge

Sae gives Tōji the photograph116 and explicitly says his remaining constraint is broken. At117–118 she retracts the earlier retained-original story: he burned the real negative, she could not replace it quickly, and the continuing threat was a bluff. This is a new participant admission. Bounded reinspection of V07_139–142/145–146 confirms the burn and Sae's earlier claim that the genuine object remained. V14 strongly discredits that earlier claim without rewriting the V07 freeze. It does not establish that every print, other image or possible copy has vanished.

Momo overhears the conversation118–124. Sae acknowledges threatening the breakup and asks whether Tōji kept away to protect Momo120. His smile at her question about lingering affection121 is not a new verbal declaration. Momo then demands the exact words128. Sae supplies the mass-media threat and amusement-park/press-conference context129. Momo's earlier accurate suspicion was not equivalent to this detailed confirmed receipt. Tōji initially does not know she heard.

Momo's private conclusion that she alone assumed victimhood130 and failed to see through his lie142 expresses revised sympathy at a cost: she risks assigning herself total responsibility for a secrecy she was denied the means to resolve. Her simultaneous wish that he had told her so they could solve it together143 preserves the alternative. The store's discussion of fictional idol Fujisaki Anri143–144 supplies a social model of scandal outlasting music. The promiscuity label is attributed gossip, not the idol's verified sexual biography. Momo's fear that her own life might have changed similarly is a counterfactual shaped by this scene, not proof publication was inevitable or concealment the only feasible action.

At155 Tōji actually learns she overheard. At156 he says he wishes he had handled matters better and apologizes for hurting her. That acknowledgment is decisive counterevidence to a flawless-sacrifice reading and to Momo's total self-blame. Her bow and thanks157 recognize his protective intention; his apology recognizes the injury his chosen method inflicted. Gratitude does not itself restore their romantic commitment.

### Parallel fireworks and a proposal still unanswered

Misao's phone call134–137 is another delayed correction. She says she never actually expressed her feelings to Ryo: at university Valentine time, discovering he had a first beloved prevented her giving the chocolate136. The pictured woman is not securely the reported dead Aki. Her happiness at Kairi's confession and renewed confidence to try Ryo137 do not rescind her sibling-only rejection of Kairi62. Kairi hears this before his next-day weakness and mistakes. The text allows emotional disturbance and physical illness together, without making every broken bowl138 a romantic symptom.

The owner notices fever153, and the doctor diagnoses influenza158 with a warning about pneumonia risk. Pneumonia is not diagnosed. Momo is not shown knowing any of this. Kairi's phone worked at133 after the earlier battery failure; its later display163 shows Momo's contact, not a completed call. His difficulty reaching spilled water162 and plea for help163 establish vulnerability, while Momo's intuitive question164 does not establish message receipt.

Tōji and Momo actually watch fireworks159. Her recollection of last year's early dating and nearly held hands is not a present handhold or reunion. She wonders about his feelings160 rather than receiving a new explicit confession there. At165 she says she would probably give up if Kairi did not come. Tōji offers seriously to restart with her if that happens166. The volume stops before her answer and before the agreed meeting outcome. Romantic urgency is therefore built from mismatched information and a future conditional, not an already enacted replacement of Kairi.

## 4. Counterreadings and prospective accountability

The idealization reading has strong explicit support in the owner's149–151 analogy and Kairi's counterfactual111. Its limit is equally material: the unpolished domestic Misao scene52–56 does not stop his attraction or confession. Familiarity alone is not a demonstrated cure; actual rejection62 and Momo's heard distress41–42 also contribute. The couple's puzzle quarrel139 supplies a small example of helpful intention missing another person's desired experience. Their counsel may guide Kairi without becoming the author's exceptionless law.

Momo's waiting combines chosen love, self-suppression and a real stopping condition. Her no-right-to-complain14 does not negate her later actual voiced pain or communicated deadline76. The asymmetry at exit is factual: the reader knows Kairi is ill while Momo does not. It would be hindsight to diagnose her future response as knowingly abandoning him from these pages, just as it would be premature to infer Kairi has already failed the appointment.

Tōji's protective intention is corroborated, but his apology156 matters as much as Momo's thanks157. Her self-blame142 is not a verdict that she should have penetrated every lie; her earlier repeated offers to solve the threat together and current wish143 remain counterevidence. An exclusive sacrifice narrative would erase both her agency and his own acknowledgment. Sae's new retraction gives the old negative story a different present evidential status; the frozen V07 uncertainty remains historical evidence of what could then be warranted.

PR0018 is SUPPORTED solely by the direct exchange13 under its original terms. PR0016 remains DISCONFIRMED under its original narrower window; new causal admissions do not reopen it. PR0019 tests actual direct first-choice words received by Momo in V15, with meaningful alternatives of silence, generic liking, failed contact and continued ambiguity. It is prospective within this newly authorized sequence, not a claim to restored cross-run blindness or model validation.
