## 2. Sequential close reading

### Classroom judgment and the kindness of concealment, i0004–i0042

Arriving together4–5 makes Momo and Tōji available to public interpretation before they have explained themselves. Misao sees them from the nurse's window6, but the call to Adachi at that page's end belongs to the classmates who approach7. A mistaken speaker would falsely start an institutional confrontation. The girls tell Momo Kairi had searched for her while her phone was off8. This is actual new receipt of effort, not the complete cause of the missed appointment. A witness saw the train return9–10. Nami's accusatory response11 converts a limited sighting into a moral account of Momo's romantic conduct.

Kairi's casual apology14 seems to Momo almost offensively easy. She hears social banter instead of grief. The reader carries his costly failed journey and solitude from V15, so the mismatch is meaningful: withholding pain can look like not caring. Her desire to be taken seriously meets his practiced surface of unseriousness. Neither her interpretation nor the reader's sympathy can replace what was actually communicated.

The short-haired supportive friend receives Momo's explicit confirmation of returning to Tōji18. Later reaction23–24 establishes that Momo has relayed the old blackmail/protective-breakup explanation across an ellipsis. It does not license an invented transcript or complete disclosure of every earlier event. The friend's praise is an opinion informed by that account; Tōji's protective intention does not erase his method's harm. This friend is not securely identified as Rikko: dialogue17 asks whether Nami and Rikko have already left. Nami can be registered; assigning the unnamed supporter that absent name would manufacture continuity.

Misao asks Kairi whether he met Momo and explained the delay21. He says no to both; he actively rejects her offer to explain22. Momo then sees him examining Misao's bruise25 and treats the visible care as confirmation that Misao still matters most26. The reader will learn the bruise's cause, but Momo does not. The bruise first appears under Misao's evasive claim that she bumped herself23. Its concealment belongs to her choices, not a source assertion of accident.

Nami's hostility has a personal history according to the supportive friend27: a boyfriend broke contact and chose someone else. Nami later apologizes33 for projecting that hurt, yet argues Momo kept Tōji as insurance34. Her partial correction does not become a definitive moral diagnosis. Momo's insistence that she could not wait BECAUSE she loved Kairi35–36 gives the five-minute agreement its subjective force: it was the final chance to be chosen. V15 showed her actually waiting hours. Repeating her shorthand as literal departure after five minutes would falsify both action and patience.

Kairi overhears and publicly accepts blame, saying he prioritized his honmei38. In context this protects Momo from the girls' accusation, but it also conceals his present feeling. His private Momo preference is not undone by a tactical public explanation. When he asks if she is back with Tōji39, she remains silent. The sequence answers one social problem by worsening the personal information problem. Momo's earlier intention29 to tell him is still an intention. Sae's nausea30–31 and private pregnancy-test scene42 introduce a different information imbalance. The object is definitely a pregnancy test; its actual result is not clearly labeled, and no clinical confirmation has occurred.

### A photograph changes hands while the hospital past changes meaning, i0044–i0089

The rumor44 strips complexity from both parties: Momo is recast as a sexual convenience whom Kairi discarded when bored. This is injurious gossip, not sexual biography. Misao hears it45. Tōji's present support46–48 matters because Momo actually feels calmer and resolves to cherish this love. Her Kairi distress cannot reduce every Tōji response to false consolation.

Kairi's camera49–51 supplies a new visual medium. Momo and Tōji pass, but the exact completed capture of Momo's later portrait is not securely shown. His race metaphor52–53 turns an almost-finished relationship into an irrecoverable fall near the goal. It expresses his sense of lost timing; it cannot establish that successful arrival would guarantee possession of Momo. The reader sees reflection before any useful explanation reaches her.

Sae cannot get Ryo to stay on the phone54. Kairi asks whether she told Momo about his storm search56. She says no. He then ostentatiously asks her to promote his effort and break up the couple, but his private aside57 states that this will ensure she does NOT relay it. The apparent scheme is reverse psychology used to suppress disclosure. Reading only his spoken request would reverse its actual function. Sae nevertheless demands that he accompany the arranged Ryo meeting and threatens to tell Momo59. His attempt to manage the messenger gives her leverage.

Misao returns the digital camera60; Sae insults her and claims Ryo. Kairi's pig retort61 targets Sae, not Misao. His inquiry opens Misao's narrated hospital flashback62. Ryo wakes, calls her Aki63, thanks her presence and describes peace65. Aki is consistent with Misao's established surname 安芸 and his present address107. It does not establish a mistake for the previously reported deceased Aki. A tempting tragic-identity explanation would here outrun the wording.

Misao actually confesses long love67. Ryo says he is very happy and had thought his own feeling one-sided69; their faces move into a kiss70. Reciprocity is his actual self-account, not merely her fantasy. Sae arrives and slaps Misao71. Ryo hits Sae72, pushes her away and tells her to disappear73. Misao later explicitly identifies his unhesitating violence75. The sequence demands two judgments at once: Sae harms Misao; Ryo's rejection is itself violent. His softness toward the woman he prefers cannot establish general kindness or reform.

Misao questions her own inability to comfort the crying Sae75 and compares Kairi's habit of helping others. This is a consequential reassessment from within attachment, not an immediate abandonment of Ryo. Kairi receives the account74–75; Momo does not. His thought that an old spell may be breaking is an interpretation of Misao, not a clinical finding.

The handbook drops78 and Momo finds it79. Her own new curly-haired portrait80 occupies the intimate space of Kairi's student identification81. The short friend initially assumes Tōji's book85; Momo corrects her86 and wants to keep it. The old Misao photograph89 is a recollected contrast, later explicitly explained98 as a worn picture Kairi could not discard. It is not a second newly discovered Momo/Kairi couple snapshot. The object's meaning reaches Momo before its maker supplies words, and she withholds the object rather than ask him. Its apparent honesty encourages another silent inference.

### Tōji stops; Misao withdraws; Ryo denies, i0090–i0122

Tōji's invitation to a movie and then his home91 makes ordinary privacy possible. His parents are at work94; a future introduction is offered, not an engagement performed. Momo plans to return the handbook secretly TOMORROW96. That plan remains unperformed in V16. Watching the DVD97, she can be happy with Tōji while mentally returning to the portrait and the old Misao image98. Neither feeling cancels the other.

Tōji kisses100; Momo says stop101. He stops and sits apart102. Both apologize103; he specifically owns rushing because her distracted look made him fear she was thinking of Kairi104. His promise105 accepts that the previous relationship is part of her current self and offers patient time. This is actual changed conduct at a live boundary, stronger evidence than a generalized claim that he is caring. Her earlier shared night does not confer continuing permission, and her guilt is not an obligation to proceed. She privately feels relief and guilt106, including happiness about Kairi's photograph. Tōji is not shown receiving that particular fact.

Misao has actually invited Ryo to a cafe107. Kairi's angry call108 reveals that Ryo forgot the six o'clock meeting because he was with her. The repeated failed appointment now exposes Ryo's selective attention. Misao pulls her arm away109 and asks to withdraw her confession110, saying she had concentrated on herself and did not intend to wreck his relationship with Sae. Ryo responds that the relationship was already over, then describes Sae as a one-sided stalker and imaginary girlfriend111. His revision conflicts with prior accepted intimacy and his own earlier premises. It should be preserved as self-serving speech, not adopted as neutral history.

Sae and Kairi actually overhear112. The spilled food gives her shock a bodily, material interruption. Ryo's claim that she is intoxicated with loving herself114 is an invalidating accusation, not the analyst's diagnosis. Her insistence on love and her earlier vulnerability cannot be erased by it. Her grip on Misao115 also remains intrusive; Kairi tries to restrain her shoulders. Ryo says they were never dating116. Momo and Tōji are elsewhere at that moment, then follow the crowd117 and recognize the group119. Their later presence does not make them recipients of every earlier remark.

Sae asks why Ryo had sex with her and promised happiness118. This is her own participant corroboration of his earlier claim, distinct from Jigoro's fabricated old outcome. No explicit act is depicted, and no worth follows from sexual status. When he tries to leave120, she announces pregnancy121 and demands responsibility122. The earlier test gives the claim narrative preparation without making the medical state or paternity established fact.

### Public defense, private regret and a confession without an answer, i0123–i0162

The actual reaction/title scene123 remains narrative. All six hear the repeated pregnancy claim124. Ryo's insinuation that a promiscuous life makes paternity unknowable125 is another allegation, not evidence of other partners. Misao, not Momo, slaps him126 and defends Sae127. Taking BOTH people aside129 is intended to make them speak; she does not lead Ryo away as a romantic prize. Sae denies sex with anyone else130, including the compensated-companionship customers: she says she took money in advance and ran. That account is relevant but not independently exhaustive proof. Misao tells Ryo to face Sae as an adult132 and leaves. This actual withdrawal gives responsibility priority over her immediate romantic wish; it neither certifies every claim nor secures his future compliance. The public applause127 rewards her intervention within the scene, not every violent action or a universal duty to forgive Sae.

Tōji says he saw only from the middle133. Kairi's brief explanation gives Momo the new fact that Misao confessed to Ryo134; it does not transfer the whole hospital narrative. Momo asks whether Sae's pregnancy could be a bluff135; Kairi thinks it possible. This is suspicion, not Sae confessing deception. Momo's thought that Kairi does not care about her and Tōji is boxed private thought. He announces pursuit of the other three, but next appears home alone137. A plan is not a completed movement.

At home the missing handbook138 reopens what his casual surface concealed. Kairi prints Momo's portrait139, remembers her happiness140–141 and cries142. His counterfactual142–143 imagines explaining and taking her with him to the hospital. The illustrated alternative must not become a fictional actual visit or proof that she would inevitably forgive. He runs144, privately insisting it has to be Momo145. This is strong present attachment evidence, still unreceived by her.

At her street146–148, he hides behind a neighbor's fence149 and quiets a barking dog150. Momo and Tōji kiss151–152; he actually witnesses. Framing153 strongly suggests Tōji notices him, without verbal acknowledgment or complete understanding. Momo goes indoors154. Kairi cries against the dog154–155 and leaves; renewed barking brings her outside156. The comic dog both exposes his intrusive embarrassment and gives his distress a tactile witness. Sympathy does not turn hiding at her home into a romantic entitlement.

Momo finally meets him157. He claims he got lost158, a face-saving lie after the depicted purposeful journey. She calls him back159 and sees his tear160. In response to her question161 he apologizes for lingering attachment, 未練がましくて, then runs162. These are actual heard words. They reveal inability to let go without explicitly comparing Momo with Misao. Momo's shock closes the volume before any clear answer. Her resumed Tōji relationship remains unspoken to Kairi under the forecast's precise requirement, even though he has ample observation from which to infer it.

## 4. Counterreadings, form and forecast accountability

A wholly indifferent Kairi reading mistakes his public performance for his private state; the home sequence142–145 is decisive counterevidence. A wholly selfless Kairi reading loses refusal of Misao's relay22, silencing tactic57, face-saving lie158 and fence intrusion149. Protective concealment preserves Momo's reputation locally while denying her facts needed for a choice. Attachment and accountable communication remain different achievements.

A wholly opportunistic Tōji reading cannot explain his actual stop102 and owned rushing104 without treating every counterexample as another scheme. An idealized patient Tōji reading still has to retain fear, initiation and his incomplete knowledge of Momo's feelings. His pledge105 is meaningful present evidence, not a fulfilled lifetime. Momo's genuine Tōji affection48 and private Kairi joy106 resist an exclusive-choice simplification before she has made one.

Sae's past lying makes the pregnancy claim doubtable, not automatically false. Her actual violence against Misao71 makes victim-only compression untenable; Ryo's hit72 and sexualized denials remain harm even when directed at a person who has herself harmed others. Misao's defense127 does not certify paternity, and crowd applause does not certify the slap126. Her departure132 gives ethical resistance an actual cost in the romance she had just obtained.

The visual argument works through audiences and surfaces. A bruise is first misexplained, then disclosed in flashback; a public honmei line conceals a private Momo preference; a photograph functions as evidence without explanation; a classroom chorus splits into accusation and support; pregnancy moves from private object42 to public statement121; the hospital alternative is drawn as counterfactual, while the doorstep kiss is actually witnessed. The small dog's noise repeatedly controls who sees whom, then its touch receives Kairi's grief before Momo sees his tear. The closing word 未練がましくて is self-reproach for lingering, not a technically complete hierarchy of love.

PR0020 is DISCONFIRMED under its original whole-V16 direct-word test. Kairi's knowledge by witnessed kiss is real but excluded from that target from the outset. Momo's private thought135 cannot be moved into a speech balloon. PR0021 concerns a separate unresolved public claim: an explicit Sae retraction of pregnancy in V17 heard by a named character. No alleged medical fact, partner outcome or failed couple-transmission forecast is smuggled into it.
