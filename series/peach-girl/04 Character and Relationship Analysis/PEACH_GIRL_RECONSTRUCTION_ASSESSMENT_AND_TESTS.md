They are not complete conversational characters, acoustic voices or a general simulation service. Misao and Ryo were assessed but no operational rule model was proposed for them.

These prototypes represent only selected tendencies. A complete mental-state graph, robust desire arbitration, contextual speech generator and unfamiliar-scenario behavior are not supplied. That incompleteness is an explicit acceptance debt. It is preferable to a fuller but invented personality.

Written interaction provides evidence of Momo's refusal/questions, Kairi's deflection/joking/direct confession, Tōji's patience and later pressure, and Sae's audience-dependent framing. It does not establish acoustic tone, accent or an unrestricted register generator. The runner emits action categories, not canonical dialogue. Speech portability is therefore untested rather than implicitly granted.

## Actual design and outcomes

The executable takes the frozen rule file and a versioned case manifest, rejects facts outside its small verified whitelist, checks the declared domain and selects the first matching categorical rule. Features are analyst-coded actor-accessible circumstances immediately before the outcome, not a neutral extraction of latent motives. Kairi's fear in particular is an interpretation. This limits causal and predictive claims.

Eight narrative probes occupy seven contextual groups: the two Tōji episodes are within the same final hotel encounter. Four additional probes test rejection of future, reader-only or unavailable capability information. Case choices, categories and scoring were authored retrospectively after source exposure. Matching a broad category is weaker than predicting a specific action, utterance or timing.

Momo's two matches support observed recurrence across stranger and intimate contexts. They do not establish a universal response or guarantee a successful departure. Kairi's false-cause match is especially weak as predictive evidence because the rule designer knew the later lie and its precise topic. His rivalry failure is substantial: instead of withdrawing, he holds on and directly claims the relation. The source provides a changed-stakes counterexample to an overgeneralized withdrawal tendency.

Tōji's two episodes expose variance within one encounter. He actually heeds wait115, then blocks the departure123–125. Counting only the first stop would make a reassuring but incomplete rule look reliable. His later stop133 is retained in literary analysis; it is not substituted for the failed exit-response test.

Sae's truthful journey report and explicit no-obligation qualification47–50 contradict the indirect-control rule, while the forged farewell falls within its broad category. The latter match does not predict suicide language, authorship from hidden composition, the surfers' attack or the later water accident. A general “Sae manipulates” statement gains little predictive specificity from being broad enough to cover many different acts.

The four guard results demonstrate implementation behavior: unavailable police-cause receipt, a future breakup, reader-only email authorship and an acoustic voice are rejected. They do not prove that a whitelist contains every fact a character knows or that the model can recognize unknown facts in arbitrary prose. The facts were explicitly labeled for these probes.

No probabilities, calibration, accuracy percentage or universal capability grade is inferred. The original sequential PR history remains separate; those tests had their own source windows and non-blind caveats. These reconstruction probes cannot be used to re-score them.

The machine receipt binds rule, runner, cases, results and pre-test commit hashes. Re-execution can reproduce the categorical outputs; it cannot reproduce a genuinely prospective experiment that never occurred.

No legitimate held-out original source remains after this full-source run. That blocks operational acceptance here.
