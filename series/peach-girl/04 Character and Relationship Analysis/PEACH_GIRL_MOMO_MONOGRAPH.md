# Momo Adachi — A visible body, an unevenly heard person

Momo's development concerns authority over the interpretation of her own life. Her appearance makes her visible before it makes her known. Later, the appearance of an encounter, a rival's account, a photograph, a silent lover and even her own earlier declarations repeatedly compete with what she presently means. The original series gives her increasing initiative, but does not replace early misrecognition with perfectly transparent self-knowledge. Its final choice is consequential because she makes it with a history of actual, conflicting attachments.

This monograph concerns PG-CHAR-0001 through the original-series end, using audited inherited V01–V04 and new full V05–V18. G2 commit342b9067abf2b200613e2b24707abbc190c59402 fixes the input. CL0001@5/CL0013@14/CL0017@15/CL0020@13 organize the central argument; CS0287 is the terminal state. Evidence is Japanese fixed-layout manga, so written interaction and panel focalization support the account; acoustic voice and a clinical personality diagnosis do not.

## Being looked at and being believed

The early appearance premise is more precise than “people judge a book by its cover.” Momo's body becomes evidence for claims she has not made. The V01–V04 audit distinguishes 外見 at V01_8 from 見た目 at V04_34: thematic recurrence, not a repeated verbatim slogan. Her concern about tanning also combines appearance anxiety with her own sporting history. The fact that a report about Tōji disliking dark skin is disowned does not make the social pressure imaginary. It changes the proposition's source and credibility.

Sae can exploit that pressure because Momo's outward anger and directness contrast with Sae's strategically legible vulnerability. A false account need not defeat an equally circulated true account; it can prevent Momo's account from becoming the one people hear. The early public correction therefore depends partly on other witnesses and exposed contradictions. Momo's refusal to restart with Tōji in V03 shows that being vindicated and wanting the former relation back are separate decisions. She does not exist only as a wronged person waiting for an apology.

The V04 reunion is similarly mixed. Momo criticizes her own conduct, but Tōji also acknowledges fault. The audit rejects a compression in which all responsibility transfers to her. Her return expresses romantic possibility and a changed willingness to risk trust, rather than proof that her refusal was irrational. Her private sign with Tōji gives them a way of communicating beneath public surveillance. That sign later becomes vulnerable to manipulation and to the problem of assuming a shared meaning survives without renewed discussion.

## Harm does not erase the person who wanted things

V05 first allows wanted intimacy and then turns to incapacitation, removal and impersonation. Momo's prior desire does not authorize the later plan. Nor does later uncertainty about an unshown act make the depicted loss of control trivial. In V06, misheard self-blame and fear produce a crisis before Tōji explicitly tells her she is not at fault. Her distress must be situated in what she believes then. The later V13 Jigoro account cannot be installed in her earlier mind as if the hurt were needless.

V13_150–157 supplies delayed participant testimony that no sexual act occurred in the old hotel interval, accepted by Momo with relief. That is a consequential correction to her belief, not proof that bodily harm matters only if one particular act happened. It also exposes how she had privately linked her supposed sexual history to Kairi's stopping. The manga gives that theory to Momo; the analysis cannot promote it into Kairi's confirmed reason. Her relief is part of her history without becoming an analyst's ranking of her worth.

Momo's response to Sae's endangered situation in V07 is another turning point. She initially participates in mirrored punishment, then rejects its continuation and leads the search. Changing course is an actual exercise of agency inside a plan she helped make. Rescuing Sae does not equal forgiving her. This distinction recurs in V13 when Momo and the others respond to Sae's filming danger despite earlier dismissals. Her care crosses the boundary of deservedness; it does not require amnesia about the cared-for person's conduct.

## Two romances have different forms of uncertainty

Momo's attachment to Kairi develops through support, attraction and the attempt to distinguish love from comfort. In V08 she still loves Tōji while admitting Kairi matters as more than a friend. The closing kisses do not by themselves establish a fully negotiated dating pact. V09 then makes public couple status and explicit Momo love actual. This phase cannot be reduced to a substitute relation that the ending retrospectively approves.

Kairi's divided attachment makes the asymmetry painful. Momo can know he cares and still lack the place she wants in his priorities. Her demand that he discard a photo, her later reconsideration and the Misao disclosures show an unstable attempt to manage uncertainty through objects and declarations. V11's willingly begun bedroom encounter and Kairi's stop are both actual. Her subsequent questions seek a causal account, not simply more affection. V12 supplies substantial history and the explicit comparison that Misao matters more, followed by Kairi's refusal to continue casually. His right to refuse remains valid even when the refusal hurts Momo.

Momo's renewed pursuit after that refusal is not automatically emancipatory because she is the heroine. The collar grab and kiss after Kairi's no in V12_149–154 exert pressure. His later responsive embrace does not supply prior permission. Her willingness to be “second for now” is also not the settled arrangement she later describes as acceptable inwardly. V18_18 explicitly revises that self-presentation: she wants exclusive cherishing and was not at peace with the lesser terms.

Tōji's return develops alongside these unresolved feelings. V15 gives Momo actual attraction to him before the missed rendezvous, a prolonged finite wait, partial knowledge of Kairi's delay and renewed responsive affection at the inn. Her private escape/flow doubts158–159 qualify the romance without making it fake. The new Codex inference of a strongly implied sexual night is withdrawn by the V18_116–117 flashback: Momo wanted to settle with Kairi first, and Tōji agreed to do nothing until morning. Actual responsive morning affection remains; an unshown completed act does not become established.

By V16, Tōji's patience and respect for a stop give Momo a specific experience of safety. In V17 she can truthfully say she is happy with him while remaining unable to forget Kairi's tears. V17_164 and V18_6–7 make her Tōji choice explicit. It is analytically important to let this choice stand at its own time. A full-series account that calls every Tōji feeling denial merely repeats the practice of telling Momo what she “really” meant before she says it.

## Learning causes, then choosing

The final volume does not resolve the triangle with one confession. Kairi's current “only Momo” declaration reaches her21, but she does not trust it22. Sae's account of the typhoon journey47–50 changes what Kairi's next-day cheerfulness means. Misao's injury/hospital account61 and first-choice relay63 change her knowledge again. Yet the police cause remains unreceived. Her remark that she would have waited if told62 does not establish what she would have chosen under a complete alternate history.

Momo also misreads the Kairi–Sae phone struggle76 as apparent intimacy. Here she is not merely the victim of a spoken lie; she makes a plausible but wrong inference from a partial view. That makes her interpretive fallibility concrete without equating it with deliberate manipulation. Her account of other people's happiness changes too: Sae's resilience provokes the question whether hurt necessarily means unhappiness34, and Tōji asks whether self-punishment would help Kairi105.

The hotel sequence then separates loving Tōji from being able or willing to continue intimacy. Momo says wait; he initially stops. His later blocked exit, pull and pin prevent a reading of the whole scene as freely negotiated. Her body-language interpretation140 belongs to her own effort to understand herself. It cannot become a universal rule that a body always reveals a single true love, particularly in a scene involving pressure.

Momo's explicit declaration that she loves Kairi143 precedes the actual drowning rescue. This order prevents a simplistic account in which saving him generates the choice from nothing. The forged farewell affects her urgency; it does not erase the prior disclosures and deliberation. On the beach she performs a concrete rescue, then declares love and initiates the kiss174–175. She is neither merely awarded to a persistent man nor solely driven by the obligation to keep him alive.

## Ordinary life, written speech and limits

School notes, exams, part-time earnings, gifts, holidays and friendly irritation matter because Momo does not inhabit only emergencies. Her planned future with Tōji is credible through such activities. She can care for Sae while refusing an expense or a scheme; she can argue over a photograph while planning a handmade gift. These domains prevent a reconstruction from reducing every choice to romantic crisis.

Her written speech often presses for distinctions: what did someone mean, why did they not say it, which relationship is actually being offered? That pattern supports a narrow account of conversational initiative. It does not produce a reliable script for every situation. Internal captions also let the reader know doubts that her partners do not receive. Directness in one exchange can coexist with silence, misinterpretation or withheld feeling elsewhere.

The ending secures Momo's present choice and reciprocal relation, not a complete emancipation from misleading appearances or a guarantee of trust. The strongest developmental reading sees a person who increasingly acts and articulates what she wants. The strongest counterreading notes that the last movement still depends on a forged crisis and spectacular rescue. Both fit the final sequence. Her development consists in a changed capacity to choose within these constraints; it is not evidence that the constraints have disappeared.
