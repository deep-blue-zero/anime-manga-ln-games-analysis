# Momo Adachi — A visible body, an unevenly heard person

Momo's development concerns authority over the interpretation of her own life. Her appearance makes her visible before it makes her known. Later, the appearance of an encounter, a rival's account, a photograph, a silent lover and even her own earlier declarations repeatedly compete with what she presently means. The original series gives her increasing initiative, but does not replace early misrecognition with perfectly transparent self-knowledge. Its final choice is consequential because she makes it with a history of actual, conflicting attachments.

This monograph concerns PG-CHAR-0001 through the original-series end. Version 1.0 used the historical G2 snapshot 342b9067abf2b200613e2b24707abbc190c59402, combining audited inherited V01–V04 with direct V05–V18 readings. This second-pass version retains that provenance and reads the unchanged histories through the dated [reconciliation and supplements](../07%20Audits%20and%20Handoffs/PEACH_GIRL_SECOND_PASS_RECONCILIATION_AND_SUPPLEMENTS_20261024.md). The character and claims ledgers own versioned current-state retrieval; this argument does not replace their histories. Japanese written interaction and panel focalization support the account; acoustic voice and a clinical personality diagnosis do not.

## Being looked at and being believed

The early appearance premise is more precise than “people judge a book by its cover.” Momo's body becomes evidence for claims she has not made. The V01–V04 audit distinguishes 外見 at V01_8 from 見た目 at V04_34: thematic recurrence, not a repeated verbatim slogan. Her concern about tanning also combines appearance anxiety with her own sporting history. The fact that a report about Tōji disliking dark skin is disowned does not make the social pressure imaginary. It changes the proposition's source and credibility.

Swimming makes this a conflict over a life Momo already has. Avoiding sun costs her access to skill, companionship and pleasure, rather than merely changing the appearance of a body presented to others. After the breakup, realizing she no longer needs the whitening regime coexists with grief; it does not show she never loved Tōji. Her return to swimming, the damaged suit and borrowed replacement place competence beside material vulnerability. Winning and receiving cheers do not compel romantic reconciliation. They give her another kind of recognition from which her refusal to restart remains intelligible. (PG-JP-NE-V03-W01 i0036–i0086, i0101–i0106, i0116–i0122, i0168–i0174; [V03](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/PEACH_GIRL_V03_DEEP_READING.md).)

Sae can exploit that pressure because Momo's outward anger and directness contrast with Sae's strategically legible vulnerability. A false account need not defeat an equally circulated true account; it can prevent Momo's account from becoming the one people hear. The early public correction therefore depends partly on other witnesses and exposed contradictions. Momo's refusal to restart with Tōji in V03 shows that being vindicated and wanting the former relation back are separate decisions. She does not exist only as a wronged person waiting for an apology.

The friendship has an ordinary origin that later cruelty does not make unreal. Momo recalls returning after rubella kept her away from the first high-school week, when groups had already formed. Sae recognizes her as the girl who had lent her an eraser at the entrance examination. In the later recalled practice scene, Momo in turn offers Sae a place beside her when she sees her standing apart. That memory helps explain why she continues to hope Sae has not done something terrible and why seeing actual humiliation can change her immediate decision to stand aside. It does not make those hopes consistently accurate. Friendship, loneliness, resentment and comparative envy remain available together. (PG-JP-NE-V02-W01 i0092–i0094; PG-JP-NE-V04-W01 i0099–i0117.)

Her own physical initiative also requires context. After she helps Kairi up following his fall, Momo asks him to release her; he refuses for another minute. During the subsequent journey she explicitly binds his wrists to prevent unwanted touching, but also drives the borrowed bicycle down stairs against his fear and drags him while he asks to sit. Her urgency and protective purpose do not justify every means; nor can the earlier refusal be erased to make her restraint contextless aggression. This is situated fallibility, not equivalence with systematic abuse. ([PG-SA2-V02-0002/0003](../07%20Audits%20and%20Handoffs/PEACH_GIRL_SECOND_PASS_RECONCILIATION_AND_SUPPLEMENTS_20261024.md), PG-JP-NE-V02-W01 i0070–i0073/i0086–i0091.)

The V04 reunion is similarly mixed. Momo criticizes her own conduct, but Tōji also acknowledges fault. The earlier audit rejects transferring all responsibility to her. Her return expresses romantic possibility and a changed willingness to risk trust, rather than proof her refusal was irrational. Their L-finger love code becomes meaningful through her request, his embarrassment and later voluntary effort. Sae can copy its shape with Jigorō without reproducing that relationship. Its V15 return renews felt attachment without settling every current doubt. The physically altered foothills arrow in V08 is a different sign mechanism: it changes a route, not the private gesture. (PG-JP-NE-V04-W01 i0104–i0108/i0118–i0128; PG-JP-NE-V05-W01 i0031–i0039; PG-JP-NE-V08-W01 i0114–i0115; PG-JP-NE-V15-W01 i0163–i0166; accepted F0017.)

## Harm does not erase the person who wanted things

V05 first allows wanted intimacy and then turns to incapacitation, removal and impersonation. Momo's prior desire does not authorize the later plan. Nor does later uncertainty about an unshown act make the depicted loss of control trivial. In V06, misheard self-blame and fear produce a crisis before Tōji explicitly tells her she is not at fault. Her distress must be situated in what she believes then. The later V13 Jigoro account cannot be installed in her earlier mind as if the hurt were needless.

Wanted intimacy itself is not a single continuous permission. At the birthday encounter, a startling touch produces a push, Tōji stops and checks, and Momo clarifies that she is not refusing and returns the kiss. That exchange differs from her later incapacity. Her earlier slap and lapel-pulled kiss during jealousy likewise keep her own pressure visible before later mutually desired contact. Desire and the ability to articulate it change within encounters; neither romance nor later victimization makes every initiative ethically sufficient. (PG-JP-NE-V05-W01 i0077–i0090/i0116–i0122; [V05](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/PEACH_GIRL_V05_DEEP_READING.md).)

V13_150–157 supplies a delayed participant account denying intercourse in the unobserved hotel interval, accepted by Momo with relief. That is a consequential correction to her belief, not proof that bodily harm matters only if one particular act happened. It also exposes how she had privately linked her supposed sexual history to Kairi's stopping. The manga gives that theory to Momo; the analysis cannot promote it into Kairi's confirmed reason. Her relief is part of her history without becoming an analyst's ranking of her worth.

Momo's response to Sae's endangered situation in V07 is another turning point. She initially participates in mirrored punishment, then rejects its continuation and leads the search. Changing course is an actual exercise of agency inside a plan she helped make. Rescuing Sae does not equal forgiving her. This distinction recurs in V13 when Momo and the others respond to Sae's filming danger despite earlier dismissals. Her care crosses the boundary of deservedness; it does not require amnesia about the cared-for person's conduct.

This reversal has concrete cost. Momo takes the lead in entry and resistance, receives Sae's palm slap and hears a critique with a real basis in the trio's own conduct. The boys' help and Kairi's later responsibility statement remain separate. Momo's refusal to let harm continue does not require her to declare forgiveness, permanent friendship or Sae's complete reform. (PG-EV-V07-0007–0009, i0031–i0045; F0007.)

## Two romances have different forms of uncertainty

Momo's attachment to Kairi develops through support, attraction and the attempt to distinguish love from comfort. In V08 she still loves Tōji while admitting Kairi matters as more than a friend. The closing kisses do not by themselves establish a fully negotiated dating pact. V09 then makes public couple status and explicit Momo love actual. This phase cannot be reduced to a substitute relation that the ending retrospectively approves.

Her candor is a development with its own time. At V08i0151–i0160 she tells Kairi that Tōji still matters, that Kairi is precious beyond a friend and that she is uncertain. Accepting company under those terms is different from an exclusive dating pact. In V09 she begins asking what she actually knows of Kairi's history rather than only describing her own pain. His tentative story of recognizing her name through a school rumor is a reported connection to an old rescue, not independent confirmation of all rescue or CPR details. (PG-JP-NE-V08-W01 i0151–i0160; PG-EV-V09-0007, i0023–i0029, read through F0008.)

The handmade case makes affection a labor. She unravels and remakes it, buys yarn at the craft shop, carries the wrapped gift out for a surprise, then reads an apparently competing object through uncertainty about her place. Kairi's later use of it gives material evidence of care, although no object settles every omitted disclosure. Her own damaging pool kick despite knowing his water fear belongs in the same account of agency as her resistance to the fans: comic retaliation is still an act with another person's vulnerability at stake. (PG-JP-NE-V09-W01 i0031–i0044/i0047–i0054/i0096–i0101/i0155–i0166; F0009; PG-JP-NE-V10-W01 i0005–i0017/i0033–i0042.)

Kairi's divided attachment makes the asymmetry painful. Momo can know he cares and still lack the place she wants in his priorities. Her demand that he discard a photo, her later reconsideration and the Misao disclosures show an unstable attempt to manage uncertainty through objects and declarations. V11's willingly begun bedroom encounter and Kairi's stop are both actual. Her subsequent questions seek a causal account, not simply more affection. V12 supplies substantial history and the explicit comparison that Misao matters more, followed by Kairi's refusal to continue casually. His right to refuse remains valid even when the refusal hurts Momo.

Momo's renewed pursuit after that refusal is not automatically emancipatory because she is the heroine. The collar grab and kiss after Kairi's no in V12_149–154 exert pressure. His later responsive embrace does not supply prior permission. Her willingness to be “second for now” is also not the settled arrangement she later describes as acceptable inwardly. V18_18 explicitly revises that self-presentation: she wants exclusive cherishing and was not at peace with the lesser terms.

Knowing the arrangement does not guarantee that living it feels as she expected. The comparative answer makes V12's offer informed; V13's ordinary jealousy and private admission that second place is not enough expose its cost; V14's spoken questions and finite appointment condition give waiting a limit. These are changes in what she can tolerate and articulate, not proof the original offer was knowingly false. Her valid hurt also does not make Kairi sexually indebted after his stop. (PG-EV-V11-0028–0036; V12-0017/0020–0031; V13-0008/0010; V14-0006/0010–0011.)

Tōji's return develops alongside these unresolved feelings. V15 gives Momo actual attraction before the missed rendezvous, ordinary work and a prolonged finite wait. The original condition was noon plus five minutes. Her full inward question about whether Kairi's urgent business must happen now is not securely spoken to him: she begins a fragment, while the complete question is reader-accessible private text. His receipt of a fully articulated challenge cannot be assumed. At the station, after the condition has long passed, she grasps Tōji's sleeve and says she cannot wait anymore; an unsupported before-deadline intervention must not take that initiative from her. (PG-EV-V15-0002/0006/0012–0013; i0071–i0073/i0078–i0085; F0012/F0013.)

Renewed responsive affection at the inn is real, while her private escape/flow doubts at158–159 qualify the romance without making it fake. The historical strong inference of a sexual night is withdrawn by V18i0116–i0117: she wanted to settle with Kairi first, and Tōji agreed to do nothing until morning. Actual morning responsiveness remains; no completed intercourse that night or morning is established. The returned L gesture and her initiated embrace make remembered affection consequential without resolving every present question. (PG-EV-V15-0025–0028 read through PG-EV-V18-0026 and CS0214@2/CM0146@2/IP0198@2.)

By V16, Tōji's patience and respect for a stop give Momo a specific experience of safety. In V17 she can truthfully say she is happy with him while remaining unable to forget Kairi's tears. V17_164 and V18_6–7 make her Tōji choice explicit. It is analytically important to let this choice stand at its own time. A full-series account that calls every Tōji feeling denial merely repeats the practice of telling Momo what she “really” meant before she says it.

## Learning causes, then choosing

The final volume does not resolve the triangle with one confession. Kairi's current “only Momo” declaration reaches her21, but she does not trust it22. Sae's account of the typhoon journey47–50 changes what Kairi's next-day cheerfulness means. Misao's injury/hospital account61 and first-choice relay63 change her knowledge again. Yet the police cause remains unreceived. Her remark that she would have waited if told62 does not establish what she would have chosen under a complete alternate history.

The Kairi–Sae phone struggle offers an ambiguous partial view. Its arrangement permits a jealousy or apparent-intimacy reading, but the source does not uniquely establish that Momo reaches that specific romantic conclusion. Winded arrival, reactions, searching for Tōji and the empty rendezvous spot coexist; absence of an explicit caption also does not prove absence of feeling. The categorical misreading formerly asserted here is narrowed rather than replaced by categorical innocence. Her fallibility is already visible in better-established gift and booking interpretations. Her account of happiness changes too: Sae's resilience provokes the question whether hurt necessarily means unhappiness34, and Tōji asks whether self-punishment would help Kairi105. (PG-EV-V18-0008, i0075–i0078, read through F0016; EV0004/0011.)

The hotel sequence separates loving Tōji from being willing or able to continue intimacy. Momo says wait; he initially stops. His later blocked exit, pull and pin prevent a reading of the whole encounter as freely negotiated. In the subsequent call, Sae originates the body-honest formulation; Momo privately agrees with the counsel and decides she cannot solve matters by remaining there. That reception is not Momo's original lexical authorship, a diagnosis or a universal bodily test of one true love. The call does not show Sae receiving the preceding hotel-restraint details. (PG-EV-V18-0012–0015, i0114–i0141, read through F0015.)

Momo's explicit declaration that she loves Kairi143 precedes the actual drowning rescue. This order prevents a simplistic account in which saving him generates the choice from nothing. The forged farewell affects her urgency; it does not erase the prior disclosures and deliberation. On the beach she performs a concrete rescue, then declares love and initiates the kiss174–175. She is neither merely awarded to a persistent man nor solely driven by the obligation to keep him alive.

## Ordinary life, written speech and limits

School notes, exams, gifts, part-time earnings, holidays and friendly irritation are activities with different purposes. Affordable dates, food preparation, apology cookies, private-sign practice and birthday pleasure make a future with Tōji credible beyond crisis. Crocheting and paid work make care a sustained material effort; shift scheduling can protect resolve while also imposing a burden she reconsiders. Study and swimming supply standards of competence that do not depend entirely on either lover's response. These ordinary domains cannot establish relational safety, but leaving them as a list would lose how choices acquire weight. (PG-JP-NE-V04-W01 i0051–i0071/i0091–i0109/i0130–i0148; PG-JP-NE-V05-W01 i0025–i0035/i0099–i0115; PG-JP-NE-V15-W01 i0015–i0042.)

Her written speech presses for distinctions without guaranteeing candor everywhere. She rejects a discarded photograph as a substitute for changing a feeling, asks progressively narrower questions until V11's yes establishes continuing Misao attachment, and later obtains the stronger comparison in V12. At V11i0128 her question asks whether she caused offense; Kairi's no-fault reassurance does not give a cause. At V14i0076 she still addresses him as Okayasu while communicating the noon/five-minute condition. V15's spoken fragment/private full question shows attempted articulation failing to become fully received disclosure. Interior captions expose doubts her partners do not automatically know. No global address transition, acoustic delivery or reliable all-situation script is inferred.

The ending secures Momo's present choice and reciprocal relation, not a complete emancipation from misleading appearances or a guarantee of trust. The strongest developmental reading sees a person who increasingly acts and articulates what she wants. The strongest counterreading notes that the last movement still depends on a forged crisis and spectacular rescue. Both fit the final sequence. Her development consists in a changed capacity to choose within these constraints; it is not evidence that the constraints have disappeared.
