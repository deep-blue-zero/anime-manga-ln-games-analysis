# Directional relationships — Reciprocity arrives at different times

A couple label is an inadequate unit for this series. It can conceal different desires, different knowledge, an unanswered proposal, a refused continuation or a private motive that the other person never receives. This study follows direction, commitment and disclosure together, using the G2 snapshot342b9067abf2b200613e2b24707abbc190c59402 and the full relationship/commitment and information ledgers. The terminal relation is Momo–Kairi; the route does not invalidate the relations that actually preceded it.

## Momo → Tōji and Tōji → Momo

Their first asymmetry concerns recognition before exclusivity. Momo organizes two years of appearance and leisure around an alleged preference; Tōji's attraction does not originate in the friend’s report that supposedly explains it. The V03 confession changes Momo's understanding of her sacrifices without making their earlier affection imaginary. His V01 practical attention and concern are specific grounds for attachment. Her defensive decoy to Sae, meanwhile, names Kairi and creates material that can circulate independently of her intention. This relation begins with real feeling and different accounts of what that feeling means (V01 i0007–0022,0069–0072; V03 i0044–0047; PG-CL-0008@2).

The early credibility problem is therefore relational. Momo wants Tōji to trust her account; he initially treats Sae, presented as her friend, as a reliable interpreter of it. Her angry behavior can then be used as confirmation against her. He does not possess the private tells shown to the reader. His later apology and public defense are meaningful changes in judgment, but they cannot oblige an immediate restart. In V03 Momo both wants him and refuses his request; in V04 she chooses renewed commitment. Wanting, answering and becoming a couple occur at different moments (V02 i0036–0067; V03 i0135–0174; V04 i0037–0051).


Momo's early attraction and their actual dating do not give Tōji equal access to her account of events. Sae's credibility intervenes between them. Tōji's direct apology can repair a local misunderstanding while the larger pattern of mediated belief persists. Public vindication later changes his information; it does not force Momo to accept his request to restart. Her V03 refusal and V04 return are separate choices.

Tōji's protective concealment during blackmail reverses an earlier epistemic failure. Instead of believing too readily what someone else says about Momo, he now withholds what he knows for her supposed protection. In both patterns Momo loses authority over the terms on which she participates. The sincerity of his motive changes the interpretation of his conduct but not her informational exclusion.

Momo's subsequent Kairi romance does not erase what she can learn from Tōji. In V12 he distinguishes forgiving an act from deciding a person's continuing importance. Her use of that counsel is not automatically romantic return. Later, his actual apology in V14 and attentive care in V15–V17 help make a renewed relation possible. Her responsive affection is depicted, not merely inferred from being present.

V18 begins with her explicit Tōji yes6–7. His waiting81, reassurance and concrete future plans give the relation substance. But the final hotel sequence shows that recognizing Momo's agency in one context does not ensure respecting it in every context. His initial stop115 is followed by blocked departure and restraint123–125, then another stop133 and actual release144. Her gratitude and his grief outlast the breakup; neither supplies continuing romantic commitment.

## Momo → Kairi and Kairi → Momo

Kairi first offers Momo recognition against the public appearance stereotype while also helping produce the intimate reputation she has to answer. Those acts cannot be averaged into a single verdict about his sincerity. The V01 rumor kiss, her refusal, his later response and the V05 correction to her own account have distinct evidential boundaries. In V02 the distinction becomes especially clear: Momo helps him up, then says to release her; he asks for another minute rather than releasing immediately. Her subsequent reliance on his help is not retroactive permission. Conversely, her urgent transport of him to the hospital gives her initiative and controlling conduct of her own, including an explicitly protective wrist restraint. Their early relation contains mutual usefulness, Kairi’s pursuit, Momo’s evolving response and disputed boundaries before reciprocal dating (V01 i0043–0067; PG-SA2-V02-0002/0003; PG-CM-0193/0194).

The V03 changing-room sequence preserves a refusal followed by its violation: Momo says not to approach; Kairi continues, grasps her wrist and draws her into an embrace. Her later comfort, attraction and self-interpretation remain real without giving advance permission (V03 i0089–0106; A02/CM0016). V04 gives his grief and then a separate exchange in which Momo recognizes his help and expresses an unfinished counterfactual; he restrains a wish to embrace before leaving. The bed-service joke remains sexualized, and gratitude does not oblige her to choose him (V04 i0018–0020,0064–0083; existing A03/A04 routes).


Kairi's pursuit begins before Momo can assign it the same category. Helpful acts, kisses and joking offers do not establish reciprocal love at a common instant. V08 leaves her distinguishing love from comfort; V09 supplies public couple status and explicit Momo love. Even then, shared status does not mean shared priority. Misao remains an unresolved attachment for Kairi.

Momo's desire for first place becomes a demand for an answer that physical affection cannot supply. V12's requested kiss81 is followed by the explicit Misao-more comparison83. Kairi's refusal to continue casually and his actual departure are a breakup, not a puzzle that Momo is required to solve by pursuing harder. Her later pressure after his no and his later response must both remain.

Kairi's attachment changes rather than merely being uncovered as eternally Momo-first. Misao's rejection in V14 and his still-qualified counterfactual precede the V15 first-choice declaration. The crucial recipient is Misao. Momo hears a generic message and vague delay, not the ranking. His exhaustive search later demonstrates effort but fails to transmit its complete meaning.

The resulting Momo–Tōji renewal is real. Kairi's witnessed kiss in V16 creates knowledge by sight, without satisfying a forecast that required Momo's direct words. V17 then gives Momo the current Kairi confession and comparison, amid contact she asks him to stop. The direction Kairi→Momo contains genuine love and coercive insistence; it cannot be summarized as either alone.

V18's forced taxi and Momo's refusal are actual. Her later reversal follows true reports, her own deliberation, an alarming forged message and a declaration to Tōji before the rescue. Final reciprocal love, initiated kiss and handholding173–178 establish the couple. They do not establish that trust has become effortless, that earlier coercion was desired or that future repair is complete.

## Momo → Sae and Sae → Momo

Momo's later account remembers being recognized after the entrance-exam eraser loan, when her missed first school week had left her outside established groups. She also interprets Sae as having lacked a group. That remembered approach and genuine happiness are not erased by later betrayal. Sae's exact original interior feeling remains unknown. Her later choices repeatedly make Momo's possessions and attachments comparative measures of value; Momo's confidence in describing her own life can then be reframed as cruelty, possessiveness or lying (V02 i0092; V04 i0112; V01 i0123–0138; V03 i0109–0112).

Momo refuses submission and an apology under false accumulated charges and collective coercion in V03; in V04 she tells Sae not to interfere in the couple's quarrel. These are specific boundaries, rather than a duty to manage Sae's whole life (V03 i0137–0146; V04 i0117). In V07 Momo leads entry and rescue from the unintended receiver of the trio's punitive plan; Sae subsequently slaps her. The rescuing actor and slapped recipient are both Momo. The rescue remains necessary, and Sae's criticism of the trio's coercion remains legitimate. Safety does not purchase gratitude, forgiveness or permission for a new blow (V07 i0031–0049; PG-SA2-F0007).


Their nominal friendship and actual rivalry never map neatly onto one stable label. Momo sometimes recognizes Sae's schemes and still offers care. Rescuing her is not forgiving everything; refusing money or warning about Ryo is not abandoning her. Sae can exploit Momo's concern while also later apologizing and providing needed information.

The V13 apology/Jigoro meeting repairs a specific uncertainty. The V18 journey report helps Momo reinterpret Kairi's conduct and explicitly carries no obligation to reunite. Yet the forged farewell controls urgency through a false author. The relation therefore contains real help and recurrent interference. The last shared walk marks continued attachment, not a completed mutual accounting.

Sae's comparison of loss and happiness influences Momo's understanding of her own choice. This matters beyond rivalry for a man: Momo is confronted with a friend-adversary who can be hurt without appearing permanently unhappy. The story permits learning from someone whose conduct remains objectionable.

## Kairi → Sae and Sae → Kairi

Kairi and Sae move from antagonistic schemes and mirrored punishment toward a final-volume rapport in grief. Her comfort96 and his tears97 are actual. The phone struggle at75–78 can invite an intimacy inference, but Momo's precise interpretation is not explicit. The reader sees a contest over a device, not evidence of a hidden romantic dyad; her knowledge of its purpose should remain unassigned. Sae's advice varies between pushing past words and respecting Momo's own choice. Kairi also refuses her proposed manipulation86–87.

## Kairi → Tōji and Tōji → Kairi

Their rivalry includes information work, tests of sincerity and cooperative harm. Kairi helps expose Sae's method to Tōji in V03; he can act against his immediate advantage by helping Momo reconcile. Tōji does not owe him surrender of Momo in return. Later, accepting or administering punishment becomes a way of addressing guilt and rivalry, but bodily suffering cannot establish that Momo has received an explanation or freely chosen a partner. Their cooperation in the revenge project makes the limits of a supposedly benevolent coalition visible: each can care about Momo and still join a plan that instrumentalizes another person's distress (V03 i0107–0143, including wrist binding i0135–0137; V06–V07 revenge/delivery sequence; relationship/commitment ledger).

Kairi and Tōji cooperate in searches and revenge while competing romantically. Kairi's “give her back” claim and Tōji's “not a dog” reply make objectification explicit in V17. Tōji's later binary pressure and Kairi's physical interference show that the better sentence does not confer permanent moral superiority. The triangular form repeatedly tests whether the two men treat Momo's answer as decisive or as an obstacle.

## Kairi → Misao and Misao → Kairi

Kairi's account makes being recognized as a distinct person a foundation of attachment. Tutoring and the interruption of comparison with Ryo matter because she takes his possible competence seriously. Her subsequent departure has practical reasons in the narrated history, while he experiences loss. Neither his emotional account nor a pictured memory makes her responsible for providing reciprocal romantic love. Later Momo cannot simply occupy the same emotional place by being kinder or more available; Kairi's changing priority must be spoken and received at its actual time (V12 i0049–0078; PG-CL-0025/0026 and their versions).

Misao's care for him is substantial and differently categorized. Familial familiarity, emergency help and concern need not be performed flirtation. Her V14 younger-brother rejection is a meaningful answer to a romantic request, not proof that she never recognized him. The V15 first-choice declaration she receives is his new account of Momo, not an invitation to transfer a romantic relationship she never granted. Her later help may transmit missing information without choosing Momo's partner for her (V14 i0057–0069; V15 first-choice declaration; V18 i0061–0065).

This structure also explains a conflict with Momo that is not reducible to a malicious female rival. Momo encounters signs of Kairi's attachment while lacking his full history; Misao can be personally reassuring about Ryo without knowing Momo's actual assault experience. In V11 her Okayasu-kun statement concerns Ryo after Momo explicitly asks about his older brother. It must not be repurposed as proof of Kairi's innocence or Misao's informed endorsement of Ryo's later conduct. Warmth, incomplete access and a firm romantic boundary coexist (V11 i0094–0097; own-note referent correction; PG-EV-V11-0022).

## Misao → Ryo and Ryo → Misao

Misao's love for Ryo survives knowledge that complicates it, but survival is not stasis. Her wish to see him well and her difficulty ending attachment help explain choices that Kairi and Momo cannot settle for her. Her own later criticism of the way she looked at him is self-interpretation, not a proof that she caused his abuses. The study must retain the distinction between a personal memory of him, her present boundary and her professional or emergency response to injury. A person can stop dating and still summon or perform care (V11 i0094–0097; V14–V18 Misao/Ryo history; V17 i0062–0066,0156–0158; V18 i0068).

Ryo's conduct toward Misao is neither identical to his treatment of Momo nor insulated from the power pattern shown elsewhere. He can receive loyalty, exploit others and later seek or accept help. The V17 injury makes his vulnerability real; it does not explain every prior choice through a concealed good intention. Misao's continued care cannot be used as a substitute for his accountability, and V18 explicitly reconfirms that they are not dating. A future reconciliation or durable reform remains unshown.

## Sae → Ryo and Ryo → Sae

Sae's love for Ryo is not evidence that she accepted every imposed term. Ryo's family wish and actual cancellation do not undo reproductive pressure or hired assault. The relationship is analytically important because tenderness, exploitation, refusal and dependency occupy the same history without becoming interchangeable.

Sae's desired recognition differs from Momo's, even when the men overlap. Approval, money, status and her hope of becoming uniquely important to Ryo give her choices personal stakes. Her film refusal and help call are not cancelled by earlier payment or performance; his asserted therapeutic purpose is not neutral description. She can be complicit in harm to Momo and still be exploited in this relation. The V16 pregnancy claim changes the asserted commitment without initially establishing clinical truth. V17 distinguishes her felt future, his rejection and reproductive pressure, her retaliatory violence, his commissioning and cancellation of an attack, and her actual shielding injury. These are separate directions of action, not a shared verdict that the relation is either caring or abusive in every moment (V13 i0080–0123; V16 i0111–0130; V17 i0010–0017,0041–0047,0097–0153).

The late possibility of family is represented partly in wishes and imagined scenes. It supplies emotional meaning without establishing a child or completed long-term repair. Ryo's cancellation and Sae's costly protection have genuine consequences. The absence of demonstrated restitution still matters; love and being wanted do not retroactively authorize filming, coercion or hired violence (V17 i0111–0114; V18 i0069–0070; PG-CL-0003@16; PG-CL-0027@6–7; PG-CL-0027 and later bounded versions).

## Family, recognition and the ending's unequal closures

The Kairi–Misao–Ryo structure creates several different meanings of being first. Kairi wants recognition apart from comparison with an admired brother; Misao's love does not follow his needs; Ryo's relative age, social polish and commercial access lend authority to accounts that younger participants cannot readily check. Momo initially enters through selected reports and a cropped phone identity. The resulting errors are produced by unequal access and credible personal histories as well as by jealousy. The dossier for a selected cutoff must therefore distinguish what each person witnessed from what a family member later claimed.

These secondary relations prevent the principal triangle from monopolizing agency. A disclosure by Misao or Sae changes what Momo can choose; neither woman chooses for her merely by making information available. Adult and commercial power also differ from peer rivalry. The same word “love” cannot flatten those contexts.

The final ensemble is deliberately more open than a pair-only resolution. Momo and Kairi are joined; Tōji remains emotionally consequential; Sae remains socially present; Misao's refusal and Ryo's accountability remain bounded. The strongest convergence reading sees reciprocal categories finally aligned for the central pair. Its strongest counterreading notes how much uncompleted work the alignment leaves outside the final handhold. The source supports both.
