# Directional relationships — Reciprocity arrives at different times

A couple label is an inadequate unit for this series. It can conceal different desires, different knowledge, an unanswered proposal, a refused continuation or a private motive that the other person never receives. This study follows direction, commitment and disclosure together, using the G2 snapshot342b9067abf2b200613e2b24707abbc190c59402 and the full relationship/commitment and information ledgers. The terminal relation is Momo–Kairi; the route does not invalidate the relations that actually preceded it.

## Momo → Tōji and Tōji → Momo

Momo's early attraction and their actual dating do not give Tōji equal access to her account of events. Sae's credibility intervenes between them. Tōji's direct apology can repair a local misunderstanding while the larger pattern of mediated belief persists. Public vindication later changes his information; it does not force Momo to accept his request to restart. Her V03 refusal and V04 return are separate choices.

Tōji's protective concealment during blackmail reverses an earlier epistemic failure. Instead of believing too readily what someone else says about Momo, he now withholds what he knows for her supposed protection. In both patterns Momo loses authority over the terms on which she participates. The sincerity of his motive changes the interpretation of his conduct but not her informational exclusion.

Momo's subsequent Kairi romance does not erase what she can learn from Tōji. In V12 he distinguishes forgiving an act from deciding a person's continuing importance. Her use of that counsel is not automatically romantic return. Later, his actual apology in V14 and attentive care in V15–V17 help make a renewed relation possible. Her responsive affection is depicted, not merely inferred from being present.

V18 begins with her explicit Tōji yes6–7. His waiting81, reassurance and concrete future plans give the relation substance. But the final hotel sequence shows that recognizing Momo's agency in one context does not ensure respecting it in every context. His initial stop115 is followed by blocked departure and restraint123–125, then another stop133 and actual release144. Her gratitude and his grief outlast the breakup; neither supplies continuing romantic commitment.

## Momo → Kairi and Kairi → Momo

Kairi's pursuit begins before Momo can assign it the same category. Helpful acts, kisses and joking offers do not establish reciprocal love at a common instant. V08 leaves her distinguishing love from comfort; V09 supplies public couple status and explicit Momo love. Even then, shared status does not mean shared priority. Misao remains an unresolved attachment for Kairi.

Momo's desire for first place becomes a demand for an answer that physical affection cannot supply. V12's requested kiss81 is followed by the explicit Misao-more comparison83. Kairi's refusal to continue casually and his actual departure are a breakup, not a puzzle that Momo is required to solve by pursuing harder. Her later pressure after his no and his later response must both remain.

Kairi's attachment changes rather than merely being uncovered as eternally Momo-first. Misao's rejection in V14 and his still-qualified counterfactual precede the V15 first-choice declaration. The crucial recipient is Misao. Momo hears a generic message and vague delay, not the ranking. His exhaustive search later demonstrates effort but fails to transmit its complete meaning.

The resulting Momo–Tōji renewal is real. Kairi's witnessed kiss in V16 creates knowledge by sight, without satisfying a forecast that required Momo's direct words. V17 then gives Momo the current Kairi confession and comparison, amid contact she asks him to stop. The direction Kairi→Momo contains genuine love and coercive insistence; it cannot be summarized as either alone.

V18's forced taxi and Momo's refusal are actual. Her later reversal follows true reports, her own deliberation, an alarming forged message and a declaration to Tōji before the rescue. Final reciprocal love, initiated kiss and handholding173–178 establish the couple. They do not establish that trust has become effortless, that earlier coercion was desired or that future repair is complete.

## Momo ↔ Sae

Their nominal friendship and actual rivalry never map neatly onto one stable label. Momo sometimes recognizes Sae's schemes and still offers care. Rescuing her is not forgiving everything; refusing money or warning about Ryo is not abandoning her. Sae can exploit Momo's concern while also later apologizing and providing needed information.

The V13 apology/Jigoro meeting repairs a specific uncertainty. The V18 journey report helps Momo reinterpret Kairi's conduct and explicitly carries no obligation to reunite. Yet the forged farewell controls urgency through a false author. The relation therefore contains real help and recurrent interference. The last shared walk marks continued attachment, not a completed mutual accounting.

Sae's comparison of loss and happiness influences Momo's understanding of her own choice. This matters beyond rivalry for a man: Momo is confronted with a friend-adversary who can be hurt without appearing permanently unhappy. The story permits learning from someone whose conduct remains objectionable.

## Kairi ↔ Sae and the male rivalry

Kairi and Sae move from antagonistic schemes and mirrored punishment toward a final-volume rapport in grief. Her comfort96 and his tears97 are actual. Momo's apparent-intimacy reading of their phone struggle76 is wrong; it should not become a hidden romantic dyad. Sae's advice varies between pushing past words and respecting Momo's own choice. Kairi also refuses her proposed manipulation86–87.

Kairi and Tōji cooperate in searches and revenge while competing romantically. Kairi's “give her back” claim and Tōji's “not a dog” reply make objectification explicit in V17. Tōji's later binary pressure and Kairi's physical interference show that the better sentence does not confer permanent moral superiority. The triangular form repeatedly tests whether the two men treat Momo's answer as decisive or as an obstacle.

## Misao/Ryo and the ensemble

Misao's recognition of Kairi and his romantic attachment are not reciprocal categories. Her younger-brother rejection should remain a meaningful boundary. Her love for Ryo, later goodbye and continued emergency care likewise cannot be read as one unchanging romantic state. V18 reconfirms non-dating after care.

Sae's love for Ryo is not evidence that she accepted every imposed term. Ryo's family wish and actual cancellation do not undo reproductive pressure or hired assault. The relationship is analytically important because tenderness, exploitation, refusal and dependency occupy the same history without becoming interchangeable.

These secondary relations prevent the principal triangle from monopolizing agency. A disclosure by Misao or Sae changes what Momo can choose; neither woman chooses for her merely by making information available. Adult and commercial power also differ from peer rivalry. The same word “love” cannot flatten those contexts.

The final ensemble is deliberately more open than a pair-only resolution. Momo and Kairi are joined; Tōji remains emotionally consequential; Sae remains socially present; Misao's refusal and Ryo's accountability remain bounded. The strongest convergence reading sees reciprocal categories finally aligned for the central pair. Its strongest counterreading notes how much uncompleted work the alignment leaves outside the final handhold. The source supports both.
