# Kairi Okayasu — Making care visible while withholding its terms

Kairi's importance lies in a recurring mismatch between what he does for someone and what he can say to that person about his priorities. He can mobilize witnesses, find people, undertake an exhausting journey and notice another person's hurt. He can also turn help into a competitive intervention, avoid a consequential explanation or physically refuse an unwanted separation. The final romance confirms actual reciprocal attachment; it does not settle those practices as harmless.

This study concerns PG-CHAR-0004 through V18. Version 1.0 used historical G2 commit 342b9067abf2b200613e2b24707abbc190c59402; this second-pass version preserves that lineage while reading source support through the [dated reconciliation](../07%20Audits%20and%20Handoffs/PEACH_GIRL_SECOND_PASS_RECONCILIATION_AND_SUPPLEMENTS_20261024.md). Character, relationship and claims ledgers retain current version retrieval and historical states. The earlier audit's rejection of a pure-savior compression remains active. This is a temporally changing literary account of desire and communication, not a timeless operational personality.

## Teasing, intervention and the possibility of seriousness

Early Kairi offers a category problem before he offers a stable solution. Provocation, flirtation, sexualized joking, reported past rescue and occasional seriousness sit beside one another. The original dispute over who gave him mouth-to-mouth two years earlier is not resolved simply by choosing the version most favorable to him. Nor does an uncertain V01 speaker become a warrant for attributing all early focal knowledge to Kairi.

Recognizing an injustice is not the same as accepting the recipient's terms. He can challenge copying and unfair bodily judgment while still ranking women's appearance. When Momo wants a rumor corrected, making unwanted contact occur does not satisfy her purpose simply by making the reported act true. The later stair sequence makes the distinction explicit: Momo helps him up after his fall; his continued hold is followed by her release demand and his refusal for another minute. His aid and desire remain real; neither supplies permission. (PG-JP-NE-V01-W01 i0023–i0057; [PG-SA2-V02-0002](../07%20Audits%20and%20Handoffs/PEACH_GIRL_SECOND_PASS_RECONCILIATION_AND_SUPPLEMENTS_20261024.md), i0070–i0073.)

His retrospective recognition story also needs its actual modality. He says hearing Momo's name through an early school rumor tentatively recalled the rescuing girl's name. Name and slur are different semantic items; this reported association does not independently restage the old rescue or settle CPR. His later admission that his prior kiss/physical conduct limits his standing supplies a particular self-qualification, rather than proof of completed reform. (PG-EV-V09-0007/F0008; PG-JP-NE-V02-W01 i0144–i0164.)

His intervention in the public conflict is concrete. Recruiting witnesses and helping expose Sae changes what other people can believe about Momo. But useful action and personal stake are compatible. He can want her restored to Tōji, want her gratitude and desire her himself in varying proportions. A joke that offers future availability is not proof of pure opportunism or pure disinterest; its effect depends on the exchange and what she accepts.

Those interventions use different mechanisms. Supplying access or witnesses can enlarge another person's opportunity to speak; arranging a hidden listener makes Sae's own incompatible performances available. Binding Tōji and delaying help instead makes Momo endure an ordeal she did not choose. Sae's threatened teacher report is coercive, but its unspoken future contents are not securely sexual or wholly fabricated, particularly given Kairi's prior face aggression. Her separate boast about apparent vulnerability remains independent evidence of strategy. A successful exposure does not validate every means used to obtain it. (PG-EV-V03-0024–0025/F0010; PG-JP-NE-V03-W01 i0107–i0146.)

Withdrawal is also part of his repertoire. Crediting a rival's borrowed words and leaving in V04i0064–i0083 (especially i0079–i0081), rather than enacting another desired embrace, counter a universal opportunism or control account. They establish particular choices, not an automatic rule of respect. The later revenge arrangement repeats a different presumption: that he can script another person's fear while retaining control of the receiver and outcome. (PG-JP-NE-V04-W01 i0025–i0032/i0064–i0083.)

The V06 revenge plan demonstrates the cost of assuming he can control an intervention. His false studio arrangement and simulated punishment presume an actor and a safe performance, yet actual incapacitation and restraint occur and the delivery goes to the wrong receiver. The later V07 rescue does not transform the original decision into a harmless test. Momo's rejection of continued revenge is necessary precisely because the helping trio has crossed into harm.

Kairi already articulates a relational account beyond rank in V07. Speaking to Sae, he says Momo's happiness beside Tōji comes from what the two have overcome and built together; taking her seat cannot reproduce it. This is his stated explanation to Sae, not omniscient access to everyone's motives or his own current demand for a place. It forms a meaningful counterpoint to his later transfer language and to ranking first, while his responsibility statement admits the room mistake only selectively. (PG-EV-V07-0011–0012; PG-JP-NE-V07-W01 i0051–i0054.)

Kairi's support after Tōji's engineered rejection becomes increasingly important to Momo. V08 includes a visit, practical aid and rescue, but also a punch at Tōji and advances whose terms she does not uniformly welcome. The audit's mixed-care formulation keeps these acts distinct. His eventual willingness to remain near her while she is uncertain is a relational offer, not a retrospective contract covering every kiss.

Daily visits, water and bandage care, clothing, food and ordinary company make that support material. Some help has imposed terms; elsewhere a joke kiss stops at refusal. After Momo candidly describes continuing Tōji attachment and uncertainty, he offers company without claiming certainty she has not supplied. The forest kiss and later conversation therefore have different permissions and knowledge states. The closing flower fragments do not create an otherwise unspoken exclusive pact. (PG-JP-NE-V08-W01 i0066–i0104/i0125–i0168; [V08](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/PEACH_GIRL_V08_DEEP_READING.md).)

## Actual romance and divided priorities

V09 establishes more than pursuit: public couple status and Momo's explicit love. Kairi wears or handles objects of attachment and gives reassurance, yet he also pressures Momo in the bedroom and threatens over the Misao issue. A prompted apology is an event, not a completed transformation. Repeated assurance that Momo matters cannot substitute for explaining another relationship that organizes his conduct.

The Misao photograph and Morika confusion expose how little a material clue alone establishes. A necklace or old picture can suggest a relation without settling identity or its current status. Kairi's actual mediation explanation in V10 distinguishes the hotel invitation from an affair, while Ryo's phone impersonation shows that some apparently Kairi-authored acts are another person's. This correction is not a general certificate that Kairi always tells the truth or that every suspicion about him is baseless.

Ryo also makes Kairi's rivalry an interpersonal history rather than a generic triangle. Kairi's explanations about competition and wanting the unavailable matter as self-interpretation. They do not verify every family memory, the dead Aki chronology or a biological hierarchy. Misao's first recognition of him carries affective weight, but the story later distinguishes that founding importance from whom he presently loves romantically.

The V12 history explains why separate-person recognition has such weight for him. Misao's Kairi-is-Kairi answer meets a narrated history of being treated as Ryo's replacement. A photo, interpreted blush, practical tutoring departure, relief embrace and reported storm search become evidence he assembles into lasting attachment. Those events and his interpretation remain distinct: care need not be romantic, and feeling abandoned does not invalidate her stated reasons. Domestic familiarity in V14 does not automatically cure idealization; attraction survives the unpolished visit before his actual confession and her answer. (PG-EV-V12-0010–0016; V14-0009.)

The V11 bedroom stop must be honored as a valid refusal. Momo's willingness does not require him to continue. The unresolved question is his limited explanation and subsequent avoidance. Momo's private theory about virginity is not his stated cause. PR0016 correctly failed because V12 did not give a clear direct reason linked to that specified stopping or avoidance, even though the volume supplied substantial attachment history. Literary understanding can grow while an exact disclosure test still fails.

Particular respect and failed explanation must be kept together. He accepts Momo's initial refusal of a Ryo visit, supplies practical spray and lets her later change her own decision. At the bedroom stop, his apology/inability and no-fault reassurance answer different questions: neither supplies the complete cause. His repeated school avoidance and denial are then observable conduct, not merely her insecurity. Even the narrow love question at91 does not transmit the precise destabilizing allegation he has not been told. (PG-EV-V11-0012/i0051; CM0091; EV0021/0028–0031. The V03 imposed hug after no is not a respected-refusal example.)

## Saying no, then changing his own attachment

V12 makes Kairi's comparison explicit: he loves Momo, but Misao more. His refusal of a casual continuation and enacted departure are not merely tactics inviting more pursuit. Momo's later collar grab and kiss follow his no; the subsequent embrace does not erase that refusal. Applying the same event-specific boundary standard to both of them prevents the analysis from granting heroine or hero an exemption.

Kairi's current attachment changes across later evidence, rather than being revealed as a fixed Momo-first truth that always existed. V14's confession to Misao and her explicit younger-brother rejection are consequential. His report to Momo gives her some knowledge; his separate counterfactual to the soba couple—that she would be first in a world without Misao—remains qualified. The soba owner's counsel challenges idealization without becoming an infallible external diagnosis.

V15 then supplies an actual first-choice declaration—but to Misao53–54. Kairi's illness, Ryo's injury, hospital accompaniment, police questioning, mistaken estimates of waiting time and failed communication all contribute to the missed meeting. They should not be collapsed into either “he chose Misao again” or “circumstances alone made him late.” Momo sets and extends a finite deadline with incomplete knowledge. Kairi makes choices inside genuine emergencies and overestimates the future time available.

The failed phone exchange refines that responsibility. Momo begins a spoken fragment, but her complete whether-it-must-be-now question appears as private text; Kairi's receipt of the full challenge is unestablished. His vague delay report remains an actual omission without inventing a complete question he knowingly ignored. Misao's offer that he leave and his assumption of more future time preserve choice inside a genuine emergency. Her prolonged wait does not become an unlimited extension. (PG-EV-V15-0012/i0071–i0076, read through F0012; EV0013.)

The typhoon search makes care materially visible to the reader: stopped train, taxi, landslide, long walk, fall and failed inn call. It does not make the true priority declaration visible to Momo. Sae's later report finally transmits much of this effort. The missed encounter is therefore not simply a question of whether he loved enough; it is about what action can and cannot communicate without a received explanation.

Ordinary work supplies a further domain of obligation. Shared convenience-store wages support a chosen plan; thirst, debt and dishwashing complicate his thinking journey; later pay and the blank puzzle answer hospitality with material gratitude. A shared picture still to be made contrasts with finishing someone else's nearly completed puzzle for them. This is a situated thematic possibility, not a rule that employment or a gift solves divided attachment. The generosity, intended future and actual first-choice words also have recipients other than Momo. (PG-EV-V14-0016/0022–0025; V15-0003/0007–0008.)

## Protecting an image of the other person's happiness

V16 gives Kairi private evidence of Momo attachment and an actual witness of her kiss with Tōji. V17 separates recipients especially sharply. Misao hears that he went to confess to Momo and cried after seeing the kiss54–57. Momo twice hears that he was crying about Misao32/77. His lie partly protects him from exposing a desire he thinks should no longer burden her. It also deprives her of the information she is trying to interpret. Protective intention and damaging informational control can coexist.

When the portrait makes concealment harder, Kairi says he loves Momo82 and distinguishes the important first recognition by Misao from present Momo love92. Those are actual received words. But they occur amid an embrace he refuses to release after Momo tells him to stop. His “give her back” language89 invites Tōji's objection that she is not a dog90. The comparison is not an analyst's invention; the manga itself challenges the transfer framing while continuing to dramatize male rivalry.

V18 escalates to pushing Tōji, grabbing Momo, tripping pursuit and forcing a taxi departure. Momo calls it kidnapping15. The eventual reunion cannot authorize it backward. She hears his present Momo-only declaration21 and nevertheless rejects him22. His sincerity does not automatically make trust reasonable on command.

Later Kairi refuses Sae's proposal to manipulate Momo through a call86–87. He describes a funeral for his feeling95, explicitly nonliteral, and tries unsuccessfully to throw away the handmade case100. This is meaningful withdrawal conduct. It should be read beside the earlier persistence, not installed as evidence that he never crosses a boundary. Sae's sink-and-float advice gives his tears a witness without making the two romantically paired.

## The case, the sea and the limits of redemption

The handmade case becomes difficult to abandon because it embodies care he cannot replace with a verbal decision to move on. He searches, begs strangers for it and enters the sea after a surfer throws it. The phone, separately borrowed by Sae, enables the forged farewell. Keeping case and phone distinct preserves the causal structure: Kairi does not send a suicide threat, does not carry out his abandoned discard and does not enter the water to die.

The attack also distributes responsibility beyond the principal pair. Three men assault him; two accompanying women respond separately, with pity, objections, concern about public visibility and departure. Their objections do not prevent the beating or the case throw, and a collective role does not give them one shared mind. Momo is not thereby a witness to every preceding action. (PG-EV-V18-0018, i0151–i0158, read through F0018.)

His near-drowning is nevertheless actual. Waves and a leg cramp overwhelm him; Momo rescues and revives him. His fear of dying without seeing her is inconsistent with treating the accident as a consummated suicide plan. Death jokes and her rebukes after revival return the scene to their established banter without retroactively making the danger unreal.

Momo has already told Tōji she loves Kairi before the rescue. The final mutual declaration and kiss therefore complete a choice underway, rather than awarding Kairi a partner for suffering. Sae's admitted authorship176 reframes the immediate trigger. The group comedy closes a social circuit, but it supplies no detailed reckoning with her deception or Kairi's earlier restraint of Momo.

The strongest affirmative reading sees Kairi learning the difference between founding gratitude to Misao and current reciprocal love. The strongest counterreading sees disclosure repeatedly deferred until crisis, with physical insistence romanticized before a last spectacular rescue. His actual local withdrawal and Momo's deliberate return complicate the latter; his repeated coercion complicates the former. Neither can be discarded.

Written interaction varies with recipient and stakes. Mock advocacy can make another's account audible while editing his own initiative; playful reassurance can become a denial contradicted by visible avoidance. V11's apology/inability at127 and Momo-chan/no-fault reassurance128 leave the cause unstated. V12i0047 links serious feelings to lost confidence; i0048 qualifies his capacity to explain himself. His V14 insistence that the confession is serious differs from teasing, and thanking Misao after refusal is actual behavior without instantaneous erasure of feeling. The untailed love words at V11i0122 are not a secure separate exclusive pledge.

Ordinary joking, part-time work, gifts and peer aid provide behavior outside direct threat, but no universal scenario portability. Public cheerfulness, private regret, direct admission and bodily release are separate acts. Written ellipses and addresses do not reconstruct acoustic performance or a global verbal tic. The endpoint is a source-bounded, temporally changing subject whose final attachment is clear and whose future accountability remains unshown.
