# Supporting ensemble — Testimony, practical help and unequal access

The triangle is never a closed system containing only three desires. School witnesses change whose account can be believed; adult recognition gives attachment a history; work and money alter exit costs; transport and strangers change what action is possible. These contributions are unevenly represented. Unequal focal access warrants bounded claims about each role, rather than invented biographies or equal-length personality reconstructions.

Misao and Ryō have sustained, changing choices that exceed an ensemble summary. Their monographs develop those trajectories, while their dossiers separate exact knowledge endpoints. This chapter retains their place in the social field and the narrower evidence for peers, parents, helpers and strangers.

## Recognition and authority enter the triangle from outside it

Misao's recognition of Kairi as a separate person becomes emotionally decisive in his V12 tutoring account. Some history is witnessed by him, some reported by Ryō, and the whole is later told to Momo. His conclusion that nobody could care as much is therefore a comparison within his account, rather than a narrator's guarantee. Misao's actual V14 younger-brother-only refusal is compatible with genuine prior care. Recognition does not create a romantic debt. Her later V18 relay of hospital interruption and Kairi's declaration changes Momo's information without selecting a partner for her. [V12 i0049–0078; V14 i0057–0063; V18 i0061–0066; PG-CL-0025/0026 histories.]

Her institutional role is likewise concrete and limited. V06 treatment of Jigorō against Kairi's objection extends aid beyond preferred allegiance. Her parental-contact assertion is unverified under F0005, rather than an established expedient lie. V09 confidentiality and private partiality toward Tōji complicate neutral-counselor status; V18 disclosure is a later action, not proof she always supplied everyone the same information. Her goodbye to Ryō and subsequent hospital care concern different commitments. V18i0068 repeats the romantic no, while the arranged meal at67 establishes no wedding. [V06 i0037,0051,0054–0062; V09 i0017–0020; V17 i0062–0066,0156–0158; V18 i0067–0068.]

Ryō brings a different kind of authority. Brotherly explanation, money, adult polish and control of a commercial venue make claims harder for younger participants to check. Useful advice can earn real trust while phone deception changes who appears to have agreed to an appointment. Later overrides of Momo's refusal and Sae's costly exit show that helpfulness and power cannot be averaged into one verdict. The distinction is social as well as psychological: commercial terms can constrain a departure without requiring a school audience to believe a rumor. [V10 i0084–0097,0154–0169; V11 i0005–0025; V13 i0093–0116.]

The later family comparison and feeling do not remove that power history. Actual cancellation of the V17 hire differs from original commissioning, subsequent attack and emergency care. V18 parenting images present an unrealized possibility, not a birth, restitution or durable reform. Misao's care and Sae's love are not substitutes for his accountability. The [directional study](PEACH_GIRL_DIRECTIONAL_RELATIONSHIPS.md) follows how those relations remain asymmetric; the distinct character studies supply their fuller phases.

## Peers change public truth without becoming one public

Classmates and former victims give Momo's early account social force that private insistence had not secured. Public correction depends on who can testify, which inconsistencies become usable and who hears them. A witness can expose a particular scheme without certifying every later accusation false. Tōji's apology and the audience's altered judgment do not oblige Momo to restart the relationship. [V03 i0135–0174; original source events and PG-CL-0001/0003/0004 histories.]

Ogawa's choices supply a particularly concrete movement within that uneven public. She participates in the underwater attack, is herself rescued by Momo, and later lends her own swimsuit with hurried thanks. The aid restores Momo's practical ability to compete; it is more specific than a sudden unanimous change of heart. Ogawa then cheers for her and resists Sae's demand to stop. The attack trio's later account of being recruited through an alleged bullying story is additional public testimony, not a complete apology for their own actions. Gratitude, useful repair, resentment and allegiance change at different moments. These are actual peer choices without a supplied complete biography. [V03 i0059–0068,0072–0075,0082–0086,0116–0122,0165–0166.]

Kyō-chin's disowning of the appearance-preference report corrects a specific origin for Momo's self-denial. It does not make the social pressure imaginary or demonstrate that nobody holds the stereotype. The distinction between reported preference and actual attraction gives a comparatively small peer intervention lasting significance without supplying a complete psychology for the speaker. [V03 i0044–0047; PG-CL-0008@2.]

Fan groups and anonymous interventions require the same specificity. The V08 black-bobbed fan is not automatically Sae. Similar timing, jealousy or rhetoric can suggest coordination without proving an order or identifying an unseen pushing hand. Local bodily interference and actual threats remain consequential even when the wider coordination is uncertain. Neither a crowd nor the familiar manipulator can absorb every unidentified act. [V08 i0101–0113; PG-EV-V08-0029/0030 and preserved identity uncertainty.]

Rumor also has separate recipients. Sae's fabricated account of the stair tableau is not validated by the genuine hold, and onlookers' hearing of Momo's release demand is unestablished. Conversely, the reader's access to that demand does not mean the whole school receives it. The V07 nickname exchange identifies the classmates' inference about **Sae**, not Momo; an analyst must follow the named subject rather than import the prior rivalry. Reputational audiences are made locally and can remain inconsistent. [V02 i0069–0073, PG-SA2-V02-0002; V07 i0149–0151.]

## The soba household gives ordinary care a recipient's perspective

The soba owner provides food, work, lodging and access to medical help during Kairi's V14 illness and displacement. This aid has duration and practical cost; it is more than a wise sentence delivered on cue. Yet the expired-soba/award priming also tests Kairi's expectations without making every statement an explicit lie or every piece of counsel authoritative. The earlier thrown sandal prevents idealizing the household as a perfectly gentle moral refuge. [V14 i0104–0112,0132–0139.]

The puzzle quarrel makes a subtler comparison. Completing a task for someone can remove an activity the person wanted to do. Care has a recipient and an interpretation, not only a benevolent intention. The couple's advice grows out of lived irritation as well as warmth; disagreement does not by itself nullify the relation, but neither does continued affection license force or prove every painful relation worth sustaining. Sanae and the unnamed owner retain distinct source identities; a wife-role analogy does not supply him a personal name. [V14 i0145–0153.]

This household offers the principal characters a form of ordinary shared life against which spectacular pursuit can be measured. Feeding, employing and making room for illness provide opportunities without deciding whom Kairi should love. Their example broadens the meaning of care while remaining an imperfect, scene-bounded example.

## Jigorō's partial account does not erase his participation

Jigorō is a separately identified participant, not Pocky or an earlier unnamed adult silently merged through a similar role. Sae initially recruits him through a false account of Momo's behavior. His stated refusal to have sexual involvement with someone he does not love is real counterevidence to treating every earlier response as immediate assent to the scheme. It is not the final boundary of his conduct: he sees Momo become incapable of acting normally, carries her into the car, participates in hotel entry and controls access to her phone. Knowing she is asleep, he asserts that this is her wish and refuses Tōji access; later he prospectively agrees to Sae's coercive demand. Momo's incapacitated perception of Tōji is not actual Tōji presence or consent to Jigorō. [V05 i0091–0098,0125–0128,0142–0145,0157–0158,0166–0167; PG-EV-V05-0032/0036/0037/0041/0045.]

The V13 meeting supplies a different kind of action: apology and a participant's no-act account received by Momo. The retrospective account revises her interpretation of the missing interval, but is not an independently replayed complete hotel history. Useful local information repair does not erase incapacitation, carriage, phone control or the earlier agreement. His account, Momo's reception and the old unobserved interval remain distinguishable. [V13 i0147–0153; the corrected Jigorō actor/recipient route in the second-pass source receipts.]

## Family and practical helpers do not explain everything

Sae's parents are local domestic participants and recipients of what is shown or said at home. Their comments and response to her circumstances cannot establish a complete developmental cause for her conduct. Similarly, family-like visits in Misao's household explain familiarity without proving a past romance. The surname 安芸 in 安芸操 does not identify her as Ryō's separately reported deceased アキ. Orthographic similarity cannot complete an unshown biography. [V11 i0148–0158; V12 i0049–0078; V14 i0134–0137; PG-CHAR-0022 uncertainty.]

Transport gives other supporting figures immediate causal importance. A driver can facilitate a necessary journey while hearing only part of its purpose. Different taxis, other vehicles and their occupants remain distinct unless continuity is established. Practical assistance does not imply complete knowledge of the triangle, and an analyst need not invent a recurring confidant to explain an event-specific exchange. V15's delays and storm make effort depend on material conditions as well as intention. [V15 i0080–0135; V18 taxi and coastal transport scenes.]

## Uncontrolled consequences and chance encounter

The V17 hired men are participants in a specific commissioned plan who then do not comply with its cancellation. Their actual attack has consequences beyond the order as first given. Distinguishing instruction, cancellation and subsequent action neither exculpates the commissioner nor gives every attacker one inferred motive. The trio elsewhere does not acquire eyewitness knowledge of the park violence merely because the manga crosscuts their scene with it. [V17 i0097,0111–0124; PG-EV-V17-0013–0015.]

The V18 surfers form a different causal group. The handmade case's appearance on a stranger and its disposal into the water create a retrieval route; waves and a cramp then create actual danger. These people are not automatically recurring figures or members of a shared malicious network. The correct object is the **case**, while Sae's borrowed **phone** has a different custody and authorship history. The resulting rescue can carry formal meaning without requiring a planned final ordeal. [V18 i0100,0110,0159–0178.]

The men are accompanied by two distinct unnamed women, rather than one repeated woman or a single shared mind. At V18i0154 the long-haired woman in patterned clothing facing the cap-wearing man calls his treatment pitiful. At i0156 both women object, including an appeal to stop and concern about public visibility; at157 both urge return of the case; at158 they condemn the behavior and leave. Exact lines remain with the pictured speakers. Their objections do not prevent either the beating or the case throw, and public visibility is one stated concern rather than proof of wholly disinterested protection. The distant dog walker is another bystander, not one of the companions. Preserving these separate choices gives minor roles agency without assigning unshown motives or inventing names. [V18 i0151–0158; PG-EV-V18-0018; PG-SA2-F0018; scoped CHAR0035 collective clarification/CS0283@2.]

The supporting cast therefore changes what choices can be made without making the central participants passive. Testimony, authority, money, meals, illness, transport and chance all have particular agents and recipients. Momo still chooses before the final rescue; others' help makes action possible without becoming ownership of her answer. The ensemble remains open where the source leaves lives and outcomes unshown.
