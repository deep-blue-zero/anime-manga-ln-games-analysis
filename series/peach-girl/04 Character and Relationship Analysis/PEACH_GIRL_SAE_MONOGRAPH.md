# Sae Kashiwagi — The power to frame a scene and the cost of loving

Sae's most consistent capacity is to intervene in how other people interpret a scene. She does not need to control every event if she can arrange who sees, hears or believes what. Across the series she becomes more vulnerable to losses she cannot script, yet that vulnerability does not produce a single completed redemption. The final forged farewell shows her familiar power working for a couple she now helps bring together. Its successful outcome complicates its narrative function; it does not make the method ethically harmless.

This study concerns PG-CHAR-0003, with historical input fixed at G2 commit342b9067abf2b200613e2b24707abbc190c59402. Current retrieval uses CL0002@17/CL0003@16/CL0017@15/CL0020@14 and CS0286/RL0310, with the accepted second-pass audit qualifications. Motive remains mixed where the source does not discriminate rivalry, status, desire and enjoyment of disruption.

## Friendship, taste and access before the schemes

Sae first appears inside an ordinary friendship that Momo already experiences as unequal. Buying the bag after disparaging it is a shown change of conduct; the larger catalogue of copied tastes is Momo's account, including her envy that the things suit Sae better. This is stronger evidence for a particular appropriation than for an all-context compulsion. At the same time, Momo's recollection of illness, missing the first school week and being approached by Sae preserves why the friendship mattered. The illness is rubella, 風疹, in the later source correction; it is not a second independent scene of Sae's original intention. Useful recognition and later betrayal can belong to the same relationship without one canceling the other. (PG-JP-NE-V01-W01/i0007–i0012; PG-JP-NE-V02-W01/i0092–i0094)

Knowledge develops rather than arriving with a villain role. Momo initially names Kairi to conceal her actual crush. Sae's ensuing approach to him is not proof she already knew the real one. The secure later frontier is the hidden listening at which she hears Momo name Tōji. Requesting permission to use his nickname performs a different kind of access from Momo's existing familiarity. These differences let her move between supposed confidante, listener and intermediary. The uncertain speech layer at V01i0033 cannot be used to assign earlier definite receipt. (PG-JP-NE-V01-W01/i0013–i0025,i0033,i0073–i0080)

Her interventions also fail. Tōji checks what is happening at the pool and responds to an emergency instead of accepting the suggested romantic reading. Kairi refuses the proposed alliance and later hypothetical exploitation of a hospitalized Tōji. Momo probes a claimed kiss with a bluff and obtains an admission about the hidden audience. Treating everyone as mere targets of an infallible manipulator removes this resistance and misdescribes the work required to make a story travel. (PG-JP-NE-V01-W01/i0092–i0098,i0151–i0160; PG-JP-NE-V02-W01/i0112–i0135)

## Manipulation as control of an audience

Early Sae's apparent vulnerability makes assertions socially usable. She can affirm or fabricate what another person allegedly said, relay only the useful part and stage an arrival that grants her version plausibility. The early appearance contrast with Momo helps explain why her account travels. This is not proof that every anonymous note or uncertain act must be hers. The source audit keeps old pool-note authorship unresolved and refuses to turn repeated resemblance of method into direct attribution.

Sae's interest in Tōji is actual, but it is not the sole key to her actions. Status gained through a relationship, comparison with Momo and pleasure in steering the social scene can reinforce romantic desire. Jigoro supplies another status resource in V05 even after she rejects him. PR0009 is therefore MIXED under its overlapping original conditions: rejection and later use both occur. A fixed “she only wants whoever Momo has” formula cannot replace the specific evidence.

The V05 plan combines incapacitation, impersonation and engineered inference. Momo's prior willingness toward Tōji is used as material for an encounter she did not choose. Tōji is given a false account of voluntary departure. Sae's ability to control a channel has physical consequences; the analysis must not confine “manipulation” to clever talk while treating the removal as incidental.

## Credibility collapses, and recognition changes form

The early pattern works through selective truth as much as invention. A genuine contact or injury can be isolated from its purpose, antecedent refusal or later explanation. At the hospital, actual visiting and practical help coexist with a cosmetic cheek mark and insinuation; the intimate bed endpoint is an interrupted near-kiss, with no current lip contact established. A later claimed injury may instead be unresolved: Tōji's V03 collapse is directly shown, whereas blood, wound reopening and prolonged admission are not established by the dark marks or Sae's report. A subsequent good-recovery forecast cannot alone prove no earlier complication. “She has lied” cannot decide the next claim's truth. (PG-JP-NE-V02-W01/i0054–i0066,i0095–i0108; PG-JP-NE-V03-W01/i0007–i0022,i0032–i0035)

V03 more securely attributes the swimsuit sabotage through her instruction and later private complaint; the other girls retain responsibility for carrying it out. Their own resentment, Ogawa's rescue by Momo and subsequent assistance, and the class's investment in winning give opinion changes several causes. Sae's boast that the first apparent victim controls belief exposes a method to the concealed Tōji, but it does not retroactively settle every rumor's author. Her conditional teacher-report threat is 乱暴されたって先生に泣きついてやる: it threatens an account of rough treatment, without explicit sexual specification or an explicit assertion of falsity. Kairi's real prior physical aggression must remain in the context. (PG-JP-NE-V03-W01/i0059–i0075,i0107–i0122)

The public collapse of her credibility follows converging accounts and her own contemptuous response. It does not make the crowd's force or blanket humiliation appropriate. In V04 she is excluded and physically mistreated; Momo's decision to include her recalls a real earlier offer of a place. Sae's exaggerated ももさま, bag carrying and shoe service then recast restored friendship as hierarchy, to Momo's discomfort. Servility is a specific response to lost status, not proof of a stable submissive nature. The tiny repeated figures and chibi strain make the excess visible. (PG-JP-NE-V03-W01/i0123–i0167; PG-JP-NE-V04-W01/i0099–i0117)

The fashion encounter gives her an independent route to recognition: playful, unusual posing can attract attention where Momo first appears more visible. It coexists with envy, actual shoe damage followed by cleaning, invented cookie authorship and an embellished account of a real studio visit. Momo knows the celebrity encounter occurred and doubts the romantic decoration. Later unnamed peers coerce Sae's confession; their leader, rather than Momo, receives Jigorō's harmful spray. The correction prevents a neat but false reversal in which Momo personally repeats every violence once done to her. (PG-JP-NE-V04-W01/i0130–i0171)

## Being harmed is not proof of becoming harmless

The trio's V06 punishment reverses the position of control. Sae is incapacitated and delivered into a situation the planners do not safely govern. Her fear is actual. In V07 Momo leads the forced entry and interrupts the attack; Sae then strikes Momo's cheek with her palm while holding a vase, with water splashing (PG-EV-V07-0007–0009, i0031–i0045). The rescue and the slap's recipient are Momo, correcting this monograph's earlier attribution to Kairi. Sae's criticism of the trio's danger remains consequential despite her own wrongdoing. Momo's decision to intervene interrupts revenge without constituting forgiveness.

Sae's later deployment of photographs and blackmail shows that experiencing danger does not automatically reform the conduct that helped organize earlier danger. Tōji's refusal, failed attempts to recover material and protective breakup create another network of mismatched knowledge. In V14 Sae admits real negatives were burned and the continuing threat was a bluff. That later disclosure corrects an earlier account, while leaving complete print/copy/publication inventory unknown.

Her relation to Momo cannot be reduced to unbroken hostility. V13 provides a specific apology and a concrete reparative act: arranging the Jigoro explanation that Momo needed. Those acts should not be withheld from Sae because she remains capable of harm. Conversely, a useful act does not retroactively repair drugging, coercion or reputational damage. “Repair” needs an object: what was admitted, what information was restored, to whom and with what remaining consequences?

## Chosen targets, manufactured signs and incomplete repairs

The V05 change of romantic target has a precise comparative structure. Real celebrity attention and enjoyment do not satisfy her when Momo appears happier. Copying the L gesture with Jigorō cannot reproduce the relationship that made it meaningful; it is distinct from her later physical turning of a foothills route arrow in V08. The gesture’s return in V15 can renew felt attachment without resolving every present question. Sae then explicitly identifies Tōji as the person she wants. His refusal remains a refusal: surveillance, tripping, traps and cheek contact despite やめてくれ are imposed access, distinct from his earlier participation in the practice kiss. Her declaration of love adds an actual stake without converting pursuit into reciprocity. (PG-JP-NE-V05-W01/i0005–i0044,i0053–i0087; PG-JP-NE-V08-W01/i0114–i0115; PG-JP-NE-V15-W01/i0163–i0166; PG-SA2-F0017)

The substitution scheme turns ordinary objects into identity evidence. Sae's familiar ももちゃん accompanies a cosmetic recommendation, but the written ラメ入りマニキュア and the bottle she holds must not be conflated. V06i0099 later supplies Tōji's account of the perfume she pressed on him and his positive response to it. The earlier shop includes a separate light-haired clerk; no disguise or collaboration is established. Matching scent and clothing can induce a false identity inference without controlling every purchase decision. The recipient of Tōji's affectionate words is the Momo he believes present, not a knowingly chosen Sae. (PG-JP-NE-V05-W01/i0101–i0117,i0128–i0152; PG-JP-NE-V06-W01/i0097–i0099)

Her later relations with other girls are not a single coordinated conspiracy. The smiling figure beside the V08 trap sequence is an unnamed Kairi fan, not securely Sae. V09's limited hair singe is real, but her order or coordination is unproved. In V10 the private disavowal of loyalty has no securely named recipient. Those limits matter precisely because supported manipulation is extensive: they prevent a character type from becoming a shortcut for actor attribution. (PG-JP-NE-V08-W01/i0111–i0113; PG-JP-NE-V09-W01/i0035; PG-JP-NE-V10-W01/i0098; the current audit’s actor/recipient qualifications.)

By V13, “apology” itself requires a scene-specific object. The sales pitch at i0015–i0019 pivots from apology into pressure through scarcity, celebrity authority and personal insecurity. Halting thanks after rescue at i0122 is not the same transaction. The later bow, particular admission and arrangement of Jigorō's explanation restore information Momo needed. In V14 she admits the negative bluff, but arranges work proximity and an overhearing without Momo's prior agreement. True information and beneficial effects are compatible with intrusive means; neither generic absolution nor refusal to credit any repair fits the sequence. (PG-JP-NE-V13-W01/i0015–i0019,i0118–i0122,i0139–i0153; PG-JP-NE-V14-W01/i0083–i0099,i0115–i0141)

## Ryo and the loss of privileged control

Sae's attachment to Ryo changes the balance between framing others and believing a story about herself. She defends him, gives him money and continues to love him despite warnings. In V13 she can suspect she is being fooled and still hope to change him. This is not the same as fully informed acceptance of every commercial or sexual term.

The filming encounter makes the distinction concrete. An earlier signature or payment claim is used as coercive leverage; Sae's actual refusal and help call remain refusals. Momo's group responds. Ryo's later claim that the danger was therapeutic shock is a self-justifying account, not proof that his intervention was safe or benevolent. Sae's persistence after rescue does not make the rescue unnecessary or every warning ineffective in principle.

Her feelings also expose a limit to the simple envy explanation. She can want a family and a place beside a particular person, not only the destruction of Momo's happiness. V17's shared-loneliness conversation107–109 gives her family pledge form. It sits beside attempted violence toward Misao and concealment of Ryo's refusal. Desire becomes more legible without becoming innocent.

The pregnancy sequence is especially vulnerable to villain-based compression. V16 establishes a claim without clinical confirmation. In V17 Sae repeats it, then misinterprets partial clinical speech as miscarriage146–148. Ryo supplies the no-pregnancy correction149–150, and she explicitly acknowledges it to Momo/Kairi/Tōji152. The source supports mistaken pregnancy belief and grief; it does not establish that she knowingly fabricated the original claim. Her history makes suspicion understandable but not sufficient proof.

Her defensive statement that she never wanted a family is challenged by her own pictured pledge. That is an internal contradiction between self-protective speech and prior desire. It does not require an analyst to call all her feeling performed. Nor does her actual shielding of Ryo and injury122–123 certify his full reform. His hired violence and later cancellation remain his responsibility.

## Ordinary dependence does not cancel agency

The Ryo relation joins financial contribution, desired approval and the hope of becoming a family. Ryo-sama at V13i0034–i0035 marks a particular elevated recipient; it is not proof that he offers a secure place. Earnings, model/editor responses, sales recruitment, retakes and school discipline give Sae ordinary institutional pressures as well as romantic ones. Some information is her boast or another character's report. Career disappointment does not prove incapacity; money handed over does not establish consent to an undisclosed filming arrangement. (PG-JP-NE-V10-W01/i0048–i0051; PG-JP-NE-V13-W01/i0021–i0046)

The difference between elective influence and survival strategy is essential. Her polite uncle-address while escaping the V07 assault is not flirtatious consent. Repeated no, release me and the location-bearing help call in V13 retain their force despite earlier bravado about earning. When she returns to Ryo after learning more, that choice is neither proof that prior danger was consensual nor proof she has no agency at all. Attachment shapes an active but constrained decision; it does not authorize the harmful terms other people concealed. (PG-JP-NE-V07-W01/i0012–i0020; PG-JP-NE-V13-W01/i0093–i0112,i0125–i0133)

V17 makes her contradictory capacities particularly legible. She directs violence toward Misao while assigning another woman responsibility for Ryo's abandonment. Later, after explicitly learning that Ryo ordered the attack and then canceled it, she runs back to shield him from the men's changed plan and is injured. The costly act is not merely passivity. Neither is it a reason to normalize endangered loyalty or erase her hammer attempt. Her admitted envy and loneliness help explain the hoped-for family without proving a single origin for every past choice. (PG-JP-NE-V17-W01/i0041–i0052,i0097–i0133)

## Violence, care and specific audiences

Sae's hammer attack on Misao is actually attributed in V17, unlike the earlier falling-pot suspicion. Kairi stops the wrist; Misao's forceful water intervention and emphatic promise never to date Ryo produce a local crisis resolution. Neither woman becomes the sole author of Ryo's behavior. Sae's demand that removing Misao will secure her own happiness makes the zero-sum logic explicit, but that logic is not adopted as the narrative's only available understanding.

After Sae's sobbing at i0153, Tōji reports her wish to be alone and urges respecting it at i0154. Respecting that request differs from rescuing her against an immediate physical threat. Her final-volume account of feeling lighter and no longer loving Ryo33 provides evidence of a new self-understanding. It remains an account at a time, rather than a guarantee that she cannot return to attachment or manipulation.

Parents' blame and educational anxiety in V18_12–14 provide social context, not a comprehensive origin story. The parents are hers, not Kairi's. No direct source establishes a causal theory that one parental remark created all her conduct. Supporting context should increase precision, not replace choices with a deterministic biography.

## Helping the couple and counterfeiting a crisis

V18 lets Sae help through true information. She tells Momo about Kairi's typhoon journey47–49 and explicitly says knowing it does not oblige a reunion50. She also comforts Kairi96 and remains beside his grief. These acts differ from her earlier advice39 to push past women's words. The contradictory advice belongs to one character adapting to different situations, not to an analytical rule that refusal is insincere.

Her relation to Kairi in this volume is consequential without becoming romantic. The phone struggle coincides with Momo's arrival and a visible reaction at i0076–i0077. An intimacy or jealousy reading is plausible, but neither Momo’s exact attention nor a specific romantic conclusion is established; a reaction cannot uniquely certify that inference. (PG-JP-NE-V18-W01/i0075–i0078; PG-SA2-F0016.) Kairi refuses a spiteful call86–87, and Sae follows him to Shiranami. When she borrows his phone110, the reader obtains a reason to doubt the later email's apparent author. Her actual composition is not shown at that moment; her admission176 resolves it.

The counterfeit farewell puts Momo under the belief that Kairi may die. Sae answers Momo's subsequent call on his phone137, hears the fear and presses her to say whom she loves. This can be read as forcing clarity in a stalled triangle. It also creates urgency through impersonated self-destruction, a cost that the final couple photograph does not erase.

Kairi's actual water emergency then results from a surfer throwing the handmade case and his retrieval attempt, not from Sae's email directing him into the sea. These causal distinctions prevent either crediting Sae with a rescue plan she did not execute or blaming her as the direct author of every physical event. Her deception shapes Momo's arrival and belief; other actors and environmental events create the immediate danger.

The final comic admission, photograph, chase and shared walk keep Sae inside the social world of the couple. A fully redeemed reading can point to real aid and recognition of their attachment. Its counterevidence is the method of the final intervention and missing restitution. A flat-villain reading can point to recurrence but cannot adequately account for grief, protection, confession of hurt and truthful disclosures. The original series chooses continued attachment without completing a moral ledger.

Sae's written register and audience performance support a model of situational framing, not a universal deception detector. A soft presentation does not prove a lie, an angry admission does not settle every motive, and a successful scheme does not prove foresight over its consequences. Her development is best understood as a widening range of genuine stakes within a still-recurring practice of controlling how others choose.

## Written framing, separate knowledge and the ending's limits

The register changes have targets and occasions. ももちゃん can carry familiarity, a cosmetic approach or an intrusive question; the marked ももさま belongs to a particular servile episode. Ryo-sama expresses an unequal hoped-for intimacy. Polite survival speech, sales assurance, repeated refusal, delayed thanks and an angry confession are not interchangeable indicators of truth. Cat forms, angelic modeling, horn shadows and imagined reversals make audiences and self-images visible; they do not literally multiply actors or prove that all tears are fake.

V18 supplies the clearest final information boundary. Sae has the phone, Momo sees an apparent Kairi sender, and Kairi has explicitly denied an intention to die. Authorship is resolved by Sae's later admission, not by the sender label. In the intervening call, 身体は正直ってこと is Sae's jagged speech balloon at i0140; Momo's later private agreement with the counsel is separate from Sae's original wording. No shown exchange tells Sae the details of the hotel restraint. The formulation is therefore her persuasive argument, not an omniscient reading of Momo's body or a general diagnostic of consent. (PG-JP-NE-V18-W01/i0094–i0095,i0110–i0111,i0119–i0120,i0137–i0140,i0176)

The strongest ending claim is uneven development. Sae can articulate closure about Ryo, feel with Kairi and give Momo consequential truth, while still manufacturing urgency through another person's device. Repaired information and new deception coexist. Her family's brief blame exchange, professional future and long-term accountability remain too thin for a complete persona or a deterministic origin story.
