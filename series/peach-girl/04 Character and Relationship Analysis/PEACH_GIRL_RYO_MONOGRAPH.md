# Ryō — Credibility, controlled exits and selective feeling

Ryō repeatedly offers the feeling of being understood, then uses the access it creates on terms the other person did not accept. His useful perception is part of that mechanism; it need not be false to become exploitable. He can accurately criticize violence, recognize Sae's habits or notice Kairi's unresolved attachment while deceiving, overriding refusal and shifting responsibility. The later family wish matters because it breaks a wholly flat account of his feelings. It cannot, by itself, repair the practices through which he has treated people as instruments. His narrative therefore needs a distinction between a changing capacity to feel and a demonstrated willingness to account for what he does.

## A favorable account can contain useful truth

The older brother first appears through reports rather than a complete portrait: spending authorization, university/tutoring connections, Kairi's resentments, Misao's praise and the recurring cross necklace. These reports arrive from people with different stakes. V10 finally supplies a name and a present behavioral test. The unnamed hospital visitor at i0022 must not automatically be merged with him; the later identified party companion has its own identity route. Morika's attack with the knife is real and blocked by the hotel guidebook, rather than proof of a harmless paint-prop performance. Correcting those small identities and objects protects the argument from an unnecessary all-powerful-trickster theory. (PG-EV-V08-0021; V09i0062–0075/PG-EV-V09-0016–0017; V10i0022–0038; PG-CS-0112.)

At the café and party he supplies a persuasive alternative to Kairi's account of the brothers' romantic past. He speaks usefully about violence and jealousy, presents protective reasons for his own conduct and asks that parts be kept from Kairi. Momo experiences real relief and later explains why: he seems to understand the person who has hurt her and offers a version of the brotherhood continuous with the Kairi she loves. The experienced comfort is not fictitious merely because the speaker exploits it. Its reality helps explain her trust. But neither the relief nor Misao's favorable report independently verifies the contested past. (V10i0068–0083,0148–0163; PG-EV-V10-0017–0019/0036–0039; PG-CL-0027@1.)

His defense against Sae is similarly consequential. Sae falsely attributes her prior hotel peril to Momo's procurement. Ryō rejects that slander, and Momo, overhearing, feels vindicated. The wrong attribution and the underlying danger are distinct: Sae's actual peril must not be dismissed simply because her new accusation is false. Ryō's correct refusal to adopt the accusation gives him credibility. It does not establish that he will apply the same standard to his own behavior. (V10i0148–0154; PG-EV-V10-0036–0037; PG-CS-0119.)

## Control begins before the private room

The phone impersonation provides an independent behavioral anchor. Ryō answers Kairi's phone without correcting Momo's mistaken addressee, lets her believe an appointment has been made and later supplies a false proxy account. Kairi's denial is corroborated by the reader's earlier view, while Momo lacks that view and judges his brotherly explanation against the more attractive brother's apparent understanding. Each pair then sees the other with a rival without seeing the causal arrangement. This is more than a generic misunderstanding: Ryō has materially altered who agreed to what. His later magnanimity toward Kairi works within the situation he helped create. (V10i0084–0097,0141–0145; PG-EV-V10-0020/0023/0035; PG-CS-0114/PG-CL-0025@2.)

His invitation to research a game also changes scope. Momo considers paid observation to fund a trip but declines the week-long arrangement for the sake of her relationship; she accepts only a questionnaire. In the room the questions shift toward income, savings, borrowing and business opportunity, distinct from the explanation that brought her there. Morika's membership/seminar promises identify the commercial environment without establishing every fee, deception or collaboration in the whole enterprise. The adult's job, polished status, money and control of the venue distinguish this power from schoolyard rivalry. (V10i0102–0113,0152–0165; PG-EV-V10-0026–0028/0037–0041; PG-CS-0120.)

Permission is differentiated in the same sequence. He requests a garden embrace; Momo hesitates, he retracts, and she gives a limited yes within the grief account. Later she refuses the necklace, which he puts on her anyway. Unrequested kissing/touching follows; she declares departure, and he restrains her. Her earlier attraction, escort pleasure and chosen hug cannot authorize these later acts. Conversely, recording the assault does not require denying that the earlier assent existed. The source's succession of narrowed agreements and overridden refusals is the argument. (V10i0124–0127,0164–0170; PG-EV-V10-0031/0041–0043; PG-CM-0081/0084–0087; PG-CL-0017@7.)

## Different listeners receive different pasts

V11 continues the confinement. Momo repeatedly says stop, kicks to escape, finds the exit locked, is seized again and finally uses a pen before running out. Kairi meets her after self-escape; he does not supply the decisive direct interruption of the room assault. Momo's fear she has killed Ryō and later self-blame are experiences in the aftermath, not proof she caused or deserved the coercion. The living, speaking injured man establishes survival without an invented clinical account of the wound. (V11i0005–0025; PG-EV-V11-0002–0005; PG-CS-0124.)

Ryō's stories now diverge by audience. To Sae, Momo initiated and he refused; the reader can reject this against the directly presented opening. To Kairi, the conduct becomes a loyalty test and Momo's defenses a result to evaluate. To Momo, an apology for forcefulness is joined to the claim she was too cute to resist. These are incompatible ways of preserving his authority after the same action, rather than three equal accounts the critic must leave unresolved. Yet proving this particular inversion does not independently disprove every detail of his alleged bereavement or the entire Morika chronology. Scenarios are something he boasts of writing; a separate falsehood does not grant an analyst access to all his past. (V11i0025,0047,0072–0075; PG-EV-V11-0005/0012/0016–0018; PG-CL-0027@2.)

The bathroom scene gives his method resistance. Momo notices him in the mirror, holds the defensive spray, refuses to put it away, maintains distance and calls Kairi. Ryō mixes apology, sexual presumption, insinuated insider knowledge and apparent pain. Her concern momentarily lowers the defense; his explicit joke reveals feigned pain and he leaves before Kairi arrives. The device does not make her invulnerable, but it changes what he can accomplish. Her agency is therefore neither absent nor sufficient to guarantee safety. (V11i0068–0085; PG-EV-V11-0016–0019; PG-CS-0129/0130.)

His “true love prevents sexual approach” rule then survives him as an interpretive tool. Momo applies it in opposite directions to Kairi's later sexual stopping: perhaps he does not love her, perhaps he treasures her. Kairi's eventual admission of Misao attachment supports a narrow problem of divided feeling; it cannot validate Ryō's universal rule or his conduct. This is one reason a credible local observation can be dangerous: partial accuracy encourages the recipient to accept a larger, interested theory. The reported deadアキ, Misao's surname安芸 and Ryō's later address to Misao must remain distinct identity questions. The source does not establish that they are one woman. (V11i0077–0081/0131, V12i0049–0078; PG-IP-0127/PG-CL-0028; PG-CHAR-0022 identity remains separately reported.)

## Earnings and the price of leaving

V13 makes the economic arrangement visible in an envelope, banknotes and praise. Ryō accepts Sae's earnings, knows how she raises them, offers advancement and affection, and invokes her freedom. Sae says she chooses him and denies being forced. Her declaration matters; treating her as an object without any will would repeat a different simplification. It does not settle whether the choice is informed, whether affection and advancement are leverage, or what the actual costs of stopping are. His later pressure when school retakes obstruct earnings gives that question practical force. (V13i0034–0045,0071–0075; PG-EV-V13-0006–0007/0011; PG-CS-0163.)

The introduced filming job tests it. Sae understands game promotion; when adult filming is disclosed, she says no, tries to leave, tears the receipt, fights away and locks herself in the bathroom. The crew claims that money paid to Ryō gives them rights over her and threatens a penalty. The receipt is depicted evidence of that claim and his named involvement, not valid authority over her body. Actual restraint, forced partial undressing and filming follow before the school group extracts her. No completed intercourse is established by those pages. Earlier photographs, bravado, eagerness for pay and rejection of Momo's warning supply no advance consent to this act. (V13i0093–0116; PG-EV-V13-0015–0019; PG-CM-0110–0118.)

Ryō first offers to pay the penalty if she dislikes it, then makes the exit conditional on never appearing before him again. The apparent rescue therefore exacts the attachment she most values. No actual payment or rescue by him is shown. Afterward he calls the arrangement a protective shock treatment and admits the business is improper. The later story must be evaluated against his previous encouragement, financial benefit, stated terms and absence when safety was needed. A softer face and apology are actual changes in presentation, not independent corroboration of benevolence. Sae knows the warnings and still returns; that return does not retrospectively authorize the crew's assault. (V13i0099–0102,0125–0133; PG-EV-V13-0016/0022–0023; PG-CS-0168/PG-CL-0027@3.)

His alleged police membership connection is likewise a spoken claim, not proof of official corruption or a legal verdict. The source permits close ethical analysis of who receives money, whose refusal is overridden and which alternatives are punished without an external legal classification. “Crime” and “human trafficking” in the confrontation belong to characters' accusations; actual responsibility need not be inflated by treating every accusation as adjudicated fact.

## Selective tenderness is a contradiction to examine

The later volumes provide genuine counterevidence to a man without feeling. Ryō calls about Kairi's absence and has provided bounded hospital help, though demands for repayment and familiar aggression complicate the brotherly gesture. He protects Misao during the attack and later welcomes her confession tenderly. He speaks of the comfort her presence gives him and says he had thought his feeling one-sided before her admission. These accounts and actions coexist with the harm he causes elsewhere. (V14i0102; V15i0059–0075; V16i0067–0073; PG-CS-0186/0203/0222.)

The juxtaposition sharpens responsibility. Immediately after tenderness to Misao, he hits Sae and leaves her crying without comfort. He denies ever dating her, reframes her love as self-love, questions paternity through a sexual slur and treats her account of intimacy/promises as an inconvenience. These are interested judgments, not a narrator's diagnosis of Sae. Misao's withdrawal and public defense of Sae show that being loved by him cannot permanently shelter his conduct from the person who loves him. He is not made accountable simply by preferring a better woman. (V16i0071–0075,0112–0132; PG-EV-V16-0012/0017–0021; PG-CS-0231/PG-CL-0027@5.)

V17's pleasant amusement-park date becomes an argument for replacement and abandonment. He uses the closing park as an analogy, says he only wants Misao, presents a pill and money, dismisses fatherhood and exploits Sae's “anything” by asking her to break up. His fictional drug/surgical-risk claims are not validated medicine. The ethical issue is the pressure, disregard of her expressed wish and self-exculpatory handling, not a universal ruling about reproductive choice. Misao explicitly rejects his method and later his claim he acted badly for Sae's benefit. No drug ingestion is established. (V17i0006–0017,0058–0066; PG-EV-V17-0001/0008; PG-CS-0242.)

## Polished explanation and the force of a short sentence

Ryō's speech can make exploitation sound like recognition. The false proxy explanation to Momo at V10i0093 is considerate in form while concealing his participation in the call. His later short 帰さない at i0169 removes that courtesy's practical promise: she has said she will leave, and he says she will not. The contrast locates control in the exchange rather than in a permanent harsh or soft register.

In V11 he gives incompatible accounts to Sae, Kairi and Momo. The bathroom's バイバイももちゃん at i0083 places familiar address beside a performance that has just displaced her defensive object. Familiarity does not make the scene mutually intimate. His general propositions about cruelty and true love recruit local experience into a larger interested theory; the narrator does not certify that theory by letting another character consider it.

The later 計画は中止だ at V17i0112 is a quieter, actual instruction to the hired man, not a public confession to Sae. At the hospital, fault and father self-identification reach a different audience and contain different information. V18i0070's tentative wish to have tried parenting is again a different speech act: an unrealized possibility rather than a plan already performed. Recipient, modality and action distinguish these utterances more reliably than an imagined single conversational voice.

## Cancellation is real, and repair remains incomplete

The commissioned attack is a separate, grave decision. Ryō orders violence against a woman, including being struck unconscious and later hospital handling, while requiring enough apparent injury to himself to make the scenario convincing. The reader hears this before Sae arrives. Before her family offer, he voices fear of his own offspring and envy of Kairi's ability to be loved. She then identifies a loneliness she shares and offers a family. These are his expressed self-understanding, not genetic facts or a diagnosis. Sae's recognition of similar loneliness helps explain their attachment; it cannot excuse the violence he has already commissioned. (V17i0097–0110; PG-EV-V17-0013–0014; PG-CS-0256.)

He actually cancels when the men arrive. They change the plan, injure him and later strike Sae when she shields him. The order, cancellation, independent reprisal and her protective act occur in that sequence. Cancellation is not an imaginary redeeming intention; it is a meaningful action before the hired violence against her is performed as ordered. It also does not render the original decision harmless. At the ambulance he asks that she be attended first; at the hospital he says it is his fault and identifies himself as the child's father. These are new acts of responsibility and care, without amounting to a full confession of the plan to the trio or verified biological paternity. (V17i0111–0137; PG-EV-V17-0015/0018–0019; PG-CS-0256/0259.)

The fictional nonpregnancy disclosure corrects both apparent pregnancy and Sae's mistaken loss interpretation. It does not make the actual blow, terror or earlier pressure unreal. The club strikes Sae's back at V17i0123; the later woman's report of abdominal concern at i0130 is separately attributed. The direct impact and subsequent report are different evidence, not an established clinical injury mechanism. In V18 Ryō studies parenting/family images and imagines that raising a child could have been more thrilling and consequential than a game. This is belated possibility, not an actual child, successful co-parenting or proven reform. The nearby woman remains unnamed in that scene. No comprehensive confession, restitution, network accountability or durable behavioral change is depicted. (V17i0143–0153; V18i0069–0070; PG-EV-V18-0007; PG-CS-0271/PG-CL-0027@7.)

That incomplete comparison defines the limit of the ending. His sharp understanding of others repeatedly exceeds his willingness to grant them control of their choices. Late feeling and emergency care complicate a one-note portrait; they do not perform the work of repair. A bounded dossier can represent the changing situations in which he seeks admiration, profit, access, control or attachment. It cannot invent the private history or validated behavioral rule that the source has not supplied.
