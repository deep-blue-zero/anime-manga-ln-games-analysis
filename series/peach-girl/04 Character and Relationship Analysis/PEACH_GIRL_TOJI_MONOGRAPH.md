# Tōji — Patience, protective concealment and the fear of losing

Tōji's trajectory makes sincerity an inadequate final explanation of romantic conduct. The early relation fails partly because he believes mediated accounts over Momo. Later, he understands more, acknowledges faults and offers concrete care, yet still tries to decide what she should know or where she may go. His last release is actual and painful. It does not make his earlier restraint disappear, just as that restraint does not make every patient act fraudulent.

PG-CHAR-0002 is examined through the original-series boundary. The historical input is G2 commit342b9067abf2b200613e2b24707abbc190c59402; CL0005@13/CL0015@13/CL0017@15 and CS0280/RL0307 remain retrieval anchors, qualified by the accepted second-pass audit findings. The study retains the early audit's refusal to make Momo solely responsible for the V04 repair and the V18 correction to the inferred V15 sexual night.

## Recognition before the mediated failure

Before he becomes the boyfriend who fails to listen, Tōji has recognized a particular person. His middle-school knowledge of Momo's swimming supplies a reason to reject the equation of a tan with sexual availability; she overhears this defense without his performing it for her approval. The brief school greeting うす and their shared acquaintance establish ordinary familiarity before the triangle becomes a sequence of emergencies. Her とーじ and Sae's 東寺ヶ森くん mark different speakers' access to him, rather than demonstrating a change in his own register. This earlier recognition helps explain why losing his confidence wounds Momo so deeply. It does not prove an egalitarian outlook untouched by appearance: his distinction between her tan and deliberate salon tanning retains a hierarchy within his reassurance. (PG-JP-NE-V01-W01/i0013–i0017,i0099–i0107)

Nor is his early response uniformly credulous. He asks Kairi whether the kiss occurred, and at the pool checks what is actually happening rather than treating rescue breaths as romantic betrayal. He helps obtain assistance. Those inquiries and practical cooperation are available counterexamples to a portrait of automatic jealousy. In the private confrontation, however, a wall punch and threat coexist with his defense of Momo. The object of concern and the means used to express it must remain separate. Momo does not witness or request that confrontation. (PG-JP-NE-V01-W01/i0068–i0072,i0092–i0098)

The café adds a quieter basis for attachment: he wants to know her food preferences and childhood and to share his own. The later failure develops inside this real mutual curiosity. He accepts secrecy without receiving her concrete reason for distrusting Sae, while Momo interprets an object and a touch through allegations he has never heard. These are unequal information states, not proof that either person's feelings were counterfeit. His spontaneous suggestion that lifesaving mouth contact need not require apology is then diverted into Sae's instruction to reassure through a wordless kiss. His refusals of practice precede actual participation; the transition does not establish that he was physically forced, and the participation does not erase the pressure. (PG-JP-NE-V01-W01/i0110–i0144)

## Trust that depends on intermediaries

Early Tōji's affection is compatible with serious epistemic failure. He can want Momo and still accept Sae's framing of her. The observed difference is not between a sincere lover and a liar pretending to love; it is between affection and the practices needed to sustain trust. Momo's appearance, classroom rumor and Sae's performance shape which explanations reach him and which he finds credible.

V02's direct visit and apology reconcile an immediate misunderstanding. That is why PR0002 was disconfirmed even though a deeper Sae-sourced belief remains. A plot summary that calls the whole arc one uninterrupted misunderstanding misses the local repair and the unresolved substrate. The distinction also prevents later public vindication from being treated as the first time any communication succeeded.

V03 gives him stronger grounds to disbelieve Sae and ask Momo to restart. Momo's refusal is an actual answer. His wish to restore the relation does not by itself determine her readiness. V04 then combines her self-criticism with his acknowledged fault. The source audit treats both as consequential: her return is not an admission that his earlier disbelief was justified.

The private L gesture offers a compact shared expression whose meaning depends on their negotiated effort and history. Sae asks Jigorō to copy its shape in V05 without acquiring that relationship; the physically turned foothills route arrow in V08 is a different sign and a different intervention. Tōji’s V15 return to the L gesture can renew Momo’s felt attachment without settling every current question. The comparison concerns what people infer from visible indications; it does not establish literal sabotage of the love gesture or make its use the cause of all later communication trouble. (PG-JP-NE-V04-W01/i0104–i0108,i0118–i0128; PG-JP-NE-V05-W01/i0031–i0039; PG-JP-NE-V08-W01/i0114–i0115; PG-JP-NE-V15-W01/i0163–i0166; PG-SA2-F0017.)

## Ordinary repair requires both people's adjustment

V02 lets Tōji identify jealousy as his own motive: Momo looked happier with Kairi, and he acted impulsively while thinking of himself as her boyfriend. His direct apology therefore does more than remove a factual misunderstanding. It names an action for which affection is insufficient justification. Later he tells her that fear of losing her preceded Sae's immediate interventions. Handholding, embarrassment and interrupted near-kissing give that vulnerability a reciprocal setting. None makes the earlier painful pulling consensual, and none guarantees that he will hear the next unwelcome explanation. (PG-JP-NE-V02-W01/i0019–i0026,i0136–i0143)

His illness complicates access without resolving the disagreement. Abdominal pain precedes the abruptly terminated call; the hospitalization announcement misses Momo. He asks Sae to relay an apology, but intended transmission is not received transmission. When Momo finally gives the meeting detail, he can acknowledge that he never changed the venue and still resist revising his belief about Sae. The eventual disclosure of Kairi's unsolicited pre-couple kiss is further misread as evidence of Momo's preference. The sequence exposes the difference between acquiring a correct fragment and allowing it to change an account. (PG-JP-NE-V02-W01/i0046–i0062,i0147–i0166)

The V03–V04 restoration also has stages. Hearing Sae's own credibility boast changes his belief, but shame initially impedes approach. Kairi's literal confinement then delays his aid; his arrival cannot be modeled as pure free deliberation. His public protection of Momo precedes her consent to restore the relationship. He subsequently admits that calling her his girlfriend was impulsive and asks privately; her refusal remains an answer. The later haircut accompanies a more precise admission that he accepted reports without checking. He turns away after apologizing; Momo chooses to approach, embrace and ask to date again. Their renewed commitment belongs to that exchange rather than to the audience's applause or his penance alone. (PG-JP-NE-V03-W01/i0107–i0115,i0123–i0158,i0168–i0174; PG-JP-NE-V04-W01/i0036–i0048)

Ordinary dating then matters as evidence in its own right: studying, movies, arcade visits, fireworks and teasing the haircut make a shared life imaginable. Encouraging swimming and accepting the tan that accompanies an activity she enjoys connects care with her competence. The private L sign grows through a small negotiation. He is embarrassed by repeated declarations; she first presses, then recognizes that she cannot demand every expression. His private rehearsal and voluntary public double sign make accommodation mutual. A sign is not a universal measure of love, and her pleasure does not remove his earlier communication boundary. At another approach she reflexively says や... and he immediately withdraws; her subsequent wish for the kiss does not invalidate that stop. (PG-JP-NE-V04-W01/i0051–i0063,i0084–i0109,i0118–i0128)

## Concrete care after harm

V05–V06 provide substantial counterevidence to the idea that his love is merely possessive performance. He detects impersonation, rejects a false voluntary-choice account, searches and actually reaches Momo. The difference between search and arrival is important: PR0010 passes at the depicted arrival, without requiring a claim that every uncertain hotel event is resolved.

The V06 no-fault response directly answers Momo's self-blame. Her crisis does not establish that she had a settled suicide plan, and his care need not be inflated into a clinical rescue protocol to matter. His words and contact help restore the possibility of intimacy after coercive removal. The analysis should retain this achievement when judging his later failures.

Tōji nevertheless joins the trio's mirrored punishment of Sae. A staged breakup can hurt even when the speaker knows it is a performance. Simulated danger can create actual danger when the participants presume they control recipients and outcomes. This episode tests a tendency to use distress as an instrument for someone else's supposed improvement. The later decision to rescue Sae is an actual reversal, not evidence that the plan was always safe.

## Protection by denying the other person's information

The V07 blackmail arc makes Tōji's protective logic explicit. He refuses Sae's demand, tries to recover material and conceals the threat from Momo. His performed cruelty and misleading kiss serve a goal of protecting her from exposure. They also make her experience a breakup whose explanation he withholds.

Momo's own inference that blackmail is involved shows the limits of that protection. Concealment neither keeps her uninvolved nor gives her a meaningful part in deciding the response. V08's continued rejection and destruction or devaluation of shared objects enact hurt in the present. A good private motive does not make the experienced acts disappear. His explanation to Misao also creates a recipient asymmetry: someone else may understand while Momo remains excluded.

The V14 disclosure changes the relation because Momo finally learns the negative-burning/bluff sequence and exact threat. Tōji's apology156 accepts his own fault. It should not be displaced by Momo's self-blame or treated as irrelevant because he was under pressure. The apology provides a source-grounded basis for recognizing development: he can name harm in his own protective conduct.

His later proposal and Momo's responses remain temporally specific. A conditional offer in V14 is not immediately accepted; V15 clarifies her earlier pretended non-hearing. At the V15 station encounter, Momo has already waited past the noon-plus-five-minute condition; Tōji arrives at i0081, offers a possible benign explanation, and she reaches his sleeve at i0085 saying she cannot wait any longer. Her communicated cutoff and prolonged waiting remain part of her decision; his before-cutoff intervention is not established. He has not received Kairi's actual emergency account. (PG-JP-NE-V15-W01/i0038–i0040,i0078–i0085; PG-SA2-F0013.) Treating all these acts as one fair contest between men obscures the actual terms Momo communicated.

## Endurance, monitoring and useful work between romances

The concealed protective bargain also shapes his relation to Sae. In V10 he follows her step for step, saying that if left alone she may do something harmful. Her stop and attempts to escape prevent this from becoming evidence of mutually comfortable companionship. Prevention is his purpose; constant monitoring is his chosen means, and neither establishes actual control of all threats. Her later boast of frequent sex is not direct evidence that he did those things. At V11 his question about Ryo receives only a softened account before he says he will not break up over it. His stated endurance should be recorded without inventing free, effortless acceptance of a complete history. (PG-JP-NE-V10-W01/i0048–i0062; PG-JP-NE-V11-W01/i0100–i0105)

V12 gives him an explicit decision language: remaining with someone need not mean forgiving everything, and the choice turns on what the person wants to preserve. He catches himself discussing this with Adachi and withdraws. The thought helps Momo later, but her decision is hers. The same volume also contains his initiated dictionary-encounter punch, a surprise embrace and renewed proposal, followed by her effective refusal and his advice to speak honestly to Kairi. Helpful counsel does not make the punch harmless or prove that the entire proposal was a benevolent test. Its motive remains partly open. (PG-JP-NE-V12-W01/i0030–i0033,i0114–i0138)

His V13 investigation and schoolwork widen the available character beyond romantic crisis. He searches for information about the company, supplies findings as findings, remains attentive to Sae's retakes, and performs well himself. When Momo later phones a location and actual danger, that actionable contact enables collective intervention. The entry punch interrupts an ongoing forced filming scene; its occasion differs from the grievance punch in V12. Rescue still does not certify every retaliatory action or establish an unseen institutional follow-up. The later Jigorō disclosure is another man's action and cannot be used as Tōji's apology or knowledge. (PG-JP-NE-V13-W01/i0028–i0033,i0047,i0057–i0062,i0107,i0114–i0133)

At the shop in V14, he is surprised to find Momo and offers to withdraw rather than claiming the work arrangement as his design. In V15 he offers less convenient shifts when she wants distance, sweeps and finishes work, and reassures her about the rendezvous after hearing noon and the five-minute condition. These mundane accommodations provide a baseline against which the later crisis can be assessed. They do not establish that her uncertainty is fully spoken to him or that he has no renewed desire. (PG-JP-NE-V14-W01/i0083–i0107; PG-JP-NE-V15-W01/i0028–i0042)

## A credible renewed relation

The seaside intervention in V15 is caring but initially uninvited. Tōji has followed Momo; she first wants to be alone. He apologizes and offers distance. His sitting apart105–106 matters because care becomes attentive to a stated preference rather than only to his desire to be useful. No settled self-harm plan is established in Momo's solitary grief.

The stranded inn provides a setting for renewed reciprocal affection. Her attraction to him predates Kairi's final absence, and her morning response is actually depicted. The V18 flashback116–117 now supplies an agreement to do nothing until morning while she settles with Kairi. This corrects the earlier strong sexual-night inference without converting the whole visit into emotionally empty room sharing. An actual renewed relation does not require invented intercourse.

V16 strengthens the experience of patience: when Momo says stop, Tōji stops and apologizes. He allows time. In V17 he chooses the quiet closing park to replace a painful memory with a better one39–40; Momo initiates an embrace. That is specific restorative attention to their shared history. The same gondola is also used by Ryo to ask Sae to leave, so the setting does not carry one automatic romantic meaning.

Tōji challenges Kairi's claim to “give her back” by insisting Momo is not a dog90. That objection recognizes her agency. His subsequent demand for an immediate choice115–116 puts pressure on that same agency, while his later no-guilt words161–162 relax it. The ordered contradiction is more informative than choosing one sentence to define him.

V18 begins with Momo's explicit yes to dating him. Future study and holiday plans make their reunion credible in ordinary life. He later waits through her delay and says she would not cancel without reason81. The reversal of waiting positions echoes Kairi's earlier lateness while demonstrating a real capacity to trust without an explanation arriving first.

## The language of agency changes with the recipient and pressure

安達 remains a useful direct-address diagnostic, not evidence of emotional coldness by itself. In V08 the explanation こうするしか安達を守る方法を思いつかなかった is given to Misao, who receives a reason denied to Momo. It states the limit of the methods he could imagine, rather than proving that no cooperative method existed. In V17 the combination of 安達, オレ and 岡安 places the decision among named people: 自分の気持ちを一番に考えていいんだよ explicitly releases Momo from giving him priority out of guilt. That permission matters because she has just faced a public binary choice. Its later breach under pressure makes the earlier statement more consequential, not meaningless. (PG-JP-NE-V08-W01/i0041–i0044; PG-JP-NE-V17-W01/i0101–i0117,i0161–i0164)

The language also returns attention to Momo's separate acts. A boy saying “choose” does not guarantee that the procedure allows time; her own statement does not remain binding forever. V18's early acceptance of dating and her later wish to leave are distinct current answers. Gratitude for his care is a feeling she can acknowledge without becoming an obligation to remain. Tōji can understand that principle in one conversation and fail to honor it in another. This is an evidenced contradiction across situations, rather than a hidden trait inferred from every オレ or every silence.

## The final hotel: stop, constraint, stop, release

Tōji's final comfort asks whether Momo's unhappiness would help Kairi. It does not demand that she hate him to justify choosing Tōji. Her wish to spend more time together108 is affirmative. The first hotel stop115 also matters: her wait is heard and the contact ceases.

The forged farewell creates a competing claim of urgency. His doubts are reasonable at the level of evidence: the message does not explicitly say die or establish Kairi's present location, and its pointed tone seems unlike him. Momo's fear is reasonable in another way, given her prior experience of Kairi's water distress. Neither knows Sae sent it. A disagreement about interpreting risk is not permission to block a departure.

Tōji blocks the door123–124, pulls Momo onto the bed124 and pins both wrists125. His instruction to break up before going128 articulates a concern about wavering and renewed hope inside physical constraint. The scene cannot be reduced to an ethical speech about clarity. Renewed intimacy130–132 follows without a newly depicted affirmative assent; Momo's tears and withdrawn response remain visible. No completed intercourse is established.

He then stops133, covers her with clothing and tells her to go135–136. This second stop is actual. It is not a retroactive eraser for the restraint. His fear of losing her explains why the patient pattern fails under this pressure; it does not excuse the failure. A reconstruction rule that “Tōji respects a stop” must face this scene as counterevidence, rather than excluding it because its stakes are inconvenient.

Momo explicitly says she loves Kairi143. Tōji hears the decision, urges her to catch the train144 and turns away145. Letting her leave is a different action from losing a competition by external force. Momo's gratitude146 preserves his place in her emotional history. It does not appoint her gratitude as a universal ethical verdict on every earlier act.

The affirmative developmental reading sees a movement from accepting others' representations toward acknowledging Momo's own decision. The counterreading sees protective concealment becoming physical constraint when fear intensifies. Both survive the ending. His final release is a real local achievement whose significance depends on retaining what it follows.

## Scope of the resulting argument

The densest evidence concerns Momo-directed romance, but it does not transfer unchanged to every relationship. Kairi elicits inquiry, threat, grievance, collaboration and demands that he release Momo. Sae elicits credulity, refusal of unwanted contact, monitoring, investigation and continued care after separation. Misao is a specific confidante and adult contact, not a proxy for all authority. His ability at school and work supports a limited ordinary-life portrait; family conflict, professional life after school and long-term recovery after the ending remain thin or absent. A literary account can explain these patterned contrasts while abstaining from filling the missing domains.
