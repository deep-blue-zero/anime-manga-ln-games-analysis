# Peach Girl — Choosing amid other people's stories

Peach Girl makes romantic choice depend on a prior struggle over who may explain a person's feelings and actions. Appearance, rumor, protective concealment, impersonation, private history and failed contact repeatedly supply a story about someone before that person can answer. The central relationships improve when an answer becomes receivable and a choice becomes actionable. Yet the ending does not complete that ethical movement: Sae's counterfeit farewell helps bring Momo and Kairi together. The original manga closes the romantic triangle while leaving the power to manage other people's choices uneasily intact.

This argument covers only the Japanese original mainline through V18_178. It depends on the committed G1/G2 reconciliation, nine G3 studies and the explicit G4 operational-readiness blocker. The provenance is composite: inherited Claude V01–V04 full readings with a substantive177-image recipient audit before V05, then new full Codex V05–V18 inspection. It is neither a second blind eighteen-volume reading nor a recovered Work analysis.

## Visibility creates a social problem before romance solves anything

Momo is first made socially legible through an appearance she cannot simply subtract. Her outward body becomes an alleged explanation of character. Sae's more strategically usable vulnerability can then turn partial scenes and fabricated accounts into credible social knowledge. This is why early vindication needs witnesses and exposure, not only an inwardly sincere heroine.

The appearance premise is neither a complete explanation of every later conflict nor an abandoned prologue. Its structure persists whenever a visible scene is mistaken for a full account: an image of intimacy, a retained photograph, a hospital conversation, a kiss witnessed from outside. In V18 Momo herself misreads Kairi and Sae's phone struggle76. Being injured by misreading has not made her immune to it. The later scene connects her to the series' general interpretive problem without equating her mistake with Sae's knowing fabrication.

Reputation also depends on which audience holds which account. The class can correct one allegation while other rumors circulate; Misao can know a cause Momo does not; Momo and Kairi can learn Sae's email authorship without Tōji being shown that disclosure. A full-series account that makes everyone gradually converge on one complete truth would remove the asymmetries that organize the action.

The social field extends beyond romantic peers. School authority, family comparison, part-time work, commercial contracts, transport and strangers affect credibility and opportunity. Misao's recognition of Kairi as himself has force because of his experienced comparison with Ryo. Ryo's adult/commercial position creates kinds of leverage different from a school rival's gossip. These domains keep the triangle from becoming a closed psychological mechanism.

## Repair begins locally and can fail elsewhere

Momo and Tōji's early local reconciliation in V02 is real; so are the deeper unresolved beliefs and the later breakup. The sequence therefore does not support one uninterrupted “misunderstanding” followed by one final disclosure. It repeatedly distinguishes repair of a particular event from repair of the practices that made the event possible.

The V04 reunion combines Momo's self-criticism with Tōji's acknowledged fault. Preserving both prevents the heroine's introspection from becoming a transfer of all responsibility to her. The private sign gives the couple an economical shared language, but its later sabotage exposes how fragile a symbol becomes when it substitutes for renewed discussion.

V05–V07 intensify the relation between interpretation and bodily control. Sae's plan supplies false apparent choices through incapacitation and impersonation. Tōji's search and no-fault response are concrete care. The trio's mirrored punishment then repeats control in the name of teaching the manipulator a lesson. The wrong delivery makes their inability to guarantee the performance brutally clear.

Momo's decision to stop that revenge and search for Sae is developmental because it changes an action she helped authorize. Rescue is not forgiveness, and recognition of Sae's danger is not proof that the earlier plan was harmless. This ethical structure recurs when Sae later calls for help in V13 despite having dismissed warnings. Care becomes possible without a verdict that the recipient deserves all past acts erased.

The same local-versus-durable distinction governs apology. Sae's specific apology and Jigoro meeting repair missing information. Tōji's V14 apology acknowledges his own protective harm. Kairi's regret about arrogance acknowledges a failure of expectation. None of these events guarantees that coercion, concealment or misattunement can never recur.

## The triangle changes because attachment changes

Kairi's early support does not mean he is already the perfectly available alternative to Tōji. Teasing, desire, competition and useful intervention coexist. Momo's V08 effort to distinguish comfort from love precedes V09's actual public couple and explicit love. The transition is significant precisely because their categories did not always match.

The Misao attachment then puts pressure on the difference between affection and first place. A requested kiss in V12_81 can be tender while the comparative answer83 remains painful. Kairi's refusal of casual continuation is a valid boundary. Momo's later pressure after his no must be recorded alongside her initiative; later response does not make the initial refusal meaningless.

Misao's role resists a generic rival formula. Her recognition helped Kairi experience himself outside his brother's shadow, but it was not a romantic promise. Her V14 younger-brother rejection matters because it permits a genuine change in his priorities. His later first-choice declaration is not evidence that Momo was secretly first throughout.

The V15 declaration reaches Misao, not Momo. That recipient difference turns a conventional “he finally knows whom he loves” event into an information failure. Illness, Ryo's injury, hospital accompaniment, police questioning, inaccurate estimates of waiting time, mechanical delay, storm and failed battery all participate. The missed meeting is neither pure choice nor pure accident.

Momo's finite waiting and incomplete knowledge make her departure intelligible. Her renewed attraction to Tōji also precedes the final disappointment. The inn's responsive affection and her later private doubts therefore coexist. V18_116–117 corrects the own V15 strong sexual-night inference by supplying agreed abstention until morning; the source does not require an invented completed act to make the romance actual.

V16–V17 give Kairi private Momo feeling and then direct confession, but his false explanation of tears and refusal to release her persist. Momo really chooses Tōji at V17_164/V18_6–7. The ending must not be used to turn that phase into mere error. Development includes real choices that change under later knowledge and feeling.

## Protective love can deny participation

Tōji's blackmail response and Kairi's false tear explanation offer different versions of acting for Momo while excluding her. Tōji tries to protect her from exposure by withholding the threat and performing rejection. Kairi tries not to burden her with his desire by giving a Misao-related cause. Their motives differ, but both limit her ability to interpret the relation she is in.

This is not an argument that sincerity is absent. Tōji detects deception, searches, comforts, waits and stops unwanted intimacy in several encounters. Kairi mobilizes aid, undertakes the typhoon journey and later tries to relinquish his pursuit. Their care is materially evidenced. The problem is the assumption that caring for someone permits deciding the terms of their knowledge or movement.

V17 makes the issue verbal when Kairi asks Tōji to give Momo back and Tōji objects that she is not a dog. V18 then gives Tōji his own failure: after an initial respected stop, he blocks the door, pulls her back and pins her wrists123–125. His demand for relational clarity occurs inside constraint. His later stop133 and release144 are actual changes, not a reason to omit the force.

The ending's real choice thus depends on keeping Momo's statement143 and departure distinct from either man's claim. She says she loves Kairi before the water rescue. Tōji hears it and lets her leave. The scene does not show all her affection for him evaporating; her gratitude remains. It shows that gratitude does not obligate continued romance.

G4's failed Tōji boundary rule is useful here as a limit on the analysis. Past patience did not predict every high-stakes response. Kairi's withdrawal rule and Sae's control rule also met counterexamples. These retrospective probes do not validate simulations, but their failures reinforce the literary need for context and ordered action.

## Sae's vulnerability widens the story without finishing her redemption

Sae's Ryo attachment gives her losses she cannot fully script. She can defend him, suspect exploitation and still hope to change him. A commercial signature or claimed payment does not erase her actual refusal of filming or help call. Ryo's shock-treatment explanation remains his justification, rather than a safe therapeutic mechanism certified by the plot.

The imagined-family arc further complicates a single fraud model. The pregnancy claim is initially unconfirmed; Sae then misreads a partial clinical explanation as miscarriage before receiving the nonpregnancy correction. Her acknowledgment to Momo/Kairi/Tōji in V17_152 supports mistaken belief and grief, not proven deliberate fabrication.

Ryo's coercive reproductive pressure and hired assault are actual. His later cancellation, emergency care and father self-identification are also actual. Sae's shielding is costly. V18's parenting wish gives him a possible desire, not a child or completed reform. Misao's care for him does not reverse her explicit goodbye; the final volume reconfirms that distinction.

Sae's final-volume lightness after loss affects Momo because it questions whether being hurt must mean being permanently unhappy. Her true journey report and comfort of Kairi are substantive help. Yet the counterfeit farewell repeats her power to create a decisive apparent act by someone else. Neither “unchanged villain” nor “redeemed friend” can contain the whole sequence.

## Form converges more fully than accountability

Objects and settings gather the long series without supplying one moral verdict. The sign condenses a shared past; photographs expose retained feeling; the portrait makes a false explanation harder to sustain. The quince blossom must remain quince, rather than becoming a convenient peach symbol because of the title. Source identity precedes thematic neatness.

Hands also recur across incompatible actions: catching danger, stopping a weapon, refusing release, pinning, rescuing and finally holding voluntarily. The final handhold gains significance through those differences. Calling every embrace care or every physical intervention domination would flatten the visual sequence.

The two V17 gondola encounters give one enclosing structure to a breakup and an attempted renewal. The sea connects earlier rescue, grief, relinquishment and reunion. But thematic association is not causal identity. Kairi's metaphorical funeral, Sae's forged message and the actual retrieval accident remain separate.

The final phone and case demonstrate this distinction with unusual precision. Sae borrows the phone and later admits sending the message. Kairi cannot discard Momo's case, loses it, finds a surfer wearing it and enters the water after it is thrown. Waves and a leg cramp create real danger. Momo's rescue breaths and his revival are depicted, unlike V12's conscious return without CPR.

Crosscutting and delayed explanation let the reader connect more than a character can know. They also create mistaken analyst inferences if adjacency is treated as shared location or a vivid memory as omniscient replay. The V15 night correction and V17 pregnancy correction demonstrate why later disclosure must revise current interpretation while preserving earlier freezes.

## What the ending resolves

The affirmative reading has strong evidence. Momo articulates her choice before the rescue, acts on it, rescues Kairi and initiates the final kiss. Kairi reciprocates. The case and sea bring material and formal histories into a recognisable reunion. Tōji's release acknowledges a decision he cannot make for her. Sae remains in their social world.

The ethical counterreading also has strong evidence. A forged self-destruction threat supplies urgency and receives comic treatment at the reveal. Past coercion does not receive comprehensive restitution. Spectacular danger can seem to solve a trust problem whose ordinary repair remains unshown. The original series does not depict a future proving otherwise.

These readings conflict only if romantic resolution is required to certify every means that produced it. The manga gives an actual couple and a sea vow, not marriage, universal health or complete accountability. Sae's proximity is not a three-person romance. Tōji's grief and Momo's gratitude are not erased by his absence from the final pair.

The ending therefore supports a bounded convergence: Momo and Kairi finally share a romantic category and express it to each other. It leaves the broader ethical question of managing another person's choices less fully resolved. That difference explains both the satisfaction of the final handhold and the discomfort of the counterfeit farewell.
