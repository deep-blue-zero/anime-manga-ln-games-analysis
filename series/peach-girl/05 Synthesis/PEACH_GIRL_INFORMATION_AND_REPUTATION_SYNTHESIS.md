# Information and reputation — A message has a sender, a recipient and a time

The series' recurrent misunderstanding is not one mechanism. It includes fabricated content, impersonated authorship, selective truth, unreceived messages, technological failure, private inference and accurate words that arrive too late to repair their consequences. Distinguishing these mechanisms explains repetition without dismissing every conflict as characters simply failing to talk.

This specialist uses G2 commit342b9067abf2b200613e2b24707abbc190c59402, CL0020@13 and the full IP/KN/TX/RP histories. “True” describes a bounded proposition, not the credibility of a person in every situation. “Received” is not equivalent to believed, and either can occur without the analyst endorsing the content.

## From appearance to socially usable claims

Momo's appearance becomes a substrate for allegations. Sae's version often travels with an audience-ready performance, whereas Momo's anger can be read through the very stereotype she contests. The early witness correction changes credibility by changing the available evidence and its social circulation. It does not prove the earlier audience was incapable of recognizing any truth or that a single correction cleans every channel.

The audit keeps old anonymous authorship unresolved when the source does not show it. Similar timing or method can support suspicion; it cannot create an admission. Likewise, a sexualized slur is evidence of reputational aggression, not a compact biography. The information ledger's revision of IP0018 prevents a rumor from becoming analytical fact by repetition.

Reputation also requires an audience. A statement heard by Momo and Kairi is not schoolwide knowledge. A fictional magazine's existence establishes publication in the story, but not every circumstance of production, nationwide reception or an accepted sexual contract. Sae's later pregnancy correction reaches the named hospital audience; Misao's receipt remains unshown.

## Impersonation changes who appears to have chosen

Sae's V05 impersonation gives Tōji an apparent Momo choice. Ryo's V10 phone use gives Momo an apparent Kairi act. Sae's V18 email gives Momo an apparent Kairi farewell. In each case, channel identity becomes a substitute for demonstrated authorship.

The reader does not always learn the deception at the same instant as a character. V18_110 shows Sae borrowing Kairi's phone before the alarming message120, while Kairi has not been shown composing it. Authorship is therefore open at120, not established as Kairi and not yet proven Sae. Her admission176 resolves it for Momo, Kairi and the reader. Tōji is not shown receiving that resolution.

Content and author must remain separately revisable. The message's emotional resemblance to Kairi's despair does not prove he wrote it. His metaphorical funeral94–95 does not make the counterfeit literal. The actual near-drowning later has a depicted retrieval cause. Shared words of death and sea invite connection, but cannot erase different authors and intentions.

## Concealment can use truth as well as falsehood

Tōji's blackmail concealment withholds a true threat for Momo's supposed protection. Kairi's V17 Misao explanation substitutes a false cause for real tears. Both can be partly motivated by a desire not to burden Momo; both reduce her ability to interpret her own relation.

A useful causal distinction is between the existence of an event, its meaning to an actor and its explanation to another person. Kairi's V15 first-choice declaration exists and matters to him. Misao hears it. Momo does not. The physical effort to reach Shiranami cannot by itself deliver the missing words. V18 finally supplies a report of the declaration63, after earlier choices have already occurred.

The V17 split is especially explicit: Misao hears the true doorstep motive54–57; Momo hears the false Misao-related cause32/77. Later, Momo hears present love82 and the Misao/Momo distinction92. These disclosures change her current knowledge without making the earlier lie disappear. A chronology that records only “Kairi confesses” would lose the actual problem.

## Failure of contact is not always failure of will

Phones fail in different ways. V12's sent screen does not prove Momo opened the message. V15's inn battery failure prevents identity and transfer; V18's coastal call passes the location before the battery fails20–21. The later V18 delay involves an out-of-service connection71, while Sae borrows Kairi's phone because her own battery is dead110.

These differences matter causally. A complete-failure summary of the coastal call cannot explain how Tōji finds Momo. An automatic-avoidance account of every missed connection ignores depicted infrastructure. Conversely, technological obstacles do not excuse the choices to omit a cause, make an estimate about someone else's waiting or maintain a false explanation.

Repetition distributes responsibility across action and contingency. Momo turns off her phone at one point; at another she tries to call and cannot connect. Kairi tries to reach her but also withholds decisive content when contact succeeds. The right question is what this particular channel carries, not whether a character is generically communicative.

## Inference, memory and the reader

Momo's V18 view of the Kairi–Sae phone contest76 produces a mistaken intimacy inference without a fabricated statement. Her emotional “he knew everything” reaction51 likewise exceeds what the source certifies. These are focal interpretations, not neutral summaries.

Memory overlays can misattribute speech if read only by the visible figure. The own-heart counsel at81–82 originates with Misao65, recalled over Tōji's image. A source-grounded account must track balloon/caption relation and disclosure sequence, not assign words to the largest face.

Nested history requires the same restraint. Kairi tells Momo some past events he witnessed and others Ryo reported to him. The manga's visualization makes the account vivid; vividness does not remove its framing. Old rescue attribution can strengthen without resolving every resuscitation step or every participant.

## What the failed forecasts reveal

PR0019/0020/0022 fail on recipient, channel and completeness, despite related truths becoming visible. PR0019 requires Kairi's first-choice words to reach Momo in V15; Misao's receipt does not count. PR0020 requires Momo's direct words in V16; Kairi's witnessed kiss does not count. PR0022 requires both injury and police cause; the final partial relay does not count.

These failures are not evidence that the manga lacks communication. They show why an analysis must specify what communication would resolve which question. Nor do supported tests certify reliable prediction: the run is non-blind and the tests vary. The record's value is inspectable correction, rather than a score detached from disclosure conditions.

The series resolves the central couple more fully than it resolves every explanation. Momo need not possess the reader's complete causal history to make a real final choice. That asymmetry is an ending limit, not a license to silently give her missing knowledge.
