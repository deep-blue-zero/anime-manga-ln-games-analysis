# Manga form and Japanese expression — What the reader is allowed to connect

Form in Peach Girl does more than illustrate already settled motives. Cropped access, visual recurrence, memory, crosscutting and material objects determine what can be inferred and when an inference becomes insecure. The Japanese source also distinguishes speaker, register, literal action and metaphor in ways a plot summary can flatten.

This study uses G2 commit342b9067abf2b200613e2b24707abbc190c59402 and the18 evidence registers. It concerns this fixed-layout Japanese witness set, read in OPF spine order with right-to-left page relations. It does not assume page equivalence to another edition, invented chapter headings or an acoustic voice from unvoiced manga.

## A page with a logo can still be a scene

The inspection classified images by actual function. Covers, posed illustrations, advertisements and colophons were viewed but excluded from mainline counts. Title placement alone did not justify exclusion: V17_44/83–84 and V18_43–44/46/85/125/127 contain consequential narrative or reprise. Removing them as decorative would delete threat, refusal, reaction or continuation.

Four recurring physical spans in a volume are not verified named chapters. The navigation often offers only cover/main. Spine ordinals therefore remain the stable locators, with printed-page references used where verified. Staff dates, serialization notes and digital edition dates are separate from represented story time. The email's9/13 display is a local datum, not a complete chronology.

These distinctions are analytical. A sequential account must know whether the reader is seeing a new event, a reprise, an image of a possible future or paratext. V10's boat fantasy is not the actual party venue. V18's parenting images are not an actual child. Posed page breaks cannot be used as new romantic encounters.

## Frames can present evidence and its failure together

The V02 hospital sequence tests visual grammar, not simply a character's trust. Sae sees the arriving pair before the clothes, fall and leaning sequence. The present lips remain apart when the door opens; Tōji's remembered earlier practice kiss with Sae is an inset with different clothes, not completed present contact with Sae. The arriving pair see the supported bodily tableau rather than its preparation. Her later almost-display aside is further evidence against treating the attempted exhibition as an accomplished kiss. A frozen sentence overreporting completion can therefore reproduce the very confusion the panel sequence distributes among characters (V02 i0095–0105,0132; PG-SA2-F0001; PG-SA2-V02-0001).

The stair scene uses a related separation on a smaller scale. Momo's help and ensuing refusal are visible before the onlookers react. Whether they hear the refusal remains unknown. The picture can travel as proof of two-timing even though the reader has seen how the hold began and continued. The ethical and epistemic questions depend on panel order and speech reception, not on whether a single image looks intimate (V02 i0069–0073; PG-SA2-V02-0002).

## Objects carry feeling without supplying every explanation

The private L gesture condenses a shared history. Sae can copy its shape with Jigorō without acquiring that history, while its return in V15 renews felt attachment. The sabotaged foothills arrow in V08 is a different sign: it changes a route rather than a love code. Both compare a visible sign with the meaning or action inferred from it, without becoming the same physical object (V04 i0104–0128; V05 i0031–0039; V08 i0114–0115; V15 i0163–0166).

Photographs and portraits work differently. Misao's old image can disclose Kairi's attachment but not, by itself, its present reciprocity. The Momo portrait exposes a contradiction between Kairi's account and retained affection. Neither object can state the complete reason for a missed meeting or a stopped encounter. A visual clue is not a transcript.

The final handmade phone case carries a gift's particularity. Kairi can decide to bury feeling and still fail to throw the case100. The phone is another object, borrowed by Sae110. Confusing them would make him discard the means of the forged message or author an act the panels do not show. The case's movement—kept, lost, worn by a stranger, thrown, retrieved—creates the actual path to the rescue.

The V09 craft-shop interval connects labor and misreading. The storefront and yarn identify a continuation of Momo's secret phone-case work; the dropped and still-held skein link shopping to her distressed observation of Kairi and Misao. Calling it a bakery cuts that material continuity. The objects give the reader simultaneous access to desired care and its interruption without making Kairi aware of her presence. Later, the rejected commercial case and the explanation of the handmade one show that the same object can carry different meanings before and after its contents are learned (V09 i0096–0101; V10 i0007–0012; PG-SA2-F0009).

The quince blossom in V08/V11 is not a peach blossom. A title-induced expectation would produce a neat symbolic reading from the wrong plant. Source-checked material identity must precede thematic interpretation.

## Hands and repeated settings are not single moral symbols

Hands catch traffic danger, stop a hammer, pull someone from water, refuse release, pin wrists, initiate affection and finally join voluntarily. Repetition invites comparison; it does not make all holding protective or all contact coercive. V18's pin125 and final handhold178 have different staging and permissions. Their shared visual vocabulary makes the difference more important.

The gondola similarly encloses two separate V17 encounters. Ryo asks Sae to find another man; Tōji proposes replacing Momo's painful memory. The structure can hold departure and attempted renewal without giving either a fixed moral status. A setting's recurrence produces a comparison, not a verdict.

The sea gathers old rescue, ambiguous death language, lost attachment and final reunion. Its formal convergence is strong. Yet V18 distinguishes a metaphorical funeral95, forged message120 and accidental retrieval danger163. Reading all three as one death wish mistakes thematic connection for causal identity.

## Bodies also act, learn and make

The conspicuous body is not only a surface misread by other people. Momo's swimming competence lets her act where Sae's imposed appearance story fails; practical escape and rescue later require movement, resistance and judgment. Her hands also make the case whose ordinary effort becomes central to the ending. The form repeatedly turns a body treated as incriminating evidence into an agent with a task. This is a supported comparison, not a claim that every sports panel secretly forecasts a rescue (V03 swim meet; V09 i0041–0060,0096–0101; V11 self-escape i0011–0020; V18 i0162–0178).

Comic distortion changes how an act is presented without deciding whether it occurred or was wanted. Cheek stretching and punches can be drawn with abrupt caricature; a chase can make fear and attraction alternate rapidly; a vase can accompany a palm slap and splash without being the striking object. The source audit must first recover the actor, implement, response and consequence. Literary evaluation can then ask how comic timing invites sympathy, minimizes duration or makes retaliation seem ordinary. Neither a realist injury invented from a comic mark nor a claim of harmlessness inferred from comedy follows automatically (V02 i0134–0135; V07 i0043–0045; PG-SA2-F0007).

## Crosscutting and withheld complements

V17 crosscuts a classroom choice with the park violence. Adjacency does not put Momo's trio at the assault. The hospital initially withholds the object of the doctor's explanation, allowing Sae's apparent-loss interpretation before the no-pregnancy correction. Recording miscarriage as established would adopt an inference the sequence is constructed to revise.

V15 alternates Momo's finite wait with Kairi's emergency and delay. The reader sees more cause than she receives. The editing can produce frustration on Kairi's behalf while leaving her departure intelligible under her actual knowledge. Retrospective omniscience would erase precisely the asymmetry the form sustains.

V18 gives the reader Sae's phone custody before the email, without showing composition. The proper interim status is open authorship, not immediate certainty. The final admission is still a disclosure. Similarly, a memory can be laid over another character's image: Misao's counsel65 returns over Tōji81–82. Speech attribution requires following the representational frame, not counting visible faces.

## Japanese wording and focal limits

The early 外見/見た目 recurrence concerns outward appearance but is not an identical quotation. In V12, 安芸操 supplies Misao's surname; the separate アキ report remains a distinct referent. Orthographic resemblance cannot complete a family genealogy.

V18's けじめ in the inn flashback116 marks a need to settle the unresolved relation before continuing. Tōji's agreement to do nothing until morning117 directly narrows the earlier sexual-night inference. The correction depends on actual wording and temporal scope: “until morning” does not certify every later unshown event.

Sae's 想像妊娠 acknowledgment in V17_152 is the explicit fictional correction she communicates. Its role in the scene does not validate a medical mechanism externally or prove deliberate invention. Sae's phone advice 身体は正直 in V18_i0140 interprets Momo's response; Momo's subsequent private caption accepts Sae's point that remaining there changes nothing. The original speaker and the person adopting advice are different. The shown call does not establish that Sae knows the hotel restraint in detail. Neither her proposition nor Momo's uptake supplies a universal scientific or ethical law.

Kairi's “funeral” is qualified by his explanation that he is burying feeling, not literally going to die. Momo's 大好き declaration174 makes final affection explicit; the action and recipient support its romantic reading. A lexical intensity scale detached from context would add less than the sequence already shows.

Written shifts between joking, indirectness, direct refusal, apology and private caption reveal situational speech practices. They cannot reconstruct acoustic tone, accent or a complete conversational generator. Uncertain crowded-page speakers remain uncertain. The early audit's refusal to assign a V01 line categorically to Kairi is part of formal accuracy, not a trivial annotation.

## Written utterances do different interpersonal work

Address is relational evidence, with limits. Early Sae's 東寺ヶ森くん and Momo's とーじ mark the women’s different histories of access to Tōji, rather than a register change performed by him. Kairi’s familiar ももちゃん can accompany unwanted holding in V02 and an accepted refusal in V11; it cannot certify an ethical stance by itself. Momo’s use of 岡安 when specifying the V14 appointment also survives well beyond first attraction. A dossier needs actual occurrences and recipients, rather than an imagined permanent switch from surname to intimate name (V01 i0013–0017; V02 i0071–0072; V11 i0051; V14 i0076).

Questions and ellipses make withheld explanation visible. After Kairi stops the V11 bedroom encounter, his ごめん and やっぱだめだ at i0127 express apology and inability. Momo asks whether she offended him; his reply that it is not her fault does not give the missing cause. In V12 his account of suddenly losing confidence and being unable to discard another feeling supplies more content without a scientific diagnosis or a declaration that Momo has always ranked first. These are different speech acts at different frontiers, not proof that either scene has disclosed everything (V11 i0127–0128; V12 i0047–0048; original PR0016 remains bounded).

The V15 call distributes speech and thought even more narrowly. Momo speaks the fragment それって… at i0071; the full question about whether the errand must happen now is presented unballooned beside her at i0072. Kairi queries the pause. Her private formulation is available to the reader without a demonstrated full verbal challenge reaching him. This is why the source correction matters to literary form: punctuation, balloon shape and panel placement delimit who can answer which question (PG-SA2-F0012; V15 i0071–0073).

Tōji’s competing explanations depend on recipient as well as sincerity. V07 breakup words reach Momo while Sae listens by phone. In V08 he tells Misao that he could think of no other way to protect Adachi. That subjective construction records the limit of his considered alternatives, not the narrator’s proof that no alternative existed. His later admission of insecurity and promise to wait are meaningful disclosures; the V18 interruption and constraint still have to be checked in action (V07 i0132–0138; V08 i0041–0044; V16 i0101–0106; V18 i0123–0144).

Sae’s changes of address and manner have different purposes. Polite uncle-address to the man assaulting her in V07 is an effort to obtain release, not willing flirtation. The teacher-report threat in V03 is conditional silencing; its broad 乱暴 wording does not supply an omitted specifically sexual allegation. The V13 apology that becomes a sales pitch, halting thanks after rescue, and her later acknowledgment of having hurt Momo and bowed apology also require different interpretations. A single label such as soft speech or manipulation would lose both tactical performance and actual gratitude (V03 i0108; V07 i0012–0020; V13 i0015–0019,0122,0139–0146).

These written patterns support characterization through forms of asking, refusing, explaining, performing and withholding. They establish neither acoustic tone nor a general-purpose conversational voice. Even exact quotation needs its local speaker, recipient and position in the evolving relationship.

The strongest convergent reading sees recurring objects and settings gathering the triangle into a final pair. The strongest critical reading observes how ellipsis and comedy can soften the experience of force or deception. Form supports both: it produces emotional satisfaction while leaving acts available for scrutiny. An analysis of the ending should explain that coexistence rather than treating formal closure as ethical proof.
