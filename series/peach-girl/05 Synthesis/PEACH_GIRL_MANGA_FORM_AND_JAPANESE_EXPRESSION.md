# Manga form and Japanese expression — What the reader is allowed to connect

Form in Peach Girl does more than illustrate already settled motives. Cropped access, visual recurrence, memory, crosscutting and material objects determine what can be inferred and when an inference becomes insecure. The Japanese source also distinguishes speaker, register, literal action and metaphor in ways a plot summary can flatten.

This study uses G2 commit342b9067abf2b200613e2b24707abbc190c59402 and the18 evidence registers. It concerns this fixed-layout Japanese witness set, read in OPF spine order with right-to-left page relations. It does not assume page equivalence to another edition, invented chapter headings or an acoustic voice from unvoiced manga.

## A page with a logo can still be a scene

The inspection classified images by actual function. Covers, posed illustrations, advertisements and colophons were viewed but excluded from mainline counts. Title placement alone did not justify exclusion: V17_44/83–84 and V18_43–44/46/85/125/127 contain consequential narrative or reprise. Removing them as decorative would delete threat, refusal, reaction or continuation.

Four recurring physical spans in a volume are not verified named chapters. The navigation often offers only cover/main. Spine ordinals therefore remain the stable locators, with printed-page references used where verified. Staff dates, serialization notes and digital edition dates are separate from represented story time. The email's9/13 display is a local datum, not a complete chronology.

These distinctions are analytical. A sequential account must know whether the reader is seeing a new event, a reprise, an image of a possible future or paratext. V10's boat fantasy is not the actual party venue. V18's parenting images are not an actual child. Posed page breaks cannot be used as new romantic encounters.

## Objects carry feeling without supplying every explanation

The private sign condenses Momo and Tōji's shared history. Its later sabotage and reappearance show both the promise and fragility of economical communication. Recognition can restore an earlier feeling without proving that all intervening uncertainty has been resolved.

Photographs and portraits work differently. Misao's old image can disclose Kairi's attachment but not, by itself, its present reciprocity. The Momo portrait exposes a contradiction between Kairi's account and retained affection. Neither object can state the complete reason for a missed meeting or a stopped encounter. A visual clue is not a transcript.

The final handmade phone case carries a gift's particularity. Kairi can decide to bury feeling and still fail to throw the case100. The phone is another object, borrowed by Sae110. Confusing them would make him discard the means of the forged message or author an act the panels do not show. The case's movement—kept, lost, worn by a stranger, thrown, retrieved—creates the actual path to the rescue.

The quince blossom in V08/V11 is not a peach blossom. A title-induced expectation would produce a neat symbolic reading from the wrong plant. Source-checked material identity must precede thematic interpretation.

## Hands and repeated settings are not single moral symbols

Hands catch traffic danger, stop a hammer, pull someone from water, refuse release, pin wrists, initiate affection and finally join voluntarily. Repetition invites comparison; it does not make all holding protective or all contact coercive. V18's pin125 and final handhold178 have different staging and permissions. Their shared visual vocabulary makes the difference more important.

The gondola similarly encloses two separate V17 encounters. Ryo asks Sae to find another man; Tōji proposes replacing Momo's painful memory. The structure can hold departure and attempted renewal without giving either a fixed moral status. A setting's recurrence produces a comparison, not a verdict.

The sea gathers old rescue, ambiguous death language, lost attachment and final reunion. Its formal convergence is strong. Yet V18 distinguishes a metaphorical funeral95, forged message120 and accidental retrieval danger163. Reading all three as one death wish mistakes thematic connection for causal identity.

## Crosscutting and withheld complements

V17 crosscuts a classroom choice with the park violence. Adjacency does not put Momo's trio at the assault. The hospital initially withholds the object of the doctor's explanation, allowing Sae's apparent-loss interpretation before the no-pregnancy correction. Recording miscarriage as established would adopt an inference the sequence is constructed to revise.

V15 alternates Momo's finite wait with Kairi's emergency and delay. The reader sees more cause than she receives. The editing can produce frustration on Kairi's behalf while leaving her departure intelligible under her actual knowledge. Retrospective omniscience would erase precisely the asymmetry the form sustains.

V18 gives the reader Sae's phone custody before the email, without showing composition. The proper interim status is open authorship, not immediate certainty. The final admission is still a disclosure. Similarly, a memory can be laid over another character's image: Misao's counsel65 returns over Tōji81–82. Speech attribution requires following the representational frame, not counting visible faces.

## Japanese wording and focal limits

The early 外見/見た目 recurrence concerns outward appearance but is not an identical quotation. In V12, 安芸操 supplies Misao's surname; the separate アキ report remains a distinct referent. Orthographic resemblance cannot complete a family genealogy.

V18's けじめ in the inn flashback116 marks a need to settle the unresolved relation before continuing. Tōji's agreement to do nothing until morning117 directly narrows the earlier sexual-night inference. The correction depends on actual wording and temporal scope: “until morning” does not certify every later unshown event.

Sae's 想像妊娠 acknowledgment in V17_152 is the explicit fictional correction she communicates. Its role in the scene does not validate a medical mechanism externally or prove deliberate invention. Momo's 身体は正直 formulation in V18_140 is her interpretation of her own response; it is not a universal scientific or ethical law supplied by the work.

Kairi's “funeral” is qualified by his explanation that he is burying feeling, not literally going to die. Momo's 大好き declaration174 makes final affection explicit; the action and recipient support its romantic reading. A lexical intensity scale detached from context would add less than the sequence already shows.

Written shifts between joking, indirectness, direct refusal, apology and private caption reveal situational speech practices. They cannot reconstruct acoustic tone, accent or a complete conversational generator. Uncertain crowded-page speakers remain uncertain. The early audit's refusal to assign a V01 line categorically to Kairi is part of formal accuracy, not a trivial annotation.

The strongest convergent reading sees recurring objects and settings gathering the triangle into a final pair. The strongest critical reading observes how ellipsis and comedy can soften the experience of force or deception. Form supports both: it produces emotional satisfaction while leaving acts available for scrutiny. An analysis of the ending should explain that coexistence rather than treating formal closure as ethical proof.
