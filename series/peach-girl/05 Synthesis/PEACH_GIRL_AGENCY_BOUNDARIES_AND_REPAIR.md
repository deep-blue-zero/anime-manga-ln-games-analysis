# Agency, bodily boundaries and repair — The sequence matters

Peach Girl repeatedly places care, desire and coercion in the same relationships. A source-faithful account must therefore identify an act and its timing before drawing a general moral character. Sincerity does not authorize force, later affection does not retroactively supply permission, and being harmed does not remove a person's capacity to harm someone else.

This specialist uses the G2 snapshot342b9067abf2b200613e2b24707abbc190c59402 and CL0017@15, with the character and commitment histories. The analysis is non-graphic and concerns depicted action, stated preference, power and narrative consequence. It makes no legal judgment or clinical diagnosis.

## Age and unequal positions

The early cast are school adolescents; V05 explicitly concerns Momo's sixteenth birthday. Later examination references do not justify inventing exact birthdays or a fully dated calendar. Misao's school-nurse/former-tutor role and Ryo's older-brother/commercial position differ from peer romance. These contextual differences affect access, credibility, dependency and practical exit options.

Neither adolescent desire nor adult authority should be erased. Momo can want intimacy and still refuse a particular encounter. Sae can state love for Ryo and still refuse filming. An adult's claim to help, mentor or cure does not settle the young person's assent. The source supplies enough to describe those power differences without supplying a jurisdiction-specific rule or an exhaustive sexual biography.

## Assent is local to an action

Early conduct supplies an important control on any claim that boundary violations begin only with late desperation. The V01 rumor kiss is Kairi’s initiative; Momo’s objection remains valid regardless of his stated interest, the old disputed rescue history or her later attraction. Existing corrective versions preserve the difference between the source’s first encounter, the original reading’s account and Momo’s V05 self-description. In V02 help after an accidental tumble is followed by a fresh question: will he let her go when she asks? His request for another minute refuses immediate release. This is an act-specific failure, not evidence that every earlier touch was forced or every later helpful act insincere (V01 i0043–0067; V02 i0069–0073; PG-CM-0008@2 and later source qualification; PG-SA2-V02-0002; PG-CM-0193).

The V03 changing-room embrace repeats a consequential distinction: his proposal receives a no, but he continues approaching, grasps Momo's wrist and embraces her anyway. Later comfort and attraction do not turn the earlier refusal into agreement. The existing A02/CM0016 qualification already preserves this sequence; a reconstruction must retrieve it rather than use the mere existence of a request as proof of restraint. A genuine later counterexample is V11 i0051: Kairi accepts Momo’s initial refusal of a proposed company visit without protest; her subsequent changed decision has its own motive and occasion. The source separates that respected no from both the V03 imposed embrace and the V11 bedroom stop initiated by Kairi himself (PG-EV-V11-0012; PG-CM-0091). Tōji’s early unwanted approach also needs its own causal account: misinformation and jealousy help explain it without making Momo responsible for managing his force. A valid refusal does not depend on proving that the other person has a wholly malicious personality (V01 i0171–0178; V02 i0007–0013; V03 i0089–0106).


V11 gives a clear example of willingly begun contact followed by Kairi stopping. His refusal is valid even though Momo is willing and hurt. The next problem is communication, not a requirement that he continue. In V12 Momo's collar grab and kiss after his no remain pressure; his later response is a later event.

The same distinction applies to Tōji. V16's stop and apology demonstrate an actual respected boundary. V18's first hotel stop115 does too. The later block/pull/pin123–125 and renewed intimacy130–132 occur after Momo's request to leave, without a newly depicted affirmative assent. Tōji then notices her tears and stops133. A summary retaining only the first or second stop sanitizes the intervening force; a summary suppressing the stops loses actual changes in conduct.

No completed intercourse is established in that sequence. The V18 flashback also corrects the own V15 strong sexual-night inference: Tōji agreed to do nothing until morning117. Actual morning affection remains. Neither romantic resumption nor a shared room can fill an unshown event.

## Consent to a relation is not ownership of a person

Kairi's V17 refusal to release Momo after she asks and his V18 forced taxi make romantic persistence physically consequential. Her final Kairi choice does not change the earlier refusals. Tōji's “not a dog” objection challenges the idea that she can be handed back between men; his own later constraint shows why the principle must be evaluated in conduct as well as speech.

Momo also revises the terms she thought she could accept. Her earlier second-place or casual-companionship offer does not become a permanent waiver of exclusive care. V18_18 says she was not inwardly at peace with it. Kairi's refusal of casual continuation in V12 remains valid; her later self-correction remains valid too. A past proposition cannot settle all future wants.

## Intervention can protect and violate

Momo’s own bodily action changes meaning with the problem she is addressing. Competitive swimming displays competence; pulling a person from danger protects life; the force used in jealousy or punishment can harm someone else. During the V02 journey she drives and pulls Kairi onward while imagining Sae with Tōji. Binding his wrists explicitly aims to prevent inappropriate touch. His fear and request to sit remain expressions of his agency. He points out that walking inside the train cannot hasten its arrival; she acknowledges it but says she cannot sit still while imagining Sae and Tōji together. The scene combines an intelligible protection purpose with anxious control. It does not establish a general right to restrain, nor does comic exaggeration make its different motives irrelevant (V02 i0086–0091; PG-SA2-V02-0003; PG-CM-0194).

Kairi’s physical punishment of Sae in V02 also matters to later narration. In V03 she threatens to tell a teacher he treated her violently if he exposes her, and warns that he will not be able to remain at school. That conditional use is coercive. The wording 乱暴された does not by itself fix a specifically sexual allegation; nor does the prior physical aggression permit calling every possible account wholly invented. Analytical precision does not require excusing either the aggression or the retaliatory threat. It requires refusing an unsupported escalation of the threatened content (V02 i0134–0135,0145–0146; V03 i0108; PG-SA2-F0010).


The V06 revenge plot uses incapacitation and control to stage suffering for Sae. Its planners' intended simulation does not make the actual physical acts fictitious. Delivery error demonstrates their limited control, while Momo's V07 reversal and the rescue show that participants can recognize a need to change course.

Rescue itself needs precise attribution. Momo self-escapes Ryo's conference-room encounter in V11; Kairi's late arrival is care without the direct interruption predicted. V12's water retrieval ends with Kairi conscious, without CPR. V18 depicts actual rescue breaths and revival. A recurring rescue motif does not entitle the analyst to merge procedures or assign the most familiar helper.

Sae's V13 help call after an actual filming refusal demonstrates agency under constraint. An earlier signature or payment assertion cannot be used analytically to overwrite the present refusal. Ryo's therapeutic justification is his claim; the rescue group does not certify it by arriving.

V17 further separates stopped assault, forceful intervention, hired violence, cancellation and shielding. Kairi stops Sae's hammer wrist before impact. Misao pours water on Sae. Ryo hires assailants, then actually cancels; the cancellation matters but does not undo commissioning the attack. Sae shields him and is struck. Neither her earlier violence nor his selective care makes that injury deserved.

## Harm, pregnancy belief and self-blame

Momo's belief about the missing hotel interval affects later shame and interpretation, even before the act question is resolved. Jigoro's V13 account revises that question; it does not remove drugging, removal, fear or the delayed explanation. The analysis should not reproduce the idea that only completed intercourse would make the experience harmful.

Sae's pregnancy sequence also requires temporal precision. An unconfirmed claim, apparent-loss interpretation and explicit nonpregnancy acknowledgment are different states. Her prior deceptions support suspicion, not proof of intentional fabrication. Fictional medical assertions about drugs or diagnosis remain attributed; the analysis does not validate them as advice.

Self-blame appears in several relationships. Momo's apologies do not allocate all fault to her. Misao's “heart glasses” criticism does not absolve Ryo. Kairi's regret about arrogance does not explain away every external obstacle. Preserving self-interpretation without adopting it wholesale prevents emotional intensity from substituting for causal judgment.

## What repair would have to repair

Care can restore practical choice without repairing every relation around it. Misao’s V06 intervention finds the room, stops continuing punishment, attends to the harmed Momo and also treats Jigoro. Treating an offender’s injury does not certify innocence. Giving Momo options about what to do next differs from recruiting her into revenge. Her asserted parental contact at reception remains unverified, rather than a proven falsehood; the later overnight notice cannot establish that no earlier call occurred. This combination of practical help, institutional access and evidential limits makes her a more useful comparison for repair than an abstract perfectly ethical rescuer (V06 i0035–0062; PG-EV-V06-0014/0019/0022; PG-SA2-F0005).

The V07 rescue similarly changes the immediate objective rather than settling the preceding wrong. Momo moves from the punishment group to forced entry because Sae faces a threat they did not control. Sae slaps Momo afterward and condemns their conduct. Momo’s intervention is still an intervention; Sae’s anger does not authorize a new blow, and gratitude cannot be demanded as payment for safety. This is an especially clear instance of restoring a person’s exit without acquiring authority over her future response (V07 i0035–0045; PG-SA2-F0007).


Repair has at least several separable objects: acknowledging an act, correcting a false account, restoring choice, changing conduct and addressing continuing consequences. The series often completes one while leaving another open. Sae's Jigoro meeting supplies missing information; it does not settle all prior harm. Tōji's V14 apology names fault; his later restraint remains counterevidence to durable change. Kairi's present-love confession supplies truth while unwanted holding continues.

The ending's release, rescue and reciprocal choice are real changes. They do not show comprehensive restitution, future trust or an end to manipulation. Sae's forged farewell becomes comic couple-making; its success does not itself repair the use of an impersonated self-destruction threat.

An affirmative reading sees people learning to stop, disclose, rescue and choose. A critical reading sees crises repeatedly replacing sustained accountability. The source does not force a choice between those readings. The most accurate account asks, at each encounter, which action was permitted, which harm occurred, which repair was performed and what remains unshown.
