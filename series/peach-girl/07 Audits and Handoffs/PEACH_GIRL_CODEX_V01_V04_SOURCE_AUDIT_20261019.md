## Overall narrative and character assessment

Claude's readings are more substantial than plot lists. They distinguish reader-only access from character belief, treat illustrated falsehoods and plans as assertions/imaginings, discuss recurring locations, visual scale, chibi comedy and withheld interiority, and preserve counterreadings. For example, the V02 hospital demand is to be heard, not an automatic demand for romance; wind causes the actual fall, and Tōji saves Momo. The drawn danger, her coercive escalation and the comic register coexist. At i0125–0135 Sae describes a coercive future scheme; it does not become an accomplished act. Kairi's feigned enthusiasm culminates in categorical refusal. These distinctions survive direct review and should be retained. Any suggestion that the proposed alliance would inevitably 'deliver' Momo must remain the schemers' intended opportunity, not a guarantee overriding her future agency.

The V03 Kyō-chin admission supplies a specific refutation of the old tan report; her replacement report about Tōji's preferences remains attributed testimony. Sae's threat and lion/deer metaphor are accessible to Tōji through overhearing, without automatically giving Momo the same knowledge. The final refusal is allowed to frustrate a rescue/reunion payoff: Momo wants to restart but refuses. Directly checked i0170–0174 support the inherited distinction between wanting, forgiving, and agreeing. No reason from later V04 is backdated as something Tōji already knew.

The chief compression risk is character category replacing conflicting action and feeling. Momo's gratitude to Kairi includes self-reported romantic fluttering and a romantic counterfactual; uncertainty about love or a chosen relationship cannot justify calling it merely friendship. Kairi can help her reunite with Tōji, sustain desire, deflect hurt through humor and still cross an earlier boundary. These are not mutually exclusive character roles. Tōji's credulity and damaging accusations do not exhaust him: his independent admission of fault matters to Momo's choice. Conversely, her self-criticism does not erase the injury or make the blame symmetrical. The corrected current projections retain these tensions.

Sae's social punishment in V04 is not evidence that she has become harmless, nor does her previous manipulation make every later humiliation imaginary. At i0109–0115 she is excluded and struck by a ball; Momo recalls her first friendly approach, rejects retaliation, and chooses to partner with her. At i0142–0144 Sae imagines acceptance, stamps Momo's shoes, imagines an accusation, then actually cleans them. At i0155–0159 she claims Momo's purchased biscuits as her own and constructs an abusive-Momo account for Jigoro. Relief at kindness, resentment, deception and audience-specific power coexist. Claude preserves much of this; its categorical persona labels need to remain tied to these different situations, not become a universal model.

Naming/reference review sampled each reading's orientation, analytical middle, evidence and exit: Momo, Tōji, Sae and Kairi remain distinct named agents; Kyō-chin, Kako, Sumire, Honda and Jigoro are not collapsed into principal roles. Unnamed peer groups are bounded rather than invented individuals. No generalized habitual name-erasure was found in that sample. This does not certify every pronoun in every uninspected panel. No reconstruction readiness or calibrated simulation ability follows from these four volumes.
