# 『妹さえいればいい。』 V01–V03 Checkpoint

## 1. Purpose and freeze semantics

This checkpoint freezes the **first prospective analytical block** of the IMOSAE project after canonical deep readings of Volumes 01, 02, and 03. It does not replace those volume readings. Its role is to preserve the first stable longitudinal state produced by reading the series in publication order while refusing knowledge from V04 onward.

The governing evidence boundary is therefore strict:

- V01 may be interpreted only from V01 evidence;
- V02 may use V01 as prior context and V02 as new evidence;
- V03 may use V01–V02 as prior context and V03 as new evidence;
- this checkpoint may synthesize **V01–V03 together**;
- **no V04–V14 event, explanation, outcome, or mature-series interpretation may be used to settle an ambiguity here.**

The checkpoint freezes:

1. the initial ensemble structure;
2. the first-block theory of authorship, talent, success, and self-worth;
3. the early sibling/family/title problem;
4. relationship baselines at the end of V03;
5. the initial creative-industry model;
6. the first stable voice/terminology and visual-form hypotheses;
7. the current formulations of `IM-C001`–`IM-C016`;
8. the strongest unresolved questions that V04–V06 must test.

“Frozen” means that later volumes must not silently rewrite this document. If later evidence changes a first-block interpretation, the transition belongs in the live claim-revision/open-question ledgers and in the next checkpoint or specialist synthesis. Factual, locator, or metadata repair remains permissible.

# 2. Executive synthesis of the first block

Volumes 01–03 establish a series about young adult creators whose lives cannot be divided cleanly into **art**, **work**, **friendship**, **family**, **romance**, and **self-conception**. Each volume sharpens a different part of that proposition.

- **V01 establishes non-equivalent value systems.** Talent, sales, artistic worth, reader impact, usefulness, friendship, desire, and happiness repeatedly fail to rank people in the same order. The volume also establishes the ensemble as a network in which each person's self-description is already under pressure from another person's perspective.
- **V02 materializes interdependence.** Writing becomes visibly embodied and infrastructural: household labor, tools, taxes, deadlines, editors, illustrators, adaptation staff, readers, lodging, travel, and bodily experience all enter the causal chain of creation. The myth of solitary authorship is weakened without denying individual artistic agency.
- **V03 identifies self-authorship as a psychological problem.** `主人公になりたい` becomes the clearest name for Itsuki's desire not merely to succeed but to experience himself as a non-derivative agent. The same drive that helped produce his vocation now threatens to turn love into a status test with no stable endpoint.

The resulting first-block thesis is:

> **IMOSAE treats creative adulthood as a struggle to become an author of one's own life inside systems, relationships, bodies, markets, and histories that no individual fully controls. The danger is not dependence itself; it is converting talent, status, romance, usefulness, or narrative centrality into a total measure of whether one deserves to exist as a full person.**

This thesis is stronger than the simpler description “a romcom about light-novel creators,” but the simpler genre description remains factually true. The analytical claim is that the comedy, romance, and industry material repeatedly organize around conflicts of valuation and agency rather than functioning as detachable subplots.

# 3. Block architecture: what each volume contributes

## 3.1 V01 — values refuse a single scale

V01 introduces almost every major first-block contradiction in embryonic form.

Itsuki is commercially functional, prolific, and professionally established, yet insecure beside Nayuta and Setsuna. Nayuta is extraordinarily talented and economically successful, yet her life has been deeply damaged by bullying and remains affectively concentrated around Itsuki. Haruto is more commercially successful than Itsuki but envies the kind of transformative reader impact that he attributes to Nayuta. Kyo, who lacks comparable creator status, mistakenly converts artistic hierarchy into human hierarchy: `可児那由多は、あたしよりも価値がある` / `あたしは、可児那由多よりも価値がない` (IM-V01-E026–E027).

The volume therefore requires separate tracking of:

- talent;
- commercial reach;
- artistic self-judgment;
- peer recognition;
- reader-life impact;
- professional reliability;
- relational value;
- happiness.

V01 also establishes that the light novel itself is materially collaborative. Cover, title, obi, blurb, illustration, editing, finance, and reader reception are not peripheral to the professional object (IM-V01-E028–E036). Games, travel, food, and alcohol simultaneously become social technologies through which people disclose themselves and generate creative input (IM-V01-E023, E037–E040).

Finally, the Chihiro reveal creates the first title-level irony. Itsuki's declaration `妹さえいればいい。けれど、いない。` is true only inside his own mistaken family map (IM-V01-E041–E042).

## 3.2 V02 — the individual creator is materially porous

V02 turns the V01 network into a causal system.

Itsuki's writing depends on sample prose, editorial legibility, bodily experience, tool habit, Chihiro's domestic criticism, illustration decisions, deadlines, and institutional schedules. His own claim to adulthood through sales/taxes/self-support is directly challenged by Chihiro's list of cooking, laundry, cleaning, clothing, rhythms, travel, alcohol, and deadline competence (IM-V02-E033–E037). The important point is not that Itsuki is “not an adult”; it is that adulthood cannot be reduced to one axis.

Haruto's anime adaptation gives the block its clearest early example of **distributed creative control**. He can participate in scripts, auditions, recording, interviews, and promotion and still reach a point where broadcast outcome lies beyond his direct control (IM-V02-E020). V02 therefore separates effort, responsibility, control, quality, sales, and reader encounter rather than looking for one morally sufficient author or villain.

Kyo's karate history similarly breaks a talent/non-talent binary. She had ability, but fear of exclusion and comparative displacement mattered more than ability alone (IM-V02-E038–E042). Her problem is increasingly one of **self-permission**: can she choose something without needing proof that she is the best, indispensable, or unusually valuable?

## 3.3 V03 — protagonist status becomes the hidden variable

V03 makes Itsuki's romantic position toward Nayuta explicit enough that “he may simply not love her” is no longer a viable first-block explanation. He wants dating, exclusivity, and marriage, but makes action conditional on becoming an `対等の主人公` beside her (IM-V03-E079–E090).

Haruto immediately exposes the structural problem: there is no objective artistic ranking capable of proving total equality. Different readers rank creators differently, sales are not identical to artistic value, and adaptation status is at most a provisional public milestone. Itsuki's criterion can therefore move indefinitely.

The bonus fiction then establishes that `主人公になりたい` predates Nayuta. After Ayane's rejection positions adolescent Itsuki as permanently `弟`, he recognizes himself as peripheral in somebody else's story, turns toward authorship, and converts heterogeneous affect—desire, heartbreak, inferiority, anger, hope, fantasy, prayer—into fiction. The local shift `僕が──いや、俺が主人公だ` marks the deliberate construction of creator agency (IM-V03-E101–E115).

V03 therefore preserves both sides of protagonist hunger:

- **productive:** self-authorship allows a marginalized adolescent to transform experience into vocation;
- **dangerous:** the same logic can make intimacy conditional on publicly demonstrable centrality.

The first block does **not** conclude that the protagonist ideal is wrong. It establishes that it is morally and psychologically ambivalent.

# 4. Frozen initial ensemble structure

## 4.1 羽島伊月 — creator identity as agency and defense

### Stable first-block facts

By the end of V03:

- Itsuki is a professional light-novel author with substantial output and real industry competence.
- He values direct editorial reaction and takes artistic completion seriously even when it costs money or comfort.
- He is capable of practical care, friendship, collaborative play, and professional trust even while performing abrasive selfishness.
- He is strongly attracted to and in love with Nayuta.
- He refuses an actual relationship not because desire is absent, but because he believes he must first become a professionally/narratively equal `主人公`.
- His sister preference predates Ayane, while Ayane's `弟` rejection intensifies and defensively reorganizes it.
- He genuinely loves Chihiro as family while misunderstanding Chihiro's sex/gender presentation within the household arrangement.
- His relationship with Keisuke is estranged and affectively cold, but Keisuke's inner motives are not yet available.

### Frozen interpretation

Itsuki's defining contradiction is **agency purchased through a rigid self-story**. He became a writer by refusing peripheral status; however, the identity that protected him from passivity can become a machine for indefinitely deferring vulnerability. His insistence on being protagonist, his horror at being “the two-years-older male colleague,” and the defensive inversion from `姉`/`弟` to `妹` all use narrative categories to protect a threatened sense of self.

This should not be reduced to narcissism alone. The same structure also produces courage, vocation, persistence, and an ability to build a life outside a damaged home. V04–V06 must test whether Itsuki can retain agency without making status the price of reciprocity.

## 4.2 可児那由多 — genius, attachment, and emerging horizontal relations

### Stable first-block facts

- Nayuta is represented as exceptionally talented and commercially powerful.
- Itsuki's fiction had transformative importance after bullying and school withdrawal.
- Her romantic desire for Itsuki is direct and persistent.
- Her sexual speech is unusually explicit, but speech should not be equated with limitless sexual confidence or consent.
- She possesses disciplined craft practices and can work with remarkable productivity.
- By V03, she develops a strong chosen-sister relation with Kyo and writes a book-length fantasy for Kyo alone.
- Jealousy causes her to recognize Itsuki for the first time as a competing `同業者`, not only an adored predecessor/savior.

### Frozen interpretation

Nayuta begins as the character most easily flattened into “genius in love,” but V02–V03 already resist that reduction. Her attachment to Itsuki remains central, yet her relation with Kyo and her first peer/rival recognition create horizontal social and professional axes that do not simply reproduce worship.

The key first-block question is whether these axes become durable enough to support a self-conception not organized primarily around Itsuki.

## 4.3 白川京 — ordinary status as a crisis of permission, not absence of depth

### Stable first-block facts

- Kyo loves Itsuki but withholds that desire.
- She is socially competent, observant, and often functions as a bridge between more eccentric creators.
- She initially interprets extraordinary talent as greater human worth.
- Her karate history proves that “ordinary” does not mean simply untalented: social exclusion and comparative anxiety shaped her withdrawal.
- She experiences usefulness to creators as a powerful source of legitimacy.
- She performs a more sexually experienced/adult persona than her actual history supports, then abandons that performance with Nayuta and receives affection unchanged.
- Haruto recognizes that Kyo loves Itsuki; no reciprocal Kyo→Haruto attraction is established through V03.

### Frozen interpretation

Kyo's first-block problem is **self-authorization**. She repeatedly asks whether her desires count if she is not exceptional, indispensable, or externally validated. Her successful truth-telling to Nayuta is therefore important not because it solves her life direction, but because it offers a local case in which honesty does not reduce her relational value.

## 4.4 不破春斗 — market craft without emotional falsity

### Stable first-block facts

- Haruto is commercially successful and deliberately studies popular forms.
- He experiences envy toward creators whose work seems more singular or transformative.
- His failed/disappointing adaptation destabilizes the defensive claim that his work is merely an `工業製品`.
- V03 recovers a positive creator principle: `人に楽しんでもらうことは、楽しい`.
- He understands Itsuki's `主人公になりたい` drive because he shares part of it, but he also gives the strongest internal critique of Itsuki's undefined equality metric.
- He is attracted to Kyo but restrains strategic pursuit after recognizing her love for Itsuki.

### Frozen interpretation

Haruto is the first block's strongest evidence against an **authenticity-versus-market** binary. Market research, packaging awareness, audience orientation, personal taste, artistic pride, envy, and sincere attachment coexist in one creator. His trajectory suggests that craft becomes healthier when audience pleasure is positively owned rather than defensively redescribed as industrial manufacture.

## 4.5 羽島千尋 — material family before correct classification

### Stable first-block facts

- Chihiro performs extensive household labor for Itsuki and derives some pleasure from being needed.
- Chihiro is Itsuki's step-sibling and is female; Itsuki alone within the family is said to believe Chihiro is male.
- The concealment arrangement and its origin/timing are not yet fully explained.
- Chihiro develops bookkeeping competence into actual paid Saturday work/training with Ashley.
- Chihiro intends eventual disclosure to Itsuki after Ashley learns the secret.
- Itsuki explicitly regards Chihiro as precious family despite his estrangement from the family home.

### Frozen interpretation

Chihiro embodies one of the block's most important inversions: **substance precedes category**. Itsuki already has a functioning sibling relation—care, irritation, dependence, shared history, ordinary outings, emotional importance—while his explicit conceptual map remains wrong. This makes the title problem epistemic and relational, not merely a one-joke reveal.

The checkpoint does not infer a romantic desire from Chihiro toward Itsuki. Care, desire for inclusion, usefulness, family affection, and anxiety around disclosure are established; romance is not.

## 4.6 Secondary but structurally important figures

### 土岐健次郎

Toki is frozen as more than a censor/gatekeeper. He rejects illegible material, communicates reader feedback, coordinates deadlines, negotiates role ownership, and mediates adaptation/industry opportunity. His tactics can be coercive, but institutional constraint and creative contribution coexist.

### 恵那刹那／ぷりけつ

Setsuna demonstrates that illustration is generative authorship rather than downstream decoration. At the same time, artistic appetite does not erase boundary violations; Itsuki's own `犯罪` language prevents slapstick framing from becoming automatic ethical exoneration.

### 大野アシュリー

Ashley begins as creator-finance infrastructure and expands into Chihiro's employer/mentor and holder of a consequential family secret. Administrative expertise is therefore part of the creative-life ecology rather than external bookkeeping trivia.

### 羽島啓輔

Keisuke is now an explicit estranged relation rather than an absent abstraction. The first block establishes chronology—wife's illness/death, intensified work absorption, remarriage, Itsuki's withdrawal—and a cold present interaction, but not sufficient interior evidence to freeze him as uncaring or malicious.

### 三田洞彩音

Ayane is a formative first-love figure whose `弟` framing wounds Itsuki's equality/self-position. She is a **major intensifier and reorganizer**, not the sole cause, of an already-existing sister preference.

# 6. First-block theory of authorship, talent, success, and self-worth

## 6.1 Authorship is distributed without becoming impersonal

The first three volumes strongly reject the fantasy that a professional novel is produced by an isolated mind acting alone. Creation repeatedly depends on:

- editors and category legibility;
- illustrators and image/prose reciprocity;
- covers, titles, obi, blurbs, and synopsis copy;
- tools and bodily habit;
- household rhythms and domestic support;
- travel and embodied input;
- deadlines and production schedules;
- tax/administrative competence;
- adaptation staff and media systems;
- players/readers as feedback environments;
- friends as emotional and conceptual interlocutors.

This does **not** erase authorial agency. Itsuki can insist on his own synopsis, Nayuta can refuse publication of a private manuscript, and Haruto can redesign a game after play. The frozen model is therefore **distributed causality with local agency**, not either solitary genius or anonymous system determinism.

## 6.2 Talent is real but normatively underdetermined

The text does not deny differences in ability. Nayuta's talent is narratively explicit; Setsuna's illustration can overwhelm Itsuki's sense of his prose; Haruto and Itsuki compare themselves to peers. What the first block destabilizes is the conversion:

`more talent → more human value → more deserved love → more legitimate life`.

Kyo articulates that conversion most starkly in V01, while V02–V03 produce repeated counterevidence. Talent does not guarantee persistence, happiness, ethical maturity, adaptation quality, or relational legitimacy.

## 6.3 Success is multidimensional

By V03, at least seven value axes must remain separate:

1. sales / aggregate market reach;
2. public recognizability;
3. peer/artistic judgment;
4. adaptation status and adaptation quality;
5. direct effect on a particular reader;
6. private relational value;
7. creator satisfaction / self-conception.

The existence of these axes explains why creator comparison is so unstable. Itsuki's desired “equality” with Nayuta cannot be measured by a single scalar because the series has already established that the relevant value systems disagree.

## 6.4 Market craft and authentic investment coexist

Haruto is the primary first-block test case. His attention to popular genres, packaging, audience preference, and saleability is genuine craft rather than proof of emotional fraud. V03's `人に楽しんでもらうことは、楽しい` gives that craft a positive motive.

Itsuki's synopsis chapter adds another complication: promotional rhetoric can be technically nonfalse while strategically generating expectations. Industry craft therefore has its own ethics and truth conditions rather than being reducible to either “lying marketing” or pure description.

## 6.5 Art may have nonmarket address

Nayuta's Kyo-only manuscript opens the strongest first-block alternative to commodity circulation. Its value partly derives from being addressed to **one person** and deliberately withheld from publication despite obvious market potential.

The checkpoint does not rank private gift-art above published art. It freezes only the distinction: **a work's value may be constituted by restricted relational address, not merely by aggregate reception.**

## 6.6 Fiction transforms experience rather than transcribing it

Across V01–V03, characters repeatedly convert life into fiction without literal one-to-one mapping:

- embodied fear unlocks a fictional scene;
- Chihiro's adulthood critique becomes Hilde's speech;
- Nayuta's private manuscript triangulates Kyo and Itsuki rather than simply copying either relation;
- adolescent Itsuki throws heterogeneous affect into an invented story;
- TRPG and storytelling games distribute authorship and expose otherwise unspoken tendencies.

The governing first-block model is therefore **transformation/refraction**, not autobiography by substitution.

# 7. Early sibling, family, and title problem

## 7.1 `妹` has four distinct first-block modes

By V03, `妹` can no longer be treated as one stable category.

### Mode A — literal/material kinship

Chihiro is Itsuki's actual step-sibling and performs everyday sibling/family labor before Itsuki knows the category correctly.

### Mode B — aesthetic/erotic trope

Itsuki has a preexisting preference for younger-sister characters and is frustrated by media structures that often deny them heroine status (IM-V03-E095–E097).

### Mode C — defensive inversion

Ayane's rejection of Itsuki as permanently `弟` is experienced as unequal, infantilized status. Itsuki responds by rejecting `姉` and declaring that only `妹` is necessary (IM-V03-E102–E110).

### Mode D — chosen relational grammar

Nayuta treats Kyo as `お姉ちゃん`, and V03 explicitly generalizes sibling terms toward relations of care, admiration, following, protection, and asymmetry.

These modes overlap but are not interchangeable.

## 7.2 The origin question is revised, not closed

The first-block evidence rules out the simplest causal claim **“Ayane caused the sister fixation.”** Preference predates her. The evidence instead supports:

> **preexisting aesthetic preference + later defensive intensification/reorganization after `弟` rejection + unresolved family/history layers.**

Mother's illness/death and Keisuke's work absorption are established chronology, but the checkpoint does not insert them as a complete causal explanation without an explicit bridge in the text.

## 7.3 Chihiro makes the title epistemic

The title irony after V01 is not merely that “he secretly has a sister.” Itsuki already possesses much of what ordinary siblinghood means—care, irritation, dependence, memory, routine, familial importance—while claiming the absence of the desired category. The title therefore opens a distinction between **having a relation** and **recognizing/naming it correctly**.

V03 further complicates the issue because sibling terms can be literal, desired, defensive, and chosen. The first block does not yet settle which of those meanings will dominate the title's mature resolution.

## 7.4 Family estrangement remains causally incomplete

The following are frozen as facts:

- Itsuki's mother is hospitalized and later dies during his youth;
- Keisuke becomes more absorbed in work afterward;
- remarriage and Itsuki's debut/withdrawal from the home occur in the same broad developmental period;
- Itsuki avoids returning home;
- Itsuki and Keisuke interact coldly in V03;
- Chihiro attempts to mediate.

What is **not** frozen is a total moral or psychological explanation of Keisuke or the estrangement.

# 8. Initial creative-industry model

The first three volumes support the following industry propositions strongly enough to freeze as the baseline against V04–V06.

## 8.1 Editorial resistance may be creative labor

Toki's “no” is not automatically anti-artistic. Reader legibility, category clarity, timing, and production coordination can be legitimate craft constraints. His coercive tactics remain separately assessable.

## 8.2 Light novels are packaged collaborative works

Illustration, cover, title, obi, blurb, synopsis, editor, author, sales structure, and promotion all participate in the published object. V02 explicitly allows prose and image decisions to influence one another.

## 8.3 Deadlines are network events

A missed deadline is not only a private author problem; it propagates into editorial, illustration, production, and promotion schedules.

## 8.4 Adaptation gives visibility while reducing authorial control

Haruto's case establishes the asymmetry clearly. Participation does not imply sovereignty, and adaptation quality can diverge from both creator effort and resulting book sales.

## 8.5 Career recognition is not terminal validation

Itsuki chooses TV anime adaptation as a provisional public marker, while Haruto correctly recognizes that it is not Itsuki's actual endpoint. Milestones are instrumental and socially legible, but they do not settle artistic or personal worth.

## 8.6 Administrative competence belongs inside creator adulthood

Taxes, expenses, bookkeeping, contracts/schedules, and household stability are part of the same life-system as composition. Ashley and Chihiro make this especially visible.

## 8.7 Precarity remains background even among successful creators

Awards, publications, and sales do not remove instability. Projects can fail; media adaptations disappoint; reputations fluctuate; work volume and deadlines remain coercive pressures.

# 9. Games, social ritual, and embedded fiction — first-block freeze

Across three volumes, games and ordinary social rituals recur too consistently to be treated as incidental garnish.

## 9.1 Repeated functions

They provide:

- lowered-stakes disclosure;
- observation of desire and rivalry;
- repair after conflict;
- collaborative authorship;
- feedback on rules and systems;
- embodied input for writing;
- alternative relations to winning and status;
- ordinary hospitality that materially sustains creative work.

This is now a **block-level resolved pattern**: V01–V03 demonstrably use leisure/social ritual as relational and creative infrastructure. The broader series-long question remains open only as a test of durability and transformation.

## 9.2 `Once Upon a Time` and contested authorship

V03's storytelling game is the strongest early formal miniature of distributed narrative control: each player can seize narrator position and pursue incompatible personal endings. This resonates with `主人公になりたい`, but the checkpoint freezes it as **formal resonance**, not direct causal allegory.

## 9.3 Documentary caution

The V03 afterword explicitly notes rule bending for narrative tempo. The presence of real works/games inside the world does not authorize treating every represented rule or practice as documentary exactness.

# 10. Japanese voice, address, and terminology freeze

The first block establishes several linguistic hypotheses important enough to preserve before later character development changes them.

## 10.1 Itsuki

Itsuki's adult voice uses forceful `俺`, abrupt judgments, professional vocabulary, and grandiose aesthetic claims, but vulnerable scenes can collapse that register. V03 gives the persona a developmental history through `僕が──いや、俺が主人公だ`. The pronoun shift is locally meaningful as adopted creator agency; it must not be universalized into a generic rule that `俺` is inherently the “true” self.

## 10.2 Nayuta

Nayuta's speech combines direct affection, obscene explicitness, playful aggression, and highly serious creator language. The first block demonstrates that verbal extremity and bodily/relational boundaries can coexist. Her directness is a character trait, not proof of limitless sexual availability.

## 10.3 Kyo

Kyo is especially register-sensitive. Socially strategic confidence and sexual-adult bravado differ from trusted disclosure. V03 confirms that part of the “experienced adult woman” register is performed.

## 10.4 Haruto

Haruto often intellectualizes creation through market/craft vocabulary, but his most important positive creator statement is plain: `人に楽しんでもらうことは、楽しい`. The simplification itself is analytically significant.

## 10.5 Chihiro

Chihiro's everyday household register supports a competent younger-family role while the Itsuki-specific identity concealment complicates address and categorization. Later voice analysis must distinguish public/social presentation from the peculiar family arrangement around Itsuki.

## 10.6 Frozen lexical targets

| Term | First-block significance |
|---|---|
| `主人公` | Not merely “main character”; becomes Itsuki's noun for non-derivative selfhood and social/romantic legitimacy. |
| `脇役` | Feared peripheral status against which Itsuki defines agency. |
| `同業者` | Can mean peer horizontalization for Nayuta or anonymous erasure in Itsuki's feared self-description. |
| `姉` / `妹` / `弟` | Literal kinship plus directional care/status vocabulary and defensive narrative coding. |
| `芸術家` / `職人` | Useful as character rhetoric, not frozen ontology. V02–V03 weaken any simple authentic/market hierarchy. |
| `工業製品` | Haruto's defensive reduction of his own work, destabilized by adaptation pain and audience-oriented creator motive. |
| `嘘は書いてない` | Useful marker for the gap between literal nonfalsity and expectation-shaping promotional rhetoric. |
| `人に楽しんでもらうことは、楽しい` | Haruto's recovered positive statement of audience-oriented craft. |

# 11. Visual and publication-form freeze

The V01–V03 visual layer is not neutral decoration. It repeatedly selects **recognition events**, relational configurations, and erotic/comic contradictions for emphasis.

The first block supports four visual propositions:

1. **Commercial packaging and narrative seriousness can coexist.** Erotic or comic presentation does not erase serious identity, trust, or relationship content.
2. **Observer-framed compositions often mark relational recognition.** V03's Itsuki/Nayuta handholding composition is a clear example: the image includes others' perception of the pair rather than functioning as a private romantic icon only.
3. **Chihiro's visual coding participates in delayed recognition.** Soft/androgynous early presentation and later fanservice-heavy disclosure are formal choices that shape the reader/Itsuki knowledge gap.
4. **Illustrations can function as interpretive emphasis but not independent ethical verdict.** A sexualized image cannot by itself prove that the narrative endorses the underlying conduct.

The block also preserves publication-form distinctions established in Phase 0: covers, narrative illustrations, in-text documents, games/diagrams, bonus paratext, and advertisements must remain separate evidence classes.

**Rejected.** Event, consent/boundary status, narrative consequence, and erotic/comic framing must be analyzed separately.

## 14.9 “Chihiro's secret is a general public gender identity claim”

**Unsupported.** Estrangement and coldness are textual facts; interior motive remains unavailable.

# 17. Frozen first-block state in compressed form

At the end of V03:

- **Itsuki** has transformed creator ambition into a theory of romantic eligibility: he loves Nayuta but believes he must first become an equal protagonist.
- **Nayuta** remains intensely attached to Itsuki but has begun to form horizontal relations with Kyo and with Itsuki-as-colleague.
- **Kyo** remains caught in comparative self-worth but has demonstrated that truthful vulnerability need not destroy intimacy.
- **Haruto** has moved from defensive market-industrial rhetoric toward a positive audience-oriented craft principle.
- **Chihiro** is already substantial family to Itsuki, has begun paid competence outside the household, and carries an unresolved future-disclosure obligation.
- **The creator group** functions as social, emotional, and creative infrastructure rather than merely a friend ensemble.
- **Publishing** is a distributed system of constraint and support rather than either villainous exploitation or frictionless collaboration.
- **Talent** is real but does not supply a valid total measure of human worth, love, or deserved outcome.
- **Success** has multiple incompatible metrics.
- **Fiction** transforms experience rather than copying it.
- **`妹`** has become literal kinship, aesthetic trope, defensive inversion, and chosen relational grammar.
- **`主人公`** has become the first block's clearest term for the desire to author one's own existence without being reduced to somebody else's supporting role.

The central unresolved problem is now sharper than it was in V01:

> **Can these characters become authors of their own lives without converting other people, professional status, or artistic hierarchy into proof that they deserve to be protagonists?**

That question is the canonical handoff from V01–V03 to V04–V06.
