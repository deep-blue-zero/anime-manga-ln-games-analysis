# 『妹さえいればいい。』 V04–V06 Checkpoint

## 1. Purpose and freeze semantics

This checkpoint freezes the **second prospective analytical block** of the IMOSAE project after canonical deep readings of Volumes 04, 05, and 06. It inherits `IMOSAE_V01-V03_CHECKPOINT.md` as frozen historical state, then records only the deltas produced by V04–V06. It does not replace the three volume readings, the mutable longitudinal ledgers, or the bounded provisional Kani Nayuta and Kenjiro Toki character monographs.

The semantic boundary is strict:

- V04 may use the frozen V01–V03 checkpoint as prior state and V04 as new evidence.
- V05 may use V01–V04 as prior state and V05 as new evidence.
- V06 may use V01–V05 as prior state and V06 as new evidence.
- this checkpoint may synthesize V01–V06, but its **new adjudicative evidence** is V04–V06;
- **no V07–V14 event, explanation, relationship outcome, family disclosure, adaptation result, or retrospective authorial knowledge may be used to settle a V01–V06 ambiguity.**

The checkpoint freezes:

1. the V06 ensemble and relationship state;
2. the second-block revision of self-authorship and `主人公` logic;
3. the V04–V06 model of media mix, adaptation, and distributed creative authority;
4. the revised theory of talent, effort, achievement, and lovability;
5. the current distinction between aptitude, institutional demand, and self-authored vocation;
6. the increasingly explicit theory of relationship maintenance and social infrastructure;
7. the V04–V06 family/sibling/title state;

9. the current visual, paratextual, and epistemic cautions;
10. the checkpoint formulations of `IM-C001`–`IM-C028`;

“Frozen” means later volumes must not silently rewrite this document. Later evidence may strengthen, revise, downgrade, reject, or answer a second-block claim only through the live ledgers and a later checkpoint or synthesis. Factual, locator, checksum, or metadata repair remains permissible.

The two provisional specialist monographs are admissible only as **bounded V01–V06 reconstruction aids**. They do not enlarge the semantic boundary and do not supersede the checkpoint.

# 2. Executive synthesis of the second block

Volumes 04–06 do not abandon the first block’s central concern with self-authorship. They make it harder.

The first checkpoint established a world in which artistic talent, sales, recognition, usefulness, romance, happiness, and human worth cannot be collapsed onto one scale. It also established Itsuki’s `主人公になりたい` as both a generative technology of agency and a dangerous status test. The second block pressures those ideas by moving the characters into larger systems: manga adaptation, television anime, awards, casting, publicity, sponsorship, editorial labor, family vocational conflict, and relationships that can no longer remain indefinitely hypothetical.

The most important revision is that **self-authorship is not the same thing as autonomy from others, nor is it the same thing as perfect consistency with one’s prior rules**.

V04 translates Itsuki’s protagonist hunger into industrial language. An anime adaptation is not destiny. A slot can open because another project falls out; a preassembled staff structure can precede the author; another title can substitute for his. Itsuki’s fear therefore becomes `代わりはいる`: he wants to become `オンリーワン`. Yet the same volume’s best adaptation material demonstrates that meaningful creative work is often produced precisely because another creator changes the source. Kaiko’s manga is valuable not because it copies the novel’s surface, but because it preserves priorities while inventing gestures, timing, visual characterization, and page logic the prose did not possess. Itsuki can be changed by her work without ceasing to be the source author. The block’s first major proposition is therefore:

> **Fidelity is not sameness, and being transformed by another creator is not automatically loss of authorship.**

V05 then corrects an overbroad version of the project’s own collaboration thesis. Nayuta is a genuine limit case. Her imaginative/content generation can be extraordinarily autonomous. It would be inaccurate to say every important creative idea is collectively authored. But her work still depends on bodies, schedules, editors, circulation, media-mix partners, readers, friendship, and affective life. The more precise model is layered: **content-level authorship may be highly individual while creative life and finished cultural production remain materially interdependent.**

V05 also pluralizes `主人公`. The rhetoric no longer belongs only to Itsuki’s private hierarchy. Other creators and professionals use protagonist-like language as a grammar of persistence and meaningful agency. This weakens the idea that “being protagonist” can ever function as an objective ranking of people. By V06, Itsuki’s own use of the concept reaches a breaking point. Nayuta collapses after converting striving into a condition of imagined lovability. Finite time, bodily fragility, and another person’s suffering make indefinite deferral morally and psychologically untenable. Itsuki does not become an equal professional protagonist before dating her. He **abandons equality as a romantic prerequisite**.

That change is not a defeat of self-authorship. It is a maturation of it.

> **Agency can include revising one’s own rule when preserving the rule would instrumentalize another person or sacrifice finite life to an abstraction.**

Nayuta provides the complementary second-block warning. Genius, success, and even genuine pleasure in growth do not protect a person from comparative self-worth. Her first experiences of collaborative media mix are real sources of excitement. But admiration of others’ effort becomes `もっと頑張らないと`; striving becomes sleep deprivation, malnutrition, overwork, and collapse. She reveals that meritocratic self-totalization can operate even at the top of the talent hierarchy. The danger is not only “I am less talented, therefore worth less.” It can also become “I must keep improving, therefore I may continue to deserve love.”

Itsuki’s hospital response explicitly rejects that condition.

The second-block thesis is therefore:

> **IMOSAE increasingly defines adult self-authorship as the ability to pursue chosen projects and relationships without converting talent, effort, consistency, usefulness, institutional recognition, or professional equality into total conditions of personhood. Creative and relational maturity require permeability: allowing other people to alter one’s work, accepting that relationships need maintenance, separating aptitude from desire, and revising self-imposed rules when bodies and finite time expose their human cost.**

This does not dissolve ambition, aesthetic conviction, or professional standards. V04–V06 repeatedly preserve strong preferences, competition, craft judgment, and institutional constraint. The revision is against **totalization**, not against excellence.

# 3. Block architecture: what each volume contributes

## 3.1 V04 — irreplaceability meets media mix

V04 takes the V03 protagonist problem and gives it industrial form.

Itsuki understands simultaneous manga/anime/publication activity as a rational brand strategy. He wants the name `羽島伊月` itself to become legible. But the anime offer reveals how little metaphysical uniqueness the industry promises. The slot is contingent, institutional, and substitutable. Another work can occupy it. Itsuki’s desire to become `オンリーワン` therefore sharpens the V03 equality problem rather than solving it.

The volume’s counterargument arrives through adaptation craft. Candidate manga artists possess different competencies; technical brilliance in one task does not automatically imply suitability in another. Kaiko’s source understanding and sequential-manga intelligence allow her to invent material not present in the prose while preserving what Itsuki recognizes as character truth. This opens the block’s mature fidelity model.

The lingerie/nudity conflict then shows compromise as potential **additive synthesis**. Itsuki and Kaiko begin with incompatible aesthetic absolutes. Research and sequencing produce a third solution neither possessed at the start. The result is not a weak midpoint but an expanded form.

Toki’s role also becomes ethically clearer. He is institutionally aligned with getting the adaptation accepted, yet makes risk and refusal legible to Itsuki. Professional care can therefore occur inside an asymmetrical system when the professional refuses to hide the cost of consent.

Haruto’s failed anime adds another distinction: **good process knowledge can survive bad outcome**. Enjoyment of auditions, music, scripts, and recording need not be retrospectively declared false because the broadcast disappoints him.

Finally, the V04 bonus fiction formalizes creative survival as cooperative rather than winner-take-all. Because it is BONUS_FICTION, it is not literal mainline documentary evidence. As formal reflection, however, it strongly reinforces the claim that peers, assistance, and relationship networks are part of professional survival.

## 3.2 V05 — autonomy is layered; protagonist language pluralizes

V05 supplies the strongest internal correction to a simplistic “everything is collaborative” thesis. Nayuta demonstrates that imaginative content can be generated with extraordinary autonomy. This requires `IM-C001` to be **REVISED**, not merely strengthened. The corpus must preserve the distinction between imaginative authorship, production, circulation/reception, career survival, and the affective/bodily conditions that make creation possible.

The volume also strengthens the media-mix model. Author, scriptwriter, and director can disagree substantially and still converge on a transformed structure they regard as better for anime. “Different from the source” is neither proof of betrayal nor proof of success. Transformation must still be judged against medium needs, source priorities, and realized quality.

Kyo becomes increasingly important because she demonstrates causal value without genius-level authorship. Editorial/social intervention and receptive attention can sustain creators and projects. Her importance is not that she secretly turns out to be the same kind of prodigy as Nayuta. It is that the series keeps expanding what counts as productive participation.

Her confession to Itsuki closes one local ambiguity: he genuinely had not recognized her romantic desire and rejects her because he loves someone else. This does not close Kyo’s larger problem of self-authorization. It makes that problem more visible.

The professional cohort material also strengthens two institutional claims. First, longevity is a success axis distinct from awards, peak sales, or genius. Second, awards and categories are not neutral meters that merely discover intrinsic worth. They are designed systems that recruit, signal, allocate money, create public narratives, and shape careers.

Finally, family concern and vocational authority are separated. Knowledge of risk and genuine care do not automatically confer the right to choose another adult’s vocation.

## 3.3 V06 — agency becomes revisable under finitude

V06 is the block’s turning point.

Itsuki first recognizes a relational error with Kyo: he had forced her into a convenient internal story rather than seeing what she actually wanted. Continued friendship is therefore treated as something requiring effort rather than something guaranteed by the label “friend.”

Nayuta’s trajectory then makes the cost of comparative self-worth bodily. Collaborative pleasure and admiration of effort are not false. The pathology appears when the logic becomes compulsory: she must shine more, work more, sleep less, eat less, and become more impressive in order to be secure in love.

Her collapse destroys the practical legitimacy of Itsuki’s indefinite equality gate. Itsuki’s protagonist ideal remains sincere, but he stops treating consistency with that prior rule as superior to Nayuta’s finite welfare. He explicitly prioritizes her over anime success and his own success and initiates the relationship.

The crucial distinction is that the professional asymmetry is **not solved**; the romantic gatekeeping function of that asymmetry is **rejected**.

V06 also sharpens Kyo’s vocational problem through `向いているものとなりたいものは違う`: what one is suited for and what one wants to become are different. Institutional recognition of competence is not yet self-authored desire.

The award-party and production material widen the field. Survival itself becomes a ritualized professional achievement. Casting is shown as a multi-objective allocation problem involving acting, profitability, events, appearance, sponsors, and paying customers. Publicity is shown to be capable of deliberate curation or fabrication. These examples require epistemic caution: institutional artifacts are situated productions, not transparent windows onto process.

# 4. What changes from the V01–V03 checkpoint

## 4.1 From “creative life is interdependent” to layered autonomy

The first block correctly rejected the myth of the fully isolated creator. V04–V06 make that claim more precise.

**Preserved:** bodies, deadlines, editors, illustrators, performers, institutions, readers, friends, money, and social life materially shape creative work.

**Revised:** this does not imply that imaginative content itself is always collectively generated. Nayuta demonstrates unusually strong content-level autonomy.

**Frozen V06 formulation:** individual authorship can be genuinely strong while finished works and creative careers remain materially recursive and interdependent.

## 4.2 From protagonist status as self-authorization to revisable self-authorship

The first block froze `主人公になりたい` as both productive and dangerous.

V04 strengthens its dangerous form by translating it into industrial non-substitutability. V05 pluralizes protagonist rhetoric beyond Itsuki and weakens any objective-ranking interpretation. V06 forces Itsuki to choose between consistency with his old rule and care for an actual person.

**Frozen V06 formulation:** `主人公` remains a useful technology of agency, but mature self-authorship includes the right and responsibility to revise one’s own story.

## 4.3 From talent-as-worth danger to effort-as-lovability danger

The first block centered people who looked upward in talent hierarchies and devalued themselves.

V06 shows the same mechanism can operate through effort even in a genius. Nayuta’s pathology is not “I have no talent.” It is “I must continue proving myself through improvement.”

**Frozen V06 formulation:** talent and effort are real goods, but neither may safely function as a total measure of worth or a prerequisite for deserving love.

## 4.4 From reciprocal desire deferred to actual coupling

The first checkpoint established Itsuki/Nayuta mutual desire but froze the equality criterion as the barrier.

V06 closes the block-level question of whether romance remains deferred: it does not. The couple forms.

The important causal claim is not “equality was achieved.” The opposite is true: Itsuki accepts that the relationship need not wait for such proof.

## 4.5 From social contact to relationship maintenance

Earlier volumes repeatedly showed games, meals, travel, and parties as disclosure technologies. V06 adds a more explicit maintenance theory. Relationships survive because people host, invite, repair, return, listen, and create structured occasions for continued contact.

The label “friend” is not causal by itself.

# 5. Frozen ensemble state at the end of V06

## 5.1 羽島伊月 — self-authorship after the broken rule

Itsuki remains ambitious, competitive, strategically aware, and invested in becoming a creator whose work and name matter. None of that disappears.

Through V04 he translates protagonist hunger into `オンリーワン` industrial ambition. Through V05 that language becomes less defensible as an objective hierarchy because protagonist-like agency appears across a plurality of creators. Through V06 he recognizes that insisting on a rule because it is “his story” can itself become a failure to see other people.

His treatment of Kyo is the first warning. He realizes he had unconsciously assigned her a role convenient to himself. Nayuta’s collapse then supplies the harder correction. Itsuki does not renounce professional ambition. He renounces its power to veto intimacy indefinitely.

**Frozen state:** Itsuki’s self-authorship has become more reflexive. The ability to revise the self-authored rule is now part of agency rather than evidence of weakness.

## 5.2 可児那由多 — genius is not immunity from meritocratic self-harm

Nayuta remains extraordinarily talented, productive, sexually direct in speech, emotionally attached to Itsuki, and capable of intense creative concentration. The second block significantly expands her beyond “genius who wants Itsuki.”

She gains real pleasure from collaborative media work. At the same time, admiration of Itsuki and Kyo’s striving becomes comparative pressure. Her desire to `輝く` escalates into bodily neglect.

The hospital explanation is decisive: she has internalized a conditional model of love in which continued greatness and effort are required.

Itsuki explicitly rejects that premise, and their relationship begins.

**Frozen state:** Nayuta enters V07 as both a newly coupled partner and a character whose most dangerous self-worth mechanism has become explicit but not yet demonstrably healed.

## 5.3 白川京 — causal importance without self-authorization solved

Kyo’s V04–V06 trajectory demonstrates that a person can matter deeply to creative systems without being the primary authorial genius. She can contribute through editorial judgment, social mediation, reception, hosting, and care.

V05 closes her romantic ambiguity with Itsuki through confession and rejection. V06 shows that she can continue to love him, preserve friendship, refuse to convert pain into hatred of Nayuta, and recognize Haruto as a plausible partner without forcing herself into a new desire.

Her vocational problem remains open. She may be suited for editorial work, but `向いているものとなりたいものは違う`.

**Frozen state:** Kyo has stronger evidence of competence and causal value, but still must discover whether she can want a life without treating usefulness to exceptional people as permission to exist.

## 5.4 不破春斗 — from rival to relational infrastructure

Haruto’s earlier market craft and adaptation failure remain important, but the second block increasingly positions him as a stabilizing node.

He understands creator insecurity, can host play and contact, can observe emotional dynamics without collapsing them into rivalry, and participates in professional solidarity. His attraction to Kyo becomes explicit enough to confess, but Kyo does not reciprocate at V06.

**Frozen state:** Haruto is both creator and infrastructure: a person whose value to the ensemble increasingly includes maintaining the conditions under which other relationships and careers continue.

## 5.5 羽島千尋 — family substance continues to outrun Itsuki’s category map

Chihiro’s paid work, practical boundaries, clothing experimentation, craft interests, and family commitments continue to expand the character beyond domestic support.

The central epistemic asymmetry remains unresolved: Itsuki still assimilates contradictory evidence into the false male category.

The second block strengthens rather than closes the title irony. `妹` functions simultaneously as fetish/aesthetic signifier, defensive relational grammar, ordinary tenderness, chosen relational language, and literal family category.

**Frozen state:** Chihiro is materially and psychologically more independent, but the concealment and its eventual consequences remain major V07+ questions.

## 5.6 土岐健次郎 — adversarial caretaker of creative production

Toki’s V01–V06 state is well represented by the bounded provisional monograph, but the checkpoint freezes only what the sequential corpus supports.

He is a blunt, reader-oriented, schedule-conscious editor who makes production externalities visible. V04 is especially important because he warns Itsuki about adaptation risk despite institutional reasons to secure approval and explicitly preserves refusal.

**Frozen state:** Toki embodies the contradiction of creator-facing professional care inside an institution that possesses real leverage. His protective function does not erase coercive tactics, and coercive tactics do not erase the value of coordination.

## 5.7 Secondary but structurally important figures

**Kaiko** establishes medium-specific competence and the possibility that conflict produces additive synthesis. Her family/vocational pressure remains unresolved.

**Setsuna** remains artistically generative while prior boundary violations remain ethically real. Artistic sincerity is not exoneration.

**Ashley** continues to demonstrate that accounting, compensation, and administrative expertise belong inside creative adulthood, while facilitating Chihiro’s independent paid competence.

**Kobe** represents knowledgeable but non-neutral institutional authority. V06 publicity material strengthens the need to distinguish institutional narration from transparent process evidence.

**Oshima** articulates a coherent but economically weighted casting model. The checkpoint does not prejudge whether the compromise will succeed.

**Aoba** supplies a V06 test of aesthetic pluralism: conviction may power creation, but personal taste becomes epistemically unstable when universalized into hierarchy.

# 7. Creative labor, media mix, and institutional power

The second block provides the corpus’s strongest early model of creative industry.

## 7.1 Authorship is layered

The checkpoint rejects two symmetrical simplifications:

1. “The author alone creates the work.”
2. “All creation is collectively authored.”

A more accurate V06 model separates imaginative/content authorship, editorial and communicative shaping, illustration and visual reinterpretation, adaptation authorship, performance, production coordination, circulation, audience/reception, and bodily/relational sustainability.

Different works and creators distribute these layers differently.

## 7.2 Adaptation fidelity is priority-preserving transformation

V04–V06 strongly support `IM-C017`.

A derivative work can be faithful while adding material unavailable to the source medium. Kaiko’s manga, anime series-composition negotiation, illustration feedback into prose, and audio/performance requirements all support this model.

However, “medium difference” is not a blank check. The checkpoint freezes a criterion, not an automatic defense: transformation should remain intelligibly connected to source priorities and be judged as work in the receiving medium.

## 7.3 Compromise can create rather than merely subtract

`IM-C018` is strongly supported. The best negotiations in the block do not simply split differences. They generate new formal possibilities.

This is especially important because the series often gives its creators intense aesthetic preferences. Cooperative production does not require weak conviction. It requires the possibility that conflict can produce a third form.

## 7.4 Institutions allocate opportunity and externalities

Awards, casting, adaptation slots, sponsorship, publicity, deadlines, and editorial hierarchies shape careers. They are not neutral mirrors of intrinsic worth.

Private preferences gain system-level consequences when institutional actors possess allocation power. Conversely, institutions can also create coordination, risk visibility, professional development, and access.

The checkpoint therefore preserves the first block’s anti-binary view: publishing is neither pure exploitation nor frictionless creative community.

## 7.5 Longevity is a distinct professional achievement

By V06 the corpus explicitly ritualizes survival. Award recognition, genius, and strong early sales do not guarantee a durable career. Health, adaptation to field conditions, relationships, and endurance matter.

The checkpoint freezes longevity as a distinct success axis without romanticizing mere persistence.

# 8. Talent, effort, comparison, and lovability

The second block significantly changes the project’s theory of self-worth.

Early evidence often showed people looking upward toward someone more talented. V06 shows that the same hierarchy can colonize a person who is already treated as exceptional.

Nayuta’s collapse demonstrates a sequence:

1. another person’s effort appears admirable;
2. improvement becomes desirable;
3. comparison changes improvement from desire into obligation;
4. effort becomes evidence of worth;
5. effort becomes imagined evidence of lovability;
6. bodily limits are treated as obstacles rather than information.

The checkpoint therefore separates growth as intrinsically rewarding, effort as craft practice, effort as social signaling, and effort as compulsory proof of worth. Only the last movement is being rejected.

This also clarifies Itsuki. His equality rule is another meritocratic intimacy condition. He does not merely want to improve for artistic reasons; he tries to make professional status determine when he is permitted to reciprocate love. V06 rejects the rule’s gatekeeping function.

# 9. Family, siblinghood, and title logic

V04–V06 strengthen the project’s conclusion that `妹` cannot be treated as a single semantic category.

The term and surrounding sibling grammar now operate across at least these modes:

1. literal kinship;
2. fetish/aesthetic ideal;
3. genre trope;
4. defensive inversion after being positioned as `弟`;
5. chosen relation;
6. ordinary tenderness;
7. public/private identity;
8. misrecognition.

Itsuki’s false premise that he has no real younger sister remains structurally productive. It helps him imagine sister desire as permanently inexhaustible and therefore compatible with endless creative striving.

Chihiro increasingly exists as material counterevidence to that story: paid work, household agency, clothing preference, family commitment, craft interests, and desire for inclusion are already present before correct classification.

The checkpoint **does not** resolve why concealment began, how long it can persist, or what the eventual disclosure will mean. Those remain protected open questions.

# 10. Social ritual and relationship maintenance

The first block established games, food, travel, alcohol, birthdays, and outings as disclosure and creative infrastructure.

The second block adds a stronger proposition: they are also **maintenance technologies**.

TRPG sessions, hosted gatherings, professional parties, shared meals, and ordinary contact create repeatable structures in which people can remain connected after conflict, rejection, failure, and career divergence.

This matters because V06 explicitly refuses the idea that relationships sustain themselves by status alone. Friendship is not a permanent resource that can be assumed without upkeep.

`IM-C024` remains OPEN longitudinally, but V06 supplies a strong baseline:

> relational continuity is enacted through reciprocal practice.

# 11. Comedy, sexuality, vulgarity, and boundaries

The checkpoint preserves the corpus’s scene-by-scene ethical method.

Sexual and vulgar comedy may disclose desire, expose embarrassment, reveal relationship asymmetry, provide craft information, intensify intimacy, commodify bodies, obscure refusal, or coexist with genuine coercion.

V04’s body-study material and continuing Chihiro/Setsuna consequences make it impossible to treat comedic framing as ethical absolution.

Known reciprocity also changes scene meaning. A sexually explicit joke between people whose desire and consent are established is not analytically identical to a scene in which objections or nonconsensual conduct are present.

The checkpoint therefore freezes `IM-C007` as **STRENGTHEN**, not as a single positive or negative verdict on the series’ vulgarity.

# 12. Voice, terminology, visual form, and epistemic caution

The Japanese-language ledger remains the canonical longitudinal home for idiolect, address, and terminology. The bounded Nayuta and Toki monographs can support reconstruction but do not alter checkpoint authority.

Several terms are especially load-bearing through V06:

- `主人公`
- `対等`
- `オンリーワン`
- `代わりはいる`
- `妹`
- `頑張る`
- `輝く`
- `向いているもの`
- `なりたいもの`

Their analytical meaning depends on speaker and context. They should not be translated into one fixed English philosophical vocabulary without preserving local pragmatic function.

The visual layer also gains methodological importance. V04 turns lingerie from gag object into craft document; character profiles and Q&A function as paratext rather than narrator-level fact; V06’s production publicity demonstrates that “behind the scenes” presentation may itself be strategically authored.

Accordingly:

> **paratext is evidence of what an artifact presents, not automatically transparent evidence of the underlying process.**

That principle is frozen as the V06 baseline for `IM-C027`.
