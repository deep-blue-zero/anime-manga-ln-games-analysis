## 1. Executive thesis: reclassification, mediated continuity, and the cost of making fantasy real

Volume 10 is best understood as a volume about **reclassification that demands re-authorship**. Categories become true, corrected, or materially consequential: Chihiro becomes publicly legible as Itsuki's sister rather than brother; Kyo stops merely considering editing and chooses an institutional route; Nayuta turns twenty; translation makes Japanese fiction foreign-language fiction without erasing authorship; adaptation makes a novel a film or anime without preserving its surface; Haruto moves from market-optimized craftsmanship toward an explicitly self-chosen creative dream; and Itsuki reaches the external success that once seemed capable of proving he had become a `主人公`.

Yet V10 repeatedly rejects the idea that correct classification automatically tells a person how to live the category. Chihiro and Itsuki experimentally imitate stock sibling behaviors, discover that these feel false, and conclude that they must become siblings `自分たちなり`--in their own way--rather than copy an available template. Kyo discovers that formalizing every professional custom can protect against hidden expectations but can also create rigid contractual structures that remove precisely the flexibility creators sometimes need. A good film adaptation is praised because it reproduces something more essential than costume-level resemblance. Taiwanese translation changes language, packaging, publisher context, and intermediary labor while the encounter with readers makes it impossible for Itsuki simply to declare the translated work no longer his. Across these domains, identity survives through **mediated transformation**, not untouched surfaces. [IM-V10-E080-IM-V10-E107] [IM-V10-E135-IM-V10-E161] [IM-V10-E162-IM-V10-E181] [IM-V10-E236-IM-V10-E280]

The volume's most consequential variation is also its darkest. Actual siblinghood preserves Chihiro and Itsuki's accumulated relationship: Itsuki explicitly says that learning the truth cannot erase their memories or the care Chihiro has given him. He rapidly adopts real-sibling sexual boundaries and tells Nayuta that real family is precisely why he will never view Chihiro romantically. At the level of relationship, truth therefore **corrects a category without annihilating history**. [IM-V10-E033-IM-V10-E063]

But the same reclassification destroys a different continuity. Itsuki's writer identity had been built around idealized, eroticized sister fiction precisely while he did **not** have a consciously recognized real sister. Once Chihiro becomes real to him as family, the imaginative grammar that once felt natural becomes repellent. He rereads his own work, can no longer identify with brother protagonists romantically desiring sisters, and experiences the collapse of the identity `妹モノを心から愛している小説家`. External success cannot solve this. His anime succeeds; the series' sales multiply; foreign readers welcome him; professional collaborators wait for his next manuscript; and he can truthfully say that he became a protagonist through his own work. None of it restores the self from which the writing used to come. [IM-V10-E340-IM-V10-E376]

This yields the central V10 formulation:

> **Correct reclassification does not complete identity; it changes the conditions under which identity must be authored. Relationships, works, and vocations can preserve continuity through transformed form, but some self-concepts depend on the distance between an imagined role and lived reality. When reality collapses that distance, fulfillment can become rupture, and external success or motivation cannot substitute for rebuilding a coherent self.**

V10 therefore revises rather than simply extends the V07-V09 sequence. The previous block moved from continuity under change, to inhabiting roles, to distinguishing assigned value from authored commitment. V10 asks what happens **after the category itself changes**. The answer is uneven. Some roles become more authentic through re-authorship; some institutions become more intelligible through negotiated ambiguity; some artworks survive translation by transformation; and one central creative identity breaks because its fantasy object becomes lived kinship.

## 2. Volume architecture and the delayed-interiority design

V10 is structurally unusual because its apparent emotional trajectory is deliberately incomplete until the penultimate narrative unit, `主人公になりたかったのに`. The author afterword states, as PF, that much of the volume intentionally restricts Itsuki's psychology to what could be inferred from outward behavior and avoids ordinary interior formulations such as `～と思った`; the closing revelation is designed to make earlier chapters read differently on retrospective rereading. [IM-V10-E377-IM-V10-E386]

This formal decision matters analytically because the earlier behavior is not simply fake. Itsuki really does accept Chihiro; he really does enjoy having her as a sister; he really does establish ethical boundaries; he really does buy her clothes, discuss future romance, and treat the accumulated relationship as durable. The later chapter does **not** reveal these acts to have been meaningless. It reveals a second, concealed state operating underneath them: the same truth that enriches his family life has destroyed the imaginative equilibrium supporting his work.

The volume can therefore be read in five movements.

First, the bonus fiction reconstructs the family deception from Keisuke, Natsume, Chihiro, and pre-debut Itsuki's earlier history. The concealment is reclassified from “Chihiro's secret” into a family-system decision with distributed adult responsibility. It also roots Itsuki's `主人公` rhetoric in an earlier vocational conflict with his father: when Keisuke argues that only a talented few can live as novelists, Itsuki answers not by denying rarity but by declaring that he will become one of the special few--a `主人公`. [IM-V10-E001-IM-V10-E032]

Second, the disclosure chapters resolve immediate family and romantic categories. Itsuki accepts Chihiro, Nayuta worries about romantic competition, and Itsuki explicitly distinguishes sister from girlfriend. Chihiro then rehearses ordinary sibling behavior with Itsuki and discovers that a real relation cannot be produced merely by copying stereotypes. [IM-V10-E033-IM-V10-E107]

Third, the middle volume generalizes that re-authorship problem across work and adulthood. Chihiro experiences categorical jealousy and replacement pressure; Kyo chooses Branch Hill over the easier prestige route and debates unwritten industry conventions with Toki; Nayuta turns twenty; Itsuki's anime succeeds; and Chihiro experiments with feminine presentation without treating earlier presentation as erased. [IM-V10-E108-IM-V10-E196]

Fourth, `砂漠`, `謝謝台湾`, and `秋葉原デート２` widen the problem beyond the household. Professional arrival is exposed as non-terminal; translation and foreign readership make authorship mediated rather than local; Haruto begins choosing creative risk over pure market optimization; and Chihiro chooses education for optionality rather than claiming a prematurely fixed vocation. [IM-V10-E197-IM-V10-E313]

Fifth, the grave visit and anime finale seem to offer an approach to maturity and success, only for `主人公になりたかったのに` to reveal the volume's hidden cost. Itsuki knows he should face Keisuke. His anime has succeeded. His sales are up. Nayuta raises marriage. Yet he still says `まだだ`, and then the final chapter reveals four months of writer's block beginning immediately after Chihiro's disclosure. [IM-V10-E314-IM-V10-E376]

The resulting form is not a cheap twist. It is a demonstration of the corpus's long-standing epistemic principle: **outward continuity can be real while focalized knowledge remains incomplete**. V10 makes `IM-C009` unusually important because the withheld interior perspective changes causal interpretation without invalidating the observed external facts.

## 3. Focalization, epistemic limits, and retroactive reinterpretation

### 3.1 The bonus fiction redistributes responsibility

The bonus chapter is not merely explanatory backstory. By focalizing Keisuke and Natsume before the concealment arrangement, it prevents the reader from assigning the entire deception to Chihiro. Keisuke's life after Waka's death is shaped by work, grief, and rigid control; Natsume enters through precarious service work; Chihiro intensely wants an ordinary family; and Itsuki's published fiction creates a believable safety concern for the adults. Chihiro proposes presenting as a boy, but the adults accept and operationalize the plan. [IM-V10-E001-IM-V10-E032]

This shifts the ethical question from “why did Chihiro lie?” to “why did a family system decide that concealment was the least-bad path?” The answer remains contestable. Keisuke's fear is not invented, but neither does concern automatically legitimate years of misclassification. V10 does not close `IM-C023`; it makes the care/control problem better specified.

### 3.2 Natsume supplies an early anti-transparency principle

When Keisuke reads `黙示録ノ妹`, he reacts as though the protagonist's desires reveal Itsuki's literal intentions. Natsume makes the obvious but essential objection: this is a novel, and the son may not think exactly as the protagonist does. Yet V10 also gives Keisuke a reason not to dismiss the text entirely, because Itsuki himself insists that the novel contains “all of me.” [IM-V10-E013-IM-V10-E032]

The correct reading is therefore neither “fiction is autobiography” nor “fiction tells us nothing about its creator.” V10 strengthens the series' more difficult model: fiction can contain intense self-investment while remaining transformed, partial, generic, exaggerated, and non-transparent. That distinction becomes decisive at the end when real life changes Itsuki's ability to inhabit the imaginative position from which he once wrote.

### 3.3 The last chapter changes causal interpretation, not event truth

For most of the volume, Itsuki's acceptance of Chihiro looks almost frictionless. He jokes, experiments with sibling roles, protects sexual boundaries, buys clothes, and appears energized by the fantasy-come-true quality of discovering a sister. The final chapter reveals that the first exuberant reaction involved `空元気`: he was suppressing bewilderment and tears while performing high-energy delight. [IM-V10-E340-IM-V10-E376]

This does not license the opposite overread that “he never wanted Chihiro as a sister.” The same final chapter confirms genuine affection and real-sibling ethical recognition. The concealed distress concerns the collision between actual family and his old creative identity. V10's focalization therefore requires a layered model:

1. outward acceptance is fact;
2. relational affection is fact;
3. performed exuberance partly masks distress;
4. creative self-collapse is fact;
5. hatred or rejection of Chihiro is **not** established.

That layered epistemic state should remain the controlling V10 endpoint.

## 4. Character state: Chihiro

### 4.1 `僕` began as a family-acquisition technology

The bonus fiction gives unusually strong evidence for the historical function of Chihiro's masculine presentation. Faced with the possibility that Keisuke will abandon marriage because he fears exposing a daughter to Itsuki's sister obsession, Chihiro proposes pretending to be a boy. The linguistic transition is explicit: `私は……いえ、僕は──家族がほしいです`. [IM-V10-E023-IM-V10-E032]

This does **not** prove that every later use of `僕` is purely instrumental, nor does it authorize a total theory of Chihiro's gender identity from one line. It does establish the origin of the concealment voice inside a concrete relational strategy: “if I am a younger brother, Itsuki will treat me normally, and this family can exist.” The masculine self-reference is therefore inseparable from attachment, safety, and family formation at origin.

### 4.2 Disclosure does not erase the Chihiro who existed before it

After the truth comes out, Chihiro does not instantaneously replace clothing, speech, hobbies, competence, or temperament. The following day, she remains in broadly unisex clothing and is hesitant about moving immediately into an overtly feminine presentation. Later shopping allows experimentation, but this is presented as newly available choice rather than recovery of a single supposedly “true” costume. [IM-V10-E080-IM-V10-E107] [IM-V10-E182-IM-V10-E196]

This matters for `IM-C015`. Correct category recognition is real and consequential, but the old period cannot be discarded as unreal. Chihiro cooked, studied, joked, befriended people, loved robotics, and formed relationships during concealment. Reclassification changes how those relations are interpreted and what boundaries apply; it does not delete the person who enacted them.

### 4.3 Chihiro's sibling ideal begins in care, not erotic fantasy

`原風景` traces Chihiro's desire for an older brother to an ordinary childhood scene: a slightly older boy protecting and consoling a younger girl. Chihiro experiences the relation as beautiful and associates it with a complete, happy family. [IM-V10-E068-IM-V10-E079]

The contrast with Itsuki is fundamental. Both desire a sibling category, but Chihiro's formative image is mundane care, while Itsuki's public creative identity eroticizes and idealizes fictional sisterhood. The series should not collapse their uses of `妹`/`兄` into a shared fetish grammar simply because both are intense.

### 4.4 Real siblinghood has to be authored

The strongest Chihiro chapter in V10 is `妹のいる生活`. She and Itsuki ask what “real siblings” are supposed to do. Chihiro tries `お兄ちゃん`; they discuss preferred address; they imitate hostile sibling banter; they consider examples from other families. None provides a satisfactory script. Itsuki's own aesthetic language becomes relational theory: what matters is not which address is objectively superior, but what fits the character. The chapter finally rejects forced imitation and concludes that they should become siblings in their own way and at their own pace. [IM-V10-E080-IM-V10-E107]

This is not a claim that social scripts are irrelevant. They need scripts in order to test them. The point is that **borrowed form becomes authentic only through selective adoption**. Siblinghood is therefore neither pure biology nor pure performance. Biological/category truth changes the relation; accumulated practice gives it content; and future practice must author its local form.

### 4.5 Replacement anxiety moves from usefulness to categorical uniqueness

Aoba and Nadeshiko pressure Chihiro differently. Aoba can cook for Itsuki and already occupies an openly welcomed pseudo-sibling role. Nadeshiko can call him `おにーちゃん` with effortless childish legitimacy. Chihiro's jealousy therefore no longer concerns only household usefulness; it concerns whether “sister” is itself a transferable character slot. [IM-V10-E108-IM-V10-E134]

This strengthens `IM-C031` but also limits it. Chihiro's attachment insecurity is not reducible to chores. Once practical indispensability weakens, categorical uniqueness can become the next object of security. Yet the game competition also shows Chihiro as more than a caregiver: she becomes absorbed, competitive, skilled, and temporarily neglectful of domestic function. Her selfhood is broad enough to exceed the role she fears losing.

### 4.6 Education and emerging romance remain open-ended

By `秋葉原デート２`, Chihiro has decided to attend university not because she has discovered a singular calling but because breadth and time may help her discover one. This is a mature answer to the vocation problem precisely because it refuses premature certainty. [IM-V10-E281-IM-V10-E313]

The Haruto scene ends with Chihiro's cheeks reddening as she watches him, while the narration explicitly states that neither Haruto nor Chihiro herself notices. This is evidence of an emerging affective possibility, not a conscious romantic commitment. The correct V10 state is **pre-reflective attraction signal / self-recognition absent**. It should not be promoted into settled romance.

## 5. Character state: Itsuki

### 5.1 `主人公` begins earlier than Nayuta

The bonus fiction is crucial because it prevents the project's `主人公` analysis from becoming too romance-centric. Teenaged Itsuki's father argues that living as a novelist is possible only for a tiny group with special talent. Itsuki does not contest the rarity. He declares that he will become that exceptional handful--a `主人公`. [IM-V10-E013-IM-V10-E018]

The rhetoric therefore predates his relationship with Nayuta and begins as a technology of vocational exception: if ordinary probabilities say “you will fail,” Itsuki answers by narrating himself as the exceptional person to whom the ordinary distribution does not apply. Later, Nayuta gives that rhetoric a comparative-romantic form, but V10 reveals the older root underneath it.

This is why `IM-C014` needs another revision. `主人公` is not merely an ethical grammar of owned agency under uncertainty. In Itsuki, it is also a self-narrative that converts exceptional ambition into identity. That can motivate real action; it can also become brittle when the self who is supposed to inhabit the role changes.

### 5.2 Acceptance of Chihiro is grounded in history before fantasy

Itsuki's immediate answer to Chihiro matters because it supplies the most durable basis of their relationship: learning that Chihiro is a girl does not erase their memories, the care he has received, or the fact that the family arrangement caused them to meet. [IM-V10-E033-IM-V10-E055]

His sister-fetish excitement is real too. The scene deliberately contains both. A rigorous reading should not sanitize Itsuki into purely sober familial ethics, but it should also not let the comic “actual sister acquired” fantasy erase the first argument. Relational continuity and fantasy gratification coexist in the initial response.

### 5.3 Real family changes sexual meaning rather than fulfilling incest fantasy

The Nayuta chapter closes an ambiguity that outside observers could otherwise exploit. Itsuki does not treat Chihiro's correct classification as making her romantically available. He states the opposite: because she is `正真正銘の家族`, he will not look at her romantically now or in the future. `妹は妹、彼女は彼女`. [IM-V10-E056-IM-V10-E063]

This strongly supports `IM-C006`. The existence of erotic sister fiction in Itsuki's bibliography does not transparently predict real-incest desire. V10 makes the gap sharper precisely by placing the fictional and real categories next to each other.

The later boundary with Setsuna does the same thing in practice. Itsuki refuses sexualized access to Chihiro even “for work” once she is legible as his actual sister. The real relation changes what he considers permissible.

### 5.4 Itsuki tries to become a better boyfriend without solving comparison

The shopping chapter shows a quieter form of self-revision. Itsuki explains that he has decided to behave more properly as Nayuta's boyfriend and chooses a conventional birthstone gift over the kind of gag present he might previously have privileged. This is not a rejection of his eccentricity; it is deliberate adaptation to another person's role expectations. [IM-V10-E182-IM-V10-E196]

Yet the `２０歳` and anime-finale chapters show that comparative worth has migrated rather than disappeared. Drunk, Itsuki apologizes for keeping Nayuta waiting. Nayuta says she is already happy and can wait indefinitely. Later she suggests that the anime's success may finally have made them `釣り合う`; Itsuki mentally recalculates his sales and international standing, sees a possible moment of parity, and still concludes `まだだ`. [IM-V10-E162-IM-V10-E181] [IM-V10-E324-IM-V10-E339]

The original equality gate was genuinely rejected as a prerequisite for *dating*. V10 therefore must not falsely restore the V01-V06 formulation. What persists is a **future-progression gate**: marriage or final self-authorization still seems to require Itsuki to feel sufficiently equal. `IM-C008` remains true at the level of coupling while `IM-C014` absorbs the remaining pathology of comparison.

### 5.5 Success reaches a peak exactly when creator identity fails

The closing reversal is one of the series' strongest attacks on external achievement as a sufficient solution to self-worth. Itsuki gets much of what his earlier self wanted. The anime reaches its ending with stable quality. Sales multiply. Public response is strong. Taiwan makes the audience visibly international. Professional collaborators are waiting. In career terms, he has powerful evidence that he became one of the exceptional creators his father once doubted. [IM-V10-E236-IM-V10-E280] [IM-V10-E324-IM-V10-E339]

But writer's block began the day after Chihiro's disclosure. The problem is not ordinary lack of motivation: Taiwan gives him renewed desire to write, and the people waiting for the next book give him abundant external reasons. He still cannot produce pages. [IM-V10-E340-IM-V10-E376]

The distinction between **motivation and capacity** is essential. Creator-care infrastructure can support recovery; it cannot command a work into existence when the imaginative self that generated the work has become incoherent.

### 5.6 The fulfilled fantasy destroys the fantasy's creative function

Itsuki rereads his earlier sister novels and experiences them as `キモチワルイ`. He no longer understands how he identified with brother protagonists who desired their sisters romantically. The narrative names the consequence directly: the identity of a novelist who sincerely loves sister fiction collapses. [IM-V10-E340-IM-V10-E376]

This is not merely embarrassment about old work. It is a change in imaginative identification. The real relation has made one fictional stance inaccessible to him. The irony is severe: Itsuki spent years performing a desire for “a little sister”; acquiring a real sister does not satisfy the creative desire. It removes the distance that allowed the desire to function as fiction.

The terminal `僕は──俺は` deserves LF attention. The line momentarily brings two self-registers into collision before `俺`: the person who became a protagonist by his own power is not experienced as a stable, singular self. V10 does not provide enough evidence to assign a fixed psychological meaning to `僕` versus `俺` here, but the typographic hesitation is formally congruent with the split the narration then names between the flamboyant sister-obsessed writer and the serious young man struggling with love and family.

The closing answer--that of course he is not simply “happy to have a sister”--therefore cannot be paraphrased as “Itsuki dislikes Chihiro.” The preceding pages establish the opposite at the relational level. The negation concerns the catastrophic cost that actual siblinghood has imposed on the self-structure that organized his writing.

## 6. Character state: Kyo

### 6.1 Vocation becomes a concrete choice

V09 established that editing is what Kyo presently wants. V10 moves one step further: she receives a concrete offer and chooses Branch Hill rather than accepting the easier route into Gift Publishing. Her reason is not that Branch Hill is objectively superior. She wants to discover something that belongs to her rather than a life obtained because she happens to be friends with Itsuki and Nayuta; she wants to test herself somewhere she cannot simply lean on those relationships. [IM-V10-E135-IM-V10-E161]

This **majorly strengthens `IM-C028`**. Aptitude, demand, desire, and actual institutional choice have now all separated and then partially recombined. Sustainable fit remains unproven, but Kyo is no longer merely considering a vocation in the abstract.

The choice also needs critique. “I want to stand somewhere I cannot depend on anyone” can express genuine autonomy, but in a character with an established tendency to compensate for insecurity through usefulness, it can also recruit a punitive ideal of self-sufficiency. V10 does not decide which tendency will dominate.

### 6.2 The three-year rule becomes a harder institutional problem

Kyo initially maintains the V09 critique: a consequential rule that can get a writer cut should be written down. Toki then supplies counterevidence. Kanzaka's situation involved not just an innocent novice versus a cruel institution but serious communication failures, resistance to editorial advice, and difficulty assigning another editor. More importantly, making every informal expectation contractual would change the ecology of publishing. A strict three-year rule becomes something closer to exclusivity; rigid deadlines with explicit liability could reduce the informal flexibility that currently allows creators to recover from lateness or move when relations fail. [IM-V10-E135-IM-V10-E161]

This forces `IM-C034` to be **REVISED**, not simply strengthened. V09's core insight about asymmetric enforcement survives, but V10 demonstrates a countervailing danger:

> **Informality is not inherently coercive. Its ethical risk depends on whether expectations are intelligible, negotiable, revisable, and accountable under unequal bargaining power. Formalization can protect weaker parties, but it can also convert flexible norms into stricter coercive structures.**

The right analytic object is therefore not “written rule versus unwritten rule.” It is the distribution of knowledge, discretion, exit options, bargaining leverage, and good-faith judgment.

### 6.3 Kyo's empathy ideal remains ethically valuable but risky

V10 does not erase V09's `一緒に泣く` editor ideal. The `砂漠` chapter actually shows its language propagating through Haruto, who uses Kyo's earlier recognition--that failure after sincere effort is simply sad--to meet Aioi's despair without pretending effort guarantees success. [IM-V10-E197-IM-V10-E235]

This is a positive consequence of Kyo's ethic. But because creator life is shown as a desert where even talented, diligent people can disappear, an editor who treats every loss as personally absorbable needs boundaries as much as empathy. V10 therefore strengthens the value of Kyo's care language while keeping its sustainability question open.

## 7. Character state: Nayuta

Nayuta's V10 state is less structurally dominant than Chihiro's or Itsuki's, but several important longitudinal pressures remain.

First, Chihiro's reclassification triggers insecurity. Nayuta briefly fears that the newly real sister might become a romantic rival, which is psychologically consistent with her established sensitivity to being replaced. Itsuki's categorical reassurance is unusually clear and allows the couple to return to intimacy without requiring Nayuta to become professionally or aesthetically less exceptional. [IM-V10-E056-IM-V10-E063]

Second, turning twenty becomes a small enacted adulthood ritual. The label “twenty” matters because it permits a long-imagined shared drinking experience with Itsuki; age is made meaningful through practice. [IM-V10-E162-IM-V10-E181]

Third, Nayuta continues to state that she is happy **now** while Itsuki frames their future as delayed by his insufficiency. This reproduces the asymmetry identified in V08: Nayuta can articulate present fulfillment and patient future desire at the same time, while Itsuki converts the future into a problem of comparative worth. [IM-V10-E162-IM-V10-E181] [IM-V10-E324-IM-V10-E339]

There is still insufficient evidence to close `IM-C025`. Nayuta's earlier tendency to make effort a condition of lovability is not decisively reactivated or resolved here. V10 should therefore PRESERVE/OPEN that claim rather than infer recovery from her happiness.

## 8. Character state: Haruto, Aioi Hajime, and creator self-authorship

### 8.1 Haruto's craftsmanship acquires a more personal telos

Haruto has long been one of the series' strongest examples of professional adaptability: he can study a market, use templates intelligently, accept editorial realities, and keep producing. V10 does not reject that competence. It gives him a desire that cannot be reduced to optimization. In Akihabara, after reflecting on Itsuki, younger writers, and his own career, he decides he wants to make an unapologetically strong SF/robot work and ultimately see a machine he designed turned into a plastic model. [IM-V10-E281-IM-V10-E313]

This is important because authentic desire emerges **through** a professional history rather than before it. Haruto does not discover a pristine childhood essence that market work corrupted. He re-authors his telos after years of craft, rivalry, mentoring, and observation. This strengthens `IM-C035`: vocation and creative commitment can be historically produced without becoming inauthentic.

### 8.2 Aioi's desert rejects arrival narratives

Aioi has already done many things that aspiring writers imagine as endpoints: she won a major award, debuted, worked hard, published multiple volumes, and entered a professional network. Her series is still canceled. She describes the field as a desert because the place reached by total effort turns out not to be the goal at all. [IM-V10-E197-IM-V10-E235]

Haruto does not answer with a meritocratic guarantee. He agrees that knowledge, skill, and talent are necessary but not sufficient; even extraordinary people may fail to find an oasis. His encouragement is explicitly not evidence-based certainty. He can only say that if she keeps moving she **may** encounter a moment that makes the suffering feel worthwhile.

This strongly extends `IM-C021` and `IM-C022`. Professional legitimation is a threshold, not durable security. Awards, talent, editorial support, market packaging, niche intensity, and luck interact; none can be promoted into a complete merit meter.

### 8.3 Editorial causality is unusually explicit

The survey of recent newcomers provides concrete causal diversity. One prize-winning manuscript is improved so extensively through editorial research, supervision, and revision that an observer credits roughly seventy percent of its success to the editor. Another writer's editor relationship collapses. A niche fetish book may remain commercially limited yet sustain passionate readers. A good work can be canceled because packaging and positioning fail. A major-prize winner can still disappear after several volumes. [IM-V10-E197-IM-V10-E235]

The series is not saying “editors create books instead of writers.” It is showing why single-agent explanations of outcome are inadequate. Authorship remains real while professional success is distributed.

### 8.4 Haruto refuses to exploit Aioi's acute vulnerability

In despair, Aioi reaches toward Haruto for romantic/sexual consolation. Haruto is attracted to her, but he physically refuses to convert the moment into a relationship. He also refuses to tell her that success is guaranteed. His immediate demand is that she finish the final volume she owes the work and its readers. [IM-V10-E197-IM-V10-E235]

The moment can sound harsh if abstracted from context, but the ethical point is strong: he does not use a distressed creator's dependency as an opening for gratification. He treats her as an author still capable of making a decision and finishing a work. That is respect for agency without a promise that agency controls outcomes.

Aioi's later return to old glasses and unfashionable writing clothes is similarly not “giving up on femininity.” She understands that Haruto's transformative reward memory centered on Kyo, accepts the romantic situation, and returns to the clothes in which she works. The visual framing of Aioi at the desk reinforces writing as her chosen battle position. [IM-V10-E197-IM-V10-E235] [IM-V10-E406]

## 9. Relationship ecology after reclassification

### 9.1 Chihiro / Itsuki: continuity plus changed boundaries

V10 supplies one of the clearest demonstrations in the corpus that relationship continuity and category continuity are not the same thing. Chihiro and Itsuki's memories do not vanish when `弟` becomes `妹`. Their accumulated care remains causally real. Yet category correction immediately changes norms around sex, touch, address, clothing, jealousy, and public explanation. [IM-V10-E033-IM-V10-E107]

The relationship should therefore be modeled in two layers:

- **continuity layer:** trust, meals, shared history, household care, humor, mutual familiarity, remembered support;
- **reclassification layer:** real-sibling status, sexual prohibition, new public language, possible feminine presentation, renegotiated address, altered third-party expectations.

Neither layer is more “real” than the other. V10's maturity lies in refusing a choice between biology and practice.

### 9.2 Nayuta / Itsuki: secure couplehood, deferred future

The romantic relation remains secure enough that Itsuki can make an absolute family/romance distinction and Nayuta can accept it. Their sexual intimacy is mutual. The unresolved issue is no longer “will Itsuki permit himself to love her?” but “what future threshold will he require before allowing the relationship to progress?” [IM-V10-E056-IM-V10-E063] [IM-V10-E324-IM-V10-E339]

This distinction is necessary to avoid undoing the V04-V06 checkpoint. The equality gate was genuinely broken at coupling. V10 reveals a successor structure rather than a restoration of the old one.

### 9.3 Haruto / Chihiro: friendship survives category correction, embodied behavior changes

Haruto's response is instructive because it lacks Itsuki's fetish structure. Chihiro remains an important friend after he learns she is a girl. Yet Haruto catches himself before casually patting her head: a behavior that felt unmarked with a male younger friend becomes questionable with a girl who is neither his sister nor partner. [IM-V10-E281-IM-V10-E313]

This is small but important evidence for `IM-C024`: status labels do not maintain relationships, but labels can legitimately change behavioral boundaries. Continuity is not sameness.

The final blush then introduces a possible future transformation without retroactively redefining the friendship. Chihiro does not consciously know herself to be in love in V10.

### 9.4 Itsuki / Keisuke: understanding increases before repair

At his mother's grave, Itsuki now understands why his father reacted so strongly to the sister novels and why the family concealed Chihiro. He recognizes that his old picture of Keisuke as simply cold was incomplete. [IM-V10-E314-IM-V10-E323]

But understanding motive is not reconciliation. Itsuki still has not faced him directly. V10 therefore strengthens the distinction between explanatory empathy and ethical closure. The project should keep `IM-C023` open.

### 9.5 Kyo / Nayuta / Chihiro: care remains a network, not a possession

Kyo's physical intervention against Miyakuni's treatment of Chihiro shows the roomshare/friend network surviving Chihiro's reclassification. Kyo does not treat the new sister label as giving Itsuki exclusive authority over Chihiro's body or identity. Care remains distributed. [IM-V10-E064-IM-V10-E067]

The scene is comic and aggressive, but its social function is clear: Miyakuni's claim that a real sister is an exploitable public asset is contained by another woman who recognizes Chihiro's personhood before the fetish category.

## 10. Creative labor, publishing institutions, and professional precarity

### 10.1 The field is not a merit ladder

`砂漠` is one of the series' most concentrated attacks on simple meritocracy. Award quality, manuscript quality, editorial contribution, packaging, niche audience, timing, sales trajectory, and cancellation all diverge. A writer can do everything apparently required and still discover that professional arrival was only entry into a probabilistic field. [IM-V10-E197-IM-V10-E235]

This does not produce nihilism. The novel continues to treat skill, effort, judgment, and care as real causal variables. What it rejects is sufficiency. `努力したから成功する` and `成功したから価値が高い` are both too simple.

### 10.2 Reliability and capacity: V10 supplies the corrective institution

V08 and V09 showed systems treating reliable workers as expandable capacity. V10 gives Toki a better response when Itsuki finally admits the writing problem: he recommends rest despite the business damage. This is a meaningful strengthening of `IM-C032` because the series now shows the inverse practice--a professional recognizes that increasing pressure on a blocked creator may deepen rather than solve the problem. [IM-V10-E340-IM-V10-E376]

Yet Itsuki resists, believing Taiwan or renewed motivation will restart him. It does not. Capacity is not equivalent to willingness, external demand, gratitude, or inspiration.

### 10.3 Informality can protect and exploit

The Kyo/Toki discussion should become a standing warning against a too-simple institutional critique. Unwritten norms can obscure consequential expectations and permit selective enforcement. The same lack of formalization can preserve escape routes and human discretion. [IM-V10-E135-IM-V10-E161]

The institutional question is therefore closer to administrative justice than to formalism: who knows the norm; who can negotiate it; who bears the cost of misunderstanding; who controls exceptions; whether reasons are communicable; whether there is a meaningful exit; and whether discretion is exercised in good faith.

This richer formulation is especially appropriate to publishing, where strict standardization can collide with the irregularity of creative work.

### 10.4 Public success does not produce the next manuscript

The anime finale is successful enough to multiply sales dramatically. This is the sort of media-mix event that industry actors often want because it enlarges the source audience. But the business logic creates a painful irony: the moment of maximum demand for a new volume coincides with Itsuki's inability to write it. [IM-V10-E324-IM-V10-E376]

The series thereby separates **demand generation** from **creative supply**. Publicity can create readers; it cannot guarantee the author still has access to the imaginative structure needed to make the next work.

## 11. Adaptation, translation, and transformed fidelity

### 11.1 `銀色景色`: fidelity beneath surface resemblance

Haruto's praise of the live-action adaptation of Nayuta's work is unusually explicit. He notes that many adaptations fail by producing weak costume imitation; this film instead understands the source and optimizes for cinema, reproducing something more essential than external appearance. [IM-V10-E162-IM-V10-E181]

This strongly reinforces `IM-C017`. Fidelity is not a percentage of unchanged details. It is an argument about which priorities of the source must remain legible after medium change.

### 11.2 Anime completion: transformed work can remain recognizably the same project

Itsuki's own anime reaches its ending after the production instability established in V09. Its survival depends on many contributors and on changes that the novelist did not personally execute. Itsuki's gratitude is therefore not self-erasure. The adapted object remains meaningfully connected to his work while its actual production is distributed. [IM-V10-E324-IM-V10-E339]

This continues the corpus's distinction between **authorship** and **realization**. A work can be “Itsuki's series” without pretending the anime is solely his labor.

### 11.3 Translation creates a harder authorship problem

Taiwan initially bothers Itsuki because language appears constitutive of what he makes. He writes at full intensity for Japanese readers, in Japanese. If a translation succeeds because a translator is exceptionally good, he wonders whether the translated object belongs more to the translator than to him. [IM-V10-E236-IM-V10-E280]

The question is not answered philosophically in dialogue. It is answered phenomenologically through reception. Readers greet him as the author; some have imported and read Japanese originals; translated and untranslated readership overlap; local publishers repackage Japanese titles into different catalog structures; and Taiwanese writers produce their own light novels inside the same broader form. The translated work is clearly mediated, but mediation does not make Itsuki irrelevant.

The best V10 formulation is therefore:

> **Translation is neither transparent transmission nor authorship replacement. It is a transformed realization in which translator labor is causally constitutive while the source author's authorship remains historically and relationally legible.**

The project should preserve this as an extension of `IM-C017`/`IM-C018` rather than opening a redundant new claim.

### 11.4 Foreign readership destabilizes the imagined audience

Itsuki wrote without adjusting for overseas readers. Taiwan reveals that the actual audience exceeds the audience imagined during composition. Some readers encounter a translation; some read Japanese; some meet the work through manga or anime. [IM-V10-E236-IM-V10-E280]

This strongly advances `IM-C029` and `IM-C033`. Public delivery is not just broadcasting information to a known recipient class. Works migrate through languages, editions, adaptations, events, and reader practices the author does not control.

## 12. Public communication and reader reciprocity

The Taiwan signing is a strong positive counterpart to V08's leaked announcement and V09's broadcast-access questions. Itsuki becomes aware that the event's economics and logistics do not obviously explain why so many people are spending scarce time to see him. His response is to make the signature itself a deliberate act of care rather than a mechanical transaction. [IM-V10-E236-IM-V10-E280]

This **majorly strengthens `IM-C033`**. Creator-reader labor includes more than the semantic content of a novel. It includes timing, access, presentation, signatures, translation, public appearance, and the acknowledgment that audience attention is finite human time.

There is also an important limit. Reader love can motivate Itsuki and make his work feel real across borders, but it cannot cure the writer's block. Reception is causally productive without being omnipotent. That distinction protects `IM-C020` from romantic inflation.

## 13. Talent, success, failure, and the desert model

V10 pushes several previously separate claims into one field model.

Aioi's cancellation shows that major prize recognition and genuine effort do not create security. Kamo's case shows that a successful authorial object can contain enormous editorial contribution without erasing the writer's achievement. The niche spanking work shows that small-scale commercial performance and intense audience fit can coexist. Poor packaging can damage an otherwise good book. Itsuki's anime shows public success arriving while the author becomes unable to produce. Haruto chooses a riskier personal dream despite understanding market constraints. [IM-V10-E197-IM-V10-E235] [IM-V10-E281-IM-V10-E313] [IM-V10-E324-IM-V10-E376]

Together these cases establish a more mature professional ontology:

- talent affects probability, not destiny;
- effort affects process, not guaranteed outcome;
- awards signal and allocate, but do not finish a career;
- editors can materially transform work;
- packaging and market fit can alter survival;
- niche intensity can be a legitimate success mode;
- public success can coexist with private creative collapse;
- persistence can create opportunities but cannot guarantee an oasis;
- creator goals can rationally differ from maximizing rank or sales.

The desert metaphor is therefore not simple pessimism. It is an anti-teleological model of professional life: there is no single finish line after which uncertainty disappears.

## 14. Family, siblinghood, and the ethics of category truth

### 14.1 Concealment was a distributed family decision

V10 substantially changes the moral map of Chihiro's concealment by showing that the adults endorsed it. Keisuke's concern originated in his reading of Itsuki's fiction and fear for Chihiro. Natsume initially supplies the fiction/reality distinction, but the family ultimately accepts Chihiro's proposed male presentation as the path that preserves the marriage and creates the family she wants. [IM-V10-E001-IM-V10-E032]

The decision cannot be evaluated solely through its benign aim. It burdens Chihiro with long-term concealment and deprives Itsuki of informed family knowledge. At the same time, the resulting relationship with Chihiro becomes deeply valuable to both. V10 refuses the fantasy of a clean counterfactual in which one can simply erase the deception and retain every good consequence.

### 14.2 Recognition makes real-sibling ethics explicit

Once the truth is known, Itsuki rapidly distinguishes family from romance. This is not merely a verbal defense to Nayuta; it reorganizes behavior. Sexualized sibling fantasy becomes unacceptable when attached to the actual person Chihiro. [IM-V10-E033-IM-V10-E063]

This is the strongest evidence yet that Itsuki's `妹` obsession has multiple ontological layers. Fictional `妹` can carry erotic and idealized grammar. Actual `妹` activates family prohibition, care, and responsibility. The shared noun does not collapse the categories.

### 14.3 Social truth does not dictate local script

V10's central family insight is that category correction creates obligations but does not provide a complete behavioral script. Chihiro and Itsuki are now correctly siblings, but “correctly siblings” does not tell them whether to say `お兄ちゃん`, how often to fight, who cooks, how affectionate they should be, or which sibling stereotype to imitate. [IM-V10-E080-IM-V10-E107]

That is why the chapter's conclusion--be siblings in their own way--matters beyond sentiment. It is the family version of transformed fidelity: preserve the relation's governing truth while adapting its form to the people actually inhabiting it.

## 15. Sexuality, comedy, and boundaries

V10 contains sexualized comedy around Chihiro's reclassification, but the ethical pattern is not “the reveal licenses access.” Miyakuni's response pushes aggressively toward objectification and modeling; Kyo physically contains her. Setsuna's curiosity does not override Itsuki's actual-sister boundary. Nayuta's romantic worry is answered through categorical separation rather than experimentation. [IM-V10-E056-IM-V10-E067]

The clothing chapter is also important because feminine presentation is not automatically sexualized into availability. Chihiro can experiment with clothes while Itsuki simultaneously imagines a future in which she might have a boyfriend and says he could accept a good partner if that makes her happy. [IM-V10-E182-IM-V10-E196]

One potentially misleading passage is Chihiro's answer that the person she thinks about most is Itsuki. V10 does not license an incest inference from this. The surrounding chapters have just established newly salient real-sibling attachment, Chihiro's lifelong older-brother ideal, and Itsuki's explicit family/romance distinction. At most, the line shows that Chihiro herself is inexperienced at parsing romantic feeling.

Comedy therefore continues the corpus rule frozen in `IM-C007`: scene-level consent and category analysis remain necessary. Comic extremity does not constitute ethical approval.

## 16. Games, social scripts, and identity rehearsal

V09 opened `IM-C036` through TRPG: a fictional role gave Chihiro a temporary space to inhabit the blocked identity of Itsuki's sister. V10 supplies recurrence, but importantly the recurrence changes form.

After disclosure, Chihiro no longer needs fiction to state the category. She and Itsuki instead rehearse **social scripts**: address forms, stereotyped insults, sibling fighting, role expectations, and comparisons with known siblings. These scripts fail when copied mechanically. [IM-V10-E080-IM-V10-E107]

The later Nadeshiko competition adds another rehearsal space. “Real sister” and “sister character” become rival claims performed through games. Chihiro's competitiveness reveals both category insecurity and personality beyond caregiving. [IM-V10-E108-IM-V10-E134]

`IM-C036` should therefore be **STRENGTHENED but remain OPEN in generality**. V10 supports a broader mechanism than TRPG alone:

> Role-play and borrowed social scripts can make identity positions experientially testable. They can reveal desire, discomfort, jealousy, and fit; they do not automatically determine the authentic form of the role.

The broader claim still rests mainly on Chihiro, so it should not yet become a universal theory of all play in the series.

## 17. Japanese voice, address, and identity-sensitive language

### 17.1 `私……僕`: origin of concealment register

The bonus chapter's `私は……いえ、僕は` is one of V10's most important LF observations. The correction happens at the moment Chihiro commits to the boy presentation in order to obtain a family. [IM-V10-E001-IM-V10-E032]

A translation that merely normalizes both pronouns into “I” loses the explicit linguistic act of self-reclassification. The line does not, by itself, settle every later question about Chihiro's gendered self-reference. It does establish that pronoun choice is narratively functional and tied to the concealment plan.

### 17.2 `兄さん` versus `お兄ちゃん`: character fit over hierarchy

After disclosure, Chihiro wonders whether `お兄ちゃん` is the more authentically sister-like address. Itsuki rejects a simple higher/lower ranking and prioritizes what fits the character. They retain `兄さん` because it is the voice their relationship actually developed. [IM-V10-E080-IM-V10-E107]

This is a linguistic miniature of V10's thesis: correct category does not require replacing lived voice with stereotypical form.

### 17.3 `妹は妹、彼女は彼女`

Itsuki's compact parallel phrasing is unusually important because it performs category separation syntactically. The same lexical family that fuels his fiction does not dissolve real-family and romantic roles into one continuum. [IM-V10-E056-IM-V10-E063]

### 17.4 `釣り合い`, `対等`, and migrated comparison

The marriage discussion reactivates the vocabulary of parity. Because the couple already exists, these terms can no longer be read as a simple dating prerequisite. Their new function is future authorization. Itsuki continues to treat comparative professional status as evidence relevant to whether he has earned the next relational step. [IM-V10-E324-IM-V10-E339]

### 17.5 `僕は──俺は`

The closing switch/hesitation should remain preserved in Japanese in any evidence-sensitive synthesis. It is not safe to assign `僕` a single hidden-self meaning and `俺` a single public-self meaning from this one occurrence. Formally, however, the interruption occurs precisely where Itsuki tries to assert that he became a protagonist by his own power and immediately confronts the collapse of the identity that wanted that status. [IM-V10-E340-IM-V10-E376]

### 17.6 `キモチワルイ` and defamiliarized self-reading

The katakana rendering `キモチワルイ` visually sharpens the estrangement of Itsuki's response before ordinary `気持ち悪い` recurs in the following self-reading. The effect is not merely “he now judges incest bad.” He experiences his own familiar prose as alien because the position of identification has changed. [IM-V10-E340-IM-V10-E376]

## 19. Counterevidence, anti-overread protections, and unresolved contradictions

V10 is a volume in which several emotionally satisfying simplifications are directly contradicted by the text. The following protections should travel forward into later synthesis.

1. **“Itsuki accepts Chihiro, so the disclosure is painless.”** False. His relational acceptance is genuine, but the disclosure triggers a four-month creative crisis that he conceals beneath functional behavior. [IM-V10-E340-IM-V10-E376]

2. **“Itsuki's final line means he hates Chihiro or wishes she were not family.”** Unsupported. V10 repeatedly establishes affection, memory continuity, protective boundaries, and genuine sibling care. The final negation concerns the cost of real siblinghood to his creator identity, not a demonstrated rejection of Chihiro as a person. [IM-V10-E033-IM-V10-E063] [IM-V10-E340-IM-V10-E376]

3. **“Keisuke was simply right about Itsuki all along.”** Too strong. V10 makes Keisuke's safety fear intelligible and shows why Itsuki's fiction alarmed him, but it also repeats that fiction is not transparent biography and leaves the long deception ethically unresolved. Understanding motive is not total exoneration. [IM-V10-E001-IM-V10-E032] [IM-V10-E314-IM-V10-E323]

4. **“Because Itsuki put all of himself into his debut novel, the protagonist literally was him.”** Wrong. Self-investment and one-to-one autobiography are different propositions. Natsume explicitly raises that distinction, and Itsuki's later real-sister boundary demonstrates it. [IM-V10-E013-IM-V10-E032] [IM-V10-E056-IM-V10-E063]

5. **“Chihiro's `僕` was entirely false, while `私` is the complete authentic self.”** Overstated. V10 gives a concrete instrumental origin for `僕` in the concealment plan, but years of lived speech and identity cannot be erased by that origin alone. Post-disclosure Chihiro changes selectively rather than replacing herself wholesale. [IM-V10-E023-IM-V10-E032] [IM-V10-E080-IM-V10-E107]

6. **“Real siblings do not need social scripts.”** False. Chihiro and Itsuki use available sibling scripts to orient themselves; the point is that copied scripts do not automatically fit. Social grammar is material for self-authorship, not irrelevant noise. [IM-V10-E080-IM-V10-E107]

7. **“Kyo rejects Gift because Gift is simply corrupt and Branch Hill is morally pure.”** Unsupported. Her stated motive is to obtain a path not given to her through famous friends and to test herself. V10 provides no basis for idealizing Branch Hill. [IM-V10-E135-IM-V10-E161]

8. **“The three-year custom proves unwritten norms are inherently abusive.”** V10 directly complicates this. Hidden and selectively enforced expectations remain dangerous, but formal exclusivity can remove flexibility and create new burdens. [IM-V10-E135-IM-V10-E161]

9. **“Therefore unwritten norms are good and Kyo's V09 criticism was wrong.”** Also false. Toki's defense depends on judgment and flexible escape; V09's bargaining-power problem remains. V10 requires a revised two-sided institutional theory, not reversal.

10. **“Aioi proves that creators should always keep going no matter the cost.”** Overread. Haruto explicitly cannot guarantee an oasis. The chapter values the possibility of meaningful persistence while acknowledging that talented, hardworking people disappear. [IM-V10-E197-IM-V10-E235]

11. **“Haruto's refusal of Aioi is proof he feels nothing for her.”** Unsupported. The scene acknowledges attraction while showing that he refuses to exploit acute distress and remains emotionally oriented toward Kyo.

12. **“Translation either transparently preserves the source or becomes the translator's independent work.”** V10 supports neither binary. Translation is constitutive mediation under continuing source authorship. [IM-V10-E236-IM-V10-E280]

13. **“Taiwan fixes Itsuki's writer's block.”** False. It restores motivation and gratitude but not writing capacity. [IM-V10-E236-IM-V10-E280] [IM-V10-E340-IM-V10-E376]

14. **“Anime success resolves Itsuki's equality problem.”** False. He briefly considers parity and then rejects the moment as sufficient. [IM-V10-E324-IM-V10-E339]

15. **“Nayuta is necessarily miserable because Itsuki says he is keeping her waiting.”** Unsupported. Nayuta explicitly says she is already sufficiently happy and willing to wait. Itsuki's description of delay is partly his own comparative frame. [IM-V10-E162-IM-V10-E181]

16. **“Chihiro is consciously in love with Haruto by the end of V10.”** Too strong. The narration supplies a blush that neither participant, including Chihiro, recognizes. The correct state is emergent affective possibility. [IM-V10-E281-IM-V10-E313]

17. **“Haruto's robot/SF dream means he repudiates market knowledge.”** Unsupported. The significance is that professional competence is no longer his complete telos; he chooses a risk he understands.

18. **“Itsuki's writer's block is ordinary burnout caused by overwork.”** The text ties onset specifically to the day after Chihiro's disclosure and to the collapse of his imaginative identification with sister fiction. General workload may matter to recovery, but V10 supplies a more specific proximal conflict. [IM-V10-E340-IM-V10-E376]

19. **“The afterword's continuity explanation resolves the V08/V10 discrepancy.”** It does not. The author explicitly acknowledges a slight contradiction and jokingly proposes fuzzy memory. This is PF about composition/provenance, not permission to silently harmonize two narrative accounts. The discrepancy should remain recorded. [IM-V10-E377-IM-V10-E386]

20. **“The afterword's statement about withheld psychology makes every earlier scene secretly false.”** No. It explains a focalization design. Earlier observable actions remain textual facts; their interior causal interpretation becomes more complex.

### 20.1 The major V10 revisions

The most important transition is `IM-C014`. The earlier checkpoint's “owned agency under uncertainty” remains a useful description of the healthy potential of `主人公`, but V10 demonstrates that Itsuki's specific protagonist rhetoric also carries an older exceptionalist burden. He wanted to become the rare person who defeats ordinary probability. By the end of V10 he has substantial evidence of doing so and still cannot write. `主人公` therefore cannot serve as a stable external achievement category.

`IM-C015` undergoes the block's largest relational phase change. The disclosure is now received. Itsuki accepts Chihiro as sister, memories persist, and the relation acquires new boundaries. Yet the truth also has consequences no simple “recognition is good” thesis can absorb: correct family knowledge destabilizes Itsuki's creative self. Family truth is ethically important without being consequence-free.

`IM-C034` receives the largest institutional revision. V09 correctly identified hidden asymmetric rules as coercive risk. V10 demonstrates why a mature theory must also model the protective function of non-contractual discretion.

`IM-C006` becomes more precise. The corpus has repeatedly resisted transparent autobiography; V10 now shows the opposite error would also be serious. Fiction can be non-literal and still depend deeply on the creator's lived capacity to identify with its imaginative structures.

### 20.2 New claim `IM-C037`

`IM-C037` is opened because the final writer's-block mechanism is not fully captured by adaptation, protagonist rhetoric, family identity, or creator-support infrastructure.

**Provisional formulation:**

> Some creative identities are sustained by a productive separation between fantasy and lived role. If life collapses that separation, fulfillment can become estrangement: the creator may retain motivation, audience, professional success, and technical competence while losing the imaginative position from which the work was authored.

V10 provides one exceptionally strong case, not enough recurrence to universalize the mechanism. The claim therefore remains OPEN and must be tested against V11-V12 rather than promoted immediately to series law.
