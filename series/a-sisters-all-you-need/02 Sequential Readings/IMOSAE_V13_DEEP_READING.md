## 1. Executive thesis: making a chosen self public without surrendering ownership

Volume 13 asks what happens **after** a person has re-authored a role and then has to expose that role to institutions, audiences, colleagues, lovers, communities, and other people's interpretations.

The central V13 movement is:

> **self-authorship under circulation and institutionalization**

V10-V12 established that identity can survive rupture through re-authored commitment rather than static restoration. V13 tests whether those commitments remain self-authored once they are no longer private.

Kyo's editorial dream becomes employment, authority, conflict, deadlines, creator management, and the possibility of harming someone despite good intentions. Chihiro's desire for an ordinary university community becomes a question of whether she must conceal herself to preserve group equilibrium. Itsuki's private love-letter novel, written for exactly one reader, becomes a commercial book through Kyo's intervention and reaches a mass audience. Haruto and Kyo finally acknowledge mutual romantic attraction but refuse to let romance automatically supersede Kyo's chosen professional priority. Ashley and Kaizu institutionalize chosen interdependence through marriage while carrying Yuu's memory into the ceremony. Itsuki's re-authored creative mode becomes not just a private proof of concept but a public new series.

The volume's answer is not that publicity corrupts authenticity or that institutions complete it. Instead:

> **A chosen commitment remains self-authored when circulation adds relationships, obligations, interpretations, and constraints without erasing the person's continuing capacity to endorse, revise, refuse, explain, and share it.**

That principle produces three especially important revisions.

First, **expertise requires legibility**. A competent editor can still create coercive experience if decisions arrive without reasons, consultation, or an intelligible account of constraints. Izumi's former GF editor was serious, experienced, and sincerely trying to make his debut succeed. The failure is therefore not reducible to villainy. From Izumi's side, however, a pattern of unexplained illustrator choice, imposed revision, and post-failure encouragement taught him that editors do not listen. Kyo nearly repeats that history when she merely says desired illustrators are unavailable without explaining why. Ayane repairs the conflict not by surrendering editorial judgment but by restoring the context in which judgment can be understood and negotiated.

Second, **community stability cannot ethically be outsourced to one person's self-concealment**. Chihiro invents a fake older boyfriend to protect a male-heavy TRPG circle from romantic competition. Haruto's response is sharper than a generic command to tell the truth: if a community can survive only by making one person carry the burden of concealment, the community's members are not jointly maintaining it. The ethical object is not truth in the abstract; it is a distribution of responsibility compatible with individual agency.

Third, **original address and later circulation are distinct**. `主人公になりたい` begins as a one-reader love letter. That origin is not falsified when Kyo reads it, becomes changed by it, edits it, and persuades Itsuki and Nayuta to let it reach a public. Nor does later public acclaim prove it secretly “belonged” to the public from the beginning. The work accumulates additional relations. V13 therefore revises the private-art claim from a static audience category into a history of address: a work can be privately originated and later publicly recontextualized without either stage retroactively cancelling the other.

The governing V13 formulation is:

> **Self-authorship is not privacy, solitude, or freedom from influence. It is the continuing ability to own a commitment while making it legible and negotiable among other agents. Mature institutions and relationships do not remove dependence; they distribute authority, explanation, risk, and recognition so that participation does not require one person to disappear inside the role.**

## 2. Volume architecture: from private ownership to public consequence

V13 is structurally a final-act “front half,” something the author afterword itself calls the first half of the conclusion. The main narrative nevertheless has its own coherent progression.

1. **Chihiro enters ordinary post-disclosure youth:** graduation, female peers, university, hobby community.
2. **Kyo enters the institution she chose:** Branch Hill transforms editor aspiration into professional responsibility.
3. **Multiple models of editorial authority appear:** Ayane, Jogamine, GF precedent, Kyo's mistakes, Haruto's client perspective.
4. **Itsuki's V12 creative solution is tested at scale:** craft remains legitimate for existing series; hybridization appears in new public work.
5. **Private art crosses an audience boundary:** Kyo wants to publish the one-reader manuscript because it changed her.
6. **External recognition returns to the protagonist problem:** Itsuki's depiction of Kyo helps Kyo see a version of herself she cannot yet fully believe.
7. **Romance is reordered rather than denied:** Kyo acknowledges Haruto romantically but keeps editing as current first priority.
8. **Interdependence becomes institution:** Ashley/Kaizu marry and explicitly include Yuu's memory rather than treating marriage as replacement.
9. **Public reception exceeds authorial targeting:** the private-origin work becomes Itsuki's most acclaimed public work.
10. **Itsuki walks forward rather than “arrives”:** the proposal closes the present action, followed only by the V13 time-jump marker.

The volume therefore keeps turning apparently terminal identities into processes. New graduate does not mean full editor. Commercial success does not mean finished growth. Mutual romantic love does not mean immediate dating. A private work need not remain permanently private. Marriage does not erase grief. A masterpiece becomes a new standard rather than an endpoint.

## 3. Kyo I — entering the profession without borrowing prestige

### 3.1 Independent entry is real, but uniqueness is harder than independence

Kyo begins work at Branch Hill in April. She has her own desk, a defined editorial office, a trainer in Mitsudou Ayane, and the ordinary insecurity of being the only new graduate in that part of the company. This is a major shift from the GF part-time role: she now occupies the institution she previously observed from its margins.

Her hiring story preserves the self-authorship problem. Kyo deliberately did **not** maximize the job-market value of her relationships with Itsuki, Nayuta, Haruto, and the GF staff because she wanted the offer to feel won `自分の力` rather than inherited from proximity to successful creators. This does not mean using networks would have been immoral. It means Kyo was specifically testing whether the vocation could stand as hers.

Then Ayane destabilizes the test. Ayane too entered the LN field through a history connected to Itsuki: Itsuki once lent her light novels; the relationship helped shape the editor path that V11 had already established as independently durable. Kyo's response is pain at learning that her own route is not `オンリーワン`.

That pain reveals the next stage of `IM-C035`. Independence from borrowed prestige is not the same as uniqueness. A self-authored life does not require a historically unprecedented path. Kyo has to learn that “mine” cannot mean “nobody else was ever influenced by the same people or made a structurally similar choice.”

### 3.2 Private access is not automatically professional capital

Ayane is an intense Nayuta fan and dreams of working with her. Kyo could immediately leverage friendship and household access to create professional advantage. She refuses to do so casually. Later, when Branch Hill's new label could obviously benefit from a creator like Nayuta, Kyo again resists treating private proximity as a business shortcut.

This is not a universal ban on friends working together—the volume later makes Kyo/Itsuki editorial partnership central. The issue is **conversion without consent**. Private access does not silently become institutional entitlement merely because the opportunity would be useful.

V13 thus adds an important professional boundary to the series' recurring care/authority architecture: when a relationship crosses domains, the crossing itself has to become legible and chosen.

## 4. Kyo II — Branch Hill and the freedom to become the wrong kind of editor

### 4.1 Jogamine's company philosophy is plural but not innocent

Branch Hill president Jogamine Nobunaga articulates an unusually permissive company philosophy: the firm exists so employees can do things they find `面白い`, provided those activities can support the business. Ayane's new light-novel label exists because *she* wanted to create it, not because a central executive ordered the market strategy.

Jogamine remembers Kyo's interview ideal: she wanted to become `作家さんと一緒に泣くことができる編集者`. After V12, Kyo has already learned that merely standing beside a suffering creator cannot be the whole job. Jogamine does not replace her ideal with a company-approved model. He tells her, in effect: if that is your ideal, become that editor; the company should be a place in which you can.

The freedom is demanding because nobody will hand her `これが正しい仕事`. Kyo realizes that pursuing an ideal without being led requires `確固たる自分自身`.

This strongly advances the protagonist framework into institutional life. Self-authorship here is not escape from employment; it is the burden of exercising professional freedom inside an organization.

But Branch Hill must not be romanticized. Ayane recounts Jogamine's earlier training regime—enormous responsibility, late-night criticism, and behavior she still describes as deeply damaging even while acknowledging that she learned. The company remains profit-bound. “Freedom” does not absolve management of power or erase the possibility of coercive training.

### 4.2 No universal editor style

V13 increasingly refuses a singular ideal editor. Ayane is exceptionally good at entering creators' emotional worlds. Jogamine is incisive and institution-building but can be interpersonally oblivious. Toki is developmentally attentive and blunt. Haruto values reliable `ほうれんそう`. Kyo is direct, emotionally invested, and still learning when directness becomes pressure.

The mature professional question becomes not “Which editor archetype is correct?” but:

> **Which obligations are role-minimums, which strengths are personal style, and which interventions require the creator's informed cooperation?**

That question dominates the Izumi conflict.

## 5. Izumi/Kamizaka: a failed debut re-enters the field under a new contract of trust

### 5.1 A writer can return without restoring the old route

The web novelist `漬物ステーキ` turns out to be the former GF newcomer published as 神坂蒼真, whose real name is 和泉颯太. After the failed debut, one-book cancellation, editor conflict, and GF ban, he stopped writing. Aoba's later success helps provoke his return, but the route changes: he writes web fiction that fits current tastes and receives several publication offers.

Crucially, he had already rejected offers. The fact that a web work is commercially attractive does not mean its creator must want professional publication. Ayane explicitly warns Kyo that publication can add noise, pressure, and cancellation risk to something that works perfectly well as a hobby. Professionalization is therefore not a universal upgrade path.

Izumi accepts Branch Hill only on a relational condition:

> `白川さんが担当じゃないなら、書籍化はお断りします`

Kyo's first real assignment is thus founded not merely on institutional allocation but on creator-specific trust.

### 5.2 Trust does not eliminate authority conflict

Once Kyo becomes his editor, she must do things friendship-like reassurance cannot accomplish. She wants the web material rebuilt into a satisfying volume rather than dumped unchanged into print. She has to reject unavailable prestige illustrators and recommend less famous creators who fit the work. Ayane reminds her: `作家さんの要望を全部通すのが編集者の仕事じゃない`.

That principle is necessary. An editor who merely executes every creator preference would abandon editorial judgment.

V13 then shows why the opposite principle—“the expert decides”—is also insufficient.

## 6. `暗黒騎士`: expertise, legibility, and the ethics of being right

### 6.1 The conflict begins with a consultation failure on both sides

Izumi submits a manuscript that does not contain the restructuring Kyo believed they had agreed upon. Kyo reasonably thinks he may have sent the wrong file. Izumi replies that he changed his mind. Kyo is angry because he did so without consultation. She sends a controlled request for reconsideration, then nearly escalates to an email explicitly warning that his behavior makes future trust difficult.

Ayane stops her.

The point is not that email is inherently bad. Ayane herself acknowledges records and communication preferences matter. The immediate problem is that the exchange has become a channel for mutually hostile inference. Kyo thinks she is calmly explaining professional necessity. Izumi reads another editor deciding what happens to his work.

### 6.2 Competent benevolence can still become experienced coercion

At the meeting, Izumi says the key line:

> `どうしてこっちばっかり、編集の言いなりにならなきゃいけないんですか……！ これは……僕の作品なのにッ！`

His history makes the statement more than adolescent resistance. His GF editor chose an illustrator without consultation, pushed revisions, and eventually justified the illustrator decision as an editor's job. The editor was not incompetent or malicious; Kyo knows him as a respected veteran who worked seriously and wanted the book to succeed. The book nevertheless failed, after which Izumi received a “next time” encouragement that sounded weightless against the consequences he had borne.

That history is analytically important because it removes an easy moral sorting. **Good intention plus expertise did not guarantee a relationship in which authority felt legitimate.**

Kyo then discovers she has reproduced a smaller version of the same pattern. When Izumi requested two illustrators, she answered that they were impossible but did not explain that they were full-time game-company employees unavailable for this kind of outside commission. From her side the constraint was obvious industry information. From his side it was another unexplained veto.

V13 thereby majorly strengthens `IM-C034`: legibility is not customer-service polish added after a correct decision. In asymmetric professional relations, explanation is part of the conditions under which judgment can become meaningfully contestable and consent-like cooperation can exist.

### 6.3 Ayane restores context before securing compliance

Ayane does not solve the conflict by telling Kyo to let Izumi do whatever he wants. She explains the illustrator constraint, invites Izumi to describe his prior experience, then apparently “wastes” hours on cake and conversation about favorite works, hobbies, origins, and romance.

The result is not magical friendship. It changes the interpretive context. Izumi no longer sees the revision request only as another faceless institutional command. Kyo no longer sees resistance only as evidence that he is a `難アリ` writer. Izumi then voluntarily accepts more revision.

Ayane herself resists universalization. Some creators prefer businesslike distance. Her own method is to know collaborators well because that helps *her* work. It is a style, not a law.

This is one of V13's clearest professional theses:

> **Trust is not agreement. Trust is a condition under which disagreement can be interpreted as something other than attempted domination.**

### 6.4 Even experts misread relationships

Jogamine later tells Kyo that Ayane's ability to enter people's hearts is essentially inimitable. He recruited her because of it. Yet Kyo has to tell him that Ayane genuinely dislikes him. His surprise is the punch line and the analytic point: a person can be extraordinarily skilled at reading creators and still be badly misread by another skilled editor—or misread that editor in return.

Professional reputation does not confer omniscience. Ensemble perspective remains necessary.

## 7. Haruto's `ほうれんそう`: the minimum viable ethics of collaboration

Kyo eventually uses a date with Haruto partly to ask what writers actually need from editors.

Haruto first names high-effort editorial support he genuinely appreciates: research, appointments, bookstore visits, specialized expertise. Then he refuses to make that extraordinary labor a baseline demand because no editor could sustainably provide it for every author.

His minimum is much simpler:

> `ほうれんそう` — 報告・連絡・相談.

He argues that a huge share of author-editor conflict comes from failures of reporting, contact, and consultation. Delayed feedback breeds distrust. Lying is especially damaging because discovery retroactively contaminates other communication.

Haruto's percentage language should not be treated as a real-world statistic. The analytic value lies in the professional principle: **reliability is communicative before it is heroic**.

This reinforces `IM-C032` as well. Sustainable systems cannot define good care as extraordinary personal overextension. A reliable minimum must be something finite people can repeat.

## 8. Kyo III — `主人公` becomes a professional rather than romantic priority

### 8.1 Romance is not denied; it is ranked

During Word Wolf aftermath, Chihiro directly tells Kyo that she still loves Haruto after rejection. Kyo's response turns the series' romance hierarchy into an explicit question. What does it mean to “lose”? Is a person a loser simply because romance does not succeed? Is romantic union automatically more important than friendship, work, or dream?

Kyo admits she is not indifferent to Haruto. She wants romance. She feels jealousy. But:

> `少なくとも今の京にとって、一番は恋愛じゃない。`
>
> `まずは編集者として一人前になりたい。`
>
> `主人公になりたい。`

This is not anti-romance. V13 later makes that unmistakable when Kyo tells Haruto that she probably likes him as a man.

The important distinction is temporal and self-authored priority. Kyo refuses the genre assumption that the moment mutual attraction becomes available, every other aspiration must move to second place.

### 8.2 Haruto and Kyo make non-immediate reciprocity explicit

In `君が好き`, Haruto confesses again. His monologue is deliberately anti-cinematic: he cannot exploit a crying, vulnerable Kyo through the familiar fictional shortcut of sudden kiss → sex → relationship. He hates his own harmlessness because he wants more than gratitude or “good person” status, but he still refuses to turn opportunity into entitlement.

He states his desire verbally:

> `オレは、白川京さんが好きです。オレと付き合ってください`

Kyo answers that she probably does love him romantically. Then she declines immediate dating because her present priority is becoming an editor she can recognize and standing beside the creators she admires. Her answer is neither a clean rejection nor a promise that binds him indefinitely:

> when she can properly recognize herself, if Haruto still loves her, `そのときは、あたしと付き合ってください`.

She explicitly says he does not have to wait. Haruto chooses to wait.

At the V13 boundary, therefore, **Haruto and Kyo are not yet a dating couple**. They have mutual romantic acknowledgment plus a voluntarily accepted future possibility conditioned on Kyo's own self-recognition.

This complicates `IM-C008`. V07-V12 correctly rejected professional equality as an objective prerequisite for romantic legitimacy. V13 does not restore that rule. It shows that an individual can still make *her own readiness* depend on vocational self-respect. The gate is psychologically and ethically hers, not a universal measure of who is worthy of love.

## 9. Chihiro: truth after the fantasy of universal responsibility

### 9.1 University gives Chihiro a place chosen from hobby rather than usefulness

Chihiro's university TRPG circle matters because she describes it as the first place she joined simply because she liked the activity. That is a direct continuation of V09's movement from “where can I be useful?” toward desire and curiosity.

The circle, however, quickly makes her gender socially consequential. As the only woman among many men, she becomes an `オタサーの姫`. The group develops an implicit no-poaching equilibrium to avoid rivalry. When asked whether she has a boyfriend, Chihiro tries to protect the community by inventing one.

Her lie escalates into an absurd claim that she has dated a much older working adult for three years, accidentally implying a relationship beginning when she was fifteen. She then recruits Haruto to impersonate the boyfriend and conceal his author identity.

The comedy is elaborate, but the ethical structure is serious.

### 9.2 Haruto rejects one-person community maintenance

Once the performance collapses and the circle members discover Haruto is a writer they already read, the feared community catastrophe does not automatically occur. Haruto tells Chihiro to tell the truth.

His strongest formulation is:

> `誰か一人に負担を強いることでしか維持できないコミュニティなんて、崩壊してもしょうがない。大事な場所を守るのは、その中にいる全員の願いと努力であるべき`

This is new enough to open `IM-C041`:

> **Communities and relationships are ethically more sustainable when maintenance burdens are distributed among participants rather than outsourced to one member's concealment, indispensability, or self-sacrifice. A structure that survives only because one person absorbs the cost is already evidence of fragility.**

This claim remains OPEN because V13 provides one unusually explicit formulation, though it converges with Kyo/Chihiro usefulness-security material and creator-capacity claims.

### 9.3 Truth does not create responsibility for every consequence

Chihiro immediately connects the issue to the revelation of her sex/sibling status and Itsuki's collapse. She asks the hard question: if truth hurts people, would a lie sometimes be kinder?

Haruto does not deny consequences. He says that even if Itsuki had never recovered, the problem would still have been Itsuki's. Chihiro cannot assume responsibility for the lives of everyone affected by her truthful existence; trying to do so is `おこがましい`.

This is not a universal narrator-issued command that truth must always override consequence. Haruto has his own history with concealment and may be especially hostile to it. The stronger analytic claim is narrower: **causal impact does not automatically create unlimited moral jurisdiction over other people's adaptation to the truth**.

That substantially strengthens `IM-C039`.

### 9.4 Rejection becomes survivable repetition rather than catastrophe

After deciding to tell the circle the truth, Chihiro confesses to Haruto again: `本当に私の彼氏になってくれませんか`. He rejects her again.

Unlike V11's first rejection, the second is incorporated into ordinary persistence. Chihiro remains disappointed but can joke, continue the relationship, keep calling him `晴彦さん`, and continue liking him. V13 therefore demonstrates that romantic non-selection no longer threatens her overall belonging in the same way earlier usefulness/replacement fears did.

This is important evidence for `IM-C031`: diversified identity and belonging do not remove insecurity, but they can make one failed bid survivable.

## 10. Itsuki after V12: craft remains legitimate, integration becomes reproducible

### 10.1 He does not retroactively condemn the craftsman mode

V13 opens a crucial correction to any triumphalist reading of V12. Itsuki accepts that he can no longer pour the old erotic sister passion into `妹すべ` and `妹法大戦`. He decides to finish those existing series through calculated craftsmanship.

And he explicitly rejects the idea that this is wrong or inferior.

The works remain successful with readers. The craft mode provides experience he considers necessary for future growth. Nayuta, exercising her own taste, chooses not to keep reading those two and instead waits for the new work. Neither preference is universalized.

This strongly confirms the checkpoint's plural grammar: V12 did not reveal that “soulful art” was the true mode and calculation false. Different tasks can legitimately call for different relations between technique, passion, history, and audience.

### 10.2 `L↔Rデイズ` proves re-authorship can become public practice

The most important creative development comes in `変わりゆく者たち`. Toki reads the first chapter of Itsuki's new ensemble youth novel and finds something that neither the pre-slump nor immediate post-slump model explains.

The work follows calculated structure while allowing extreme personality and passion to erupt inside it. Sister characters remain present but stand alongside other protagonists rather than monopolizing the imaginative world.

Toki names the result:

> pre-slump artist + post-slump craftsman = hybrid
>
> `変化ではなく、昇華`

V12 had shown integration in a one-reader private task. V13 demonstrates it in a **public new serial**, which is more powerful evidence for `IM-C018` and `IM-C037`. Re-authorship is becoming reproducible rather than remaining a single crisis performance.

Yet the volume preserves humility. Itsuki still treats the V12 private manuscript as a higher benchmark he is imitating. The new work does not prove he has permanently mastered that level. Growth becomes direction rather than completed state.

## 11. Toki and the possibility of editorial succession

Toki's reaction to `L↔Rデイズ` contains a subtle professional transition. He has spent years helping Itsuki develop, sometimes acting as critic, manager, institutional translator, and stabilizer. Now he finds himself with almost nothing to say except “let me read the next chapter.”

The feeling is partly pride and partly loss. Itsuki may have reached a stage where he no longer needs exactly the same editor relationship; another editor might make him fly farther.

V13 does not establish a formal handoff at this point. But it strengthens the series' view that good developmental support should not define success as permanent dependence. An editor can causally shape a creator without owning the creator's future trajectory.

That prepares the later Kyo/Itsuki partnership without making it a betrayal of Toki.

## 12. `主人公になりたい`: private address becomes public history

### 12.1 Kyo is the first evidence that the one-reader work has a second life

Kyo read the V12 manuscript after Nayuta. Itsuki assumed anyone other than the intended reader would find the work embarrassing or bad. Kyo does not.

She reads two layers simultaneously:

- an intense love letter directed to Nayuta;
- an orthodox youth novel about a young man confronting death, family conflict, defeat, inferiority, loss, and aspiration.

The second layer affects her profoundly. The phrase `主人公になりたい`, already latent in her, acquires explicit form. The narration states: `人の想いは物語を通して伝染する` and notes that Itsuki does not know his book has produced another protagonist.

The phrasing is narratively strong but should not be converted into mystical causation. Kyo already had ambitions. The work supplies language and recognition that crystallize them.

### 12.2 Kyo decides to become Itsuki's editor, not merely his admirer

During `1st Priority`, Kyo asks Itsuki what her strength is. His answer—her `バカみたいに真っ直ぐ` quality—is deliberately double-edged. Her ignorance once let her challenge industry deference during the anime process. Professionalization has since taught her status, atmosphere, and constraint. Itsuki warns that knowledge becomes dangerous if it turns into a shackle preventing action toward what she genuinely wants.

He does **not** tell her to reject expertise. He says acquire knowledge and experience, then consciously override them when they conflict with a sufficiently important purpose.

Kyo turns that lesson toward a concrete professional act. She asks:

> `羽島伊月先生。あなたが前に、なゆ一人のためだけに書いた小説を……あたしに担当させてくれませんか？`

and then:

> `あたしが一番担当したい作家は…あんた──羽島伊月なの`

This is the strongest V13 completion of the editor-vocation arc. Her work is no longer simply “editing as a career.” It contains a specific editorial desire: identify a work she believes needs a broader audience, obtain the relevant people's consent, accept institutional friction, and take responsibility for changing the work's circulation state.

### 12.3 Consent across institutional boundaries matters

Kyo does not simply upload or leak the manuscript. She asks Itsuki. She asks Nayuta, the original intended reader and person whose relationship is embedded in the text. She then goes to GF, where Kobe acknowledges that if Itsuki agrees, GF has no right to prevent publication through Branch Hill. Toki supports the project despite sadness.

The process matters as much as the outcome. Private-to-public transition is not appropriation because the relevant agents choose the transition.

This majorly strengthens `IM-C039` and `IM-C040`: influence creates stakes; shared projects can cross institutions; no single prior relationship automatically confers ownership.

## 13. Revision changes the work without erasing its origin

Itsuki does not merely publish the private manuscript unchanged. He rewrites it.

A thin, Yuma-like attractive rival is removed. Kuroyama Kei, previously a convenient supporting character, expands into what the narration calls a third protagonist. The heroine can be drawn toward Kei; intimacy becomes morally and emotionally complicated rather than a simple villain obstacle; the protagonist/Kei confrontation asks what love and happiness mean rather than merely delivering cathartic violence.

Then Itsuki tells Kyo that Kei is modeled on **Kyo herself**—not as literal transcript but as `俺から見た白川京像`, with fictional amplification. Nayuta immediately recognized the resemblance and the romantic implication.

This strongly advances `IM-C006`: autobiographical transformation can include not only the author's own life but the author's interpretation of other people. The represented person may learn something from the representation without the fiction becoming an objective psychological diagnosis.

Kyo's response is particularly important. She cannot fully identify with the brave, attractive Kei. But learning that people she values see something like Kei in her gives her evidence with which to challenge her own low self-assessment:

> she wants to live with enough confidence not to insult the people who sincerely find her admirable.

External recognition here does not replace self-authorship. It becomes **evidence available to self-interpretation**.

## 14. Public reception does not retroactively define artistic purpose

`主人公になりたい` becomes a major public success—larger in reception than Itsuki's deliberately audience-facing works. Readers call it a favorite, a masterpiece, life-changing, and a reason to want to become protagonists themselves.

The irony is explicit. The work consciously written for only one person becomes the one most intensely embraced by many.

This requires a revision of `IM-C016`:

> **Private one-reader art remains a legitimate original value mode, but original address does not permanently fix circulation or reception. With authorial/relational consent, a privately originated work can acquire public life, new readers, and new meanings without retroactively ceasing to have been written for one person.**

That is more precise than either “private art becomes public art” or “the public discovered its true universal purpose.” The temporal history matters.

It also protects `IM-C022`. Public acclaim is real and materially consequential. It is not proof that Itsuki's earlier works were worthless, that every private text should be published, or that mass reception is the ultimate aesthetic tribunal.

## 15. The masterpiece becomes a future burden—and Itsuki keeps walking

Success immediately creates a new comparison problem. Itsuki imagines every future book being measured against `主人公になりたい` and anticipates being dismissed as a one-hit wonder.

His response is now strikingly different from the old exceptionalist protagonist model. He does not promise instant transcendence or deny the fear. He identifies the published masterpiece as an ideal self ahead of him and chooses incremental continuation:

> `地道に一歩ずつ、一文字ずつ、自分が主人公の物語を進めていくことだけだ`

This is perhaps the strongest V13 strengthening of `IM-C014`. Protagonist agency has become **directional persistence under comparison**, not a proof of special status.

The narrative then literalizes the metaphor: `主人公が、ヒロインのもとへ歩いていく`.

Itsuki proposes to Nayuta—whose real name is explicitly given as 本田和子. The response is narratively elided as obvious. The safest V13 freeze is therefore: **proposal spoken; narration strongly implies acceptance but does not quote the answer**. Do not substitute V14 marital state for this V13 endpoint.

## 16. Haruto/Kyo and Chihiro: romance as agency under non-exclusive priority

The Haruto/Kyo/Chihiro triangle is unusually useful because V13 refuses to reduce it to winner/loser sorting.

Chihiro continues pursuing Haruto after explicit rejection. Kyo acknowledges that she likely loves Haruto and asks him to try again later if he still wants her. Haruto chooses to wait. Chihiro learns about that conditional future and continues to see a chance.

Kyo feels jealousy but also explicitly says she has no right to order Chihiro to stop. Chihiro admits that stereotypically “girlish” romantic competition is fun partly because she never had the chance to inhabit such youth before.

The ethically relevant facts are therefore:

- Haruto has repeatedly rejected Chihiro;
- Chihiro continues voluntarily, without Haruto promising reciprocity;
- Haruto and Kyo mutually acknowledge attraction but are not dating at V13;
- Kyo chooses work/self-recognition as current first priority;
- Haruto voluntarily agrees he will wait, while Kyo explicitly releases him from obligation to do so.

This is not a clean harem equilibrium, and it should not be projected beyond V13.

## 17. Ashley and Kaizu: chosen interdependence becomes marriage without erasing Yuu

The V12 checkpoint left `IM-C040` open after Ashley rejected Kaizu's attempt to exclude her from shared risk “for her own good.” V13 gives the strongest continuation available within the current boundary: they marry.

The wedding date is Yuu's death anniversary. A photograph of Yuu, Kaizu, and Ashley occupies the center of the welcome display. Ashley's vow is not “I have moved on from Yuu”; it is:

> `ワタシたちは、幸せになるわ。見ていてね、幽`

This is grief integrated into continuation rather than grief erased by replacement.

Marriage here is not proof that every shared-risk decision is wise. It does strengthen `IM-C040`: a relationship can institutionalize reciprocal interdependence while preserving memory, individual work, and known uncertainty.

## 18. Shiori and ordinary family continuation

V13's `アホ` material is intentionally low-register comedy, including deliberately ridiculous embodied sibling behavior around baby Shiori. It should not be inflated into a grand symbolic scene.

Its analytic value is modest but real: Shiori is no longer merely a V12 birth event or bookmark symbol. She is present inside ordinary household weirdness involving Itsuki and Chihiro as older siblings. Family continuity is being lived rather than only narrated as resolution.

The comic sexualized/nursing material must not be treated as evidence of actual incestuous desire between Itsuki and Chihiro. V10-V13 consistently distinguish real sibling category from the old erotic fantasy grammar.

## 19. Nadeshiko and `本物`: authenticity becomes parody when made absolute

Itsuki tells Nadeshiko that now that he has a real adorable sister, he has graduated from playing at pseudo-sisterhood with unrelated girls. He means this as a change in his own imaginative habits. Nadeshiko hears something harsher: `赤の他人` and `遊び` devalue a relationship category she had emotionally inhabited.

Her response is an absurd handwritten story about `本物`, obvious metafictional parody of another light-novel authenticity discourse. Toki's reaction becomes a joke about publishing it as if it belonged to that other series.

The scene is useful precisely because it should **not** be elevated into narrator-certified metaphysics. It shows how adult categorical language creates emotional consequences and how `本物` can become ridiculous when treated as a simple hierarchy of real over pseudo.

The safer family conclusion remains the frozen one: correct category truth matters, but enacted care that occurred under a pseudo-category was not therefore unreal.

## 20. Creative labor and industry ecology in V13

### 20.1 Professionalization is optional, not an automatic ascent

Izumi's web-writing history explicitly demonstrates that a creator may rationally refuse commercial publication. Publication can create deadlines, criticism, cancellation exposure, and loss of hobby pleasure. Commercial opportunity is an option, not a moral promotion.

### 20.2 Editors need repeatable minimums, not heroic overwork

Haruto's `ほうれんそう` argument resists defining good editing through extraordinary devotion. Intensive research and creator support can be valuable gifts, but a system that requires that level for every assignment would consume finite capacity.

### 20.3 Sales validate an outcome without validating every process

`暗黒騎士` sells well and gets an early reprint. That result does not prove Kyo's earlier mistakes were harmless. The volume carefully preserves process critique even when the product succeeds.

### 20.4 Editors are creative causal agents without becoming authors

Kyo's contribution to `主人公になりたい` is enormous: she identifies public potential, obtains permission, secures an institutional path, becomes editor, and prompts revision/circulation. Itsuki remains author. V13 gives perhaps the clearest example yet of distributed creative causality without authorship collapse.

## 21. Japanese voice and translation-sensitive findings

### 21.1 `一人前`

Kyo repeatedly wants to become `一人前の編集者`. “Full-fledged” is serviceable but can sound like a clear credential threshold. V13 makes the term psychologically fuzzy: Kyo does not know exactly what would make her count. She can already shepherd a successful book and still feel half-made. That ambiguity is precisely why Haruto/Kyo's conditional future cannot be converted into a simple calendar deadline.

### 21.2 `オンリーワン`

Kyo wants to be `オンリーワン`, first negatively—hurt that Ayane's path resembles hers—and later more maturely, when Jogamine reminds her she cannot become Ayane and must develop her own editor style. The term moves from **historical uniqueness** toward **non-substitutability of personal professional grammar**.

### 21.3 `主人公になりたい`

The phrase now circulates across subject positions:

- Itsuki's long-running self-story;
- Kyo's professional aspiration;
- the title of the private-origin/publicly edited novel;
- readers' reported response to that novel.

Its semantic force is therefore contagious without becoming identical across speakers. The work provides a language in which multiple people can articulate self-authorship.

### 21.4 `ほうれんそう`

The familiar workplace abbreviation for 報告・連絡・相談 carries more than procedural efficiency here. Haruto uses it as the minimum infrastructure of author-editor trust. The important translation-sensitive point is its ordinariness: the series deliberately contrasts routine communication with heroic editor devotion.

### 21.5 `言いなり`

Izumi's `編集の言いなり` expresses more than “following advice.” It carries submission to another's will. That is why Kyo's objectively reasonable professional recommendations become ethically unstable when reasons and alternatives are not legible.

### 21.6 `変化ではなく、昇華`

Toki's description of `L↔Rデイズ` rejects replacement logic. `昇華` marks transformation in which prior modes are preserved and reworked into something more capable. The word strongly supports `IM-C018` while remaining Toki's evaluative language rather than objective metaphysics.

### 21.7 `本物`

V13's Nadeshiko chapter treats `本物` as a comic/metafictional pressure point. It should not be conflated with V09's Aoba/Nayuta `本物` discourse without context. In both cases, however, the term becomes dangerous when it is used as if one authenticity category automatically nullified every lesser or derivative relation.

### 21.8 `晴彦さん`

Chihiro's use of Haruto's real given name survives the fake-boyfriend episode. The address marks a uniquely intimate linguistic relation even after the performance is disclosed as false. It does **not** make them a couple.

## 23. The bonus movie and media-mix self-reflection

The bonus rewrite begins with an explicit firewall: it is unrelated to the main story, and readers are warned that it may destroy the emotional afterglow of the main ending. Its escalating zombie/sister/incest absurdity should therefore be read as deliberately detached genre play.

The author afterword provides more analytically significant PF. Hirasaka says he regrets having stayed completely uninvolved with a previous live-action adaptation because the detachment protected him from hurt but also prevented him from gaining anything. By contrast, he participated heavily in the `妹さえ` anime and says he gained both tangible and intangible things.

That directly resonates with the series' own transformed-fidelity / collaborative-labor architecture:

> avoiding involvement can reduce risk, but participation can create experience unavailable from protected distance.

This remains authorial PF about his own career, not a command that every creator must always join every adaptation. It is especially useful because V13's main narrative is itself about entering relations and institutions rather than protecting identity through isolation.
