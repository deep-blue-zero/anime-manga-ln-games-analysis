# 『妹さえいればいい。』 Volume 07 Deep Reading

## 0. Authority, method, and prospective boundary

This is the canonical prospective deep reading of Volume 07. It inherits the two frozen checkpoints through V06 and treats V07 as the only new semantic evidence. Later numbered volumes are excluded from interpretation even though their raw files exist in the frozen source corpus. The Japanese EPUB is ultimate textual authority; `IMOSAE_V07_NORMALIZED_SOURCE.md` is the canonical retrieval derivative, and `IMOSAE-VISUAL-INDEX-1.0` governs visual locators.

Mainline prose, internal Q&A/profile pages, narrative illustrations, author afterword, and retailer/publisher payload remain source-class separated.

The reading tests, rather than assumes, the V06 freeze: actual Itsuki/Nayuta coupling; the rejection of professional equality as a prerequisite for love; relationship maintenance as practice; effort/lovability pathology; Kyo's unresolved self-authorization; Chihiro's family-role misrecognition; creative longevity; institutional mediation; and the distinction between aptitude, ambition, success, and well-being.

## 1. Executive thesis

Volume 07 is best understood as a **continuity-under-change** volume. Itsuki and Nayuta becoming a couple does not reset the ensemble, close the protagonist problem, or terminate the series' creator-life inquiry. Instead, the volume repeatedly asks what it means for a person, relationship, vocation, genre, or community to remain recognizably itself while circumstances change.

The opening makes this explicit. Itsuki has abandoned the rule that he must first become Nayuta's professional equal before accepting love, but he has **not** abandoned the ambition to become someone who can stand beside her. He returns from the hospital to the drama-CD recording because becoming a better creator still matters. Romantic revision therefore does not equal vocational surrender. The V06 revision of `主人公` is strengthened: self-authorship becomes capable of changing its rules without losing its projects. [IM-V07-E002–IM-V07-E012]

The middle of the volume distributes this problem across the ensemble. Kyo's friendship survives, but not because pain disappears; Chihiro fears that a girlfriend—and later even a robot vacuum—will make the practical role through which Chihiro secures belonging unnecessary; Itsuki's new emotional life leaks into his fiction despite his fantasy of pure aesthetic autonomy; and the apartment group continues to play games while their seating, reactions, and relational meanings have changed. The recurring social forms persist, but the people inside them are not frozen. [IM-V07-E020–IM-V07-E032] [IM-V07-E053–IM-V07-E064] [IM-V07-E082–IM-V07-E105] [IM-V07-E116–IM-V07-E127]

The creator chapters then scale the same question up to genre and industry. Aioi Hajime's fear of being dismissed as “template” fiction becomes a defense of specificity inside convention: shared structures do not abolish authorial intention, local variation, love of genre, or difference in execution. What looks repetitive from a distance may still be singular from within. [IM-V07-E144–IM-V07-E158]

The Sekigahara Yuu / Kaizu Makina history darkens the entire volume. Talent, commercial success, adaptation, reader love, and institutional prestige do not guarantee happiness or psychological safety. Public visibility multiplies not only readers but hostile spectators; the sensitivity required to write can become a vulnerability under mass criticism. Yuu's terminal illness prevents a crude causal story in which criticism simply “causes” her death, but her history still exposes the instability of success as a human telos. Kaizu's final counter-vow—to remain a moderately happy, ordinary working writer—revalues survival and ordinary well-being as an affirmative professional achievement, not a consolation prize for those who failed to become geniuses. [IM-V07-E159–IM-V07-E182]

Volume 07 therefore strengthens the project's anti-totalization thesis while adding a new formulation:

> **Continuity is not stasis. A durable self, relationship, genre, or career persists by absorbing change without allowing any single metric—romance, usefulness, originality, fame, talent, sales, or prestige—to define the whole person or work.**

## 2. Volume architecture and narrative movement

The volume is deliberately modular. Its early sequence is a chain of “reactions” to the new relationship: `恋人ができた日`, `女友達の反応`, `担当の反応`, `義弟の反応`, `親友の反応`, and `税理士の反応`. This makes coupling an ensemble event rather than a private romantic endpoint. The relationship becomes a perturbation propagated through friends, family roles, editorial practice, and self-image. [IM-V07-E001–IM-V07-E081]

`変わらないものと変わりゆくもの` then gathers the core five back into Itsuki's apartment. The ritual is recognizably old—food, teasing, games, competition—but formal details have shifted. Itsuki and Nayuta now sit together as a couple; Kyo, Haruto, and Chihiro react from altered positions; and the game converts interpersonal knowledge into explicit competitive inference. The chapter title states the volume's governing dialectic with unusual clarity. [IM-V07-E082–IM-V07-E105]

`LEVEL4` moves from social adjustment to sexual intimacy. It confirms that becoming a couple changes the ethical and practical conditions of bodily intimacy, while also preserving awkwardness, mutual inexperience, embarrassment, and the need to infer rather than assume readiness. [IM-V07-E106–IM-V07-E115]

`ライバル出現` relocates the “replacement” problem into Chihiro's domestic role. The supposed rival is a robot vacuum. The comedy works because Chihiro's fear is not really technological: it is the fear that usefulness is the basis of belonging and that efficient substitution can therefore become relational erasure. [IM-V07-E116–IM-V07-E127]

`出会いと再会` and `テンプレート・テンプテーション` expand the creator cohort with Aioi Hajime and Kasamatsu Aoba, turning generational mentorship, debut revision, genre convention, originality, attraction, and professional sociality into a new layer of the ensemble. [IM-V07-E128–IM-V07-E158]

`THE LAST CRY IN HADES` is the volume's retrospective center. It reconstructs Sekigahara Yuu and Kaizu's cohort history and reveals a prior-generation version of the same protagonist/side-character, genius/ordinary, success/happiness problems that have structured Itsuki, Nayuta, Haruto, and Kyo. [IM-V07-E159–IM-V07-E182]

`聖夜` returns to Itsuki and Nayuta after that history has been disclosed. Christmas is simultaneously romantic present and memorial date for the writer who made Itsuki want to become a novelist. The juxtaposition refuses to isolate romance from creative inheritance, grief, and household care. [IM-V07-E183–IM-V07-E192]

The bonus track then turns professional labor, genre mismatch, intertext, competition, and friendship into comedy. Itsuki begins with a principled demand for compensation, then joins once the unpaid task becomes personally and creatively meaningful. This is not simple hypocrisy; it distinguishes **a norm of fair compensation** from **voluntary gift labor chosen by the worker**. [IM-V07-E193–IM-V07-E202]

## 3. Narration, focalization, and epistemic structure

V07 broadens focalization beyond Itsuki at exactly the moment when a romance-centered reading might tempt the corpus to narrow. Kyo's reaction, Chihiro's fear, Haruto's self-restraint and mentorship, Toki's retrospective editorial view, Ashley's past, Kaizu's rivalry, and Yuu's public exposure all require access to perspectives that Itsuki cannot supply. This strongly preserves `IM-C009`: the series remains structurally ensemble-driven. [IM-V07-E020–IM-V07-E028] [IM-V07-E043–IM-V07-E052] [IM-V07-E053–IM-V07-E081] [IM-V07-E128–IM-V07-E182]

The volume also uses misrecognition productively. Itsuki sees Chihiro's domestic devotion but cannot explain why the “younger brother” wants to remain so involved. Chihiro's own fear is clearer to the reader than to Itsuki because the gender/family secret remains unresolved. The narration therefore continues to place the reader in an asymmetrical epistemic position without authorizing full certainty about Chihiro's deepest motive. [IM-V07-E060–IM-V07-E064]

Toki's focalized reflection is especially valuable because it limits an overcontrolled-editor reading. He recognizes Itsuki's growth over five years and explicitly concludes that the present Itsuki no longer needs to be controlled from the beginning. Yet Toki's observations remain a situated editor's judgment, not omniscient truth. [IM-V07-E043–IM-V07-E051]

The Yuu retrospective similarly layers viewpoints. Yuu, Kaizu, Ashley, Kobe, the publishing field, fans, non-reader spectators, and later survivors all occupy different positions. The chapter does not yield a single transparent causal account of Yuu's suffering; it instead demonstrates how public success produces an epistemically noisy environment in which praise, hostility, concern, market expansion, illness, and professional responsibility coexist. [IM-V07-E159–IM-V07-E182]

## 4. Character-state analysis

### 4.1 羽島伊月 — a revised rule, not a revised vocation

The strongest V07 correction is that V06 did not abolish Itsuki's protagonist ambition. The text directly states that his decision to become someone who can stand alongside Nayuta remains. What changes is the ambition's jurisdiction: it no longer determines whether love is permitted. [IM-V07-E003–IM-V07-E012]

Itsuki also demonstrates increased professional responsibility. After abandoning the recording for a genuine emergency, he returns, apologizes, recognizes that others must wait for his review, and resumes work. The volume therefore does not romanticize “choosing love” as exemption from collaborative obligation. [IM-V07-E014–IM-V07-E016]

His immediate new-project response is revealing. The desire to grow produces a competent slice-of-life concept, but Toki notices that Itsuki has unconsciously modeled multiple heroines on women he knows and written intimacy that reflects his newly altered life. Itsuki's horror is comic, but analytically important: experience enters fiction despite the author's fantasy of a clean boundary between “real girlfriend” and “pure sister ideal.” [IM-V07-E034–IM-V07-E042]

V07 thus strengthens `IM-C006`: fiction transforms life rather than reproducing it transparently. It also complicates Itsuki's self-concept. He is not simply abandoning fantasy for realism; rather, the expanded adult life supplies new materials that can alter what his fantasy practice is capable of producing.

### 4.2 可児那由多 — coupling does not immediately cure conditional self-worth

Nayuta enters V07 after bodily collapse and romantic realization. Her relationship with Itsuki is real, mutual, and physically advancing. Yet the volume is careful not to present coupling as instant psychological cure. In the hospital she explicitly recognizes that Kyo's kindness is something she could over-rely upon and says she should not `甘えすぎ`—depend too heavily on it. [IM-V07-E020–IM-V07-E032]

Her ordinary voice and behavior rapidly return: provocative sexual language, direct desire, playful competition, deference to Itsuki as `先輩`, and enjoyment of games remain recognizable. This continuity matters. A romantic status change does not replace her idiolect or personality architecture. [IM-V07-E082–IM-V07-E115]

At the same time, V07 offers no evidence that the V06 “I must strive to deserve love” schema has been fully dismantled. The relationship is a corrective experience, not proof of completed internalization. That question must remain OPEN.

### 4.3 白川京 — friendship after loss becomes active ethical practice

Kyo's reaction to the couple is one of V07's most mature relational moments. She experiences the situation as emotionally difficult, yet asks Itsuki to make Nayuta happy rather than punishing Nayuta or demanding that the couple hide their happiness. [IM-V07-E020–IM-V07-E027]

This strengthens the V06 maintenance thesis. Friendship after rejection is not “normality restored.” It is an active choice to preserve care under changed conditions. Kyo can remain hurt, remain attached, and still refuse to make another person's happiness illegitimate.

Her ability to participate in the apartment ritual also demonstrates continuity without denial. The group remains available to her, but the positions have changed. [IM-V07-E082–IM-V07-E105]

### 4.4 不破春斗 — mentorship, self-knowledge, and the value of ordinary craft

Haruto continues to function as relational infrastructure while gaining a more explicit mentor role. With Aioi Hajime he identifies her earlier contempt for “template” fiction, recognizes her actual love of isekai, and articulates a theory of genre convention as a platform for difference rather than evidence of creative emptiness. [IM-V07-E144–IM-V07-E158]

His romantic conduct also remains more cautious than his “yarichin prince” persona implies. He recognizes attraction to Hajime and his own vulnerability, but deliberately avoids a private meeting because he does not trust himself to keep a clean emotional boundary while still attached to Kyo. This is imperfect and comic, but it is positive evidence of self-knowledge rather than entitlement. [IM-V07-E128–IM-V07-E143]

Haruto's V07 position therefore strengthens the earlier revision of the “craftsman” binary. Market awareness, genre fluency, audience orientation, affection for convention, mentorship, insecurity, and genuine artistic attachment coexist.

### 4.5 羽島千尋 — usefulness as belonging becomes explicit

Chihiro receives one of the volume's most important character developments. The fear triggered by Itsuki's girlfriend is not framed primarily as dislike of Nayuta. Chihiro worries that a couple will no longer need the visits, cooking, cleaning, and care that constitute Chihiro's everyday place in Itsuki's life. [IM-V07-E053–IM-V07-E064]

This is a major sharpening of the project's “usefulness as self-worth” line. Kyo has often treated usefulness to exceptional people as permission to value herself; V07 shows a structurally related mechanism in Chihiro, where domestic usefulness becomes security of attachment.

The robot-vacuum episode makes the mechanism comic and almost painfully literal. Chihiro initially evaluates the machine by whether it can replace human cleaning, reassures themself that human work is better, remembers the unfulfilled promise of another outing with Itsuki, and eventually becomes affectionately attached to the machine. [IM-V07-E116–IM-V07-E127]

The episode is not anti-technology. Its real subject is role anxiety. Chihiro needs evidence that being family is not equivalent to performing irreplaceable domestic labor.

### 4.6 土岐健次郎 — creator management yields to creator development

V07 gives the strongest retrospective Toki material yet. His five-year history with Itsuki is explicitly one of mutual early immaturity, conflict, and growth. He now believes Itsuki possesses enough self-correction that total editorial control is no longer necessary. [IM-V07-E043–IM-V07-E047]

This strengthens the provisional Toki monograph's “adversarial caretaker” formulation while adding a developmental endpoint: good creator management should make some forms of management less necessary. Editorial success is not permanent dependency.

Toki also explicitly separates author personality from work persona. Bizarre work does not reliably imply bizarre author; ordinary work does not imply ordinary behavior. That is an important methodological warning for the project itself: fictional voice cannot be used as transparent biography. [IM-V07-E048–IM-V07-E052]

### 4.7 関ヶ原幽 and 海津真騎那 — genius and ordinariness as rival life-models

Yuu is deliberately constructed as an older-generation genius who differs from Nayuta in one critical respect: she is outward-facing and loves moving readers. She studies trends and research rather than remaining fully inside a self-contained imaginative world. [IM-V07-E159–IM-V07-E166]

Kaizu initially maps this disparity onto protagonist/mob logic. Yuu looks like the protagonist; he looks like background. V07 therefore reveals that Itsuki's status metaphor is not unique to him but belongs to a broader creator psychology of comparison. [IM-V07-E162–IM-V07-E168]

The later history destroys the assumption that protagonist-like success solves life. Yuu's popularity, adaptation, influence, and reader love coexist with mass hostility and terminal illness. Kaizu's response is not to claim hidden equality with Yuu. He chooses another telos: long, ordinary, moderately happy continuation. [IM-V07-E173–IM-V07-E182]

This is the volume's strongest support for `IM-C021`: longevity and survivable happiness are independent professional achievements.

## 5. Relationship ecology

### 5.1 Itsuki ↔ Nayuta — the ordinary work of becoming a couple

V07 converts a long-deferred romance into mundane practice. The first embrace is awkward. They must tell friends. They must navigate work, dates, sex, privacy, and other people's reactions. The relationship's value comes partly from this anti-climactic ordinariness: achievement creates new responsibilities rather than ending the story. [IM-V07-E002–IM-V07-E012] [IM-V07-E066–IM-V07-E074] [IM-V07-E106–IM-V07-E115] [IM-V07-E183–IM-V07-E192]

Itsuki's continued ambition and the couple's work schedules show that love does not replace vocation. The healthier change is jurisdictional: professional status no longer determines whether he is allowed to love her.

### 5.2 Itsuki ↔ Kyo — maintenance without erasure

Kyo's response makes the post-rejection friendship ethically substantive. She does not pretend the situation is painless, but she treats Nayuta's happiness as real and worth protecting. [IM-V07-E020–IM-V07-E027]

The apartment-group chapter then demonstrates that continued inclusion is enacted, not merely declared. [IM-V07-E082–IM-V07-E105]

### 5.3 Kyo ↔ Nayuta — rival/friend synthesis remains stable

V07 preserves the V06 synthesis: rivalry does not eliminate sister-like affection, and affection does not erase the romantic history. Nayuta recognizes Kyo's kindness and the risk of over-dependence; Kyo can threaten Itsuki on Nayuta's behalf. [IM-V07-E020–IM-V07-E027]

### 5.4 Itsuki ↔ Chihiro — role security remains the hidden fault line

Itsuki reassures Chihiro by emphasizing the couple's practical incapacity, which comforts Chihiro but does not answer the deeper question. Chihiro wants to know whether there will still be a place to come when practical necessity disappears. [IM-V07-E053–IM-V07-E064]

The text leaves Itsuki puzzled by the intensity of Chihiro's desire to care for him. The epistemic asymmetry therefore remains active.

### 5.5 Haruto ↔ Hajime / Kyo — desire and responsibility do not align automatically

Haruto can find Hajime attractive, admire her effort, and still remain emotionally oriented toward Kyo. The volume refuses the idea that a “good option” automatically becomes the desired relationship. This preserves the V06 distinction between suitability and self-authored desire. [IM-V07-E128–IM-V07-E143]

## 6. Creative labor, publishing, and industry

V07 substantially strengthens the industry ledger.

First, Toki's retrospective reveals an editor-author developmental relation rather than static gatekeeping. The editorial objective can be creator maturation: the author increasingly internalizes the ability to notice and correct excess. [IM-V07-E043–IM-V07-E050]

Second, the newcomer material shows that award-winning manuscripts undergo different levels of revision. Aioi is in the ordinary process of revising a prize manuscript with an editor, while Nayuta's debut was exceptional enough to be published with little beyond typo correction. The contrast keeps “genius” materially visible without converting it into total worth. [IM-V07-E136–IM-V07-E143]

Third, the genre discussion distinguishes market trend from cynical imitation. Haruto acknowledges that popular genres can be easier to pitch and safer commercially, yet argues that creators often enter them because reading a compelling work creates an urge to respond, vary, compete, or extend. Trend is therefore simultaneously market structure and intertextual desire. [IM-V07-E144–IM-V07-E158]

Fourth, Yuu's history shows scale effects. A series crossing a longevity threshold becomes easier to expand through media mix; successful adaptation multiplies attention far beyond the increase in actual source readership. Public visibility therefore changes the creator's risk environment. [IM-V07-E166–IM-V07-E179]

Fifth, the bonus track makes compensation itself thematic. Itsuki's defense of paying skilled workers is strong and general. His later willingness to participate for free does not refute it because he chooses to reinterpret the task as a personally valuable challenge and social gift. The key distinction is between **expected free labor because of acquaintance** and **voluntary uncompensated labor chosen by the creator**. [IM-V07-E193–IM-V07-E202]

## 7. Talent, success, failure, self-worth, and protagonist status

V07 extends the project's anti-totalization model from talent to **success itself**.

Yuu has the things younger creators imagine as victory: genius reputation, durable sales, media mix, broad attention, influence on later writers, institutional authority, and passionate readers. None guarantees happiness. This does not make success meaningless; it means success is not sufficient. [IM-V07-E159–IM-V07-E179]

Kaizu's earlier protagonist/mob comparison mirrors Itsuki's younger psychology. But the older Kaizu does not resolve the comparison by proving that he was secretly the true protagonist. He chooses a different evaluative axis: a long, moderately happy working life that genius peers cannot reproduce simply by being geniuses. [IM-V07-E162–IM-V07-E182]

This strongly **STRENGTHENS** `IM-C010` and `IM-C021`: sales, prestige, artistic value, influence, longevity, health, and happiness remain non-equivalent.

The Yuu chapter also complicates the idea of the reader. Yuu loves readers and wants to move them. Yet mass visibility creates a public larger than the reader community, including people who know the property through adaptation or discourse and may attack without meaningful engagement. “Audience” therefore needs internal differentiation: purchaser, source reader, adaptation viewer, fan, critic, spectator, and hostile participant are not interchangeable social roles. [IM-V07-E173–IM-V07-E179]

## 8. Family, siblinghood, and identity

Chihiro remains the central family problem. V07 does not disclose the secret to Itsuki and therefore cannot resolve the title-level collision. Instead it deepens the meaning of care before disclosure.

Chihiro's fear is relational displacement. The practical household role is not merely labor; it is a route to presence. This strengthens `IM-C015`: family identity is enacted through care, routine, access, and remembered promises as well as biology and naming. [IM-V07-E053–IM-V07-E064] [IM-V07-E116–IM-V07-E127]

The robot-vacuum chapter also demonstrates why domestic competence should not be romanticized as a pure gift. Competence can become a defensive identity: “I am needed because I do this better.” The healthier future question is whether Chihiro can remain secure when family belonging is not earned through service.

Haruto's bonus-track reflection on his own sister adds another ordinary sibling axis. His memory of watching children's media with her becomes a source of genre knowledge, undercutting Itsuki's abstract sister expertise with lived sibling experience. [IM-V07-E193–IM-V07-E202]

## 9. Sexuality, vulgar comedy, and boundaries

V07 moves Itsuki/Nayuta from hypothetical desire into consensual sexual intimacy. The text preserves mutual desire and embarrassment rather than treating relationship status as automatic mastery. [IM-V07-E106–IM-V07-E115]

The ethical baseline from earlier volumes remains necessary: coupling does not eliminate the need to read scene-level reciprocity. Here the relationship changes what forms of contact are mutually welcomed, but the characters still negotiate through speech, hesitation, bodily response, and prior knowledge.

The chapter's comedy after sex prevents the event from functioning as adult-completion symbolism. Sexual experience does not turn either character into a finished adult. The broader adulthood model remains distributed across work, health, care, money, judgment, and relationship maintenance.

Haruto's attraction to Hajime similarly demonstrates that acknowledging bodily attraction need not entail acting on it. His avoidance strategy is comic and not perfectly direct, but it recognizes a risk of using another person while emotionally attached elsewhere. [IM-V07-E128–IM-V07-E143]

## 10. Games, food, alcohol, and social ritual

`変わらないものと変わりゆくもの` is the clearest V07 demonstration of ritual continuity. The apartment gathering survives the romantic transition. Games again function as social technology: they reveal how well participants understand one another, create safe channels for competition, and allow changed relational positions to coexist without requiring a solemn processing conversation. [IM-V07-E082–IM-V07-E105]

The dinner gathering around Haruto, Kaizu, Hajime, Aoba, Chihiro, and Ashley performs a different function: professional generational mixing. Alcohol, food, and hobby talk reduce hierarchy enough for newcomers and veterans to reveal taste, defensiveness, insecurity, and shared subculture. [IM-V07-E128–IM-V07-E143]

Christmas food supplied by Chihiro carries domestic presence into Itsuki/Nayuta's private romantic evening even when Chihiro is absent. Care is materially portable. [IM-V07-E183–IM-V07-E192]

## 11. Fiction within fiction, template theory, and intertext

V07's `テンプレート・テンプテーション` is one of the series' clearest statements about genre ontology.

Aioi's argument is not that templates are imaginary. Shared structures plainly exist. The claim is that abstraction is lossy: “special bloodline,” tournament, isekai transfer, powerful protagonist, or stock character type does not determine local causality, emotional history, ethical problem, scene construction, or outcome. Genre sameness at one scale can coexist with work-level difference at another. [IM-V07-E146–IM-V07-E154]

Haruto adds a creator-intent layer. Even when market conditions reward a trend, writers may participate because another work awakened desire: “I want to try this,” “I would do this differently,” or “I can make this more interesting.” Convention is therefore a site of dialogue among works, not merely industrial duplication. [IM-V07-E150–IM-V07-E157]

Aioi's fear of being dismissed without being read is epistemically distinct from receiving negative criticism after reading. The former denies the work individual status before encounter. This becomes a creator-level analogue to the series' broader resistance to totalizing categories of people.

The bonus children's-story contest then parodies intertext without respect: Itsuki treats famous children's franchises as interchangeable attention-grabbing components despite weak knowledge. Haruto's irritation supplies the counterpoint that actual genre/fandom knowledge creates constraints. [IM-V07-E193–IM-V07-E202]

## 12. Japanese voice, naming, and terminology

Several V07 terms deserve longitudinal tracking.

### `対等の主人公`

The phrase remains alive after coupling. Itsuki has revised the temporal rule governing confession, not erased the aspiration itself. This is decisive evidence against reading V06 as abandonment of protagonist identity. [IM-V07-E003–IM-V07-E012] [IM-V07-E066–IM-V07-E074]

### `成長`

Itsuki explicitly frames his post-coupling response as needing to grow `人間として、作家として、男として`. The triad is important because it refuses a single-axis adulthood model. Human, professional, and gendered/self-concept growth are related but not identical. [IM-V07-E028–IM-V07-E040]

### `甘える`

Nayuta's worry about relying too much on Kyo makes `甘える` a relational boundary term: care is valuable, but excessive dependence can threaten agency or reciprocity. [IM-V07-E020–IM-V07-E028]

### `テンプレ`

V07 sharply differentiates category recognition from dismissive totalization. `テンプレ` can name recurring structure neutrally, but in Aioi/Aoba discourse it can also become an epistemic shortcut that refuses individual works serious encounter. [IM-V07-E144–IM-V07-E158]

### `幸せ`

Yuu and Kaizu turn `幸せ` into an industry term rather than only a romantic one. Yuu argues that successful creators must visibly be happy so the field remains aspirational; Kaizu responds by choosing a modestly happy long life as his own counter-telos. The term therefore joins talent, sales, prestige, and longevity as a tracked professional value axis. [IM-V07-E173–IM-V07-E182]

### Voice continuity

Nayuta's polite/deadpan sexual explicitness remains stable after becoming a girlfriend; Itsuki retains grandiose declaratives and sudden shame collapses; Toki's rough familiarity with Itsuki contrasts with his awareness that older or unfamiliar authors require a different register; Chihiro's `僕` / `兄さん` softness continues to sustain the false male category. V07 therefore shows that relationship-state change alters pragmatic permission more than baseline idiolect. [IM-V07-E033–IM-V07-E064] [IM-V07-E106–IM-V07-E127]

## 13. Illustration and publication-form analysis

V07 contains 39 indexed non-gaiji visual assets, but only 21 narrative/internal-paratext assets (`V07-ILL-007` through `V07-ILL-027`) are used as deep-reading evidence here. Five publisher ads and seven Gagaga 10th-anniversary retailer bonus covers are retained in the source inventory but excluded from narrative inference.

The visual sequence performs three functions.

First, the early illustrations and Q&A pages externalize the ensemble reaction structure. Coupling is visually distributed across Kyo, Toki/editorial context, Chihiro, Haruto, and Ashley rather than monopolized by the couple.

Second, the robot-vacuum illustration gives disproportionate formal weight to a comic domestic episode, supporting the interpretation that technological substitution is a serious role-security problem for Chihiro beneath the gag.

Third, Yuu receives retrospective narrative illustrations and a dedicated character profile. This formally upgrades the supposedly absent “person Itsuki admires” into a concrete creator whose life can bear structural comparison with the younger cast. The sequence visually separates the Yuu/Kaizu history from the later Christmas romance, preventing the darker creator-history chapter from being absorbed into couple fanservice.

The internal Q&A/profile pages remain PF rather than narrator-level TF. They can support characterization and publication emphasis, but must not silently override prose ambiguity.

## 14. Counterevidence and anti-theses

### Anti-thesis A — “V07 proves Itsuki has abandoned protagonist ideology.”

**Rejected.** The text explicitly says the resolve to stand beside Nayuta has not disappeared. What changed is the rule that romance must wait for proof of equality. [IM-V07-E003–IM-V07-E012]

### Anti-thesis B — “Becoming a couple fixes Nayuta's self-worth problem.”

**Not supported.** V07 provides reassurance and ordinary intimacy but no evidence that the V06 effort/lovability schema is fully extinguished.

### Anti-thesis C — “Kyo is simply over Itsuki.”

**Rejected.** Her ethical support for the couple demonstrates chosen care under pain, not proof of absent feeling. [IM-V07-E020–IM-V07-E027]

### Anti-thesis D — “Chihiro dislikes Nayuta because of jealousy.”

**Too narrow.** The text locates the immediate fear in displacement from Itsuki's household and loss of a needed role. [IM-V07-E053–IM-V07-E064]

### Anti-thesis E — “Automation threatens Chihiro because the series is anti-technology.”

**Rejected.** Chihiro is interested in appliances and eventually treats the robot affectionately. The conflict concerns replacement and belonging, not technological ideology. [IM-V07-E116–IM-V07-E127]

### Anti-thesis F — “Template fiction is either identical mass product or completely unique.”

**Rejected as a false binary.** V07 accepts recurring structures while insisting that abstraction does not exhaust execution, intention, affect, or local causal form. [IM-V07-E144–IM-V07-E158]

### Anti-thesis G — “Yuu's suffering proves success is bad.”

**Rejected.** The volume preserves her love of writing, readers, influence, and achievement. The argument is insufficiency: success cannot guarantee health or happiness. [IM-V07-E159–IM-V07-E182]

### Anti-thesis H — “Online criticism killed Yuu.”

**Unsupported causal compression.** The chapter explicitly includes terminal pancreatic cancer and withholds a simple monocausal account. Public hostility is psychologically important but must not be substituted for medical causation. [IM-V07-E173–IM-V07-E182]

### Anti-thesis I — “Kaizu's ordinary-life goal is resignation.”

**Downgraded.** His vow is framed as a positive thing geniuses cannot simply imitate: durable moderate happiness becomes a rival success model. [IM-V07-E180–IM-V07-E182]

### Anti-thesis J — “Itsuki asking for payment in the bonus track means friendship has been commodified.”

**Too simple.** He articulates a general labor norm, then voluntarily waives compensation once he chooses the task as a challenge and group activity. [IM-V07-E193–IM-V07-E202]
