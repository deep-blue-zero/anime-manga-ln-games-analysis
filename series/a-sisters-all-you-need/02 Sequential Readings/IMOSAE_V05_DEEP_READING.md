# 妹さえいればいい。 Volume 05 — Deep Reading

## 1. Executive thesis

Volume 05 is the series' clearest sustained move so far from **exceptional creators** toward the much larger ecology that makes creative life possible. V01–V03 established multiple non-equivalent value systems and the danger of converting talent, success, or narrative centrality into total human worth. V04 translated that problem into media mix: Itsuki wanted to become professionally irreplaceable while every adaptation process demonstrated dependence on other people's medium-specific skill. V05 widens the frame again. Editors, assistants, part-timers, exhausted production staff, veteran middle-tier writers, award judges, readers, family members, and emotionally receptive friends all become causally important. The volume repeatedly asks a deceptively simple question: **who counts as a meaningful actor when not everyone is a genius, sovereign author, or obvious protagonist?** [IM-V05-E024–IM-V05-E046, IM-V05-E066–IM-V05-E095, IM-V05-E138–IM-V05-E175]

The answer is not that talent ceases to matter. Nayuta remains a genuine extreme case. She can write with extraordinary speed, cares little about tools, and regards substantive editorial intervention as unnecessary. Yamagata herself admits that even with ample time she would probably change only errors. [IM-V05-E032–IM-V05-E040, IM-V05-E108–IM-V05-E111] V05 therefore supplies important **counterevidence to an overbroad version of interdependence**. Nayuta's imaginative/content-generating core can be unusually autonomous. But that does not make the whole creative system autonomous. Her deadline, communication, affective environment, production timing, illustration orders, circulation, and market consequences remain embedded in other people and institutions. Kyo's friendship becomes production infrastructure precisely because formal editorial channels cannot reach her. [IM-V05-E030–IM-V05-E046] This requires `IM-C001` to be **REVISED**, not merely strengthened: content-level autonomy and creative-life interdependence must be distinguished.

V05 also exposes how institutions manufacture the categories through which creative merit becomes legible. Kobe's drunken speech is comic, but its substance is explicit: publishers do not simply observe genres and trends; they can deliberately proliferate a successful pattern until it becomes a market category and an image of an era. [IM-V05-E046] The newcomer-prize chapter makes the same point more rigorously. Award rank does not reliably predict sales; packaging and marketing may matter more; the grand prize is partly a public signal to future applicants; the rules changed when guaranteed top prizes attracted more submissions; and the final winner emerges through compromise among incompatible judging principles. [IM-V05-E138–IM-V05-E155] V05 therefore opens `IM-C022`: **awards, genres, and institutional recognition are designed signaling and recruitment systems, not neutral meters that discover intrinsic worth already sitting inside a work.**

That institutional critique does not collapse into cynicism. The volume is equally attentive to competence, care, and professional responsibility. Kyo is hired through a mixture of connection and appearance, and Toki explicitly recognizes the latter as sexual-harassment logic. Yet the narration immediately warns against treating her entry condition as her total value: she later becomes useful because she is serious, observant, self-starting, socially connected, and willing to intervene. [IM-V05-E008–IM-V05-E017, IM-V05-E138–IM-V05-E141] The distinction is crucial. **Unfair or contingent access can be real without making all later contribution fraudulent.** V05 is interested in institutions precisely because the path into a role, the work done inside it, and the value produced by it are different questions.

The same pluralization transforms the long-running `主人公` motif. In V03, protagonist status was primarily Itsuki's technology of self-authorship and a dangerous comparative test for romantic worth. In V04, it became industrial: he wanted to become professionally non-substitutable. V05 now distributes the language to other people. Tarui chooses a forward-looking stance because it feels like an anime protagonist. Haruto sees the unglamorous fourteen-year survival of a middle-tier writer as more protagonist-like than genius. The afterword explicitly calls older editors, directors, scriptwriters, and veteran writers `主人公候補たち`. [IM-V05-E089, IM-V05-E163–IM-V05-E167, IM-V05-E198–IM-V05-E199] This requires `IM-C014` to be **REVISED**. `主人公` is no longer adequately modeled as an objective rank Itsuki is trying to win. It is a **portable rhetoric of self-authorization, persistence, and meaningful agency**. Itsuki still uses it competitively and sometimes destructively, but the text increasingly refuses to let him monopolize it.

Haruto's admiration for Kaizu supplies another decisive correction to the genius hierarchy. Kaizu is described almost aggressively as `そこそこ`: decent, reasonably sellable, not innovative enough to change the field, not prestigious enough to dominate rankings, not distinctive enough to build a cult identity. Yet Haruto respects him precisely because he has survived fourteen years while a genuine genius from the same generation disappeared. [IM-V05-E161–IM-V05-E167] Survival is not glamorous residual success. It becomes its own axis of professional achievement. V05 therefore opens `IM-C021`: **longevity and endurance are independently meaningful forms of success, irreducible to peak talent, awards, sales, or trend-setting importance.**

Kyo's storyline then moves the volume from industry sociology into moral psychology. She enters publishing partly because proximity to exceptional people intensifies her sense that she is `空っぽ`. Working beside dream-driven creators initially makes this worse, not better. [IM-V05-E008–IM-V05-E011, IM-V05-E168–IM-V05-E169] Haruto answers not by inventing a hidden genius inside her, but by changing the category of contribution. Her tears over his failed anime mattered. They kept him from collapsing. The hope that one's work can reach another person is itself part of what allows creators to keep working. [IM-V05-E170–IM-V05-E173] This opens `IM-C020`: **reception, care, and emotional response can be causally productive creative infrastructure.** A person need not create the novel, manga, or anime in order to materially sustain the ecosystem that lets creation continue.

That conclusion also explains why V05's romance matters beyond pairing mechanics. Nayuta and Itsuki now know their desire is mutual, and that knowledge makes their interactions *less* carefree. Nayuta can no longer make every sexual approach without consequence; she wants to be seen as a girl while fearing a move that would irreversibly change the relationship. [IM-V05-E056–IM-V05-E063] Kyo, by contrast, spends much of the volume acting for Itsuki while he interprets her actions as loyalty to his work. She finally corrects the category: she did not intervene because she loved `妹のすべて`; she intervened because she loved **him**. [IM-V05-E184–IM-V05-E190] Itsuki rejects her immediately because he loves someone else, and Kyo's heartbreak is allowed to be final without making her life narratively terminal. The last line explicitly sends *her* story into a new stage. [IM-V05-E191–IM-V05-E196] The volume thus turns Kyo from observer/helper into a person who makes a costly claim on her own behalf.

The family material operates on the same distinction between care and authority. Kaiko's father is not reduced to an ignorant villain: he has researched the economics of manga, understands cancellation risk, worries about income and employability, and has a recognizable history of loving his daughter. [IM-V05-E120–IM-V05-E124] But none of that automatically grants ownership over an adult vocational decision. Kyo's intervention and Kaiko's own declaration separate **legitimate concern** from **legitimate control**. [IM-V05-E116–IM-V05-E125] This opens `IM-C023`. Itsuki's grave visit reaches a parallel insight from the other side: he still resents his father's remarriage, but his own experience of uncontrollable love makes a one-dimensional moral judgment harder to sustain. [IM-V05-E096–IM-V05-E107] The volume repeatedly returns to `ままならない`: real people cannot be simplified into the clean relational roles available in fantasy.

Finally, V05 gives V04's adaptation theory its strongest test. The anime begins as a replacement project whose schedule, committee, staff formation, economic assumptions, and overseas-market concerns predate meaningful author control. [IM-V05-E066–IM-V05-E089] Staff members have different expertise and different injuries. Hirugano cannot understand the source but is not lazy; he is mourning a canceled project and protecting his own career. Tarui is tasked with loss minimization but refuses to create as though defeat were certain. Kobe's advice to defer to professionals is not wholly wrong; Kyo's insistence that professionals are not infallible is not wholly wrong either. Itsuki's eventual deep involvement carries real artistic possibility and real burnout risk. [IM-V05-E075–IM-V05-E095] By `白川京`, the conflict has produced a transformed series composition that author, scriptwriter, and director all regard as better *as anime*, despite knowingly inviting complaints that it differs from the novels. [IM-V05-E176–IM-V05-E183] `IM-C017` and `IM-C018` are therefore strongly **STRENGTHENED**.

### V05 thesis in compressed form

> **V05 dismantles the equation “important person = autonomous genius/protagonist.” Its most self-contained genius still depends on production and affective conditions; institutions manufacture categories and recognition; a middle-tier survivor can embody professional heroism more convincingly than a genius; readers and friends can sustain creators without producing the work themselves; and the volume progressively pluralizes `主人公` from a rank into a language of agency. Creative adulthood is not the achievement of sovereignty. It is the difficult work of acting meaningfully inside systems and relationships one cannot fully control.** [IT]

## 3. Volume architecture and narrative movement

V05 is unusually coherent as a sequence of **institutional entry → hidden labor → contested authorship → evaluation → personal declaration**.

1. **`小説家は妹キ●ガイⅣ`** begins with a failed pitch rather than a triumph. Itsuki learns that becoming more successful does not simplify evaluation; it raises the standard against which the next project is judged. The chapter also reveals that his grandiose response to praise is partly deliberate self-management. [IM-V05-E001–IM-V05-E007]
2. **`面接`** moves Kyo from creator-adjacent friend into paid publishing labor. The chapter deliberately makes her entry morally messy: connection and appearance matter, the latter is explicitly recognized as harassment-adjacent logic, yet later competence is foreshadowed. [IM-V05-E008–IM-V05-E017]
3. **`羽島千尋の昼休み`** briefly leaves the creative industry entirely. Chihiro's school friends reveal ordinary adolescent gender discourse and, more importantly, show that Chihiro's private familiarity with Nayuta has insulated her from understanding Nayuta's public stature. [IM-V05-E018–IM-V05-E023]
4. **`編集部のバイトはじめました`** is the volume's first major labor chapter. It inventories invisible editorial work, explains deadline interdependence, and then turns Kyo's friendship with Nayuta into an emergency production resource. [IM-V05-E024–IM-V05-E046]
5. **`水着回１–３`** are not analytically disposable. They juxtapose observational craft, selective adaptation fidelity, sexual vulnerability after mutual love, and an explicit prose-to-illustration handoff in which the narrator refuses to invent an event merely to fill pages. [IM-V05-E047–IM-V05-E065]
6. **`顔合わせ（アニメ編）`** expands V04's media-mix analysis into a multi-agent conflict. It refuses both author sovereignty and technocratic deference to “professionals.” The real question is how differently situated professionals negotiate incompatible expertise, incentives, injuries, and responsibilities. [IM-V05-E066–IM-V05-E095]
7. **`墓参り`** folds the industrial theme back into family. Itsuki's own experience of uncontrollable love makes his father's remarriage harder to narrate as a simple moral offense. [IM-V05-E096–IM-V05-E107]
8. **The long mailing-error/Kaiko chapter** pairs two problems of professional legitimacy. Yamagata's relation to Nayuta separates love of work from liking the creator. Kaiko's family conflict separates practical risk assessment from authority over an adult vocation. [IM-V05-E108–IM-V05-E127]
9. **`羽島千尋の趣味`** shifts domestic care toward reciprocity. Itsuki recognizes household equipment as his responsibility and Chihiro reveals a technically developed hobby independent of cooking or caring for him. [IM-V05-E128–IM-V05-E137]
10. **`新人賞選考会`** turns evaluation itself into an object of analysis. Awards are shown as labor-intensive, market-facing institutions whose ranking rules have recruitment and signaling functions beyond identifying “the best work.” [IM-V05-E138–IM-V05-E155]
11. **`凡人の星`** answers the award chapter by elevating endurance and reception. Kaizu's middle-tier persistence becomes admirable; Kyo's emotional receptivity becomes causally valuable; Haruto finally confesses. [IM-V05-E156–IM-V05-E175]
12. **`白川京`** closes both the anime and romance movements. Collaborative conflict yields a transformed adaptation structure, while Kyo refuses Itsuki's interpretation of her actions as work-loyalty and names their personal motive directly. [IM-V05-E176–IM-V05-E196]
13. **Afterword** explicitly names the volume's older professionals `主人公候補たち`. This is useful corroboration of the protagonist-pluralization thesis, but it remains PF rather than diegetic evidence. [IM-V05-E197–IM-V05-E199]

The structural movement is therefore not simply “Kyo gets a job.” It is **Kyo enters a system, discovers how many forms of labor and value the system contains, and eventually stops interpreting her own value only through what she can do for exceptional people.** Her confession fails romantically, but succeeds as self-authorization.

## 4. Narration, focalization, and epistemic structure

### 4.1 Kyo becomes the volume's epistemic bridge

V05 still belongs to an ensemble, but Kyo is its most important bridge character. She moves among:

- Itsuki's apartment;
- Nayuta's private writing environment;
- GF editorial labor;
- Kaiko's manga work and family;
- anime production conflict;
- the newcomer-award institution;
- Haruto's private insecurity;
- and finally her own romantic position.

Because she enters these spaces without a creator's fixed professional identity, her focalization exposes their assumptions. She asks the “naive” questions that institutions have normalized: why not delay a book for quality? Why must someone win the grand prize? Why should an author simply defer to professionals? [IM-V05-E027–IM-V05-E029, IM-V05-E146–IM-V05-E155, IM-V05-E075–IM-V05-E095] The questions are often incomplete, but they force other characters to articulate systems that would otherwise remain invisible.

This role is different from being an omniscient moral judge. Kyo is herself compromised by comparison. She enters publishing because she hopes proximity to exceptional people will show her what she lacks; later she interprets everyone else's conviction as evidence that she is empty. [IM-V05-E008–IM-V05-E011, IM-V05-E168–IM-V05-E169] Her focalization is valuable precisely because it is partial.

### 4.2 The narrator repeatedly distinguishes public rationale from operative motive

The interview gives the cleanest example. Kobe's official word is `誠実さ`; his private explanation is `顔だ`. [IM-V05-E013–IM-V05-E017] This does not mean sincerity is irrelevant. It means institutions can produce respectable explanations that do not exhaust causal reality.

The same pattern recurs elsewhere:

- Nayuta says she neither hates nor distrusts Yamagata; the operative relation is lack of expectation. [IM-V05-E039–IM-V05-E040]
- Yamagata dislikes the author but loves the work strongly enough to remain her editor. [IM-V05-E108–IM-V05-E111]
- Tarui speaks the language of loss minimization but still chooses a protagonist-like commitment to victory. [IM-V05-E083–IM-V05-E089]
- Kyo appears to help `妹のすべて`; her actual motive is love for Itsuki. [IM-V05-E184–IM-V05-E190]

V05's people are therefore repeatedly **misdescribed by single categories**. Work-role, affection, market logic, resentment, duty, aspiration, and desire can coexist.

### 4.3 Comic narration is often analytically exact

The volume uses absurdity to state serious institutional facts without becoming a treatise. The `天使`/`魔王` deadline taxonomy is comic, but it encodes bargaining power and the way a publisher may tolerate destructive scheduling behavior from a sufficiently valuable author. [IM-V05-E041–IM-V05-E045] Kobe's drunken genre speech is ridiculous in performance but unusually explicit about active category construction. [IM-V05-E046] The newcomer-award debate similarly uses exaggerated works and personalities to make visible conflicts among completion, originality, future potential, brand identity, recruitment, and marketability. [IM-V05-E138–IM-V05-E155]

The analytical rule remains the same as earlier volumes: comedy is **framing**, not automatic endorsement or dismissal.

## 5. Character-state changes

### 5.1 羽島伊月 — from protagonist hunger toward recognition of uncontrollability

V05 adds three important layers to Itsuki.

First, his grandiosity is more deliberate than it can appear from the outside. Toki explicitly recognizes that Itsuki knows when he is being praised strategically and performs being carried away in order to regulate his own motivation. [IM-V05-E005–IM-V05-E007] The `俺`-centered author persona is therefore not simply a false self or narcissistic delusion. It is partly **instrumental self-narration**: a way to produce the emotional state required to keep working. This revises the meaning of `IM-C014` even before the volume pluralizes protagonist language across other people.

Second, Itsuki's theory of authorship becomes internally contradictory in a productive way. He wants to be the protagonist of his life and becomes furious when anime staff marginalize what he considers central to his work. Yet he also insists that his own “supporting” characters cannot be treated as disposable because every character has motives, history, and a story. [IM-V05-E070–IM-V05-E075] He therefore rejects inside fiction the same kind of hard protagonist hierarchy he often applies to himself outside it. This contradiction is not necessarily hypocrisy. It may be one of the series' strongest mechanisms for criticizing his self-model from within his own creative ethics.

Third, `墓参り` forces a limit on the clean explanatory system that fictional-sister absolutism offers. Itsuki still dislikes his father, but his own love teaches him that desire can be involuntary and morally complicating. [IM-V05-E101–IM-V05-E107] He explicitly imagines how easy life would be if he could simply live for fictional sisters, then admits that reality is tangled by `しがらみ`. The sister ideal is not merely unconscious compensation anymore. Itsuki can partially recognize its **simplifying function**.

### 5.2 可児那由多 — genuine autonomy inside dependence

Nayuta is the volume's best counterexample to simplistic relational theories of creativity.

Her content-generation process really is unusually self-sufficient. She can write on nearly any device, at extraordinary speed, and she does not want substantive editorial intervention. Yamagata agrees that the prose arrives in a state where she sees little to change. [IM-V05-E032–IM-V05-E040, IM-V05-E108–IM-V05-E111] Calling Nayuta “not autonomous” would therefore erase real evidence.

But the volume simultaneously shows the boundary of that autonomy. She is chronically late, difficult to reach, economically important enough to destabilize discipline, and able to finish the emergency manuscript because Kyo supplies access, attention, interruption shielding, and reward structure. [IM-V05-E030–IM-V05-E045] Her work's *content* may be close to self-contained; her **professional existence is not**.

Romantically, mutual recognition makes her more ordinary rather than less. Before she knew Itsuki loved her, aggressive sexual comedy carried comparatively low perceived relational risk. Now a modest swimsuit and the direct question “do you desire me?” become difficult. [IM-V05-E056–IM-V05-E063] The series does not remove her eccentricity; it shows that exceptional talent and sexual candor do not abolish ordinary vulnerability.

### 5.3 白川京 — from observer/usefulness toward self-authorization

Kyo's V05 arc is the most substantial in the volume.

At the start, she chooses editorial work because she feels behind. Itsuki and Nayuta have public identities and visible trajectories; she experiences herself as someone who merely lets days pass. [IM-V05-E008–IM-V05-E011] The job does not immediately solve that problem. It exposes her to even more people with strong professional commitments, intensifying the conclusion that she is `空っぽ`. [IM-V05-E168–IM-V05-E169]

But the objective evidence of the volume increasingly contradicts her self-description. Kyo:

- asks systems to explain themselves;
- recovers Nayuta's publication schedule;
- becomes a trusted communication bridge;
- intervenes in the anime conflict;
- confronts Kaiko's father;
- is praised for finding work without waiting to be ordered;
- gives Haruto psychologically consequential reception;
- and finally states her own desire directly. [IM-V05-E024–IM-V05-E046, IM-V05-E116–IM-V05-E125, IM-V05-E138–IM-V05-E141, IM-V05-E170–IM-V05-E196]

Haruto's answer to her emptiness is particularly important because he does **not** discover a hidden exceptional talent. He argues that kindness and emotional receptivity are valuable in their own form. Kyo's tears sustained him. [IM-V05-E170–IM-V05-E173] The text thus refuses the easy solution “Kyo is secretly a genius too.” Her value can be real without duplicating the value-system of the people she admires.

The confession completes this movement. Itsuki thinks she worked for his creation; Kyo insists she acted for him. [IM-V05-E184–IM-V05-E190] The correction is painful because it produces rejection, but it is also a refusal to let useful labor stand in for personal desire. The final line makes heartbreak developmental rather than annihilating. [IM-V05-E191–IM-V05-E196]

### 5.4 不破春斗 — longevity, reception, and direct desire

Haruto's V05 development is quieter but philosophically important.

His highest professional respect goes to Kaizu, not to Nayuta or some canonical genius. What he admires is sustained survival under repeated uncertainty. [IM-V05-E161–IM-V05-E167] This reorients Haruto's earlier envy of originality. A career can be admirable because it **continues**.

His interaction with Kyo then demonstrates the reciprocal version of that principle. A creator needs some reason to believe the work reaches another person. Kyo's response to his failed anime gave him that reason at a moment when institutional success had failed him. [IM-V05-E170–IM-V05-E173] Haruto therefore recognizes audience/care as part of creator survival, not as a passive aftereffect of creation.

Romantically, he also stops routing himself through Itsuki. Their first two-person outing is explicitly independent of the ensemble. [IM-V05-E156–IM-V05-E157] He ultimately confesses without manipulation, and Kyo answers with genuine deliberation rather than immediate reciprocity. [IM-V05-E174–IM-V05-E175] This preserves both characters' agency.

### 5.5 羽島千尋 — autonomous hobby and increasing reciprocity

V05 continues to separate Chihiro from a pure service-function reading.

The school chapter shows Chihiro as an ordinary girl among peers, with Itsuki-specific gender concealment remaining a private relational arrangement rather than her general social presentation. Her friends interpret cooking through gender/status vocabulary that Chihiro herself does not need in order to justify the activity. [IM-V05-E018–IM-V05-E023]

The birthday chapter is more important. When Chihiro asks for a knife, Itsuki recognizes that the household's equipment is poor and decides replacing it is **his responsibility as the resident/beneficiary**, not a personal gift to Chihiro. [IM-V05-E128–IM-V05-E132] That is a small but real movement toward reciprocal care.

Chihiro also reveals an autonomous craft hobby: roughly thirty Gunpla, with painting and weathering competence beyond Itsuki's own ability. [IM-V05-E133–IM-V05-E137] Itsuki's reaction is analytically perfect: he knows Chihiro extremely well in practical life and yet uses this new evidence to reinforce the wrong categorical belief that Chihiro is male. Intimacy does not automatically cure misrecognition.

### 5.6 三国山蚕 / Kaiko — vocation as adult self-claim

Kaiko's family conflict is deliberately allowed more complexity than “artist versus conservative parent.”

Her father does have legitimate information. He understands that monthly manuscript income is insufficient, tankobon revenue is delayed, cancellation is possible, and Kaiko lacks work experience. Narration explicitly says he researched the profession. [IM-V05-E120–IM-V05-E124] The text therefore grants him **epistemic legitimacy** on risk.

It does not grant him ownership. Kaiko states that the choice is hers and that she will remain a professional even at the cost of family rupture. [IM-V05-E118–IM-V05-E119] Kyo's intervention makes the normative distinction explicit: love and concern do not justify degrading the work or negating the adult decision. [IM-V05-E115–IM-V05-E117]

Kaiko's own dream also refuses a simple break with childhood. She once wanted to make world-class underwear from family silk; now she wants to communicate underwear's beauty through manga and remain at the professional forefront to do it. [IM-V05-E125–IM-V05-E126] The medium changes; the underlying obsession is continuous. Her vocation is not rebellion for rebellion's sake. It is a revised form of inherited desire.

### 5.7 Editors and anime staff — professionals as persons, not functions

V05 greatly expands the adult professional cast without turning them into background machinery.

- **Toki** is shown managing motivation, not merely manuscripts. [IM-V05-E005–IM-V05-E007]
- **Yamagata** can dislike Nayuta personally while loving the work enough to remain its first reader. [IM-V05-E108–IM-V05-E111]
- **Kobe** carries institutional memory and risk knowledge, but also embodies compromised hiring logic and defensive deference to professional expertise. [IM-V05-E013–IM-V05-E017, IM-V05-E090–IM-V05-E094]
- **Hirugano** is not a convenient villain. He cannot understand the source, but he cares about the canceled project, cares about his own craft, and has professional exposure to failure. [IM-V05-E079–IM-V05-E082]
- **Tarui** is assigned `敗戦処理`, thinks in budgets and loss minimization, yet refuses to psychologically treat the work as already dead. [IM-V05-E083–IM-V05-E089]
- **Kaizu** supplies a model of unglamorous longevity that younger writers need. [IM-V05-E161–IM-V05-E167]

The afterword's `主人公候補たち` is therefore not arbitrary. [IM-V05-E198] V05 has spent the whole volume dramatizing the interior stakes hidden inside professional functions.

## 6. Relationship ecology

### 6.1 Itsuki ↔ Nayuta — mutual desire increases risk

The relationship is stronger but less behaviorally simple. Itsuki's quiet `する` confirms sexual desire without needing grand romantic rhetoric. [IM-V05-E060–IM-V05-E061] Nayuta's subsequent interiority matters more: once mutual love is known, her former strategy of escalating erotic contact becomes frightening because actions can now transform an actually possible relationship. [IM-V05-E062–IM-V05-E063]

This is a substantial **STRENGTHEN** for `IM-C008`. Mutuality does not solve the romance; it makes boundary negotiation real.

### 6.2 Itsuki ↔ Kyo — friendship survives explicit asymmetry

Kyo's confession eliminates one of the first block's largest ambiguities. Itsuki had genuinely not recognized her romantic feelings, and his heart is already directed elsewhere. [IM-V05-E187–IM-V05-E193] The rejection is immediate rather than strategically deferred.

The important residue is the `じゃあまたね`. [IM-V05-E194] Kyo does not demand that failed romance erase friendship, nor does the text claim that maintaining friendship makes the pain unreal. The relationship enters a new state whose future cannot yet be determined within V05.

### 6.3 Haruto ↔ Kyo — direct relation replaces mediated interest

Haruto's earlier attraction frequently existed through Itsuki's awareness or group context. V05 gives them a first independent outing, then an explicit confession. [IM-V05-E156–IM-V05-E157, IM-V05-E174–IM-V05-E175]

Kyo's “let me think” is neither a romantic yes nor a dismissal. It should remain **OPEN** prospectively. What is established is that Haruto has become willing to speak directly and Kyo is willing to treat the confession as worthy of genuine consideration.

### 6.4 Nayuta ↔ Kyo — friendship becomes professional infrastructure

The relationship still carries chosen-sister intimacy, but V05 adds a new function. Kyo can get Nayuta to write when the professional editor cannot. [IM-V05-E030–IM-V05-E040] This is not evidence that Kyo is a better literary editor than Yamagata. It is evidence that **different relationship types grant different capacities**. Friendship can produce access, trust, bodily comfort, and motivation that formal authority cannot simply command.

### 6.5 Itsuki ↔ Chihiro — reciprocal care grows under persistent false categorization

Itsuki's decision to replace household tools on his own account is a small but important change from earlier dependence. [IM-V05-E128–IM-V05-E132] Yet the Gunpla scene shows the epistemic gap remains structurally active. [IM-V05-E133–IM-V05-E137] He can become a more reciprocal family member while still misunderstanding the family member in front of him.

### 6.6 Kaiko ↔ father — concern, recognition, and authority

The reconciliation should not be reduced to “father learns artist good.” He already understands some practical risks. What changes is his willingness to recognize Kaiko's vocation as hers. The final shift to `三国山、蚕先生` marks that recognition formally. [IM-V05-E120–IM-V05-E127]

## 7. Creative labor, publishing, and industry

### 7.1 Editorial work is both creative mediation and infrastructure

V05 provides the clearest inventory yet of tasks that occur around the manuscript rather than inside its sentences. Shipping, copies, proof handling, internal approvals, surveys, phone coverage, errands, event preparation, schedule management, author communication, and crisis containment all appear as ordinary work. [IM-V05-E024–IM-V05-E029]

This matters because an author-centered account of literature systematically hides these tasks. The book reaches a reader through a coordinated chain whose members experience different consequences when the schedule slips.

### 7.2 Deadlines are relational promises, not private time-management preferences

Kyo's instinct is straightforward: if haste creates errors, delay the book. Yamagata's answer is not “quality does not matter.” It is that a publication date is a coordination point across many actors. [IM-V05-E026–IM-V05-E029]

This sharpens `IM-C013`. Distributed causality includes distributed **obligation**. An author's lateness is not confined to author and editor because the system has already allocated labor around the promised date.

### 7.3 Market power can invert discipline

The deadline taxonomy is comic sociology. `天使` writers are easy to schedule; `魔王` writers are repeatedly late. The institutional contradiction is that the worst offenders may be the writers the label can least afford to lose. [IM-V05-E041–IM-V05-E045]

This is not simply “publishers exploit authors.” Sometimes a creator's market power constrains the institution. Power is multi-directional and specific to the resource at stake.

### 7.4 Editorial contribution is not identical to textual intervention

Nayuta and Yamagata are a useful limit case. Yamagata does not claim hidden substantive contribution to Nayuta's prose. She thinks the work is too complete for her to intervene meaningfully and finds that professionally frustrating. [IM-V05-E108–IM-V05-E111]

Yet editorial relation still exists around deadlines, publication, coordination, first readership, and circulation. V05 therefore distinguishes **content editing** from **publishing labor** rather than inflating either into the whole profession.

### 7.5 Publishers do not merely observe genres; they can help make them

Kobe's genre speech is one of the volume's most important industry statements. He rejects claims that any era becomes literally homogeneous, but then identifies a real mechanism behind those perceptions: once a successful pattern appears, publishers actively seek and promote related works until they become a recognizable category and market. [IM-V05-E046]

`ジャンル`, `ムーブメント`, `イメージ`, `流行`, and `時代` are therefore partly outputs of institutional action. This complicates any model in which market categories simply emerge bottom-up from reader taste.

### 7.6 The newcomer award is a machine for field reproduction

The prize chapter shows more than judges deciding quality. It includes:

- labor-intensive filtering;
- evaluation sheets;
- hidden applicant profiles;
- brand signaling;
- prize-money incentives;
- applicant recruitment;
- packaging/marketing forecasts;
- revision-potential disputes;
- and negotiated compromise. [IM-V05-E138–IM-V05-E155]

The final rank is therefore an **institutional decision under multiple objectives**, not a metaphysical revelation of worth.

This opens `IM-C022`.

## 8. Talent, success, failure, self-worth, and protagonist status

### 8.1 Talent remains real; totalization remains the problem

V05 never pretends that all works or creators are equally capable.

- Nayuta's output and sales are extraordinary. [IM-V05-E032–IM-V05-E045, IM-V05-E108]
- `ソラノキオク` is genuinely admired yet judged derivative relative to Nayuta. [IM-V05-E145–IM-V05-E151]
- Kaizu is explicitly middling by several prestige standards. [IM-V05-E161–IM-V05-E167]

The problem is what follows from these comparisons. A work can be good and still lose institutional status because a superior predecessor occupies the comparison field. A writer can be middling and still possess extraordinary longevity. A non-creator can materially sustain a creator. V05 keeps adding axes until no single ranking can carry the full moral weight.

### 8.2 `劣化コピー` is a relational judgment

The award discussion of `ソラノキオク` is important because “degraded copy” does not mean “bad book.” Haruto's own craft evaluation is strongly positive. [IM-V05-E145] The problem is comparative positioning: the work resembles Nayuta closely enough that every relevant dimension is judged against an already dominant model. [IM-V05-E149–IM-V05-E151]

Institutional originality is therefore partly **field-relative**. The same work can possess local excellence and weak positional distinctiveness.

### 8.3 `凡人の星` creates a longevity axis

Kaizu's fourteen-year career is the most direct challenge yet to the series' genius-centered imagination. He has not changed the era. He is not the best seller. He has no special prestige aura. He remains. [IM-V05-E161–IM-V05-E167]

Haruto's admiration recognizes what a younger successful writer fears: careers are vulnerable to disappointment, new entrants, changing taste, and psychological exhaustion. Remaining professionally viable through repeated uncertainty is itself a rare achievement.

This opens `IM-C021`.

### 8.4 Kyo and receptive value

Kyo's `空っぽ` formulation assumes that a valuable person should have a dream, vocation, belief, or output comparable to the creators around her. [IM-V05-E168–IM-V05-E169] Haruto rejects that premise at the level of causal consequence. Her response to his work changed whether he could keep going. [IM-V05-E170–IM-V05-E173]

This is more rigorous than generic “everyone is special” consolation. The text identifies an actual function: **reception can feed back into production**. Audience/care is not necessarily passive.

This opens `IM-C020`.

### 8.5 Protagonist status is pluralized

V05 gives the motif at least four forms:

1. **Itsuki:** protagonist identity as deliberate self-motivation and comparative self-worth. [IM-V05-E005–IM-V05-E007]
2. **Itsuki's fiction:** explicit refusal to treat “supporting” characters as people whose stories do not matter. [IM-V05-E070–IM-V05-E075]
3. **Tarui:** protagonist rhetoric as a chosen stance toward a structurally pessimistic project. [IM-V05-E089]
4. **Kaizu / afterword professionals:** persistence and professional struggle as forms of protagonist-like agency outside genius centrality. [IM-V05-E163–IM-V05-E167, IM-V05-E198–IM-V05-E199]

`IM-C014` must therefore be **REVISED** from “protagonist status is Itsuki's selfhood technology” to a wider claim: protagonist language is a portable way characters frame agency, dignity, persistence, and meaningful participation. Itsuki's comparative use remains important, but it is one use among several.

## 9. Family, siblinghood, and identity

### 9.1 `しがらみ` versus simplified family fantasy

The grave chapter places Itsuki's fictional sister absolutism beside family history without declaring them identical. The important new evidence is his own awareness of contrast. He wishes reality could be simplified to one chosen attachment, then recognizes that it cannot. [IM-V05-E104–IM-V05-E107]

This **STRENGTHENS `IM-C005`**. The sister fixation remains multi-causal, but V05 makes its simplifying/defensive utility more consciously available to Itsuki himself.

### 9.2 Love does not erase moral judgment; it complicates it

Itsuki does not suddenly forgive his father. He says his experience of love makes it harder to condemn the remarriage as simply as before. [IM-V05-E101–IM-V05-E103] The distinction matters. Empathy can revise confidence without producing reconciliation.

### 9.3 Kaiko's family conflict and adult vocational authority

Kaiko's father has care and evidence. He does not thereby possess final decision authority. [IM-V05-E115–IM-V05-E125]

This opens `IM-C023`:

> **Family love and legitimate practical concern do not by themselves confer ownership over an adult person's vocational choice. Risk assessment, emotional support, and coercive control must remain analytically distinct.**

### 9.4 Chihiro: family intimacy under categorical error

Chihiro's autonomous hobby makes the identity problem especially sharp. Itsuki can know her tastes, cooking, daily behavior, and now craft skill while still misclassifying her gender because new information is assimilated into the existing assumption. [IM-V05-E128–IM-V05-E137]

This is a useful general model of misrecognition: **more data do not automatically correct a bad category if the category controls interpretation of the data.**

## 10. Sexuality, vulgar comedy, and boundaries

### 10.1 Kaiko's swimsuit problem is craft and fanservice at once

The chapter's joke works because the distinction Kaiko cares about is absurdly specific and technically real. Swimwear and underwear differ in material, wetness, transparency, movement, and expected context. Her observational research improves the manga. [IM-V05-E047–IM-V05-E055]

At the same time, the research is eroticized and depends on Kyo again being used as a body model. The volume does not force us to choose one description. **Craft purpose does not neutralize sexual framing; sexual framing does not make craft evidence unreal.**

### 10.2 Selective fidelity

Itsuki allows Kaiko to redesign the heroine's swimsuit freely while having fought intensely over replacing nudity with underwear. [IM-V05-E052–IM-V05-E054]

This is important for `IM-C017`: source fidelity is not evenly distributed across details. Creators implicitly rank which details carry character/theme/style and which are disposable. Adaptation disputes are partly disputes over that hierarchy.

### 10.3 Mutual love changes the consent/meaning landscape

Nayuta's swimsuit approach is unusually valuable because it shows how established mutuality changes her behavior. [IM-V05-E056–IM-V05-E063]

Earlier sexual comedy could be unilateral, absurd, and insulated by assumed impossibility. Now a similar move may actually change the relationship. The text gives Nayuta fear, embarrassment, strategy, and desire for recognition. This **STRENGTHENS `IM-C007` and `IM-C008`** while preserving the need to separate comic framing from relational meaning.

### 10.4 `水着回３` as publication-form joke

The Chihiro beach chapter explicitly declines to narrate an event and hands the reader to the illustrator. [IM-V05-E064–IM-V05-E065, IM-V05-E207]

This is more than a throwaway gag. It makes the light novel's composite form visible: prose can cede representational labor to image, and the book can openly acknowledge the commercial/visual expectation of a “swimsuit episode.”

## 11. Games, food, alcohol, and social ritual

V05 is more work-centered than earlier volumes, but social ritual remains important.

The Haruto/Kyo Belgian-beer-bar scene is the strongest example. Removing the group changes what can be said. Haruto can explain his admiration for Kaizu, Kyo can confess her self-loathing, and Haruto can finally articulate what Kyo's emotional response meant to him. [IM-V05-E156–IM-V05-E175, IM-V05-E213]

TRPG remains a smaller but revealing creative bridge. Kyo's statement that she looks forward to the next game immediately motivates Haruto, and Haruto frames player unpredictability as something his professional craft should answer. [IM-V05-E158–IM-V05-E160] Social play continues to function as a miniature laboratory for responsiveness, authorship, and audience.

This **STRENGTHENS `IM-C004`**, though V05's larger innovation is showing that even ordinary interpersonal reception can enter the professional resilience system.

## 12. Fiction-within-fiction, adaptation, and intertext

### 12.1 Itsuki's “supporting characters” critique rebounds onto his self-model

Itsuki argues that no character in his fiction is disposable and that the Oni siblings are hidden protagonists because their relationships carry thematic structure. [IM-V05-E070–IM-V05-E075]

This creates a powerful internal analogy. If fictional importance can be distributed beyond the nominal protagonist, then Itsuki's own fear of becoming a “supporting character” in Nayuta's life becomes less defensible as an objective ontology. V05 does not have him explicitly make that inference. The text supplies the contradiction; the longitudinal analysis should preserve it as **SI/IT**, not TF.

### 12.2 Media difference is necessary but not self-justifying

Hirugano's phrase about understanding media difference is, in abstract, consistent with V04's adaptation thesis. Itsuki's objection is not that transformation is illegitimate. It is that the phrase can become an excuse for a bad transformation. [IM-V05-E076–IM-V05-E078]

V05 therefore improves `IM-C017`: **medium-specific change must be justified by the receiving medium and by an intelligible relation to source priorities. “Different medium” is not a blank check.**

### 12.3 Anime as `総合芸術`

Hirugano's defense of anime as `総合芸術` is not an anti-author slogan. It identifies a real fact of the medium: many specialists share causal and reputational stakes. [IM-V05-E078–IM-V05-E082]

The volume then refuses to let that fact become technocracy. Professionals can misunderstand a source; institutional roles can be inherited through accidents; production constraints can predate artistic discussion. Kyo's intervention matters because “professional” is not synonymous with “correct.”

### 12.4 The final structure as additive synthesis

By the final chapter, the staff have not returned to the source unchanged. They have restructured, added, deepened, and repurposed material. [IM-V05-E176–IM-V05-E183]

The important fact is shared conviction after conflict. Itsuki, Hirugano, and Tarui all believe the new structure is the best anime form. This is the strongest mainline support yet for both:

- `IM-C017` — transformation can be faithful;
- `IM-C018` — conflict can produce a third form that is more than compromise-by-subtraction.

## 13. Japanese voice, style, and terminology

### 13.1 `ほどよく個性的`

Toki's phrase is deceptively revealing. [IM-V05-E003] It names the editor's impossible-seeming optimization problem: enough difference to matter, enough familiarity to remain legible. The phrase should be tracked because it captures publishing's mediation between singularity and audience access more precisely than a simple art-versus-commerce opposition.

### 13.2 `建前`

Kobe's use of `建前` after the interview separates publicly legitimate rationale from actual motive. [IM-V05-E013–IM-V05-E016] The volume repeatedly stages similar splits even when it does not use the term explicitly.

### 13.3 `期待してない`

Nayuta carefully distinguishes expectation from both liking and trust. [IM-V05-E039] The line gives her relation with Yamagata more precision than “bad relationship.” She can tolerate and even trust a person while regarding the person's editorial contribution as irrelevant.

### 13.4 `総合芸術`

Hirugano's `総合芸術` frames anime as an art of coordinated specialties. [IM-V05-E078] The term has argumentative force because it places authorial knowledge inside, not above, a production system.

### 13.5 `敗戦処理`

Tarui's baseball-derived `敗戦処理` is brutally pragmatic. [IM-V05-E083] It names the institutional expectation that the project should minimize damage rather than win. His later `主人公みたい` stance is meaningful because it is chosen **against** that assigned frame. [IM-V05-E089]

### 13.6 `劣化コピー`

In the award chapter, `劣化コピー` is not a synonym for worthless. [IM-V05-E149] It captures positional inferiority within a similarity field. This makes it useful for the talent ledger but dangerous if detached from Haruto's prior positive craft evaluation.

### 13.7 `そこそこ` and `永遠の中堅作家`

The repeated `そこそこ` makes Kaizu's value deliberately anti-spectacular. [IM-V05-E161–IM-V05-E164] The prose first strips away obvious prestige categories and only then reveals endurance as admirable.

### 13.8 `空っぽ`

Kyo's self-description `空っぽ` is not a neutral fact about her. [IM-V05-E169] It is the endpoint of a comparison system in which value requires a dream or exceptional productive identity. The rest of the chapter supplies evidence against that self-description without simply telling her she is secretly exceptional.

### 13.9 `ままならない`

The word recurs at the grave and after Kyo's rejection. [IM-V05-E107, IM-V05-E193] It becomes a compact series vocabulary for the fact that desire, family, vocation, and recognition resist clean authorial control.

### 13.10 `主人公候補`

The afterword's `主人公候補たち` is PF, but its wording matters. [IM-V05-E198] It explicitly extends protagonist potential to older professionals who would be background functions in a narrower youth-genius story.

## 14. Illustration and publication-form analysis

V05's visual layer continues the documentary/formal expansion established in V04 while placing more direct emphasis on Kyo's changing position.

### 14.1 Front color plates

The frontmatter foregrounds swimsuit/fanservice and the Kyo/Haruto bar pairing before the industry-heavy narrative begins. [IM-V05-E200–IM-V05-E202] This packaging does not tell us what the volume “is really about.” It establishes the commercial and relational expectations against which the prose later complicates those scenes.

### 14.2 Embedded industry documents

`V05-ILL-008` and the award materials continue the series' use of embedded documents to materialize invisible systems. [IM-V05-E203, IM-V05-E211–IM-V05-E212] Pitches, submission summaries, award rules, and evaluation objects become visible parts of the light novel's formal field rather than exposition that exists only in prose.

### 14.3 Labor and bodies

The editorial-office and Nayuta/Kyo illustrations give visual presence to otherwise easily invisible supporting conditions around writing. [IM-V05-E204–IM-V05-E205] Conversely, the swimsuit-study image eroticizes craft labor. [IM-V05-E206] V05 again requires us to hold formal eroticization and labor analysis together without letting either erase the other.

### 14.4 Anime conflict

The anime-meeting illustration gives the multi-agent dispute visual weight. [IM-V05-E208] This matters because the chapter's thesis depends on refusing an abstract “author versus industry” binary. The image makes the creative conflict interpersonal.

### 14.5 Kyo's movement from observer to actor

The late bar illustration isolates Kyo with Haruto, while the confession illustration gives her direct emotional action a major formal anchor. [IM-V05-E213–IM-V05-E214] This continues the V03 pattern in which illustrations frequently mark **recognition events**, but V05 changes who acts: Kyo is no longer merely someone whose feelings are seen by others.

### 14.6 Formal thesis

**STRENGTHEN / EXPAND:** the visual layer increasingly functions as part of the series' model of creative systems. It can show bodies, offices, documents, rules, meetings, and direct relational action. V05 also makes the prose-image division of labor itself a joke in `水着回３`, where narration explicitly yields the page to Kantoku. [IM-V05-E065, IM-V05-E207]

## 15. Counterevidence and anti-theses

A rigorous V05 reading should resist several tempting overstatements.

### Anti-thesis 1: “All creativity is collaborative.”

**Too strong.** Nayuta is strong counterevidence. Her prose content appears unusually complete before editorial intervention, and both she and Yamagata describe substantive editing as minimal. [IM-V05-E032–IM-V05-E040, IM-V05-E108–IM-V05-E111]

**Better formulation:** creative *life and production* are interdependent even when a creator's imaginative/content core is unusually autonomous.

### Anti-thesis 2: “Kyo's hiring proves meritocracy is fake, so her later competence does not matter.”

**Too strong.** The entry process is compromised, but V05 separately establishes later work quality and contribution. [IM-V05-E012–IM-V05-E017, IM-V05-E141]

**Better formulation:** access conditions and subsequent competence are separate causal/moral questions.

### Anti-thesis 3: “Editors create trends, therefore readers have no agency.”

**Unsupported.** Kobe describes active industry attempts to manufacture categories and capitalize on success, not total control of audience reception. [IM-V05-E046]

### Anti-thesis 4: “The newcomer prize is meaningless.”

**Unsupported.** The prize allocates publication, money, attention, and institutional identity. The point is not meaninglessness; it is that the meaning is **constructed and multi-objective**, not a pure measure of intrinsic merit. [IM-V05-E138–IM-V05-E155]

### Anti-thesis 5: “Kaizu proves mediocrity is better than genius.”

**Unsupported.** Haruto admires a dimension—endurance—that genius does not guarantee. V05 adds an axis; it does not invert the hierarchy into a new total ranking. [IM-V05-E161–IM-V05-E167]

### Anti-thesis 6: “Kyo's kindness is her vocation.”

**Premature.** Haruto establishes that her receptivity has real value, but V05 does not establish that Kyo should make “being emotionally useful” into a total identity. Doing so would risk recreating the very self-erasure the chapter criticizes.

### Anti-thesis 7: “The anime proves authors should seize control.”

**Too strong.** V05 explicitly shows the limits of author expertise, the stakes carried by scriptwriter/director, and the burnout risk of deep author participation. [IM-V05-E076–IM-V05-E095]

**Better formulation:** no single professional role owns correctness; good adaptation requires negotiated authority responsive to medium and source.

### Anti-thesis 8: “Kaiko's father is simply abusive or simply right.”

**Both flatten the evidence.** He exercises coercive authority and demeans her chosen work, but he also possesses legitimate information about professional risk and demonstrable care. [IM-V05-E115–IM-V05-E127]

### Anti-thesis 9: “Itsuki has forgiven his father.”

**False within V05.** He remains estranged. What changes is confidence in the simplicity of his earlier condemnation. [IM-V05-E096–IM-V05-E107]

### Anti-thesis 10: “Kyo's confession means she has become a protagonist in the same sense as Itsuki.”

**Overfitted.** The volume gives her direct agency and the final line says her story enters a new stage, but it does not make her adopt Itsuki's exact protagonist ideology. The stronger claim is that V05 pluralizes agency without requiring everyone to use the same self-theory.

No prior claim is rejected.

## 17. Open questions after V05

1. **Can Kyo build a positive self-conception that does not depend on being useful to exceptional people?** Her confession is an act of self-authorization, but heartbreak does not answer the vocational question.
2. **What becomes of Haruto/Kyo after “let me think”?** V05 establishes sincerity, not outcome.
3. **Will Itsuki's protagonist criterion for Nayuta remain professionally comparative now that the text itself increasingly pluralizes protagonist status?**
4. **Can Nayuta ever develop a substantive editorial relation, or is her content-level autonomy stable?** V05 provides strong counterevidence against assuming all creators need the same form of collaboration.
5. **How will the anime handle the transformed structure in execution?** V05 establishes a collectively endorsed plan, not production outcome.
6. **Does deep anime participation damage Itsuki's novel work or self-regulation as Kobe fears?**
7. **Will Kyo's editorial competence become an independent vocational desire, or remain a context in which she searches for herself?**
8. **Does Kaiko's family recognition survive practical career pressure?** The emotional settlement does not eliminate income/cancellation risk.
9. **Will Chihiro's autonomous hobbies and paid work continue to reduce the domestic-service imbalance with Itsuki?**
10. **How long can Itsuki assimilate evidence about Chihiro into the false male category before the category itself becomes unstable?**
11. **Does `IM-C020` generalize beyond Kyo/Haruto?** The series has many reader-response examples, but V05 gives the strongest explicit causal formulation so far.
12. **Does `IM-C021` remain a serious success axis when a middle-tier writer faces actual decline or exit?**

## 19. V05 end-state

### 19.1 Strongest supported theses at this boundary

1. **Creative autonomy must be disaggregated.** V05's Nayuta material shows unusually high content-level autonomy alongside strong dependence in deadline, communication, production, circulation, and affective conditions. [IM-V05-E030–IM-V05-E046, IM-V05-E108–IM-V05-E111]
2. **Institutional recognition is designed and consequential rather than neutral.** Genres, trends, awards, prize levels, and applicant behavior are partly manufactured through institutional decisions. [IM-V05-E046, IM-V05-E138–IM-V05-E155]
3. **Protagonist language is becoming plural and portable.** It can describe Itsuki's self-motivation, Tarui's defiant work ethic, Kaizu's endurance, and older professionals' continuing struggle. [IM-V05-E005–IM-V05-E007, IM-V05-E089, IM-V05-E163–IM-V05-E167, IM-V05-E198]
4. **Longevity is a distinct success axis.** Kaizu's middle-tier persistence is treated as admirable without being redescribed as hidden genius. [IM-V05-E161–IM-V05-E167]
5. **Reception can feed back into creation.** Kyo's emotional response to Haruto's work is explicitly identified as something that kept him able to continue. [IM-V05-E170–IM-V05-E173]
6. **Professional roles do not erase personhood or incompatible stakes.** Editors, scriptwriters, directors, authors, and part-timers each carry different expertise, incentives, vulnerabilities, and attachments. [IM-V05-E024–IM-V05-E046, IM-V05-E066–IM-V05-E095]
7. **Adaptation fidelity is selective and medium-specific.** V05 both rejects empty “media difference” rhetoric and ends with a radically transformed structure collectively regarded as optimal. [IM-V05-E076–IM-V05-E095, IM-V05-E176–IM-V05-E183]
8. **Kyo's value cannot be reduced either to ordinariness or usefulness.** The volume establishes real causal contribution while ending with her direct personal self-claim and heartbreak. [IM-V05-E008–IM-V05-E017, IM-V05-E168–IM-V05-E196]
9. **Family concern and family authority are separable.** Kaiko's father can have legitimate risk knowledge without owning her adult vocational choice; Itsuki can understand his father's emotional complexity without reconciling. [IM-V05-E096–IM-V05-E127]
10. **`ままならない` is becoming a central experiential grammar.** Love, family, career, adaptation, and recognition resist the fantasy that life can be authored with the control of fiction. [IM-V05-E101–IM-V05-E107, IM-V05-E191–IM-V05-E196]
