# 妹さえいればいい。 Volume 06 — Deep Reading

## 1. Executive thesis

Volume 06 is the first decisive **collision between self-authorship and finitude** in *妹さえいればいい。*. Earlier volumes repeatedly allowed characters to imagine that enough talent, effort, professional success, or narrative centrality could eventually put life into the right configuration. Itsuki's strongest version of that fantasy was the V03 resolution that he would confess to Nayuta only after becoming her `対等の主人公`—an equal protagonist. V05 had already destabilized `主人公` as a literal hierarchy by distributing protagonist-like agency among ordinary professionals, survivors, editors, and friends. V06 goes further: the resolution itself becomes ethically and existentially untenable once Itsuki is reminded that the person he loves is mortal and cannot simply be placed on hold while he perfects himself. [IM-V06-E019–IM-V06-E024, IM-V06-E090–IM-V06-E106]

The volume's major romantic event is therefore not merely that **Itsuki and Nayuta become a couple**. It is that Itsuki chooses a living person over the purity of his own self-authored rule. His earlier principle is not quietly forgotten; the narration explicitly recalls it and declares it broken. [IM-V06-E099–IM-V06-E105] He experiences that break as painful because protagonist equality has been part of his dignity structure. Yet the volume insists that a principle can become self-defeating when it transforms another person into a future reward whose continued availability is assumed. The decisive correction is mortality: Itsuki has already lost his mother and Sekigahara Yuu; people die, disappear, and cannot be guaranteed to wait. [IM-V06-E090–IM-V06-E094] V06 thus revises the protagonist thesis again. Self-authorship includes not only choosing a rule for oneself, but **recognizing when reality and relationship demand that the rule be relinquished**.

This is inseparable from Nayuta's own crisis. She begins the volume from what should appear to be the privileged side of the talent hierarchy. She is a near-limit case of creative ease: success, praise, and professional demand have come without the prolonged struggle that structures Itsuki's and Kyo's self-conceptions. But precisely because she sees Itsuki and Kyo `輝いて` through effort, she comes to experience effortless success as a deficit. She undertakes an enormous media mix `自分を磨くため`, hoping to see the same `景色` they do. [IM-V06-E033–IM-V06-E040] Collaborative production initially gives her something genuinely new: for the first time since debut, she says work is fun. [IM-V06-E068] Yet that discovery mutates into comparative compulsion. `私も輝かなきゃ` becomes the formally obsessive `もっと頑張らないと`, and eventually overwork, sleep deprivation, malnutrition, and collapse. [IM-V06-E070, IM-V06-E086, IM-V06-E094–IM-V06-E097]

The resulting revelation is one of the series' clearest separations of **talent, achievement, effort, lovability, and human worth**. Nayuta has inferred that Itsuki loves the extraordinary writer: if she is not striving, shining, and professionally amazing, he will stop seeing her. Itsuki answers by explicitly denying the equivalence. Even if she were not striving, not impressive, not shining, and not a great writer, he would still love her. [IM-V06-E096–IM-V06-E098] The scene is not a repudiation of achievement. Both remain creators and both continue to care about work. What it repudiates is achievement as a condition of being lovable. V06 therefore makes the danger in `IM-C002` more precise: **talent and effort can become internalized not merely as measures of competence, but as imagined prerequisites for relational legitimacy**.

The same volume also gives the clearest statement so far that **relationships are maintained practices rather than self-sustaining statuses**. After rejecting Kyo, Itsuki cannot assume friendship will reset itself. Chihiro tells him that if both people want the relationship, they must act so that it continues; Itsuki explicitly internalizes the lesson. [IM-V06-E007–IM-V06-E011] The later TRPG session operationalizes it. Itsuki, Kyo, and Haruto deliberately use a game as a low-pressure structure in which conversation can resume without directly rehearsing romantic injury; the narration says their hidden purpose is fulfilled. [IM-V06-E044–IM-V06-E047] The afterword then adds a useful paratextual correction: Haruto's apparently smooth session is not a neutral rules demonstration but careful hosting, including occasional hidden dice manipulation. [IM-V06-E109] Play works because someone is doing relational labor.

V06 continues to deepen the series' creative-industry analysis at the same time. The cast audition is not a simple contest in which the best actor naturally rises to the top. Dozens of competent people compete for a handful of roles while producers, sponsors, event strategy, visual marketability, long-term talent development, adaptation quality, and authorial preference pull in different directions. [IM-V06-E048–IM-V06-E057] The producer's deliberately extreme formulation—good anime is anime that earns profit—is not endorsed as the only correct position, but neither is it treated as stupidity. The narration explicitly says the conflict arises from professional standpoint, and the final casting choice is a negotiated middle rather than a victory for one pure criterion. Crucially, it then refuses retrospective certainty: nobody yet knows whether the casting is correct. [IM-V06-E053–IM-V06-E057] This strongly strengthens the series' model of creative production as **multi-objective coordination under uncertainty**.

The audio-production chapters make the same point from another medium. Itsuki discovers that drama CD writing cannot simply delete narration from prose; dialogue and sound effects must carry functions prose previously handled. [IM-V06-E065–IM-V06-E067] At recording, author, director, scriptwriter, sound director, performers, and manga reporter all contribute. Itsuki explicitly asks actors to make his beloved characters shine with *their* power. [IM-V06-E087–IM-V06-E089] The work's final form is therefore neither sovereign authorial expression nor institutional theft. It is derivative co-creation—sometimes messy, sometimes ethically compromised, and sometimes aesthetically additive.

That last qualifier matters because V06 is unusually alert to the difference between **collaboration** and **manipulation**. The report manga is instructed to depict a harmonious cast before such harmony exists. Kobe's `世の中とはそういうものだ` makes promotional paratext a curated public narrative rather than transparent documentary evidence. [IM-V06-E087–IM-V06-E088] The same epistemic caution applies elsewhere: the TRPG record is shaped by Haruto's hidden hosting; afterwords are creator paratext rather than narrator fact; and comedy can frame coercive behavior without ethically neutralizing it. Pablo's assaultive attempt to expose Chihiro's body remains a boundary violation even though it is staged as vulgar farce. [IM-V06-E059–IM-V06-E064]

The chapter `新人賞授賞式` meanwhile gives the strongest argument yet for **aesthetic pluralism without aesthetic nihilism**. Aoba's mistake is not that she possesses strong preferences or even that rebellious absolutism is artistically useless. The narration criticizes her for confusing personal value with universal rightness and for collapsing liking/disliking into correct/incorrect and superior/inferior. It immediately adds that precisely this painful youthful absolutism can become genuine creative fuel. [IM-V06-E075–IM-V06-E079] The volume therefore refuses both easy relativism and purity-policing certainty. Strong convictions can produce important art; they become epistemically dangerous when converted into total authority over what everyone else must value.

Professional survival receives a parallel deepening. The award party is explicitly a ritual at which creators confirm that they have survived another year; only two of Itsuki and Haruto's five-person award cohort remain present and professionally active five cycles later. Kaizu asks younger writers about health checks as part of career maintenance. Haruto hopes even his competitors survive for a long time. [IM-V06-E071–IM-V06-E084] V05 opened longevity as an independent success axis; V06 makes its infrastructure visible: health, peers, informal mentoring, parties, friendship, receptive readers, and practical care all participate in staying alive—professionally and literally.

Kyo's vocational thread also reaches a sharper formulation. The GF Bunko staff value her not because she is secretly another genius but because of ordinary reliability: initiative, attention, punctuality, communication, willingness to ask questions, and honesty about mistakes. The institution can plausibly absorb her as a full-time employee. Yet she asks the more fundamental question: `向いているものとなりたいものは違う。自分は本当に編集者になりたいのか？` [IM-V06-E041–IM-V06-E043] V06 therefore refuses to let aptitude or external demand decide vocation automatically. A person can be good at something, useful to others, and wanted by an institution without yet knowing whether that role is the life they want.

The volume's most important structural achievement is that these strands converge rather than remaining separate subplots. Kyo's decision to keep moving makes Nayuta want to strive; Nayuta's striving activates massive institutional machinery; that machinery gives her meaningful new creative experience but also generates externalities and eventually bodily collapse; the collapse forces Itsuki to confront mortality; confronting mortality breaks his protagonist-equality rule; breaking that rule produces the romantic coupling. The ending therefore does not resolve “romance” while leaving “work” untouched. **Work, body, love, institutional power, self-worth, and narrative identity causally produce one another.** [IM-V06-E001–IM-V06-E018, IM-V06-E033–IM-V06-E040, IM-V06-E068–IM-V06-E070, IM-V06-E086, IM-V06-E090–IM-V06-E106]

### V06 thesis in compressed form

> **Volume 06 turns the series' earlier ideal of self-authorship against its own limits. Relationships survive through maintenance rather than inertia; talent and effort become dangerous when mistaken for prerequisites of lovability; institutions translate private desire into consequences for strangers; collaboration remains plural, negotiated, and medium-specific; and Itsuki finally learns that being the protagonist of one's life cannot mean making loved people wait until one's self-concept is complete. V06's decisive movement is from “I will become worthy and then live” to “life is finite, so principles must answer to people.”** [IT]

## 3. Volume architecture and narrative movement

V06 is unusually legible as a sequence of **conversion problems**: how does one form of value become another, and what is lost in translation?

1. `人間力` converts romantic rejection into a problem of relationship maintenance.
2. `主人公ズ` converts a love triangle into plural agency rather than a winner/loser closure.
3. `男友達` converts heartbreak into male friendship and potentially into fiction.
4. `ちんこ` converts travel and visual interpretation into prose revision.
5. `新たなるケツ路` converts unfamiliar fine art into Setsuna's new artistic identity.
6. `那由多の景色` converts private romantic insecurity into industrial-scale media-mix mobilization.
7. `白川京さん送別会` converts ordinary work competence into institutional demand while leaving vocation unsettled.
8. `クロニカクロニクル④` converts play into relationship-repair infrastructure.
9. `キャストオーディション` converts character interpretation into a multi-objective personnel decision.
10. `パブロ・プリケッソ` tests the limit between artistic desire, social circumstance, and bodily boundary.
11. `妬心` converts collaborative discovery into jealousy and comparison.
12. `新人賞授賞式` / `二次会` convert competition into professional community and survival ritual.
13. `その頃の彼女` compresses self-improvement into obsessive formal repetition.
14. `メインヒロイン` converts bodily collapse into a crisis of value hierarchy and finally into romantic action.

The architecture matters because the final confession is causally prepared by much more than romantic dialogue. Nayuta's collapse arises from a work project she chose because of a self-worth problem generated by how she perceives Itsuki and Kyo. Itsuki's decision changes because professional interruption exposes the superiority of a person over a product and because mortality reveals the hidden arrogance of indefinite postponement. [IM-V06-E033–IM-V06-E040, IM-V06-E068–IM-V06-E070, IM-V06-E086, IM-V06-E090–IM-V06-E106]

The chapter ordering therefore creates a broad progression:

> **rejection → maintenance → plural protagonists → friendship → art encounter → striving → institutionalization → repair → production negotiation → boundary test → comparative jealousy → professional survival → compulsion → collapse → reprioritization → coupling.**

The final `A new story begins.` is consequently exact rather than decorative. [IM-V06-E106] The volume closes one major rule governing Itsuki's romantic behavior while opening a new state whose consequences are still unknown.

## 4. Narration, focalization, and epistemic structure

### 4.1 Kyo's focalization corrects the “loser heroine” template

The opening refuses to treat rejection as narrative erasure. Kyo is hurt, but she does not wish the confession undone; movement is preferable to remaining permanently unrecognized. [IM-V06-E001–IM-V06-E002] Her conversation with Nayuta then refuses a second stock simplification. She does not decide Nayuta is an enemy, and Nayuta does not ask for moral absolution by surrendering Itsuki. Kyo can name prior wrongdoing, preserve friendship, and still continue desiring the same person. [IM-V06-E003–IM-V06-E006]

The narration's later counterfactual—Kyo *might* have accepted Haruto without the Nayuta conversation—is carefully hedged. [IM-V06-E017] This is important evidence discipline. The text gives causal significance to the conversation without claiming deterministic access to an alternative timeline.

### 4.2 Itsuki's self-story becomes an object of criticism from inside his own perspective

One of V06's most consequential lines is Itsuki's recognition that he created `手前勝手な物語` in his head and pushed another person into it. [IM-V06-E007] This is the inverse of his usual novelist privilege. His skill at constructing narrative becomes a social hazard when applied to people as if they were characters whose motivations can be assigned for convenience.

This makes the volume's meta-fiction unusually concrete. “Narrativizing one's life” is neither celebrated nor rejected wholesale. It can authorize action (`主人公`), help process emotion through fiction, or generate solidarity. It can also erase the opacity and autonomy of other people. V06 increasingly distinguishes **self-authorship** from **authorship of others**.

### 4.3 Promotional narratives are explicitly unreliable

The report-manga instruction in `メインヒロイン` is a direct epistemic warning. The actual pre-recording room is not yet harmonious, but the public-facing account is ordered to present it that way. [IM-V06-E087–IM-V06-E088] This is not merely a joke about publishing. It establishes within the fiction that “behind-the-scenes” paratext can itself be authored toward promotional goals.

For the corpus, this supports a retrieval rule: represented promotional documents, report manga, cast copy, and marketing language must be analyzed as situated artifacts, not automatically elevated to transparent documentary truth.

### 4.4 The afterword also corrects surface appearance

The TRPG session appears to succeed through ordinary play. Hirasaka's afterword says Haruto is actively hosting and sometimes altering dice outcomes. [IM-V06-E109] Because this is `PF`, it should not be retroactively converted into narrator-level fact about every unseen die roll. But it does materially support the larger formal reading: the social smoothness of the game is **produced labor**, not an unmediated property of the rules.

## 5. Character-state changes

### 5.1 Hashima Itsuki — from worthiness deferral to finite-time choice

V06 begins by making Itsuki confront a relatively small failure of social perception. Kyo's feelings were visible to Haruto and Nayuta; Itsuki instead invented a convenient model of Kyo and failed to see her. [IM-V06-E007–IM-V06-E012] He interprets the problem as `人間力`, a useful term because the deficit is neither intelligence nor talent. It is the capacity to understand that another person exceeds the role one has assigned them.

His conversation with Chihiro then introduces a new relational ethic: wanting friendship is insufficient unless both parties act so the relationship continues. [IM-V06-E009–IM-V06-E010] This seems modest compared with the finale, but it prepares the decisive move. Itsuki's recurring flaw is not simply that he possesses grand ideals. It is that he treats desired future states as though they can remain available without present maintenance.

The `男友達` chapter temporarily hardens rather than dissolves his protagonist rhetoric. He does not want to be “a romcom protagonist”; he wants to be protagonist of an ideal, cool story. [IM-V06-E019–IM-V06-E020] The distinction clarifies that `主人公` has always been about existential self-evaluation more than genre occupancy.

The finale then supplies the missing limit: **time**. Nayuta's collapse activates memories of two deaths and makes Itsuki recognize the falsity built into “wait for me until I become worthy.” [IM-V06-E090–IM-V06-E094] His confession is therefore a change in value ordering. He does not suddenly decide success is meaningless. He explicitly says love matters more than anime success *and his own success*. [IM-V06-E099–IM-V06-E102]

This is why `IM-C014` requires another revision. V05 had transformed protagonist status into a portable rhetoric of agency. V06 adds that self-authored agency is mature only when it can revise itself in response to finite embodied life. A protagonist ideology that cannot bend becomes another script imposed on reality.

### 5.2 Kani Nayuta — from effortless genius to compulsive striving

Nayuta's V06 movement is structurally opposite Itsuki's. Itsuki loosens a self-imposed criterion; Nayuta invents one.

Her initial motivation is psychologically intelligible. She sees Itsuki and Kyo striving, changing, and shining. Because her own extraordinary success has been comparatively effortless, she cannot access the sense of development that appears to accompany their struggle. [IM-V06-E033–IM-V06-E036] Choosing media mix for `自分を磨くため` is not inherently pathological. Indeed, it produces a genuine discovery: collaboration is interesting, other creators can notice things she never consciously put into the work, and for the first time since debut she finds work fun. [IM-V06-E068]

The danger appears when development becomes comparison. Itsuki's praise of hardworking performers triggers `私も輝かなきゃ`. [IM-V06-E069–IM-V06-E070] The later formal block `もっと頑張らないと` removes ordinary syntax and argument: the imperative simply reproduces itself. [IM-V06-E086] The collapse then makes the body an authority that ambition cannot negotiate away. [IM-V06-E094–IM-V06-E095]

Her hospital explanation reveals the deeper mistake. She has interpreted Itsuki's admiration of her talent as conditional love: unless she remains a “great writer” who shines through effort, she is not lovable. [IM-V06-E096–IM-V06-E098] This makes V06 a direct challenge to totalizing meritocracy at the intimate level. The genius is not protected from worth anxiety; genius can intensify it because achievement becomes the identity through which one imagines relationship is secured.

### 5.3 Shirakawa Kyo — agency after rejection and the aptitude/desire split

Kyo's strongest V06 development is that rejection does not return her to passivity. She explicitly prefers moving forward to waiting, refuses to make Nayuta her enemy, and recognizes a horizontal equality with Nayuta as two people in love regardless of age, status, or talent. [IM-V06-E001–IM-V06-E018]

Professionally, the GF staff's farewell confirms that her value is not merely “being nice.” Ordinary work behaviors—reliability, communication, initiative, honesty, attention—have made her unusually useful. Kobe can imagine her entering the company permanently. [IM-V06-E041–IM-V06-E043] But the volume wisely refuses to turn external validation into vocation. Her question `向いているものとなりたいものは違う` opens a distinct problem: **being suited to a role and wanting the life organized around that role are different facts**.

### 5.4 Fuwa Haruto — care, competition, and professional realism

Haruto is increasingly positioned as one of the ensemble's relational professionals. He understands Kyo because he watches her; he can sustain a TRPG session that functions as emotional repair; he mentors newcomers; and he hopes competitors survive. [IM-V06-E012–IM-V06-E017, IM-V06-E044–IM-V06-E046, IM-V06-E071–IM-V06-E085]

His “craftsman” separation from work remains useful as a character-specific working model, but V06 does not authorize it as an ontological hierarchy between “artists” and “craftsmen.” [IM-V06-E022–IM-V06-E023] He is emotionally affected by work and by Kyo; he simply routes those relations differently from Itsuki and Nayuta.

### 5.5 Hashima Chihiro — relational wisdom and constrained selfhood

Chihiro supplies some of the volume's most mature advice while still carrying unresolved constraints. Chihiro rejects harem fantasy in favor of an ordinary happy household and explains friendship as reciprocal maintenance. [IM-V06-E009–IM-V06-E011]

The Pablo chapter then gives Chihiro an independent focal problem. Athletic potential did not translate into extracurricular participation because earlier poverty and household labor constrained available choices; even after remarriage removes the material barrier, Chihiro still chooses family time. [IM-V06-E058] The point is neither “family oppression” nor “pure free choice.” Past scarcity shapes habit, identity, and priorities; current attachment makes domestic commitment meaningful in its own right.

Chihiro's rebuke to Pablo—do not casually tell someone to do what they want without knowing their circumstances—therefore carries more weight than a generic prudence lesson. [IM-V06-E060–IM-V06-E061] V06 repeatedly opposes abstract freedom to situated freedom.

### 5.6 Ena Setsuna / Pablo Prikesso — revelation, vocation, and boundary failure

Setsuna's response to Cubism is one of V06's comic but genuine conversion experiences. Itsuki and Haruto initially cannot read Picasso; Setsuna sees an opening and reorganizes his identity around it. [IM-V06-E028–IM-V06-E032] The transformation shows that aesthetic encounter can matter independently of professional utility.

Yet the Pablo chapter refuses to equate artistic intensity with moral exemption. He apologizes for an earlier forcible underwear incident and then commits another bodily violation. [IM-V06-E059–IM-V06-E063] Comedy does not cancel consent. The abandoned painting's later high valuation, unknown to its creator, then separates three axes that are often collapsed: **artistic production, market valuation, and creator recognition**. [IM-V06-E064]

## 6. Relationship ecology

### 6.1 Kyo ↔ Nayuta — rivalry without enemy conversion

The relationship becomes one of the volume's strongest counters to zero-sum romance. Kyo and Nayuta want the same person. The volume does not deny the conflict. But they can retain affection, acknowledge wrongdoing, refuse resentment, and even understand one another through sibling-like language. [IM-V06-E003–IM-V06-E006]

Kyo's thought that Nayuta is `妹みたいに愛らしい` is especially important for `IM-C015`. [IM-V06-E006] “Little sister” continues to function as a relational grammar of care and asymmetry beyond biological classification—and, crucially, outside Itsuki's fetish system.

### 6.2 Itsuki ↔ Kyo — friendship as post-rejection work

Itsuki and Kyo cannot simply “go back to normal,” because normal was partly built on misunderstanding. V06 names the maintenance problem directly and later gives it a social technology: structured play. [IM-V06-E007–IM-V06-E010, IM-V06-E044–IM-V06-E046]

This supports new `IM-C024`: **relationships persist through repeated maintenance practices, not through labels alone**. Friendship is not a static status that survives confession automatically.

### 6.3 Kyo ↔ Haruto — viable possibility, not compensatory prize

Kyo seriously considers that Haruto could make a good partner and might even make her happier. She nevertheless declines because she is not ready to abandon her own desire. The narration's counterfactual preserves the possibility that timing and the Nayuta conversation mattered. [IM-V06-E012–IM-V06-E017]

Haruto is therefore not narratively converted into a “reward” for Kyo's rejection. His desire remains real, and so does Kyo's refusal. The relationship moves into a new state without being resolved.

### 6.4 Itsuki ↔ Nayuta — coupling through de-totalization of worth

The hospital scene is the decisive romantic movement because both characters' self-worth systems are exposed simultaneously. Itsuki assumes he must become worthy of Nayuta; Nayuta assumes she must remain extraordinary to be loved by Itsuki. Each has been using achievement as a gate to intimacy from opposite directions. [IM-V06-E096–IM-V06-E102]

Itsuki's confession breaks the symmetry by denying the gate itself. He loves her even in the counterfactual where she is not a great writer. [IM-V06-E098] This is more significant than saying “he finally accepts her confession.” He abandons the premise that career hierarchy should determine whether love can be acted upon.

The volume does not establish that all asymmetry has disappeared. Nayuta's dependence is openly heavy; Itsuki's protagonist vocabulary remains; both careers continue. [IM-V06-E104–IM-V06-E106] The new relationship is a new problem-state, not a final equilibrium.

### 6.5 Friendship groups as resilience infrastructure

The Hakone trip, TRPG session, award party, and second party collectively show that casual sociality is not analytically trivial. [IM-V06-E024, IM-V06-E044–IM-V06-E046, IM-V06-E071–IM-V06-E085] These spaces:

- absorb heartbreak;
- permit indirect disclosure;
- restore routine after relational disruption;
- circulate professional advice;
- normalize health maintenance;
- let creators encounter people with radically different motives and tastes;
- produce competition without requiring annihilation of competitors.

V06 therefore strengthens `IM-C004` and `IM-C019` together: play and social ritual are forms of creative/professional infrastructure because people must remain psychologically and socially viable in order to keep working.

## 7. Creative labor, publishing, and industry

### 7.1 Illustration as generative authorship

The Hakone sequence gives unusually direct evidence that an illustrator can alter the prose source itself. Setsuna's earlier image for `新世界の創妹記` clarifies a world Itsuki had only vaguely imagined; Itsuki then substantially rewrites the novel, and narration explicitly attributes improved textual quality and likely downstream sales to that visual stimulus. [IM-V06-E025–IM-V06-E027]

This is strong evidence against treating light-novel illustration as merely decorative packaging. In at least this represented case, the causal chain is:

> prose draft → illustration interpretation → clearer authorial world-model → prose revision → changed finished novel → likely changed reader/market outcome.

That is collaborative authorship without requiring equal control over every layer.

### 7.2 Media-mix power and systemic externality

Nayuta's media-mix chapter expands `IM-C003` and `IM-C013` beyond ordinary production. Her prestige attracts elite directors, writers, actors, manga creators, sponsors, and money with remarkable ease. [IM-V06-E037–IM-V06-E038] The same concentration removes resources from elsewhere. Narration deliberately shifts perspective toward unknown projects whose budgets, personnel, schedules, or futures are harmed by that transfer. [IM-V06-E039–IM-V06-E040]

This should not be reduced to “Nayuta is morally bad.” She neither sees nor directly commands all downstream losses. The more precise point is that **individual desire becomes institutionally amplified**, and power can generate externalities without malicious intent. V06 thus adds political-economic scale to the series' treatment of talent.

### 7.3 Casting as multi-objective optimization

The cast-audition chapter is one of the strongest industrial sections in the corpus so far. Around thirty candidates may contend for a single character. [IM-V06-E048] Acting skill is only one criterion. The production also considers:

- sponsor relationships;
- age;
- appearance;
- singing and event participation;
- radio/television availability;
- career development;
- unit balance;
- audience conversion;
- author/source fit;
- production economics.

Haruto's complaint about his own adaptation is ethically intelligible: an author may reasonably reject having a life-defining work treated mainly as somebody else's training platform. [IM-V06-E050] But the producer's standpoint is also intelligible: industries need new performers, products need revenue, and event/package economics can determine whether projects survive. [IM-V06-E051–IM-V06-E055]

The narration explicitly refuses to declare one side simply wrong. [IM-V06-E055] The resulting midcareer compromise is not framed as principled perfection; nobody knows yet whether it is the correct decision. [IM-V06-E056–IM-V06-E057] This is exactly the kind of prospective uncertainty the analytical method must preserve.

### 7.4 Drama CD as medium-specific craft

Itsuki initially assumes audio-only writing should be easier because prose narration disappears. He discovers the opposite constraint: dialogue and sound must communicate information prose could otherwise narrate. [IM-V06-E065–IM-V06-E067]

This strongly strengthens `IM-C017`. Medium-specific fidelity requires transformation because the communicative resources differ. “Faithfulness” cannot mean identical surface form when a medium literally lacks the source medium's tools.

### 7.5 Recording as additive co-creation

At recording, Itsuki asks performers to make his characters shine through their power. [IM-V06-E089] This is a significant shift from an author guarding source integrity against intrusion. It acknowledges derivative performance as capable of adding value not already complete in the source.

The production still contains hierarchy and curation. The report manga's manufactured harmony shows that institutional storytelling can distort process. [IM-V06-E087–IM-V06-E088] V06 therefore does not romanticize collaboration. It distinguishes **creative addition** from **public-relations control**.

### 7.6 Awards, parties, and field reproduction

The newcomer-prize ceremony is not only celebration. It reproduces the professional field:

- older writers meet newcomers;
- creators measure another year of survival;
- health advice circulates;
- competition becomes motivation;
- aesthetic ideologies are performed and challenged;
- social capital forms in afterparties;
- heterogeneous motives become visible. [IM-V06-E071–IM-V06-E085]

Only two of Itsuki and Haruto's five-person cohort remain in this represented professional scene five cycles later. [IM-V06-E074] V06 therefore strongly strengthens `IM-C021`: longevity is neither guaranteed by award recognition nor reducible to peak talent.

## 8. Talent, success, failure, self-worth, and protagonist status

### 8.1 Talent remains real

V06 does not flatten talent into social construction. Setsuna's intuitive visual ability, Nayuta's extraordinary writing, experienced performers, and industry experts all possess differentiated capacities. [IM-V06-E026–IM-V06-E032, IM-V06-E033–IM-V06-E040, IM-V06-E048–IM-V06-E057]

The analytical correction remains `IM-C002`: **real difference is not total human ranking**.

### 8.2 The genius can be the most vulnerable to meritocratic self-totalization

Nayuta is a critical case because external success cannot solve the underlying worth problem. Her anxiety is not “I might fail commercially.” It is “if I am not amazing and striving, the person I love may stop choosing me.” [IM-V06-E096–IM-V06-E098]

This requires a **REVISE** transition for `IM-C002`: totalization occurs not only when observers rank people by talent. People can internalize achievement as a condition for deserving love, attention, or continued existence in another person's story.

### 8.3 `主人公ズ`: protagonist plurality

The title `主人公ズ` pluralizes the motif directly. Kyo, Haruto, Nayuta, and Itsuki do not occupy a single objective protagonist ladder. They act from different centers of value. [IM-V06-E012–IM-V06-E020]

Itsuki's statement that he wants to be protagonist of his own “super-cool” story remains sincere. [IM-V06-E020] But V06's ending shows that a self-authored story can become a prison if preserving its elegance matters more than living.

### 8.4 Broken resolution as development, not failure

`決意は、今ここに折れた` is among the most important formal recurrences in the first six volumes. [IM-V06-E100] The text does not conceal inconsistency. It stages it.

Itsuki's prior rule was meaningful because it protected a damaged sense of dignity. Breaking it hurts. Yet he explicitly prefers love to personal success and prefers risking the dream to risking Nayuta's loss. [IM-V06-E101–IM-V06-E102] V06 therefore reframes a “broken principle” as potential maturity when the principle's hidden cost becomes visible.

This produces a revised `IM-C014`:

> `主人公` remains a technology of self-authorization, but mature self-authorship includes the capacity to revise one's own story when rigid consistency would instrumentalize others or ignore finitude.

### 8.5 Aesthetic pluralism and productive immaturity

Aoba's confrontation supplies a separate anti-totalization argument. Narration criticizes her for treating her values as universal and conflating taste with correctness and hierarchy. [IM-V06-E075–IM-V06-E076] But it immediately refuses the easy opposite conclusion: youthful absolutism can energize creation and even produce field-changing work. [IM-V06-E077–IM-V06-E079]

This opens `IM-C026`: **aesthetic pluralism does not require weak conviction**. The epistemic problem begins when conviction is mistaken for universal jurisdiction.

## 9. Family, siblinghood, and identity

### 9.1 Chihiro's family orientation is chosen and constrained

Chihiro's nonparticipation in sports once had material causes: limited money and household labor. Current circumstances are different, yet Chihiro still prioritizes the new family. [IM-V06-E058] This distinction matters because it prevents two errors:

- treating domestic orientation as timeless essence;
- treating present family preference as nothing but imposed deprivation.

V06 presents biography as cumulative: earlier constraint can shape later values without mechanically determining them.

### 9.2 Sibling language continues to exceed biology

Kyo's description of Nayuta as `妹みたいに愛らしい` reinforces the project claim that sibling terms operate as affective/relational positions. [IM-V06-E006] The term here has nothing to do with Itsuki's sexualized ideal. It marks tenderness, asymmetry, protectiveness, and chosen closeness.

### 9.3 Itsuki and his father — empathy is not exoneration

At the finale, Itsuki thinks he can no longer simply condemn his father because he now understands how love can become more important than a dream. [IM-V06-E101–IM-V06-E103] This must not be overread. V06 does **not** establish that every action his father took was justified or that coercive authority disappears because the emotional motive is intelligible.

The correct delta for `IM-C023` is therefore **PRESERVE**. V06 increases Itsuki's empathy for relational priority; it does not establish that love grants authority over another adult's life choices.

## 10. Sexuality, vulgar comedy, and boundaries

V06 continues the series' use of sexual vulgarity as both comedy and social test. The penis-comparison sequence is intentionally ridiculous and should not be inflated into a major ethical thesis. It does, however, lightly echo the series' larger comparison anxiety: even in a joke, bodies become ranked and identity becomes vulnerable to relative measurement.

The Pablo/Chihiro material is more consequential. Pablo explicitly acknowledges having tried to remove Chihiro's underwear by force, then later creates another physical boundary violation in the name of artistic desire. [IM-V06-E059–IM-V06-E063] Chihiro refuses and physically enforces the boundary.

The analytical method therefore requires a dual reading:

1. the scene is framed as broad vulgar comedy;
2. the represented act remains coercive and non-consensual.

Comedy is **framing**, not automatic ethical acquittal. This strengthens `IM-C007` and supplies a useful test case for later boundary analysis.

Nayuta's sexual/romantic behavior also changes meaning once coupling occurs, but V06 ends immediately after the relationship forms. No later sexual dynamic may be inferred yet.

## 11. Games, food, alcohol, and social ritual

The fourth `クロニカクロニクル` sequence provides the strongest evidence yet for games as **relational infrastructure**. The participants do not merely happen to heal while playing. They schedule play because ordinary conversation after confession/rejection is difficult. [IM-V06-E044–IM-V06-E046]

The game offers:

- a shared external object of attention;
- role distance from autobiographical identity;
- predictable turn-taking;
- permission for joking;
- a reason to remain physically/socially present together;
- a low-pressure bridge back to post-game conversation.

The afterword's disclosure that Haruto is actively tuning the session strengthens the point: successful play is hosted. [IM-V06-E109]

The award party and second party perform analogous professional work. [IM-V06-E071–IM-V06-E085] Drinking and socializing create spaces in which formal hierarchies loosen enough for health advice, career stories, erotic eccentricity, rivalry, mentoring, and encouragement to circulate. V06 therefore further rejects the idea that “play” is merely decorative downtime beside the serious plot of career and romance.

## 12. Fiction-within-fiction, adaptation, and intertext

### 12.1 Fiction as emotional transformation

Itsuki advises Haruto to put heartbreak, fantasy, and distorted desire into fiction. [IM-V06-E021–IM-V06-E023] The narration then qualifies the advice by distinguishing Haruto's working mode. This supports the project's transformation model while preventing universalization: creators do not all metabolize biography through art in the same way.

### 12.2 `新世界の創妹記` — work crossing media in both directions

The Setsuna example reverses the common source→adaptation arrow. An image derived from prose returns to change the prose itself. [IM-V06-E025–IM-V06-E027] This is a compact model of media feedback rather than one-way adaptation.

### 12.3 `景色` — interpretation exceeding conscious authorial intention

Nayuta finds it exciting that adaptation staff have read her work so closely that they debate emotional structures she did not consciously plan. [IM-V06-E068] This is important evidence against an intentionalist model in which only meanings consciously formulated by the author can be legitimate.

The volume does not say staff interpretation is automatically correct. It demonstrates that a work can become an object of disciplined interpretation whose structures exceed the creator's explicit self-report.

### 12.4 Drama CD and voice performance

The audio chapters distinguish source identity from medium realization. [IM-V06-E065–IM-V06-E067, IM-V06-E087–IM-V06-E089] Characters do not become less “Itsuki's” because performers contribute vocal bodies; Itsuki himself asks them to make the characters shine. This extends `IM-C017` and `IM-C018` from anime restructuring into performance.

## 13. Japanese voice, style, and terminology

### 13.1 `人間力`

`人間力` is intentionally broad and colloquial rather than a technical psychological construct. In V06 it means something like practical human competence: perception, empathy, relationship handling, and the ability not to reduce others to one's own story. [IM-V06-E007–IM-V06-E011]

It should not be translated analytically as a stable quantified trait. The joke works because Itsuki is highly capable in some domains and conspicuously poor in this one.

### 13.2 `主人公ズ`

The pluralized chapter title is formally significant. [IM-V06-E012–IM-V06-E020] It weakens any assumption that the series accepts Itsuki's singular protagonist hierarchy at face value. Multiple characters can occupy agentic centers simultaneously.

### 13.3 `輝く` and `景色`

Nayuta's `景色` vocabulary becomes psychological rather than merely the title of her novels. She wants to see the “landscape” created by striving and to `輝く` like Itsuki and Kyo. [IM-V06-E033–IM-V06-E040] Later, `私も輝かなきゃ` compresses that aspiration into compulsion. [IM-V06-E070]

The lexical chain is therefore:

> observe others shining → desire their landscape → choose self-cultivation → discover collaboration → compare again → must shine → must work harder → collapse.

### 13.4 `もっと頑張らないと`

The one-paragraph repetition is a formal event, not merely repeated dialogue. [IM-V06-E086] Ordinary explanatory language disappears. The phrase is no longer a reasoned proposition but a loop. Typography and duration make the reader experience the narrowing of Nayuta's cognition.

### 13.5 `折れた`

The narration does not say Itsuki “changed his mind” neutrally. His `決意` is said to have **broken**. [IM-V06-E100] The word preserves cost. Development here is not frictionless enlightenment; it is the painful failure of a self-binding rule.

### 13.6 `メインヒロイン`

Itsuki's final `俺の物語のメインヒロイン` keeps the life-as-story metaphor rather than abandoning it. [IM-V06-E104] The correction is therefore not “stop narrativizing life.” It is “do not make story logic a reason to postpone the person who is living beside you.”

### 13.7 `A new story begins.`

The English line closes the volume after coupling. [IM-V06-E106] Its function is prospective: romantic attainment is a transition into a new narrative state, not an ending that abolishes work, family, friendship, or uncertainty.

## 14. Illustration and publication-form analysis

V06's visual layer remains Tier 1B evidence. The front color plates establish a strongly character-centered paratextual frame before the dense professional chapters begin. [IM-V06-E110–IM-V06-E111]

Narrative illustrations are distributed across the volume's major shifts rather than isolated in erotic comedy. The early relationship block, Hakone recovery/art sequence, media-mix/vocational transition, TRPG repair, casting, Chihiro/Pablo chapter, newcomer-award confrontation, and final hospital/coupling sequence all receive visual anchoring. [IM-V06-E112–IM-V06-E125]

The most important placement is `V06-ILL-035`, inserted between the narration of Itsuki's anger at breaking his own resolution and the explicit spoken proposal to date. [IM-V06-E125] The image therefore occupies a formal hinge: the visual pause separates internal rupture from external action.

The embedded documents around the award/second-party chapters also matter as publication form. [IM-V06-E122–IM-V06-E124] They reinforce that this series repeatedly represents professional life not only through prose summary but through pseudo-documents and visualized field artifacts. Such images should be routed as narrative documents rather than treated as ordinary illustrations.

Finally, V06 demonstrates why represented “behind-the-scenes” media must be distinguished from actual volume paratext. The fictional report manga can be ordered to lie about cast harmony. [IM-V06-E087–IM-V06-E088] A visual artifact *inside the story* may itself be evidence of institutional image management rather than evidence that the represented event occurred as depicted.

## 15. Counterevidence and anti-theses

### Anti-thesis 1: “V06 proves Itsuki's protagonist ideal was false from the beginning.”

**Too simple.** The ideal helped him authorize creative ambition and recover from earlier injury. V06 demonstrates a limit: the rule becomes harmful when it requires indefinite postponement of another person. [IM-V06-E019–IM-V06-E020, IM-V06-E092–IM-V06-E103]

### Anti-thesis 2: “Itsuki chooses love over career, so career no longer matters.”

**False.** He continues to care deeply about work, leaves a recording only under crisis, and explicitly frames the comparison as a hierarchy under emergency. [IM-V06-E065–IM-V06-E070, IM-V06-E089–IM-V06-E102]

### Anti-thesis 3: “Nayuta's media mix is simply self-destructive.”

**Incomplete.** It gives her genuinely meaningful collaborative experience and makes work enjoyable in a new way. The pathology lies in conversion of development into comparative compulsion and disregard of bodily limits. [IM-V06-E033–IM-V06-E040, IM-V06-E068–IM-V06-E070, IM-V06-E086, IM-V06-E094–IM-V06-E098]

### Anti-thesis 4: “Nayuta's collapse proves ambition is bad.”

**Unsupported.** V06 criticizes a specific self-worth structure: effort becomes mandatory because she believes she must shine to be loved. [IM-V06-E086, IM-V06-E094–IM-V06-E098]

### Anti-thesis 5: “Kyo should become an editor because she is good at it.”

**Rejected by the chapter's own question.** Aptitude and desire are explicitly distinguished. [IM-V06-E041–IM-V06-E043]

### Anti-thesis 6: “The producer is the villain of the casting chapter.”

**Unsupported.** The narration explicitly locates conflict in different professional positions and preserves uncertainty about the compromise result. [IM-V06-E048–IM-V06-E057]

### Anti-thesis 7: “Profit defines quality because the producer says so.”

**Category error.** The line is a situated professional criterion, not neutral narrator truth. V06 stages conflict among economic viability, acting quality, source fit, talent development, and audience strategy. [IM-V06-E050–IM-V06-E057]

### Anti-thesis 8: “Aoba is simply wrong and has nothing to teach the veterans.”

**Too strong.** Her universalization is criticized, but the narration explicitly recognizes the creative power of youthful absolutism; Itsuki is energized by the challenge. [IM-V06-E075–IM-V06-E079]

### Anti-thesis 9: “If Pablo's painting is worth millions, his behavior is validated.”

**False.** Market recognition and interpersonal ethics are independent axes. His boundary violation remains a boundary violation. [IM-V06-E059–IM-V06-E064]

### Anti-thesis 10: “Itsuki now forgives his father.”

**Not established.** He gains empathy for the power of love to reorder priorities. The text does not retroactively authorize every parental choice or erase estrangement. [IM-V06-E101–IM-V06-E103]

### Anti-thesis 11: “Itsuki and Nayuta are now equals because they are dating.”

**Unsupported.** The earlier equality gate is abandoned, not proven satisfied. Career asymmetry remains. The relationship forms precisely because Itsuki stops making objective equality a prerequisite. [IM-V06-E096–IM-V06-E106]

### Anti-thesis 12: “The TRPG healed the group automatically.”

**False.** The game is chosen for repair, and Haruto's paratextually disclosed hosting labor helps make it work. [IM-V06-E044–IM-V06-E046, IM-V06-E109]

## 17. Open questions after V06

1. **What does Itsuki/Nayuta look like as an actual relationship rather than a deferred desire structure?** V06 establishes coupling, not compatibility under ordinary time.
2. **Does Itsuki's protagonist-equality ideal disappear, migrate into career-only ambition, or return in another form?** The romantic gate breaks; the broader self-authorship motif remains.
3. **Can Nayuta preserve the genuine joy she discovered in collaboration without returning to compulsive comparative striving?**
4. **Will Nayuta internalize Itsuki's unconditional statement, or continue to treat achievement as necessary for love?**
5. **How durable is Kyo/Itsuki friendship after the TRPG repair once the new Itsuki/Nayuta relationship changes the group equilibrium?**
6. **Does Kyo's editorial competence become a chosen vocation?** V06 explicitly leaves aptitude and desire separated.
7. **Does Haruto continue pursuing Kyo once the social field changes?** Aioi's interest adds another unresolved relation but does not redirect him in V06.
8. **How does the anime casting compromise perform in practice?** V06 explicitly refuses to tell us whether the choice is correct.
9. **Can the series maintain the distinction between commercial viability and artistic quality without reducing either side to caricature?**
10. **How far does `IM-C024` generalize?** Friendship is the clearest V06 case, but family and creative teams may also require deliberate maintenance.
11. **Does `IM-C025` remain specific to Nayuta, or do Itsuki/Haruto/Kyo exhibit analogous effort-as-worth pathologies?**
12. **Will the field-level externalities of Nayuta's media mix ever become visible to her?** V06 shows consequences she does not know about.
13. **Does Setsuna/Pablo's fine-art vocation survive contact with ordinary livelihood, institutions, and ethical responsibility?**
14. **Will Chihiro develop desires that compete with family-centered time allocation?** V06 shows both past constraint and current choice.
15. **How reliable are future in-world publicity/report artifacts?** `IM-C027` now requires explicit skepticism toward curated behind-the-scenes material.
16. **Does Aoba's creative absolutism mature into pluralism, or remain productive precisely because it is combative?**
17. **What happens to the cohort-survival thesis as the series progresses?** V06 makes attrition explicit enough to track systematically.
18. **Does Itsuki's new empathy toward his father lead to relational repair, or merely to a more complex private judgment?**
19. **How will the series handle the still-unresolved Chihiro disclosure problem after coupling changes the family/romantic structure?**
20. **Does `A new story begins.` signal a durable formal shift in the series' center of gravity, or only the start of the next local arc?**

## 19. V06 end-state

### Canonical V06 state

By the end of Volume 06:

- **Itsuki and Nayuta are canonically a romantic couple.** This occurs because Itsuki abandons his earlier requirement that he first become Nayuta's professionally equal protagonist. [IM-V06-E099–IM-V06-E106]
- **The equality problem is not solved; its role changes.** Professional asymmetry remains, but it no longer functions as a prerequisite for romantic legitimacy.
- **Nayuta has discovered both the pleasure and danger of striving.** Collaborative media production genuinely expands her creative experience, while comparison converts effort into bodily destructive compulsion. [IM-V06-E033–IM-V06-E040, IM-V06-E068–IM-V06-E070, IM-V06-E086, IM-V06-E094–IM-V06-E098]
- **Kyo remains agentic after rejection.** She preserves friendship with Nayuta, continues to desire Itsuki, refuses Haruto for now, and has a serious but unresolved publishing-career possibility. [IM-V06-E001–IM-V06-E018, IM-V06-E041–IM-V06-E043]
- **Itsuki/Kyo friendship has been intentionally repaired enough for ordinary group interaction to resume**, but this is understood as maintenance rather than automatic restoration. [IM-V06-E009–IM-V06-E010, IM-V06-E044–IM-V06-E046]
- **Haruto remains oriented toward Kyo** while functioning increasingly as relational/professional support; Aioi introduces a new unresolved romantic pressure. [IM-V06-E012–IM-V06-E017, IM-V06-E080–IM-V06-E085]
- **Creative-industry analysis has moved from adaptation structure into casting, sponsorship, publicity, voice performance, health, and field survival.** [IM-V06-E048–IM-V06-E089]
- **Setsuna/Pablo has undergone a genuine artistic conversion**, while the text keeps artistic value separate from interpersonal ethics and from market recognition. [IM-V06-E028–IM-V06-E032, IM-V06-E058–IM-V06-E064]
- **Chihiro remains a major unresolved family/identity axis.** V06 strengthens Chihiro's relational intelligence and situated-choice profile but does not resolve disclosure to Itsuki. [IM-V06-E009–IM-V06-E011, IM-V06-E058–IM-V06-E063]
