# 妹さえいればいい。 Volume 04 — Deep Reading

## 1. Executive thesis

Volume 4 is the first volume after the V01–V03 checkpoint, and it does not overturn the first-block model. It **tests it under expansion**. The first three volumes established a world in which talent, sales, artistic worth, happiness, intimacy, and moral legitimacy are non-equivalent; in which creation is materially interdependent; and in which Itsuki increasingly treats professional centrality as evidence that he has become the protagonist of his own life. V04 asks what happens when that protagonist-hunger enters **media mix**: anime committees, adaptation slots, manga artists, illustrators, editorial departments, family economies, and game systems whose outputs no single participant controls. [IM-V04-E003–IM-V04-E010]

Itsuki is much more strategically ambitious than his theatrical `妹` persona initially suggests. He wants the anime not merely to succeed but to increase the recognizability of `羽島伊月` across simultaneous projects. The narration calls that strategy valid, while Toki warns that the labor burden is real. [IM-V04-E004–IM-V04-E007] The volume then gives that ambition a more vulnerable formulation. The anime slot was not created uniquely for him; another work fell out, a preexisting production formation remains, and if he refuses another comparably selling work can replace him. Itsuki condenses this into `代わりはいる` and accepts the adaptation partly because he wants to become someone who is not replaceable. [IM-V04-E028–IM-V04-E040] V03's `主人公になりたい` therefore persists, but V04 translates it into the language of industrial substitutability.

The strongest counterargument comes from adaptation itself. V04 repeatedly shows that a derivative work can become valuable **by not being a surface copy**. Candidate manga artists reveal several non-equivalent competencies: composition, cute-character rendering, stylistic imitation, sequential readability, commercial radicality, source attachment, and medium-specific storytelling. Kaiko wins not because she copies the source most literally but because she can invent gestures Itsuki never wrote while making the characters feel unmistakably themselves. [IM-V04-E056–IM-V04-E068] Later, her lingerie-centered revision changes Itsuki's own aesthetic values. [IM-V04-E074–IM-V04-E084] The source creator is therefore not merely a sovereign whose meaning others must preserve intact. He can recognize that another creator has reconstructed the work and added something the original did not contain. The volume eventually names that process explicitly as `メディアミックス`. [IM-V04-E151–IM-V04-E152]

This creates V04's central formal proposition: **fidelity is not sameness.** Good adaptation is medium-specific transformation that preserves the source's governing priorities while allowing another creator's technique and desire to become productive. Kaiko's work is faithful because she understands what Itsuki cares about, not because every costume, pose, or beat is reproduced exactly. Conversely, a technically excellent manga can be a poor adaptation if its source relation is purely instrumental. [IM-V04-E059–IM-V04-E068] This opens `IM-C017`.

The manga conflict then sharpens the point. Itsuki and Kaiko initially use the absolutist language of `原作レイプ`, but their disagreement is symmetrical: he wants nudity because he personally loves it; she wants underwear because she personally loves it. Toki's reader-service argument does not explain either creator's core motive. [IM-V04-E074–IM-V04-E079] The eventual solution is not a bland midpoint. Kaiko adds a sequential lingerie progression and still delivers the nude payoff; Itsuki learns to treat underwear as characterization rather than obstruction. The collaboration produces a third form that neither side had at the start. [IM-V04-E080–IM-V04-E084, IM-V04-E092–IM-V04-E104] That opens `IM-C018`: compromise can be **additive synthesis**, not merely dilution.

Haruto's failed anime provides the volume's most mature separation of process from outcome. He can say that the broadcast was agonizing while refusing to deny that auditions, design meetings, music, scripts, dubbing, and participation in another medium were genuinely enjoyable. [IM-V04-E105–IM-V04-E112] His younger sister then gives him a form of solidarity unavailable from industry politeness: she is furious on his behalf, and her close criticism proves she cared more than her dismissive surface admitted. Haruto discovers that another person's anger can save him as much as shared grief, and he converts failure into a future-oriented resolution to act more effectively next time. [IM-V04-E113–IM-V04-E117] Failure is neither erased nor allowed to define all prior labor.

The title's sibling irony also becomes structurally sharper. The opening repeats the joke that Itsuki has no real sister; the ending explicitly says his sister-absence is what makes the ideal inexhaustible. [IM-V04-E008–IM-V04-E010, IM-V04-E141–IM-V04-E145] Yet Chihiro is simultaneously becoming more integrated into his social world, more professionally independent, more comfortable articulating material preferences, and more explicit about real-sister claims that Itsuki cannot decode. [IM-V04-E118–IM-V04-E130, IM-V04-E138–IM-V04-E145] The false premise is no longer just a gag imposed on him by the plot. It is a **self-explanation that actively powers his career**.

Finally, the bonus fiction `ラノベ作家の人生` states in game form what the main narrative has been demonstrating by dispersion. Money and happiness are separate variables; the apparent competition turns out to be cooperative; an isolated player is structurally unlikely to finish; peers can rescue deadlines, form new institutions, and survive irrational professional shocks together. [IM-V04-E159–IM-V04-E171] Because this is BONUS_FICTION rather than baseline chronology, it should not be used as a literal factual statement about the mainline industry. As formal reflection, however, it is remarkably explicit: **professional survival and happiness are not winner-take-all achievements. Relationships are part of the survival architecture itself.** That opens `IM-C019`.

### V04 thesis in compressed form

> **V04 converts the first block's protagonist problem into an adaptation problem. Itsuki wants to become irreplaceable, yet every successful media-mix process demonstrates that no creator is self-sufficient and that fidelity can require transformation by another person's skill and desire. The volume's strongest creative successes emerge not from solitary sovereignty but from medium-specific reconstruction, conflict that becomes synthesis, and people who can remain envious, hurt, or competitive while still sustaining one another.** [IT]

## 3. Volume architecture and narrative movement

V04 has a strikingly coherent sequence despite its episodic chapter titles.

1. **`小説家は妹キ●ガイⅢ`** — new-pitch failure is immediately juxtaposed with anime success; the contradiction between “rejected idea” and “successful author” prevents career status from becoming a single number. The chapter also states Itsuki's deliberate brand-expansion strategy. [IM-V04-E001–IM-V04-E010]
2. **`６月６日`** — birthday intimacy, anime risk, Haruto's failed-adaptation perspective, and Nayuta's response to the V03 confession converge. Private gifts and industrial opportunity are deliberately placed in the same chapter. [IM-V04-E011–IM-V04-E053]
3. **`コミカライズ` / `顔合わせ`** — adaptation fidelity becomes a practical selection problem. Kaiko enters as a creator who understands source priorities without imitating the surface. [IM-V04-E054–IM-V04-E075]
4. **`マンガ家ＶＳイラストレーター` / `逆襲の全裸`** — a grotesquely comic fetish argument becomes a serious argument about medium specificity, artistic identity, study, weakness, and revision. [IM-V04-E076–IM-V04-E100]
5. **`ニアミス`** — Chihiro's focalization prevents the surrounding creator-comedy from erasing the consequences of Setsuna's prior nonconsensual conduct. [IM-V04-E085–IM-V04-E091]
6. **`妹が可愛いわけがない`** — Haruto's adaptation failure is reassembled into a mixed memory of joy, pain, sibling anger, and renewed agency. [IM-V04-E101–IM-V04-E113]
7. **`税理士の気晴らし`** — family conflict, money, work compensation, and clothing move Chihiro's identity away from a single concealment axis toward concrete adult choices. [IM-V04-E118–IM-V04-E130]
8. **`クロニカクロニクル③`** — the TRPG literalizes distributed narrative causality: GM intention, player promise, character value, rule structure, and dice refuse to collapse into one author. [IM-V04-E127–IM-V04-E133]
9. **`予兆`** — Kyo takes a first vocational step; Itsuki explicitly praises transformative media mix; the false belief “I have no real sister” is named as the engine of inexhaustible desire. [IM-V04-E134–IM-V04-E145]
10. **BONUS: `ラノベ作家の人生`** — writer life is converted into a formal system whose hidden victory condition is cooperation rather than ranking. [IM-V04-E159–IM-V04-E171]

The movement is therefore from **professional expansion → derivative authorship → conflict over fidelity → distributed causality → cooperative survival**. The volume starts with Itsuki wanting the world to know his individual name and ends, in its bonus formal mirror, with the proposition that no one survives the profession alone.

## 4. Narration, focalization, and epistemic structure

### 4.1 Ensemble focalization remains necessary to correct creator self-narratives

V04 repeatedly gives decisive knowledge to characters other than Itsuki. Haruto's perspective distinguishes the pleasure of production from the pain of reception. Chihiro's perspective establishes ongoing fear of Setsuna that the comedy around Setsuna cannot erase. Ashley's chapter reveals how work compensation and clothing preference look from outside Itsuki's household. Kyo's late-volume focalization turns her “ordinary” self-description into a vocational question rather than a settled deficiency. [IM-V04-E085–IM-V04-E091, IM-V04-E105–IM-V04-E130, IM-V04-E138–IM-V04-E150]

This strengthens `IM-C009`: ensemble structure is not decorative. It is the mechanism by which the text prevents any one character's self-story from becoming the ontology of the world.

### 4.2 Dramatic irony around Chihiro becomes an active causal structure

The volume does more than repeat that Itsuki is wrong about Chihiro. Toki can sincerely think Itsuki's endless sister hunger is sustainable because he has no sister; Itsuki can explain his own endless career drive through the same premise. [IM-V04-E008–IM-V04-E010, IM-V04-E141–IM-V04-E145] At the same time, Chihiro uses “real sister” language, becomes closer, and produces in Itsuki a growing unease that he misreads as evidence of non-blood relation. [IM-V04-E138–IM-V04-E145]

The hidden fact therefore influences both **the reader's interpretation and Itsuki's behavior**. His false epistemic model is productive: it helps him imagine his desire as permanently unfulfillable, which in turn legitimizes permanent striving.

### 4.3 Adaptation scenes systematically separate source intention from derivative meaning

The manga chapters refuse a simple author-versus-adapter frame. Itsuki has source authority, but he is also a reader of Kaiko's work. Kaiko is a fan, but she has an artistic identity not reducible to serving him. Editors manage talent pipelines, but their career-development motives may not align with either source love or artist desire. [IM-V04-E054–IM-V04-E068] The narration consequently treats media mix as a system of **multiple legitimate perspectives with unequal formal powers**.

## 5. Character-state changes

### 5.1 羽島伊月: from protagonist hunger to irreplaceability—and from sovereignty to being changed

The clearest V04 development is that Itsuki's ambition is not accidental. He understands anime attention as a strategic window and wants to use it to make the author-name `羽島伊月` itself globally legible. [IM-V04-E003–IM-V04-E007] This is the public-professional version of V03's fear of becoming an unnamed `同業者` beside Nayuta.

The anime offer then exposes the cost of making recognizability a measure of selfhood. Itsuki learns that the slot is transferable, that the staff configuration preexists him, and that another work can replace him if he refuses. [IM-V04-E028–IM-V04-E038] He does not merely want a good adaptation; he wants to advance from `代わりはいる` to `オンリーワン`. [IM-V04-E038–IM-V04-E040] `IM-C014` is therefore strongly strengthened while remaining longitudinally open.

Yet V04 also gives him one of his healthiest creator developments. Kaiko's manga changes his mind. He can begin from absolutist disgust, study what she is doing in manga time and panel sequence, and finally say that underwear is not bad. [IM-V04-E074–IM-V04-E084] By `予兆`, he defines media mix positively as another creator reconstructing his work and adding new attractions absent from the source. [IM-V04-E151–IM-V04-E152] His authorship remains possessive, but no longer requires the derivative work to be a passive vessel.

Itsuki's romantic state also becomes less ambiguous and more paradoxical. The SD-card scene makes physical desire explicit while showing that he can refuse immediate gratification for work. [IM-V04-E020–IM-V04-E027] Nayuta's interiority later confirms that she knows his feelings and interprets his protagonist language as marriage-directed. [IM-V04-E047–IM-V04-E053, IM-V04-E073–IM-V04-E075] The barrier is no longer information. It is his own legitimacy criterion.

### 5.2 可児那由多: mutuality becomes professional co-motivation, not permission to shrink

V03 ended with Nayuta having overheard the confession but not verbally resolved its meaning. V04 partially answers that question. She understands that Itsuki loves her and that he wants some form of equal-protagonist status before initiating the relationship himself, but she still cannot fully comprehend the status arithmetic. [IM-V04-E047–IM-V04-E050]

Crucially, her response is not to make herself smaller. Itsuki loves `小説家としての可児那由多`; lowering her quality to engineer equality would betray the self he values. Her conclusion is therefore to keep advancing as a novelist while continuing to pursue him romantically. [IM-V04-E051–IM-V04-E053] This substantially strengthens the possibility that romance and professional identity can become **mutually generative** rather than a zero-sum contest.

The SD-card episode also complicates the interpretation of her sexual register. Her usual polite obscenity can create the impression of unlimited readiness, but when Itsuki unexpectedly accepts the offer, she hesitates and destroys the card. [IM-V04-E020–IM-V04-E025] The scene is comic, but the character evidence is serious: rhetorical boldness is not a blanket waiver of vulnerability, embarrassment, or the right to stop.

### 5.3 白川京: usefulness moves from self-erasure toward a possible vocation

Kyo still begins from comparative self-disqualification: `あたしなんかじゃ無理`. [IM-V04-E014] Late in the volume, however, she reframes the same difference in ability. She cannot write like Itsuki or Nayuta and cannot draw well, but perhaps helping capable creators and good work reach readers can itself be worthwhile. [IM-V04-E138–IM-V04-E145] Toki immediately offers editorial-office work, and Kyo accepts even though the first tasks are described as chores. [IM-V04-E136–IM-V04-E138]

This is not yet proof that she has escaped usefulness-based self-worth; in fact, the new vocation is still articulated through helping others. But it is a meaningful change in **self-authorization**. Instead of merely admiring creators from outside, she enters the institutional system in which works are made publishable. `IM-Q033` therefore receives partial resolution but remains open.

### 5.4 不破春斗: adaptation trauma becomes integrated memory and prospective agency

Haruto's V04 material is among the series' strongest early treatment of artistic failure. He refuses two simplifications: that the production was secretly worthless because the result was bad, and that good intentions make the result acceptable. [IM-V04-E105–IM-V04-E112] He can remember being happy to participate in auditions, designs, music, scripts, and dubbing while also describing the broadcast as weekly pain.

His younger sister then provides a relationship unavailable inside the production. She can be furious without professional diplomacy. Haruto recognizes that she is voicing anger he cannot comfortably voice as an implicated author and that her anger itself is a form of care. [IM-V04-E113–IM-V04-E116] His response is forward-looking: `次はもっと上手くやる`. [IM-V04-E113]

The important development is not that he becomes controlling. V04 ends this episode with **agency rather than mastery**: he intends to do better next time, but the system remains larger than him. `IM-Q039` should therefore ask whether later agency becomes wiser participation or defensive overcontrol.

### 5.5 三国山蚕: fan, adapter, medium specialist, and aspiring protagonist

Kaiko is not merely “the manga artist.” Her candidacy compresses several V04 problems into one person. She is a fan who understands the source's value hierarchy, a newcomer whose manga skill is already strong, an artist with a distinctive underwear obsession, and a young adult facing parental pressure to abandon an unstable profession. [IM-V04-E062–IM-V04-E066, IM-V04-E092–IM-V04-E104]

Her conflict with Itsuki is therefore not obedient adapter versus possessive author. Both are creators trying to protect what they treat as authentic desire. Her career problem also parallels Itsuki's protagonist problem: she wants public results that can make manga authorship legible as a real life choice, not merely private talent acknowledged by an editor. [IM-V04-E094–IM-V04-E099]

Nayuta's attack—perhaps she did not choose underwear but fled from nudity—hurts because it converts identity into a possible defense against weakness. [IM-V04-E102] Kaiko's response is productive: learn enough to prove that underwear is an affirmative aesthetic choice. [IM-V04-E104] The volume values commitment most when it can survive contact with its own limitation.

### 5.6 羽島千尋: work, family, clothing, and boundary memory broaden identity

Chihiro's V04 material resists reducing identity to the concealed sex/gender fact. The character works for Ashley, refuses unearned gifts, enjoys experimenting with clothing once the opportunity is materially available, and still preserves the desire for Itsuki to participate in that world someday. [IM-V04-E118–IM-V04-E130]

This evidence should not be flattened into “Chihiro secretly wants to be feminine.” Clothing behavior is situational: ordinary presentation around work/Itsuki is practical and largely unisex; skirts were discarded around the remarriage; shopping is initially hesitant but genuinely becomes enjoyable. [IM-V04-E122–IM-V04-E130] The correct prospective conclusion is plurality, not essence.

The Setsuna material is equally important. Chihiro remembers the prior forced-undressing attempt as a reason for avoidance and vigilance. [IM-V04-E085–IM-V04-E091] That memory must retain ethical authority even when the series gives Setsuna comic/artistic charisma elsewhere.

## 6. Relationship ecology

### 6.1 Itsuki ↔ Nayuta: the relationship is mutual; the timeline remains self-imposed

The relationship's V04 change is epistemic. Nayuta now understands enough of Itsuki's confession to know that lack of feeling is not the obstacle. She treats protagonist language as a coded marriage statement. [IM-V04-E047–IM-V04-E053, IM-V04-E071–IM-V04-E075] This strengthens revised `IM-C008`: romance and professional self-recognition are not merely adjacent; Itsuki has explicitly made one the condition of the other.

The irony is that Nayuta does not accept his comparative premise. To her, he is already `オンリーワン`. [IM-V04-E050] The asymmetry is therefore not “one loves more.” It is that Itsuki evaluates relation through a public-comparative framework that Nayuta does not share.

### 6.2 Itsuki ↔ Toki: professional care includes refusing the incentive to sell

Toki is unusually important in the anime scene. He has every ordinary employee reason to obtain Itsuki's permission, yet explicitly warns him that adaptation can break creators and confirms that refusal remains available. [IM-V04-E031–IM-V04-E036] Once Itsuki chooses to proceed, Toki shifts into commitment: `地獄まで付き合ってやる`. [IM-V04-E040]

This is an unusually clean example of institutional relationship without romanticizing the institution. Toki remains part of a company and is still a gatekeeper; care consists in **making the risk legible before obtaining consent**, not in abolishing the professional structure.

### 6.3 Haruto ↔ younger sister: abrasive siblinghood as emotional representation

The actual younger-sister chapter is a direct counterweight to Itsuki's ideal. Haruto's sister is rude, dismissive, and angry—but her detailed criticism proves attention, and her anger gives Haruto emotional permission he cannot easily grant himself. [IM-V04-E113–IM-V04-E117] Real siblinghood here is not unconditional sweetness. It is familiarity strong enough to carry unfiltered judgment and care simultaneously.

### 6.4 Itsuki ↔ Chihiro: closeness grows faster than conceptual recognition

Itsuki and Chihiro now plan clothing outings; Chihiro is delighted by the prospect; Itsuki recognizes Chihiro's attractiveness; and Chihiro is fully integrated into the friend group. [IM-V04-E017–IM-V04-E019, IM-V04-E138–IM-V04-E145] Yet Itsuki's conceptual model becomes more wrong, not less: increasing closeness produces unease that he interprets as evidence of non-biological relation. [IM-V04-E132–IM-V04-E135]

The relationship thus illustrates a recurring series principle: **correct attachment can coexist with incorrect explanation**.

### 6.5 Kaiko ↔ Itsuki: source authority becomes mutual creator recognition

Their relationship begins with admiration and delegated authority but quickly becomes conflict. The decisive feature is that the conflict changes both sides. Itsuki learns from Kaiko's manga; Kaiko accepts that a claimed aesthetic identity must survive study and comparison with a weakness. [IM-V04-E067–IM-V04-E084, IM-V04-E092–IM-V04-E104] By the end, adaptation is not service from subordinate to master but a negotiated creative relation in which asymmetrical formal authority coexists with reciprocal aesthetic influence.

## 7. Creative labor, publishing, and industry

### 7.1 Anime adaptation is an opportunity architecture, not a reward dispenser

V04 is explicit that anime adaptation is produced through opportunity, timing, prior staffing, sales thresholds, and substitution. [IM-V04-E028–IM-V04-E039] Itsuki's work has earned enough market position to enter the slot, but the slot is not metaphysically “his.” This strongly strengthens `IM-C003` and `IM-C013`.

The volume also separates formal right from practical control. Itsuki can refuse, but accepting does not mean he controls staffing or the resulting work. [IM-V04-E031–IM-V04-E036] Creator agency therefore exists inside constraint rather than outside it.

### 7.2 Comic adaptation reveals a multidimensional talent space

The four candidates function almost like an experiment. One has composition without sufficient cute-girl execution; one has surface resemblance without manga readability; one has radical manga force but weak source attachment and conflicted career motivation; Kaiko has medium competence, source understanding, and fan investment. [IM-V04-E054–IM-V04-E066]

This materially revises the detail of `IM-C002`. “Talent” is not just difficult to compare across people; it is **domain-structured inside one person's practice**. Setsuna can be the overwhelming illustrator and still be less suited to this particular manga job because sequential page grammar is a distinct craft. [IM-V04-E084]

### 7.3 Source fidelity is preservation of priorities through transformation

Kaiko's strongest fidelity evidence is not copied design. It is that she can invent behavior the prose never states and still make Itsuki recognize the characters as themselves. [IM-V04-E064–IM-V04-E068] Her source love is therefore not measured by obedience to every surface decision. It is measured by grasping which values are load-bearing.

This is the basis for new claim `IM-C017`.

### 7.4 Additive compromise: the third form

The nude-versus-lingerie dispute initially looks irreconcilable because each side frames personal desire as aesthetic necessity. [IM-V04-E074–IM-V04-E079] Kaiko's revision does not average the two desires. It changes the scene's sequencing so lingerie becomes a narrative progression and nudity remains a culmination. [IM-V04-E080–IM-V04-E084, IM-V04-E092–IM-V04-E104]

This is a strong early example of **productive compromise through addition**. The collaboration can become more specific, not less, because conflict forces each participant to articulate what actually matters.

### 7.5 Professional failure can leave positive process knowledge

Haruto's adaptation work establishes that a failed output does not make every component of the process worthless. [IM-V04-E105–IM-V04-E112] This is important for later industry analysis because it prevents moralizing simplifications such as “bad anime means every participant failed” or “sincere effort makes the anime good.” V04 insists on distributed causality and non-equivalent evaluation.

## 8. Talent, success, failure, self-worth, and protagonist status

### 8.1 Replaceability is the new professional wound

V03 feared nameless supporting-character status. V04 gives that fear a market mechanism. Another sufficiently successful work can occupy the same anime slot. [IM-V04-E038–IM-V04-E040] Itsuki's desire for `オンリーワン` status is therefore psychologically comprehensible but analytically dangerous: industrial substitution is real, but it does not logically establish human or artistic replaceability.

### 8.2 Kaiko demonstrates that weakness can be inside a genuine vocation

Nayuta's harsh criticism matters because it does not prove Kaiko's underwear identity false. It identifies a domain Kaiko may have avoided and turns the alleged identity into a testable question. [IM-V04-E095–IM-V04-E100] Kaiko responds not by abandoning underwear but by learning enough to choose it more deliberately.

This is a different relationship to insecurity than Itsuki's. Itsuki often seeks external status proof; Kaiko, at least in this episode, turns critique into a craft problem.

### 8.3 Haruto's failure becomes integrated rather than disavowed

Haruto neither romanticizes failure nor permits it to colonize every memory of the project. [IM-V04-E105–IM-V04-E112] The resulting resolution—do better next time—is a model of self-worth that depends less on declaring the past a success than on remaining capable of action after it failed.

### 8.4 Kyo begins converting “ordinary” into professional relation

Kyo does not suddenly discover hidden genius. The volume instead offers an alternative to genius as the only legitimate position inside creative life: editing/support labor. [IM-V04-E146–IM-V04-E150] That is potentially liberating but not automatically so, because her language still routes value through helping exceptional others. The distinction remains open.

## 9. Family, siblinghood, and identity

### 9.1 Real siblings are plural, abrasive, and materially embedded

Haruto's sister is not a cute ideal. She is hostile, observant, emotionally protective, and capable of saying what the author cannot. [IM-V04-E113–IM-V04-E117] Chihiro is domestic, professionally developing, cautious, playful, and increasingly integrated into Itsuki's adult world. [IM-V04-E114–IM-V04-E126, IM-V04-E132–IM-V04-E135] These two sibling structures make `妹` less reducible to a single trope.

### 9.2 Chihiro's clothing evidence should remain prospective and non-essentialist

The shopping chapter gives direct facts: practical/unisex ordinary clothing, discarded skirts around remarriage, initial hesitation in a more feminine styling environment, then genuine pleasure and self-directed trying-on. [IM-V04-E122–IM-V04-E130] None of these facts alone establishes a total gendered essence. They show that material opportunity, privacy, family history, and relational setting all shape presentation.

### 9.3 Money and family harmony intersect without becoming identical

Ashley encounters inheritance conflict professionally; Chihiro responds strongly to the proposition that family harmony is better. [IM-V04-E114–IM-V04-E117] The chapter therefore links money to family fracture without claiming money causes all fracture. It also makes Chihiro's refusal of unmotivated gifts meaningful: accepting care and preserving agency are not the same thing. [IM-V04-E123–IM-V04-E126]

### 9.4 The “no real sister” premise is now visibly a false engine of selfhood

The volume closes with Itsuki explicitly saying his endless sister quest is safe because he has no real sister and only an actual sister's sudden appearance could threaten it. [IM-V04-E166–IM-V04-E171] Inside the reader's evidence state, this is wrong. The importance is not merely comedic irony. A false family belief has become part of his **career theory**.

## 10. Sexuality, vulgar comedy, and boundaries

### 10.1 Desire and restraint coexist in the SD-card scene

Itsuki wants the photographs. The text eventually says so without euphemism. [IM-V04-E026] But the scene also shows that Nayuta's aggressive erotic style can outrun her readiness once Itsuki accepts. She backs away and destroys the card. [IM-V04-E020–IM-V04-E025] The ethical/character lesson is not “Nayuta did not really want him” or “anything she jokes about is pre-consented.” It is that desire is specific and revocable.

### 10.2 Setsuna remains the clearest boundary counterexample

Chihiro's perspective retains memory of forced attempted undressing after refusal, avoidance, and vigilance. [IM-V04-E085–IM-V04-E091] Setsuna's artistic aspiration can be genuine at the same time. The project should continue refusing the collapse “artistically sincere → ethically harmless.”

### 10.3 The body-study sequence is intentionally harder to classify than pure consensual fanservice

The Kaiko/Nayuta/Kyo research sequence is erotically comic and serves a real craft purpose in the plot, but it also includes pressure, reluctant participation, and objections that should not be erased by the gag frame. The visual layer intensifies the erotic packaging. [IM-V04-E180–IM-V04-E181] `IM-C007` therefore strengthens in complexity: comedy can disclose desire and craft while simultaneously making boundaries harder to see clearly.

## 11. Games, food, celebration, and social ritual

### 11.1 Birthday ritual puts career and intimacy in the same room

The birthday celebration joins age, friendship, editor intimacy, family gifts, anime success, sexual tension, and career fear. [IM-V04-E011–IM-V04-E027] This strongly supports the checkpoint answer to `IM-Q014`: ordinary ritual is not filler but a recurrent infrastructure in which otherwise separate domains become mutually legible.

### 11.2 TRPG gives contingency formal authority over authorial desire

The group begins with Haruto's invitation to make a new story. [IM-V04-E131] Itsuki then tries to make `妹` feeling produce a miracle, but the dice/rules refuse: `奇跡は起きなかった`. [IM-V04-E135–IM-V04-E136] The GM's own story plan subsequently collapses. [IM-V04-E137]

The formal significance is unusually direct. Narrative outcome emerges from authored world design, player intention, character commitments, procedural rules, and chance. No single layer is sovereign. This is V04's cleanest miniature of `IM-C013`.

### 11.3 Bonus game converts competitive ranking into cooperative survival

`ラノベ作家の人生` initially looks like a rankable writer-career game but reveals that higher score does not define victory. Everyone who reaches the end with positive happiness wins. [IM-V04-E176–IM-V04-E178] Cooperation is not optional thematic sweetness; it is embedded in the rules. [IM-V04-E166–IM-V04-E171]

Because this is bonus fiction, the project should treat it as formal reflection rather than documentary sociology. Within that limit, its conceptual force is high.

## 12. Fiction-within-fiction, adaptation, and intertext

### 12.1 Media mix becomes a theory of transformation

V04 provides an explicit definition: another creator reconstructs the source and adds attractions the original did not contain. [IM-V04-E151–IM-V04-E152] This is stronger than saying adaptation is “faithful enough.” It treats new value as part of the point.

### 12.2 Kaiko's manga is a work-about-reading

Kaiko's successful adaptation depends on having read not only what happens but what matters. Her added gestures feel true because she has reconstructed character priority, not merely copied illustration surface. [IM-V04-E061–IM-V04-E066] In that sense, adaptation becomes **interpretation made executable in another medium**.

### 12.3 TRPG and bonus-game systems externalize the limits of protagonist sovereignty

The TRPG can deny the miracle Itsuki thinks sister-feeling should cause; the bonus game can reveal that the apparent competition was misread because the players assumed ranking where the rules only required shared survival. [IM-V04-E131–IM-V04-E137, IM-V04-E159–IM-V04-E171] Both are formally relevant to the protagonist thesis: being a protagonist is not the same thing as controlling the system.

## 13. Japanese voice, style, and terminology

### 13.1 `代わりはいる` / `オンリーワン`

These terms turn industrial substitutability into existential vocabulary. [IM-V04-E038–IM-V04-E040] `代わり` is factually true at the slot level: another sufficiently selling title can fill the vacancy. `オンリーワン` is psychologically larger: Itsuki uses it to describe the writer he wants to become. The analysis must not silently treat those as the same proposition.

Nayuta's usage is the counterpoint. Itsuki is already `かけがえのないオンリーワンの作家` to her. [IM-V04-E050] The same word therefore names two incompatible metrics: public scarcity for Itsuki, relational irreplaceability for Nayuta.

### 13.2 `対等の主人公`

V04 does not clarify an objective standard; it clarifies Nayuta's reception. She knows the phrase, does not fully understand its professional arithmetic, but knows enough to treat it as a future-directed love commitment. [IM-V04-E047–IM-V04-E053] The term therefore remains psychologically powerful precisely because it is semantically under-specified.

### 13.3 `原作レイプ`

The phrase is used hyperbolically by multiple creators in the manga conflict. [IM-V04-E076] Its comic extremity should not obscure its function: each speaker initially treats deviation from personal aesthetic priority as violation of the work. The chapter then dismantles that absolutism through successful transformation. The project's use of the phrase should therefore remain source-attributed and never be adopted as neutral analytical terminology.

### 13.4 `不誠実`

Itsuki's later self-description is important because he applies moralized craft language to his own former inattentiveness to clothing. [IM-V04-E095] The point is not that underwear has acquired ethical sanctity; it is that learning changes what he believes a creator owes to characterization.

### 13.5 `リアルの妹がいない`

At V04's end, this is both LF and dramatic irony. [IM-V04-E156] It is an explicit proposition in Itsuki's self-model and false relative to the reader's established knowledge. The ensuing causal statement—absence enables infinite fantasy—therefore becomes a privileged site for later revision when disclosure pressure finally reaches him.

## 15. Counterevidence and anti-theses

### 15.1 “Anime adaptation proves Itsuki has become objectively unique”

**REJECT.** The production slot is explicitly transferable, another work originally occupied it, and another comparably selling work can replace him. [IM-V04-E028–IM-V04-E039] Anime is evidence of substantial professional success, not ontological uniqueness.

### 15.2 “A faithful adaptation copies the source as closely as possible”

**REJECT.** Surface imitation can produce poor manga readability, while Kaiko's unwritten gestures and altered designs can feel perfectly faithful to character and source priorities. [IM-V04-E054–IM-V04-E066]

### 15.3 “The original author is the final aesthetic authority in every medium”

**DOWNGRADE.** Itsuki retains formal source-author power, but Kaiko's manga changes his values, and Setsuna's superior illustration skill does not automatically make him the better manga adapter. [IM-V04-E074–IM-V04-E084]

### 15.4 “Haruto's bad anime means the production experience was entirely bad”

**REJECT.** Haruto explicitly preserves joyful process memories while condemning the broadcast result. [IM-V04-E105–IM-V04-E112]

### 15.5 “Nayuta's obscene verbal style means she is always ready for any sexual escalation she proposes”

**REJECT.** When Itsuki seriously accepts the SD-card offer, she hesitates and destroys it. [IM-V04-E020–IM-V04-E025]

### 15.6 “Chihiro's enjoyment of feminine clothing proves one simple hidden essence”

**Not supported.** V04 shows situational clothing practices, family-history effects, work practicality, initial hesitation, genuine enjoyment, and financial boundaries. [IM-V04-E122–IM-V04-E130] The evidence supports plurality and context, not a one-line essence claim.

### 15.7 “Real siblinghood in V04 is idealized sweetness”

**REJECT.** Haruto's sister is abrasive and angry; Chihiro can criticize Itsuki's clothes and make him sad; both relations nevertheless carry care. [IM-V04-E017–IM-V04-E019, IM-V04-E109–IM-V04-E113]

### 15.8 “Professional life is fundamentally winner-take-all”

**Directly contested by the bonus formal system.** The game's hidden victory condition is cooperative survival with positive happiness, not highest score. [IM-V04-E163–IM-V04-E171] The source class limits how literally this may be generalized, but the formal anti-thesis is explicit.

## 19. V04 end-state

### 19.1 Character end-state

- **羽島伊月:** professionally ambitious in explicit strategic terms; anime opportunity exposes his fear of replaceability and translates `主人公になりたい` into a desire for `オンリーワン` author status. At the same time, successful manga adaptation proves he can be aesthetically changed by a collaborator and still experience the result as more, not less, his work's world.
- **可児那由多:** now consciously understands the broad structure of Itsuki's romantic condition. She rejects the premise that equality should be achieved by shrinking herself and instead treats professional advance as part of remaining the self he loves. Erotic bravado is shown to coexist with vulnerability and retreat.
- **白川京:** comparative self-disqualification persists, but editorial-office work opens the first concrete path through which “helping creators” may become vocation rather than merely inferiority management.
- **不破春斗:** adaptation failure becomes integrated rather than disavowed. He can preserve joyful process memories, accept his sister's anger as care, and formulate a future intention to act more effectively.
- **三国山蚕:** enters as a serious new creator whose source love, medium-specific manga craft, fetish identity, insecurity, family pressure, and self-authorship become analytically inseparable.
- **羽島千尋:** gains further adult independence through paid work and compensation boundaries; clothing preference becomes contextually plural; fear of Setsuna remains active; family harmony remains strongly valued. Itsuki's closeness to Chihiro grows even as his explanation of that closeness becomes more mistaken.
- **土岐健次郎:** demonstrates editor-care most clearly by making adaptation risk and refusal legible despite company incentive to obtain approval; afterward he commits to accompanying Itsuki through the consequences.
- **恵那刹那:** remains artistically exceptional, but V04 provides evidence that raw illustrative power and manga-specific craft are distinct; Chihiro's avoidance keeps the ethical cost of prior conduct visible.
- **大野アシュリー:** expands from accountant/employer into an adult who frames family conflict, compensation, and embodied self-presentation without collapsing them into one issue.

### 19.2 Thematic end-state

V04 makes the first-block checkpoint more precise rather than obsolete. **Self-authorship does not require solitary sovereignty.** In fact, the volume's strongest creative successes occur when sovereignty is relinquished enough for another creator, another medium, a rule system, or another person's emotional response to modify what the original author thought he knew.

Itsuki wants to become irreplaceable, but the work that most satisfies him is one that has been reconstructed by somebody else. Haruto suffers a derivative work he could not control, yet refuses to erase the joy of making it. Kaiko protects her artistic obsession by learning the domain she may have avoided. Kyo enters creation not by discovering genius but by joining the labor that lets other people's work reach the world. Chihiro's real siblinghood continues to shape Itsuki before he can name it. The TRPG refuses the miracle the player-author wants. The bonus game refuses the premise that the highest score must define victory.

The volume's governing question can therefore be stated more sharply than at the checkpoint:

> **Can a person become the protagonist of his own life while accepting that the best parts of that life may be co-authored, transformed, or made possible by people and systems he does not control?** [IT]
