# 妹さえいればいい。 Volume 09 Deep Reading

## 0. Authority, method, and prospective boundary

This is the canonical prospective deep reading of Volume 09. It inherits the two frozen longitudinal checkpoints through V06 and the canonical V07-V08 readings as prior interpretive state, while treating V09 as the only new semantic evidence. No event, characterization, explanation, or outcome from V10-V14 is used to solve V09 ambiguities. That prospective restriction matters especially at the ending: the volume stops immediately after Chihiro's disclosure, so the reactions and consequences of that disclosure remain unknown here even though later numbered volumes exist in the source lock.

The Japanese EPUB is ultimate textual authority. `IMOSAE_V09_NORMALIZED_SOURCE.md` is the canonical retrieval derivative and `IMOSAE-VISUAL-INDEX-1.0` governs visual locators. Evidence labels follow `IMOSAE_ANALYTICAL_METHOD_V1.md`: TF textual fact, LF linguistic fact, PF paratextual fact, FR formal recurrence, SI strong inference, IT interpretive thesis, and SP speculation. The active V07-V09 block enters V09 with several unresolved pressures: whether Chihiro can maintain belonging without making usefulness the proof of attachment; whether the concealed male-younger-brother category can survive a widening social life; whether Kyo's editorial aptitude becomes a chosen vocation; whether creator reliability becomes structurally exploitable; whether public communication and adaptation decisions become forms of audience responsibility; and whether Itsuki can keep `protagonist` ambition from becoming a total measure of personal worth. V09 does not merely answer these questions. It makes them collide.

## 1. Executive thesis

Volume 09 is best understood as a volume about **assigned value versus authored commitment**. Characters repeatedly enter roles whose social meaning is supplied by somebody else: profitable author, derivative writer, useful roommate, little brother, promising applicant, expendable newcomer, valuable star author, television creator, audience-friendly adapter, player character, sister. The central question is not simply whether those labels are accurate. It is whether the person can distinguish an externally assigned status from a role they actually choose to inhabit--and what happens when institutions or relationships reward them for remaining useful inside the assigned category.

This begins in an apparently mundane tax discussion. Incorporation forces Itsuki to convert artistic ambition into a prediction about stable future income. He would like to answer that a writer worthy of standing beside Nayuta should obviously keep earning at a high level, but the material economics of the profession resist that fantasy. Quality, effort, adaptation status, trends, and sales do not map predictably enough to justify certainty. The adult business decision therefore exposes the gap between aspirational identity and probabilistic career reality. Itsuki can want to stand beside Nayuta without possessing a reliable financial forecast that proves he will. [IM-V09-E001-IM-V09-E012]

Aoba's arc then attacks a harsher version of the same problem. She has treated resemblance to Nayuta as proof that she belongs to the same category of `本物` talent. Her editor treats the same resemblance as evidence of derivative inferiority but cannot clearly verbalize the supposed essence that separates Nayuta from Aoba. Once publication exposes Aoba to the harsher public label of `劣化コピー`, she inverts her earlier grandiosity into self-erasure: if she lacks talent, perhaps she lacks `資格`, `価値`, or `意味` to keep writing. Itsuki's answer is one of the most important corrections in the corpus. He does not claim that talent, criticism, or market response are unreal. He denies that any of them are prerequisites for the basic permission to create. The relevant first question is whether Aoba still `書きたい` and whether she remains able to say that she `好き` or even `愛している` her own work. [IM-V09-E013-IM-V09-E018] [IM-V09-E038-IM-V09-E046]

That distinction also turns back on Itsuki. His reassurance that a counterfeit might one day surpass the real is explicitly partly self-addressed. His old `主人公` language survives, but V09 gives it a healthier possible formulation: protagonist status can name sustained authorship in the face of judgment rather than proof that one has already defeated every rival. The TRPG finale later radicalizes this. Haruto states that players are protagonists because they choose and live through the story; even failure or tragedy can constitute a successful session if the characters fully inhabit the consequences of their choices. Protagonism is therefore separated from winning. [IM-V09-E038-IM-V09-E046] [IM-V09-E219-IM-V09-E257]

Kyo supplies the institutional mirror. V08 established that she had aptitude for editing without proving she wanted the life. V09 closes that narrower question: after self-analysis and actual exposure to the work, editing is what she presently wants. But the volume refuses to treat vocational clarity as institutional innocence. Job interviews subject her to opaque codes such as `平服` that are stated one way and judged another; a self-publishing company sells publication prestige to authors-as-customers rather than selecting work it believes in; and GF's unwritten `3年縛り` is enforced against a weak newcomer while Kobe openly admits that an author as valuable as Nayuta would be retained even if the same convention had to be broken. The word `常識` thus becomes an ideological object. It can name shared practical knowledge, but it can also disguise a rule whose actual force depends on bargaining power. [IM-V09-E141-IM-V09-E190]

Kyo's own ethical answer is moving and dangerous at once. She wants to be an editor who can `一緒に泣く` with an author--someone who does not forget that for the editor each writer is one among many, while for the writer the editor may be the primary institutional partner. That aspiration is a genuine moral insight into asymmetrical care. Yet V09 has already shown Kyo taking over nearly all roomshare labor and experiencing a dark satisfaction from being indispensable to two successful creators. Her compassionate editor ideal therefore cannot be promoted uncritically as mature resolution. The same desire to stay close to another person's burden can become self-erasing if usefulness becomes the price of belonging. [IM-V09-E019-IM-V09-E024] [IM-V09-E162-IM-V09-E190]

Chihiro is where all of these structures converge. V08 weakened the equation practical replaceability = relational replaceability by giving Chihiro friendships, hobbies, paid work, and reciprocal recognition outside household service. V09 stress-tests that revision rather than reversing it. When Chihiro withdraws from Itsuki's apartment to protect the secret and Aoba immediately offers equally competent cooking plus an uncomplicated pseudo-sister role, the old anxiety returns sharply. The issue is no longer only whether Itsuki will eat. Aoba can occupy two categories Chihiro has made central to attachment--caregiver and openly welcomed younger-sibling figure--without carrying the secret that makes Chihiro's own claim impossible to state. [IM-V09-E078-IM-V09-E106]

Haruto's university conversation begins a different route out. He asks Chihiro to consider interest, enjoyment, optionality, the possibility of failure, and the fact that education can widen rather than merely optimize a career path. Chihiro remembers that science was not chosen solely because it was useful: robots were genuinely interesting. This is a small but structurally important act of self-authorship. Chihiro starts asking not only "Where can I be useful?" but "What do I want to learn or enjoy?" [IM-V09-E129-IM-V09-E140]

The pregnancy chapter then creates a much deeper crisis. A new child of Keisuke and Natsume would be an indisputable biological sibling to Itsuki in a way Chihiro believes she cannot openly be. Chihiro fears losing `居場所`, but the text does not show anyone actually excluding her. This is the interior logic of threatened category security, not an objective family verdict. [IM-V09-E206-IM-V09-E212]

The final TRPG turns fiction into an identity laboratory. Chihiro's character is female and is Itsuki's character's sister. The game allows her to inhabit, in Itsuki's presence, the very role reality has blocked. The author afterword explicitly identifies the TRPG as the one world in which Chihiro could be her "real self" before Itsuki; as PF, this confirms deliberate staging without erasing the textual process by which the confession occurs. In play, Chihiro can be protected as a sister even when mechanically weak, can become the battle's protagonist because a formerly disadvantageous ability becomes uniquely important, and can receive Itsuki's delighted judgment that she is the best little sister. [IM-V09-E219-IM-V09-E257] [IM-V09-E258-IM-V09-E276]

But play cannot permanently solve a recognition problem whose stakes are outside play. Chihiro briefly asserts that a "real sister" cannot lose to a game-only sister. Nayuta, with no malice and no knowledge of the secret, points out that Chihiro too is a sister only in the game setting. The correction destroys the temporary shelter precisely because it is socially reasonable from everyone else's perspective. The narration then strips categories away in sequence: not merely a game sister, not a little brother. The first-person form changes to `私`, and Chihiro blurts out that she is Itsuki's `本当の妹`. The confession is not a strategically planned revelation. It is the point at which a role that has become experientially real can no longer be contained inside fiction. [IM-V09-E219-IM-V09-E257] [IM-V09-E312]

Volume 09 therefore advances the corpus with a stronger formulation:

> **A role becomes self-authored when a person can distinguish being valued for occupying it from actually choosing its commitments. External judgment may describe talent, usefulness, market power, family category, or institutional standing, but none of those by itself supplies permission, vocation, identity, or meaning. Fiction can temporarily make a blocked self inhabitable; once that lived self acquires enough reality, however, the gap between enactment and recognition becomes unstable.**

## 2. Volume architecture and narrative movement

V09 has twenty short-to-medium narrative units plus a long TRPG finale. Their variety is not fragmentation. The volume repeatedly moves between four levels of assigned value: economic value, professional/institutional value, relational usefulness, and identity category.

The tax opening makes career uncertainty quantitative. "Successful writer" must become a corporate decision about whether high income will remain stable enough to justify incorporation. The sequence is valuable because it denies the fantasy that artistic seriousness creates a forecastable income curve. [IM-V09-E001-IM-V09-E012]

Aoba's first chapter shifts from money to aesthetic classification. Her editor knows that the manuscript feels Nayuta-like and believes it is inferior, but the language of `本物` cannot itself explain how to revise it. The scene therefore distinguishes an evaluator's real perception from the adequacy of the evaluator's vocabulary. "Authenticity" may name a judgment without providing a usable method. [IM-V09-E013-IM-V09-E018]

The roomshare chapter moves the same structure into domestic life. V08 established reciprocal rules; one month later, actual habit has redistributed work toward Kyo. Her roommates' creative status and her own financial dependence make that unequal load feel compensatory: if she cannot be their professional equal, she can at least be necessary. V09 explicitly marks the satisfaction as dark rather than treating service as uncomplicated virtue. [IM-V09-E019-IM-V09-E024]

The cherry-blossom and hangover chapters widen Kyo's relational field. She actively consumes editor-centered fiction to imagine a future role, remains unable to say Haruto means nothing to her, and forms a more direct connection with Hajime. Ashley's half-conscious wish that everyone can see next year's blossoms introduces continuity as something hoped for rather than guaranteed. Kaizu's handling of her intoxication, meanwhile, is careful rather than opportunistic. These small episodes keep friendship and adult intimacy grounded in practice. [IM-V09-E025-IM-V09-E037] [IM-V09-E286-IM-V09-E288]

Aoba's second chapter provides the volume's first major rupture. Public reception knocks her from "same kind of genius as Nayuta" to "I have no right to write." Itsuki refuses both poles. The point is not to replace inflated self-certainty with unconditional claims of professional excellence. It is to separate evaluation from permission. A person can be derivative, unsuccessful, or still learning and nevertheless choose to continue making work. [IM-V09-E038-IM-V09-E046] [IM-V09-E289-IM-V09-E290]

The anime production crisis then externalizes the cost of work. A production runner disappears; the planned episode count can no longer be delivered in the same form; material must be recompressed; and an original ending becomes viable because prior failed/discarded work has produced understanding rather than returning the team to zero. This is a strong V09 contribution to the corpus's revision model: **discarded output can leave durable process knowledge.** [IM-V09-E047-IM-V09-E054] [IM-V09-E291]

The "if" conversation turns Chihiro's secret into self-sacrifice. Itsuki says that an actual sister might satisfy some of the hunger that drives his idealized sister-writing. Chihiro therefore decides disclosure could obstruct his path. This reason is grounded in something Itsuki really says, which makes it more serious than pure fantasy; but it remains Chihiro's causal inference. Nothing in V09 proves that learning the truth would actually destroy Itsuki's creative career. [IM-V09-E055-IM-V09-E063] [IM-V09-E292]

Nadeko's visit then repeatedly brushes the secret against innocent perception. A child reads Chihiro as an older sister; Itsuki corrects the category to younger brother; Chihiro actively repeats `僕` and the male claim. The same chapter also shows Itsuki practicing a form of audience appropriateness: despite his own taste for extreme sister fiction, he does not simply hand an elementary-school child the erotically packaged work. The comedy therefore coexists with evidence that he can distinguish his interests from an audience's age/context. [IM-V09-E064-IM-V09-E068] [IM-V09-E293-IM-V09-E294]

The Setuna/Puriketsu encounter is an important boundary correction. Setuna's prior sexualized pursuit remains inappropriate, but he now explicitly rejects force and says he should obtain actual agreement. Chihiro refuses contact information and gives only a first name. The chapter therefore contains real behavioral improvement without retroactively making prior coercive comedy harmless. The reveal that this odd boy is Itsuki's trusted illustrator immediately converts a private boundary problem into a secret-management problem. [IM-V09-E069-IM-V09-E077]

`Chihiro's anxiety` is the volume's sharpest test of V08's belonging revision. Chihiro withdraws from Itsuki's apartment to prevent an accidental encounter with Setuna, lies that study is the reason, and then watches Aoba volunteer to replace the cooking function. Aoba's competence is genuine and her praise of Chihiro is sincere. That sincerity matters: the threat does not require a hostile rival. A role can feel precarious simply because another good person can perform it. [IM-V09-E078-IM-V09-E106] [IM-V09-E295-IM-V09-E296]

Aoba's meeting with Nayuta then creates the opposite kind of recognition. Aoba no longer meets Nayuta only as a hierarchy to imitate; she meets the author whose work moved her enough to start writing. Nayuta, who is accustomed to large-scale fame but less accustomed to direct uncomplicated fan gratitude, becomes visibly affected. V09 again separates audience scale from interpersonal reader recognition. [IM-V09-E107-IM-V09-E112] [IM-V09-E297]

The dubbing chapter makes distributed authorship concrete. A script labeled final can still change through storyboard, editing, performance, or later visual adjustment. A `storyboard film` is a sign of incomplete production under time pressure, but the text also explains a genuine creative affordance: recording before all visuals are locked can allow performance timing to shape later animation rather than forcing dialogue into predetermined cuts. V09 continues to resist binary categories such as finished/unfinished or faithful/unfaithful. [IM-V09-E113-IM-V09-E128] [IM-V09-E298]

The next four chapters form a vocational sequence. Haruto teaches Chihiro that education can preserve options and that enjoyment is a legitimate part of a life decision. Kyo identifies editing as her present desire, encounters contradictory hiring conventions and a predatory self-publishing model, then learns from Toki that a genuine vocation can emerge from an accidental or impure beginning. Finally, the `3年縛り` conflict shows that institutional "rules" may actually be discretionary conventions whose enforcement tracks market value. [IM-V09-E129-IM-V09-E190] [IM-V09-E299-IM-V09-E302]

The broadcast-regulation chapter refuses a simple artist-versus-censor morality play. Itsuki initially treats the producer as a non-creator who cannot understand the injury of changing a line. Oshima answers from a different responsibility position: television has a very low access barrier, accidental viewers matter, sponsors matter, and station workers are professionals rather than anonymous enemies. Itsuki ultimately chooses a rewrite instead of reserving the uncensored wording only for a package release because younger readers may be unable to buy the discs. He explicitly does not claim the decision is universally correct. This is bounded audience responsibility rather than surrender dressed as virtue. [IM-V09-E191-IM-V09-E205]

Natsume's pregnancy collapses Chihiro's identity problem back into family biology. The feared newcomer is not a pseudo-sister like Aoba or a fictional sister like Nayuta's TRPG character. It is a future child with unambiguous blood relation to Itsuki. Chihiro's fear that her `居場所` will disappear is therefore strongest precisely when the objective evidence of rejection is weakest: no one has rejected her yet. V09 shows how category insecurity can generate its own anticipatory displacement. [IM-V09-E206-IM-V09-E212] [IM-V09-E303]

The white-box screening returns from private crisis to collaborative creation. Itsuki can see flaws, compromises, and the regulated-line change, yet still recognizes the result as unmistakably his work. The team did not preserve every surface; they preserved enough identity across transformation that he experiences the adaptation as `Imouto no Subete`. The production process itself becomes `flesh and blood`--experience that remains even when particular work is discarded or compromised. [IM-V09-E213-IM-V09-E218] [IM-V09-E304]

The TRPG finale then binds nearly every thread together: protagonist status, usefulness, alternate selves, sibling category, play, risk, trust, and disclosure. The session is not an appendix. It is the formal mechanism by which V09 lets Chihiro *live* the role before forcing the difference between lived role and socially recognized identity into speech. [IM-V09-E219-IM-V09-E257] [IM-V09-E305-IM-V09-E312]

## 3. Focalization and narrative authority

V09 is one of the strongest demonstrations so far that ensemble focalization is not ornamental. No single character possesses the volume's moral or institutional truth.

Itsuki focalization gives access to professional uncertainty, mentorship, adaptation frustration, and the continuing tendency to translate intimacy into achievement. He is authoritative about his own desire and experience; he is not authoritative about whether Chihiro's disclosure would actually ruin his creative drive. Chihiro hears a counterfactual and turns it into a reason for silence. That distinction must remain visible. [IM-V09-E001-IM-V09-E012] [IM-V09-E055-IM-V09-E063]

Yamagata's focalization on Aoba is equally limited. She can perceive derivative resemblance and predict hostile reception with impressive accuracy, but she cannot turn her sense of Nayuta's `本物` talent into concrete revision instructions. V09 thereby creates a sophisticated epistemic state: the editor may be right that there is a problem and still be unable to articulate the problem in a form useful to the creator. [IM-V09-E013-IM-V09-E018]

Kyo's interiority is essential because the roomshare looks superficially functional. Only Kyo's own thought exposes the hidden reward structure: being needed by gifted roommates makes unequal domestic labor emotionally satisfying. An external observer could easily misclassify the same behavior as generous competence. [IM-V09-E019-IM-V09-E024]

Chihiro's focalization repeatedly marks feared futures as fears. The imagined Setuna blackmail scene, for example, is Chihiro's anxious scenario, not evidence that Setuna actually blackmails her. The pregnancy chapter likewise records anticipated loss of place, not a family decision to exclude Chihiro. This distinction is especially important because Chihiro's inner models are causally powerful even when they are not accurate predictions. [IM-V09-E069-IM-V09-E106] [IM-V09-E206-IM-V09-E212]

Oshima's perspective corrects Itsuki's first framing of broadcast restrictions. Itsuki experiences a line change as damage to expression. Oshima sees a chain of access, complaints, sponsors, station employees, and production accountability. Neither perspective alone exhausts the problem. V09's institutional method is to move the reader between positions rather than reveal one villain behind every constraint. [IM-V09-E191-IM-V09-E205]

Haruto's GM voice functions as a temporary meta-narrative principle inside the fiction. He states a model of successful play in which the players' owned decisions matter more than achieving a predetermined objective. Because this appears inside a game chapter, it should not automatically be promoted into an authorial manifesto. But its alignment with Itsuki's Aoba mentoring and Chihiro's final disclosure makes the recurrence analytically strong. [IM-V09-E219-IM-V09-E257]

Finally, the author afterword confirms one formal design choice: the TRPG was deliberately prepared as the one world in which Chihiro could be her true self in front of Itsuki. That PF validates the identity-laboratory reading. It does **not** authorize importing later reactions into the cliffhanger or treating every TRPG event as a one-to-one allegory. [IM-V09-E258-IM-V09-E276] [IM-V09-E313]

## 4. Character-state analysis

### 4.1 Hashima Itsuki -- from worth-proving protagonist toward desire-preserving authorship

Itsuki remains ambitious, competitive, vulgar, generous, and vulnerable to comparison. The tax chapter shows that his wish to stand beside Nayuta still recruits an economic fantasy: a writer at her level should be able to assume continuing high income. But unlike an earlier Itsuki who might have converted aspiration into certainty, V09 lets him admit that publishing is too volatile to make the corporate decision on pride alone. [IM-V09-E001-IM-V09-E012]

His mentoring of Aoba is the more important movement. Itsuki refuses to let `才能`, `資格`, `価値`, or `意味` become a gate in front of `書きたい`. This is not anti-merit romanticism. He knows Aoba has been criticized for derivative work, and he himself later tells her the Nayuta influence is visible. His point is about the order of questions. Market judgment can tell a professional something about reception; it cannot decide whether a person is permitted to make the next work. [IM-V09-E038-IM-V09-E046]

That is also self-revision. Itsuki explicitly remembers his own debut-era fear that lacking talent would mean he could never be a `主人公`. By telling Aoba that even a counterfeit can keep writing and perhaps surpass the real, he constructs a protagonist identity compatible with not-yet-proven worth. The old competitive structure remains--he still wants to surpass Nayuta--but its psychological basis has widened from "I must already deserve to create" toward "I can keep authoring the attempt." [IM-V09-E038-IM-V09-E046]

His anime work demonstrates further maturation. He can accept that the production cannot preserve the original plan, accept an original ending when it emerges from rereading and internalization, write a script inside someone else's production system, and later choose a broadcast rewrite because audience access matters. He also recognizes a compromised but identifiable final episode as genuinely `Imouto no Subete`. These are not signs that he has become indifferent to control. They show greater ability to distinguish core priorities from replaceable surfaces. [IM-V09-E047-IM-V09-E054] [IM-V09-E191-IM-V09-E218]

V09 therefore **strengthens but does not sanitize** Itsuki's protagonist model. He continues to translate love into rivalry and achievement. The healthier insight and the dangerous comparison coexist.

### 4.2 Hashima Chihiro -- from useful concealed sibling to declared sister

Chihiro is V09's center of gravity.

At the start of the volume Chihiro remains extraordinarily competent in practical domains. That competence, however, no longer prevents the text from exposing insecurity underneath it. Aoba can cook. The robot can clean. Nayuta and Kaiko can live with Kyo. Haruto can be a friend without needing services. Each fact erodes the fantasy that attachment can be guaranteed through unique utility. [IM-V09-E078-IM-V09-E106]

The stress response is revealing. Chihiro does not simply feel proud that Aoba praises the cooking. The praise coexists with competitive panic because Aoba can openly call Itsuki "big brother" and can replace the meal function during Chihiro's absence. The feared substitution is therefore categorical and relational as much as practical.

Haruto's life-advice chapter is the volume's first affirmative counter-movement. Chihiro begins to recognize that an interest in robots can be a valid reason to study science, not merely an employability calculation. Haruto's emphasis on fun, optionality, and recoverable failure gives Chihiro a language of desire that is not immediately translated into service. [IM-V09-E129-IM-V09-E140] [IM-V09-E299]

The pregnancy crisis then exposes how fragile that shift remains. Chihiro imagines a future blood sibling as proof that the family already has the kind of sister/brother relation Chihiro has been unable to claim. The fear is existential--loss of `居場所`--even though no one has actually reassigned Chihiro's place. [IM-V09-E206-IM-V09-E212]

TRPG provides the decisive formal intervention. As `Chi`, Chihiro can be a girl, Itsuki's sister, mechanically awkward, protected, trusted, and eventually central. When Chi is ineffective against the first boss, Itsuki's character values her survival above victory. When her anti-magic ability later becomes uniquely suited to the enemy, utility returns, but now it is not the sole condition of worth: the relationship was affirmed **before** she became tactically indispensable. This is a crucial refinement of `IM-C031`. [IM-V09-E219-IM-V09-E257]

Itsuki's statement that Chi is the best little sister creates an ecstatic recognition that is both real and insufficient. It is real because Chihiro experiences it in a relationship with Itsuki. It is insufficient because everybody understands the category as fictional. The final movement from `僕` to `私` is therefore not a simple pronoun reveal. It marks the point where the socially performed younger-brother self can no longer contain the claimed sister identity. Chihiro's confession is impulsive, but it is not semantically random. The entire volume has been building pressure between enacted role and recognized category. [IM-V09-E219-IM-V09-E257] [IM-V09-E312]

V09 ends before anyone responds. The only canonical V09 state is: **the secret has been verbally disclosed to the room; reception is unresolved.**

### 4.3 Shirakawa Kyo -- vocation becomes chosen, care becomes a risk

Kyo makes major progress without reaching a simple resolution.

The roomshare shows a failure of the equality system she herself designed. Domestic labor has drifted toward her, partly because she is the most conventionally capable household manager and partly because she feels less entitled to equal standing while supported by her parents. The dangerous reward is explicit: being indispensable to two professionally successful creators compensates for feeling ordinary. [IM-V09-E019-IM-V09-E024]

Her job search, however, is genuine authorship rather than drift. She recognizes that the conventional finance applications mostly followed the path implied by peers and an economics degree. After self-analysis, she identifies editing as the work she actually wants. She even refuses to rely on the most obvious personal connection to GF because she wants to know whether she can enter through her own effort. That decision may contain pride, but it is evidence of chosen vocation rather than passive aptitude. [IM-V09-E141-IM-V09-E161]

The interviews strip away idealization. Kyo encounters contradictory dress-code signaling, commodified self-publication, repeated rejection, and an industry whose tacit norms are not transparent to newcomers. Toki's own origin story helps because it destroys the idea that a vocation must begin with pure destiny: he did not enter editing from a lifelong sacred commitment to publishing, yet the work became something he genuinely cares about. [IM-V09-E141-IM-V09-E190]

The Suma conflict then crystallizes Kyo's editor ideal. She wants an editor able to share an author's grief and joy. The interviewer immediately names the danger: one editor handles many authors and also serves the company. V09 does not invalidate Kyo's ideal; it refuses to let compassion erase capacity and institutional conflict. Given Kyo's roomshare pattern, the warning is personally diagnostic. Her maturation will require learning whether closeness can be practiced without turning indispensability into identity.

### 4.4 Kani Nayuta -- secure love, direct fan recognition, institutional market power

Nayuta's role in V09 is quieter but analytically dense.

Her jealousy toward Aoba is comic but occurs inside a stable relationship rather than restoring the old confession crisis. Once Aoba reveals herself as an emotionally overwhelmed fan rather than a romantic rival, Nayuta shifts rapidly. She has been exposed to large audience numbers, awards, and industry recognition; uncomplicated face-to-face gratitude affects her differently. [IM-V09-E107-IM-V09-E112]

Aoba's relation to Nayuta also destabilizes the simple `本物` hierarchy. Aoba can admire Nayuta without needing to be the same person. The fan encounter converts influence from a status comparison into a lineage of inspiration. That does not prove Aoba has already developed an independent voice, but it makes such development conceptually possible.

Nayuta's other major role is institutional rather than psychological. Kobe's admission that the `3年縛り` convention would not be enforced against Nayuta because she is too valuable makes her an involuntary test case for bargaining power. The point is not that Nayuta behaves badly. Her market value exposes that the rule's apparent neutrality is conditional. [IM-V09-E162-IM-V09-E190]

### 4.5 Fuwa Haruto -- mentor of optionality and GM of owned consequence

Haruto is increasingly important as a character who can speak about risk without turning risk into either cowardice or romantic self-destruction.

His university advice to Chihiro comes from a mixed history: he pursued higher education as insurance even after receiving a writing prize, delayed work for entrance exams, and later moved more fully into writing when adaptation success made the path feel sustainable. He neither universalizes his own choice nor says practical concerns are meaningless. Instead, he argues for preserving options and recognizing enjoyment as evidence. [IM-V09-E129-IM-V09-E140]

The TRPG chapter gives this worldview a formal analogue. A session can succeed even if the party fails its nominal goal, provided the protagonists fully live the consequences of their choices. Haruto's GM stance is permissive but not consequence-free: he tries to respect player decisions maximally rather than forcing a preferred story. [IM-V09-E219-IM-V09-E257]

This is an important contrast with the institutions in the employment chapters. Haruto makes constraints explicit and choice-bearing; the `3年縛り` norm is coercive partly because its constraints are withheld until violation.

### 4.6 Kenjiro Toki -- contingent origin, acquired vocation, and institutional sight

V09 adds the most important Toki backstory since the provisional V01-V06 monograph was created, but that monograph should not be silently rewritten because its declared source boundary remains V01-V06.

Toki did not enter editing through an immaculate vocational calling. An older manga-artist girlfriend, gaming, social connections, and circumstance contributed. He had not organized his identity around publishing beforehand. Yet sustained practice turned that contingent entry into expertise and commitment. [IM-V09-E162-IM-V09-E190]

This matters because V08 separated aptitude, institutional demand, and desire. V09 adds time: **the desire attached to a vocation can itself be formed through practice**. An accidental beginning does not make later commitment false.

Toki also quietly suspects Chihiro's sex/gender secret after seeing Setuna's portrait but does not immediately expose it. V09 therefore continues his pattern of possessing institutional/interpersonal information without automatically converting knowledge into unilateral action. The post-disclosure consequences remain beyond V09.

### 4.7 Kasamatsu Aoba -- from authenticity grandiosity to permission without guarantee

Aoba's trajectory is not "bad writer learns self-esteem." She begins with an inflated metaphysics of authenticity, is shattered by public comparison, and then must discover a reason to continue that does not depend on proving herself equal to Nayuta in advance.

Itsuki's intervention works because he does not promise eventual success. He asks whether Aoba loves the work enough to make another one. This preserves the possibility that criticism contains useful truth. It also prevents criticism from becoming a total verdict on personhood or creative permission. [IM-V09-E013-IM-V09-E018] [IM-V09-E038-IM-V09-E046]

Her later fan encounter with Nayuta further separates influence from identity. The open question is whether that separation produces a distinct artistic voice; V09 does not yet answer it.

### 4.8 Ena Setuna / Puriketsu -- boundary improvement without retroactive absolution

V09 makes a real change in Setuna's behavior: he explicitly says force is wrong and reframes his pursuit around obtaining agreement. Chihiro still refuses contact information. This is meaningful movement toward consent, but neither the sexualized request nor earlier behavior should be normalized merely because the character learns one boundary. [IM-V09-E069-IM-V09-E077]

The reveal that Setuna is Puriketsu is structurally important because social categories collide. Chihiro knows him as a strange boy with an inappropriate fixation; Itsuki knows the same person as a trusted professional collaborator. Neither category alone is the whole person.

### 4.9 Ashley and Kaizu -- care after comedy

The hangover aftermath gives Kaizu a compact but meaningful relational signal. He gets an intoxicated Ashley safely home, leaves hydration/food, and does not exploit vulnerability. Ashley's later playful communication suggests the relationship can contain attraction without collapsing care into entitlement. [IM-V09-E033-IM-V09-E037]

This remains a developing relationship, not a completed romance claim.

## 5. Relationship ecology

### 5.1 Itsuki <-> Chihiro -- the concealment phase ends

The relationship's pre-V09 contradiction is now impossible to sustain indefinitely: Itsuki loves the category "little sister" while failing to recognize the actual sister who has been enacting care beside him. V09 intensifies the irony by surrounding Chihiro with pseudo-sisters and game-sisters who can receive open sibling language.

The "if" conversation is the last major rationalization for silence. Chihiro interprets Itsuki's creative counterfactual as an ethical duty not to interfere with his path. The final TRPG breaks that logic not through argument but through experience. Chihiro discovers that being recognized as a sister, even fictionally, matters too much to keep subordinating indefinitely to a model of Itsuki's career. [IM-V09-E055-IM-V09-E063] [IM-V09-E219-IM-V09-E257]

The disclosure is involuntary in timing but owned in content. The `私` statement is not a slip about an incidental fact; it asserts a relationship. The next checkpoint must freeze the boundary carefully: **V09 establishes disclosure, not acceptance, rejection, repair, or family reconciliation.**

### 5.2 Chihiro <-> Aoba -- replacement anxiety without villainy

Aoba is threatening to Chihiro precisely because she is kind, competent, and sincere. She can cook, admires Chihiro's cooking, wants Itsuki's mentorship, and freely calls him big brother. The volume thereby refuses a melodramatic rival structure. Chihiro's pain is produced by role overlap, not malicious competition. [IM-V09-E078-IM-V09-E112] [IM-V09-E295-IM-V09-E297]

This is strong evidence that relational security cannot be built on uniqueness of function. A person will eventually encounter someone else who can cook, clean, mentor, entertain, or admire. Durable belonging needs another basis.

### 5.3 Chihiro <-> Haruto -- advice that treats desire as evidence

Haruto does not solve Chihiro's family problem. He does something more bounded: he treats Chihiro as a young person with interests and options rather than as Itsuki's support system. The conversation gives Chihiro permission to say robots are interesting without immediately translating that interest into service value. [IM-V09-E129-IM-V09-E140]

That makes the relationship developmentally important even though it is not romantic.

### 5.4 Kyo <-> Nayuta/Kaiko -- reciprocity rules fail under lived asymmetry

V08's roomshare rules were ethically mature. V09 shows that rules require maintenance. Talent schedules, personality, and Kyo's own need to be useful erode the initial balance. [IM-V09-E019-IM-V09-E024]

This is not evidence that Nayuta and Kaiko deliberately exploit Kyo. It is evidence that unequal care systems can emerge without a single malicious actor. Kyo's own psychological reward helps reproduce the arrangement.

### 5.5 Kyo <-> Haruto/Hajime -- emotional ambiguity remains

Kyo still cannot honestly say she feels nothing about Haruto. Her contact with Hajime develops independently. The volume does not convert either relationship into a chosen romance. Avoid treating jealousy as a contract or Hajime's presence as proof of a triangle outcome. [IM-V09-E025-IM-V09-E032]

### 5.6 Itsuki <-> Aoba -- mentorship as reciprocal self-address

Itsuki's advice helps Aoba, but the narration makes clear that he is also speaking to his own fear. This prevents the relationship from becoming a one-directional wise-senpai fantasy. Mentorship is one way a character articulates principles he still needs himself. [IM-V09-E038-IM-V09-E046]

### 5.7 Itsuki <-> production collaborators -- authority becomes responsibility-sharing

The anime chapters show Itsuki learning to work with people who have distinct expertise and constraints. Hiru-gano can understand the source without copying it. Oshima can represent access/sponsor responsibility without being artistically indifferent. Voice performance can affect later visual timing. The adaptation is therefore neither "Itsuki's work taken away" nor "everyone equal in every decision." It is layered authority under interdependence. [IM-V09-E047-IM-V09-E054] [IM-V09-E113-IM-V09-E128] [IM-V09-E191-IM-V09-E218]

## 6. Creative labor, publishing, adaptation, and industry

V09 substantially strengthens the corpus's institutional model.

### 6.1 Career income is a probability distribution, not a moral score

The incorporation discussion makes a creator's business form depend on expected future income rather than present prestige. Itsuki's emotional comparison to Nayuta pushes toward confidence; tax reality pushes toward uncertainty. The chapter is valuable because it materializes a theme often left abstract: ambition does not eliminate variance. [IM-V09-E001-IM-V09-E012]

### 6.2 Editorial judgment can be real and linguistically inadequate

Yamagata may be correct that Aoba's manuscript is too close to Nayuta, but she cannot explain the essence of Nayuta's talent or give concrete instructions that would create it. This prevents a simplistic anti-editor reading. The problem is not that she sees nothing; it is that evaluative perception exceeds her actionable vocabulary. [IM-V09-E013-IM-V09-E018]

### 6.3 Production breakdown is systemic, and revision is not reset

The missing production runner forces structural changes to the anime. The narrative explicitly treats disappearance under pressure as a recognizable production phenomenon and draws a parallel to writers/illustrators who disappear from editorial contact. Within the story world, this materially strengthens `IM-C032`: sustained production systems can consume the people who seem responsible for absorbing excess load. [IM-V09-E047-IM-V09-E054]

More importantly, Hiru-gano's discarded/reworked work is not wasted in an absolute sense. Rereading the source and trying to solve earlier versions produce an internalized understanding that enables the original ending. **A revision can destroy output without destroying learning.** This is one of V09's strongest additions to the fiction/adaptation ledger.

### 6.4 A final script is not necessarily the final audiovisual object

The dubbing chapter makes authority temporally distributed. A line can move after a script is submitted because storyboards, edit decisions, acting, and timing are still active. The storyboard-film method, while associated with schedule trouble, can also let performance influence later animation. V09 thus continues to treat craft constraints as ambivalent rather than morally pre-classified. [IM-V09-E113-IM-V09-E128]

### 6.5 Job seeking reveals the hidden curriculum of institutions

Kyo's interviews show that entry institutions test more than stated qualifications. `平服` can become a trap because applicants are expected to know an unstated conventional interpretation. A newcomer is judged for failing to read the institution's atmosphere rather than for violating an explicit rule. [IM-V09-E141-IM-V09-E161]

The self-publishing company sharpens the issue. The economic customer is the aspiring author, and the product sold is publication status, memory, or prestige rather than reader-demand-tested editorial selection. V09 does not claim all self-publishing has this structure. It depicts one predatory business that monetizes the cultural authority of "being published."

### 6.6 The three-year rule is a bargaining-power experiment

The `3年縛り` sequence should become a major evidence node. The convention is unwritten, unknown to Suma, justified after the fact through development cost and label reputation, and called `常識` by insiders. Kyo's objection is procedural before it is ideological: if a restriction matters enough to expel someone, tell the newcomer that the restriction exists.

The decisive test is Nayuta. Kobe admits the same conduct would not produce the same outcome because Nayuta's market value gives the institution a reason to bend or break the convention. This warrants a new active claim (`IM-C034`): **an unwritten norm can function as coercive institutional power when its visibility and enforcement are asymmetric, especially when bargaining power determines who receives exceptions.** [IM-V09-E162-IM-V09-E190]

The claim should remain bounded to the fictional institution until independently supported elsewhere. V09 is not evidence for a universal empirical statement about Japanese publishing.

### 6.7 Vocation can be acquired through practice

Toki's entry into editing was contingent rather than destiny-driven. His later commitment is nonetheless real. This warrants `IM-C035`: **the authenticity of a vocation is not determined by the purity of its origin; sustained practice can convert contingent entry into self-authored commitment.** [IM-V09-E162-IM-V09-E190]

The claim is not "any job becomes a calling if one stays long enough." The necessary evidence is that the person later endorses and inhabits the work rather than merely remaining trapped in it.

### 6.8 Broadcast adaptation distributes responsibility across audiences

Oshima's argument adds a missing institutional perspective. Television is not only a delivery pipe for existing fans. It reaches accidental viewers and depends on sponsors and station risk judgments. That does not make every restriction artistically wise. It explains why a producer can mediate rather than simply capitulate. [IM-V09-E191-IM-V09-E205]

Itsuki's final rewrite choice is especially important. He could preserve the exact line only in the package release. He instead changes the broadcast wording because some younger source readers may lack the money to access the package and he does not want the widely accessible version to carry a damaged scene. This materially strengthens `IM-C033`: public presentation is creator-reader labor with distributional consequences.

### 6.9 White-box recognition establishes priority-preserving identity

The completed episode contains visible flaws and compromises, yet Itsuki recognizes it as unmistakably his work. Fidelity is not identical surface preservation. The adaptation has retained a recognizable identity through transformed form. [IM-V09-E213-IM-V09-E218]

The sequence also supports a process-capital model. Production experience becomes `flesh and blood`; the value of the project includes capabilities acquired while making it, not only the finished object's quality.

## 7. Talent, success, failure, and the permission to create

V09 requires a significant refinement of `IM-C002`.

Earlier volumes established that talent and effort become dangerous when totalized into human worth or lovability. Aoba adds a new gate: **creative permission**. She assumes that if she lacks exceptional talent, her continued writing requires justification through `価値` or `意味`. Itsuki refuses the premise. [IM-V09-E038-IM-V09-E046]

The correct formulation is not "talent does not matter." Talent can matter to craft, market reception, opportunity, and comparative achievement. Nor is the formulation "all work is equally good." V09 contains harsh criticism and editorial judgment. The stronger claim is that professional evaluation cannot logically become a license determining whether a person may attempt the activity at all.

This distinction allows V09 to hold three propositions simultaneously:

1. Aoba's first book may genuinely be too derivative.
2. Public criticism can wound her and may contain information worth processing.
3. Neither fact proves she should cease creating if she still wants to write.

That is a much stronger and less sentimental account of artistic autonomy than unconditional praise.

The same logic transforms `本物`. Early in the volume, the term operates as status ontology: Aoba imagines herself as the same kind of "real" talent as Nayuta, while Yamagata imagines an essence she cannot verbalize. Later, Itsuki makes imitation temporal rather than ontological: a derivative creator can continue and potentially become something else. The relevant question is development, not metaphysical caste.

The TRPG then broadens `主人公` beyond artistic competition. A protagonist can fail. What matters is owned action under consequence. This strongly expands `IM-C014`: protagonist language is most defensible when it refers to self-authorship rather than guaranteed victory or superior rank. [IM-V09-E219-IM-V09-E257]

## 8. Family, siblinghood, identity, and disclosure

V09 completes the concealment phase of the title's central family irony.

### 8.1 The secret has acquired more than one justification

Chihiro originally concealed sex/gender and sibling identity as part of family strategy. By V08, concealment also protected friendships formed under the male-younger-brother presentation. V09 adds a third justification: Chihiro hears Itsuki say that an actual sister might alter the creative hunger driving his ideal-sister work. Chihiro therefore frames silence as protection of his vocation. [IM-V09-E055-IM-V09-E063]

This makes disclosure harder precisely because the concealment can be narrated as care. But the same narrative also deprives Chihiro of being cared for openly as a sister.

### 8.2 Pseudo-sisters reveal what Chihiro cannot request

Aoba, Ashley, Nadeko, and the TRPG characters repeatedly occupy sibling language without needing biological truth. Their freedom exposes Chihiro's constraint. Chihiro can perform sister-like service but cannot openly demand sister-like recognition.

The Haruto-sister vignette reinforces this from another direction: an ordinary sister can be inconvenient, demanding, or casual and remain a sister. Chihiro's ideal of earning belonging through competence therefore looks increasingly unlike ordinary kinship.

### 8.3 Pregnancy turns category insecurity into replacement fear

Natsume's pregnancy is not just "another sibling." It creates a future relation Chihiro imagines as category-proof: a child whose biological connection to Itsuki cannot be questioned. The resulting fear of losing `居場所` is psychologically coherent while remaining evidentially prospective. [IM-V09-E206-IM-V09-E212]

The correct reading must preserve both facts: the pregnancy really changes family structure, and no V09 character has yet told Chihiro that this change reduces Chihiro's belonging.

### 8.4 TRPG makes the barred self inhabitable

The final session is the volume's formal masterstroke. Chihiro is not merely talking about sisterhood; Chihiro plays it in Itsuki's presence. The role is female, sibling-coded, emotionally gratifying, and socially permitted because everybody believes it is fiction. [IM-V09-E219-IM-V09-E257] [IM-V09-E305-IM-V09-E312]

The game also breaks usefulness as the sole basis of attachment. Chi is initially poor against the boss and apologizes for being dead weight. Itsuki's character responds that keeping the sister alive matters more than winning. Only later does Chi's anti-magic ability become tactically decisive. Relational value therefore precedes instrumental value inside the game.

This is not proof that real-life Itsuki would react identically to disclosure. It is proof that Chihiro has now *experienced* a version of the relationship in which sisterhood does not have to be purchased through service.

### 8.5 The confession is a category assertion, not only a biological fact

The final language matters. Chihiro rejects "game-only sister" and "little brother," shifts into `私`, and asserts `本当の妹`. The claim contains sex/gender, kinship, and relational recognition simultaneously. [IM-V09-E219-IM-V09-E257]

This warrants `IM-C036`: **fictional role-play can function as an identity rehearsal space in which a socially blocked self becomes temporarily inhabitable; that successful enactment can intensify the pressure for real-world recognition.** The author afterword strongly supports the designed function of the TRPG as PF. [IM-V09-E258-IM-V09-E276]

The claim should not be inflated into a universal psychological law. It is a formal/character mechanism demonstrated strongly in Chihiro's case.

## 9. Sexuality, vulgar comedy, consent, and boundaries

V09 continues to require the project's rule that comic framing does not erase information asymmetry or consent.

Setuna is the clearest developmental case. The character explicitly abandons force as an acceptable method and says actual agreement matters. That is meaningful boundary learning. Chihiro still declines contact information, and the underlying request remains sexualized and uncomfortable. The correct transition is **behavioral improvement, not retrospective absolution.** [IM-V09-E069-IM-V09-E077]

The roomshare material contains sexualized comedy but its more important ethical feature is ordinary boundary fatigue: Kyo becomes the person repeatedly managing bodies, mess, and household expectations. That labor should not be romanticized as proof that she is naturally meant to care for creators.

Kaizu's care for intoxicated Ashley is comparatively straightforward positive evidence. He does not convert vulnerability into sexual opportunity. V09 can therefore register adult attraction and restraint in the same relationship. [IM-V09-E033-IM-V09-E037]

The Nadeko chapter is deliberately provocative in its lolicon-adjacent jokes, but the character-level evidence should remain precise. Itsuki recognizes age/context appropriateness strongly enough not to give a child the erotically packaged work he actually wrote. This does not erase the jokes; it demonstrates an internal distinction between fantasy taste and permissible treatment of an actual child. [IM-V09-E064-IM-V09-E068]

The TRPG contains exaggerated sexual-control mechanics as comedy. They belong principally to the game's genre parody and should not be mined as literal consent positions of the real-world characters. The analytically relevant real-world layer is how Chihiro experiences sibling role and recognition through the game.

## 10. Games, food, alcohol, and social ritual

V09 continues the series' practice of using leisure as relationship infrastructure rather than filler.

Food is particularly important because it becomes a contested care role. Chihiro's cooking has been ordinary maintenance for years; when Aoba can perform it, the mundane act becomes a test of whether care function and relational identity have been fused too tightly. [IM-V09-E078-IM-V09-E106]

The child-oriented board game with Nadeko introduces a small but useful counter-model to Itsuki's heroic self-image. He tends to valorize reckless fighting as protagonist behavior; the game's risk/reward logic lets a child gently expose that maximizing confrontation is not always rational. The scene is comic, but it participates in the volume's broader separation of agency from compulsive victory.

TRPG is the central ritual. Haruto treats it as collaborative narrative authorship; players can deviate; the GM preserves consequence rather than forcing success. Real anxieties leak into fictional roles without making the game a transparent confession machine. Kyo's job-hunt concerns, Chihiro's gender/sibling identity, Itsuki's sister ideology, Nayuta's possessiveness, and Haruto's creator worldview all become playable material. [IM-V09-E219-IM-V09-E257]

The session's formal importance is precisely that play is neither "just fake" nor simply equivalent to reality. It is a bounded space where real dispositions can be reorganized through fictional permission.

## 11. Fiction within fiction, intertext, and adaptation theory

V09 adds four major formal principles.

### 11.1 Imitation is developmental, not necessarily ontological

Aoba's work begins as a visible Nayuta imitation. The volume does not pretend otherwise. But Itsuki refuses to turn present derivativeness into permanent caste. Influence can be a starting condition from which later difference develops. [IM-V09-E013-IM-V09-E018] [IM-V09-E038-IM-V09-E046]

### 11.2 Revision can destroy text while preserving understanding

The anime's abandoned structure and original ending demonstrate that creative work can be thrown away at the output level while remaining causal at the process level. Hiru-gano's rereading and failed attempts produce understanding that informs the replacement. "Back to zero" is therefore often descriptively false in creative development. [IM-V09-E047-IM-V09-E054]

### 11.3 Fidelity can preserve identity through transformed surfaces

The white-box episode is not identical to Itsuki's source and contains compromises he notices. He nevertheless recognizes it as his work. This strengthens the V08 afterword-derived adaptation thesis with **narrative-internal** evidence: priority-preserving transformation is not only something the real author says about adaptation; it is something fictional Itsuki experiences. [IM-V09-E213-IM-V09-E218]

### 11.4 Role-play is an identity technology

The TRPG allows Chihiro to become a female sibling in the exact social space where that identity is otherwise unavailable. The author afterword explicitly states this design function. This is stronger than ordinary fiction-as-mirror. The game is an enacted counterfactual with interpersonal witnesses. [IM-V09-E219-IM-V09-E276]

The chapter also contains direct intertextual parody and references to real TRPG/replay culture. Those references help establish genre literacy but should not be promoted into independent thematic proof unless the local narrative actually uses them.

## 12. Japanese voice, terminology, and reconstruction-sensitive findings

V09 materially improves future character simulation because several important concepts are carried by lexical contrasts that flatten easily in English.

### 12.1 `本物` / `紛い物` / `劣化コピー`

Aoba's arc moves through a cluster of authenticity terms. `本物` is initially treated as an essential category of talent. `紛い物` and `劣化コピー` become humiliating social verdicts. Itsuki's intervention does not prove Aoba is secretly `本物`; it denies that the label determines whether she may keep writing. A simulation that makes Itsuki say "everyone is equally talented" would therefore be wrong. His actual stance is much more combative: keep authoring even before worth is proven. [IM-V09-E013-IM-V09-E046]

### 12.2 `資格` / `才能` / `価値` / `意味` versus `書きたい` / `好き` / `愛している`

The scene's rhetoric works by refusing category substitution. Aoba keeps answering a desire question with external-legitimation concepts; Itsuki repeatedly returns to wanting and loving the work. This is a central Itsuki voice pattern in V09: sharp negation, repeated narrowing of the question, then an emotionally extravagant protagonist formulation.

### 12.3 `常識` and `3年縛り`

The employment chapter uses `常識` not as neutral common knowledge but as an insider's accusation against someone who was never told the rule. Kyo's anger is procedural and linguistic: if it is binding, articulate it. This should be preserved in translation-sensitive analysis because "industry standard" would make the convention sound more formal than the scene presents it. [IM-V09-E162-IM-V09-E190]

### 12.4 `平服`

The dress-code interview illustrates pragmatic language as social sorting. The literal instruction and expected behavior diverge. Kyo is judged for failing to infer the hidden conventional reading. This is useful evidence for her future speech/behavior model: she is capable of reading social context but becomes angry when institutions convert unstated expectations into retroactive fault.

### 12.5 `一緒に泣く`

Kyo's desired editor is one who can "cry together" with the author. The phrase conveys emotional co-presence rather than simply sympathy from a distance. It should remain coupled to the interviewer's warning about workload/company responsibility, because otherwise the phrase can be mis-modeled as pure self-sacrifice.

### 12.6 `僕` -> `私`

Chihiro's first-person system is now a major reconstruction constraint. `僕` remains the socially inhabited male-younger-brother presentation. The final disclosure switches to `私` at the moment the concealed female/sister identity is asserted in public. English "I" erases this formal event. [IM-V09-E219-IM-V09-E257]

The change should not be overgeneralized into a claim that `僕` was therefore "fake" in every emotional sense. Chihiro has lived real relationships through that presentation. V09 shows a conflict between socially stabilized voice and claimed identity, not a simple true/false binary.

### 12.7 `本当の妹`

Chihiro does not merely say "I am a girl." The disclosure is relational: "I am big brother's real little sister." Biological/sex truth and desired kinship recognition arrive in one sentence. The word `real` is especially charged after an entire volume organized around `本物` and imitation. The series uses authenticity language in both creative and familial domains without making them identical.

### 12.8 `主人公`

V09 gives two complementary uses. Itsuki tells Aoba it would be protagonist-like for an imitation to continue and perhaps surpass the original. Haruto describes TRPG players as protagonists even when the outcome is failure or tragedy. Together these uses strengthen a reconstruction rule: late-middle Itsuki's protagonist language is not only domination; it increasingly names active authorship under uncertainty.

### 12.9 `居場所`

Chihiro's pregnancy reaction is a fear of losing place/belonging, not merely losing utility. That distinction is central after the V08 revision of `IM-C031`.

### 12.10 `妹ジネーション`

Itsuki's comic coinage for ideal-sister imagination is still useful voice evidence. It captures his tendency to transform obsession into grandiose pseudo-theoretical vocabulary. In V09, however, the joke also sits beside a serious counterfactual: real kinship might alter imaginative hunger.

### 12.11 Afterword phrase `それでも、面白いからここにいる`

As PF, Hirasaka identifies the anime-original line roughly meaning "even so, I am here because it is interesting" as a summary of the creator-work side of the series. This aligns strongly with V09's vocation arguments but remains author-afterword evidence, not dialogue spoken by novel-continuity Itsuki in this volume. [IM-V09-E258-IM-V09-E276]

The front package gives Chihiro unusually strong visual presence across the volume's ensemble framing. This is formally consistent with the prose shift toward Chihiro as the block's decisive identity character, but cover prominence alone is not used to infer interior state. [IM-V09-E277-IM-V09-E283]

The cherry-blossom images formalize future continuity as an ensemble wish rather than a guaranteed future. Kyo/Hajime/Haruto relational ambiguity and Ashley's emotional fragility are given visual weight without resolving either. [IM-V09-E286-IM-V09-E288]

Aoba's mentorship receives a direct narrative illustration and profile treatment. The art emphasizes emotional collapse and face-to-face intervention rather than abstract publishing theory, reinforcing that the talent/value discussion is psychologically lived. [IM-V09-E289-IM-V09-E290]

The production-trouble and "if" sections receive internal Q&A/parataxt pages, which is appropriate to V09's pattern of making industry mechanics and counterfactual identity questions explicit objects of reflection. These remain PF/FR rather than main-narrative omniscience. [IM-V09-E291-IM-V09-E292]

Nadeko, Chihiro's replacement anxiety, and the Aoba/Nayuta encounter each receive dedicated narrative imagery. The visual sequence therefore repeatedly places Chihiro's category anxiety next to other girls who can enter sibling/fan roles openly. [IM-V09-E293-IM-V09-E297]

Kyo's career material receives substantial formal space: life-advice Q&A, job-hunt Q&A, a narrative illustration, and another Q&A panel. This makes vocation one of V09's principal visual as well as textual domains. [IM-V09-E299-IM-V09-E302]

The white-box illustration gives Itsuki's emotional recognition of the adaptation a face rather than leaving the sequence at the level of industrial explanation. [IM-V09-E304]

The TRPG receives the largest visual apparatus: in-world documents, map/game diagrams, character sheets, positioning/board information, and the final Chihiro illustration. The quantity itself matters formally because the game is presented as a fully built secondary world, not a few lines of metaphor. [IM-V09-E305-IM-V09-E312]

Most important is `V09-ILL-036` / `IM-V09-E312`. It appears at the emotional crest after Itsuki calls Chihiro's character the best little sister and before Nayuta's innocent correction that Chihiro is only a game-setting sister. The image isolates a flushed, delighted Chihiro. Its placement creates dramatic irony: the visual freezes the brief moment in which fictional recognition feels sufficient immediately before prose demonstrates that it is not. The subsequent confession therefore reads not as an arbitrary cliffhanger but as the rupture of an already visually marked emotional maximum.

The author-afterword art remains PF and should not be used as narrative evidence. [IM-V09-E313]

## 14. Counterevidence and anti-overread controls

1. **Do not read Itsuki's Aoba advice as "talent does not exist" or "criticism never matters."** He separates permission to create from judgments of quality; the volume itself preserves criticism and editorial evaluation.

2. **Do not treat `本物` as an objective metaphysical category validated by the narrator.** Yamagata's inability to verbalize the distinction is part of the evidence.

3. **Do not say Aoba has already escaped Nayuta imitation.** V09 gives her permission and a new relational stance, not proof of a mature independent voice.

4. **Do not say Kyo's roomshare companions maliciously exploit her.** Unequal labor emerges through practice and Kyo's own compensatory desire to be needed.

5. **Do not celebrate Kyo's editor ideal without the capacity warning.** `一緒に泣く` is ethically attractive and potentially self-erasing.

6. **Do not claim Kyo is now a professional editor.** She chooses editing as a desired path; institutional entry remains unresolved.

7. **Do not universalize the fictional `3年縛り` as an empirical rule of all Japanese publishing.** Its analytical value here concerns opaque/selective enforcement in the represented institution.

8. **Do not infer that every unwritten norm is illegitimate.** `IM-C034` concerns consequential hidden rules plus asymmetric enforcement, not all tacit coordination.
