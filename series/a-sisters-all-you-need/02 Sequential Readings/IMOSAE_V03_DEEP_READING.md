# 妹さえいればいい。 Volume 03 — Deep Reading

## 1. Executive thesis

Volume 3 supplies the first term that can organize a large portion of the series' already-established tensions without collapsing them into a single value scale: **`主人公になりたい` — “I want to be the protagonist.”** [LF/IT] V01 established that sales, talent, artistic worth, happiness, moral legitimacy, romance, and recognition are non-equivalent. V02 made creation materially relational and showed that individual effort is embedded in households, collaborators, industries, bodies, and outcomes no one person fully controls. V03 now reveals why 羽島伊月 nevertheless keeps trying to convert those plural systems back into a personal ranking problem. He does not merely want success. He wants to experience himself—and to be publicly legible—as an agent whose name cannot be reduced to somebody else's supporting cast. [IM-V03-E079–IM-V03-E090]

This matters most because V03 finally makes 伊月's relationship to 可児那由多 explicit. The ambiguity “does he actually like her?” is no longer sustainable. He says he loves her, wants to date her, cannot tolerate another man having her, and even wants to marry her. [IM-V03-E079–IM-V03-E081] His refusal is therefore not a disguised lack of romantic desire. It is a **conditional theory of romantic legitimacy**: until he can imagine himself as an equal protagonist beside a genius whose public identity dwarfs his own, he will not accept the relationship he says he wants. The imagined humiliation is linguistic and narrative before it is economic. He pictures a news article in which she remains `大人気女性作家の可児那由多` while he becomes `２歳年上の同業者の男性`; he objects that this would erase `羽島伊月` as a named subject. [IM-V03-E082–IM-V03-E085]

The volume neither simply endorses nor simply mocks this position. 春斗 recognizes the underlying drive because he shares it: the need to be the protagonist is presented as a creatorly hunger powerful enough to outrank ordinary romantic gratification. [IM-V03-E086–IM-V03-E087] At the same time, 春斗 supplies the counterargument the volume needs. What would “equal” even mean when readers rank writers differently and sales cannot constitute an adequate total criterion? If 伊月 cannot specify the threshold, the condition can become infinitely deferrable. [IM-V03-E088] The immediately announced television adaptation satisfies the temporary milestone he chooses, but the prose explicitly says anime adaptation is not his endpoint. [IM-V03-E089–IM-V03-E092] V03 therefore dignifies the desire for self-authorship while exposing the danger of making intimacy contingent on an impossible external proof of ontological equality.

The in-volume bonus fiction, `番外編　羽島伊月の誕生`, makes that formulation much deeper without granting permission for monocausal psychoanalysis. It establishes that 伊月's attraction to younger-sister characters **predates** his formative relationship with 三田洞彩音. Before meeting her he already tended to prefer sister characters and was frustrated that they were often structurally barred from main-heroine status. [IM-V03-E095–IM-V03-E097] 彩音 therefore did not simply “cause” the fetish. What her rejection does is recruit and intensify an existing aesthetic preference into a defensive identity. She tells him she has always seen him as a younger brother; he experiences that position as non-equality, protects a frightened pride by rejecting her, declares that he needs no older sister, and immediately pivots to `僕には──妹さえいればいい`. [IM-V03-E102–IM-V03-E106] Soon afterward he interprets himself as not even a minor role in someone else's story and says the same thing the adult 伊月 will say years later: `主人公になりたい`. [IM-V03-E107–IM-V03-E109]

The causal picture after V03 is therefore **layered rather than solved**. [IT]

1. A prior aesthetic preference for sister characters exists before 彩音. [IM-V03-E095–IM-V03-E097]
2. A humiliating adolescent experience of being categorized as `弟` rather than an equal romantic subject turns sister/older-sister language into defensive relational grammar. [IM-V03-E102–IM-V03-E106]
3. Heartbreak intensifies sister-media consumption. [IM-V03-E110]
4. Authorship becomes a mechanism for transforming sister desire, heartbreak, inferiority, anger, hope, and the desire to be a protagonist into an authored world. [IM-V03-E111–IM-V03-E115]

That mechanism also revises the project's understanding of fiction-within-fiction. V02 demonstrated that lived affect can be transformed into fictional material. V03 shows the inverse scale: **writing itself can be a technology of self-authorship**. The young 伊月 does not merely put autobiographical facts into a novel. He decides to throw `自分の中にあるすべて` into fiction and changes grammatical self-position in the act: `僕が──いや、俺が主人公だ`. [IM-V03-E113–IM-V03-E115] The `俺` voice that reads as comic arrogance in the adult present is thus retrospectively linked, within the V03 boundary, to a deliberate move out of adolescent passivity. That does not make the persona transparent authenticity; it gives the persona a history and function.

V03 simultaneously complicates `妹` in a second direction. 那由多 calls 京 an `お姉ちゃん` because 京 teaches, comforts, cries, protects, and gives her a relational position of admiration. 伊月 then formulates—absurdly but revealingly—that blood is unnecessary: one becomes a younger sister in relation to someone one admires and follows. [IM-V03-E013–IM-V03-E014] The series' key term is therefore no longer only a literal kinship category or erotic fetish. It can also describe a **relational position of admiration, care, asymmetry, and chosen attachment**. That broadening intensifies rather than resolves the irony around 千尋: the actual younger sister continues to build a material sibling relation with 伊月 while he remains epistemically unable to apply his own category to the person closest to him.

Finally, V03 opens a major distinction in its theory of art: **a work can be valuable as a private relational gift rather than as a market commodity.** 那由多 writes 京 a novel of roughly book length, makes 京 the protagonist, and refuses publication precisely because it belongs to 京 alone—even after 春斗 immediately notes its enormous commercial value. [IM-V03-E031–IM-V03-E034] 京's response to that gift makes her confess the lie about being sexually experienced, because continued performance feels intolerable before someone who has given her something so singular. [IM-V03-E036–IM-V03-E040] The manuscript is not merely “content.” It changes the conditions under which truth can be spoken.

### V03 thesis in compressed form

> **V03 turns “protagonist” from a genre position into a theory of vulnerable selfhood. 伊月 wants love, but refuses to receive it while he imagines himself as a nameless supporting character in someone else's story; the bonus fiction reveals that this wound predates 那由多 and that becoming a novelist was already an attempt to convert inferiority and rejection into authored agency. Yet the volume also contests solitary protagonist logic through chosen-sister relations, private artistic gifts, ensemble recognition, collaborative games, family care, and Haruto's challenge that no objective metric can finally prove equality.** [IT]

## 3. Chapter and structural map

V03's structure looks episodic, but its episodes increasingly converge on questions of **recognition, role, gift, and authorship**. The climax `主人公になりたい` is not an isolated late thesis; earlier chapters have already asked who gets to define another person's role, who gets to narrate desire, who owns a work's public presentation, and whether private value must become market value.

The volume's ordering is especially effective. It spends most of its length making “role” concrete before naming the abstraction. Only then does 伊月 state the underlying wound in its most compressed form: `主人公になりたい`. [FR/IT]

## 4. Narration, focalization, and epistemic structure

### 4.1 Ensemble focalization continues to be explanatory, not decorative

V03 further strengthens the V01–V02 finding that 伊月 cannot explain the series alone. Kyo's focalization understands something about 伊月/那由多 before 伊月 verbalizes it: she judges that he likes Nayuta but that liking is not the only variable controlling his decision. [IM-V03-E007–IM-V03-E009] Haruto later sees something Kyo herself has not confessed to him: the expression with which she watches Itsuki and Nayuta leads him to recognize her love. [IM-V03-E047–IM-V03-E051] Nayuta, in turn, receives a career-state change through narrated jealousy: she first recognizes Itsuki as a colleague competing in the same world. [IM-V03-E016]

No single focalizer is therefore the oracle. Kyo's reading of the accidentally tsundere LINE message is a deliberate counterexample. Because Itsuki is a professional light-novel writer, she concludes that he must know the message's genre coding and therefore may have produced it intentionally. [IM-V03-E010] Her reasoning is plausible and wrong-footed by the fact that professional genre competence does not guarantee competent self-presentation. V03 uses **romcom literacy as a source of error**: knowing the trope can make a character overfit ordinary awkwardness to narrative pattern.

This is important for the project's method. Characters repeatedly understand each other through fiction, but fiction is not an infallible decoder ring. It can disclose, distort, and create expectations at the same time.

### 4.2 The bonus narrator is retrospective but not omniscient license for all later causality

`羽島伊月の誕生` differs from the main chapters because its narrator can contrast adolescent 伊月 with “the current successful professional writer.” [IM-V03-E104] That permits stronger psychological statements than the present-day characters can make about themselves. It explicitly calls his post-rejection wall a defense of `臆病な自尊心`. [IM-V03-E104] Yet the same bonus also blocks an easy total explanation of the sister fixation by establishing the preference before 彩音. [IM-V03-E095–IM-V03-E097]

The narratorial authority therefore narrows the causal model rather than authorizing speculative completion. What V03 allows is: prior preference + later defensive intensification. What it does **not** allow is: mother's death automatically caused sister desire; 彩音 created the preference from nothing; or every later use of `妹` has one unconscious meaning.

### 4.3 Dramatic irony around Chihiro becomes more precise

The reader has known Chihiro's sex since V01. V03 makes the asymmetry socially narrower: Chihiro says that within the family, Itsuki is the only person who believes Chihiro is male, while school proceeds normally with Chihiro as a girl. [IM-V03-E056] This changes the nature of the secret. It is not a globally maintained gender performance. It is an **Itsuki-specific relational fiction** maintained around one person and extended to some of his associates.

Ashley becomes a new witness and immediately asks the question the story itself is structurally storing: what will a sister-obsessed man do when he learns that he actually has a sister? [IM-V03-E057–IM-V03-E058] Chihiro's answer is not indefinite avoidance; Chihiro says the truth will be told someday. The tension therefore shifts from whether disclosure is imaginable to **when, why, and under what relational conditions disclosure becomes possible**.

### 4.4 Embedded fiction keeps changing the level on which “truth” operates

Three embedded-fiction systems matter in V03. Itsuki's unwritten-volume synopsis makes claims designed to be exciting before the underlying scenes fully exist; he defends the wording as not false. [IM-V03-E017–IM-V03-E025] Nayuta's private fantasy transforms Kyo and Itsuki into fictional protagonist structures while also functioning as a real-world gift. [IM-V03-E031–IM-V03-E044] `Once Upon a Time` explicitly makes control over the narrator position the game's competitive mechanism. [IM-V03-E075–IM-V03-E078]

These are not interchangeable allegories. Together, however, they establish a recurring formal concern: **stories do not merely represent already-settled realities; they organize expectation, social position, and possible endings.** [FR/IT] That is the formal environment in which “I want to be the protagonist” acquires force.

## 5. Character-state changes

### 5.1 羽島伊月: love is explicit; legitimacy remains conditional

The largest V03 correction is straightforward: any V01–V02 hedge that 伊月's feelings for Nayuta might be primarily artistic fascination, sexual attraction, protectiveness, or competitive fixation is now insufficient. He says he loves her. He wants to date her. He does not want another man to have her. He wants marriage. [IM-V03-E079–IM-V03-E081]

The complication is why he still refuses. His imagined marriage article makes clear that the wound is not simply “she sells more books.” It is the reduction of his proper name to a relational description: `お相手は２歳年上の同業者の男性`. [IM-V03-E082] His fear is to become a derivative identity—someone grammatically defined by relation to the famous woman who occupies the headline. He then translates that media hierarchy into fictional grammar: she is the protagonist; he is a supporting role. [IM-V03-E083–IM-V03-E084]

This produces a revealing contradiction with the V01–V02 theory of creative interdependence. 伊月's actual life already demonstrates that no creator is autonomous: Chihiro cooks and critiques, Toki edits and packages, Setsuna illustrates, readers confer response, friends supply experience, and institutions distribute reach. Yet his romantic self-worth still seeks a form of **sovereign named status**. V03 thus exposes a tension between the relational ontology of his real creative life and the protagonist ontology through which he evaluates himself. [IT]

The ambition is not reducible to vanity. The bonus fiction gives it a morally more sympathetic origin: young Itsuki had experienced being peripheral, acted upon, bereaved, rejected, and categorized in another person's relational terms. Writing becomes a way to claim agency. [IM-V03-E093–IM-V03-E115] But adulthood creates a new danger: the mechanism that once transformed passivity into agency can become a condition that indefinitely postpones reciprocal love.

Haruto's objection is therefore not merely practical romantic advice. It is epistemological. There is no stable cross-person scale on which two writers become objectively equal. Different readers rank them differently; sales is inadequate; any chosen line risks arbitrariness. [IM-V03-E088] V03 leaves this contradiction OPEN rather than resolving it.

### 5.2 可児那由多: from worshipper toward colleague, sister, and gift-giver

V03 adds important axes to Nayuta that do not eliminate her intense orientation toward Itsuki. Her relationship with Kyo becomes explicitly sibling-coded in the opposite direction: Kyo is the person she admires, learns from, depends on emotionally, and calls older-sister-like. [IM-V03-E013] The crucial point is that Nayuta is not only receiving care. She gives Kyo something nobody else can receive: a full private novel written exclusively for her. [IM-V03-E031–IM-V03-E034]

That gift demonstrates an autonomous creative capacity because its value is deliberately removed from the market. Haruto immediately sees that a new Nayuta manuscript could sell in huge numbers; Nayuta refuses that conversion. [IM-V03-E033–IM-V03-E034] The act is therefore not less “professional” because it is private. It shows that she can allocate creative labor according to relational value rather than market maximization.

The manuscript also reveals how multiple attachments coexist inside one work. Kyo is the protagonist and sole intended reader, but the knight is modeled on Itsuki. [IM-V03-E044] This is more complex than a one-to-one autobiographical code. Nayuta's affection for Kyo, erotic/comic imagination, and desire for Itsuki are recomposed into a new fictional relation.

Professionally, jealousy over Kyo's favorite-author status yields an especially significant sentence: Nayuta recognizes Itsuki for the first time as a `同業者` competing in the same world. [IM-V03-E015–IM-V03-E016] V01 and V02 made Itsuki the rescuer/ideal. V03 begins adding **peer recognition**. That does not prove the worship dynamic has vanished; it means her model of him is acquiring a horizontal professional axis.

### 5.3 白川京: truth, desire, and the right to be ordinary

V02 revised Kyo from “ordinary noncreator” to someone who habitually disqualifies her own desires through comparison, conformity, and usefulness. V03 places that problem under direct pressure at her twenty-first birthday. She says she expected people in their twenties to feel more adult; Haruto agrees that formal adulthood has not produced a matching internal state. [context surrounding IM-V03-E035] Kyo then turns the comparison back on the writers: what they call childishness can become work, and from her position they belong to the “functioning” side of adulthood. [IM-V03-E035]

The birthday sequence's most important change, however, is not age but **deperformance**. Kyo has maintained a fiction of sexual experience because she feared how she would be read. After receiving Nayuta's private novel, she can no longer justify that performance to the person who has trusted her most extravagantly. [IM-V03-E036–IM-V03-E038] Truth-telling is accompanied by relief. Nayuta's reply attacks the value premise underneath the lie: Kyo was loved because she was kind, not because she fit a sexually confident adult-woman archetype; experience or virginity would not change her older-sister position. [IM-V03-E039–IM-V03-E040]

That is a small but real answer to V02's self-permission problem. Kyo discovers a relationship in which a socially performed marker of adulthood is unnecessary for worth. The scene does **not** establish that she has solved her broader vocational insecurity. It does show that she can receive affection that is not earned by superiority, usefulness, or successful performance.

Romantically, V03 makes her attachment to Itsuki much harder to treat as merely latent. She already concludes he loves Nayuta. [IM-V03-E007–IM-V03-E009] At the amusement park, she watches their physical and temperamental compatibility and calls them well matched; Haruto reads her gaze and understands. [IM-V03-E045–IM-V03-E051] This makes the potential Haruto/Kyo path asymmetrical at V03. Haruto's interest is real, but his recognition that using Itsuki as matchmaker would wound Kyo causes him to withdraw the request. [IM-V03-E050–IM-V03-E051]

Kyo/Nayuta intimacy must remain carefully classified. The near-kiss and illustration are deliberately intimate; Kyo herself declines because she understands Nayuta's romantic orientation toward Itsuki. [IM-V03-E041, IM-V03-E125] V03 supports deep embodied affection and chosen-sister language. It does not establish a reciprocal Kyo/Nayuta romance.

### 5.4 不破春斗: audience pleasure becomes an explicit creative origin

V02 broke Haruto's protective `工業製品` theory by showing that he genuinely loves his work. V03 offers a positive replacement principle. While revising the TRPG setting after actual play, he admits to having secretly altered dice outcomes to keep inexperienced players alive; he now wants to redesign the system so appropriate tension and enjoyment arise from better balance rather than hidden intervention. [IM-V03-E059] This produces the sentence `人に楽しんでもらうことは、楽しい`, which the narration identifies as the origin of Haruto as a writer even while sales, acclaim, and rivalry remain genuine motives. [IM-V03-E060–IM-V03-E062]

This is a substantial strengthening of C012. Market orientation and audience orientation are not identical. Haruto can strategically engineer accessible fiction because giving readers pleasure is itself one of his authentic pleasures. The V02 failure did not reveal a secret pure artist hidden beneath commercial craft; V03 shows craft becoming emotionally intelligible as **service to an audience**.

Kyo's character sheet reinforces this recovery. She likes people who are trying hard and hates mocking them. [IM-V03-E063–IM-V03-E064] To Haruto, who continues to see his work publicly mocked, Kyo becomes evidence that not every reader or observer converts failure into contempt. [IM-V03-E065] His attraction to her is therefore psychologically continuous with the empathy that stabilized him in V02.

But V03 simultaneously weakens the probability of reciprocity. Haruto recognizes Kyo's love for Itsuki and stops asking Itsuki to facilitate his pursuit. [IM-V03-E048–IM-V03-E051] This is not defeatism; it is an ethical recognition that using the person Kyo loves as a tool for courting her would be cruel.

### 5.5 羽島千尋: from invisible household labor toward explicit family and outside work

Chihiro's V03 development moves on two fronts. Professionally, the bookkeeping interest opened in V02 becomes actual paid work at Ashley's office. [IM-V03-E054–IM-V03-E055] That is important because it creates a competence domain not performed directly inside Itsuki's apartment. The motivation remains relationally entangled, but the work is externally situated, supervised, and skill-producing.

Second, the identity secret stops being confined to the reader and a few implied family members. Ashley accidentally discovers it and learns that Itsuki is the only family member who thinks Chihiro is male. [IM-V03-E056, IM-V03-E127] Chihiro explicitly accepts eventual disclosure as an obligation. [IM-V03-E057–IM-V03-E058] The secrecy is therefore no longer best modeled as a total identity. It is an Itsuki-centered relational arrangement with an acknowledged expiration problem.

The aquarium chapter gives the sibling bond its clearest affirmative statement so far. After describing the hostile aftermath of the father's remarriage and the later repair generated by living apart, games, visits, and reading, the narration states that Chihiro is the one person in the Hashima household Itsuki regards as precious family. [IM-V03-E066–IM-V03-E069] His warm attention to Chihiro's aquarium excitement makes this ordinary and embodied rather than declarative only. [IM-V03-E070, IM-V03-E122]

Yet the family relation remains split. Itsuki will accompany Chihiro home but refuses to stay. Keisuke's thank-you for old travel souvenirs is experienced as dutiful and emotionally flat, and Chihiro unsuccessfully tries to prolong the father-son exchange. [IM-V03-E071–IM-V03-E074, IM-V03-E128] Chihiro is thus both the **bridge into family** and evidence of how narrow Itsuki's family acceptance remains.

### 5.6 土岐, 刹那, Ashley, and Keisuke

Toki's V03 importance lies less in coercive deadline management than in publishing pragmatism. When Itsuki dislikes editorial synopsis copy, Toki accepts that the better synopsis writer should do the job “for the work.” [IM-V03-E017–IM-V03-E018] This supports a model of editing in which role ownership can be flexible. The later anime-announcement scene also makes Toki the institutional messenger who converts Itsuki's self-chosen milestone into professional fact. [IM-V03-E092]

Setsuna remains a case in which artistic appetite and ethical boundary problems coexist. Itsuki values future collaboration and therefore has a rational professional interest in Setsuna's growth, but he also explicitly labels the previous forced clothing removal criminal. [IM-V03-E026–IM-V03-E028] The comedy does not erase that language.

Ashley becomes more important as a cross-domain adult: employer, accountant, confidante, and holder of Chihiro's secret. The visual packaging of the discovery is overtly erotic, but the narrative consequence is confidentiality and future disclosure pressure. [IM-V03-E056–IM-V03-E058, IM-V03-E127]

Keisuke enters direct present-time relational evidence. V03 does not yet give his interiority, so the father's motivations must remain underdetermined. We can say that the encounter is emotionally cold from Itsuki's focal position and that he acknowledges the gifts; we cannot yet conclude that he feels nothing for his son. [IM-V03-E072–IM-V03-E074]

## 6. Relationship ecology

### 6.1 Itsuki ↔ Nayuta: mutual desire, unequal self-position

V03 converts the relationship from ambiguity into a known asymmetry of **timing and self-assessment**, not a known asymmetry of love. Itsuki openly wants the relationship and refuses it for a self-imposed status condition. [IM-V03-E079–IM-V03-E085] Nayuta's pursuit therefore cannot be read as unknowingly chasing someone who does not return her feelings.

The amusement park supplies embodied evidence before the confession. Nayuta takes his hand; Itsuki does not release it; Kyo calls them well matched. [IM-V03-E045–IM-V03-E047] The monochrome illustration foregrounds the pair as a visible couple-like unit while Kyo/Haruto's perspective occupies the scene's interpretive outside. [IM-V03-E126]

The major danger is that Itsuki's condition makes romance subordinate to a professional ranking system with no stable endpoint. Haruto's challenge is decisive counterevidence to any reading that treats “equal protagonist” as an objectively measurable state. [IM-V03-E088] V03 does not answer whether Itsuki can eventually accept relation without first securing that proof.

Nayuta's colleague recognition complicates the same relationship from the other side. She begins to see him not only as rescuer/ideal but as another author whose relation to readers can provoke jealousy. [IM-V03-E015–IM-V03-E016] That may be a necessary horizontalization, but V03 alone cannot tell whether it becomes durable.

### 6.2 Kyo ↔ Nayuta: chosen sisterhood and the ethics of truthful intimacy

The relationship acquires an explicit vocabulary: Nayuta describes Kyo as `お姉ちゃん`, and Itsuki immediately abstracts the relation into a theory of `妹`. [IM-V03-E013–IM-V03-E014] Unlike Itsuki's erotic-aesthetic idealization, however, the concrete relation is defined through teaching, comfort, shared emotion, admiration, and reciprocal trust.

The private novel is the strongest object in that relationship. Kyo is not merely a supportive audience; she becomes protagonist and sole reader. [IM-V03-E031–IM-V03-E034, IM-V03-E042] In response, Kyo makes herself more legible by abandoning a lie she had used to manage social expectation. [IM-V03-E036–IM-V03-E040]

The near-kiss is boundary-sensitive in a way that the series' vulgarity can obscure if summarized carelessly. Nayuta offers permission; Kyo declines because she recognizes Nayuta's desired romantic claim toward Itsuki. [IM-V03-E041] The scene is intimate, but the text itself supplies a reason not to collapse intimacy into established romance.

### 6.3 Haruto → Kyo: attraction becomes ethically constrained by knowledge

Haruto's V02 interest becomes more developed but less hopeful. Kyo's empathy remains central to why he cares for her, and her character sheet reinforces the value he sees in her. [IM-V03-E001–IM-V03-E006, IM-V03-E063–IM-V03-E065] At the amusement park, however, he recognizes that Kyo's gaze is directed toward Itsuki. [IM-V03-E048–IM-V03-E049]

He responds by refusing the instrumental shortcut of Itsuki's matchmaking help because it would be cruel to Kyo. [IM-V03-E050–IM-V03-E051] This gives Haruto's romantic arc an ethical dimension: desire does not authorize optimization when the method would disregard the desired person's existing attachment.

### 6.4 Itsuki ↔ Chihiro: the real sibling relation strengthens under false classification

V03 intensifies the project's central irony without reducing it to a punchline. Itsuki now explicitly regards Chihiro as precious family, even while he thinks Chihiro is a younger brother. [IM-V03-E066–IM-V03-E070] Meanwhile his abstract `妹` theory expands to include non-blood relational positioning. [IM-V03-E013–IM-V03-E014] The material relationship and categorical fantasy therefore come closer together conceptually while remaining separated by one concealed fact.

Chihiro's eventual-disclosure commitment makes this a genuinely unstable arrangement rather than a static series premise. [IM-V03-E057–IM-V03-E058] The aquarium/home sequence also shows that sibling intimacy does not automatically heal the larger family system. Chihiro can connect to both Itsuki and Keisuke, but cannot make them connect to each other. [IM-V03-E071–IM-V03-E074]

### 6.5 Itsuki ↔ Haruto: rivalry gains philosophical intimacy

Haruto's most important friendship function in V03 is to understand Itsuki without simply validating him. He recognizes the protagonist hunger because he has it too, but then attacks the weak point in Itsuki's theory: the absence of a coherent equality criterion. [IM-V03-E086–IM-V03-E090] This is a stronger form of friendship than agreement. Haruto can say, in effect, “I know exactly why this matters to you, and I still think your metric may trap you.”

The same scene immediately turns back into rivalry: Haruto sees that Itsuki treats anime adaptation as only a foothold and renews his own refusal to lose. [IM-V03-E089–IM-V03-E090] Friendship, recognition, envy, and creative ambition remain braided rather than mutually exclusive.

## 7. Creative labor, publishing, and industry

### 7.1 Synopsis writing: packaging is a creative practice with different truth conditions

The `あらすじを考えよう` chapter is one of the series' best early demonstrations that publishing labor does not begin and end with writing a manuscript. Itsuki explains that synopses are required two or three months before release because promotion and announcement infrastructure needs them, even when the actual novel does not yet exist in finished form. [IM-V03-E019]

That temporal inversion is especially difficult for his improvisational series. He deliberately does not outline `妹すべ`, claiming to value a live feeling in which even he does not know exactly what will happen. [IM-V03-E020] Yet promotional copy must imply an appealing future without spoiling it. [IM-V03-E021] The synopsis is explicitly `パッケージングの一部`; weak copy can alter a continuation purchase. [IM-V03-E022]

V03 therefore separates at least three skills:

- generating a long-form fictional work;
- compressing its atmosphere/trajectory into short copy;
- strategically creating expectation under spoiler and length constraints. [IM-V03-E023]

Itsuki's joke about a “misunderstanding” that has not yet been written makes the ethical tension visible. He can choose wording likely to generate one reader expectation while retaining literal deniability: `嘘は書いてない`. [IM-V03-E024–IM-V03-E025] The text plays this for comedy, but analytically it is an early example of how **truthful packaging can still be rhetorically manipulative**. That is not equivalent to fraud, nor is it neutral description.

### 7.2 Flexible role ownership inside publishing

The synopsis chapter also develops Toki. Itsuki takes over synopsis writing after disliking Toki's debut-volume copy; Toki accepts that whoever does it better should do it “for the work.” [IM-V03-E017–IM-V03-E018] That is a small but important counterexample to a rigid author/editor division. The production system contains formal roles, but competence can redistribute particular tasks.

This strengthens C003: the industry is neither a frictionless community nor an external machine that merely constrains authors. It is an allocation system in which authority, expertise, scheduling, promotion, and aesthetic judgment overlap.

### 7.3 Haruto: from outcome shock to audience-oriented craft

V02 showed that adaptation failure can emerge despite sincere labor distributed across a production system. V03 asks what Haruto does after learning that lesson. His answer is not to abandon design or audience awareness. In the TRPG setting-material chapter he studies actual play, recognizes that his previous hidden intervention was compensating for bad balance, and revises the system. [IM-V03-E059]

The resulting principle—giving people enjoyment is enjoyable—provides the positive formulation missing from the V02 `工業製品` crisis. [IM-V03-E060–IM-V03-E062] Haruto's commercial competence can now be understood as one possible implementation of an audience ethic. It remains compatible with rivalry, status desire, and sales ambition; V03 explicitly says those motives are also genuine. The important correction is that none exhausts him.

### 7.4 Anime adaptation as milestone, not transcendent reward

V02 had already dismantled the fantasy that adaptation is uncomplicated validation. V03 makes that knowledge part of peer comparison. Itsuki selects television anime adaptation as a provisional milestone because it increases public recognizability and may reveal a new professional landscape. [IM-V03-E089] Haruto, whose own adaptation has been painful, notices the casual `とりあえず` logic and understands that Itsuki does not regard adaptation as a final goal. [IM-V03-E090]

The next-day announcement that `妹のすべて` will indeed be animated is therefore structurally ironic. [IM-V03-E092] The event fulfills the first metric almost immediately after Haruto has explained why the larger metric cannot be made objective. V03 closes the career beat with progress but not with solution.

### 7.5 Private art interrupts market equivalence

Nayuta's birthday novel for Kyo is the strongest counterweight to publishing-market logic. Haruto's instinctive price/sales calculation is reasonable because a new manuscript from a famous author has obvious market value. [IM-V03-E033] Nayuta refuses the translation. The work was made for one person and is valuable partly because nobody else has access. [IM-V03-E034, IM-V03-E042]

This opens C016: art can function as a private relational gift whose value depends on restricted circulation. That claim should not be romanticized into “private art is purer than commercial art”; the same volume demonstrates sophisticated commercial craft. The point is **non-equivalence**. Market value and gift value are different modes of value, and a creator can knowingly choose one over the other.

## 8. Talent, effort, success, failure, and self-worth

### 8.1 “People who work hard should be rewarded” remains normative, not predictive

Itsuki's V03 belief that effort deserves reward follows Haruto's adaptation collapse and prospective romantic recovery. [IM-V03-E006] The wording is ethical (`べき`) rather than causal. That distinction is essential because V02 has already shown sincere effort failing, while V03 itself remembers an unnamed successful writer who insisted that successful writers must be happy and then did not become happy. [IM-V03-E004–IM-V03-E005]

The pair of propositions therefore operates as aspiration under counterevidence. Itsuki wants a world in which effort and success can be redeemed by happiness. The series does not yet guarantee that world.

### 8.2 Itsuki makes professional status carry existential weight

C002 previously tracked the instability of using talent as self-worth. V03 strengthens and revises the mechanism. Itsuki's problem is not merely “Nayuta is more talented than me.” It is that professional recognition becomes evidence about whether he occupies a sufficiently central position in reality to accept love. [IM-V03-E082–IM-V03-E085]

This is a more severe structure because it converts a relative professional comparison into a condition of personhood. The public label “male colleague two years older” would not change his actual relationship with Nayuta, but he experiences the imagined wording as proof that he is not yet himself enough. [IT]

Haruto's objection introduces the needed anti-thesis: artistic rank is plural and audience-relative. [IM-V03-E088] The V01 doctrine “sales ≠ worth” now has a direct consequence: if sales cannot settle equality, then a creator who demands objective equality may never know when the demand has been satisfied.

### 8.3 Kyo and Haruto model alternative relationships to effort

Kyo's character sheet says she likes people who work hard and hates mocking them. [IM-V03-E063–IM-V03-E064] This is consistent with the empathy that helped Haruto survive V02. Her value judgment does not ask whether effort wins. It asks whether effort should be treated with dignity.

Haruto's TRPG revision similarly accepts imperfect prior performance without converting it into personal worthlessness. He identifies a design flaw and works on the system. [IM-V03-E059–IM-V03-E062] That is a materially healthier response to failure than the catastrophic collapse around the anime, although V03 cannot establish whether it generalizes across his career.

### 8.4 Nayuta's jealousy is productive evidence of peer formation

Nayuta's jealousy at discovering that Kyo's favorite author is Itsuki is not admirable simply because it is competitive. Its analytical importance is that the narrator marks it as the first time she perceives Itsuki as a professional in the same competitive world rather than only as the absolute object of worship. [IM-V03-E015–IM-V03-E016]

If sustained, that shift could reduce the verticality of the relationship. At V03, however, it is an opening rather than a completed maturation.

## 9. Family, siblinghood, and identity

### 9.1 V03 revises the sister-fixation model without closing it

The strongest correction to C005 is chronological. The adolescent bonus states that Itsuki already frequently preferred sister characters, complained about their structural relegation to sub-heroine/mascot roles, and increasingly refined that preference before Ayane's rejection. [IM-V03-E095–IM-V03-E097] Therefore the proposition “he likes sisters because an older girl rejected him” must be rejected as a monocausal account.

But the rejection matters greatly. Ayane does not merely say she likes someone else; she classifies Itsuki as a younger-brother-like person whom she cannot imagine as a man. [IM-V03-E102–IM-V03-E103] For a boy already lacking confidence, the category `弟` becomes the name of relational inferiority. The retrospective narrator explicitly frames his response as defense of cowardly pride. [IM-V03-E104]

His answer—`僕に姉は必要ありません` followed by `僕には──妹さえいればいい`—is therefore best read as a **defensive inversion layered onto a preexisting preference**. [IM-V03-E105–IM-V03-E106] It makes the sister ideal partly attractive because the imagined relation reverses the position in which he was placed: he no longer wants to be the cared-for younger one outside romantic equality; he fantasizes the role from which he can be central, desired, and protective.

That is a strong inference, not a complete diagnostic theory. V03 gives no license to explain every later sister joke through Ayane, and the preference's earlier existence must remain active counterevidence.

### 9.2 Chosen sisterhood pluralizes the term

Nayuta/Kyo provides a radically different use of sibling vocabulary. Nayuta calls Kyo older-sister-like because of concrete care. Itsuki's absurd enthusiasm then yields a serious relational proposition: blood is not required for someone to occupy the younger-sister position relative to an admired other. [IM-V03-E013–IM-V03-E014]

This matters because `妹` is becoming semantically heterogeneous inside the series. At least four modes are now present by V03:

1. literal family position;
2. aesthetic/erotic character category;
3. defensive fantasy structure;
4. chosen relational position organized by admiration and care. [LF/IT]

Future analysis should resist forcing all four into one essence.

### 9.3 Chihiro is material family before becoming legible as `妹`

The aquarium chapter establishes that Chihiro and Itsuki's bond was not automatic after remarriage. Itsuki initially withdrew from the household; living separately and Chihiro's visits later made relation possible. [IM-V03-E066–IM-V03-E068] The process involved exactly the kinds of ordinary practices V01–V02 have treated as social technology: games, reading, food, repeated visits. The result is explicit: Chihiro is precious family. [IM-V03-E069]

That makes the hidden-sister premise more than a reveal mechanism. Itsuki has already built sibling love without the category he fetishizes. The eventual category disclosure, whenever it occurs, therefore threatens to collide with a relationship whose material substance precedes correct classification.

### 9.4 Father-son estrangement cannot yet be reduced to villainy

The bonus supplies background: mother hospitalized, then dies; Keisuke, already busy, becomes even more absorbed in work. [IM-V03-E093–IM-V03-E094, IM-V03-E098] The present-day aquarium/home chapter shows Itsuki still refusing the family home and responding to Keisuke with hostility. Keisuke's thanks are flat and duty-like from the focalized description. [IM-V03-E071–IM-V03-E074]

What the volume does **not** provide is Keisuke's interior explanation. The correct V03 state is estrangement plus sparse failed contact, not “father is proven uncaring.” Chihiro's unsuccessful attempt to prolong the conversation shows that the family system contains at least one active mediator.

## 10. Sexuality, vulgar comedy, and boundaries

V03 continues the series' practice of placing serious relational information inside aggressively sexual or vulgar framing. The method therefore requires keeping event, framing, and ethical conclusion separate.

### 10.1 Setsuna: artistic appetite does not sanitize violation

Setsuna imagines seeing a particular girl's body as a path to artistic “level up.” [IM-V03-E026] Itsuki can understand why illustrator growth matters to future collaboration, but he also explicitly states that Setsuna's prior forced clothing removal was criminal. [IM-V03-E027–IM-V03-E028] The scene's comedy therefore coexists with unusually clear in-text boundary language. It would be inaccurate either to claim the violation is morally neutral because the scene is funny or to claim the entire chapter is simply condemnation.

### 10.2 Kyo/Nayuta: sexualized intimacy can contain real consent and refusal

At Kyo's birthday, erotic disclosure and affection escalate toward a possible kiss. Nayuta states willingness; Kyo does not take that as entitlement and stops. [IM-V03-E041] The scene remains playful and intimate, but its internal logic distinguishes desire from permission and permission from chosen action.

That is analytically useful because it prevents the project's sexual-comedy category from becoming undifferentiated. Some scenes obscure boundaries; some actively articulate them; some do both.

### 10.3 Chihiro/Ashley: fanservice packaging surrounds a substantive identity event

Ashley's discovery occurs through undressing/bath circumstances and is illustrated as fanservice. [IM-V03-E127] Yet the actual narrative result is that an outside adult learns the secret, agrees not to expose it immediately, and tells Chihiro indefinite concealment is untenable. [IM-V03-E056–IM-V03-E058]

The image's erotic framing is therefore a publication fact, not a substitute for the scene's identity/confidentiality function. V03 offers another strong example of why “sexualized illustration” and “the ethical/narrative meaning of the event” must be recorded as separate analytical layers.

### 10.4 Nayuta's obscene fiction is also a serious gift

The private manuscript's sexual extremity is converted by Kyo's reading into something she jokingly experiences as mythic. [IM-V03-E043] The front color plate spectacularizes that material before the reader even reaches the birthday chapter. [IM-V03-E120] But the same work is an act of singular care, a first experiment in fantasy, and the catalyst for Kyo's truth-telling. [IM-V03-E031–IM-V03-E040]

V03 thereby strengthens C007: vulgarity can be simultaneously commodified spectacle, creator signature, intimacy language, and cover for vulnerable emotional exchange.

## 11. Games, food, travel, and social ritual

### 11.1 The amusement park is observational technology

The park does not merely provide date scenery. It creates a shared physical environment in which compatibility becomes observable. Nayuta grabs Itsuki's hand; he keeps holding it; Kyo names them well matched; Haruto watches Kyo watch them. [IM-V03-E045–IM-V03-E051] The outing therefore transmits information that nobody had successfully communicated in direct conversation.

This continues C004: shared leisure is not filler around the “real” plot. It is one of the mechanisms by which the ensemble becomes legible to itself.

### 11.2 The zoo turns a gag into a compact autonomy statement

Nayuta jokes that zoo animals are “winners” because danger, food, medical care, and even mating can be administratively solved. [IM-V03-E052] Itsuki answers that he would still reject a safely domesticated life in favor of surviving through his own power in a harsh world. [IM-V03-E053]

The surrounding naked-animal joke limits how much philosophical architecture should be built on the scene. Still, it is unusually congruent with the protagonist thesis. Itsuki values self-direction even when external provision would be materially easier. [SI] That preference becomes admirable as autonomy and dangerous when converted into refusal of interdependence.

### 11.3 Once Upon a Time is a formal miniature of contested authorship

The card game is the volume's most self-reflexive social ritual. Players jointly produce a story, can seize the position of narrator when their cards match emerging elements, and each tries to redirect the shared fiction toward a private ending. [IM-V03-E075–IM-V03-E077] The fact that the writers produce a grotesque, meandering story they can barely finish is comic anti-romanticism: professional authorship does not magically create frictionless collaborative storytelling. [IM-V03-E078, IM-V03-E129]

The resonance with `主人公になりたい` is strong but should remain **FR/IT rather than literal allegory**. The game is about narrator control, not protagonist status. Its formal contribution is to place competing desired endings and unstable narrative authority directly before the conversation in which Itsuki explains his desire to occupy the central position in his own life.

### 11.4 Food and hospitality continue to materialize relationships

Kyo's birthday party, Chihiro's cooking, drinks after games, and aquarium food-gags maintain the series' ordinary-life infrastructure. The key change in V03 is that social ritual increasingly becomes a site of **gift exchange**: food, live animals, an autograph, a private novel, time, and attention all circulate without reducing to equivalent prices.

## 12. Fiction-within-fiction and intertext

### 12.1 The private Nayuta novel: fiction as one-person communication

Nayuta's Kyo-only fantasy is the clearest early counterexample to the idea that published audience reach is art's default purpose. It has a single intended reader and refuses publication despite obvious market value. [IM-V03-E031–IM-V03-E034, IM-V03-E042]

Its contents also demonstrate transformational rather than literal autobiography. Kyo is protagonist; Itsuki is encoded into the knight; Nayuta's erotic imagination pushes the narrative into absurd myth. [IM-V03-E043–IM-V03-E044] The work is therefore a relational composite rather than a disguised diary.

### 12.2 In-universe `妹さえいればいい！` becomes a transmission object

The bonus fiction gives an in-universe sister-centered novel a formative role. Young Itsuki recommends it to Ayane, and her enjoyment teaches him how powerful it feels to transmit a valued work successfully to another person. [IM-V03-E099–IM-V03-E100] Later, the same series' newcomer-award notice provides the route into professional authorship. [IM-V03-E111–IM-V03-E112]

Fiction here mediates both relationship and vocation. A book is first something that connects him to another person and later something that shows him a path by which he might become an active creator.

### 12.3 Writing as conversion rather than confession

The bonus's list of material to be thrown into fiction is remarkably broad: sister desire, longing, heartbreak, failure, inferiority, anger at self and world, hope, dreams, prayer, and the desire to be protagonist. [IM-V03-E113] This is stronger evidence than merely identifying one character as “based on” one real emotion. The proposed process is **conversion of heterogeneous affect into form**.

The final shift `僕が──いや、俺が主人公だ` makes grammar itself part of that conversion. [IM-V03-E114] The creator identity `羽島伊月` is narrated as something born through the act of claiming authorial agency. [IM-V03-E115]

### 12.4 The afterword reinforces permeability but sets a reality boundary

Hirasaka's afterword says the series' adult/non-school cast changes the scheduling affordances available to a romcom, notes that some character material draws partially from himself or real writers while the characters are basically not direct portraits, and treats most real-world works as coexisting in the fictional world. [IM-V03-E116–IM-V03-E118] It also corrects the `Once Upon a Time` rules and admits the played version is not exact rules documentation. [IM-V03-E119]

These are production/parataxis facts, not diegetic psychology. They are valuable because they caution against two opposite errors: assuming the work is a transparent roman à clef, or pretending its industry/game references have no contact with real practice.

## 13. Japanese voice, style, and terminology

### 13.1 `主人公` shifts from technical noun to existential predicate

The most important V03 term is `主人公` (shujinkō, protagonist/main character). In ordinary narrative vocabulary it names a formal role. Itsuki applies it to social reality: the desired condition is to be a protagonist rather than a `脇役` in a story defined around Nayuta. [IM-V03-E083–IM-V03-E085] The bonus fiction repeats the same mapping years earlier after Ayane's rejection. [IM-V03-E107–IM-V03-E109]

The repetition matters more than English abstraction such as “self-actualization.” `主人公` preserves the work's metanarrative register: Itsuki understands identity through the grammar of stories because stories are how he learned to organize desire and agency.

### 13.2 `同業者` is both professional fact and feared erasure

`同業者` (dōgyōsha, person in the same profession/industry) changes value by context. Nayuta's recognition of Itsuki as a fellow professional is growth toward horizontal peerhood. [IM-V03-E016] Itsuki's imagined newspaper phrase `２歳年上の同業者の男性`, however, is experienced as erasure because it removes his proper name. [IM-V03-E082–IM-V03-E083]

The same noun can therefore mean equality in one focal frame and anonymity in another. V03's status problem is not solvable by professional category membership alone.

### 13.3 `妹` / `姉` / `弟` become relational grammar

V03 produces three highly consequential uses:

- Nayuta: Kyo is `お姉ちゃん` because of enacted care and admiration. [IM-V03-E013]
- Itsuki: blood is unnecessary for the relational position he calls `妹`. [IM-V03-E014]
- Ayane: young Itsuki is `弟` and cannot be seen otherwise. [IM-V03-E102–IM-V03-E103]

The bonus's defensive exchange—`僕に姉は必要ありません` → `僕には──妹さえいればいい`—makes the opposition linguistically explicit. [IM-V03-E105–IM-V03-E106] The terms are not interchangeable kinship labels; they encode directionality of admiration, care, dependency, and perceived status.

### 13.4 `僕` → `俺` marks performed agency

Young Itsuki's narration culminates in `僕が──いや、俺が主人公だ`. [IM-V03-E114] The correction is not a general claim that `俺` is “more authentic” Japanese than `僕`. In this local scene it dramatizes a deliberate adoption of the more forceful self-position associated with the adult author persona. [LF/SI]

Future voice modeling should track whether adult Itsuki's `俺` grandiosity softens under vulnerability, whether adolescent `僕` recurs in memory or emotional regression, and how other characters address him across professional/private contexts.

### 13.5 Kyo's register performs then abandons adulthood

Kyo's confession about fabricated experience clarifies that sexual sophistication was partly a social persona rather than a transparent account of biography. [IM-V03-E036–IM-V03-E040] The adult-language layer should therefore distinguish what Kyo says to maintain group expectations from what emerges under trusted intimacy. V03 makes that discrepancy a character fact, not merely reader suspicion.

## 14. Illustration and publication-form analysis

### 14.1 Frontmatter sells the volume's contradictions before prose explains them

V03's front color plates are unusually programmatic. One transforms Nayuta's private erotic fantasy into cosmic, mythic sexual spectacle; another packages the zoo's animal-body joke; a third presents Itsuki and Chihiro together at the aquarium in warm blue light while retaining the “ingredient” gag. [IM-V03-E120–IM-V03-E122]

The trio anticipates the volume's three major registers: obscene creator imagination, social/leisure comedy, and ordinary family intimacy. Their coexistence warns against treating fanservice as a detachable layer with no relation to theme. The packaging often selects thematically central material—but selection and ethical endorsement remain different questions.

### 14.2 The monochrome illustrations increasingly privilege recognition moments

Kyo's blushing phone reaction, the birthday embrace, the amusement-park handholding, the father-son encounter, and the post-`主人公` creator scene all crystallize **moments of relational recognition** rather than only spectacle. [IM-V03-E123, IM-V03-E125–IM-V03-E130] V03 thus strengthens the visual ledger's hypothesis that illustrations do interpretive work by choosing which states deserve stillness and emphasis.

The amusement-park image is especially efficient. Itsuki/Nayuta occupy the central embodied action while the foreground perspective makes being observed part of the composition. [IM-V03-E126] It visually matches the prose's chain: pair compatibility → Kyo's response → Haruto's recognition.

### 14.3 Chihiro's disclosure is simultaneously consequential and commodified

`V03-ILL-019` eroticizes Ashley's accidental discovery of Chihiro's sex through bath/undressing framing. [IM-V03-E127] That fact belongs in the visual record because the publication package chooses to turn a major identity disclosure into fanservice. But the image cannot replace the surrounding prose, which makes confidentiality and future disclosure the durable narrative consequence.

### 14.4 The bonus illustration anchors the backstory as a relationship, not merely an explanation

`V03-ILL-028` shows young Itsuki with Ayane in the maid-costume episode. [IM-V03-E131] The choice matters because the backstory is not presented only as abstract trauma exposition. It first constructs fascination, shared media talk, awkward care, and adolescent attraction before the rejection. That prevents a reductive “single bad event explains him” reading even at the visual level.

## 15. Counterevidence and anti-theses

### 15.1 “Itsuki refuses Nayuta because he does not really love her”

**REJECT after V03.** He explicitly states love, desire to date, exclusivity, and desire to marry. [IM-V03-E079–IM-V03-E081] What remains open is whether his status condition is ethically/psychologically sustainable.

### 15.2 “Ayane caused the sister fixation”

**REJECT as a monocausal formulation.** Sister preference and frustration with sister-character narrative status predate Ayane. [IM-V03-E095–IM-V03-E097] The stronger formulation is that rejection as `弟` substantially intensifies and defensively reorganizes an existing preference. [IM-V03-E102–IM-V03-E110]

### 15.3 “The protagonist thesis is simply the author's endorsement of ambition”

**Too simple.** Haruto understands the desire but immediately attacks the absence of an objective equality metric. [IM-V03-E086–IM-V03-E090] The text gives the drive dignity and counterargument in the same scene.

### 15.4 “Nayuta only creates for Itsuki”

**Contradicted by V03.** She writes a book-length private novel for Kyo and refuses commercialization. [IM-V03-E031–IM-V03-E034] Itsuki remains encoded inside it, so the evidence does not prove full independence from his imaginative gravity; it does prove that her creative address can be directed toward someone else.

### 15.5 “Kyo/Nayuta intimacy proves reciprocal romance”

**Not supported.** The relationship is physically intimate, emotionally deep, and explicitly sister-coded. Kyo declines the kiss because she recognizes Nayuta's orientation toward Itsuki. [IM-V03-E013, IM-V03-E039–IM-V03-E041] Romance/orientation beyond that remains unestablished.

### 15.6 “Haruto's interest in Kyo is now a mutual romantic route”

**DOWNGRADE.** Haruto's interest continues, but he recognizes Kyo's love for Itsuki and withdraws matchmaking help because it would be cruel to her. [IM-V03-E048–IM-V03-E051] No reciprocal Kyo→Haruto attraction is established in V03.

### 15.7 “Keisuke's cold manner proves he does not care about Itsuki”

**Not established.** The focalized encounter is emotionally cold and the thanks is duty-like, but Keisuke's interiority is absent. [IM-V03-E071–IM-V03-E074] Estrangement is fact; total indifference is inference beyond the evidence.

### 15.8 “Art has one proper value system: market or purity”

**Rejected by the volume's own contrasts.** Synopsis writing and anime promotion are market-facing craft; Haruto's audience pleasure is sincere; Nayuta's private novel is deliberately nonmarket; Itsuki's writing begins as conversion of private affect into public vocation. [IM-V03-E017–IM-V03-E025, IM-V03-E031–IM-V03-E034, IM-V03-E059–IM-V03-E062, IM-V03-E111–IM-V03-E115]

## 16. Cumulative delta — V01 to V03

### 16.1 New V03 claims

`IM-C014` — **OPEN (new): Protagonist status is a recurring selfhood technology.** For Itsuki, “being the protagonist” names the desire to be an agent with a non-derivative identity; authorship originally converts peripheral adolescent experience into self-authored agency, while adult comparison risks turning the same drive into an endless status test. Primary evidence: IM-V03-E082–IM-V03-E090, IM-V03-E102–IM-V03-E115.

`IM-C015` — **OPEN (new): Sibling terms can encode chosen relational positions.** `姉`/`妹` operate not only as blood/legal family categories or fetish tropes but as directional relations of admiration, care, following, and protection. Primary evidence: IM-V03-E013–IM-V03-E014, contrasted with IM-V03-E102–IM-V03-E106.

`IM-C016` — **OPEN (new): Art can function as a private relational gift distinct from market commodity.** Nayuta's Kyo-only manuscript derives part of its value from restricted address and nonpublication despite obvious commercial value. Primary evidence: IM-V03-E031–IM-V03-E044.

## 17. Open questions after V03

### 17.1 Existing questions under pressure

- **IM-Q001 — origin/function of the sister fixation:** **REVISE / PARTIALLY RESOLVED.** Preexisting aesthetic preference is established; Ayane's `弟` rejection demonstrably intensifies and defensively reorganizes it. Mother's death and other causal layers must not be invented beyond the text. [IM-V03-E093–IM-V03-E110]
- **IM-Q002 / IM-Q003 — Chihiro's concealment:** stronger. The secret is now shown to be Itsuki-specific within the family, and Chihiro accepts eventual disclosure. The reason for maintaining the arrangement and its planned timing remain OPEN. [IM-V03-E056–IM-V03-E058]
- **IM-Q004 — what does Chihiro want beyond usefulness?** Family love and independent paid skill are stronger; romantic interpretation remains unproven. [IM-V03-E054–IM-V03-E058, IM-V03-E066–IM-V03-E070]
- **IM-Q005 / IM-Q006 — family-home history and Keisuke:** **STRENGTHEN / PARTIAL.** Mother's illness/death, father's work absorption, remarriage withdrawal, and current coldness are now direct evidence; Keisuke's interior motives remain OPEN. [IM-V03-E066, IM-V03-E071–IM-V03-E074, IM-V03-E093–IM-V03-E098]
- **IM-Q009 — Nayuta beyond Itsuki:** **STRENGTHEN toward autonomy.** She writes privately for Kyo and begins to see Itsuki as professional peer/rival, while he remains encoded in her fantasy. [IM-V03-E013–IM-V03-E016, IM-V03-E031–IM-V03-E044]
- **IM-Q010 — Kyo self-permission:** **STRENGTHEN.** She abandons a performed adult/sexual identity and receives affection unchanged; vocational self-direction remains OPEN. [IM-V03-E035–IM-V03-E040]
- **IM-Q012 — artist/craftsman vocabulary:** **STRENGTHEN revision.** Haruto's audience pleasure and synopsis craft further undermine any authentic/inauthentic binary. [IM-V03-E017–IM-V03-E025, IM-V03-E059–IM-V03-E065]
- **IM-Q021 — adulthood:** **STRENGTHEN.** Twenty-one does not feel like categorical adulthood to Kyo; Chihiro's outside work adds another competence axis. [IM-V03-E035, IM-V03-E054–IM-V03-E055]
- **IM-Q022 — Haruto after adaptation failure:** **STRENGTHEN toward integration.** TRPG design reconnects him to audience pleasure; longer durability remains OPEN. [IM-V03-E059–IM-V03-E065]
- **IM-Q024 — Chihiro's bookkeeping:** **STRENGTHEN.** Interest becomes real employment and training rather than hypothetical usefulness. [IM-V03-E054–IM-V03-E055]
- **IM-Q025 — Haruto/Kyo reciprocity:** **DOWNGRADE.** Haruto identifies Kyo's love for Itsuki and withdraws matchmaking help; no Kyo→Haruto romance is established. [IM-V03-E048–IM-V03-E051]

### 17.2 New V03 questions

1. **IM-Q028:** What criterion, if any, can Itsuki accept as sufficient for being an “equal protagonist” beside Nayuta, given Haruto's demonstration that no objective artistic ranking exists?
2. **IM-Q029:** Can Itsuki preserve the productive self-authoring function of `主人公になりたい` without making love contingent on public status?
3. **IM-Q030:** What does Nayuta understand or decide after overhearing Itsuki's confession/status condition? V03 gives visible emotional reaction but not a full interior statement. [IM-V03-E091]
4. **IM-Q031:** How will the series negotiate the multiple meanings of `妹`—literal kinship, erotic/aesthetic trope, defensive inversion, and chosen relational position—once they collide more directly?
5. **IM-Q032:** Does Nayuta's first explicit colleague/rival recognition develop into a stable horizontal professional relation with Itsuki?
6. **IM-Q033:** Can Kyo's successful truth-telling in intimacy generalize into vocational self-authorization rather than remaining local to friendship?
7. **IM-Q034:** Is private gift-art a recurring alternative value mode, or a one-volume exception enabled by Nayuta's unusual productivity?
8. **IM-Q035:** Can Chihiro act as a bridge between Itsuki and Keisuke, or does that mediator position become a burden Chihiro cannot solve?

## 19. V03 end-state

### 19.1 Character end-state

- **羽島伊月:** romantic desire for Nayuta is explicit and strong; refusal is now tied to public/professional/narrative self-position rather than lack of feeling. `主人公になりたい` is shown as a pre-Nayuta drive linked to adolescent rejection and the birth of his creator identity. Sister fixation is materially revised: prior preference + later defensive intensification, not a single-cause trauma explanation.
- **可児那由多:** attachment to Itsuki remains intense, but V03 broadens her relational and professional agency. Kyo is explicitly older-sister-like; Nayuta produces a major private work exclusively for her; jealousy generates first recognition of Itsuki as colleague/rival. Her reaction to overhearing Itsuki's confession is visible but not yet verbally resolved.
- **白川京:** romantic attachment to Itsuki becomes externally legible to Haruto. More importantly, she successfully abandons a performed sexual-adult persona with Nayuta and receives unchanged affection, offering a local counterexample to her self-disqualification pattern. Vocational self-direction remains unresolved.
- **不破春斗:** V02's artistic collapse gains a constructive successor: audience pleasure becomes an explicit creative origin. He understands Itsuki's protagonist hunger but supplies the strongest internal critique of its impossible equality metric. His interest in Kyo persists, while probability of reciprocity is downgraded by recognition of her love for Itsuki.
- **羽島千尋:** paid work and bookkeeping training expand competence outside the apartment. The gender secret becomes an explicit future-disclosure obligation after Ashley discovers it. The aquarium chapter establishes Chihiro as precious family to Itsuki while simultaneously showing that sibling closeness has not repaired the father-son rupture.
- **土岐健次郎:** flexible task allocation and the anime announcement continue to position him as interface between authorial aspiration and publishing institution.
- **恵那刹那:** artistic hunger remains valued as future collaborative potential, while Itsuki's explicit `犯罪` language keeps the prior nonconsensual conduct visible beneath slapstick.
- **大野アシュリー:** moves from creator-finance specialist to Chihiro's employer and holder of a consequential family/identity secret.
- **羽島啓輔:** enters direct present-time relation as an estranged father capable of minimal acknowledgment; interior motives and full history remain open.
- **三田洞彩音:** bonus-fiction figure now directly anchors the adolescent rejection through which `弟`, `姉`, `妹`, and `主人公` become linked in Itsuki's self-narrative. She is not sufficient cause of the preexisting sister preference.

### 19.2 Thematic end-state

After three volumes, the project can no longer describe the series simply as “creative adults whose work and relationships overlap.” V03 identifies a more precise conflict: **people live inside relationships and systems they do not control, but they also hunger to experience themselves as authors rather than supporting characters.** Itsuki embodies the most extreme version of that hunger. The same drive that helped an isolated adolescent turn grief, rejection, inferiority, and fantasy into a vocation now risks making reciprocal love wait upon an impossible proof of equality.

The volume's counterweights are all relational. Nayuta writes a book for one person rather than a market. Kyo tells the truth because she has received a gift that makes performance intolerable. Haruto recognizes Kyo's desire and refuses an efficient but cruel romantic strategy. Chihiro builds real siblinghood before the correct kinship label is known. Games distribute narrative control. Toki allocates work pragmatically. Readers determine incompatible rankings of artistic value. Even the word `妹` escapes Itsuki's attempt to own it, becoming a chosen position inside somebody else's relation.

V03 therefore does not abolish the protagonist ideal. It asks the first serious question of it: **can a person become the protagonist of his own life without requiring everyone and everything around him to function as proof that he has won the role?** [IT]
