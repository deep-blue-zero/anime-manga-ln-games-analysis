# 妹さえいればいい。 Volume 12 Deep Reading

## 0. Authority, method, and prospective boundary

This is the canonical prospective deep reading of Volume 12. It inherits the three frozen checkpoints through Volume 09 and the canonical V10-V11 sequential readings. Volume 12 is the **only new semantic evidence** admitted here. No event, explanation, relationship state, or later retrospective from Volumes 13-14 is used to settle the V12 endpoint.

This boundary is especially important because V12 completes the crisis sequence begun in V10 and therefore invites teleological back-reading. The present reading must instead preserve the actual order of discovery: V10 breaks Itsuki's old imaginative position; V11 demonstrates that he can regain professional output through technique while remaining alienated from the writer-self Nayuta recognizes; V12 first confirms that this technical recovery was genuinely a recovery from writer's block, then asks whether and how a different form of creative, relational, and ethical continuity can be deliberately authored.

The Japanese EPUB is ultimate textual authority. `IMOSAE_V12_NORMALIZED_SOURCE.md` is the canonical retrieval derivative. `IMOSAE-VISUAL-INDEX-1.0` governs visual locators. The author afterword is PF, not sovereign narrative interpretation, but V12 contains unusually important afterword evidence because Hirasaka explicitly corrects a plausible reader inference about V11's writer's-block resolution. That PF can clarify authorial design without erasing the narrative's own plurality of values.

## 1. Governing thesis: `正解のない道` — continuation after false binaries

Volume 12's governing idea is stated most directly in the chapter title `正解のない道`: **there is no single correct path**.

That does not mean the volume collapses into relativism. It repeatedly makes strong judgments about bad choices, self-neglect, coercion, evasion, dishonesty, and failures to respect another person's agency. What it rejects is the idea that one morally or artistically pure mode can solve every rupture.

The V10-V12 sequence has accumulated a series of apparent binaries:

- passionate artist versus disciplined craftsman;
- `主人公` versus ordinary person;
- vocational identity versus work as merely a job;
- waiting gently versus confronting forcefully;
- protecting another person versus respecting that person's choice;
- self-authorship versus dependence on other people;
- romantic attachment versus autonomous vocation;
- lived truth versus fictional transformation;
- stopping/resting versus abandoning one's story.

V12 refuses to choose one side as universally correct. Instead it asks a more demanding question:

> **Can a person re-own a commitment after rupture—knowing what they are doing, for whom, and why—without pretending that the mode they choose is universally superior?**

That is why Itsuki's V11 technique-only writing must be reclassified carefully. The afterword explicitly says that the interpretation “Itsuki never escaped the slump” is mistaken. He **did** escape the writer's block in V11. Writing without personal feeling is neither inherently wrong nor artistically inferior; creators capable of doing so can be extremely skilled; for the majority of in-world readers and the publisher, Itsuki's change was arguably desirable. Passion-driven sharp work and carefully calculated broad-access work cannot be ranked absolutely. [IM-V12-E543-IM-V12-E546]

The V12 resolution therefore cannot be “Itsuki finally abandons fake craftsmanship and becomes a true artist again.” That would contradict both the main text and the afterword.

What changes in `蘇生` is **task and purpose**. Itsuki writes an unpublished work for exactly one reader. For that relational act, neither the old uncontrolled passion nor the V11 technique-only mode is sufficient by itself. The narration names the synthesis `芸術家の魂と、職人の技術` and describes love as the `乳化剤` that forcibly mixes two tendencies that normally repel one another. [IM-V12-E479-IM-V12-E492]

The hybrid is ideal **for this particular communicative problem**: telling Nayuta what he cannot adequately say otherwise. It is not established as a universal hierarchy of art.

The same logic governs the rest of the volume. Kyo learns that being a good friend to Nayuta and being a good editor may sometimes require different acts. Miku's harsh intervention succeeds where Kyo's acceptance does not, but the chapter title itself refuses to canonize harshness as the one correct method. Kaizu's ordinary-writer ideal is admirable as a refusal of prestige teleology yet becomes self-sacrificial when he unilaterally decides nobody should share his precarious future. Ashley does not “rescue” him by making him more successful; she chooses the burden with full knowledge and adds practical infrastructure. Shiori's name turns rest into a bookmark: a pause that preserves the place from which one may continue.

The V12 synthesis is therefore:

> **Mature continuation is not purity of mode. It is the capacity to re-author a commitment after rupture, distinguishing chosen purpose from defensive evasion and reciprocal interdependence from imposed dependence. Different creative, relational, and professional modes can be legitimate when the person owns the reason for them and respects the agency of the other people who bear their consequences.**

## 2. Volume architecture: breakup, failed cures, competing models, and re-authored continuation

V12 is structurally a recovery mosaic rather than a straight reconciliation plot.

### 2.1 `破局` and `夢追い人のなれの果て`: the breakup becomes material

The opening converts V11's terminal dialogue into an actual relationship rupture. Itsuki does not immediately reinterpret the fight as temporary. He drinks heavily, neglects himself, and returns to the old `天才`/`凡人` comparison: Nayuta was selected by the age; he is an ordinary man who should never have been beside her. [IM-V12-E001-IM-V12-E051]

Haruto refuses the self-pity. He reminds Itsuki of the `主人公` ambition and, more importantly, demonstrates forward motion by announcing his own robot-project goal. He will not remain in place merely because Itsuki and Nayuta have stopped. The rivalry that once supported Itsuki's exceptionalist scorekeeping is thus transformed into a contrast between stagnation and chosen movement.

### 2.2 `笠松青葉ルート（罠）`: bottoming out and boundary recovery

Aoba's care initially becomes dangerous because gratitude and admiration make her willing to offer far too much of herself. The volume stages an exaggerated alternate-route/nightmare sequence that demonstrates how catastrophic a sexualized boundary collapse with the seventeen-year-old could become. The sequence is not an actual sexual encounter and must not be entered into the relationship ledger as one. [IM-V12-E052-IM-V12-E077]

When awake, Itsuki refuses the offer explicitly: the situation is unlawful, `駄目なものは駄目だ`, and Aoba should value herself more. [IM-V12-E063-IM-V12-E066]

This is a small but important recovery of ethical agency. Itsuki recognizes that being broken, ordinary, or unsuccessful does not license becoming someone who consumes a vulnerable younger person's gratitude.

He then overcorrects. Alcohol is poured away and he remakes himself as an ascetic writing machine, treating desire itself as contamination. The result is commercially effective but psychologically rigid. V12 thus rejects both self-indulgent collapse and purity-through-amputation as complete solutions.

### 2.3 `グレる` and `ミッションインポッシブル`: Nayuta's collapse exposes relationally dependent vocation

Nayuta's post-breakup collapse parallels Itsuki's without duplicating it. She games compulsively, neglects food and sleep, abandons grooming, and cuts her long silver hair because maintaining it no longer seems to have a reason. [IM-V12-E078-IM-V12-E101]

More consequentially, she explains that writing itself had been organized around Itsuki: she began to meet him, continued to stay beside him, and worked on non-novel media-mix duties so he would keep looking at her. With the relationship gone, she says the reason to try has disappeared and announces `私、作家やめます`. [IM-V12-E102-IM-V12-E125]

This is the strongest counterpressure yet on an overly optimistic version of `IM-C035`. A vocation can begin relationally and become self-authored, as Kyo and Ayane demonstrate. But V12 shows that this transition is not automatic. Nayuta's writing identity still contains a dependency so deep that removing Itsuki initially collapses the rationale for the work.

The rest of V12 does not simply prove that Nayuta “actually loved writing all along.” It broadens the motivational ecology around her: Miku's anger, reader effects, film work, Yuma's interview project, creative competitiveness, and an expanding social world all become new reasons to engage. Itsuki remains central, but he ceases to be the only conceivable bridge between Nayuta and the world.

## 3. Kyo's editorial education: the difference between caring for a person and stewarding a vocation

Kyo receives one of the most important professional corrections in the series.

### 3.1 The first ethical instinct is restraint

In `春斗と京`, Kyo correctly distinguishes Itsuki's old `書けない` from Nayuta's present `書かない`. Nayuta is making a choice, however distressed the choice may be. [IM-V12-E126-IM-V12-E149]

Kyo therefore resists the publisher's temptation to fix the romance for business reasons. Love is intensely private; a couple separated after colliding heart-to-heart; `会社が困るから` does not obviously give an editor the right to intrude. This is a strong continuation of `IM-C023`: institutional stake is not equivalent to authority over intimate life.

She also tells Haruto that she is over Itsuki. Yet the narration immediately complicates the declaration: Kyo is partly using the clarity of a future career to avoid confronting unresolved feelings toward both Itsuki and Haruto. Her vocational choice remains real; its psychological use as shelter is also real.

### 3.2 Kyo's first persuasion fails because it speaks institutionally rather than personally

In `説得`, Kyo tells Nayuta that publishers, adaptation staff, and readers need her. Nayuta asks the question that breaks the argument: does **Kyo herself** need Nayuta's novels? [IM-V12-E150-IM-V12-E177]

Kyo likes the books and cares deeply about the person, but cannot honestly claim the works are existentially necessary to her in the same way they may be to fervent readers or creators shaped by them. She realizes the persuasion was `仕事`—professionally rational but lacking personal `心`.

When Nayuta later disappears without her phone, Kyo's fear becomes explicitly relational rather than productive. After Nayuta returns, Kyo says she can keep gaming and even stop writing; what Kyo cannot bear is her disappearing without saying anything. Kyo then tells Kobe that forcing someone to write against her will feels wrong and that they may simply have to wait. [IM-V12-E167-IM-V12-E177]

This is ethically serious care. It is not, however, the endpoint of Kyo's editorial development.

### 3.3 `正解のない道`: Miku attacks the hidden equation “kindness = best care”

Itsuki's V7 is commercially successful and receives a revealing split reception. Anime-acquired readers often prefer its accessibility, setup/payoff discipline, relatability, and reduced eccentricity. Long-time readers notice less of the old wildness but many still evaluate the volume positively. Kyo herself thinks it is `普通に面白い` and perhaps prefers it to the earlier books. [IM-V12-E311-IM-V12-E344]

Miku then supplies the volume's key pluralist proposition: she respects passionate creators and calculated craftsman-type creators alike; `作り手の在り方に、絶対的に正しいただ一つの答えなどありません`. [IM-V12-E318]

Nayuta's claim that a true fan should reject Itsuki's wrong path is therefore challenged from inside fandom and creation rather than by anti-art cynicism.

Miku's next move is deliberately harsher. As a creator, she says, a person making something—even a terrible work—is vastly more admirable than a creator who will neither make nor try to make anything. The insult is excessive and comic, but it successfully enrages Nayuta back into writing. [IM-V12-E321-IM-V12-E329]

Kyo objects to the cruelty. Miku replies that kindness alone solves everything only in a harem romantic comedy and says she would not want an editor who functions merely as an endlessly affirming mother. Kyo then rethinks her old ideal of the editor who `作家と一緒に泣く`: the editor is neither mother nor ordinary friend, and professional stewardship may sometimes require being disliked. [IM-V12-E330-IM-V12-E337]

This does **not** establish “editors should coerce creators.” The entire chapter is called `正解のない道`. Kyo's earlier authority objection remains valid; Miku's intervention happens to work in this case; Nayuta's agency remains the thing that converts anger into writing.

The more defensible revision to `IM-C023` is:

> **Care is role-sensitive. The act that appropriately preserves someone as friend, lover, or family member may differ from the act justified by a professional stewardship role. Neither maximal gentleness nor maximal force is universally ethical; intervention must be judged through role, consent, power, stakes, reversibility, and whether the person's agency is preserved rather than replaced.**

## 4. Itsuki: from exceptionalist collapse to task-specific integration

### 4.1 V12 confirms the V11 recovery was real

The main text already describes Itsuki as having escaped the hellish slump before the breakup. The afterword makes the point explicit: readers who thought he had never exited the slump were mistaken. [IM-V12-E025-IM-V12-E051] [IM-V12-E543-IM-V12-E546]

That forces a revision of `IM-C038`.

V11's distinction between function and identity remains useful, but “identity recovery” cannot be treated as the hidden true recovery that invalidates technical recovery. Itsuki had genuinely regained the ability to work. The problem was that this form of recovery did not preserve the particular self-relation and relational meaning that Nayuta valued, and that Itsuki later decided he wanted to recover differently.

A stronger formulation is:

> **Functional recovery and integrated continuity with prior values/identity are distinct dimensions. Functional recovery can be completely real even when self-recognition or relational continuity remains contested; conversely, a personally meaningful identity does not by itself guarantee functional capacity. Neither dimension automatically invalidates the other.**

### 4.2 The “monk” phase demonstrates that discipline can become another avoidance strategy

After the Aoba episode, Itsuki remakes himself into an ascetic caricature: shaved appearance, ritualized abstention, and writing as duty. The comedy matters because it shows how easy it is to turn a legitimate technique—discipline—into a fantasy of purifying the self by eliminating desire.

This mode produces work. It is not another writer's block. What remains unresolved is why Itsuki is choosing the mode and whether it represents a life he actually wants.

### 4.3 Jealousy returns before entitlement does

Across Christmas, New Year, and the Yuma material, Itsuki continues to react to the possibility that Nayuta might move on. He often tries to deny himself even the right to feel jealousy because he has “given up” being the `主人公`. [IM-V12-E225-IM-V12-E310] [IM-V12-E393-IM-V12-E427]

That is another misuse of the protagonist idea. Respecting Nayuta's autonomy does require accepting that she may choose someone else. It does not require pretending one's own desire no longer exists.

Yuma becomes important precisely because he is not an obviously inferior rival who can be dismissed. Itsuki sees him as protagonist-like: talented, attractive, serious, socially capable, and professionally legitimate. If Nayuta chose Yuma, external scorekeeping would give Itsuki no easy superiority claim.

The resolution therefore cannot be won by restoring Itsuki's old comparative status hierarchy.

## 5. Nayuta: from one-person vocation toward a larger world without losing one-person love

### 5.1 `私、作家やめます` is real evidence, not melodramatic noise

Nayuta explicitly grounds her career in Itsuki. This must be allowed to revise the corpus rather than being dismissed because later she writes again. [IM-V12-E102-IM-V12-E125]

Her authorial identity was more relationally dependent than Kyo's or Ayane's. The breakup removes not only a lover but the organizing telos through which she had interpreted writing, adaptation work, effort, and recognition.

### 5.2 Reader need is insufficient as a command

Kyo's failed persuasion proves that “many people need your work” cannot by itself compel a creator. Nayuta did not originally write for those readers, and generalized demand does not substitute automatically for the specific relational reason she lost. [IM-V12-E150-IM-V12-E177]

This is a major strengthening of `IM-C039`. Creative influence creates real stakes; it does not create unlimited ownership.

### 5.3 Miku reopens creation through conflict rather than duty

Miku does not successfully argue that fans own Nayuta. She attacks the contradiction between condemning Itsuki's active creative choice and refusing to create anything herself. Nayuta's anger becomes generative and she starts writing. [IM-V12-E311-IM-V12-E344]

The motive is not saintly service to readers. It is competitiveness, fury, pride, and the urge to prove herself through work. V12 thereby broadens legitimate creative motivation instead of replacing one pure motive with another.

### 5.4 Yuma expands Nayuta's world beyond Itsuki

The interview with Takashina Yuma matters professionally before it matters romantically. Yuma explains that `銀色景色` taught him to attend not only to the character he himself played but to other characters' stories, and that this habit expanded toward curiosity about real people outside his assigned role. [IM-V12-E345-IM-V12-E374]

Nayuta admits a gap between effect and conscious method. She has comparatively little life experience and does not systematically construct every psychological background that readers/actors can find in the work. Much emerges intuitively. This is another limit on creator self-knowledge: a creator can produce effects she cannot fully reverse-engineer.

The professional/interpersonal field around her thus widens. Itsuki is no longer literally the only person through whom her work can touch another life.

### 5.5 Yuma's confession tests whether expanded possibility displaces actual history

In `新しい恋`, Yuma offers a serious alternative. He chooses a place around Nayuta's tastes, talks about his own relation to work, and eventually confesses. [IM-V12-E393-IM-V12-E427]

His work philosophy is itself a correction to simplistic passion discourse. He says he acts because he likes acting and could imagine quitting if he truly ceased to like it. Nayuta initially tries to use this as proof against Itsuki's V11 mode. Yuma complicates the logic: if someone continues clinging to a job after love, passion, or pride disappears, that person may have a reason more important than those things. Work is not necessarily the whole life.

Then Nayuta rejects him.

The rejection is important because it occurs **after** her world has begun expanding and because Yuma has many externally desirable traits. Nayuta does not claim Itsuki is objectively superior. She says that despite his flaws, he is `最高のヒーロー`, the one person she loves, and that she loves him with her whole soul; she cannot imagine a future with anyone else. [IM-V12-E413-IM-V12-E420]

This decisively weakens any interpretation that her bond with Itsuki is merely an effect of professional parity or lack of alternatives. Contingency still matters—she acknowledges that meeting order could have mattered—but actual history is itself ethically and psychologically constitutive. The person one became through a relationship cannot be reduced to a hypothetical optimal partner ranking.

## 6. Kaizu and Ashley: ordinary survival becomes sustainable only when protection stops denying the other's agency

The Kaizu/Ashley sequence is the volume's strongest counterpoint to the central couple.

Kaizu explains that after Sekigahara Yuu's death he made a vow: remain a mediocre, ordinary writer in the industry, keep producing unglamorous work, and thereby show more talented and sensitive juniors that even someone like him can survive and that they can live more lightly. [IM-V12-E196-IM-V12-E224]

This deepens `IM-C030`. Kaizu's moderate telos is not laziness; it is an anti-suicidal cultural intervention. But the very seriousness of the vow creates a paradox. He calls the intended message `お気軽に`, while the narration recognizes a severe will behind it. He expects the career may become a slow death and refuses marriage because he will not make another person accompany such a risky life.

Ashley sees the hidden self-sacrifice. Her response is not to demand Kaizu become ambitious. She tells him to marry her, says she is uniquely willing to accompany him “to hell,” and immediately translates emotional commitment into material infrastructure: as a skilled tax professional, she will design finances so he can survive even if he cannot publish a book for ten years. [IM-V12-E206-IM-V12-E224]

The key ethical point is **chosen burden-sharing**.

Kaizu believed refusing partnership protected a hypothetical spouse from his precarious life. But once Ashley understands the risk and voluntarily chooses it, continued unilateral exclusion would also deny her agency.

This opens `IM-C040`:

> **Self-authorship is compatible with reciprocal interdependence when burdens are disclosed and knowingly chosen rather than silently assigned. Unilateral exclusion “for the other person's good” can become another form of control if it refuses a competent person's right to choose shared risk.**

This claim should remain OPEN until later recurrence, but it has strong backward resonance with roomshare reciprocity, creator-care infrastructure, and family burden-sharing.

## 7. Shiori: rest as continuity rather than surrender

The chapter `栞` supplies the V10-V12 block's most compact symbolic answer to the false choice between relentless forward motion and abandoning one's story.

Itsuki returns home during Natsume's labor because Chihiro is anxious. He deliberately does not use alcohol to calm himself while Chihiro must remain equally anxious; they wait together. The family practice established after disclosure is therefore now available during a real crisis. [IM-V12-E428-IM-V12-E478]

After the safe birth, Itsuki and Chihiro celebrate the baby as `俺たち二人の妹`. The category that once destroyed Itsuki's old fantasy now becomes ordinary family joy.

At the hospital, Itsuki tells Natsume to call him Itsuki and addresses her as `お母さん`. This is the strongest direct maternal acceptance yet. Keisuke and Natsume then ask him to name the child.

The name emerges from a bookmark in one of Itsuki's books: `栞`.

Itsuki explains the image as something that waits when one becomes tired of advancing a story, preserving a place and serving as a guide so that one can rest and later walk again. The wish is that Shiori become such a presence for the family. [IM-V12-E448-IM-V12-E468]

This symbol revises both `IM-C014` and `IM-C030`.

A protagonist need not prove agency by constant forward motion. Pausing is not necessarily background-character surrender. A livable story requires places from which one can stop without losing continuity.

The family scene also changes Itsuki's future imagination. Seeing a household he can now belong to makes him want to build a family of his own someday, and there is only one person he can imagine in that future: Nayuta.

The decisive shift is not that family life replaces artistic ambition. It makes the future concrete enough that romantic desire can no longer be anesthetized by protagonist rhetoric or professional scorekeeping.

## 8. `蘇生`: one-reader fiction as communication rather than scorekeeping

The final chapter resolves the immediate crisis by changing the communicative problem.

After learning that Yuma confessed and Nayuta rejected him, Itsuki's suppressed desire becomes impossible to treat as irrelevant. When Kyo asks what he intends to do, his answer is simply `待ってろ`. He does not immediately negotiate status, sales, genius parity, or whether he has become objectively worthy. He writes. [IM-V12-E479-IM-V12-E488]

### 8.1 The audience changes from market to one specific person

The new work is untitled and not written for publication. Itsuki has written from the beginning for contests and eventually for an indefinite public. This is the first time he writes a novel for exactly one reader: Nayuta. [IM-V12-E496-IM-V12-E510]

This is the strongest recurrence yet of `IM-C016`, the V03 claim that art can function as a private relational gift distinct from market commodity. The manuscript's value is partly produced by restricted address. Itsuki tells Nayuta that it was written only for her and would be meaningless otherwise. [IM-V12-E510]

The act is not anti-market in general. The series has spent twelve volumes treating publishing as legitimate adult work. Here, however, the task is not market success. The task is to communicate a relational truth that ordinary conversation failed to carry.

### 8.2 Autobiography returns as transformation, not transcript

The novel is a romance about an ordinary male writer and a younger genius female novelist. It is plainly autobiographical in source material. Yet Itsuki later defends the use of embellishment because it is both `私小説` and entertainment. Nayuta immediately objects to the invented/distorted rival material involving a Yuma-like character. [IM-V12-E520-IM-V12-E530]

This further strengthens `IM-C006`: autobiographical fiction is neither opaque nor transparent. The source relation can be obvious while selection, arrangement, exaggeration, and invention remain constitutive of the work.

### 8.3 Passion and technique are combined because this task requires both

The narration names the two resources directly: `芸術家の魂と、職人の技術`. Itsuki's earlier passionate drive and the professional technique sharpened by the V11 mechanical-writing phase are both retained. Love is described as an emulsifier mixing forces that tend to separate. [IM-V12-E489-IM-V12-E495]

This is not a repudiation of V11 craftsmanship. It is an appropriation of it.

The two months of technique-only writing were not wasted or fake. They become one component of a new authorial mode. This is a crucial strengthening of `IM-C018`: transformed grammar can be integrated into an existing self rather than simply replacing the old self.

The volume also deliberately makes the process excessive. Itsuki produces roughly a novel-length manuscript in days and nearly collapses. The emotional effectiveness of the act does **not** establish that such overwork is healthy or sustainable. `IM-C019`/`IM-C032` continue to require capacity analysis even when heroic-looking output succeeds.

### 8.4 The manuscript succeeds because the addressed reader recognizes the address

Nayuta reads while crying and calls it the best of Itsuki's works. Itsuki says the novel contains all of his present feelings toward her. He asks her to become his `メインヒロイン` again; Nayuta answers that Itsuki has always been and will remain her `主人公`. [IM-V12-E505-IM-V12-E519]

The reconciliation matters because it is achieved through **specific communication**, not restored professional parity.

Yuma has already provided a credible alternative. Itsuki has already achieved commercial success. Neither fact decides the relationship. What changes is that Itsuki stops treating love as something he may feel only after passing a comparative score and instead takes responsibility for expressing what he wants while leaving Nayuta able to accept or refuse.

### 8.5 `主人公` is re-authored as commitment, not monopoly

After the reconciliation, the narration explicitly rejects the idea that reality arranges itself for a protagonist. Hesitation can cause loss; miracles are not guaranteed. Itsuki resolves to carry through the story in which he is protagonist until death. [IM-V12-E520-IM-V12-E531]

This is the most mature version of `IM-C014` so far.

The term no longer needs to mean:

- the uniquely special person in a world of background characters;
- the most talented creator;
- the commercially dominant writer;
- the person objectively worthy of the heroine.

It becomes closer to **non-transferable commitment to one's own choices and story**. Other people remain protagonists too. Being one does not guarantee victory. It names a refusal to use plurality, ordinariness, or uncertainty as excuses for abandoning agency.

Shiori's bookmark metaphor supplies the necessary moderation: even protagonists can stop, rest, preserve their place, and continue later. `主人公` is therefore no longer identical to relentless motion.

## 9. Kyo's romantic closure: choosing another couple without becoming background in their story

Kyo's V12 endpoint resolves a long-running ambiguity more strongly than any earlier declaration.

Throughout the volume, she continues to avoid some of her own romantic feelings through work. Haruto directly reiterates that he likes her. During Christmas and Comiket scenes, Kyo reacts to signs that Itsuki had likely planned to propose to Nayuta and does not always name the source of the pain she feels. Haruto's possessive discomfort at the thought of Itsuki seeing Kyo's cosplay also confirms that his feelings remain active, while Kyo does not yet reciprocate explicitly. [IM-V12-E225-IM-V12-E284]

After Itsuki and Nayuta reconcile, Miku asks whether Kyo regrets helping. Kyo's answer is unusually self-authored: she likes Nayuta when Nayuta is with Itsuki and Itsuki when he is with Nayuta; not inserting herself between them and helping them reconcile were things **she wanted**. [IM-V12-E535-IM-V12-E542]

This is not proof that every residual feeling has vanished. It is stronger: Kyo can now locate her action in her own desire rather than in self-denial, inferiority, or passive resignation.

The closing narration says one protagonist has revived, one love story has fully closed, and `主人公・白川京` begins a new story. At this V12 boundary, that supports closing the old **Kyo→Itsuki pursuit trajectory** as an active romance while leaving Kyo/Haruto unresolved.

Kyo is not reduced to supporting heroine status by choosing the other couple. The protagonist vocabulary is now plural enough to let her choice become the beginning of her own next story.

## 10. Haruto, Chihiro, and post-rejection continuity

V12 largely respects the V11 nonreciprocity rather than manufacturing immediate reversal.

Chihiro continues to have feelings for Haruto, and Itsuki sometimes teases or facilitates contact. The New Year material makes clear that first rejection has not instantly erased attraction. Yet the relationship is not reclassified as mutual romance. [IM-V12-E285-IM-V12-E310]

Haruto's attention remains directed toward Kyo. He compliments her cosplay, crosses into a sexualized remark, and apologizes when Kyo names it harassment. That small correction matters: attraction does not cancel the recipient's right to set the interactional boundary.

Chihiro's larger V12 movement is therefore not romantic payoff but **continuity after disappointment**. She studies, participates in family life, worries about Natsume, receives a new sister, and maintains friendships. First love becomes one experience within a larger self rather than a total identity verdict.

## 11. Work, audience, and public inference

### 11.1 The fake Christmas romance shows that public meaning exceeds literal words

Itsuki had reserved an exceptionally famous Christmas restaurant for Nayuta a year in advance. After the breakup he uses the nonrefundable reservation with Haruto. Haruto posts a joke implying the two men are dating. Because the venue itself conventionally signals serious romance, the post spreads and is interpreted literally. [IM-V12-E225-IM-V12-E261]

The pair ultimately do not issue a full correction because explaining why Itsuki was at that restaurant would expose private breakup information.

This is a strong extension of `IM-C033`:

> public communication is constituted by wording **plus context, timing, venue, prior knowledge, social convention, and the privacy cost of correction**.

Literal truth/falsity is not the whole ethical problem. Sometimes correcting a false inference requires disclosing another truth that the speaker has legitimate reasons to keep private.

### 11.2 Itsuki V7 demonstrates plural audiences rather than a single correct fan response

The split response to V7 matters beyond the craft debate. Anime newcomers and long-time novel readers enter with different baselines and therefore notice different losses/gains. The publisher benefits from the more accessible work; some older fans experience a reduction in distinctive excess. Neither audience is exposed as fake. [IM-V12-E311-IM-V12-E344]

The text therefore resists using “true fan” as a universal authorization device. Nayuta is a profoundly consequential reader, but she cannot make her response identical to all legitimate reception.

### 11.3 Yuma demonstrates reception that exceeds authorial intention

`銀色景色` changes the way Yuma reads fictional people and then the way he attends to real people. Nayuta did not consciously engineer every psychological layer through which the work affected him. [IM-V12-E345-IM-V12-E374]

Creative influence is therefore real even when partially unintended. This strengthens `IM-C039` while preserving its autonomy boundary: unintended influence can create gratitude and relational stake without retroactively turning the creator into the reader's property.

## 12. Talent, genius, and the limit of creator self-knowledge

V12 continues to treat `天才` as descriptively meaningful but ethically dangerous when it becomes total explanation.

Itsuki's breakup collapse returns to `天才`/`凡人` as a theory of romantic impossibility. Nayuta's own collapse shows the opposite weakness: genius does not produce autonomous motivation, emotional resilience, or omniscient understanding of one's creative method. [IM-V12-E025-IM-V12-E125]

The Yuma interview makes this explicit. Nayuta can create psychologically resonant characters without always knowing the full conceptual route by which she does so. An actor can find and elaborate patterns in the work beyond the creator's explicit model. [IM-V12-E345-IM-V12-E374]

Thus talent cannot be used as a shortcut for:

- emotional maturity;
- self-knowledge;
- vocational independence;
- relationship superiority;
- universal insight into readers;
- moral authority over another creator.

At the same time, V12 does not deny the material reality of differential ability. Nayuta and Itsuki do not suddenly become identical creators. The series' stronger position remains anti-totalization rather than denial of talent.

## 13. `IM-C035` under stress: relational origins, autonomous practice, and motives that can diversify

V12 provides both a counterexample and a recovery path for the acquired-vocation claim.

Kyo and Ayane previously showed that a path entered contingently through others can become one's own. Nayuta initially appears to show the reverse: a career can remain so dependent on one relationship that rupture removes its felt reason to exist. [IM-V12-E102-IM-V12-E125]

But the volume does not leave the model binary. New motivations begin to accumulate:

- Miku provokes creative pride;
- film work reconnects Nayuta to collaboration;
- Yuma demonstrates unplanned reader/performer transformation;
- professional interviews widen her social field;
- competition and anger become productive;
- her own work's existence starts connecting her to people she did not write for initially.

The revised `IM-C035` should therefore be dynamic:

> **Vocation can emerge from contingent relational causes and become self-authored, but that ownership is not all-or-nothing or permanently guaranteed. It is tested when the original motive disappears; new practice, relationships, meanings, and endorsements can diversify or reconstitute the commitment.**

At the V12 endpoint Nayuta has resumed writing, but the checkpoint should avoid claiming her vocation is now fully independent of Itsuki. Her love for him remains central and the final reconciliation is one of the new conditions under which she moves forward.

## 14. Creative plurality: art, craft, and fit-for-purpose rather than hierarchy

V12 gives unusually explicit textual and paratextual protection against an auteurist overread.

Miku says there is no single correct creator type. The afterword reiterates that emotionally detached mechanical creation is not wrong or inferior, that V11's slump was genuinely overcome, and that broad-reader calculated work may often receive better reception. [IM-V12-E311-IM-V12-E344] [IM-V12-E543-IM-V12-E546]

The final artistic synthesis therefore has to be read **locally**.

For the one-reader reconciliation manuscript, `芸術家の魂` plus `職人の技術` is powerful because the act needs both intense specific feeling and sufficient technique to make that feeling transmissible as a novel. Another creator, another work, or another career goal may legitimately optimize differently.

This materially revises `IM-C026` and `IM-C038`:

- quality is plural and audience-sensitive;
- creative legitimacy does not require maximum autobiographical passion;
- technical professionalism is not a lower ontological state of art;
- personal integration can matter enormously for a specific creator/relationship without becoming a universal artistic standard.

## 15. Japanese voice, terminology, and translation-sensitive findings

### 15.1 `書けない` versus `書かない`

Kyo's distinction is analytically foundational. Itsuki's earlier crisis was inability despite desire; Nayuta's initial V12 position is refusal because the reason to write has collapsed. Translation must preserve capacity versus volition rather than rendering both as “not writing.” [IM-V12-E126-IM-V12-E149]

### 15.2 `絶対的に正しいただ一つの答え`

Miku's phrase explicitly rejects a single absolute answer concerning creator mode. It anchors the volume's pluralism and should not be weakened into generic “different styles are okay.” [IM-V12-E323]

### 15.3 `普通に面白い`

Kyo independently uses the same evaluative register around Itsuki V7 that Toki used in V11. Again it is not “bad.” It is genuinely entertaining in an ordinary professional sense, and Kyo may prefer it to earlier material. [IM-V12-E311-IM-V12-E322]

### 15.4 `お母さん`

Itsuki's address to Natsume as `お母さん` is a major relational action. It is not merely politeness. After years of family fracture and the concealed sibling truth, the kinship term performs acceptance. [IM-V12-E452-IM-V12-E457]

### 15.5 `栞`

The name means bookmark, and Itsuki explicitly explains the metaphor: something that preserves one's place when advancing the story becomes tiring and waits as a guide for starting again. [IM-V12-E466-IM-V12-E472]

The metaphor is central because it makes rest compatible with narrative continuity.

### 15.6 `芸術家の魂と、職人の技術`

The line names two creative resources, not good art versus bad art. The afterword's clarification must control the overread. [IM-V12-E489] [IM-V12-E545]

### 15.7 `愛を乳化剤にして`

Love as `乳化剤` is an unusually concrete craft metaphor: two normally repelling modes are forced into a temporary functional mixture. It supports **integration**, not purification. [IM-V12-E493]

### 15.8 `最高のヒーロー` and `主人公`

Nayuta calls Itsuki her highest/true hero despite explicitly acknowledging his flaws and lack of objective superiority to Yuma. Later she says he has always been and will remain her `主人公`. [IM-V12-E419-IM-V12-E424] [IM-V12-E519]

This is relational designation, not a universal merit ranking.

### 15.9 `メインヒロイン`

Itsuki's reconciliation request returns to meta-fictional vocabulary: `もう一度、俺のメインヒロインになってくれ`. The language is affectionate but structurally important because the couple now uses narrative-role vocabulary after the series has pluralized protagonisthood. Nayuta is not reduced to support character; she simultaneously names Itsuki her protagonist. [IM-V12-E516-IM-V12-E519]

## 17. Counterevidence and anti-overread protections

1. **Do not say Itsuki failed to escape writer's block in V11.** V12's main text and afterword explicitly say the slump itself had been overcome.
2. **Do not say V11's technique-only mode was objectively false, bad, or inferior art.** The afterword explicitly rejects that hierarchy.
3. **Do not turn `芸術家の魂 + 職人の技術` into a universal formula for superior fiction.** It is the successful synthesis for Itsuki's specific one-reader relational task.
4. **Do not treat V12 as rejecting professionalism for authenticity.** Professional technique is one of the resources that makes `蘇生` possible.
5. **Do not treat Miku's harsh intervention as proof that coercion is the correct editorial method.** The chapter is `正解のない道`, and Kyo's prior autonomy objections remain valid.
6. **Do not treat Kyo's gentleness as simple professional failure.** Her person-before-output care prevents relational abandonment and reflects legitimate non-editorial obligations.
7. **Do not infer publishers or readers own Nayuta because they need her work.** Generalized demand fails to persuade her and does not automatically create authority.
8. **Do not infer Nayuta never cared about writing.** Her original and continuing motives are heavily Itsuki-centered, but V12 also shows anger, craft, collaboration, reader effects, competition, and widening social curiosity becoming motivational resources.
9. **Do not declare Nayuta's vocation fully independent of Itsuki by V12.** It has diversified, not been proven autonomous from the relationship.
10. **Do not treat Yuma as a straw-man rival.** He is professionally serious, attentive, ethically restrained, and offers a credible alternative that Nayuta rejects for historically specific love rather than objective ranking.
11. **Do not restore professional equality as the condition for Itsuki/Nayuta romance.** Reconciliation occurs through direct relational communication, not proof that their talent/status has become equal.
12. **Do not equate Nayuta calling Itsuki her `主人公` with objective superiority.** She explicitly chooses him despite recognizing flaws and alternatives.
13. **Do not treat Kyo's old feelings as having never existed.** V12 gives stronger evidence that the old pursuit trajectory closes because of a choice Kyo can own.
14. **Do not treat Haruto/Kyo as canonically coupled at V12.** Haruto's love is explicit; Kyo's new openness is not yet a relationship.
15. **Do not reclassify Chihiro/Haruto as mutual after V11's rejection.** Chihiro continues to have feelings; Haruto remains oriented toward Kyo.
16. **Do not romanticize Itsuki's five-day overwork in `蘇生` as a sustainable creator-care model.** Success does not erase capacity cost.
17. **Do not romanticize Nayuta's self-neglect or hair cutting as uncomplicated liberation.** The initial change occurs inside breakup collapse and loss of maintenance motive.
18. **Do not treat Aoba's nightmare/alternate-route material as an actual sexual relationship with Itsuki.** The waking Itsuki explicitly refuses the underage boundary violation and tells her to value herself.
19. **Do not treat Kaizu's ordinary-writer telos as simple healthy moderation.** V12 reveals a self-sacrificial vow and expectation of precarious slow decline.
20. **Do not treat Ashley as rescuing Kaizu against his will.** She learns the risk, chooses it, and makes the burden more materially sustainable; the ethical point is reciprocal informed choice.
21. **Do not infer family repair is finished.** It is substantially strengthened, but later practice remains necessary.
22. **Do not treat Shiori's bookmark metaphor as an argument for passivity.** It explicitly links rest to later continuation.
23. **Do not treat the fake Haruto/Itsuki Christmas rumor as merely a misinformation joke.** The correction problem is constrained by legitimate privacy around the breakup.
24. **Do not assume author afterword commentary overrides narrative evidence.** It is PF clarifying design and reader misinterpretation; the story's own plurality remains independently evidenced.
25. **Do not use V13-V14 to settle any V12 uncertainty.** The checkpoint must freeze this exact boundary first.

### 18.1 The largest V12 corrections

The most consequential revision is `IM-C038`. V11's wording risked implying a hidden hierarchy in which technical recovery was merely false recovery. V12 corrects that. The technical recovery was real. The unresolved issue was **which dimensions of recovery mattered for which person and purpose**.

`IM-C014` also changes substantially. Itsuki no longer needs `主人公` to mean singular exceptional status. The mature form is a commitment to inhabit one's own story without demanding that others become background characters or that the world guarantee success.

`IM-C023` becomes role-sensitive rather than simply anti-control. Kyo's first instinct to wait is ethically defensible as friendship; Miku's challenge succeeds as creator-to-creator confrontation; Kyo realizes an editor may owe a different kind of stewardship. V12 does not supply a universal intervention algorithm.

`IM-C030` gains a second boundary. V11 distinguished chosen ordinary happiness from resignation. V12 shows that even an “ordinary life” ideal can become self-sacrificial exceptionalism when Kaizu turns survival into a solitary mission. Ashley's informed choice reintroduces reciprocity.

### 18.2 New claim `IM-C040`: chosen interdependence

**Provisional formulation:**

> Self-authorship does not require solitary burden-bearing. When risks and costs are disclosed, another competent person can voluntarily choose to share them without making the original commitment less authentic. Conversely, unilaterally excluding others in the name of protecting them can deny their agency.

Kaizu/Ashley is the strongest V12 case. The claim also has backward resonance with roomshare reciprocity, family care, and collaborative creator infrastructure, but it remains OPEN until checkpoint adjudication.
