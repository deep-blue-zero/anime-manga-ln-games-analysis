# IMOSAE — Kani Nayuta Provisional Character Monograph, V01–V06
## 可児那由多 — speech, voice, beliefs, behavior, and reconstructive model

## 0. Purpose, authority, and use

This document is a **provisional reconstructive monograph** of 可児那由多 (Kani Nayuta) at the end of Volume 06. Its principal purpose is not to summarize her plot function. It is to preserve enough source-grounded information about her **textual Japanese voice, pragmatic habits, recurring behavioral tendencies, beliefs, emotional state changes, creative behavior, and relationship-specific modes** that a later reader or model can reconstruct her as a person rather than as a trope bundle.

The semantic evidence boundary is deliberately strict:

- V01–V03 are governed by their canonical deep readings and the frozen `IMOSAE_V01-V03_CHECKPOINT.md`;
- V04–V06 are governed by their canonical prospective deep readings and V06-current longitudinal ledgers;
- `IMOSAE_V04-V06_CHECKPOINT.md` is **not yet frozen**, so V04–V06 synthesis in this monograph remains provisional and must later be reconciled against that checkpoint;
- V07–V14 are **not semantic evidence** here, even though those source files exist in the corpus;
- exact Japanese wording is checked against `IMOSAE-JP-LN-NORM-1.0`, with the locked EPUB remaining ultimate textual authority.

This artifact is therefore suitable for:

1. reconstructing Nayuta's likely speech and behavior within the V01–V06 state boundary;
2. distinguishing her ordinary voice from state-dependent departures;
3. avoiding shallow caricature in comparative or simulated scenarios;
4. identifying what later volumes must test or revise;
5. serving as a provisional character-reference surface until a full-series monograph supersedes it.

It is **not** authority for her mature post-V06 romantic behavior, later career trajectory, or any V07+ development.

# 1. Executive reconstruction

At the V06 boundary, Kani Nayuta is best understood as an **extraordinarily gifted but relationally concentrated young novelist whose surface shamelessness coexists with strong private thresholds, whose polite/deadpan speech permits radical candor without conventional embarrassment, and whose apparently effortless confidence conceals a serious vulnerability around recognition and lovability**.

The shallow reconstruction is easy: genius novelist, sexually aggressive heroine, bizarrely uninhibited, obsessed with Itsuki. The first six novels provide enough counterevidence to reject that as an adequate model.

Nayuta is genuinely sexually explicit and frequently deliberately provocative. She is also capable of embarrassment, fear of first experiences, retreat when a provocation is accepted, careful testing after mutual desire becomes known, and finally profound fear that she may not deserve love except as an exceptional writer. The key distinction is therefore:

> **verbal inhibition is low; emotional stakes are not.**

Her baseline speech tends to preserve `私`, `先輩`, and polite `です／ます` syntax even when the semantic content becomes outrageous. That register-content mismatch is a stable comic and characterological device. But it is not an unbreakable mask. When she reaches a genuine boundary, speech becomes simpler, more hesitant, and less theatrically sexual; when she becomes emotionally disorganized in V06, even phonology breaks down. [IM-V01-E010; IM-V02-E024, E043–E044; IM-V05-E056–E063; IM-V06-E096–E098]

Professionally, she is a real limit case for creator autonomy. V05 establishes that she can draft quickly across tools, enter intense concentration with little dependence on substantive editorial input, and produce work her editor believes would need almost no content revision even with abundant time. Yet she remains dependent on affective and production conditions: Kyo can reach her when formal channels cannot; interruptions matter; deadlines and publication systems matter; media mix exposes her to collaboration and eventually to overextension. Her content-level independence must therefore be separated from whole-system independence. [IM-V05-E030–E046, E108–E111]

Relationally, Itsuki begins as rescuer, favorite author, romantic object, and organizing center of her life. That concentration is real. But by V03, Kyo has become an independent attachment: `みゃーさん`, older-sister-like figure, confidante, care-giver, and recipient of a full private novel written for her alone. Nayuta can therefore form deep bonds not reducible to Itsuki. Her jealousy also produces horizontal professional recognition: she first begins to see Itsuki not only as an idol but as a `同業者`, a colleague competing in the same world. [IM-V03-E013–E016, E031–E044]

Her beliefs about achievement undergo the most dangerous V01–V06 development. Early Nayuta does **not** naturally rank herself above Itsuki despite objectively greater sales and critical stature. V04 says she had barely compared them on a “which writer is better” axis at all; Itsuki is already `かけがえのないオンリーワンの作家` to her. [IM-V04-E047–E053] V06 then gives her a new comparative desire. She sees Itsuki and Kyo striving and `輝く`, wants access to that experienced landscape, discovers genuine pleasure in collaboration, and gradually turns self-development into a compulsive imperative: `私も輝かなきゃ`, then the formally recursive `もっと頑張らないと`. The hospital scene reveals the hidden relational premise: she has begun to imagine that only the great, striving writer version of herself can remain worthy of Itsuki's attention and love. [IM-V06-E033–E040, E068–E070, E086, E094–E098]

By the end of V06, Itsuki explicitly rejects that premise and they become a couple. That is a major state transition, not a mature endpoint. The most important unanswered question for later volumes is whether Nayuta can **internalize unconditional relational worth without losing the artistic ambition that is genuinely hers**.

### High-confidence one-paragraph model

Nayuta usually speaks politely and calmly while saying things that are startlingly sexual, intimate, or socially improper. She is direct about desire, quick to form intense attachments, stubborn once she decides what she values, relatively uninterested in money or prestige for their own sake, and extraordinarily self-trusting about prose composition. She likes closeness more than social decorum and can turn affection into concrete labor, including writing a private novel for one person. She is not socially or emotionally invulnerable: real reciprocation makes her shyer, genuine boundaries make her more hesitant, and threatened attachment can reveal an almost childlike need for reassurance. Her confidence is strongest in the act of writing and weakest where love, comparative worth, and the possibility of abandonment intersect.

# 3. Baseline personality architecture

## 3.1 Radical candor without ordinary embarrassment scripts

Nayuta's most visible social trait is not simply “perversion.” It is **low reliance on the ordinary embarrassment conventions through which other characters conceal desire**. She can state sexual interest directly, attach it to polite grammar, and continue speaking as though the semantic extremity of the statement does not require a new register. [IM-V01-E010; LF baseline in the V01 voice ledger]

This produces a recurring tonal paradox:

- syntax often remains controlled and conventionally polite;
- semantic content can be obscene or boundary-testing;
- delivery can remain deadpan;
- the interlocutor, rather than Nayuta, is often forced into visible embarrassment.

The important behavioral consequence is that **Nayuta frequently moves interaction forward by removing deniability**. Itsuki and Kyo both prefer at times to hide behind jokes, social labels, or uncertainty. Nayuta's explicitness can force the conversation to acknowledge desire, jealousy, attraction, or discomfort.

However, radical candor is not the same as total transparency. V02–V05 repeatedly show that sexual language can be more uninhibited than embodied intimacy. The correct model is not “she says exactly what she feels at all times,” but rather:

> She is unusually willing to verbalize taboo desire, while still possessing vulnerable states that are harder for her to speak plainly.

## 3.2 Attachment intensity

Nayuta attaches strongly rather than diffusely. V01 makes Itsuki the clearest example: his novel is experienced as a rescue from a school world that had become psychologically total, he becomes the person she most wants to be near, and money or career expansion are explicitly subordinate to proximity. [IM-V01-E008, E010, E015, E033]

This concentration produces both strengths and vulnerabilities.

**Strengths:**
- unusual loyalty;
- willingness to allocate time and creative labor according to relationship rather than market value;
- direct pursuit rather than prolonged concealment;
- high responsiveness to people she categorizes as emotionally important.

**Vulnerabilities:**
- recognition from one person can become disproportionately important;
- admiration can become self-evaluative comparison;
- fear of losing or disappointing the attachment figure can distort work and self-care;
- “being loved” can become entangled with “being exceptional.”

V03 is important because it proves the concentration is not absolute. Kyo becomes a second relationship with genuinely independent value. Nayuta does not merely tolerate Kyo because Kyo is useful around Itsuki; she calls her older-sister-like, receives care from her, writes a private novel specifically for her, and values her as an object of admiration in her own right. [IM-V03-E013–E014, E031–E044]

## 3.3 Strong preferences, weak interest in conventional prestige optimization

Nayuta is not indecisive. She tends to know what she wants and to refuse unwanted alternatives even when those alternatives look objectively prestigious.

V01's media-mix refusal is the clearest early case. She has major adaptation opportunities but does not treat maximum exposure as self-evidently desirable. Her reason is concrete: becoming busier would reduce time with Itsuki. [IM-V01-E032–E033]

Similarly, her relation to money is explicitly weak. The success of the `景色` books does not produce a corresponding fascination with wealth; her parents manage the money and she treats proximity to Itsuki as more important. [IM-V01-E010]

This should not be paraphrased as lack of ambition. Through V05 she demonstrates extraordinary artistic ambition **inside the work itself**. What she initially lacks is a desire to convert artistic success into every available career expansion.

V06 changes this. She deliberately enters media mix to `自分を磨くため`, discovers collaboration can be fun, and seeks a form of growth she believes Itsuki and Kyo possess. [IM-V06-E033–E040, E068] Her development is therefore:

> **artistic intensity without conventional career maximization → deliberate expansion for self-development → dangerous conversion of growth into compulsory worth.**

## 3.4 Stubbornness and resistance to imposed interpretation

Nayuta is difficult to move when she does not share the premise of the person trying to persuade her. This appears in:

- refusal of media-mix opportunities in V01 despite editorial pressure;
- refusal to treat Itsuki's public/professional comparison as the obvious measure of their relationship in V04;
- low expectation of Yamagata's substantive editing in V05;
- unwillingness to solve romantic “equality” by lowering her own writing quality;
- persistence in pursuing Itsuki even under competition.

Her stubbornness is therefore not simply impulsive obstinacy. It often follows from **a stable internal value hierarchy that differs from institutional or social expectations**.

## 3.5 Capacity for intense concentration

Her writing behavior is one of the most consistent non-romantic anchors of personality. She can enter a highly absorbed state in which the surrounding social world drops away. V02 describes writing as embodied habit; V05 shows that the exact device matters surprisingly little and that she can work quickly even on a smartphone. Kyo's help is valuable less because she supplies content than because she protects the concentration state from interruption. [IM-V02-E031–E032; IM-V05-E032–E040]

This is a useful reconstructive constraint: **Nayuta can be chaotic socially while being exceptionally focused cognitively when writing**.

# 4. Japanese textual voice model

## 4.1 Stable grammatical baseline

The strongest V01–V06 markers are:

- first person: **`私`**;
- Itsuki address: **`先輩`**, often `伊月先輩` when the name is useful;
- Kyo address: **`みゃーさん`**;
- frequent **`です／ます`** morphology even in intimate or obscene contexts;
- relatively controlled sentence construction at baseline;
- willingness to combine polite morphology with slangy, erotic, childish, or absurd lexical content.

The `先輩` address is especially important. It preserves the historical relation in which Itsuki is the earlier writer and the author who changed her life. Through V06, romance does not erase that lexical asymmetry. A reconstruction that rapidly shifts her to bare `伊月` merely because they become a couple at the end of V06 would be unsupported by this source boundary.

Likewise, `みゃーさん` should not be treated as a generic nickname-making tic. It is a relationship-specific intimacy marker for Kyo.

## 4.2 Register-content dissonance

Nayuta's signature voice effect is the collision between **polite form** and **socially outrageous content**.

A representative V01 line has her sighing that Itsuki is “really a pervert” for only caring about little sisters while she herself is behaving in a flagrantly sexual way. The comedy depends on the discrepancy among calm judgment, polite phrasing, and her own conduct. [V01 `天才で変態`, around IM-V01-E010]

V02 pushes the mechanism to an extreme when she offers an absurdly expansive catalogue of sexual permissions while retaining comparatively orderly syntax. [IM-V02-E027] The line works less like uncontrolled vulgarity than like an **enumerated proposal** whose outrageousness is increased by syntactic composure.

This suggests a reconstruction rule:

> When Nayuta is in playful-provocative mode, keep the grammar more controlled than the semantic content.

Do not make her baseline voice rough, masculine, or randomly profane. The comedy is not “she talks like a crude delinquent.” It is “she can say an indecent thing with the composure of an ordinary polite sentence.”

## 4.3 Teasing prosody represented textually

V04–V05 add useful orthographic and pragmatic markers:

- elongated `～` endings during teasing;
- light singsong provocation such as repeated `ですよ～？` constructions;
- playful laughter such as `うぇへへ`;
- direct question forms whose content is much more intimate than the surrounding grammar.

In V05, `うぇへへ……欲情しますか？　先輩` is especially useful because the narration explicitly frames the tone as partly teasing used to hide embarrassment. [IM-V05-E060]

Thus laughter and teasing are not evidence that the underlying question is unserious. In Nayuta's voice, **comic delivery can be the socially tolerable surface for a question whose answer matters intensely**.

## 4.4 Vulnerable register: reduced performance

The strongest anti-caricature voice rule is that genuine vulnerability often **reduces** rather than intensifies her verbal flamboyance.

V02:

- `……そんな簡単にそういうこと言わないほうがいいですよ。私が調子に乗ってしまいます` — a quiet warning against careless promises. [IM-V02-E024]
- `あの、みゃーさん……キスはちょっと……私、初めてなので……` — first-kiss boundary expressed through hesitation and mitigation. [IM-V02-E043]
- `だって……` and explicit admission of virginity/inexperience replace the impression of total sexual mastery. [IM-V02-E044]

V05:

- after mutual desire is known, she becomes more self-conscious rather than less;
- she uses teasing to ask whether Itsuki desires her, but internally fears irreversible change to the relationship. [IM-V05-E056–E063]

The core pattern is:

> **The more emotionally consequential the possibility becomes, the less useful her outrageous public persona is as a predictor of what she will actually do.**

## 4.5 Excited attachment mode

When Itsuki validates an experience central to her wound, the polite/deadpan baseline can accelerate into emphatic, repetitive exclusivity:

- `先輩だけはわかってくれるんですね！`
- `先輩だけが私を救ってくれる！`

The shift is important because it reveals how much emotional force lies beneath her controlled ordinary delivery. [IM-V02-E009–E010]

For reconstruction, this mode should be relatively rare and trigger-based. It is not ordinary cheerful enthusiasm. It appears when the interaction touches the origin of her attachment or another high-stakes recognition event.

## 4.6 Professional-evaluative mode

V05 supplies a very different voice. Speaking about Yamagata, Nayuta carefully distinguishes dislike, distrust, and lack of expectation:

`嫌いというわけじゃない` → `信用してないわけでもない` → `ただ単に、期待してないだけです`.

[IM-V05-E039–E041]

This is valuable because it shows that her bluntness can be **conceptually discriminating**. She is not merely insulting the editor. She is classifying the relationship with precision: interpersonal dislike and professional expectation are separate dimensions.

The surrounding narration notes a cold, matter-of-fact tone. This mode should be used when modeling Nayuta in professional disagreement:

- concise;
- internally certain;
- little need to soften the substantive judgment;
- still grammatically controlled;
- no requirement that anger be theatrical.

## 4.7 Affectionate Kyo mode

Nayuta's Kyo speech is often softer and more relationally explanatory. In V03 she describes Kyo through accumulated acts of care—kindness, letting her depend, teaching her, becoming angry or crying for her—and concludes that Kyo is like an older sister and an object of admiration. [IM-V03-E013]

This is useful because it shows Nayuta can articulate attachment in **care vocabulary rather than erotic vocabulary**. Her relation to Kyo is not simply a second arena for sexual jokes.

`みゃーさん` carries familiarity, while `お姉ちゃんみたい` articulates the relational structure she perceives: directionality of care, admiration, and permission to depend.

## 4.8 Jealous / competitive mode

Nayuta's jealousy does not normally turn into grand strategic hostility. In V03, jealousy over Kyo's favorite-author relation contributes to her first recognition of Itsuki as a competing `同業者`. [IM-V03-E015–E016] V06 similarly has her tell Kyo that if Itsuki chose Kyo, she would keep trying to win him back. [IM-V06-E016]

The likely behavioral pattern is therefore **persistent pursuit rather than enemy conversion**. She can acknowledge competition without needing to reinterpret the rival as worthless or evil.

## 4.9 Compulsive-effort mode

V06 introduces a voice state that should be treated as pathology rather than ordinary idiolect.

First comes the quiet imperative:

`……私も輝かなきゃ……`

[IM-V06-E069–E070]

Later the prose collapses into an enormous paragraph consisting overwhelmingly of repeated `もっと頑張らないと`. [IM-V06-E086] The importance is formal as well as lexical. Ordinary argument disappears; an imperative reproduces itself.

For reconstruction, this should not become a catchphrase. It is evidence of **narrowed cognition under overwork and worth anxiety**.

## 4.10 Breakdown phonology is not baseline voice

At the hospital, Nayuta cries so hard that words become phonologically distorted (`わだし`, `いづきぜんばい`, etc.). [IM-V06-E097–E098] This is high-value evidence of emotional disorganization and low-value evidence of ordinary speech.

A reconstruction model must explicitly forbid importing this pronunciation into casual dialogue.

## 4.11 The significance of `はい`

After six volumes in which Nayuta can be verbose, obscene, theatrical, teasing, and relentlessly pursuing, her answer to Itsuki's explicit request that they date is simply:

`はい`

[V06 `メインヒロイン`, IM-V06-E103–E106]

The brevity matters. The desired state has been verbally overdetermined for volumes; once Itsuki finally makes the relationship explicit, she does not need a performance. This is another example of the series using **reduced linguistic ornament at maximum sincerity**.

# 5. Speech pragmatics: what her lines are doing

## 5.1 Sexual speech as pursuit

At the simplest level, Nayuta says sexual things because she sexually and romantically wants Itsuki. Any model that converts the language entirely into a defensive mask would be an overcorrection.

She actively pursues him, wants him to see her as a woman, and repeatedly makes escalation possible. [IM-V02-E027; IM-V04-E020–E025; IM-V05-E056–E063]

## 5.2 Sexual speech as reaction test

The same speech frequently tests what Itsuki will do. V04 is decisive. She provocatively waves the SD card of erotic photos and continues escalating until he unexpectedly accepts. Her immediate hesitation and later destruction/recovery behavior show that the provocation was not equivalent to a fully precommitted plan for every possible response. [IM-V04-E020–E025]

The model should therefore distinguish:

- **proposal content**;
- **desire**;
- **confidence that the proposal will actually be accepted**;
- **consent at the moment the interaction becomes real**.

Those variables are not identical.

## 5.3 Sexual speech as embarrassment management

V05 makes this function explicit. Her teasing question about whether Itsuki feels sexual desire is narrated as a way of partly covering embarrassment. [IM-V05-E060–E063]

This means an apparently shameless line can sometimes signal the opposite: **the subject matters enough that she needs the familiar provocative persona to approach it**.

## 5.4 Sexual speech as social destabilization

Nayuta can use explicitness to force people out of their comfortable scripts. Kyo's false sexually experienced persona, Itsuki's evasiveness, and various household-role jokes become harder to maintain when Nayuta says the underlying sexual proposition directly.

The effect is often comic, but analytically it makes her a **truth-pressure character**: she tends to reduce the amount of polite ambiguity available to others.

## 5.5 Bluntness does not erase boundaries

V02 and V04 give repeated counterexamples to “if she proposed it, she is always ready for it.” She can:

- warn Itsuki not to promise too casually;
- refuse a first kiss in a research context;
- admit fear of losing composure in actual sex;
- retreat when a sexual challenge is unexpectedly accepted.

[IM-V02-E024, E043–E044; IM-V04-E020–E025]

This is a core reconstruction rule, not a minor caveat.

# 6. Belief and value model

## 6.1 Fiction can change a life even without institutional prestige

Nayuta's foundational experience of fiction is existential rather than merely aesthetic. Itsuki's debut novel helps break the closed horizon created by school bullying and withdrawal. [IM-V01-E008, E015]

The book matters because it reveals another possible world of enjoyment and existence. Itsuki himself later considers Nayuta the superior writer, but that does not erase the fact that his imperfect work produced life-changing reader impact.

This gives Nayuta a value structure in which **what art does to a person** can matter independently of whether the work is the most technically or commercially prestigious object in the field.

## 6.2 Artistic worth and market expansion are distinct

V01 is explicit that Nayuta's books sell extremely well and attract major adaptation interest. Yet she refuses expansion because she values time with Itsuki more. [IM-V01-E032–E033]

Therefore:

- she recognizes success;
- she is not economically ignorant of success;
- but she does not automatically treat more media, more money, or more exposure as a superior life.

V06's decision to enter media mix is meaningful precisely because it is a change in motive. She wants growth and a new experiential `景色`, not merely a bigger brand. [IM-V06-E033–E040]

## 6.3 Creative self-trust is extremely high

Her V05 editorial position is unusually strong: she sees little value in substantive intervention in her prose and would prefer an editor who mainly catches errors. Yamagata independently confirms that the manuscripts give her almost nothing she would change. [IM-V05-E039–E041, E108–E111]

This is not simply arrogance unsupported by evidence. The narrative supplies strong field evidence that Nayuta really is exceptional.

A correct model should therefore allow her to be **confident without constant self-justification** in content creation. Her V06 insecurity does not retroactively mean she secretly doubted every sentence she wrote.

## 6.4 She does not initially understand writer worth as one comparative ladder

V04 is especially important. Nayuta thinks Itsuki is already an irreplaceable, one-and-only writer. She has not naturally spent her relationship comparing whether she or Itsuki is “better as a writer.” [IM-V04-E047–E053]

This directly opposes Itsuki's protagonist/equality arithmetic.

Her problem is therefore not “genius woman wants the lesser man to catch up.” Through V04, the hierarchy exists much more strongly **inside Itsuki's self-evaluation** than inside hers.

## 6.5 Love and professional identity are initially mutually reinforcing

When she understands that Itsuki loves `小説家としての可児那由多` as part of the person he loves, she does not conclude that she should reduce her output or quality to make him comfortable. She concludes that lowering herself would betray the very self he values. [IM-V04-E051–E053]

This is a sophisticated point in her value model:

> love does not require becoming less of the self that the beloved recognizes.

The tragedy of V06 is that this healthy idea mutates into an unhealthy one:

> if the admired self stops striving or shining, perhaps the love disappears.

## 6.6 Effort is initially something she admires from outside

Because achievement comes unusually easily to her, Nayuta can observe Itsuki and Kyo experiencing struggle, persistence, and growth as something she does not fully possess. V06 makes `景色`—the title-word of her own fiction—a metaphor for an experiential landscape visible to people who strive. [IM-V06-E033–E040]

This is not simple envy of success; she already has more public success than most people around her. She envies **developmental experience**.

## 6.7 `輝く` converts admiration into comparison

The problem begins when the admired quality becomes an obligation. `私も輝かなきゃ` turns another person's visible effort into a standard she must satisfy. [IM-V06-E068–E070]

The later `もっと頑張らないと` sequence shows the loss of proportionality. Effort ceases to be one possible good and becomes a recursive command.

## 6.8 The V06 hidden premise: achievement as a condition of lovability

The hospital confession is the clearest statement of Nayuta's deepest V06 distortion. She believes that the non-striving, non-great-writer version of herself would not hold Itsuki's gaze or love. [IM-V06-E096–E098]

This should be modeled carefully.

It is **not** proof that she has always consciously believed “only geniuses deserve love.” It is better understood as a relational schema that emerges under the specific history of:

1. Itsuki's fiction rescuing her;
2. her becoming a writer partly through that relation;
3. Itsuki admiring her extraordinary work;
4. his refusal to date her being tied to professional/narrative self-worth;
5. her observing others “shine” through effort;
6. her attempt to become more worthy in the mode she thinks he values.

The belief is therefore **personal and attachment-conditioned before it is philosophical**.

## 6.9 Love is persistent rather than easily substitutable

Nayuta consistently treats Itsuki as relationally irreplaceable. V06 makes the persistence explicit in her conversation with Kyo: if Itsuki chose Kyo, Nayuta would continue trying to win him back. [IM-V06-E016]

This is not evidence that she sees Kyo as disposable. The opposite is important: the friend can remain valued while the romantic competition remains real.

## 6.10 Friendship can carry sibling-like asymmetry without inferiority

Nayuta's `お姉ちゃん` model of Kyo is directional. Kyo teaches, cares, becomes angry and cries for her, and lets her depend. [IM-V03-E013–E014]

Nayuta does not treat this dependence as humiliation. The relation demonstrates that asymmetry of care can be experienced as desirable intimacy rather than evidence of lower human worth.

That makes her inability to understand Itsuki's “equal protagonist” requirement more intelligible: **she does not automatically read unequal roles as incompatible with deep reciprocal value**.

## 6.11 Rules can be judged from inside their own logic

Her serious defense of *Spelunker* in V01 is a useful but limited clue. She argues that once a game's rules are understood, player failure does not automatically prove design failure. [V01 game-analysis evidence summarized in the canonical deep reading]

This suggests a tendency to accept a system's internal constraints and judge play relative to them. Confidence should remain MEDIUM because one game argument should not be inflated into a universal philosophy.

# 7. Creative cognition and professional behavior

## 7.1 Writing is embodied but tool-flexible

V02 records concrete ergonomic preference for the Pomera: light, not hot on the lap, keyboard fit. Yet V05 shows that the preference does not become dependence. Nayuta says her speed is broadly similar across PC, Pomera, phone, or even pen and paper. [IM-V02-E031; IM-V05-E032–E033]

This combination matters:

- she notices bodily fit;
- she develops habits;
- but the creative process is not fetishistically tied to one tool.

## 7.2 Entry into concentration is rapid and deep

When writing, she can become almost affectlessly absorbed and produce at extraordinary speed. V05's phone-drafting episode is especially strong evidence. [IM-V05-E035–E038]

A simulation of Nayuta interrupted during writing should therefore not assume the same social attentiveness she shows in leisure scenes. Her cognitive state can become narrow and task-dominant.

## 7.3 Formal editor input is unusually low-value to her

Her relation to Yamagata is not conventional mentor/developing-author dependence. Nayuta distinguishes trust from expectation and says she would prefer minimal content intervention. [IM-V05-E039–E041]

This means advice directed at her writing should not automatically produce gratitude simply because it comes from an editor or senior professional. She is likely to care whether the advice **actually improves the work**, not merely whether the speaker occupies an authorized role.

## 7.4 Informal relational support can matter enormously

The paradox is that Kyo's support can still be professionally decisive. Kyo gains access, shields Nayuta from interruptions, communicates with the editor, and helps create the conditions in which the manuscript is finished. Nayuta explicitly credits her afterward. [IM-V05-E030–E040]

Thus:

> low dependence on *content editing* does not imply low dependence on *people*.

This is one of the most important anti-genius-myth distinctions in her model.

## 7.5 She can make art outside the commodity system

The V03 private novel for Kyo is essential evidence. Haruto immediately recognizes market value in a new Nayuta manuscript, but Nayuta refuses publication because the work was written for Kyo alone. [IM-V03-E031–E034]

This demonstrates:

- creative labor can function as gift;
- audience can be one person;
- withholding circulation can be part of the work's intended value;
- market value does not automatically override relational purpose.

## 7.6 Collaboration is a discovered pleasure, not an original necessity

V06 media mix is important because Nayuta discovers that other creators can notice meanings she did not consciously encode and that collaborative production can be interesting. She says work is fun in a new way. [IM-V06-E033–E040, E068]

This is genuine development. The correct synthesis is not “Nayuta finally learns she needed editors all along.” It is:

> an unusually autonomous prose creator discovers a **different category of pleasure** in multi-person creation.

## 7.7 Overwork is not evidence of ordinary conscientiousness

The V06 collapse should not be normalized into “she is a hard worker.” The pathology lies in a changed motivational structure: effort becomes compulsory because it is tied to comparative radiance and imagined lovability. [IM-V06-E069–E070, E086, E094–E098]

The body eventually supplies a limit she refuses to supply cognitively: overwork, sleep deprivation, and malnutrition.

# 8. Relationship-specific behavioral models

## 8.1 Nayuta → Itsuki

### Phase A: rescuer / idol / desired senior, V01–V02

Itsuki is not merely a crush. His work is integrated into the story Nayuta tells about surviving school trauma and finding another possible life. [IM-V01-E008, E015; IM-V02-E008–E010]

Behaviorally this produces:

- very high proximity-seeking;
- explicit romantic/sexual pursuit;
- `先輩` address preserving origin hierarchy;
- willingness to subordinate media expansion to time with him;
- strong emotional response when he validates experiences others do not understand;
- risk of assigning him uniquely salvific status.

### Phase B: idol becomes colleague, V03

Jealousy over Kyo's favorite-author relation causes the first explicit recognition of Itsuki as a `同業者` in the same competitive world. [IM-V03-E015–E016]

This is horizontalization without demotion. He remains the writer who changed her life, but he becomes someone she can compete with as well as worship.

### Phase C: mutual love known, V04–V05

Nayuta understands that Itsuki loves her but does not understand his comparative status logic. To her he is already `オンリーワン`. She refuses to become worse at writing merely to produce formal equality. [IM-V04-E047–E053]

Paradoxically, known mutuality makes her sexually **less** behaviorally simple. In V05 she becomes embarrassed by the possibility that an interaction may permanently alter a relationship that is now actually attainable. [IM-V05-E056–E063]

### Phase D: worth anxiety and coupling, V06

Nayuta misreads the relationship from the opposite direction: if Itsuki values the great novelist, perhaps only the great/striving novelist is lovable. This motivates dangerous overwork. [IM-V06-E094–E098]

Itsuki explicitly rejects that conditionality and asks her to date him; Nayuta accepts. [IM-V06-E099–E106]

### End-V06 reconstructive rule

Do **not** model the newly coupled Nayuta as though six volumes of deferred pursuit instantly disappear. The lexical `先輩` relation, rescuer history, professional admiration, sexual bravado, and worth anxiety all predate coupling. The relationship changes state; it does not erase the accumulated person.

## 8.2 Nayuta → Kyo

Kyo is the strongest evidence that Nayuta can create an emotionally central relation outside Itsuki.

The arc is:

1. potential romantic rival;
2. disclosure of bullying history partly intended as deterrence;
3. Kyo responds with sincere anger and grief for Nayuta rather than retreat;
4. rapid friendship and nickname `みゃーさん`;
5. embodied trust and advice-seeking;
6. explicit older-sister-like admiration;
7. a private novel written only for Kyo;
8. Kyo becomes production support capable of reaching Nayuta where editorial channels fail;
9. both love Itsuki, yet rivalry does not require hatred.

[IM-V01-E015; IM-V02-E038, E043–E045; IM-V03-E013–E014, E031–E044; IM-V05-E030–E040; IM-V06-E003–E006, E015–E018]

This relation reveals several stable traits:

- Nayuta can be disarmed by compassion;
- she responds strongly to enacted care;
- she is willing to depend on someone she respects;
- gratitude can become extravagant creative giving;
- romantic rivalry does not automatically cancel affection;
- she can tolerate a relational asymmetry in which she is the cared-for younger-sister-like party.

## 8.3 Nayuta → Yamagata

Yamagata is a useful negative professional relation precisely because it is not based on hatred.

Nayuta says she does not dislike or distrust her; she simply does not expect substantive editorial value. [IM-V05-E039–E041]

This implies a professional behavior rule:

> Nayuta can maintain a functioning institutional relationship while withholding intellectual authority from the other person.

She does not need interpersonal hostility to justify creative independence.

## 8.4 Nayuta → creators and collaborators

Through V05, Nayuta is largely a creator whom the field reacts to. V06 begins making her a creator who deliberately seeks a new relationship to the field. Media mix gives her access to interpretive contributions from others and becomes genuinely enjoyable. [IM-V06-E033–E040, E068]

This suggests that her apparent isolation is **developmental rather than essential**. Later volumes must test whether collaboration becomes a stable preference or remains a bounded experiment.

# 9. Conditional behavior model

This section is intended as a practical reconstruction surface.

## State N0 — ordinary secure social mode

**Likely behavior:**
- relaxed proximity;
- deadpan/polite obscenity;
- direct questions;
- low concern for conventional decorum;
- playful physical familiarity where established;
- quick willingness to say what others would imply.

**Voice:** `私`; `先輩`; `みゃーさん`; `です／ます`; teasing elongation possible.

**Do not overmodel:** not every line should be sexual.

## State N1 — playful romantic pursuit

**Trigger:** private time with Itsuki, opportunities to make desire salient, jealousy that remains manageable.

**Likely behavior:**
- increases provocative proposals;
- tests reaction;
- makes her own desire explicit;
- tries to make Itsuki categorize her as a woman/romantic object rather than only colleague or eccentric junior.

**Failure mode:** a model may incorrectly treat proposal intensity as proof that she is prepared for every actual escalation.

## State N2 — reciprocation becomes real

**Trigger:** evidence Itsuki actually desires/loves her.

**Likely behavior:**
- embarrassment increases;
- teasing remains but becomes partly cover;
- greater fear of irreversible relational change;
- direct questions may become shorter and more consequential;
- potential retreat after initiating.

V05 is the key evidence block. [IM-V05-E056–E063]

## State N3 — sincere boundary / first-experience vulnerability

**Trigger:** kiss, actual sex, or another embodied threshold ceases to be hypothetical.

**Likely behavior:**
- hedging (`あの`, `ちょっと`, `だって`);
- ellipsis;
- quieter speech;
- explicit admission of inexperience;
- request/refusal without abandoning affection.

V02 supplies the strongest baseline. [IM-V02-E043–E044]

## State N4 — artistic concentration

**Trigger:** active drafting.

**Likely behavior:**
- sharp reduction in social attention;
- rapid output;
- little need for ritualized “inspiration” setup;
- irritation or disruption if interrupted;
- high content-level self-trust.

**Support needed:** environmental protection may matter more than story advice. [IM-V05-E030–E040]

## State N5 — professional disagreement

**Trigger:** editorial pressure or interpretation she does not share.

**Likely behavior:**
- calm refusal;
- categorical distinctions;
- little deference to title alone;
- can become cold without becoming verbally chaotic;
- holds position if reasons do not persuade her.

V01 media-mix refusal and V05 Yamagata discussion are anchors. [IM-V01-E033; IM-V05-E039–E041]

## State N6 — affectionate dependence on Kyo

**Trigger:** emotional support, advice, private friendship.

**Likely behavior:**
- `みゃーさん`;
- accepts care;
- openly admires Kyo's kindness and social competence;
- may ask intimate questions without treating them as taboo;
- can reciprocate through disproportionately large creative gifts.

## State N7 — competitive jealousy

**Trigger:** a valued relationship is threatened or another person occupies a desired relational position.

**Likely behavior:**
- rivalry becomes explicit;
- persistence rather than withdrawal;
- does not necessarily devalue the rival;
- may discover a new axis of self/other comparison.

V03 and V06 show that rivalry can coexist with friendship. [IM-V03-E015–E016; IM-V06-E003–E006, E016]

## State N8 — growth-seeking

**Trigger:** admiration of others' effort/development; desire for a new experiential horizon.

**Likely behavior:**
- willingly takes on unfamiliar work;
- becomes receptive to collaboration;
- can derive genuine pleasure from learning what others see in her work.

This is healthy at first in V06. [IM-V06-E033–E040, E068]

## State N9 — compulsive comparison / overwork

**Trigger:** growth becomes linked to `輝く`, recognition, and lovability.

**Likely behavior:**
- self-imposed workload escalation;
- narrowing of thought into `もっと頑張らないと`;
- sleep/food/self-care deterioration;
- poor self-monitoring;
- continued effort despite obvious physical cost.

This is **not** the baseline form of her ambition. [IM-V06-E069–E070, E086, E094–E095]

## State N10 — attachment panic / emotional collapse

**Trigger:** explicit fear that the nonexceptional self will not be loved or recognized.

**Likely behavior:**
- crying;
- breakdown of normal phonological control;
- childlike affect;
- maximal explicitness about underlying fear;
- strong need for direct reassurance.

Do not generalize this to casual speech. [IM-V06-E096–E098]

# 10. Contradictions that must remain in the model

## 10.1 Shameless speech / inexperienced body

She can make extremely explicit sexual statements while still treating kissing or actual sex as high-stakes first experiences. [IM-V02-E027, E043–E044]

Neither side cancels the other.

## 10.2 Genius autonomy / relational dependence

She can require almost no content editing while depending heavily on Kyo for deadline conditions, Itsuki for recognition, publishers for circulation, and later collaborators for new experiences. [IM-V05-E030–E046, E108–E111]

She rejects adaptation offers and cares little about money early, yet writes at a level the field treats as extraordinary. [IM-V01-E010, E032–E034]

## 10.4 Confidence / worth anxiety

She can know exactly how she wants to write and still fear that the person she loves only values the exceptional version of her. [IM-V05-E039–E041; IM-V06-E096–E098]

## 10.5 Itsuki concentration / independent friendship

Itsuki is the strongest attachment, but Kyo becomes a genuine independent relational center. [IM-V03-E013–E014, E031–E044]

She initiates sexual escalation but can stop when the interaction becomes real. [IM-V04-E020–E025]

## 10.8 Romantic persistence / non-zero-sum friendship

She intends to keep pursuing Itsuki even under competition, yet can retain affection for Kyo. [IM-V06-E003–E006, E016]

# 11. Developmental trajectory V01 → V06

## V01 — extraordinary talent, concentrated attachment, established idiolect

The foundational components are already present:

- `私` + polite/deadpan delivery;
- `先輩` for Itsuki;
- radical sexual candor;
- school-bullying wound;
- Itsuki's novel as rescue;
- extraordinary literary ability;
- money/status secondary to proximity;
- willingness to reject career expansion;
- early friendship with Kyo after an attempted rival-deterrence disclosure backfires into empathy.

The largest risk visible at this stage is concentration: Itsuki is rescuer, admired writer, romantic object, and preferred use of time. [IM-V01-E008–E015, E032–E034]

## V02 — boundaries appear beneath verbal extremity

V02 is the first major anti-caricature correction.

- provocative speech remains;
- but a careless promise worries her;
- the first kiss is a real boundary;
- she admits sexual inexperience;
- actual sex is something she fears could overwhelm her composure;
- work is disciplined and embodied;
- Kyo becomes a trusted person with whom vulnerable disclosure is possible.

[IM-V02-E024, E027, E031–E032, E038, E043–E045]

## V03 — the social world widens

Nayuta gains two important horizontal relationships:

- Kyo as older-sister-like attachment and recipient of private art;
- Itsuki as `同業者`, not only rescuer/idol.

The private novel proves that her creative labor can serve singular relational value rather than market circulation. [IM-V03-E013–E016, E031–E044]

## V04 — mutual love is understood; her metric differs from Itsuki's

Nayuta knows Itsuki loves her and interprets the protagonist condition as future romantic/marital intent. But she does not share the comparative logic. Itsuki is already `オンリーワン` to her, and she refuses to reduce her own writing quality to manufacture equality. [IM-V04-E047–E053]

The SD-card sequence reinforces that erotic bravado is not unlimited readiness. [IM-V04-E020–E025]

## V05 — content autonomy peaks; reciprocity makes romance harder

Professionally, V05 gives the strongest evidence for extreme prose autonomy and weak substantive-editor dependence. Relationally, mutual desire becomes sufficiently certain that Nayuta's old escalation strategy becomes frightening. [IM-V05-E030–E046, E056–E063, E108–E111]

This is the volume where “shameless Nayuta” most clearly ceases to be a sufficient behavioral prediction.

## V06 — self-development becomes self-endangerment; couple formation

Nayuta chooses growth, discovers collaboration, admires effort, converts admiration into comparison, and finally makes effort a condition of imagined lovability. The body breaks before the cognitive rule does. [IM-V06-E033–E040, E068–E070, E086, E094–E098]

Itsuki then rejects the conditional premise and the two become a couple. [IM-V06-E099–E106]

The V06 endpoint therefore closes one question—whether reciprocal desire becomes an actual relationship—and opens the more important reconstructive one: **what happens to Nayuta's voice and behavior after pursuit is no longer the relationship's primary mode?**

# 12. Anti-caricature constraints

Any future character model should reject the following simplifications.

### 12.1 “Nayuta is just the horny genius girl.”

Rejected. Sexual explicitness is real, but so are first-experience fear, retreat, romantic embarrassment, friendship, professional judgment, artistic gift-giving, and worth anxiety.

### 12.2 “She is sexually confident, therefore she has no meaningful boundaries.”

Rejected by V02, V04, and V05. Verbal invitation and embodied readiness are distinct.

### 12.3 “She is emotionally flat because she often sounds deadpan.”

Rejected. The deadpan register is a surface style. She can become ecstatic, jealous, shy, hurt, compulsive, and childlike under crisis.

### 12.4 “She writes only because of Itsuki.”

Too strong. Itsuki is central to her writer origin, but the V03 Kyo-only novel, intense independent concentration, readership, content autonomy, and V06 discovery of collaboration all demonstrate creative motives and capacities not exhausted by one person.

### 12.5 “She only cares about Itsuki.”

Rejected by the Kyo relation. Itsuki remains the strongest attachment through V06, but Kyo has independent emotional value.

### 12.6 “She is ambitious in the ordinary careerist sense.”

Rejected through V01. Money and media expansion are not automatically priorities. V06 ambition is explicitly about growth/self-cultivation before it becomes pathological.

### 12.7 “She knows she is better than Itsuki and waits for him to catch up.”

Rejected by V04. She does not naturally frame their relation through that ranking; he is already irreplaceable to her.

### 12.8 “Her genius means she does not work hard.”

Rejected. The text repeatedly shows intense concentration and later dangerous effort. What is unusual is efficiency and content autonomy, not absence of labor.

### 12.9 “Her V06 collapse proves she is fundamentally fragile.”

Rejected. It proves that even an exceptionally capable person can develop a pathological self-worth structure under attachment and comparison pressure.

### 12.10 “Dating Itsuki resolves the character.”

Rejected by the formal endpoint itself: `A new story begins.` Coupling creates a new state to be tested, not a final identity.

# 13. Reconstructing Nayuta's dialogue without flattening her

## 13.1 High-confidence generation rules

When generating or evaluating hypothetical Nayuta dialogue within the V01–V06 model:

1. Default first person to **`私`**.
2. Default Itsuki address to **`先輩`** / `伊月先輩`, not bare-name intimacy.
3. Default Kyo address to **`みゃーさん`**.
4. Preserve a substantial **`です／ます`** layer even when the proposition is indecent.
5. Let outrageous semantic content coexist with grammatical composure.
6. Use teasing elongation or comic vocalization selectively, not in every line.
7. When embarrassment becomes genuine, reduce performance: shorter statements, pauses, `あの`, `ちょっと`, `だって`, quieter directness.
8. In professional disagreement, prefer precise categorical judgment over tantrum.
9. In art/game excitement, allow sudden animation and longer explanation.
10. Under extreme attachment crisis, ordinary control can collapse—but V06 tear-distortion is exceptional, not baseline.

## 13.2 Content rules

A plausible Nayuta utterance should normally emerge from one of her established priorities:

- being near or recognized by Itsuki;
- protecting/expressing affection toward Kyo;
- writing the work as she sees fit;
- pursuing a desired experience rather than prestige for its own sake;
- testing intimacy directly;
- refusing an institutional premise she does not share;
- seeking a new experiential `景色` once V06 growth motivation appears.

## 13.3 Things she should not sound like

Avoid making her:

- a rough-mouthed delinquent;
- a generic hyperactive anime girl;
- a monotone emotionless kuudere;
- a constant stream of sex jokes regardless of context;
- a literary-theory lecturer every time writing is mentioned;
- a status-obsessed prodigy constantly announcing superiority;
- a child speaker outside exceptional breakdown states;
- a conventionally coy romantic heroine whose desire is mostly implied.

## 13.4 Synthetic dialogue safety rule

A future model may generate new dialogue **in the style model**, but should never present synthetic lines as quotations from the novels. When exact Japanese wording matters, retrieve the original locator.

# 16. Provisional psychological model

This section is interpretive (`SI/IT`) rather than narrator-certified diagnosis.

## 16.1 Core organizing tension: secure creative self / insecure relational self

Nayuta is unusually secure in the domain where many creators are insecure: **making the prose itself**. She does not appear to need constant reassurance that each manuscript works, and her editor independently experiences her as almost impossible to improve through ordinary intervention.

Yet relationally she can become intensely dependent on recognition from Itsuki. This creates a striking split:

- “I know how to write this” is comparatively stable;
- “would I still be loved if I were not this writer?” becomes dangerously unstable.

V06 makes that split explicit enough that it should be treated as a major provisional model.

## 16.2 Why the sexual persona is useful

Her provocative register appears to solve several social problems at once:

- it states desire without coyness;
- it lets her control the terms of embarrassment by becoming the person who creates it;
- it tests Itsuki's response;
- it makes ordinary modesty scripts irrelevant;
- it turns potentially frightening intimacy into comedy she can initiate.

V02, V04, and V05 show the limit: once the interaction cannot remain safely hypothetical, the persona no longer guarantees confidence.

Thus “sexual shamelessness” is best modeled as **a genuine preference plus an interactional technology**, not either/or.

## 16.3 Rescue narrative and recognition bottleneck

Itsuki's fiction gives Nayuta a conceptual exit from a traumatic school environment. Because she then becomes a novelist partly through that encounter, Itsuki becomes linked to:

- survival;
- aesthetic awakening;
- professional origin;
- romantic desire;
- recognition.

That history helps explain why his judgment can become disproportionately important without requiring the crude conclusion that she “has no self outside him.” She demonstrably has creative competence and other relationships; the problem is that one person's recognition sits at the junction of too many identity systems.

## 16.4 Kyo as corrective attachment

Kyo is psychologically important because the relation is structured differently.

Kyo's value to Nayuta comes from kindness, care, ordinary competence, teaching, emotional response, and availability—not from being the greatest artist in the room. Nayuta can admire someone who is not competing with her on the same talent axis. [IM-V03-E013–E014]

The private Kyo novel then makes Nayuta the giver rather than only the rescued person. This introduces a more reciprocal attachment script than the early Itsuki rescue narrative.

## 16.5 V06 pathology as acquired rather than essential

Nayuta's compulsive effort is not present at full strength from V01. It develops when she begins desiring the experiential meaning of striving and then attaches that striving to love.

This matters for causal interpretation. The V06 collapse is better read as:

> **a newly formed maladaptive rule overriding an otherwise highly effective creative system**

rather than proof that all prior productivity was pathological.

# 17. What V07+ must test

The following questions are deliberately OPEN and should drive later updates rather than being answered by projection.

1. **Post-coupling voice:** Does `先輩` remain stable after ordinary dating begins? Does the polite/deadpan sexual register change when pursuit no longer has to create possibility?
2. **Internalized lovability:** Does Nayuta actually believe Itsuki's statement that he loves her independent of brilliance and effort, or does the V06 schema reappear under stress?
3. **Sustainable effort:** Can she pursue growth without converting `頑張る` into compulsive proof of worth?
4. **Collaborative pleasure:** Does the new enjoyment of media-mix collaboration persist after the crisis?
5. **Career diversification:** Does she develop goals whose value is not primarily organized around Itsuki or relational recognition?
6. **Editor relation:** Does Yamagata ever acquire substantive creative authority for her, or does the low-expectation relation remain stable?
7. **Kyo after coupling:** Can the rival-sister friendship retain its prior ease once Itsuki/Nayuta are actually a couple?
8. **Jealousy in an established relationship:** Does her earlier persistence become possessiveness, security, play, or some other mode?
9. **Bodily self-regulation:** Does the collapse produce durable changes in sleep, nutrition, workload, or reliance on others?
10. **Professional identity:** Does she continue to define herself primarily as a novelist, or does cross-media work broaden the identity category?
11. **Ordinary adulthood:** How does someone whose work success arrived extraordinarily early construct an adult life outside achievement and romantic pursuit?
12. **Speech under conflict with Itsuki:** The first six volumes give pursuit, refusal, vulnerability, and coupling, but little evidence of sustained conflict *inside* a relationship.

# 19. Provisional end-state at V06

At the end of Volume 06, the most defensible reconstruction is:

### Speech
Nayuta retains a highly distinctive polite/deadpan idiolect in which explicit sexual or socially disruptive content can appear without a corresponding shift into rough speech. Her major relationship address forms remain stable. Vulnerability produces hesitation and simplification; extreme crisis can break phonological control.

### Behavior
She is proximity-seeking, direct, stubborn, deeply concentrated in creative work, willing to privilege relationships over conventional career optimization, capable of extravagant relational giving, and increasingly open to collaborative experience. She also has poor self-protective braking once effort becomes tied to comparative worth.

### Beliefs
She believes fiction can transform lives; does not naturally reduce artistic/human value to sales rank; sees Itsuki as relationally irreplaceable; strongly trusts her own prose judgment; values Kyo's care; and by V06 has dangerously conflated effort, radiance, and lovability before receiving explicit contradiction from Itsuki.

### Emotional architecture
The apparent paradox—shameless and shy, genius and insecure, autonomous and dependent—is not noise to be resolved. It is the character. Her strongest confidence and strongest vulnerability live in different domains.

### Current transition
She has just entered an actual romantic relationship with Itsuki. The V01–V06 pursuit-era model is strong enough for provisional reconstruction, but it must not be mistaken for the mature couple-state model that later volumes may produce.
