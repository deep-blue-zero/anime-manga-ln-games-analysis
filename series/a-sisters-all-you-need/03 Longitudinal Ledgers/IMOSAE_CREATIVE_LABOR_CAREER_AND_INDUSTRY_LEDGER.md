## V02 - career and industry deltas

### I-10 - Private concept intensity does not guarantee reader legibility
V02's opening sample-prose and monster-sister discussion distinguishes the creator's internally coherent concept from the editor's problem of making the intended category/atmosphere legible to readers. This STRENGTHENS I-01/I-02 rather than reducing editing to market censorship. Evidence: IM-V02-E001-E006.

### I-11 - Illustration can be generative rather than downstream decoration
Author/editor direction varies by work and scene; at one extreme the illustrator receives broad freedom and prose can be adjusted around the image. This STRENGTHENS the collaborative-product thesis. Evidence: IM-V02-E013-E015.

### I-12 - Creative input is embodied and transformable
Mixed-bathing fear becomes access to a fictional hero's fear; Chihiro's rebuke becomes dialogue for Hilde. The transfer is affective/functional rather than literal autobiography. Evidence: IM-V02-E016-E019, E033-E037.

### I-13 - Tools are part of embodied craft
Nayuta's Pomera/other-device discussion presents tool preference as habit, fit, and bodily workflow rather than ideology. Evidence: IM-V02-E031-E032.

### I-14 - Deadlines are network events
The distinction between provisional deadline and true production deadline exposes externalities: a late manuscript shifts work onto editor, illustrator, designer, proofreader, editorial hierarchy, sales, and household support. Toki's confinement tactics are comic/coercive; the underlying production dependency is analytically separate. Evidence: IM-V02-E047-E060.

### I-15 - Professional systems can fail without a sufficient single villain
Haruto's anime adaptation involves sincere participation and effort, yet resource diversion and cascading production pressure produce a result he experiences as failure. The represented scenario supports systemic/emergent causation, not a universal documentary claim about anime production. Evidence: IM-V02-E071-E083.

### I-16 - Market calculation and authentic attachment can coexist
Haruto researched marketable structures but genuinely likes the structures he chose. His `industrial product` metaphor collapses when the adaptation hurts and reader encounters restore him. This REVISES any simple artist/craftsman authenticity binary. Evidence: IM-V02-E074-E089.

### I-17 - Reader encounter is a distinct value axis
After loss of control over adaptation, seeing people buy the novels re-establishes a direct author-reader relation without restoring total control over the media system. Evidence: IM-V02-E084-E089.

### I-18 - Creative careers remain precarious even among award winners
The hanami/professional-cohort discussion records attrition and instability. Persistence must not be romanticized as inevitable destiny. Evidence: IM-V02-E062-E063.

## V03 additions

### I-19 — Synopsis copy is anticipatory publishing craft

Synopses may be required two to three months before release, including before the underlying manuscript is finished. They must preserve reader pleasure, generate anticipation, fit strict length/voice constraints, and function as packaging. This is a distinct professional skill rather than a miniature version of novel-writing. Evidence: IM-V03-E017–E025.

### I-20 — Role ownership can be competence-driven rather than rigid

Itsuki assumes responsibility for his own synopsis after disliking Toki's debut-volume copy; Toki accepts the arrangement because the person who can do it better should do so `作品のため`. This strengthens the model of publishing as coordinated role allocation rather than a fixed author/editor boundary. Evidence: IM-V03-E017–E018.

### I-21 — Promotional truth can be strategically underdetermined

Itsuki writes suggestive synopsis language about scenes not yet drafted and defends the copy as literally nonfalse. V03 therefore distinguishes factual falsity from rhetoric designed to induce an expectation. Evidence: IM-V03-E024–E025.

### I-22 — Audience enjoyment is Haruto's positive craft principle

After V02's `工業製品` defense collapses, V03 identifies a constructive origin: `人に楽しんでもらうことは、楽しい`. TRPG redesign after actual play becomes a small laboratory for improving systems rather than secretly compensating through GM intervention. Evidence: IM-V03-E059–E065.

### I-23 — A work can be deliberately withheld from the market

Nayuta writes roughly a bunko-volume fantasy for Kyo alone. Haruto immediately recognizes large commercial value, but Nayuta refuses publication because the work is a private gift. This opens a nonmarket value mode without implying that private art is inherently superior. Evidence: IM-V03-E031–E044.

### I-24 — Recognition milestones are instrumental, not terminal

Itsuki chooses television anime adaptation as a provisional foothold toward becoming publicly legible beside Nayuta; Haruto recognizes that adaptation is not his goal. Toki then announces the adaptation. V03 therefore distinguishes adaptation status from creator endpoint or total validation. Evidence: IM-V03-E082–E092.

### I-25 — Creative vocation can be self-authoring conversion

Bonus fiction directly frames Itsuki's first novel as an attempt to transform sister desire, longing, heartbreak, inferiority, anger, hope, and protagonist hunger into fiction. The move from consumer/recommender to entrant in a newcomer award is simultaneously vocational and psychological. Evidence: IM-V03-E099–E115.

### I-26 — Creator support can become independent paid competence

Chihiro's V02 bookkeeping interest becomes actual Saturday employment/training at Ashley's office. The support skill is no longer confined to unpaid domestic labor inside Itsuki's apartment. Evidence: IM-V03-E054–E055.

## V03 state summary

V03 **STRENGTHENS** the adult-life-system model while adding two non-equivalent value modes. ## V04 additions

### I-27 — Brand strategy is an explicit creator skill and risk multiplier

Itsuki wants simultaneous anime/manga/publication activity to increase recognition of `羽島伊月`. The narration treats the strategy as rational while Toki warns that the workload is materially dangerous. Career strategy is therefore neither vanity nor free upside. Evidence: IM-V04-E004–E007.

### I-28 — Adaptation opportunity is a substitutable industrial slot, not metaphysical reward

The anime offer exists because another work falls out of a preexisting production formation; another comparably selling title can replace Itsuki if he refuses. The opportunity is real and partly earned by sales, but not uniquely destined for him. Evidence: IM-V04-E028–E041.

### I-29 — Professional care can require preserving refusal against institutional incentive

Toki has ordinary company reasons to secure approval yet explicitly warns Itsuki about creator damage and confirms that refusal remains available. Coordination and care are compatible when risk is made legible before consent. Evidence: IM-V04-E031–E040.

### I-30 — Creative competence is medium- and task-structured

The manga candidates separate composition, cute-character rendering, stylistic imitation, sequential readability, source understanding, commercial force, and source attachment. Even a spectacular illustrator is not automatically the best sequential-manga adapter. Evidence: IM-V04-E054–E068, E084.

### I-31 — Fidelity can require medium-specific transformation

Kaiko's strongest adaptation work invents gestures and page logic absent from the prose while preserving the priorities that make the characters recognizable to Itsuki. Fidelity is therefore not surface sameness. Evidence: IM-V04-E059–E068. Claim route: `IM-C017`.

### I-32 — Compromise can be additive synthesis rather than dilution

Itsuki's nudity priority and Kaiko's lingerie priority initially conflict as mutually exclusive aesthetic demands. Research and sequencing produce a third form in which lingerie becomes characterization/progression and nudity remains culmination. Evidence: IM-V04-E074–E084, E092–E104. Claim route: `IM-C018`.

### I-33 — Bad outcome does not erase good process knowledge

Haruto can judge the broadcast as painful while preserving enjoyable auditions, design meetings, music, scripts, and recording as genuine professional experiences. Process quality, output quality, and future agency are analytically separate. Evidence: IM-V04-E105–E112.

### I-34 — Support labor can become a self-authored creative-industry vocation

Kyo begins to see editorial work as a concrete professional possibility. This does not require discovering hidden artistic genius, but it remains unclear whether she can choose the work for herself rather than primarily to support exceptional others. Evidence: IM-V04-E146–E150.

### I-35 — Professional resilience may be cooperative rather than winner-take-all

The BONUS_FICTION writer-life game separates money from happiness, reveals a cooperative victory condition, and models peers/family as survival infrastructure. Because the source is bonus fiction/game formalization, this is a strong formal proposition rather than documentary baseline chronology. Evidence: IM-V04-E159–E171. Claim route: `IM-C019`.

## V04 state summary

V04 converts the distributed-causality model into a theory of **media mix and professional substitutability**. Institutions can offer real opportunity without making a creator unique; collaborators can alter a work without betraying it; and conflict can generate a more specific form than either creator initially imagined. The strongest corrective to solitary sovereignty is not that authors lack agency, but that agency is exercised inside negotiated systems where other creators, media grammars, production structures, audiences, and peers can materially change both work and creator.

## V05 — creative labor / career / industry delta

### 1. Editorial labor becomes visible as infrastructure

V05 inventories the work around the manuscript: shipping author proofs/samples/contracts/goods, copying, proof preparation, internal approvals, surveys, phone coverage, errands, event preparation, schedule communication, and crisis handling. Toki explicitly adds **motivation management** to the editor's job. Evidence: IM-V05-E005–E007, E024–E029.

**State:** STRENGTHEN. Publishing remains neither pure textual collaboration nor pure administration; its invisible coordination layer is materially necessary.

### 2. Deadline discipline is a network obligation

Yamagata rejects the idea that a release date is a private agreement between author and editor. Delay propagates to readers, distributors, bookstores, printers, proofreaders, publicity, sales, and company trust. Evidence: IM-V05-E026–E029.

The comic deadline taxonomy adds bargaining power: a `魔王` may be nearly impossible to discipline precisely because sales make the author indispensable. Evidence: IM-V05-E041–E045.

**State:** STRENGTHEN `IM-C003`, `IM-C013`.

### 3. Nayuta forces a refinement of creative autonomy

Nayuta can write rapidly across devices and rejects substantive editorial intervention; Yamagata independently says she would change almost nothing but errors. Evidence: IM-V05-E032–E040, E108–E111.

At the same time, Kyo supplies access, deadline structure, affective comfort, interruption shielding, and communication. Evidence: IM-V05-E030–E038.

**State:** REVISE `IM-C001`. Distinguish **content/imaginative autonomy** from **production, schedule, circulation, and affective interdependence**.

### 4. Publishers as category-makers

Kobe explicitly distinguishes false totalizing claims that “recent light novels are all X” from the real institutional mechanism by which publishers can multiply successful patterns into genres, markets, movements, images, and eras. Evidence: IM-V05-E046.

**State:** STRENGTHEN `IM-C003`; contributes directly to new `IM-C022`.

### 5. Anime production: inherited constraints and plural professional stakes

The anime is a replacement project. Schedule, sponsor/committee formation, staff, and business assumptions preexist meaningful author control. Hirugano's weak first structure is not evidence of simple laziness; he loved the canceled project and has his own career at risk. Tarui is tasked with `敗戦処理` and describes concrete cost/cast/action/package constraints. Evidence: IM-V05-E066–E095.

Itsuki's participation creates a real tradeoff with novel pace and burnout. The final structure emerges only after repeated author/scriptwriter/director conflict. Evidence: IM-V05-E090–E095, E176–E183.

**State:** STRENGTHEN `IM-C013`, `IM-C017`, `IM-C018`.

### 6. Editorial contribution can persist without content intervention

Yamagata is important because the series does not rescue editorial dignity by inventing hidden story-level input. She says Nayuta's prose gives her almost nothing to change. Her professional attachment instead resides in publication, responsibility, and first readership. Evidence: IM-V05-E108–E111.

**State:** OPEN. Track whether later volumes give Yamagata a different form of contribution.

### 7. Newcomer awards as field-reproduction systems

The award chapter establishes:

- layered labor from readers and editors;
- anonymized profiles as an anti-bias aspiration;
- weak relation between prize rank and later sales;
- packaging/marketing influence;
- conflict between completed-work fairness and revision potential;
- the grand prize as the award's public `顔`;
- guaranteed top prizes as applicant recruitment;
- and a final result produced through compromise. Evidence: IM-V05-E138–E155.

**State:** OPEN new `IM-C022` — awards and institutional recognition are constructed signaling/recruitment systems, not neutral intrinsic-worth meters.

### 8. Longevity becomes a separate career metric

Kaizu is intentionally described as middle-tier, not secretly elite. Haruto admires fourteen years of continued professional survival while a genuine genius contemporary disappeared. Evidence: IM-V05-E161–E167.

**State:** OPEN new `IM-C021` — career endurance/longevity is a distinct success axis.

### 9. Reception feeds back into production

Haruto states that Kyo's tears after his anime failure kept him from collapsing, and narration generalizes that creators need hope that their work reaches someone. Evidence: IM-V05-E170–E173.

**State:** OPEN new `IM-C020` — audience/reception/care can be causally productive creative infrastructure.

## V06 — creative labor / career / industry delta

### Illustration can rewrite prose

`新世界の創妹記` supplies the strongest mainline example so far of bidirectional media influence. Setsuna's visual interpretation clarifies a world Itsuki had only vaguely imagined; Itsuki then substantially revises the prose, and narration attributes improved finished quality and likely sales retention to the change. **STRENGTHEN `IM-C001`, `IM-C017`, `IM-C018`.** Evidence: IM-V06-E025–E027.

### Nayuta's field power produces externalities

Nayuta's decision to pursue three simultaneous media-mix projects mobilizes elite directors, writers, actors, manga creators, sponsors, and budgets with exceptional ease. Narration then changes scale: other projects lose people/resources and unknown creators' dreams/futures are harmed without Nayuta necessarily knowing it. The correct industrial claim is not individual villainy but **institutional amplification of private desire into field-level consequences**. Evidence: IM-V06-E033–E040.

### Kyo: competence is not vocation

The GF staff's unusually strong farewell is grounded in ordinary work reliability—initiative, attention, punctuality, communication, questions, honesty about mistakes—rather than hidden genius. Kobe can imagine full-time hiring, but Kyo explicitly separates `向いているもの` from `なりたいもの`. **OPEN `IM-C028`.** Evidence: IM-V06-E041–E043.

### Cast selection is multi-objective optimization

The audition field contains dozens of plausible candidates per role. Acting ability competes with youth, appearance, singing/event work, sponsor relationships, agency politics, audience conversion, source fit, career development, and project economics. Haruto's objection to using a life-defining work as a newcomer training ground is intelligible; Oshima's need to produce a financially sustainable media product is also intelligible. Narration explicitly locates the conflict in professional position and preserves uncertainty about whether the final middle-ground cast is correct. Evidence: IM-V06-E048–E057.

**Industry inference:** “best cast” is not a single-axis ranking discoverable before production. It is a negotiated decision under multiple objectives, asymmetric stakes, and incomplete information.

### Drama CD: medium and schedule change authorship

Itsuki discovers that removing prose narration creates new craft problems: dialogue and sound effects must carry situation and setting. Special-edition production also moves the true deadline earlier and reduces postponement flexibility because more people and packaging steps depend on the manuscript. Evidence: IM-V06-E065–E067.

At recording, Itsuki explicitly asks actors to make his characters shine through their power. The author therefore recognizes derivative performance as additive rather than merely preservative. Evidence: IM-V06-E087–E091.

### Publicity is production too—and can be nontransparent

The in-world report manga is instructed to depict pre-recording harmony regardless of the observed room. `世の中とはそういうものだ` converts publicity from transparent record into managed representation. **OPEN `IM-C027`.** Evidence: IM-V06-E087–E088.

### Survival infrastructure becomes explicit

The newcomer-award party functions partly as a ritual for confirming another year of professional survival. Only two of Itsuki/Haruto's five-person cohort remain present/active five award cycles later; Kaizu pushes younger writers toward health checks; Haruto hopes new competitors survive. V05's longevity hypothesis is therefore strongly reinforced. Evidence: IM-V06-E071–E085.

## V07 additions

### I-44 — Professional maturation can reduce necessary control without reducing artistic identity

Toki's five-year perspective on Itsuki indicates that the author now requires less direct management/control than before. Itsuki also distinguishes the author-self from what appears in fiction. This strengthens a developmental model of author/editor relations rather than treating dependence or resistance as fixed traits. Evidence: IM-V07-E033–E052.

### I-45 — Genre templates are shared structures, not proofs of work-equivalence

The `テンプレ` debate separates recurring structures from total sameness. Aioi's argument is that abstraction can reveal common form without exhausting differences among actual works; Haruto adds that market calculation can coexist with genuine affection for a genre. Evidence: IM-V07-E144–E158.

### I-46 — Visibility changes the exposure ecology of authorship

Yuu's large success and media expansion increase the scale at which attention reaches her. The represented history distinguishes engaged readers from a wider public environment in which hostility and judgment can reach a creator who did not choose each encounter. Evidence: IM-V07-E159–E182. Claim route: `IM-C029` **OPEN**.

### I-47 — Durable moderate happiness can be an affirmative professional telos

Kaizu's response to Yuu's death reframes the ambition to remain an ordinary, moderately happy writer over a long career as a chosen success criterion rather than consolation for failing to become a genius. Evidence: IM-V07-E159–E182. Claim route: `IM-C030` **OPEN**.

### I-48 — Skilled labor may change value mode without ceasing to be skilled labor

In the bonus child-story sequence, Itsuki initially insists that professional-quality writing should be properly compensated, then later chooses to participate voluntarily as challenge/gift. The change in transaction does not make the work unskilled; payment, gift, play, and vocation are distinct value modes. Evidence: IM-V07-E193–E202.

## V10 — creative labor, career, and industry delta

**Ledger rule added:** awards, debut, and even adaptation are thresholds within a probabilistic career field, not terminal validation. This strengthens longevity/recoverability tracking and should be used when later volumes discuss “making it.”

### `3年縛り`: revised institutional-governance model

Kyo preserves the objection that consequential hidden expectations can be unfair. Toki adds that strict codification may create de facto exclusivity and remove informal escape routes or deadline flexibility. The governing V10 formulation is therefore:

> Informal norms are ethically risky when knowledge, exception power, or exit is asymmetrically distributed; formalization can improve transparency but can also harden discretion into stricter coercion. Evaluate transparency, negotiability, accountability, bargaining power, and exit options rather than treating written/unwritten status as a moral binary.

Evidence: IM-V10-E135–E161.

Kyo chooses Branch Hill despite Gift offering a more obvious status/network route. She wants a path not acquired through Itsuki/Nayuta and a place where she can test herself. The `銀色景色` film is praised for understanding the source and optimizing it for film rather than reproducing superficial appearance. ### Capacity protection

Toki recommends that Itsuki rest even though the media-mix moment makes delay costly. This is the positive counterpart to V08/V09 over-allocation: professional stewardship includes recognizing when pressure cannot manufacture capacity. Evidence: IM-V10-E340–E376.

## V10 industry-state synthesis

V10's industry model is neither meritocracy nor determinist exploitation. Creative careers are **distributed probabilistic systems**: writer skill and effort matter, but so do editors, packaging, norms, translation, adaptation, public timing, niche readers, market conditions, capacity, and luck. Institutional flexibility can be protective or abusive depending on how discretion is governed. Most importantly, demand for a work and capacity to create the work are distinct variables.

### V13 industry synthesis

V13's professional thesis is **legible pluralism**.
