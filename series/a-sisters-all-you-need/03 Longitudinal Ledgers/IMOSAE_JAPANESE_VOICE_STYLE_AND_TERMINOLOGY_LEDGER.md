# IMOSAE Japanese Voice, Style, and Terminology Ledger

This ledger records **textual Japanese voice**, not acoustic acting/performance.

## V01 idiolect baselines

### 羽島伊月
- First person: `俺`.
- High-frequency theatrical posture: `フハハ`, mock-villain/genius bombast.
- Rough address/lexicon: `貴様`, `馬鹿め`, `愚問だな`, insults, deliberately overwrought declarations.
- Under embarrassment or emotional pressure, grandiosity breaks into fillers, stammering, or nonsense-like vocalization.
- To 那由多: frequently `カニ公`, irreverent deformation that preserves intimacy through insult rather than honorific politeness.
- **Interpretive marker:** register itself is defensive performance; track when it collapses.

### 可児那由多
- First person: `私`.
- To 伊月: `先輩`.
- Frequently polite/deadpan `です／ます` even when content is sexually extreme or socially outrageous.
- To 京: `みゃーさん`, signaling friendly intimacy.
- Can shift from flat composure into passionate technical/aesthetic defense (e.g., game rules) or elaborate improvisational prose.
- **Marker:** content/register dissonance is a core comic voice device.

### 白川京
- First person: `あたし`.
- Contemporary colloquial speech, quick direct objections, self-correction under embarrassment.
- Less stylized than creator peers; useful “ordinary” social register against which their eccentricity reads.
- Inner language becomes much harsher and more hierarchical when she evaluates her worth relative to 那由多.

### 不破春斗
- First person: `オレ`.
- Casual young-male register: `～だろ`, `～じゃん`, easy conversational smoothing.
- Comfortable with marketing/industry terminology and teasing.
- Interior voice becomes more severe around originality/talent comparison than surface banter suggests.

### 羽島千尋
- First person: `僕`.
- To 伊月: `兄さん`.
- Soft, careful, often polite register; minimal aggressive language.
- `僕` supports the externally assumed male frame but cannot alone establish gender identity; final narration explicitly reclassifies 千尋.
- Track future changes in address/politeness around disclosure.

### 土岐健次郎
- Rough professional directness; frequent strong imperatives/exclamations.
- Publishing/editorial vocabulary integrated into ordinary speech.
- Anger/slurring under alcohol exaggerates but does not replace competence.

### 恵那刹那／ぷりけつ
- First person: `自分`.
- Sentence ending/tic: `～ッス`.
- To 伊月: `センセー`.
- Energetic youthful slang and spontaneous enthusiasm.

### 大野アシュリー
- First person: `ワタシ`.
- Controlled feminine/high-handed register (`～かしら`, directive endings) combined with technical tax/accounting vocabulary.
- Comic dominance operates through composure rather than 伊月-style shouting.

## V02 - idiolect and register deltas

### Itsuki
- Grandiose creator/aesthetic language remains stable, especially around the opening `sister` experiment.
- At embodied pressure points, performance drops into plainer language: the fiction/reality distinction, fear, deadline exhaustion, and relational memories are stronger voice-model evidence than the surrounding bombast.
- His deadline rationalization is linguistically important: he distinguishes provisional from true deadline and attempts to distribute blame, showing how professional vocabulary can become self-exculpatory.

### Nayuta
- Polite/deadpan delivery continues to carry extreme sexual content with relatively little surface register change.
- Serious boundaries simplify the voice: warnings against promises, first-kiss language, and admissions of inexperience are less performative than the sexual jokes.
- Voice reconstruction should therefore not model every Nayuta line as maximal provocation; plain/shy boundary language is now a required state.

### Kyo
- V02 adds a much richer interior voice: counterfactual, comparative, self-disqualifying, and repeatedly organized around whether she is useful, entitled to persist, or sufficiently talented.
- The earlier lie about sexual experience persists as an internal burden, illustrating how ordinary social performance becomes self-monitoring.

### Haruto
- Controlled market/craft language culminates in the `industrial product` metaphor, then collapses into repetitive, emotionally uncompressed attachment language when the adaptation fails.
- Behavioral rule: Haruto tends to metabolize creative vulnerability through analytic market language until pressure exceeds that defense.

### Chihiro
- Direct, concrete criticism becomes a major contrast with Itsuki's abstract claims. The adulthood rebuke is practical rather than philosophical and is strong enough to become usable fictional dialogue for Itsuki.

### Toki
- Publishing jargon and coercive deadline hyperbole coexist. GPS/crime/confinement language is comic exaggeration and should not be generalized as normal publisher practice, but it is central to his character voice under production crisis.

## V03 voice / terminology additions

### L-18 — `主人公になりたい`

`主人公` shifts from a technical narrative role to Itsuki's preferred vocabulary for non-derivative selfhood. He contrasts it with `脇役` and the anonymous relational description `２歳年上の同業者の男性`. Bonus fiction repeats the same desire years earlier after Ayane's rejection. Evidence: IM-V03-E082–E090, E107–E115.

**Translation caution:** “I want to be the protagonist” should normally preserve the metanarrative noun. Flattening it to “I want to matter” or “I want to be successful” loses the genre/self-authorship structure.

### L-19 — `同業者`

The same term carries opposite affective charge by focalization. Nayuta's recognition of Itsuki as `自分と同じ世界で競い合っている同業者` is horizontalization/peer recognition. Itsuki imagines being reduced to an unnamed `同業者の男性` as erasure. Evidence: IM-V03-E016, E082–E083.

### L-20 — `姉` / `妹` / `弟` as directional relation vocabulary

Nayuta calls Kyo `お姉ちゃん` because of enacted care and admiration; Itsuki generalizes `妹` beyond blood; Ayane rejects adolescent Itsuki as `弟`; he defensively replies that he needs no `姉` and only an `妹`. The lexicon therefore tracks directionality of care/status as well as literal kinship. Evidence: IM-V03-E013–E014, E102–E110.

### L-21 — `僕` → `俺` in the creator-birth scene

The bonus culminates in `僕が──いや、俺が主人公だ`. The self-correction marks a local adoption of forceful creator agency and connects the adolescent voice to adult Itsuki's grandiose `俺` persona. It must not be universalized into a general Japanese rule that `俺` is inherently more authentic. Evidence: IM-V03-E114–E115.

### L-22 — Kyo's performed sexual-adult register

Kyo explicitly admits that the persona of extensive romantic/sexual experience was false. V03 therefore confirms a speech-performance split previously inferred from discomfort. In future voice modeling, socially strategic bravado and trusted disclosure must remain distinct modes. Evidence: IM-V03-E036–E040.

### L-23 — `嘘は書いてない` and promotional literalism

Itsuki's synopsis defense uses literal nonfalsity to justify expectation-generating wording about unwritten events. The phrase is useful for tracking his rhetorical ethics around packaging: technically true can still be strategically suggestive. Evidence: IM-V03-E017–E025.

### L-24 — Haruto's creator origin in plain diction

`人に楽しんでもらうことは、楽しい。` strips away the earlier `工業製品` abstraction. The sentence's simplicity matters: audience pleasure is framed less as theory than recovered first principle. Evidence: IM-V03-E060–E062.

## V03 voice-state summary

V03 makes metanarrative vocabulary central to character psychology. Itsuki speaks about love and worth through literary roles; sibling terms carry status and directional care; and a pronoun shift marks the deliberate construction of his author persona. Kyo's confession confirms that register itself can function as social disguise, while Haruto's most important creator statement becomes simpler rather than more technical.

## V04 voice / terminology additions

### L-25 — `代わりはいる` / `オンリーワン`

V04 turns industrial substitutability into existential vocabulary. At the anime-slot level `代わりはいる` is a concrete institutional fact: another sufficiently successful title can occupy the opportunity. Itsuki enlarges that fact into a desired identity, wanting to become an `オンリーワン` writer. Nayuta uses the same word relationally: he is already a `かけがえのないオンリーワンの作家` to her. The shared lexeme therefore encodes incompatible metrics of scarcity. Evidence: IM-V04-E038–E041, E050.

**Translation caution:** do not flatten both uses to generic “special.” Itsuki's use is comparative/public and career-loaded; Nayuta's is relational/irreplaceable.

### L-26 — `対等の主人公` remains semantically under-specified but pragmatically legible

V04 does not provide an objective equality metric. It clarifies Nayuta's reception: she knows the phrase, understands its future-directed romantic force, and does not share the professional arithmetic behind it. The phrase remains powerful partly because Itsuki can load multiple forms of self-worth into an undefined narrative metaphor. Evidence: IM-V04-E047–E053.

### L-27 — `原作レイプ` is source-attributed polemical creator language

Multiple creators deploy `原作レイプ` hyperbolically when adaptation deviates from a personal aesthetic priority. The chapter then undermines the absolutism by producing a successful transformed work. This corpus must therefore preserve the phrase as character/source rhetoric and not adopt it as neutral analytical terminology. Evidence: IM-V04-E075–E084.

### L-28 — `不誠実` shifts aesthetic learning into moralized craft language

Itsuki calls his former inattentiveness to underwear/clothing `不誠実`. The term does not make clothing morally sacred; it records a change in what he believes a creator owes to characterization after Kaiko changes his perception. Evidence: IM-V04-E092–E095.

### L-29 — `リアルの妹がいない` is simultaneously LF and dramatic irony

The phrase is an explicit proposition in Itsuki's self-description and false relative to reader knowledge. Its surrounding claim—that absence permits infinite sister fantasy—must therefore be tracked as character-language causal theory rather than narrator-certified ontology. Evidence: IM-V04-E156–E158.

## V04 voice-state summary

V04 intensifies the series' habit of using **professional and narrative nouns as selfhood vocabulary**. `代わり`, `オンリーワン`, and `主人公` migrate across industrial, romantic, and existential registers; adaptation-conflict vocabulary (`原作レイプ`, `不誠実`) shows creators moralizing aesthetic priorities before collaboration revises them. Itsuki's false `リアルの妹がいない` proposition is especially important for voice reconstruction because confident declarative language can encode a sincere but factually wrong self-model.

Toki explicitly states that Itsuki understands strategic praise and deliberately performs being carried away in order to motivate himself. Evidence: IM-V05-E005–E007.

The swimsuit chapter shifts Nayuta from fluent sexual aggression toward embarrassment and careful testing. Her direct question `欲情しますか？` receives Itsuki's quiet `する`; narration follows with `恋ってほんとに難しい`. Evidence: IM-V05-E056–E063.

### V05 voice-model caution

These should be modeled as character-positioned arguments, not automatically as authorial doctrine. The afterword can corroborate thematic design only as PF.

## V06 — Japanese voice, style, and terminology delta

### `人間力`

V06 uses `人間力` as colloquial relational/human competence rather than a technical trait: seeing other people accurately, managing awkwardness, and sustaining relationships. Itsuki can be professionally capable while explicitly deficient on this axis. Evidence: IM-V06-E007–E011.

### `主人公ズ`

The plural chapter title is a formal pressure on Itsuki's singular protagonist ideology. The volume can hold Kyo, Haruto, Nayuta, and Itsuki as distinct centers of agency rather than one objective protagonist ladder. Evidence: IM-V06-E012–E020.

Itsuki's explicit line remains:

`俺はラブコメの主人公になりたいんじゃない。俺が理想とする超かっこいい物語の主人公になりたいんだ`

This confirms that `主人公` is existential/self-evaluative rather than merely a genre role. Evidence: IM-V06-E020.

### `景色` / `輝く`

Nayuta's series title becomes a vocabulary for experiential difference. She wants to see the `景色` visible to Itsuki and Kyo through striving. `輝く` initially names admired growth and later becomes an imperative: `私も輝かなきゃ`. Evidence: IM-V06-E033–E040, E068–E070.

### `もっと頑張らないと`

The single enormous paragraph repeating `もっと頑張らないと` is a direct **LF/FR** event. Explanation and ordinary syntax collapse into a recursive imperative. The form itself renders narrowed cognition and compulsive effort. Evidence: IM-V06-E086.

### `向いているものとなりたいものは違う`

Kyo's formulation cleanly separates **aptitude** (`向いている`) from **desired vocation/identity** (`なりたい`). Evidence: IM-V06-E043. Preserve the distinction in later career analysis; do not translate “good at editing” into “wants to be an editor.”

### `立場の違い`

The cast-audition narration explicitly frames disagreement as arising from standpoint rather than simple right/wrong error. Evidence: IM-V06-E055. `立場` here is institutional/professional position: participants optimize different goods because they answer to different stakeholders and risks.

### `折れた`

The narrator says Itsuki's earlier `決意` has `今ここに折れた`. Evidence: IM-V06-E100. “Broken” retains cost and discontinuity more strongly than a neutral “changed his mind.” His development is presented as a painful failure of a self-binding rule.

### `メインヒロイン`

Itsuki's `俺の物語のメインヒロイン` retains the life-as-story metaphor even after he abandons the equality gate. Evidence: IM-V06-E104. The volume does not reject narrative self-construction; it revises how that construction may treat other people.

### `A new story begins.`

The English final line occurs only after the couple is explicitly established. Evidence: IM-V06-E105–E106. It marks coupling as transition rather than telos/ending.

### V06 voice-state caution

Nayuta's tear-distorted phonology in the hospital scene (`わだし`, `いづきぜんばい`, etc.) is situational breakdown, not her normal idiolect. It is useful evidence of emotional disorganization but should not be added to her baseline voice model as ordinary pronunciation. Evidence: IM-V06-E097–E098.

## V07 additions

### 羽島伊月 — `対等の主人公` survives the relationship transition

V07 is important because Itsuki's old formulation remains linguistically available after coupling. The phrase no longer functions as a gate that prevents reciprocating Nayuta, but it still names his aspirational self-ranking and professional ambition. This supports **continuity with semantic revision**, not disappearance of the concept. Evidence: IM-V07-E002–E012, E066–E074.

### 可児那由多 — `甘える` and ordinary couple self-regulation

Nayuta's warning to herself not to `甘えすぎ` adds a different register of relational self-monitoring from V06's `もっと頑張らないと`. The former concerns reciprocity/dependence inside an achieved relationship; it should not automatically be translated as proof that the earlier achievement-for-lovability schema is resolved. Evidence: IM-V07-E020–E032.

### 羽島千尋 — `邪魔`, `僕`, and `兄さん`

Chihiro's fear of becoming `邪魔` is lexically compact but analytically heavy: the problem is not merely loss of chores, but whether continued presence itself will be unwanted. The established `僕` / `兄さん` register continues to sustain the false male category while expressing ordinary family intimacy. Evidence: IM-V07-E053–E064, E116–E127.

### `テンプレ`

The template debate makes `テンプレ` a contested analytical term rather than a neutral label. Speakers use it variously for recognizable structures, market tendencies, dismissive criticism, and claims about originality. Preserve those pragmatic differences rather than translating every occurrence as “formulaic.” Evidence: IM-V07-E144–E158.

### `幸せ`

Kaizu's creator-life aspiration gives `幸せ` professional-theoretical weight: “moderately happy” is positioned against maximal success/genius rather than against ambition itself. Evidence: IM-V07-E159–E182.

## V07 terminology state

V07 strengthens the need to treat `主人公`, `対等`, `甘える`, `テンプレ`, and `幸せ` as speaker- and context-dependent terms. Their English glosses should not erase changes in pragmatic function across relationship, career, criticism, and retrospective grief contexts.

## V08 additions

### `好きでなければやってられない職業`
Toki's description of editing, explicitly paralleled with writing, defines the occupation through burdens that external reward alone cannot justify. Intrinsic attachment is required to remain in a job whose hours, emergencies, interpersonal strain, and compensation can otherwise look irrational. Evidence: IM-V08-E001–E010.

### `可児那由多に相応しい主人公`
Itsuki's shrine vow preserves both `主人公` and the relationally comparative `相応しい` (“fitting / worthy of”). Because Itsuki and Nayuta are already a couple, this does not restore the old equality gate as romantic permission; it does show that professional comparison remains tied to his desired self-position beside her. Evidence: IM-V08-E022–E034.

### `心を込めて`
Itsuki describes the planned anime announcement as something the involved creators/staff prepared to deliver wholeheartedly to readers. The phrase supports reading publicity form and timing as relational labor rather than information alone. Evidence: IM-V08-E035–E045.

### `ケジメ`
Toki's shaved head is framed as personal `ケジメ`: drawing a line / marking responsibility for himself. The surrounding narration explicitly distinguishes this symbolic act from undoing the consequences, so translation should not inflate the term into restitution or adequate repair. Evidence: IM-V08-E035–E045, E066–E074.

### `対等`
Kyo says that moving in with Nayuta and Kaiko has not made her `対等` with them because they earn professional income while her parents still support her. Yet she has already imposed reciprocal household rules against one-sided burden. V08 therefore requires contextual tracking of `対等`: psychological status-comparison and ethical reciprocity are not identical senses. Evidence: IM-V08-E114–E140.

### `僕は／私は`
Chihiro's final internal split preserves two first-person positions on the page. `僕` is continuous with the socially inhabited younger-brother presentation; `私` surfaces the concealed female self-position. The punctuation/alternation is translation-sensitive evidence and should not be flattened into one unmarked English “I” in reconstructive analysis. Evidence: IM-V08-E183–E191.

### V08 voice-state deltas
- **Itsuki:** retains blunt self-command, creative comparison, sudden earnestness, and embarrassed tsukkomi; romantic maturity has not erased the `主人公` register.
- **Nayuta:** `にゃはは`, explicit sexual directness, `先輩`, and abrupt shifts into sincere tenderness remain stable after coupling/roomshare.
- **Kyo:** practical boundary language and care-oriented imperatives coexist with strong self-deprecating comparison.
- **Haruto:** explanatory hobby fluency, mentoring reassurance, teasing warmth, and sociable topic shifts become increasingly reconstructible.
- **Chihiro:** polite `です/ます` outside family coexists with youthful hobby enthusiasm and sexual-information gaps; `僕は／私は` adds a crucial audience-/identity-sensitive first-person marker.
- **Toki:** occupational bluntness and sardonic realism remain, while V08 adds explicit self-critique, apology, and non-romanticized description of editorial work.

## V08 terminology state
V08 makes language of **vocation, worthiness, responsibility, equality, and self-reference** converge on the same problem: labels do not settle lived states. `編集者`, `主人公`, `対等`, and even the first-person pronoun require attention to the practical relation being enacted, not dictionary category alone.

## V09 additions

### `本物` / `紛い物` / `劣化コピー`
Aoba initially treats shared sensibility with Nayuta as evidence that she too is a `本物`; critical/editorial discourse instead casts her as a weaker imitation. V09 ultimately refuses to settle creative ontology through either label. Itsuki’s intervention shifts the operative question from category purity to owned desire and continued practice. Evidence: IM-V09-E013–E018, E038–E046.

### `資格` / `才能` / `価値` / `意味` versus `書きたい` / `好き` / `愛している`
Itsuki successively rejects qualification, talent, value, and meaning as **permission conditions** for Aoba to continue writing. He then asks whether she wants to write and whether she likes/loves the novel she made. Translation must preserve this logical distinction: the passage does not claim talent/value are unreal; it denies their authority to issue the basic license to create. Evidence: IM-V09-E038–E046.

### `常識`
Kyo’s employment arc repeatedly encounters “common sense” that is difficult or impossible for a novice to infer in advance. The term can mark shared practical convention, but V09 is explicitly suspicious when consequential norms are hidden and enforced after the fact. Evidence: IM-V09-E141–E190.

### `3年縛り`
The “three-year binding” is an unwritten publisher expectation applied to newcomer-award writers. Because it is not stated in the rules and would be waived for a creator with Nayuta’s leverage, the phrase is central to the volume’s bargaining-power analysis. Evidence: IM-V09-E162–E190.

### `平服`
The interview instruction literally permits ordinary/plain clothing, yet Kyo is criticized for not wearing the expected suit. The scene depends on pragmatic contradiction between literal wording and tacit institutional code. Evidence: IM-V09-E141–E161.

### `作家さんと一緒に泣くことができる編集者`
Kyo’s desired editor is one who can “cry together” with authors. The phrase expresses solidarity and shared emotional stakes, not mere sentimentality. It is also a reconstruction-sensitive risk marker because Kyo can derive self-worth from being needed. Evidence: IM-V09-E162–E190.

### `僕` → `私`
The V08 split becomes a V09 event. Chihiro remains `僕` within the socially inhabited younger-brother presentation. At the disclosure threshold the narration strips away the game-only sister and little-brother categories and Chihiro’s first-person self-reference changes to `私` immediately before the truth claim. For voice reconstruction, this is a high-value audience/context switch and must not be normalized into an undifferentiated English “I.” Evidence: IM-V09-E219–E257.

### `本当の妹`
Chihiro’s final declaration uses `本当の妹`: not metaphorical, in-game, pseudo-, or merely enacted sisterhood. The phrase asserts the category whose absence has structured the family plot. V09 establishes that Chihiro claims it; it does not establish how Itsuki interprets it. Evidence: IM-V09-E219–E257.

### `主人公`
Haruto’s TRPG formulation sharpens the series’ protagonist vocabulary. Players are protagonists because they make choices and live the resulting story; even failure or tragedy can be a successful session if the protagonists fully inhabit those choices. This expands `IM-C014` beyond achievement into owned consequence under uncertainty. Evidence: IM-V09-E219–E257.

### `居場所`
Natsume’s pregnancy prompts Chihiro to fear losing `居場所`, literally a place to be/belong. The language is relational and existential rather than merely residential. No actual expulsion has occurred. Evidence: IM-V09-E206–E212.

### `妹ジネーション`
The playful coinage condenses `妹` and imagination into a vocabulary for Itsuki’s generative sister-fantasy system. V09 makes the term particularly important because a real sister’s disclosure is imagined by Chihiro as potentially interfering with the creative lack that fuels that system. Evidence: IM-V09-E055–E063.

### Afterword PF: `それでも、面白いからここにいる`
The afterword identifies an anime-original line—roughly, “even so, I’m here because it’s interesting/fun”—as especially apt for the creator-work dimension of the series. Treat this as authorial PF about thematic emphasis, not as retroactive omniscient narration. Evidence: IM-V09-E258–E276.

### V09 voice-state deltas
- **Itsuki:** the theatrical/blunt register remains, but mentoring increasingly uses stripping questions and permission language rather than status claims. His professional voice can now concede uncertainty without abandoning ambition.
- **Chihiro:** polite/youthful competence remains, but the `僕`/`私` divide becomes behaviorally decisive. Career talk also exposes genuine hobby enthusiasm rather than only service-oriented practical speech.
- **Kyo:** practical care language gains institutional-critique force; she is unusually willing to challenge `常識` when tacit norms harm a weaker person.
- **Haruto:** explanatory mentorship and playful GM authority share a common pattern: widen options, state stakes, then let the other person choose.
- **Toki:** self-deprecating disclosure about his own contingent career origin complicates the image of the all-knowing professional editor.
- **Aoba:** grandiose authenticity language collapses into direct statements of liking/loving her work; this is a major voice-state change in one volume.

## V09 terminology state

V09 places **category language under pressure**. `本物`, `常識`, `才能`, `価値`, `主人公`, `居場所`, and `本当の妹` all look like labels that might settle reality. The volume repeatedly asks who has authority to apply them, whether their rules were disclosed, and whether the person occupying the role actually chooses it.

## Next update gate

The V07–V09 checkpoint should freeze the `僕`→`私` disclosure transition exactly at V09 and preserve the distinction between narrative dialogue and afterword PF. No later register normalization belongs in the checkpoint.

## V10 — Japanese voice, style, and terminology delta

### 千尋: `私……いえ、僕`

The bonus fiction gives the clearest origin locator for Chihiro's concealment register. At the decision to present as a boy so that the desired family can exist, she says `私は……いえ、僕は──家族がほしいです`. The self-correction is LF evidence that `僕` is narratively tied at origin to the concealment strategy. It does **not** by itself prove every later `僕` is psychologically false or settle Chihiro's entire gender identity. Evidence: IM-V10-E001–E032.

### `兄さん` versus `お兄ちゃん`

After disclosure, Chihiro tests `お兄ちゃん` as a stereotypically sister-like address. Itsuki explicitly rejects an objective hierarchy between address forms and says the important criterion is character fit (`一番大事なのはキャラに合っているかどうか`). They retain `兄さん` because it fits the relationship actually lived. Evidence: IM-V10-E080–E107.

**Translation-sensitive rule:** do not flatten the address experiment into generic “brother.” The difference between `兄さん` and `お兄ちゃん` is part of the volume's authored-role argument.

### `妹は妹、彼女は彼女`

Itsuki's parallel syntax performs categorical separation between family and romance. This phrase should remain available as a high-priority locator whenever the project discusses whether fictional sister fetish maps onto real-incest desire. Evidence: IM-V10-E056–E063.

### `普通`, `本当`, `正真正銘`

V10 repeatedly distinguishes `本当の妹`/`正真正銘の家族` from stereotype imitation. `本当` establishes correct relation/category; it does not mean there is one compulsory style of sibling behavior. The lexical contrast is important to the volume's ontology of real category versus locally authored practice.

### `慣例`, `明文化`, `専属契約`

The Kyo/Toki exchange gives these institutional terms a deliberately non-binary semantic field. `明文化` can increase transparency, while `慣例` can preserve discretionary freedom; `専属契約` names the risk that codification changes the actual institution rather than merely describing it. Evidence: IM-V10-E135–E161.

### `砂漠`

Aioi's industry metaphor becomes a compact vocabulary for non-terminal professional arrival: debut is not a goal, talent/effort do not guarantee an oasis, and continued movement offers possibility rather than certainty. Evidence: IM-V10-E197–E235.

### `釣り合い` / `対等`

The anime-finale discussion reactivates parity language after couplehood is already established. The correct linguistic state is therefore **migrated future-progression gate**, not restored love-permission gate. Evidence: IM-V10-E324–E339.

### `キモチワルイ` → `気持ち悪い`

Itsuki's rereading of his own fiction begins with visually marked katakana `キモチワルイ`, followed by repeated ordinary `気持ち悪い`. The sequence formalizes estrangement from familiar prose after the position of identification changes. Evidence: IM-V10-E340–E376.

### `僕は──俺は`

At the point where Itsuki asserts that he became a protagonist by his own power, the line hesitates between `僕` and `俺`. Do not assign a fixed binary meaning from one occurrence. Preserve it as LF because it coincides with the narration's explicit split between the flamboyant sister-obsessed author identity and the serious young man confronting love/family. Evidence: IM-V10-E340–E376.

### afterword focalization terminology

The author afterword states that through much of V10 the prose intentionally avoids explicit `～と思った`-style interior attribution for Itsuki and limits psychology to outwardly visible behavior. This is PF about composition and validates the formal category **delayed interiority**, but must not be used to invent hidden thoughts not supplied by the final chapter. Evidence: IM-V10-E377–E386.

## V10 voice-state synthesis

V10 makes linguistic form part of identity re-authorship. Pronouns, sibling address, institutional vocabulary, parity language, and the closing first-person hesitation all show that a correct category does not automatically stabilize the words through which a character inhabits it.

## V13 — Japanese voice, style, and terminology delta

- **`一人前`**: Kyo's desired status of full-fledged editor remains deliberately undefined. She can acquire a successful first book and still feel she has not arrived. The ambiguity matters to Haruto/Kyo's deferred relationship.
- **`オンリーワン`**: moves from Kyo's fear that a similar life path makes her derivative toward a professional problem of developing a non-substitutable personal editor grammar.
- **`主人公になりたい`**: now names Kyo's aspiration, the title of Itsuki's private-origin/publicly edited novel, and readers' self-descriptions. The phrase becomes shareable language for non-exclusive self-authorship.
- **`ほうれんそう` (報告・連絡・相談)**: Haruto uses ordinary workplace shorthand as minimum author-editor trust infrastructure, deliberately contrasting repeatable communication with heroic overdevotion.
- **`言いなり`**: Izumi's phrase for submission to editorial will; the term captures why unexplained but expert demands become experienced as domination.
- **`変化ではなく、昇華`**: Toki's evaluation of `L↔Rデイズ`; prior artistic passion and acquired craft are integrated rather than one replacing the other.
- **`本物`**: in Nadeshiko's metafictional comedy, authenticity language becomes parody when used as a simple hierarchy that invalidates pseudo-relations.
- **`晴彦さん`**: Chihiro retains Haruto's real-name address after the fake-boyfriend lie is abandoned. It indexes linguistic intimacy but not romantic reciprocity.
- **`羽島伊月先生`**: Kyo switches to professional address when asking to become Itsuki's editor, marking a deliberate domain transition from friend/reader to editor-author relation.
- **`主人公が、ヒロインのもとへ歩いていく`**: V13 literalizes the protagonist metaphor in the proposal sequence; movement is incremental rather than triumphant arrival.
