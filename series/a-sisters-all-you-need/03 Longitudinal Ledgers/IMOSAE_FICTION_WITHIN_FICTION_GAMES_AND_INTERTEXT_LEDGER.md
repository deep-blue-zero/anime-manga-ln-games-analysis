## V02 - internal fiction and transformation model

### Bare-signifier sister experiment
The opening manuscript functions as a conceptual stress test: after ordinary beauty, humanity, gender, and communicative cues are stripped away, can the category `sister` still generate affect? Even Itsuki's marginal acceptance and Toki's reader-legibility critique show that private category intensity is not self-translating public form. Evidence: IM-V02-E003-E005.

### Zeke/Hilde - affective transmutation
Embodied mixed-bathing fear gives Itsuki access to Zeke's fear of death; Chihiro's critique of Itsuki's adulthood becomes functional dialogue for Hilde. These are strong cases for **transformation of affect/language across situations**, not one-to-one autobiography. Evidence: IM-V02-E016-E019, E033-E037.

### Haruto's genre preferences
Haruto's wish-fulfillment structures are professionally researched and genuinely liked. Taste can be personal and deliberately engineered at the same time. Evidence: IM-V02-E074-E079.

## V02 - games/social forms

### Cat & Chocolate - school edition
- Randomized prompts surface material that characters avoid in ordinary confession.
- Nayuta's school response shifts from game improvisation into real anger at institutional abandonment and relief that Itsuki validates escape.
- Kyo's unused romantic-rivalry card externalizes an internal conflict she refuses to voice.
- Evidence: IM-V02-E008-E012.

### TRPG II
- Haruto explicitly values player freedom within rules.
- Professional pattern-reading, sibling role-play, and the decision to burn the fortress rather than follow an anticipated route make emergent/co-authored narrative visible.
- The GM supplies conditions, rules constrain action, players redirect the fiction; authorship is distributed rather than sovereign.
- Evidence: IM-V02-E064-E068, IM-V02-E101.

## V02 - social ritual as creative infrastructure

Food, lodging, travel, hanami, and post-confinement eating remain both ordinary life and potential creative input. The important rule is not that leisure is secretly work at every moment; it is that experience can later be transformed into work without losing its original social function. Evidence: IM-V02-E016-E019, E056-E059, E062-E063, E090-E091, E096.

## V02 interpretive rule update

**STRENGTHEN:** Fiction-within-fiction refracts creator desire/anxiety without literal identity. V02 supplies a more precise mechanism: event identity can change while affective structure, language, taste, or moral relation is transmuted into fiction. Hirasaka's afterword is PF support for this general method, not diegetic proof about any particular character. Evidence: IM-V02-E090-E091.

## V03 additions

### X-11 — Nayuta's Kyo-only fantasy: private fiction as relation

A roughly bunko-length unpublished fantasy makes Kyo its protagonist and has one intended reader. Haruto identifies obvious market value; Nayuta refuses publication because the work is for Kyo alone. The knight is modeled on Itsuki, so the work triangulates multiple attachments rather than mapping one character directly onto one real relation. Evidence: IM-V03-E031–E044.

### X-12 — `Once Upon a Time`: contested narrator position

The game's formal mechanism lets players seize narrator position and steer a jointly created story toward individually assigned endings, which can conflict. Itsuki/Nayuta/Haruto's professional-writer session becomes grotesque and difficult to close. This is a strong formal resonance with the later protagonist discussion but is not a literal allegory of it. Evidence: IM-V03-E075–E078, IM-V03-E129.

### X-13 — Synopsis as prospective fiction-adjacent rhetoric

Because `妹すべ` is deliberately unplotted, synopsis copy may precede actual event construction. Itsuki's `嘘は書いてない` defense exposes a threshold between fiction, metadata, and promotional expectation. Evidence: IM-V03-E017–E025.

### X-14 — in-universe `妹さえいればいい！` as recommendation and vocation bridge

Young Itsuki recommends Sekigahara Yuu's sister-centered novel to Ayane and discovers the pleasure of successful cultural transmission. Later, the series' newcomer-award notice provides a path from consumer to writer. Evidence: IM-V03-E099–E112.

### X-15 — Writing as transformation of heterogeneous affect

The bonus explicitly lists sister desire, longing, heartbreak, failure, inferiority, anger, hope, dreams, prayer, and protagonist hunger as material Itsuki wants to throw into fiction. The appropriate model remains transformation/refraction rather than one-to-one autobiography. Evidence: IM-V03-E113–E115.

### X-16 — Real-work coexistence is paratextually broad but rules representation is not documentary

The afterword says real-world works other than Imosae generally exist in the story world, while also correcting the `Once Upon a Time` rules and admitting tempo-driven deviation. Intertextual presence can therefore be treated as real diegetic cultural environment without assuming every represented rule/practice is documentary exactness. Evidence: IM-V03-E118–E119.

## V04 additions

### X-17 — Media mix becomes a theory of transformation

The manga-adaptation material distinguishes fidelity from copying. Kaiko can invent gestures and page logic absent from Itsuki's prose while preserving what he recognizes as the characters' governing priorities. Derivative work therefore becomes a site where source meaning is reconstructed through another medium and another creator. Evidence: IM-V04-E054–E068, E151–E152.

### X-18 — Adaptation conflict can generate a third form

The nude/lingerie dispute begins as mutually exclusive creator desire. Research and revised sequencing produce a form that neither party initially proposed: lingerie acquires character/narrative function while the nude payoff remains. This is the strongest V04 formal instance of additive compromise. Evidence: IM-V04-E074–E084, E092–E104.

### X-19 — TRPG gives contingency formal authority over authorial intention

The V04 TRPG material makes dice/rules capable of defeating the outcome a player-author desires. Authored world, player intention, character commitments, procedural rules, and chance jointly determine narrative outcome. Evidence: IM-V04-E131–E140, IM-V04-E182–E185.

### X-20 — `ラノベ作家の人生` converts apparent competition into cooperative survival

The BONUS_FICTION game separates money from happiness and reveals that the hidden victory condition is not highest score but reaching the end with positive happiness. Peer/family relations function mechanically as survival infrastructure. This is strong formal evidence, but its bonus-fiction/game source class prevents silent conversion into mainline documentary fact. Evidence: IM-V04-E159–E171, IM-V04-E187.

### X-21 — Internal documents continue to materialize creative work as systems

Project/pitch material, Q&A, character profiles, clothing diagrams, TRPG sheets, and game graphics keep moving the book between prose narrative and document-like publication forms. The series repeatedly makes the infrastructure of creation visible rather than leaving it as exposition alone. Evidence: IM-V04-E172–E187.

## V05 — fiction / games / intertext delta

### 1. Selective fidelity becomes explicit

Kaiko treats swimwear as a material/observational problem and Itsuki freely permits a source costume change, despite earlier conflict over changing nudity to underwear. Evidence: IM-V05-E047–E055.

**State:** STRENGTHEN `IM-C017`. Fidelity is selective: creators implicitly rank which details carry source identity and which can change freely.

### 2. `妹すべ` supporting characters as hidden protagonists

Itsuki argues that the Oni siblings are thematic counterpoints and effectively `裏の主人公`, and refuses to treat any character as a disposable “supporting role.” Evidence: IM-V05-E070–E075.

**Analytical consequence:** this creates strong internal counterpressure against Itsuki's own fear of becoming a supporting character in Nayuta's life. The analogy is IT/SI, not an explicit self-recognition by Itsuki.

### 3. Media-difference rhetoric is necessary but contestable

Hirugano invokes media difference to defend anime restructuring; Itsuki rejects the phrase when it functions as cover for a structure he judges weak. Evidence: IM-V05-E076–E082.

**State:** REVISE detail of `IM-C017`: medium-specific transformation is not self-justifying. It must be supported by the receiving medium's needs and an intelligible account of source priorities.

### 4. Anime as multi-author system

The project begins as an inherited replacement with schedule/business constraints, then becomes a contested collaboration among author, scriptwriter, director, editor, and committee. Evidence: IM-V05-E066–E095.

By the final chapter, episode order is changed, an original enemy is added, the Oni siblings are deepened, and added erotic material receives structural function; author/scriptwriter/director all believe the transformed form is optimal for anime. Evidence: IM-V05-E176–E183.

**State:** strong STRENGTHEN for `IM-C017` and `IM-C018`.

### 5. TRPG as responsiveness laboratory

Haruto frames unpredictable player action as a challenge his professional craft should answer, while Kyo's anticipation motivates preparation. Evidence: IM-V05-E158–E160.

**State:** STRENGTHEN `IM-C004`; games continue to model author/audience responsiveness rather than serving as detached pastime.

### 6. Newcomer submissions as fiction inside institutional comparison

The award chapter treats multiple fictional manuscripts as objects whose value changes with evaluation criteria: present polish, future revision, originality, field similarity, award branding, and market packaging. Evidence: IM-V05-E138–E155; visual document support IM-V05-E211–E212.

**State:** contributes to new `IM-C022` and strengthens `IM-C006`: the meaning of a work is partly refracted through institutional context without being reducible to it.

### 7. Formal prose-to-image handoff

`水着回３` explicitly says there is no noteworthy narrative event and directs the reader to Kantoku's illustration. Evidence: IM-V05-E064–E065, E207.

**State:** new formal marker. The series openly exposes the light novel's division of representational labor between prose and illustration.

## V06 — fiction / games / intertext delta

### `新世界の創妹記` — image-to-prose feedback

Setsuna's illustration clarifies Itsuki's imagined world strongly enough to trigger substantial prose revision; narration links the change to improved finished quality and probable sales retention. Evidence: IM-V06-E025–E027.

**Function:** this is a reverse adaptation arrow. A derivative/paired visual interpretation feeds back into the nominal source text, strengthening the corpus model of creative media as recursive rather than one-way.

### Picasso / Cubism intertext

Hakone fine-art encounter operates differently for three creators. Haruto accesses unfamiliar art through game/fantasy analogy; Itsuki and Haruto initially joke that Picasso may not understand art; Setsuna experiences Cubism as transformative revelation and becomes `パブロ・プリケッソ`. Evidence: IM-V06-E028–E032.

**Caution:** the sequence is comic fictional reception of Picasso, not art-historical authority.

### `景色` media mix

Nayuta's work becomes a multi-medium industrial object—animated feature, live-action film, manga—because she wants self-development rather than market expansion. Evidence: IM-V06-E033–E040. The project therefore tracks how a work's institutional footprint can far exceed the creator's private motive.

### `クロニカクロニクル④` — game as relationship-repair technology

The session is explicitly scheduled because Itsuki/Kyo/Haruto fear awkwardness will otherwise damage ongoing friendship. The narration says the hidden purpose is fulfilled. Evidence: IM-V06-E044–E046.

Afterword paratext adds that Haruto is actively hosting and sometimes quietly changing dice results. Evidence: IM-V06-E109.

**Revision:** game function is now not merely disclosure/socialization but **deliberate relational repair implemented through hosted rules play**.

### Cast audition as performance interpretation

Voice casting makes character interpretation embodied. Roughly thirty candidates per role may offer different plausible realizations; selection includes source fit but also event, sponsor, career, and commercial objectives. Evidence: IM-V06-E048–E057.

### Drama CD as medium-specific adaptation

Itsuki discovers that audio cannot inherit prose narration directly; dialogue and sound effects must perform its functions. Evidence: IM-V06-E065–E067. At recording he asks actors to make his characters shine through their own power. Evidence: IM-V06-E087–E091.

**STRENGTHEN `IM-C017` / `IM-C018`:** transformation is not loss by default; performance can be additive while remaining recognizably derivative.

### Newcomer-prize narratives

V06 broadens the internal library of “why people write.” Yanagase writes from sincere fetish; Kiso enters at sixty-seven without a conventional LN career trajectory; Kamo treats the prize as a desperate last gamble; Aoba writes from combative aesthetic conviction. Evidence: IM-V06-E075–E085.

**Function:** no single psychological theory of authorship survives the ensemble. Desire, economic necessity, ideology, play, status, and survival all produce fiction.

### Public report manga as unreliable in-world paratext

The recording report is told to depict harmony regardless of observed pre-recording interaction. Evidence: IM-V06-E087–E088. This opens a standing source-criticism rule for later embedded reports/promotional artifacts.

## V07 additions

### Apartment games after coupling

The core-five gathering shows a previously established social ritual persisting after the relationship configuration changes. Seating, jokes, and play can carry memory of an older equilibrium while allowing a new one to form. Games therefore continue to function as relationship-maintenance infrastructure rather than static recurrence. Evidence: IM-V07-E082–E105.

### `テンプレ` as an internal theory of genre

The writer gathering explicitly debates whether recognizable templates make works interchangeable. Aioi argues that abstraction does not exhaust concrete difference; Haruto combines genre affection with market awareness and fears critics dismissing a work without reading it. This turns “template” from a fandom insult into an in-universe theory of genre, reception, and creator vulnerability. Evidence: IM-V07-E144–E158.

### 関ヶ原幽's work as creator–reader mirror

Yuu's fiction and public success are not merely background titles. Her history links writing, reader love, media expansion, unwanted attention, hostility, illness, and memory. The creator–reader relation therefore appears as both sustaining and exposure-producing. Evidence: IM-V07-E159–E182.

### Bonus child-story contest

The bonus sequence returns to fiction as social challenge/gift. Itsuki's initial insistence on fair professional compensation and later voluntary participation separates the value of skilled labor from the transaction mode under which it is offered. Evidence: IM-V07-E193–E202.

Genre structures organize expectations; readers can nourish careers; wider visibility can expose creators; and voluntary gift-writing can coexist with a principled defense of paid labor.

## V08 additions

### X-31 — `黙示録ノ妹` becomes an explicit test of autobiographical overreading
Keisuke's family history demonstrates that fictional obsession can strongly affect real relationships while still being misread as literal confession. The work has biographical consequences without becoming a transparent map of Itsuki's intended behavior toward Chihiro. Evidence: IM-V08-E102–E113.

### X-32 — `戦国剣風伝` models self-authored genre translation
Kiso deliberately studies `萌え`, adds a heroine by his own initiative, and integrates the new grammar into a work whose historical atmosphere and character depth remain valued. Genre learning is represented as repertoire expansion rather than simple surrender to a template. Evidence: IM-V08-E056–E065.

### X-33 — `モテねば！` is a deliberately reductive model whose limits produce meaning
The board game turns courtship into resource placement, optimization, competition, and loss-cutting. Chihiro can optimize ruthlessly because the pieces do not carry lived attachment; Kyo and Haruto repeatedly expose the gap between game-theoretic rationality and actual desire. Evidence: IM-V08-E086–E101.

### X-34 — Adaptation fidelity can be priority-preserving rather than surface-preserving
The V08 afterword states that the anime changes chronology, setting details, and some material while aiming to preserve the source's atmosphere/themes. As authorial PF, this directly supports a model in which fidelity is evaluated by transformed priorities rather than identical event order or surfaces. Evidence: IM-V08-E192–E200.

### X-35 — Real production history and fictional causality require source-class separation
The afterword confirms that a real publisher leak inspired the fictional mechanism but explicitly denies a real Hoshii-equivalent creator collapse. Real-world provenance may explain a fictional device without authorizing one-to-one mapping of consequences. Evidence: IM-V08-E192–E200.

### X-36 — Haruto's desired creator life is increasingly transmedial
Comiket prompts Haruto to articulate interest in doujinshi, board games, game scenarios, anime, scripts, and lyrics in addition to novels. Intertext/media movement becomes part of character vocation rather than merely a property of the fictional industry. Evidence: IM-V08-E011–E021.

## V08 intertext/game thesis
V08's internal works and games are not decorative mirrors. They model **how forms travel**: a parent misreads fiction as biography, an elderly author learns a new genre grammar, a board game intentionally strips romance down to mechanics, and the afterword frames anime adaptation as transformation under priority constraints. The common lesson is that representation is neither identical to its source nor independent of it.

## V09 additions

### X-37 — Imitation is a developmental relation, not a permanent caste
Aoba begins by treating resemblance to Nayuta as proof of authentic genius and is then publicly reduced to “degraded copy.” Itsuki refuses both ontologies as final permission structures. An imitative work can still be loved by its maker and become a stage from which a distinct practice develops. Evidence: IM-V09-E013–E018, E038–E046.

### X-38 — Revision can discard the artifact while retaining interpretation
The failed anime plan is not zeroed out when production collapses. Hirugano’s rereading and thinking remain available to the new ending. Fictional production therefore models revision as destructive at the output level but cumulative at the cognitive/process level. Evidence: IM-V09-E047–E054.

### X-39 — Adaptation identity can survive transformed surfaces
The completed anime has altered chronology, wording, an original ending, schedule compromises, and visible limitations. Itsuki nonetheless recognizes the white-box episode as unmistakably his work. This is the strongest narrative support so far for priority-preserving rather than surface-preserving fidelity. Evidence: IM-V09-E191–E218.

### X-40 — TRPG defines protagonist status independently of victory
Haruto states that players are the protagonists who weave the story, and that failure or tragedy can still constitute successful play when choices are fully inhabited. The game formalizes protagonist agency as authorship of consequential action rather than optimization toward victory. Evidence: IM-V09-E219–E257.

### X-41 — Role-play can operate as an identity laboratory
Chihiro can be a girl and Itsuki’s younger sister in the TRPG without initially altering real-world categories. The fictional role is emotionally real enough to produce intense validation, yet safely deniable as “only the setting” until Nayuta’s correction collapses that buffer and prompts disclosure. Evidence: IM-V09-E219–E257. Claim route: `IM-C036` **OPEN NEW**.

### X-42 — Fiction can separate relational worth from instrumental worth
Chihiro’s character is initially weak in battle, but Itsuki’s character prioritizes protecting the sister before her special ability later becomes tactically essential. The role-play thus gives Chihiro a temporary experience of being worth preserving prior to proving usefulness. Evidence: IM-V09-E219–E257.

### X-43 — Paratext explicitly confirms the TRPG’s designed identity function
The afterword says the TRPG was conceived as the one world in which Chihiro could become the true self in front of Itsuki. This is unusually strong PF support for X-41 but remains subordinate to the narrative for consequences. Evidence: IM-V09-E258–E276.

### X-44 — Broadcast wording demonstrates medium-specific audience transformation
The television dispute is not simply censorship versus purity. The same scene must survive different access barriers, sponsor relations, accidental audiences, and package/broadcast versions. Itsuki chooses a rewritten line because preserving the scene for broadcast-only younger viewers matters more than exact lexical identity. Evidence: IM-V09-E191–E205.

## V09 intertext/game thesis

V09 treats fiction and games as **transformative laboratories rather than transparent mirrors**. Imitation can become development; discarded scripts can leave knowledge; adaptation can remain the same work across changed surfaces; and role-play can make an otherwise socially barred self temporarily inhabitable. The danger is to confuse this freedom with consequence-free unreality. Chihiro’s TRPG sisterhood becomes emotionally powerful precisely because the “only a game” distinction can no longer contain what has been rehearsed.

## V10 — fiction, adaptation, translation, games, and intertext delta

### Fiction is non-transparent but deeply self-invested

Keisuke reads `黙示録ノ妹` as evidence of literal danger. Natsume objects that a novel protagonist need not equal the author, while Itsuki himself insists that the novel contains all of him. V10 therefore rejects both transparent autobiography and total separation. Fiction can transform genuine creator investment without becoming one-to-one confession. Evidence: IM-V10-E001–E032.

The final chapter supplies the reverse causal direction: lived reality can change whether a creator can still inhabit the imaginative structure of earlier fiction. After Chihiro becomes real family, Itsuki can no longer identify with brother protagonists romantically desiring sisters. Evidence: IM-V10-E340–E376.

### New provisional mechanism: productive fantasy distance (`IM-C037`)

V10 opens the possibility that some creative identities depend on distance between imaginative object and lived role. Actualizing the fantasy can destroy rather than satisfy its creative function. This remains OPEN because the mechanism is strongly evidenced mainly in Itsuki.

### Real siblinghood as script testing

V09 used TRPG as a blocked-identity rehearsal space. V10 moves the mechanism into ordinary life: Chihiro and Itsuki test sibling address, mock fighting, borrowed sibling stereotypes, and rivalry with `妹キャラ`, then reject mechanical imitation. Role-play/social scripts reveal fit but do not dictate authentic form. Evidence: IM-V10-E080–E134.

### `銀色景色` live-action adaptation

The film is praised for not simply reproducing costumes/appearance. It is understood as successful because staff understood the source and optimized it for cinema while preserving more essential features. This is high-value evidence for priority-preserving transformed fidelity. Evidence: IM-V10-E162–E181.

### Anime completion

Itsuki's anime reaches a successful ending after the production transformations already documented in V09. The finished adaptation remains recognizably his series while being causally distributed across production staff. Evidence: IM-V10-E324–E339.

### Translation and work identity

Itsuki initially worries that if a translator makes his work popular overseas, the result might be the translator's work rather than his. Taiwan reception complicates the binary: translated readers, Japanese-original readers, local publishers, event staff, and local LN creators coexist. Translation is constitutive intermediary authorship/labor without severing source authorship. Evidence: IM-V10-E236–E280.

### Transnational paratext / publishing form

Taiwan editions regroup works by local publisher contracts rather than Japanese label structures and can carry different packaging/extras. This is a strong reminder that publication form is not a transparent wrapper: work identity travels through reorganized bibliographic systems. Evidence: IM-V10-E236–E280.

### Haruto intertext and creator telos

Haruto reaches for a `フルメタル・パニック！` mecha model as he articulates a desire to write a serious robot/SF work and eventually see his own machine modeled. Intertext here is aspirational craft lineage, not simple reference decoration. Evidence: IM-V10-E281–E313.

## V10 fiction/intertext synthesis

V10's governing form principle is **continuity through transformation, except where transformation destroys the position of identification itself**. Adaptation and translation preserve work identity by changing surface/form; sibling-role rehearsal preserves relationship by rejecting copied surface; Itsuki's creator identity fails because real family changes the imaginative subject-position from which the old fiction was possible.

## V11 — fiction-within-fiction, games, and intertext delta

### `妹が多すぎる。`

The game converts sister identity into hidden, ranked, strategically asserted information. Players can mislead, veto, and reverse apparently settled categories. Its absurd outcome separates formal category assignment from lived relationship. The afterword identifies the special-edition game as a collaboration intended to let readers share Itsuki/Haruto's play experience. Evidence: IM-V11-E055–E088, E337–E345, E352–E353.

### Failed non-sister manuscript

The war-chronicle experiment shows genre substitution alone cannot rebuild authorial stance; television language invades the prose. The document image makes failed writing materially present. Evidence: IM-V11-E021–E028, E350.

### `霞とシェリア`

Ashley reveals that Sherria was modeled on her and that transformed intimate relationship material entered Sekigahara Yuu's novel. Fiction is therefore materially autobiographical without being a transparent transcript or an entitlement to all private source events. Evidence: IM-V11-E157–E198, E366–E367.

### Author-label intertext

Nadeshiko's mistaken belief about Itsuki's author identity produces comedy around protective lying and intertextual quotation, but the reputational cost of repeating the false claim is diegetically real. Evidence: IM-V11-E089–E104.

### Sister fiction after real sisterhood

Aoba's intervention makes Itsuki decompose the ethics of his earlier fiction: romantic sister desire is no longer personally inhabitable, while the non-erotic ideal of helping a hurting sister remains actionable. Evidence: IM-V11-E224–E274.

### V11 synthesis

V11 treats fiction as mediated relation rather than either sealed fantasy or transparent life: games rehearse classification, novels transform intimate biography, fiction can preserve ethical fragments after its erotic premise becomes unavailable, and intertext can generate real social costs.

## V12 — fiction-within-fiction, games, and intertext delta

### The Aoba route is explicitly a trap/alternate nightmare, not enacted romance

The exaggerated route sequence functions as comic ethical simulation: following vulnerable gratitude into sexual involvement with the seventeen-year-old leads toward disaster. Waking Itsuki refuses the actual offer and tells Aoba to value herself. The sequence belongs in role/possibility analysis, not the factual relationship ledger as an encounter. Evidence: IM-V12-E052–E077.

### V7 reception turns a work into an audience-splitting object

Anime newcomers and long-time readers encounter different gains/losses in Itsuki's more calculated V7. The work therefore becomes evidence that aesthetic identity and audience competence are relational rather than single-score properties. Evidence: IM-V12-E311–E344.

### Yuma reads beyond assigned role

`銀色景色` teaches Yuma to attend to other characters' lives and then to real people outside his assigned part. Fiction functions as a technology for decentering one's immediate role, and its effect exceeds Nayuta's conscious design. Evidence: IM-V12-E345–E374.

### The one-reader novel is autobiographical transformation

Itsuki writes an unpublished romance based unmistakably on himself and Nayuta, then admits professional embellishment while calling it both `私小説` and entertainment. The artifact is simultaneously communication, fiction, and transformed biography rather than transcript. Evidence: IM-V12-E479–E542.

### Private address changes artifact value

Because the novel is written for Nayuta alone, its success cannot be generalized from market reception. Nayuta's “best work” judgment is partly an addressed-reader judgment, which is precisely the point of the artifact. Evidence: IM-V12-E496–E519.

## V13 — fiction-within-fiction, games, and intertext delta

### Word Wolf and social misreading

The game chapter continues the series' use of imperfect-information play, but its main V13 function is to transition into Kyo/Chihiro's explicit romance/work/protagonist conversation rather than provide a new identity-rehearsal case.

### `L↔Rデイズ`

Itsuki's new public ensemble fiction represents the re-authored creative grammar: sister characters remain present without monopolizing the narrative, and Toki reads the work as artist/craftsman `昇華`.

### `主人公になりたい`

The V12 private manuscript undergoes a second fictional life. Public revision removes a simplified Yuma-like rival and expands Kuroyama Kei—modeled substantially on Kyo—into a third protagonist. The transformation complicates love and happiness rather than preserving a simple victory structure.

The work remains autobiographically rooted but not transparent biography. Kyo is a model filtered through Itsuki's perception; fictional sexual/romantic events are not evidence that the real Kyo/Nayuta relationship literally occurred.

### Nadeshiko's `本物`

The intentionally absurd Oregairu-like story is metafictional authenticity parody prompted by Nadeshiko's hurt. It should not be treated as narrator-endorsed philosophical doctrine.

### `妹・オブ・ザ・デッド`

The special-edition movie rewrite explicitly declares itself unrelated to the main story. Its extreme sister/zombie/incest escalation is WFW/media-mix comedy only. The afterword notes that CD and prose versions have different endings, reinforcing adaptation as transformation rather than exact duplication.

### V13 intertext synthesis

Fiction increasingly acts as **circulating recognition**: private autobiography becomes public narrative, Kyo learns about herself through being fictionalized, readers adopt `主人公` language, and parody exposes the dangers of over-literal authenticity categories.
