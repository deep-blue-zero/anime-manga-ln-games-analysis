# Chiramune social status, inclusion, and group-dynamics ledger

## Responsibility

Track how informal hierarchy is produced, perceived, reproduced, resisted, and used; how Saku's group manages inclusion/exclusion; and how identical behavior changes meaning under different levels of accumulated social trust.

V01 makes status neither illusion nor destiny. It is a real emergent structure with unequal effects, but characters differ in whether they treat rank as an end, a byproduct, a resource, or a threat.

## V01 working model

### Status is emergent rather than formally assigned

Seating, who can start conversations, who may tease whom, whose mistakes are forgiven, and whose presence defines a group all produce a de facto hierarchy without an official caste system.

### Status is sticky

Saku can make himself the butt of a joke, lose badly at soccer, or perform a humiliating punishment without ceasing to be high-status. Kenta initially cannot. This means confidence is not simply a momentary attitude; it is supported by accumulated relational capital.

### Rank may be a byproduct or an objective

Saku's group claims that they spend time with people they like and pursue things they value, with high rank following as a side effect. Atomu's group is framed as monitoring rank itself and attacking others to preserve relative superiority.

This distinction is useful but does not erase privilege. A person who already has robust status can afford to say rank is unimportant more easily than someone whose every awkward act risks exclusion.

## Group-member status styles

### Saku

High-status coordinator. Uses humor, competence, beauty/athletic advantage, and demonstrated reliability. Claims low attachment to rank but understands hierarchy intimately and can weaponize it when protecting someone.

### Yuko

"Natural" social center in Saku's taxonomy. Her warmth and openness lower relational barriers; her attractiveness also lends strong endorsement when she treats Kenta as worth time.

### Yua

Bridge figure. Less overwhelming presentation and history of acquired prominence make her particularly useful for linking quieter students to the group.

### Yuzuki

Controlled high-status performer. Uses poise and desirability strategically; can counter aggression without overtly appearing aggressive.

### Haru / Kaito

Athletic status. Their embodied competence and directness provide a different route to visibility from Saku/Yuzuki's verbal control.

### Kazuki

Competence/status realist. More willing than Saku to state that some people simply do not offer enough value for daily closeness; therefore important evidence that the group is not universally inclusive.

### Kenta

Begins with almost no local social capital and interprets hierarchy as a fixed moral order. Over V01 he acquires skill, trust ties, and a limited amount of borrowed legitimacy, but the book does not establish that he has become top-caste or that this is necessary.

## Inclusion is selective, not universal

Saku's rhetoric is often broad, but the group does not practice unconditional inclusion. Kazuki explicitly notes he can like Kenta as an occasional friend without wanting the same daily intimacy Saku gives closer companions. Saku likewise recognizes a carrying limit by the end.

This is analytically important: V01's answer to caste is **not** "everyone should be equally close to everyone." It is closer to:

- do not convert rank into human worth;
- do not deny others entry merely to defend status;
- choose relationships by actual affinity and reciprocal value;
- use strength to protect rather than manufacture subordinates;
- allow different distances without pretending distance itself is moral failure.

## Structural privilege audit

V01's self-improvement thesis must always be read alongside these asymmetries:

- Saku's attractiveness and athleticism make ordinary presentation look better.
- Yuko/Yuzuki's beauty changes how their behavior is received.
- Saku's established reputation allows him to survive public embarrassment.
- Kenta's transformation is endorsed by people whose approval has high social value.
- Saku's group can publicly redefine Kenta's social meaning in ways Kenta could not initially accomplish alone.

Therefore **"status doesn't matter" is not a supported V01 claim**. The better finding is that status is real but need not be the final authority for choosing values or friends.

## V02+ watch conditions

- a high-status character losing rank and testing the "byproduct" thesis;
- romantic competition converting friendship capital into zero-sum status;
- Yua's own account of becoming socially prominent;
- situations where group protection becomes clique enforcement.

# V02 update — visibility, envy, and distributed correction

## V02 governing revision

Volume 01 emphasized status as accumulated relational capital that makes mistakes survivable. Volume 02 adds the inverse: **high visibility also magnifies envy, policing, sexual competition, and permission to attack.**

Saku and Yuzuki's public fake relationship creates a concentrated status event. Two unusually visible people becoming a pair does not merely combine their protection; it reorganizes how peers classify Yuzuki and makes her a target for anonymous punishment.

## Group as corrective institution

V02 strengthens the view that "Team Chitose" is not simply a court surrounding Saku. Members have differentiated authority:

- **Yua:** emotional/relational accountability and low-temperature directness.
- **Yuko:** overt affection plus forceful demand that Saku remember the people waiting for him.
- **Haru:** embodied directness; challenges excessive optimization and over-management.
- **Kazuki:** skeptical strategic peer; trusted to preserve evidence under distress.
- **Kaito:** physical solidarity and uncomplicated willingness to act, though Saku sometimes excludes him because that directness would disrupt a plan.
- **Kenta:** increasingly normal participant whose willingness to help demonstrates continuity beyond V01.

Saku remains the strongest agenda-setter, but the group has enough internal differentiation to constrain his model.

## Status and permission to punish

Yuzuki's experience shows that admiration and hostility are convertible. Her earlier strategy was to become so exceptional that envy would feel pointless; the fake relationship with Saku restores a direct zero-sum comparison. Anonymous observers can now narrate her as "the girl who got Saku," making attack feel socially licensed.

**HYPOTHESIS:** the series may treat status less as a ladder than as a network of interpretive permissions: who may joke, desire, reject, fail, intrude, accuse, or be believed without disproportionate cost.

## V02 revision state

- Status as protection → **PRESERVE but qualify with exposure cost**.
- Group as Saku's followers → **DOWNGRADE; distributed correction is too strong for that model**.
- Kenta's incorporation → **STRENGTHEN**.
- New question: how does romantic competition alter internal group trust once feelings become explicit?

## Group topology after V03

The group is neither egalitarian nor merely Saku's court. Saku remains the principal connector and romantic scarcity object, but other members mediate identity and action independently:

- Yuzuki can privilege Saku's important bond over her competitive advantage;
- Yuko can use femininity as a shared option rather than a hierarchy;
- Haru can give baseball knowledge a noninstitutional social home;
- Yua can name and materially answer ordinary loneliness;
- Kenta can contribute interpretive insight and lead through expertise;
- Kazuki can articulate a risk ethic the narrative treats as coherent rather than cowardly.

V03 therefore **strengthens** distributed corrective authority. It does not prove that rivalry will remain cooperative once choices become mutually exclusive.

## V03 revision state

- status as interpretive permission → **PRESERVE**; Saku's competence and intimacy continue to license intrusions that should remain contested.
- New question: can distributed care survive when Saku's eventual commitments impose real exclusion rather than playful competition?

V04 adds a distinction between **goal legitimacy** and **method legitimacy**. The basketball team can genuinely want the same destination while disagreeing about hours, risk, rhetoric, and who may define another member's effort. Conversely, different groups can legitimately contribute to one outcome without becoming one undifferentiated family: Saku's current friends prepare him, the old team completes the game, and he keeps their histories and memberships distinct.

## Group topology after V05 main

The group now has three simultaneous truths:

- independent dyads and girl friendships are demonstrably real;
- Saku remains the romantic scarcity center and a major connector;

The V03–V04 cooperative-rivalry claim therefore survives only in revised form. Cooperation can persist before and during explicit bids, but it does not prevent an answer from changing who can occupy the same room and how care is allocated afterward.

## Group rule after V06

V06 replaces the binary of intact group versus destroyed group with a network model:

1. collective absence can coexist with active dyadic survival;
2. distributed competencies reduce dependence on Saku as sole social architect;
3. explicit romantic scarcity changes access without making friendship impossible;
4. repair requires direct answer and apology but need not equal restored innocence;
5. inclusion after conflict is a renewed choice made within altered status knowledge.
