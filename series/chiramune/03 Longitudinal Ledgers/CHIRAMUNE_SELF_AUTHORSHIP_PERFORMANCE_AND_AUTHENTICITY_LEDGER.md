# Chiramune self-authorship, performance, and authenticity ledger

## Responsibility

Track how characters construct, perform, revise, conceal, or become constrained by chosen social identities. The ledger exists because V01 repeatedly returns to this problem across unrelated scenes: classroom leadership, messaging, clothes, athletics, friendship, romance, Kenta's rehabilitation, and the Ramune-bottle imagery.

The governing question is **not** "real or fake?" It is: what is chosen, what is learned, what is concealed, what is socially reinforced, what is costly, and when does an authored identity cease to feel optional?

## Current V01 synthesis

V01 distinguishes at least four different phenomena that are often collapsed into "social performance":

1. **stable learned competence** — conversational timing, reading a room, presentation, status awareness;
2. **playful theatricality** — Saku and Yuzuki adopting roles for amusement or social temperature control;
3. **impression management** — selecting a face appropriate to recipient and goal;
4. **identity obligation** — continuing to enact an ideal because failing to do so would feel like ceasing to be oneself.

Saku inhabits all four. Kenta begins by seeing them as one category called "fake riajuu behavior" and gradually learns to differentiate them.

## Current claims

### SA-C1 — performance is not the opposite of authenticity

**State:** PRESERVE after V01
**Confidence:** high

V01 repeatedly treats practiced behavior as potentially genuine. A chosen habit may become part of a person rather than evidence that the person underneath is false.

### SA-C2 — Saku's central identity risk is self-authored imprisonment

**State:** OPEN but strongly supported
**Confidence:** medium-high

He is not primarily enslaved to popularity. He is constrained by the demand that he remain worthy of his own idealized role. This is why external criticism does not easily move him while direct evidence that he has failed his own standard does.

### SA-C3 — Kenta's development is successful when borrowed scripts become selectable tools

**State:** PRESERVE after V01
**Confidence:** high

The endpoint is not a fully spontaneous "true self" stripped of social learning. It is a Kenta who can decide when to use learned skills without treating the old identity category as destiny.

# V02 update — mutual mask literacy and the right to name feeling

## V02 governing revision

Volume 02 sharply rejects a binary model in which performance is false and vulnerability is true. Saku and Yuzuki become intimate partly because each can recognize the other's authored presentation without demanding its abolition.

The key distinction is between **authored form that remains revisable by the person** and **a role imposed by someone else's fantasy**.

## Authenticity rule after V02

The best current model is:

> **Authenticity is not absence of performance. It is retained authorship over the performance, including the ability to revise it when new values, relationships, or evidence demand change.**

By this measure Yuzuki becomes more authentic in V02 not because she stops being polished, but because she can add anger, apology, desire, fear, direct refusal, and imperfect action to the repertoire she recognizes as hers.

Tomoya is the negative case: he denies Yuzuki authorship by treating his preferred model as more real than the person speaking to him.

## Naming and narrative jurisdiction

The prologue/epilogue opposition adds another responsibility for this ledger. Naming a feeling can either:

- freeze another person inside an idealized story; or
- authorize the speaker to take responsibility for their own movement toward the other person.

Yuzuki's love is provisionally placed in the second category because disconfirming knowledge strengthens rather than destroys it. Saku's refusal to name his feeling remains ambiguous between epistemic restraint and fear of self-binding.

## V02 revision state

- "Saku/Yuzuki are mirrors" → **REJECT in strong form; preserve shared-tool similarity**.
- "performance versus authenticity" → **REVISE toward authorship versus imposed identity**.
- "Saku's persona constrains weakness" → **STRENGTHEN**, now especially in romantic choosing and self-risk.
- New question: can Saku tolerate a named identity/relationship that limits future options without treating limitation as loss of authorship?

# V03 update — learned selves, authored futures, and defensive perfection

## V03 governing revision

Volume 03 gives both Saku's and Asuka's polished selves an origin without reducing either person to that origin. A learned or imitative form becomes authentic when the performer keeps selecting, practicing, revising, and owning it. Conversely, even an internally chosen ideal can become coercive when it demands invulnerability or lets another person's recognition override their present speech.

## Authenticity rule after V03

The V02 rule survives and becomes more precise:

> **Authenticity is retained jurisdiction over a self formed through influence. Origin in imitation, expectation, or relationship does not make a practiced identity false; loss of the ability to revise, refuse, or choose what the form demands is the stronger warning sign.**

Saku and Asuka are reciprocal cases. She borrowed his childhood freedom and made it a durable practice. He built a heroic form around real care and then narrowed his own permissible weakness. Each helps the other recover authorship, but neither thereby acquires final interpretive authority over the other.

## V03 revision state

- Saku's self-authored imprisonment → **STRENGTHEN and historicize** through direct formation evidence.
- Performance versus authenticity → **PRESERVE**, with influence/origin explicitly separated from present authorship.
- Saku's refusal to name feeling → **REVISE / OPEN**: he can name love toward Asuka after projection is disentangled, but exclusive commitment and his wider withholding remain unresolved.
- Asuka as distant authentic ideal → **REJECT in strong form**; preserve her real competence while replacing imagined effortless freedom with learned, constrained, revisable practice.
- New question: can either Saku or Asuka receive ordinary dependency without converting it into identity failure?
