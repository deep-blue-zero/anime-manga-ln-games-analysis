# Saku Chitose — cumulative character monograph through V09 main

## Scope and authority

This is a cumulative, source-bounded **Volumes 01–04 model**, not a definitive series model. Sections 1–17 preserve the complete V01 baseline because V01 provides unusually dense Saku evidence across several weeks, multiple social contexts, independent peer observations, internal narration, athletic behavior, conflict, ordinary life, and one temporary focalization outside Saku that shows how he appears from below. The boundary-labeled V02, V03-main, V03-booklet, and V04 revision sections update that baseline without rewriting it.

Later volumes are expected to change the current model. They must do so through explicit revision rather than retroactively treating later knowledge as though V01 or V02 already contained it.

# 1. Compressed V01 model

Saku Chitose is a socially powerful adolescent who has made **chosen form** into an ethical practice. He is attractive, athletic, verbally quick, highly observant, and unusually capable of organizing people, but V01's central interest is not that he possesses advantages. It is what he does with them and what sustaining the resulting identity costs him.

His public self is not a false mask hiding an opposite "real Saku." It is a crafted, practiced, and substantially authentic persona: funny, capable, sexualized, difficult to embarrass, responsive when relied upon, willing to become villain or clown when a scene needs it, and committed to acting in ways he can call beautiful. The problem is that the persona has become normative. To remain himself, he feels he must continue being the Chitose Saku others expect `[part0017:p087]`.

This creates the V01 paradox:

> **Saku's self-authorship is genuine, but the identity he authored has become a constraint on his freedom to be weak, uncertain, dependent, or ordinary.**

His helping style expresses the same paradox. He is genuinely generous and extraordinarily willing to invest effort, but he can treat another person's life as a problem whose successful resolution validates his own identity. V01 corrects this twice: Haru reminds him that Kenta owns Kenta's success and failure, and Kenta reminds him that "helpful inclusion" can become coercion when the recipient does not share his assumptions.

By the end, Saku has not abandoned his aesthetic, confidence, or interventionist temperament. He has become marginally more permeable. Asuka's image is precise: the immaculate park may now permit a sparkler.

# 2. Epistemic caution: Saku is his own most eloquent mythmaker

Because most of V01 is first-person, Saku supplies both the evidence and the interpretation of much of his behavior. He is also unusually skilled at narrativizing himself. He converts events into genres, roles, metaphors, jokes, and aesthetic judgments almost as quickly as they happen.

Three rules are therefore necessary.

### Rule A — do not believe every boast literally

His sexual and social bravado is often comedic performance. A provocative line may be doing relational work rather than reporting biography.

### Rule B — do not invert every boast merely because it is performative

Saku really is socially influential, physically talented, attentive, and trusted. The novel supplies independent evidence through class election, teacher reliance, athletic scenes, group behavior, Kazuki's observations, Haru's interaction, and Kenta's external focalization.

### Rule C — treat self-mythologizing as evidence about values

The mostly fictional story of a gifted boy who learns to fly too high to be dragged down cannot be used as literal childhood history. It **can** be used as evidence that Saku finds the image emotionally meaningful: ability becomes safety when it carries the self beyond the reach of resentment `[part0020:p124-p148]`.

# 3. Capability profile

## Social perception

Saku rapidly infers motives, status concerns, face-saving needs, romantic misreadings, and likely reactions. He does this with sufficient accuracy to engineer social outcomes repeatedly.

His strongest skill is not merely "reading the room" in the passive sense. It is **designing the next room state**. He recognizes who needs a joke, who needs an exit, who should be asked directly, which person will lower threat, and which person will puncture a defensive narrative.

### Limit

He is weaker when the relevant preference is unlike his own. The soccer rupture proves that sophisticated social reading can still miss a basic subjective fact: Kenta may genuinely prefer not to participate. Saku's ability is therefore high-resolution, not omniscient.

## Verbal control

He can move between teasing, flirtation, formal politeness, intimidation, self-deprecation, sincere argument, and theatrical role-play. He often uses humor to make an emotionally difficult proposition socially survivable.

He also understands that conversational competence has a moral substrate: wanting to know another person and allowing oneself to be known. Technique without that orientation is merely a surface.

## Preparation

One of the most important V01 corrections to his image is how much advance work hides behind apparent spontaneity. He pre-researches eyewear, reads Kenta's light novels, designs intervention sequences, anticipates social interpretations, and plans achievable training.

His effortless appearance is therefore partly a professionalized illusion: **the result looks easy because he takes preparation seriously enough that others do not have to see it.**

## Athleticism

Saku remains athletically exceptional despite leaving baseball. He is competitive, learns physical techniques quickly, and derives uncomplicated pleasure from embodied challenge. Haru's presence reveals a version of him less protected by verbal superiority because she can defeat him cleanly in basketball.

This is psychologically useful: physical contest can temporarily suspend the role in which Saku must already know the answer.

# 4. Core values

## Beauty / coolness as moral-aesthetic criterion

Saku's explicit maxim is that a life that cannot be lived beautifully is barely distinguishable from being dead `[part0015:p037]`. He later clarifies that he wants to live beautifully like the moon or the Ramune marble `[part0033:p217-p224]`.

"Beauty" in V01 is not mere appearance. It bundles:

- keeping one's word;
- answering reliance;
- refusing cowardly status aggression;
- owning one's choices;
- being willing to absorb the cost of a chosen act;
- making room for another person to become protagonist;
- sometimes accepting hatred rather than acting beneath oneself.

The criterion is powerful because it provides internal motivation independent of applause. It is dangerous because it is subjective and can aestheticize dramatic, invasive action.

## Self-respect over rank

Saku says what matters is whether he can respect himself, not where others rank him. His contempt for "mounting" behavior is partly contempt for outsourcing one's worth to downward comparison.

This is sincere. He can lose publicly and turn the humiliation into fun because he does not interpret a soccer loss as ontological demotion.

### Contradiction

His self-respect criterion contains the expectations of others. He has built "the person others expect Chitose Saku to be" into his own ideal. He is therefore less externally dependent than a status-seeker but not socially independent.

## Agency / authorship

He tells Kenta to take responsibility for the story he wants to draw. He admires people who decide and then act. Kenta's most beautiful act, in Saku's eyes, is not becoming attractive but deciding to move and then actually moving.

This is why Haru's criticism strikes so deeply: if Saku takes all responsibility, he contradicts the autonomy principle he preaches.

## Loyalty and protection

Once Saku accepts someone as a chosen person, his protective response is strong. At Starbucks he abandons neutrality and says, in effect, that if protecting a friend makes him the villain, he accepts the role.

This loyalty is **partial**, not universal. He later recognizes that carrying everyone would endanger the people and commitments already on his back.

# 5. Motivation architecture

Saku's actions cannot be reduced to one motive. V01 supports at least five recurrent motives operating simultaneously.

### 1. Aesthetic self-consistency

He wants to act in ways that allow him to remain the person he admires.

### 2. Genuine care

Kazuki explicitly challenges Saku's attempt to explain his helping as mere self-display. Saku does costly things privately and habitually answers direct reliance.

### 3. Enjoyment of difficult social play

He likes a good scene. Complicated human situations engage his intelligence and theatrical instinct.

### 4. Expectation pressure

Because he does not want to say "I cannot," accepting a problem generates pressure to produce an excellent result.

### 5. Desire to be admired / desired

V01 does not require pretending Saku is beyond ego. He enjoys being attractive, liked, and surrounded by desirable people. The point is that these motives coexist with rather than cancel his care.

The stable interpretation is **motivational plurality**, not a hidden single truth beneath every act.

# 6. Self-authorship and the role called "Chitose Saku"

The most important V01 identity line is his statement that he must continue to be the Chitose Saku everyone imagines in order to live as himself `[part0017:p087]`.

That formulation contains a loop:

1. Saku chooses an ideal.
2. Repeated successful performance teaches others to expect it.
3. Others' expectations become evidence of who Saku is.
4. Failing the expectation threatens his own self-conception.
5. He therefore performs the ideal more rigorously.

This is why Asuka's park metaphor is not a complaint that he is fake. The park really is beautiful. The problem is that its rules exclude play.

V01 suggests that Saku's developmental task is not "take off the mask and reveal the true self." It is **gain jurisdiction over the rules of a self he genuinely chose**.

# 7. Vulnerability profile

## Failure that damages self-definition

Ordinary failure is tolerable. Saku can lose at basketball or soccer without collapse. What frightens him is failure that means he was not the person he promised himself he was.

Kenta therefore becomes dangerous to him not because Saku needs Kenta to praise him but because Kenta's collapse could demonstrate that Saku accepted responsibility he could not honor.

## Dependence

He resists leaning on others, literally and figuratively. During the bicycle scene with Haru, he avoids putting his weight on her even when she invites him to. Later he needs a wager to legitimate speaking honestly about his worry.

This makes Haru's observation important: Saku appears more attractive to her when he is striving openly than when he performs finished omniscience.

## Being known without controlling the frame

Kazuki, Yuzuki, Haru, and Asuka each unsettle him because they infer things he has not chosen to display. He can enjoy being admired; being accurately read is more threatening.

## Romantic nonreciprocity

Saku acknowledges that genuine affection may fail regardless of competence. This creates a domain his normal self-improvement model cannot master. V01 does not identify the full wound or test it deeply enough to settle the issue.

# 8. Humor as both intimacy and defense

Saku's jokes have several functions:

- reduce embarrassment;
- convert status attacks into shared comedy;
- prevent gratitude from becoming debt-heavy;
- flirt without forcing literal commitment;
- give himself distance from vulnerable statements;
- turn losing into a scene he still authors;
- create face-saving exits for others.

Humor is therefore not merely avoidance. It is one of his most prosocial tools.

But it can become avoidance when every serious moment is immediately converted into genre play. The V01 endpoint does not remove this tendency; it simply demonstrates that some people can now get beneath it.

# 9. Social philosophy

## What Saku gets right in V01

- social rank has real consequences but need not define worth;
- people often misread categories instead of learning individuals;
- communication improves through curiosity and practice;
- presentation can express rather than negate selfhood;
- effort can change many socially relevant variables;
- confidence is partly produced by accumulated action;
- humiliating others to maintain relative rank is self-defeating;
- friendship can tolerate different status, competence, and interests;
- a person must ultimately choose their own direction.

## Where his philosophy overreaches

- he generalizes from unusually high competence and support;
- he sometimes treats voluntary participation as universally desirable;
- he underweights how his own status changes the meaning of identical behavior;
- he can convert "I should help" into "I must control the outcome";
- his language of change can sound meritocratic beyond what Kenta's heavily supported case proves.

V01 is strongest where it lets these overreaches become actual plot problems rather than merely objections a reader must supply from outside.

# 10. Intervention style

Saku's preferred intervention method has five stages:

1. **diagnose the story the target tells about the problem;**
2. **disrupt the conditions that let the story remain self-sealing;**
3. **create repeated experiences that generate contrary evidence;**
4. **lend enough support/status that the target can survive early failures;**
5. **withdraw the mentor structure so authorship returns to the target.**

The Kenta arc broadly follows this sequence.

The model's risk appears at stages 2 and 4. Disruption can become coercion; support can become dependency. Haru and Kenta force Saku to see both risks.

# 11. Relational map

## Kenta — from object of intervention to fellow protagonist

Kenta begins as a task, becomes a pupil, becomes a responsibility, becomes a critic, and finally becomes an ordinary friend. The relationship succeeds when Saku stops needing it to remain an explicit rescue relationship.

Kenta idealizes Saku almost religiously, so his Chapter 5 praise should not be used as neutral proof. Yet his criticism during the soccer rupture has high evidentiary value precisely because it breaks through that admiration.

## Yuko — warm affirmation

Yuko believes in Saku's goodness more straightforwardly than he does. She follows him into the Kenta project because she wants time with Saku, then forms her own relation with Kenta. Her idealization provides emotional support but may also reinforce the hero role Saku finds burdensome.

## Haru — corrective equality

Haru's athletic superiority in her domain gives their relationship an important symmetry. She can tell him he is being foolish without making him feel reduced to a patient. She is one of the few people who can make vulnerability feel competitive rather than humiliating.

## Asuka — aspirational other

Saku admires Asuka for acting from an internal compass without visible dependence on judgment. He treats her as evidence that a freer form of self-authorship exists.

Because this is Saku's perspective, V01 does not establish that she is as unconstrained as he imagines. Her function in his psychology is nevertheless clear: she represents a mode of being he cannot yet inhabit.

## Yuzuki — mirror and reader

Yuzuki shares his comfort with managed surfaces and can read the motive beneath a strategically true explanation. Their mutual flirtation is less about one person overwhelming the other than about enjoying the fact that each can keep up.

## Kazuki — skeptical peer

Kazuki calls out Saku's inaccurate self-exculpatory accounts and treats status more coldly. He is useful because he can validate Saku's kindness without idealizing him.

# 12. Ordinary-life profile

V01 provides enough mundane evidence to constrain simulation.

### Clothing

Saku favors relaxed, basic, durable, combinable clothing; does not meaningfully chase trends; likes a small number of long-lasting silver accessories and practical bags. He wants style to look like an extension of the person rather than proof of fashion literacy.

### Food and drink

He has strong local/ordinary preferences rather than only aspirational tastes. 8-ban ramen, Karamen, convenience-store food after exercise, Royal Sawayaka, and Starbucks all coexist without status anxiety. He can enjoy a fashionable venue without treating it as inherently superior.

### Exercise / play

He enjoys competition intensely. Athletic games can make him more direct and less narratively guarded. He remains proud of physical competence and notices others' skill seriously.

### Reading / media

He is willing to consume unfamiliar light novels and anime when doing so creates a basis for understanding another person. V01 does not establish him as a preexisting otaku; it establishes curiosity and low contempt for unfamiliar taste.

### Local attachment

The narration repeatedly notices Fukui-specific ordinary life. The tension between Saku's grand self-myth and a small provincial environment is part of his texture rather than an embarrassment he tries to escape.

# 13. Anger and aggression

Saku usually manages conflict through words, humor, and status. His anger at Starbucks is therefore notable. It appears when Kenta's **attempt to become an agent** is mocked, not merely when Saku himself is insulted.

He uses physical intimidation—wall impact, collar-grabbing proximity—without actually striking the aggressor. Afterward he rejects the premise that moral defense requires preserving aggressors from all emotional harm.

This gives a bounded V01 aggression model:

- low propensity for direct bodily violence;
- high willingness to intimidate when a chosen friend is being maliciously attacked;
- high willingness to retaliate socially/verbal-rhetorically;
- accepts personal reputational cost;
- does not extend equal solicitude to aggressor and defended friend in the moment.

Do not generalize this into a claim that Saku is violent. The scene is exceptional and he explicitly describes himself as disliking violence.

# 14. Development across V01

## Opening state

Saku is highly effective and internally regulated. He can orchestrate other people's scenes while remaining mostly outside the emotional cost.

## Asuka diagnosis

The park metaphor names the problem before Saku is ready to alter it.

## Kenta intervention

He exercises his model at maximum intensity and initially treats success as something he must manufacture.

## Haru correction

He learns that total responsibility denies Kenta's agency.

## Kenta rupture

He learns that benevolent participation can become bullying if recipient meaning is not included.

## Starbucks

He violates his polished detachment and becomes openly angry for someone else's sake, then deliberately hands the scene back to Kenta for closure.

## End state

He recognizes load limits and retains ordinary friendship after mentor authority ends. Asuka perceives a small increase in permitted spontaneity.

**Development magnitude:** meaningful but bounded. Saku is not "fixed." His core ideal remains intact.

# 15. Rival models

## Model A — narcissistic social manipulator

**Evidence for:** constant self-praise, theatrical control, manipulation, beauty/status awareness, pleasure in admiration.

**Why insufficient:** costly private follow-through, responsiveness to reliance, willingness to admit fault, rejection of exploiting Yua romantically, and continuing friendship after the intervention ends.

## Model B — secretly insecure nice guy wearing a fake popular mask

**Evidence for:** vulnerability, expectation pressure, fear of failing identity.

**Why insufficient:** popularity, confidence, sexualized humor, athleticism, and social enjoyment are themselves genuine. The public self is not simply opposite the private self.

## Model C — flawless hero / author mouthpiece

**Evidence for:** extraordinary competence, rhetorical victories, Kenta/Yuko's admiration.

**Why insufficient:** Haru and Kenta correct him on central principles; Asuka identifies an unresolved constraint; his own narration admits manipulative motives and blind spots.

## Model D — meritocratic evangelist

**Evidence for:** effort rhetoric, Kenta transformation, contempt for excuses.

**Why insufficient:** he explicitly acknowledges unequal talent and appearance, relies on social support, and preserves Kenta's subcultural identity. Still, V01 sometimes rhetorically overstates how generalizable Kenta's case is, so this rival cannot be fully dismissed.

# 16. Current simulation constraints after V01

A V01-grounded Saku simulation should predict that he is likely to:

- answer a direct, bounded request for help if he believes he can act without betraying a more important obligation;
- hide preparation behind apparent ease;
- prefer humorous or theatrical framing over solemn therapeutic language;
- resist pity directed at him;
- respond strongly to accusations that he has violated his own standard, more than to generic insults;
- use social intelligence actively rather than merely observe;
- protect chosen friends even at cost of being disliked;
- prefer an intervention that allows the other person to become agent, though he may initially over-control;
- enjoy competition and respond well to peers who can genuinely beat or challenge him;
- avoid defining people solely by high/low status or otaku/riajuu category;
- care about the aesthetics/story of an action in addition to its efficiency.

A V01-grounded simulation should **abstain** from confident claims about:

- the true reason he left baseball;
- his full romantic history;
- whether his public sexual bravado corresponds to extensive sexual experience;
- the origin of his perfectionistic identity;
- his family dynamics;
- his behavior under severe grief, adult responsibility, prolonged failure, or irrevocable loss;
- which romantic relationship he would ultimately choose.

# 17. V01-defined revision targets entering V02

The highest-value later evidence will be anything that can test:

1. the origin and durability of the Chitose Saku role;
2. baseball as unresolved identity history;
3. his response to informed refusal rather than solvable need;
4. his behavior when competence cannot secure the desired relational outcome;
5. whether he can receive care without converting it into a contest or joke;
6. whether Asuka is genuinely freer or primarily Saku's idealization;
7. whether his partiality produces conflict among people he values;
8. whether repeated performance becomes more flexible or more imprisoning.

## V01 monograph verdict

Saku is best modeled not as "the popular protagonist" but as a **self-fashioned informal leader whose greatest strength and central vulnerability are generated by the same mechanism**. He turns competence into a chosen aesthetic, the aesthetic into a role, and the role into an obligation. That obligation makes him unusually reliable; it also tempts him to mistake carrying someone for respecting them.

V01's first corrective movement is therefore not toward abandoning social performance. It is toward a more difficult form of authorship: remaining recognizably Chitose Saku while allowing other people—and eventually himself—more room to act outside the script.

# V02 longitudinal revision

## 1. V02 does not reveal a hidden opposite Saku

Volume 02 strengthens the V01 model that Saku's public competence is real and his performed identity is substantially authentic. The new evidence instead clarifies **where his chosen form becomes rigid**.

He can:

- read Yuzuki's mask without demanding she discard it;
- distinguish public performance from private vulnerability;
- cook, study, tease, host, and share ordinary space without needing to dominate the scene;
- accept corrections from friends and modify later plans;
- sustain tactical composure under direct threat.

The difficulty appears when he must let another person influence the structure of his own obligations. He is more comfortable being the person who decides how much pain to absorb than being the person whose safety is partly entrusted to others.

## 2. Jurisdictional isolation replaces the simpler self-sufficiency model

V01 sometimes made Saku appear self-contained. V02 supplies better language.

His home is full of relational traces: shampoo recommended by Yua, a dryer from Yuko, routines and objects connected to friends. He enjoys companionship, asks for practical help, trusts Kazuki with a difficult role, and is capable of ordinary dependence.

What he guards is **final jurisdiction over direction**. He tends to believe that once a problem reaches him, he should decide what the correct action is and bear the cost himself.

Asuka's formulation captures the paradox: his life always seems to contain others and yet, at the deepest level, only himself; it seems to contain only himself and yet others are always there.

**V02 revision:** Saku is not best described as emotionally isolated. He is relationally embedded but reluctant to make others co-authors of risk, direction, and choice.

## 3. Yua and Yuko expose the moral cost of self-disposability

Saku initially treats his own body as an optimization variable. If taking a hit or entering danger produces the best outcome, the cost seems his to spend.

Yua and Yuko reject the premise. Their objection is relational: if he is injured, they are harmed too. Yuko reframes the same dangerous action around protecting someone and returning alive rather than abstract correctness.

Saku accepts the criticism enough to promise consultation. His final V02 operation is still extreme, but he tells the group beforehand and distributes roles.

This is a genuine development:

> responsibility expands from "I will bear the consequences" toward "I must count the people who bear consequences with me."

It remains incomplete because Saku still ultimately controls the plan.

## 4. Helping: better threshold, dangerous methods

Saku makes an important V01 correction when Yuzuki is deteriorating. He explicitly recognizes that she asked for a fake boyfriend/bodyguard, not for him to solve her interior life, and refuses to cross that line solely because watching her suffer is painful for him `[p-0027:274-285]`.

When she later explicitly asks for help, he marks the boundary change: once she chooses reliance, the problem can become shared `[p-0031:101-106]`.

This is one of the strongest pieces of evidence that Saku is capable of learning agency discipline.

The apartment reenactment immediately demonstrates the remaining failure mode. He treats permission to help as authority to select a psychologically coercive method without prior consent. He predicts Yuzuki correctly, and she later values the outcome, but the method exposes how quickly Saku can move from respecting jurisdiction to assuming expert discretion.

**Standing V02 contradiction:** he has learned to ask "is this mine to enter?" more reliably than "is this method mine to choose?"

## 5. Beauty is procedural, not gentle

V02 makes clear that Saku's ideal of a "beautiful" way of living is not conventional kindness.

He can consider the following compatible with his self-concept when he believes the situation warrants them:

- deception;
- baiting and evidence traps;
- controlled humiliation;
- physical violence;
- threats backed by institutional knowledge;
- accepting injury as tactical cost.

His contrast with Yanagishita is not pacifism versus violence. It is that Saku believes force must sit inside a defensible structure of purpose, proportionality, evidence, and responsibility, whereas Yanagishita treats the ability to violate rules as strength.

This makes Saku ethically more complex than a benevolent social fixer. His danger lies partly in the fact that he is smart enough to build procedures around extreme acts.

## 6. Rule literacy as power

Saku's final confrontation with Yanagishita clarifies one source of his social power: he understands that rules are not merely restrictions. They are resources.

He first uses school jurisdiction and a teacher to neutralize a confrontation. Later he allows Yanagishita to generate clear evidence of assault and then fights back under a self-defense frame. His threat is chilling precisely because he claims he will use lawful/social mechanisms relentlessly if Yuzuki is targeted again.

**INFERENCE:** Saku's competence includes institutional literacy. He is dangerous to rule-breakers not because he is willing to become lawless, but because he can exploit the asymmetry between someone who disregards systems and someone who understands them.

**Counterpoint:** legal/procedural defensibility is not identical to ethical proportionality. Future readings should not let "within the rules" substitute for moral analysis.

## 7. Romance reveals his inability to name his own movement

Yuzuki is the strongest V02 pressure on Saku's self-authorship.

He can diagnose her performance, protect her, desire her, become jealous of special access, share domestic space, risk himself for her, and feel the pain of impending separation. What he cannot do is name the resulting feeling.

At the festival he experiences a sudden sadness that the moment cannot be preserved and says it is too soon to name the emotion. Near the end he repeats that the name can wait until the very last moment.

There are at least two serious readings:

### Reading A — epistemic discipline

The volume's prologue warns against prematurely calling admiration love. Saku may be correctly refusing to turn intense crisis intimacy into a totalizing label.

### Reading B — freedom-preserving evasion

Kazuki warns that eventually Saku will have to choose one thing and discard another. Saku experiences that day as distant. Refusing names may preserve optionality and prevent relationships from making binding claims on him.

Both remain live at V02.

## 8. Yuzuki breaks the mirror and becomes more important

Saku initially interprets Yuzuki as a person like himself. Her existence promises frictionless mutual recognition.

V02 dismantles that fantasy. Yuzuki's own perspective reveals a real difference: she optimizes around obstacles; Saku, though capable of the same, often chooses collision when his values demand it.

The romance does not weaken when the analogy breaks. It strengthens.

This matters for Saku because it is one of the first indications that he may desire a relationship that cannot simply reflect his own self-concept back to him.

## 9. Asuka moves from ideal to possible loss

Asuka's Tokyo dilemma is a separate pressure on Saku's refusal to choose.

He learns that she may leave Fukui and experiences pain. Her aspiration is also unexpectedly ordinary, which produces both disappointment and relief. When she asks whether he would come if she wanted him to, he evades. She tells him the white-dress version of herself is probably not someone he can love `[p-0031:484-539]`.

**INFERENCE:** Saku may be most comfortable with Asuka when she remains distant enough to function as an aesthetic horizon. A concrete Asuka who chooses a city, asks something of him, and can leave requires a different kind of reciprocity.

## 10. Family / baseball clues remain insufficient

V02 adds several facts but not the explanatory account:

- Saku says the previous spring was consumed by baseball.
- he has elite-enough batting experience to read high-speed pitches and uses that history tactically in the final fight;
- his parents are divorced (mentioned in relation to a childhood Tokyo trip);
- he currently lives alone in a renovated apartment.

These facts increase the importance of the V01 baseball/family open questions but do not yet justify a causal story about why he left baseball or why he lives alone.

## 11. V02 stable model

The V01 paradox survives and becomes sharper:

> **Saku is a genuine self-author whose strongest virtue and strongest failure arise from the same source: he believes a person should choose a beautiful way to live and then accept the cost. He increasingly recognizes that other people own their own stories, but he has not fully recognized that intimacy can make his own story partly answerable to others—or that permission to help does not give him sole authorship over the method.**

## 12. V03 watchlist

- Does Saku name romantic desire when naming would constrain him?
- Can another person successfully help him using a method he did not choose?
- Does he learn consent-to-method, not merely consent-to-intervention?
- What happened with baseball?
- What is the current family arrangement and why is he living alone?
- How does he react if Asuka actually chooses Tokyo?
- Does his threat-based rule literacy receive meaningful counterevidence?
- Can he remain "Chitose Saku" while choosing one relationship at the cost of another possibility?

# V03 longitudinal revision

## 1. Formation history grounds the role without debunking it

Volume 03 supplies Saku's first direct account of the childhood sequence behind his present form. He began as a capable, prosocial child who repeatedly helped because he could. Peers converted repetition into entitlement: ordinary limits became betrayal, sabotage became a way to “balance” the gifted child, and hidden labor disappeared under claims of natural talent `[p-0031#p0203-p0270]`.

A teacher's praise of him as a hero was benevolent in context. Saku transformed it into a mandate to become so perfect that no one could approach closely enough to impose or weaponize expectation. The current persona is therefore a defensive architecture around real competence and care, not a false opposite self.

The parts now fit:

- arrogance preempts accusations of false modesty;
- advertised romantic unreliability limits entitlement to him;
- humor vents pressure and keeps claims deniable;
- concealed weakness prevents weaponization;
- disclaimed altruism resists unlimited care debt;
- self-sacrificial rescue preserves the childhood impulse while treating only himself as expendable.

This history **explains** the form. It does not legitimate every act performed through it.

## 2. The marble is a self-model inherited through relationship

Saku says he aimed for the moon but became the Ramune marble: visible and beautiful, yet enclosed and looking outward `[p-0031#p0307-p0322]`. Asuka's childhood focalization reveals that this meaning was relational from the start. He called the marble lonely; she called it floating, loved, and moonlike—like him `[p-0032#p0270-p0287]`.

Saku's most private metaphor is therefore not purely self-generated. Another person's recognition entered his later self-narration. This strengthens the model of relational embeddedness: even the language through which he imagines isolation bears another person's contribution.

## 3. He can receive an interpretation he did not author

After narrating his history, Saku lets Asuka “edit” it. She does not deny the facts; she rejects his habit of minimizing their narrative value, sees the continuing child hero inside imperfect present rescues, and gives crescent/moon/marble forms value before perfection `[p-0031#p0323-p0398]`.

He accepts this reconstruction and her consensually guided physical witness. This is the strongest evidence through V03 that another person can help Saku by a method he did not design. The scope remains narrow: the exchange occurs with an exceptional intimate who shares origin history, and he does not generalize the lesson to his own intervention procedures.

## 4. Outcome jurisdiction improves; method jurisdiction does not

Saku's ethically strongest action is helping Asuka leave. He does not promise Tokyo will solve her conflict and ultimately refuses to make Fukui intimacy a reason she must stay. When he begins to promise lifelong responsibility for failure, Asuka interrupts and claims both vocation and consequence; he accepts the correction `[p-0032#p0643-p0676]`.

At the same time, V03 accumulates serious procedural counterevidence. Saku imagines another man entering Yuko's room and receiving her whole private self, then tests the same future with Yua, Yuzuki, Haru, and Asuka. He says each produces equal pain and can finally call the category love `[V07:part0019#p0268-p0300]`.

## His safe-senior category reproduces the postponement he can now see

Saku accepts Momiji as a dance partner because junior innocence appears to keep the festival outside the older romantic field. Yuzuki hears distress during a work call and elicits the Asuka conflict. Momiji separately asks why he seems troubled and receives his account of the group's stagnation `[V07:part0022#p0016-p0056]`, `[V07:part0019#p0630-p0653]`.
