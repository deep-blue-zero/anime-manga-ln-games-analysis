# Yuko Hiiragi — V01–V06.5 character monograph

## Scope and promotion basis

Volume 05 crosses the independent-model threshold for Yuko Hiiragi (柊夕湖). V01–V04 establish public exuberance, direct affection, social centrality, intervention participation, domestic self-development, and the answer moratorium. V05 adds prologue and sustained first-person focalization, the first-year origin of love, ordinary female friendship, maternal witness, explicit reflection on privilege and jealousy, regional/domestic practice, a completed public request, and behavior under refusal.

This is a cumulative model through the V05 special-edition booklet. Its placed earlier evidence revises the current account below without rewriting the regular-V05 main freeze or changing that volume's refusal outcome.

## 1. Compressed model

Yuko is an exceptionally visible social initiator who longs for the ordinary friction her visibility often prevents. Her warmth, beauty, humor, and agenda-setting power are real capacities, not merely a façade. They also create a transparent distance: other people may yield, praise, or protect her before she can know what they wanted.

Saku becomes special because he treats her as correctable and then assumes the social cost of making refusal legible. Yuko's love therefore contains a central contradiction. She wants the person beneath exceptional treatment, yet idealizes the burden-bearing hero form through which he first reached her. V05 makes her development an act of authorship: she examines jealousy without acting it out as sabotage, builds friendships with rivals, and chooses a truthful request knowing it may end the world she wants to preserve.

## 2. Evidence planes

### Yuko focalization

The V05 prologue and first-year flashback provide direct access to her experience of exceptional distance, ordinary longing, love origin, jealousy, friendship, and deliberation. These passages outrank Saku's guesses about her motives.

### Public speech and behavior

Across V01–V05 she initiates calls, trips, rituals, jokes, affection, gifts, class action, and finally confession. Her behavior is unusually legible but not exhaustive; cheerful directness can also protect grief.

### Saku's witness

Saku experiences her as energizing, uncalculated, dependable in ordinary rhythm, and capable of showing him landscapes he would otherwise miss. His hero anxiety also makes him doubt whether she sees the present person or an ideal.

### Kotone, Yua, Nazuna, and group witness

Kotone identifies both Yuko's power and her growing other-attention. Yua supplies long friendship without exceptional distance. Nazuna gives non-deferential counsel. The other girls' behavior shows that Yuko can become a genuine friend to romantic rivals without dissolving rivalry.

## 3. Exceptional visibility and the transparent wall

Yuko's early problem is not exclusion from attention. It is attention without ordinary reciprocity. Praise and deference create a transparent wall through which she can see and speak but cannot reliably be opposed or handled as an equal `[part0013#p0004-p0074]`.

The class-representative origin demonstrates the material risk. Yuko nominates Yua and says refusal is possible, but her popularity and the class's applause make that option socially costly. Harm occurs without malice `[part0013#p0131-p0236]`.

Yuko's immediate apology and attention to Yua's body matter. She does not defend intention as sufficient. Yet the scene also establishes that constant self-surveillance cannot be the whole solution; status works through other people's anticipatory yielding, not only through Yuko's conscious commands.

## 4. Saku as ordinary opposition and heroic burden

Saku first appears to Yuko as another flattering boy. He becomes different when he names the coercive structure, accepts Yua's rebuke, and volunteers to bear the class presidency and irritation himself `[part0013#p0242-p0343]`.

Yuko falls in love because he breaks exceptional politeness. She is delighted to be corrected, fought with, and regarded as someone whose actions can matter negatively. This is a powerful form of recognition.

It is also the origin of idealization. Saku secures ordinary treatment for Yuko by making himself the conspicuous burden carrier. She later asks him always to remain the Saku she loved and interprets his interventions for others as proof that he must be her hero `[part0012#p0222-p0241]`, `[part0026#p0541-p0560]`. Love for the real act and attachment to a constraining persona coexist.

## 5. From hated exception to desired `special`

Yuko's `special` has two directions:

- imposed exception separates her from peers;
- mutually chosen singularity would give her one irreducible place beside Saku.

The second desire does not negate the first. Ordinary equality with a social world and particular romantic commitment from one person are different goods. The conflict arises because Yuko's requested position is structurally scarce and the friend group contains other valid claims `[part0007#p0004-p0032]`.

## 6. Ordinary love without a dramatic entitlement

Yuko worries that falling quickly after one school incident makes her love narratively weaker than bonds formed through childhood, crisis, or rescue. Nazuna refuses that hierarchy. If Yuko experiences Saku as irreplaceable and the encounter changed her life, an ordinary causal beginning does not make the current choice unreal `[part0013#p0429-p0455]`.

This correction is important because it separates validity from outcome. Yuko's love can be fully real without becoming a claim that Saku owes matching feeling.

## 7. Jealousy, restraint, and rival friendship

Yuko can identify concrete envy toward Yuzuki's pseudo-romantic history, Asuka's access to weakness and possible Tokyo future, and Haru's baseball relation. She judges some of her thoughts harshly. Nazuna distinguishes jealousy from licensed harm and points out that Yuko's public “wife” position may itself pressure rivals `[part0013#p0507-p0587]`.

Behaviorally, Yuko repeatedly refuses sabotage. She helps Haru access a desired feminine presentation, coordinates rather than ranks style with Yuzuki, and develops independent pleasure and future plans with the girls `[part0013#p0591-part0015#p0024]`. Generosity is not proof of emotional neutrality; it is chosen conduct under conflict.

## 8. Yua and the possibility of ordinary friendship

Yua is the durable counterexample to the transparent wall. Their history supports bluntness, care, styling, touch, and maternal familiarity. The V05 paired blue obi flowers and beach image materialize a relation in which two distinct people can appear to hold hands without becoming identical.

Yuko's growing circle matters because it answers one of her earliest desires independently of Saku. The painful limit is that friendship does not remove their incompatible romantic directions. During the girls' love question, Yuko speaks while understanding that the others' evasions protect her and conceal their own truth.

## 9. Kotone, inherited power, and family form

Kotone's affection is physically and verbally easy. She explains the danger of Yuko's innocent preference causing another person to withdraw, and says Yuko increasingly considers the feelings of people important to her `[part0012#p0084-p0139]`.

This maternal account is informative but not neutral. Kotone thanks Saku and then apologizes, recognizing that praise can burden him with responsibility for Yuko's growth. The family scene also reveals a capacity Yuko offers Saku: not rescue, but entry into noisy, affectionate domestic belonging.

## 10. Ordinary-life profile

Through V05 Yuko:

- organizes calls, meals, trips, fireworks, clothing, and photographs;
- uses exuberant flirtation and comic escalation as an ordinary social language;
- learns cooking and practical skills so future dependence will not decide her choices;
- loves clothing and can translate style into another girl's confidence rather than only competition;
- retains family closeness and honors Kotone's sacrifices as chosen use of finite youth;
- wants photographs and shared objects because current people and days will not recur exactly;
- creates group participation by inviting Saku into experiences he might otherwise avoid.

## 11. Confession as authored risk

Yuko understands that waiting may preserve current propriety while letting another relationship make her expression impossible. She rejects the passive phrase “before I knew it” and decides to face other people's feelings and her own `[part0026#p0586-p0598]`.

She gathers the entire group in the empty classroom and asks to become Saku's special person. Public witness forces seriousness and prevents routine from hiding the answer, but also makes the event collective and reduces privacy `[part0027#p0032-p0284]`.

The decision is not ethically pure or simply selfish. It is a legitimate request whose form knowingly changes the people who must witness it.

## 12. Refusal and the failure of protective performance

Saku refuses and names another, unidentified girl in his heart. Yuko first tries to preserve the room through cheerful recovery. She then lets that form fail and says through tears that only Saku will do `[part0027#p0359-p0398]`.

Her grief does not invalidate her prior warmth or impose a duty on Saku. It shows that directness cannot guarantee closure. When Kaito attacks, Yuko stops him and separates Saku's kindness to many people from romantic consent. This is an important defense of Saku's ethical personhood under her own pain `[part0029#p0034-p0047]`.

Her claim that she simply failed to become a girl he could love is serious counterevidence. It converts a nonreciprocal relation into personal deficiency and protects Saku by narrowing a complex history to her insufficiency. The current model must not endorse it.

## 13. Stable tendencies versus state effects

### More stable through V05 main

- social initiative and high embarrassment resilience;
- visible affection and pleasure in shared ritual;
- sensitivity to exceptional distance and desire for ordinary opposition;
- capacity to learn from direct correction;
- loyalty expressed through invitations and presence;
- increasing ability to perceive status effects on others;
- generosity toward rivals without absence of jealousy;
- attachment to tangible objects and images that preserve finite time.

### State- or role-conditioned

- `wife` performance and sexual-comic escalation around Saku;
- belief that Saku must remain the hero she first saw;
- cheerful recovery after refusal;
- self-blame as romantic insufficiency;
- public rather than private confession, selected under finite-time pressure;
- catastrophic fear that one answer may erase an entire group future.

## 14. Major contradictions and risks

### She wants ordinary treatment but singular romantic exception

This is not a logical error. It becomes dangerous only if the requested singularity is treated as owed or if refusal is read as loss of general worth.

### She recognizes status power but cannot neutralize it completely

Awareness and apology improve conduct. They do not remove the social radiance through which other people pre-emptively yield.

### She loves Saku's care but may preserve his prison

The act that made her ordinary also made him heroic through self-burden. Her wish that he remain that person can support his values and constrain his revision at once.

### She protects others while converting harm inward

Yuko stops Kaito and defends Saku's nonromantic kindness, then blames her own insufficiency. Refusing violence does not yet produce a just account of herself.

## 15. Rival readings

### “Yuko is only the cheerful main-wife archetype”

Rejected. V05 establishes reflective focalization, status analysis, ordinary longing, jealousy, independent friendship, fear, choice, and failed performance.

### “Her popularity makes her socially free”

Rejected in strong form. It grants real leverage while constraining opposition and ordinary reciprocity.

### “Her confession selfishly destroys the group”

Incomplete. Public form raises collective cost, but continued moratorium would also preserve an unsustainable ambiguity and restrict her later action.

### “Saku's refusal proves her love was mistaken”

Rejected. Reality of one person's love and reciprocity from the other are separate questions.

### “Stopping Kaito makes Yuko responsible for comforting Saku”

Rejected. She has standing to stop violence done in her name; she has no obligation to manage Saku's aftermath while grieving.

## 16. V05-main current model

> **Yuko Hiiragi is a socially radiant initiator whose deepest longing is to be treated as an ordinary, correctable person. She turns that recovered equality into a desire for chosen singularity with Saku, while learning that her warmth and preferences carry power even without malice. Her V05 achievement is not winning love but authoring a truthful request after examining jealousy, friendship, and finite time. Her unresolved danger is measuring herself by a refusal and preserving Saku's burden-bearing hero form because it was the form that first broke her enclosure.**

## 17. Post-main watchlist

- whether the V05 supplement adds safely placeable Yuko history;
- whether she can reject personal-deficiency explanations after refusal;
- whether ordinary friendship with Yua, Yuzuki, and Haru survives changed romantic knowledge;
- whether her relation with Saku can acquire a non-moratorium form;
- whether she can love Saku's care while permitting him to change the hero role;
- whether Kaito's violence changes her trust in him or in the wider group;
- whether public initiative remains a strength without becoming the only way she can make need legible.

# V05 special-edition longitudinal revision

## The hero/person asymmetry begins near the relationship's origin

In a Volume 01 interstitial, Yuko recognizes that Saku's vague old “biography” concerns his own past. He refuses to tell her because he expects she would romanticize the pain, absolve him, and preserve a heroic story `[V05S:p-0042#p0040-p0068]`.

This does not retroactively invalidate her love. It shows that Saku's Volume 05 concern has a long relational basis and that Yuko's early affirmation could make his vulnerable ordinary self harder to expose. Her wish for future mutual complaint, argument, and disclosure is contemporaneous counterevidence `[V05S:p-0042#p0074-p0084]`.

## She can recognize ordinary strain without claiming its explanation

After Yuzuki's crisis, Yuko uses an arcade trip and plush-toy play to ask after Saku. He realizes that “compensation” was a pretext for easing the tension she had observed `[V05S:p-0060#p0047-p0065]`. She does not demand the hidden cause or convert care into debt.

Yuko therefore does not see only a hero. She sees some ordinary symptoms accurately, even while the deeper role still structures what Saku believes he may reveal.

## Desire is active before confession

Yuko directly investigates Saku's color and underwear preferences during the Volume 2 decoy operation `[V05S:p-0054#p0027-p0048]`. Before the Volume 4 pool cleaning she offers to replace Haru, openly combining practical help with her wish to be alone with Saku `[V05S:p-0067#p0012-p0019]`.

These scenes historicize her confession as the endpoint of long active preference-gathering, competition, and bounded bids rather than a sudden eruption from passive devotion.

## Yua friendship is independently primary

The sleepover conversation identifies Yuko and Yua as unique best friends or sisters, with shared ordinary interests and protected future disclosure `[V05S:p-0058#p0029-p0052]`. Yuko's rivalry with Yua therefore threatens something she values independently of Saku. It also supplies a relationship through which ordinary correction and care can continue after romantic disappointment—if both girls choose it.

The booklet contains no post-refusal scene, so survival cannot be assumed.

## Post-special current model

> **Yuko's love grows from being treated as an ordinary, correctable person, yet it also attaches to a heroic Saku form he has long feared she would preserve. That asymmetry is real but partial: Yuko can see strain, offer non-extractive ordinary care, cultivate primary friendship, and act deliberately on desire. Her Volume 05 refusal remains valid and painful without proving her love false or herself insufficient. The next test is whether she can sustain ordinary personhood and chosen friendships after refusal while permitting Saku's hero role to change.**

- whether Saku/Yuko acquires a form other than restored moratorium or total severance;

# V06 longitudinal revision

## The first-year triangle was partly protected through jealousy

Yuko's jealousy begins when Saku and Yua share a domestic/familial bond. She momentarily treats Yua's painful family history as a romantic advantage, recognizes the thought as ugly, then pre-emptively tells Yua she loves Saku. Her suspended non-answer confession preserves her priority while allowing the group to continue `[V06:p-0027#p0230-p0449]`.

This historicizes the `wife` performance as both sincere affection and territorial practice. It does not make the later friendship false.

## The public confession was meant to end a veto, not guarantee success

Yuko says she expected refusal and chose witnesses because Saku would privately hide an answer to preserve stasis. Public knowledge would release Yua, Yuzuki, Haru, and Saku from treating her position as a barrier `[V06:p-0027#p0542-p0686]`, `[V06:p-0029#p0002-p0029]`.

Her motive remains mixed. She admits loving the old triangle, resenting new entrants, and that she would have accepted Saku's reciprocation. Agency here is accountable imperfection rather than selfless purity.

## Refusing Kaito gives her reciprocal knowledge of refusal

Kaito directly confesses his long love. Yuko says she loves him deeply but cannot transform that friendship category into her love for Saku. She refuses him clearly while preserving relation `[V06:p-0024#p0230-p0448]`.

The experience lets her understand the pain Saku carried when refusing someone important. It neither makes her own love insufficient nor creates a romantic obligation for Saku.

## She demonstrates ordinary recognition of Saku

Yuko answers Saku's fear that she loves an ideal. She lists small habits, defensive gestures, lonely phone behavior, admirable traits, and faults learned over a year and a half. Initial attraction became maintained attention `[V06:p-0033#p0103-p0217]`.

This strongly qualifies the hero/person asymmetry. She may still reinforce his hero performance, but she also sees and loves ordinary contradiction.

## Fighting with Yua makes friendship more equal

Yuko refuses to let Yua mediate without revealing herself. Their first fight exposes mutual jealousy and non-primacy. Yuko then thanks Yua for choosing Saku, holding both ends, and finding the feeling she hid `[V06:p-0031#p0002-p0303]`, `[V06:p-0033#p0002-p0094]`.

The repair is independently primary and does not require either girl to surrender romantic desire.

## She retains love without extracting a promise

Saku says he cannot date Yuko and may choose another girl. Yuko accepts the answer, reserves her right to confess again, and joins the changed group `[V06:p-0033#p0261-p0342]`.

The ordinary epilogue reframes her desired specialness as ordinary naming, walking, talking, and presence in dark nights `[V06:p-0034#p0001-p0033]`. This is continued desire after refusal, not couple formation.

## Post-V06 current model

> **Yuko is a socially powerful initiator whose love contains jealousy, territoriality, group-liberating courage, sustained ordinary observation, and the capacity to accept refusal without surrendering desire. V06 rejects both the pure-victim and pure-manipulator readings. Her relationship with Yua becomes more equal through conflict and admitted non-primacy; her refusal of Kaito gives reciprocal knowledge of Saku's position. She remains in Saku's heart as a girl, but he refuses dating again and no couple or promised selection exists.**

# V06.5 supplemental longitudinal revision

## Refusal ends one attempt without falsifying continued love

Yuko voluntarily tells Yuzuki and Nazuna what happened. She owns the public confession, rejects regret, and distinguishes outcome from feeling: that summer's romantic attempt ended because Saku actually refused it, but she continues to think of him `[V065:p-0013#p0142-p0231]`.

She also names the unresolved injury that only she has been refused and another girl's request might receive a different answer `[V065:p-0013#p0247-p0308]`. Acceptance is therefore not painless closure or proof that she has moved on.

## She originates two low-pressure reconnections

Yuko chooses one souvenir as a joking route back into ordinary conversation with Saku and a distinct parallel gift as a reason to contact Kaito because she wants their friendship `[V065:p-0013#p0627-p0685]`, `[V065:p-0013#p0807-p0927]`.

The group video call does not reverse Saku's refusal. Sparse conversation becomes Yuko's renewed decision to love, and she replaces the prior `bye-bye` with `see you in second term` `[V065:p-0015#p0115-p0175]`, `[V065:p-0017#p0001-p0033]`. This is changed continuity, not restored moratorium.

## Travel friendship acquires a nonromantic future horizon

Yuko, Yuzuki, and Nazuna promise a return to Kanazawa in ten years regardless of future partners `[V065:p-0017#p0110-p0201]`. Their shared clothing, food, apology, humor, and name exchange make the trip an independently primary female relation rather than recovery organized only around Saku.

Yuzuki's refusal-symmetry reasoning helps Yuko approach Kaito, but Yuko owns the choice. Continuing friendship does not revoke either Kaito's confession or her refusal.

## Post-V06.5 current model

> **Yuko's V06.5 achievement is to let an actual refusal end one romantic attempt without treating it as the end of love, ordinary contact, or friendship. She owns the pain and fear that another girl might receive a different answer, then originates low-pressure reconnection with both Saku and Kaito. Her Kanazawa bond with Yuzuki and Nazuna gains an explicit future independent of romantic outcomes. Nothing reverses Saku's refusal or establishes a couple.**

- whether Yuko can seek private support before a public or crisis climax;
- whether the ten-year female friendship horizon survives divergent partners and destinations;

- direct reciprocal evidence only before any couple claim.

# V07 longitudinal revision

## Appearance change is self-revision, not romantic proof

Yuko cuts the long hair she had valued and frames it as becoming a new self. Saku responds with strong bodily and emotional attention, tells her the present look suits her, and receives her request that he keep watching `[V07:part0008#p0297-p0388]`.

The scene advances mutual recognition after refusal. It does not reverse the refusal or make change a condition of deserving love.

## She states rivalry without attacking the newcomer

After Momiji's dance, Yuko says it was beautiful, admits she also wants to dance with Saku, and asks Momiji to watch him when he overextends `[V07:part0011#p0401-p0425]`. She neither conceals desire nor treats a rival's success as contamination.

This is a significant refinement of her public directness: the statement is accountable, bounded, and compatible with care for both people.

## Continued love gains Saku-side romantic recognition without selection

Yuko asks to remain beside Saku longer; he privately wants the same. He later identifies the pain of imagining another man in her future as love and places it equally beside four other women `[V07:part0011#p0882-p0916]`, `[V07:part0019#p0268-p0300]`.

The result is not another suspended promise. Saku's second refusal remains governing, and equal romantic pain is not an answer to Yuko's request for singular status.

## Post-V07 current model

> **Yuko's V07 strength is continued desire without either self-erasure or rival attack. She changes her appearance as an authored new phase, asks Saku to keep watching, praises Momiji's dance while naming her own wish, and asks for more ordinary time. Saku now directly includes Yuko in a five-person field of romantic pain and privately shares the wish for longer presence. That recognition deepens their relation but does not reverse his refusal, promise selection, or create a couple.**

## V08 watchlist

- whether ordinary Saku contact remains compatible with the completed refusal rather than creating another veto;
- whether Kaito friendship is enacted after Yuko's V06.5 intention;
- whether appearance change remains self-authored rather than treated as romantic causation;
- whether Yuko seeks private support before a public or crisis climax;
- direct reciprocal evidence only before any couple claim.
