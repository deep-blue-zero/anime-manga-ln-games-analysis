## I. Governing movement: from the admired answer to an authored choice

Volume 03 tests whether love and care can recognize another person's possibility without appropriating the right to decide for them. Asuka's conflict appears to ask whether Tokyo or Fukui is objectively correct. The volume refuses that formulation. Tokyo offers professional community, anonymity, and unavailable experience; Fukui offers family, continuous local recognition, ordinary intimacy, and a life she genuinely loves. The developmental act is not selecting the branch with the higher abstract value. It is becoming the person who can investigate, choose, bear loss, and later make the choice right.

Kenta supplies the cleanest process statement. An adult may foresee a plausible answer, but imposing it skips the deliberation through which a young person becomes capable of owning that future `[p-0024#p0010-p0025]`. Kazuki supplies the serious countermodel: he prefers attainable, lower-risk returns and refuses the kind of heat that obscures footing `[p-0024#p0031-p0042]`. The book therefore values ambitious movement without declaring prudence morally deficient.

The strongest conclusion is:

> **Volume 03 defines self-authorship as jurisdiction over the process by which a life becomes one's own. Recognition can help a person see capacities they cannot presently name; it becomes domination when the recognizer treats that insight as authority to choose, monitor, or absorb the consequences for them.**

## II. Asuka's distant role becomes an ordinary person

Saku begins by discovering that the senior he treated as solitary and exceptional has an ordinary social world. His jealousy is possessive but also epistemically useful: he must concede that Asuka exists outside the encounter form he values. Their same-year counterfactual—perhaps daily intimacy, perhaps no attraction at all—cannot authorize a different present `[p-0011-p0013]`.

Asuka then asks for a conventional date. She names herself as an ordinary high-schooler and says she has long loved him `[p-0017#p0117-p0152]`. Her strict teacher parents denied common youth experiences, so the person Saku read as effortless freedom developed under substantial constraint `[p-0017#p0189-p0200]`. Billiards mistakes, embarrassment, shopping, food, and crude bodily comedy do not destroy Saku's admiration. Ordinary exposure disconfirms idealization without disconfirming value.

Her vocation is also ordinary in the strongest sense: she loves books and wants to edit fiction, yet worries that love is not a sufficiently dramatic reason and may not survive employment. Saku validates the modest motive. Later, an observed author/editor meeting gives it material content: the editor rejects imitation, draws out lived experience through questions, and recognizes a story the author cannot yet see alone `[p-0029#p0507-p0558]`. Editing becomes disciplined recognition and transmission rather than praise or proofreading.

## III. Tokyo and Fukui are rival goods, not moral opposites

Asuka loves Fukui's dense continuity and fears it. A familiar community can catch a person when she falls, but can also script her adulthood before she chooses it. Tokyo's anonymity can liberate, but can also abandon. The journey supplies both claims: bodily disorientation, polluted air, constrained sky, danger, expense, and incompetence coexist with books, tastes, professional peers, and the exhilaration of possibility `[p-0029#p0105-p0219]`, `[p-0029#p0287-p0356]`, `[p-0029#p0601-p0677]`.

The old local-versus-metropolitan binary is explicitly reversible. An elderly Tokyo resident offers warmth; Fukui's attentiveness can become surveillance. Being seen and being ignored each have protective and coercive forms `[p-0029#p0435-p0460]`.

On campus Asuka can imagine an actual life, not a postcard. She and Saku acknowledge that a one-year lead will generate separate peers, work, competence, and perhaps romance. Linking little fingers promises forward motion without promising preserved proximity `[p-0029#p0360-p0408]`. Their attachment grows ethically stronger when Saku offers to make the remaining Fukui time something she can return to rather than using it as a reason she must stay `[p-0017#p0481-p0488]`.

## IV. Recognition, imitation, and the two Asukas

The chapter-four focalization confirms that Asuka was the child in the prologue. Across three summers and seven days, young Saku transformed a supposedly empty town into a field of possible adventure, asked what she wanted rather than what her father permitted, and took her past familiar boundaries `[p-0032#p0077-p0160]`. The white dress, river, left-ear signal, festival escape, and warm left hand are the source of present forms in both characters.

Asuka recognized Saku in high school and deliberately built “Asuka-nee” as repayment. She used his childhood mode of freedom to guide the enclosed adolescent he had become `[p-0032#p0291-p0343]`. She calls the result imitation and fears that Saku loved a projection through which he pursued his lost self. Saku gives the necessary correction: imitation may explain origin, but years of selected practice belong to the person who performed them. The present Asuka is not invalidated by having learned from someone `[p-0032#p0348-p0412]`.

This extends the V02 authenticity rule. Authenticity is retained authorship over a learned form, not absence of influence. The ending allows both “Asuka-nee” and Nishino Asukaze to remain usable parts of one life. Saku does not decide which is real; Asuka does `[p-0034#p0043-p0046]`.

## V. Saku's formation story and defensive heroism

The hotel conversation provides the first direct history behind Saku's role. He was a genuinely prosocial, high-performing child. Peers converted repeated competence into entitlement to his labor, treated ordinary limits as betrayal, sabotaged him to balance accounts, and erased hidden effort by calling achievement natural gift `[p-0031#p0203-p0270]`. A teacher's benevolent request that he serve as a hero became, in his interpretation, a mandate to become too perfect to approach.

His present persona is therefore both capacity and defense:

- he disclaims altruism so care cannot create an unlimited debt claim;
- conceals weakness so it cannot be weaponized;
- performs arrogance to neutralize accusations of false modesty;
- advertises romantic unreliability to preempt unwanted expectations;
- uses crude humor to vent pressure and keep intimacy deniable;
- preserves the original rescuing impulse through self-sacrifice, making himself the apparent disposable cost.

This history **explains** his behavior but does not automatically justify it. It also verifies that his competence and care preceded the polished high-school role. The role protects a real impulse rather than manufacturing one from nothing.

Asuka “edits” his account without replacing it. She identifies the supposedly lost child hero in the continuing imperfect rescues and rejects perfection as the threshold for value. A crescent or a marble can already be another person's treasure `[p-0031#p0323-p0398]`. She then asks before guiding physical touch, making consensual embodiment the proof that his light has reached someone `[p-0031#p0403-p0419]`.

## VI. The title metaphor becomes relational and co-authored

Saku says he pursued the moon but became the marble: beautiful, visible, and sealed inside the bottle, able to look outward but not arrive `[p-0031#p0307-p0322]`. The retrospective reveals that the image was never his alone. At the childhood festival he called the marble lonely; Asuka called it floating, loved, beautiful, and moonlike—like him `[p-0032#p0270-p0287]`.

The series title now has at least four live functions:

1. V01's beautiful enclosure;
2. V02's removable lens and mediated/inverted perception;
3. V03's divergent interpretations of one bounded object;
4. a relational symbol whose meaning enters Saku's self-model through Asuka's recognition.

The prologue imagines one distant moon and an imitation worthy of standing beside it. The epilogue rejects the monopoly: everyone is someone's moon, and either person may orient the other when lost `[p-0035#p0004-p0028]`. Reflected or borrowed light is not therefore false. It becomes one's own through relation and practice.

## VII. Saku's intervention ethics improve at the goal level and regress at the method level

Saku repeatedly articulates a defensible limit: he cannot solve Asuka's conflict or guarantee success; he can provide time, experience, and recognition so she can meet herself `[p-0029#p0084-p0100]`. At the final conference he initially tries to assume responsibility for supporting her after failure. Asuka interrupts and rejects transfer from paternal to romantic guardianship. She claims the risk and vocation herself `[p-0032#p0643-p0676]`. Saku accepts that correction.

His methods remain ethically unstable:

- he reveals Asuka's private editor dream to Kuranosuke without explicit permission because he reads the adults as asking him to break an impasse `[p-0020#p0018-p0047]`;
- he kicks Asuka into the river to disrupt resignation, combining accurate recognition with bodily coercion `[p-0020#p0092-p0155]`;
- he organizes a secret overnight Tokyo trip with incomplete disclosure to her parents and unsafe lodging decisions;
- at the hotel, he uses a physical/sexual feint while claiming to identify her “real” desire, which Asuka forcefully contests `[p-0031#p0081-p0158]`;
- he secretly plants an open-call phone in her bag to monitor the final family conference `[p-0032#p0428-p0437]`, `[p-0032#p0581-p0584]`.

The narrative often makes these acts useful, but usefulness is not prospective consent. V03 strengthens the distinction established by V02: authorization to help does not authorize any method. Saku can now concede that the other person owns the outcome while still manipulating the procedure around her.

## VIII. Parental authority receives a serious case and serious counterevidence

Asuka's father has real knowledge of competitive employment, financial dependency, former students' failed dreams, and alternative local careers. His concern is neither ignorant nor loveless `[p-0022#p0023-p0117]`. Kuranosuke supplies the best adult corrective: teachers accumulate knowledge of failure without becoming infallible, and a chosen future must ultimately be made right by its chooser `[p-0022#p0197-p0250]`.

The father converts risk assessment into unilateral control, slaps Asuka after her return, and escalates to a threat of family estrangement. He later explains that opposition was partly a stress test and admits the ultimatum exceeded the method because his abandoned music dream, projection, and jealousy entered the scene `[p-0032#p0689-p0724]`.

**VALUE JUDGMENT:** the explanation humanizes him and clarifies motivation; it does not retroactively legitimate physical punishment, emotional coercion, or a test whose subject was not informed. Recognition of Asuka's resilience and final permission are positive changes, not proof that the route was healthy.

## IX. Love becomes mutual recognition without exclusive resolution

Asuka and Saku repeat one another's language of love after the childhood truth emerges `[p-0032#p0348-p0412]`. This makes affection mutually explicit. It does **not** establish an exclusive dating relationship. They complete the childhood first-love/imitation story so Nishino Asukaze's own story can begin, while preserving future contact and a promised visit to his grandmother.

Their mutual moon promises turn a one-way hero relation into reciprocal orientation. The promise also contains a risk: if Asuka makes career success proof of Saku's worth, failure could become relational betrayal. Later readings must test whether they can inspire one another without making either life evidentiary collateral for the other.

At the farewell, Asuka vows to shed a lifetime's tears so she will not rely on anyone once she leaves `[p-0034#p0002-p0004]`. This is intelligible resistance to guardianship, but it may reproduce the invulnerable role she has just escaped. Saku's shared grief demonstrates a healthier possibility: need can be witnessed without becoming rescue or debt.

## X. The peer group: differentiated care rather than a romantic scoreboard

Yuzuki acts from named love by urging Saku toward the important feeling he may lose, knowingly aiding a romantic rival `[p-0022#p0260-p0297]`, `[p-0024#p0144-p0166]`. This answers the V03 entry question strongly: her love operates as costly recognition, not territorial possession. Her crescent-lamp gift in the pre-V03 bonus provides an ordinary-life counterpart.

Haru restores obsolete baseball expertise to a relational present. She creates a catch partnership, says she likes the invested teaching Saku, and refuses former teammates' belated authority over whether he should return `[p-0015#p0123-p0225]`. Her directness remains important, but V03 does not materially extend the specific Haru/Yuzuki optimization dialectic.

Yuko learns domestic skills so gendered incapacity cannot narrow her future, helps Haru experiment with a dress without treating athletic presentation as deficient, and shifts jealousy toward a promise to offer her own best. Yua's practical care exposes the loneliness beneath Saku's solitary competence. Both show that ordinary meals, gifts, objects, and maintenance can create claims without demanding crisis or exclusivity.

Kenta's inclusion is decisively durable. He joins a release-day outing, leads through specialist knowledge, absorbs frank acknowledgement of Kazuki's former contempt, eats with the group, and receives present-tense membership grounded in continuing effort `[p-0022#p0379-p0465]`. He is an ordinary, sometimes comic friend—not a renewed project.

## XI. Baseball is grief, play, and a reopened possibility—not yet a resolved return

Haru engineers Saku's first recreational throwing after nearly a year. Sensory memory releases pleasure and grief; teaching glove care makes formerly stranded knowledge usable `[p-0015#p0123-p0191]`. Yusuke asks him to return, and Saku's anger at “you of all people” exposes unresolved betrayal. Atomu's later competence and challenge show that neither boy's attachment to baseball is dead `[p-0015#p0197-p0225]`, `[p-0017#p0002-p0031]`.

V03 therefore resolves the V01/V02 demand for formation history but not the future of baseball. The sport shifts from an explanatory absence to an active relational and developmental field. Whether Saku returns to institutional competition remains `OPEN`.

## XII. Ordinary life and family loneliness

Saku's apartment has accumulated three place settings through Yuko and Yua, but after visitors leave the silence still verifies his loneliness. His parents are divorcing; their ethic has been to respect his choice while making him bear the consequences. Solitary living initially felt like freedom, yet he admits lonely nights and gratitude for the group's presence `[p-0015#p0017-p0103]`.

Cooking, shared meals, glove care, bicycles, trains, bookstores, bathing embarrassment, and gift exchange prevent the volume's autonomy thesis from becoming abstract. Self-sufficiency can secure jurisdiction and still fail to answer the ordinary need for company.

## XV. In-EPUB bonus: “王様とバースデー” (`BONUS_FICTION`)

The bonus explicitly says it is a prose rewrite of the mail-order drama-CD script, with minor differences from the audio, and that its event falls between V02 and V03 `[p-0037#p0002]`. It places Saku's seventeenth birthday in late May. Its gifts operate as a material relationship map:

- Yuzuki gives a crescent bedside lamp and asks to remain within memory without demanding reciprocity;
- Yua gives the exact phone case and protective film suited to him, then asks to be ordinary and always near rather than elevated as distant and precious;
- Yuko gives a yukata and future fireworks promise, choosing self-authored romantic effort over rivalry policing;
- Haru buys herself a glove, offering Saku a catch partner and giving the baseball absence a shared present;
- Asuka gives matching turquoise wireless earbuds, demonstrating old knowledge of him and making “unconnected” connection the gift's paradox;
- Kenta participates as an ordinary party member and judge.

The lamp, glove, and earbuds explain objects or actions in the main volume. This permits a continuity update only with the bonus class visible. It does not allow the bonus to be silently treated as part of the main chapter sequence.

## XVII. Rival readings and hard limits

### Rival A — Asuka is only Saku's idealized muse

**Rejected.** Her focalization, childhood authorship, vocation research, family argument, responsibility claim, ordinary embarrassment, and grief exceed that function. Saku's projection matters, but the plot explicitly transfers final authority to Nishino Asukaze.

### Rival B — Tokyo is freedom and Fukui is repression

**Rejected.** Each place combines protection and constraint. Asuka's choice is informed by concrete vocation, not a universal hierarchy of cities.

### Rival C — successful outcomes vindicate Saku's methods

**Rejected as too strong.** The source repeatedly supplies retrospective benefit, but also direct refusal, privacy violation, bodily coercion, unsafe planning, and Asuka's rejection of transferred guardianship.

### Rival D — mutual love establishes an exclusive couple

**Unsupported.** Saku and Asuka explicitly recognize love, but do not enter an exclusive dating contract. Other directional affections remain live and the language deliberately resists simple categorization.

### Rival E — the father's opposition is either pure wisdom or pure tyranny

**Both are reductive.** His risk knowledge and care are real; so are his coercive authority, slap, ultimatum, projection, and ethically invalid stress-test method.

## Freeze statement

Within the Volume 03 main-witness boundary, Asuka's departure does not solve a geographic puzzle; it clarifies an ethical one. The future belongs to her because she researched, chose, spoke, accepted loss, and refused both paternal and romantic ownership of the consequences. Saku's major development is allowing love to support departure and receiving Asuka's account of him. His unresolved failure is procedural: he increasingly respects another person's final answer while still treating invasive methods as his discretionary tools.
