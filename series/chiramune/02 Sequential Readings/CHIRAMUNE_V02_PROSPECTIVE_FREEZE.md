## Frozen V02 findings

### F13 — Yuzuki's social persona predates the Yanagishita trauma

**Class:** FACT
**Confidence:** high

Yuzuki describes her calibrated presentation as an incremental response to ordinary social friction around beauty, competence, envy, and unwanted attention. She explicitly rejects the need for a single dramatic trauma-origin story `[p-0032:002-013]`.

**Do not later rewrite as:** the assault incident created her mask.

### F14 — Yuzuki's trauma exposed a missing inner jurisdiction, not merely fear of pain

**Class:** FACT + INFERENCE
**Confidence:** high

She identifies the deeper terror as discovering that when social skill and persuasion failed, she could find no principle inside herself that remained decisive under force `[p-0032:015-023]`.

### F15 — Saku and Yuzuki share social tools but are not strong-form mirrors

**Class:** FACT + INFERENCE
**Confidence:** high

Yuzuki explicitly revises the initial similarity model. She avoids and optimizes around obstacles; Saku often collides with them despite possessing the skill to evade them `[p-0032:045-055]`.

### F16 — Yuzuki consciously names her feeling for Saku as romantic love by V02 end

**Class:** FACT
**Confidence:** high

Her Chapter 4 focalization explicitly says she has put a name to the feeling, and the epilogue frames the resulting movement as genuine love rather than possession/projection.

### F17 — Saku does not reciprocally name his feeling as love within V02

**Class:** FACT
**Confidence:** high

He shows strong attachment and attraction but explicitly delays naming the emotion at the festival and near the ending `[p-0026:070-075]`, `[p-0034:261-262]`.

### F18 — Saku has learned that entering another person's problem requires stronger authorization

**Class:** FACT + INFERENCE
**Confidence:** high

He consciously refrains from taking over Yuzuki's interior problem before she asks, distinguishes the requested boyfriend/bodyguard role from unsolicited psychological rescue, and marks her explicit request for help as the transition from "her problem" to "our problem" `[p-0027:274-285]`, `[p-0031:101-106]`.

### F19 — that improvement does not solve consent-to-method

**Class:** FACT + VALUE JUDGMENT
**Confidence:** high

Saku deliberately reenacts coercive elements of Yuzuki's trauma without prior consent. Yuzuki later experiences the intervention as catalytic and thanks him, but retrospective approval does not establish prospective informed consent. This remains a major ethical counterexample to any blanket claim that his intervention style is justified by competence.

### F20 — relationships create claims on Saku that his self-disposability model initially ignores

**Class:** FACT + INFERENCE
**Confidence:** high

Yua and Yuko object that dangerous choices involving his own body also injure those who care about him. Saku subsequently consults the group before his final dangerous plan rather than acting entirely alone.

### F21 — Haru is a structural corrective to optimization

**Class:** FACT + INFERENCE
**Confidence:** high

Yuzuki chose Fujishi after losing to Haru's direct, persistent basketball style. Haru later challenges Yuzuki's tendency to remain outside the contest waiting for the clean line. This parallels Haru's V01 correction of Saku.

### F22 — Tomoya is Kenta's negative parallel, not evidence that low status causes predation

**Class:** INFERENCE
**Confidence:** high

Kenta accepts disconfirmation, embarrassment, practice, and reciprocal contact. Tomoya protects an idealized Yuzuki by surveillance, manipulation, and manufactured vulnerability. The meaningful contrast is willingness to let the other person contradict one's story.

### F23 — Asuka's concrete future choice destabilizes Saku's idealization

**Class:** FACT + INFERENCE
**Confidence:** medium-high

Asuka is considering remaining in Fukui or going to Tokyo. Saku discovers her aspiration is both more ordinary and more consequential than his distant ideal of her. He cannot directly answer whether he would follow if she asked `[p-0031:484-539]`.

### F24 — Saku is relationally embedded but jurisdictionally isolated

**Class:** INFERENCE
**Confidence:** high within V02

His home and routines contain practical traces of friends, and he genuinely accepts companionship and ordinary care. His isolation lies more in reserving final authorship and burden-bearing to himself than in lacking relationships.

### F25 — status amplifies exposure as well as protection

**Class:** FACT + INFERENCE
**Confidence:** high

Publicly pairing two high-status students generates anonymous hostility and competitive punishment. Accumulated status can make mistakes survivable, but visibility also increases envy, policing, and reputational attack.

### F26 — the Ramune motif cannot be frozen as enclosure alone

**Class:** INFERENCE
**Confidence:** medium-high

At the festival the removed marble becomes a lens through which Saku and Yuzuki view an inverted, beautiful world. V02 broadens the motif toward mediated perception, intimacy, impermanence, and transformation `[p-0026:086-098]`.
