## 3. Chapter 1 — `波打ち際の青`

### Misaki's athletic crisis is a crisis of repertoire and gendered self-recognition

Second-year Misaki has stalled at 170 centimeters while her Ashiba teammate and former equal, Tominaga Rei, has grown to 180 and become the obvious ace. Misaki understands abstractly that 170 is still an advantage and that basketball permits many styles; affectively, she has tied scoring, height, competitive womanhood, and first love into one model. The gap therefore feels like a transparent ceiling rather than an ordinary role change `[V095:p-0012#p0271-p0349]`.

Her problem is not lack of effort. It is repetition of the approach that once succeeded. She has let one physical advantage define the whole game and then treated its limit as the limit of her self. The source preserves the structural reality that height matters while refusing the inference that one body can support only one authored style.

### Kuranosuke's first intervention establishes method before romance

The previous July, three boys corner Misaki in a game center after Tominaga leaves. Kuranosuke interrupts, maintains enough distance to give her an exit, attracts public attention, and sends her away rather than demand disclosure or gratitude. He is hurt after she escapes. Misaki later tells the discreet Nishino-sensei, who treats safety and her privacy as compatible responsibilities `[V095:p-0014#p0038-p0120]`, `[V095:p-0016#p0001-p0112]`.

The rescue does not become ownership. Misaki chooses when to find Kuranosuke, tends his wounds, thanks him, and begins visiting the roof he cleans. The episode still contains an adult-protection gap—the students avoid police and formal process—but its relational effect comes from Kuranosuke's refusal to turn danger into a claim on her.

### Two vocational authorships are formed through reciprocal recognition

Misaki privately tells Kuranosuke that she wants to become a high-school teacher and women's basketball coach. She wants to transmit not only joy and success but pain, failure, regret, and the unresolved first love of sport `[V095:p-0018#p0083-p0107]`. This is a durable desire later enacted in the main corpus, not a vocation retrospectively invented after she became a coach.

Kuranosuke, by contrast, marks `employment` on his career form because he has already dismissed his own possibility. Nishino-sensei does not denigrate construction work. He rejects the absence of Kuranosuke's choice and explains that any durable trade requires accumulated technique, qualification, and responsibility `[V095:p-0012#p0184-p0246]`.

At Akiyoshi, Nishino describes an intentionally ordinary Fukui life: family, familiar places, teaching, and continuity. Its outward shape resembles Kuranosuke's imagined future, but Nishino can call it happy because he selected and sustains it `[V095:p-0020#p0123-p0155]`. The distinction is not glamorous versus provincial life; it is authored commitment versus anticipatory surrender.

### The shrine conversation returns interpretation to Misaki

Kuranosuke has previously seen Misaki cry at a shrine and later watched her play. He brings a drink after practice and waits until she chooses to disclose the athletic crisis. His basketball-as-love analogy asks whether a beloved object that accepted her when she was short could only love a tall player. He recalls the skills created before height and reads Tominaga's conspicuous effort as trust that Misaki will return as the hidden tournament weapon `[V095:p-0020#p0160-p0235]`, `[V095:p-0022#p0001-p0125]`.

The interpretation matters, but it does not manufacture her capacity. Misaki chooses the uptake, returns to Tominaga, and rebuilds her game from handling, passing, speed, shooting, footwork, and a body newly understood as well suited to that repertoire `[V095:p-0022#p0172-p0419]`.

Her narration recodes the performance from a sexless warrior ideal into a self-chosen `戦う女`. Romance is not purified away from sport; neither is the man made the sole author of recovery. The match's visual and verbal grammar lets her attract basketball and Kuranosuke while retaining technical ownership `[V095:p-0024#p0001-p0018]`.

### Misaki's recovery gives Kuranosuke permission to imagine himself

Watching her, Kuranosuke recognizes that his words may have shortened the time of suffering without creating the ability he admires. That limited efficacy is enough to reopen his own discarded possibility. Misaki's earlier joke that he seems suited to teaching becomes a live question, and he asks Nishino how to become a teacher `[V095:p-0024#p0020-p0067]`.

The chapter explicitly frames Misaki and Kuranosuke as already in love before either fully recognizes it `[V095:p-0024#p0001-p0018]`. That establishes mutually framed historical romantic feeling. It does not establish that they dated then or that their adult relation has a named couple status.

## 4. Chapter 2 — `月見草`

### Nazuna's middle-school loss explains both her cessation and her diagnostic style

Third-year middle-school Nazuna enters a prefectural quarterfinal expecting Yuzuki to be a manageable rival. Yuzuki's first steal, three-pointer, no-look pass, mirrored counter, and ankle break reveal not only talent but a repertoire made calm through immense repetition `[V095:p-0026#p0058-p0145]`, `[V095:p-0026#p0152-p0206]`.

Nazuna responds by exposing her own best asset, adhesive defense, but is still surpassed. She then watches Yuzuki lose to Aomi Haru and Aomi lose to Todo Mai. The sequence teaches her that people more gifted than she is can also work harder. She stops rather than continue through that escalating chain `[V095:p-0026#p0208-p0224]`, `[V095:p-0028#p0001-p0007]`.

This is neither proof that she lacked seriousness nor a claim that the hierarchy was objectively impassable. Her current narration calls the old decision unfinished: she wonders whether she could have fought longer and asks the women who continued to show her the path she abandoned.

### Current Nazuna chooses witness, friction, and limited participation

In the cool-autumn frame, exercise has not restored competitive heat, but neither has she fully extinguished it. She finds Atomu alone with a large supply of hardballs, practicing enough pitches to be physically exhausted and adding a sweeper to his repertoire. He says he must stay close enough to kick Saku's back if needed `[V095:p-0028#p0015-p0049]`, `[V095:p-0030#p0001-p0016]`.

This is concrete renewed baseball practice for Atomu, stronger than a passing mood. It remains private and noninstitutional: there is no team, school return, educational plan, or vocation.

Nazuna asks Atomu to teach her throwing and catching. The brief catch is not a basketball comeback or baseball commitment. It lets her feel the skill embodied by the people she watches and inspect the seriousness of Aomi, Yuzuki, and Atomu rather than remain a distant commentator `[V095:p-0030#p0017-p0053]`.

She then chooses a durable supporting posture: if the others become self-pitying, the low-growing `nazuna` can trip or kick them back into motion while demanding the continuation she did not live `[V095:p-0030#p0054-p0066]`. The stance is caring and interested, not neutral; she converts regret into pressure on others without reopening her own competitive path.

## 5. Chapter 3 — `寄せては返す`

### The roof becomes an explicit intergenerational institution

Adult Misaki and Iwanami are now colleagues at Fujishi. Their old roof register returns privately, while professional address and distance govern ordinary school conduct `[V095:p-0032#p0021-p0065]`.

Iwanami identifies Asuka Nishino as the first roof cleaner in the present Fujishi succession, chosen partly as a return to her father. Saku later inherits access, and Saku appoints Momiji as the third generation. The line is therefore not one man's private territory: it is a chain of entrusted access and maintenance whose authority can move across generations `[V095:p-0032#p0066-p0085]`.

The source also closes the older loop. Nishino-sensei first assigned teenage Kuranosuke to roof cleaning; Kuranosuke later creates a line beginning with Nishino's daughter. Institutional memory becomes reciprocal rather than linear.

### Adulthood is ordinary maintenance, not achieved purity

Misaki and Iwanami discuss changing media, old music, coffee, smoking, work, cars, and the familiar places students still use. They understand Nishino's love of repeated Fukui life more fully because they now help maintain it for another generation `[V095:p-0032#p0087-p0148]`.

Their cars materialize different versions of chosen imperfection. Iwanami's old Rasheen fails because he postponed maintenance; Misaki's older Land Cruiser is carefully customized and prepared, including booster cables and self-installed soundproofing. Preference becomes adult practice only when supported by labor `[V095:p-0032#p0150-p0223]`, `[V095:p-0032#p0224-p0308]`.

Misaki's unplanned ride becomes a deliberately lengthened route. Mutual teasing, remembered forms of address, shared music, and her choice to join the Akiyoshi gathering all establish continuing intimacy and reciprocal attraction. Their jokes about girlfriend and fiancée do not name an actual relationship `[V095:p-0034#p0001-p0057]`.

When Nishino meets Misaki, he recognizes her as the woman who changed the student he once worried over. Iwanami privately confirms that Misaki, not Nishino alone, initiated his teaching vocation, but leaves the causal gift unclaimed rather than use it to obligate her `[V095:p-0034#p0058-p0079]`.

The adult close accepts `ろくでもない大人` as a maintained, revisable form rather than a heroic endpoint. Work, friendship, drink, memory, cars, and care for students make an ordinary life that is chosen repeatedly `[V095:p-0034#p0080-p0094]`.
