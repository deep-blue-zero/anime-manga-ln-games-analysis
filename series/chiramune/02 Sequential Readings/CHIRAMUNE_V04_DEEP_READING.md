## I. Governing movement: passion must be translated, not imposed

Volume 04 builds two linked sports crises around one ethical question: how can one person's heat move another without claiming authority over the other's answer? Haru's force helps Saku confront the baseball scene he abandoned. The same force damages her basketball team when she and Yuzuki secretly define the teammates' seriousness, divide leadership into coercion and consolation, and treat expanded sacrifice as the true meaning of a shared goal.

The volume distinguishes outcome, method, and uptake. Haru may identify real avoidance in Saku and Sen, yet recognition does not grant her the right to determine their decisions. Saku's return becomes constructive only after Haru refuses to decide for him; the basketball team recovers only when its members can identify their own limits and contribute their distinct capacities. Coach Misaki states the procedural principle through sport: it is the players' basketball, so recognition of the limit must become theirs `[p-0025#p0088-p0100]`.

The strongest conclusion is:

> **Volume 04 treats passion as ethically generative when it becomes an offered intensity that another person can translate into self-authored action. The same passion becomes coercive when its bearer defines another person's interior seriousness, hides the terms of leadership, or makes bodily extremity the compulsory proof of commitment.**

## II. Haru's sun: disciplined heat and fear of extinction

Haru's prologue supplies the first sustained view from inside her athletic identity. Defeat does not primarily threaten status. It threatens the red flame by which she recognizes herself: the possibility that she will become someone who smiles thinly, accepts the limit, and calls surrender maturity `[p-0010#p0004-p0025]`.

Her response is disciplined bodily repetition. She wants to discover whether her all-out present will avert future regret and prove that muddy, passionate effort is not embarrassing `[p-0010#p0026-p0029]`. Yet the night sky exposes a relational need. The person named for the sun still seeks a sufficiently large moon capable of receiving her heat `[p-0010#p0031]`.

That need prevents a simple self-sufficient-athlete reading. Haru's public directness and relentless movement are real strengths. They are also vulnerable to becoming a performance in which doubt, reliance, rest, and methodological disagreement feel like betrayal of the self.

## III. Basketball reveals the difference between a shared goal and a shared method

Haru's team unanimously chose her as captain and unanimously named an Inter-High championship. Those facts establish genuine collective aspiration, not retroactive consent to every training demand. Haru and Yuzuki privately decide that teammates are protecting themselves through premature limits. They create a hidden arrangement in which Haru will be the hated demon and Yuzuki the soothing intermediary `[p-0023#p0237-p0313]`, `[p-0023#p0264-p0025#p0063]`.

The scheme fails in three ways:

1. it converts distributed leadership into a concealed binary;
2. it treats Haru and Yuzuki's reading of teammates' inner effort as authoritative;
3. it makes Haru's visible self-endangerment evidence that others owe equivalent sacrifice.

Sen's complaint is therefore not reducible to laziness. She affirms the goal but rejects the longer hours and Haru's authority to declare her existing best unreal. Haru can point to a specific play Sen abandoned, but moves improperly from conduct to total interior judgment `[p-0025#p0067-p0082]`. The team's walkout shows that a correct diagnosis delivered through adversarial authority may still destroy the cooperation it needs.

Coach Misaki refuses to become an imposed villain and explicitly compares the mistake to Wataya's management of Saku. Her intervention corrects both Haru and Saku: a noble outcome and accurate expertise do not make unilateral method legitimate `[p-0025#p0088-p0100]`.

## IV. Yuzuki becomes an independent co-leader and rival

Yuzuki initially helps sustain the hidden compact: she completes permissions Haru forgets, absorbs complaints, and delays public confrontation. Her optimization is not coldness; it is loyal labor. But loyalty has placed her inside an arrangement she increasingly doubts.

After Haru collapses, Yuzuki directly asks teammates whether they still want the championship and what training they consider good. Their answers preserve the goal while rejecting uniform sacrifice `[p-0023#p0131-p0166]`. During the break she organizes voluntary practices, preserving readiness without pretending that command has already repaired consent `[p-0032#p0030-p0068]`.

In the practice match she makes the team's missing interdependence visible through play, then joins Haru openly, takes difficult shots, and promises not to leave her alone `[p-0032#p0127-p0280]`. This is the clearest consequential group direction yet led by someone other than Saku. Haru names Yuzuki a rival in both basketball and love, recognizing correction, competition, and intimacy within the same relationship.

Yuzuki still does not directly ask Saku for romantic clarity. Her hurt appears through understatement, name register, and a remark about his formidable faith in Haru `[p-0025#p0370-p0380]`. V04 therefore strengthens her active agency while leaving the deniable-bid problem open.

## V. Saku discloses baseball grief and accepts another person's repertoire

Saku never stopped swinging. Daily practice and the wood bat he bought after quitting show that baseball survives as embodied routine even while he rejects Fujishi's institution `[p-0015#p0079-p0118]`. Wataya's contempt disturbs his body before he is ready to narrate why.

When former captain Yusuke reveals that Wataya kept Saku on every roster, Saku rejects reconciliation offered only when the old team needs his ability. Haru prevents him from punching concrete with his dominant hand, identifies herself as his current catch partner, and stays present without pretending the immediate interruption settles the conflict `[p-0017#p0063-p0199]`.

At Asuwayama she invokes their genuinely tied wager and asks for the weakness he owes. Her promised response is not unconditional validation but a full relational repertoire: listening, shared anger, rebuke, grief, and courage `[p-0020#p0503-p0555]`. Saku then gives the first complete account of Wataya's abusive control, Yusuke's benching, his own resistance, and the teammates' final gifted/ungifted betrayal `[p-0020#p0559-p0723]`.

Haru condemns the adults and team, but is angriest that Saku abandoned the sport which had constituted his life and once restored her own will. Saku realizes that he wanted someone to rebuke the self who fled, cries openly, and accepts sustained physical and emotional accompaniment `[p-0020#p0727-p0022#p0020]`.

The ethical hinge follows: Haru refuses to choose for him. She distinguishes the parts of his account she loves and rejects, then leaves the decision with him `[p-0022#p0024-p0049]`. This is not rescue by superior insight. It is relational pressure followed by restored jurisdiction.

## VI. The one-game return closes an institution without extinguishing play

Saku chooses one game as a self-authored “retirement game.” He does not return for Yusuke, duty, or a repaired club. Wataya accepts his external-helper status, separate training condition, and bounded participation `[p-0025#p0228-p0303]`. Saku distinguishes acting “for Haru” from acting “thanks to Haru”: her intervention altered what he could see, but the purpose and limit are his `[p-0025#p0307-p0329]`.

The wood bat materializes continuity with the year after quitting. Saku first misses, recalibrates, and trains with Atomu rather than receiving effortless restoration `[p-0025#p0387-p0027#p0026]`. Atomu supplies a paired closure: he quit after intentionally walking Saku rather than confronting fear. Saku gives him the missed at-bat, not consolation or a command to resume baseball `[p-0027#p0200-p0273]`.

In the game Saku preserves Yusuke's captaincy, plays in his own style, and makes the old group's change visible. Wataya praises the backhand catch he once would have condemned `[p-0028#p0061-p0189]`. After the win, Saku refuses continued participation, names the prior summer finished, and thanks the teams and sport `[p-0030#p0748-p0032#p0027]`.

V04 therefore resolves the institutional-return question only in bounded form. Saku does not rejoin Fujishi baseball. His relation to chosen, noninstitutional, or later baseball remains open.

## VII. Distributed support replaces the solitary hero outcome

The current friend group builds Saku's practice through differentiated, ordinary contributions: food, water, pitching, fielding, ball return, heckling, and witness. Asuka joins without displacing the others; Kenta participates as an ordinary friend. The scene could exist only because Saku's life did not reset to the old team `[p-0027#p0119-p0196]`.

The game repeats this structure. Saku's home run is a spectacular individual act, but his final hit reaches only the fence. When he considers an unsafe run home, Hirano orders him to stop, then brings him in; injured Yusuke delivers the winning home run `[p-0030#p0600-p0737]`. The illustration of teammates supporting Saku makes the anti-solitary endpoint visually explicit (`p-0031#img01`).

The corresponding basketball sequence distributes capacity in the same way. Sen's defense, Yo's rebounding, Yuzuki's risk-taking, Haru's speed, and a movement Haru translates from Saku's batting each change what the others can do `[p-0032#p0356-p0510]`. The volume's unit of meaningful effort is ultimately an interdependent system, not an exceptional body proving everyone else inadequate.

## VIII. Adult change is behaviorally real but does not erase harm

Wataya privately apologizes. He identifies his projection, his prejudice against giftedness, and the mistake of taking away the one thing Saku should have been allowed simply to enjoy `[p-0030#p0215-p0242]`. More importantly, he accepts Saku's external-helper boundary and praises the style he previously suppressed. Those behaviors support real change.

He does not demand forgiveness. His refusal to apologize publicly because it might negate the team's intervening year is understandable but contestable; it leaves collective history incompletely addressed. Saku's conclusion—that coach, teammates, and he himself were each partly right and wrong—rejects both total absolution and a single-villain explanation.

## IX. Effort is freed from guaranteed victory but remains entangled with injury

Mai defeats Haru without needing the three-point advantage. Height, strength, speed, and skill refuse a consoling claim that effort makes structural difference unreal `[p-0018#p0273-p0020#p0036]`. Saku gives Haru the strongest non-guarantee: effort does not ensure victory, but only Haru can discover what her fullest attempt can become `[p-0020#p0090-p0171]`.

The basketball team later loses the practice game by three. This is not narrated as failed inspiration. Their credible contest and restored coordination matter without erasing the remaining difference `[p-0032#p0541-p0564]`.

The volume's major counterevidence is bodily risk. Haru collapses after skipping food and hydration. Saku and Atomu continue practice with damaged hands. Saku severely injures his left wrist, conceals it, and refuses Wataya's medically prudent request to stop `[p-0023#p0004-p0127]`, `[p-0030#p0023-p0323]`. Haru again approaches collapse in the final match.

**VALUE JUDGMENT:** self-authored intensity can be meaningful without making injury a valid proof of seriousness. The narrative supplies some dissent but repeatedly converts damage into heroic visibility. Safety, rest, and sustainable effort remain materially underweighted.

## X. Haru and Saku form a reciprocal circuit, not a savior hierarchy

Saku's final middle-school game once kept Haru from accepting defeat. Her present witness and rebuke let him reopen his abandoned ending. Each gives the other a form of heat that can be translated into a separate field `[p-0027#p0277-p0302]`.

The epilogue rejects a one-way rescue reading. Haru admits she projected Saku as the large moon who could receive her passion and prove her way of living. She then concludes that his own fire remained and that he would principally have reached an answer for himself `[p-0035#p0004-p0025]`. She wants to run beside him, not orbit him or own his recovery `[p-0035#p0026-p0032]`.

Their best acts preserve this reciprocity. Haru does not decide the baseball answer; Saku does not supply an abstract solution to her team conflict. They exchange evidence, embodied trust, correction, and a promise of visible support.

## XI. Direct love creates a real bid without settling the relationship

Haru repeatedly tells Saku that she loves him. After his home run, he displays her wristband directly to her; she answers publicly before the other girls with an explicit declaration `[p-0030#p0001-p0019]`. The selection is real at the scene level without becoming an exclusive commitment.

Haru also imagines ordinary dating—exercise, food, massage, daily bodily life. Saku calls it attractive but declines present romance while she still has an unfinished athletic fight; he promises to cheer for that pursuit `[p-0027#p0027-p0074]`. This can respect her active life, but it also lets him postpone his own classification.

At the final Ramune scene, Haru's attempted lip kiss lands on his throat because of their height difference. She names the remaining distance as Saku's future move `[p-0034#p0001-p0025]`. The gesture transfers next-move jurisdiction rather than manufacturing reciprocity. Saku neither rejects her nor answers in equivalent love language.

The current state is therefore **direct Haru love + received intimacy + a Saku promise of support + an explicitly transferred but unanswered next move**. It is not an established couple.

## XII. V04 narrows the Saku/Asuka love classification

V03 remains historically accurate: Saku and Asuka repeated love language. V04's later Saku interiority says that he likes and is drawn to Asuka but does not know whether to call the feeling romantic love `[p-0015#p0130-p0166]`. This does not erase the earlier utterance or Asuka's long-named love. It does reject the stronger timeless formulation that Saku now possesses a settled romantic category.

Asuka recognizes the unseen labor in his continuing swings and presses against his usual deflection, but he says he could not show even her the uncool reason he quit `[p-0015#p0186-p0017#p0008]`. She later joins the distributed baseball support. V04 materially revises categorical certainty while preserving attraction, importance, shared history, and nonexclusivity.

## XIII. Other relationship movements remain directional

- **Yua:** Saku notices her ordinary need and buys the nearly sold-out bread before she asks. She values being noticed and later promises to stand beside him for a future saxophone performance; he invites the performance but cannot yet answer its meaning `[p-0011#p0405-p0013#p0160]`.
- **Yuko:** Saku voluntarily tells her about the baseball invitation after recalling their agreement to speak honestly. She names him as her special person. Nazuna challenges Yuko's moratorium on his answer, revealing that delayed confession also constrains his opportunity to choose while relationships change `[p-0018#p0101-p0267]`.
- **Yuzuki:** she becomes Haru's public co-leader and explicit rival but still does not ask Saku directly for a romantic answer.
- **Kenta:** ordinary teasing and practical participation continue without renewed client status.
- **The group:** Haru's public declaration is absorbed as meaningful competitive action rather than a forced ranking; no relationship is converted into an exclusive couple.

## XIV. Ramune is opened through coordinated asymmetry

Saku's wrist injury makes one-handed opening impossible. He steadies the bottle with his right hand while Haru's left pushes the marble; their hands remain joined as the seal opens `[p-0032#p0574-p0034#p0008]`.

This scene revises the title complex without cancelling earlier functions:

1. the bottle remains an enclosure that cannot be opened by solitary force in this state;
2. opening is collaborative but asymmetrical, with distinct tasks rather than fused identity;
3. the marble becomes retrievable when Saku removes it after finishing the drink;
4. raised against a divided sky, it becomes a lens and marker for Haru's light `[p-0034#p0027-p0042]`.

The prologue's Haru-sun/Saku-moon arrangement also reverses. Saku experiences Haru as a sun; Haru experiences Saku as a red sun; borrowed sunlight lets moonlight matter in another field `[p-0027#p0331-p0338]`, `[p-0030#p0632-p0737]`, `[p-0035]`. Celestial roles are relational positions of giving and receiving light, not fixed character essences.

## XV. Ordinary life and visual evidence

Meals, condiments, pool cleaning, bought bread, sleeping in a classroom, practice routines, sports equipment, a carefully selected dress, and walking home keep the two crisis plots embodied in daily life. Haru's requested romance is likewise mundane rather than purely symbolic.

The cover and color frontmatter make Haru the volume's title-image claimant and juxtapose basketball, baseball, pool play, date-like presentation, and Ramune. Ten interior illustrations trace the same movement: Yua's noticed need (`p-0012#img01`); Haru's undefended sleep (`p-0014#img01`); Asuka holding Saku's bat (`p-0016#img01`); Haru straining against Mai (`p-0019#img01`); Haru's tearful rebuke (`p-0021#img01`); the Haru/Yuzuki compact (`p-0024#img01`); Saku/Atomu practice (`p-0026#img01`); the wristband home-run signal (`p-0029#img01`); Saku carried by the old team (`p-0031#img01`); and Haru's throat kiss with the Ramune bottle (`p-0033#img01`).

The sequence repeatedly moves from an individual under pressure to a dyad or team that can receive the pressure, then returns to an intimate bid whose answer remains open.

## XVIII. Rival readings and hard limits

### Rival A — Haru saves Saku through force of personality

**Rejected.** Her testimony and anger materially change his available view, but she refuses to decide for him; the epilogue insists his own fire remained. The better model is relationally enabled self-authorship.

### Rival B — the basketball team proves Haru was right all along

**Rejected as too strong.** Teammates had named the championship goal and later confront their own excuses, but their objections to training quantity, hidden governance, and interior judgment were reasonable. Repair does not fully address those harms.

### Rival C — one game means Saku has returned to baseball

**Rejected.** He carefully bounds the appearance, preserves Yusuke's captaincy, calls it a retirement game, and refuses continuation.

### Rival D — Haru's declarations establish a Saku/Haru couple

**Unsupported.** Her love and his directed wristband signal are real. He does not reciprocally name love or accept an exclusive relationship, and she explicitly assigns him the future move.

### Rival E — V04 proves V03's Saku/Asuka love statement false

**Rejected.** V03's speech event remains fact. V04 changes the current interpretation of Saku's categorical certainty; it does not erase the historical mutual wording, attraction, or importance.

### Rival F — injuries validate effort

**Rejected as an ethical inference.** The volume celebrates extreme exertion and gives it efficacy, but chosen risk does not make bodily damage the proper test of seriousness.

## Freeze statement

Within the Volume 04 boundary, passion becomes relation without becoming ownership. Haru's heat helps Saku choose and close one unfinished summer because she eventually refuses to make the choice for him. The same heat injures basketball trust when she and Yuzuki hide governance and define teammates' seriousness from above. Both sports plots recover through distributed action, not solitary heroism. The volume's unresolved costs are equally structural: injury is romanticized, team repair is incomplete, and Haru's direct love still awaits Saku's authored answer.
