## I. Governing movement: repair requires surrendering unilateral protection

V05 ends with Saku treating honest refusal as a debt payable through exclusion and pain. V06 rejects that equation without declaring the refusal wrong. Its characters repeatedly show that protecting another person's feelings by withholding one's own can become control: Kaito delegates Yuko's happiness to Saku, Yuko tries to end everyone else's inhibition, Yua hides desire inside care, and Saku decides what burdens others may bear.

Repair does not restore innocence. It becomes possible when each participant claims authority over their own desire, jealousy, refusal, and willingness to remain. The governing formulation is:

> **Volume 06 separates responsibility from control. People remain answerable for conduct and disclosure, but they may not pre-empt another person's choice by deciding which pain, uncertainty, friendship, or love that person is permitted to carry. Repair begins when hidden mixed motives become shareable and continued relation becomes a renewed choice rather than an unchanged default.**

## II. Saku: from punishment to humiliating disclosure

Yua first rejects Saku's self-punishment directly. Yuko would not want him harmed, and suffering for her is only a thin route to self-forgiveness. Saku recognizes the distinction and promises not to harm or neglect himself `[p-0010#p0124-p0168]`.

That is real improvement but still externally initiated. Asuka later identifies a deeper problem: Saku reports events without sharing why he refused Yuko. Kenta finally reverses their former roles and returns Saku's own demand for mutual understanding and action `[p-0020#p0263-p0417]`, `[p-0024#p0582-p0679]`.

At the festival meeting, Saku admits what his heroic self-image hid. He initially resented Yuko's first-year confession, accepted its suspended form to preserve the group, enjoyed her affection, and delayed an answer. He then says Yuko occupies his heart as a girl while other girls matter equally and he cannot identify which feeling is romantic `[p-0029#p0129-p0257]`.

The progress is disclosure rather than romantic selection. Saku repeats that he cannot date Yuko now, promises no result, and acknowledges he may choose somebody else. What he does promise is to face each relation and, if he eventually recognizes love for Yuko, speak first `[p-0033#p0261-p0342]`.

## III. The V05 singular phrase becomes a plural attachment problem

V05's `他の女の子` sounded singular and supplied no identity. V06 does not reveal that one hidden girl. Saku first says Yuko is in his heart, then says other girls of equal importance are also there and names different relational forms: desired companionship, familylike belonging, similarity, an equal running partner, and an admired ideal `[p-0029#p0152-p0257]`.

The evidence state is therefore **plural valued girls + unresolved romantic naming + no couple**. Any claim that V06 identifies Yuko, Yua, Yuzuki, Haru, or Asuka as the singular V05 referent exceeds the text. The later line that Saku might someday confess to Yuko is a contingent process statement, not a mutual promise or selection.

## IV. Yuko: mixed motive, refusal, and chosen continuation

Yuko's public confession is recontextualized through her first-year history. When Saku and Yua form a domestic/familial bond, Yuko feels jealousy, treats Yua's painful family history as a threat, and pre-emptively tells Yua she loves Saku. She recognizes the thought as ugly yet still uses a suspended non-answer confession to preserve her place `[p-0027#p0230-p0449]`.

In the present, Yuko says the group confession was designed to end that arrangement. She believed Saku would conceal a private refusal and continue as before; public knowledge would release Yua, Yuzuki, Haru, and Saku from treating her position as a veto. Her action was not pure sacrifice: she had enjoyed the stable triangle, resented new entrants, used wife/mistress humor territorially, and would have accepted success `[p-0027#p0455-p0686]`.

Yuko's agency is strongest when it does not seek purification. She owns mixed motive, asks not to be hated, preserves her love, and accepts Saku's second refusal without extracting a promise. She also corrects his belief that her love is idealization by supplying a year and a half of concrete observation of his virtues, faults, performances, and loneliness `[p-0033#p0103-p0217]`.

## V. Yua: repair architect who refuses facilitator-only status

The prologue and first-year history identify Yua's `ordinary` as a survival performance. After her mother leaves, she studies, manages the household, avoids social risk, and hides wants so her father and brother need not worry. Her mother's insistence that ordinary family life was happiness makes `ordinary` both cherished memory and defensive proof `[p-0015#p0003-p0128]`, `[p-0017#p0843-p0988]`.

Saku's early intervention combines genuine recognition with coercive method. He sees Yua's forced smile, catches her on the stairs, breaks her glasses, and tells her her life belongs to her. Later he creates room for family disclosure and asks directly for recurring cooking help with reciprocal shopping labor `[p-0015#p0131-p0239]`, `[p-0017#p1491-p1662]`.

Present Yua admits that she wants Yuko repaired and also enjoys being the only person beside an injured Saku. She visits Yuko across three days, accepts temporary refusal, names mutual weakness, and stages the festival conversation. Her plan serves friendship, Saku, and her own desire at once `[p-0019#p0002-p0050]`, `[p-0025#p0203-p0458]`.

Yua's central intervention is political as well as intimate: each girl owns her own romance, including the right to wait, withdraw, speak, or remain silent. Saku cannot make their feelings morally binding and cannot use them as a reason to deny his own. Yua then lets Yuko expose her hidden jealousy and declares that replacing sacrifice “for family” with sacrifice “for Saku” would repeat the same self-erasure `[p-0029#p0300-p0515]`, `[p-0031#p0002-p0303]`, `[p-0033#p0220-p0255]`.

## VI. Yuko and Yua: friendship through conflict and non-primacy

Yuko refuses to let Yua disappear into a neutral mediator role. Their first real fight makes each name the disloyal-looking truth: Yua chose Saku first during the rupture; Yuko withheld her confession and used friendship while defending romantic priority. Both admit jealousy, anger, and fear `[p-0031#p0061-p0303]`.

The repair is not achieved through equal ranking. Each tells the other she is not her first priority. They nevertheless choose continued friendship, future disagreement, and the right to wound and repair. Yuko's dream of fighting with a best friend signifies equality more strongly than unbroken politeness did `[p-0033#p0002-p0094]`.

## VII. Kaito: direct desire replaces delegated sacrifice

Kaito reveals that he has loved Yuko since she defended him and tied his necktie at the entrance ceremony. He withdrew because she visibly loved Saku and made Saku's success with her the condition under which his own sacrifice would have meaning `[p-0024#p0230-p0329]`.

His confession corrects that displaced structure. He asks Yuko directly rather than assigning Saku an obligation. Yuko directly refuses: she deeply loves Kaito as a friend but says that feeling will not become her love for Saku. Kaito accepts the answer and reframes shared rejection as a route back into the group `[p-0024#p0329-p0448]`.

Yuko thereby understands Saku's refusal from inside the refusing position. Empathy does not retroactively prove that Saku owed acceptance. The later Saku/Kaito apology and handshake repair conduct without erasing the assault `[p-0033#p0480-p0522]`.

## VIII. Yuzuki, Haru, and Asuka: distinct care under romantic scarcity

Asuka distinguishes event disclosure from heart disclosure and calls Saku too accustomed to being loved to know how to love. Her private aside reveals that she suppresses romantic self-interest to remain an ethically clean `moon`, reproducing the non-disclosure she criticizes `[p-0020#p0263-p0428]`.

Yuzuki abandons curated elegance and cooks local sauce katsudon. She wants Saku to say it is delicious and regain strength, not merely recognize a performance as “Nanase-like.” Her instruction not to misdirect pride applies their shared aesthetic to its human addressee. Her private aside still admits she cannot say all she wants `[p-0022#p0001-p0342]`.

Haru chooses a care practice she can authenticate: she recruits Atomu for full-strength baseball and gives Saku bodily release from rumination. She then states the team's distributive ethic—before being men and women, they are companions who can carry one another—while privately admitting this may be the only way she can remain beside him `[p-0022#p0391-p0573]`, `[p-0024#p0001-p0057]`.

V06 advances all three trajectories without making any one the answer. Their different methods—interpretive confrontation, aesthetic-practical care, and embodied team support—remain irreducible to a ranking.

## IX. Baseball: enacted ordinary pleasure, durable path still open

Haru and Atomu bring Saku back into active pitching and hitting outside school structure. The session is playful, competitive, and socially shared rather than proof-driven punishment. Atomu tells Saku to contact him if he decides to return seriously `[p-0022#p0428-p0539]`.

This is the first enacted post-Fujishi baseball pleasure, but not a vocational decision or institutional return. Baseball becomes selectable ordinary practice with an available peer route; long-term stakes and safety remain open.

## X. Group repair: changed continuity rather than reset

The grandmother's Obon lesson provides the model: separation becomes final through intentional cutting, while a held end can preserve a path `[p-0020#p0072-p0259]`. The festival converts that metaphor into action. Yua summons the group, Yuko apologizes to Yuzuki and Haru, Saku corrects his false distance from Yuzuki, and Saku/Kaito address the assault.

The ending explicitly refuses a reset. Shared food, Ramune, jokes, and fireworks resume, but altered gaze, spacing, vocal tone, and awkwardness show that everyone has moved forward `[p-0033#p0353-p0558]`. Continuity is reconstructed after disclosure, not recovered unchanged.

## XI. Ordinary and special as paired, non-ranked desires

The paired epilogues prevent an easy winner. `あなたのフツウ` belongs to Yuko: after losing ordinary proximity, she realizes the special she wanted was naming, walking, talking, and holding on during dark nights `[p-0034#p0001-p0033]`.

`あなたの特別` belongs to Yua: being the sole caregiver, seeing Saku's tears, and then losing exclusivity makes it impossible to reseal her desire. She wants to face him fully and become his special person `[p-0035#p0001-p0033]`.

Yuko moves from fetishized singularity toward chosen ordinary continuity. Yua moves from protective ordinariness toward avowed singular desire. These vectors cross; the text does not rank them.

## XII. Fukui, seasonal ritual, and relational infrastructure

V06 makes local ritual causal. Obon at Asuka's grandmother's house supplies the bond model; family kitchens and sauce katsudon translate care into ordinary regional practice; the Asuwa riverbank supports repeated disclosures; the late-August shrine festival and Yokokan Garden create the spatial passage from private dyads to triadic truth and collective return.

Summer's end is not decorative mood. Welcoming fire, sending fire, sudden rain, sunset, festival closure, and sparklers structure departure, return, and transformed continuity. The afterword's wish that the August publication accompany the reader's own late-summer atmosphere is paratextual confirmation of intended seasonal framing `[p-0036#p0003-p0015]`.

## XIII. Ramune, hands, glass, and the opened social field

Glass remains both defense and medium. Yua's old glasses and transparent wall protect by preventing contact; the Ramune bottle holds a visible marble in a bounded field. V06 shifts emphasis from opening to circulation: three bottles lead Saku, Yua, and Yuko toward speech, and six more join the full group's festival practice `[p-0025#p0132-p0199]`, `[p-0033#p0525-p0558]`.

Hands do the volume's relational work. A held end prevents disappearance; Yua joins Saku's and Yuko's hands; layered hands distribute burden; the women separate those hands to fight and rejoin through embrace; Saku and Kaito end with a handshake. Connection is neither enclosure nor fusion but repeated, revisable holding.

## XV. Rival readings and hard abstentions

- **“Saku reveals Yuko as the V05 girl.”** Rejected. He names Yuko and multiple other girls as equally important, then says he cannot identify love.
- **“Saku and Yuko are now provisionally dating.”** Rejected. He explicitly refuses dating again and makes no outcome promise.
- **“Yua selflessly mediates Yuko's romance.”** Incomplete. She seeks repair while pursuing her own first priority and later refuses self-erasure.
- **“Yuko's confession was pure sacrifice.”** Rejected. It combines liberating intent, territorial history, fear, and willingness to accept success.
- **“The group returns to normal.”** Rejected. Shared practice resumes with explicitly altered distances and knowledge.
- **“Kaito's confession repairs his violence.”** Rejected. It corrects displaced desire; apology and renewed relation do not erase assault.
- **“Saku has learned to ask for help.”** Not established. He accepts care and reveals need after direct pressure, but no unprompted request is shown.
- **“Baseball is Saku's decided future.”** Unsupported. Play and a route reopen; durable commitment remains absent.

## XVI. Visual evidence

The cover isolates Yua holding a Ramune bottle, matching the volume's choice to make her both intimate focalizer and repair architect. Interior art gives embodied weight to Yuko's collapse and to the Yuko/Yua embrace after their first fight. The sequence moves from isolated girl images and dyadic caregiving toward bodies physically supporting one another, while never depicting a new couple state.

## XVII. Prospective close

V06 freezes on five revised asymmetries:

1. Saku can disclose shame and accept shared burden, but still has not clearly originated a request for support.
2. Yuko can preserve love after refusal without letting it veto other people's choices.
3. Yua can repair others' relation while naming her own desire and refusing caretaker self-erasure.
4. the group can reconnect without returning to its uninformed former equilibrium.
5. multiple attachments can be real before Saku can responsibly call one of them romantic.
