# Chiramune Volume 01 deep reading

## Control statement

This is the current-eligible, active-provisional reading produced from the locked Japanese Volume 01 EPUB. All interpretations below are prospectively bounded to Volume 01. Later volumes may revise the current model but must not be used to rewrite what Volume 01 itself supported.

The EPUB was independently re-hashed before reading and matched the source-lock SHA-256 exactly. Narrative text, same-edition illustrations, and the author afterword were reviewed. The afterword is treated as paratext rather than character evidence.

## V01 zero-state: questions before interpretation

Volume 01 has no prior numbered-volume freeze. The correct prospective starting point is therefore not a set of character conclusions but a set of questions raised by the book's opening configuration:

- Is Saku Chitose's conspicuous confidence mostly self-advertisement, or is the social competence beneath it independently demonstrated?
- Does the novel equate popularity with shallowness, or does it distinguish social performance from deception?
- Is Saku's intervention in Kenta Yamazaki's withdrawal primarily benevolence, vanity, coercive social engineering, or some unstable combination?
- Can the novel's repeated language of self-improvement coexist with its explicit recognition of unequal beauty, athleticism, charisma, and inherited social advantage?
- Is Saku actually as internally self-sufficient as his rhetoric of absolute self-evaluation suggests?
- What does the title image of the marble inside the Ramune bottle signify within the V01 boundary?
- Which members of Saku's peer group merely affirm him, and which can perceive or correct him?

The answers below are V01 findings, not premises imported into the read.

# I. Volume architecture: Kenta's rehabilitation is also an audit of Saku

On a surface plot level, Volume 01 is organized around Saku being asked by his teacher to bring the withdrawn Kenta back to school and then helping him confront the social wound that precipitated his withdrawal. Structurally, however, Kenta functions as more than a rehabilitation target. His arc forces Saku's own social philosophy into progressively harder tests.

The sequence is roughly:

1. **Prologue — social scripting as apparently effortless mastery.** Saku converts a jealous-boyfriend confrontation into a reconciliation by accepting the useful role of villain. The scene establishes his instinct to treat social conflict dramaturgically.
2. **Chapter 1 — public competence and private constraint.** Saku takes control of a new classroom without overtly dominating it; his peers and teacher independently confirm his unusual reliability. Asuka then identifies the cost of this identity: he is a beautiful but over-regulated park.
3. **Chapter 2 — coercive entry into Kenta's closed world.** Saku selects different companions as different social instruments, breaks Kenta's window with parental permission, debates Kenta's worldview, and constructs a three-week intervention.
4. **Chapter 3 — practice, incorporation, and self-fashioning.** Kenta returns to school, learns conversational and presentation skills, and begins participating in Saku's group. The intervention moves from abstract argument to repeated ordinary practice.
5. **Chapter 4 — the model receives counterevidence.** Haru corrects Saku's tendency to appropriate another person's responsibility; Kenta then demonstrates that Saku's definition of inclusive participation can itself become paternalistic when the recipient experiences it differently.
6. **Chapter 5 — focalization shifts to Kenta.** Kenta attempts to act without Saku directing him. When he falters, Saku intervenes not as a neutral mentor but as an openly partial friend. Kenta's pupil role ends; friendship remains.
7. **Epilogue — ordinary continuity.** The decisive sign of Kenta's development is not that he becomes a replica of Saku but that he can ask for help without ritualized shame and can remain in the group without a formal teacher-disciple relation.

The volume therefore does **not** simply present Saku as a finished ideal teaching an inferior boy how to become socially acceptable. It constructs Saku as a highly competent but incomplete model whose intervention succeeds partly because other people correct the weaknesses of his own philosophy.

# II. Focalization: Saku is privileged evidence about Saku, not objective social truth

## 1. His self-narration is aggressively stylized

Saku narrates himself through jokes, sexual bravado, genre language, role labels, and aesthetic metaphors. He repeatedly calls his life a "riajuu harem story," treats interpersonal encounters as scenes with appropriate dramatic payoffs, and often selects a deliberately outrageous formulation when a plainer one would suffice.

This makes a naive reading in either direction inadequate:

- **Not every boast is literal fact.** His narration often performs confidence for his own amusement.
- **But performativity does not make the underlying competence false.** Other characters repeatedly rely on him, respond to him as socially powerful, and identify qualities he is reluctant to name as virtues.

The important distinction is not "real Saku" versus "fake Saku." V01 instead presents multiple layers that are all genuinely his: spontaneous appetite, learned social technique, self-chosen theatricality, idealized role obligation, concealed vulnerability, and habits built by repeated performance.

## 2. The book supplies independent witnesses

Saku's own account is checked by other characters:

- **Kazuki** notices that Saku's claim to be helping Kenta merely because it makes him look good does not fit his actual behavior: Saku does not publicize the details that would maximize status payoff and habitually responds when directly relied upon.
- **Haru** detects his troubled expression and forces a disclosure through competition rather than pity. Her criticism of his responsibility model is accepted by Saku as valid.
- **Asuka** identifies the rigidity beneath his polished competence before the Kenta intervention begins, then later notices a small change.
- **Kenta**, during the focalization shift, first idealizes Saku but also remembers and articulates the social cost of Saku's overwhelming advantage.
- **Yuko** idealizes Saku strongly, which is itself evidence of his effect on others but weak evidence for any claim that he is morally flawless.

This distributed structure is important. V01 invites admiration for Saku while building in several mechanisms that prevent his self-description from functioning as omniscient authorial truth.

# III. Saku's competence is real, learned, and socially productive

## 1. Classroom leadership is not mere popularity

At the opening of the school year, Saku rapidly shapes classroom atmosphere. He uses his own negative reputation as material for a joke, absorbs attention, and creates an environment in which quieter classmates can participate without being required to fight for the floor. He accepts the class-president role and immediately thinks about neutral procedures rather than converting it into personal rule.

**FACT:** the room responds to him; peers elect him; the teacher already regards him as unusually reliable. This is not merely Saku saying he is popular.

**INFERENCE:** his informal authority is partly based on accumulated credibility. He can spend status—making himself the joke, taking responsibility, lending legitimacy to Kenta—because the group expects him to remain socially intact afterward.

## 2. He prepares much more than his persona advertises

Saku's charisma often looks spontaneous, but V01 repeatedly reveals hidden preparation:

- he thinks carefully about which friend to bring to Kenta and why;
- he reads the light novels Kenta challenges him with rather than dismissing an unfamiliar subculture;
- he researches glasses before the shopping trip and has already narrowed the likely fit;
- he structures Kenta's three-week program around achievable steps rather than dramatic failure-and-recovery beats;
- he watches group dynamics and anticipates how status cues will be interpreted.

This turns "effortless cool" into a presentation effect. The competence is real; the **effortlessness is partly crafted**.

## 3. Social skill is described as interest plus practice

Saku's most important explicit communication thesis is that conversation begins with a desire to know the other person and to be known by them. Technique matters, but technique without that orientation becomes hollow facade. Kenta later paraphrases this insight in his own words, showing that the lesson has been internalized rather than merely obeyed.

The volume thus rejects two reductions at once:

- social competence is **not** an innate magical property possessed only by beautiful extroverts;
- social competence is also **not** a bag of manipulation tricks detached from genuine curiosity.

Practice matters, but practice must attach to an outward-facing interest in other minds.

# IV. The central contradiction: self-authorship versus compulsory performance

Saku repeatedly says that what matters is whether he can be proud of himself rather than how others rank him. He contrasts his group's "absolute evaluation" with status-seekers who live through relative comparison `[part0028:p069]`.

Yet V01 also gives a strikingly different formulation of his identity: to live as himself, and for "Chitose Saku" to remain the Chitose Saku everyone imagines, he **must continue to be that person** `[part0017:p087]`.

These claims are not easily reduced to simple hypocrisy.

## 1. His public role began as choice and became obligation

Saku has actively authored a style of being: capable, funny, attractive, reliable, unflustered, willing to answer expectations. That style is not merely imposed by society. He likes it. He thinks it is beautiful. He often enjoys the work required to sustain it.

But a role repeatedly chosen can become a rule one no longer experiences as optional. His language shifts from "I want to" toward **"I must continue to be."** This is why Asuka's park metaphor is so incisive. The park is beautiful because it is regulated; the same regulations exclude fireworks, ball games, dogs, dangerous play equipment, and therefore much of the messy life a park might contain `[part0017:p193-p197]`.

## 2. "Absolute evaluation" is therefore incomplete

Saku sincerely resists living by other people's ranking. Yet his self-evaluation has incorporated external expectation into the self he evaluates. He does not need applause in the ordinary sense; he needs to remain the kind of person who could deserve the role he has allowed others to assign him.

This is a deeper form of social dependence than wanting popularity.

**V01 formulation:** Saku is not primarily trapped by wanting other people to like him. He is trapped by wanting to be the person who can withstand being liked, relied upon, envied, hated, and tested without betraying his own aesthetic of Chitose Saku.

# V. Kenta's arc: not conversion into a riajuu, but recovery of authorship

## 1. The initial problem is not simply poor grooming

Kenta begins from a worldview in which social hierarchy appears externally fixed and morally contaminated. Attractive or popular people possess advantages he cannot access; kindness from a socially desirable girl is interpreted through romantic scarcity; humiliation confirms his belief that outsiders are props in other people's lives.

Some of this perception is grounded. V01 never denies that:

- looks and athletic ability are unequally distributed;
- status changes how identical behavior is received;
- high-status sponsorship makes Kenta's mistakes more socially survivable;
- people do use caste differences to humiliate others;
- Saku, Yuko, Yuzuki, Kazuki, Haru, and Kaito possess advantages Kenta cannot reproduce simply by deciding harder.

The error is the move from **unequal starting conditions** to **no meaningful agency**.

## 2. Saku attacks learned helplessness through action

Saku's training program is significant because it converts abstraction into repeated embodied evidence. Kenta walks, returns to school, speaks badly and tries again, changes grooming, chooses clothes, reads and watches outside his previous habits, learns to ask questions, and eventually goes alone to face his old group.

The crucial result is not "Kenta becomes handsome." It is that Kenta accumulates experiences in which action changes his world enough to make further action credible.

His own Chapter 5 focalization confirms the internal shift: his original motive of making Miki regret rejecting him has largely dissolved. The three weeks themselves have become something he can be proud of. He understands that the confrontation is now a test of **his chosen direction**, not a transaction for obtaining the girl who hurt him.

## 3. His otaku identity is preserved rather than purged

Saku does not require Kenta to abandon light novels, anime, or subcultural taste. Indeed, Saku enters Kenta's interests by reading the works Kenta names. During the fashion lesson, he explicitly frames fashion as one possible hobby rather than a universal duty and tells Kenta to find a personally intelligible style rather than mimic fashionable people.

This matters because it distinguishes **self-fashioning** from **assimilation**.

The desired endpoint is not "become Saku." It is closer to: stop using a social category as an alibi for refusing authorship; learn enough competence to choose what kind of Kenta you want to be.

## 4. But the success is heavily subsidized

A serious rival reading must emphasize how exceptional Kenta's support environment is. He receives sustained attention from a highly competent, high-status peer; access to several socially successful girls and boys; personalized grooming and clothing advice; public status protection; exercise companionship; social rehearsal; and eventual direct rescue.

Therefore V01 does **not** establish the strong proposition "any isolated teenager can become socially successful by effort alone." What it establishes much more securely is that Kenta's deterministic belief that effort is meaningless is false **in this supported case**.

The novel's rhetoric occasionally outruns the evidence. That gap should remain open for later volumes rather than being silently resolved in Saku's favor.

# VI. Intervention ethics: Saku helps through manipulation, but he has internal limits

Saku is not a non-directive helper. His method is interventionist and at times coercive.

## 1. He deliberately engineers people and situations

Examples include:

- choosing Yua as a low-threat first contact because of her social profile;
- choosing Yuko as a confrontational second contact because her blunt authenticity can puncture Kenta's theory;
- manipulating his presentation to Kenta's mother while avoiding direct falsehood;
- breaking Kenta's window with the mother's permission to create a face-saving reason for Kenta to respond;
- using public humor when Kenta returns to school to deprive future gossip of hidden shame;
- constructing a training schedule and later observing Kenta's confrontation without announcing his presence.

This is paternalism, not merely encouragement.

## 2. He does have nontrivial constraints

His methods are not consequence-maximization without principle. He rejects the easiest route—letting Kenta transfer romantic dependence onto Yua—because using her affection as bait is, in his terms, not beautiful. He promises not to physically harm Kenta even after breaking the window. He uses Kenta's mother's consent before damaging property. He repeatedly insists that Kenta ultimately owns his own story.

His ethical vocabulary is aesthetic rather than rule-theoretic: **what kind of action can he live with as Chitose Saku?**

That produces both admirable restraints and danger, because "beautiful" is personal and can justify dramatic escalation.

## 3. Haru supplies the first decisive correction

Saku worries that if Kenta fails, the failure will be Saku's because Saku pushed him toward the confrontation. Haru rejects the totalizing responsibility claim: a coach teaches and supports, but the player remains an agent who chose to play. To appropriate all failure is also to appropriate all success `[part0030:p093-p105]`.

Saku immediately sees the problem. He had started to imagine Kenta as someone who required total control, contradicting his own rhetoric of mutual understanding.

This is a major V01 development. Saku's instinct to take responsibility is not presented as pure virtue. In excess, it becomes **paternalistic erasure of another person's authorship**.

## 4. Kenta supplies the second decisive correction

During soccer, Saku distributes the ball to less-skilled students because he believes participation makes play meaningful. Kenta later says that for someone who dreads sports, being left unobtrusively at the edge may be genuinely preferable. Saku's "everyone should participate" norm had universalized the preference of competent athletes.

Saku does not win the argument by invoking good intentions. He accepts that if Kenta experienced the teasing and engineered participation without sufficient trust, then by Saku's own standard it became bullying `[part0030:p481]`.

This is perhaps V01's strongest evidence that the novel is capable of criticizing its protagonist from within his own concepts.

# VII. Leadership and informal authority: status as infrastructure

V01 gives a nuanced picture of social hierarchy.

## 1. Hierarchy is real even when nobody formally creates it

Cafeteria seating, conversational access, who may tease whom, and how identical mistakes are interpreted all reveal an informal status network. Saku and Kazuki explicitly recognize these effects rather than pretending the class is socially flat.

Teacher commentary adds a useful model: many people locate themselves comparatively because they do not trust an internal compass; hierarchy forms around people who move first with unusual confidence. The teacher does **not** say such people know the objectively correct direction. They act as if their compass can be trusted.

## 2. Saku distinguishes status as byproduct from status as goal

His group claims not to pursue caste rank as an end. They enjoy one another, cultivate competence, and happen to accumulate status. By contrast, Atomu's group is described as using downward comparison and attacks to maintain relative position.

The distinction is analytically useful but should not be accepted without qualification. Saku's group may not pursue rank, yet it benefits from rank constantly. Yuzuki can redirect an insult because her own beauty/status make the counterattack costly. Kenta's awkwardness becomes legible as harmless when Saku publicly affiliates with him. Saku himself can lose a soccer bet spectacularly without losing status because his status is robust enough to absorb embarrassment.

**V01 tension:** not caring about rank is easier when one possesses enough accumulated capital that rank survives ordinary failure.

## 3. Their best use of status is redistributive

At their strongest, Saku and friends treat social capital as something that can make a room safer for less-secure people. Saku absorbs hostility, Yua buffers Kenta, Yuzuki redirects aggression, Yuko lends endorsement, Kazuki offers skill instruction, and the group normalizes Kenta's presence.

This makes informal authority ethically consequential. The question is not simply whether hierarchy exists but **what high-status people do with the asymmetry they already possess**.

# VIII. Friendship: reciprocal knowledge rather than a rescue contract

Kenta initially interprets relationships transactionally: kindness can imply romantic possibility; help creates debt; joining the group requires becoming worthy enough to remain. Saku often speaks transactionally too—Kenta may "use" him while Saku uses Kenta to satisfy his own aesthetic—but the lived relationships repeatedly exceed this framing.

The endpoint of the three-week intervention is especially important. Saku insists that the **teacher-disciple structure** is over, not the friendship. Kenta misreads the separation because he still assumes access to the group is conditional on developmental status. Yuko corrects him: if they have spent this time together and want to keep talking, eating, and playing games, friendship does not disappear simply because the formal intervention has ended.

The epilogue then normalizes this relation. Kenta can ask Saku for study help; Saku can complain and bargain for ramen; the others greet Kenta without ceremonial inclusion. The relationship becomes ordinary.

That ordinariness is a more significant success condition than "Kenta joins the top caste."

# IX. Saku's relationships as different diagnostic instruments

V01 avoids making the female ensemble interchangeable. Each prominent relationship reveals a different aspect of Saku.

## Yuko Hiiragi — affirmation, openness, and heroic idealization

Yuko is unusually direct about affection and social warmth. She participates in Kenta's intervention because Saku asks and then develops genuine friendship with Kenta. Her view of Saku is strongly idealizing: she calls his willingness to believe in Kenta a condition of heroism and resists his attempts to reduce his behavior to vanity.

Her testimony establishes that Saku inspires trust, but because she is emotionally invested in him it should not be treated as neutral moral adjudication.

## Yua Uchida — low-threat care and acquired social confidence

Saku chooses Yua for the first Kenta visit because he expects her to manage distance gently and to appear less overwhelming than the group's most visibly high-status members. Later he explicitly identifies her as someone who became more socially prominent after entering the group rather than as a pure natural-born center.

This makes Yua important evidence against a completely fixed-caste model. V01, however, does not yet provide enough of her interiority to reconstruct the process independently of Saku's account.

## Yuzuki Nanase — mirror of controlled presentation

Saku recognizes Yuzuki as socially similar to himself: both possess natural advantages and consciously manage how those advantages appear. Their banter operates through layers, tests, and mutual reading. She identifies that "because you are Chitose Saku" is itself part of why he accepted Kenta's case.

Their relation is therefore less simple affirmation than mutual recognition between two people comfortable with crafted surfaces.

## Haru Aomi — embodied competence and corrective reciprocity

Haru can beat Saku decisively in her own athletic domain and does not romanticize his invulnerability. She creates a game-based route through which he can disclose weakness without being pitied, then directly rejects his inflated responsibility claim.

Her preference for the Saku who chases a ball wholeheartedly over the Saku who smiles as though everything is already understood is one of V01's clearest challenges to his detached, perfected persona.

## Asuka Nishino — aspirational freedom and the limit case of intimacy

Saku idealizes Asuka because her river intervention was not optimized for reputation, moral credit, or role fulfillment. She acted because the playful solution was what she wanted. For Saku, this looks like extraordinary self-authorship.

Asuka in turn sees the cost of Saku's regulation. Her "beautiful park" metaphor identifies that the same internal rules that produce safety and polish can exclude spontaneity. After the Kenta arc she says his park may now permit a sparkler `[part0033:p321]`, suggesting a small V01 change rather than a wholesale transformation.

Their closing exchange preserves distance. She suggests they may be best as admired senior and interesting junior rather than converting immediately into a conventional romantic relation. Within V01, she remains both person and projection surface: Saku himself may be idealizing her freedom.

## Kazuki Mizushino — peer-level skeptical witness

Kazuki is one of the best counterweights to Saku's narration. He sees social hierarchy clearly, is more openly selective than Saku, and detects when Saku's explanation of his own motives is too convenient. His observation that Saku does not actually behave like someone maximizing reputation weakens Saku's claim that his helping is merely aesthetic self-interest.

# X. The title system: bottle, marble, moon, park, window, compass, story

V01's metaphors converge on one problem: **how to author a self without turning the authored form into a prison**.

## 1. Moon — aspiration at a distance

The moon represents the self or life one reaches toward because it is distant, beautiful, and difficult. Saku's autobiographically suggestive story imagines flying so high that others cannot drag him down. Kenta later "reaches for the moon" when he decides to change despite the possibility of failure.

The same image can therefore signify both liberation and isolation.

## 2. Ramune bottle and marble — beauty enclosed by the structure that displays it

Saku explicitly connects his aesthetic to a marble sunk inside a Ramune bottle whose top cannot simply be opened `[part0033:p217-p224]`. The marble is attractive partly because of the container that traps it.

Within V01, the strongest reading is that **Saku himself is the marble**: the carefully maintained Chitose Saku is beautiful, visible, and difficult to reach, but the form that preserves that beauty also limits his freedom to be weak, ordinary, dependent, or inconsistent.

This is an **INFERENCE**, not an explicit authorial gloss. The text strongly supports it through the park metaphor and Saku's "must continue to be" language, but later volumes must be allowed to revise the formulation.

## 3. Park and fireworks — regulated perfection versus lived mess

Asuka's park is the clearest complementary metaphor. A beautiful park can be made so safe and orderly that it ceases to be a place where children play. The end-of-volume "sparkler" comment marks a tiny relaxation of Saku's internal regulation, not demolition of the park.

## 4. Window/glass — enclosure and forced permeability

Saku physically breaks Kenta's window to create an exit from his closed room. Kenta later describes the act as if the glass surrounding his heart broke too. The image is deliberately double-edged: liberation occurs through an invasive act. That matches the ethics of Saku's intervention generally.

## 5. Compass — confidence without objective certainty

The teacher rejects the idea of an objectively guaranteed compass. The socially influential person often differs because they trust their own direction strongly enough to move first. This complicates Saku's status: leadership demonstrates conviction, not truth.

## 6. Story — autonomy as narrative authorship

Saku and Kenta repeatedly use story language. At first Saku uses it jokingly to demote Kenta to a side character in Saku's harem comedy. But the joke becomes an autonomy lesson: in Kenta's life, Kenta is protagonist and must take responsibility for the story he wants to draw. Chapter 5's temporary focalization makes the formal structure enact that idea by actually giving Kenta narrative control.

# XI. The Chapter 5 focalization shift is formally important

The beginning of Chapter 5 switches from Saku's first-person narration to Kenta's first-person perspective for the confrontation with his former friends.

This is not only a novelty. It does three analytical jobs:

1. **It proves Kenta has internalized concepts rather than merely obeying instructions.** He recalls Saku's phrases but applies them in his own reasoning.
2. **It withholds Saku's control.** The reader must experience the scene through the person who is supposed to act independently.
3. **It exposes the scale of Kenta's idealization.** His language turns Saku into a near-divine figure, which confirms Saku's impact while warning against treating Kenta's praise as neutral description.

When Saku enters the scene, the perspective makes his intervention feel overwhelming by design. The text is showing what high-status competence looks like from below, not just letting Saku describe himself from within.

# XII. Retaliation and partiality: Saku is not a universalist saint

The Starbucks confrontation marks a boundary in Saku's ethics. He humiliates Kenta's former friends, exposes their insecurities, threatens through physical proximity, and says afterward that even if he hurt them deeply he does not particularly care: when a friend is being beaten down, he would rather be the villain than preserve the aggressor from every consequence.

This prevents a sanitized reading of him as simply kind.

His moral orientation in V01 is strongly **relational and partial**:

- people who rely on him generate obligation;
- chosen friends receive active protection;
- malicious aggression can justify harsh retaliation;
- he accepts being hated as a cost of acting according to his aesthetic;
- he does not claim equal responsibility for every stranger.

This partiality is also what makes his earlier over-responsibility problem tractable. By the end, he explicitly recognizes a load limit: if he puts everyone on his back, something already precious may fall off `[part0033:p245]`.

# XIII. Ordinary-life evidence

V01's low-stakes material is unusually useful because Saku's philosophy appears in mundane choices.

- **Clothing:** he prefers durable, basic, comfortable items and a few long-lived accessories; he does not chase trends. His style embodies the distinction between self-authorship and status fashion.
- **Food:** he has specific local preferences (e.g. 8-ban ramen, Karamen; Royal Sawayaka), which tie his glamorous self-image to ordinary Fukui life rather than cosmopolitan abstraction.
- **Exercise:** despite leaving baseball, athletic competence and competitive pleasure remain deeply embodied. Haru can access a less-defended Saku by returning him to play.
- **Reading:** he is willing to read material outside his previous interest when a person matters enough to make it relevant. This supports curiosity as behavior, not slogan.
- **Humor:** Saku uses jokes to control temperature, create face-saving exits, absorb embarrassment, and prevent sentimental closure from becoming too total. Humor is both genuine play and emotional regulation.

The local setting also matters. Chain stores, regional speech, school customs, bicycles, riverbanks, and ordinary food keep the novel's ideal of "bright youth" grounded in a specific provincial environment. Saku's self-mythologizing occurs in a world he repeatedly calls small.

# XIV. Major V01 claims with epistemic status

## Claim A — Saku's social competence is substantially real

**Status:** FACT + strong INFERENCE.

Independent peer/teacher behavior, successful group regulation, preparation, athletic competence, and Kenta's observable change support it. His narration exaggerates style, not the entire underlying capability.

**Counterevidence/limit:** he makes meaningful social errors, especially by assuming his own norms of participation and responsibility generalize to Kenta.

## Claim B — Saku is a deliberate self-fashioner, not simply a naturally popular boy

**Status:** FACT + strong INFERENCE.

He explicitly manages messages, roles, appearance, expectations, and the image called Chitose Saku. He is aware that a face repeatedly shown becomes difficult to remove.

**Counterevidence/limit:** some of his ease is plainly temperamental and advantaged; V01 does not show the full origin of the persona.

## Claim C — his persona is authentic and constraining at the same time

**Status:** strong INFERENCE.

The role expresses values he genuinely endorses, but his "must continue to be" language and Asuka/Haru's observations show that it narrows available ways of being.

**Rival reading:** the constraint may be ordinary adolescent self-consciousness dramatized by ornate narration rather than a deep identity problem. V01 cannot yet establish long-term severity.

## Claim D — V01 endorses self-improvement but not simple meritocracy

**Status:** strong INFERENCE.

The narrative celebrates Kenta's effort while repeatedly acknowledging unequal starting points and the effect of status. Kenta's change is enabled by exceptional support.

**Rival reading:** because antagonistic counterpositions are often caricatured, the novel sometimes rhetorically tilts the debate toward Saku more strongly than its own evidence warrants.

## Claim E — Saku's helping ethic contains a paternalistic failure mode

**Status:** FACT + strong INFERENCE.

Haru names the responsibility problem; Kenta names the participation problem; Saku accepts both corrections. His methods involve explicit manipulation.

**Counterevidence/limit:** Saku also sets boundaries, seeks consent where relevant, protects Kenta's ultimate agency, and changes behavior when shown a blind spot.

## Claim F — V01's deepest developmental movement is mutual, not one-way

**Status:** strong INFERENCE.

Kenta becomes more agentic; Saku becomes slightly more permeable. Asuka's park moves from "no fireworks" to perhaps allowing a sparkler. Haru gets him to share weakness; Kenta forces him to confront the limits of his inclusion model.

# XV. Rival readings and adversarial checks

## Rival reading 1: "This is just makeover wish fulfillment"

There is real wish-fulfillment machinery: elite friends, rapid grooming changes, status sponsorship, and a humiliating reversal against former tormentors. A reading that ignores this would be evasive.

But it is incomplete because the novel explicitly de-emphasizes fashion as mandatory, preserves Kenta's otaku interests, shifts the motivational endpoint away from winning Miki, and makes Kenta's final achievement one of authorship and ordinary friendship rather than conquest of a status tier.

## Rival reading 2: "Saku is an unreliable narcissist and therefore his philosophy can be discarded"

His self-narration is narcissistic and theatrical, but independent evidence repeatedly validates much of his competence and generosity. The correct move is not to invert every claim. The text asks the reader to distinguish **performed interpretation** from **observable behavior**.

## Rival reading 3: "Saku is simply the author's mouthpiece and always right"

The antagonists often are rhetorically weak, which makes this danger real. Yet Haru and Kenta directly defeat important assumptions inside Saku's framework, and Saku changes his view rather than receiving narrative vindication. Asuka's critique predates the intervention and remains unresolved. V01 therefore gives him privileged narrative force without making him infallible.

## Rival reading 4: "Popularity is depicted as morally superior"

The book plainly finds Saku's group attractive, but its own taxonomy distinguishes popularity from status aggression and shows popular characters with different moral qualities. It also insists that social rank is not the proper end. The stronger thesis is that **competent, other-regarding social participation** is valued; popularity is often a consequence, not the moral criterion itself.

# XVI. V01 character baselines

These are bounded starting models, not definitive series monographs.

### Saku Chitose

Highly socially perceptive; theatrically self-confident; genuinely capable; preparatory beneath apparent spontaneity; aesthetic rather than purely reputational self-regulation; strong response to direct reliance; selective but intense protective partiality; prone to over-responsibility and paternalistic control; uncomfortable with unstructured weakness; strongly invested in being a self-authored ideal; athletic identity remains live beneath his departure from baseball; aspires toward Asuka's freer self-authorship.

### Kenta Yamazaki

Initially defensive, category-driven, romantically scarcity-minded, resentful, and avoidant; nevertheless capable of sustained effort once persuaded and unusually willing to internalize lessons through action. His strongest V01 growth is not status acquisition but movement from grievance-based externalization toward ownership of choices. Still vulnerable to old shame and social comparison; friendship with Saku's group becomes a stabilizing resource.

### Yuko Hiiragi

Naturally socially open, emotionally direct, high-energy, and unusually willing to state preferences without elaborate defensive framing. Can be blunt toward Kenta without pretending altruistic motive. Strongly idealizes Saku but also provides emotional continuity around Kenta's integration.

### Yua Uchida

# XIX. V01 freeze

Within the Volume 01 boundary, the strongest integrated reading is:

> **Chiramune V01 treats social competence as a real, partly learnable human practice rather than a synonym for falseness, but it refuses to let its socially dominant protagonist remain a frictionless ideal. Saku's chosen identity gives him unusual capacity to organize, protect, and dignify other people; the same identity can become a self-imposed enclosure that makes weakness difficult and encourages him to overtake other people's agency. Kenta's apparent makeover story therefore doubles as Saku's first ethical correction. Kenta learns that unequal social conditions do not erase authorship; Saku learns that helping another protagonist requires leaving some of the story in that person's hands.**

The image of the Ramune marble condenses the unresolved problem: beauty can be produced and preserved by form, but form can also trap the thing it displays. V01 ends not by breaking Saku's bottle, but by making the enclosure slightly more permeable.
