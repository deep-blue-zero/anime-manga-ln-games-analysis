# 銀河英雄伝説
## Japanese Terminology and Passage Index
### Exact-language retrieval layer for the complete prose corpus

**Complete spoilers for the ten main novels and all five gaiden volumes.**

## Purpose and limits

This appendix is a retrieval layer for the analytical corpus. It does not replace the arguments developed in Documents 01–14. Its functions are narrower and more exact:

- standardize names and romanization;
- distinguish Japanese terms that collapse into the same English gloss;
- identify military concepts whose wording matters;
- track address forms and first-person pronouns as relationship evidence;
- index recurrent narratorial formulas;
- and provide short, chapter-level passage anchors for claims used elsewhere.

The Japanese novels remain the governing evidence. The English renderings below are **working analytical translations**, not claims about an official English edition. They prioritize semantic and political precision over elegance. Each excerpt is deliberately short; the locator and anchor phrase are meant to return the reader to the source rather than reproduce it.

The archive uses chapter-level locators defined in `CORPUS_INVENTORY_AND_LOCATOR_MAP.md`:

- `M03-C05` — Main Volume 3, Chapter 5, 「査問会」;
- `G04-C08` — Gaiden 4, Chapter 8, 「過去からの糸」;
- `G05-INTERVIEW` — the Tanaka Yoshiki interview, treated as paratext rather than narrative canon.

When a chapter contains several relevant scenes, a descriptive anchor follows the locator. These anchors are retrieval labels, not invented internal headings.

# I. Naming and romanization policy

The Japanese text transliterates many European names through katakana. A reverse romanization is not mechanically unique. This corpus therefore uses the spellings already stabilized across the project and preserves the Japanese form when exact identification matters.

## 3. Calendars and offices

- `宇宙暦` — **Space Calendar / Space Year**. This corpus usually writes “Space Year” in chronological summaries.
- `帝国暦` — **Imperial Calendar / Imperial Year**, the Goldenbaum chronology.
- `新帝国暦` — **New Imperial Calendar**, inaugurated by the Lohengramm dynasty.
- `皇帝` — Emperor; `カイザー` appears as Germanic flavor in dialogue and titles.
- `皇妃` — Imperial consort / Empress-consort; Hilda’s political position cannot be reduced to marital status.
- `元帥`, `上級大将`, `大将`, `中将`, `少将`, `准将` — Marshal, senior admiral/general, admiral/general, vice admiral/lieutenant general, rear admiral/major general, commodore/brigadier general. Exact service equivalence is less important than hierarchy.
- `統帥本部総長` — Chief of the Supreme Command Headquarters.
- `軍務尚書` — Minister of Military Affairs.
- `国務尚書` — Minister of State / head of civil state administration.
- `憲兵総監` — Inspector General of Military Police.
- `総督` — Governor-General.
- `共和政府主席` — Chair/President of the republican government.

| 民主共和制 / 民主共和政 | minshu kyōwasei / kyōwasei | democratic republican system | Institutional form meant to make exceptional saviors unnecessary; also the tradition Iserlohn claims after the Alliance state dies. | `M02-C07`; `M03-C05`; `M08-C09` |

## 3. Translation cautions

1. `戦略` and `戦術` must not be treated as synonyms for “clever plan.” The novels often make their hierarchy explicit.
2. `宙域管制` is not simply “controlling territory.” It emphasizes temporary access, routes, and political option value.
3. `兵站` should not be reduced to ammunition. In LOGH it includes the civic ability to feed occupied populations and sustain a polity.
4. `要塞` is not merely a weapon platform. Iserlohn becomes home, government, and archive.
5. Campaign titles such as `神々の黄昏` are part of political theater. They do not neutrally describe operational plans.

# IV. Address, pronouns, and relationship registers

Japanese characterization often resides in who may call whom what, and under which circumstances.

## 1. First-person forms

| Form | Typical user/function | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 予 | Reinhard as sovereign | Ceremonial imperial “I”; the speaker presents himself as office and ruler. |
| おれ | Reinhard in private youth; several informal male speakers | Direct personal self, especially important with Kircheis. The shift from `おれ` to `予` marks political distance. |
| 私 | Yang and many professional adults | Neutral/formal “I”; Yang’s use supports low theatricality and professional restraint. |
| ぼく | Julian, especially in the diary | Youth, candor, apprenticeship, and situated witness. His public vocabulary expands without requiring a total pronoun conversion. |
| 吾々 / 我々 | Political and military collectives; conspicuous in Trunicht’s rhetoric | “We” can designate genuine institutional responsibility or let a speaker occupy the nation’s voice while hiding personal cost. |

## 2. Titles and forms of address

| Form | Working rendering | Relational force |
|---|---|---|
| 陛下 | Your Majesty / His Majesty | Imperial sovereignty. |
| 皇妃 | Empress / Imperial Consort | Hilda’s dynastic position; context determines whether marital or governing role is foregrounded. |
| 閣下 | Excellency | High office or rank, respectful but not intimate. |
| 卿 | my lord / you, honored subject | Reinhard’s sovereign address to elite subordinates: recognition inside hierarchy. |
| 提督 | Admiral | Professional title; around Yang it becomes name, office, affection, and eventually memorial identity. |
| 元帥 | Marshal | Highest military rank; can become honorific and historical label. |
| さま | honorific of marked respect | `ラインハルトさま` preserves Kircheis’s affection and hierarchy simultaneously. |
| 伯爵 / 侯爵 / 公爵 / 男爵 | count / marquis / duke / baron | Aristocratic title; in the old Empire it encodes legal privilege, not merely courtesy. |
| フロイライン | Fräulein / young lady | Germanic court coloring; often marks aristocratic gender and status. |

## 3. Relationship-specific patterns

- **Reinhard ↔ Kircheis:** `ラインハルトさま` is neither ordinary subordination nor full equality. It is childhood intimacy carried inside a hierarchy that later becomes politically consequential.
- **Reinhard ↔ Hilda:** formal court language becomes intellectual equality and then conjugal/sovereign partnership. Hilda’s deference does not signal surrendered judgment.
- **Reinhard ↔ Yang:** both preserve formal respect without becoming ruler and subject. “Equal standing” is a political achievement in their encounters.
- **Yang household:** title, teasing, food, money, and domestic routine lower heroic distance. The household is where public figures retain ordinary personhood.
- **Reuenthal ↔ Mittermeyer:** professional title coexists with horizontal friendship. Their tragedy begins when public command roles make private equality politically impossible.
- **Frederica ↔ Yang:** aide-to-commander language does not disappear when intimacy grows; their marriage preserves professional recognition.
- **Julian ↔ Yang:** filial, pedagogical, domestic, and military registers overlap. Calling Julian merely “disciple” or “son” loses the plurality.

# V. Narratorial and historiographic formulas

| Formula | Working meaning | Evidentiary caution |
|---|---|---|
| 後世 | later generations / posterity | Signals retrospective horizon, not a single settled verdict. |
| 後世の歴史家 | later historians | A plural and often disagreeing field. Their judgments may be corrected by the narrator. |
| 史家 / 歴史家 | historian | May refer to military, Imperial, Alliance, or ideologically situated historians. |
| 記録 | record | Durable evidence, but never automatically complete or unbiased. |
| 回想 / 回顧録 | recollection / memoir | Valuable private witness shaped by memory and self-understanding. |
| 資料 | source material / documents | The basis for later reconstruction; distance can improve analysis even as living testimony disappears. |
| 史実 | historical fact | A claim about what occurred, distinct from moral or symbolic “truth.” |
| 事実 | fact | Objective or externally verifiable occurrence, though access may remain partial. |
| 真実 | truth | Lived, interpretive, or personal truth; not automatically superior to fact when imposed on others. |
| 伝説 | legend | Concentrated public memory; may embellish, preserve, mobilize, or distort. |
| 虚像 | false/enlarged image | Socially constructed persona, especially the posthumous Yang as personified democracy. |
| とされている | is regarded as / is said to be | Marks received judgment rather than direct demonstration. |
| であろう | probably / would / may be | Narratorial inference or counterfactual rather than hard fact. |
| かもしれない | may / might | Explicit uncertainty. It should survive translation. |
| もし | if | Counterfactual history; reveals contingency and causal structure, not alternate canon. |
| 結果論 | result-oriented hindsight | The temptation to judge earlier choices only by known outcomes. |

The most important retrieval discipline is to ask: **who knows this, when, from which source, and with what degree of certainty?** A narrator’s report of later controversy is not equivalent to a directly dramatized event. A character’s private interpretation is not the work’s final verdict. The interview can illuminate construction but cannot retroactively settle narrative ambiguity.

# VI. Load-bearing passage index

Each entry contains a short Japanese anchor, a working translation, the principal interpretive use, and the documents where the passage is most important. The excerpts are intentionally fragmentary.

## P01 — dictatorship as drastic medicine

- **Locator:** `M01-PRO`, Rudolf’s rise.
- **Japanese anchor:** `〝独裁〟という名の劇薬`
- **Working translation:** “the drastic drug called dictatorship.”
- **Use:** Institutional disease can make authoritarian cure attractive. The metaphor concedes diagnosis while foregrounding side effects.
- **Primary homes:** 01, 08, 11.

## P02 — responsibility for producing dictators

- **Locator:** `M01-C01`, Yang Tai-long speaking to young Yang.
- **Japanese anchor:** `独裁者は出現させる側により多くの責任がある`
- **Working translation:** “Those who bring a dictator into being bear the greater responsibility.”
- **Use:** Anti-Caesarist responsibility extends beyond the ruler to passive publics and institutions seeking relief from self-government.
- **Primary homes:** 03, 08, 10.

## P03 — Reinhard’s early rejection of hereditary entitlement

- **Locator:** `M01-C06`, Reinhard’s inner resolution.
- **Japanese anchor:** `皇帝にはなろう。しかし帝位を自分の子孫に伝えるようなことはしない`
- **Working translation:** “I will become emperor. But I will not pass the throne to my descendants.”
- **Use:** His revolution begins with anti-hereditary intent, later complicated by dynasty, Hilda, and Alexander Siegfried.
- **Primary homes:** 02, 04, 08.

## P04 — no absolute-good war

- **Locator:** `M02-C07`, Yang’s reflection during the Alliance coup.
- **Japanese anchor:** `〝絶対善と絶対悪の戦い〟などなかった`
- **Working translation:** “There has never been a battle of absolute good against absolute evil.”
- **Use:** The line rejects moral Manichaeism, not moral judgment. Competing subjective righteousness can still be assessed by conduct and institutions.
- **Primary homes:** 03, 10, 11.

## P05 — Kircheis on Westerland

- **Locator:** `M02-C08`, Westerland confrontation.
- **Japanese anchor:** `民衆を犠牲になされば、手は血に汚れ`
- **Working translation:** “If you sacrifice the people, your hands will be stained with blood.”
- **Use:** Consequential success does not wash away authorship of preventable civilian harm.
- **Primary homes:** 02, 04, 10.

## P06 — democracy under ordinary people

- **Locator:** `M03-C05`, Yang under inquiry.
- **Japanese anchor:** `凡人が集まって運営する民主主義`
- **Working translation:** “democracy administered by ordinary people gathered together.”
- **Use:** Yang’s preference is procedural and anti-heroic, not an empirical claim that democratic rulers are wiser.
- **Primary homes:** 03, 08, 10.

## P07 — democracy as a system that should not need heroes

- **Locator:** `M03-C05`, Yang’s state reflection.
- **Japanese anchor:** `英雄や偉人が存在する必要をなくすための制度`
- **Working translation:** “a system meant to eliminate the need for heroes and great men.”
- **Use:** The Alliance’s tragedy is that it becomes dependent upon Yang, contradicting the institutional ideal he defends.
- **Primary homes:** 03, 06, 08, 11.

## P08 — the state as instrument

- **Locator:** `M04-C05`, Yang and Julian before Fezzan.
- **Japanese anchor:** `国家なんてものはたんなる道具にすぎない`
- **Working translation:** “A state is nothing more than a tool.”
- **Use:** States are judged by human purposes, not granted immortal moral personality.
- **Primary homes:** 03, 08, 10.

## P09 — posterity does not certify subjective righteousness

- **Locator:** `M04-C05`, Yang’s reflection on historical judgment.
- **Japanese anchor:** `自分が正義と信じていれば後世もそれを認めてくれる、などと考えたことはない`
- **Working translation:** “He had never thought posterity would recognize him merely because he believed himself just.”
- **Use:** Separates sincerity from legitimacy and anticipates the corpus’s treatment of historical memory.
- **Primary homes:** 03, 10, 11.

## P10 — certainty as a source of cruelty

- **Locator:** `M04-C05`, Yang on moral absolutism.
- **Japanese anchor:** `人間がもっとも…残酷に…なりうるのは、自分の正しさを確信したときだ`
- **Working translation:** “Human beings can become most cruel when convinced of their own rightness.”
- **Use:** Core warning against righteous violence; ellipses mark omitted intensifiers, not uncertainty in the source.
- **Primary homes:** 03, 10, 11.

## P11 — space-area control

- **Locator:** `M02-C07`, Yang formulates `宙域管制`; retrospectively assessed in `M07-C04`.
- **Japanese anchor:** `必要な時間に必要な空間を確保しておく`
- **Working translation:** “secure the necessary space for the necessary time.”
- **Use:** Yang’s strategy values temporary option control over permanent possession or battle for its own sake.
- **Primary homes:** 03, 09.

## P12 — democracy as equal friendship

- **Japanese anchor:** `民主主義とは対等の友人をつくる思想`
- **Working translation:** “Democracy is an idea for making equal friends.”
- **Use:** A relational definition of democracy opposed to mastery and servitude.
- **Primary homes:** 06, 08, 10.

## P13 — the problem of remaining a good ruler

- **Locator:** `M07-C01`, retrospective assessment of Reinhard.
- **Japanese anchor:** `名君にとって最大の課題は、名君でありつづけること`
- **Working translation:** “The greatest challenge for a good ruler is to remain a good ruler.”

- **Primary homes:** 02, 08, 11.

## P14 — the derisive name for Iserlohn

- **Locator:** `M09-C01`, after establishment of the republican government.
- **Japanese anchor:** `〝孤児と未亡人による連合政権〟`
- **Working translation:** “a coalition government of orphans and widows.”
- **Use:** Hostile historiography gender-codes and belittles survivor government; the narrative tests the insult against actual continuity.

## P15 — the torch of inherited intention

- **Locator:** `M09-C01`, Julian after meeting a young admirer.
- **Japanese anchor:** `想いのたいまつはリレーされていく`
- **Working translation:** “the torch of intention/feeling is passed along in relay.”
- **Use:** Inheritance is transmission, not duplication. `想い` includes thought, care, aspiration, and memory.

## P16 — the dead Yang as political personification

- **Locator:** `M08-C09`, foundation of the August government.
- **Japanese anchor:** `〝死せるヤン・ウェンリー〟`

- **Use:** Republican survival depends upon posthumous personification of democracy, creating the very heroic concentration Yang distrusted.

## P17 — sword and pen

- **Locator:** `M08-C09`, Julian’s reflection on records.
- **Japanese anchor:** `剣はペンにたいして一時的な勝利しかなしえない`
- **Working translation:** “The sword can win only a temporary victory over the pen.”
- **Use:** Power can suppress records temporarily, not guarantee final control of history.
- **Primary homes:** 06, 11.

## P18 — victory, survival, and who gets to speak

- **Locator:** `G04-C08`, 730 Mafia prehistory.
- **Japanese anchor:** `勝者だけが戦いを語り、生者だけが人生を語る`
- **Working translation:** “Only victors speak of battle; only the living speak of life.”
- **Use:** The text recognizes archive bias, then argues that even partial record creates a baseline from which dissent can excavate shadow.
- **Primary homes:** 11, 12, 15.

## P19 — later distance can improve historical understanding

- **Locator:** `G04-C09`, Yang on historical method.
- **Japanese anchor:** `後世の人のほうが、しばしば事件の本質を正しく把握できる`
- **Working translation:** “People of later ages can often grasp an event’s essence more correctly.”
- **Use:** Rejects the claim that only eyewitnesses can understand, while preserving the need for evidence and testimony.
- **Primary homes:** 03, 11, 12.

## P20 — fact and truth

- **Locator:** `G04-C04`, Miriam Rosas challenging Yang.
- **Japanese anchor:** `あなたは事実と真実を混同してるんじゃないの？`
- **Working translation:** “Aren’t you confusing fact with truth?”
- **Use:** Opens a distinction between objective infringement and the meanings people make of their own lives. Neither category automatically cancels the other.
- **Primary homes:** 11, 12.

## P21 — Julian’s situated-record ethic

- **Japanese anchor:** `見たこと聞いたことをできるだけ正確に書いておこう`

## P22 — tactical accident and strategic necessity

- **Locator:** `M10-C07`, epigraph attributed to Yang.
- **Japanese anchor:** `戦術レベルにおける偶然は、戦略レベルにおける必然の、余光の破片`

- **Japanese anchor:** `まず、陛下、憲法をおつくりください`
- **Working translation:** “First, Your Majesty, create a constitution.”
- **Use:** Julian’s objective is institutional transformation and protected political continuity, not military conquest of the Empire.

## P24 — Reinhard makes dynasty conditional

- **Locator:** `M10-C10`, Reinhard speaking to Hilda.

- **Working translation:** “There is no need deliberately to preserve the Lohengramm dynasty.”

## P25 — transition from legend to history

- **Locator:** `M10-C10`, final sentence.

- **Working translation:** “The legend ends, and history begins.”
- **Use:** Responsibility moves from concentrated heroic biography to successors, institutions, records, and contestable memory. It does not mean everything before was false.

## P26 — the democratic choice after Reinhard

- **Locator:** `M10-C10`, final retrospective synthesis.
- **Japanese anchor:** `凡人の集団が試行錯誤をかさねながら`
- **Working translation:** “a body of ordinary people, accumulating trial and error.”
- **Use:** Democracy is chosen as an ongoing method without guarantee, not as a claim to superior immediate performance.
- **Primary homes:** 03, 08, 10, 13.

## P27 — the state’s mortality

- **Locator:** `G04-C06`, Yang’s political-medical analogy.

- **Working translation:** “Eternity is impossible. There is no need to desire eternity.”
- **Use:** The political aim becomes a state that lives as long and healthily as possible, not an immortal institution demanding limitless sacrifice.

## P28 — legend as a baseline for dissent

- **Japanese anchor:** `伝説なら伝説で、基準となるものがあってこそ異論も存在する`
- **Working translation:** “Even if it is legend, dissent can exist only because there is a baseline.”
- **Use:** LOGH does not imagine history without public stories; it imagines responsible argument against and through them.

# VIII. Final caution

LOGH’s Japanese vocabulary is politically alive. `民主主義`, `自由`, `忠誠`, `正統`, `革命`, `国家`, `歴史`, and `伝説` do not possess one moral value wherever they appear. Demagogues can speak of freedom. Loyal officers can become rebels. A legitimate dynasty can become politically empty. A conquering autocrat can perform real reform. A democratic hero can become the posthumous object of a personality cult. A legend can preserve the evidence needed to criticize itself.

The exact-language discipline of this appendix is therefore not antiquarian. It protects the series from interpretation by slogan. The words must be returned to speaker, institution, scene, historical layer, and consequence.
