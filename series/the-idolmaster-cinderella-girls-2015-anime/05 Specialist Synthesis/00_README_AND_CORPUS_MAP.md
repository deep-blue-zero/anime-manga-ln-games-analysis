# 00 — README and Corpus Map
## THE IDOLM@STER CINDERELLA GIRLS (2015)
### Reader guide, source boundary, retrieval map, and interpretive orientation

This directory is the canonical analytical corpus for the 2015 television anime **THE IDOLM@STER CINDERELLA GIRLS**.

It is not a transcript archive, a franchise encyclopedia, a game-character reference, or a general history of the *THE IDOLM@STER* franchise. Its primary object is the 2015 anime itself, read as a Japanese-original audiovisual work through a sequential, source-grounded method.

The mainline analytical scope is **Episodes 1–25**. Episode 25 remains the canonical mainline ending. **Episode 26 / Extra** is analyzed separately as a supplementary animated epilogue or paratext. It may confirm, relax, or retrospectively reframe patterns already established by the mainline, but it does not replace Episode 25 as the series' dramatic ending.

The mature full-series thesis is:

> **A dream becomes livable when people and institutions build forms through which an unfinished person can act, be seen, fail, revise herself, and continue—without allowing those forms to become a final claim of ownership over who she is allowed to become.**

The compact version is:

> **The magic of *THE IDOLM@STER CINDERELLA GIRLS* is the collective production of futures that no participant can fully predict or own.**

The series begins with the possibility that another person may see something in you before you can name it yourself. It eventually asks a harder question: whether relationships, units, producers, audiences, and institutions can remain responsible to developing people after those people become more differentiated than the forms that first made them visible.

That is the corpus's governing interpretive problem.

---

# I. What kind of corpus this is

The analytical process proceeded in layers rather than jumping directly from episode viewing to a final thesis.

The logical chain is:

> **primary audiovisual source → prospective episode reading → retrospective audiovisual audit → claim/locator provenance → specialist synthesis → continuous full-series synthesis → reader-facing corpus map → final archival audit**

Each layer has a different responsibility.

- The **primary source** governs what happened, what was said, what was shown, how something sounded, and when it occurred.
- The **episode readings** preserve what could responsibly be said at a particular chronological horizon.
- The **claim ledger** records mature claims, locators, confidence/audit state, and revision history.
- The **specialist documents** isolate major analytical problems so that character, institution, performance, ethics, voice, and visual form do not collapse into one undifferentiated interpretation.
- The **full-series synthesis** recombines the stabilized specialist findings into one continuous literary argument.
- This **README** tells a reader which layer to consult for which question and records the corpus's known provenance limitations.

A later document does not automatically overrule an earlier one merely because it is later. The evidence layer remains primary. The specialist and final syntheses are interpretations of that evidence, not substitutes for it.

# III. Source hierarchy

The governing evidence hierarchy is:

1. **Japanese program audio and Japanese dialogue layer**
2. **Complete visual staging in the episode itself**
3. **Episode chronology and causal structure**
4. **Recurring patterns established by already-reviewed episodes**
5. **Later reviewed episodes, only in explicitly retrospective analysis**
6. **Official scripts, storyboards, interviews, fanbooks, and production notes**
7. **Franchise/game context**
8. **External criticism and reception**

Different claims require different evidence.

For exact wording, Japanese dialogue/audio governs.

For facial expression, blocking, costume, framing, architecture, gesture, written visual information, or staging, the actual image sequence governs.

For vocal delivery, program audio governs.

For music, silence, ambience, audience response, and transitions between speech and song, program audio governs.

For performance as a dramatic event, audio, visual sequence, Japanese dialogue, lyrics where available, audience behavior, and surrounding causal structure must be read together.

# IV. Japanese audio and subtitle status

Episodes 1–25 are analyzed with Japanese-original program audio and Japanese dialogue evidence.

For the dedicated voice synthesis, the corpus was re-grounded against two complete audio/subtitle archives:

- **Bundle 1:** E01–E12, twelve full-program MP3s plus twelve matched Japanese ASS files.
- **Bundle 2:** E13–E26, fourteen full-program MP3s plus fourteen matched ASS files.

For E13–E25, the subtitle layer is Japanese. Episode 26 contains original Japanese program audio but the supplied subtitle layer is English. Accordingly:

- E26 audio may support claims about vocal performance, silence, timing, music, and scene transition;
- the E26 English subtitle may assist navigation and broad semantics;
- it is **not** treated as quotation-grade evidence for fine Japanese lexical/register claims.

This limitation is enforced explicitly in Document 11.

The analytical Drive mirror stores source locks, hashes, locators, methods, analysis, and audit artifacts. It is **not** intended to redistribute copyrighted audiovisual source payloads.

# V. Chronological discipline: prospective and retrospective readings

The governing method uses a strict rule:

> **Episode N may know Episodes 1–N.**

During sequential analysis, an unreviewed later episode could be staged or checksum-verified, but not semantically consumed.

This prevents later revelations from silently rewriting what an earlier episode actually allowed a viewer to know.

The corpus therefore distinguishes:

- **prospective reading** — what was supportable at the episode's own spoiler horizon;
- **retrospective synthesis** — what later reviewed evidence allows the completed series to recognize about earlier material.

These are not interchangeable.

A later synthesis may say that an early scene became newly legible. It should not pretend the later interpretation was already certain when the early episode first aired.

## The E13 checkpoint

The end-of-Cour-1 horizon is especially important because Episode 13 can plausibly feel like a satisfying ending before the second cour changes the scale of the problem.

The authentic standalone historical E13/Cour-1 checkpoint could not be recovered as a complete artifact. It has **not** been retrospectively reconstructed and falsely labeled historical.

Its preservation record is:

- [CG2015_E13_FROZEN_CHECKPOINT_PROVENANCE.md](../04%20Checkpoints/CG2015_E13_FROZEN_CHECKPOINT_PROVENANCE.md)

The mature corpus therefore preserves the E13 prospective boundary through provenance rather than pretending to possess a recovered document it does not have.

# VI. Known historical artifact gaps

The corpus is analytically complete, but the archival history is not perfectly symmetrical.

Three gaps should remain visible.

## 1. Episodes 1–15 standalone deep-reading files

The original E01–E15 analyses were completed and their source-audited conclusions survive through cumulative provenance, later synthesis, retrospective audio work, and the 410-claim ledger.

However, the complete set of standalone E01–E15 canonical Markdown deep-reading files was not recoverable in the final local/Drive workspace.

They must **not** be silently reconstructed with full-series hindsight and presented as if they were the original prospective documents.

## 2. Standalone Cour-1 provisional synthesis

The authentic standalone Cour-1 synthesis is not recoverable. The frozen provenance marker exists precisely so this absence does not erase the historical E13 horizon.

## 3. Early retrospective sound addenda

Standalone retrospective music/sound addenda survive individually for later portions of E01–E15, but not every early episode has a separate retained addendum file. Their validated findings survive in the cumulative claim ledger and final sound audit.

These are archival gaps, not unresolved interpretive gaps.

# XI. Core interpretive results

The specialist layer is intentionally distributed, but several results define the mature corpus as a whole.

## 1. Development is not a confidence meter

The series repeatedly changes what counts as development.

The broad progression is:

> **recognition → representation → embodiment → differentiation → unequal opportunity → failure interpretation → unit-specific competence → Project-scale coordination → institutional contest → plural self-authorship → asynchronous development → recoverable continuation**

The strongest structural rule is:

> **Solutions become the premises of later problems.**

Cour 2 does not expose Cour 1 as false. Cour 1 succeeds strongly enough to produce relationships, units, identities, professional value, and institutional stakes that Cour 2 must govern.

## 2. New Generations matures by becoming non-exclusive

New Generations is not a fusion of three people into one final unit identity.

Its mature form allows:

- Rin to pursue Triad Primus;
- Mio to pursue acting;
- Uzuki to confront a less specialized but still valid desire;
- the trio to remain historically and emotionally meaningful without possessing every future each member may have.

Its final strength is that three people can remain bound without demanding that belonging exhaust personhood.

## 3. Producer's responsibility is possibility without possession

Producer does not mature by becoming more controlling or by becoming irrelevant.

He learns to distinguish domains:

- command where bodily safety and live operations legitimately belong to him;
- intervene when evidence shows a strategy has stopped functioning;
- delegate where the performer owns the desire;
- yield where peers possess relational standing he lacks;
- create tests when more evidence is needed;
- remain accountable for conditions even when authorship belongs elsewhere.

Production becomes:

> **responsibility for possibility without possession of personhood.**

## 4. Cinderella Project is a professional collective, not a family ontology

The Project begins as an administrative roster and developmental cohort. It becomes a network, task-specific team, and community of practice.

Its mature umbrella description is:

> **professional collective**

The family metaphor is locally available as affection but analytically insufficient as a governing category. It obscures unequal intimacy, professional obligation, permeability, accountability, and the right to maintain outside futures.

A central distinction is:

> **redundancy is a property of functions; non-fungibility is a property of persons.**

## 5. 346 is both enabling infrastructure and consequential power

346 is not merely the benevolent place where dreams happen, nor merely the machine that oppresses them.

It allocates access to transformation through:

- selection;
- training;
- staffing;
- songs;
- costumes;
- creators;
- schedules;
- media;
- branding;
- evaluation;
- visibility;
- safety;
- risk;
- continuation.

Producer and Mishiro articulate coherent but incomplete production philosophies.

Mishiro fears institutional false positives: maintaining forms that no longer justify scarce professional resources or the castle's aspirational promise.

Producer fears developmental false negatives: discarding people or possibilities before sufficient evidence exists to know what they could become.

Episode 25 does not declare one omniscient and the other defeated.

## 6. Persona is not falsity

The anime does not oppose a constructed public self to one pure private truth.

A professional persona can be:

- sincere;
- aspirational;
- playful;
- marketable;
- defensive;
- artistically authored;
- institutionally constrained;
- appropriated after initially being imposed.

The decisive question is whether the performer can recognize, inhabit, revise, and act through the form without being reduced to it.

The mature authenticity model is therefore:

> **authored non-alienation inside mediation**

rather than “just be yourself.”

## 7. Performance generates knowledge

Performance is not only the public display of development completed elsewhere.

It repeatedly creates evidence.

A character may learn what she wants, what she fears, what an audience response means, or what an existing relationship can bear only by entering a stage, recording, MC, acting role, or public task.

Success must therefore be separated into different axes, especially:

- commercial/market success;
- technical/professional success;
- social/recognitional success;
- personal/experiential success.

Audience volume is not an infallible truth-meter.

## 8. Developmental risk is legitimate only under conditions

The series is not anti-risk.

Its preferred developmental challenge is:

> **bounded, interpretable, supported, revisable, and meaningfully owned by the performer, while adults and institutions remain accountable for the conditions they create.**

Respect for autonomy is not passive acceptance of every first explanation. Care is not control. Protection is not automatically paternalism. Failure does not automatically justify disposability.

The mature principle is:

> **Development requires permission to remain unfinished without making unfinishedness consequence-free.**

## 9. Vocal maturation is pragmatic expansion, not normalization

The Japanese voice synthesis rejects a cast-wide model in which characters become mature by becoming less stylized, quieter, plainer, or more conventionally adult-sounding.

Marked voices remain marked.

Maturation appears when an established voice becomes capable of carrying more kinds of work:

- authority;
- intimacy;
- uncertainty;
- conflict;
- professional coordination;
- self-description;
- public performance;
- care.

Uzuki's repeated `頑張ります` is the clearest longitudinal example: the wording remains recognizable while its pragmatic function changes dramatically.

## 10. Cinderella imagery is elastic, not a codebook

Castle, stairs, shoes, stars, clocks, dream, magic, silence, and recurring song fields do not possess one fixed symbolic translation.

The mature formal method asks:

1. what literally recurs;
2. what it does locally;
3. how later placement changes its function;
4. what interpretive ceiling prevents over-symbolization.

The series' fairy-tale grammar becomes more material rather than disappearing. Microphone checks, technical labor, electrical failure, staff, rehearsal, scheduling, bodies, and actual objects do not disprove magic. They show the material conditions through which magic becomes livable.

The mainline ends with practice rather than definition:

> **`一歩ずつこの階段を` — one step at a time.**

# XII. Sound and music guardrails

The final sound audit established several guardrails that should remain binding in future use of the corpus.

1. **Silence has context-dependent functions.** Do not assign one stable symbolic meaning to every quiet passage.
2. **Do not assign causal agency to non-diegetic music when action precedes it.** Music can frame, enlarge, connect, or reinterpret action without causing it.
3. **Audience loudness is not identical to recognition.** Audience response must be addressable and interpretable in context.
4. **Trans-diegetic continuation does not make lyrics omniscient interior narration.**
5. **Recurring songs must be interpreted through changing dramatic placement, not fixed symbolic code.**
6. **Successful performance does not imply success on every axis.**
7. **Technical production does not demystify Cinderella magic out of existence.**

These constraints are particularly important when reusing Documents 07 and 10 comparatively.

# XIII. Terminology and analytical glossary

## `笑顔` / smile

Not a universal moral law and not a simple facial-expression metric. It begins as Producer's under-explained recognition/selection criterion and becomes meaningful only through histories of relation, authored development, audience response, and causal effect.

## `頑張ります`

Uzuki's recurrent effort formulation. Its importance lies in changing pragmatic function rather than lexical novelty.

## Cinderella / `灰かぶり`

Not a fixed one-to-one role mapping. The series distributes Cinderella, prince, castle, magic, shoes, midnight, and transformation language elastically.

## Magic / `魔法`

The series deliberately refuses an exhaustive final definition. Feeling, effort, relation, professional infrastructure, recognition, and authored continuation may all participate without any one becoming the total ontology.

## Common time

An analytical term for coordinated professional/social timing that permits differentiated people to act together without becoming identical. E11–E13 and E25 are central.

## Recoverability

The capacity to preserve meaningful collective action through disruption by redistributing functions, attention, knowledge, and support without requiring every person to be invulnerable.

## Non-fungibility

The principle that another person may cover a function without thereby becoming the same person or erasing the meaning of absence.

## Authored non-alienation

Document 08's mature authenticity model: a person may inhabit a constructed professional form authentically when she can recognize, use, revise, and meaningfully ratify it rather than merely being displayed by it.

## Contestable continuity

The mature 346 institutional condition in which competing production philosophies and professional forms remain active inside the organization without either total philosophical merger or simple winner/loser resolution.

## Community of practice

The mature learning dimension of Cinderella Project: experience, competence, interpretation, precedent, and corrected practices circulate across members and units.

# XIV. Comparative-use rules

Document 12 makes the corpus suitable for future comparative work, but several rules should remain binding.

1. **Describe CG2015 on its own terms before comparing it.**
2. Do not turn a nearby work's preferred vocabulary into CG2015's hidden vocabulary.
3. Preserve differences in institutional setting: corporate professional production is not a school club, magical service organization, independent band, or child-idol division.
4. Preserve differences in performer age, labor conditions, authority, and source medium.
5. Use comparison to reveal contrast, not to force conceptual equivalence.

# XVII. What remains unresolved

The corpus is not designed to manufacture ambiguity where the anime has already supplied a stable answer.

Several questions are considered settled absent new primary evidence:

- New Generations does not return to exclusive identity.
- Producer does not simply defeat Mishiro morally or institutionally.
- Persona is not equivalent to falsity.
- Audience size is not equivalent to recognition.
- Vocal maturity is not equivalent to normalization.

- Episode 26 does not replace Episode 25.

- whether the Producer/Mishiro equilibrium would remain stable under longer-term organizational pressure;

- how long-term market pressure interacts with performer-authored persona;
- whether the Project's community-of-practice character survives increasingly divergent careers.

These are future questions, not holes that invalidate the current synthesis.

# XVIII. Final synthesis orientation

A reader approaching only one analytical document after this README should read:

- [CINDERELLA_GIRLS_FULL_SERIES_SYNTHESIS.md](../08%20Final%20Synthesis%20and%20Release/CINDERELLA_GIRLS_FULL_SERIES_SYNTHESIS.md)

Its central conclusion can be restated from the perspective of this corpus map:

The anime begins with unfinished girls approaching a castle from outside. It gradually reveals that neither castle, producer, unit, audience, persona, song, friendship, nor self-description can permanently define what an idol will become.

Those forms matter precisely because they are real. They distribute resources, create stages, make people visible, allow relationships to develop, produce audiences, preserve memory, constrain action, and sometimes injure the people they are supposed to support.

The ethical task is therefore not to escape form.

It is to build forms capable of revision.

A good unit must survive its members becoming more than the unit.

A good relationship must survive another person's legitimate outside future.

A good producer must create possibility without claiming authorship of personhood.

A good institution must allocate scarce resources without pretending present legibility exhausts future value.

A good persona must remain inhabitable rather than becoming a cage.

A good developmental challenge must expose a person to reality without abandoning her to it.

A good audience relation must make recognition possible without turning spectators into owners.

A mature voice must be able to do more without ceasing to sound like the person who speaks through it.

That is why the final Cinderella problem is no longer whether magic exists.

The question is what remains usable after magic stops functioning as a guarantee.

The series' answer is neither cynicism nor permanent enchantment.

It is continued practice, relation, revision, and another step.

> **`一歩ずつこの階段を`**
