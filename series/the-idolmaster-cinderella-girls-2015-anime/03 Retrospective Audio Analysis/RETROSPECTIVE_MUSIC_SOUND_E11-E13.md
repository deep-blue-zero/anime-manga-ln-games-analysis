# THE IDOLM@STER CINDERELLA GIRLS
## Retrospective Music/Sound Audit — Episodes 11–13

## Batch thesis

Episodes 11–13 form an unusually coherent sonic progression:

> **E11 teaches two performers to sound different together.  
> E12 teaches the Project to keep time together.  
> E13 turns that shared time into music—after first exposing how fragile the infrastructure that makes public music possible actually is.**

This is not a retrospective rewrite of the first-cour ending. The canonical E11–E13 prospective readings and frozen E13 checkpoint remain unchanged. The v2 pass adds the audiovisual mechanisms that make several existing claims materially stronger.

Canonical addendum: `CG2015_EP11_MUSIC_SOUND_RETROSPECTIVE_ADDENDUM.md`

# E12 — togetherness becomes countable

Canonical addendum: `CG2015_EP12_MUSIC_SOUND_RETROSPECTIVE_ADDENDUM.md`

E12 expands the problem from a two-person unit to the whole Project.

Three sonic developments matter.

First, Asterisk's recently developed live grammar becomes Project-level labor: Minami selects Miku and Riina as MCs because their earlier MC was strong, and their differentiated markers remain audible while they jointly regulate the games.

Second, the games convert abstract coordination into counted bodily rhythm. `１・２`, breath, footfalls, falls and recovery make `結束力` something the audience can hear.

Third, `ススメ☆オトメ` sings differentiated dreams becoming connected and the joy of understanding one another immediately before the soundtrack gives way to the rope exercise:

> song → `せーのっ` → count → failure → distributed repair → new onset → Minami count.

That transition makes the episode's production philosophy unusually concrete.

The Project does not become coordinated because someone tells it to “be united.”

It develops a shared temporal structure.

Minami's leadership changes accordingly:

> directive authority → peer-requested temporal reference.

New claims:
- `CG-REL-0032`
- `CG-PERF-0027`
- `CG-VOICE-0021`

# E13 — common time survives contingency and becomes public music

Canonical addendum: `CG2015_EP13_MUSIC_SOUND_RETROSPECTIVE_ADDENDUM.md`

E13 first attacks the performance system itself.

Lightning, screams, emergency PA, power loss, restoration instructions and a brief near-total acoustic dropout replace idol spectacle with infrastructure. The system normally hidden beneath the performance becomes audible precisely when performance stops.

The episode then rebuilds relation in stages.

New Generations is told in advance that the audience has not fully returned. Mio contextualizes the reduced turnout and chooses the people present. The live produces repeated audible audience response.

This corrects E06 without pretending E06 lacked recognition. The change is:

> **recognition becomes interpretable.**

Then `GOIN'!!!` turns the first-cour collective thesis into musical form.

E12 ended with:

> `せーのっ` → `1 2 3 ... 10`

E13's collective song contains:

> `一体感流れる奇跡`  
> `歌を歌を歌を歌おう / 一緒に`  
> `(Yeah 1･2･3･4!)`

The anime has moved from bodies learning common time to public music containing common time.

Afterward, recognition changes medium again:

> crowd sound → fan letters/surveys → private interpretation.

The recurring ending-song field can therefore return beside `迷いもあるけれど`, Rin's `楽しかった…と思う`, Mio's audience-smile recognition and Uzuki's `夢じゃない` without requiring uncertainty to vanish.

New claims:
- `CG-INST-0016`
- `CG-PERF-0028`
- `CG-REL-0033`
- `CG-MOTIF-0019`

# Batch-level progression

The E11–E13 sound arc is a scale-expansion sequence.

## E11 — unit scale

The problem is:

> Can two strongly differentiated performers make one shared expressive object?

Answer:

> yes, through negotiation and co-authorship rather than assimilation.

## E12 — Project rehearsal scale

The problem is:

> Can differentiated units/people occupy common time?

Answer:

> yes, through shared rhythm, repair, and leadership as coordination.

## E13 — institutional/public scale

The problem becomes twofold:

> Can the shared performance system survive material contingency?  
> Can the resulting public relation remain meaningful without maximal scale?

Answer:

> yes, but only because technical infrastructure, adult responsibility, performer adaptability, audience responsiveness, and collective timing all remain active.

This produces a stronger first-cour synthesis:

> **Cinderella magic is neither pure fantasy nor individual charisma. It is a recoverable coordination system joining authored difference, professional labor, material infrastructure, and an audience capable of answering back.**

# First-cour sonic synthesis

The first cour's sound development can now be described more precisely.

Early episodes ask whether:

- desire can become public;
- a body can enter a stage;
- an audience can be heard correctly;
- a persona can remain authored under production.

The middle episodes diversify the answers across different unit ecologies.

E11–E13 then integrate those lessons.

The series does not resolve heterogeneity.

It learns to coordinate it.

That is why the humble count matters so much.

A shared count does not tell anyone:

- what to desire;
- what persona to inhabit;
- what voice to use;
- what unit to belong to.

It supplies only enough common structure for different people to move without ceasing to be different.

`GOIN'!!!` enlarges exactly that achievement.

The mature first-cour sound thesis is therefore:

> **professional ensemble is not the production of sameness. It is the production of usable common time.**
