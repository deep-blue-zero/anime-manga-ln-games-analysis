# THE IDOLM@STER CINDERELLA GIRLS
## Retrospective Music/Sound Audit — Episodes 14–15
### Final pre-v2 backfill

## Batch thesis

E14–E15 complete the retrospective sound backfill by turning the first cour's achievements into a new problem:

> **What survives when the institutional container that helped create a developmental form is threatened with reorganization?**

The answer is not that institutions cease to matter.

The answer is that development has produced capacities, relationships, styles, memories and audience connections that cannot be reduced to the project chart itself.

The sound progression is:

> **E14: lived continuity sings, then executive authority interrupts.**  
> **E15: music returns, becomes argument, preserves differentiated voices, and follows the countermodel into the executive room.**

At the same time, the Cinderella metaphor undergoes its most important musical revision so far:

> E14: `私たちは魔法を掛けてもらいました`  
> E15: `12時過ぎの魔法` = courage to move forward `この自分の靴で`.

This is not a rejection of Producer, peers, institutions or audience.

It is the maturation of developmental assistance into something the performer can increasingly carry.

# E15 — self-carried magic

Immediately after Mishiro's reset policy, the new opening song asks what the magic after midnight is.

Its answer:

> `それはこの自分の靴で`  
> `今進んで行ける勇気でしょう？`

This revises the Cinderella metaphor from:

> being transformed

toward:

> becoming able to continue moving after the transforming event cannot simply be held still.

New claim:

- `CG-CIND-0013`

The distinction is developmental, not libertarian atomism.

The girl's “own shoes” exist after:

- recognition;
- production;
- training;
- peers;
- stages;
- failure;
- repair;
- infrastructure;
- audience.

The developmental system has succeeded when its effects are no longer wholly dependent on constant external authorship.

# Kaede — the performance itself becomes philosophy

Kaede's small-venue live is the batch's strongest music-dramaturgy sequence.

She first establishes the venue as:

- her first post-debut stage;
- a site of remembered anxiety;
- a site where fans supported and laughed with her;
- an important professional place.

Her song begins visibly on that stage.

Then New Generations asks why she refused Mishiro.

The visual narrative enters the remembered executive conversation.

The song continues.

Thus Kaede's chosen performance **scores the memory of her refusal to abandon that performance**.

The music later returns to foreground lyric and audience applause.

This converts a policy disagreement into enacted audiovisual evidence.

Mishiro says:

> larger scale → larger professional value.

Kaede's sequence answers:

> professional value can be historical, relational and performer-authored as well as scalar.

New claim:

- `CG-PERF-0030`
