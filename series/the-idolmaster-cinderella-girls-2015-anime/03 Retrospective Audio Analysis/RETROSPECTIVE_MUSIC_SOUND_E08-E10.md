# Episode 8 — translation becomes singable

The canonical E08 thesis—production should translate performer-owned distinctions without normalizing the performer—is strengthened at the level of the finished musical object.

Ranko's final song remains fully inside her gothic symbolic vocabulary, but it includes:

- `貴方と手を携え`;
- `この新たな旋律抱いて`.

Immediately after the song she thanks Producer and the others with:

- `私の声を聴き取ってくれて`.

This sequence matters because the collaboration that produced the work has become **content the work can carry without ceasing to sound like Ranko**. The successful production does not reveal a supposedly ordinary “real Ranko” underneath the persona. It demonstrates that another person can enter her authored expressive world without replacing it.

**New claim:** `CG-PERF-0021` — `VERIFIED_STRENGTHENED`.

The post-song shift `我が友` → direct `プロデューサー` / `私の声` → `我が魂の赴くままに` also supports the existing performed-vocal-state thesis: relational development expands usable registers rather than establishing plain speech as the only authentic one. No additional voice claim was created because that point was already substantially present in the canonical E08 reading.

# Episode 9 — a unit song can be portable without homogenizing the unit

`Happy×2Days` bookends the episode in two professional contexts.

At the opening it accompanies a fan-facing support/signing encounter. Near the close the girls explicitly tell the television audience that the song currently playing is their recently released debut single, explain its title, and ask listeners to hear it.

**New claim:** `CG-PERF-0022` — `VERIFIED_STRENGTHENED`.

The final sung section then contains differentiated comic spoken material inside the shared song, including explicitly Anzu-coded `フリーダム` language, joking withdrawal, and a `なんでやねん` response. The musical object therefore does not model Candy Island as three interchangeable voices. It can hold uneven comic functions, weakness, affection and collective identity simultaneously.

**New claim:** `CG-REL-0029` — `VERIFIED_STRENGTHENED`.

This strengthens rather than sanitizes the canonical E09 ethics. Chieri's panic can remain real even while Candy Island possesses a coherent, bright, professional musical identity. Public coherence and private cost remain different variables.

# Episode 10 — music moves from environment to social action

E10 contains one of the clearest explicit media statements in the first cour:

> `期間中はショッパーや店内BGMも 凸レーションです`

Producer treats recorded unit music as ambient promotional exposure inside retail space. Dekoration can be sonically present even when the performers are not.

**New claim:** `CG-MEDIA-0009` — `VERIFIED_STRENGTHENED`.

Later, the girls use `LET'S GO HAPPY!!` during their improvised city solution. The chorus is immediately followed by:

> `私たちに興味ある人ついてきてねえ`

The song therefore participates causally in gathering attention and forming a moving audience. A produced musical identity that began the episode as managed environmental exposure becomes a resource the performers themselves deploy under contingency.

**New claim:** `CG-PERF-0023` — `VERIFIED_STRENGTHENED`.

The ending returns the same central chorus while inserting separate public callouts for Rika, Miria and Kirari. That gives the episode's `凸凹` model a compact musical analogue: collective coordination does not require member interchangeability.

**New claim:** `CG-REL-0030` — `VERIFIED_STRENGTHENED`.

# Batch-level synthesis

The E08–E10 sound audit produces a coherent progression:

> **E08 makes translation singable.**  
> **E09 makes unit asymmetry portable inside music.**  
> **E10 lets music leave the fixed stage and actively reorganize public space.**

Across the three episodes, songs stop looking like interchangeable “idol anime performance rewards.” They perform different professional functions:

- translate an idiosyncratic performer into public form;
- carry a unit identity across fan and media contexts;
- preserve differentiated roles inside a shared object;
- become ambient branding;
- and become a tool performers actively use to make an audience.

This strengthens the existing E08–E11 comparative-laboratory thesis: good production is person- and relation-specific, and the **sonic form of the resulting work changes with that ecology**.
