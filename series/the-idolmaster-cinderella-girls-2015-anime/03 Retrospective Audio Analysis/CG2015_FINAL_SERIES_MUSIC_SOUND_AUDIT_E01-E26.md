# THE IDOLM@STER CINDERELLA GIRLS (2015)
## Final Bounded Music / Sound / Performance Audit — E01–E26
### From possibility becoming audible to differentiated people learning how to share time

## 0. Purpose and boundary

This document is the **final bounded audiovisual audit before mature specialist synthesis**. It is not the full-series literary synthesis, and it does not replace the sequential episode readings.

Its task is narrower and evidentiary:

1. consolidate recurring song fields and changing dramatic placements;
2. reconcile the E01–E15 retrospective sound backfill with the E16–E25 native-v2 sound observations;
3. treat E26 only as supplementary epilogue/paratext;
4. map silence, ambience, audience sound, technical/backstage sound, diegetic/non-diegetic/trans-diegetic transitions, performed-vocal states, and major performance structures;
5. identify genuine contradictions, qualifications, and new longitudinal claims;
6. produce a stable audiovisual substrate for Documents 07, 10, 11, and related specialist syntheses.

The governing methodological question remains:

> **What does the soundtrack make dramatically possible that dialogue and image alone do not?**

The answer across the complete animated corpus is not one thing. Music and sound repeatedly perform different jobs:

- they organize bodies into shared time;
- reveal the difference between rehearsed performance and spontaneous public speech;
- make audiences addressable rather than merely visible;
- create audiences under contingency;
- carry authored identity across media and spaces;
- let performance become evidence before a character can explain a decision;
- expose the material infrastructure hidden beneath “magic” when systems fail;
- create intervals in which nobody else can supply a character's missing words;
- allow one performance to continue across backstage or remembered institutional conflict;
- and preserve continuity after a crisis without forcing every person back into the same trajectory.

The mature sound proposition is therefore:

> **Cinderella Girls uses sound less as a secret emotional decoder than as a technology of coordination, relation, recognition, interpretation, and continued authorship. Professional growth is audible when differentiated people become increasingly capable of entering shared time without surrendering the particular voices, desires, and histories that make the shared form worth preserving.**

That proposition **strengthens** the E01–E15 retrospective conclusion. E16–E26 do not overturn it; they make its limits and later consequences more precise.

# 2. The largest longitudinal pattern: sound from scaffold to shared authorship

The full series can be heard as a change in **who controls the temporal and expressive structure of performance**.

## 2.1 E03: borrowed time

New Generations' first major live places the rookies inside Mika's already functional professional grammar. The pre-stage `フライドチキーン` call gives them a tiny synchronized onset (`CG-PERF-0016`), and `TOKIMEKIエスカレート` gives them stable pulse, choreography, center logic, and an experienced performer around whom they can coordinate.

But when the song ends and Mika gives them an open microphone, their speech fragments into fillers, pauses, and nonlexical vocalizations (`CG-VOICE-0020`).

The distinction is foundational:

> **being able to inhabit a supplied performance structure is not the same competence as being able to author public time once the structure stops speaking for you.**

## 2.2 E11–E13: authored difference learns common time

Asterisk advances the problem. Miku and Riina do not merely learn to stay on beat. They first generate incompatible lyrical/performance vocabularies, enter one another's audience grammar, and produce `ΦωΦver!!`, whose finished unit sound contains both sides rather than neutralizing them (`CG-PERF-0024`).

E12 then strips togetherness down to bodies and count. `ススメ☆オトメ` represents differentiated dreams joining; immediately afterward the soundtrack yields to `せーのっ`, count, rope, breath, footfall, failure, repair, and renewed onset (`CG-PERF-0027`). Shared time becomes something bodies must manufacture.

E13 scales that skill into public performance. The count language of rehearsal becomes Project-level musical timing in `GOIN'!!!`; material contingency attacks the system, yet performers, staff, audience, and technical infrastructure recover enough common time for the event to continue.

This is the point at which “ensemble” becomes more than many people sounding at once.

> **Common time is an achieved social capacity.**

## 2.3 E15 and E25: shared time no longer requires a single voice

E15's Project-room sequence gives a spoken analogue: under one musical field, Miku, Riina, Ranko, Kirari, Anzu, Chieri, Anastasia, Mio, and others retain their established lexical/vocal forms while orienting toward compatible action (`CG-VOICE-0022`).

E25 then reprises `GOIN'!!!` as a bridge into `M@GIC☆` (`CG-PERF-0049`). The first-cour achievement of common Project time is not discarded. It becomes infrastructure for a later collective capable of holding:

- solo work;
- multiple units;
- acting;
- Project Krone;
- different professional routes;
- and unresolved differences between adults.

### Final audit conclusion

> **The series' sonic model of maturity is not unison. It is differentiated coordination.**

This becomes new claim `CG-PERF-0054` below.

# 3. Recurring song fields: recurrence means changed placement, not fixed meaning

A major risk in full-series synthesis is treating a recurring song as though recurrence itself proves one stable symbolic meaning. The complete audit supports the opposite method:

> **The most important recurring songs are elastic frames whose meaning changes because their placement changes.**

## 3.1 `夕映えプレゼント` / linked-hands–steps–stars–rainbow field

The strongest recurring field develops through multiple distinct placements.

### E02 — optimism beside skepticism

The song begins while Rin is still questioning whether the first live opportunity has arrived too easily (`CG-MOTIF-0015`). Musical optimism does not silence epistemic caution.

### E04–E05 — possibility acquires institutional form

As CD/unit structures become real, the field can accompany possibility becoming organized without implying that organization exhausts personal trajectory.

### E07 — re-beginning

After rupture and explicit repair, the song enters after Producer's `もう一度一緒に見つけに行きましょう` and the Project's renewed `一緒に一歩ずつ` language (`CG-MOTIF-0018`). Beginning becomes re-beginning.

### E13 — achieved but non-final recognition

After fan letters and public recognition, the field still contains `迷いもあるけれど`; it can coexist with Mio's gratitude, Rin's qualified enjoyment, and Uzuki's `夢じゃない` (`CG-MOTIF-0019`). Recognition does not terminate uncertainty.

### E14 → E15 — an institutional-boundary change

E14 lets the relational/developmental field end before Mishiro's sparse `白紙に戻す`. E15 keeps the recurring field active beneath members' differentiated articulation and into Producer's `代案を持って来ました` (`CG-MOTIF-0020`). The same music changes sides of the institutional boundary.

### E16 — network expansion

After cross-department cooperation, linked-hands language now exceeds the original Cinderella Project container (`CG-MOTIF-0021`).

### E20 — continuity during divergence

The field enters after Mio flees the New Generations rupture, yet continues across images and announcements of newly branching paths (`CG-MOTIF-0027`). Linked hands no longer require identical direction.

### E21 — non-synchronous development

Producer explicitly pairs `それぞれの課題` with `皆で支え合い`; the recurring song then supplies linked-hands/arrival language (`CG-MOTIF-0028`). Collective continuity now includes asynchronous routes.

### E26 — ordinary continuation

After the mainline conflict is closed, `夕映えプレゼント` surrounds an anthology of work, jokes, experiments, misunderstandings, and memory (`CG-MOTIF-0037`). The relational grammar has become livable ordinary time.

### Final audit conclusion

The song is **not** a fixed code for “friendship.” It is a recurring formal field in which the series repeatedly tests what togetherness is capable of containing.

Its trajectory is:

> possibility → institutional form → repair → recognition → institutional contest → network expansion → divergence → asynchronous continuity → ordinary continuation.

This becomes new claim `CG-MOTIF-0038`.

# 4. `Shine!!`, magic, and the difference between framing and causation

The second-cour opening's after-midnight / own-shoes language becomes a recurring orientation for what persists after externally supplied transformation cannot remain fixed.

E15 prospectively establishes:

> `12時過ぎの魔法` is linked to courage to move forward `この自分の靴で` (`CG-CIND-0013`).

E17 extends the road/path language without turning identity into a predetermined route (`CG-MOTIF-0022`).

E24 materially stages the principle by bringing Uzuki back in ordinary school uniform before another fully restored princess transformation (`CG-PERF-0046`).

E25's `M@GIC☆` offers performer-level meanings of magic—feeling, heart, effort, light, bonds—while final narration still says `魔法が何なのかまだ分からない` (`CG-CIND-0022`).

E26 returns to the old Project room, enters a short near-silent archive interval, and then brings `Shine!!` back as remembered orientation (`CG-MOTIF-0035`).

This recurrence supports a mature distinction:

> **music can preserve an orientation without providing a causal command.**

The series repeatedly refuses soundtrack determinism.

- E18: Chieri re-approaches the craftsman **before** the ending song enters (`CG-MOTIF-0024`).
- E24: Uzuki chooses, returns, refuses the prompt, passes through silence, answers the audience, and reclaims `頑張ります` **before** `S(mile)ING!` begins (`CG-VOICE-0035`, `CG-PERF-0047`).
- E23: the sustained song field does not generate Rin's testimony; it enters while that testimony is already happening and recontextualizes the smile as relational illumination (`CG-PERF-0045`).

By contrast, some **diegetic performance sound really is causal**:

- E10's `LET'S GO HAPPY!!` gathers passersby and helps form an audience (`CG-PERF-0023`).
- E16's Usamin call changes the room's interaction field (`CG-PERF-0031`).
- E20's `Trancing Pulse` rehearsal supplies Rin with experiential evidence before she decides (`CG-PERF-0039`).
- E22's emergency MC/call-response buys time for backstage repair.

### Final audit rule

> **Non-diegetic music in CG2015 generally frames, extends, or reorganizes the meaning of agency already underway; diegetic performed sound can itself become an intervention that changes social reality.**

This is a methodological guardrail, not an absolute ontology. The classification must always follow the specific scene.

This becomes new claim `CG-PERF-0052`.

# 5. Diegetic → trans-diegetic expansion: performance can exceed the stage without becoming omniscient narration

The series repeatedly establishes a song in a concrete performance or media situation and then lets the audible field cross visual/spatial boundaries.

## 5.1 E04 — performance becomes reproducible media

The E03 live can be replayed and reframed as mediated material (`CG-MEDIA-0008`). This is an early reminder that a performance does not cease to exist when bodies leave the stage; it becomes a professional object that can be reviewed, circulated, and interpreted.

## 5.2 E15 — Kaede's song becomes a policy argument

Kaede begins singing in the small venue she deliberately chose. The visual narrative moves into New Generations' question and the remembered Mishiro/Kaede dispute while the song continues, then returns to the live (`CG-PERF-0030`).

The effect is formally precise:

> **the performance does not merely illustrate Kaede's production philosophy; it scores the memory of the dispute about the value of that very performance.**

## 5.3 E22 — `Trancing Pulse` embeds new-unit identity inside the support system

Triad Primus begins the song onstage. The song remains audible while the visual narrative moves through:

- Mishiro confronting Producer;
- Fumika recovering;
- Fumika/Arisu repairing their relationship;
- backstage preparation.

Then the visual field returns to the stage (`CG-PERF-0044`).

The formal result is important: the new unit's sound does **not** isolate Rin from the old network. Its public musical identity is acoustically embedded inside the broader organizational and relational response that makes the event possible.

## 5.4 E25 — live sound remains beneath theory

Producer and Mishiro do not debate idolhood in a silent philosophical vacuum. Their argument is intercut with active Ball performances and crowd response. The performers remain acoustically present as evidence while adults attempt to theorize them.

### Final audit conclusion

> **Trans-diegetic extension is one of the series' preferred ways to let a performance become an argument about relations or institutions without turning song lyrics into an omniscient narrator.**

The physical performance remains historically anchored; its audible field is allowed to organize broader causal material.

# 6. Silence and near-silence: recurring operation, not universal symbol

The complete corpus supports strong claims about silence, but **not** a single fixed “silence motif.”

The functions differ sharply.

## 6.1 E03 — pressure and subtraction

Mio's pre-stage quiet becomes conspicuous against the surrounding venue/audience field. Later, the empty auditorium creates a major acoustic subtraction after spectacle while the girls call the experience `夢みたい` (`CG-MOTIF-0016`).

Silence/quiet here helps expose the gap between event and interpretation.

## 6.2 E07 — repair needs lower acoustic scale

The public-live crisis contracts into quieter ordinary-space dialogue. The relevant change is not “quiet equals truth.” It is that explanation and listening become possible after the crowd-scale reference system stops dominating the scene (`CG-PROD-0033`).

## 6.3 E11 — public result → acoustic reset → ratification

Asterisk's completed public performance fades toward near-silence before the office conversation in which the performers ratify continuation and the institution supplies the final name.

The acoustic reset separates:

> lived public test

from:

> reflective/administrative ratification.

## 6.4 E13 — infrastructure failure

The storm and power loss replace spectacle with lightning, screams, evacuation PA, technical instructions, and an approximately 0.9-second near-total dropout (`CG-INST-0016`). Here near-silence belongs to **system failure**, not introspection.

## 6.5 E21–E22 — vocabulary insufficiency and shrinking private scale

E21's pauses matter because characters cannot yet fully formulate what they mean. E22's late Uzuki material moves from huge Autumn Live success into smaller, sparser sound spaces as the public system succeeds and the private self-definition problem becomes harder.

Neither should be collapsed into E13's technical silence.

## 6.6 E24 — authorship space

This is the strongest deliberate silence construction in the series.

After Uzuki refuses the offered prompt:

- approximately **4.93 seconds of near-silence** follow;
- audience members call her name and encourage her;
- another roughly **4.14-second low-level interval** follows;
- only then does she say `島村卯月頑張ります` (`CG-VOICE-0035`).

The crucial fact is negative:

> **nobody supplies the missing words.**

The audience gives address, presence, and encouragement—not content.

Silence here creates a temporal space in which authorship cannot be delegated.

## 6.7 E26 — archival transition

The old Project-room exchange gives way to roughly 1.89 seconds of near-silence before `Shine!!` begins (`CG-MOTIF-0035`). Here quiet marks the transition from archived room/history into remembered musical orientation.

### Final audit conclusion

> **Silence/near-silence is a recurring dramaturgical operation, not one stable symbol. It redistributes attention. Depending on context it can expose pressure, subtract spectacle, permit repair, reveal technical failure, mark lexical insufficiency, create authorship space, or convert a lived room into an archive.**

This becomes new claim `CG-MOTIF-0039` and a required guardrail for specialist synthesis.

# 7. Audience sound: from threatening scale to interpretable relation and operational resource

The audience is one of the series' most important sound systems because its development is **not** simply “the crowd gets louder.”

## 7.1 E03 — audience as scale

For novice New Generations, the venue's density and open attention produce pressure. The crowd is initially a large social field whose scale exceeds the girls' ability to interpret it.

## 7.2 E06 — recognition exists before Mio can use it

The audience repeatedly calls Mio by name and positively addresses her (`CG-PERF-0020`). Her collapse therefore cannot be explained as objective nonrecognition.

This is a foundational correction:

> **social recognition can be present while personal interpretation of success still fails.**

## 7.3 E09 — usable audience reaction is not harmlessness

Candy Island's vulnerability can create broadcast value. Audience response/variety utility therefore cannot be treated as proof that the performers experienced the situation as benign.

## 7.4 E10 — performers make an audience

`LET'S GO HAPPY!!` is deployed in public space, gathers attention, and is followed by direct invitation (`CG-PERF-0023`). Audience formation becomes a professional skill rather than a crowd simply waiting at the destination.

## 7.5 E11 and E16 — call-response as interaction grammar

Miku's `にゃー` and later the Usamin call show that audience response can be structured through performer-owned interaction grammars. These calls alter atmosphere and help create the public form of a persona/unit.

The audience participates, but participation does **not** grant it ownership over Miku's or Nana's identity.

## 7.6 E13 — recognition becomes interpretable

The post-storm New Generations performance occurs with a reduced crowd. Producer warns the trio, Mio contextualizes the situation, chooses the people actually present, and the performance receives repeated audible responses (`CG-PERF-0028`).

The developmental change from E06 is not that recognition suddenly starts existing.

It is that recognition can be **read proportionately**.

## 7.7 E22 — audience interaction becomes operational slack

Emergency MC and call-response keep the audience engaged while staff reorganize the show after Fumika's crisis. Audience sound becomes a production resource: social interaction creates time for technical/organizational recovery.

## 7.8 E24 — address without authorship substitution

The Christmas audience calls `卯月ちゃん` and `頑張れ`, but does not give Uzuki a sentence, identity, or metaphysical proof. The crowd reopens the public relation while leaving the act of speech to her.

## 7.9 E25 — public sound as evidence field

The Ball's audience remains audible while Producer and Mishiro debate the institution's philosophy. Public response becomes one kind of evidence among others, not a final court that determines the correct theory.

### Final audit conclusion

The audience-sound trajectory is:

> **scale → addressable recognition → interactive grammar → self-created audience → interpretable recognition → operational resource → relational support.**

But there is a permanent guardrail:

> **audience loudness is never an infallible truth-meter, and audience response never owns performer identity.**

This becomes new claim `CG-PERF-0053`.

# 8. Technical and backstage sound: the material infrastructure of Cinderella magic

The complete corpus makes a strong materialist claim without becoming anti-romantic.

## 8.1 E01 — material sound precedes fairy-tale sound

Clock/material-event sound exists before the sustained piano-led fairy-tale field (`CG-MOTIF-0013`). The dream is superimposed on a material city/event/production world.

## 8.2 E04–E05 — media/recording make professional path dependency audible

A lived performance can become playback/media material. Recording work converts selected future paths into actual sound while other performers are still imagining possible paths.

## 8.3 E13 — failure reveals the hidden system

Power interruption makes electricity, safety, staff communication, evacuation PA, and restoration procedure audible (`CG-INST-0016`). The show is not revealed as “fake.” Instead, its conditions of possibility become visible/audible because they fail.

## 8.4 E22 — human contingency reveals organizational infrastructure

Fumika's inability to continue transforms polished performance sound into urgent staff speech, schedule decisions, emergency MC, and backstage coordination. The institution becomes audible as a network of judgments rather than a static hierarchy.

## 8.5 E25 — microphone test comes before the fairy tale

The finale begins with `ワンツゥ / テステス` before the Cinderella narration (`CG-CIND-0020`). This is one of the cleanest full-series formal statements:

> **technical production does not demystify the fairy tale; it is what lets the fairy tale become repeatable public experience.**

## 8.6 E26 — ordinary set/workplace sound after climax

The special's productions, rooms, shoots, games, and objects restore mundane professional/social acoustics after the finale. “Magic” now lives alongside routine work rather than replacing it.

### Final audit conclusion

> **CG2015 repeatedly makes infrastructure audible at thresholds—before myth, during breakdown, under contingency, and after climax. The result is a materialist Cinderella grammar in which production labor enables, limits, rescues, and reproduces enchantment rather than disproving it.**

This becomes new claim `CG-INST-0042`.

# 9. Performance as epistemic action

Across the series, performance does more than communicate a conclusion characters already possess. It often becomes a method for **finding out** what is true, usable, wanted, or sustainable.

## E08 — production tests whether Ranko has actually been heard

The completed song retains her gothic symbolic field while putting collaboration inside it (`貴方と手を携え`, `新たな旋律`), so `私の声を聴き取ってくれて` follows an artifact that has already enacted what being heard produced (`CG-PERF-0021`).

## E10 — public performance tests whether unit identity can reorganize space

The street performance makes an audience and solves a practical media problem (`CG-PERF-0023`).

## E11 — the song precedes the name

Asterisk becomes publicly and musically legible before the quiet office ratification and final administrative naming. The institution does not define the completed relation in advance.

## E13 — recovered performance tests whether recognition can be interpreted differently

Mio does not receive a magically perfect crowd. She chooses the present audience and then experiences response under a changed reference frame (`CG-PERF-0028`).

## E15 — Kaede's performance is evidence in a policy dispute

Her chosen small venue does not merely accompany her philosophy; the sequence makes remembered audience relation, history, and performance value audiovisually present (`CG-PERF-0030`).

## E19 — collaboration tests whether musical expansion must mean unit betrayal

Riina can sing Asterisk-associated material with Natsuki without the musical crossing logically declaring Asterisk insufficient.

## E20 — `Trancing Pulse` is an experiment before a decision

Nao and Karen sing first; Rin listens; later she joins. Producer remains outside the room. Rin's eventual `分からないから / それを確かめたい` follows musical evidence she has personally experienced (`CG-PERF-0039`).

## E21 — acting creates perspective-taking capacity

The crucial performed object is spoken theater rather than idol song. Mio's professional acting work becomes practice in inhabiting another perspective before she can answer Rin relationally.

## E24 — return to stage tests possibility before certainty

Uzuki cannot first prove a unique essence and then perform. She chooses to re-enter, passes through silence, speaks, and only then sings (`CG-VOICE-0035`, `CG-PERF-0047`).

## E25 — performers may exceed adult theories

The Ball is not merely evidence that Producer was right. Mishiro and Producer remain philosophically distinct, while the live field gives both adults evidence they cannot completely contain.

### Final audit conclusion

> **Performance in CG2015 is often epistemic: it creates conditions under which performers, peers, producers, institutions, and audiences can learn something that could not be known with equal force in advance.**

This conclusion is routed primarily to specialist Document 07. It is not a license to treat every lyric as revelation.

# 10. Performed vocal states: maturation expands function rather than normalizing voice

The complete sound record strongly verifies the longitudinal voice proposition.

Examples include:

- Mio's E03 silence and fragmented open-mic speech against her later capacity to MC, lead, act, and synthesize group positions;
- Ranko's E08 theatrical → direct → theatrical register movement without fake/real hierarchy;
- Minami's E12 peer-requested `せーのっ` as temporal anchoring;
- Nana's E16 tired private speech, constrained externally imposed “plain Nana,” and fluent self-owned Usamin register (`CG-VOICE-0023`);
- Rika's E17 commanded rehearsal delivery versus fluent self-authored final broadcast (`CG-VOICE-0024`);
- Chieri's E18 growth into longer outward-directed speech while nervousness remains (`CG-VOICE-0025`);
- Kirari's serious self-explanation remaining inside `ハピハピ` rather than becoming de-stylized (`CG-VOICE-0026`);
- Riina's E19 movement from defensive knowledge-performance toward candid novice-practice language;
- Ranko's E21 shift from ornate framing into plain testimony when communicative transparency requires it;
- Uzuki's E22–E24 transformation of `頑張ります` from recursive defensive closure into a phrase with a chosen present-tense referent (`CG-VOICE-0035`).

The series repeatedly rejects this ladder:

> stylized → plain → authentic.

It instead supports:

> **established voice → wider pragmatic repertoire.**

A mature performer may become capable of using:

- private speech;
- persona speech;
- MC projection;
- relational directness;
- professional explanation;
- hesitation;
- song;
- silence;

without one register invalidating the others.

This becomes new claim `CG-VOICE-0037` and should govern specialist Document 11.

# 11. The full-series musical bookend: `お願い！シンデレラ`

The strongest genuinely new claim produced by this final audit is the E01 ↔ E25 relation.

## 11.1 E01

The first episode moves from fairy-tale narration into `お願い！シンデレラ` at approximately 00:51. The transition converts suspended piano-led dream language into denser, pulsed, embodied public performance with singing bodies and audience (`CG-PERF-0014`). The song already places dream/magic language beside accumulation and `リアルなスキル`.

At that point, the central girls have not yet formed the relationships, units, competencies, institutions, and self-descriptions through which the series will make “Cinderella” meaningful.

The song is encountered as **a public world of possibility**.

## 11.2 E25

Near the end, narration progresses through:

> `私達は夢をみてる`  
> `私達は夢を生きてる`  
> `私達は夢を信じている`

and culminates in:

> `こんにちはシンデレラガールズです`

Then `お願い！シンデレラ` enters with:

> `夢は夢で終われない`  
> `動き始めてる輝く日のために`

(`E25-T23:19.04-24:01.98`, `E25-ASS0379-0386`, `E25-CS49`).

The same broad franchise/title-song language now occupies a radically different dramatic position.

The series has moved from:

> **Cinderella as spectacle encountered before formation**

to:

> **Cinderella as a plural public identity inhabited after formation, differentiation, rupture, institutional conflict, uncertainty, and return.**

Yet the final lyric still emphasizes movement toward another shining day rather than completed arrival.

### Final audit conclusion

> **`お願い！シンデレラ` forms a full-mainline musical bookend: E01 uses it to turn narrated possibility into embodied idol spectacle; E25 returns to it only after `夢をみてる → 夢を生きてる → 夢を信じている → シンデレラガールズです`, converting the once-observed Cinderella world into an inhabited plural identity while preserving forward motion rather than terminal arrival.**

This becomes new claim `CG-PERF-0055`.

The audit does **not** claim that every arrangement, cut, or performance version is identical; the claim concerns title-song recurrence and dramatic placement.

## 12.2 Important qualifications

### Qualification A — no universal silence symbol

Any future synthesis language implying that “silence means X” should be rejected. Silence is a recurring operation with context-specific functions.

### Qualification B — music does not generally cause courage

Where action precedes non-diegetic song (especially E18 and E24), the music must be described as framing/sustaining/recontextualizing, not causing the decision.

### Qualification C — audience loudness is not recognition

Recognition requires addressability and interpretability. E06 already proves that audible recognition can exist while personal success collapses; E13 proves that a smaller crowd can become more usable when it is interpreted proportionately.

### Qualification D — trans-diegetic continuation is formal, not omniscient

A performance established in-world can continue over remembered or backstage scenes without making every lyric literal interior narration.

### Qualification E — recurring songs are elastic

Recurrence should be analyzed through changing placement and social configuration, not by assigning one fixed symbol to the song.

# 16. Final bounded-audit synthesis

The entire animated corpus can now be described sonically without collapsing it into “music represents feelings.”

At the beginning, professional sound is often something the girls enter:

- a fairy-tale performance already exists;
- an experienced idol supplies the stage grammar;
- a producer offers a possibility;
- a song can carry a person from public invitation into private deliberation.

The first cour progressively teaches the cast and the audience to distinguish:

- structured performance from spontaneous speech;
- recognition from one's ability to interpret recognition;
- public musical identity from private comfort;
- individual vocabulary from shared unit form;
- togetherness as an idea from togetherness as counted bodily time;
- spectacle from the infrastructure required to sustain it.

The second cour then asks what those capacities do under pressure.

Sound becomes a means to test:

- whether persona can remain self-authored under institutional simplification;
- whether a new route can coexist with an old relation;
- whether acting can produce perspective;
- whether a public system can remain functional while a private self-definition fails;
- whether a person can reclaim speech without somebody else supplying it;
- whether adult theories can remain answerable to performers who exceed them.

By E25, the series can begin its finale with a microphone check, perform fairy-tale language without denying the labor underneath it, carry first-cour common time into `M@GIC☆`, refuse to define magic finally, and close with the title song as **renewed movement**.

E26 then demonstrates that the songs no longer need to hold every minute of life. The girls can work, joke, experiment, misunderstand one another, revisit rooms, and continue. The large musical grammar survives, but ordinary life fills the intervals.

The mature sonic thesis is therefore:

> **The series begins with girls entering sounds and structures that already know how an idol should move. It ends with a plural professional world in which performers can make, revise, borrow, cross, interrupt, inhabit, and carry those structures themselves. Music does not erase difference; at its most important moments, it gives difference a time in which it can become relation. Silence does not reveal one hidden truth; it creates or exposes the conditions under which somebody must speak, listen, repair, or wait. Audiences do not bestow personhood; they become increasingly legible partners in an exchange. Infrastructure does not dispel magic; it is one of the things that makes magic durable enough to survive failure.**

This is the canonical audiovisual substrate for mature specialist synthesis.

**The final full-series synthesis has not begun.**
