# THE IDOLM@STER CINDERELLA GIRLS — Episode 14
## Retrospective Music and Sound Audit
### Achieved social continuity meets institutional interruption

## Scope

Episode 14 is an anti-epilogue.

The canonical sequential reading already established that the first cour's achievements remain real while the governing problem changes: Cinderella Project has become a lived professional/social form, but that form exists inside a corporation whose executive authority can reorganize or abolish projects.

The v2 sound audit strengthens that transition in two places.

First, Riina's encounter with Natsuki turns “rock” from a mainly verbal/aspirational category into an **audible practiced object**. The scene does not expose Riina as fraudulent; it gives her an external embodied standard that her sincere aspiration has not yet reached.

Second, Episode 14's late ending-song field warmly carries ordinary social belonging, Rin's widening peer network, and lyrics about linked hands, stepping forward and collective arrival. The music then **ends** before Mishiro's final meeting. The episode acoustically resets into a sparse executive room where `白紙に戻す` arrives without the relational song carrying through it.

That contrast becomes crucial when the same ending-song material returns differently in Episode 15.

No E15 semantic evidence is used in the E14-specific claim below. The E14→E15 recurrence is reserved for the E15/batch addendum.

The important retrospective caution is that this does not invalidate the materialist first-cour reading. E13 has already made the dream materially real through labor, infrastructure, contingency, audience response, and collective performance.

# III. Natsuki makes Riina's aspirational category audible

Riina first appears practicing:

> `新しいコード覚えたから 練習してるんだ`  
> `結構難しくてさ`

Natsuki notices the guitar, asks to borrow it, notes that she normally plays left-handed, and then performs a short guitar passage before returning it.

The subtitle track goes deliberately sparse during the actual demonstration. Visually, Natsuki occupies the instrument rather than talking about it; the program audio carries the guitar performance itself.

Afterward:

> `サンキュー あんたの相棒クールだな`

Riina immediately introduces herself:

> `私 多田李衣菜`  
> `ロックなアイドル目指してるんだ`

and Natsuki answers:

> `ロックなアイドルか じゃ私と一緒だな`

Locators:

- `E14-AUDIO15:01.30-16:01.77`
- `E14-ASS0290-0308`
- `E14-FRAME_000789`
- `E14-FRAME_000800`
- `E14-FRAME_000822`
- `E14-CS21-22`

This matters because E04 and E11 had already established that Riina's rock identity is sincere but aspirationally ahead of her expertise.

E14 does not resolve that tension by humiliating her.

Instead, it gives the category an **external audible embodiment**.

Natsuki's rock relation is not primarily announced through slogans in this scene. It appears as:

- a learned chord;
- instrumental familiarity;
- confident handling of someone else's guitar;
- and a short competent demonstration.

Riina therefore encounters a version of the identity she wants that has become practiced enough to sound through an instrument.

The comparison sharpens rather than invalidates her aspiration.

The relevant developmental distinction becomes:

> **sincere self-authorship can precede expertise, but growth eventually requires contact with practices that resist purely verbal self-definition.**

# VIII. Episode-14 sonic conclusion

Episode 14 sounds like a world that has genuinely become inhabited.

The girls can:

- joke;
- widen friendships;
- practice;
- encounter peers with different expertise;
- and move through 346 as people who now belong there.

That ordinary continuity is precisely why the final reset order matters.

The episode does not prove that Mishiro can erase what happened in Cour 1.

It proves something more institutionally difficult:

> **what the performers have become and what the corporation can formally reorganize are not identical objects.**

The sound design marks the boundary.

First the relational world sings.

Then the song stops.

Then executive authority speaks.
