# THE IDOLM@STER CINDERELLA GIRLS — Episode 4
## Retrospective Music and Sound Audit
### The live becomes media: recorded sound, Ranko's “angel voice,” and the return of 「夕映えプレゼント」

## Scope

Episode 4 is formally about mediated identity, so its sound deserves the same attention already given to cameras and editing.

Three sonic structures materially sharpen the canonical reading:

1. the cold open's reduced/mediated return of `TOKIMEKIエスカレート`;
2. Ranko's use of an ordinary 5 p.m. public announcement as `天使の声` — “the voice of angels”;
3. the return of `夕映えプレゼント` immediately after the CD-debut/unit announcement.

Together they produce one governing refinement:

> **Episode 4 does not merely film identities. It shows that sound itself can be recorded, reframed, decoded and authored.**

# I. E03's live spectacle returns as a media object

Episode 4 opens on the musical material of `TOKIMEKIエスカレート`, visually contained inside camera/viewfinder grammar before the PR-video assignment is introduced.

The significance is not that the series “recaps” Episode 3. It changes the ontological status of the event.

In Episode 3, `TOKIMEKIエスカレート` occupied the full live field:

- large stage;
- audience roar;
- strong continuous mix;
- Mika-centered spectacle;
- bodies synchronized in public space.

At the beginning of Episode 4, that same musical material is acoustically reduced and visually enclosed by recording grammar.

A matched mixed-track comparison of the `DOKIDOKI` lyric segment is directionally clear:

- E03 live segment mean RMS: ~0.077;
- E04 replay/recorded segment mean RMS: ~0.010;
- the E04 segment also carries reduced high-frequency energy;
- chroma comparison strongly supports that the same musical passage is being re-presented rather than a merely similar cue.

These are program-mix measurements, not source-isolated stems, so exact numbers are supplementary. The dramatic fact is much simpler:

> **yesterday's overwhelming live has become something recordable and reproducible.**

Immediately afterward, New Generations is asked to make a PR video.

The transition is therefore exceptionally economical:

> **performer inside media → performer becomes media producer.**

This strengthens Episode 4's camera argument. Professional experience does not remain a pure lived moment. It becomes footage, circulation and future representational labor.

# II. Ranko authors the ordinary soundscape

Ranko's cryptic invitation tells the others to wait at the sacred spring when the “voice of angels” sounds at twilight.

At approximately 14:35, an ordinary civic announcement begins:

> `５時になりました　よい子の皆さん お家に帰る時間です`

The others realize:

> `天使の声ってこれのことだったの？`

Locators:

- `E04-AUDIO14:35.35-14:56.83`
- `E04-ASS0316-0322`
- `E04-FRAME_000730` or nearest retained twilight clock frame
- `E04-FRAME_000735` or nearest fountain frame
- `E04-SCENE03`

The exact retained frame numbers vary slightly in the extracted montage, but the visual sequence clearly moves clock/chime → fountain → Ranko's entrance.

This is an unusually useful sound example for the persona argument.

Ranko does not need the ordinary world to disappear in order to inhabit her theatrical language. She **re-describes an ordinary public sound as aesthetic material**.

The municipal announcement remains what it is. At the same time, within Ranko's authored symbolic system, it can function as “the voice of angels.”

Crucially, her peers can decode it.

That means her persona is neither:

- a private delusion inaccessible to others;
- nor a fake shell that must be stripped away before communication can occur.

It is a translation system placed over shared reality.

> **Ranko authors the soundscape without denying the soundscape's ordinary source.**

This is stronger evidence for persona-as-authorship than a purely visual gothic prop would be, because it shows the process operating on something she did not create: the civic environment itself.

# III. 「夕映えプレゼント」 returns at a changed developmental point

The CD-debut announcement closes at 22:39.09. The ending-song field becomes established immediately afterward, with the first lyric at 22:45.09.

Locators:

- `E04-AUDIO22:39-23:52.24`
- `E04-ASS0495-0509`
- `E04-FRAME_001161`
- `E04-FRAME_001165`
- `E04-FRAME_001166`
- `E04-FRAME_001173`
- `E04-FRAME_001184`
- `E04-FRAME_001188`

This is the same recurring ending song used in Episode 2, but its dramatic position has changed.

In Episode 2, the song begins while the first live opportunity is still being debated emotionally:

- Rin questions how easily the opportunity has been granted;
- Mio narrates confidence;
- the soundtrack sounds hopeful without resolving the disagreement.

In Episode 4, the song begins after a formal institutional statement:

> Minami/Anastasia and Uzuki/Rin/Mio will debut as units on CD.

The song's language about each person having a story, walking as oneself and being able to begin now therefore lands differently.

The first use scores **reachable possibility**.

The return scores **possibility acquiring administrative form**.

The recurrence is important precisely because the lyrics do not change into a homogenizing group anthem when unitization begins. The soundtrack still emphasizes personal story and self-directed movement while the institution groups people into formal units.

That creates a useful tension to preserve:

> **institutional form can organize difference without yet proving that it will preserve difference.**

Episode 4 ends at the moment the experiment becomes real enough to test.

# IV. Sound completes the episode's media thesis

The canonical Episode-4 reading argued that the camera records bounded public versions rather than uncovering one hidden “real self.” The retrospective sound audit adds a parallel proposition:

> **sound also becomes representational material.**

Three forms appear in one episode:

- a prior live is reduced into recorded/replayed media;
- an ordinary civic chime is re-authored into Ranko's symbolic language;
- a recurring ending song changes meaning because its narrative placement changes.

In each case, the raw sound does not possess one immutable meaning.

Meaning comes from:

- framing;
- placement;
- relation;
- memory;
- and the person/institution that uses it.

This is exactly the same anti-essentialist logic the episode applies to visual persona.

# VI. Retrospective claim revisions

## `CG-MEDIA-0008`

Episode 4's opening should be strengthened as an audiovisual media transition: E03's live musical material returns acoustically reduced and visually camera-bounded immediately before the girls become PR-video producers.

## `CG-MOTIF-0017`

Ranko's “angel voice” is the ordinary 5 p.m. public announcement. Her persona therefore operates by aesthetic re-description of shared sound rather than by substituting a private reality for the common one.

## `CG-CIND-0011`

The return of `夕映えプレゼント` marks a developmental change from E02's reachable-but-uncertain opportunity to E04's formal CD-debut/unit beginning. The recurrence preserves individualized-story language even as 346 imposes unit form.

---

# VII. What this audit does not establish

It does not establish that:

- all recorded performance is diminished relative to live performance;
- Ranko's symbolic language is always easy for peers to decode;
- the civic chime has a universal series-wide symbolic meaning;
- recurring ending-song placement proves management will respect individual autonomy;
- unit formation is already fully performer-authored at Episode 4.

The safe E04 sound conclusion is:

> **Episode 4 extends its anti-essentialist media argument into sound: lived performance can become footage, ordinary sound can become authored symbol, and the same song can acquire new dramatic meaning as professional possibility becomes institutional form.**
