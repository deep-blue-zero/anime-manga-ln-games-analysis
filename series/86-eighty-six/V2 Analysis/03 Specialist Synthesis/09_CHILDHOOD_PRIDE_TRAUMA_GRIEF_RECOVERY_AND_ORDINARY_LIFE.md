# 09 — Childhood, Pride, Trauma, Grief, Recovery, and Ordinary Life

## Scope and authority note

This document is the primary Phase-6 home for *86*'s developmental argument: child soldierhood, prematurely acquired competence, interrupted schooling and family life, pride, wounds, grief, survivor guilt, ordinary adolescence, food, sleep, hobbies, art, fashion, play, embarrassment, romance, education, rest, and the question of what recovery can mean when there is no untouched prewar self to restore.

It does **not** diagnose characters clinically unless the novels themselves provide enough behavioral evidence to justify a descriptive claim. Terms such as trauma, dissociation, grief, guilt, hypervigilance, avoidance, or self-instrumentalization are used analytically and behaviorally rather than as remote medical diagnoses. The governing method is to describe what the characters do, fear, remember, conceal, seek, and refuse before assigning a psychological abstraction.

The central question is:

> **What can recovery mean for young people who became extraordinarily competent at war before receiving ordinary opportunities to develop outside it?**

The answer cannot be a return to childhood. The children they were have been changed by bereavement, violence, responsibility, deprivation, and time. Nor can the answer be compulsory civilianity. Military vocation may remain meaningful. Pride may remain real. The dead may remain loved. The decisive issue is whether those histories remain **one part of a widening life** or become the only terms under which the person is allowed to exist.

The V14 boundary remains open. The corpus has demonstrated substantial developmental expansion, but it has not shown postwar life. It cannot yet establish what happens when crisis stops supplying common work, repeated necessity, or an externally imposed reason to continue becoming.

---

# I. Executive thesis

The young soldiers of *86* are neither ordinary children temporarily wearing military roles nor miniature adults whose combat competence has erased childhood.

They are **asymmetrically developed adolescents**.

War has accelerated some capacities to extraordinary levels:

- tactical judgment;
- threat recognition;
- weapons maintenance;
- command responsibility;
- casualty processing;
- emotional suppression;
- practical self-sufficiency;
- rapid decision under lethal uncertainty.

The same history has narrowed or disrupted others:

- sustained schooling;
- safe experimentation;
- family continuity;
- ordinary dependence on adults;
- privacy;
- vocation exploration;
- transport and consumer habits;
- long-horizon planning;
- nonlethal failure;
- the expectation that a birthday, school term, festival, relationship, or hobby will recur.

The mature developmental thesis is therefore:

> **Recovery in *86* is not restoration of an untouched childhood. It is the construction of enough material, relational, bodily, and temporal room that people whose early lives were organized around survival can add desires, skills, pleasures, roles, memories, obligations, and futures without being required to disown what kept them alive.**

This formulation changes how pride, trauma, grief, and ordinary life should be read.

**Pride** begins as anti-dehumanization. It protects dignity when San Magnolia says the Eighty-Six are livestock and machine parts. The danger is not `誇り` itself but `誇りしか`: pride becoming the only legitimate ground of worth [V03-L012; V08-L033].

**Trauma** does not disappear when recovery becomes real. V14's Rei material explicitly allows renewed pain to coexist with changed interpretation, love, and future choice [V14-L192–V14-L199]. A wound recurring is not proof that the person secretly failed to heal.

**Grief** does not require guilt. Kurena's grief for Rito is painful precisely after Theo rejects the claim that the death proves her failure [V14-L029–V14-L031]. The dead may be remembered without turning the living into permanent custodians of the dead person's endpoint.

**Ordinary life** does not begin in Giad. Volume 1 already insists that life contains sweets and coffee [V01-L007], while Alter.1 preserves birthdays, jokes, food, mending, animals, songs, teasing, and curiosity under Sector conditions. Giad's contribution is not to make the Eighty-Six human. It makes more of their humanity **repeatable**.

This gives ordinary life a stronger function than relief between battles. A meal, lesson, dance, sketch, book, birthday, festival, shopping trip, period of sleep, embarrassing crush, or stupid game can be evidence that time is no longer entirely terminal. Ordinary practices become developmental infrastructure because they let a person try something, dislike it, return to it, fail safely, acquire a skill, become embarrassed, form a preference, and expect another occasion.

The deepest positive contrast is therefore not:

> war versus peace.

It is:

> **terminal time versus repeatable time.**

The Sector can contain laughter and pleasure while still making long-horizon planning irrational. Giad can provide school and leisure while still paternalistically deciding what a recovered child ought to become. V13 can destroy an ordinary tomorrow without killing the capacity to remember it. V14 can preserve education, Christmas ornaments, coffee, songs, and recipes while the war remains existential.

The series' developmental ethic is consequently not about becoming normal. It is about acquiring **more permissible ways to remain alive**.

---

# II. Method: trauma is history embodied, not a master explanation

The word *trauma* is useful only if it does not become an interpretive solvent that explains every choice.

The Five's reenlistment cannot be reduced to trauma merely because military life began under coercion. Their pride cannot be called pathological merely because it emerged inside genocide. Shin's restraint cannot automatically be called emotional suppression. Kurena's love cannot be dismissed as compensation. Anju's composure cannot be assumed to conceal one simple wound. Raiden's stability cannot be treated as proof that he is less affected. Theo's post-amputation role cannot be explained only as inability to leave soldierhood.

The corpus instead supports a layered developmental method.

First, identify the **historical condition**: child soldiering, racial persecution, bereavement, institutional abandonment, combat exposure, bodily injury, forced self-reliance, or disrupted family life.

Second, identify the **present behavior**: avoidance, concealment, fixation, crying, anger, guilt, inability to imagine a future, hyper-competence, dependence, reluctance to rely on adults, or need to remain useful.

Third, identify **counterevidence**. Does the same person laugh, learn, refuse, love, rest, ask for help, change roles, enjoy ordinary life, or maintain long commitments? If so, the person cannot be reduced to the wound.

Fourth, distinguish **origin from authority**. A role can originate in harm and still become meaningful. Undertaker is historically assembled through trauma, promise, squad dependence, and Shin's own choices; that does not make it fake. Conversely, because a role became meaningful does not make the trauma necessary or good [V10-L059–V10-L060].

Fifth, test whether the history has become **exhaustive foreclosure**. The question is not whether a wound remains. It is whether the wound controls the only legitimate answer to life.

This is why V14's formulation around Rei is so important. `傷を抉られたら、痛いのは当然です` permits a simple and humane proposition: old injury may still hurt because it was real. Pain persistence does not establish moral failure, secret unforgiveness, or developmental stasis [V14-L192–V14-L199].

The document therefore prefers terms such as **wound persistence**, **future narrowing**, **self-instrumentalization**, **grief**, **misassigned guilt**, **role enclosure**, and **developmental deprivation** when those are more precise than a blanket clinical label.

---

# III. Child soldierhood: accelerated competence and deprived rehearsal

The most important developmental mistake in reading *86* is to use competence as a proxy for adulthood.

A teenager who can command a platoon, repair a weapon, perform triage, kill under fire, or make decisions that affect hundreds of lives has real competence. Adults who ignore that competence are patronizing. But competence in one domain does not supply the developmental experiences missing from another.

The Eighty-Six have enormous practice making decisions where error means death. They have much less practice with errors whose consequences are embarrassment, wasted money, a bad grade, an awkward date, a hobby abandoned after three weeks, or discovering that a subject is simply boring.

This asymmetry explains why ordinary institutions matter.

In Volume 2, the Five rapidly demonstrate that they are not incapable of civilian activity. The Phase-5 ordinary-life control records Raiden doing courier work, Anju taking cooking lessons, Theo sketching, Kurena window-shopping, and Shin visiting libraries and museums within a month of rescue [T06-E004; V02 item/xhtml/p-006.xhtml ¶85]. Their civilian competence directly falsifies the claim that they know only war.

Yet Ernst's wish to return them to `ただの子供` is still inadequate [V02-L025]. There is no untouched state available. A child who has buried comrades, killed enemies, led units, and lived under exterminatory policy cannot be restored by giving them a safe bedroom and forbidding military identity.

The problem is best called **developmental rehearsal**.

Children and adolescents normally encounter possible adult lives through repeated, low-stakes exposure: family occupations, teachers, public institutions, transit, clubs, hobbies, school subjects, part-time work, media, friendships, mistakes, and observing adults who occupy different roles. The Eighty-Six have had far more rehearsal for battlefield adulthood than for civilian adulthood.

Volume 7's explicit `まだ大人になりきらない少年` framing matters because it appears after extraordinary competence has already been established. The novels refuse to make maturity a one-axis scale. They can be reliable officers and still be inexperienced teenagers.

The ethical implication is two-sided:

> **Adults must take young people's demonstrated judgment seriously without using that judgment as permission to stop building safer conditions.**

This is why Theo's later refusal to let younger people reproduce his own exposure matters beyond personal sympathy. His experience does not prove that early competence is admirable training. It gives him reason to oppose the system that made such competence necessary.

# IV. Ordinary life before rescue: humanity under terminal time

The Sector is not socially empty.

That point must be established before any analysis of recovery, because otherwise later civilian scenes can quietly reproduce San Magnolia's own dehumanizing premise: that the Eighty-Six become fully human only after entering a better polity.

Volume 1 already gives the compact principle `人生には菓子とかコーヒーとか` [V01-L007]. The line is almost trivial, which is precisely why it matters. Life contains sweets and coffee. It also contains jokes, music, art, cooking, irritation, flirting, basketball, maintenance chatter, books, animals, personal habits, and social roles that are not reducible to the war.

Theo's V01 attack on Lena begins from particularity: `名前さえ、一回だって訊いたことがない` [V01-L011]. Abstract pity is insufficient because actual ordinary life belongs to named people. Once the group is recognized as particular, their mundane practices stop looking like decorative characterization and become evidence that the Republic's administrative category was false from the beginning.

Alter.1 strengthens this point because its low-pressure stories are deliberately built around `日常`. Birthdays, gifts, cooking failures, coffee, mending, teasing, folklore, cats, and small celebrations exist before rescue. The anthology does not prove that the Sector was livable. It proves that **ordinary capacities survive inside unlivable conditions**.

The distinction is temporal.

A group awaiting near-certain death can still enjoy tonight's coffee. What the Sector destroys is the expectation that the pleasure will become a stable practice across years. Birthdays become administratively useless. Careers become irrational to plan. Family formation becomes structurally foreclosed. Travel becomes fantasy. A hobby can exist, but not necessarily a life organized around having time for it.

This is why T06's strongest concept is **repeatable time**.

A one-time pleasure before expected death is real pleasure. It is not the same developmental structure as believing there will be another birthday, another school term, another festival, another chance to try the recipe, another trip, another relationship stage, another morning after a mistake.

Giad's moral gain is therefore not the invention of humanity. It is the institutional creation of **recurrence**.

# V. Giad after rescue: capability expansion without compulsory normalization

Giad's first developmental challenge is that it can recognize the Five as children and still misunderstand what protecting children requires.

Ernst's instinct is humane. He is appalled by child soldiering and wants to remove the Five from war. The error is that he begins with a desired endpoint: `ただの子供に戻って` [V02-L025]. Frederica's `哀れみの檻` names why this becomes paternalistic. Pity can protect the body while enclosing the future.

The better institutional line is `選べる道はなるべく多い方がいい`: as many paths as possible should be available. The aim is not to choose civilianity for the Five but to make military service one usable path among others.

That is why apparently small infrastructure matters.

The Five receive money, housing, transit, libraries, education, part-time work, museums, shops, cooking classes, medical care, and ordinary social contact. These do not manufacture curiosity or personality. They reduce the cost of expressing capacities already present.

The Alter.1 public-transport story is a precise control. Shin can independently encounter something interesting in a library and decide to investigate it. Curiosity and initiative are already his. What is missing is the behavioral assumption that public transit is an ordinary tool available to him. Infrastructure changes what agency can **reach**.

The same applies to school. Education is valuable not because it converts former child soldiers into proper civilians, but because it expands the set of lives they can understand well enough to choose or reject.

Volume 12 later provides the mature version through Shiden: `わからないから知りたい、学びたい` [V12-L033]. She can encounter unfamiliar knowledge, ask questions, explore it, and eventually decide a field is not for her. A capability has succeeded when it creates an informed option—including the option to say no.

This is why normalization is the wrong metric for recovery. A recovered person does not have to become average. The better question is whether the person has more **usable alternatives**, including the ability to retain an unusual vocation without being trapped inside it.

# VI. Pride: dignity, `しか`, and the difference between scaffold and cage

Eighty-Six pride must be read historically before it is read therapeutically.

Under San Magnolia, pride protects the minimum claim that the state cannot determine what kind of person the condemned soldier is. Names, conduct, refusal to beg, remembered comrades, and unit identity preserve self-authorship under a system designed to reduce people to livestock and processing devices.

T14 therefore rejects the claim that Eighty-Six pride is intrinsically pathological (`T14-C07`). Any analysis that begins by calling pride a trauma symptom repeats the logic of an outsider who sees only damage and not the moral work the identity performed.

The series' critique begins with a smaller word: `しか`.

Volume 3's `戦い抜く誇りしか` marks the dangerous form [V03-L012]. Pride becomes enclosure when it is the only positive content left: no home, no future, no role other than combat, no legitimate self outside the ethic of fighting to the end.

Volume 8 then supplies the explicit revision: `誇りしかないとはもう言わない` [V08-L033]. Shin does not repudiate pride. He rejects exclusivity. The positive alternative includes `誇り以外の懊悩も感情も欲も願いも`: pride alongside grief, desire, fear, love, embarrassment, curiosity, and ordinary wants.

Volume 9 generalizes the problem with `誇り`, `願い`, `絆`, `祈り`, and `呪い`. These are not five fixed moral categories. They are different ways a past, promise, relationship, or identity can bind a person. `呪い` does not simply mean bad attachment. A binding becomes curse-like when it forecloses reinterpretation, refusal, additional identity, or continued belonging after change.

The Milmelkoleo comparison is the group-level warning. Shared wound and heroic function become a `奴隷の首輪` when changing or leaving the role looks like betrayal [V09-L051–V09-L053]. The wound acquires jurisdiction over membership.

This leads to the mature pride rule:

> **Pride is healthy when it supports standing; it becomes dangerous when worth is conditional on remaining the same wounded performer forever.**

T14 adds a necessary counter-control (`T14-C08`). Healthy identity does not have to be maximally plural. One vocation, one love, one promise, or one community may remain central for life. The question is not how many identities a person has. It is whether the central commitment erases all legitimate answer-space if the bearer changes.

# VII. Wounds and recurrence: recovery does not require anesthesia

*86* repeatedly rejects a fantasy in which successful recovery makes the past stop hurting.

The most decisive example is Shin and Rei.

Early development might tempt an analyst to construct a linear sequence: Shin misunderstands Rei, learns Rei loved him, forgives him, gains Lena and a future, and therefore resolves the childhood wound. Volume 14 destroys that neat chronology. The wound is reopened and Shin breaks down violently enough to cling to the absent Lena and cry aloud [V14-L199].

The interpretive mistake would be to say that this proves the earlier growth was false.

Lena's response is more exact: `傷を抉られたら、痛いのは当然です`. If a wound is gouged open, it is natural that it hurts [V14-L192–V14-L199]. Pain persistence is compatible with changed knowledge, affection, forgiveness, and future orientation.

This distinction protects recovery from two opposite distortions.

The first is **purity psychology**: the healed person no longer feels anger, grief, fear, jealousy, bodily panic, or recurring pain.

The second is **trauma totalization**: if an old response reappears, the trauma must still be the person's true organizing identity underneath everything else.

The series supports neither.

A person can have changed while remaining vulnerable to a historically meaningful stimulus. The body and memory do not have to update at the same rate as conscious interpretation. The return of pain can demonstrate how consequential the original injury was without determining what the person now chooses to do with it.

This is why T03 separates `WOUND_PERSISTENCE` from `IDENTITY_ENCLOSURE`. A wound may remain for life. Enclosure occurs only when the wound becomes the exclusive authority over future action.

The developmental standard is therefore not symptom elimination. It is whether the person can experience pain **without the pain monopolizing the only possible next action**.

# VIII. Grief, guilt, and the right not to make loss into self-prosecution

Grief and guilt often arrive together in the series, but the corpus repeatedly separates them.

This matters because child soldiers are especially vulnerable to interpreting survival as responsibility. A unit culture built around mutual protection can make every death look retrospectively preventable: if I had been stronger, faster, more alert, more useful, the other person would still be here.

Kurena's V14 grief over Rito is the clearest late correction. She emphasizes how hard she tried to become stronger after Theo and Shana were gone [V14-L029]. Her pain then seeks an explanatory form: if she improved and still lost Rito, perhaps the improvement was insufficient.

Theo answers by removing the prosecution without removing the loss. It is not Kurena's fault. It is not Rito's fault. The remaining truth is that it is lonely and painful [V14-L029–V14-L031].

That distinction is psychologically and morally important:

> **Grief can become more accurate when guilt is removed.**

The mourner no longer has to prove love by convicting the self.

Volume 11 develops the same grammar at larger scale. Rito can remain unable to forgive and still arrive at `もういいや`: release without absolution. The change is not that the Republic's wrong becomes smaller. It is that continuing to organize one's future around the offender is no longer treated as an obligation to the dead.

The revenge Shepherds provide the negative case. Grievance survives after the original target has become unavailable and seeks new subjects. Memory becomes purpose-capture. The dead do not merely remain remembered; the past claims permanent authority over who must be harmed next.

Document 08 established that the dead can be wronged without being resurrected. Document 09 adds the developmental corollary:

> **The living can owe memory without owing imitation.**

Remembering Daiya does not require Anju to refuse later love. Remembering dead Processors does not require Shin to remain Undertaker forever. Rito can be publicly vindicated without being converted into a clean martyr-symbol. Richard can reject memorialization that would recruit the dead into a heroic story they did not choose.

This is additive grief: the dead remain part of a life whose future is still allowed to acquire new content.

# IX. Small desires: why fried bread matters as much as grand purpose

One of the series' most important developmental corrections is that a future does not need to begin as a vocation.

Shin's early problem is often described as inability to imagine the future. Volume 3 makes that explicit: `あの夜から本当は何も、望めてはいない` [V03-L028]. Yet the series does not answer this emptiness by demanding an inspiring life mission.

Raiden's `死にたくねえ。それだけだ` supplies a minimal positive threshold: simply not wanting to die is already a meaningful preference for continued existence.

Shin's future then develops asymmetrically. At first he can imagine survival for another person before he can imagine it for himself. He may not want to see the sea personally, but he wants to show it to Lena. By Volume 6, `まだそれしか、望めない` explicitly marks a future concentrated in one relation [V06-L040]. Volume 7 changes the grammar again: he wants to see the sea **with** her, while also identifying himself as someone who enjoys ordinary leave [V07-L034].

Volume 9 then supplies the anti-grandiose theory. Annette's fried-bread example treats a tiny appetite as developmentally sufficient: hear that something is good, want to try it, fulfill the desire, discover another desire afterward. A person does not need a total theory of adulthood before being entitled to the next small want.

Theo's post-amputation `でも、死ななくていい` gives the same principle after catastrophic subtraction [V09-L011]. He does not immediately need a replacement identity equal in grandeur to frontline combat. Continued life can precede the answer to what life will become.

The developmental model is iterative:

> **desire → action → experience → revised desire → another occasion.**

This is ordinary life's deepest function in the series. It trains a temporal rhythm in which not every decision has to justify the whole future at once.

# X. Birthdays, festivals, food, and the politics of repeatable time

The birthday chronology is one of the best measures of future capacity in the V2 corpus.

Inside the Sector, birthdays lose practical relevance. Survival is too precarious for the calendar to function normally. Dates are forgotten or administratively erased. Survivors can still use a remembered birthday as an excuse to celebrate whoever remains alive, but recurrence is fragile.

After rescue, records restore dates. Lena proposes celebrations. The Eighty-Six adopt gift-giving partly because it looks enjoyable. Celebrations survive delay, deployment, injury, and catastrophe. Anju's birthday can become attached to a future flower-and-return promise. Theo can participate in birthdays after amputation.

The progression is:

> **survivor excuse → recovered calendar → chosen peer ritual → expected recurrence → ritual that survives disruption and bodily change.**

A birthday is therefore not merely cute characterization. It presupposes another year.

Festivals perform similar work. Lena's V11 insistence that she has not given up on Revolution Festival fireworks remains ordinary and politically significant during catastrophe [V11-L046]. The desire is not strategic. Its very triviality protects a horizon not exhausted by strategic necessity.

Food works on several levels.

At the simplest level, meals create pleasure and social rhythm. At a developmental level, cooking classes, recipes, taste preferences, sweets, bad coffee, and curiosity teach preference and competence. At a political level, V14 recipe circulation makes Eighty-Six particularity publicly legible: these were people who laughed, cooked badly, liked specific foods, and imagined feeding others.

V14's sensory-loss material then prevents food from becoming a sentimental symbol. Shin's capacity to taste and smell is impaired. Ordinary pleasure can be materially lost even when the future remains meaningful [V14-L059–V14-L060]. Theo's response—to accompany him and describe tastes rather than continually testing him—shows how ordinary life can be **reconstructed relationally** without pretending the old body has returned.

Ordinary ritual is thus not proof of healing. It is infrastructure through which life remains available even when healing is incomplete.

# XI. School, study, curiosity, and the right to be inexperienced

School matters in *86* because it creates a domain where ignorance need not be fatal.

That is a profound contrast with the battlefield. In combat, not knowing may kill the person or someone nearby. In education, not knowing can become the beginning of curiosity.

Rito's `楽しいですね、学生というのは` captures the shift [V09-L007]. The significance is not that school turns him into a normal teenager. It is that studenthood makes a different kind of incompleteness legitimate.

Volume 11 broadens `勉強` beyond formal lessons. Books, foolish games, friendship, romantic confusion, and ordinary social experience are all forms of education for adolescents denied developmental rehearsal. The point is not to erase military experience but to prevent military experience from monopolizing competence.

Volume 12 gives the clearest explicit model. Shiden says she wants to know because she does not understand [V12-L033]. The educational environment makes information accessible, permits questions, offers deeper material, and leaves continuation voluntary. When she decides a subject is not for her, the process has not failed. She has acquired enough understanding to reject it knowingly.

This is a major recovery principle:

> **A humane developmental environment allows young people to discover that they are bad at something, bored by something, confused by something, or simply uninterested—without that failure threatening life or belonging.**

Volume 14 makes study an explicit act of resistance to terminal time: `勉強をしましょう。こんな時だからこそ。──戦争が終わる日のために` [V14-L019]. Education during siege is not denial of war. It is refusal to let war own every unit of time.

The study scene also prevents postwar preparation from becoming abstract policy. Learning is something done **now** because a peaceful day is treated as possible enough to prepare for.

# XII. Rest and sleep: recovery requires time that is not immediately useful

A child soldier system weaponizes time as much as bodies.

If every waking period must serve survival, maintenance, command, or readiness, there is little room for desire to emerge independently of necessity. This is why rest becomes more than comfort.

Volume 12 states it directly: `休むのも仕事だと思って` [V12-L006]. The phrase is still framed in work language, which reveals how difficult noninstrumental rest remains to justify. Yet the institutional point is important: rest is recognized as something the system must protect rather than something a useful soldier has to steal.

Medical leave for Lena develops the same principle. The narration links cleared mental space with future dreams, pleasant plans, and love. Rest does not simply restore productivity. It creates cognitive room for thoughts that do not need to solve the immediate crisis.

Alter.1 and later adult observation of the Five asleep reinforce the same point. Extraordinary combatants can look, in sleep, like exactly what they also are: exhausted teenagers.

This does not mean adults should ignore the responsibilities those teenagers actually hold. It means the capacity to function while exhausted must not become evidence that exhaustion is acceptable.

The positive standard is:

> **Competence under deprivation creates an obligation to reduce deprivation, not an excuse to normalize it.**

This principle matters again in V14, where morale goods—coffee, sweets, magazines, clean space, seasonal ornaments, music—become part of sustaining people through siege. Such goods are not militarily sufficient, but they preserve the person who must continue to act.

# XIII. Body, disability, and developmental reconstruction

Document 08 established that disability does not reduce standing. Document 09 asks what bodily change does to development.

Theo's amputation is the clearest case because it closes a future that had been central to peer belonging. `僕はもう、──一緒には戦えないや` names more than functional loss [V08-L056]. He is losing the embodied way he had expected to remain alongside the others.

Volume 9's `僕はもう、戦えない` / `でも、死ななくていい` then separates two propositions that the Sector had fused [V09-L011]: inability to perform the old function and loss of entitlement to continued life.

The moral achievement is not that disability opens a better path. The amputation is real loss. The achievement is that the social world around Theo contains enough support, medicine, prosthetics, technical alternatives, relationships, and time that **loss of function does not become loss of future**.

This is developmental reconstruction rather than restoration.

A prosthetic does not return the exact old body. A technical role does not prove that Theo has been healed. Art did not appear only after injury; he was already drawing. His later possibilities therefore draw upon older traits while creating something new.

Shin provides the counterexample to a smooth reconstruction narrative. By V14 he has multiple reasons to live and a wider future, yet he conceals sensory loss because disclosure threatens frontline identity [V14-L059–V14-L060]. Future orientation and bodily self-instrumentalization coexist (`T14-C20`).

The developmental question is therefore not simply whether a character has found another useful role. It is whether the person can inhabit bodily change **without being forced to prove continued worth through replacement usefulness**.

Theo's open question remains exactly that: can adapted service stay chosen, or will adaptation become a second test that he deserves standing only if he remains useful (`T14-OQ-22`)?

---

# XIV. Support, dependence, and adults who do not disappear behind competence

Forced self-reliance is often mistaken for maturity.

The Five can cook, repair, fight, command, care for one another, and survive with minimal adult support because adults and institutions repeatedly failed them. Those capacities are admirable. Their origin also represents deprivation.

Volume 10 makes this especially clear in Shin's history. Self-sufficiency is an adaptation assembled through repeated loss, not the highest form of adulthood. V10's Gentle World makes the anti-theodicy explicit: `傷なんて、負わなくてよかった` and `おれは死神でいなくてよかった` [V10-L059–V10-L060]. The competence and relationships that emerged afterward remain real; the suffering is denied any claim that it was necessary training.

T09's mature positive model is **interruptible interdependence**. People may depend on others without surrendering judgment. Support is healthy when it can challenge distorted action and then return decision-making to the person rather than becoming permanent guardianship.

This matters particularly for adolescents because the alternatives are easy to polarize:

- paternalism: adults know best and may decide the child's future;
- abandonment: the child is competent, therefore adults no longer owe protection or scaffolding.

The series seeks a middle position.

Ernst is right that children should not be casually consumed by war. He is wrong when protection predetermines their authentic future. Raiden is justified in stopping Shin when judgment collapses, but the intervention is bounded. Lena's V14 neural rescue is justified by acute incapacity and danger, yet its extremity makes it unsuitable as a normal model of care. Theo's concern about Shin's sensory loss becomes healthier when he stops repeatedly testing him and shifts toward accommodation.

The developmental criterion is therefore:

> **Support should expand the young person's capacity to answer, not replace the young person as the author of every answer.**

---

# XV. Romance, sexuality, embarrassment, and ordinary adolescence

Romance in *86* is often analyzed primarily through plot or relationship ethics, but it also has a developmental function.

Young people whose emotional lives were organized around survival gain space for embarrassment, attraction, jealousy, awkwardness, teasing, anticipation, and physical desire that do not need to justify themselves through military utility.

This matters because adolescent sexuality is one of the ordinary domains war does not erase but can compress into strange forms. Attraction and flirtation already exist under Sector conditions. Later school and leave give those feelings more room to become recurring social experiences rather than brief intensities under expected death.

The developmental value is not that romance completes adulthood. The series repeatedly rejects that simplification. Shin's future does not become healthy merely because Lena becomes central. Kurena's unreturned love does not make her development a failed romance plot. Anju's attachment to Dustin does not require Daiya to become a discarded stage. Frederica's growth is not measured by romance at all.

Instead, romantic life contributes to **plural futurity**.

A crush can be embarrassing rather than fatal. A promise can point to a festival or return. A kiss can become something to argue about. Unreturned love can remain meaningful without granting entitlement. A new attachment can coexist with grief for the dead.

This is why V11's command that the adolescents worry about romance belongs in the same developmental field as books and games. These are practices through which a person learns what wanting another person means when the desire is not immediately subordinated to survival.

The same caution from T14 applies here: strong attachment is not automatically enclosure. Love may become central and lifelong. It becomes dangerous only when the relationship claims permanent jurisdiction over the other's identity, future, or right to change.

---

# XVI. The Gentle World: development after harm does not justify harm

Volume 10's Gentle World is indispensable because long-form character development creates a powerful interpretive temptation: if the characters become admirable, loving, capable people through suffering, perhaps the suffering was somehow constitutive or necessary.

The text refuses that theodicy.

`傷なんて、負わなくてよかった` [V10-L059] is not a denial of the actual bonds created afterward. It says the wound did not need to happen.

`おれは死神でいなくてよかった` [V10-L060] does not prove Undertaker is false. It proves Undertaker was contingent. Shin could have been someone else and still possessed a life worth living.

This is critical to any developmental synthesis of child soldiers.

The Eighty-Six may possess extraordinary competence because they survived the Sector. That competence does not retroactively make the Sector educationally valuable. They may love comrades they met only because of genocide. That love does not justify the genocide. Shin may value who he has become. That does not mean he ought to celebrate every wound required to produce the present configuration.

The Gentle World protects a moral asymmetry:

> **Meaning can be made from harm without turning harm into a necessary source of meaning.**

This also prevents recovery from becoming a demand to reject the actual self. One can believe the child should never have been wounded while still refusing the fantasy of erasing all people, memories, and commitments that now constitute the adult future.

Development is not vindication of suffering. It is what the living do with a history they were not obligated to receive.

# XVII. Volume 11–12: ordinary life inside renewed catastrophe

The later corpus becomes more, not less, insistent on ordinary development as the strategic situation worsens.

Volume 11 is especially important because it refuses the familiar narrative sequence in which ordinary adolescence is postponed until after the crisis. School, books, stupid games, friendship, romance, festivals, and future travel remain legitimate topics while catastrophe is already underway.

Lena's Revolution Festival fireworks provide a clean example [V11-L046]. The desire is not a reward for winning. It is a claim that the future contains specific occasions worth reaching.

Theo's post-amputation `どうなるのかな、これから` remains unanswered by a grand new mission. The open question itself becomes legitimate. Not knowing what comes next is allowed to replace terminal certainty.

Volume 12 then formalizes ordinary development as capability and self-government.

Shiden's curiosity [V12-L033], educational pacing, simplified materials for low-literacy soldiers, leave, rest, cooking, films, reading, moon-viewing, coffee, cake, riding, and family contact all belong to the same architecture. These activities widen the repertoire from which a future can be chosen.

The political counterexample is equally important. Former serfs and authoritarian movements show that being given formal choice does not automatically make choice comfortable. People can experience judgment as burden and prefer obedience. Education can enable agency but cannot guarantee its use.

For child-soldier recovery, the lesson is precise:

> **A person needs both permission to choose and enough practice choosing that freedom is not merely another impossible task imposed from above.**

This is why recovery cannot be measured by compliance with an adult's preferred healthy lifestyle. Supported self-government includes the right to learn, reject, revise, rest, reenlist, change roles, or remain uncertain.

---

# XVIII. Volume 13: the stolen ordinary tomorrow

Volume 13 makes childhood deprivation politically explicit through Chitri and the Fawns.

The prologue begins with repetition: school every day, play afterward, go home, `また明日ね` [V13-L003–V13-L004]. The ordinary future is not spectacular. Its value lies in recurrence.

The Fawn program then destroys exactly that structure. A girl's biological condition, military utility, and inherited position are allowed to close school, family, travel, adulthood, and ordinary social time in advance.

Chitri's later desire list is correspondingly mundane: parents, teacher, friends, school, travel, family, adulthood, home. The extremity of her weaponization is measured by how ordinary the stolen alternatives are.

This is one reason Document 08's anti-disposability argument and Document 09's developmental argument converge. The wrong is not only that a dangerous body may be killed. It is that an adolescent person is denied the right to discover what else the body, mind, and life might become.

Volume 13 also gives a mature account of adulthood. Choices increasingly affect people for whom one bears responsibility. That is real moral development. But responsibility does not mean personal desire becomes illegitimate. An adult is not someone who stops wanting; adulthood changes how wants must be weighed against commitments.

The final minefield then converts future into geography. Plans, loves, and identities can remain psychologically present while institutions physically remove the route home [V13-L121–V13-L127]. A future therefore needs more than imagination. It needs a material path through which return, refusal, rest, and exit are possible.

The developmental principle is:

> **Futurity is infrastructural.**

A person may know what they want and still be unable to inhabit it when roads, borders, institutions, bodies, or political categories make the future unreachable.

# XIX. Volume 14: study, Christmas, recipes, Rito, and the refusal of terminal time

Volume 14 is the strongest current test because political and military collapse might seem to make ordinary life absurd. The text insists on the opposite.

Christmas ornaments, children exploring an empty city, coffee, sweets, magazines, clean rooms, jokes, music, and seasonal ritual continue under siege [V14-L013–V14-L018]. `笑えなくなったら負け` returns as a survival doctrine [V14-L017]. Laughter is not proof that danger is small. It is refusal to let danger determine the only available mode of being.

Study becomes explicitly postwar-directed: `勉強をしましょう。こんな時だからこそ。──戦争が終わる日のために` [V14-L019]. The phrase turns education into resistance to terminal time. War may dominate the present, but it is denied ownership of the future calendar.

Rito then gives this thesis its most painful negative form.

He had learned to do things he previously could not. He wanted to study the sea and whales. He wanted to see more because `何をしてもいい` [V14-L090]. His death is therefore not only subtraction of a soldier or friend. It closes an actively expanding option space.

This makes public memory ethically difficult. A clean martyr narrative would preserve the fact of death while erasing the developmental particularity of the life that had begun to widen. Recipes, jokes, preferences, travel interests, and remembered incompetences resist that flattening.

The same volume keeps recovery contradictory.

Shin chooses the future over the reopened Rei wound and still collapses into grief [V14-L192–V14-L199]. He has multiple reasons to live and still conceals sensory loss [V14-L059–V14-L060]. Raiden assumes responsibility and discovers that his prior stability depended partly on Shin's restraint [V14-L081–V14-L083]. Kurena has deliberately grown stronger and still cannot save Rito [V14-L029]. Anju continues to act while visibly afraid. Frederica brings childish Christmas objects into a world where her dynastic body is again becoming politically consequential.

None of these contradictions invalidate development.

They define it.

> **Recovery is not the disappearance of fear, grief, dependence, pain, or inherited identity. It is the increasing capacity to experience those things without allowing any one of them to become the only permitted future.**

# XX. Major-character developmental profiles

The Phase-5 ordinary/climactic matrix is retained here as a control against reading characters only through breakdowns or battlefield peaks. For the V02 Five, the shared ordinary montage is the locked direct-source control `T06-E004` at V02 `item/xhtml/p-006.xhtml ¶85`; the individual activity differs by character.

| Character | Ordinary/developmental control | Crisis/climactic control | Developmental use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shin** | libraries/museums after rescue (`T06-E004`) | Rei wound and crying [V14-L199] | ordinary curiosity and severe recurrence coexist |
| **Lena** | dance/social synchronization [V07-L044] | maximum Para-RAID intervention [V14-L176] | adolescence/intimacy coexist with command and self-spending |
| **Raiden** | courier work (`T06-E004`) | leadership burden / Shin as moral brake [V14-L081] | competence is both personal capacity and relationally supported labor |
| **Theo** | sketching (`T06-E004`) | recognition that old combat-togetherness is over [V08-L056] | art predates disability; function loss does not create personhood |

## 1. Shin — from terminal competence to future-bearing plurality

Shin's ordinary control is not a late romantic scene but the V02 civilian interval: libraries and museums after rescue [T06-E004; V02 item/xhtml/p-006.xhtml ¶85]. His crisis control at the current boundary is the V14 breakdown around Rei [V14-L199]. The pair is useful because neither scene cancels the other.

His development involves several partially independent movements:

- from little positive future desire [V03-L028] to relational and then broader futurity;
- from `誇りしか` logic toward plural identity [V08-L033];
- from solitary Undertaker to role revision and distributed dependence;
- from assuming the battlefield is the only place available to imagining research and other trajectories;
- from inability to locate ordinary wants to genuine enjoyment;
- from treating others' return as his responsibility to accepting that others may also carry him.

Yet V14 leaves self-instrumentalization active. Shin can want life and still hide bodily damage to preserve frontline usefulness. His postwar vocation and Undertaker identity remain open (`T14-OQ-17`, `T14-OQ-18`).

The mature claim is therefore not that Shin is healed. It is that **death and usefulness no longer possess exclusive jurisdiction over what his life can mean**.

## 2. Raiden — competence as care, and the danger of becoming only the stabilizer

Raiden's V02 courier work is the ordinary control [T06-E004]. It demonstrates immediate civilian adaptability. His V14 leadership crisis supplies the climactic control: he realizes that he could indulge anger partly because he trusted Shin to stop him [V14-L081–V14-L083].

Raiden's developmental problem is easy to miss precisely because he appears stable. He cooks, mends, mediates, corrects, leads, and makes other people's lives more manageable. Those capacities can become a role enclosure of their own.

V14 reveals that the stabilizer also needs to be stabilizable. His future cannot be defined only by returning Shin, protecting the group, or holding command together.

The open question is therefore positive rather than diagnostic: **what does Raiden want when nobody needs him to be the reliable one?** (`T14-OQ-21`).

## 3. Theo — art, anger, bodily loss, and a future not justified by usefulness

Theo's sketching after rescue provides an ordinary control that predates disability [T06-E004]. This prevents the later artistic/technical future from looking like a consolation identity created after amputation.

His V08 `僕はもう、──一緒には戦えないや` is the bodily rupture [V08-L056]. V09 then separates inability to fight from the right to live [V09-L011].

Theo's anger also matures. V01's attack on Lena can be cruel but correctly identifies abstract sympathy that has not learned names. Later he becomes capable of changing the form of care when Shin says constant testing is intrusive. His developmental strength is therefore not merely blunt honesty; it is increasing respect for another person's boundary.

The unresolved risk is that technical/adapted service becomes a second usefulness test. Theo must be allowed to choose military contribution without his continued belonging depending on proving that he can still contribute (`T14-OQ-22`).

## 4. Kurena — usefulness, failed protection, and independent worth

Kurena's ordinary control is window-shopping after rescue [T06-E004]. It is deliberately unheroic: fashion, looking, wanting, adolescent preference. Her V14 crisis is the grief over Rito despite deliberate efforts to become stronger [V14-L029].

Her developmental wound is not reducible to unreturned love for Shin. A deeper fear concerns powerlessness and failed usefulness. If worth is tied to being able to protect the people she loves, then every death becomes evidence against the self.

Theo's V14 correction matters because it removes causal guilt without demanding that grief disappear [V14-L029–V14-L031].

Kurena's open frontier is whether she can build durable worth that does not depend on successful protection, romantic reciprocity, or being useful to loved people (`T14-OQ-23`).

## 5. Anju — gentleness as practice, not absence of wound

Anju's V02 cooking class is the ordinary control [T06-E004]. Her V14 crisis control is fear acknowledged while continuing to act. The pairing rejects the idea that calm competence proves emotional untouchedness.

Her relationship to Daiya is the most important grief control. Later attachment does not need to delete earlier love. `忘れないまま...好きになってもいい` provides the additive-memory principle. Dustin can enter a future without Daiya becoming a hurdle that must be narratively defeated.

Anju's kindness should likewise not be romanticized into natural serenity. Gentleness is repeatedly something maintained while fear, scars, grief, anger, and danger remain real.

The open question is whether future commitment to Dustin can coexist with Daiya memory without converting one relationship into proof that the other has finally been overcome (`T14-OQ-24`).

## 6. Lena — adolescent ordinary life, adult command, and overresponsibility

Lena is not an Eighty-Six child soldier, but the developmental framework still applies because the series refuses to make rank equivalent to completed adulthood. Her V07 dance with Shin is the ordinary control [V07-L044]: social timing, embarrassment, intimacy, and bodily synchronization exist outside command. Her V14 maximum Para-RAID intervention is the crisis control [V14-L176].

The contrast matters because Lena's growth can otherwise be narrated entirely through competence. She becomes an extraordinary commander and political actor, but authority does not eliminate developmental needs for rest, friendship, romance, festival anticipation, and ordinary future planning. V12 medical leave is therefore not a decorative pause. `休むのも仕事だと思って` [V12-L006] becomes part of learning that responsibility has limits.

Her recurring risk is **overresponsibility**: converting accurate moral concern into the belief that she must personally absorb every consequence. Ordinary adolescence does not make her less serious; it provides another field in which she is allowed to exist without command being the only identity she deserves. The V14 crisis shows that this remains incomplete (`T14-OQ-19`).

## 7. Frederica — child status, political inheritance, and the right to become her own authority

Frederica is essential because she exposes the danger of using the word *child* to erase judgment.

She is young enough for ordinary peer meals, ornaments, childish behavior, fear, dependence, and developmental protection to matter. She is also politically knowledgeable, historically burdened, capable of moral judgment, and repeatedly able to tell adults to stop.

The correct response is neither adultification nor infantilization.

Her child status increases adult duties not to sacrifice or instrumentalize her. Her demonstrated judgment increases the weight her answers must receive.

V12's aspiration to become `己一人であっても己の王` turns inherited monarchy toward self-rule. V14 then destabilizes that achievement by returning literal dynastic authority around her body.

Her developmental open question is therefore political as well as personal: what does self-rule mean when everyone around her can use bloodline, memory, protection, and crisis to tell her what she must become (`T14-OQ-25`)?

# XXI. Counterreadings and adversarial controls

Document 09 rejects several attractive but insufficient readings.

## Counterreading A — “The Eighty-Six need to learn how to be normal children.”

**Rejected.** Ordinary capacities exist before rescue [V01-L007; Alter.1]. The missing element is not humanity but safe, repeatable developmental opportunity. There is also no untouched child-self to restore [V02-L025; V10-L059–V10-L060].

## Counterreading B — “Their pride is a trauma pathology they must outgrow.”

**Rejected.** Pride is historically anti-dehumanizing and remains meaningful in V14. The problem is exclusivity: `誇りしか`, not `誇り` [V03-L012; V08-L033; T14-C07].

## Counterreading C — “A healthy identity should be maximally plural.”

**Rejected.** Durable vocation, love, unit belonging, inherited memory, and lifelong promises can remain legitimate. Plurality is protection against foreclosure, not a requirement to keep every commitment shallow (`T14-C08`).

## Counterreading D — “Reenlistment proves the Five never recovered.”

**Rejected.** They demonstrate civilian capability before reenlisting. Continued soldierhood can become chosen vocation. The unresolved issue is historical conditioning, not the authenticity of every military choice (`T14-C16`, `T14-C17`, `T14-OQ-28`).

## Counterreading E — “Future orientation means healing is basically complete.”

**Rejected.** V14 Shin has future desire and conceals disability [V14-L059–V14-L060]. Future-bearing selfhood and self-instrumentalization coexist (`T14-C20`).

## Counterreading F — “Leisure scenes prove psychological recovery.”

**Rejected.** Pleasure can coexist with fear, grief, avoidance, anger, and unresolved bodily effects. Ordinary life is evidence of capacity and temporal breadth, not a diagnostic certificate.

## Counterreading G — “Theo's amputation is either pure tragedy or liberating growth.”

**Rejected.** The injury closes real capabilities and changes his future. The moral gain lies in survival, standing, and reconstructible options, not in the loss itself (`T14-C18`).

## Counterreading H — “Competent child soldiers are functionally adults.”

**Rejected.** Specialized competence changes the weight adults should give their judgment; it does not erase developmental youth (`T14-C21`).

## Counterreading I — “Not taking revenge means the grievance has been healed.”

**Rejected.** Nonrevenge, nonforgiveness, grief, release, and repair remain distinct (`T14-C22`). Rito's V11 release and Shin's V14 punishment grammar make this explicit.

## Counterreading J — “Remembering the dead is always healthy.”

**Rejected.** Memory can preserve particularity or become enclosure. Revenge Shepherds and other cases show grievance can monopolize action (`T14-C33`).

## Counterreading K — “Peace will finish the developmental problem.”

**Open and methodologically prohibited.** The corpus has not shown stable postwar life. Peace could widen options or create new fixed expectations about what a recovered veteran, citizen, partner, disabled person, or former child soldier is supposed to become (`T14-C41`).

# XXII. Canonical findings through Volume 14

The following claims are sufficiently supported to function as canonical Phase-6 findings at the current boundary.

1. **Ordinary humanity predates rescue.** V01 and Alter.1 demonstrate ordinary practices inside the Sector.
2. **Giad materially expands repeatability, safety, infrastructure, education, rest, and option breadth without creating personhood.**
3. **Child soldier competence and developmental youth coexist.** Combat skill does not equal completed adulthood.
4. **Forced self-reliance is both genuine competence and evidence of deprivation.**
5. **Recovery cannot mean restoration of a prewar child-self.** History is not reversible.
6. **Pride is not intrinsically pathological.** The dangerous form is exclusivity: `誇りしか`.
7. **Persistent wounds do not prove failed recovery.** Pain can recur after interpretation and priorities have changed.
8. **Grief does not establish guilt.** Removing false self-blame does not remove loss.
9. **Memory can be additive.** The dead may remain loved without governing every later attachment or vocation.
10. **Future need not begin as grand purpose.** Small iterative wants can generate broader futurity.
11. **Repeatable time is a major developmental good.** Birthdays, school terms, festivals, meals, travel, study, and recurring relationships structure expectation beyond immediate survival.
12. **Education is capability infrastructure, not normalization.** A subject may learn enough to reject an option.
13. **Rest creates psychological and temporal room for future desire.** It is not merely productivity repair.
14. **Ordinary pleasure does not prove safety or completed healing.**
15. **Civilian life is not the required moral endpoint.** Military vocation can remain compatible with recovery when choice is usable, revisable, and nonexclusive.
16. **Disability closes real options without reducing standing.** Recovery after bodily loss is reconstruction rather than restoration.
17. **Support is healthiest when it returns judgment.** Protection must not become permanent future ownership.
18. **Romance is one developmental domain among many, not a cure.**
19. **Meaning made after harm does not justify the harm.** The Gentle World is the decisive anti-theodicy.
20. **A usable future is infrastructural.** Psychological desire requires material routes through which return, study, rest, care, and role change can actually occur.
21. **Public memory should preserve ordinary particularity.** Martyr-symbols can reproduce enclosure by reducing a life to the manner of death.
22. **Recovery is compatible with fear, anger, grief, dependence, pain, strong identity, and continuing military commitment.**
23. **The recurring danger is not intensity but exhaustive foreclosure.** A role, wound, identity, relationship, or grievance becomes destructive when it eliminates all legitimate routes of answer and change.
24. **V14 is a developmental gate, not closure.** Postwar stabilization remains unseen.

# XXIV. Final synthesis

The deepest developmental claim of *86* is not that damaged children can become normal again.

They cannot become the children they would have been without war, because those children are counterfactual people who never got to live. Nor does the series ask them to abandon pride, soldierhood, memory, grief, or intense attachment in order to prove recovery.

The more demanding proposition is that history should lose the right to be **exhaustive**.

The Eighty-Six were already laughing, cooking, drawing, teasing, singing, remembering, desiring, and creating social rituals while San Magnolia expected them to die. Rescue does not create those capacities. Better institutions make them safer, more varied, more repeatable, and more capable of supporting futures.

This is why a birthday matters. It assumes another year.

A bus matters because curiosity can travel farther.

School matters because not knowing can become a question instead of a lethal vulnerability.

Rest matters because the mind can contain something besides the next emergency.

A bad hobby matters because the person can quit it without losing standing.

Romance matters because desire can be embarrassing rather than terminal.

Food matters because preference can survive ideology, injury, and public abstraction.

A prosthetic or accommodation matters because bodily change does not have to end social participation.

A remembered dead person matters because memory can add to life without demanding reenactment.

Pride matters because the persecuted needed a language in which the persecutor did not own the meaning of their lives.

And `しか` matters because even the identity that saved a person can become another cage if it becomes the only identity that person is permitted to retain.

The mature developmental standard can therefore be stated simply:

> **Recovery is the expansion of livable recurrence.**

It is the growing possibility that there will be another meal, another lesson, another argument, another birthday, another role, another home, another way to remember, another chance to fail safely, another person to love, another body to learn how to inhabit, and another future that does not require pretending the past never happened.

That is not the same as endless revision. A person may eventually settle into a durable vocation, family, friendship, identity, or place. Stability is not failure. The ethical question is whether the stability was reached with enough standing, material capability, answer-space, and continued belonging that it is **a life the person can inhabit rather than a role history has made impossible to leave**.

At the Volume 14 boundary, the Eighty-Six have demonstrated that ordinary life can survive atrocity, that pride can survive critique, that grief can survive release, that childhood can coexist with competence, and that recovery can survive the return of pain.

What the final arc has not yet demonstrated is whether those achievements can outlive the war that forced them into motion.

That question remains open.
