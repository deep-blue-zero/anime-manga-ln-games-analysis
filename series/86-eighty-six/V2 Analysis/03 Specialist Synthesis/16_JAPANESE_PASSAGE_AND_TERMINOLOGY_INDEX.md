# 16 — Japanese Passage and Terminology Index

## Scope and authority note

**Phase-8 verification note.** The complete 214-entry registry was re-routed to the current Japanese EPUB binaries after source-hash verification. All registry locator IDs remain valid; all 28 Phase-5 terminology locks and all 55 high-value passage controls resolve in the current source. `EXACT_ANCHOR` remains a deliberately narrower state and has not been silently promoted. Two compact-anchor presentation defects were repaired without changing their locator IDs or interpretations: `JP-ADDR-011` now displays the attested callsign → rank/full-name → personal-name sequence, and `JP-RETURN-015` now displays the source wording `帰還だけは、許可できません` rather than an English analytic suffix.


This document is the canonical **passage-level retrieval layer for Japanese-language evidence** in the V2 corpus through Volume 14. It is intentionally different from Document 12. Document 12 argues what the Japanese evidence changes analytically; Document 16 makes that evidence **findable, reusable, and auditable**.

The governing rule is:

> **Index the Japanese property actually present in the source before attaching the English abstraction used in synthesis.**

Accordingly, this file does not treat “return,” “home,” “personhood,” “support,” “freedom,” “justice,” “curse,” “Queen,” or “Death God” as one-to-one lexical units. It preserves source coordinate, locator, evidence class, quote state, lexical family, and the canonical home where the passage has already been interpreted.

This index is quotation-light. The Japanese anchor is deliberately short; the locked locator is the route to the complete primary-source paragraph. A short anchor is not permission to reconstruct missing context from memory.

# XX. Narration and quotation protocol

1. **Identify the voice.** Before using a passage as fact, distinguish narrator assertion, close focalization, free-indirect coloring, character speech, memoir testimony, technical explanation, enemy/recruitment claim, and paratext.
2. **Preserve mood and modality.** Potential forms, requests, imperatives, negation, restrictive particles, and temporal operators may carry the analytical burden.
3. **Quote only what the argument needs.** The anchor in this index is a retrieval key, not a substitute for context.
4. **Use the locked coordinate.** If an earlier locator and Phase-5 coordinate differ, the Phase-5 coordinate governs retrieval while the correction ledger preserves provenance.
5. **Do not English-back-project.** An English synthesis term may unite several Japanese systems at a higher level, but it must not be described as one repeated source keyword.
6. **Do not upgrade speaker conviction into omniscience.** Strongly phrased Lena, Ernst, Frederica, Vika, No Face, or narrator-colored focalization remains evidence of the stated evidence class.
7. **Alter.1 remains supplemental.** It can stabilize low-pressure idiolect and relational register without overriding mainline publication-boundary evidence.

# XXII. Final indexing thesis

The Japanese corpus is most reliable when treated as a **network of distinctions rather than a glossary of hidden master concepts**.

> **Source-language precision is a constraint on interpretation: the index should make it easier to discover when an English synthesis has merged several Japanese systems, strengthened a speaker claim into narrator fact, converted possibility into destiny, or turned a relation-specific title into an essence.**

Document 16 therefore has a deliberately modest authority. It is authoritative for **where the verified Japanese evidence is and how it is classified**. Document 12 remains the primary linguistic argument; Documents 01–14 and 17 remain the primary homes of the substantive conclusions; the locked Japanese novels remain the governing source when a wording dispute requires full context.
