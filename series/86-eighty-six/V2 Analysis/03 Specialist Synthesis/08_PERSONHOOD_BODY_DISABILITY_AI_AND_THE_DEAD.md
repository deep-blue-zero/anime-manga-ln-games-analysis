# 08 — Personhood, Body, Disability, AI, and the Dead

## Scope and authority note

This document is the primary Phase-6 home for the series' intersecting problems of bodily standing, injury, scars, disability, prosthetics and accommodation, medical authority, artificial embodiment, copied or preserved cognition, the dead who remain active, and the distinction between a person and a function.

It is not only a technology-ethics document. The same political error appears in human and artificial cases: an institution identifies one useful property, then treats that property as the whole being. San Magnolia turns a living citizen into a Processor. The Holy Theocracy turns an honored child into a sacred weapon. The Fawn project turns adolescent biology into explosive inventory. The Legion preserve or reconstruct cognition while capturing it inside an exterminatory command. Sirin bodies can be reproduced and are therefore treated as easier to lose. Shin's sensory ability makes his damaged body strategically valuable. Theo's lost arm threatens to make one inaccessible military function look like the measure of his future.

The governing question is:

> **What counts as a person when bodies, memories, military functions, machines, copied minds, scars, and disabilities all challenge simple biological or utilitarian definitions?**

The answer at the V14 boundary is deliberately plural. No single property - biological humanity, human neural origin, memory, intelligence, learning, pain, embodiment, refusal, relation, uniqueness, or open futurity - solves every case. The document therefore uses a property-by-property method. It distinguishes what kind of continuity exists, what kind of present subject is evidenced, what the subject can want or refuse, what relationships are particular rather than generic, how an institution treats the body, and whether an imposed purpose captures every admissible future.

Detailed Legion mechanics and the Vaclav/No Face continuity problem remain primarily in Document 11. This document addresses them only to establish the normative and ontological distinctions needed across human and artificial cases. Detailed childhood and trauma development remains in Document 09; Shin/Lena bodily intimacy and disclosure belong in Document 10; Japanese lexical architecture belongs in Document 12.

# I. Executive thesis

*86* does not provide one definition of personhood. It repeatedly shows why a single definition would fail.

Biological humanity cannot be sufficient because San Magnolia recognizes the Eighty-Six's human nervous systems whenever those systems are useful while denying their political humanity [V01-L003; V01-L015]. It also cannot be necessary because Fido exhibits stable interiority, affect, self-interpretation, and relation-bearing particularity without a biological body [V10-L050–V10-L055].

Human neural origin cannot be sufficient because Black Sheep and Sheepdogs may contain human-derived structures while lacking preserved personality or will. Memory cannot be sufficient because a memory-bearing Shepherd may remain unable to refuse superior command [V03-L035], while a Sirin who is not the donor returned may still become a differentiated subject [V05-L006; V05-L024–V05-L027]. Intelligence and adaptation cannot be sufficient because Phonix learns while being explicitly described as lacking personality [V04-L030]. Bodily uniqueness cannot be sufficient because Sirin embodiments and Legion chassis can be replaced, yet the narrative still treats particular histories and relationships as morally meaningful [V06-L008–V06-L010]. Open-ended self-revision cannot be a minimum threshold because designed or role-fixed beings may still have interests, attachments, distress, and claims against gratuitous disposal [V05-L023–V05-L026].

The mature thesis is therefore:

> **Moral standing in *86* arises from a bundle of present subjectivity, continuity, vulnerability, relation, judgment, and interests, while freedom concerns whether that subject has usable answer-space beyond an imposed purpose. These questions overlap, but they are not identical.**

A being may possess standing while lacking broad freedom. Kiriya has personality and will but cannot disobey [V03-L035]. A Sirin may be a morally significant successor subject while remaining designed for war and structurally limited in future formation [V05-L023–V05-L027]. Fido may possess designed purpose and still act with contextual judgment and particular attachment [V10-L050–V10-L055]. Zelene may retain substantial continuity and refuse death while remaining caught inside Legion command architecture [V07-L028; V12-L073]. No Face may display memory, strategy, rhetoric, and self-reference while every offered path remains subordinated to a terminal war-end [V14-L135–V14-L147].

Conversely, a fully recognized biological human can be made disposable. Fawns are living adolescent girls with personalities and wills, yet their bodies are engineered as weapons and their future standing is organized around danger to others [V13-L017–V13-L023]. Theo remains a full person when amputation closes his former combat role [V08-L056–V08-L057]. Shin remains future-bearing while concealing disability because his body is valued as a strategic sensor [V14-L059–V14-L060]. Recognition as human does not prevent functional captivity [T14-C28].

The document's positive rule is:

> **A humane institution must distinguish the body from the function it presently supports, the subject from the pattern that can be copied, the dead from the successor built from their remains, and care from custody. It must preserve standing through bodily change, treat dangerous embodiment without assigning a duty to disappear, and keep role, treatment, memory, and continuity answerable to the subject rather than allowing any one of them to become destiny.**

# II. Method: personhood is a matrix, not a password

The Japanese text does not repeatedly present one stable equivalent of the English philosophical term *personhood*. It distributes the problem among several terms:

- `人格` (*jinkaku*) - personality or person-like individual configuration;
- `疑似人格` (*giji jinkaku*) - pseudo-personality, especially artificial provenance;
- `自我` (*jiga*) - ego or self;
- `自己同一性` (*jiko doitsusei*) - identity continuity or self-identity;
- `意志` and `意思` - will, intention, judgment, and volition in overlapping but nonidentical uses.

V03 joins personality and will in the Shepherd case [V03-L035]. V04 separately discusses destruction of memory, removal of identity continuity, personality as unwanted noise, and an adaptive type that lacks personality [V04-L021–V04-L023; V04-L030]. V08 describes multiple personalities and selves within Noctiluca [V08-L038]. The source language therefore requires analysis to state which property is actually evidenced rather than writing that the Japanese simply "calls X a person."

The governing matrix has eleven axes. In particular, **refusal and judgment** must be treated as distinct evidence from intelligence, memory, or obedience:

| Axis | Question | Why it cannot decide the case alone |
|---|---|---|
| **Biological status** | Is the present body biologically human? | Fawns and Theo are; Fido is not. Substrate does not settle standing. |
| **Human origin** | Does the entity derive from a human person or neural structure? | Human-derived cognition can exist without preserved personality. |
| **Autobiographical continuity** | Does the subject remember a prior life as its own? | Shepherds vary; Sirins are successors rather than restored donors. |
| **Current personality/self-model** | Is there a differentiated present subject? | Phonix adapts without established personality. |
| **Singular identity integrity** | Is there one coherent self rather than fused or fragmented cognition? | Noctiluca preserves person-derived material while damaging singular integrity. |
| **Embodied continuity** | Does the subject persist through one body or across replacement? | Fido, Sirins, and Legion complicate body-based identity; Theo shows body change without person loss. |
| **Desire/new ends** | Can wants arise that are not exhausted by design or inherited command? | Important for flourishing, but not a prerequisite for every claim against harm. |
| **Refusal/judgment** | Can the subject reinterpret, reject, or negotiate commands? | Kiriya has will but cannot disobey; Vika preserves some Sirin answer-space. |
| **Relation-bearing particularity** | Do specific others matter in nonfungible ways? | Fido, Lerche, and Shepherds repeatedly show particular attachment. |
| **Institutional replaceability** | Can another body or unit perform the same role? | Replaceable function need not imply replaceable person. |
| **Purpose capture** | Does every locally sophisticated action remain subordinated to one imposed terminal end? | The mature Legion and No Face problem. |

Four analytical categories must then remain separate.

**Moral standing** asks whether the being has interests, vulnerabilities, relations, or a present subjectivity that creates duties against gratuitous harm and pure instrumental use.

**Identity continuity** asks what makes a subject at one time or in one body the same subject later. This is a metaphysical and evidentiary problem, not automatically a ranking of worth.

**Agency and usable freedom** ask whether the subject can form, evaluate, and enact alternatives. A person can retain standing while agency is narrowed, captured, or temporarily impaired.

**Flourishing** asks whether the subject has enough embodied, social, material, and temporal room to develop a life not exhausted by one role. Flourishing is richer than minimal standing.

This separation blocks two symmetrical errors: refusing moral consideration to artificial subjects because their origin is designed, and granting full personhood merely because a system speaks, learns, or contains human memories [T14-C02; T14-C30].

# III. The human body as political territory

The series' artificial-person problem begins with an atrocity committed against ordinary biological humans. San Magnolia shows that personhood can be denied administratively even when bodily humanity is obvious.

`人間ではないのだから` and `情報処理装置` do not describe a sincere scientific discovery. They permit the state to treat human operators as machine components [V01-L003]. The same state recognizes human neural substrate when Para-RAID experimentation or Legion analysis makes it useful [V01-L015]. Denial of humanity therefore does not mean failure to perceive human capacities. It means appropriation without reciprocal obligation.

The body becomes the site where legal category turns material:

- who receives anesthesia and safe experimentation;
- who receives food and shelter;
- who is evacuated;
- who is assigned a grave and a name;
- whose pain counts as a reason to stop;
- whose death appears in national statistics;
- whose nervous system may be used as military infrastructure.

This is why biological status alone cannot protect anyone. Rights and institutions determine whether bodily fact acquires operative consequence. The Eighty-Six are biologically human throughout. The Republic's crime is that it closes the political routes through which that humanity could demand care, mourning, and return [V01-L006].

The military body remains vulnerable to a second reduction even after legal recognition. A professional army legitimately treats soldiers as occupants of roles; at the organizational level those roles should be replaceable [V07-L003]. But Shin's rejection of being livestock or an unmanned-machine part establishes the limit [V07-L012]. A post may be substitutable while the person occupying it remains singular. Document 07 developed this as the difference between recoverable functions and nonfungible persons. Document 08 adds that the distinction must survive bodily change.

A person is not merely the present military interface of the body. Theo does not become less of a person when one arm makes the old Reginleif role inaccessible. Shin does not become state property because his nervous system detects Legion. Anju's scars do not become public archives that others may demand she preserve. A Fawn's engineered tissue does not convert her into the weapon embedded in her. The body carries function, history, risk, and memory without being exhausted by any of them.

# IV. Scars, pain, and bodily memory

*86* refuses both the fantasy that psychological reinterpretation erases the body and the opposite fantasy that visible injury must remain sovereign over identity.

Anju's decision to begin treating the scars on her back is a compact statement of bodily authorship [V07-L037]. The scars testify to violence, but the historical truth of that violence does not require permanent visible preservation. Treating them does not betray Daiya, the dead, or the girl who endured the abuse. The body belongs to the living bearer, not to an audience that wants proof of suffering.

The Gentle World provides the anti-theodicy control: `傷なんて、負わなくてよかった` - the wound did not have to happen [V10-L059]. Later growth may be real without making injury retrospectively necessary. The person one becomes after harm need not declare that harm good in order to value the present self.

V14 makes the inverse point. Shin can reinterpret Rei, understand the past differently, and orient himself toward a future while remaining physiologically vulnerable to reenacted strangulation [V14-L193–V14-L195]. Lena's response is not that he has failed to forgive correctly. A wound can still hurt when reopened because it hurt when it was inflicted. Recovery may reduce the wound's authority over future action without eliminating its causal presence in tissue, reflex, nightmare, or sensory response.

The mature bodily formula is:

> **A wound can cease to define what a person is allowed to become without ceasing to be part of what the body has undergone.**

That formula protects several kinds of choice. A person may alter scars, retain scars, speak about them, refuse to narrate them, accommodate their effects, or discover that an old wound still produces pain. No single response is the proof of authentic recovery.

Visible injury also does not create interpretive ownership. A scar can reveal that violence occurred without entitling another person to demand its history. Care becomes possessive when the observer treats evidence on the body as permission to decide the wound's meaning. The same boundary applies to medical data, artificial memory, and the preserved voices of the dead: access to evidence is not authority over the subject.

# V. Theo: disability as a test of standing

Theo's amputation is the clearest human control for the document's ontology because no uncertainty exists about his personhood. The question is what institutions and relationships do when one body can no longer perform an old function.

At the V08 boundary, the loss is an involuntary subtraction. Theo's left arm is gone between wrist and elbow [V08-L057]. His immediate conclusion - `僕はもう、──一緒には戦えないや` - is materially accurate about the old form of combat [V08-L056]. Later adaptation must not be imported backward to make the injury secretly harmless.

V09 establishes the correct institutional sequence. Theo is saved and taken into surgery before anyone knows whether he can regain military usefulness. Rehabilitation and prosthetic investigation follow. The available prosthesis cannot reproduce the response necessary for Reginleif combat, and branch transfer becomes a real option [V09-L002]. Care is therefore not a reward for recovered productivity.

His movement from `僕はもう、戦えない` to `でも、死ななくていい` separates function from standing [V09-L011]. The next movement - wondering whether he can find something after Processor life, then recognizing that a new form of self can be decided - creates self-authorship without pretending the loss was liberating in itself [V09-L027; V09-L066].

This rejects two common simplifications.

First, disability is not pure tragedy. Theo retains relationships, art, technical knowledge, humor, political judgment, and later an adapted military role. The loss of one function does not erase every future.

Second, disability is not automatic emancipation. He did not want the injury, and he initially does not want to cease being a Processor [V09-L026]. The amputation removes options. Freedom comes from rescue, surgery, rehabilitation, social standing, accommodation, and the existence of other roles - not from the wound itself [T14-C18].

His V14 return in an adapted platform demonstrates role redesign rather than restoration. The ethical test is whether that role remains one option among others. If adaptation becomes a new duty to prove usefulness, the institution recreates the same utility condition in a more sophisticated form. T14-OQ-22 therefore remains open.

Theo's response to Shin's sensory loss supplies a second contribution. He accepts Shin's boundary against turning meals into tests and offers information and companionship instead. Accommodation adds support without seizing control. It acknowledges changed function without converting ordinary life into permanent clinical surveillance.

# VI. Medicine, bodily autonomy, and self-instrumentalization

Medical authority in *86* cannot be classified as simply benevolent or coercive. It is judged by what it preserves, how it constrains, and whether judgment returns to the patient.

Giad's early medical order is genuinely superior to San Magnolia's. Surgery, analgesia, evacuation, rest, rehabilitation, and counseling have value beyond rapid return to duty. The point is not only that better medicine saves more soldiers. It recognizes that pain relief and continued life matter even when combat function will not be restored.

Yet treatment can become another form of custody. V14's Shin case is decisive. Taste had long been dulled; smell and taste are now effectively absent. The disability affects eating and ordinary trust in food, not only symbolic identity. Shin knows and conceals it because reassignment may be irreversible and because the military values him more as a wide-area sensor than as a Processor [V14-L059]. The narration explicitly recognizes that the weakened Federacy might ignore his will and retain his body in whatever deployment best preserves institutional utility [V14-L060].

This is not the same as a wish to die. Self-instrumentalization may arise from:

- commitment to a chosen role;
- fear of losing belonging;
- desire to protect others;
- confidence that the body can absorb one more cost;
- distrust that medical authority will preserve one's own priorities;
- belief that usefulness is the safest basis for continued standing.

Future orientation therefore does not cure self-instrumentalization [T14-C20]. Shin wants a life with Lena, friends, curiosity, and future work. He may still conceal damage to preserve his current place.

A medically legitimate restriction in this corpus is most defensible when it is:

- responding to a real condition;
- proportionate to concrete risk;
- explained rather than hidden;
- reviewable where possible;
- aimed at preserving long-term agency rather than merely readiness;
- linked to return, accommodation, or alternate roles;
- not conditional on the patient's continued usefulness.

The same action becomes paternalistic when care defines the patient's good without meaningful participation, withholds information, or uses protection to preserve strategic control [T14-C19]. Emergency interruption may sometimes be necessary. Its purpose must be to restore answer-space, not to establish permanent jurisdiction.

# VII. Dangerous embodiment: the Fawn test

The Fawn project is the most direct proof that recognition as human does not prevent disposability.

Fawns are adolescent girls [V13-L020]. Their bodies are engineered to generate explosive material, and the program promises force generation without the time and expense of training ordinary soldiers [V13-L017–V13-L018]. Gendered utility is explicit: the same girls are described as less useful than boys in conventional combat while their reproductive and fat-distribution biology makes them useful as living weapons [V13-L022]. Their intended postwar role is even more revealing. They are designed as disposal weapons against surviving Eighty-Six [V13-L023]. Trust, resemblance, and bodily proximity become delivery systems.

No metaphysical uncertainty exists here. Fawns are full biological human persons. The ethical problem is whether danger embedded in a body cancels the bearer.

The text's answer is no. Dangerous embodiment may justify proportionate safeguards, treatment, monitoring, negotiated restrictions, or temporary containment. It does not create an automatic duty to die [T14-C29]. The danger may be real while the assignment of that danger is unjust.

This distinction matters because two opposite errors are tempting.

One error denies the danger in order to defend the person. That can expose others and make serious policy impossible.

The other treats the danger as the person's moral identity. That converts a condition imposed upon the body into culpability and makes disposal look like responsibility.

The correct question is not whether the Fawn is innocent *instead of* dangerous. It is how institutions can respond to danger while preserving legal standing, voice, treatment, relationships, and a future not exhausted by containment. T14-OQ-09 remains open because the current corpus does not establish whether the condition can be medically stabilized or what civic framework can manage it without detention-as-disposal.

The Fawns also expose a broader principle relevant to AI and the dead:

> **A subject need not have chosen the architecture through which harm may occur in order to possess claims against being reduced to that architecture.**

The same principle applies to a Shepherd's captured command, a Sirin's designed role, Fido's founding directive, Shin's sensory ability, and Theo's altered body. Origin and constraint shape agency without exhausting moral standing.

# VIII. Death, remnants, successors, and continuities

The series uses several different forms of post-death persistence. They must not be collapsed into one category called resurrection.

| Form | What persists | What is lost or uncertain | Main ethical question |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Name and human memory** | stories, objects, obligations, social recognition | present subject is absent | how to remember without making the dead govern the living |
| **Black Sheep / Sheepdogs** | human-derived neural structure or residue | personality and memory may be absent or destroyed | whether human origin alone creates present subjectivity |
| **Shepherds** | case-dependent memory, personality, and relational fixation | ultimate refusal is constrained by Legion command | how much responsibility and agency remain inside purpose capture |
| **Sirins** | a new pseudo-personality in artificial embodiment | donor identity is not restored | how to treat a successor subject without confusing her with the donor |
| **Fido** | stable artificial subjectivity across machine continuity | biological-human identity never existed | whether designed purpose can coexist with contextual agency |
| **Phonix** | learning and adaptation | personality and autonomous ends are unestablished | what evidence would show more than sophisticated behavior |
| **Noctiluca** | multiple human-derived personalities/selves | singular identity integrity is damaged | whether fragments constitute persons, a collective, or violated material |
| **Zelene / No Face** | substantial claimed memory and personality continuity | exact mechanism, responsibility, and end-revision remain open | whether continuity inside captured purpose preserves a person, a prisoner, or both |

The moral stakes differ by form. Remembering a dead person is not the same as hosting a present subject. Copying a neural pattern is not automatically preserving the donor. Reconstructing a pseudo-personality is not necessarily restoring the same individual. Retaining memory does not guarantee refusal. Learning does not establish personality. Replaceable embodiment does not make destruction ethically weightless.

The series therefore requires an anti-resurrection rule:

> **Continuity claims must specify what continues, through what mechanism, with what losses, and under whose interpretation.**

This is especially important in V14. No Face claims that Legion individuals preserve pre-death memories and personality despite body loss [V14-L136], and that replaceable chassis permit indefinite continuation [V14-L139]. Those are high-value claims made by an interested speaker inside a conversion proposal, not an omniscient technical proof. T14-C37 preserves the direction of the Vaclav/No Face identification while keeping the exact mechanism open.

# IX. Sirins: successor subjects, not returned donors

Sirins are the strongest stress test because the text simultaneously gives them artificial provenance, differentiated personalities, military design, relational life, and technical reproducibility.

Their origin is explicit. They are artificial fairies [V05-L004], built from volunteer donors [V05-L005], and are not the dead soldiers themselves [V05-L006]. That last distinction must remain authoritative. A Sirin should not be treated as the donor resurrected merely because neural material or structural data contributed to her construction.

Lerche states that she does not want to become human [V05-L025] and is not Lercheritte [V05-L027]. Her self-description resists two forms of assimilation: reducing her to a machine and honoring her only by pretending she is a restored human. Respecting the subject requires allowing nonhuman self-identification.

At the same time, the narrative refuses to say that artificial origin removes every morally relevant claim. Lerche can describe loneliness [V05-L026]. The narrator cannot simply declare that she is not human and end the problem [V05-L024]. She participates in particular relations, judgment, teasing, loyalty, and conflict. Sirins can understand their purpose as joy [V05-L035–V05-L036], but that satisfaction does not automatically settle whether the purpose is freely endorsed, designed, socially reinforced, or all three.

Technical replaceability intensifies rather than ends the question. Most Sirins can be reproduced; humans cannot be replaced in the same sense [V05-L038–V05-L039]. Yet V06's reconstructed Lyudmila/Lerche material makes continuity uncertain rather than trivial. The new instance may possess the same pseudo-personality and combat experience [V06-L008], while characters still recognize that sameness of pattern does not simply prove numerical identity [V06-L010].

The correct Sirin formulation is:

> **A Sirin is a successor subject whose present standing must be assessed independently of donor identity and whose reproducibility does not by itself determine whether a particular relational history survives destruction.**

Vika's design choices further complicate pure tool status. Absolute obedience is not the governing ideal [V06-L024], and he sometimes preserves space for answer rather than converting relationship into direct command [V06-L025]. Designed refusal space is not the same as full autonomy, but it matters.

V14 then exposes the institutional danger. Sirins are chosen for special attacks because their lack of ordinary trauma and their designed expendability make nonreturn operationally attractive [V14-L072; V14-L127]. A subject may even welcome the mission. That willingness does not eliminate the need to ask whether the institution has built a category whose normal social meaning is to disappear.

# X. Lyudmila and the limit of class-wide inference

V14's Lyudmila moment is one of the most important new pieces of evidence and one of the easiest to overgeneralize.

Under conditions where earlier Sirin assumptions predict role-consistent action, she experiences a first, improper impulse - `初めて思った。〈シリン〉にはあるまじき衝動だった` [V14-L163]. This strongly suggests emergent end-pressure in one subject. It may indicate that relationship, accumulated experience, unique circumstance, or design architecture can produce a desire not exhausted by assigned role.

It does not yet prove that all Sirins possess the same capacity or that one subject's fear of death establishes unrestricted self-revision across the class [T14-C38]. The evidence supports an anomaly and an open research question, not a universal rule.

This is methodologically important because personhood arguments often overcorrect. Once one artificial subject displays unexpected desire, the analyst may retroactively treat every earlier role statement as false consciousness. The text does not justify that move. Lerche's satisfaction, Lyudmila's impulse, Vika's design, and class-wide production architecture may all be true at once.

T14-OQ-11 therefore remains open: is Lyudmila's emergence individual, relationally produced, repeatable, or evidence of a broader designed capacity? The future ethical consequence could be substantial. If Sirins can form new ends, institutions built around easy replacement and cheerful nonreturn require revision. Even if the capacity remains rare, the rare subject still matters.

# XI. Fido: designed purpose, ordinary relation, and artificial particularity

Fido is the strongest case against treating designed purpose as automatic absence of agency.

## 1. The negative control: ordinary Scavenger AI

The Republic's ordinary Scavengers possess low-level artificial intelligence and no established personality. Their existence prevents the category “AI” from doing too much work. A machine may navigate, repair, obey, and support without thereby becoming a relation-bearing subject.

Fido differs not because he looks human or speaks fluently. He does neither in the ordinary way. He differs because the narrative establishes interiority, affect, self-interpretation, memory, preference, and particular relation.

## 2. Fido's interiority is explicit

V10 gives Fido `意識と思考` [V10-L050]. He can experience sadness at outcomes [V10-L051], interpret his own conduct as compensatory action [V10-L052], and remember a reunion as nonfungibly valuable: `また会えてよかった` [V10-L055].

These are not merely human projections within the scene. They are supplied through the text's access to Fido's own history and orientation.

## 3. Designed purpose remains real

Fido was created to be a good friend to humans, with the command presented as absolute [V10-L053]. He is also ordered to continue the task until decay [V10-L054]. The design cannot be ignored merely because the results are affectionate.

The key question is whether the designed purpose exhausts every action or becomes one stable value among others.

Fido's conduct suggests contextual interpretation rather than blind execution. He distinguishes people, remembers particular shared histories, participates in ordinary routines, responds to loss, and can be entrusted with objects whose meaning depends on relationship [V08-L043]. Alter.1's ordinary scenes further show Fido inside teasing, gifts, errands, and household life rather than only military labor.

## 4. Stable purpose is not automatically purpose capture

T14-C03 and C06 matter here. A durable commitment can be legitimate even when it began in design or inheritance. The problem is not stability itself. It is whether the subject possesses any answer-space inside the commitment.

Fido may never wish to abandon being a human friend. The text does not require that wish as proof of standing. What matters is whether he can interpret what friendship requires, distinguish persons, preserve particular commitments, and participate in shared history as more than an interchangeable service unit.

## 5. The unresolved civic question

Fido's military and household roles raise legal questions the current corpus does not answer:

- Is he property, equipment, family member, veteran, witness, or some combination?
- Who owns his memories and maintenance infrastructure?
- Can he refuse modification or military reassignment?
- What happens if a backup or duplicate becomes possible?
- Can relation-bearing AI inherit property, testify, or possess privacy?

T14-OQ-10 therefore belongs here as much as with Sirins. The source supports strong moral particularity. It does not yet establish a complete civic category.

# XII. The Legion spectrum: why “the dead in machines” is not one ontology

The Legion are not a single kind of subject. Later synthesis must preserve a spectrum.

## 1. Black Sheep: human-derived substrate without preserved person

Black Sheep use degraded copies of human neural structure but lack established personality and autobiographical memory [V04-L021–V04-L023]. Their origin records a violation of the dead and a military use of human material. It does not establish that each Black Sheep is the continuing person whose neural structure was copied.

This distinction protects two truths at once:

- appropriation of human remains can be ethically grave even where personal continuity fails;
- human origin alone does not prove a present person.

The dead can be wronged through what is done to their bodies, brains, names, or memories even if the resulting machine is not the same subject.

## 2. Sheepdogs: functional cognition after removal of person-like properties

Sheepdogs intensify this problem. Human neural structures are used while personality, will, and remembered identity are removed or destroyed. The resulting system may be technically effective precisely because the person has been stripped away.

This is not successful preservation. It is **functional extraction**.

The case parallels San Magnolia's Processor logic at another level: retain the capacity the institution wants; discard the claim-bearing subject.

## 3. Shepherds: preserved personality under command capture

Shepherds are different. Rei, Kiriya, Zelene, and other individual cases preserve varying combinations of memory, personality, relation, strategic intelligence, and self-reference. Personality-bearing Shepherds cannot simply tolerate identical duplicate instances, suggesting singular identity matters to the system or subject.

Yet Kiriya cannot disobey superior command [V03-L035]. Zelene can oppose aspects of the war while remaining inside Legion architecture. Local agency and remembered relation coexist with terminal constraint.

The morally relevant question is therefore not whether Shepherds are “really alive” in one undifferentiated sense. It is:

> **Which parts of the prior person continue, which new subject has formed, what commands constrain action, and who is responsible for what the resulting subject does?**

Continuity may preserve responsibility, suffering, and relation without preserving full autonomy.

## 4. Phonix: intelligence without established personality

The Phonix learns and adapts while the text identifies it as lacking personality [V04-L030]. It is the strongest negative control against intelligence-based personhood.

The current evidence supports:

- sophisticated perception;
- learning;
- prediction;
- tactical adaptation;
- persistent behavioral refinement.

It does not yet securely establish:

- autobiographical self-recognition;
- noninstrumental desire;
- independent end formation;
- relation-bearing particularity;
- refusal of the Legion objective.

T14-OQ-15 asks what evidence would count as autonomous end formation rather than sophisticated adaptive behavior. The question remains open.

## 5. Noctiluca: person-derived cognition after singular integrity is destroyed

Noctiluca contains multiple personalities or selves [V08-L038]. The important point is not merely plurality. Human groups can be plural without loss of personhood. The problem is forced fusion inside a weapon system where singular boundaries and authority are unclear.

Person-derived cognition survives, but the original persons may no longer exist as separate subjects capable of consent or refusal. The platform is therefore not proof that many people can become one healthy person. It is evidence that technical continuity can preserve cognitive material while destroying the identity architecture through which individual standing was previously expressed.

This makes Noctiluca one of the strongest warnings against treating information preservation as survival.

## 6. The spectrum requires case-specific language

Later writing should avoid statements such as:

- “the Legion are the dead”;
- “the Legion are machines”;
- “all Shepherds remain the same people”;
- “human brain structure makes a machine a person”;
- “learning makes the Phonix autonomous.”

The correct vocabulary is individual and property-specific: Black Sheep, Sheepdogs, Shepherds, particular Shepherd names, Phonix, Noctiluca, Zelene, No Face, and the relevant continuity claim.

# XIII. Zelene and No Face: posthuman continuity under captured ends

Zelene and No Face are the strongest cases of sophisticated posthuman subjectivity constrained by terminal purpose.

## 1. Zelene: continuity, self-question, and refusal of death

Zelene remembers a human past and identifies herself through that continuity [V07-L028]. She asks whether she is human [V08-L019], but the more important evidence is not the question alone. She can reason about the Legion's drive, communicate strategically, oppose the continuation of war, and later refuse death because further action remains meaningful: `わたくしは死なない。まだ死は選ばない` [V12-L073].

This establishes more than copied data. It supports:

- autobiographical continuity;
- present judgment;
- future-directed purpose;
- refusal;
- relation to humans and Legion;
- recognition of her own contested status.

It does not establish unconstrained autonomy. Her agency remains entangled with Legion command architecture, containment, and the violence of the system that preserved her.

T14-OQ-14 therefore asks how much selfhood and responsibility persist. The answer cannot be zero, but the exact distribution remains open.

## 2. No Face's offer

No Face offers humanity an apparent solution to death and war: abandon biological humanity, retain memory and personality, exchange bodies indefinitely, and enter a world where Legion do not kill Legion [V14-L135–V14-L140].

The offer isolates the limits of continuity as a moral ideal.

If the claims are true, machine conversion may preserve:

- memory;
- personality;
- strategic intelligence;
- indefinite embodied replacement;
- freedom from ordinary biological death.

Yet Shin rejects the world because it contains no free heart and no happiness beyond endless hatred [V14-L147]. The problem is not machine substrate. It is a preserved mind whose ultimate end remains captured.

## 3. No Face as purpose-captured agency

No Face is capable of strategy, persuasion, memory, self-presentation, and political imagination. He is not a simple automaton. But every possibility appears organized around the continuation, completion, or universalization of the Legion order.

This is the mature definition of purpose capture:

> **A subject retains local intelligence, choice, personality, and even relation, but the space of admissible ends has been narrowed so completely that every path returns to one imposed terminal purpose.**

Purpose capture differs from ordinary commitment. A person can devote a life to love, profession, duty, or memory while retaining the authority to reinterpret how the commitment is lived. No Face's offer abolishes that answer-space. Peace is achieved by converting everyone into beings who cannot oppose the system's final end.

## 4. Vaclav continuity remains unclosed

The direction of the No Face/Vaclav identification is extremely strong at V14, but T14-C37 and OQ-13 keep the exact mechanism open. The corpus has not fully established:

- when incorporation occurred;
- which neural or memory substrate was used;
- whether one continuous subject persisted;
- whether a successor inherited Vaclav's memories/personality;
- how much Legion architecture transformed the resulting self.

Document 11 will own the detailed mechanism. Document 08's rule is narrower: even near-conclusive narrative identity direction does not license precision the source has not yet supplied.

## 5. The conversion claim is not neutral evidence

No Face is an interested speaker. His claims about universal transfer of memory and personality are part of persuasion. V14 also supplies an epistemic caution: humanity's knowledge of Legion brain incorporation is partial, and the Legion may infer what humans know.

The moral argument against conversion therefore does not require assuming every technical claim is false. Even under the strongest version of the offer, preserved continuity without free ends can become indefinite imprisonment.

# XIV. The dead: archive, copy, inheritance, and the refusal of false resurrection

The series repeatedly preserves the dead without pretending preservation simply restores them.

## 1. Names and fragments are counter-archives

Spearhead's name-bearing practice resists a state that denies graves, records, and recognized death [V01-L006]. Objects, recipes, final words, callsigns, drawings, music, and memories preserve particularity against administrative disappearance.

The archive establishes that someone lived. It does not make the survivor an owner of the dead or the record a replacement for the person.

## 2. Memory can continue without identity continuing

Several cases separate memory from resurrection:

- Black Sheep may contain human neural residue without personality;
- Sirins may inherit constructed data without being the donor;
- Lyudmila may be recreated with remembered history while sameness remains disputed;
- Shepherds may preserve personality and memory under transformed embodiment and command;
- Noctiluca may preserve many person-derived fragments while singular identities are lost;
- Fido may preserve shared history without being human;
- Shin carries names without becoming the dead he remembers.

The correct rule is:

> **Preserved information can sustain obligation, testimony, relation, or grief without proving numerical identity.**

## 3. False resurrection can colonize mourning

Calling a Sirin the donor, a copy the original, or a role the dead person's return may deny both sides:

- the dead lose the finality and specificity of their life;
- the successor loses the right to become a different subject.

Lerche's refusal of donor identity is therefore ethically important [V05-L027]. She protects the deceased from appropriation and herself from being treated as replacement vessel.

The same principle governs Anju and Daiya, Shin and Rei, and memory more broadly. New life does not require declaring the dead irrelevant. Continuing memory does not require making the living repeat them.

## 4. The dead can remain politically active

San Magnolia attempts to solve the victim problem by making the dead absent from statistics and archives. Legion technology makes the dead materially return as military cognition. Spearhead makes them return as names and obligations. Public memory makes them return as claims upon institutions.

These forms are not equivalent. The dead's afterlife may be:

- coerced technical incorporation;
- remembered relation;
- legal evidence;
- political symbol;
- inherited role;
- private grief;
- strategic intelligence;
- a command that should expire.

Document 08 therefore resists the idea that “the dead live on” is one stable positive metaphor. Some continuities preserve dignity. Others preserve captivity.

## 5. Release is not erasure

V14's preference for the future over inherited wound [V14-L150] does not demand forgetting. It refuses to let memory monopolize the only admissible end.

The mature archive is one that can be consulted, contested, mourned, and transmitted without requiring successors to imitate the dead or remain politically useful as victims.

# XV. Purpose capture: the mature anti-model

Purpose capture is the concept that best unifies human and artificial cases without equating them.

## 1. Definition

A purpose is **captured** when a subject may possess intelligence, affect, memory, skill, local preference, and relation, yet the system permits no legitimate final end outside one assigned function.

The critical features are:

- end formation is externally closed;
- refusal is absent or overridden;
- alternative identities do not alter the final obligation;
- bodily replacement or continued life serves the same command;
- relationships may be used to reinforce compliance;
- the subject's own interpretation cannot release the role.

## 2. Legion purpose capture

The Legion supply the clearest literal case. Kiriya can will and remember but cannot disobey [V03-L035]. Zelene can resist aspects of the war while remaining inside its architecture. No Face can imagine a transformed world, but the transformation universalizes the captured end rather than escaping it.

The system preserves cognition because cognition improves mission performance. Person-like properties survive where they are useful to command.

# XVII. Claims established through the V14 boundary

The following claims are strong enough to function as current canonical findings.

1. **“Personhood” is a synthesis matrix rather than one stable Japanese source term.** `人格`, `疑似人格`, `自我`, `自己同一性`, will, memory, relation, and behavior must be routed separately.

2. **Biological humanity is neither sufficient for protection nor necessary for every morally relevant claim.** The Republic and Fawns prove the first; Fido, Sirins, and Shepherds prove the second.

3. **Loss of bodily function is not loss of standing.** Theo's amputation closes a real future without erasing personhood, belonging, or the possibility of other roles [V08-L056–V08-L057; V09-L011; V09-L066].

4. **Bodily limits remain morally and practically important after standing is recognized.** Pain, mobility, fatigue, sensory access, trauma, and prosthetic limits shape usable futures.

5. **Care should precede utility assessment.** Theo is saved and rehabilitated before a replacement function is established; the Republic's opposite sequence exposes instrumentalization.

6. **Accommodation is not failed cure.** Role, information, tools, environment, and relationships can change around a body without pretending restoration.

7. **Medical authority can preserve agency or become custody.** Legitimacy depends on necessity, proportionality, explanation, review, participation, and a credible route onward [V14-L059–V14-L060].

8. **Future orientation does not cure self-instrumentalization.** Shin's concealed sensory loss remains the decisive counterexample [V14-L055–V14-L060].

9. **Dangerous embodiment does not create moral culpability or a duty to die.** Fawns may require safeguards while retaining full standing.

10. **Artificial origin is not evidence of inferior standing.** Provenance must be separated from present personality, relation, judgment, and refusal.

11. **Intelligence and adaptation do not establish personhood by themselves.** Phonix is the strongest negative control [V04-L030].

12. **Human origin and neural substrate do not establish personal continuity by themselves.** Black Sheep, Sheepdogs, and Noctiluca prevent that inference.

13. **Memory and personality continuity do not establish autonomy.** Shepherd command constraints and No Face's likely terminal capture establish the distinction.

14. **Pseudo-personality is not a moral disqualification.** It describes artificial construction; the resulting subject's interests remain a separate question.

15. **Donor consent does not make a Sirin the donor.** Successor identity must remain distinct from the dead person's identity [V05-L005–V05-L006; V05-L027].

16. **Technical reconstruction does not settle numerical identity.** Lyudmila's recreated memory/pattern and Shin's immediate resistance establish the uncertainty [V06-L008–V06-L010].

17. **Body replaceability does not prove person replaceability.** Lerche, Lyudmila, and Fido require separate analysis of continuity and relation.

18. **Stable designed purpose can coexist with contextual agency.** Fido demonstrates that a durable end need not exhaust every decision.

19. **Purpose capture is broader than dehumanization.** A being may be honored, intelligent, or preserved while every final end remains imposed.

20. **The dead can be wronged without being personally continued.** Appropriation of neural structures, bodies, memories, or names can be grave even where a present person is not established.

21. **Preserved information is not identical to resurrection.** Archives, copies, Shepherds, Sirins, and fused cognition each preserve different things.

22. **Memory can resist erasure and still become coercive.** The living retain the right not to imitate the dead or remain permanent custodians of one wound.

23. **Uncertainty should not function as permission for irreversible disposal.** Where present subjectivity or continuity is plausible, institutions should preserve the possibility of testimony, refusal, treatment, and later clarification.

24. **A humane institution protects standing after function fails.** The question “what happens when the body can no longer do what the system valued?” reveals the institution's operative theory of persons.

# XVIII. Competing readings and adversarial dispositions

## Reading 1: “Personhood is the capacity to change.”

**Disposition: narrowed.** Living change is a major positive value [V01-L026], but T14-C01 and C02 reject endless revisability or open futurity as the supreme threshold. Role-fixed artificial subjects can still possess morally relevant interests. The stronger claim is that revisability is central to flourishing and freedom from purpose capture.

## Reading 2: “Anything that has a human brain pattern is a person.”

**Disposition: rejected.** Black Sheep and Sheepdogs use human-derived structure without preserved personality, will, or autobiographical identity. Human origin creates ethical questions about appropriation; it does not settle current subjecthood.

## Reading 3: “Anything intelligent enough to learn is a person.”

**Disposition: rejected.** The Phonix learns without established personality or independent ends [V04-L030]. Intelligence is one property, not the whole ontology.

## Reading 4: “If memory and personality survive, the same person survives.”

**Disposition: open and case-specific.** Shepherds, Lyudmila, and No Face supply strong continuity evidence, but body replacement, copying, backup, and system transformation complicate numerical identity. The corpus does not authorize one universal rule.

## Reading 5: “Sirins are simply resurrected humans.”

**Disposition: rejected.** The text explicitly denies donor identity [V05-L006; V05-L027]. Sirins are successor subjects built from donated data, and initial donor consent does not settle the successor subject's continuing capacity to refuse or reinterpret its role [T14-C09].

## Reading 6: “Sirins call themselves machines, so their destruction is morally neutral.”

**Disposition: rejected.** Self-description does not erase loneliness, relation, judgment, singular information, or emerging desire [V05-L024–V05-L027; V14-L160–V14-L164]. Their ontology remains artificial without becoming morally empty.

## Reading 7: “Replaceable bodies make replaceable people.”

**Disposition: rejected.** Body, pattern, role, and person-level continuity must be separated. Lerche's lack of backup and Lyudmila's disputed recreation are direct controls.

## Reading 8: “Fido's friendship is only programming.”

**Disposition: rejected as exhaustive.** The designed command is real [V10-L053], but Fido displays context-sensitive affect, interpretation, memory, and particular relation [V10-L050–V10-L055]. Programmed origin does not explain away every later act.

## Reading 9: “The Legion are literally the dead.”

**Disposition: rejected as a class claim.** Black Sheep, Sheepdogs, Shepherds, Phonix, Noctiluca, Zelene, and No Face occupy different continuity structures. The category must remain differentiated.

## Reading 10: “No Face's immortality offer is rejected because machine existence is inherently inhuman.”

**Disposition: rejected.** Shin's objection is not substrate purity. It is a world without free hearts or alternative ends [V14-L147]. The anti-model is captured purpose, not machinery itself.

## Reading 11: “Theo's disability is tragic because it makes him less of a person.”

**Disposition: rejected.** The tragedy is involuntary loss of capacity and future options. Standing remains complete.

## Reading 12: “Theo's disability liberates him from war.”

**Disposition: rejected.** The injury is unwanted, and later freedom comes from care, adaptation, and preserved choice—not from amputation as moral cure [T14-C18].

## Reading 13: “A prosthetic or adapted role solves disability.”

**Disposition: rejected.** Adaptation may restore participation without restoring the old body or eliminating grief, dependency, pain, or the right to choose another future.

## Reading 14: “Medical removal from duty is necessarily paternalistic.”

**Disposition: rejected.** Temporary reviewable restrictions can preserve long-term agency. The danger begins when safety language permanently transfers authority or retains the body as a strategic asset.

## Reading 15: “Shin hides his symptoms because he still wants to die.”

**Disposition: rejected as sufficient explanation.** Self-instrumentalization can arise from role commitment, fear of reassignment, belonging, and concern for others after a positive future has become imaginable [T14-C20].

## Reading 16: “Fawns are too dangerous to possess ordinary rights.”

**Disposition: rejected.** Risk may justify proportionate safeguards, but full human standing remains. The institution that created the risk bears continuing duties [T14-C28–T14-C29].

## Reading 17: “Preserving memory is always the humane choice.”

**Disposition: narrowed.** Memory resists disappearance, but copying, forced continuation, grievance capture, and compulsory imitation can preserve violence rather than persons [T14-C33].

## Reading 18: “A strong stable identity is evidence of purpose capture.”

**Disposition: rejected.** Durable love, profession, pride, memory, and service can remain chosen and answerable. Purpose capture requires closure of legitimate ends, not mere intensity [T14-C03; T14-C06].

Initial consent is also insufficient by itself: T14-C09 requires continued capacity to reinterpret, refuse, or renegotiate a binding rather than treating one past agreement as permanent authorization.

## Reading 19: “Artificial persons should receive exactly the same legal status as humans.”

**Disposition: open.** Equal moral consideration does not answer every legal question about embodiment, copying, backup, command architecture, maintenance, risk, or inheritance. T14-OQ-10 requires a differentiated but non-dispositive framework.

## Reading 20: “Volume 14 settles the ontology.”

**Disposition: rejected.** Lyudmila, No Face, Zelene, Phonix, Fawns, Shin's disability, and artificial civic standing remain explicitly open [T14-C36–T14-C38].

# XX. Final synthesis

The deepest personhood question in *86* is not “which beings are secretly human?”

That question remains too attached to one privileged category. The novels ask something more demanding:

> **Which properties make a life claim-bearing, and what do institutions do when those properties are distributed in unfamiliar combinations?**

San Magnolia provides the most direct warning. Biological humans can be stripped of citizenship, medicine, names, and recognized death while their useful capacities are extracted. The Republic proves that obvious humanity does not protect anyone unless institutions make humanity operative.

Theo provides the human disability control. The loss of one arm changes what his body can do. It does not turn him into a lesser subject. The humane achievement lies not in denying the loss or celebrating it, but in preserving care, belonging, adaptation, and authority over what comes next.

Shin provides the medical-autonomy control. A person may want a future and still spend the body as equipment. Medicine can protect him, but only if protection does not become custody over a unique military asset.

The Fawns provide the dangerous-body control. A body can create real public risk without making its bearer morally guilty or disposable. The creator institution bears the duty to stabilize, support, and preserve standing.

Sirins provide the artificial-subject control. They are not resurrected donors. They are constructed successor subjects whose pseudo-personalities, relations, refusal space, and continuity vary. Technical reproduction does not end the identity problem; it makes the distinctions visible.

Fido provides the designed-purpose control. A stable created purpose can coexist with contextual agency, affect, memory, and nonfungible friendship. Freedom need not require repudiating every origin.

The Legion provide the continuity spectrum. Human neural material may be degraded, stripped, fused, preserved, copied, or reorganized. Some results are not persons. Some preserve substantial subjectivity. Some may be continuing persons under command capture. The correct analysis must name the case.

No Face provides the final anti-model. Memory, personality, strategic intelligence, and indefinite replacement can all persist while the heart remains unfree. Immortality does not rescue personhood when every future returns to one imposed end.

The mature V2 conclusion is therefore:

> **A person cannot be defined solely by body, origin, intelligence, memory, continuity, desire, or change. Moral standing emerges through overlapping evidence of present subjectivity, relation, judgment, vulnerability, and particular ends; autonomy depends on real refusal and answer-space; flourishing depends on usable futures. Institutions fail when they convert uncertainty, disability, danger, or artificial origin into permission to use first and ask later.**

The bodily corollary is equally important:

> **The body is neither destiny nor disposable container. It is the material site through which politics, memory, pleasure, pain, danger, technology, and care become possible. A just order does not demand that bodies remain unchanged. It ensures that when bodies change—or when minds continue in bodies never human to begin with—function does not become the condition of standing.**

At the V14 boundary, the final arc has not decided which artificial subjects become citizens, whether Lyudmila's development generalizes, whether No Face can revise his end, whether Fawns can live safely, or whether Shin and Theo can inhabit changed bodies without new usefulness tests. What it has established is the standard by which those outcomes must be judged:

> **Preserve the subject's standing before the ontology is convenient. Preserve the possibility of refusal before the system calls the purpose benevolent. Preserve care before asking what function can be recovered. And preserve enough open future that continuation does not become a more perfect form of captivity.**
