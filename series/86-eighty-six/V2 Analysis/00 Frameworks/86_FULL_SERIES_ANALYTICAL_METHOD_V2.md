# 『86―エイティシックス―』
## Full-Series Analytical Method V2
### Original-Japanese second-pass close reading, cumulative evidence, and synthesis protocol

## 0. Purpose and status

This document governs a new, source-grounded second-pass analysis of *86*.

The project already possesses a substantial legacy synthesis through Volume 12. That earlier work is valuable because it identified durable interpretive questions: personhood, recognition, war and identity, racialized institutions, memory, heroism, the dead, bodily utility, childhood, freedom, home, reciprocal dependence, and the possibility of life beyond military function.

The second pass must not simply expand that synthesis.

The earlier analysis is a **hypothesis bank and retrieval aid**, not the evidentiary authority of the V2 project. Every load-bearing conclusion must be reconstructed from the original Japanese novels. The new corpus should be able to say, with traceable support:

- what each volume establishes at its own publication boundary;
- what later volumes revise;
- how character voice and narration produce meaning in Japanese;
- how military, political, ethical, technological, relational, and psychological levels interact;
- which prior conclusions survive close rereading;
- which become more precise;
- which were overgeneralized;
- and which must be rejected.

The governing principle is:

> **Read *86* as a war novel, political novel, genocide narrative, coming-of-age story, relationship drama, military science-fiction work, and meditation on personhood at the same time—without letting any one layer erase the others.**

A second principle is:

> **Recognition, legal status, military usefulness, social belonging, moral legitimacy, psychological recovery, and freedom are different states. Never collapse them into a single category called “being treated as human.”**

A third principle is:

> **The purpose of the second pass is not to prove the legacy thesis. It is to find the strongest formulation the Japanese text can actually sustain.**

---

# I. Corpus boundary and source hierarchy

## 1. Expandable corpus boundary

The verified legacy project contains the original Japanese numbered novels through Volume 12.

The V2 framework must remain expandable. If later numbered volumes, side stories, bonus fiction, short-story collections, author notes, or other canonical prose materials are supplied, they should be incorporated without redesigning the method.

Therefore the governing corpus is defined as:

> **all supplied and verified original-Japanese canonical prose in publication order.**

Do not treat Volume 12 as a final-series endpoint merely because the earlier synthesis ended there.

Each analytical artifact must declare its own spoiler boundary.

---

## 2. Tier 1 — Original Japanese narrative prose

The primary authority is the complete original Japanese light novel.

For every volume, read in spine order:

- front matter where narratively meaningful;
- prologue;
- all chapters and interludes;
- epilogue;
- embedded letters, reports, messages, quotations, inscriptions, lists, or in-world documents;
- and the full prose surrounding any passage later used as evidence.

Search results, remembered scenes, summaries, translations, anime scenes, fan wikis, and the legacy synthesis are not substitutes for reading the source.

When the prose and a remembered adaptation scene differ, the prose governs the literary analysis.

---

## 3. Tier 2 — Illustrations, maps, diagrams, and collected-volume apparatus

Light-novel images are paratextual and visual evidence.

Examine:

- cover illustrations;
- color inserts;
- monochrome illustrations;
- maps;
- diagrams;
- weapons or unit schematics;
- character lineups;
- chapter-opening art;
- afterword illustrations;
- publisher-supplied visual apparatus.

These materials can reveal:

- editorial emphasis;
- body and uniform presentation;
- spatial relationships;
- military scale;
- symbolic framing;
- character pairings;
- how the publication asks the reader to visualize race, age, technology, intimacy, disability, and combat.

They do not override prose when the two differ.

A map can clarify operational geography. It cannot by itself prove a character motive.

---

## 4. Tier 3 — Afterwords and official paratext

Author afterwords, official guide material, interviews, and publisher commentary should be tagged as **paratext**.

They may clarify:

- production intent;
- terminology;
- research influences;
- chronology;
- military references;
- historical analogues;
- character-design decisions;
- or the author's retrospective understanding.

They must not silently replace what the novels dramatize.

Use separate formulations:

> **The novel establishes...**

> **The author later states...**

> **A reasonable interpretation is...**

Agreement raises confidence. Disagreement is analytically meaningful.

---

## 5. Tier 4 — Official translations

Official English translations are secondary evidence for a Japanese-primary analysis.

They are useful for:

- identifying translation-sensitive passages;
- locating places where military, political, relational, or emotional nuance shifts;
- supporting a later bilingual audit;
- checking widely circulated English terminology.

They must never overrule clear Japanese wording.

If a translation issue materially changes a conclusion, record it in the Japanese terminology/translation ledger rather than burying it in prose.

---

## 6. Tier 5 — Anime and other adaptations

The anime is an independent interpretation.

It should not be used to fill gaps in the novels during the primary literary pass.

A later adaptation study can ask:

- how voice acting changes character perception;
- how music affects grief, battle, and memory;
- how direction reorders or emphasizes material;
- what internal narration is lost or externalized;
- how visual framing handles race, bodies, machinery, childhood, and death;
- which themes the adaptation intensifies or compresses.

Novel claims and adaptation claims must remain visibly distinct.

---

## 7. Tier 6 — External scholarship, criticism, and reception

Systematic external criticism should come only after the independent source-grounded model is mature.

Possible later domains:

- genocide studies;
- racism and racialization;
- military ethics;
- just-war theory;
- trauma studies;
- childhood and child-soldier scholarship;
- disability studies;
- AI/personhood;
- memory studies;
- nationalism and citizenship;
- comparative military science fiction;
- romance and dependency;
- Japanese light-novel criticism.

External theory may sharpen a reading. It must not manufacture a theme for which the novels provide no meaningful evidence.

# II. Epistemic discipline: two readings of every volume

The V2 reread knows later material. That knowledge is useful, but dangerous.

Every volume must therefore preserve two distinct interpretive modes.

## 1. Prospective / publication-boundary reading

Ask:

> **What could a careful reader know, infer, or reasonably suspect at the end of this volume when it first appeared?**

Record:

- open mysteries;
- apparent motives;
- current institutional picture;
- relationship state;
- current understanding of Legion ontology;
- what characters know;
- what the narrator has disclosed;
- what remains ambiguous.

Later revelations may not be silently inserted here.

This preserves causal and emotional development.

---

## 2. Retrospective / full-series rereading

After the prospective reading is complete, add a clearly labeled retrospective section:

> **What does later material make newly visible in this volume?**

Examples of legitimate retrospective work include:

- a line whose significance changes after a later revelation;
- an early behavior that becomes part of a long-term pattern;
- an institutional assumption later shown to be false;
- a relationship gesture later revealed as foundational;
- a military practice later shown to have political consequences;
- language that acquires thematic recurrence.

Retrospective knowledge may revise an earlier interpretation, but it may not rewrite what the earlier volume actually allowed the reader to know.

---

## 3. Correction ledger

Every time later evidence modifies an earlier conclusion, record one of:

- `CONFIRMED`
- `STRENGTHENED`
- `NARROWED`
- `COMPLICATED`
- `OVERTURNED`
- `STILL_UNRESOLVED`

The goal is to preserve interpretive history rather than replacing old uncertainty with false hindsight.

---

# III. Evidence classification

Every important claim should be classifiable.

Use the following working labels.

| Code | Category | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `PF` | Primary fact | Event, condition, action, or directly established world fact |
| `NV` | Narratorial framing | Claim or description supplied by the narrator |
| `CS` | Character statement | What a character explicitly says, believes, alleges, remembers, or claims |
| `BA` | Behavioral evidence | Action, hesitation, refusal, choice, bodily response, repeated conduct |
| `LI` | Linguistic evidence | Japanese wording, pronoun, title, address form, register, metaphor, repeated lexical choice |
| `MI` | Military/institutional evidence | Orders, doctrine, organizational structure, operational consequence, legal or political mechanism |
| `VI` | Visual/paratextual evidence | Illustration, map, diagram, typography, collected-volume apparatus |
| `PT` | Paratext | Afterword, interview, guidebook, official commentary |
| `SI` | Strong inference | Not stated directly, but strongly supported by converging evidence |
| `IT` | Interpretation | Higher-order thematic or philosophical reading |
| `AM` | Ambiguity | Text supports multiple readings or lacks enough evidence |
| `RR` | Retrospective revision | Later material changes the earlier reading |

Do not convert `CS` into `PF` merely because the speaker sounds confident.

A character's self-description is evidence about the character even when factually wrong.

Do not convert `IT` into fact through repetition.

---

# IV. Source locator standard

The V2 corpus should allow a later claim to be traced back to the original Japanese novel.

For every load-bearing piece of evidence, record:

1. volume number;
2. chapter / interlude / epilogue;
3. internal EPUB spine file or section identifier where available;
4. nearest stable Japanese phrase or short anchor;
5. scene description;
6. evidence classification;
7. optional translation gloss;
8. which synthesis documents use the evidence.

Recommended human-readable locator:

`V06 / Ch.4 / [EPUB spine href if stable] / anchor: 「短 Japanese phrase」`

If exact EPUB anchors are unstable, do **not** invent page numbers.

Use:

- verified volume;
- verified chapter;
- unique Japanese anchor;
- and scene context.

A precise but false locator is worse than an honest coarse locator.

---

# V. Quotation and translation discipline

## 1. Three quotation states

Every Japanese passage used in analysis should be one of:

- `EXACT_VERIFIED` — checked against the original Japanese source;
- `WORKING_TRANSCRIPTION` — readable but pending final verification;
- `PARAPHRASE` — no quotation marks and no implication of exact wording.

Final synthesis documents should use only `EXACT_VERIFIED` wording for load-bearing quotation.

---

## 2. Translation glosses

When translating a short Japanese phrase:

- preserve the semantic range before polishing;
- note military or institutional technical meaning;
- note interpersonal register;
- avoid importing emotional intensity absent in the original;
- distinguish idiomatic English from literal structure when the distinction matters.

For major recurring terms, keep a terminology index rather than re-explaining the word differently in every document.

# VI. Per-volume reading workflow

Each numbered volume receives a canonical artifact:

`86_VXX_DEEP_READING.md`

This is the foundational evidence layer.

## Pass 0 — Source audit

Record:

- exact source filename;
- checksum where available;
- internal title;
- publication order;
- chapter list;
- spine order;
- illustrations and maps;
- afterword / paratext;
- extraction or encoding issues.

---

## Pass 1 — Causal reconstruction

Before thematic interpretation, reconstruct the volume.

Track:

- scene order;
- location;
- who knows what;
- command decisions;
- political decisions;
- relationship decisions;
- battle objectives;
- information asymmetries;
- failures;
- consequences;
- unresolved questions.

The purpose is to prevent analysis from drifting into a collection of favorite scenes.

---

## Pass 2 — Focalization and voice

For every major scene ask:

- whose consciousness organizes perception?
- what is narrator fact and what is character-colored perception?
- where does free-indirect style blur the boundary?
- what information is withheld?
- what judgments belong to the narrator?
- what judgments belong to Shin, Lena, another focalizer, an institution, or an enemy?

The series frequently shifts scale between intimate consciousness and military/political overview. Preserve that movement.

---

## Pass 3 — Character state

For each major character present in the volume record:

- current desire;
- fear;
- role;
- self-description;
- external description;
- recurring defense;
- dependency structure;
- moral responsibility;
- contradictions;
- what changes;
- what remains stable.

Do not reduce characters to thematic functions.

A character may embody an issue while also resisting the interpretation imposed on them by others.

---

## Pass 4 — Relationship state

Track relationships as changing systems rather than labels.

For each major pair or group:

- forms of address;
- trust;
- command authority;
- dependence;
- conflict;
- physical proximity or distance;
- secrets;
- care;
- resentment;
- obligation;
- reciprocity;
- ability to contradict one another;
- future-oriented commitments.

Key relational domains include:

- Shin / Lena;
- Spearhead as a collective;
- the Five surviving Spearhead members;
- Shin / Frederica;
- Lena / Annette;
- Eighty-Six / Federacy adults;
- Eighty-Six / Republic survivors;
- soldiers / civilians;
- living / dead.

---

## Pass 5 — Political and institutional analysis

Every institution should be read at multiple levels.

Track:

- formal law;
- actual practice;
- propaganda;
- administrative classification;
- citizenship;
- military command;
- welfare;
- education;
- social prejudice;
- elite incentives;
- public opinion;
- state capacity;
- legitimacy;
- responsibility distribution.

Do not reduce San Magnolia to “racism is bad.”

Ask exactly how racial ideology becomes administrative reality.

Do not reduce Giad to “the good state.”

Ask where benevolence becomes paternalism, where rights become materially usable, and where emergency war needs constrain freedom.

---

## Pass 6 — Military analysis at four levels

Never collapse military success into one scale.

### Tactical
Immediate battlefield execution:

- formations;
- weapons;
- maneuver;
- sensor use;
- terrain;
- fire and movement;
- command latency;
- individual skill.

### Operational
Campaign linkage:

- objective sequencing;
- force preservation;
- logistics;
- reconnaissance;
- reinforcement;
- tempo;
- casualty replacement;
- intelligence;
- coordination across fronts.

### Strategic
War-level goals:

- political objectives;
- industrial base;
- attrition;
- manpower;
- technology;
- alliances;
- territorial survival;
- war termination.

### Political / constitutional
Who has the authority to decide:

- who fights;
- who may be sacrificed;
- who bears risk;
- who receives rights;
- what the war is for;
- what follows victory or defeat.

A tactically brilliant decision can be strategically foolish.

A strategically necessary act can remain ethically horrifying.

A legal order can remain politically illegitimate.

Keep these judgments separate.

---

## Pass 7 — Technology and personhood

Treat technology as social structure, not gadget catalog.

Track:

- Juggernaut / Reginleif;
- Para-RAID;
- Legion cognition;
- Shepherds and Black Sheep;
- Sirins;
- artificial or preserved minds;
- prosthetics and bodily injury;
- sensory extension;
- communications technology;
- autonomous weapons;
- command-and-control systems.

Questions:

- What part of a person is being treated as equipment?
- What does continuity of memory imply for identity?
- When does a machine become a participant in shared history?
- When does preservation become imprisonment?
- How does technological dependence redistribute responsibility?
- Which systems require exceptional people to function?

---

## Pass 8 — Body, race, age, disability, and material life

The body is one of the series' principal political sites.

Track:

- hair, eyes, ancestry, and racial legibility;
- scars;
- uniforms;
- hunger;
- sleep;
- injury;
- chronic strain;
- disability;
- sexualized or coercive bodily violation where present;
- medical treatment;
- prosthetics;
- developmental age;
- puberty and adolescence;
- physical rest;
- clothing outside military function.

Avoid treating bodily injury only as symbolism.

Ask what the body materially permits, prevents, remembers, and requires.

For disability:

> **loss of combat function is not loss of personhood.**

But do not turn that proposition into a sentimental abstraction. Trace the practical changes in role, identity, dependency, and future.

---

## Pass 9 — Childhood and interrupted development

One major working hypothesis from the legacy reading is that the Eighty-Six exhibit forms of emergency competence without complete ordinary development.

Test this carefully.

Do not equate:

- combat competence with maturity;
- stoicism with recovery;
- leadership with adulthood;
- emotional restraint with health.

Track ordinary developmental spaces:

- play;
- school;
- food;
- festivals;
- art;
- fashion;
- romance;
- embarrassment;
- boredom;
- privacy;
- hobbies;
- family formation;
- career imagination.

Ask whether “recovery” means return to a lost childhood, resumed development, or creation of an adult life that never had an ordinary precursor.

---

## Pass 10 — Death, memory, grief, and inheritance

Track the dead not merely as casualties but as continuing agents in memory.

Distinguish:

- remembrance;
- obligation;
- survivor guilt;
- memorial practice;
- revenge;
- inheritance;
- role continuation;
- hallucination or supernatural/technological persistence where applicable;
- political use of the dead;
- private mourning;
- public myth.

Central question:

> **When does carrying the dead preserve human continuity, and when does it allow the dead to govern the living?**

Do not assume “moving on” requires forgetting.

---

## Pass 11 — Pride, heroism, and irreplaceability

The Eighty-Six's pride can function as:

- dignity;
- resistance;
- competence;
- solidarity;
- refusal of pity;
- refusal of imposed salvation.

It can also harden into:

- refusal of help;
- death orientation;
- role fixation;
- contempt for civilians;
- inability to imagine peace;
- pressure on other Eighty-Six to conform.

Treat both sides.

Likewise, distinguish:

- a **person** being irreplaceable to loved ones;
- a **military function** being dangerously dependent on one exceptional individual.

Heroic necessity may be evidence of institutional failure.

---

## Pass 12 — Freedom and paternalism

Do not treat freedom as binary.

Track:

- legal freedom;
- material capacity;
- education;
- knowledge;
- social support;
- ability to refuse;
- alternative livelihoods;
- political participation;
- psychological ability to choose;
- responsibility;
- vulnerability to demagogues or savior figures.

Key question:

> **What makes freedom usable?**

A benevolent actor can still become paternalistic by defining another person's good without sufficient room for revision or refusal.

---

## Pass 13 — Ethics of command, violence, and responsibility

For every major ethical conflict distinguish:

- necessity;
- legality;
- proportionality;
- authority;
- agency;
- foreseeable harm;
- alternatives;
- role obligation;
- personal responsibility;
- institutional responsibility;
- moral injury;
- retrospective judgment.

Do not use “war is hell” as a substitute for analysis.

Ask who constructed the conditions under which the terrible choice became necessary.

When evaluating a character, state both:

- the strongest defense;
- the strongest criticism.

---

## Pass 14 — Ordinary life and future imagination

The series repeatedly contrasts military existence with ordinary life.

Track concrete future-bearing acts:

- travel;
- school;
- art;
- meals;
- celebrations;
- clothing;
- career discussion;
- romance;
- family;
- housing;
- domestic routines;
- return promises.

Do not dismiss these as “breather scenes.”

They may be the primary evidence that a character is becoming capable of imagining time beyond the next battle.

---

## Pass 15 — Japanese voice and relational language

For every major recurring character, build a voice profile.

Track:

- first-person pronouns;
- titles and rank address;
- given name vs family name;
- nicknames;
- sentence endings;
- politeness;
- military register;
- slang;
- emotional disfluency;
- ellipsis;
- code-switches between official and private speech;
- euphemism;
- repeated metaphors;
- speech changes under fear, intimacy, command, grief, anger, or embarrassment.

Relationships often become legible before characters explicitly define them.

Forms of address and register change should receive their own cumulative ledger.

---

## Pass 16 — Motifs and symbolic systems

Track recurring material without assuming every recurrence is thematic.

Potential systems to test include:

- names and records;
- graves and memorials;
- headless imagery;
- flowers;
- hands;
- voices;
- radios / communication;
- blood and color;
- fences / walls / borders;
- fields, roads, sea, sky;
- uniforms versus civilian clothes;
- food;
- drawings;
- medals and marks;
- mechanical remains;
- home;
- doors and thresholds;
- birds / flight where textually sustained.

For each motif ask:

1. where does it recur?
2. in whose focalization?
3. does its meaning remain stable?
4. is it visual, linguistic, narrative, or all three?
5. does later material revise it?

---

## Pass 17 — Adversarial hypothesis test

End every volume with at least three hypotheses.

For each hypothesis record:

- supporting evidence;
- counterevidence;
- alternative interpretation;
- confidence level;
- what future evidence would falsify it.

Examples of corpus-wide hypotheses to test rather than assume:

1. The series expands personhood from recognition to the conditions required for revisable life.
2. The Eighty-Six's pride is simultaneously anti-dehumanization technology and a potential prison.
3. The series is anti-war without being pacifist.
4. Home is chosen and relational rather than reducible to birthplace.
5. Mature interdependence is distinguished from saviorism and total self-sufficiency.
6. The Legion literalizes the dead becoming unable to revise their final commands.
7. Exceptional heroism is treated as both admirable and institutionally dangerous.
8. Shin's arc moves from death-facing function toward future-bearing selfhood.
9. Lena's arc moves from witnessing injustice toward responsibility without omnipotent guilt.
10. Romance matters because it changes each protagonist's relation to future time, not because it merely rewards survival.

Any of these may be narrowed or rejected.

# IX. Special protocols for major analytical domains

## 1. Shin

Do not define Shin only through “death” or “Undertaker.”

Track separately:

- sensory burden;
- brother and family;
- execution of comrades;
- leadership;
- relation to names and tags;
- violence;
- usefulness;
- loneliness;
- survivor identity;
- dependence on Spearhead;
- relation to Lena;
- desire;
- ordinary future;
- inability or ability to imagine self outside combat.

Question:

> **When does Undertaker function as chosen responsibility, and when does it become a role that prevents Shin from being authored by anything else?**

---

## 2. Lena

Do not define Lena only as “idealistic Alba who learns reality.”

Track:

- institutional authority;
- class/racial position;
- guilt;
- witness;
- command responsibility;
- political consciousness;
- coercive capacity;
- willingness to use people;
- moral rhetoric;
- relationship to Republic collapse;
- relationship to the Eighty-Six as individuals rather than representatives;
- dependence on subordinates and peers;
- desire;
- limits of responsibility.

Question:

> **Can Lena act against structural evil without turning responsibility into a claim that she must personally save everyone?**

---

## 3. Spearhead / Eighty-Six collective

Do not treat the Eighty-Six as a single trauma culture.

Track disagreements among:

- Shin;
- Raiden;
- Anju;
- Theo;
- Kurena;
- later Eighty-Six groups;
- older/younger Processors;
- different national or cultural contexts.

Question:

> **What is shared cultural adaptation, what is individual personality, and what is later mythologization of “the Eighty-Six”?**

---

## 4. The Republic

Treat San Magnolia as a functioning apparatus of dehumanization.

Analyze:

- law;
- census/classification;
- military bureaucracy;
- media;
- language;
- urban geography;
- schools;
- consumption;
- memory;
- complicity;
- bystanders;
- dissent;
- post-collapse behavior.

Distinguish:

- ideological believers;
- opportunists;
- passive beneficiaries;
- dissenters;
- resisters;
- children;
- later survivors.

Avoid collective moral flattening when the text itself differentiates.

---

## 5. The Federacy

Treat Giad as a comparative political experiment, not moral absolution.

Track:

- citizenship;
- welfare;
- military command;
- democratic/republican legitimacy;
- imperial inheritance;
- child protection;
- paternalism;
- exceptional-unit dependence;
- political use of symbols;
- public attitudes;
- treatment of Frederica;
- treatment of the Eighty-Six.

Question:

> **What can a relatively humane state still fail to understand about people formed by atrocity?**

---

## 6. The Legion

Analyze at three levels:

### Military
Enemy system, force structure, sensors, production, doctrine.

### Metaphysical / cognitive
Memory, copied minds, preserved final states, identity continuity.

### Thematic
Death made operational; command without revision; human desire converted into permanent machinery.

Do not let the thematic reading erase literal mechanics.

Do not let the literal mechanics prevent philosophical analysis.

# X. Legacy-analysis audit

The prior synthesis through Volume 12 should be treated as a structured set of hypotheses.

Create `86_LEGACY_V1_VS_V2_REVISION_LEDGER.md`.

For each major legacy claim record:

- exact claim in concise form;
- source volumes implicated;
- strongest confirming evidence;
- strongest complicating evidence;
- whether the claim was too broad;
- whether it confused character belief with narrator stance;
- whether later material changes it;
- V2 disposition.

Special priority should be given to prior claims about:

- recognition → conditions;
- anti-war framing;
- pride;
- heroism;
- childhood / interrupted development;
- body as political territory;

The goal is not to embarrass the first pass. It is to make improvement visible.

# XI. Checkpoint syntheses

Do not wait until the final volume to synthesize.

Use narrative transitions rather than a rigid numerical interval.

A checkpoint should contain:

- current series thesis;
- character architecture;
- relationship map;
- institutional map;
- military-state update;
- Japanese voice changes;
- confirmed motifs;
- open hypotheses;
- legacy claims currently strengthened or weakened.

Checkpoint documents are provisional and should be marked as such.

They are scaffolding, not final authority.

---

# XII. Search and retrieval protocol

Search is a retrieval tool, not a substitute for reading.

Use targeted retrieval for:

- names;
- forms of address;
- recurring political terms;
- racial terminology;
- freedom / choice language;
- home / return language;
- death / memory vocabulary;
- military ranks and doctrine;
- repeated metaphors;
- specific technologies;
- character-specific speech habits.

Every retrieved hit used analytically must be read in local context.

Do not build claims from concordance counts alone.

Frequency may tell us what to inspect. It does not tell us what the word means in each occurrence.

# XIII. Final synthesis standard

The final multi-document synthesis should satisfy five conditions.

## 1. Traceability
A major conclusion can be routed:

> synthesis claim → specialist document → volume analysis / evidence ledger → source locator → original Japanese scene.

## 2. Anti-duplication
Every major question has one primary home.

## 3. Chronology
Later insight does not erase earlier ambiguity.

## 4. Adversarial strength
Every major normative or philosophical claim has been tested against counterevidence.

## 5. Human readability
The corpus remains readable as criticism rather than becoming only a database.

# XVI. Governing question

The first synthesis asked, in broad terms:

> **What does *86* argue about recognizing people as human?**

The V2 pass should ask something harder:

> **How, volume by volume and voice by voice, does the series distinguish being recognized as a person from actually possessing the material, institutional, relational, and temporal freedom to continue becoming one—and where does the text resist our cleanest formulation of that argument?**
