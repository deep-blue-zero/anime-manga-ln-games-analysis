# 86 Phase 7 — Contradiction and Adversarial Audit

## Scope and authority note

This document is the canonical Phase-7 adversarial audit of the V2 specialist corpus and of the `active_provisional` capstone, `18_FULL_SERIES_CONTINUOUS_SYNTHESIS.md`, at SHA-256 `9f19fd985aaf292890adc1dffc31bf6253c970aa1dd0cb0eef8f3a379efb6704`. It does not replace the canonical specialist documents, the chronological evidence ledger, the Phase-5 locks, or the original Japanese sources. Its task is deliberately hostile:

> **Assume the most attractive V2 formulations are overstated, identify the strongest alternative reading, and retain a thesis only after its scope, evidence class, counterevidence, and unresolved limit have been made explicit.**

The source boundary remains locked original-Japanese Volumes 1–14, with Alter.1 in its audited supplemental role and Alter.2 excluded from mainline evidence. Nothing in this audit uses V15+ assumptions.

The audit result is:

> **PASS WITH MANDATORY REVISION.**

Document 18 contains no fatal contradiction with the canonical specialist layer or Phase-5 locks. Its principal argument survives. It is not ready for canonical promotion. Nine wording, scope, scale, and balance revisions are mandatory before promotion, and Phase 8 must still verify the load-bearing Japanese in full source context.

The most important finding is that **exhaustive foreclosure survives only as a second-order diagnostic of one recurring effect—loss of legitimate answer-space—not as a common cause, ontology, or universal remedy.** Racism, disability, military command, romantic dependence, medical authority, artificial design, grief, and constitutional regression remain analytically distinct.

---

# I. Executive audit verdict

The provisional capstone succeeds at the most difficult task it set itself: it integrates the series without reverting to the V1 error of treating endless revisability as the supreme good. It consistently affirms durable pride, vocation, citizenship, memory, love, family, obligation, and inherited objects. It preserves all forty-one T14 counterevidence constraints and all thirty-two final-arc questions. It refuses Republic/Giad equivalence, romance-as-cure, anti-heroic reduction, biological personhood shortcuts, and Volume-14 closure.

The strongest case against the capstone is nevertheless serious. Its preferred vocabulary—**standing, answer-space, living continuity, recoverability, and exhaustive foreclosure**—can make unlike phenomena sound as though they share one mechanism. A racial state revoking citizenship, a lover speaking possessively, a disabled soldier confronting changed function, and a Shepherd unable to revise a terminal command can all be described as threats to answer-space. That resemblance is real at the level of effect. It does not explain why the effects arise, what duties follow, who is responsible, or what remedy is appropriate.

The audit therefore draws a hard line:

> **The master vocabulary may organize comparison after the property has been named. It may not replace the domain-specific account.**

A valid use looks like this:

- San Magnolia closes legal and political standing through racial denaturalization, concentration, expropriation, military euphemism, and survivor elimination.
- Medical paternalism can close bodily authority through diagnosis, detention, or reassignment.
- A romantic relation can become possessive through fear, dependency, interpretive confidence, or hostage leverage.
- Legion command architecture can preserve local intelligence while closing revision of the terminal end.

Only after those mechanisms are established may the analysis observe that all four can produce a loss of answer-space. The common description does not make them morally, historically, or causally equivalent.

No core thesis is rejected. The capstone requires nine mandatory revisions before it can become canonical. The revisions are not cosmetic. They prevent the continuous synthesis from becoming smoother than the corpus it summarizes.

# II. Audit method, states, and authority hierarchy

The audit applies five tests to every major claim.

1. **Source test:** Does the claim remain supported by the locked evidence and the relevant canonical specialist?
2. **Steelman test:** What is the strongest plausible counterreading rather than the easiest caricature?
3. **Mechanism test:** Does the claim name the specific causal, institutional, bodily, relational, linguistic, or technical mechanism?
4. **Scale test:** Does the claim distinguish personal, relational, tactical, operational, strategic, political-constitutional, and metaphysical levels?
5. **Boundary test:** Does the claim remain inside the V14 source boundary and preserve open questions as open?

The audit uses six dispositions:

| Disposition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `PASS` | The claim survives in its current substantive form. |
| `PASS_WITH_NARROWING` | The central claim survives, but its scope must be made more explicit. |
| `PASS_WITH_MANDATORY_REVISION` | The claim survives only after a specified capstone revision. |
| `OPEN_PRESERVED` | Directional evidence exists, but V14 does not authorize closure. |
| `DOWNGRADE` | A claim remains useful as inference or analogy but not at its prior confidence or breadth. |
| `REJECT` | The proposed claim conflicts with the locked corpus. |

The authority order remains:

> locked Japanese source → Phase-5 locator/correction locks → sequential deep readings → longitudinal ledgers and T01–T14 dossiers → Documents 01–17 → Document 18 → this audit’s revision routing.

Document 18 remains `active_provisional`. This audit is canonical for what must be changed in that provisional draft, but it does not silently revise the draft itself.

# III. The central self-attack: has “exhaustive foreclosure” become another totalizing master abstraction?

## 3.1 The strongest case against the framework

The criticism has five parts.

### A. It risks low falsifiability

Almost any serious harm can be redescribed as closing a future: racism closes citizenship; injury closes bodily possibilities; grief closes emotional possibility; military command closes refusal; romance closes independence; posthuman command closes terminal-end revision. If every harm becomes foreclosure, the concept stops discriminating.

### B. It risks causal flattening

San Magnolia’s racial-genocidal administration and Shin/Lena’s possessive language do not arise from the same cause. One is a state system of law, territory, expropriation, labor, propaganda, and planned witness elimination. The other emerges from love, fear, dependency, asymmetric knowledge, and emergency. Calling both foreclosure can be accurate only after the causal difference has been protected.

### C. It risks normative smuggling

The framework can imply that openness is always better than stability. The text does not support that implication. Pride, promises, military vocation, citizenship, family, love, and inherited objects can remain stable and life-giving. Some choices legitimately close alternatives. A commitment is not unhealthy merely because it is difficult to reverse.

### D. It risks lexical reification

`exhaustive foreclosure`, `living continuity`, `answer-space`, and `interruptible interdependence` are analytical glosses. They are not hidden Japanese master terms. Document 12 and Document 16 demonstrate that the source instead distributes the relevant distinctions across multiple lexical, grammatical, address, and modal systems.

### E. It risks protagonist selection bias

Because the capstone is organized around Shin, Lena, their relationship, and the political structures most directly touching them, the master thesis may look stronger than it would if Grethe, Shiden, Rito, Annette, Marcel, Vika, Lerche, and other context-major figures received equal space. The corpus must demonstrate that the framework illuminates without requiring every character to become an instance of it.

## 3.2 Why the framework survives

The concept survives because the canonical corpus already contains counterexamples that limit it.

- Strong Eighty-Six pride remains legitimate.
- Military vocation can remain chosen and meaningful.
- Shin/Lena can be deeply dependent without the bond becoming pure ownership.
- Fido can possess a stable designed purpose without being exhausted by simple programming.
- Citizenship can be durable and indispensable.
- Memory can remain a duty without requiring revenge.
- An inherited object can preserve history without dictating one future use.

These cases falsify the proposition that intensity, durability, or exclusivity of commitment alone constitutes foreclosure. The threshold is narrower: **the role or relation acquires exclusive jurisdiction over standing and eliminates legitimate answer, refusal, accommodation, or divergent future.**

The concept also survives because the specialist corpus specifies different mechanisms and remedies. Document 05 requires legal and reparative analysis of genocide. Document 08 requires bodily, medical, and property-by-property personhood analysis. Document 10 requires relationship and command-boundary analysis. Document 11 requires technical separation of memory, personality, identity, and terminal command. The master concept cannot do that work by itself.

## 3.3 Revised canonical formulation

The Phase-7-approved version is:

> **Exhaustive foreclosure is a second-order comparative description of a recurring effect: a role, wound, category, body, relation, institution, grievance, or inherited purpose becomes the sole condition under which a subject continues to count, and legitimate answer-space collapses. It is not a common cause, a source-language keyword, a complete theory of harm, or a universal remedy.**

This formulation passes. Document 18 must carry the boundary at first use rather than postponing it to the later counterthesis.

**Verdict:** `PASS_WITH_MANDATORY_REVISION`.

# IV. Adversarial audit of the positive vocabulary

The negative concept is not the only possible source of overreach. The positive terms require independent limits.

## 4.1 Standing

Standing names the condition under which a subject’s claims matter to law, institutions, relationships, or moral judgment. It is useful because San Magnolia’s central act is to make humanity politically inoperative. It is not identical to identity, autonomy, health, happiness, or flourishing.

Theo retains standing after amputation while losing real capability. A detained artificial subject might possess standing while lacking freedom. A citizen may retain formal status while social conditions make participation dangerous. A loved person may retain relational standing while remaining misunderstood.

Document 18 sometimes lets `standing` carry too much positive content. Phase 7 requires an explicit sentence:

> **Standing is a floor of claim-bearing recognition, not a complete account of agency, identity continuity, health, or flourishing.**

**Verdict:** `PASS_WITH_MANDATORY_REVISION`.

## 4.2 Answer-space

Answer-space does not mean unlimited options, costless refusal, or freedom from obligation. It means that the subject’s response can still matter and that the role or authority does not pre-decide every legitimate answer.

Military command can narrow options drastically without eliminating all answer-space. Emergency interruption can suspend judgment temporarily without claiming it permanently. A lifelong promise can close some alternatives while remaining answerable. A dangerous body can require safeguards while preserving the person’s participation in those safeguards.

**Verdict:** `PASS_WITH_NARROWING`.

## 4.3 Living continuity

Living continuity is useful because it preserves the value of durable identity against V1’s tendency to make change itself normative. It must not imply that every continuity is literally alive, that persistence guarantees moral value, or that continuity without revision is always captivity.

The phrase is strongest when it names a relation that preserves history while allowing changed use: a name fragment, military vocation, citizenship, love, a unit identity, an inherited title, or an object. It is weakest when used to adjudicate metaphysical identity in artificial subjects.

**Verdict:** `PASS_WITH_DOMAIN_LIMIT`.

## 4.4 Recoverability and return

This is the most vulnerable positive term. Document 12 and 16 separate `帰る`, `戻る`, `還る`, `帰還`, and `生還`; Document 14 rejects one universal return metaphor. The capstone largely preserves that distinction and explicitly names irreversibility. It still uses English `return` often enough that the analogy can begin to look like lexical unity.

The approved Phase-7 rule is:

> **Use “return” only after naming what returns and by what mechanism: a person comes home; a force returns alive; judgment recovers; legal standing is restored; a polity re-enters corrigible order; a dead-derived continuity is released. These are analogically related, not one event.**

**Verdict:** `PASS_WITH_MANDATORY_REVISION`.

# V. Stress test 1 — “The Republic is evil; Giad is good.”

## 5.1 Why the simplification is tempting

The contrast is real. San Magnolia denaturalizes, concentrates, expropriates, exploits, administratively erases, and plans the disappearance of surviving witnesses. Giad grants citizenship, housing, medicine, education, money, military status, public casualty recognition, officer training, and an expectation of return. Treating those differences as cosmetic would be analytically perverse.

The capstone is therefore correct to refuse false equivalence. The Republic’s `unmanned` system begins from the exclusion of operators from counted humanity [V01-L002–V01-L006]. Giad’s rescue and citizenship materially restore reciprocal obligation, even where Ernst’s preferred model of recovery becomes a `哀れみの檻` [V02-L025; V02-L028; V02-L031].

## 5.2 The strongest counterargument

Giad’s V13 regression is not minor. It includes scapegoating, coercive reserve politics, proposals to transfer danger to refugees and socially weak groups, Fawn weaponization, hostage leverage, and a front closed from both directions [V13-L043; V13-L119–V13-L127]. A reader can reasonably argue that the difference between good and evil institutions becomes unstable under existential pressure.

## 5.3 Why the capstone survives

The counterargument demonstrates fragility, not identity. Giad still possesses records of its prior obligations, investigation, apology, internal disagreement, distributed institutions, and citizens attempting repair. Rito’s killer is investigated; command apologizes; Shin refuses category-wide demonization [V13-L108–V13-L111]. Those mechanisms do not cancel the regression. They establish why regression is a meaningful failure of a better order rather than proof that no difference ever existed.

The correct comparison remains asymmetric:

- **San Magnolia:** racial denaturalization, mass expropriation, concentration, technical nonpersonhood, zero-casualty fiction, and planned survivor elimination.
- **Giad V13:** recognized citizens subjected to scapegoating, concentrated exposure, narrowing refusal, hostage coercion, and blocked return during institutional breakdown.

The shared properties justify a Sector-relation comparison. The unmatched genealogy prevents historical equivalence.

## 5.4 Audit verdict

Document 18 passes. Its language should continue to state Giad’s superiority positively rather than only through the negative formula “not San Magnolia.” Its moral achievement lies in actual citizenship, capability, professional military institutions, and routes of correction. Its failure lies in not protecting those achievements against fear.

**Verdict:** `PASS_WITH_ASYMMETRY`.

# VI. Stress test 2 — “The Eighty-Six simply need to stop fighting.”

## 6.1 The strongest case for the simplification

They are former child soldiers whose competence was produced through persecution. Continued fighting exposes them to death, narrows ordinary life, and reinforces the institution’s dependence on their exceptional skill. The series repeatedly shows bodies, relationships, and futures damaged by military service. A straightforward anti-child-soldier reading can therefore conclude that recovery requires civilianization.

## 6.2 The strongest case against it

The Five do not merely fail to imagine alternatives. They also value competence, comradeship, duty, tactical mastery, protection of others, and military vocation. Giad’s officer-school compromise is ethically superior to Ernst’s compulsory normality because it enlarges future roles without making soldierhood proof of pathology [V02-L028; V02-L031].

The corpus also distinguishes historical conditioning from absence of agency. Their choice is shaped by war-made identity and limited civilian experience, but it remains a choice. The correct phrase remains **chosen vocation under historical gravity**.

## 6.3 The unresolved boundary

The source has not shown the postwar environment in which military necessity no longer supplies the next task. T14-C16 and T14-OQ-28 remain open. A later volume could reveal that some members continue military careers, some leave, some adapt to disability, and some discover that earlier preference depended on crisis. Phase 7 may not decide the distribution.

The ethical test is not whether they stop fighting. It is whether military service remains revisable in use; whether injury, dissent, transfer, or retirement preserves standing; and whether institutions can release exceptional soldiers without treating departure as betrayal.

## 6.4 Audit verdict

Document 18 passes. Its emphasis on civilian alternatives, ordinary capability, and institutional release prevents soldierhood from becoming mandatory. Its refusal to make civilianization the normative endpoint prevents the opposite paternalism.

**Verdict:** `PASS_WITH_OPEN_VOCATION_BOUNDARY`.

# VII. Stress test 3 — “Pride is purely pathological.”

## 7.1 Why the simplification is tempting

Eighty-Six pride can make pity intolerable, discourage treatment, sustain combat identity, and render ordinary safety morally suspect. It can function as the internal voice of a persecutory system after escape. A therapeutic reading can therefore describe pride as trauma armor that must be discarded.

## 7.2 Decisive counterevidence

Pride is also the language through which the Eighty-Six refuse the Republic’s definition of them as livestock, machines, or passive victims. It protects dignity, solidarity, memory, and authority over one’s own response. Volume 8’s decisive wording is not a rejection of `誇り`; it rejects the exclusive construction `誇りしかないとはもう言わない` [V08-L033].

The restrictive `しか` changes the thesis. Pride becomes dangerous when it is the only admissible ground of worth, not because pride itself is inherently unhealthy.

## 7.3 Audit verdict

Document 18 passes strongly. It treats pride as durable anti-dehumanization and isolates the danger in exclusivity. The capstone should retain the Japanese anchor in Phase 8 because the English distinction depends materially on the restrictive construction.

**Verdict:** `PASS`.

# VIII. Stress test 4 — “Lena saves the Eighty-Six.”

## 8.1 The strongest case for the claim

Lena’s interventions save lives. She learns names, improves technical command, uses artillery and institutional knowledge, continues fighting after the Republic’s abandonment, becomes a competent commander, and helps make return imaginable. Her voice and eventual embodied presence matter profoundly to Shin and Spearhead.

A causal account that minimized her would be false.

## 8.2 The strongest counterargument

The Eighty-Six possess agency, ordinary culture, ethics, competence, and mutual care before Lena knows them. Theo corrects her. Spearhead limits what she can know and do. Raiden, Anju, Theo, Kurena, Frederica, Fido, Shiden, Grethe, Annette, Rito, and others provide support, institutional capacity, correction, memory, and practical survival. Giad’s material institutions matter. Shin’s own choices matter.

Lena also has limits. Her concern can become overresponsibility. Her insight can become interpretive confidence. Her love can become self-spending or possessive. Maximum Para-RAID intervention in V14 saves Shin and endangers her body [V14-L173–V14-L179].

## 8.3 Audit verdict

Document 18 rejects Lena-as-savior explicitly and therefore passes. Its continuous form still underrepresents some of the non-Lena causal ecology. Phase 7 requires a bridge paragraph naming the independent work of context-major figures and institutions so that “Lena does not save them alone” becomes visible structurally rather than only asserted.

**Verdict:** `PASS_WITH_DISTRIBUTED_CAUSALITY` and `P7-R07`.

# IX. Stress test 5 — “Shin’s problem is simply depression or trauma.”

## 9.1 Why a clinical shorthand is tempting

Shin experiences repeated bereavement, intrusive dead voices, emotional constriction, self-expendability, bodily neglect, dissociation-like distance, abandonment fear, and acute collapse. Trauma and depression vocabulary can describe parts of this pattern.

## 9.2 Why the shorthand fails

The text does not reduce Shin to a diagnosis, and Phase 7 cannot supply one from outside the corpus. His development involves at least eight separable structures:

1. historical death-seeking;
2. present aversion to dying;
3. self-expendability;
4. Undertaker as a socially assembled office;
5. responsibility to the dead;
6. sensory ability and military function;
7. fear of abandonment and dependency;
8. positive future desire.

These variables do not move together. V14 shows future desire coexisting with sensory concealment and self-instrumentalization. The Gentle World counterfactual denies that his wounds or Death-God identity were necessary: `傷なんて、負わなくてよかった`; `おれは死神でいなくてよかった` [V10-L060].

Shin is also not defined only by crisis evidence. Libraries, museums, food, curiosity, teasing, command, and ordinary relationships belong to the same character. His compressed voice expands in what it can admit rather than becoming conventionally therapeutic.

## 9.3 Audit verdict

Document 18 passes. It uses trauma descriptively without making a clinical diagnosis the causal master key. Phase 8 must verify the load-bearing Japanese anchors and the focalization around V14’s breakdown.

**Verdict:** `PASS_WITH_NONCLINICAL_RECONSTRUCTION`.

# X. Stress test 6 — “The romance heals both protagonists.”

## 10.1 The strongest case for the claim

Shin and Lena make survival and future emotionally addressable for one another. Their voices persist across absence. Embodied reunion changes the relation. Shin asks not to be left behind. Lena asks him to return. Confession, touch, reliance, and rescue make dependence speakable. V14’s intervention restores Shin from the No Face encounter.

Romance is not incidental.

## 10.2 The strongest counterargument

Neither protagonist begins as an empty subject awaiting the other. Shin’s capacities and relations include Rei, prior squadrons, Raiden, Fido, Frederica, military practice, curiosity, ordinary life, and his own decisions. Lena’s conscience, command development, institutional learning, Shiden, Annette, Grethe, and her own political commitments are not authored by Shin.

The relationship also retains risk. It contains possessive rhetoric—`その人はわたしのものだ！`—dependency, fear, asymmetry, and hostage vulnerability [V14-L175–V14-L179]. Lena can interpret too confidently; Shin can conceal bodily harm. Love makes future legible without making the future materially secure.

## 10.3 Audit verdict

The capstone’s concept of **interruptible interdependence** survives. It is strongest when intervention returns judgment and when the couple remains embedded in a plural support ecology. It must not become a disguised purity claim.

Document 18 passes substantively but requires the ensemble-balance revision `P7-R07` and the analytical-gloss revision `P7-R09`.

**Verdict:** `PASS_WITH_BALANCE_REVISION`.

# XI. Stress test 7 — “The Legion are only a metaphor for the dead.”

## 11.1 Why the metaphor is powerful

The Legion literalize unfinished relation, weaponized memory, voices without bodies, grief, inherited commands, and the persistence of final wounds. Rei, Kiriya, Aldrecht, Zelene, revenge Shepherds, and No Face make the battlefield a technological archive of the dead.

## 11.2 Why “only metaphor” fails

The Legion are a literal military and technological system with differentiated processors, command hierarchy, tactical adaptation, network authority, industrial reproduction, sensor systems, and technical constraints. Black Sheep, Sheepdogs, Shepherds, Phonix, Noctiluca, Zelene, and No Face preserve different properties. Human neural origin does not establish one metaphysical category.

The Phonix is adaptive without established personality. Noctiluca preserves person-derived cognition while damaging singular identity. Kiriya possesses `人格` and `意志` while remaining unable to disobey superior command [V03-L035]. No Face’s conversion account is an interested claim, not omniscient engineering documentation [V14-L135–V14-L147].

## 11.3 Audit verdict

Document 18 passes. It preserves literal mechanics and uses philosophical interpretation only after disaggregating the properties. The No Face/Vaclav mechanism remains open.

**Verdict:** `PASS`.

# XII. Stress test 8 — “The series is pacifist.”

## 12.1 The strongest case for the reading

The novels depict child soldierhood, mass death, bodily ruin, political exploitation, heroic consumption, and technological persistence of war. Peace is a central desired horizon. The series repeatedly criticizes institutions that make violence normal and use exceptional people as permanent solutions.

## 12.2 The strongest counterargument

The novels admire tactical competence, courage, command, professionalism, loyalty, logistics, engineering, and justified force. Some battles must be fought. Some lethal actions protect civilians or free human-derived continuities from military exploitation. The series does not present refusal of all violence as morally sufficient.

The anti-hero statements are institutional. `英雄など今の世に、あるべきではない` [V07-L002] and `英雄とはもはや、弱者の戦術だ` [V05-L042] criticize force design dependent on singular heroes. They do not deny the reality or value of heroic acts.

## 12.3 The scale problem

The capstone must keep four levels visible:

- **tactical:** Was force necessary in the immediate engagement?
- **operational:** Did the mission include support, extraction, reserves, and return?
- **strategic:** Did the action advance a viable war aim and preserve the force?
- **political-constitutional:** Who authorized the burden, whose lives carried it, and did citizenship/refusal/return survive?

A tactically necessary attack may remain politically unjust. An operational success may be strategically consuming. A heroic act may expose institutional weakness.

## 12.4 Audit verdict

Document 18 passes the pacifist stress test but requires explicit level labels at several cross-scale claims. This is `P7-R08`.

**Verdict:** `PASS_WITH_MILITARY_LEVEL_GUARD`.

# XIII. Stress test 9 — “A humane institution solves dehumanization.”

## 13.1 Why the claim is tempting

Giad’s citizenship, medicine, education, housing, military professionalism, and public recognition solve real problems created by San Magnolia. A humane institution can transform life materially.

## 13.2 Why the claim fails as completion

Recognition can coexist with disposability. The Fawns are known to be human and still designed as dangerous sacrificial bodies [V13-L019–V13-L027]. Medicine can become custody. Education can become compulsory normalization. Citizenship can become hollow when return and refusal close. Democratic institutions can regress. Love and household care can become paternalistic.

The correct institutional thesis is not “good institutions solve the problem.” It is:

> **Humane institutions create enforceable standing, capability, and routes of correction; their humanity is tested when fear makes those routes expensive.**

## 13.3 Audit verdict

Document 18 passes. It treats Giad as a genuine improvement and an incomplete achievement. It preserves corrigibility rather than institutional moral identity as the relevant standard.

**Verdict:** `PASS`.

# XIV. Stress test 10 — “Personhood equals biological humanity.”

## 14.1 Why biological humanity remains decisive in one domain

San Magnolia’s racial lie is not vindicated by the later artificial-subject problem. The Eighty-Six are biological humans whose humanity is denied for political convenience. Their rights do not depend on passing a sophisticated personhood test.

## 14.2 Why biology cannot settle every later case

Fido, Sirins, Shepherds, Phonix, Noctiluca, Zelene, and No Face distribute morally relevant properties across different substrates and continuity structures. Artificial origin is not proof of inferior standing. Human neural origin is not proof that the original person continues. Intelligence is not proof of personality. Memory is not proof of numerical identity. Personality is not proof of terminal-end freedom.

The correct method remains property-by-property:

- present subjectivity and interests;
- vulnerability;
- autobiographical continuity;
- personality/self-model;
- singular identity integrity;
- relation-bearing particularity;
- refusal and judgment;
- body continuity;
- designed or inherited purpose;
- command constraint;
- and civic claim.

## 14.3 Audit verdict

Document 18 passes. Its phrase “personhood is a question of consequence,” however, must be revised because moral standing is not created by institutional response. That is `P7-R03`.

**Verdict:** `PASS_WITH_MANDATORY_WORDING_REVISION`.

# XV. Additional adversarial attacks required by T14

The architecture’s ten required simplifications are not exhaustive. Phase 7 applies further attacks drawn from T14.

## 15.1 “Future desire cures bodily self-instrumentalization.”

Rejected. Shin wants a future and still conceals sensory loss to protect frontline usefulness [V14-L191–V14-L199]. Positive desire and harmful self-use can coexist.

## 15.2 “Disability becomes meaningful because it opens a new path.”

Rejected. Theo loses a real body and combat future. His later options matter because standing survives before replacement utility is found [V08-L056]. New meaning does not redeem injury.

## 15.3 “Citizenship is merely paperwork compared with authentic home.”

Rejected. Denaturalization is one of San Magnolia’s foundational crimes. Citizenship does not create felt home, but it creates reciprocal legal obligations that affect residence, medicine, records, and protection.

## 15.4 “Home is purely chosen.”

Rejected as a binary. Birthplace, homeland, citizenship, household, blood relation, memory, unit, beloved person, and chosen destination can coexist. San Magnolia can remain origin and crime scene without becoming the required destination.

## 15.5 “Memory is always healthy.”

Rejected. Names, records, recipes, photographs, and objects resist erasure. Memory can also demand imitation, preserve grievance as purpose, or turn the living into monuments.

## 15.6 “Initial consent makes a binding permanently healthy.”

Narrowed. Consent matters, but a role, promise, vocation, or relationship must remain interpretable and answerable over time. Prior consent is not an unlimited waiver of bodily or political authority.

## 15.7 “Interruption is agency-preserving because it prevents harm.”

Narrowed. Raiden, Lena, and Shiden provide positive emergency examples where intervention returns judgment. Pity, surveillance, indefinite medical custody, or interpretive sovereignty provide negative controls.

Open. Narrative direction is near-conclusive, while incorporation, substrate, interruption, numerical identity, and terminal-end revision remain unresolved [V14-L095–V14-L098; V14-L203–V14-L204].

Rejected. V14 explicitly opens the final arc. Constitutional order, emergency powers, reparations, Fawn futures, artificial standing, Shin’s body, Lena’s responsibility, Spearhead divergence, war termination, and postwar identity remain open.

## 15.10 “Peace stabilizes identity and ends the becoming problem.”

Open and methodologically unsafe. Peace could expand ordinary capability or impose a new compulsory normality. The corpus has not shown the answer.

# XVII. Military-level and institutional-scale audit

Document 18 generally distinguishes competence, necessity, heroism, and legitimacy. Three compression risks remain.

## 17.1 “Return” across levels

Operational return means extraction, survival, resupply, and force preservation. Political-constitutional return means the state continues to recognize the exposed person as someone expected to come back. Relational return means another person waits and makes survival matter. These can reinforce one another but are not one doctrine.

## 17.2 “Necessity” across levels

An action can be tactically necessary because the enemy must be stopped immediately. That does not establish that the campaign was operationally well-designed, that the strategy was sustainable, or that the burden was politically legitimate. The capstone should label the level whenever it moves from battlefield action to moral judgment.

## 17.3 “Heroism” across levels

Heroic action can be ethically admirable at the personal level while revealing strategic and institutional weakness. Distributed command can reduce single-point failure while preserving the singular value of exceptional people. Redundancy applies to functions, not persons.

## 17.4 Audit verdict

Document 18 survives but requires `P7-R08`. Phase 8 should verify the Japanese anti-hero anchors and military vocabulary.

# XVIII. Return-language and recoverability audit

Document 18 explicitly acknowledges lexical plurality and irreversibility. The audit nevertheless finds that the English prose occasionally reunifies what Document 12 and 16 separated.

The main vulnerability is not quotation error. It is **analogical drift**.

- `帰る` can orient movement toward home, destination, or addressee.
- `戻る` can mark re-entry, restoration, recovery, or reactionary return to a prior state.
- `還る` can mark deathward or ontological release in relevant contexts.
- `帰還` and `生還` belong first to operational/military return.

The capstone may compare these only after the specific property is named. `recoverability` remains a useful English analytical gloss for the capacity to re-enter standing, relation, or functioning. It is not a direct translation of all five systems.

The highest-risk capstone sentences are routed in `P7-R01`, `P7-R04`, and `P7-R05`.

# XXIII. Constitutive tensions that should not be “resolved”

Several tensions survive because the text values both terms.

## 23.1 Pride and openness

Pride can preserve dignity and narrow future imagination. The correct response is not to choose one description universally; it is to ask when pride remains one source of standing and when it becomes the only one.

## 23.2 Protection and authority

Protection can be necessary. A commander, doctor, parent, friend, or lover may have reason to interrupt. The tension remains whether intervention returns judgment or expands into permanent jurisdiction.

## 23.3 Vocation and conditioning

Military service can be authentic and historically conditioned. The corpus does not permit conditioning to erase agency or consent to erase developmental history.

## 23.4 Memory and future

Remembering the dead can be fidelity, archive, grief, and political obligation. It can also become compulsory imitation or grievance capture. Neither forgetting nor permanent custodianship is the universal answer.

## 23.5 Excellence and redundancy

Exceptional people matter. Institutions should not make their permanent self-expenditure the ordinary operating model. Functional redundancy protects persons precisely because persons are not fungible.

## 23.6 Citizenship and belonging

Citizenship is indispensable. It does not automatically create felt home. The tension is additive, not a hierarchy in which “authentic belonging” makes law superficial.

## 23.7 Love and dependence

Love can make the future addressable and still create vulnerability, fear, possessive rhetoric, and leverage. Ethical relation is not dependence-free; it is dependence that remains answerable and plural.

## 23.8 Continuity and change

Stable identity can be a good. Change can be a good. The text refuses to make either one the universal proof of health.

These tensions should remain visible in the revised capstone. Eliminating them would make the synthesis less accurate, not more decisive.
