# 86-Eighty-Six V2 — Character Relationship Register Matrix
## CMR-7 completed directed Japanese-language and behavioral relation states

# 0. Purpose and authority

This matrix is a **directed reconstruction surface**, not a conventional relationship essay. Its basic object is not “the relationship between A and B” but **A speaking/acting toward B under a defined temporal and situational scope**. `A → B` and `B → A` therefore remain separate evidence objects even where both directions are intimate, familial, adversarial, or mutually protective.

The matrix was originally seeded around Frederica because CMR-2 was the method pilot. It now incorporates all sixteen completed profiles and the CMR-7 directed completion audit. Historical seed sections remain as provenance; current coverage and unresolved limits are controlled by Section XII and the CMR-7 audit artifacts.

Authority order remains:

> **Japanese primary source → Phase-5/Phase-8 locks → canonical deep readings/ledgers → specialist corpus → canonical Document 18 → CMR profile/index/matrix.**

A matrix row never creates canon. Where it uses a CMR diagnostic ID, that ID routes back to an exact source coordinate and, when available, a frozen Phase-5 locator. Where a useful reverse-direction passage has no CMR diagnostic row yet, the exact Japanese EPUB route is preserved directly rather than inventing a locator.

---

# I. Row semantics and controlled interpretation

Each row minimally records the fields required by CMR-1: speaker, target, time/scope, context, self-reference, target address, politeness/register, command/request style, relational behavior, significant shift, locator route, and confidence. Stable `86-CMR-RM-*` row IDs are added so later profiles can cite a particular directed state without relying on table position.

Three restrictions govern interpretation:

1. **No symmetry inference.** If only `A → B` is well evidenced, no `B → A` row is created merely for visual balance.
2. **No marker determinism.** First-name use, honorific omission, roughness, archaizing forms, kinship labels, or rank language matter only inside narrative/behavioral context.
3. **No state collapse.** One dyad can legitimately receive several rows when household, battlefield, grief, political, or teasing contexts produce materially different interactional behavior.

`Not diagnostic in sampled line` means the current evidence route does not justify a stable claim about that field. It is preferable to leaving a false impression of certainty.

# III. Asymmetry findings from the seed

The seed already demonstrates why a directed matrix is necessary.

## 1. Frederica ↔ Shin

Frederica's relation to Shin is **intervention-heavy**: she asks, scolds, interprets, clings, challenges self-disposal, and deliberately preserves a naming boundary between Shin and Kiriya. Shin's relation to Frederica is much more **low-excess and practical**: food limits, material help, direct instructions, dry teasing, protection, and—at decisive moments—return of judgment to her. The same closeness therefore produces different care grammars.

The Alter.1 shopping material is especially useful because it proves that intimacy does not erase either person's baseline voice. Frederica can *perform* a sweeter conventional child register and then explicitly return to the archaizing form she regards as part of herself; Shin remains dry and ordinary rather than responding with a special intimate register.

## 2. Frederica ↔ Raiden

Raiden's roughness is not emotional distance. In V02 he cooks and gives simple practical instruction without changing into a soft caretaker register. By V12, he can ask directly what is wrong, listen, advise against carrying everything, and offer intervention. Frederica receives that care while still resisting overprotection through `過保護の兄様`.

V14 then expands the dyad into open emotional argument and comedy. Frederica can physically interrupt him, name his anger, and fail spectacularly at a joking “goddess” offer. Raiden can accept the serious part while maintaining an age-aware boundary. This is strong evidence for familial/affectionate closeness and weak evidence for present romance.

## 3. Frederica ↔ Ernst

The directions are ethically asymmetric. Frederica speaks upward without much deference: interruption, criticism, mock-epithets, refusal to let political office author the household relation. Ernst speaks/acts downward as guardian and head of state, and his strongest register is a categorical refusal to treat a child as strategic material.

Neither direction should be normalized into “parent says / child obeys.” V13 reverses the care direction when Frederica urgently recruits Theo to stop Ernst's violent collapse. The matrix deliberately does **not** invent a stable literal father-address where the mainline speech evidence more often uses his name or mocking forms.

## 4. Frederica ↔ Lena

This dyad demonstrates address negotiation. Frederica can be domestically blunt and explicitly invite `フレデリカ`; Lena initially retains `ローゼンフォルト補佐官` even in a private shared-room scene. By V09, Lena directly uses `フレデリカ`. The shift is meaningful precisely because it is not automatic.

Frederica, meanwhile, can use Lena's full personal name and tease her while providing practical care. Neither first-name use nor teasing alone establishes a relation category; the behavioral context supplies the older-sister-like/household familiarity.

## 5. Frederica ↔ Vika

Mutual intellectual seriousness does not produce mutual softness. Frederica rejects Vika's authority in her own archaizing political language and insults him as `鎖蛇`. Vika answers in a blunt aristocratic register, initially calls her `お飾り`, then shifts to `卿` when her political/self-rule argument makes her stature newly legible. His later self-preservation advice is protective without ceasing to be abrasive.

This is a useful anti-flattening pair: respect can rise while surface warmth remains limited.

## 6. Frederica ↔ Kiriya

The relation is historically asymmetric in form—sovereign and knight—but not reducible to office. Frederica remembers a childhood playmate who combed her hair, brought flowers, and indulged ordinary wishes. Kiriya's `姫様` therefore carries both role-deference and personal attachment. Under political collapse, protection becomes purpose-captured.

The row boundary is intentionally historical. A remembered pre-death address and behavior should not be treated as proof that every part of Kiriya's earlier idiolect survives Legion conversion.

## 7. Frederica ↔ Spearhead collective

Collective rows are useful only at the level of shared household function. They cannot replace future individual rows. Kurena's direct `フレデリカ`, Anju's gentler `フレデリカちゃん`, Claude's `ちびちゃん`, Raiden's rough `お前`, and Shin's low-excess plain speech are not one neutral “Spearhead voice.”

The shared pattern is behavioral: mutual irreverence, child-sensitive protection, refusal to treat imperial status as command authority, and increasing willingness to promise return. CMR-4 must preserve the individual forms inside that collective function.

# IV. Deliberately asymmetric / one-direction seed rows

CMR-3 includes several one-direction rows because symmetry would be false precision.

- **Frederica → Annette** is supported as an interpretively forceful moral interruption in V04. The current Frederica-centered seed does not yet establish an Annette → Frederica register worth stabilizing. Annette's eventual profile should test whether one exists.
- **Anju → Frederica** is already linguistically distinctive in V11 (`フレデリカちゃん`, gentle but firm exclusion from deployment), even though a reciprocal Frederica → Anju row is not yet sufficiently specific to deserve its own state.
- **Kurena ↔ Frederica** has enough evidence for both directions, but the current rows are narrow: Frederica's post-conflict practical care in V09 and Kurena's protective physical/direct care in V11. Neither should be generalized into the characters' entire relationship before Kurena's profile.
- **Theo ↔ Frederica** is currently stabilized only for V13 crisis coordination. It shows mutual familiarity and competence-recognition under urgency, not a complete household register.

The absence of a row is therefore not evidence of emotional distance. It means only that the current seed lacks a sufficiently diagnostic directed state.
