# 86-Eighty-Six V2 — Erwin Marcel / Marcel Character Reference Profile
## CMR-6 scaled profile: source-grounded reconstruction of voice, grief, blame, responsibility, injury adaptation, friendship, practical care, professional competence, ordinary life, and predictive limits

# I. Authority and scope

This document reconstructs **Erwin Marcel / エルウィン・マルセル**, usually referred to simply as **Marcel / マルセル**, across the locked Japanese corpus through Volume 14 plus audited Alter.1 ordinary-life material. It is a derived character-reconstruction surface. It does not replace the Japanese novels, the Phase-5 locator authority, the Phase-8 source-verification layer, canonical V2 deep readings and ledgers, or specialist synthesis.

Marcel poses a specific reconstruction risk because his earliest memorable action is morally ugly and narratively loud. After Eugene's death, he accuses Shin of preferring to destroy Legion rather than save a comrade and spits out `戦狂いのエイティシックスが！` (`86-CMR-MAR-001`). Volume 3 then gives him an unusually explicit interior account of the psychological mechanism beneath that attack: overwhelming injustice and helplessness create a desire to make *someone* responsible (`MAR-014`). He understands, in the same moment, how ordinary people can convert unbearable powerlessness into persecution.

A weak reconstruction therefore tends to make Marcel one of three things:

- **the guy who blamed Shin for Eugene**;
- **the guy who later apologized**;
- or **comic Federacy background staff** once that plot has been resolved.

All three are incomplete.

Marcel's mature value as a character lies precisely in the fact that the source keeps him alive, socially present, professionally useful, and morally self-aware after the apology. He does not erase what he did, and the text does not require Shin to pretend nothing happened. Instead, Marcel gradually turns an episode of displaced grief into a standing theory of personal responsibility. By Volume 12 he can explain from experience why it is psychologically comfortable to obey without thinking and then say that any failure was someone else's fault: `考えないって、楽だから` (`MAR-132`). His private question is no longer whether Shin deserved what was thrown at him, but whether Marcel himself has become someone who can carry his own fear and pain without dumping them onto another person (`MAR-134`).

At the same time, his profile contains much more than penitence. Marcel is:

- a former Vánargand pilot whose right-leg injury permanently removes him from high-speed mobile combat;
- a control officer who acquires substantial operational competence;
- a socially rough, expressive young man with a strong embarrassment response and a taste for stupid jokes;
- a friend who continues caring for Eugene's sister Nina years after Eugene's death;
- a peer who becomes easy enough with Shin to trade jokes, discuss his romantic problems, and receive Shin's grief without immediately moralizing it;
- a staff officer who can coordinate with Lena with very little ceremony;
- someone who goes fishing with Rito and Fido, watches chess, participates in birthday-gift culture, deadpans through Halloween, remembers home cooking, and gets dragged into group sparring;
- and, by Volume 14, an injured former pilot who returns to direct combat not through miraculous physical restoration but through a slow adapted heavy platform whose gunner role fits his surviving capacities.

The governing reconstruction question is therefore:

> **How does a young soldier who once escaped unbearable grief by blaming another person learn to remain emotionally reactive, frightened, rough-edged and imperfect while taking increasing ownership of his own judgment, responsibilities, relationships and altered body?**

This profile uses **198 source-verified diagnostic controls** across V02–V14 and Alter.1. Six controls reuse frozen Phase-5 locator authority; the other 192 preserve exact EPUB/XHTML routes and Japanese anchors under explicit `LOCATOR_GAP` status. Six are audited Alter.1 ordinary-life controls. The chronology-breaking perfume-battle material remains excluded. No synthetic dialogue is admitted as evidence.

# II. Reconstruction thesis

Marcel is best reconstructed as a **rough-spoken, emotionally transparent Federacy officer whose central moral development is the movement from displaced responsibility to owned responsibility, without requiring him to become emotionally serene, socially polished, physically restored, or morally infallible**.

The decisive contrast is between Volume 2 Marcel and Volume 12 Marcel.

In Volume 2, Eugene's death is unbearable because it combines several forms of impotence. Marcel cannot undo the death. He cannot restore Nina's brother. He cannot reconcile the fact that Shin is extraordinarily capable with the fact that Eugene nevertheless died. The result is a story in which Shin *could* have saved Eugene but did not. That story is emotionally useful because it turns helplessness into accusation. The racialized word `エイティシックス` then supplies a ready category through which to make the accusation total (`MAR-001`).

Volume 3 makes the mechanism explicit. Marcel recognizes that when people encounter an irrational catastrophe they cannot bear and a powerlessness they cannot master, they want someone to blame (`MAR-014`). The frightening point is that insight does not instantly save him from the mechanism. He remains capable of enacting it. Knowing why scapegoating occurs is not the same as having ceased to scapegoat.

The mature change appears in Volume 5 when he goes to Shin and says, in substance and at length: I should not have told Nina the way I did; I wanted Eugene's death to be someone's fault; I made it your fault; **I was wrong** (`MAR-025`). This is not an excuse framed as grief. He names grief as the cause of his behavior while retaining ownership of the behavior.

Volume 12 generalizes that ownership. Marcel now recognizes a larger pattern:

> obeying without thinking is comfortable because responsibility can be externalized.

He knows this not because he has become a detached philosopher but because he has lived the same structure. The mature ethical Marcel is therefore not a man who never projects, never fears, or never misreads. He is a man who has learned to treat **“whose responsibility is this?”** as a question that must include himself.

His physical arc rhymes with that moral arc. The right-leg injury does not vanish. He cannot resume ordinary high-speed Vánargand piloting. The source repeatedly preserves that material fact. Marcel instead changes function: control work, mapping, battlefield communication, analysis, fixed-fire support, and finally the gunner role in the improvised `ズートロフィオン`. Volume 14's return to combat is therefore not “he gets his old self back.” It is:

> **he gets another way to participate.**

That return is joyful. He and Theo shout at one another about how absurdly slow the machine is; Marcel protests that ordinary tanks do not have wire anchors (`MAR-175`–`MAR-176`); he happily asks Theo for a cool personal mark (`MAR-180`–`MAR-181`); and when the heavy demolition gun fires successfully he laughs with obvious exhilaration (`MAR-188`–`MAR-191`). Adaptation has not made him solemn.

The concise reconstruction formula is:

> **`俺`-based rough young-male register + clipped contractions and blunt emotional transparency + flexible `です/ます` / `〜っす` upward politeness + high embarrassment reactivity + grief that once externalized blame + unusually explicit later self-accountability + continuing Eugene/Nina care + practical peer friendship + competent control/analysis work + pilot-disqualifying leg injury + role adaptation without identity erasure + stupid humor + ordinary appetite/home texture + fear that does not preclude action.**

# III. Stable personality baseline

## 1. Marcel is emotionally legible

Marcel does not possess Shin's low-excess affective surface. His face, posture and speech often reveal the state before he has formulated it.

He bites his lip around Nina. He goes pale when frightened. He grimaces at disturbing information. He averts his eyes when embarrassed. He can go stiff when Vika threatens something grotesque. He can answer in a rush, complain loudly, laugh conspiratorially or show obvious relief. After knocking down a threatening Legion unit at close range, his face turns almost deathly pale once the immediate crisis passes (`MAR-037`–`MAR-041`).

This matters because “reactive” should not be converted into “weak.” The same person who visibly panics can still move first.

## 2. His roughness is genuine but not cruelty by default

Marcel's ordinary vocabulary is informal and sometimes abrasive:

- `つーか`;
- `わかんねえ`;
- `食ってけねえ`;
- `お前`;
- `〜だろ`;
- `〜じゃねえ`;
- `おうよ`.

He is not a polished courtly speaker. Even when he knows something technical or historical, the knowledge comes out in the voice of a young Federacy officer rather than an academic lecturer.

But roughness and cruelty have to remain separate. His early attack on Shin is cruel because of its content and motive, not because colloquial male speech is itself aggression. By V07 the same rough register can carry an invitation to Shin to meet Eugene's family later, a gift for Nina, or an embarrassed apology for interrupting a private moment (`MAR-052`, `MAR-055`–`MAR-059`).

## 3. He is highly sensitive to social awkwardness

Marcel is one of the corpus's more reliable embarrassment sensors.

In Volume 5, Lena asks why he refuses to turn around while she is wearing the revealing `ツィカーダ`. He replies that asking him to look is basically ordering him to die because Shin will kill him (`MAR-035`). In Volume 7, the mixed-bath situation drives him to mutter Federacy military songs as a desperate avoidance behavior. In Volume 9, a girl's direct gaze makes him look away awkwardly even while he gives a competent institutional explanation (`MAR-087`–`MAR-090`). Alter.1 has him understand Lena's mortification about wearing Shin's uniform before she fully articulates it and close the blast door for privacy (`MAR-195`, `MAR-197`).

This is not simply prudishness. It is a broader social trait: Marcel often notices when everyone knows something that someone would prefer not be foregrounded.

## 4. He can be tactless, but he is not socially oblivious

His early moral failure may suggest poor interpersonal perception. The later corpus shows something subtler.

Marcel can be blunt and sometimes says the wrong thing. But he is often quick at reading shared context. He understands Lena's unstated intent about Republic petition letters and joins her in making them “disappear” (`MAR-105`–`MAR-108`). He knows when a question has made a room uncomfortable. He notices when someone is likely to be upset or embarrassed. He can help manage social situations without turning them into formal interventions.

The correct baseline is therefore:

> **socially rough, not socially blind.**

## 5. He is capable of ordinary warmth without sentimental language

Marcel's care often looks practical.

He buys Nina a large cat plush while traveling abroad (`MAR-055`). He accepts Shin's money and says he will tell her it came from her brother's friend (`MAR-057`). He asks Shin to meet the family someday because Eugene wrote about him (`MAR-058`). He continues checking on Nina years later and becomes visibly distressed when ethnic backlash in the Federacy places a silver-haired girl at risk (`MAR-161`).

Nothing about this becomes ornate. The warmth remains embedded in ordinary masculine colloquialism.

# IV. Developmental state model

## 1. V02 — Eugene's death becomes a blame story

The earliest Marcel state is grief under conditions of perceived asymmetry.

Eugene dies despite the presence of a soldier Marcel knows to be exceptionally capable. Marcel sees Shin after the mercy killing, with Eugene already beyond rescue. Instead of interpreting the scene through battlefield triage, Eugene's own consent, or the limits of any one fighter's reach, Marcel constructs a simpler causal story: Shin is strong, Eugene died, therefore Shin chose not to save him.

The final accusation crosses from personal blame into category-based othering:

> `戦狂いのエイティシックスが！` (`MAR-001`).

His superior immediately stops him and asks whether he intends to sink to the same level as the Republic's persecutors. Marcel falls silent.

This is important. The novel does not grant his grief immunity from moral judgment. He has been hurt; he is also doing something wrong.

## 2. V03 — he recognizes the scapegoating mechanism while still inside it

Volume 3 places Marcel in a military hospital with a badly injured right leg. He encounters Nina outside the gate searching for Eugene (`MAR-008`, `MAR-011`). The physical scene is gentle: he goes to her, gets down to her height and tries to explain what “return” means when a soldier returns in a sealed coffin.

The psychological scene is not gentle.

Nina's inability to understand Eugene's death makes Marcel feel accused even though she is not accusing him. He begins mentally listing what Shin failed to do. The logic shifts from grief to moral transfer: **not my fault → his fault**. Then he understands something terrifying about persecution itself. Faced with irrational catastrophe and one's own impotence, a person wants someone to blame (`MAR-014`).

This is one of the most important Marcel passages because it refuses a simple moral binary. He is not an ignorant bigot who cannot perceive what he is doing. He can perceive the mechanism from inside it.

That perception becomes useful only later when it is joined to ownership.

## 3. V05 — apology without self-exoneration

When Marcel next confronts Shin directly, his body and career have changed.

The leg injury has left enough nerve damage that ordinary walking is possible but the reaction demands of high-speed Feldreß piloting are no longer safe. Shin assumes Marcel may have retired. Marcel reacts practically and somewhat bitterly: `退役ってなんだよ`; ordinary special-officer-track soldiers cannot simply leave and expect an easy civilian livelihood (`MAR-020`–`MAR-022`).

The conversation then turns to Nina and Eugene.

Marcel's apology is unusually explicit (`MAR-025`). He says he should not have told Nina how Eugene died in the way he did; should not have made that information into something she carried before a potentially fatal operation; and wanted Eugene's death to be somebody's fault, so he made it Shin's fault.

The moral structure is worth preserving:

- **grief explains**;
- **grief does not absolve**;
- **Marcel names his own choice**.

Shin's response is quiet and nonpunitive. The conversation then moves immediately to Nina's wellbeing, and Marcel is surprised to discover that Shin does care (`MAR-023`, `MAR-026`–`MAR-028`). This is the first major correction to Marcel's old image of him as emotionally empty.

## 4. V05 — fear and competence coexist

The same volume makes it impossible to define Marcel only through repentance.

He contributes tactical academy knowledge when the enemy uses catapulted lightweight Legion (`MAR-031`). During a command-post emergency he is frightened enough that the adrenaline crash leaves him nearly grey-faced—but he is also the one who physically rises and smashes the threatening Eintagsfliege out of the air before Vika finishes it (`MAR-037`–`MAR-041`).

The source explicitly reconnects this action to his pre-injury identity as a Feldreß pilot. The embodied reflex remains even when the leg no longer permits his old occupation.

## 5. V06–V09 — professional reintegration and ordinary friendship

Marcel's injury does not become an extended self-worth collapse.

By Volume 6 he can still contribute in fixed-fire support, even though high-mobility combat is unavailable. More importantly, he becomes part of the ordinary social world around Shin. Other characters recognize Marcel as one of the people with whom Shin can simply talk.

Volume 7 makes that visible. At breakfast Marcel jokes that Shin's romantic behavior would have been unbelievable to anyone who knew him at officer school (`MAR-050`). He gets trapped in mixed-bath embarrassment with the rest of the boys. He watches and comments on the Shin/Lena situation. He becomes part of the group that can complain, speculate and laugh around Shin rather than orbiting him as an object of guilt.

The plush-toy scene is the clearest proof of relational repair. Marcel is abroad shopping for Nina. Shin contributes money. Marcel smiles and promises to tell her the gift also came from Eugene's friend. He then asks Shin to meet Eugene's family someday (`MAR-055`–`MAR-059`).

The relation is no longer organized around accusation. Eugene remains present, but as a **shared dead friend whose surviving relationships can be cared for together**.

## 6. V09 — competence without performative expertise

Marcel can explain the origins of the Federacy's volunteer/territorial noble military formations to Hjerna in a loose but knowledgeable `〜っす` register (`MAR-087`–`MAR-090`). The scene is useful because it shows him neither bluffing nor shrinking. He knows the material, explains it fluently, and then questions an inconsistency.

He also continues control-officer work under battlefield pressure. The injury has altered his military specialty; it has not expelled him from professional identity.

## 7. V11 — the V02 relationship structure is reversed

Volume 11 provides Marcel's strongest Shin relationship control.

Shin erupts emotionally at Republic refugees and then immediately worries that he has implied weak people deserve death. Marcel follows him. Shin apologizes and tries to explain.

Volume 2 Marcel would have made another person's death into an accusation against Shin.

Volume 11 Marcel says:

> `わかってる。それは、わかってるから。` (`MAR-115`).

He knows Shin is not saying Eugene deserved death. He can distinguish the unbearable sight of people dying without fighting from the claim that weakness makes death deserved. Marcel answers from his own grief: Eugene fought and still died; that is exactly why watching people die without any attempt at agency feels so terrible (`MAR-116`–`MAR-119`).

This scene is not just reconciliation. It demonstrates **distributed emotional support**. Shin can bring a morally dangerous feeling to Marcel, and Marcel can help interpret it without either idolizing Shin or weaponizing Eugene against him.

## 8. V11 — low-formality staff trust with Lena

Marcel's working relationship with Lena has also matured.

He brings her a pile of petitions from Republic military personnel asking for special transport favors (`MAR-105`). She tells him the letters have unfortunately all been lost. Marcel immediately understands and grins: `だよな。了解` (`MAR-108`).

The scene is small but diagnostically useful. His later register with Lena can be direct and familiar even inside professional work because the underlying authority relationship is stable. There is no need to perform deference in every sentence.

## 9. V12 — responsibility becomes a general self-standard

The Volume 12 lunch conversation is Marcel's mature moral center.

When the group tries to understand why people can choose obedience and refuse learning even when the consequences are catastrophic, Marcel says he understands part of it. `考えないって、楽だから` (`MAR-132`). If all one does is follow orders, then failure can always be attributed to the person who gave them.

The narration immediately makes the autobiographical link. Marcel once could not carry what had happened to Eugene, so he made it someone else's responsibility. Now he asks himself whether he has become the kind of person who can bear at least his own fear and pain rather than offloading them (`MAR-134`).

This is not self-flagellation. Shin's presence matters because Marcel no longer sees Shin as an impossible flawless standard. He now knows Shin also has regret, uncertainty, weakness and mistakes. The difference is that those traits had often been hidden.

Marcel's growth therefore includes **deidealizing the person he once demonized**. Shin becomes neither monster nor moral saint, but a peer whose own fallibility makes responsibility more, not less, meaningful.

## 10. V14 — adapted combat is return without restoration

Volume 14 pairs Marcel with Theo in an improvised combat-engineering Vánargand.

Both have bodily reasons they cannot simply return to their former combat roles. Theo's amputation prevents his old three-dimensional Reginleif fighting; Marcel's leg injury makes high-speed mobile piloting unsafe. The solution is not to erase either impairment. They crew a slow, heavy, adapted platform together.

The emotional tone is important. They are delighted.

Marcel complains that he cannot keep up with Theo's fantasies of wire-anchor acrobatics (`MAR-175`–`MAR-176`). He asks for a cool personal mark and protests when Theo immediately suggests a hedgehog because of his hair (`MAR-180`–`MAR-181`). In combat he handles the different sighting and firing procedure, fires the demolition gun and laughs with obvious satisfaction (`MAR-186`, `MAR-188`–`MAR-191`).

The scene confirms a mature principle that already governs several 86 character arcs:

> **continuity of person does not require continuity of function.**

Marcel does not need to become his pre-injury pilot self again in order to remain a soldier, combatant, friend or competent professional.

# V. Japanese voice fingerprint

## 1. First-person baseline: `俺`

Marcel's ordinary first person is strongly `俺`.

It appears across anger, embarrassment, professional explanation, self-reflection and banter. This gives him a contemporary masculine baseline that is substantially rougher than Shin's low-excess speech and less theatrically stylized than Vika or Lerche.

A generated Marcel using relaxed default `僕` or `私` would normally sound wrong.

## 2. Contractions are central

His voice frequently uses contractions and colloquial reductions:

- `つーか`;
- `食ってけねえ`;
- `わかんねえ`;
- `じゃねえ`;
- `してねえ`;
- `死なねえ`;
- `やりきれねえ`.

These are not limited to comedy. Marcel can use the same register while discussing grief, politics or technical issues. The emotional seriousness changes; the sociolect often does not.

## 3. Upward politeness is flexible rather than stiff

Marcel can use standard operational politeness, especially in formal reporting. But in less formal cross-status interactions he often uses casual-politeness forms such as `〜っす`.

His explanation to Hjerna is a good control (`MAR-087`–`MAR-090`). He gives detailed information about Imperial aristocratic military structures in a relaxed `〜っすよ / 〜っす` style. Knowledge does not transform him into a formal lecturer.

## 4. Shin is generally `ノウゼン`

Even after reconciliation Marcel frequently preserves surname address: `ノウゼン`.

That detail matters. The relationship becomes warm and ordinary without needing to imitate Spearhead's forms of intimacy. Marcel's friendship with Shin develops inside a distinct linguistic route.

## 5. Lena moves between rank and first-name familiarity

In the V05 command-post scene Marcel answers `大佐` (`MAR-035`). By V11 he can walk into the command tent and simply say `レーナ` while discussing work (`MAR-105`).

This is a clear status-distance shift. It does not mean military hierarchy disappears. It means stable collaboration permits more ordinary address.

## 6. Hesitation marks guilt and social discomfort

Marcel's speech often contains `……`, `あー`, `えっと`, `その`, repeated fragments and self-restarts when the emotional load is high.

The V05 apology is not a smooth prepared speech. The V12 self-analysis ends in `だから……なんつうか` (`MAR-135`). These hesitations are part of his authenticity: he can understand something before he can say it elegantly.

## 7. Combat and excitement shorten the line

When danger or excitement spikes, Marcel's speech compresses:

- `多分、射出機です！` (`MAR-031`);
- `おうよ！` (`MAR-186`);
- `よっしゃ、次探すぞ！` (`MAR-189`).

This is not the same register as his guilt-heavy reflective scenes. Generated dialogue should distinguish the two.

# VI. Behavioral grammar

## 1. Grief pattern: pain → blame temptation → later ownership

Early Marcel's dangerous pattern is:

1. an event feels intolerably unjust;
2. he cannot repair it;
3. he searches for an agent who *could* have acted;
4. responsibility expands beyond evidence;
5. blame reduces helplessness.

Mature Marcel has not become immune to pain. What changes is the interrupt:

> **“Am I making someone else carry what I cannot?”**

That is the most important predictive rule in his profile.

## 2. Embarrassment pattern: avert → joke/complain → remain present

Marcel rarely meets embarrassment with cool silence. He looks away, complains, jokes defensively or finds something else to stare at.

He does not usually flee the relationship itself. The discomfort is socially noisy rather than avoidantly absolute.

## 3. Professional pattern: practical question → available competence → report

As a control officer Marcel is useful because he works from what is actually available:

- map analysis;
- historical training memory;
- communications;
- route prediction;
- data relay;
- gunnery;
- battlefield reporting.

He does not need to be the smartest person in the room to contribute decisively.

## 4. Fear pattern: visible fear does not cancel action

Marcel can go pale, stiffen or complain loudly and still act.

This is important because his V02 hostility partly came from feeling judged by Shin's emotional flatness. Mature reconstruction should not reproduce Marcel's own early mistake and treat visible fear as inferiority.

## 5. Care pattern: preserve a relationship through mundane tasks

Marcel's strongest care evidence is not speechifying.

It is buying Nina a plush toy; passing messages; checking her safety; helping Lena; fishing with peers; asking whether absent people have been seen; remembering family food; sitting at the same noisy lunch table.

He makes care durable by **continuing to show up**.

## 6. Conflict pattern: rough speech can coexist with trust

Marcel's later banter with Theo, Shin and others can be loud and irritated without signaling relational fracture.

A generated model should therefore not overinterpret every `うるさい`, `やめてくれ`, or shouted retort as serious hostility.

# VII. Injury, embodiment and professional identity

Marcel's right-leg injury is a permanent state change inside the current source boundary.

The text is precise. He can walk and run; the problem is the small reaction delay produced by nerve damage. In ordinary life that delay is manageable. In a Feldreß where fractions of a second decide survival, it makes high-speed mobile piloting unsafe.

Three constraints follow.

First, **do not erase the injury**. Volume 14 does not establish recovery to old pilot standards.

Second, **do not turn the injury into total incapacity**. Marcel becomes a control officer and remains technically/operationally valuable.

Third, **do not treat the V14 gunner role as a symbolic consolation prize**. He is openly excited by it. The adapted platform gives him a form of embodied military participation he actually enjoys.

His trajectory is therefore a useful parallel to Theo's while remaining distinct. Theo's arm loss changes what he can physically manipulate and how he fights; Marcel's leg/nerve injury changes reaction demands and mobility. Their shared V14 platform works precisely because neither needs to pretend the old body-role relation still exists.

# VIII. Responsibility, guilt and moral reasoning

## 1. Marcel's guilt is specific

He is not generically guilty about being alive.

His central guilt concerns what he **did with Eugene's death**:

- he blamed Shin;
- he weaponized the Eighty-Six category;
- he gave Nina information in a way that helped direct grief at Shin;
- he wanted causality to be morally simpler than it was.

That specificity matters because it keeps the profile from turning him into a permanently self-loathing penitent.

## 2. Accountability does not require endless punishment

Marcel apologizes. Shin says he no longer cares about the insult. The relationship repairs.

The story does not imply Marcel must keep begging forgiveness forever to prove moral seriousness. His later responsibility is shown through changed behavior and self-understanding.

## 3. V12 expands guilt into a theory of agency

`考えないって、楽だから` (`MAR-132`) is Marcel's mature insight.

The claim is not that every obedient person is malicious. It is that nonthought can function as **responsibility anesthesia**. If the decision was someone else's, then the pain of examining one's own contribution can be avoided.

His own life supplies the counterexample. He wants to become someone who can carry at least his own fear and suffering (`MAR-134`).

## 4. He does not idealize Shin anymore

An important part of this maturation is realizing Shin also has weaknesses and mistakes.

Early Marcel experienced Shin's calm as superiority or emotional indifference. Mature Marcel understands that Shin often hid pain rather than lacking it. This deidealization prevents accountability from becoming another hierarchy in which Marcel is the bad weak person and Shin the flawless strong person.

# IX. Relationship-conditioned registers

## 1. Marcel → Shin

This is Marcel's most important longitudinal relation.

### V02–V03: hostile projection

Shin is the person Marcel makes answer for Eugene's death. `ノウゼン` becomes attached to fear, resentment and perceived emotional coldness.

### V05: direct apology

Marcel names his own displacement and apologizes without demanding Shin comfort him (`MAR-025`).

### V07 onward: ordinary peer relation

He can interrupt Shin and Lena, joke about Shin's school-era personality, include Shin in Nina's gift, ask him to meet Eugene's family, discuss romance gossip, and work beside him.

### V11–V12: reciprocal moral peer

Shin can confess a morally dangerous feeling to Marcel and be understood (`MAR-113`–`MAR-119`). Marcel can then use Shin's survival of Marcel's own hatred as part of his internal responsibility standard without turning Shin into a saint (`MAR-134`).

The mature relation is therefore **repaired but historically informed**. The past matters; it no longer governs every interaction.

## 2. Marcel → Eugene

Eugene is Marcel's lost friend, schoolmate and wartime peer.

The relationship persists after death through memory and Nina. Marcel's growth does not require reducing Eugene to “the reason Marcel learned a lesson.” Eugene remains someone Marcel misses.

## 3. Marcel → Nina

Nina is one of the strongest continuity relationships in the profile.

Marcel first approaches her in V03 while still trapped inside grief displacement (`MAR-008`, `MAR-011`). By V07 he is buying her gifts and managing what she knows. By V13 she calls him `マルセルお兄ちゃん`. By V14 he panics over her safety during anti-silver ethnic backlash (`MAR-161`).

The relation is best modeled as **older-brother-like stewardship rooted in Eugene but sustained in its own right**.

## 4. Marcel → Lena

Marcel begins as one of Lena's control officers.

His behavior toward her combines professional responsiveness and very ordinary embarrassment. V05 gives formal `大佐` plus comic self-preservation (`MAR-035`); V11 gives first-name familiarity and tacit political coordination (`MAR-105`–`MAR-108`). Alter.1 shows him discretely protecting her privacy rather than teasing her when her embarrassment crosses a threshold (`MAR-195`, `MAR-197`).

## 5. Marcel → Theo

Volume 14 turns this into one of Marcel's strongest action partnerships.

Both are former high-mobility combatants now operating under altered bodies. Their banter is immediate and equality-rich. Marcel complains, Theo mocks him, Marcel laughs and asks for a cool personal mark (`MAR-174`–`MAR-181`).

The relation demonstrates **adapted competence without pity**.

## 6. Marcel → Rito

The corpus gives them ordinary cohort proximity: fishing with Fido, lunch-table discussion, jokes and wider group activity. Marcel can function as a knowledge source while remaining willing to admit that he also only partially understands the subject (`MAR-129`).

This relation is important because Rito dies after that ordinary world has been established. Marcel should not be reconstructed only as someone around other characters' major tragedies.

## 7. Marcel → Vika

Marcel finds Vika socially and physically alarming in ways the text treats comically. Threats about resurrection and skinning can turn him pale. Yet he remains in the same working/social world rather than maintaining formal aristocratic distance.

The relation is **intimidated familiarity**, not reverence.

## 8. Marcel → Grethe / command staff

Grethe's work can place Marcel in uncomfortable roles—including direct co-piloting—but he functions as reliable staff. Lena and other commanders regularly use him for control, mapping and communications.

## 9. Marcel → Hjerna / foreign or less-familiar peers

With unfamiliar people, Marcel's rough register becomes more polite without becoming elegant. `〜っす` is a useful fingerprint (`MAR-087`–`MAR-090`).

## 10. Marcel → wider cohort

The mature Marcel belongs in the room.

He eats with Spearhead, participates in romantic gossip, watches chess, goes fishing, gets dragged into fights, joins holiday customs, and shares home-food memories. This is narratively important because V02 placed him outside Shin's social world as a hostile observer. Later volumes place him **inside the ordinary relational field**.

Marcel has enough mundane evidence to prevent the profile from collapsing into guilt and military function.

## Food

He eats aggressively enough that the text can catch him biting into hot sausage and talking while recovering from it. In V14 he remembers a central-Federacy home dish: salted cabbage cooked with sausage or salted pork (`MAR-169`). That is concrete family/home texture rather than abstract national identity.

He fishes with Rito and Fido. He watches Shin and Vika play chess. He participates in gossip and group spectating.

The mixed bath, Lena's `ツィカーダ`, Shin/Lena romance and Alter.1 uniform scene all show him responding to sexual/romantic embarrassment with defensive noise, averted eyes and attempts to make himself socially absent while remaining physically present.

# XI. Professional and tactical profile

Marcel is not a strategic genius, but he is a competent professional.

His useful domains include:

- control-room operations;
- map checking and anomaly detection;
- battlefield communication;
- visual/data analysis;
- remembered academy tactics;
- institutional military knowledge;
- gunner operation;
- and rapid physical response in emergencies.

His V05 catapult recognition (`MAR-031`) demonstrates stored tactical memory. V12 has him relay analyzed imagery and timed enemy-position data (`MAR-140`–`MAR-142`). V14 demonstrates successful operation of a firing system different from his old Vánargand procedures (`MAR-186`, `MAR-188`).

The correct competence model is therefore:

> **trained officer + former pilot experience + practical control specialization + enough intellectual humility to say when he only partly understands something.**

He should not be written as either a helpless desk officer or a hidden supergenius.

# XII. Emotional-state deltas

## Baseline

Rough, alert, easy to embarrass, socially responsive, capable of complaining while working.

## Anger

Speech gets sharper and causal judgment can narrow. Early Marcel is most dangerous here because anger gives grief a target.

## Guilt

Pacing slows. Ellipses increase. He looks down or away. He names concrete actions rather than making abstract apologies.

## Embarrassment

Eyes avert; volume can rise; defensive jokes or complaints emerge quickly.

## Fear

Face can pale; body may stiffen; he can verbalize reluctance directly.

## Immediate danger

The noise can disappear into action. The V05 command-post strike is the best control.

## Relief/excitement

Grins broaden; speech becomes exclamatory and youthful. V14 adapted combat produces this state strongly.

## Grief shared with trusted peer

Voice becomes quieter and more reflective without becoming formal. V11's `死なねえでくれたら、その方がよかったよな` is the strongest example (`MAR-119`).

# XIII. Counterexample audit

## “Marcel is the guy who hates Shin.”

False as a mature model. It accurately describes a real early state and becomes misleading if generalized beyond V03.

## “Marcel's apology erases the earlier prejudice.”

False. The apology matters because the earlier harm remains real.

## “Marcel only lashed out because he loved Eugene, so he did nothing morally wrong.”

False. The text explicitly rebukes the racialized accusation in V02 and later gives Marcel responsibility for it.

## “Marcel becomes morally wise after V05.”

Too simple. Volume 12 frames maturity as a continuing self-question, not completed enlightenment.

## “Marcel is a coward.”

False. He is visibly frightened more often than Shin, but fear and action coexist. V05 gives direct proof.

## “Marcel cannot fight anymore.”

Too broad. He cannot safely perform his old high-speed mobile pilot role. V14 demonstrates an adapted combat role.

## “V14 means his leg recovered.”

False. The adapted vehicle exists specifically because his old mobility demands remain inappropriate.

## “Marcel becomes an Eighty-Six-style soldier.”

False. He remains a Federacy officer with a different social history and different pride structure. Integration does not require identity assimilation.

## “Marcel worships Shin after realizing he was wrong.”

False. Mature familiarity includes teasing, complaints and disagreement. The repair produces peerhood, not devotion.

## “He forgives himself and forgets Eugene.”

False. Eugene remains active through Nina, memory and Marcel's moral reasoning.

## “Nina is just a guilt object for Marcel.”

False. The relationship persists across years through gifts, messages, protection and family continuity.

## “Marcel is only comic staff after Volume 5.”

False. V11 and V12 give major moral and relational evidence; V14 gives embodied-role development.

# XIV. Mischaracterization traps

A poor reconstruction will often:

- make Marcel permanently hostile to Shin;
- make him permanently ashamed and apologetic around Shin;
- smooth his rough register into generic polite dialogue;
- make him emotionally calm because he “matured”;
- treat visible fear as lack of courage;
- forget the permanent right-leg/nerve injury;
- restore him to ordinary Vánargand piloting without evidence;
- overstate his strategic authority;
- make every Eugene reference a trauma spiral;
- ignore his ongoing responsibility toward Nina;
- make him a sex-comedy creep rather than an embarrassment-prone observer;
- remove his competence because he is often comic;
- or remove his stupidity/comedy because he has serious moral development.

# XV. “Would sound wrong if...” negative constraints

A generated Marcel will usually sound wrong if:

- relaxed speech defaults to `私`;
- he speaks in polished essay-like abstractions without contractions;
- he addresses Shin with reverential deference in ordinary mature scenes;
- he treats Eugene's death as fully resolved;
- he asks Shin to repeatedly reassure or absolve him;
- he denies being afraid in order to appear brave;
- he claims his leg is fully healed;
- he behaves as though control work is beneath a “real” pilot;
- he treats Nina as merely Eugene's memorial object rather than a living girl he cares about;
- he remains outside Spearhead/Strike Package ordinary social life;
- he is indifferent to awkward sexual/romantic situations;
- or he becomes so guilt-defined that he cannot enjoy food, jokes, gifts, fishing, combat excitement or stupid group behavior.

# XVI. Predictive model

## High-confidence predictions

### If Marcel realizes he has unfairly blamed someone

He is likely to become uncomfortable before he becomes eloquent. A mature response would eventually name what **he** did rather than explaining only what the other person did to provoke him.

### If a peer is frightened but still functioning

He is unlikely to treat visible fear itself as shameful. His own later record gives him little basis to do so.

### If a commander asks for technical information he knows

He will answer directly, often in casual military language, and may add remembered field/academy context.

### If he does not know

He is more likely to say so in rough language than bluff expertise. V12's nuclear discussion is a strong control (`MAR-129`).

### If a close peer is in immediate physical danger

He can act before emotional composure catches up. V05 is the governing example (`MAR-037`–`MAR-041`).

### If Shin is emotionally distressed

Mature Marcel is more likely to listen, distinguish the actual claim from its ugliest possible interpretation, and answer from shared grief than to moralize from outside (`MAR-113`–`MAR-119`).

### If Nina is threatened

Expect disproportionate concern, practical checking and urgency. V14 makes this high confidence (`MAR-161`).

### If Theo proposes something ridiculous in an adapted vehicle

Expect loud protest, technical realism and willing participation anyway (`MAR-174`–`MAR-181`).

### If a group social situation becomes painfully embarrassing

Expect averted eyes, muttering, joking, attempts to look busy or literal physical privacy management.

### If someone offers a genuinely new role compatible with his injury

Expect practical evaluation rather than automatic refusal. If the role reconnects him to active soldiering without pretending the injury is gone, excitement is plausible.

## Moderate-confidence predictions

Marcel is likely to be a useful mediator for younger officers who externalize failure, precisely because he can speak from experience rather than superiority. The current corpus supports the mechanism but does not show him repeatedly occupying an explicit mentoring role.

He may have meaningful postwar civilian interests through food, family and ordinary Federacy life, but the locked boundary does not establish a specific postwar vocation.

## Open / insufficient evidence

The V12 forward question remains relevant: **does Marcel sustain owned responsibility under a future failure as personally devastating as Eugene's death?** The current corpus strongly supports maturation but does not supply an exact repeat test of the original wound.

The final arc also remains open. V14 confirms an adapted military role but not the ultimate duration of that role or Marcel's postwar endpoint.
