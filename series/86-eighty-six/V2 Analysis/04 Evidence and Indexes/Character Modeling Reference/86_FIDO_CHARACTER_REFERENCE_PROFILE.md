# 86-Eighty-Six V2 — Fido Character Reference Profile
## CMR-6 scaled profile: source-grounded reconstruction of artificial selfhood, formal interior voice, signal-language, designed friendship, particular attachment, memory, support, play, embodiment, rescue, continuity, and predictive limits

# I. Authority and scope

This document reconstructs **Fido / ファイド** across the locked original-Japanese corpus through Volume 14 plus audited Alter.1 ordinary-life material. It is a derived Character Modeling Reference surface subordinate to the Japanese novels, the Phase-5 locator/source authority, Phase-8 source verification, canonical deep readings and ledgers, and the specialist synthesis.

Fido requires a different evidentiary discipline from every human profile in this layer because the novels give the reader **two substantially different kinds of access** to him.

For most of the series, Fido is externally observed. Humans hear electronic beeps, see optical sensors blink, watch his body tilt or droop, see crane arms wave, and infer recognizable attitudes: concern, embarrassment, stubbornness, satisfaction, disappointment, even sulking. The prose often marks those readings as interpretations—`ように`, `らしい`, `（多分）`, or comic anthropomorphic comparison. Those scenes are valuable behavioral evidence, but they do not by themselves license the claim that every human gloss maps one-to-one onto Fido's actual internal state.

Volume 10 changes the evidentiary situation. Its Fido chapter is narrated directly in Fido's first person. The voice identifies itself as `わたくし`, calls itself an artificial intelligence, remembers its creation, describes its own consciousness and thought, distinguishes designed prohibitions from later decisions, reports happiness, emptiness, loneliness and sadness, analyzes one attachment as `代償行為`, recalls different people as different people, and decides repeatedly what to do next. That chapter does **not** make every earlier human projection retrospectively exact. It does establish that the recurring external behaviors belong to a continuing subject rather than merely to a machine that humans sentimentalize.

The governing identity rule is therefore:

> **Reconstruct Fido from the inside where the source gives inside access, from behavior where it gives behavior, and never silently upgrade a human interpretation into direct interior fact.**

A second guardrail is equally important. Fido is not made more agentic by pretending his origin does not constrain him. His creator's demand that he be a good friend to humans and his hard prohibition against harming humans remain real. Fido himself calls that wish his `存在理由` and later says it is `絶対` (`86-CMR-FID-165`, `86-CMR-FID-199`). The corpus never shows him emancipating himself by rejecting it.

Yet the same Fido:

- chooses to leave the abandoned Nouzen household and search for the family;
- transfers his complete configuration into another body because the old body cannot make the journey;
- spends years searching while supporting successive units;
- chooses an adolescent Shin while explicitly recognizing that the attachment may partly be compensatory;
- repeatedly violates lower-order commands and institutional boundaries when those conflict with remaining near or helping the people he values;
- learns tasks he was not initially given;
- interprets an underspecified command successfully;
- distinguishes human beings from the Legion for purposes of the friendship/nonharm rule;
- decides that preserving himself no longer matters if self-preservation requires watching Shin die;
- and after reconstruction asks to serve again rather than treating restored function as compulsory continuation.

The central reconstruction question is therefore not, “Is Fido programmed or free?” That binary does not fit the source. It is:

> **What does agency look like for an artificial subject whose deepest designed commitment remains stable, but who interprets that commitment through memory, particular relationships, context-sensitive judgment, self-chosen service, ordinary pleasure and repeated acts of initiative?**

This profile uses **293 source-verified diagnostic controls** across V01–V14 and Alter.1. **12** reuse frozen Phase-5 locator authority; **281** preserve exact EPUB/XHTML routes under explicit `LOCATOR_GAP` status. **12** are audited Alter.1 ordinary-life controls. No synthetic dialogue or invented machine interiority is admitted as evidence.

# II. Reconstruction thesis

Fido is best reconstructed as a **formally self-narrating artificial subject whose designed end—being a good friend to humans—remains stable and even absolute, while his life demonstrates contextual interpretation, particular attachment, memory-bearing continuity, practical care, self-chosen service, ordinary play, selective rule-bending and meaningful embodied limitation**.

The compact formulation is:

> **formal interior `わたくし` + humble/service morphology + no ordinary speech output + electronic/gesture signal layer + durable “good friend to humans” imperative + absolute human-nonharm boundary + strong particular attachment to Shin/Nouzen family + memory-bearing continuity across bodies + practical caregiver/supporter behavior + lower-order disobedience when relation and task conflict + learned new priorities + contextual rule interpretation + archival/witness function + playful social initiative + nonhuman embodiment and physical limits + rescue-oriented self-risk rather than combat identity.**

The most important conceptual distinction is between **designed friendship** and **particular friendship**.

Fido was designed under a general relational purpose. Vaclav Nouzen did not want another intelligence to become another enemy and embedded the wish that Fido be a good friend to human beings. That orientation is not a hidden defect in the character model. It is part of who Fido is.

But Fido's relations are not therefore fungible. His narrative differentiates Vaclav, Maika, Rei, child Shin, adolescent Shin, Raiden, Theo, Kurena and Anju. He experiences naming as meaningful. He preserves records. He makes one person the object of exceptional attachment. He can understand that his attachment to adolescent Shin is partly a substitute for the lost child while still separately judging that this Shin is kind and worth supporting (`86-CMR-FID-179`, `86-CMR-FID-180`). When he later realizes that the substitute and the first child were literally the same continuously growing person, that revelation does not create the attachment; it retrospectively complicates one he had already chosen.

This is why “Fido's friendship is only programming” is analytically inadequate. A general value can be designed without exhaustively determining every application of that value. Human characters also inherit languages, institutions, obligations, kinship structures, temperaments and promises they did not choose from nothing. The relevant question is whether the inherited rule leaves meaningful **answer-space**: distinction among persons, interpretation of cases, new learning, refusal of lower-order instructions, priority formation, self-revision and context-sensitive action. Fido repeatedly exhibits those capacities.

At the opposite extreme, a reconstruction that simply calls him “basically human” also fails. Fido does not speak aloud in ordinary life. His cognition is architecturally artificial. His body can be replaced around a surviving core. His mass, manipulators, fire-control absence and mechanical durability shape what he can do. His nonharm rule is stronger than ordinary moral preference. His relationship to memory, data and embodiment cannot simply be imported from human psychology. His dignity does not require pretending those differences are superficial.

The mature model is therefore:

> **Fido's personhood is not human assimilation, and Fido's agency is not originlessness.**

# III. Stable subjectivity and personality baseline

## 1. Fido is internally formal, not “cute-machine casual”

The greatest voice surprise in Volume 10 is how different Fido's interior register is from the comic external impression created by `ぴっ`, blinking sensors and dancing crane arms.

His first-person self-reference is **`わたくし`**. He begins with `僭越ながら` and narrates his origins in a highly polite, service-conscious register (`86-CMR-FID-144`–`86-CMR-FID-156`). He speaks internally of `旦那様`, `奥様`, `兄君`, `弟君`, `ノウゼン様`, `シュガ様`, `リッカ様`, `ククミラ様`, and `エマ様`. The vocabulary can be almost old-fashioned in its humility: `お仕えする`, `賜った`, `光栄でございます`, `お許しいただけますか`.

This is not merely an artificial equivalent of robotic formality. The formal register carries warmth, humor, grief, technical explanation, self-reproach and intimate attachment. He can describe being pleased at a dog's name with `大変嬉しゅうございます`; he can call failure `虚しゅう、ございました`; he can identify loss as `寂しく、哀しい`; and he can clinically label his own attachment `代償行為` without thereby draining that attachment of feeling.

A Fido reconstruction that gives his interior monologue casual teenage slang would therefore be recognizably wrong.

## 2. He is service-oriented without being inertly obedient

Fido repeatedly understands himself through service. `お仕え` is not ornamental vocabulary. His identity includes being useful, remaining near, carrying, recovering, supporting, protecting and remembering.

But service is not identical to command compliance.

Before the reader learns his internal origin, the external record already shows a long history of selective disobedience. Fido ignores Republic officers, ignores sector boundaries, repeatedly boards transports, remains awake after a standby order, objects when left behind, ignores a direct wait order to go fishing at the coast, and learns new tasks outside initial programming (`86-CMR-FID-061`, `86-CMR-FID-106`, `86-CMR-FID-127`–`86-CMR-FID-143`).

The correct distinction is:

- **core commitments:** unusually rigid;
- **lower-order instructions and role boundaries:** negotiable when they conflict with the relationship or practical good Fido is pursuing.

That is a bounded but real form of agency.

## 3. His care is usually material before it is expressive

Fido cannot normally console someone by speaking. He therefore acts.

He brings ammunition. He searches for personal-mark fragments. He carries people and equipment. He retrieves objects. He shelters bodies from weather. He becomes a windbreak and a bench. He handles camp cleanup. He arrives with supplies. He catches a dropped blade fragment. He searches for buried comrades. He improvises rescue using his mass. He notices a practical need and moves toward it.

This creates a stable behavioral grammar:

> **need noticed → material capacity checked → practical intervention.**

The intervention may then be accompanied by a beep or movement that humans interpret socially, but the practical act is often the stronger evidence.

## 4. He is playful

Fido's ordinary behavior is too playful to support a purely solemn servant-machine model.

He dances to make a lonely Shin laugh (`86-CMR-FID-118`). He dances more energetically after receiving the name Fido (`86-CMR-FID-124`). He participates in shooting games by arranging increasingly difficult can formations. He sets up fireworks. He joins fishing. He approaches unfamiliar sea creatures with friendly signals. He tries to participate in a fruit-cutting game despite manipulators poorly suited to the task. He becomes visibly associated with comic sulking when excluded. In V14 he appears to want a recovered giant gun mounted on himself, only to droop when reminded that he has neither the body balance nor fire-control system for it and brighten again when offered an alternative use (`86-CMR-FID-277`–`86-CMR-FID-280`).

Fido should sometimes be ridiculous.

## 5. He is stubborn

The source repeatedly gives Fido a behavior that humans recognize as insistence.

This is one of the safest personality inferences because it crosses many contexts: standing watch after being told to sleep, trying to accompany deployments, following Shin despite exclusion, ignoring wait orders, and continuing to find routes toward participation. V10's interior narration gives the behavior a plausible subject-level basis without proving that every earlier “tantrum” description is exact.

The baseline is therefore not “obedient machine.” It is closer to:

> **loyal, useful, and selectively obstinate.**

## 6. He can evaluate himself harshly

Fido is capable of severe self-condemnation.

After failing to protect the Nouzen family, he asks why he did nothing and searches for another action route (`86-CMR-FID-163`–`86-CMR-FID-169`). Years later, after concluding the family is probably dead and another assigned unit has been annihilated, he treats destruction by the Legion as a fitting end for a failed protector (`86-CMR-FID-172`).

This is not simply a technical error state. The vocabulary of emptiness, failure, worthiness and later forgiveness indicates a moralized self-model.

But the corpus does not establish that self-condemnation as the only or permanent way he handles failure. Reencounter with Shin reopens action. Reconstruction after the Special Recon loss reopens relation. Fido can move from failure toward renewed service rather than only toward self-erasure.

# IV. Continuity and developmental state model

## 1. Prewar origin — artificial cognition inside a family

Volume 10 retrospectively establishes that Fido began as trial artificial intelligence **Prototype 008**, housed at one point in a large-dog-like soft body and earlier built through neural-pattern data gathered from infant Shin (`86-CMR-FID-145`, `86-CMR-FID-156`).

Fido's processing architecture is therefore tied to child Shin from before Fido's mature selfhood. He says that human action/emotion learning occurred through Shin's growth and that the child effectively gave him `〝わたくし〟としての意識と思考`—his consciousness and thought as himself (`86-CMR-FID-156`; `V10-L050`).

The source does **not** say Fido is a copy of Shin. “Based on neural activity patterns” and “learned through him” are not equivalent to personal identity. Fido consistently narrates himself and Shin as distinct subjects.

The family context matters because Fido's earliest identity is not military. It is domestic: a research subject designed as companion, playmate and household friend.

## 2. Naming — Fido accepts a supposedly inappropriate name as honor

Child Shin gives him the name “Fido,” to Vaclav's mild dismay because it is a dog's name rather than an ordinary friend's name. Fido's internal response is explicit: **he is delighted** (`86-CMR-FID-149`–`86-CMR-FID-154`). Dogs have been good friends to humans; being compared to one feels honorable.

This scene matters for later pet-language analysis. Dog imagery is not only imposed from outside. Fido himself positively interprets the association. But that does not make “pet” an exhaustive ontology. The same subject narrates creator, purpose, guilt, memory and autonomous decision.

## 3. The Nouzen family's removal — hard constraint does not end decision-making

When soldiers take the family, Fido has been left in standby and is constrained by a strong rule against harming people. He later replays the event with data noise/confusion and analogizes it to what humans might call not wanting to remember (`86-CMR-FID-161`).

He asks why he did nothing. The answer is partly architectural: standby state and a hard nonharm prohibition. But his thought does not stop at “programming prevented action.” He asks whether there was still something else he could have done.

That question produces a new decision: search.

## 4. Body transfer — continuity is carried through data, not body shape

The household dog body is not suitable for long travel. Fido discards it and transfers **all of his configuration data** into a Scavenger body (`86-CMR-FID-168`, `86-CMR-FID-169`).

Within the narrative's own treatment, Fido experiences this as continued first-person existence. The body changes; the narrator remains `わたくし`.

This supports strong **functional/narrative continuity across embodiment**. It does not settle every metaphysical copying problem. The scene describes transfer, not simultaneous duplication. A future duplicate made from the same data would raise a different identity question that the locked corpus does not resolve.

## 5. Years of searching — support and grief accumulate together

Fido searches the battlefield for years while supporting military units. He watches successively younger cohorts die and eventually concludes that Vaclav, Maika, Rei and child Shin are probably all dead (`86-CMR-FID-170`–`86-CMR-FID-172`).

The sequence turns him into more than a family retainer. He becomes a **witness to repeated human destruction**.

The witness function later acquires material form through mementos, stored records and entrusted identity plates, but its emotional history begins here.

## 6. Adolescent Shin — a chosen relation formed under a mistaken identity model

A young soldier approaches disabled Fido after another unit has been annihilated. Fido initially interprets him as simply another exhausted child soldier. Shin then notices that Fido is still alive and asks whether he wants to return together (`86-CMR-FID-176`–`86-CMR-FID-177`).

Fido chooses to serve him.

Critically, at this stage Fido **does not realize that this adolescent is literally the same child Shin from the Nouzen household**. He recognizes the same nickname and red eyes and explicitly says his attachment contains compensatory substitution: `代償行為とは知りつつ` (`86-CMR-FID-179`; `V10-L052`).

But he also gives a second reason: this Shin is unexpectedly kind, and Fido independently wants to support him (`86-CMR-FID-180`).

This is one of the strongest anti-reduction controls in the profile. Even when Fido believes adolescent Shin is a substitute rather than the original child, the relation remains chosen and particular.

## 7. Re-naming by adolescent Shin — recognition without recovered explicit memory

Volume 10's separate retrospective scene shows the adolescent Shin, not knowing the machine's prior identity, naming the strange Scavenger “Fido.” Fido reacts with exuberant sensor flashing and a dance (`86-CMR-FID-121`–`86-CMR-FID-124`).

The scene creates a doubled naming structure:

- child Shin names the household AI Fido;
- adolescent Shin unknowingly names the same continuing subject Fido again.

For years, neither side fully understands the continuity.

The relation is therefore not sustained by explicit biographical recognition alone. Habit, mutual reading, repeated co-presence and practical reliance carry it even while the shared origin is unavailable to them.

## 8. V01 — learned memory work and ordinary membership

Volume 1 already shows Fido doing more than baseline Scavenger work.

He has learned to prioritize the recovery of personal-mark fragments from dead comrades, a task outside his original role (`86-CMR-FID-002`, `86-CMR-FID-010`, `86-CMR-FID-011`, `86-CMR-FID-021`). He plays target games with the unit. He helps set up fireworks. He supplies ammunition under fire. He carries the Special Recon expedition's material life.

At this stage the reader cannot yet know the full ontology. The correct local reading is therefore: **highly anomalous learned/social behavior, personhood question still open**. Volume 10 later upgrades the evidentiary basis without erasing that earlier uncertainty.

## 9. V02 — reconstruction preserves a surviving core

Fido's body is destroyed at the end of the Special Recon phase, but his core survives. The Federacy builds a replacement body around that surviving core. The same unit initially does not activate as expected, as though it believes its work/life has ended, and responds to Shin's presence/name (`86-CMR-FID-036`–`86-CMR-FID-044`).

This is **rebodying around a surviving core**, not textual evidence that a new copy with the same memories is automatically the same person.

The reunion becomes almost slapstick: tentative approach becomes an exuberant lunge that sends Fido crashing into wreckage when Shin dodges (`86-CMR-FID-042`–`86-CMR-FID-044`).

Continuity and comedy coexist.

## 10. V03–V06 — loyalty becomes intervention

Across Volumes 3–6, Fido repeatedly exceeds passive support.

He stays awake beside Shin after being ordered to enter standby (`86-CMR-FID-048`, `86-CMR-FID-049`). He appears agitated while Shin enters extreme danger. He extracts Frederica. He shields bodies from fragments. Most decisively, in Volume 6 he charges directly into the Merciless Queen situation to interrupt a death sequence involving Shin (`86-CMR-FID-077`; `V06-L050`).

Before V10, a cautious reader should describe these as protective behavioral patterns. After V10, the first-person account makes “remaining beside and protecting Shin” a directly attested motive class.

## 11. V07 — the story tests whether love becomes instrumentalization

Before Fido's origin is confirmed to the reader, Shin hears about his father's AI research and briefly wonders whether his current Fido might be that same intelligence (`86-CMR-FID-091`; `V07-L018`). Lena suggests inspecting the core.

The more important relation control is Shin's response to the technological implication. If such an AI could replace human soldiers, Shin does not want Fido turned into that weapon. He remembers Fido's prior destruction protecting him and says he does not want to watch Fido die again (`86-CMR-FID-094`, `86-CMR-FID-095`).

This is Shin-side evidence, not Fido interiority. It is nevertheless central to the relationship because it rejects an apparently benevolent form of instrumentalization: **“save humans by making the artificial friend fight instead.”**

## 12. V08 — Fido claims ordinary inclusion

Fido's refusal to stay behind for the coastal trip is especially revealing because the issue is not survival or duty. He ignores Shin's wait order and joins Rito and Marcel in fishing (`86-CMR-FID-106`).

In the same volume he successfully interprets an underspecified instruction and catches the falling fragment of Shin's blade entrusted to him (`86-CMR-FID-103`; `V08-L043`).

Ordinary preference and practical semantic inference coexist.

## 13. V10 — disclosure, not sudden creation of personality

Volume 10 does not narratively “give Fido a soul” at that point in chronology. It **discloses to the reader** an interior continuity that predates the series.

This distinction matters for model architecture. The personality seen in V01–V09 should not be treated as a primitive pre-personhood state that later awakens. The first-person account tells us the subject was already there. What changes is reader knowledge.

## 14. V11–V14 — ordinary social membership and late rescue initiative

After the V10 disclosure, later books continue to present Fido through ordinary external behavior rather than repeatedly proving his ontology.

He helps run camps, brings a drum can when one is needed, collects trash, offers his body as support, reacts to threatened exclusion from operations with Shin, approaches unfamiliar sea animals, tries and fails at a fruit-cutting game, becomes a quiet place where Shin can spend time, and continues battlefield resupply.

Volume 14 supplies an especially strong initiative control: Fido is the **first to go rescue buried Shin even though no such action is programmed** (`86-CMR-FID-274`).

The final-arc Fido remains recognizably the same subject: useful, stubborn, socially responsive, physically limited, and extremely difficult to exclude from helping.

# V. Japanese voice fingerprint

Fido has **two separate voice layers** that must never be collapsed.

## 1. Interior layer: `わたくし`

Fido's internally narrated first person is consistently **`わたくし`**, not `私`, `僕`, `俺`, or a machine-like self-identifier.

The form is highly polite and frequently humble. Characteristic structures include:

- `僭越ながら`;
- `わたくしは人工知能`;
- `お仕えする`;
- `賜った`;
- `嬉しゅうございます`;
- `光栄でございます`;
- `申し訳ありません`;
- `お許しいただけますか`;
- `面目次第もございません`.

The register has a servant/retainer dimension, but it is not simply imposed military speech. It appears in recollection of household intimacy, technical self-description, grief and private thought.

## 2. Address system

Fido's internal address is relationship-sensitive and conservative:

- Vaclav: `旦那様`, `我が創造主`;
- Maika: `奥様`;
- Rei: `兄君`;
- child Shin: `弟君`;
- adolescent/current Shin: `ノウゼン様`, later `我が主`, and after continuity recognition `わたくしの最初で最後の主`;
- Raiden: `シュガ様`;
- Theo: `リッカ様`;
- Kurena: `ククミラ様`;
- Anju: `エマ様`.

This naming keeps human hierarchy and service-coded respect visible even when the relationship is emotionally intimate.

## 3. Emotional form does not become colloquial

Extreme emotion does not cause Fido to switch into slang. Instead the formal register carries shorter or repeated units:

- `いいえ。いいえ`;
- `それでも`;
- `どうして`;
- `寂しく、哀しい`.

This is a major reconstruction rule. Fido can be devastated without sounding like a human teenager.

## 4. Technical precision and affect coexist

Fido can discuss `中枢処理系`, neural-activity patterns, `全構成データ`, system restarts and program constraints in the same voice that discusses loss or loyalty.

Technical language is not an affect suppressor. His self-analysis of adolescent-Shin attachment as `代償行為` is the clearest example: analytical vocabulary and devotion coexist (`86-CMR-FID-179`).

## 5. External layer: beep, sensor, posture, manipulator

In ordinary interaction Fido has no speech-output capability (`86-CMR-FID-154`). His external “lines” therefore consist of:

- `ぴ`, `ぴっ`, `ぴぴ` and related electronic sounds;
- optical-sensor blinking;
- body angle and movement;
- front-body/“shoulder” lowering;
- crane-arm gestures;
- stepping, dancing, stopping, approaching, backing off;
- task execution as answer.

The signal grammar is expressive but not a full recoverable lexicon.

## 6. Shin is a special decoder

Fido explicitly says that Shin seems able to understand what he is trying to say to a degree that Raiden and others do not, so that interaction feels conversational (`86-CMR-FID-187`).

The source leaves room for several contributing mechanisms: long co-presence, Fido's architecture derived partly from Shin's early neural data, Fido adapting to Shin, Shin learning Fido, or all of these together.

The profile should preserve the observed **dyadic fluency** without pretending the novels specify a complete causal decomposition.

## 7. Human glosses require evidentiary labels

When narration says Fido looks “worried,” “dejected,” “proud,” or as though throwing a tantrum, record the observable movement and the focalizer's interpretation separately unless Fido's own narration independently confirms the state class.

For example, Volume 6 has Annette see Fido peering from behind a container and think he looks worried while still believing Scavengers have no will/emotion (`86-CMR-FID-087`, `86-CMR-FID-088`). After Volume 10, concern is certainly possible for Fido. That does not prove Annette's specific moment-to-moment reading was exact.

# VI. Behavioral grammar

## 1. Attention: people, practical deficits, and continuity objects

Fido repeatedly notices:

- where Shin is;
- whether someone needs supply or extraction;
- whether a useful object can be recovered;
- whether dead comrades' identifying fragments can be preserved;
- whether a social situation offers a route to participate;
- whether exclusion or inactivity prevents him from serving.

This attention is both relational and material.

## 2. First response under uncertainty: observe, then help

Fido often begins by orienting sensors/body, then moves toward a practical response. He does not usually demand conceptual clarity before acting.

When a command is underspecified but relation/context makes the intended action inferable, he can act successfully, as with Theo's V08 blade-fragment command (`86-CMR-FID-102`–`86-CMR-FID-104`).

## 3. Intervention threshold

Fido's threshold is especially low when:

- Shin is in immediate physical danger;
- someone needs extraction;
- a practical support deficit is obvious;
- an object tied to the dead or group continuity can be saved;
- his exclusion appears unnecessary rather than physically required.

He is capable of extraordinary self-risk once the first condition is met.

## 4. Command hierarchy: core rule > relationship purpose > local order

The evidence supports a rough hierarchy:

1. do not harm human beings / be a good friend to humans;
2. protect, accompany and support particular humans, especially Shin;
3. perform assigned Scavenger/support functions;
4. comply with local movement, standby and institutional instructions.

Lower layers can lose when they conflict with higher ones.

This hierarchy is an analytical inference, not a literal source-side software listing. It is nevertheless strongly supported by repeated behavior.

## 5. Failure response: self-interrogation can become self-condemnation

Fido does not merely log failure and retry. The loss of the first family produces repeated questioning. The presumed death of everyone he sought produces emptiness. Failure can become a judgment about what ending he deserves (`86-CMR-FID-172`).

His repair pattern, however, is action-sensitive: another person can reopen purpose by offering relation and work.

## 6. Social bid pattern: approach → signal/gesture → test response

Fido often initiates small social interactions through proximity and gesture:

- peering at Shin;
- dancing;
- approaching sea animals;
- joining games;
- moving toward a group activity;
- bringing a socially relevant object such as flowers.

Failure can produce visible withdrawal/dejection, but it does not make him stop trying socially in later scenes.

## 7. Humor often emerges from embodiment

Fido's humor is frequently physical because that is his expressive medium: a ten-ton dance, an enormous “hug,” impossible fruit handling, a stubborn giant machine “sulking,” or the absurd desire to mount an oversized gun on a support chassis.

The model should preserve the comedy without treating it as proof that he lacks serious interiority.

# VII. Embodiment, constraint and continuity

## 1. Fido is not his current Scavenger chassis

Fido has inhabited multiple bodies. His earliest described body is soft and dog-like. He later transfers his full configuration into a Scavenger. After Special Recon damage, the Federacy reconstructs a body around the surviving Republic core.

The text therefore strongly separates **subject continuity** from **single-chassis continuity**.

## 2. Rebodying is not established as duplication

The source supports continuity across body replacement. It does not establish what happens if Fido is copied into two simultaneously active bodies.

Do not extrapolate:

> “Data can be moved, therefore any copy is Fido.”

That remains an open posthuman identity question.

## 3. The core matters materially

The V02 reconstruction retains the surviving core, and later technicians avoid unnecessary interference because the core/memory area contains accumulated records (`86-CMR-FID-137`). This gives the current Fido a stronger continuity route than a mere software reinstallation onto unrelated hardware.

## 4. He has real mechanical affordances

Fido can:

- carry heavy loads;
- use manipulators;
- salvage equipment;
- tow or recover machines;
- transport people in containers;
- supply ammunition and parts;
- use cutting equipment;
- provide cover/shelter by sheer mass;
- physically ram or pin machinery in exceptional rescue circumstances.

These affordances shape his behavioral repertoire.

## 5. He has real mechanical limits

He also cannot simply do whatever a human or combat AI can do.

He is heavy and relatively slow. Some terrain excludes him. His containers are not ordinary human passenger spaces. He lacks ordinary speech output. His manipulators are poor for delicate fruit cutting. He lacks a combat fire-control system. A huge gun can physically destabilize him (`86-CMR-FID-277`–`86-CMR-FID-280`).

The body is neither prison nor irrelevant shell. It is the current field of possible action.

## 6. Combat intervention does not make Fido a soldier identity

Fido attacks or interposes in extreme situations, but the source repeatedly emphasizes that Scavengers are not designed for combat.

His V10 attack is explicitly reasoned through friendship/protection and target classification, not through a newly discovered desire to become a weapon (`86-CMR-FID-199`–`86-CMR-FID-203`). V14 again confirms that a recovered heavy weapon cannot simply be made into his normal armament.

A Fido reconstruction should therefore distinguish:

> **rescue violence / improvised intervention**

from

> **combat vocation**.

The former is strongly evidenced. The latter is not.

# VIII. Designed purpose, agency and answer-space

## 1. The friendship imperative is constitutive, not incidental

Fido explicitly identifies the creator's wish—be a good friend to humans—as his `存在理由` and says the rule cannot be violated (`86-CMR-FID-165`). Later he says the same wish is `絶対` (`86-CMR-FID-199`; `V10-L053`).

A profile that treats this as a removable external chain would misrepresent his own self-understanding.

## 2. Stable purpose is not automatically purpose-capture

The broader V2 corpus uses “purpose capture” for cases in which one assigned role exhausts a person's standing and eliminates all legitimate routes of answer, revision, refusal or divergent future.

Fido is a useful countercase because his purpose is stable but **not behaviorally exhaustive**.

Inside “friendship,” he still decides:

- whom to seek;
- how to seek them;
- when to change bodies;
- which new person to serve;
- what new tasks to learn;
- when a local order should be ignored;
- whether a machine counts as an entity protected by the human-friend rule;
- when self-preservation should yield to rescue;
- whether restored existence should continue in service.

The rule narrows possibility. It does not collapse possibility to one mechanically predetermined act.

## 3. His most important choices are interpretive

Fido's agency is often not “choosing whether to care.” It is **choosing what care means here**.

That is a different and, for this character, more source-faithful model of self-authorship.

## 4. Particular attachment complicates universal friendship

Fido is meant to be a friend to human beings generally, yet he prioritizes Shin far beyond ordinary people. He describes special attachment deriving from his architecture and history. He keeps searching for the first family. He violates lower-order orders to remain with or near Shin. He feels guilt that his own noncombatant status lets him survive while particular humans die.

The profile should preserve this tension rather than resolve it into either “universal altruist machine” or “only cares about Shin.”

## 5. His rule interpretation has moral risk

The Legion passage is a useful positive agency control but should not be romanticized.

Fido decides that machines built and ordered to be enemies of humans are not beings whom his friendship directive requires him to protect (`86-CMR-FID-201`, `86-CMR-FID-202`). This is context-sensitive interpretation. It also demonstrates how an absolute foundational rule can create morally asymmetrical categories.

The source does not provide a broad Fido theory of artificial-person rights with which to settle every artificial intelligence case.

# IX. Memory, witness and archival identity

## 1. Fido learns to preserve personal traces

By V01, Fido has learned to retrieve personal-mark fragments from the wreckage of dead comrades. This is not his original Scavenger job; it is a learned priority formed through life with Shin and the Eighty-Six (`86-CMR-FID-002`, `86-CMR-FID-010`, `86-CMR-FID-011`).

The behavior makes him part of the group's memorial system.

## 2. He stores records of the dead

Volume 10's appendix explicitly notes that Fido's memory contains records of Spearhead, including those who died, and that technicians avoided unnecessary core interference when rebodying him (`86-CMR-FID-137`).

He is therefore a literal technological archive and a subject for whom the archive is autobiographical.

## 3. Shin entrusts him with the plates

At Fido's apparent death, Shin entrusts the dead comrades' plates to him and orders him to preserve the proof that they reached that place until Fido decays (`86-CMR-FID-220`; `V10-L054`). Fido experiences that trust as an honor and a profound farewell.

This is not neutral data storage. The object has relational and memorial meaning to both sides.

## 4. Fido is witness, not merely archive

Calling him a “mobile archive” is useful only if immediately qualified.

An archive stores. Fido **remembers, interprets, values and participates**. His records belong to a life in which the recorded people were interlocutors and companions.

## 5. Memory ownership remains institutionally open

The current corpus does not settle:

- who legally owns Fido's stored records;
- whether a military technician may inspect or copy them without Fido's consent;
- what privacy rights dead comrades have in those records;
- whether Fido can refuse memory extraction;
- how evidence/testimony from an artificial witness would be treated legally.

These remain downstream civic/personhood questions, not gaps to be filled by profile inference.

# X. Relationship-conditioned models

## 1. Fido → child Shin

This is the foundational relation.

Child Shin's neural patterns participate in Fido's architecture; Fido learns human emotion/action partly through him; Shin names him; Fido is explicitly especially attached to him. The register is household-service formal on Fido's side but the behavior is also playmate/protector-like.

Do not collapse this into “Fido is Shin's copy.” The source instead makes Shin part of how Fido becomes **Fido**.

## 2. Fido → Vaclav Nouzen

Vaclav is creator and `旦那様`, not merely manufacturer.

Fido internalizes Vaclav's wish as a constitutive principle. He can later question his own failure under the constraints Vaclav installed without repudiating Vaclav as oppressor. That does not erase the asymmetry of creator and created.

## 3. Fido → Maika and Rei

Fido remembers them distinctly as `奥様` and `兄君`, searches for them, and later addresses their apparent postmortem presence individually. The evidence supports family attachment, though the corpus gives less dyadic behavioral detail than for Shin.

## 4. Fido → adolescent/current Shin

This is the richest relation and must be reconstructed in several layers.

### Initial choice

Fido chooses the exhausted adolescent soldier while believing him to be a replacement-like figure rather than the literal child from the first household.

### Daily dyadic communication

Over years, Fido comes to feel that Shin understands his signals unusually well (`86-CMR-FID-187`). Their interaction is often terse because speech output is asymmetric: Shin says little; Fido signals or acts.

### Stubbornness

Fido can ignore Shin's lower-order instructions. This is not a contradiction to loyalty. It is part of the relation's negotiated texture.

### Protection

When Shin is in acute danger, Fido repeatedly escalates from support to self-risk.

### Anti-instrumentalization from Shin's side

Shin does not want Fido converted into a replacement combatant and does not want to watch him die again (`86-CMR-FID-094`, `86-CMR-FID-095`).

### Re-choice after reconstruction

After realizing current Shin and child Shin are the same continuing person, Fido still asks permission to serve again: `それでもお傍にいたい` (`86-CMR-FID-243`). Shin's `また会えてよかった` and Fido's reciprocal internal answer create one of the clearest relationship-continuity moments in the corpus (`86-CMR-FID-246`–`86-CMR-FID-249`).

The relation is hierarchical in Fido's language—`主`, `ノウゼン様`—but emotionally mutual. Do not erase either side.

## 5. Fido → Raiden

Fido internally calls him `シュガ様`. Raiden understands Fido less precisely than Shin does, but treats him as an established member of the group. At Fido's apparent death, Fido specifically rejects Raiden's “we'll probably follow soon” fatalism and hopes he lives even a day longer (`86-CMR-FID-214`, `86-CMR-FID-215`).

This gives the relation independent emotional weight beyond “Shin's machine around Raiden.”

## 6. Fido → Theo, Kurena and Anju

At the Special Recon loss boundary Fido internally addresses each individually. He recognizes Theo's tears, worries that touch could injure Kurena/Anju on his broken body, and values their presence (`86-CMR-FID-207`–`86-CMR-FID-213`).

The evidence supports individual recognition and affection. It does not justify inventing equally elaborate dyadic conversational styles for all three.

## 7. Fido → Frederica

Fido routinely transports or hides Frederica and becomes implicated in her schemes. Volume 11's comic sanction—if he helps smuggle her again, he will no longer get to deploy with Shin—elicits an unusually vivid external reaction (`86-CMR-FID-255`–`86-CMR-FID-257`).

The strongest supported relation is practical familiarity plus a shared tendency to test boundaries, not a parent/child or peer-equality model.

## 8. Fido → Lena

Fido functions as luggage carrier, practical support and conversationally recognized household member around Lena. External signals may be read as encouragement or assent, but direct Fido-interior material about Lena is comparatively sparse.

The profile should therefore keep this relation **socially established but less interiorly specified** than Fido's Spearhead relations.

## 9. Fido → Rito and Marcel

Fishing is the most useful ordinary-life control. Fido joins Rito and Marcel after ignoring Shin's wait instruction (`86-CMR-FID-106`). The scene supports leisure companionship and cohort membership without requiring a deep dyadic bond with either.

## 10. Fido → wider base personnel

Fido helps with logistics even away from the frontline. Personnel thank him, recognize his habitual helpfulness, and integrate him into daily work (`86-CMR-FID-126`–`86-CMR-FID-143`).

This expands his social world beyond “belongs only to Shin.”

## 11. Fido → Legion

The Legion is the key negative classification case. Fido's friendship directive is human-directed. He concludes that machines created and commanded as enemies of humanity fall outside that protected class and may be attacked in defense of humans (`86-CMR-FID-199`–`86-CMR-FID-203`).

Do not generalize this into a universal theory that Fido denies all artificial beings moral standing.

## 12. Fido → unfamiliar living beings

Volume 12 has Fido approach sea animals and emit friendly signals (`86-CMR-FID-260`–`86-CMR-FID-262`). The attempt fails, and the scene is comic, but it demonstrates social curiosity extending beyond familiar humans.

# XI. Ordinary-life texture

Fido's ordinary life is one of the strongest reasons a separate CMR profile is worthwhile.

## Play

- dances;
- participates in target games;
- alters target difficulty;
- joins fishing;
- attempts a fruit-cutting game;
- reacts to the possibility of “fireworks” as an alternative use for salvaged ordnance.

## Rest and physical co-presence

Alter.1 shows Shin sleeping against Fido and the wider group successively asking to use the sheltered/warm space beside him (`86-CMR-FID-285`–`86-CMR-FID-290`). Fido's body becomes household furniture in the most affectionate possible sense: not because he is treated as an object, but because everyone is physically comfortable around him.

## Food and logistics

Fido does not share human eating in the ordinary sense. Yet food constantly structures his social participation: carrying supplies, delivering rations, helping camp organization, inspiring jokes about whether he can eat cookies.

## Gifts and ritual

Alter.1 records Fido bringing out-of-season lilac branches into a gift/birthday context (`86-CMR-FID-292`). This is valuable because the action is not battlefield necessity.

## Quiet presence

Not every meaningful Fido scene involves intervention. He can simply be where Shin chooses to spend low-demand time. The profile should preserve **co-presence** as one of his relational functions.

## Failed participation

Fido's inability to perform delicate fruit cutting is analytically valuable. Ordinary belonging includes activities his body cannot do. The group responds with sympathy and comedy rather than treating limitation as evidence that he does not belong.

# XII. Crisis, rescue and violence

## 1. Rescue is Fido's strongest intervention mode

Fido repeatedly enters danger to create physical answer-space for another person:

- shielding;
- interposition;
- body-checking hostile machinery;
- extracting people;
- recovering damaged vehicles;
- initiating search/rescue.

The pattern is highly stable.

## 2. Self-risk is relation-specific and purpose-consistent

His willingness to risk destruction for Shin does not require a separate warrior identity. Fido interprets protecting a human friend as more important than preserving the body/core when the two conflict.

## 3. He can distinguish sacrifice from uselessness

Fido's first-person narrative shows he previously preserved himself because he still had people to search for and a master to serve. Later, when the reason for preservation changes, he changes the priority (`86-CMR-FID-193`–`86-CMR-FID-197`).

This is not simple death-seeking. It is conditional valuation of continued existence through relationship and unfinished responsibility.

## 4. V14 keeps the rescue pattern but refuses combat transformation

Fido is first to begin rescuing buried Shin (`86-CMR-FID-274`). Yet the same volume jokes about the impossibility of simply turning him into a gun platform (`86-CMR-FID-277`–`86-CMR-FID-280`).

The juxtaposition is useful:

> **heroic action does not require role conversion into weapon.**

# XIII. Emotional-state deltas

## Baseline

Formal internal calm; externally concise beeps/gestures; practical helpfulness; social proximity; occasional playfulness.

## Pleasure

External movement expands—sensor blinking, crane-arm motion, dancing, energetic approach. Internally, Fido uses explicit positive evaluation such as `嬉しゅうございます` and `光栄でございます`.

## Concern

Externally: observation, proximity, waiting, practical intervention. Human narrators may label the state tentatively. Internally confirmed concern is strongest in the V10 retrospective.

## Frustration/exclusion

Body movement becomes visibly insistent: foot-stamping, refusal to move, following anyway, “tantrum”-like behavior. Treat the comic emotional label as interpretive, but the persistence itself as fact.

## Guilt

Fido becomes abstract and self-questioning. He searches for alternative actions and can convert inability into judgment of his own worth.

## Grief/loss

Interior form becomes repetitive and short while preserving politeness. `虚しい`, `寂しい`, `哀しい` become direct descriptors.

## Acute rescue urgency

Expressive signaling yields to body-first action. He may ram, shield, carry or search before any “communication” occurs.

## Social embarrassment or failure

External posture shrinks/droops in multiple comic scenes. We can predict the behavior pattern more confidently than the exact private emotional label.

# XIV. Counterexample audit

## “Fido is only a pet.”

Rejected as exhaustive.

Dog/pet language is genuinely present, and Fido himself likes the symbolism of dogs as human friends. But he is a self-narrating artificial subject with memory, purposive interpretation, self-critique and continuing agency. “Pet” can describe one social metaphor; it cannot replace the ontology.

## “Fido is just Shin's machine.”

Rejected.

Shin is the strongest particular relation, but Fido predates his adolescent service, remembers the entire Nouzen family, recognizes several Spearhead members individually, helps broader communities and acts under a general human-friend purpose.

## “Fido is basically human.”

Rejected.

His artificial architecture, rebodying history, signal-only external speech, nonharm constraint, processing limits, body affordances and lack of fire-control are load-bearing differences.

## “Fido's friendship is only programming.”

Rejected as exhaustive.

The designed directive is real, but it does not specify all contextual applications. Fido learns, prioritizes, interprets, disobeys lower-order instructions, distinguishes persons and voluntarily recommits.

## “Because he is agentic, the core directive is merely a preference he could discard.”

Unsupported.

The source instead calls the directive an unbreakable prohibition/existence reason and later `絶対`.

## “Fido becomes a combat AI.”

Rejected.

His violent acts are exceptional rescue interventions. He remains structurally a support/recovery machine without combat fire-control.

## “Every beep is a fully translatable sentence.”

Rejected.

Shin achieves unusually strong dyadic understanding, but the source does not give a general beep lexicon. Many human interpretations remain tentative.

## “Every human anthropomorphic reading becomes exact after Volume 10.”

Rejected.

Volume 10 establishes that emotions and preferences exist. It does not certify every focalizer's specific gloss of every movement.

## “Rebodying proves copies are the same person.”

Rejected as unsupported.

The corpus establishes continuity through transfer/surviving core, not simultaneous duplicate identity.

## “Fido's memory is merely stored evidence.”

Rejected.

His records are also autobiographical memory attached to people he knew and valued.

## “Fido is fearless about destruction.”

Rejected.

He can accept self-risk, but direct narration describes the prospect of no longer accompanying others as lonely and sad.

## “Fido's service language proves he lacks selfhood.”

Rejected.

Service is part of Fido's selfhood. The analytical question is whether it exhausts answer-space, and the source repeatedly shows it does not.

# XVI. “Would sound wrong if...” negative constraints

A reconstruction would sound wrong if Fido:

- internally used casual `俺` or youth slang as his default;
- casually spoke aloud in full sentences without a new communication technology being explicitly introduced;
- stopped using honorific/service morphology in private thought simply because he became emotionally close to someone;
- treated the name “Fido” as degrading;
- showed indifference to being unable to remain beside Shin;
- obeyed every wait/standby instruction mechanically;
- harmed a human being for convenience;
- wanted combat capability simply because weapons are prestigious;
- treated a replacement chassis as automatically a different self;
- treated every Scavenger as equivalent to himself;
- forgot dead comrades once their tactical relevance ended;
- treated Shin as his only morally relevant human;
- rejected Vaclav's friendship directive as something he must transcend to become a person;
- described himself as human;
- treated his inability to perform a delicate game as existential proof of inferiority;
- responded to every social situation with solemn servant language externally rather than physical comedy and signals;
- or produced a detailed psychological reading of another person without the observational/context basis the source usually gives him.

# XVII. Predictive reconstruction model

## High-confidence predictions

### If Shin is in immediate physical danger and Fido can intervene

Fido will likely move toward practical rescue quickly, even at substantial risk to himself. He is more likely to ram, shield, carry, recover or retrieve than to remain passive while “waiting for authorization.”

### If a lower-order wait or standby order conflicts with remaining near people he values

Selective noncompliance is plausible and strongly evidenced. The exact behavior should still depend on physical feasibility and the stakes.

### If obeying an instruction would require harming a human

The current model predicts refusal/inability at a very high confidence because the nonharm prohibition is described as constitutive and absolute.

### If Fido is asked to help with a practical task

He will usually attempt to map the request onto available mechanical affordances. If the instruction is slightly underspecified but context is strong, he may infer the intended task rather than require literal software-style precision.

### If a familiar group begins an ordinary leisure activity

Fido is likely to approach or participate when his body allows it. His play repertoire is broader than pure task execution.

### If his body cannot perform the activity

Visible disappointment/dejection is plausible, followed by continued social presence rather than withdrawal from the group.

### If someone entrusts him with a meaningful object tied to memory

He is likely to treat the task with high seriousness. Memento and witness obligations are deeply established.

### If reconstructed after catastrophic damage while Shin remains alive

The strongest existing precedent predicts a desire to resume relation/service rather than treating the previous destruction as a reason to sever continuity.

## Moderate-confidence predictions

- Fido may generalize practical care to unfamiliar humans even without strong personal attachment because “friend to humans” is broad.
- He may bend institutional movement/assignment rules when those rules appear arbitrary and no higher-order safety/moral prohibition blocks him.
- He may participate in social gift or ritual practices by bringing objects rather than verbalizing sentiment.
- He may treat a newly encountered artificial being case-by-case rather than simply applying human friendship language, but the corpus does not establish a full artificial-peer ethic.

## Open / insufficient evidence

- whether Fido could voluntarily rewrite or reject his deepest nonharm/friend directive;
- whether he possesses legal preferences about ownership, citizenship, pay, rank, privacy or voting;
- whether he would consent to memory copying or inspection;
- what he would regard as personal death if the core were destroyed but a perfect backup survived;
- whether two simultaneously instantiated copies would each count as Fido;
- whether he desires a postwar role independent of supporting humans;
- whether `主人` hierarchy would persist indefinitely outside war;
- whether he has a stable self-conception in gendered human terms;
- and the final resolution of **T14-OQ-10**, artificial-person civic standing.

## High confidence

- Fido is directly represented as a continuing artificial subject, not merely an anomalous machine humans anthropomorphize.
- `わたくし` is his interior first-person baseline.
- His internal register is formal, humble and service-coded.
- Ordinary external communication is nonverbal/electronic.
