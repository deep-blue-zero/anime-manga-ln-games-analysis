# 86-Eighty-Six V2 — Rito Oriya Character Reference Profile
## CMR-6 scaled profile: source-grounded reconstruction of voice, fear, humor, command, Eighty-Six pride, curiosity, ordinary futurity, nonrevenge, death, and predictive limits

# I. Authority and scope

This document reconstructs **Rito Oriya / リト・オリヤ** across the locked Japanese corpus through Volume 14 plus audited Alter.1 material. It is a derived reconstruction surface subordinate to the Japanese novels, the Phase-5 locator authority, Phase-8 source verification, canonical V2 deep readings and ledgers, and the mature specialist synthesis.

Rito requires one unusually strict methodological guardrail: **his death in Volume 13 must not become the organizing cause of his earlier personality**. Volume 14 makes his death politically and emotionally enormous. It drives grief, anger, institutional crisis, Lyudmila's first fear of death, and the unresolved problem of whether public vindication can preserve a particular person instead of turning him into a martyr-symbol. Those effects are real. They are downstream effects.

The living Rito must first be reconstructed as the person who existed before those effects:

- a conspicuously talkative and bright younger Eighty-Six;
- a veteran who is still openly frightened by death;
- a former subordinate of Shin and Raiden who grows into command of Claymore Squadron and later a battalion;
- a boy whose mouth often gets ahead of his organization of thought;
- a tactical improviser capable of both excellent reflexes and emotional overextension;
- a socially porous teenager who befriends strangers across language barriers;
- a movie fan, gossip participant, fisherman, cake eater, library browser and collector of possible futures;
- someone who can say the Republic may never be forgiven without needing personal revenge;
- and someone who finally refuses to let another Eighty-Six girl's grievance be exhaustively converted into an identity as a killer.

The governing reconstruction question is therefore:

> **How does an Eighty-Six who never fully stops fearing death preserve his pride as a soldier while learning that survival, curiosity, pleasure, study, friendship and an indefinite plurality of future wants are also legitimate parts of who he is?**

This profile uses **162 source-verified diagnostic controls**. Sixteen rows reuse frozen Phase-5 locator authority; the remainder retain exact EPUB/XHTML routes and Japanese anchors under transparent `LOCATOR_GAP` status. Three Alter.1 controls are retained; the chronology-breaking perfume-battle material is explicitly excluded. No synthetic dialogue is admitted as evidence.

# II. Reconstruction thesis

Rito is best reconstructed as a **bright, future-facing and socially permeable young commander whose fear of death remains genuine even as he learns to make that fear compatible with Eighty-Six pride, professional responsibility and an expanding appetite for life**.

The crucial point is that Rito does not “overcome cowardice.” The Japanese source does not define his fear as a defect that disappears. In Volume 5 he quietly admits to Shin that dying and having others die still frighten him. In Volume 6, after the Sirin corpse-road breaks his combat function, he says again that he is afraid. The change is not fear → fearlessness. It is **fear → articulated choice under fear**.

When Shin offers him a legitimate route out of the next operation, Rito refuses removal. He does not claim the danger has become unreal. He says `怖いです`, and then insists he does not want to run from battle or accept what he experiences as his own disgrace. This is not a blanket endorsement of death-pride: the same arc makes him recoil from the Sirins precisely because their cheerful expendability reveals the horrifying terminus of an Eighty-Six identity organized only around fighting until death.

His later development therefore revises pride from:

> **I prove myself by fighting until I die**

into something closer to:

> **I can remain an Eighty-Six and a soldier while also protecting the particular life that is mine.**

Volume 8 makes the positive side explicit. Rito starts collecting possibilities: marine creatures, university study, fossils, flying machines, monster movies, unfamiliar places, whatever seems interesting enough to carry home and examine later. His key formulation is not a fixed career plan but `だから俺、いろいろ望みたいです`—he wants to want many things. Futurity for Rito is **proliferative rather than programmatic**.

That future orientation does not make him less of a soldier. He remains a commander, reacts rapidly in combat, supports Shin's independent action, and repeatedly chooses to return to the field. Nor does soldier identity make him morally indiscriminate. In Volume 9 he hesitates when enemy vehicles contain people he personally knows. In Volume 11 he can rescue Republic civilians he still does not forgive. In Volume 13 he refuses a Fawn girl's request for revenge killing because he does not want her grievance to foreclose her into becoming a killer. His final mercy action occurs under a different constraint and must not be collapsed back into the revenge request he refused.

The concise reconstruction formula is:

> **`俺`-centered energetic colloquial male voice + occasional softer `僕` in reflective relation-specific speech + conspicuous talk-first cognition + gallows humor + visible fear + strong corrigibility + quick tactical reflexes + front-leading command energy + socially porous youthfulness + curiosity without shame about educational gaps + Eighty-Six pride revised away from compulsory death + ordinary pleasure as legitimate future + unforgiving nonrevenge + direct anti-foreclosure care + terminal self-identification as Rito and Eighty-Six.**

# III. Stable personality baseline

## 1. He is noisy in a way that signifies life rather than superficiality

Rito's reunion with Shin and Raiden establishes the rhythm immediately. He reacts to men he believed dead with shock, then leaps almost instantly into a joke about Shin having died and “job-changed” into a real death god and perhaps everyone else already being dead too. Raiden laughs; Shin sighs. The comedy is not random garnish. It establishes a social reflex: when intensity spikes, Rito often **speaks into it**.

That tendency persists. He blurts what he thinks about Republic architecture, gets physically shut up by Taiga, asks questions before fully organizing them, spills gossip, joins speculative brainstorming, reacts loudly to strange proposals, and makes himself useful company to quieter peers precisely because silence is not his default.

Volume 9 gives the strongest external control through Yuto. Yuto experiences Rito as unusually noisy but valuable because Rito does not wear the same “shadow of death” as many Eighty-Six. Rito talks as if tomorrow will exist. He behaves as though surviving today and continuing tomorrow are ordinary assumptions. That quality is not naïveté; it is a hard-won developmental difference.

## 2. Fear is visible and speakable

Rito is not emotionally opaque. He pales at casualty projections. He curls into himself after the Sirin corpse-road. His voice can edge toward crying. He admits fear in plain words. When frightened, his dignity does not depend on denying the state.

This is one of the clearest contrasts with a stereotyped veteran reconstruction. Rito has combat experience and command competence, but those do not erase his teenage nervous system or his continued attachment to other people's survival.

The correct model therefore never uses “veteran” as a synonym for “desensitized.”

## 3. He is curious without protecting himself through false expertise

Rito asks elementary questions that more self-conscious characters might conceal. He asks why destroying Legion reinforcements is not itself the objective. Later he asks what nuclear weapons actually are, what they can do, and why they are dangerous. He and the other Eighty-Six know their formal education is incomplete. Rito does not convert that deprivation into performative certainty.

His curiosity is often fast and messy. He can leap to an idea before testing whether it is sensible. But this is also why his future expands so quickly once permission exists. Knowledge does not threaten his pride. It gives him another thing to want.

## 4. He is socially porous

In the Regicide Fleet Countries, Rito and other young soldiers form friendships across language barriers through food, cards, exercise and competitive nonsense. This matters because the social ease is not guaranteed by his history. He has every reason to treat outsiders as potential threats. Instead, once basic safety exists, he is unusually willing to play.

This permeability also explains why he can build relationships with artificial persons. Lyudmila begins as something he finds terrifying. She does not remain a category. He listens to her, answers her, remembers her, and eventually addresses the moral lesson she offers him without claiming that she has become human.

## 5. He is direct enough to become tactless

A recurring social flaw is explicit in the text: Rito tends to say what occurs to him before arranging it into the most useful or diplomatic form. Michihi can correct him for skipping steps. Others tease him for embarrassing history. He can ask an awkward question in a briefing because he genuinely wants the answer.

This is not the same as low intelligence. The pattern is **high immediacy, incomplete filtering**.

# IV. Developmental state model

## 1. Earlier Eighty-Six period — dependence before command

Alter.1 preserves a useful earlier control. Rito is young enough, and dependent enough on the older veterans, that Anju calls him `甘えん坊のリト君` and warns him that relying on Shin during battle will get him killed. He nearly cries when reminded that older protectors cannot always be there. Raiden reassures him by ruffling his hair.

This scene prevents later rank from becoming retroactive maturity. Rito had to learn independent battlefield judgment. He did not begin as the self-sufficient commander seen later.

## 2. V04 — veteran skill and younger-brother energy coexist

By Volume 4 Rito is already Second Lieutenant and commander of Claymore Squadron. His experience visibly exceeds Dustin's. He moves through training with the physical ease of someone whose body has internalized combat.

Yet the voice remains youthful. He races past exhausted comrades, jokes with former commanders, blurts disrespectful observations about Republic infrastructure and answers tactical requests with loose `りょーかいです` rather than stiff officer formality.

Rank has expanded his responsibility without erasing the person who talks too fast.

## 3. V05 — the Sirins expose the death-pride limit

Volume 5 is the first major reconstruction hinge.

Rito remembers that Shin was his first squadron commander. Surviving his most vulnerable beginner period under elite veterans gave him enough skill to protect later newcomers. But it also meant he never normalized watching everyone beside him die to the same degree as older survivors. He still fears both death and bereavement.

The Sirins force him to see what Eighty-Six pride can become if “fight until death” is treated as complete identity. Their road of destroyed bodies feels like an obscene mirror. His horror is not proof that he was never an Eighty-Six. He explicitly insists that he is one. The crisis is that he can no longer accept a definition in which being an Eighty-Six means cheerful self-consumption.

This is why `V05-L002` and `V05-L048` belong together: **fear of death and critique of meaningless death are not repudiations of pride; they are what begins revising it.**

## 4. V06 — choice under fear

After Sirin trauma impairs his battlefield function, Rito apologizes for consequences to Shin. Shin offers him a legitimate noncombat option for the next operation. The offer matters because it creates real answer-space.

Rito says no.

The scene must not be read as proof that he secretly was not frightened. He explicitly says he is. He also says that fleeing battle and accepting that form of disgrace would violate how he wants to live. He intends to regulate the fear enough to function and asks not to be excluded.

That is self-authorship under constraint, not a cure.

## 5. V06 — Lyudmila gives fear a positive meaning

Lyudmila asks whether Sirins frighten him. Rito initially resists because admitting fear to the young-looking artificial girl wounds his pride; then he says `うん`.

Lyudmila tells him the fear is good evidence: he does not want to become like the endlessly dying birds. The conversation shifts his problem from “why am I weak?” to “what does the fear tell me I value?”

Later he answers in a gentler register. `忘れない。考えるよ。……僕にはそれがまだ、できるんだから`. The `僕` here is significant but bounded. It is not evidence that his normal first person changes globally. It appears in reflective, relation-specific speech as he acknowledges a living capacity an artificial dead soldier has asked him to use.

## 6. V08 — futurity proliferates

By the Fleet Countries arc, Rito's future is no longer merely “do not die.” He collects possibilities because they look fun.

He is excited by `原生海獣`, monster films, universities, fossils and strange technology. He has not selected a single vocation. That is the point. Eighty-Six life taught him not to want because wanting made loss worse. New conditions allow him to practice wanting before he knows where it ends.

His `いろいろ望みたい` is therefore a mature identity claim, not indecision.

## 7. V09 — command matures without becoming Shin-like

Rito is now a battalion commander. He detects ambush cues, orders dispersal, improvises movement solutions and continues to direct the unit when personally injured.

But his command style does not become Shin's. It is more visibly energetic, more improvisational and more socially contagious. When he develops a flashy movement solution he grins and compares it to Shin or Theo. Other commanders can think he is overdoing it.

This is a useful bounded competence model:

- high combat reflex;
- strong local improvisation;
- real unit leadership;
- less strategic abstraction than Lena or Shin;
- and emotional/social conditions that can affect judgment.

## 8. V11 — he claims his own dead

The Aldrecht confrontation is a crucial independence scene.

Rito tells Shin that **he**, not Shin, last spoke with Aldrecht and saw him off; therefore confronting the Legionized Aldrecht is Rito's role. The scene redistributes memorial responsibility. Shin is not the universal owner of every dead Eighty-Six merely because he is the Reaper.

Afterward, Rito is angry enough to speak sharply to a rescued Republic woman. He wanted to save Aldrecht. The rescue does not require emotional reconciliation with the Republic.

## 9. V11–V12 — nonrevenge without forgiveness

Rito says he probably will never forgive the Republic. That statement should remain intact.

At the same time, he no longer needs to personally push Republic civilians further down. Their refugee suffering reminds him too closely of his own childhood displacement for `いい気味だ` to become satisfying. This is neither pardon nor sentimental sympathy. It is **refusal to make revenge a necessary performance of grievance**.

Volume 12 further shows him imagining a different ending for Aldrecht: leaving the war, meeting wife and daughter, and being allowed a life after service. Rito's revision of pride now applies backward to the older generation as well.

## 10. V12 — ordinary recovery repertoire

When comrades notice that killing Aldrecht still hurts, Rito initially says he is fine. They tell him not to force it. He accepts the correction and constructs a day off: wander, go to the library, find weird books, eat too much cake.

That scene is extremely useful for prediction. Rito can receive care. He does not have to convert every emotional disturbance into private endurance. His repair repertoire is concrete, sensory and exploratory.

## 11. V13 — anti-revenge care becomes action

The Fawn girl asks for revenge. Rito opens his canopy because the answer should be delivered face-to-face and says no.

His reason is not that her grievance is invalid. He does not want her to become a killer, even if revenge is what she presently wants. The next locked line makes the relation explicit: she is another Eighty-Six, and he does not want her to be forced into further grief.

This is the mature form of Rito's future ethic. A justified wound must not become an exhaustive telos.

The later mercy killing occurs after circumstances change into an imminent lethal trap. It should not be retroactively substituted for the revenge killing he refused.

# V. Japanese voice fingerprint

## 1. First-person baseline: `俺`

Rito's ordinary masculine first person is **`俺`**. It appears in casual interaction, fear admission, soldier identity, command and future talk. Generated Rito should therefore default to `俺`, not formal `私` and not a uniformly soft `僕`.

## 2. `僕` exists as a bounded softer shift

The Lyudmila scene demonstrates `僕` in reflective/gentle speech: `僕にはそれがまだ、できるんだから`. This is valuable precisely because it is not the baseline. It marks a quieter interpersonal stance in which Rito is listening rather than projecting command energy.

Do not convert this into a permanent first-person switch.

## 3. Terminal `おれ` is damage-state orthography

His final `おれはリトで、えいてぃしっくすだよ` occurs during catastrophic brain injury. The hiragana simplification belongs to a terminal cognitive/embodied state. It is not evidence that ordinary Rito preferentially writes or speaks his first person in hiragana.

## 4. Upward politeness remains casual

Rito can use `です/ます`, `隊長`, ranks and polite requests with seniors while preserving a loose conversational rhythm. Forms such as `りょーかいです`, contractions, fillers, ellipses and excited interjections keep hierarchy from becoming ceremonial distance.

## 5. Excitement expands the line

Curiosity produces exclamation, repetition and lexical enthusiasm: `すげー`, rapid questions, elaborate speculative proposals, eager lists of things he wants to see. Excited Rito should sound **more verbally expansive**, not colder or more formal.

## 6. Combat compresses

When immediate danger arrives, the chatter can collapse into commands such as `散開っ！`, brief status reports and clear unit direction. This contrast is important. Talkativeness is not inability to prioritize under fire.

## 7. Strong emotion remains audible

Rito's voice can approach a cry, hitch, go quiet or become simple and stripped. The profile should not give him Shin's low-excess emotional compression. He often lets the listener know that he is frightened, angry, embarrassed or excited.

# VI. Behavioral grammar

## 1. Attention pattern: interesting thing → immediate verbalization

Rito notices novelty and often speaks before deciding whether the thought should be filtered. This explains both intellectual curiosity and social tactlessness.

## 2. Learning pattern: ask rather than bluff

If he does not understand the mechanism or strategic purpose, he asks. Educational deprivation does not become a reason to pretend mastery.

## 3. Social pattern: join first, formalize later

With peers he joins games, food, gossip or competition quickly. Social belonging is enacted through doing things together more often than through explicit declarations of intimacy.

## 4. Threat pattern: rapid local response

When danger is recognizable, Rito acts quickly: dispersal, support movement, relief, improvisational maneuver. His response time is one of his command strengths.

## 5. Moral-conflict pattern: direct human recognition can interrupt tactical simplicity

He hesitates when a technically valid target contains people he knows. He opens the canopy to answer the Fawn girl face-to-face. Abstraction becomes less sufficient when a person is directly present.

## 6. Fear pattern: admit → seek/receive regulation → choose

He does not reliably deny fear. He may become impaired by it. Once acknowledged, he can accept help, request continued participation or rest, and make a decision with the fear still present.

## 7. Care pattern: keep options open

Rito's mature care repeatedly protects the other person's continued answer-space: Lyudmila should be remembered; Aldrecht should have been allowed to leave war; Republic civilians need not be punished by him; the Fawn girl should not be forced into revenge identity; younger or injured comrades should be able to continue living.

# VII. Command and tactical profile

Rito is a real commander, not merely a cheerful junior attached to the protagonists.

By Volume 4 he commands Claymore Squadron. By Volume 9 he carries battalion responsibility. His strongest tactical traits are speed, local reading, willingness to improvise and a front-leading kinetic style. He can recognize ambush, coordinate relief and continue command after injury.

His limits are equally important:

- he asks strategic questions that Shin or Lena can answer more readily;
- he can become operationally impaired by trauma;
- his energetic solutions can become reckless;
- and his direct social cognition can skip explanatory steps.

A generated scenario should therefore not make him a strategic mastermind. He is a **competent and growing field commander** whose strengths are closer to fast local adaptation, group momentum and practical action.

# VIII. Relationship-conditioned registers

## 1. Rito → Shin

The relationship begins in former-command dependence. Shin helped him survive the most lethal beginner period of Processor service. Rito's `隊長` therefore contains both formal role memory and genuine attachment.

The mature relation changes in two directions. Rito can still seek Shin, tease him and defer to tactical judgment; but he also claims Aldrecht as **his** responsibility and acts independently in support of Shin rather than merely waiting for orders. The younger subordinate becomes a commander who can possess his own dead, choices and moral tasks.

## 2. Rito → Raiden

Raiden is an older stabilizing figure who can interrupt spirals without demanding elegant self-disclosure. Earlier material preserves almost literal older-brother handling. Later material allows rougher equality, but the regulatory trust remains.

## 3. Rito → Theo

Theo can see Rito as a younger-brother figure while also recognizing that Rito gets to futurity before he does. Rito's growth therefore becomes evidence for Theo rather than merely receiving guidance from him.

## 4. Rito → Michihi

This is a useful command-peer relation. Michihi can structure or correct Rito's impulsive verbalization without treating him as incompetent. Their banter exposes both equality and complementary strengths.

## 5. Rito → Yuto

Rito's noisy companionship is a form of care. With injured, quiet Yuto he offers presence, future talk and ordinary social pressure rather than solemn therapeutic language. Yuto values exactly that absence of death-shadow.

## 6. Rito → Lyudmila

This relation should never be romanticized without evidence. It is important because terror becomes dialogue. Rito acknowledges fear, receives Lyudmila's interpretation, and promises to remember and think while he still can. The softer `僕` register belongs here.

## 7. Rito → Aldrecht

Aldrecht is former caretaker, witness and inherited dead. Rito initially cannot tell Shin the whole story. Later he asserts that the final encounter was his and therefore the responsibility of stopping the Legionized Aldrecht is his as well. This is grief becoming owned rather than outsourced to the Reaper.

## 8. Rito → Republic civilians

He does not forgive them. He can rescue them anyway. The distinction is essential.

## 9. Rito → Federacy peers

The Federacy gives him conditions in which friendship and future can expand, but he never treats it as morally incapable of failure. He can question its strategy and later becomes the victim of a Federacy soldier's catastrophic misrecognition. The profile does not invent posthumous hatred on his behalf.

## 10. Rito → Claymore / Second Battalion

His unit-facing register is energetic and compressed under danger. He leads from the front enough that excitement can propagate through the formation. The same quality that makes him inspiring also creates overextension risk.

## 11. Rito → Fawn girl

This is the clearest late moral register: simple `俺`/`君`, face-to-face, direct refusal, no ideological performance. He does not tell her she is wrong to grieve. He refuses the proposed action because he does not want grief to exhaust her future.

# IX. Ordinary-life and future texture

Rito's ordinary preferences are not decorative. They define what “future” means for him.

Attested interests and behaviors include:

- monster movies and kaiju;
- marine creatures / `原生海獣`;
- possible university study;
- fossils;
- flying-machine or flying-bike curiosity;
- fishing with Marcel and Fido;
- libraries and “weird books”;
- cafes and a deliberately excessive amount of cake;
- Halloween costumes and face paint;
- gossip and romantic surveillance of older comrades;
- card games, food exchange and physical games with foreign peers;
- watching chess with the familiar cohort;
- wanting battle-camera footage of a sea monster badly enough to ask during an operation.

The pattern is not one hobby. It is **permission to be interested**.

# X. Eighty-Six pride: preserve, revise, do not invert

Rito never concludes that Eighty-Six pride was simply false.

He still dislikes running from battle. He still chooses soldier duty. He still identifies himself as Eighty-Six at death. What changes is the inferential chain from pride to expendability.

The Sirins show him that a subject who exists only to die usefully has lost something he wants to preserve. The Fleet Countries show him that wanting ordinary futures is not betrayal. Aldrecht shows that the older generation also deserved an exit from war. The Fawn girl shows that even righteous grievance should not be allowed to author the whole remaining self.

The mature Rito therefore fits the corpus-wide anti-foreclosure thesis especially well: **a stable identity can remain meaningful without becoming exhaustive**.

# XI. Republic, resentment, forgiveness and revenge

Four propositions must remain separate:

1. Rito believes the Republic wronged the Eighty-Six.
2. He does not think forgiveness is required and says he may never forgive.
3. He no longer needs personal revenge or further suffering by Republic civilians to validate the wrong.
4. He will still act to save people he does not forgive.

Collapsing these into “Rito forgives the Republic” erases his language. Collapsing them into “Rito still wants revenge” erases his behavior.

# XII. Emotional-state deltas

## Baseline
Bright, quick, noisy, physically energetic, easy to engage.

## Excitement
More words, more exclamation, speculative lists, `すげー`, rapid proposal generation.

## Embarrassment
Visible fluster; peers can expose old stories; may protest rather than become cold.

## Fear
Paling, hesitation, near-tears, direct admission; severe trauma can impair combat function.

## Regulated fear
Still says he is afraid, but decision-making returns. He can request participation or accept retreat/rest.

## Anger/grief
Speech sharpens and loses playfulness. With Aldrecht/Republic material he can sound bitter without converting bitterness into blanket revenge.

## Command danger
Short imperative/status language; banter drops until immediate problem is controlled.

## Terminal injury
Language and orthography simplify dramatically. This state must never be used as baseline voice training material.

# XIII. Death and posthumous impact

Rito's death is analytically major precisely because the preceding corpus established a person who had started expecting tomorrow.

His terminal self-identification—`おれはリトで、えいてぃしっくすだよ`—should not be romanticized as a slogan that explains his whole life. It is a damaged, final attempt to resist a category imposed by the shooter: “murderer.” He remembers himself as a particular person and as Eighty-Six. The scene does not deny that Rito is a soldier who has killed in war. It rejects the context-erasing identity imposed on him by propaganda and panic.

Volume 14 then shows what can go wrong after a death. Different people use Rito's death as grief object, anger object, political symbol, institutional indictment and memory anchor. Some of those uses are justified; none should replace the particular boy who liked monster movies, wanted to study strange sea creatures and was noisy enough to make Yuto feel tomorrow was normal.

`T14-OQ-26` therefore remains OPEN:

> **Can public vindication preserve Rito's particularity instead of converting him into a clean martyr-symbol?**

The Character Modeling Reference cannot resolve that before new primary-source evidence.

# XIV. Counterexample audit

## “Rito is the cheerful kid who dies.”
REJECT. Cheerfulness is real, but so are command responsibility, fear, grief, intellectual curiosity and moral independence. Death is downstream.

## “Rito is a coward who becomes brave.”
REJECT. His fear persists. Bravery is action and choice under acknowledged fear.

## “Rito is a mini-Shin.”
REJECT. His talkativeness, social permeability, visible affect and proliferating future desire sharply distinguish him.

## “Rito becomes anti-war or pacifist.”
REJECT. He remains a soldier and commander and chooses combat after being offered a legitimate way out.

## “Rito forgives the Republic.”
REJECT. He explicitly anticipates perhaps never forgiving it.

## “Rito wants revenge on the Republic.”
DOWNGRADE/REJECT as mature state. He rejects needing personal retaliation and rescues civilians despite unresolved grievance.

## “Fear proves he is less Eighty-Six than the others.”
REJECT. He repeatedly identifies as Eighty-Six; development revises what that identity may require.

## “The Sirins teach him that artificial people are basically human.”
REJECT. Lyudmila teaches him something through difference. Human-equivalence is not the lesson.

## “Rito and Lyudmila are romantic.”
OPEN/UNSUPPORTED. Their relationship is emotionally significant; the locked corpus does not establish romance.

## “Rito is a strategic genius.”
REJECT. He is a capable field commander and improviser who still asks basic strategic and technical questions.

## “His optimism is empty.”
REJECT. His future orientation emerges after explicit trauma and is repeatedly converted into concrete plans, interests and social behavior.

## “V14 tells us who Rito really was.”
REJECT. V14 is downstream memory and interpretation. Living-source evidence remains primary for reconstruction.

# XV. Mischaracterization traps

Do not write Rito as:

- permanently fearless;
- ashamed of asking questions;
- solemn and taciturn;
- strategically omniscient;
- only comic relief;
- only a younger-brother mascot;
- politically forgiving because he rejects revenge;
- revenge-driven because he remains angry;
- someone whose soldier identity disappears when he wants a civilian future;
- someone who sees Sirins as interchangeable machines;
- a character whose importance begins at death;
- or a martyr whose symbolic utility is more important than his particular life.

# XVI. “Would sound wrong if...” negative constraints

A generated Rito would sound wrong if he consistently used `僕` instead of `俺`; if every sentence were polished before speaking; if he hid ignorance to protect status; if he delivered cold strategic abstractions in Lena's register; if he treated fear as shameful weakness; if he stopped joking with older comrades after promotion; if ordinary future plans were narrowed to one fixed career; if he forgave the Republic in order to prove maturity; or if his Fawn-girl refusal were rewritten as generic pacifism.

# XVII. Predictive model

## High-confidence predictions

### If he encounters a strange new technology or creature
He will likely ask what it does and how it works, often before checking whether the timing is ideal. If it sounds fun, he may immediately connect it to something he could see, study or try later.

### If an older commander gives a clear tactical reason
He is likely to accept the rationale even if he initially asked a simplistic question. Corrigibility is part of competence.

### If a peer is frightened
He is unlikely to demand fearlessness as the price of belonging. His own development gives him reason to distinguish fear from inability to choose.

### If he himself is frightened
