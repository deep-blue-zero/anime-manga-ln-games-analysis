# 86-Eighty-Six V2 — Viktor Idinarohk / Vika Character Reference Profile
## CMR-5 scaled profile: source-grounded reconstruction of personality, Japanese voice, extreme cognition, creator power, princely duty, attachment, personhood ethics, humor, and predictive limits

# I. Authority and scope

This document reconstructs **Viktor Idinarohk / ヴィークトル・イディナローク**, usually called **Vika / ヴィーカ**, across the locked Japanese corpus through Volume 14 plus audited Alter.1 material. It is a reconstruction surface, not a replacement for the Japanese novels, the Phase-5 locator authority, the Phase-8 verification layer, or the canonical specialist corpus.

Vika is unusually vulnerable to flattening because the source itself repeatedly offers seductive totalizing descriptions of him:

- a genius;
- a prince;
- the creator of the Sirins;
- a cold-blooded serpent;
- a person who has violated taboos around corpses and identity;
- someone capable of analyzing people with unnerving precision;
- someone who describes himself as a monster wearing human form;
- and someone others sometimes perceive in nearly the same terms.

All of those descriptions carry evidence. None is sufficient by itself.

The central reconstruction problem is therefore:

> **How does a person who can turn death, cognition, bodies, military force, dynastic status, and even grief into solvable systems nevertheless form attachments, accept duties, preserve some forms of refusal, and repeatedly choose to bind his own exceptional power?**

That question requires preserving several contradictions rather than dissolving them.

Vika genuinely has a lower conventional taboo response than most characters. At five, he cut into his dead mother's preserved brain because he wanted to restore her. He later transformed the technology behind that failed resurrection attempt into the Sirin system. He can speak of expendable artificial soldiers with operational ruthlessness and authorize their sacrifice. He can study another person's abnormal ability with the avidity of a researcher. His family maintains a literal list of things he must not be permitted to do because ordinary interpretive latitude around him is dangerous.

Yet the same source shows him designing Lerche without absolute obedience, refusing to preserve her as an indefinitely replaceable backup, asking rather than commanding when she invokes a private boundary, apologizing to an individual Sirin who burns, carrying Lerche's severed remains, retaining a bodily link so that no one can remove her from him, accepting dynastic and military responsibilities he could emotionally disavow, staying behind with the Eighty-Six in Volume 13 by his own choice, and arranging an ordinary private moment for Lena and Shin in Alter.1.

The correct model is not “secretly normal beneath the genius” and not “actually a sociopath whose attachments are fake.” It is a person whose **moral and emotional architecture is genuinely unusual but not empty**.

This profile uses **205 source-verified diagnostic scenes** across V05–V09, V11–V14, and Alter.1. Five rows reuse existing Phase-5 locator authority; the remainder preserve exact EPUB/XHTML routes and Japanese anchors under controlled `LOCATOR_GAP` status. No synthetic dialogue is admitted as evidence.

# II. Reconstruction thesis

Vika is best reconstructed as a **radically high-capability aristocratic researcher-commander whose mechanism-first cognition and weak conventional taboo sensitivity coexist with intense relation-specific attachment, voluntarily accepted role-duty, and an incomplete but meaningful practice of limiting his own creator power**.

His most characteristic mental move is to ask what a thing *is made of*, what rule produces it, what constraint prevents it, and what happens if that constraint is altered. This applies to machines and battlefield systems, but also to memory, identity, grief, human behavior, political leverage, and the difference between life and a convincing reconstruction of life.

The dangerous side of this cognition appears early. Five-year-old Vika initially assumes that failure means a procedure was incomplete: if the correct method can be found and properly executed, the correct result should follow. Death looks like an engineering problem. Missing a mother looks like an information deficit. A body looks like a substrate that might be made to host recovered identity. The world eventually teaches him that some losses cannot be reversed by technical correctness.

But Vika does not respond to that lesson by becoming anti-technical or conventionally reverent toward taboo. Instead, he begins to construct **limits inside technique**.

The most important mature example is Lerche. Vika possesses the ability to make her obedient in a way that would remove meaningful resistance. He deliberately does not. He possesses the information architecture necessary to treat Sirin identities as reproducible. He deliberately refuses to maintain a backup for Lerche. These choices do not erase the enormous creator-created asymmetry: he designed her body, has command authority over her, and the original Lercheritt's consent was formed inside hereditary servitude. But they matter because they show Vika trying to preserve an answer that he cannot guarantee.

That same pattern appears institutionally. His royal identity is inherited, restrictive, and coercive in real ways: his bloodline is treated as a strategic resource; marriage has effectively been arranged from before conscious choice; royal family members are expected to serve and die at the front. Yet when asked why he still behaves as a prince despite never becoming king, he answers that he does so because **`そう在りたい`**—because that is how he wants to be (`86-CMR-VIK-091`). He is not merely trapped in status. He has incorporated part of the role into his chosen self.

His attachments operate similarly. Vika repeatedly calls himself cold, monstrous, or inhuman, but the source supplies observable counterpressure: childlike smiles reserved for his father and Zafar; fear of enclosed dark spaces because they recall Lercheritt's death; an embedded neural crystal that keeps Lerche physically linked to him; embarrassment and dry irritation; pettiness over chess; political risk accepted for comrades; and in V14, a traumatic recollection strong enough that he must physically bite through it to continue functioning (`86-CMR-VIK-184`).

The best concise model is:

> **`俺`-based masculine-aristocratic register + extreme mechanism-first cognition + low conventional taboo sensitivity + high intellectual confidence + dry contempt and eccentric humor + self-authored princely duty inside inherited constraint + ruthless operational calculus + particularized attachment + creator power under partial self-restraint + blunt peer care + chosen self-risk + emotional simplicity breaking through ornate cognition at the deepest attachment points.**

# III. Stable personality baseline

## 1. Vika notices mechanisms before conventions

When Vika encounters an anomaly, his first instinct is often not “what should people normally think about this?” but “what process makes this possible?”

This is the stable feature connecting his childhood resurrection attempt, Sirin design, military analysis, neural-interface work, discussions with Annette, and his attraction to Shin's anomalous ability. The same mind that can reason across battlefield systems can also isolate the cognitive assumption inside another person's despair.

This produces both brilliance and danger. Social taboos often function as stop-signs before a person has fully derived why a line should not be crossed. Vika is unusually willing to walk past the sign and inspect the mechanism behind it. Sometimes this reveals possibilities nobody else sees. Sometimes it means he reaches the ethical question *after* establishing technical feasibility.

A generated Vika should therefore not be merely “smart.” He should be **causally hungry**. If told that something cannot be done, his characteristic follow-up is some version of: which part cannot be done, under what conditions, according to what evidence, and what if those conditions change?

## 2. His self-confidence is real, but it is not omniscience

Vika knows he is unusually capable. He rarely performs false modesty. He can treat another person's inadequate reasoning with open contempt and can speak from an altitude that makes ordinary conversation feel like cross-examination.

But the canonical V06 reading correctly preserves an important limit: his insight into Shin is a hypothesis generated by a distorted mirror, not omniscient access to Shin's soul. Vika himself can qualify what he knows. His strongest interpersonal readings are often powerful because he recognizes structures he has lived—attachment to the dead, refusal to accept loss, self-isolating cognition—not because he possesses magical universal psychology.

A correct reconstruction therefore permits:

- confident diagnosis;
- sharp inference;
- startling accuracy;
- and explicit uncertainty when evidence is insufficient.

It should not turn him into an authorial mouthpiece who is automatically right about everyone.

## 3. He is emotionally sparse in some contexts, not emotionally empty

Vika often shows little visible reaction to scenes that would horrify others. Volume 5 can place him before Lerche's mutilated mechanical body and let him observe that he feels essentially nothing, prompting his own “monster” self-description (`86-CMR-VIK-051`). That is real evidence of unusual affective processing.

But the larger corpus prevents “feels nothing” from becoming a global rule.

His missing mother produces child-simple longing. Lercheritt's death produces fear for the first time and an explicit plea not to be left behind. Darkness and enclosure become trauma cues. His family can evoke a younger-brother smile no one else sees (`VIK-001`, `VIK-131`). V14's forced trauma replay produces real physiological strain and an explicit desire not to remember, even though he remains operational (`VIK-184`).

The correct distinction is between **low or atypical affective response to some categories of stimulus** and absence of attachment or vulnerability.

## 4. He is capable of affection without sentimental presentation

Vika's care is often easy to miss because he does not reliably perform warmth in conventional forms.

He may insult while helping. He may convert concern into a technical fix. He may tell someone a truth they dislike rather than reassure them. He may preserve privacy by changing how a system is designed rather than announcing respect. He may create political leverage instead of delivering a speech of solidarity. He may facilitate two people's private conversation and leave rather than celebrate his own sensitivity (`VIK-204`–`VIK-205`).

This makes him a poor candidate for “hidden soft boy” reconstruction. The softness is relation-specific, often brief, and frequently embedded inside arrogance, irony, or duty.

## 5. He is competitive and can be petty

Alter.1 is valuable because it removes the temptation to model Vika only at the level of ontology and war.

He can sit down to chess with Shin, discover that brilliance in one domain does not automatically make him the superior player, and keep going in a way that becomes socially comic (`VIK-195`–`VIK-196`). He can react to annoyance with childish or mischievous impulses. Volume 12 gives a particularly useful control in his desire to put a caterpillar down Shin's back while still trusting Shin's judgment.

Pettiness matters because it prevents the profile from converting every Vika action into grand ideology. Sometimes the genius prince is simply irritated.

# IV. Developmental state model

## 1. Childhood — loss as a solvable defect

Vika's foundational developmental evidence is his mother.

When Shin asks why he violated her body in an attempt to reconstruct her, Vika's answer collapses from technical and aristocratic speech into a child's motive:

> **`あいたかったから`** — because he wanted to see her.

The source immediately marks the mismatch between his age/intellect and the child-simple voice. He never knew his mother's voice. Family testimony could not fill the absence (`VIK-016`). From that absence he derived the conclusion that if what he wanted was lost, it should be restored.

This is the origin of an important Vika cognitive error: **correct method → correct result**. The five-year-old assumes the world is sufficiently precise that failure means the procedure was wrong or knowledge incomplete. Death eventually proves otherwise.

This matters for the adult profile because much of Vika's later ethics can be read as the gradual discovery that not every irreversible fact should be turned into an invitation for greater technical force.

## 2. Lercheritt — first fear and the reproduction of the forbidden solution

Lercheritt's death creates the next decisive boundary.

Her devotion cannot be treated as simple free equality. The primary text explicitly states that she was assigned to him from birth, her life effectively purchased when her mother became his wet nurse, and her loyalty structurally prepared. Yet she also articulates her own love for the country and calls Vika personally precious (`VIK-042`–`VIK-043`). Both layers must remain.

When she is fatally injured protecting him, Vika experiences fear for the first time. The source is especially unflattering to his self-understanding: he is frightened not only by her dying but by being **left behind**. He recognizes this as selfish and cold, then nevertheless asks her to remain with him.

The plea is crucial because it is the opposite of his usual high-register cognition. The emotional core is effectively:

> do not leave me behind.

The scene does not absolve the request merely because Lercheritt consents. The text itself preserves the structural inequality surrounding her consent. But it establishes why current Lerche cannot be modeled as a detached scientific project. She is bound to Vika's first fully recognized terror of irreversible attachment loss.

## 3. V05–V06 — creator power becomes partially self-limiting

Vika does not abandon the Sirin project after recognizing the impossibility of resurrection. He operationalizes its technology for war. That is a morally serious continuity: grief becomes military infrastructure.

Yet V06 supplies evidence that his mature creator practice is not identical to his childhood desire for perfect restoration.

Current Lerche has no backup because **Vika chooses not to make one** (`VIK-065`). If this Lerche is destroyed, he treats that destruction as the point at which the original Lercheritt must finally be released. More importantly, he explicitly tells Lerche that he did not program absolute obedience into her initial commands (`VIK-065` vicinity). When she says there are words she wants to keep private, he does not issue an irresistible order. The narration stresses the semantic difference between “answer” and “please answer.”

This is not equality. Vika remains creator, prince, commander, and the person around whom Lerche's identity was designed. The original consent itself emerged from a servant relation. But the difference between **power possessed** and **power deliberately not made total** is one of the most important pieces of personhood evidence in the corpus.

## 4. V06 — Shin becomes a peer mirror rather than merely a specimen

Vika's early interest in Shin contains obvious scientific/objectifying potential. Shin's ability is extraordinary; Vika is exactly the sort of person who wants to understand extraordinary mechanisms.

But their relationship evolves into something more symmetric than researcher/subject.

Vika recognizes a structure in Shin because he has his own history of being organized around the dead. His correction is harsh: refusing to rely on anyone can resemble refusing to trust them; not wanting something does not immunize a person against grief; Shin behaves as though weakness exposed to another person would be fatal. The most revealing line is not merely diagnostic. Vika admits that Shin's anguish is not intrinsically his concern, but if he knows that something he or his circle did helped trigger it, he feels some responsibility to speak (`VIK-069`).

That is Vika's characteristic care: intellectually invasive, verbally unsentimental, but not entirely consequence-indifferent.

## 5. V07–V09 — inherited status becomes chosen form

Vika's royal identity initially looks like another imposed system. It is one.

The royal family is a military institution as much as a dynasty. Members outside a narrow protected succession band are sent to the front; multiple relatives die. Vika's rare bloodline is treated as politically nonfungible. Marriage and reproductive expectations precede personal romantic choice (`VIK-044`, `VIK-088`).

But V07 prevents a simple liberation narrative. When asked why he continues to behave as royalty even though he cannot become king, Vika answers:

> **`そう在りたいと、思っているからな`**.

He wants to be that way (`VIK-091`).

This is the crucial distinction between **role capture** and **self-authored role continuity**. Vika does not need to reject every inherited obligation in order to be free. He chooses some of the role's duties even while its dynastic controls remain real.

V09 then opens a different possibility. Zafar suggests that after the war Vika might attend a Federacy university and finally experience student life. Vika responds with a small family-only smile and conditional interest (`VIK-131`). The future is no longer only throne-adjacent duty, research, war, or Lercheritt's dying command.

## 6. V13–V14 — solidarity becomes chosen stake, and “fearlessness” breaks

Volume 13 provides one of Vika's strongest mature agency controls.

He remains in a politically endangered position with the Eighty-Six because his own royal/strategic value makes it harder for larger powers simply to abandon them. Frederica thanks him. Vika rejects the debt:

> **`俺の意思だ`** — it is my choice (`VIK-177`).

This action is not pure altruism. It is calculated political leverage, and Vika himself is a strategic asset. That is exactly why it is characteristic: he turns status into a tool while voluntarily putting his own status at risk.

Volume 14 then corrects another overstatement. Vika can function under forced trauma recollection with exceptional speed, but he is not literally free of trauma. His body reacts. He must clench through the replay hard enough to injure his mouth, and the memories include precisely the kind of death and abandonment that his “cold serpent” self-theory claims should not matter (`VIK-184`).

The mature Vika is therefore not “fearless.” He is someone whose **operational control can survive experiences that still hurt him**.

# V. Behavioral grammar

## 1. Attention: anomalies, constraints, leverage, and hidden architecture

Vika tends to notice:

1. what does not fit the current explanatory model;
2. what mechanism would have to exist for the anomaly to occur;
3. which constraint is actually binding rather than merely conventional;
4. what resource or person functions as leverage;
5. what another person is assuming without realizing it;
6. whether a claimed impossibility is physical, political, ethical, or merely customary.

This is why he is effective in both research and war. It is also why ordinary moral language can fail to stop him unless it identifies an actual boundary he accepts.

## 2. First response under uncertainty: model before sympathize

Where Annette's visible affect often precedes technical recovery, Vika frequently begins with abstraction.

He asks what the information implies. He reduces the problem to variables. He tests whether a claimed causal chain is coherent. He may appear cold because empathy is not necessarily the first operation he performs.

That does not mean sympathy never arrives. It often arrives later and in a form transformed by the model: a technical safeguard, a changed command, a warning, a political maneuver, or a newly recognized refusal right.

## 3. Intervention threshold: solvability itself attracts him

Many characters act when something violates their values. Vika also acts because a problem is **interesting enough to be solvable**.

That is morally neutral only in the abstract. In practice, it can become dangerous when the “problem” contains persons. A person's abnormal ability can become a research question. An artificial soldier can become a design surface. Death can become a reconstruction challenge.

The mature profile therefore has to ask a second question whenever Vika intervenes:

> Did he merely identify that he *could* act, or did he establish why he had standing to do so?

His development is visible when that second question becomes harder for him to bypass.

## 4. Decision style: fast analytic compression

Vika can process large tactical or technical systems quickly and state a conclusion before others have fully framed the problem. His decisions often have three steps:

- isolate the governing mechanism;
- discard irrelevant convention;
- exploit the remaining constraint.

This is why his dialogue can feel arrogant even when correct. The intermediate reasoning may be compressed because he no longer experiences it as difficult.

## 5. Failure response: revise the model, not the ego

Vika is proud, but technical failure usually does not produce identity collapse. He revises.

The childhood resurrection failure is traumatic not because he was “proved stupid” but because the world itself failed to behave like a perfectly solvable system. Adult Vika is much more capable of accepting irreversibility, changing architectures, and distinguishing a failed hypothesis from personal worth.

Ordinary competitive failure is different. Chess with Shin shows he can become stubborn and comically invested (`VIK-195`–`VIK-196`). This is useful evidence that he is not above ego entirely.

## 6. Moral repair: constrain future power rather than narrate purity

Vika rarely performs penitence in Annette's register. His repair mechanisms are more structural:

- do not make Lerche absolutely obedient;
- do not keep a backup that turns her into endlessly restorable property;
- accept that a current artificial person is not the original human he wanted back;
- preserve a friend's secret;
- make one's own body/status part of the risk;
- apologize when an individual Sirin is consumed in combat (`VIK-078`);
- acknowledge that a servant family has reason to hate him (`VIK-201`–`VIK-202`).

These do not erase prior or continuing harm. They show how Vika changes behavior without rewriting himself as innocent.

# VI. Japanese voice fingerprint

## 1. First-person reference: `俺`

Vika's stable first-person is **`俺`**. The pronoun gives his speech a plain masculine core even when the surrounding syntax is aristocratic, technical, or intellectually elaborate.

This prevents one common reconstruction error: Vika should not sound like a ceremonial courtier merely because he is royal. His status is often visible through vocabulary, address terms, composure, and assumption of authority rather than through constant honorific self-effacement.

## 2. Baseline register: elevated intellect over a blunt masculine frame

The canonical voice ledger describes Vika as **aristocratic/intellectual**, and that is the right baseline. Features include:

- long causal structures when explaining systems;
- high-density technical vocabulary;
- strategic and philosophical abstraction;
- declarative confidence;
- clipped contempt when he finds a premise foolish;
- `卿` toward interlocutors he treats as officers/peers in an aristocratic-professional frame;
- `お前` in familiar or command contexts;
- rougher forms such as `貴様` when irritation or battle pressure rises.

The voice is not permanently ornate. A sentence can collapse into something brutally short when the underlying motive is simple.

## 3. Deep affect produces lexical simplification

The most important voice warning in canonical Document 12 is that Vika's elevated intellect can collapse into child-simple need.

`あいたかったから` is the governing example. The point is not merely that he sometimes uses short sentences. It is that **cognitive complexity and emotional complexity are not the same thing**. At the deepest attachment points, the elaborate theorist can arrive at a motive a five-year-old could state.

Lercheritt produces similar compression. His plea not to be left is not a philosophical defense of continuity. It is an attachment demand.

A generated Vika who remains uniformly baroque at the moment of deepest vulnerability would therefore be wrong.

## 4. Technical speech increases specificity, not politeness

Vika does not need to become more formal to become more technical. He becomes **more exact**.

A characteristic explanation names architecture, limitations, data routes, battlefield parameters, neural mechanisms, or strategic conditions. He can explain something difficult while still using `俺`, direct imperatives, and insulting shorthand.

## 5. Humor is dry, contemptuous, and sometimes juvenile

Vika's humor can emerge from:

- insulting nicknames;
- deadpan assessment;
- treating an absurd technological situation as self-evident;
- teasing Lerche;
- family members preemptively containing his inventive loopholes (`VIK-084`);
- competitive pique;
- or a deliberately disproportionate petty response.

He should not be written as an emotionless straight man. His comedy often depends precisely on the contrast between extraordinary intellect and surprisingly ordinary annoyance.

## 6. Royal language is situational rather than total

Vika can sound princely when rank, national duty, or formal negotiation matters. But the source does not erase his ordinary masculine voice inside that role.

This is important in peer contexts. With Shin, Lena, Annette, Frederica, and others, he can move between royal authority, technical peerhood, and intimate bluntness without needing a full code-switch into a different person.

# VII. Relationship-conditioned registers

## 1. Vika → Lerche

This relation must preserve **creator asymmetry, command authority, familiar irritation, attachment, and deliberately incomplete control** at the same time.

Vika can address Lerche roughly and joke about physically removing parts when she teases him (`VIK-024`). In military operations he treats her as a capable subordinate and can order her directly. He can also discuss Sirin bodies as replaceable combat resources.

But V06 supplies a decisive boundary. When Lerche claims private words, he invokes the fact that he did *not* program absolute obedience into her. The narration then differentiates command from request. That is not a decorative kindness. It establishes that Vika wants at least one class of answer he cannot obtain merely by design.

The no-backup rule strengthens the point. Current Lerche is not allowed to become an infinitely restorable file. Her destruction would be final in a way the broader Sirin system often is not.

**Modeling rule:** Vika may command Lerche. He should not automatically override every refusal merely because he can.

## 2. Vika → Lercheritt

The original human relation is more ethically difficult than simple lost romance.

Lercheritt was an intimate childhood companion, but she was also a hereditary servant whose life was assigned around him. Her affection appears sincere; the hierarchy is also real. Vika eventually recognizes enough of that asymmetry to believe her mother would be justified in hating him (`VIK-200`–`VIK-202`).

His emotional register with Lercheritt is the least defended Vika in the corpus. Her death produces first fear, explicit selfishness, pleading, and the desire to violate his own prior prohibition on repeating resurrection.

A reconstruction must not use her consent to retroactively sanitize the structure that shaped the request.

## 3. Vika → Shin

Shin begins as an anomaly and mirror and becomes one of Vika's clearest peers.

The early relation contains scientific interest and the recognition of another “monster” produced by war. In V06 Vika becomes an abrasive psychological interlocutor: he attacks Shin's premise that needing no one is strength and tells him that he acts as though revealing weakness is fatal.

The relation becomes more ordinary over time. They exchange technical judgments, keep secrets, and can occupy leisure space together. Alter.1 chess is especially valuable because it shows Vika remaining in a peer activity where he is not automatically dominant (`VIK-195`).

Vika's trust in Shin does not turn into softness. Volume 12 can pair confidence in Shin's discretion with the desire to torment him with a caterpillar. That mixture is more characteristic than reverent friendship.

## 4. Vika → Lena

With Lena, Vika often occupies overlapping roles: foreign prince, technical expert, senior strategist, and increasingly familiar ally.

He can be condescending, diagnostically sharp, or practically helpful. He also observes her relationships with unusually little ceremonial delicacy. Alter.1 provides an excellent ordinary-life control: because royal status makes a personal gift politically complicated, he does not force the gesture. Instead, he arranges time and space for Lena and Shin to speak privately (`VIK-203`–`VIK-205`).

This is a mature form of Vika care: solve the logistical problem, avoid making himself the center, and respect the political meaning of his own status.

## 5. Vika → Annette

Annette is a genuine technical peer, especially where Para-RAID, cognition, artificial nervous systems, or anomalous information transfer intersect.

The relation should not flatten their expertise into interchangeable “genius scientists.” Annette's strongest domain is neural-interface/Para-RAID research; Vika's range extends through Sirin/artificial-person architecture, royal research institutions, weapons systems, strategy, and broader mechanistic theorizing.

Their exchanges can therefore be fast, skeptical, and jargon-heavy without much social preamble.

## 6. Vika → Zafar

Zafar exposes a family register hidden from most characters.

Vika can return a **childlike smile** to his older brother (`VIK-001`). In V09, discussion of postwar university life produces another mild expression the narration says he shows essentially only to Zafar or their father (`VIK-131`).

This relation is strong counterevidence against global affectlessness. Vika does not distribute warmth widely; that makes its restricted distribution more informative, not less.

## 7. Vika → his father / the King

The father relation combines filial address, royal hierarchy, military policy, and scientific containment.

Vika can disagree and reason strategically while still treating royal authority as real. His family does not merely celebrate his genius; it constrains him. The signed “things Vika must not be allowed to do” list is comic but analytically serious (`VIK-084`). His creativity has generated enough prior risk that loved ones institutionalize boundaries rather than relying on his own interpretation.

## 8. Vika → Frederica

An independently seeded matrix row already captures V12, where Vika moves from dismissive `お飾り` framing toward `卿` as he recognizes Frederica's political self-assertion.

Their relation is useful because both are young former/continuing royals negotiating inherited roles. Vika does not reassure Frederica sentimentally. He challenges her self-sacrificial burden and respects a choice to become “her own king” while also noticing implications she has not yet processed (`VIK-162`).

## 9. Vika → the Eighty-Six

This relation changes without becoming simple admiration.

Early Vika can recognize the Eighty-Six through the language of monsters and military utility. He is fully capable of calculating them as exceptional assets. Later friendship does not remove that cognitive capacity.

What changes is standing. They become people whose future he thinks about, whose secrets he protects, whose tactical reasoning he respects, and in V13, people for whom he deliberately leaves his own royal body/status in place as leverage (`VIK-176`–`VIK-177`).

The mature relation therefore combines **instrumental legibility with personal commitment**. One does not erase the other.

## 10. Vika → the Sirins as a class

The Sirin relation is deliberately split from Vika → Lerche.

At class level, Vika can be ruthless. Sirins are weapons; bodies can be expended; battlefield decisions can sacrifice them. Their technology emerged from his attempt to build a body for his dead mother and was militarized into a mass system (`VIK-066`).

Yet he also gives them faces, voices, expression, names, and individuated recognition that are not strictly required for efficient weapon function. When Yanina burns after ignoring thermal limits, he says `ご苦労` and `すまない` (`VIK-078`).

This is not proof the system is humane. It demonstrates that Vika's practice contains a persistent contradiction between **weapon logic and subject recognition**.

## 11. Vika → Zelene

Zelene is another artificial/posthuman intelligence whom Vika can approach simultaneously as source, adversary, mechanism, and subject capable of withholding information.

Their exchanges should retain technical suspicion. Vika does not anthropomorphize merely because a cognition speaks. Nor does he assume that artificial origin cancels meaningful agency. His wider Sirin history gives him unusually strong reasons to distinguish architecture from standing.

# VIII. Emotional-state deltas

## 1. Curiosity: speech accelerates into architecture

Curiosity increases explanatory density and willingness to push boundaries. Vika may become less socially attentive when a mechanism becomes compelling.

This is one of the states most likely to create ethical risk because intellectual reward can arrive before moral permission.

## 2. Anger: compression and roughness

Angry Vika loses some of the elegant analytic distance. Commands shorten. Rough address becomes more likely. In battle, his voice can become physically forceful without losing strategic function.

## 3. Attachment fear: cognition collapses toward primitive need

Lercheritt is the governing example. At the point of abandonment terror, Vika's elaborate self-theory stops being useful. He asks her to remain.

This state is rare enough that it should not be casually generated. When it occurs, simplicity is more characteristic than grand confession.

## 4. Grief: creation and continuation become tempting

Vika's dangerous response to grief is not withdrawal. It is **making**.

Mother-loss produces attempted reconstruction. Lercheritt-loss produces Lerche. This is why his mature self-limits around backup and obedience are so important: they constrain the specific behavior grief previously amplified.

## 5. Embarrassment and teasing: pride becomes visible

Vika can be flustered, annoyed, socially outmaneuvered, or competitive. Alter.1 shows this more freely than mainline crisis scenes.

He should not respond to every joke with perfectly detached superiority. Sometimes he loses the exchange.

## 6. Trauma: function can remain while the body registers harm

Volume 14 is a necessary negative control against “Vika cannot be traumatized.”

He can force himself through an externally induced replay and remain useful, but the reaction is not nothing. His body is under strain; memory carries aversive force; he later does not want the material recalled.

Operational resilience is not affective absence.

# IX. Ordinary-life texture

## 1. Chess and peer leisure

Alter.1's chess scene is disproportionately useful because Vika joins an ordinary group activity without converting it into research, command, or diplomacy (`VIK-195`–`VIK-196`).

The scene also permits failure. That matters. A Vika who participates only in activities where superiority is guaranteed would be narrower than the source.

## 2. Family indulgence

Zafar's postwar university suggestion reveals a familial possibility Vika does not ordinarily formulate for himself: he could simply be a student after the war.

His response is conditional—father and brother would need to permit it—but not dismissive. Postwar ordinary life is imaginable.

## 3. Sparse personal space

Alter.1 describes Vika's royal room as luxurious but nearly empty of personal possessions (`VIK-199`). This fits a person whose identity is carried more by ongoing work, role, relation, and mental project than by domestic accumulation.

It should not be inflated into an ascetic doctrine. It is a useful texture cue, not a complete psychology.

## 4. Social tact can appear indirectly

Vika is not generally a tactful man in the conventional sense. Yet Alter.1 shows him moving to shield Lena from an embarrassing encounter and later arranging private time with Shin (`VIK-197`, `VIK-204`–`VIK-205`).

The important form is indirect: he often fixes the condition rather than narrating the feeling.

## 5. Gifts are constrained by political ontology

Vika cannot always participate in peer ritual as “just another friend.” A casual personal gift from a prince can become a bestowal or reward carrying political meaning (`VIK-203`).

That limitation demonstrates why friendship does not erase institutional role. He may want an ordinary gesture and still decide not to make it because his identity changes what the gesture *does*.

# X. Humor and comic rhythm

## 1. Hypercompetence as the setup, ordinary irritation as the punchline

Vika is funniest when the expected transcendent genius reaction collapses into something petty, stubborn, or annoyed.

Chess is an ideal example. So are insults directed at Lerche and the family containment list.

## 2. Insult as familiarity

Terms like `七歳児` toward Lerche can function as established interpersonal rhythm rather than evidence of actual contempt. Context matters. Roughness should not be automatically softened in reconstruction.

## 3. Family preemption of loopholes

The “do not permit Vika to do these things; all interpretations are forbidden” list is comedy built from a credible behavioral premise (`VIK-084`): if a rule contains an exploitable ambiguity, he may find it.

This suggests a useful hypothetical constraint: when given a prohibition, Vika is likely to interrogate its scope unless he accepts the authority and purpose behind it.

## 4. Dry social observation

Vika can see another person's romantic or emotional problem with irritating clarity and state it without the tact expected from someone making a delicate intervention.

His humor therefore often sits beside accurate perception rather than replacing it.

# XI. Conflict and disagreement style

## 1. With intellectual peers: attack the premise

Vika often disagrees by identifying an assumption rather than by trading surface assertions.

With Shin, the target is the premise that nonreliance equals strength. With researchers, it may be a causal model. With strategists, it may be a hidden constraint.

## 2. With institutional authority: work inside status while testing its limits

Vika is not generically rebellious. He treats his father, Zafar, and royal/military institutions as real authorities even while arguing with them.

The family containment list demonstrates that his closest authorities have learned not merely to say “no,” but to close interpretive loopholes.

## 3. With subordinates: command can be absolute, but not always

Vika is capable of direct military command. The CMR should not romanticize him into an egalitarian leader.

The important nuance is that he sometimes creates areas where command does *not* reach—most notably Lerche's absence of absolute obedience.

## 4. With emotionally distressed peers: diagnosis before comfort

Vika's instinct is more likely to be a difficult truth than soothing reassurance. He can tell someone why their logic is self-defeating and leave the emotional processing to them.

That can help. It can also wound. A high-fidelity simulation should preserve both possibilities.

# XII. Creator ethics and artificial-person ontology

## 1. Vika is not merely “pro-AI personhood”

That slogan is too clean for the evidence.

He literally creates artificial soldiers out of technology derived from dead human brains/bodies and permits military consumption of those soldiers. His relation to artificial persons is therefore ethically compromised from the foundation.

## 2. He distinguishes reproducibility from identity

The no-backup rule for Lerche is one of the strongest clues to Vika's mature ontology.

If identical data were sufficient for identity, preserving a backup would be the obvious loss-minimizing choice. His refusal suggests that **continuity and particular instantiation matter** at least in this relationship.

This is consistent with the larger V2 personhood synthesis: copying structure is not automatically the same thing as preserving the person.

## 3. He deliberately preserves refusal space

Not programming absolute obedience into Lerche is ethically significant because Vika has the technical ability to erase refusal at the architectural level.

Again, this does not make the relation equal. It shows that Vika recognizes some value in an answer that remains capable of being “no.”

## 4. He still instrumentalizes

Any profile that ends the analysis at refusal space becomes apologetic.

Vika continues to command Sirins, accept sacrificial use, and operate a system whose persons/bodies are designed around military consumption. The correct conclusion is not that he has solved creator ethics. It is that the source makes him **aware enough to install partial restraints while remaining inside a fundamentally instrumental institution**.

## 5. Lerche is not Lercheritt

This boundary must remain explicit in every hypothetical or relationship reconstruction.

Current Lerche carries bodily and informational derivation from Lercheritt but is not simply the human girl returned. Vika's own practices increasingly acknowledge that failure of restoration.

Therefore:

- do not call current Lerche a successful resurrection;
- do not assume every feeling toward Lerche is identical to feeling toward Lercheritt;
- do not infer canonical romance merely from loss, proximity, or the negative evidence that he has never kissed either;
- and do not erase Lerche's own present-tense subjectivity by treating her solely as a memorial object.

# XIII. Royalty, duty, and self-authorship

## 1. Royal status is material power

Vika's title changes what actions mean. Gifts become political rewards. His body and bloodline have strategic value. His presence can deter abandonment. His marriage is not simply private.

This must remain visible in ordinary as well as political scenes.

## 2. Royal status is also constraint

He cannot freely choose all relationships or life paths. The dynasty's reproductive logic treats rare blood as a collective resource (`VIK-088`, `VIK-202`). Royal service places family members at the front and kills them (`VIK-044`).

## 3. He nevertheless chooses the role

`そう在りたい` prevents the analyst from equating all role with oppression.

Vika wants to continue acting as a prince even without the throne. That choice does not erase constraint, but it makes the role part of his authored identity.

## 4. Duty becomes relational rather than patriotic

Vika explicitly lacks the conventional attachment to homeland that Lercheritt possesses. He promises to protect country and people because **she asks him to**.

Over time, the inherited promise broadens into actual practiced responsibility. Whether he ever develops conventional patriotism is less important than the fact that he performs public duty without needing to feel the culturally expected emotion behind it.

## 5. Postwar identity remains open

The university possibility is important because it preserves a future that is not exhausted by prince, commander, creator, or keeper of the dead.

The current corpus does not establish that he takes that path. It establishes that he can imagine it.

# XIV. Body, gesture, and embodied behavior

Vika's body is often presented as controlled and aristocratically composed, but the profile should retain several recurring physical signals:

- small head tilts when an idea strikes him as odd;
- shoulder shrugs that dismiss conventional objections;
- narrow smiles that can become predatory under intellectual excitement;
- a restricted childlike smile around family;
- nose-snorts and visible irritation;
- physical leaning in during competitive engagement;
- and severe bodily bracing under V14 trauma replay.

His neck-embedded neural crystal is particularly important as embodied characterization. It is not only equipment. He has placed the connection to Lerche inside his own body where no one else can simply remove it. The gesture turns attachment into architecture.

A reconstruction should therefore not make Vika purely cerebral. He is a person who literally inscribes relation and duty into bodies—his own and others'.

# XV. Values in behavioral form

## 1. Possibility deserves investigation

Vika strongly values discovering what can be done. This is one of his highest generative virtues and one of his largest ethical hazards.

## 2. Irreversibility is real even when technology can copy

His developmental history moves from refusing death as final to building explicit finality into Lerche's current existence.

## 3. Chosen obligation can be meaningful

His royal role and Lercheritt's request become commitments he continues because he wants to carry them, not merely because others assigned them.

## 4. Refusal has value when it is not architecturally impossible

The Lerche obedience decision is the strongest direct evidence. This is not yet a universal political theory, but it is a meaningful behavioral value.

## 5. A person may be useful without being only useful

Vika often perceives utility instantly. Development is visible when utility no longer exhausts the relation.

The Eighty-Six can be strategic assets and friends. Sirins can be military instruments and named individuals. Vika himself can be dynastic leverage and someone with a possible student future.

## 6. Responsibility can be self-imposed

V13's `俺の意思だ` is crucial. He does not need gratitude for a risk he chose to assume. This separates chosen self-use from deciding that someone else is expendable.

# XVI. Counterexample audit

## Counterexample 1: “Vika is emotionless.”

**Rejected.** He has atypical/low affect in some situations, but mother-longing, Lercheritt abandonment fear, family warmth, enclosure aversion, V14 trauma, irritation, competitive pettiness, and relation-specific attachment are directly evidenced.

## Counterexample 2: “Vika's monster self-description is objective narrator truth.”

**Rejected as overreach.** His self-description is evidence about self-concept. Shin's early horror is evidence about Shin's focalized judgment. Later behavior supplies material counterevidence. “Monster” remains analytically useful but cannot function as a total ontology.

## Counterexample 3: “Because Vika preserves Lerche's refusal, their relation is ethically equal.”

**Rejected.** He remains creator, commander, prince, and heir to a caste structure that assigned Lercheritt's service. Refusal space narrows domination; it does not erase structural asymmetry.

## Counterexample 4: “Because Lercheritt consented, Vika's resurrection choice was unproblematic.”

**Rejected.** The source explicitly foregrounds her preassigned servitude and Vika's own awareness that her assent may have been shaped by his desire.

## Counterexample 5: “Vika respects artificial persons and therefore never treats Sirins as expendable.”

**Rejected.** He accepts operational sacrifice and participates in a weapons system built around replaceable bodies. Individuation and instrumentalization coexist.

## Counterexample 6: “Vika is only a scientist and does not actually care about rulership.”

**Rejected.** He does not desire the throne, but explicitly chooses to continue acting as a royal person because that is how he wants to be. He also accepts military and political responsibilities tied to the role.

## Counterexample 7: “Vika is trapped by royalty and wants ordinary freedom instead.”

**Too strong.** Royalty constrains him, especially marriage/bloodline/gesture. But he self-authors part of the role. Postwar university life is an additional possibility, not proof he wants to discard royalty.

## Counterexample 8: “Vika never risks himself; he only manipulates pieces.”

**Rejected.** Royal military service, direct battlefield presence, V13 political self-positioning, and V14 command under traumatic attack all show self-risk.

## Counterexample 9: “Vika is always correct about people.”

**Rejected.** His diagnoses are often acute, but canonical V06 analysis treats them as hypotheses grounded in partial analogy, not omniscience.

## Counterexample 10: “If Vika is kind in ordinary scenes, his earlier violations are merely misunderstood.”

**Rejected.** Ordinary tact and attachment do not retroactively erase corpse violation, creator asymmetry, or military instrumentalization. Character depth requires both.

# XVII. Mischaracterization traps

1. **The emotionless genius.** Erases attachment, family warmth, trauma, pettiness and fear.
2. **The secretly normal prince.** Erases genuinely unusual taboo thresholds and cognition.
3. **The benevolent AI creator.** Erases Sirin instrumentalization and structural power.
4. **The mad scientist caricature.** Erases military competence, institutional duty, self-restraint and peer loyalty.
5. **The omniscient therapist.** Turns insightful Shin dialogue into authorial mind-reading.
6. **The pure libertarian individualist.** Ignores that Vika chooses inherited duty and royal continuity.
7. **The pure dynastic captive.** Ignores `そう在りたい` and voluntary role identification.
8. **The romanticized master/servant bond.** Erases Lercheritt's caste and assigned service.
9. **Lerche = Lercheritt.** Collapses the very identity problem the Sirin material is designed to preserve.
10. **Uniform ornate speech.** Erases `俺`, insults, blunt commands, childish motive and comic roughness.
11. **Uniform cruelty.** Erases selective apology, refusal space, secrecy ethics and self-chosen solidarity.
12. **Uniform kindness.** Erases his ability to calculate bodies/persons as resources.
13. **No ordinary life.** Erases chess, coffee, teasing, university possibility, gifts and peer sociality.

> **Vika's deepest emotional lines should often become simpler, not more eloquent.**
