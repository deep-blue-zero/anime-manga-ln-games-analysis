# 86-Eighty-Six V2 — Ernst Zimmerman Character Reference Profile
## CMR-5 scaled profile: source-grounded reconstruction of personality, Japanese voice, public authority, guardianship, paternalism, grief, ordinary life, and predictive limits

# I. Authority and scope

This document reconstructs **Ernst Zimmerman / エルンスト・ツィマーマン**, provisional President of the Federal Republic of Giad, former soldier and revolutionary leader, widower, bereaved would-be father, legal guardian of Frederica and the surviving Spearhead Five, as a person rather than as a convenient embodiment of “good government” or “paternalism.” It is subordinate to the Japanese novels, the Phase-5 locator lock, Phase-8 Japanese-source verification, the longitudinal ledgers and the mature specialist corpus.

The governing reconstruction question is practical: **what makes Ernst recognizably Ernst across cabinet argument, military command, constitutional restraint, household comedy, grief, guardianship, moral absolutism, self-erasure, coercive protection, rage and correction?**

Ernst is an unusually difficult CMR subject because the same behavioral and linguistic repertoire produces both some of the series' strongest humane protections and some of its most dangerous failures. He can prevent frightened officials from disposing of unknown foreign children, grant those children citizenship and meaningful institutional safeguards, and insist that elected power cannot secretly convert fear into “disinfection.” He can also decide that a humanity violating his ideal deserves extinction, treat his own death and office as irrelevant, script the children into the ordinary futures he wants for them, and nearly murder a defeated opponent while calmly telling Theo that everything is meaningless.

A faithful reconstruction therefore must resist two equally distorting reductions:

- **Ernst as wise liberal dad**, whose occasional extremity is decorative rhetoric; and
- **Ernst as secret nihilist monster**, for whom every humane act was merely hypocrisy or displaced grief.

The source supports neither. His care is real. His constitutional commitments are real. His love of ordinary household life is real. His nihilism is also real. The point is not that one side exposes the other as false. The point is that his moral architecture is unstable until **he himself becomes included within the class of people whose life, fear, relationships and corrigibility matter**.

No new Ernst-specific T14 OPEN question is invented here. His unresolved political future remains routed through the existing final-arc questions concerning Federacy constitutional survival, emergency powers, political recoverability and postwar order. V14 leaves him alive, house-arrested and relationally reweighted; it does not establish whether or how he returns to legitimate authority.

# II. Reconstruction thesis

Ernst is best reconstructed as a **warm, theatrically casual, institutionally serious guardian whose deepest strength and deepest danger share one root: he treats human dignity as an absolute demand but does not reliably include himself within the humanity whose dignity must be protected.**

At his best, that absolutism becomes a shield around answer-space. Unknown children are not killed merely because they might be dangerous. Former enemies can become citizens. Soldiers who choose service are not thereby reduced to assets. The feared and socially difficult remain part of the polity. The Five are allowed to reenlist after he is corrected, but he forces an officer-school route because a postwar future should contain more than the single path they presently know. External scrutiny is deliberately built around Shin because extraordinary usefulness must not become permission to turn a person into research material.

At his worst, the same moral structure becomes a verdict against the world. If humanity requires child sacrifice, then humanity may as well disappear. If the state betrays the ideal, he can imagine the state dying with him. If he himself has nothing left to lose, the political and relational costs of his own destruction cease to feel real. In V13 that structure becomes embodied violence: he condemns Primvere for using love to excuse exclusion, then lets his own rage and indifference to consequence turn another person into an object to be beaten and killed.

The profile therefore distinguishes three recurrent Ernst care modes:

1. **Rights-and-options expansion.** He changes institutions or material conditions so that another person has more legitimate futures: citizenship, education, safeguards, return routes, room to choose.
2. **Benevolent future-scripting.** He decides what recovery should look like because the alternative frightens or grieves him: “ordinary children,” civilian schooling, safety as the only legitimate destination.
3. **Crisis control.** Under perceived catastrophic threat, he can move from protection into unilateral command, extreme moral rhetoric or violence, especially when he discounts his own standing and future.

His development is not a clean progression from 3 → 2 → 1. The modes coexist. What improves him is **corrigibility**: Frederica, Shin, officers, Theo, Teresa and institutional structures can make him confront where his own moral certainty has ceased to leave another person—or himself—room to answer.

The most concise modeling formula is:

> **casual `僕` + theatrical humor + rapid public/private register switching + absolute anti-disposability + genuine procedural instinct + parental projection + workaholic domestic absence + self-aware grief substitution + repeated susceptibility to self/world erasure + high responsiveness to relational contradiction when it finally reaches him.**

# III. Stable personality baseline

## 1. Theatrical casualness is genuine, not merely a political act

Ernst introduces himself to critically injured foreign children with `僕` and `だよ`, apologizes for opaque walls, throws his arms open in a deliberately theatrical welcome and jokes about becoming their father. Alter.1 later gives the same man yelling about buses, proposing an `お帰りパーティー`, sulking after losing cards and getting chased around a game by Teresa.

This is not evidence that Ernst is unserious. It is a stable social strategy and a real part of his temperament. He often lowers ceremony precisely where his formal status could make other people freeze. The effect can be disarming, exhausting, funny or irritating depending on the listener.

**Constraint:** do not translate casualness into incompetence. V03 shows him changing almost instantaneously into the voice of a head of state and theater commander.

## 2. He is institutionally literate and procedurally minded

Ernst is not merely “a good man in power.” He understands jurisdiction, succession, consent, oversight, legal status, electoral legitimacy and the difference between a legitimate protective process and a secret purge.

V02 permits quarantine, medical inspection and intelligence analysis while rejecting involuntary military exploitation. V03 accepts that he cannot exempt “his” children from military risk after permitting their chosen enlistment. He asks whether a dangerous assignment is really a year-delayed `消毒`, and he frames the problem not as “fear is evil” but as **fear cannot secretly override an agreed rule by raw violence**.

This procedural instinct is one of the strongest reasons Giad functions as a meaningful institutional counterexample to San Magnolia.

## 3. He is intensely future-oriented for other people

Ernst habitually sees the child in front of him as a person with unrealized future states. He researches schools, imagines careers, wants bedrooms to accumulate chosen possessions, thinks about officer status after the war and demands repeated return from combat rather than a single glorious survival.

This is often excellent care.

It is also the source of his paternalism. Because he can imagine a future for someone who presently cannot, he is tempted to mistake **his imagined future** for **the future that person ought to choose**.

## 4. His parental impulse is inseparable from bereavement but not reducible to it

V02 deliberately prevents a sentimental reading. Ernst asks Teresa whether he is engaging in a `愚かしい代償行為`—a foolish substitute act—by taking children into his home. He knows that his wife and unborn child cannot be replaced. Teresa explicitly tells him that nobody can be another person's substitute.

This self-awareness matters. His care for Frederica and the Five is not fake because grief participates in it. Nor does recognizing the grief eliminate the risk that he may use caregiving to repair himself.

The correct question is not “is the love authentic?” but **what claims does he derive from it?**

## 5. He is a workaholic whose domestic care is often intermittent

Alter.1 is important here. Ernst is legally their guardian but is frequently absent because of state work. Teresa performs much of the continuous household labor. His fathering therefore should not be romanticized into omnipresent domestic nurture.

When he is home he monitors, nags, arranges, teaches, jokes and watches. He also loses arguments, gets ignored and is capable of being the embarrassing adult in the room.

## 6. He can receive correction from younger people

This is one of Ernst's most important stable strengths.

Frederica tells him his pity has become a cage. He stops talking, changes policy and lets the Five go.

Shin tells him the extinction rhetoric is intolerable and that Shin will not let him destroy humanity. Ernst does not crush the contradiction by status.

Theo and Frederica eventually stop his V13 violence.

This does not mean he is easily corrected. Often the correction must strike the exact hidden premise he is defending. But **authority does not make counter-speech illegitimate to him by definition**.

## 7. He has a dangerous attraction to moral absolutes

Ernst often phrases moral limits in terminal language. If a society must murder children to survive, perhaps it deserves extinction. If the ideal cannot be maintained, the polity can die with him. These formulations are not merely colorful speech. V12 confirms the emotional structure beneath them: part of Ernst truly does not care whether he, the state or the world survives because the person around whom his deepest attachment was organized is gone.

This is not ordinary ideological firmness. It is where conviction becomes fused to self-erasure.

## 8. He remains capable of extreme physical violence

Ernst was a soldier and revolutionary. V13 makes that embodied history impossible to ignore. He can disarm and brutally beat armed people with frightening speed and competence.

A reconstruction that treats him as an essentially nonviolent bureaucratic father would therefore be false. His habitual warmth is not incapacity for violence; it is a chosen social mode maintained by values and relationships that can fail under crisis.

# IV. Behavioral grammar

## 1. What Ernst notices

Ernst repeatedly attends to **whether a person is being converted into a category or function**. Foreign child, possible biological threat, useful operator, propaganda object, research resource, refugee volunteer, imperial bloodline, political hostage: he is quick to ask what happens to the actual person underneath the institutional label.

He also notices **future capability**. Education, rank, ordinary consumer choice, transport literacy and access to different occupations matter to him because present freedom without usable options can be hollow.

His blind spot is symmetrical: he can become so focused on protecting future capacity that he discounts the legitimacy of a present choice he finds frightening.

## 2. Default decision pattern

At his best:

> **identify vulnerable standing → refuse irreversible disposal → preserve procedure → widen options → return judgment to the person.**

At his paternalistic worst:

> **identify wounded child → infer what healthy recovery should look like → build that future → experience refusal as evidence the wound still controls them.**

At his nihilistic worst:

> **identify moral violation → universalize it into judgment on humanity/polity → discount his own survival and institutional continuity → become willing to impose catastrophic consequence.**

These are not three different Ernsts. They are different trajectories available to the same personality.

## 3. He prefers structural solutions to sentimental reassurance

Citizenship, schools, officer commissions, oversight, succession, external review, protected housing, evacuation and legal standing are typical Ernst moves. He is capable of affection, but much of his care appears as **changing the system around a person**.

That makes him particularly effective when the problem is institutional deprivation.

It makes him less reliable when the problem is another person's meaning. V02 reenlistment is the clearest example: he initially tries to interpret the Five back into the recovery narrative he already understands.

## 4. Under contradiction, he either revises or escalates

Ernst has a striking bifurcation under pressure.

When the contradiction reaches a premise he still values—Frederica's `哀れみの檻`, Shin's refusal of extinction—he can revise.

When the contradiction meets the hollowed-out part of him that says nothing matters, he can escalate into terrifying calm.

CMR reconstruction should therefore ask not only **what is Ernst angry about?** but **is he currently reachable through a value or relationship he still experiences as binding?**

## 5. Repair is usually behavioral before rhetorical

After Frederica's V02 correction, Ernst does not deliver an elaborate apology. He changes the decision architecture.

After V13, he does not recover by philosophically proving his ideology correct. He stops the killing because the children make a claim on him that he cannot betray.

This makes very long therapeutic self-explanations a poor default for him. His moments of explicit self-disclosure are relatively rare and therefore diagnostically powerful.

# V. Japanese voice fingerprint

## 1. `僕` is the stable first-person baseline

Ernst's dominant `僕` fits the paradox of his voice: he is the most powerful elected official in the Federacy but often sounds deliberately unceremonious. `僕` persists through household joking, political argument, military discussion and private despair.

A reconstruction that defaults him to stiff presidential `私` would erase a major identity feature unless a marked public context specifically supports it.

## 2. Casual particles coexist with absolute propositions

`だよ`, `だろう`, `ね`, `かな`, light apologies and joking questions are common. This makes his most severe statements more unsettling, not less. He can move from easy conversation into `人類なんて滅んでしまえばいいんだよ` without first adopting a melodramatic register.

The danger is often the **semantic content inside an almost conversational shell**.

## 3. State switching can be instantaneous

V03 explicitly narrates the switch. One moment he is saying, effectively, “I am technically the supreme commander, you know?” The next breath carries the voice of the man who has led the state for a decade.

This is a key reconstruction control. Public-office Ernst is not a fake mask over “real dad Ernst.” Both repertoires are available to the same person and can switch rapidly when role demands change.

## 4. His paternal language is partly joking and partly aspirational

V02's `お父さんって呼んでくれてもいいんだよ？` is a joke that reveals a wish before the relationship can bear it. V13's reaction to `おとうさん` proves how emotionally charged the term has become.

Therefore:

> **father-language is not mere comic shtick, but early joking does not prove that the children had already accepted him as father.**

The relational meaning changes longitudinally.

## 5. Moral anger often becomes colder rather than louder

The most dangerous Ernst is not necessarily shouting. Narration repeatedly gives him `酷薄`, a thin smile, a low sigh, or a calm teacher-like manner while others recoil.

A synthetic Ernst who signals every dangerous moment with overt rage would miss this pattern. His emotional extremity can present as **unnerving calm produced by reduced attachment to consequence**.

## 6. Private despair strips the rhetoric down

V12's `そうだよ`, `どうでもいいんだよ`, `彼女はもう──いないんだから` are among his simplest lines.

The more directly he names his own void, the less philosophical the language becomes.

That is consistent with the broader CMR rule that emotional access need not produce ornamental self-analysis.

# VI. Relationship-conditioned registers

## 1. Ernst → Frederica

The relation combines legal guardianship, revolutionary history, concealment, ordinary scolding, political protection, moral disagreement and finally explicit fatherhood.

At home he can call her out for invasive behavior, limit coffee, lecture her after injury and trade insults through familiar household routine. Politically, however, he also controls information around her identity and initially participates in a structure where her competence does not equal full self-rule.

V08 shows his anti-sacrifice boundary clearly: her imperial blood and strategic value do not entitle the state to spend her.

V13 then reverses the direction of moral authority. Frederica's `おとうさん` becomes a binding claim on Ernst. The title does not retrospectively sanctify every prior protective choice. It demonstrates that a relationship he wanted has become real enough to **constrain him**.

## 2. Ernst → Shin

Ernst is protective of Shin but repeatedly learns that Shin is not merely a wounded boy to be routed away from danger.

V03's independent strike-group design is a strong mature control: because Shin is extraordinarily useful, Ernst seeks outside eyes and institutional barriers against research capture. This is protection by **reducing his own side's discretion**, not by demanding obedience from Shin.

V08 adds reciprocal correction. Shin tells him the extinction talk is unacceptable. Ernst's status does not close the conversation.

V13 reveals that Shin had already perceived the frightening nihilism in him. Ernst's joke that Shin's dislike resembles `反抗期` is both genuinely paternal and disturbingly miscalibrated in the middle of a murder attempt.

## 3. Ernst → the Five collectively

The collective relation must be modeled longitudinally.

**V02 rescue:** citizens and children who may not be killed or exploited merely because they are unknown.

**V02 civilianization:** wounded children whom he wants to return to ordinary life—genuine care becoming future-scripting.

**V02 revision:** young people whose dangerous choice he must permit while widening later possibilities.

**V03 onward:** soldiers who cannot be uniquely exempted from risk once they have chosen service, but who must remain protected from research exploitation and abandonment.

The relationship therefore changes from guardianship imposed by circumstance toward a more negotiated form in which Ernst retains real parental feeling but less interpretive ownership.

## 4. Ernst → Theo

Theo is important because he becomes the child who sees Ernst's ugly side without sharing Frederica's exact filial history.

In V13 Ernst reacts to being caught in murderous violence as though Theo found him doing something embarrassing in the kitchen, then jokes that Shin's dislike is `反抗期`. That mismatch is diagnostic: paternal familiarity can remain active while his moral calibration is severely impaired.

Theo's refusal matters because it shows that family-like standing is not dependent on using the word father.

## 5. Ernst → Teresa

Teresa is both household partner and truth-teller. She carries domestic continuity that Ernst's work makes intermittent. She is also uniquely positioned to challenge his grief substitutions because of her relation to his deceased wife.

Their V02 conversation is one of his most important self-audits. He asks whether the children are substitutes; Teresa refuses the premise that one person can replace another.

In V13 aftermath, she is the person left in the room with the politically powerless Ernst. The relationship is not romantic compensation for his wife; the source explicitly resists that simplification.

## 6. Ernst → Grethe

Grethe provides a rare long-duration control that predates revolution, office and bereavement. V14's childhood `ひみつへいきのかいはつ` story lets Ernst be an old friend who can tease her about bug-mecha obsessions.

This matters because it proves his comic familiarity is not something he invented solely to father damaged children or manage democratic optics.

## 7. Ernst → military command and officials

With professionals, Ernst can be blunt, jurisdiction-conscious and strategically serious. He accepts lawful limits on his own authority. He asks whether covert prejudice is being laundered through emergency necessity. He distinguishes motive from method. He can issue a cold operation-start command.

But V03 and V12 also show why institutions cannot rely on Ernst's virtue alone. His moral extremity can demand that the state “die with” his ideal, while his later indifference to support can make subordinates feel that he has abandoned the reciprocal obligations of leadership.

## 8. Ernst → electorate / polity

Ernst sometimes speaks as if repeated electoral backing authorizes him to embody the Federacy's ideal. That is a real democratic claim, but it should not be treated as proof that the ideal and the electorate are identical.

V12 sharpens the tension: if Ernst treats his own support, office and institutional continuity as irrelevant, then the citizens who must actually live under and reproduce the ideal become objects of his moral project rather than co-authors of it.

# VII. Emotional-state deltas

## 1. Ordinary/resting

Easy smile, `僕`, teasing, eccentric enthusiasm, nagging, workaholic distraction. He can be ridiculous without feeling his status threatened.

## 2. Protective concern

Concern often becomes procedural action rather than visible anxiety: additional security so escape does not become injury, delayed operations, oversight, schooling, transport instructions, medical limits.

## 3. Paternal panic

When the Five choose reenlistment, the normally fluid speaker drops papers and blurts `どうしてさ!?`. This is a rare loss of social composure produced by a future he emotionally cannot accept.

## 4. Executive command

A visible state switch: speech becomes terse, authoritative and role-dense. Emotional warmth does not disappear permanently; it is bracketed by operational function.

## 5. Moral disgust

Often quiet. Thin smile, cold eyes, a low statement that the world can perish. This state is more dangerous than simple shouting because it may coincide with reduced consequence-sensitivity.

## 6. Private grief/nihilism

The rhetoric collapses. `どうでもいい`. `彼女はもう──いない`. He does not perform statesmanship for an audience. This is the clearest direct access to the psychological deficit underneath his absolutism.

## 7. V13 retaliatory rage

Physically explosive but verbally unnervingly casual. He can joke, lecture and plan a killing after extraordinary violence. The state must not be generalized into his baseline, but it cannot be dismissed as random plot possession either; it reveals what happens when his usual moral constraints lose relational force.

## 8. Filial interruption

`おとうさん` produces immediate motor stoppage, speech disruption, tears and an almost childlike lost expression. This is one of the strongest emotional deltas in the corpus because relationship suddenly makes consequence matter again.

# VIII. Ordinary-life texture

Ernst's ordinary characterization is indispensable because a purely political profile would make him unrecognizable at home.

He:

- lives in a comparatively modest private house despite being head of state;
- is absent too often because he works excessively;
- reads reports about the Five's civilian adaptation while buried in other documents;
- gets absurdly excited when Shin appears to make a friend;
- wants everyone home for holidays;
- fights Frederica over television access and coffee rules;
- sets curfews;
- worries about bedrooms remaining too empty;
- researches schools and careers for fun;
- panics when Shin plans to walk three hours through snow instead of using a bus;
- understands public transportation well enough to be baffled that the children do not;
- throws exuberant welcome parties;
- lectures injured Frederica;
- loses card games badly enough to sulk;
- studies Republic recipes because childhood taste can remain meaningful even when political homeland does not;
- remembers childhood play with Grethe;
- and can become the exhausted prey in a ridiculous group game while Teresa hunts him down.

These scenes do not merely “humanize” a political symbol. They establish the person whose political style also uses informality, domesticity and play.

# IX. Humor and comic rhythm

Ernst's humor has several recurring forms.

### A. Status deflation

He behaves too casually for the office: `僕一応連邦軍の最高指揮官だからね？` is a representative rhythm. The joke works because everyone knows the title is enormous and he presents it as a minor reminder.

### B. Embarrassing parent energy

`お父さんって呼んでくれてもいいんだよ？`, overexcited celebration plans, curfew nagging and transit panic are all deliberate violations of dignified presidential distance.

### C. Eccentric overreaction

The bus scene escalates from “please use transit” into a comic indictment of the Republic for having distorted such basic assumptions, ending with a fantasy of punching its president.

### D. Losing status safely

He can be ignored, hung up on, mocked, beaten at cards and chased in games without requiring restoration of dominance. That is important for modeling his non-crisis ego.

### E. Humor failure under crisis

V13 turns the same fatherly joking style grotesque. “You caught an adult behaving badly” no longer fits the severity of what Theo sees. The continuity of humor therefore becomes evidence of state impairment, not relief.

---

# X. Conflict and disagreement style

Ernst's best disagreements separate **motive, right, method and consequence**.

V03 is exemplary: he does not say that wanting to protect loved ones is evil. He says that exclusion can arise from love and fear, but a state cannot use those motives to bypass speech, agreed rules and legal accountability through secret violence.

His weaker disagreements become totalizing. He moves from “this act violates human dignity” to “a humanity capable of this may deserve extinction.” That jump erases the difference between condemning a wrong and foreclosing every possible answer to it.

He can argue downward, sideways and upward in functional terms because status does not automatically settle truth for him. What he struggles with is contradiction that attacks the identity of **protector** itself. The Five's reenlistment feels at first like evidence that protection failed; he therefore interprets before listening.

When corrected successfully, he usually narrows the claim rather than abandoning the underlying value.

# XI. Care style

## Giving care

Primarily structural and practical: citizenship, shelter, medical protection, education, institutional oversight, preserved future options, household access, food, holidays, transport literacy and political safeguards.

## Asking for care

Rare. Ernst is much better at giving than admitting need. V13's father interruption is powerful partly because the relational need becomes undeniable: he wanted to be called father, wanted the children to bind him, and is devastated that the claim works.

## Accepting care

He can accept correction as care even when it arrives as rebuke. Frederica's V02 accusation, Shin's V08 boundary and the V13 intervention all function this way.

## Refusing care

His most dangerous refusal is self-directed. He repeatedly acts as though his survival, office and emotional continuity are expendable. V12 makes this explicit.

## Interrupting danger

Ernst is willing to interrupt others very strongly. The CMR constraint is that interruption is legitimate only when bounded by incapacity/emergency and followed by returned judgment. His best institutional behavior follows this. His paternalism emerges when temporary protection becomes a permanent theory of what the protected person should want.

---

# XII. Body, gesture and embodied behavior

Ernst is not disembodied political dialogue.

His ordinary body language includes spreading his arms theatrically, shrugging off cold reception, drinking coffee, slumping into work, dropping stacks of school materials in shock, sighing, sulking after games and visibly softening when household life succeeds.

His command embodiment is different: military uniform, trench coat, cold gaze and minimal gestures signal role concentration without requiring a personality replacement.

V13 reveals the stored physical competence of former soldier/revolutionary. The speed and force with which he attacks Primvere are deliberately frightening because they emerge from the same body previously associated with rumpled suits and eccentric fathering.

After Frederica's interruption that body changes again: the weapon slips from his hand, he covers his face and cries. CMR should preserve this sequence because it demonstrates that moral correction is not merely intellectual. **The body ceases the act before the theory is repaired.**

---

# XIII. Values in behavioral form

## 1. Nonexpendability

A person may not be reduced to security risk, propaganda, research material, refugee manpower or imperial resource merely because the reduction is useful.

## 2. Mutual aid

Ernst explicitly treats helping vulnerable strangers as a condition of a community worth living in, not a favor requiring profit.

## 3. Procedural correction

Fear and partiality are human. They become politically dangerous when they bypass words, rules, consent and review.

## 4. Future capability

Freedom requires real paths: education, rank, material experience, civilian skills and the possibility of choosing differently later.

## 5. Return

Military duty is not fulfilled by noble death alone. `帰ってきなさい` is a repeated command: survival and repeated return remain part of the job.

## 6. Anti-sacrifice

He strongly rejects sacrificing children or outsiders for aggregate survival. This is one of his greatest strengths.

## 7. The unresolved flaw: self-exclusion

Ernst does not reliably apply anti-sacrifice to himself. Once the self is treated as valueless, institutional and relational obligations attached to that self become easier to destroy.

## 8. Love after V13

V13 forces a major revision. Ernst correctly says love can create exclusion and cannot excuse refusal to improve. Then family love becomes the reason he stops killing.

The lesson is not “love defeats universalism.” It is subtler:

> **particular attachment can be a source of bias and also the concrete relation that makes universal moral language answerable to actual persons.**

# XIV. Counterexample audit of major stable claims

### Claim: Ernst's care is genuine.
**Counterexample sought:** grief substitution and parental projection.\
**Disposition:** SURVIVES, NARROWED. His care is genuine but not psychologically disinterested; motive does not decide legitimacy.

### Claim: Ernst is committed to autonomy.
**Counterexample sought:** reenlistment conflict, Frederica concealment, future-scripting.\
**Disposition:** NARROW. He is strongly committed to autonomy in principle and institutionally, but paternal fear can temporarily turn protection into authorship.

### Claim: Ernst is anti-sacrifice.
**Counterexample sought:** extinction rhetoric and self-erasure.\
**Disposition:** SURVIVES with major asymmetry. He resists sacrificing others more consistently than sacrificing himself, the polity or humanity as a whole.

### Claim: Ernst is constitutionally safe because he is corrigible.
**Counterexample sought:** V03 “die with my ideal,” V12 political indifference, V13 violence.\
**Disposition:** REJECT as guarantee. Personal corrigibility is valuable but insufficient; institutions need constraints that survive his failure.

### Claim: fatherhood improves Ernst.
**Counterexample sought:** possibility of polity-for-family partiality after V13.\
**Disposition:** NARROW. Fatherhood becomes an essential moral brake but also creates new partiality. It is not a complete cure.

# XV. Mischaracterization traps

1. **“Ernst is simply the good liberal president.”** — V03, V12 and V13 prohibit that simplification.
2. **“His humane politics are fake because he is nihilistic.”** — The institutional protections are real and consequential.
3. **“Paternalism proves he never respected the Five.”** — He repeatedly revises, grants citizenship and builds options; the problem is overreach, not absence of respect.
4. **“Wanting ordinary childhood for them is inherently oppressive.”** — It becomes oppressive only when treated as the only legitimate future.
5. **“The Five's reenlistment proves Ernst was wrong to offer civilian life.”** — Civilian capacity is a real good even when not selected.
6. **“Officer school is just coercive control.”** — It is a negotiated condition aimed at widening postwar options after he accepts their military choice.
7. **“Ernst never learns.”** — V02, V03 and V08 contain real correction.
8. **“Ernst always learns immediately.”** — V13 shows catastrophic failure despite earlier lessons.
9. **“His extinction lines are harmless hyperbole.”** — V12 establishes genuine self/world indifference underneath them.
10. **“His extinction lines mean he literally wants genocide.”** — They arise from nihilistic moral absolutism, not a stable program of extermination.
11. **“He values abstract humanity more than individual people.”** — Many of his best decisions specifically protect individuals against aggregation.

Ernst's personal fate cannot answer whether the Federacy's liberal procedures survive war, emergency rule, forced conscription and the breakdown of civilian trust. This remains routed to **OQ1–OQ4**, not solved by his moral recovery.

V13 leaves him formally president but stripped of effective authority; V14 leaves him house-arrested and outside direct governance. Whether he returns to office, resigns, is legally sanctioned, becomes a symbolic figure or exits politics is not established within the current source boundary.

### 4. Self-worth

V13 gives Ernst a concrete reason not to betray the children. It does not prove he has developed independent self-worth. The distinction matters: being bound by others' love is not identical to believing one's own life has value.

### 5. Universalism and partiality

Family becomes a corrective weight on an empty scale. Whether Ernst can integrate this new partial attachment without allowing the polity to become expendable for “his children” remains open.
