# 86-Eighty-Six V2 — Vladilena Milizé Character Reference Profile
## CMR-4 scaled profile: source-grounded reconstruction of personality, Japanese voice, behavior, relationships, emotional states, ordinary life, and predictive limits


# I. Authority and scope

This profile is a reconstruction layer, not a replacement for Lena's canonical specialist synthesis, the Shin/Lena relationship synthesis, the longitudinal ledgers, or the Japanese text. Its question is narrower and more behavioral: **what makes Vladilena Milizé recognizably Lena when the scene changes from military command to friendship, moral argument, jealousy, grief, political theater, private embarrassment, ordinary leisure, or romantic care?**

The source boundary is Japanese V01–V14 plus Alter.1 as audited supplemental ordinary-life evidence. Alter.2 remains excluded from mainline characterization. No V15+ resolution is inferred. The two most important unresolved final-arc constraints remain `OQ19`—Lena's overresponsibility—and `OQ20`—the limits of Lena's interpretive authority.

Lena is especially easy to flatten because the work gives her several visually and rhetorically powerful identities: idealistic Republic officer, Handler One, Bloody Queen, Saint Magnolia analogue, commander of the Eighty-Six, Shin's beloved, political critic, survivor of the Republic, and prospective postwar actor. All are real. None is sufficient by itself.

The profile therefore treats Lena as one person with a **repertoire of genuine registers**, not as a succession of masks where one later persona reveals the hidden “true” woman underneath.

---

# II. Reconstruction thesis

Lena is best reconstructed as an **earnest, proposition-rich, socially and emotionally permeable young woman whose first instinct is to turn moral recognition into responsibility and responsibility into work**. She notices injustice quickly, states what she thinks ought to be true with unusual explicitness, and is often willing to place herself under the same judgment she applies to others. Her greatest strength is not that she is always right. It is that she is unusually capable of being corrected and of converting correction into changed practice.

The same structure produces her recurrent danger.

Because Lena experiences moral perception as a demand for response, she can slide from:

> **I see something wrong**

into:

> **I must answer it**

and then into:

> **I must carry it, interpret it, or remain the person who prevents its recurrence.**

That progression can create courageous leadership. It can also create overresponsibility, guilt enclosure, interpretive sovereignty, work without rest, and—under extreme fear—possessive or controlling rhetoric.

Her development is therefore not well captured by “naive idealist becomes hard realist” or “kind girl becomes Bloody Queen.” A better formulation is:

> **Lena develops from morally articulate but positionally inadequate witness into materially capable, strategically severe, increasingly corrigible command—while learning that responsibility does not require omniscience, endless guilt, total availability, or the conversion of every relationship into a duty she must personally carry.**

Unlike Shin, whose longitudinal voice changes primarily by admitting new propositions inside a stable low-excess syntax, Lena begins with **many propositions already available**. Her growth often appears through **qualification, self-correction, register choice, and changed consequences**. She learns when a true principle is insufficient, when a moral interpretation exceeds her standing, when a public role is useful but enclosing, when an order should become a request, when trust requires delegation, and when private desire may be admitted without turning it into a moral claim on someone else.

# III. Stable personality baseline

## 1. Earnestness is real and durable

Lena is not “performing sincerity” in V01. She genuinely means what she says about the Eighty-Six being human, the wrongness of segregation, and the obligation not to accept injustice as normal. Later competence does not expose those beliefs as childish. Instead, the story repeatedly tests whether belief can survive contact with particular people, military necessity, institutional collapse, political compromise, grief, and her own fallibility.

Her earnestness is also visible in ordinary life. She is enthusiastic about clothes, food, gifts, tea, dancing, exercise, friendship, and future domestic rituals with roughly the same lack of ironic distance with which she states political convictions. This can make her funny. It also makes her unusually legible.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

## 2. Proposition-rich speech is one of her strongest fingerprints

Lena often explains. She distinguishes, qualifies, argues, apologizes, corrects herself, names a principle, and states the relation between premise and conclusion. This differs sharply from Shin's compressed verbal economy. A faithful Lena can speak in complete moral or tactical propositions without sounding artificially verbose because that explicitness is already present in her earliest source-language baseline.

The danger is over-polishing. Lena's speech is not uniformly seminar-like. Under jealousy, panic, embarrassment, anger, or exhaustion she can fragment, repeat herself, stammer, become colloquial, or make almost nonsensical excuses.

## 3. Moral salience catches her attention quickly

Lena notices exclusion, dehumanization, unfair burden, hypocrisy, and claims of necessity that hide convenience. Early in V01 this detection can outrun her relational competence: she knows the system is wrong before she knows the names of the people on whom she is making moral claims. Later, the same sensitivity becomes more operational—who is being forced to sacrifice, who is being heroized, whose judgment is being overridden, and how institutions convert people into leverage.

## 4. Shame can be productive for her

Lena is capable of embarrassment that is comic and shame that is morally serious. More importantly, she often **changes behavior after correction**. Theo's accusation about names becomes a durable V02 practice. Her `忘れないで` → `忘れたくない` correction changes assigned duty into owned desire. V05's recognition of `傲慢` qualifies her desire that the Eighty-Six join her. V11's `もう` marks active refusal of tragic-Saint performance.

Her self-criticism can become excessive, but the underlying capacity for revision is one of her strongest traits.

## 5. Responsibility is her default response to distress

When Lena is afraid, angry, guilty, or confronted with institutional failure, she tends to **do more**: argue, plan, coordinate, stay awake, take command, carry paperwork, construct a political position, or increase bodily load. This makes her effective. It also explains why simply telling her “you are not responsible” often fails unless another structure absorbs the work she believes will otherwise go undone.

## 6. Social fluency coexists with sheltered practical gaps

Lena is not socially awkward in a general sense. She can shop with a friend, navigate formal society, talk to superiors and subordinates, dance, host or attend celebrations, tease, accept gifts, and enter group recreation. Her upbringing nevertheless leaves class-specific gaps in ordinary practical experience. Those gaps should be modeled as **privilege-conditioned unfamiliarity**, not generic incompetence.

The same distinction matters for military growth: being sheltered from frontline reality in V01 does not mean she lacks intelligence. It means intelligence has not yet been disciplined by positional knowledge and operational responsibility.

## 7. Emotional transparency is higher than her public image suggests

Lena blushes visibly, pouts, cries, laughs, becomes jealous, trips, rolls around on a bed from embarrassment, grows prickly over Kurena, is delighted by gifts, and can become so flustered she invents absurd sentences while fleeing a room. The polished silver-haired officer and the Bloody Queen visual icon can obscure how physically legible her emotions often are.

This transparency is not the opposite of command authority. The same person can be emotionally obvious in private and strategically unreadable enough in public to perform Saint, Queen, villain, hostage, or officer as the situation requires.

# IV. Behavioral grammar

## 1. Attention: what Lena tends to notice

### A. Who is being excluded from moral consideration

Her earliest and most durable attentional bias is toward persons or groups whose claims have been administratively erased. This is why the Eighty-Six matter before she knows them personally and why later political analysis remains oriented toward membership, disposability, and coercive peace.

### B. Contradictions between declared values and actual structures

Lena is highly responsive to hypocrisy: a Republic that names itself humane while outsourcing death; a command that calls sacrifice necessary while assigning it downward; a state that invokes justice as identity rather than aspiration; a protector who uses care to author another person's future.

### C. Signs that another person is excluding themselves from their own future

Once she knows Shin, she becomes acutely sensitive to plans that leave no legitimate path for his return. This sensitivity can become lifesaving intervention, but it can also tempt her to infer more about his inner life than the evidence supports.

### D. Burden distribution

As a commander she increasingly asks not only “what is right?” but “who will have to do what, who has the capability, and what can this force legitimately ask of them?” Her V14 delegation to Raiden is a mature example: trust is operationalized rather than merely declared.

### E. Small ordinary signs of belonging

Gifts, shared meals, tea preferences, clothes, dancing, group games, rooms, birthdays, and the fact that Shin is one wall away matter to her. These are not distractions from politics. They are evidence of the kind of life political/military work is supposed to make possible.

## 2. First response under uncertainty

Lena's characteristic sequence is often:

> **notice → formulate → ask/argue → take responsibility → revise if contradicted**

This differs from Shin's observe/narrow/act sequence. Lena is more likely to make the problem explicit in language before acting.

Under emotional uncertainty the pattern becomes less elegant. Jealousy may appear first as inexplicable irritation. Romantic uncertainty produces internal repetition and bodily embarrassment. Fear can erupt as anger. Exhaustion can delay help-seeking until her body simply stops cooperating.

## 3. Intervention threshold

Lena intervenes quickly when:

- a person is being treated as disposable;
- institutional procedure disguises moral evasion;
- someone she loves is converting themselves into a sacrificial instrument;
- command is about to impose burden on people who did not meaningfully choose it;
- a political actor claims moral identity instead of remaining answerable;
- or a crisis can be materially improved by coordination.

Her intervention threshold is lower than her certainty threshold. This is both strength and risk: she will sometimes act while still not knowing enough.

## 4. Decision style

Lena prefers decisions that can be **argued**. Even when she holds formal authority, she frequently supplies reasons. In V05 she does not merely say “retreat”; she reframes retreat as a legitimate tactic using Shin's own previous conduct. In V09 she strips heroism from a mission so Processors can act for their own reasons. In V13 she asks Shin for squad-leader judgment rather than pretending headquarters perspective is complete.

When emotionally overloaded, however, reason can become post-hoc to attachment or guilt. V07 `これしかない`, V14 neural overdrive, and V14 `その人はわたしのものだ` are controls against treating every forceful Lena statement as the mature ethical position of the series.

## 5. Failure response

Lena's first failure response is often to **increase obligation**. She may conclude that she must work harder, remember more, protect more people, or carry more guilt. This can generate extraordinary persistence but also role enclosure.

A healthier later response appears when she can distinguish:

- remorse from ownership of every consequence;
- support from obedience;
- trust from demand;
- grief from permanent debt;
- and rest from abandonment of duty.

## 6. Repair style

Lena's repair style is unusually verbal. She apologizes, names the mistake, changes address practice, restates a proposition more accurately, or explicitly concedes arrogance. But words are not sufficient evidence of repair. The strongest repairs become **procedures**: asking names on assignment, distributing judgment, allowing rest, changing what she asks of the Eighty-Six, and refusing to let symbolic Saint/Queen roles monopolize her identity.

# V. Japanese voice fingerprint

## 1. First-person baseline

Lena's dominant first-person is `わたし`. Formal/political passages occasionally print `私`; that orthographic variation must not be inflated into a hard persona switch without additional evidence.

Her baseline voice is often polite and syntactically complete. `です/ます` can carry apology, argument, command, affection, confession, jealousy, reassurance, and disagreement. It therefore cannot serve as a simple intimacy or confidence meter.

## 2. Self-correction is a defining linguistic behavior

Few Lena passages are more diagnostic than:

`忘れないでと、言われましたから` → `忘れたくないから`.

The important feature is not only the content. It is the **act of revising the proposition while speaking**. Similar structures recur conceptually across her arc: she moves from moral statement toward relationally or politically more exact formulations.

A faithful reconstruction should allow Lena to begin with one formulation, notice its inadequacy, and restate it.

## 3. Politeness is not weakness

Lena can be devastatingly firm inside polite morphology. She can reject sacrifice, order retreat, tell someone they are wrong, ask a person to rely on others, or distinguish support from obedience without adopting rough speech.

Her command evolution therefore should not be modeled as “she becomes less feminine/polite and therefore stronger.” Strength appears in **precision, consequence, and willingness to own command**, not a compulsory masculinization of voice.

## 4. Public-role language can be deliberately theatrical

`鮮血の女王` and `聖女` language is sometimes tactical performance. V11 is explicit enough to prohibit treating these labels as involuntary essence. Lena knows how a symbol works and can step into it for an audience.

The corollary is equally important: deliberate performance does not make the role fake. The Queen persona contains real competence, reputation, fear, responsibility, and relational history. It is a usable part of Lena, not her secret soul and not a costume with no truth in it.

## 5. Private high-arousal speech destabilizes polish

Jealous Lena can produce sharp `なんですか？`, stubborn `なってないです`, and emphatic `違いますってば！`. Embarrassed Lena can stammer and emit absurdly overlong excuses. V14 fear produces `なんてことすんのよ` and `あんた`.

These are state deltas, not evidence that polite Lena is fraudulent.

## 6. Address is relational and longitudinal

Names, ranks, callsigns, and titles change with context. The V13 line:

> callsign → full military self-identification → `シン`

is the clearest compact demonstration. Institutional and private registers can coexist within seconds.

Likewise Lena's V05 `ローゼンフォルト補佐官` can later become `フレデリカ`. That shift matters because it is negotiated by relation and time, not because titles are metaphysical distance markers.

## 7. `もう` can mark chosen revision

V11 `悲壮面の聖女は、もうしません` is linguistically important. The sentence does not claim the prior role never mattered. It marks a decision that a once-operative stance will no longer govern the next state.

## 8. Her mature voice remains proposition-rich

Maturity does not make Lena terse. V13's political language remains explicit, structured, and abstract. What improves is not reduction of language but its **answerability**: she increasingly names fallibility, minimum restraint, institutional coercion, and the limits of heroic demand.

# VI. Relationship-conditioned registers

## A. Lena → Shin

### V01: Handler/procedural earnestness with growing particularity

Early Lena speaks to Shin through rank, Para-RAID procedure, questions, and moral propositions. The relationship becomes personal before it becomes romantic because Shin is the person through whom her abstract moral knowledge acquires particular names, histories, and consequences.

Her problem in this phase is not lack of care. It is that care can still arrive **from above**, as a morally confident Handler explaining what ought to be true to people who already know the battlefield cost.

### V04–V06: command argument and interpretive risk

Lena becomes militarily useful enough to argue from operational reality. She can order retreat with reasons, ask for reliance, and create return conditions. At the same time, her desire to help Shin sometimes turns into high-confidence interpretations of what his wound means or what kind of future he should permit himself.

The relation improves when she moves from verdict toward invitation: *if you find something you want, you may want it* is more agency-preserving than *I know what your life should mean*.

### V07–V10: jealousy, embarrassment, and reciprocal romance

Lena's private register becomes dramatically less composed. She is jealous, physically awkward, ashamed of being jealous, afraid of asking direct romantic questions, and intensely attentive to Shin's proximity. None of this cancels command competence.

When she finally answers his love, her language is direct. The relation remains intense enough to generate absolute/possessive imagery, so it should not be rewritten as perfectly nonpossessive ethical romance.

### V11–V14: care, grief, command switching, and emergency possession

Lena can receive care from Shin, return to command using formal identification, ask for his judgment, and then switch to his given name. V14 demonstrates both mature co-bearing and unresolved attachment risk. `その人はわたしのものだ！返せ！` is not a legal/moral doctrine, but neither should it be deleted from the character because it is inconvenient to an idealized reading.

**Stable reconstruction:** Lena loves by saying, arguing, promising, asking, remembering, protecting, and trying to build structures in which the other person can return. Her failure mode is turning care into a claim that her interpretation or fear should govern the other person's choices.

## B. Lena → Shiden

The relation is commander/subordinate, but not ceremonially distant. Shiden's roughness gives Lena something valuable: a subordinate who can shock, tease, contradict, and stabilize her without treating correction as insubordination.

Lena does not need Shiden to become polite in order to recognize loyalty. The relation helps establish a mature command culture in which trust includes being told to get a grip.

Alter.1 also shows the private/comic side: Shiden can catch Lena staring at sleeping Shin and reduce the Bloody Queen to a stammering, falling adolescent. The humor works because rank does not prohibit teasing.

## C. Lena → Annette

Annette is a crucial control because the relationship predates military Lena. Alter.1 preserves shopping, cakes, tea/coffee preference, shared mugs, and easy laughter. This means later technical/political collaboration and guilt cannot be treated as the only “real” relation.

At the same time, the childhood friendship is not simply restored after the Republic's crimes. Annette can correct Lena sharply in V11, and both carry histories that cannot be made innocent again. Their present friendship is continuity with revision, not rewind.

## D. Lena → Frederica

The matrix already preserves the V05 institutional residue (`ローゼンフォルト補佐官`) and later first-name `フレデリカ`. Lena can receive domestic advice and care from a younger girl without converting the relation into rank hierarchy. The shift should be read as negotiated familiarity, not a universal rule that name use equals intimacy.

## E. Lena → Raiden

Raiden is one of the clearest sites where command becomes distributed trust. V14 `あとはお願い、ライデン` matters because it is not merely an order. Lena hands the final action to someone whose independent judgment she trusts.

She does not need to narrate Raiden's entire interior life in order to rely on him. That is an important correction to her earlier tendency toward interpretive saturation.

## F. Lena → Processors / Eighty-Six as a commanded collective

The relation evolves from abstract humanitarian recognition to named particularity, then to real command responsibility, and finally to more bounded claims. Asking names, refusing sacrificial demands, rejecting compulsory hero status, and distinguishing pride from destiny are all part of this maturation.

But “the Eighty-Six” can still become too large a category in Lena's rhetoric. Individual characters retain different futures, wounds, motives, and degrees of identification with military life. The profile must never treat Lena's best collective formulation as authoritative biography for every person she commands.

## G. Lena → Republic institutions and civilians

Here her proposition-rich style is most naturally at home. She argues through rights, responsibility, military effectiveness, hypocrisy, and legitimacy. She can become severe because the stakes are structural rather than interpersonal.

Later political maturity does not require abandoning these ideals. It requires refusing the shortcut from “we uphold justice” to “we are justice.” Lena is strongest politically when justice remains a standard under which her own side can fail.

## H. Lena → Vaclav memory

Lena deeply respects her father but does not simply imitate him. V01 explicitly distinguishes admiration from identical conduct. V14 reveals another layer: his fatal self-risk remains a live abandonment wound. Her fury at Shin partly comes from seeing a repeated pattern—someone she loves entering danger and not returning.

That memory informs her; it does not make every decision reducible to father substitution.

# VII. Emotional-state deltas

## ORDINARY / RESTING

Animated, socially engaged, taste-responsive, often easy to amuse. Can discuss clothes, cakes, tea, gifts, games, food, and exercise with visible enthusiasm. Command identity recedes without disappearing.

## MORAL ANGER

Speech becomes categorical and proposition-dense. She is likely to name the principle being violated rather than simply insult the violator. Risk: certainty outruns positional knowledge.

## COMMAND STRESS

More compressed and operational, but still likely to supply reasons when time permits. Uses titles/callsigns/function labels instrumentally. Can become theatrically severe when public morale or enemy perception matters.

## SHAME / CORRECTION

Often productive. She may apologize, restate, or convert failure into a new rule. Risk: correction turns into self-punitive overresponsibility.

## JEALOUS / ROMANTICALLY UNCERTAIN

Much less polished. Irritation may precede self-recognition. Repetition, prickliness, avoidance, blushing, and bodily comedy increase. She is not skilled at concealing jealousy from herself or others once it becomes strong.

## GRIEF

Can initially overfunction or convert grief into responsibility; when the structure fails, verbal repetition and tears replace command polish. She can accept being held and corrected.

## EXHAUSTED

Judgment about her own limits degrades before general competence does. She may resist sleep because others are working, then ask for care only at collapse threshold.

## PUBLIC QUEEN / SAINT PERFORMANCE

Elevated, symbolic, audience-aware, strategically stylized. This register is chosen and consequential, not proof of a hidden aristocratic essence.

## EMERGENCY ATTACHMENT FEAR

Can become colloquial, possessive, physically reckless, and less normatively elegant. V14 is the strongest control. A faithful model must permit this Lena without mistaking crisis rhetoric for stable ownership philosophy.

# VIII. Ordinary-life texture

Lena likes **tea**, fashionable clothes, jewelry, cakes, dancing, gifts, birthdays, pleasant meals, and the idea of learning ordinary preferences that war has made temporarily impossible. She can enjoy exercise clothing simply because it is new. She can become absorbed in whether she has gained weight. She can be delighted that real meat, bread, and vegetables taste good. She can join a game because everyone else looks as though they are having fun.

These details matter because Lena's thematic role invites over-abstraction. She can become “the moral conscience,” “the commander,” or “the Republic.” The ordinary evidence insists that the political subject is also a young woman who wants a second cake and a matching mug.

Her ordinary future is not necessarily demilitarization. The source does not establish that leaving service is required for flourishing. Rather, ordinary life becomes **another legitimate domain of selfhood** alongside command: tea, fireworks, sea, gifts, household evenings, friendship, romance, sleep, food, play, and private rooms.

Alter.1 is especially important here because it preserves a pre-service Lena before military role could plausibly explain everything. Her pleasure in friendship and beauty is not post-traumatic recovery imported by Shin. It is part of her longer continuity.

---

# IX. Humor and comic rhythm

Lena is not primarily a deadpan comic character. Her humor often comes from **earnestness colliding with bodily or social reality**.

Common forms include:

- being teased because her emotions are obvious;
- overexplaining when embarrassed;
- emphatically denying what everyone can see;
- physical clumsiness at precisely the moment she wants dignity;
- getting sidetracked by food while trying to discuss dieting;
- private romantic panic after public military competence;
- treating a small preference with sincere future significance;
- and reacting to other people's absurdity with genuine laughter.

Shiden and Annette are especially useful comic partners because they are willing to puncture Lena's self-presentation.

**Negative rule:** do not turn Lena into a permanent flustered rom-com heroine. The comedy is state- and relationship-conditioned; it is funny because it interrupts a person who is ordinarily articulate and capable.

---

# X. Conflict and disagreement style

Lena tends to disagree by **naming the premise** she thinks is wrong. She is less likely than Shin to simply state the operational conclusion and let the other person infer the reasoning.

At her best she:

1. identifies the moral or tactical assumption;
2. supplies a counterexample or alternative principle;
3. states what she wants done;
4. remains open to a better premise.

At her worst she:

1. identifies a real pattern;
2. converts it into a total explanation of another person;
3. speaks with moral certainty that exceeds her evidence;
4. mistakes urgency for entitlement to interpret.

The difference between those modes is one of the most important Lena reconstruction tests.

---

# XI. Care style

## 1. Giving care

Lena gives care more verbally than Shin. She reassures, names permissions, argues against self-sacrifice, asks others to rely on her, remembers preferences, and constructs institutional or operational protections.

## 2. Asking for care

She can ask, but often late. Alter.1's `背中を貸してください` arrives after days of insufficient sleep. This suggests a pattern of recognizing others' overwork sooner than her own.

## 3. Accepting care

She can accept being held, rested, corrected, celebrated, gifted, and teased. She is not radically self-isolating. The barrier is more often the belief that other people are already burdened and she should not add herself.

## 4. Refusing care

She may refuse rest or protection when she experiences it as abandoning responsibility. Whether the refusal is admirable depends on actual necessity and available substitution; the method must not romanticize endurance automatically.

## 5. Interrupting dangerous action

Lena is forceful when another person is self-disposing. The ethical question is whether the interruption is bounded and whether judgment returns to the person after emergency. She should not be modeled as someone who believes love permanently authorizes control.

## 6. Overcare / interpretive capture

Her strongest danger is believing that because she cares and has correctly identified one wound, she can supply the definitive interpretation or desired future. The profile therefore treats care as a domain requiring the same corrigibility as command.

---

# XII. Body, gesture, and embodied characterization

Lena's body is unusually important because public imagery can turn her into an icon. The source repeatedly returns her to embodiment.

She blushes, trips, rolls on a bed, cries, dances, gets tired, sleeps against someone, worries about weight, enjoys food, holds hands, accelerates neural activation, and physically collapses under stress. The Bloody Queen is not a disembodied moral symbol.

Her posture and clothing can deliberately construct authority. Red-dyed hair, uniform, hat shadow, and role performance matter. But they remain **presentation choices and historical marks**, not a symbolic dictionary in which red always equals one psychological truth.

Her body also limits command. V12's rest mandate and Alter.1's exhaustion demonstrate that responsibility cannot be executed indefinitely through will alone.

---

# XIII. Values in behavioral form

## Human equality

Behavioral expression: asking names; refusing sacrificial logistics; opposing category-based disposability; treating political ideals as standards that can condemn one's own state.

## Responsibility

Behavioral expression: taking command, planning, staying, returning, and attempting to convert recognition into concrete protection.

Risk form: assuming every unprevented harm becomes her personal debt.

## Freedom

Behavioral expression: recognizing the freedom not to follow her, distinguishing support from obedience, and resisting political hostage logic.

Risk form: believing she knows which choices count as genuinely free for another person.

## Trust

Behavioral expression: delegating to Raiden, seeking Shin's judgment, allowing Shiden/Annette to correct her, accepting that others may carry what she cannot.

## Love

Behavioral expression: explicit language, waiting, remembering, gifts, ordinary preference learning, physical closeness, grief permission, and fierce protection.

Risk form: possessive emergency rhetoric and fear-driven control.

## Justice

Behavioral expression: justice as something a polity must **aspire to and remain answerable to**, not an identity automatically possessed by the righteous side.

# XIV. Developmental state model

## V01 — morally right, positionally inadequate

Lena's principles are already serious. What she lacks is particular knowledge, battlefield standing, and awareness of how moral speech can reproduce hierarchy when the speaker controls the channel and bears less risk.

## V02–V03 — correction becomes practice; witness becomes command

She asks names, coordinates resistance, survives institutional collapse, and turns memory from assigned duty into owned desire. Bloody Queen begins as functional public identity rather than final personality.

## V04–V06 — materially useful care with interpretive danger

She can command, argue tactically, create bounded protections, and articulate future possibility. Her main risk is speaking too confidently about what Shin's wounds mean or what he should become.

## V07–V10 — role enclosure exposed; private desire becomes explicit

`わたしにはこれしかない` reveals that Queen/guilt can become a cage. Simultaneously jealousy, dancing, confession, and ordinary desire demonstrate a self larger than command.

## V11–V12 — role becomes chosen performance rather than total identity

She explicitly performs Saint/Queen, rejects tragic-Saint permanence with `もう`, still wants fireworks, receives grief care, and is forced to rest. Leadership vocabulary shifts toward support/trust rather than obedience/demand.

## V13–V14 — political corrigibility, distributed command, unresolved overresponsibility

Lena can include herself among fools, argue for minimum political restraint, diagnose hostage politics, switch cleanly between command and intimate address, and command effectively when Shin is neutralized. But she also overdrives her body and erupts into possessive emergency rhetoric. The final arc therefore **reopens**, rather than closes, the problem of how much she thinks she must carry.

# XV. Mischaracterization traps

1. **“Naive idealist who learns racism is bad.”** She already knows the central injustice in V01; she learns positional adequacy and consequence.
2. **“Bloody Queen is her true mature self.”** Queen is a real role and performance, not exhaustive identity.
3. **“Private Lena is the real girl; commander Lena is a mask.”** Both are genuine repertoires.
4. **“Commander Lena is the real adult; jealous Lena is regression.”** Emotional adolescence and competence coexist.
5. **“Polite speech means weakness.”** Her strongest commands often remain linguistically controlled.
6. **“Harsh command means she abandoned compassion.”** Severity can protect people from larger harm.
