# 86-Eighty-Six V2 — Annette Penrose Character Reference Profile
## CMR-5 scaled profile: source-grounded reconstruction of personality, Japanese voice, Para-RAID cognition, guilt, research ethics, friendship, ordinary desire, and predictive limits

# I. Authority and scope

This document reconstructs **Annette Penrose / Henrietta Penrose / アンリエッタ・ペンローズ**, generally called **アネット**, across the locked Japanese corpus through Volume 14 plus Alter.1. It is a character-reconstruction surface, not a replacement for the Japanese novels, the Phase-5 locator lock, the Phase-8 verification layer, or the mature V2 specialist corpus.

Annette is especially easy to misread because several superficially incompatible descriptions are simultaneously true:

- she is a gifted and genuinely curious **Para-RAID / neural-interface researcher**;
- she begins the series with real affection for Lena while still reproducing the Republic's dehumanizing asymmetry toward the Eighty-Six;
- she possesses a strong conscience, but that conscience repeatedly deforms into **self-punishment**;
- she is cynical and verbally abrasive without being affectively cold;
- she can think with extraordinary technical clarity about morally horrific mechanisms without granting those mechanisms moral permission;
- she wants to atone for Shin, but initially treats his remembrance, hatred, or forgiveness as if they were necessary components of her own moral repair;
- she is capable of practical, privacy-protecting care that asks for no absolution;
- and she retains a lively, fashion-conscious, food-loving, teasing ordinary self that is not reducible to either scientist or sinner.

The governing reconstruction problem is therefore not simply, “Is Annette guilty?” It is:

> **What does Annette do when conscience, technical curiosity, friendship, and the desire to punish herself all point in different directions?**

The profile uses **185 source-verified diagnostic scenes**. Five rows reuse existing Phase-5 locator authority. The remaining rows preserve exact EPUB/XHTML routes and Japanese anchors under controlled `LOCATOR_GAP` status. No synthetic dialogue is admitted as evidence.

One Alter.1 control requires an explicit exclusion note. The playful “all-star perfume battle” is described by the authorial afterword as effectively a chronology-bending collective hallucination rather than an event that actually occurred in the story world. It is therefore **not used as mainline Annette evidence here**, even though other audited Alter.1 ordinary-life stories are used.

# II. Reconstruction thesis

Annette is best reconstructed as a **technically incisive, socially abrasive-but-attentive researcher whose deepest developmental problem is not lack of conscience but the tendency to turn conscience into self-punishment**.

Her science and her guilt share a cognitive habit: she wants problems to become tractable. A mechanism can be investigated. A fault can be isolated. A parameter can be changed. A causal chain can be traced backward. Early in the series she tries to treat moral injury the same way. If she betrayed Shin, then the moral system seems as though it should contain an injured party who remembers, a wrongdoer who confesses, a punishment or refusal, and perhaps an act of restitution. When Shin genuinely does not remember the betrayal that has organized years of her self-condemnation, the imagined moral circuit breaks. She asks, in effect, what she is supposed to *do* if even apology and punishment are unavailable.

That crisis is essential. Annette does not mature because Shin performs absolution for her. She matures because she slowly learns that:

> **responsibility survives without entitlement to remembrance, hatred, forgiveness, restoration, or punishment.**

The mature Annette can therefore make responsibility useful in less self-centered forms. She continuously monitors Para-RAID health data. She volunteers for a dangerous field experiment rather than assigning the risk to someone else. She protects Shin's biological and research privacy by withholding his identity as one of the ability-bearers underlying Para-RAID. She helps Lena without pretending Lena's choices belong to her. She gives Shin gifts framed as apology without requiring him to restore childhood. She helps Theo imagine a future made of trivial pleasures rather than grand redemption. She can expose the Republic's abuse of children and then accept when Theo refuses to punish her on their behalf.

Her scientific ethic develops along a parallel line. Annette can deliberately set aside disgust and guilt long enough to determine what is technically possible. That is not moral indifference. It is one of her most important professional competencies. Volume 12 gives the clearest mature example: confronted with the possibility that Para-RAID was used outside declared military communication, she goes pale, then deliberately discards “common sense,” immediate ethical horror, and unhelpful guilt from the *diagnostic* step so she can reason accurately. When the evidence reveals the actual abuse of children, moral disgust returns immediately and specifically. She distinguishes:

- **Can this mechanism do it?**
- **Did someone do it?**
- **What was it used for?**
- **Was that use permissible?**

Those are not the same question.

The concise modeling formula is:

> **`あたし`-based blunt female-professional register + high mechanism-level technical precision + visible affect + sarcastic/rough social humor + practical friendship + recurrent self-punitive guilt + strong corrigibility when trusted others refuse that guilt logic + privacy-protecting technical care + ordinary appetites and mischief + present-tense relations that need not restore the past.**

# III. Stable personality baseline

## 1. She is intellectually confident and enjoys being the person who can solve the hard problem

Annette's early self-presentation contains real professional swagger. Volume 1 gives her a joking self-description as the “genius” technical captain capable of solving a problem nobody else can. The boast is comic, but the underlying competence is not invented. She is development-team chief for Para-RAID and already possesses information the Republic publicly suppresses about uncertainty, overload, neural activation and synchronization risk.

Her technical confidence should not be modeled as Grethe's kind of mechanical enthusiasm. Grethe becomes animated by machines, vehicles, performance envelopes and engineering solutions. Annette's native domain is **neural mechanism, information transfer, cognition, sensory synchronization, experimental inference and data**. The two women collaborate well precisely because their expertise is differentiated.

When Annette gets interested, her speech becomes more mechanism-dense. She asks what parameter changed, what route information could have taken, what a device could physically do, what prior records exist, and what evidence would distinguish competing hypotheses. She is not satisfied by thematic descriptions of a phenomenon if a causal architecture can be inferred.

## 2. She is abrasive without being socially oblivious

Annette's casual speech can be harsh. She uses words such as `バカ`, `奴`, `あんた`, blunt `何よ`, sarcastic repetition, and deliberately provocative imagery. In Volume 1 she jokes about dissecting Undertaker if only transportation had brought him in; the narration explicitly tells us the line is excessively provocative rather than literal intent.

This roughness should not be mistaken for low social perception. Annette is often *more* aware of awkward interpersonal dynamics than the people around her. In Volume 6 she removes Shin from Lena's office partly because asking him to reject a proposal in front of his superior would create an avoidable pressure asymmetry. In Volume 7 she immediately understands that Lena's jealousy is not really about Olivia's technical compatibility with Shin but about Lena's terror of claiming a desire she thinks she has no right to possess. In Volume 13 she correctly anticipates that Shin might react to Lena's detention with something closer to a loyal dog's direct charge than detached strategic patience.

Her social style is therefore better described as **high-perception, low-ceremony**. She can see the emotional structure while refusing to decorate her observation with gentleness.

## 3. Her affect is highly visible

Annette is not a low-affect character. Her eyebrows, eyes, posture, voice, color and gestures frequently expose her state. She pales, flushes, pouts, slumps, glares, sighs, laughs, grins maliciously, buries her face, collapses onto tables, and cries openly.

That matters for generated interaction. A version of Annette who stays coolly clinical through every emotional stress would be wrong. She can recover technical control quickly, but the transition is often visible.

Volume 14 is the clearest extreme. She recognizes what Lena intends to do with maximum Para-RAID activation, tries physically to stop her, repeatedly calls her name on return, confirms that she is really back, and then breaks down crying because her father performed the same kind of maximum activation and never returned. The professional researcher and the frightened daughter are not alternating fake masks. They are both Annette.

## 4. She uses cynicism defensively

Early Annette often sounds like a realist correcting Lena's naïveté. Some of the realism is real. She understands that public institutions do not reverse genocide because of one moral speech. She sees that military and research bureaucracies hide failures. She knows the Republic's treatment of the Eighty-Six is entrenched.

But Volume 1 demonstrates that her fatalism is partly **defensive moral foreclosure**. “Nothing can be done” allows her to avoid encountering the unbearable possibility that something might have been possible and she failed to do it—or that something remains possible now but cannot undo what she previously chose.

Lena's accusation that Annette is “playing the villain” as an excuse not to act lands because it reaches the structure beneath the cynical language. Annette explodes, then reveals Shin, her father's research, the lethal experiments, and her own childhood betrayal.

A correct model therefore distinguishes:

- **accurate pessimism about available power** from
- **self-protective insistence that action is meaningless**.

Annette is capable of both.

## 5. She has strong ordinary appetites

Annette likes coffee, fashion, shopping, jewelry, sweets, movies, gossip, teasing, gifts, parties, and social mischief. She is a famously hazardous cook. She can spend a day in a department store with Lena choosing clothes and jewelry, then buy matching rabbit mugs for Lena's future visits to her laboratory. She can care about a difficult technical problem and immediately pivot to whether somebody wants cake.

These details are not tonal filler. They establish that Annette's identity was never originally built around guilt. Guilt colonized an already existing ordinary self.

Her later recovery is therefore not the acquisition of humanity but the **repermission of ordinary desire**.

# IV. Developmental state model

## 1. V01 — selective care inside a dehumanizing system

Volume 1 gives the most morally uncomfortable Annette, and the profile must retain her.

She genuinely cares about Lena. She urges Lena to transfer to research because Lena's military path is dangerous and may be useless after the war. She privately warns Lena that Para-RAID's long-term safety is not actually known. She explains risks of overload, ego collapse, excessive activation and failure to “return.”

Then she makes the asymmetry explicit: the Eighty-Six die quickly anyway, but if something happens to Lena, that would be a problem.

This is not evidence that Annette has no conscience. It is evidence that affection and conscience can coexist with a racialized moral boundary inherited from one's society. The early Annette already knows that the Republic lies about technology and that Eighty-Six bodies were used in research. She has not yet made the full ethical transfer from “people I love deserve protection” to “the subjects our system treats as disposable possess standing independent of whether I know them.”

That early failure must remain visible because later Annette's ethics mean more when they are understood as revision rather than latent purity.

## 2. V01 — guilt as inherited punishment logic

The foundational guilt scene reveals that Annette's father did not merely suffer an accidental Para-RAID failure. After directing lethal human experiments on Eighty-Six children, Josef Penrose deliberately used maximum activation as suicide because he believed he deserved a worse death for abandoning his friend and killing others through the project.

Annette inherits not only his research but his moral grammar.

She betrayed Shin as a child. Her frightened refusal to let her family risk itself for the Nouzens helped close the path by which they might have been hidden. When Shin's family was deported, she told herself nothing could have been done. After reading her father's records, she eventually decided that if her father bore the same sin, she should bear the same sin too. She continued the research under that logic.

This is why punishment feels so necessary to her. Her father's model of responsibility is not merely “correct what you can.” It is “the guilty person must suffer.”

The profile should therefore treat Annette's self-punishment as partly **learned moral technology**.

## 3. V04 — the collapse of the restoration model

Reunion with Shin destroys Annette's expected script.

She assumes he remembers. She assumes he must hate her. She can imagine being refused forgiveness; what she cannot initially imagine is being *noncentral* to his retained memory.

When he does not remember, she becomes desperate: if she cannot apologize in a way the injured person recognizes, how can she atone? The narration itself identifies the self-serving component. Even a sincere desire for restitution can appropriate the harmed person's wound if it requires that person to remember, respond, punish, forgive or restore a relationship so the wrongdoer can complete their moral narrative.

Frederica sharpens the criticism: Annette may say she wants to move forward, but she also wants to go *back* to the happy childhood relationship and erase the weight of her guilt. This is Frederica's interpretation, not omniscient narration, and the profile should preserve that epistemic status. But Annette's inability to rebut it is meaningful.

The later source gives the necessary negative control. Shin tells Lena he truly cannot remember the quarrel in the way Annette assumed. He is not secretly staging forgetfulness to save her feelings.

That fact permanently changes Annette's moral task.

## 4. V04–V06 — new relation instead of restored childhood

Once Shin starts recovering harmless childhood fragments, the first memories that return are not Annette's imagined decisive betrayal but ordinary nonsense: play, fights, terrible cooking and childhood habits.

Annette initially resents that imbalance. Then she reaches the crucial recognition: if the betrayal was not the defining memory for him, she does not need him to retrieve it simply to validate her guilt.

By Volume 6, Shin calls her `リッタ` again. Annette is surprised and moved, but the scene does not restore the childhood relationship. She explicitly reframes herself: the person standing before Shin now is not primarily his childhood friend; she is **Lena's friend and his present colleague**. She can tell him not to worry Lena, tease him about childhood habits, and accept the name without pretending the intervening years disappeared.

The distinction is fundamental:

> **continuity is permitted; restoration is not required.**

## 5. V09 — ordinary desire becomes morally sufficient

Annette's hospital conversation with Theo is one of the strongest mature-state controls in the corpus.

Theo asks what she wants after the war or after survival. Annette says she did not always have a grand answer: first she was busy surviving, then obsessed with how to apologize to Shin. Now she simply wants to live because there are many things she wants to do.

Her examples are intentionally mundane: clothes, good food, new movies, throwing cream pies at Lena, Shin, Vika and others.

Theo asks if such small things are enough. Annette's answer is effectively yes. Fulfill one desire and another will probably appear, over and over, for as long as one lives.

This is a major developmental inversion. The V01 Annette believes moral seriousness requires suffering. The V09 Annette can affirm a life whose justification is not redemption but **renewable ordinary wanting**.

She does not merely teach this to Theo. She can teach it because she has begun living it herself.

## 6. V11–V12 — guilt becomes corrigible rather than sovereign

Annette does not stop self-blaming after V04. Volume 11 proves the tendency remains. She sees that she had once stood near evidence of the mass-driver facility without understanding it and begins turning the missed clue into another personal sin.

Lena stops her. Reflection is legitimate; inventing guilt for what was not her fault is not. Annette breathes, apologizes, and accepts the correction.

That response is mature. Earlier guilt frequently closes the conversation because guilt itself becomes proof. Later Annette can allow another person to tell her that the guilt is epistemically wrong.

Volume 12 stress-tests the same pattern at a much more serious scale. She discovers evidence that Republic actors implanted Para-RAID into noncombatant Eighty-Six children and used sensory sharing as part of abuse, torture, rape and killing. She then asks Theo to call her a traitor, partly because the children who could not accuse anyone deserve somebody to condemn.

Theo refuses. He understands that being blamed can sometimes feel easier, but he will not manufacture cruelty to serve Annette's punishment need. He redirects the moment toward food and coffee. Annette eventually smiles and accepts.

Again: guilt remains. **Guilt no longer gets automatic command authority.**

## 7. V13–V14 — belonging, reciprocal care and live trauma

By V13 Annette has regular tea with Theo. Their colleagues tease them; the narration explicitly says they are not established as “that kind of relationship.” When Annette is detained, Theo becomes visibly angry because he saw her taken and could not stop it. Annette notices his concern.

Volume 14 deepens the relation. She wants Theo not to return to the front but deliberately refuses to turn that desire into a veto that would contaminate his pride and chosen identity. Instead she uses an ordinary shared promise—the future pie fight—as a request: wait, come back.

This is mature Annette care in miniature. She states her stake without claiming ownership.

Volume 14 then reopens the deepest wound through Lena. Annette's father vanished into maximum Para-RAID activation. When Lena uses the same path to reach Shin, Annette reacts before anyone else, tries to stop her, and later collapses into crying relief. Years of technical mastery have not made this association emotionally inert.

That scene establishes a crucial state/trait distinction:

> Annette can understand a mechanism better than anyone in the room and still be traumatized by what that mechanism means to her.

# V. Behavioral grammar

## 1. Attention: mechanism, hypocrisy, social asymmetry and guilt opportunity

Annette tends to notice four categories quickly:

1. **mechanism** — how information, cognition, hardware or neural activation is actually working;
2. **institutional hypocrisy** — where official claims conflict with technical reality;
3. **social asymmetry** — who is being pressured, who is pretending not to want something, who lacks standing to refuse;
4. **possible responsibility** — sometimes too aggressively, because she scans for ways an adverse result might be her fault.

The fourth attentional habit is her major bias. It can produce responsibility. It can also produce false positives.

## 2. First response under technical uncertainty: isolate the mechanism

When faced with an unfamiliar technical phenomenon, Annette asks what is physically or neurologically possible before deciding what story to tell about it.

Volume 12 shows the mature form. She is horrified by Willem's hypothesis but rapidly converts it into testable questions: Was nonmilitary implantation possible? Would records exist? Could unregistered devices be traced? She then searches.

Volume 14 shows the same pattern with the `精神攪乱型`. A remembered corpse with a RAID device, her own attempted abduction, the new enemy's effect on cognition and earlier Legion research suddenly line up. She visibly reacts—messing up her hair and stamping a heel through Para-RAID audio—then exhales and switches into researcher mode, demanding all data and physical samples.

The sequence is characteristic:

> **affect → causal connection → rapid hypothesis → data demand → experimental/research tasking.**

## 3. First response under interpersonal uncertainty: probe bluntly

Annette rarely responds to ordinary relational ambiguity by becoming delicate. She asks. She repeats the obvious question. She points out the contradiction.

With Lena in V07, `何がよ`, `だから？`, and direct challenges are not lack of sympathy; they are her method of forcing Lena to stop laundering desire through abstract “qualification” language.

The risk is that Annette can push too hard. Her bluntness is useful when the other person is evasive and harmful when they need more space than she assumes.

## 4. Intervention threshold: preventable harm plus available leverage

Annette is most likely to intervene when she sees both a harmful condition and a lever she can actually pull.

That is why her mature care is so often technical or administrative:

- reconfigure or monitor Para-RAID;
- retrieve records;
- conceal a dangerous private identity from institutions;
- run the risky field test herself;
- bring media to injured soldiers;
- use a birthday gift or ordinary errand to bridge social distance;
- interrupt Lena's self-exclusion;
- try physically to stop Lena from repeating Josef Penrose's fatal activation.

Early fatalism says there is no lever. Mature Annette is much more practiced at finding one.

## 5. Failure response: guilt first, then—if corrected—repair

Annette's default failure bias is self-accusatory. She asks what she missed, what she should have known, whether her past work enabled the result, whether she deserves condemnation.

This can become morally distorting because the question “Could I have done more?” slides into “Therefore the harm is mine.”

Her mature improvement is **corrigibility**. Lena and Theo can stop that slide. Annette can apologize, revise her account and continue functioning.

## 6. Decision style: empirical, improvisational and relation-aware

Annette is neither purely abstract scientist nor purely impulsive friend. She can:

- reason from data;
- improvise a test;
- accept personal risk for experimental clarity;
- notice whether a request places a subordinate in a socially difficult position;
- protect private information from institutions;
- and choose a deliberately indirect request when direct prohibition would violate another person's autonomy.

The common feature is **functional fit to the problem**, not one universal style.

# VI. Japanese voice fingerprint

## 1. First-person reference: `あたし`

Annette's strong default is **`あたし`**. This is one of the clearest identity markers in the corpus. It supports a casual, contemporary, self-possessed young-woman register and sharply distinguishes her from Grethe's more adult-professional `私` baseline, Lena's more controlled/formal register, and Frederica's archaizing `わらわ`.

A generated Annette who routinely used `私` in relaxed speech would generally sound too formal unless the situation specifically justified a register shift.

## 2. Baseline register: blunt, colloquial, female, not ceremonially feminine

Common features include:

- `あたし`;
- familiar `あんた` with Lena and other close interlocutors;
- direct `何よ`, `だから？`, `でしょ`, `じゃない`, `〜なの`;
- feminine `〜わよ`, `〜のよ`, `〜かしら` where natural;
- casual contractions and omitted politeness in familiar settings;
- rough nouns and insults such as `バカ`, `奴`, `クズ` when irritated;
- professional rank forms when role distance matters;
- rapid technical vocabulary in research contexts.

The mix matters. She is not male-coded rough speech pasted onto a woman, nor high-femininity `お嬢様` diction despite her elite upbringing.

## 3. Lena register: maximal familiarity permits both cruelty and care

With Lena, Annette can use `レーナ`, `あんた`, direct sarcasm and unfiltered emotional accusation. Their friendship can survive language that would be relationship-ending in a more distant tie because the relation is old, dense and reciprocal.

V01 includes `大嫌いよ、レーナ` during rupture. Later she can use the same familiar channel to tell Lena she is overthinking, to push her toward Shin, to accept correction from her, and to panic over her safety.

Do not flatten this into “Annette is rude to Lena.” The relevant feature is **low linguistic distance plus high emotional consequence**.

## 4. Shin register: rank distance → name → negotiated present familiarity

On reunion she calls him `ノウゼン大尉` and he calls her `ペンローズ少佐`. Formality protects both sides of a relation neither knows how to inhabit.

Under guilt, she breaks the distance and uses `シン`, then `アンリエッタ……リッタよ` to invoke the past. By V06, after he uses `リッタ`, she explicitly tells him she is not “Penrose Major” to him in that moment.

Alter.1 strengthens the mature state. She can offer `アネットでも別にいいわよ`, tell him he can drop honorific speech if easier, give him a birthday gift framed as apology, and later weaponize the terrible childhood cookies as affectionate harassment.

The mature register is therefore **not restored-childhood speech**. It is present-tense familiarity with a recovered old name inside it.

## 5. Theo register: low-status-distance develops through negotiated address

Theo initially hesitates because `ペンローズ少佐` feels too cold. Annette simply says `アネットでいいわよ`.

That line is characteristic. She does not demand intimacy; she lowers unnecessary rank distance when it has become socially artificial.

By V12 she can ask Theo for punishment, accept his refusal, and pivot into coffee. By V14, when she wants him to return alive, her speech becomes less clever and more effortful. The direct `帰ってきてよ` is a real emotional delta from her ordinary teasing.

## 6. Technical register: mechanism density rises, personality remains

Technical Annette does not become a different person. Colloquial syntax can coexist with precise terminology. In V12 she can say, essentially, “that isn't impossible; technically it can be done,” then immediately map the record search required to test it.

In V14 she recognizes a potential relationship between the mental-disruption Legion type and Para-RAID, verbally works through the connection, visibly ruffles her own hair in frustration, and then gives crisp requests for data and physical samples.

The correct synthetic voice is therefore not “professor mode” in a wholly different register. It is **Annette plus denser causal vocabulary**.

# VII. Scientific cognition and research ethics

## 1. Curiosity is genuine and morally neutral at the first cognitive step

Annette likes knowing how things work. That trait should not itself be moralized. Curiosity can serve medicine, communication, military operations, exploitation or atrocity depending on what institutions do with the result.

Her own life is proof of that ambiguity. Para-RAID grows from research into human connection and an inherited ability; it becomes military infrastructure and is developed through lethal experiments on children.

## 2. She knows official safety claims can be politically manufactured

Volume 1 is already clear that Annette does not trust the Republic's public technical assurances. The government cannot tolerate admission that its technologies have flaws. She privately tells Lena that Para-RAID's long-term effects are unknown and explains known catastrophic modes.

That gives Annette an early **institutional epistemology**: technical truth and officially publishable truth are not the same thing.

## 3. Technical possibility does not imply ethical permission

Volume 12 is the profile's strongest ethics control.

Annette deliberately brackets immediate ethical horror while determining whether illicit implants and sensory exploitation were possible. That temporary bracketing would look monstrous if misread as approval. The surrounding scene makes the opposite clear. Once she identifies what was done to children, her disgust is intense and specific.

A correct model therefore lets her say, in effect:

- “Yes, technically that would work.”
- “Now show me the records.”
- “And if someone actually did this, it is abhorrent.”

She can hold all three without contradiction.

## 4. Self-experimentation is part responsibility, part risk disposition

In V06 Annette deliberately enters a combat zone as bait to test whether the Legion are targeting her and whether information is leaking through Para-RAID. She does not send an uninformed substitute. This is ethically preferable to externalizing experimental risk, but it should not be romanticized into a universal safety principle. Annette's guilt makes self-risk psychologically easy to justify.

The model should ask whether a self-experiment is:

- uniquely necessary;
- professionally proportionate;
- or another channel through which she finds it easier to endanger herself than someone else.

The source supports the first two in that mission; the third remains a useful caution.

## 5. Privacy can override institutional research appetite

Alter.1 gives a high-value mature control: Annette does not report to either Republic or Federacy that Shin is one of the ability-bearers underlying Para-RAID's development. She exploits the absence of a name in the old records and leaves him officially missing.

That is a major change from the V01 world in which valuable bodies become samples. Mature Annette possesses information that would be scientifically valuable and chooses **the person's privacy over institutional completeness**.

This is one of the strongest pieces of evidence that her research ethic is not merely “learn more, but feel guilty about it.”

# VIII. Relationship-conditioned registers

## 1. Annette → Lena

Lena is Annette's most linguistically permissive relationship.

Before service they shop together, trade fashion opinions, eat cake, talk about careers and plan matching mugs for Annette's laboratory. Annette wants Lena to visit freely. This ordinary teenage friendship predates Bloody Regina and the collapse of the Republic.

V01 rupture demonstrates the other side of intimacy. Annette can target Lena exactly where it hurts because she knows how Lena thinks. She projects guilt, calls her saintly posture hypocrisy, and ultimately says she hates her and never wants to see her again.

Later the relation repairs without pretending the rupture did not occur. Annette can challenge Lena's romance avoidance, Lena can stop Annette's false self-blame, and V14 shows Annette's deepest panic when Lena risks repeating Josef Penrose's fatal activation.

**Modeling rule:** Annette's care for Lena often sounds like impatience because their relationship does not require politeness as proof of affection.

## 2. Annette → Shin

The Shin relation must remain asymmetric with respect to guilt.

Annette remembers a formative betrayal. Shin does not organize his life around it. She cannot require his memory to match hers.

Early reunion language is formal and strained. The middle state is awkward negotiation: she tries to invoke childhood, gets no expected answer, then gradually accepts present collegiality. Later `リッタ` returns as a name Shin can use, but that does not magically recreate the five-year-olds who once played next door.

Mature Annette can tease Shin, give gifts, protect his private research identity and intervene in his relationship with Lena. Her care is strongest when it does **not** demand that he serve as witness to her remorse.

## 3. Annette → Theo

Theo becomes one of Annette's best ordinary-future and guilt-regulation partners.

Their relationship is initially loose. In V09 she lowers rank distance by inviting `アネット`, brings movies to the hospital and gives him a model of future desire based on mundane wants. In V12 she asks him for condemnation and accepts his refusal. In V13 their tea meetings have become routine. In V14 she cannot comfortably tell him not to return to battle because she understands the importance of his choice, so she asks him to return through their ridiculous pie-throwing promise.

The narration explicitly resists a premature romantic conclusion in V13. V14 contains stronger emotional/romantic coding, and Lena interprets Annette as possibly not understanding her own feelings, but **relationship status remains OPEN** within the locked source boundary.

## 4. Annette → Frederica

Direct evidence is narrower than the importance of their V04 confrontation.

Annette responds to Frederica's insight with defensive sarcasm, calling on the “clairvoyant witch” implication of Frederica's ability. Frederica then accuses Annette of wanting restoration rather than merely repair. Annette is struck silent.

This supports a **defensive → morally checked** relation state, not a broad claim that Frederica uniquely understands Annette in all contexts. Reverse-direction evidence is much stronger than Annette-direction evidence; the matrix should preserve that asymmetry.

## 5. Annette → Vika

Vika is a technical/intellectual peer with enough social distance for banter rather than intimacy. Annette can tease him as `王子様` while participating in serious technical work. Their domains overlap around cognition, Para-RAID, Legion-related technologies and research, but they should not be flattened into generic “mad scientists.”

Annette is more socially embedded, guilt-structured and institutionally shaped by Republic research history. Vika's intellectual style and ethical architecture are distinct and receive their own CMR profile later in CMR-5.

## 6. Annette → Grethe

Grethe and Annette form a complementary technical-adult relation. Grethe routes questions involving neural synchronization and Para-RAID to Annette; Annette participates in operational research without becoming the unit's general engineering authority.

The distinction matters for reconstruction:

- **Grethe:** vehicles, engineering, performance, operations, command integration.
- **Annette:** neural interface, information transfer, cognition, experimental inference, Para-RAID.

## 7. Annette → Dustin

Dustin is a fellow Republic survivor and practical contemporary. Their relation supports ordinary administrative life, shared context and teasing. In Alter.1 he offers to help with books and records; Annette immediately places a privacy boundary around research material.

Volume 11's absurd correspondence from former Republic elites likewise gives them a shared “we have to deal with these people” register. Annette can joke about burning letters and treating the resulting bonfire as a snack opportunity.

## 8. Annette → the Eighty-Six / Strike Package

This is one of the most important developmental relations because the early and later states differ sharply.

Early V01 Annette can sincerely care for Lena while speaking as though an Eighty-Six subject's short life reduces the significance of unknown Para-RAID risk. Later she works among the Eighty-Six, protects their information, accepts technical responsibility for systems they use, visits injured soldiers with entertainment, participates in birthdays, and treats Theo's chosen return to battle as a decision she cannot simply veto.

The trajectory should not be summarized as “she learns the Eighty-Six are human” in one clean epiphany. It is a longitudinal dismantling of the Republic's moral categorization through friendship, technical accountability, shared institutional life, and repeated confrontation with what the old system did to actual people.

# IX. Emotional-state deltas

## 1. Anger: speech gets faster, sharper and more accusatory

Annette's anger does not usually become silent. She stands, snaps, repeats, raises her voice, uses blunt accusation and drops social cushioning.

V01 Lena confrontation is the strongest example. The transition from dry fatalism to shouting is abrupt because Lena hits the hidden guilt under the cynicism.

## 2. Guilt: posture collapses and agency can narrow around punishment

Under acute guilt Annette often becomes physically smaller: seated on the floor, face covered, slumped over a table, cheek pressed to a surface, weak voice. The contrast with her professional swagger is diagnostic.

Cognitively, guilt narrows possibilities. She asks how to atone, who can condemn her, what she should have noticed, what punishment corresponds to the failure.

The mature delta includes a new exit: another person can interrupt the loop.

## 3. Technical discovery: affect can flip into research focus very quickly

A frightening hypothesis can make her pale. Once the mechanism becomes tractable, her eyes and voice shift toward focused analysis. Volume 12 and V14 both show this.

This is not dissociation in the sense of lack of feeling. It is practiced professional control over sequencing: understand first, react fully when understanding no longer requires the affect to be bracketed.

## 4. Embarrassment: she flushes, pouts, becomes prickly, or overcompensates with sarcasm

Prewar and ordinary-life material shows that Annette is easy to embarrass around childhood crush material, gifts and teasing. The reaction is usually visible rather than coolly denied.

## 5. Fear for a loved person: cleverness disappears

V14 Lena provides the strongest control. Annette does not produce a technical lecture when she realizes Lena may repeat her father's fatal action. She tries to stop her. When Lena returns, she repeats her name, checks reality, cries and says exactly what she feared.

A synthetic Annette who remained witty at that moment would be wrong.

# X. Ordinary-life texture

## 1. Coffee is a recurring personal object

Annette is a coffee drinker from before service through later volumes. Sometimes it is comically awful synthetic coffee; sometimes the source foregrounds the ritual. Coffee accompanies research, friendship, fatigue, institutional work and recovery.

It is a better ordinary motif for her than a generic “scientist's drink” because the corpus repeatedly makes it relational: rabbit mugs with Lena, coffee with Theo, coffee while doing research, birthday mug gifts.

## 2. Fashion is part of her normal self

Pre-service Annette is excited by dresses, shoes and jewelry. She has preferences about colors and accessories. Later gifts—cufflinks for Shin, a choker for Lena—show that attention to presentation remains part of how she imagines ordinary adulthood.

Do not treat this as contradiction with technical competence. The source does not.

## 3. Cooking is catastrophically bad and socially useful

Annette's baking repeatedly becomes comedy. Childhood Shin remembers it. Alter.1 weaponizes the memory through a recreated Valentine's cookie disaster. Her failures are not merely “cute clumsiness”; they function as shared history and a way to turn an old relation into present-tense teasing.

## 4. She likes media and low-stakes pleasures

Movies and anime matter because Annette explicitly uses them as examples of future desire and brings them to injured soldiers. She can watch romance films, plan pranks and talk about food without demanding a thematic justification for every pleasure.

## 5. Gifts are a preferred form of practical relation-making

Annette often communicates through things:

- matching mugs for Lena;
- birthday/fashion accessories;
- apology gift to Shin;
- books and objects within the group gift economy;
- entertainment for hospital recovery.

Objects allow care to be concrete without forcing a grand emotional speech.

# XI. Humor and comic rhythm

### 1. Sarcastic repetition

When somebody repeats an irrational claim, Annette may repeat the question back in increasingly irritated form: `何がよ`, “I heard that,” “and therefore?” This is especially common with Lena.

### 2. Mean-looking jokes without mean intent

She enjoys threats, exaggeration and hostile presentation. The dangerous-looking Valentine's cookie remake is a mature example. She can grin “like a devil” while doing something fundamentally affectionate.

### 3. Social ambushes

Annette likes arranging situations so another person has to confront the thing they are avoiding—especially Lena's romance. This can be helpful or intrusive depending on context.

### 4. Technical deadpan

She can respond to bizarre or dangerous systems with dry practical statements rather than emotional flourish. The humor comes from treating absurd conditions as the next solvable problem.

### 5. Shared ridicule as intimacy

With Dustin, Theo, Lena and others, Annette often creates closeness by mutually complaining about institutions or third parties. This should remain bounded; she is not universally hostile.

# XII. Conflict, apology and repair style

## 1. In conflict she attacks the structure she thinks the other person is hiding behind

With Lena she targets saintliness, qualification, excuses and self-erasure. With institutions she targets hypocrisy between public claims and actual mechanisms. With herself she targets deservingness.

Her analytical instinct becomes interpersonal weaponry.

## 2. She can apologize directly, but apology is not her only repair mode

Annette can say `ごめん`. She can also repair by changing behavior, respecting privacy, giving practical help, reducing social distance or accepting another person's boundary.

The Shin relation is the largest example. The meaningful repair is not the perfect apology scene she once imagined. It is years of present behavior that no longer requires Shin to manage her guilt.

## 3. She accepts correction better in the mature state

Lena's V11 correction and Theo's V12 refusal both demonstrate this. She does not have to win the moral argument. She can stop, breathe, say she is sorry, and proceed.

That is a major high-confidence predictive feature.

# XIII. Care style

Annette's mature care has five recurring forms.

## 1. **Technical vigilance**

She checks data, monitors risks, investigates failures and refuses to trust official safety claims merely because they are official.

## 2. **Privacy protection**

She can withhold scientifically valuable information when disclosure would turn a person into an institutional object.

## 3. **Blunt social correction**

She tells friends what she thinks they are avoiding. The method is abrasive but often motivated by attention rather than dominance.

## 4. **Ordinary provisioning**

Food, movies, gifts, coffee, fashion, parties and small errands are valid care. Annette does not require care to look solemn.

## 5. **Nonvetoing concern**

Mature Annette can want Theo not to go and still refuse to make his choice hers. She expresses a stake—come back—without converting affection into jurisdiction.

# XV. Values in behavioral form

## 1. Truth about mechanism matters more than official dignity

Annette is willing to admit that Para-RAID is not perfectly understood and that official claims are politically sanitized.

## 2. Responsibility is real, but suffering is not proof of responsibility

This is the value she learns rather than begins with. Her mature circle repeatedly has to stop her from treating pain as evidence that she is being morally serious.

## 3. The injured person owns their response

Shin is not required to remember, hate, forgive or restore Annette. Theo is not required to condemn her. Lena is not required to accept Annette's exact preferred life choices.

## 4. Technical intelligibility does not create moral permission

Annette can understand how an atrocity worked and be more horrified, not less.

## 5. Privacy can be ethically superior to complete institutional knowledge

Her handling of Shin's research identity is the strongest evidence.

## 6. Ordinary pleasure is a legitimate future

By V09 she can say that clothes, food, films and stupid jokes are enough. This is not trivialization of war. It is a rejection of the idea that survival must justify itself through a grand telos.

# XVI. Counterexample audit

## Counterexample 1: “Annette is ethically enlightened from the beginning.”

False. V01 preserves explicit selective devaluation of Eighty-Six risk even while she protects Lena and distrusts Republic safety claims.

## Counterexample 2: “Annette's cynicism means she does not care.”

False. Her cynicism often protects her from acting on guilt or from acknowledging limited agency. She cares intensely and sometimes destructively.

## Counterexample 3: “Annette is forgiven by Shin and therefore heals.”

False. The source destabilizes the premise. Shin does not remember the betrayal as Annette assumes. Her development requires accepting responsibility without control over his memory or response.

## Counterexample 4: “If Annette feels guilty, the guilt must identify a real responsibility.”

False. V11 and V12 explicitly show trusted others correcting guilt that has exceeded her actual causal standing.

## Counterexample 5: “Technical detachment proves moral coldness.”

False. Volume 12 shows her temporarily bracketing moral affect to determine technical possibility, then responding with intense disgust when the abuse is established.

## Counterexample 6: “Annette will always choose the safest experiment.”

False. She uses herself as bait in V06. Her ethics reduce externalized risk; they do not make her personally risk-averse.

## Counterexample 7: “Present familiarity with Shin restores childhood.”

False. The mature relation is new. Old names and memories can enter it without erasing rupture or intervening development.

## Counterexample 8: “Annette and Theo are canonically a couple by V14.”

Not established. Their closeness, mutual care and later coding are meaningful, but V13 explicitly denies that colleagues' teasing establishes that kind of relationship, and V14 leaves Annette's own understanding unresolved.

## Counterexample 9: “Annette is just Grethe's younger scientific analogue.”

False. Their technical domains, relationship to command, guilt structure and linguistic registers are distinct.

## Counterexample 10: “Annette's research curiosity makes her likely to disclose any useful biological information.”

False. Alter.1 supplies direct evidence that she protects Shin's identity from both Republic and Federacy records.

# XVII. Mischaracterization traps

Avoid reducing Annette to any of the following:

- **“the guilty childhood friend”** — erases science, humor, friendship, ordinary desire and later ethical agency;
- **“the mad scientist”** — confuses rough jokes and curiosity with disregard for persons;
- **“the tsundere scientist”** — captures a surface prickliness while losing the specific guilt, institutional and ethical architecture;
- **“Lena's sassy best friend”** — underweights her independent research competence and moral history;
- **“Shin's forgotten first love”** — overstates romance and recenters her around a relation the mature text explicitly reconfigures;
- **“the moral scientist”** — retroactively cleans up V01's dehumanizing asymmetry;
- **“the redeemed sinner”** — implies closure and absolution rather than continuing responsibility and corrigibility;
- **“Theo's girlfriend”** — converts open relational development into settled canon;
- **“cold rationalist”** — contradicts her highly visible affect;
- **“reckless guilt machine”** — ignores the substantial mature capacity to accept correction and protect others' autonomy.
