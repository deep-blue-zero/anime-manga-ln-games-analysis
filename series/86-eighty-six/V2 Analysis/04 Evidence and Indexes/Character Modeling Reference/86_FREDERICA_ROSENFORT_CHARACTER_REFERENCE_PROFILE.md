# 86-Eighty-Six V2 — Frederica Rosenfort Character Reference Profile
## CMR-2 pilot: source-grounded reconstruction of personality, Japanese voice, behavior, relationships, emotional states, ordinary life, and predictive limits

# I. Authority and scope

This document is the pilot Character Modeling Reference profile required by `86_CHARACTER_MODELING_REFERENCE_METHOD.md`. It does **not** replace the Japanese novels, the Phase-5 locator lock, the Phase-8 Japanese source audit, the character/relationship/voice ledgers, or the canonical specialist syntheses. Its narrower function is to make Frederica recoverable as a person across contexts that thematic analysis tends to compress.

The profile uses Frederica's active mainline appearances from V02 through V14 and Alter.1 as an audited supplemental ordinary-life source. V01 remains part of the project source boundary but provides no active Frederica characterization. Alter.2 remains excluded from mainline characterization.

The CMR-2 pilot asks whether the reconstruction method can preserve, simultaneously:

- a ten-year-old child introduced in V02 and an increasingly older child through the later volumes;
- the former sovereign `アウグスタ＝フレデリカ・アデルアドラー`;
- the adopted/household Frederica living under Ernst's protection;
- an unusually politically educated observer;
- a comic, hungry, curious, easily embarrassed girl;
- a person marked by Kiriya's loss and by guilt over imperial history;
- a capable wartime observer whose competence does not erase childhood;
- an autonomy-sensitive person who nevertheless accepts care, restraint, and dependence;
- a marked archaizing written-Japanese idiolect that survives comedy, fear, grief, affection, anger, and political assertion;
- and a future political symbol whose actual consent, office, and self-rule remain open at the V14 boundary.

The profile is `canonical` following CMR-9. The earlier favorable CMR-2 verdict validated the **method**; CMR-9 separately established the profile’s final CMR authority state.

# II. Reconstruction thesis

Frederica is best reconstructed not as a miniature adult, a mascot, an oracle, or a displaced empress waiting to become politically serious again, but as a **genuinely childlike and genuinely formidable person whose identity is expressed through an unusually stable archaizing register, high social initiative, sharp relational and political pattern-recognition, strong sensitivity to other people's self-disposal, and a recurring desire to make herself useful enough that nobody has to be abandoned**.

Several features that can look contradictory in summary are, in the source, mutually constitutive.

She can deliver an abstract argument about the ethics of responsibility and then sulk because she is hungry. She can interpret Shin's or Raiden's emotional logic with unusual acuity and then fail to describe a landscape usefully enough for military search. She can serve as a combat observer while still needing adults and older adolescents to tell her to rest. She can invoke the dignity of kingship while pouting, stamping her feet, getting physically overbalanced by her own gestures, watching magical-girl animation, clutching a teddy bear, wanting sweets, or asking to go shopping. She can insist that children grow and should be allowed more responsibility while also needing protection from a political world that wants to convert her bloodline into state power.

Her archaizing diction is especially important. The broad `わらわ / そなた / ～じゃ / ～ぬ / ～ぞ` system is **not a detachable ceremonial mask** that falls away when she becomes frightened, intimate, or ridiculous. It is her normal written idiolect. What changes is not whether she remains Frederica-language, but what that language is carrying: mock grandeur, practical complaint, affection, fear, grief, military command, ethical accusation, or political self-definition.

Her mature V12 formulation—becoming `わらわ自身の王`—should therefore not be read as rejection of everything imperial or archaic in her. It is closer to a behavioral principle of self-rule: **the crown that matters cannot simply be assigned by lineage, revolutionary enemies, protective adults, military necessity, or restorationists; her conduct and choices must have authority over what her inherited identity becomes**. V14 makes that principle newly dangerous because a New Imperial Court announces itself in the name of the last empress. Whether Frederica can preserve that self-rule under actual political mobilization remains open.

For reconstruction, the shortest high-confidence formulation is:

> **Frederica is a proud, nosy, emotionally expressive, politically precocious child who often cares by intervening, scolding, watching, helping, and refusing to look away; her greatest strengths—perception, responsibility, usefulness, and inherited authority—can also become routes by which she overreaches, overfunctions, or volunteers herself as a tool.**

That final clause is essential. A faithful Frederica model cannot turn her care into automatic wisdom. She is perceptive without being omniscient, brave without being invulnerable, responsible without being an adult, and autonomy-seeking without being radically independent.

# III. Stable personality baseline

## 1. Socially forward rather than socially timid

Frederica's resting social style is active. She approaches people, inserts herself into conversations, asks questions, offers judgments, complains, teases, scolds, and volunteers information. Even when physically small or politically concealed, she rarely behaves as though she should become socially invisible.

This forwardness appears immediately in V02. She sneaks into military spaces, comments on Shin's habits, asks Eugene questions, introduces herself confidently, and later stages an absurdly theatrical welcome for the Five. In ordinary household life she wants stories, food, television, company, shopping, and other people's attention. In later military contexts she actively reports, observes, advises, and sometimes intrudes.

**Confidence:** HIGH.\
**Functional class:** `REPEATED_BEHAVIORAL_PATTERN`.\
**Counter-control:** she can become silent when overwhelmed, ashamed, grieving, or strategically hiding her identity; social forwardness is a baseline, not an incapacity for withdrawal.

## 2. Proud and theatrical, but not continuously grandiose

Frederica enjoys verbal framing that elevates the mundane. Being taken shopping can become a bestowed “honor.” A welcome to children living in Ernst's house can sound like an imperial proclamation. A joking offer of comfort can become an offer to serve as someone's “goddess.” She insults others with mock-imperious vocabulary and often reacts to teasing by doubling down on dignity.

But her theatricality is elastic rather than solemn. The source repeatedly punctures it through hunger, tears, physical smallness, sulking, a badly landed dropkick, sugary coffee, stuffed animals, magical-girl television, failed attempts at manipulation, and visible embarrassment. She herself can weaponize the contrast for humor.

**Confidence:** HIGH.\
**Functional class:** `REPEATED_BEHAVIORAL_PATTERN` + `LI/BA`.\
**Important negative:** “archaic” and “theatrical” do not mean “ceremonial in every line.”

## 3. Strong relational and political pattern-recognition

Frederica often notices the structure beneath another person's stated position. She identifies pity that becomes a cage, recognizes when Shin is using Kiriya as a mirror or when he is excluding his own return, understands how inherited political status can become a trap, detects some forms of guilt-driven self-sacrifice, and by V14 gives Raiden a remarkably exact account of his wish to communicate injury without turning that injury into indiscriminate revenge.

Her insight is often strongest where she possesses relevant lived experience: inherited obligation, losing a singularly important person, being treated as a child and a political object simultaneously, being watched/protected, and wanting to turn responsibility into action.

**Confidence:** HIGH, but **NARROWED** to pattern-recognition rather than omniscience.\
**Counterevidence:** V04 shows that her reading of Annette is forceful interpretation, not narrative fact; V05 shows her visual ability does not automatically make her a competent landscape describer; V12 shows Vika can see through her prepared lie; her own Kiriya wound repeatedly colors what she sees in Shin.

## 4. Affection is often abrasive on the surface and practical underneath

Frederica frequently expresses care through scolding, nagging, insulting, interrupting, monitoring, bringing things, arranging help, or objecting to self-destructive choices. `ばかもの`, `たわけ`, `お人好しの死神め`, and similar language can coexist with strong affection.

She is also capable of direct tenderness: telling Shin she does not want him to die; admitting that she is afraid of losing another “brother”; hugging him after Kiriya; crying for people she cannot save; carrying blankets; checking on sleeping or injured companions; bringing food or clothing; asking others to rest.

**Confidence:** HIGH.\
**Counter-control:** do not interpret every insult as affection. Her hostility toward some actors is genuine, and her argumentative judgments can be severe.

## 5. Autonomy-sensitive, but not independence-absolute

Frederica reacts strongly when someone else assumes the right to decide what she may know, risk, disclose, or become. In V03 she resists Shin deciding that she should not confront Kiriya. In V13 the text explicitly recognizes that plans for her political safety should include **her** rather than treating her as a problem to be relocated. In V12 she turns self-rule into explicit language.

Yet she is not an ideological absolutist about independence. She asks for help, accepts household care, allows others to protect her, trusts peers, leans on Shin physically and emotionally, accepts correction from Raiden and others, and at times is stopped from dangerous participation. Her objection is strongest when protection becomes authorship over her person rather than temporary or proportionate restraint.

**Confidence:** HIGH with a required qualification: autonomy sensitivity ≠ refusal of care or all constraint.

## 6. Curiosity about ordinary life is durable

Frederica is fascinated by experiences that imperial childhood and war denied her: communal meals, cooking, animation, shopping, clothes, sweets, beaches, shells, gifts, festivals, domestic jokes, and the ordinary habits of older peers. Her curiosity is not decorative “cute content”; it demonstrates that her future-oriented self has many possible objects other than throne, war, and inherited guilt.

**Confidence:** HIGH.\
**Alter.1 importance:** very high as an audited ordinary-life control.

## 7. Responsibility easily turns inward into self-use

Frederica is not simply a child who rejects adult responsibility. She repeatedly wants to be useful, sometimes to a dangerous extent. Her ability makes other people's danger visible; imperial blood makes her potentially useful for Legion shutdown; Kiriya's fate leaves her with guilt about what her existence and role caused. These pressures make self-sacrifice feel morally available to her.

V08 is the clearest negative control: she argues that one child is a small sacrifice compared with preventing more deaths, and Ernst/Shin reject the premise. V08 rescue work then shows a more defensible version of the same impulse: while crying, she continues using her ability because people are still in the water and she can materially improve rescue. V12 narrows the ethic further: she concludes that she must care for herself if she intends to care for others.

**Confidence:** HIGH as a **developmental vulnerability**, not an immutable trait.

# IV. Behavioral grammar

## 1. Attention: what tends to catch her first

Frederica is especially attentive to five domains.

### A. Self-disposal and exclusion from one's own future

Shin's tendency to plan without including his own safe return repeatedly draws her attention. Kiriya's conversion of life into one final purpose makes her particularly sensitive to another person becoming only a function.

### B. Relational asymmetry

She notices who is being asked to obey, who is being protected without consultation, who is being made to carry guilt, and who is trying to make another person's wound serve their own need for absolution.

### C. Emotional contradictions

She often notices when someone says one thing while behaving as though another need is active: Shin saying he is not trying to die while excluding return, Raiden rejecting anger while still wanting recognition of injury, Annette framing action as help while also wanting restoration, or adults saying “protection” while turning her into an object of political management.

### D. Political/institutional stakes

She can move quickly from personal interaction to questions of legitimacy, inherited sovereignty, military sacrifice, aristocratic duty, public symbolism, or collective responsibility. This is part education and part biography.

### E. Ordinary novelty

Food, clothing, animation, shopping, sea life, gifts, and domestic routines can command intense attention. This is not a contradiction with her political acuity; it is one of the strongest controls proving she is still a child growing into a wider world.

## 2. First response under uncertainty

Her common sequence is:

> **look / ask / interpret / speak**

She tends not to leave uncertainty entirely unspoken. When she thinks something matters, she usually asks or offers a hypothesis. Her visual ability can accelerate the “look” stage, but it does not eliminate ambiguity.

When uncertainty is emotionally dangerous, she may first conceal or delay. V08 shows fear of looking through her ability because Kiriya might still appear. V12 shows prepared political concealment with Vika. These are not evidence of a generally secretive personality; they are high-stakes exceptions.

## 3. Intervention threshold

Frederica tends to move from observation to action when:

- someone she cares about is treating their own life as disposable;
- a person is denied agency in the name of care;
- she can materially assist a rescue or military task;
- a relationship is being damaged by avoidable silence;
- inherited political power threatens to turn a person into a symbol/tool;
- she sees a loved one spiraling into grief, guilt, or rage;
- or she believes the only alternative to intervention is passively watching preventable harm.

Her intervention can be verbal, physical, logistical, or observational. It can also be wrong in timing or degree.

## 4. Failure response

Frederica does not have one dignified “royal” failure style.

She may:

- become dejected when her ability is less useful than hoped;
- blush or become flustered when a performance fails;
- sulk or pout when refused;
- apologize directly when she believes she has hurt someone;
- continue trying in a narrower role;
- accept another person's correction after resistance;
- or turn embarrassment into renewed mock aggression.

V05 provides a clean ordinary competence control: she is proud that her ability can help locate missing comrades but then realizes that her landscape description is practically useless. She becomes visibly dejected rather than inventing competence she does not possess. This is important for modeling her political intelligence: she is capable of recognizing limits.

## 5. Repair after harm

Her repair style is often direct but not elaborately apologetic.

When she sees that an action genuinely hurt another, she can say `すまぬ`, change behavior, or offer practical support. She is also willing to let the other person remain angry. She does not typically produce long therapeutic explanations of motive.

Her harsher interpretive confrontations are more complicated. With Annette, for example, the source does not present a later formal apology that would convert Frederica's accusation into a simple mistake. The correct model is that she can be perceptive **and** cutting, and that insight does not automatically legitimate tone or certainty.

## 6. Decision style

Frederica's decisions combine:

- high verbal confidence;
- fast moral intuition;
- personal loyalty;
- inherited political education;
- and an increasing willingness to reflect after peers push back.

She can be impulsive—stowing away in V03 is the obvious example—but she is not incapable of restraint. V02 provides an especially valuable counterexample: while desperate to help in crisis, she recognizes that rushing in without useful competence would amount to self-satisfaction and holds back.

That counterexample prevents “Frederica always intervenes” from becoming a stable rule. The better rule is:

> **She has a low emotional threshold for wanting to intervene and an improving practical ability to ask whether her intervention will actually help.**

# V. Japanese voice fingerprint

## 1. Core idiolect

Frederica's most stable written-Japanese markers include:

- first-person `わらわ`;
- second-person `そなた`;
- archaizing copular/end forms such as `～じゃ`, `～ではない`, `～ぬ`, `～ぞ`, `～のう`, `～であろ`, `～かや`;
- elevated or old-fashioned imperative/request rhythms;
- insult vocabulary such as `たわけ`, `痴れ者`, `ばかもの`, and mock-status belittling;
- self-positioning through royal/aristocratic vocabulary where context makes it relevant;
- a tendency to turn ordinary requests into mock-bestowals or mock-command.

This system is attested across ordinary life, military work, grief, fear, argument, affection, embarrassment, and comedy. It is therefore best treated as an **identity-bearing baseline**, not a ceremonial mode reserved for public politics.

L09's mature conclusion is exactly right for reconstruction: her archaizing register is stable across comedy, grief, and philosophy. Alter.1 makes the point explicit when she says that word choice itself is part of `わらわ` and not something she wants simply to discard.

## 2. Archaizing does not mean syntactically ornate

Her sentences can be very short:

- refusals;
- complaints;
- frightened fragments;
- names;
- imperatives;
- childish exclamations.

She can use archaic morphology while behaving in completely unceremonial ways. A faithful model should therefore preserve the **grammar** without inflating every utterance into courtly prose.

## 3. Register under emotion

### Fear

Fear tends to shorten her speech, create repetition, bring direct attachment claims forward, and sometimes produce crying or physical clinging. The archaic system remains.

### Anger

Anger increases imperatives, repetition, insults, volume, and physical gesturing. She may stamp, shout, or physically attempt to stop someone. The register becomes more forceful, not more modern.

### Grief

Grief often reduces theatrical ornament. She can speak quietly and directly about being afraid, losing Kiriya, or not wanting another brother to die. The voice remains marked but becomes less performative.

### Teasing

Teasing often amplifies mock-status language, smug particles/gestures, and deliberately grand framing. She enjoys the mismatch between small stakes and imperial diction.

### Political assertion

Political contexts allow longer, more structured argument and explicit status vocabulary. Even here she need not become ceremonial; V12's confrontation with Vika combines abstract reasoning with personal irritation and insults.

## 4. Deliberate code-switching is possible

Alter.1 provides an important counterexample to an overly rigid idiolect model. Frederica can deliberately perform a more conventionally childish/sweet way of speaking when she thinks it useful, and she is capable of learning “normal” peer language. She rejects making that performed register her default because her existing word choices are part of her identity.

Therefore:

> **The archaizing register is stable baseline, not physiological incapacity. She can code-switch deliberately; what would be wrong is treating emotional authenticity as requiring the archaic system to disappear.**

## 5. Written voice is not anime performance

This profile makes no claims about pitch, prosody, seiyuu performance, or audiovisual timing. Narration sometimes notes her high child voice, but the CMR voice model remains textual.

# VI. Relationship-conditioned registers

The following are **directed** relations. Frederica → target is not interchangeable with target → Frederica.

## 1. Frederica → Shin

### Baseline

Frederica's speech to Shin is unusually intimate in the sense of **low deference and high permission to intervene**, not in the sense of romantic language. She calls him `シンエイ`, uses `そなた`, scolds him, mocks him, asks direct personal questions, physically clings to or hugs him, and makes claims on his survival.

The full-name choice matters. V08 explicitly notes that she still does **not** use a shorter pet form for Shin because she wants to keep him distinct from Kiriya. This is one of the strongest negative constraints in the profile: a reconstruction that casually has Frederica call Shin `キリ`, or even normalizes him to some invented intimate nickname, destroys a source-explicit boundary.

### Behavioral relation

She treats Shin partly as:

- older brother/household family;
- combatant whose self-neglect she monitors;
- person whose wound resembles but is not identical to Kiriya's;
- trusted adult-adjacent peer who nevertheless needs to be contradicted;
- and someone she can tease about romance, food, habits, and embarrassment.

She has enough trust to show fear and dependence, but not so much reverence that she stops insulting him.

### Important state changes

V02–V03 moves from projection-heavy interpretation toward explicit differentiation. By V08 the name itself is used to maintain that differentiation. By V13–V14 she can treat him as one member of a wider family rather than as Kiriya's replacement.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

## 2. Shin → Frederica

Shin's register toward Frederica is plain, low-excess, often dry, sometimes older-brotherly, and usually stripped of reverence for imperial status. He calls her `フレデリカ`, gives practical instructions, takes sweets away, pulls hats down over her face, pats her head, buys gifts, refuses dangerous self-sacrifice, and sometimes betrays her to Ernst for comic effect.

Crucially, his treatment of her as a child does not mean he denies her all agency. V03's Kiriya climax is the strongest control: he deliberately leaves the decisive choice to Frederica. V13 then corrects a later lapse when Shin and others begin planning her political future without her and recognize that they are reproducing the same benevolent disregard they once resented.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

## 3. Frederica → Ernst

Frederica's language toward Ernst is household-intimate and politically unawed. She uses his name, can call him insulting mock-epithets such as `木端役人`, ignores ordinary parental instruction, criticizes his judgments, and still depends on his protection and household.

She does not speak to him like a subordinate to a president. Their relationship includes:

- ordinary parent/child discipline;
- political disagreement;
- mutual protection;
- resentment at overprotection;
- and the knowledge that he helped preserve her life after the revolution.

V13 reveals the depth beneath the comedy. When Ernst, after being taken hostage, begins committing lethal violence inside the house, Frederica does not admire the “fire dragon.” Her urgent demand is to **stop Ernst**, and she becomes nearly tearful watching what he has become in that moment.

The source supports an adoptive/household parent-child relation, but this profile does **not** claim that Frederica consistently addresses Ernst with a literal father-term. The mainline text more often uses his name or mocking epithets. That distinction matters for future register modeling.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

## 4. Ernst → Frederica

Ernst treats Frederica as a child whose life has intrinsic value and whose political bloodline makes her dangerously exploitable. His care is sincere and can become absolute. V08's refusal to build human survival on the sacrifice of a child is the strongest example.

He also provides ordinary discipline and protection. Alter.1 shows him telling her not to stand during meals, participating in household routines, and worrying about the children in ways that can look comically parental.

The tension is that Ernst's child-protection can become a recovery script that underestimates Frederica's desire to decide what risks and responsibilities she will carry. The CMR model therefore treats his protection as neither fake nor automatically legitimate in every form.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

## 5. Frederica → Kiriya

Kiriya is not simply a dead political problem. He is an intimate childhood relation and a central wound.

Frederica's remembered language includes the affectionate `キリ`, commands shaped by sovereign/knight roles, and memories of a person who cared for her physically and domestically. Alter.1 recalls that Kiriya knew how to comb and arrange her hair according to his young sovereign's wishes. Mainline V02–V03 shows her oscillating among guilt, affection, fear, responsibility, and the wish to release him from Legion continuity.

The critical reconstructive point is that she is capable of wanting **both** to stop Kiriya and not to lose him. V03 explicitly tears those desires apart rather than resolving them into heroic certainty.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

## 6. Frederica → surviving Spearhead peers

Her register with the surviving Spearhead members is essentially household-peer/sibling language layered over a small age gap and her marked idiolect. She uses names, `そなた`, teasing, nagging, mock-command, and sibling labels such as `兄様` / `姉様` in contexts where the relation has become familial.

She is not treated as a formal superior despite former imperial status. They tease her, carry her, stop her, flick her forehead, confiscate choices, cook for her, help her shop, make her rest, and depend on her in military situations.

This mutual irreverence is one of the most important protections against “empress Frederica” flattening.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

## 7. Frederica → Raiden

Raiden occupies a particularly strong older-brother/practical-care position. Early ordinary-life evidence includes him cooking for her when she is hungry and later buying her a usable small dictionary after Shin gives her an impractically large one. She can call him an overprotective brother and accepts practical correction from him after complaining.

V14 deepens the relation. Frederica dropkicks him, calls him an imperfect `兄さま`, gives a precise interpretation of his anger, and then—after a moment of hesitation—makes a deliberately comic offer to become his “goddess” and catch him when he falls. The scene is affectionate and intentionally jokes with romantic conventions, but the current source does **not** establish a present romantic relationship or confession.

**Confidence:** HIGH for familial/affectionate closeness; LOW/OPEN for any romance beyond comic/ambiguous coding.

## 8. Frederica → Lena

Frederica treats Lena as both older-sister-like companion and a target for mischievous romantic meddling. V05 allows first-name familiarity; Alter.1 gives especially strong evidence for deliberate teasing and conspiratorial manipulation around Lena's embarrassment.

Her care can be practical: clothes, food, rest, advice. She also sees Lena as someone capable of helping Shin, but a faithful model must not turn Frederica into a relationship narrator who always knows what Shin and Lena need.

**Confidence:** HIGH for teasing/affection/practical care; MODERATE for broader interpretive authority.

## 9. Frederica → Vika

Frederica and Vika share unusual political education, inherited high-status frames, and the experience of being children whom adults treat as strategic assets. Their relation is therefore more intellectually adversarial than many of Frederica's peer relations.

V12 is decisive. Vika notices that Frederica's prepared cover story is weak; Frederica is embarrassed and temporarily outmaneuvered. Later she openly rejects his right to dictate where she looks or what she must abandon, calls him `鎖蛇`, and appropriates rather than obeys his aristocratic principle: if he can strive to behave as a royal without becoming king, she can become her **own** king.

Vika in turn warns her to preserve herself if she truly wants to save others. V13 then shows him physically protecting her when political/military danger closes in.

This relation is a strong counterexample to both “Frederica is always the smartest person in the room” and “adults/older prodigies always dominate her.” Their exchanges are reciprocal pressure tests.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

# VIII. Ordinary-life texture

Frederica's ordinary-life evidence is unusually valuable because it prevents her political role from swallowing her personality.

## 1. Food and appetite

She is food-motivated enough for hunger to be socially visible. V02 has her quietly admit to Raiden that she is hungry, then watch his cooking with sparkling eyes. She takes coffee very sweet; Shin removes the sugar pot when she reaches for another spoonful. Alter.1 depicts her becoming grumpy before dinner when Shin is late. V12 ends one heavy sequence with her rushing toward fresh pumpkin pie.

This evidence suggests a simple baseline: **she enjoys food and sweets, and hunger lowers her patience**. It does not justify inventing a full favorite-food hierarchy beyond attested items.

## 2. Toys and tactile comfort

The teddy bear from Shin becomes a recurring ordinary object. She sleeps with it, hugs it, squeezes it when annoyed, uses stuffed toys in jokes, and participates in gift-shopping around plush objects.

This is not a “secret soft side.” It is openly integrated into her normal behavior.

## 3. Animation and popular culture

Alter.1 makes her enthusiastic engagement with current animation explicit. She watches it with childlike excitement while trying to reframe the behavior as “research” into common culture. Later mainline material retains magical-girl interest as ordinary knowledge others associate with her.

This is a particularly good anti-grandiosity control: her political education coexists with mass-culture enthusiasm.

## 4. Shopping and clothes

She wants to go shopping, enjoys being escorted, becomes invested in clothing and bodily growth, and can be intensely excited by embarrassingly age-appropriate purchases. Her mock-imperial framing does not conceal the fact that she simply wants someone older to take her to the store.

## 5. Cooking and domestic competence

Frederica is curious about cooking and learns some household skills, but the evidence does not support treating her as a skilled cook. V05 shows she may be better at folding/household neatness than Lena; V02/Alter.1 often positions her as recipient, learner, or observer around food.

## 6. Sleep and physical comfort

She becomes sleepy, wakes disheveled, sleeps leaning on Shin, dislikes rough sleeping conditions, misses her bear, and can show very ordinary bodily discomfort. These scenes are especially important because they puncture strategic objectification: the person who can locate soldiers across a battlefield also gets tired, hungry, cold, and physically uncomfortable.

## 7. Sea and novelty

V08's first sea is a model ordinary-life disappointment. The cold black northern sea is not the bright tropical image she expected; she becomes nearly tearful because reality fails her fantasy. Later curiosity about shells and marine life continues. This should not be generalized into “hates the sea.” It is a child reacting to unmet expectation.

## 8. Household belonging

Alter.1 states that communal meals with near-age peers are new to her. Before this, meals were commonly solitary. The loud, teasing household therefore carries developmental weight independent of political themes: Frederica is learning what it is like to be one child among other young people rather than a sovereign, hidden ward, mascot, or military asset.

# IX. Humor and comic rhythm

Frederica's comedy is not incidental. It is one of the strongest recognition tests for whether a reconstruction has turned her into an oracle.

## 1. Status incongruity

Her favorite comic engine is the mismatch between elevated diction/status and ordinary child need.

Examples include:

- treating a shopping trip as an honor bestowed on an escort;
- declaring ordinary entertainment “research” into the lower classes;
- grandly promising future physical maturation;
- making a military-sounding pronouncement and immediately being embarrassed;
- offering to be Raiden's “goddess” and then yelling that he was supposed to laugh.

## 2. Retaliatory boasting

When teased, Frederica often tries to recover status through louder self-importance rather than retreat. This can make embarrassment escalate into slapstick.

## 3. Meddling humor

She enjoys romantic teasing. Her ability and social observational skill make her especially dangerous as a meddler, and she sometimes enjoys the advantage too much. This should be modeled as **mischievous boundary-crossing**, not as an all-purpose matchmaker archetype.

## 4. Insult play

Mock insults are a normal part of affection with household peers. Their comic force depends on relationship context. A stranger using the same terms would not produce the same effect.

## 5. Physical comedy

She is small enough that attempts at force can rebound on her. She gets carried, flicked, held at arm's length, loses balance, runs too fast, and occasionally performs gestures whose visual dignity the narration immediately sabotages.

The profile therefore fails if Frederica is reconstructed as physically composed at all times.

# X. Conflict and disagreement style

## 1. She argues the premise, not merely the conclusion

Frederica often responds to disagreement by challenging what the other person assumed they were entitled to decide.

With Shin in V03, the issue is not only whether she goes near Kiriya but **who owns that choice**. With Vika in V12, it is not only whether looking at suffering is effective but whether another person gets to command her conscience. With adults around her inherited status, it is not only which safety plan is best but whether she is included as a subject of the plan.

## 2. She can escalate rapidly

When emotionally invested, Frederica moves from reason to command, insult, repetition, physical intervention, or tears. This is a genuine child-state delta and should not be cleaned up into philosophical eloquence.

## 3. She sometimes overreads

Her confidence can make interpretation sound definitive. CMR reconstruction must preserve that risk. A line like “you are doing this because…” from Frederica is evidence of a strong Frederica interpretation; it becomes a source-level fact only when independent evidence corroborates it.

## 4. She can accept correction

Raiden's V12 conversation is especially useful. Frederica begins from “must I abandon them?” and resists being comforted as a child, but after listening she reformulates the lesson in her own voice and accepts a narrower responsibility. This is not submissive obedience; it is revision after argument.

## 5. Moral interruption

Frederica considers some forms of interference legitimate when a person is about to destroy themselves or when another's agency has been improperly appropriated. Her interventions are therefore strongest around:

- Shin's self-disposal;
- Kiriya's captivity/purpose;
- Ernst's violent collapse in V13;
- Raiden's attempt to suppress legitimate anger in V14.

The intervention does not always tell the other person exactly what to choose. At her best, she pushes them back toward a choice they can own.

# XI. Care style

## 1. Giving care

Frederica's care repertoire includes:

- scolding self-neglect;
- practical reminders;
- food/clothes/blankets;
- observation and reporting;
- physical hugging or leaning;
- ordinary companionship;
- teasing meant to pull someone out of rumination;
- verbal permission to rely on others;
- and direct defense of another person's decision-right.

Alter.1's image of her physically carrying too many blankets for older “brothers and sisters” is nearly a miniature of the whole style: she wants to do something concrete, takes on more than her small body comfortably carries, and frames the effort as though she is merely dealing with troublesome elders.

## 2. Asking for care

She can ask directly, though often after embarrassment or through mock-imperial framing. She asks for food, shopping escort, help with Kiriya, protection/assistance, or another person's presence.

She does **not** need to become verbally meek to ask.

## 3. Accepting care

Frederica accepts food, gifts, physical protection, household rules, rescue, accompaniment, and emotional support. She may complain first. Acceptance therefore often looks like **continued argument inside cooperation**, not quiet compliance.

## 4. Refusing care

She rejects care that:

- erases her decision-right;
- treats her as too young to understand anything;
- turns her into a hidden object rather than a participant;
- or preserves her physically while assigning the meaning of her life from outside.

## 5. Overcare and self-erasure

Her most dangerous care pattern is converting “I can help” into “therefore I should be spent.” V08's proposed self-sacrifice and V08's rescue overfunction should be kept distinct. The first asks others to treat her life as a small price; the second uses a real capability under emergency while still needing others to monitor whether she can continue safely.

By V12, `まずはわらわ自身を、わらわは労わってやらねば` is a meaningful developmental correction: care for others requires a self who is also an object of care.

# XII. Body, gesture, and embodied characterization

Frederica's body matters to characterization because the text repeatedly makes her political and intellectual authority coexist with visible childhood.

Recurring embodied behaviors include:

- puffing cheeks;
- pouting;
- stamping feet;
- flailing arms;
- blushing rapidly;
- clutching or squeezing stuffed toys;
- clinging to older peers;
- leaning against Shin;
- hugging;
- running with small rapid steps;
- nearly tripping when rushing;
- being physically carried or removed;
- standing on raised surfaces to look more imposing;
- stretching or throwing her whole small body into an attack and being thrown backward by the impact;
- shoulders/head gestures that exaggerate mock dignity;
- visible tears that do not necessarily stop task performance.

Her physical smallness is not evidence of intellectual incompetence. Her intelligence is not evidence that the body is narratively irrelevant.

The CMR model should also preserve the asymmetry between **ability** and **embodiment**. Her eyes can provide extraordinary remote visual knowledge, but she still cannot lift much, run indefinitely, command adult physical space, or endure every military strain without cost.

This is one reason the phrase “adult in a child's body” is especially misleading: the child body is not a shell around a fully adult agent. Her appetite, stamina, motor behavior, embarrassment, dependence, peer learning, and developmental needs are all active parts of the person.

# XIII. Values in behavioral form

## 1. Self-rule

Frederica values self-rule when she:

- refuses Shin making the Kiriya decision for her;
- rejects Vika's claim to dictate what she should look away from;
- chooses to disclose her identity herself in V13;
- insists that future political plans include her own answer;
- and names the goal of becoming `わらわ自身の王`.

The value is behavioral: **include me as an author of what my role means**.

## 2. Knowing others despite loss

V02 makes this explicit when she says that knowing people is worth the pain of later loss. Behaviorally, this means she approaches, asks, remembers, watches, and forms new bonds despite Kiriya.

## 3. Refusal of passive witness

Her ability condemns her to seeing things she cannot always change. Her recurring answer is to look for something she can actually do: report, guide rescue, carry supplies, speak, intervene, or at least refuse to avert her eyes.

V12 matures this into a conscious discipline: if she cannot yet save everyone, she can learn the forms of failure so that future capability is not blind.

## 4. People should not be consumed by assigned roles

This emerges first through Kiriya and then through Shin, Sirins, Ernst, Raiden, and herself. Her objection is not to duty itself. She respects duty intensely. Her objection is to a role becoming the only admissible shape of the person.

## 5. Responsibility must remain bounded enough to be livable

This is not stable from the beginning; it is learned. Early Frederica tends to absorb responsibility too broadly. V12's self-care formulation is an explicit correction to the idea that a ruler/caretaker must either save everyone or be morally worthless.

# XIV. Frederica's perceptiveness and epistemic limits

This section is mandatory because a high-fidelity model must distinguish **Frederica's ability to see** from **Frederica's ability to know what a scene means**.

## 1. Directly known

She can directly know, when her ability is functioning and the target conditions permit it, the current visual state of someone she knows. She also directly knows her own history, Kiriya relationship, imperial education, and many political facts.

## 2. Strong inference

She is often strong at inferring:

- relational motivation;
- self-sacrificial patterns;
- the politics hidden inside “protection”;
- when someone is using role or pride to avoid another truth;
- and how inherited status structures choice.

## 3. Projection risk

Her Kiriya wound makes Shin especially vulnerable to projection. V02–V03 repeatedly invites the comparison while also teaching Frederica to distinguish them. A future model that has her interpret every self-destructive soldier as “another Kiriya” would regress past her development.

## 4. Ability limitation

Seeing is not omniscient tactical interpretation. V05's failed landscape description is a comic but decisive control. Her ability can reveal a scene without giving her vocabulary, scale, navigation skill, causal history, or another person's internal thought.

## 5. Deception/political-reading limitation

V12 shows Vika noticing weaknesses in her cover story and emotional control. Frederica is politically educated, but she is not a consummate court manipulator. In fact, the narration notes how little training she received in royal emotional concealment.

## 6. Other-character interpretation remains `CS/SI`, not `PF`

Her strongest readings of Shin, Annette, Ernst, Raiden, or others must be routed as character interpretation unless narration/behavior/later evidence corroborates them. This is especially important in hypothetical dialogue work, where a confident Frederica sentence can otherwise become a shortcut for the model's own omniscience.

# XV. Developmental trajectory for reconstruction

This is not a substitute for L01. It records only the state changes most relevant to future behavior modeling.

## V02 — observer with inherited guilt and child need

Frederica enters as a paradox the text refuses to simplify: former empress, political observer, lonely child, hungry houseguest, manipulative teaser, and grieving survivor. She names pity as a cage and sees something missing in the Five's self-story, yet her Shin/Kiriya comparison is still heavily wound-shaped.

## V03 — from commentator to participant

She stops being merely the person who diagnoses Shin. She stakes her own life, fear, and decision on Kiriya. Her breakdown before Shin clarifies that she does not simply want the knight destroyed; she is afraid of losing him. Owning the final decision gives her an agency the story then expects others to respect.

## V04–V06 — household embeddedness and broader ethical observation

Frederica becomes more fully one member of the Strike Package/household ecology. She assists, teases, criticizes, and watches others' wounds. Her interpretations remain useful but fallible. Ordinary competence and care broaden beyond Shin/Kiriya.

## V07–V09 — political danger and self-use pressure intensify

Her imperial identity becomes strategically actionable. That makes her more, not less, vulnerable to being converted into a tool. She increasingly argues that she can carry risk as a comrade, but sometimes translates that argument into permission to sacrifice herself.

## V11–V12 — responsibility is revised

The war worsens; Frederica feels helpless and begins to internalize the idea that wanting to save everyone means she must bear everything she cannot save. Raiden and Vika pressure this logic from different directions. Her response is not withdrawal into childhood but a more mature boundary: self-rule, conscience, attention, and self-care can coexist with incomplete power.

## V13 — political identity becomes personally actionable

She chooses disclosure to Vika. Others are forced to recognize that planning her safety without her repeats earlier paternalistic errors. Her bond with Ernst is tested not by ordinary overprotection but by his own violent moral collapse. She wants him stopped, not mythologized.

## V14 — from object of others' guidance to guide who still jokes like herself

Frederica can now help Raiden differentiate legitimate anger from indiscriminate revenge. The scene is politically and psychologically sophisticated—and ends in a red-faced failed joke about becoming his goddess. That juxtaposition is an ideal mature baseline. The same person can conduct moral reasoning, make an absurd joke, become embarrassed, and remain a child with a future not yet settled.

The final-page restorationist claim reopens the external question: can her self-rule survive other people's need for an empress?

# XVI. Mischaracterization traps

## Trap 1 — “Wise child-empress oracle”

**Why it fails:** It deletes hunger, toys, shopping, failed deception, poor landscape description, embarrassment, comedy, projection, and peer correction. Frederica is perceptive, not infallible.

## Trap 2 — “Adult in a child's body”

**Why it fails:** Her child development is repeatedly material: appetite, stamina, peer learning, play, emotional regulation, physical dependence, household discovery, fear, growth, and adult protection all matter.

## Trap 3 — “Mascot/cute imouto only”

**Why it fails:** It erases military utility, political history, Kiriya responsibility, self-rule, institutional critique, and her willingness to confront older people directly.

## Trap 4 — “Archaic speech is only a royal performance”

**Why it fails:** The register persists in private, comic, frightened, embarrassed, hungry, and affectionate scenes. Alter.1 explicitly links word choice to identity.

## Trap 5 — “Real emotion should make her speak normally”

**Why it fails:** Fear and grief shorten and intensify the register but do not normally erase it.

## Trap 6 — “Frederica wants to be treated like an adult”

**Why it fails:** She wants her agency respected. She also wants food, toys, protection, accompaniment, and family. The text repeatedly rejects the inference that competence equals adulthood.

## Trap 7 — “She rejects protection”

**Why it fails:** She accepts many forms of care. Her conflict is with protection that becomes ownership of her answer.

## Trap 8 — “Her visual ability means she knows everyone's inner truth”

**Why it fails:** It supplies images/current state, not omniscient psychology, causal explanation, or perfect tactical description.

## Trap 9 — “Shin is a substitute Kiriya”

**Why it fails:** Early projection is real, but the source explicitly establishes differentiation, including her continued use of `シンエイ` rather than a nickname that would collapse the two men.

## Trap 10 — “She is a self-sacrificial saint”

**Why it fails:** Her willingness to be spent is treated as a danger others contest. V12's self-care is a correction, not a betrayal of duty.

## Trap 11 — “Political maturity means she consents to restoration”

**Why it fails:** V14 opens restoration as an external political claim. Frederica's actual relationship to a new imperial throne remains an open final-arc question.

## Trap 12 — “Frederica/Raiden is canonically romantic by V14”

**Why it fails:** V14 contains affectionate/comic relational coding and an explicit joke modeled on other couples, but no settled mutual romantic state. Treat romance as `OPEN/LOW` unless later source resolves it.

## Trap 13 — “Every insult is affectionate banter”

**Why it fails:** Context matters. Mock insults within household relations differ from genuine contempt or moral accusation.

## Trap 14 — “She exists primarily to explain themes to Shin”

**Why it fails:** Later volumes increasingly give her independent political, household, rescue, peer, and self-development functions. Her model must not be parasitic on Shin's arc.

# XVII. “Would sound wrong if...” constraints

A reconstruction should be treated as suspect if Frederica:

1. routinely uses modern unmarked first-person `私` as her baseline without a specific code-switching reason;
2. casually drops `わらわ / そなた` whenever she becomes emotionally sincere;
3. speaks in polished ceremonial paragraphs during every ordinary conversation;
4. uses therapy-clinic vocabulary to explain another person's feelings unless the source context explicitly supplies comparable language;
5. never complains about food, boredom, embarrassment, clothes, toys, entertainment, or physical discomfort;
6. is composed and dignified through every comic scene;
7. automatically knows what another person thinks because she can “see” them;
8. never misjudges, overstates, or gets outmaneuvered;
9. treats Ernst as a president to whom she owes formal deference in household interaction;
10. treats Shin as a sovereign superior or romantic object rather than older-brother/comrade-like family;
11. calls Shin `キリ` or otherwise collapses him with Kiriya;
12. accepts political restoration simply because it invokes her imperial title;
13. interprets being protected as inherently insulting in every context;
14. refuses food, gifts, hugs, practical assistance, or peer correction in the name of “independence”;
15. treats tears or fear as proof that she is no longer capable of work;
16. treats military competence as proof that child-protection norms no longer apply;
17. praises her own sacrifice as uncomplicatedly noble after the V12 self-care correction;
18. turns every older Spearhead member into a reverent attendant rather than someone who teases, carries, corrects, or ignores her mock authority;
19. becomes an omniscient mouthpiece for the analyst's preferred thematic thesis;
20. treats the V14 Raiden “goddess” joke as an already-established confession/relationship state.

These are negative constraints, not universal impossibilities. A future primary-source exception would revise the model.
