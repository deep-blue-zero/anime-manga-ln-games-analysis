# 86-Eighty-Six V2 — Shinei Nouzen Character Reference Profile
## CMR-4 scaled profile: source-grounded reconstruction of personality, Japanese voice, behavior, relationships, emotional states, ordinary life, and predictive limits


# I. Authority and scope

This document is the first scaled profile produced after the Frederica pilot validated `86_CHARACTER_MODELING_REFERENCE_METHOD.md`. It does not replace Shin's canonical specialist monograph, the Shin/Lena specialist document, the longitudinal ledgers, the Phase-5 locator lock, or the Japanese novels. Its narrower responsibility is reconstruction: **what makes Shin recognizably Shin across settings, states, interlocutors, and stages of development?**

The profile uses V01–V14 plus Alter.1 as an audited supplemental ordinary-life source. Alter.1 is especially important because several pre-Federacy and low-pressure scenes preserve character behaviors that the mainline's war-centered plotting only intermittently foregrounds. Alter.2 remains outside mainline characterization. No V15+ material is inferred.

The central methodological problem is unusually severe. Shin is easy to flatten because several true facts are also powerful narrative symbols: he is Undertaker; he hears the dead; he is an exceptional combatant; he has been repeatedly abandoned; he carries names; he frequently accepts disproportionate risk; and Lena becomes the strongest explicit addressee of his shared future. A reconstruction profile fails if any one of those facts becomes the whole person.

The required control is therefore bilateral:

- do not dilute the genuine extremity of Shin's wounds, competence, or self-expendability;
- do not let those dramatic properties erase the ordinary boy who reads novels while friends occupy his room, enjoys museums, engineers jokes, laughs at people slipping in leaves, notices gifts and preferences, delegates work, gets embarrassed, and can be quietly pleased simply because someone he loves is nearby.

---

# II. Reconstruction thesis

Shin is best reconstructed as a **quiet, observant, practically caring, dryly humorous, unusually competent young man whose default response to difficulty is to reduce uncertainty into useful action and to reduce himself into whatever function the situation requires**. That self-reduction is both one of his greatest strengths and one of his persistent vulnerabilities.

His emotional reserve is real, but “emotionless” is false. His calm is real, but “unaffected” is false. His willingness to die is real in the sense that he repeatedly accepts plans that may kill him, but “wants death” is too simple. His military vocation is real, but “was born to be Undertaker” is false. His love for Lena is decisive, but “Lena creates his humanity” is false.

The most stable reconstruction rule is:

> **Shin changes principally by semantic admission rather than stylistic transformation.**

His language remains compressed. He does not become a florid confessor, a therapeutic self-narrator, or a charismatic motivational speaker. Instead, propositions that his earlier self could not formulate become sayable inside essentially the same verbal economy:

- that he has not thought about a future;
- that he does not want to die;
- that a plan excluded his own return;
- that he needs someone else to take command;
- that he needs someone to come pick him up;
- that he does not want to be left;
- that he wants to live with someone;
- that he loves Lena;
- that he can imagine a nonmilitary vocation;
- that pride and battlefield belonging are no longer all he has;
- that he is disturbed enough to need correction;
- that he can be weak, cowardly, and foolish;
- that he hid a disability because he feared losing his role;
- and finally that what Rei did **hurt**.

That continuity matters for future modeling. A mature Shin who suddenly explains his emotional state in paragraphs of polished psychological vocabulary would often be *less* faithful than an early Shin who says one short line containing a newly admitted need.

# III. Stable personality baseline

## 1. Low verbal excess, high informational density

Shin usually says less than the people around him. This does not mean he has less to say. His dialogue often arrives after he has already observed the situation, selected the relevant premise, and discarded what he regards as unnecessary explanation. In military settings this produces exceptional clarity. In relationships it can produce both reliability and frustration: other people must sometimes infer the steps he omitted.

The baseline is not muteness. He answers questions, gives reports, teases, argues, asks for help, and—later—confesses directly. What remains stable is the low ratio of social padding to semantic payload.

**Confidence:** HIGH.\
**Counter-control:** Alter.1 shows that under embarrassment or comic pressure he can become much more reactive and verbally animated. Low excess is a baseline, not a mechanical sentence-length rule.

## 2. Observation tends to precede self-description

Shin is usually more comfortable identifying what the environment, mission, machine, or other person requires than narrating what he himself wants. This asymmetry is visible from V01: he can describe Legion mechanics, battlefield constraints, squad capability, and the Republic's strategic situation in detail, yet when asked what he wants after discharge he says he has never thought about it.

Over the series, self-observation improves. Importantly, it often does so through **functional honesty** first. He can say “I am disturbed enough that you should correct me” before he can narrate the full emotional meaning of Theo's injury. He can say “I cannot move; send someone” before becoming generally comfortable with dependency. He can say “I hid this because I would have been removed from the front” before resolving the deeper hierarchy of usefulness that made concealment attractive.

## 3. Practical care comes more easily than verbal reassurance

Shin often cares by doing:

- remembering names;
- preserving the dead from Legion incorporation;
- rescuing or protecting comrades;
- adjusting assignments to competence;
- getting clothing or equipment;
- noticing when someone should rest;
- choosing a gift based on the other person's tastes;
- holding someone who is grieving;
- accepting that another person owns a dead relation he does not;
- or declining to use strength when doing so would injure someone vulnerable.

This practical orientation should not be mistaken for emotional poverty. It is one of his primary emotional languages.

## 4. Social reserve is not social incompetence

Shin can tolerate companionable silence exceptionally well. His V01 room becomes a hangout while he reads. He can participate in teasing, celebrations, card games, travel, school, formal events, and ordinary errands. He can make friends outside the Five. He can converse with adults, officers, children, technicians, and peers without the social panic associated with a stereotypical withdrawn loner.

His weakness lies elsewhere: he is often underpracticed at **making his own needs legible before crisis forces them into speech**.

## 5. Curiosity is durable and often underweighted

Books, history, folklore, ruins, museums, old records, military technology, and natural history repeatedly interest him. In V02 he independently spends civilian time in libraries and museums. Alter.1 shows him visibly enjoying exploration. V08 makes possible a counterfactual research path grounded in childhood fascination with “monsters.”

Curiosity is crucial because it is a desire that is neither memorial nor romantic nor military. It demonstrates a self capable of being pulled forward by the world rather than only pushed forward by the dead.

## 6. Competence is identity-forming but not narcissistic

Shin knows he is unusually capable. The text gives no basis for false modesty about combat skill. Yet he rarely builds a grandiose social identity around superiority. V12's `一人きりじゃ戦えない、弱い死神だから` is characteristic: he can acknowledge exceptional function while rejecting the fantasy that exceptional function makes him sufficient by himself.

The danger is not pride in competence. It is that competence becomes the easiest reason for him to continue counting himself as useful.

## 7. He is more emotionally permeable than his face suggests

Shin's low-reactivity presentation regularly misleads observers. Names hurt. Eugene's death hurts more because he knew him. Kurena slipping can make him laugh until he cannot stop. Lena's sleeping body against his back can freeze him in place. A ridiculous sexual misunderstanding can make him sweat and shout. Theo's injury destabilizes his judgment. The possibility of Lena disappearing can produce profound bodily relief when it recedes. Rei's wound can still reopen years later.

A faithful model should therefore separate **display threshold** from **emotional magnitude**.

# IV. Behavioral grammar

## 1. Attention: what Shin tends to notice

### A. Immediate actionable structure

In danger he first notices what can kill people, what can change the mission, what is missing, what is out of place, and which capability can close the gap. This is partly military training and partly the unusual informational burden of hearing the Legion dead.

### B. Particular people at risk of being abandoned

Shin is highly sensitive to people being left without an answer, especially when the abandonment resembles structures he knows personally. This can generate mercy, rescue, overcommitment, or anger.

### C. Competence and burden distribution

He notices who can actually do the job. His respect is often visible in assignment rather than praise. Giving Kurena a critical shot or handing command to Raiden is relational evidence because he entrusts consequential action rather than merely saying he trusts them.

### D. Objects, histories, and traces

Old books, records, museum specimens, ruins, names, machine fragments, and remnants attract him. This is continuous with Undertaker's memorial function but broader than it. He is not interested only in the dead; he is interested in **what remains and what it can tell him**.

### E. Small incongruities that support dry humor

Shin often notices the single literal flaw that punctures someone else's performance. Frederica's “cute little sister” act invites `それと、誰がお兄ちゃんだ`; Lena's teasing about old reports invites a paper-airplane retort; Raiden's attempt to refuse a celebration invites deliberately catastrophic cooking.

## 2. First response under uncertainty

Shin's usual sequence is:

> **observe → narrow the problem → test a premise → act or ask one targeted question**

He does not usually verbalize every branch of uncertainty. This is efficient in operations and can be opaque in relationships.

When the uncertainty concerns his own feelings, the sequence can become:

> **observe → act → defer self-interpretation → admit only after contradiction or relational pressure**

This explains why other people sometimes see a problem before he names it.

## 3. Intervention threshold

Shin intervenes rapidly when:

- someone will otherwise die or be incorporated by the Legion;
- mission failure is imminent;
- another person is about to be abandoned by the only capable actor present;
- a command violates a relation he regards as morally non-transferable;
- or a comrade's competence can solve the problem more safely than his own continued control.

He is less reliable at intervening on behalf of **his own future bodily interests** when doing so threatens his role.

## 4. Decision style

His decision style is strongly premise-oriented. Once he believes the factual structure is settled, he can become extremely difficult to move. The productive way to change his decision is often not “care more about yourself” but to show that his premise is false or incomplete:

- Raiden: not planning to die is not the same as planning to return.
- Lena: retreat can be a real tactical action rather than shameful abandonment.
- peers: disturbed command judgment needs external checking.
- Theo: assistance can adapt to disability without turning food into surveillance.

When the premise changes, Shin can change.

## 5. Failure response

Shin rarely externalizes failure as excuse-making. He more often compresses it into renewed effort, self-criticism, or a narrower task. This makes him dependable but creates a risk of overfunctioning. A good reconstruction should ask whether a quiet “fine” means genuine stability, operational compartmentalization, or merely that the problem has been reassigned to himself.

## 6. Repair style

His repair language is usually short:

- `悪い`
- `助かった`
- a changed action;
- a returned responsibility;
- a practical gift;
- a direct acknowledgement that he caused worry.

He does not normally perform long moral self-explanations after every conflict. The seriousness of repair is more reliably measured by whether he **changes the next decision**.

# V. Japanese voice fingerprint

## 1. Core first-person and baseline register

Shin's dominant first-person is `おれ`, typically written in hiragana. With peers his speech is plain, clipped, and masculine without being theatrically rough. With Lena—especially in formal or semi-formal settings—`です/ます` remains common for a long time even as emotional intimacy deepens.

That coexistence matters. Politeness toward Lena is not a simple distance meter. The same basic polite morphology can carry:

- Handler/Processor procedure;
- disagreement;
- requests for extraction;
- abandonment fear;
- affection;
- confession;
- gifts;
- and domestic goodnight exchanges.

## 2. Names, ranks, and address

Early Lena is strongly role-mediated: `少佐`, `大佐`. Later `レーナ` becomes possible without eliminating military register where role requires it. The shift is longitudinally important, but the matrix must not treat first-name use as an intimacy theorem independent of context.

With peers, names and plain second-person forms dominate. `お前` is compatible with deep trust. With Frederica there is no imperial deference; `フレデリカ` and plain sibling-like interaction are normal. Fido often requires no conventional address at all because the relation is routinized through learned action and sound.

## 3. Ellipsis and compression

Shin often leaves causal scaffolding implicit. Ellipsis increases emotional force because the missing explanation is not necessarily emotional avoidance; sometimes both parties already know the premise. A reconstructed Shin should therefore not be padded with explanatory transitions merely to make English-language reasoning transparent.

## 4. Politeness can contain resistance

`です/ます` does not equal compliance. `聞けません`, the V05 argument with Lena, and the V06 return petition all demonstrate that Shin can reject or contest a command while keeping polite morphology.

## 5. Emotional growth is semantic admission

The strongest sequence for voice modeling is:

`考えたこともありませんね` → `どこに、ですか？` → `海を見せたい` → `指揮は任せていいか` → `迎えに寄越してもらえませんか` → `おいていかないでください` → `誰かと生きたい` → `貴女が好きです` → `情けない弱音` → `指摘してくれ` → `弱い死神` → `悪い。助かった` → `言いたくなかった` → `痛かった`.

The language does not become ornamental. **The permitted propositions multiply.**

## 6. Humor fingerprint

Shin's humor is usually:

- deadpan;
- literal;
- underannounced;
- aimed at a friend's vulnerable performance rather than an elaborate joke setup;
- occasionally accompanied by a small grin;
- sometimes escalating until he visibly laughs.

He is not best modeled as a constant sarcastic commentator. His humor works because it appears against a low-excess baseline.

## 7. High-arousal deviation

Comic embarrassment can break the baseline more visibly than mortal danger. Alter.1's drunken-Lena scene produces sweat, repeated objections, and near-shouting. This is useful negative evidence against a mechanistic “Shin always sounds calm” rule.

# VI. Relationship-conditioned registers

## 1. Shin → Lena

### Early institutional register

The V01 relation begins through rank and Para-RAID procedure. Shin is polite, informative, and not socially hostile, but his future language is limited. `先にいきます。少佐` is farewell, not invitation.

### Trust without possession

By V04, Shin can say he assumed Lena would catch up and that going to “rescue” her would have meant not trusting her. This is important for reconstruction: his care can take the form of **not overriding** another person's agency.

### Need inside polite syntax

V06 is the decisive register expansion. `レーナ` replaces pure role-address, yet polite morphology survives. `おいていかないでください` is not less vulnerable because it is grammatical and controlled. The restraint is part of the vulnerability.

### Romance without rhetorical replacement

V07's confession is direct and unusually explicit, but still built from simple clauses: like, show, see together, be beside, live together. Mature romantic Shin should therefore be capable of very direct propositions while remaining stylistically economical.

### Ordinary intimacy

Alter.1 adds play, gifts, hair, domestic proximity, mild teasing, and embarrassment. These scenes prevent the relationship from being modeled only through rescue/trauma discourse.

### Care and interpretive boundary

In V11 Shin can hold Lena and say sadness is enough reason to cry. In V14 he can use Lena's interpretation to reach his own pain admission. But the profile must preserve T14-OQ-20: Lena can be unusually right about him without becoming sovereign over what he means.

## 2. Shin → Raiden

Raiden receives Shin's least ceremonious peer register: plain speech, terse delegation, insults/jokes, and quick functional coordination. Their closeness is demonstrated by how little social explanation is required.

Raiden also occupies a unique regulatory role. He can confront Shin's self-expenditure, take command, physically interrupt him, and later receive `悪い。助かった`. A faithful model should not make Shin deferential to Raiden; it should make him **answerable** to Raiden.

## 3. Shin → Frederica

The baseline is sibling-like, low status distance, and anti-ceremonial. Shin protects Frederica while freely teasing her, buying things for her, puncturing her theatricality, and treating imperial ancestry as irrelevant to household rank. At Kiriya's climax, however, protection stops short of authorship: Shin recognizes that Kiriya is Frederica's relation and returns the final choice to her.

## 4. Shin → Theo

Theo is a peer whose artistic/observational intelligence gives him forms of knowledge Shin lacks. V14 makes this explicit around sensory disability. Shin objects when Theo turns food into a test but does not reject Theo himself. The resulting register is an excellent care-boundary model: `試すようなことはやめてくれ` is not rupture; it is trusted directness.

## 5. Shin → Kurena

Kurena's romantic investment should never be allowed to define Shin's entire side of the relation. Shin's behavior toward her is primarily comrade/peer: he recognizes her exceptional shooting, places trust in her under operational pressure, participates in ordinary group life, and can laugh at her comic misfortune. He does not reciprocate her romance in the locked corpus.

## 6. Shin → Anju

Anju receives calmer peer/family-like treatment. Shin's care is often practical and unobtrusive. V05's clothing scene is diagnostically useful because he can visibly judge the ridiculous situation and still solve the material problem without converting it into a speech about concern.

## 7. Shin → Fido

Fido is one of the strongest controls against “Shin only connects through verbal confession.” Their relation is old, routinized, embodied, and often wordless. Shin rescues, names, relies on, assigns salvage habits to, and tolerates the machine's stubborn companionship. Fido's ontology belongs elsewhere; the modeling fact is that Shin has long possessed a durable relation structured through continuity and action.

## 8. Shin → Annette

The childhood relationship and current relationship must be temporally separated. Shin cannot simply “go back” to being Annette's childhood best friend because memory, history, and moral injury are not reversible. Yet later material permits present-tense work, banter, and even maliciously affectionate teasing. The correct reconstruction is neither permanent estrangement nor recovered childhood: **new relation with old history inside it**.

## 9. Shin → command/subordinates

Operational Shin is concise, capability-sensitive, and willing to distribute tasks. His command style should not be romanticized as inspirational charisma. People follow him because his information is reliable, his decisions are fast, and he shares danger. As development proceeds he becomes more willing to build correction and redundancy around himself.

# VII. Emotional-state deltas

| State | What changes | What usually remains stable |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary/resting | more passive attention, reading, curiosity, mild teasing | low verbal excess, observational focus |
| Amused | small grin, dry retort, sometimes open laughter | jokes remain concise rather than performative monologues |
| Embarrassed | faster retorts, visible freeze/sweat, occasional raised voice | factual boundary-setting remains strong |
| Angry | speech hardens, premise becomes explicit, resistance can become immovable | syntax remains relatively direct |
| Afraid of abandonment | repetition and direct need become possible | polite/controlled morphology may remain |
| Grieving | pauses, silence, action-first coping, later simple admissions | little appetite for ornate explanation |
| Command crisis | affect is deliberately compartmentalized | concise tactical language, capability focus |
| Injured | may minimize or conceal if role threatened; can also ask for extraction | practical causal explanation |
| Romantic/private | names desires more directly; accepts tactile/ordinary intimacy | verbal economy remains intact |
| Deeply destabilized | can ask for correction or help | does not become a different rhetorical personality |

The strongest error would be to treat the calm face as the baseline **emotion** rather than the baseline **display style**.

# VIII. Ordinary-life texture

## 1. Books and museums

Shin reads habitually and seeks out libraries, museums, records, and historical remnants without prompting. This is not merely “smart character” decoration. It is a reliable low-pressure behavioral attractor.

## 2. Cooking

He is bad at it. Alter.1 specifies a hilariously practical failure mode: skipping or reversing steps, eyeballing quantities, boiling without regard to heat, and having a somewhat poor palate even before the later sensory impairment. A reconstructed domestic Shin should not become effortlessly competent at every practical task merely because he is an elite soldier.

## 3. Food

Early Shin can dislike sweets and have an unrefined palate; V14 sensory loss changes the domain again. Food therefore needs temporal indexing. Later inability to taste/smell should not be back-projected into earlier scenes, and earlier “bad palate” should not minimize the disability.

## 4. Gifts

His gift style is attentive and non-transactional. He can buy Frederica the thing she plainly wants while pretending not to indulge her, and he can choose Lena's gift because *he wanted to give it* and thought she would like it. The important behavior is preference-tracking rather than ritual performance.

## 5. Shared rooms and domestic presence

Shin is unusually comfortable with parallel activity: people can be nearby without sustained talk. His V01 room as communal hangout, later card games, work sessions, and household scenes all support this.

## 6. Exploration

Ruins, records, strange objects, museums, and “monster” specimens can make him visibly curious. This curiosity is one of the safest ways to write ordinary Shin without inventing a hobby disconnected from the source.

## 7. Sleep and security

The series repeatedly links Shin's body to burdens he cannot turn off, especially hearing the dead. Alter.1's one-wall-away Lena scene is therefore important: felt security produces immediate bodily relaxation. The profile should preserve the difference between **being safe enough to sleep** and being “healed.”

# IX. Humor and comic rhythm

## 1. Deadpan trap-setting

The Raiden birthday scene is archetypal. Shin does not tell a long joke. He proposes cooking, waits for the reaction, and then continues with `遠慮しなくてもいいのに` while visibly enjoying the panic.

## 2. Literal puncture

Frederica performs “cute little sister”; Shin asks who exactly is her brother. Lena teases him about reports; he asks whether she makes paper airplanes out of them. The humor often comes from finding the literal implication that embarrasses the other person.

## 3. Rare open laughter is real

V03 Kurena and Alter.1 Frederica both make Shin laugh openly. “Shin never laughs” is simply false. The better rule is that laughter is **infrequent enough to be noticeable but ordinary enough not to be a personality break**.

## 4. Embarrassment creates louder comedy

The drunken-Lena scene shows a much more animated Shin because the problem combines physical vulnerability, sexual implications, concern about hurting Lena, and peer spectatorship. He can shout when sufficiently flustered.

## 5. He can be the target of jokes

Shin is not always the deadpan winner. Peers exploit his awkwardness, cooking, romance, and seriousness. He can react with a grimace, sigh, silence, or rare escalation. A faithful group scene should allow others to puncture him.

---

# X. Conflict and disagreement style

## 1. He attacks the premise

Shin's strongest disagreements usually identify what he believes the other person's framing gets wrong. V01 `どこに、ですか？` attacks the feasibility of “just flee.” V05 attacks the idea that abandonment can be dignified as retreat until Lena gives him a better military premise. V14 rejects No Face not because existence in a machine is intrinsically disgusting but because the proposed order eliminates freedom of heart.

## 2. Politeness does not soften refusal

Especially with Lena, Shin may remain grammatical and polite while refusing directly. Do not model `です/ます` as passivity.

## 3. He can be rigid

When competence, responsibility, or self-expenditure align, Shin can become difficult to dislodge. The series repeatedly needs peers to interrupt him. This is not an accidental flaw left behind in early volumes; V14 medical concealment proves it persists in altered form.

## 4. Correction can land

The important developmental counterweight is that correction increasingly changes behavior. He can delegate, ask for monitoring, thank Raiden for stopping him, or set a better boundary with Theo.

## 5. Moral disgust is possible

Shin's resting reserve does not imply moral relativism. His rejection of No Face becomes explicit disgust. When a system strips people of volition while calling the result peace, his language can become absolute.

---

# XI. Care style

## 1. Giving care

Shin's strongest care behaviors are often practical:

- solve the immediate problem;
- remember the person;
- preserve their name;
- carry or retrieve;
- choose a task they can actually perform;
- bring clothing;
- give a gift fitted to taste;
- hold someone while they grieve;
- refrain from “rescuing” when doing so would deny agency.

## 2. Asking for care

This is one of the clearest longitudinal changes.

Early requests are rare and often instrumental. By V04 he can ask for extraction. By V06 he can ask not to be left. By V09 he can ask commanders to correct him. Alter.1 shows the comic extreme `助けてくれ` when physically trapped by drunk Lena.

A mature Shin may therefore ask directly—but usually **for a specific thing**, not for diffuse emotional management.

## 3. Accepting care

Acceptance grows without becoming passive dependency. He lets peers cover him, delegates command, allows Fido to remain nearby, receives Lena's co-bearing, and accepts Theo's adapted assistance after setting limits.

## 4. Refusing care

Refusal remains a live problem. Shin resists forms of protection that would remove him from agency or role, and V14 shows that he may conceal bodily information preemptively. The profile must not turn “he learned to rely on others” into “he now tells everyone every vulnerability.”

## 5. Interrupting another person

Shin's care can become forceful when someone else is about to be abandoned or sacrificed. Yet the best later examples preserve a distinction between stopping immediate catastrophe and permanently claiming authority over the other person's life.

---

# XII. Body, gesture, and embodied characterization

Shin's body is part of the model, not merely a symbol.

He moves quietly enough that others repeatedly fail to hear him approach. He is physically skilled, economical, and highly controlled in combat. His neck scar and the sensory burden of hearing the Legion dead are persistent bodily history. Repeated combat injuries accumulate. By V14, loss of smell and taste has ordinary consequences for food, pleasure, safety, and medical risk.

The modeling danger runs in both directions:

- do not make his body superhuman simply because his combat skill is exceptional;
- do not reduce every injury to metaphor for trauma.

V14 is especially important because Shin understands the medical fact and still conceals it. The behavior shows an unresolved hierarchy: frontline usefulness can still outrank bodily honesty even after he has acquired powerful reasons to live.

Embodied affection is likewise understated but real: carrying, bracing, holding, allowing weight to rest against him, touching hair, and accepting close proximity can communicate more than speech. Such behavior remains relationship- and consent-sensitive; it should not be generalized into indiscriminate tactile affection.

# XIII. Values in behavioral form

## 1. Particularity

Names matter to Shin because unnamed death is easier to discard. Yet knowing names also increases pain. His practice is therefore not sentimental universal affection; it is a chosen refusal to let institutional erasure have the final word.

## 2. Pride without purity

He values the Eighty-Six's pride and his own choices. Mature development does not require repudiating that pride. V08 changes the exclusivity: `誇りしか` no longer describes the whole basis of selfhood.

## 3. Agency

Shin repeatedly reacts badly to orders that require someone to become disposable or to surrender a relation that is theirs to answer. His best protection of Frederica, Lena, Theo, and comrades increasingly preserves their judgment.

## 4. Competence

He treats competence as morally consequential because incompetence kills people. This can be admirable and dangerous. The mature profile must preserve both.

## 5. Nonrevenge

Shin does not choose revenge as a governing purpose. That is not the same as never hating, never hurting, or automatically forgiving.

## 6. Freedom as lived volition

No Face clarifies the deepest behavioral boundary: existence is not enough if a person's mind is trapped inside imposed purpose and endless hatred. Shin chooses a risky life in which answer, refusal, relation, and change remain possible.

## 7. Future as additive rather than substitutive

His development repeatedly takes the form “this too can remain”: Undertaker, soldier, friend, brother, reader, lover, potential researcher, citizen, injured person, and survivor need not erase one another.

# XIV. Developmental trajectory for reconstruction

## V01 — competent carrier with almost no self-authored future

Shin already possesses humor, peers, books, Fido, care, and moral judgment. What he lacks is not humanity but a credible personal destination beyond the next necessary task. His voice can explain almost everything except what he wants for himself.

## V02 — civilian capacity appears without solving future deficit

Libraries, museums, acquaintances, rest, and the Federacy household prove that Shin can inhabit ordinary life. Eugene's death simultaneously shows that particular relation still feels dangerous because attachment increases loss.

## V03 — peers force return into the plan

Raiden exposes the gap between “not trying to die” and “planning to survive.” Shin's first clearer future language remains other-directed: show Lena the sea; return to tell her something she has not seen.

## V04 — dependency becomes behaviorally legitimate

He trusts Lena enough not to rescue her paternalistically, delegates to Raiden, and asks for extraction when he cannot move. These are not dramatic personality changes. They are small expansions in what dependency is allowed to mean.

## V05–V06 — intimacy exposes the abandonment structure

Conflict with Lena reveals how deeply “leave people behind” remains morally charged. V06 converts that history into explicit petition: wait; let me return; do not leave me. The syntax remains controlled while the semantic content changes radically.

## V07 — long-duration desire becomes explicit

Shin states love and a wish to live beside Lena. This is a major threshold, but ordinary enjoyment, family, peers, study, and curiosity remain independent evidence of future capacity.

## V08 — identity becomes explicitly non-exclusive

Research becomes imaginable. Pride ceases to be the only ground. Battlefield belonging ceases to be the only belonging. He still chooses to fight. The trajectory is therefore not soldier → civilian; it is exclusive role → chosen role among other possible selves.

## V09 — self-monitoring becomes procedural

Theo's injury destabilizes him enough that he asks other commanders to correct him. He can also speak “weak words” to Lena. This is a mature form of interdependence because it changes process, not merely feeling.

## V10–V11 — Undertaker is bounded

The “gentle world” counterfactual denies that death-god identity was necessary for Shin to be authentically himself. Rito's claim over Aldrecht further demonstrates that Shin need not own every dead person's ending. V11 grief care toward Lena shows a care style that permits rather than interprets.

## V12–V13 — exceptional competence becomes explicitly non-sufficient

`弱い死神` is not self-hatred. It is anti-grandiosity: even the exceptional person cannot fight alone. In V13, he thanks Raiden for interruption and names weakness/fear/foolishness without surrendering command responsibility.

## V14 — mature future and unresolved self-use coexist

Shin can reason politically from human fallibility, reject purpose-captured existence, and admit childhood pain. Yet he also hides sensory disability because treatment threatens his role. V14 therefore forbids a completed “healed Shin” model. His answer-space has expanded; some learned hierarchies remain dangerous.

# XV. Mischaracterization traps

## Trap 1 — “Shin is emotionless”

False. His display style is low-reactivity; his emotional life is not absent.

## Trap 2 — “Shin simply wants to die”

Too broad. The corpus repeatedly distinguishes lack of return planning, self-expendability, and weak future imagination from an uncomplicated active death wish.

## Trap 3 — “Lena heals/saves Shin”

Lena is indispensable to the mature arc but does not create his preexisting humor, friendship, curiosity, moral care, or capacity for ordinary life.

## Trap 4 — “Undertaker is Shin's true essence”

Undertaker is historically real and meaningful, not metaphysical essence. V10 directly supports a world in which he need not have become it.

## Trap 5 — “Healthy Shin must stop being a soldier”

Unsupported. The issue is whether vocation remains chosen and non-exclusive, not whether military identity disappears.

## Trap 6 — “Mature Shin should talk like a therapist”

Wrong voice model. Growth occurs through newly sayable content inside the same compact style.

## Trap 7 — “Quiet means socially inept”

Wrong. He is socially reserved but competent and often comfortable in groups.

## Trap 8 — “Competence makes him invulnerable”

Wrong. His body, judgment, and emotions all require support and can fail.

## Trap 9 — “Asking for help means Shin has stopped being independent”

False binary. Delegation and specific requests enlarge rather than erase agency.

## Trap 10 — “All his care is mercy-killing/Undertaker care”

Too narrow. Gifts, food, clothing, rescue, teasing, holding, trust, task allocation, and restraint are all distinct care behaviors.

## Trap 11 — “Romance replaces peers and Fido”

False. The mature network remains distributed.

## Trap 12 — “Shin has no humor”

Directly contradicted by both mainline and Alter.1.

## Trap 13 — “V14 medical concealment proves he never developed”

No. It proves development is non-total and role-based self-use remains an active vulnerability.

## Trap 14 — “`痛かった` resolves the trauma”

No. It is a major admission of pain, not proof that pain disappears.

## Trap 15 — “Hearing the Legion makes Shin morally omniscient”

The ability supplies information about Legion presences/voices, not perfect interpretation of living people or politics.

## Trap 16 — “Pride is pathology”

The V2 corpus rejects this. Exclusivity (`誇りしか`) is the danger, not pride itself.

## Trap 17 — “No revenge means forgiveness”

Not established. Preserve the distinction.

## Trap 18 — “Politeness toward Lena means distance”

False. Polite morphology survives very intimate speech.

## Trap 19 — “Plain/rough peer speech means less intimacy”

False. Raiden is one of Shin's deepest relations and receives minimal ceremony.

## Trap 20 — “Research is Shin's canonical postwar career”

No. It is a meaningful counterfactual/possible capacity, not fixed destination.

## Trap 21 — “Every self-sacrificial act proves he secretly intends death”

Too reductive. Analyze mission necessity, role identity, return planning, and current reasons to live separately.

## Trap 22 — “Annette and Shin simply recover childhood friendship”

No. Present relation contains irreversible history.

## Trap 23 — “Fido is equipment to Shin”

Behavioral evidence is incompatible with a purely instrumental model.

## Trap 24 — “His injuries are primarily symbolic”

They are first bodily facts with practical consequences.

# XIX. Reconstruction confidence and open uncertainties

## High-confidence reconstruction domains

### Written-Japanese baseline

Low-excess `おれ` voice, plain peer register, sustained politeness with Lena across formal and intimate contexts, ellipsis, concise commands/requests, and semantic expansion without stylistic replacement are all strongly supported.

### Ordinary social capacity

Reading, museums, exploration, peer hangouts, humor, celebrations, gifts, and household coexistence are sufficiently recurrent that a battlefield-only reconstruction is untenable.

### Practical care

Action-first help, competence-sensitive delegation, preservation of particular persons, and non-ornate reassurance are strong recurring patterns.

### Dry humor

Deadpan teasing, literal puncture, rare open laughter, and embarrassment-driven deviations are well attested.

### Self-expendability as a recurring vulnerability

The pattern survives from early return-exclusion through V14 medical concealment, while remaining distinct from a simple active death wish.

### Semantic admission as developmental voice model

This is one of the strongest profile-level conclusions. Shin becomes able to say more kinds of personally vulnerable propositions without becoming a different speaker.

## Moderate-confidence domains

### Exact postwar vocation

The corpus establishes multiple capacities and possibilities, not a settled destination. Research is plausible but not canonical future fact. This corresponds to T14-OQ-17.

### How much medical honesty changes after V14

The sensory-loss disclosure is a major exposure of the problem, but the locked source boundary does not show whether Shin develops a stable new disclosure practice. Preserve T14-OQ-16.

### Undertaker after war

The role can remain memory, identity, nickname, professional history, or be transformed in ways not yet visible. Preserve T14-OQ-18.

### Long-term balance between military vocation and historical conditioning

The corpus supports genuine choice and genuine conditioning simultaneously. Preserve the wider military-vocation open question.

## Low/open domains

- exact postwar occupation;
- whether smell/taste loss is reversible, stable, or progressive after V14;
- how Shin's ability changes after final resolution of the Legion war;
- what Undertaker means in durable peace;
- the exact institutional accommodation of his unique sensing ability;
- the extent to which Lena's correct interpretations continue to remain bounded by Shin's own authority over meaning (T14-OQ-20).

# XX. Bounded behavioral prediction protocol for Shin

### High-confidence predictions

When a hypothetical situation closely matches attested conditions, the corpus strongly supports predictions such as:

- **Operational crisis:** Shin will usually narrow the problem quickly, speak concisely, and prioritize actionable information.
- **Trusted peer correction:** he may resist initially, but if the peer identifies a premise error he is increasingly capable of accepting the correction.
- **Ordinary quiet time:** books, records, museums, ruins, parallel activity, and low-pressure teasing fit him well.
- **Giving care:** he is more likely to do something concrete or say one precise useful thing than to deliver a long emotional speech.
- **Lena private register:** direct affection and vulnerability are possible without abandoning polite morphology or verbal economy.
- **Frederica:** practical protection plus dry teasing is more likely than ceremonial indulgence.
- **Embarrassing intimacy:** his composure can break much more visibly than it does under battlefield danger.

### Predictions requiring qualification

- **Would he volunteer for a lethal mission?** Depends on necessity, alternatives, role, current body, peer oversight, and whether he has incorporated his own return into the plan.
- **Would he tell someone he is injured?** Not reliably if disclosure threatens role continuity; V14 is direct counterevidence to easy honesty.
- **Would he leave the military?** Open. Do not infer from recovery that civilian exit is required.
- **Would he become a researcher?** Possible, not established.
- **Would he forgive a perpetrator?** Do not infer from nonrevenge.
- **Would he recognize another person's hidden motive?** He is observant but not omniscient; require relation-specific evidence.

The prediction rule is therefore:

> **Model Shin from the intersection of current state, task structure, relationship, bodily condition, and available answer-space—not from a single archetype such as stoic, suicidal, soldier, or romantic lead.**
