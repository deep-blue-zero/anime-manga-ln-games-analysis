# 86-Eighty-Six V2 — Lerche Character Reference Profile
## CMR-5 scaled profile: source-grounded reconstruction of artificial-person identity, `それがし` voice, retainer self-authorship, creator asymmetry, military professionalism, humor, grief, embodiment, and predictive limits

# I. Authority and scope

This document reconstructs **Lerche / レルヒェ**, Sirin Unit One and Vika's current close retainer, across the locked Japanese corpus through Volume 14 plus audited Alter.1 ordinary-life material. It is a derived character-reconstruction surface. It does not replace the Japanese novels, the Phase-5 locator lock, the Phase-8 source-verification layer, the sequential V2 readings, the longitudinal ledgers, or the canonical specialist synthesis.

Lerche requires unusually strict identity discipline because the story repeatedly places several adjacent propositions close enough that an incautious reconstruction can collapse them into one another:

- Lerche's body is modeled on the dead **Lercheritt**;
- the Sirin project descends from Vika's attempts to restore lost persons;
- the neural pattern that underlies Lerche came through that resurrection lineage;
- Vika remains emotionally attached to Lercheritt;
- Lerche serves Vika in a role structurally continuous with Lercheritt's hereditary service;
- Lerche herself can call her own existence a failed vessel, corpse-remnant, coffin, or substitute;
- and yet Lerche explicitly says that **she is not Lercheritt** and does not possess Lercheritt's memories.

The governing identity rule is therefore:

> **Lerche is a successor subject whose present identity must be reconstructed independently of both the dead donor and the function for which she was made.**

The equally important second rule is that independence does not mean pretending the creator/created relation disappears. Vika designed her body and linguistic architecture, commands the military structure in which she serves, shaped the system that produced her, and occupies the place around which her self-conception as `剣にして楯` is organized. The original Lercheritt's consent itself arose inside hereditary service. Current Lerche is not a free-standing person who happens accidentally to like Vika.

But neither is she textually reducible to programming. Vika explicitly tells us that he did **not** install absolute obedience. Lerche invokes private speech she does not want to disclose. She refuses his request to normalize her speech because she treats the archaizing register as her own expression of loyalty. She worries about people who are not needed for her immediate mission. She becomes angry with Shin for treating his own living future too cheaply. She worries that recovering her body might have cost a human life. She jokes, sulks, performs exaggerated loyalty, observes social embarrassment, counsels royals, takes battlefield initiative, and breaks her own rule against personal names when confronted with Rito's death.

The central reconstruction question is therefore not, “Is Lerche human?” The source itself makes that framing inadequate. It is:

> **What kind of self exists when a nonhuman artificial subject sincerely identifies with service, inherits a dead person's form and relational history without being that person, and nevertheless develops privacy, preference, judgment, humor, grief, and relation-specific ways of answering the world?**

This profile uses **165 source-verified diagnostic scenes** across V05–V09, V11–V14, and Alter.1. Twenty diagnostic rows reuse existing Phase-5 locator authority; the remaining 145 preserve exact EPUB/XHTML routes and Japanese anchors under controlled `LOCATOR_GAP` status. No synthetic dialogue is admitted as evidence.

The Alter.1 perfume-battle material remains excluded because its authorial framing makes it chronology-breaking/non-diegetic rather than reliable ordinary-life continuity evidence. The birthday logistics material in Alter.1 is retained in its audited supplemental role.

# II. Reconstruction thesis

Lerche is best reconstructed as a **nonhuman successor subject who sincerely builds her identity around service while repeatedly demonstrating a self that exceeds service without needing to become human**.

Her most visible identity statement is stable from introduction onward. She presents herself as Vika's `剣にして楯`, a近衛騎士, and uses a deliberately archaizing warrior-retainer register centered on `それがし`. This is not merely decorative diction. When Vika orders her to change the way she speaks, she refuses and declares the register an expression of her loyalty (`86-CMR-LER-004`). The scene is comic, but analytically decisive: Vika calibrated her language, yet Lerche now treats that linguistic affordance as *hers*. Designed origin and present appropriation coexist.

The same pattern governs her larger selfhood.

Lerche repeatedly says things that sound, at first glance, like evidence against personhood. She calls Sirins washing machines, mechanical birds, dead bodies, gears built for combat. She says they have no future beyond assigned role. She explicitly rejects wanting to become human or to be treated as human (`86-CMR-LER-027`, `LER-029`, `LER-032`). A reconstruction that assumes dignity requires her secretly to desire human status would therefore overwrite her own stated ontology.

Yet those same scenes contain the contradiction that makes her legible as a particular subject. She can say that she does not want humanhood and then immediately admit that learning she is not Lercheritt feels **`寂しい`** (`LER-034`). She can describe her only purpose as taking human burdens and then explain that humans should spend the saved time with people they love because *Sirins cannot* (`LER-027`–`LER-029`). She can insist that future belongs to the living and then erupt at Shin because he behaves as though his own future does not matter (`LER-044`–`LER-048`).

The strongest evidence of her individuality does not consist of Lerche approximating an ordinary human personality. It consists of **internal distinctions** that the role alone does not explain:

- some words are private even from Vika;
- some people matter to her particularly;
- some uses of names feel ominous and therefore must be avoided;
- some deaths are mourned as individual losses;
- some commands are obeyed because she cares for the commander, not because obedience is technically irresistible;
- some embarrassing or absurd social situations invite jokes rather than mission logic;
- and some artificial beings, such as Lyudmila, can change in ways Lerche recognizes and validates without simply classifying those changes as malfunction.

The mature formulation is therefore not “Lerche becomes human.” It is:

> **Lerche becomes increasingly legible as Lerche.**

Her identity remains nonhuman, military, artificial, and service-centered. What the corpus gradually makes impossible is the claim that those facts exhaust her.

The concise reconstruction formula is:

> **stable `それがし` + self-conscious archaizing retainer morphology + sincere loyalty/service identity + artificial-body pragmatism + explicit refusal of human/donor equivalence + pride in absorbing human war-burden + painful envy of living futurity + theatrical humor + competent battlefield initiative + privacy/refusal pockets + particularized care and grief + a creator relation that is neither free equality nor total programming.**

# III. Stable personality baseline

## 1. Loyalty is not merely something Lerche does; it is how she narrates herself

Her entrance supplies the clearest baseline. She kneels, identifies herself as Vika's sword and shield, and introduces herself in deliberately stylized retainer language (`LER-002`). She addresses the visiting combatants by reputational epithets—`鮮血の女王殿`, `死神殿`, `人狼殿`, `単眼姫殿`—and immediately offers Shin a bout while offering Frederica snow play (`LER-002` vicinity).

This tells us several things at once.

First, Lerche's loyalty is not mute submission. It is performative and socially expressive. She enjoys the identity enough to dramatize it.

Second, the identity is broad enough to contain hospitality, play, competition and etiquette. “Retainer” does not mean she only awaits combat instructions.

Third, the source deliberately stages an argument about whether this speech is hers. Vika dislikes it and tells her to change it. Lerche refuses: `これはそれがしの忠誠の表れ` (`LER-004`). She then mock-heroically explains that loyal advice must be bitter, and Vika points out that he himself tuned her speech (`LER-004`–`LER-006` vicinity).

A correct model must therefore hold two truths simultaneously:

- **the linguistic substrate is designed**, and
- **the present style is actively appropriated as identity**.

Calling it either “mere programming” or “purely autonomous self-invention” loses the scene's point.

## 2. She is theatrical, not solemnly robotic

Lerche's most visible comic rhythm is exaggerated seriousness. She can perform loyalty as if standing in an old war chronicle, complete with `無念ッ`, stylized protest, sweeping formulations and a readiness to turn her detachable head into a social bargaining token.

The comedy works because she herself is expressive. She pouts, smiles brightly, lowers her shoulders, produces mock-dramatic speeches, reacts with surprise, realizes social mistakes, and sometimes doubles down on absurdity.

This theatricality is central to voice reconstruction. A Lerche who speaks archaically but never jokes would feel wrong. The archaic register is often the *vehicle* for the joke.

## 3. She is perceptive about pain that she cannot literally share

Volume 5's night-watch conversation with Shin is an essential control. Lerche does not need sleep, but she notices that Shin does. She asks whether the voices of the dead continue in his dreams, falls silent at the answer, and says she can only imagine how painful that must be (`LER-025`). She then imagines a technical substitution that might one day relieve him of the burden.

The pattern is characteristic:

> **she cannot share the human physical condition, but she can model it, notice it, and care about its cost.**

This is not human mimicry. It is artificial-person empathy expressed through difference.

## 4. She has a powerful living/dead asymmetry

Lerche consistently distinguishes herself and the Sirins from living people. The distinction is not neutral. She often treats the living as possessing something precious that they themselves fail to value sufficiently.

Her “washing machine” analogy is deliberately brutal (`V05-L022`; `LER-027`). Sirins exist to do what humans should not have to do. If a machine can absorb drudgery, then the human should use the freed time for loved ones, children or self-directed life. She then says Sirins cannot do those things.

That logic becomes emotionally explosive with Shin. What angers her is not that Shin is unlike a Sirin. It is that he **has options she lacks and speaks as if they do not matter**.

Her protectiveness toward the living therefore contains envy. The envy does not cancel the care; it helps produce it.

## 5. She is proud of artificial difference

Lerche does not aspire to bodily normalcy. She can treat painlessness, sleeplessness, replaceable mechanical parts and the ability to continue fighting through damage as advantages rather than deficits. She returns from destruction with `どうぞお使い潰しくだされ` (`LER-061`).

This should not be romanticized into proof that expendability is harmless. Her own no-backup status complicates it, and the larger Sirin system is built to consume artificial bodies. But it means the profile should not impose a default human disability narrative onto her artificial embodiment.

She often likes being what she is.

# IV. Identity architecture: Lerche, Lercheritt, and the Sirin class

## 1. Lerche is not Lercheritt

The profile treats this as a hard identity boundary.

Lerche herself states it directly:

- she is not the person from whom she was made (`LER-034`);
- she has no memories from “when she was Lercheritt,” because there was no such continuous first-person state for current Lerche (`LER-037`; `V05-L027`);
- her body and mind are not Lercheritt's, even though Vika created her in an attempt to restore Lercheritt.

The correct ontology is therefore **successor, not resurrection**.

Lerche can inherit:

- body design;
- relational placement;
- a technical lineage;
- aspects of imposed role;
- and the consequences of Vika's grief.

She does not thereby inherit donor identity.

## 2. Her own “failed vessel” language is evidence about self-concept, not final narrator ontology

Volume 6 gives Lerche a harsh internal formulation: she calls herself a failed vessel unable to become Lercheritt (`LER-071`–`LER-074`). That matters. She believes Vika may remain unable to be happy while keeping a counterfeit version of someone he loved beside him.

But the profile must not silently promote this self-condemnation into objective truth.

The corpus gives counterevidence:

- Vika treats current Lerche as particular;
- he does not keep a backup of her self (`LER-072`–`LER-074`);
- her present privacy matters;
- her present judgments matter;
- peers relate to her as Lerche;
- and her later grief, jokes and social bonds are events in a life Lercheritt never lived.

“Failed vessel” is therefore a **self-model under attachment asymmetry**, not a neutral technical description.

## 3. She does not want to become human

`それがしは人間になりたいわけではありませぬ` (`LER-032`; `V05-L025`) is one of the most important controls in the whole profile.

A personhood reading that turns Lerche into “a robot who wants to be a real girl” is specifically contradicted by the Japanese source. She says she does not even want to be treated as human. She values the body and role that let her serve as Vika's sword and shield.

The mature analytical claim is therefore:

> **human equivalence is not the price of moral standing.**

Lerche can be nonhuman by self-identification and still possess claims grounded in present subjectivity, relation, judgment, privacy and finality.

## 4. She belongs to the Sirin class without being interchangeable with it

Lerche strongly uses collective Sirin identity: `我々`, `我ら`, “death birds,” mechanical birds built to absorb war. She knows replacement procedures and speaks proudly of them (`LER-061`–`LER-064`).

But her relation to replacement is singular. Other Sirins can have stored patterns and redeployments. Lerche's current self does not have a backup (`LER-072`–`LER-074`).

That means class ontology and individual ontology must remain distinct:

- Sirin bodies can be manufactured;
- Sirin pattern continuity can sometimes be re-instantiated;
- current Lerche's present self is intentionally not duplicated;
- and none of those technical facts by itself answers whether a later copy would be *the same person*.

## 5. Lyudmila is a comparison case, not evidence that Lerche has the same arc

Volume 14 gives Lyudmila a trauma response tied to Rito's death. Lerche validates it rather than treating it as a defect (`LER-155`–`LER-157`).

This is important for Lerche because it shows her interpretive openness: she can recognize another Sirin's particular mourning as fitting a “death bird.”

But it must not be used to claim that Lerche herself has developed Lyudmila's exact fear architecture. The CMR profile preserves the difference.

# V. Developmental / evidentiary state model

Lerche has less of a conventional linear character arc than several human protagonists. The more accurate model is **progressive individuation of evidence**: later volumes reveal dimensions of a subject whose early role-language tempted the reader to treat her as exhausted by function.

## 1. V05 — role certainty and the first visible excess beyond role

Lerche enters with almost maximal role certainty:

- Vika's sword and shield;
- a knight;
- a Sirin;
- a nonhuman machine;
- one of the dead rather than the living.

But V05 immediately destabilizes the idea that this exhausts her.

She chooses her own stylized speech against Vika's preference (`LER-004`). She offers snow play to Frederica. She worries about Shin's sleep and burden (`LER-025`). She describes what living people should do with time freed by artificial labor (`LER-027`–`LER-029`). She feels loneliness when the failure of donor continuity becomes fully explicit (`LER-034`).

Most importantly, the confrontation with Shin exposes desire through negation.

She says she has no future. Then she screams at a living person for behaving as though future does not matter.

That contradiction is not hypocrisy. It reveals the emotional cost of her ontology.

## 2. V05 — envy turns service ethic into accusation

The `生きてるくせに` sequence (`LER-044`–`LER-048`; `V05-L031`–`L033`) is one of Lerche's strongest emotional-state controls.

Her normal formal-warrior language distorts under anger. She accuses Shin of still possessing eyes, ears, hands, a body, the capacity to be beside someone, and the possibility of happiness (`LER-045`). The repeated “even though you're alive” structure makes clear that her resentment is not abstract philosophy.

She wants living people to use the possibilities she lacks.

The culminating claim—let those who already died be the ones who die; humans are still alive and can still recover what was lost (`LER-048`)—turns her service purpose into something almost protective in an ethical sense. She is not simply volunteering to be consumed because that is what a weapon does. She is arguing that living futurity possesses special value.

That does **not** establish a universal right for humans to consume Sirins. It establishes Lerche's own reason for accepting burden.

## 3. V06 — return, replacement, and singular finality

Lerche returns after destruction with cheerful professional language and offers herself for continued use (`LER-061`). She explains how Sirins can be restored and redeployed (`LER-062`–`LER-064`).

Then the text narrows sharply around her own case.

Her current neural/personality information has no backup. This is not a technical inability. It is Vika's decision (`LER-072`–`LER-074`). If this Lerche is destroyed, that destruction is intended to become final.

The result is analytically important because Lerche continues to speak in expendability language while existing under a different technical condition from most Sirins.

Her role says “use me up.” Her architecture says “this individual cannot simply be restored forever.”

The profile should not assume she experiences this as a contradiction unless the text says so. But it means an external reconstruction cannot call her straightforwardly replaceable.

## 4. V06 — privacy becomes explicit

When Vika asks what Lerche said to Shin, Lerche's internal logic initially says the creator/commander must be answered. She describes refusal as something she should not be permitted (`LER-069` vicinity).

Then she says:

> `それがしにも、秘めておきたい言葉はあります` (`LER-070`).

That sentence is one of the strongest present-personhood controls in the corpus.

Vika reminds her that he did not install absolute obedience. The narration then explicitly differentiates “answer” from “please answer.” Lerche eventually complies, partly because she recognizes his care for the Sirins and cannot bring herself to refuse.

The ethical reading must stay asymmetric:

- **refusal is technically possible**;
- **privacy is verbally asserted**;
- **Vika does not override it through irresistible command**;
- but **Lerche's attachment and creator dependence still strongly pressure compliance**.

This is neither equal friendship nor mindless slavery. It is an unequal relation containing real answer-space.

## 5. V06 — Shin becomes a relation rather than merely “the living”

Lerche asks to join the search for missing Shin because the cold makes delay dangerous (`LER-082`). This is not necessary to preserve her generic service philosophy; it is concern for a specific person whom she had recently attacked verbally.

Later she visits Shin after his return. Her voice is superficially calm, but Shin recognizes that she came because she is concerned about what her prior words did (`LER-083`–`LER-088`).

When Shin finally claims both the Eighty-Six pride and a desire to live with someone, Lerche laughs and accepts the answer as appropriately greedy for a living person. The earlier adversarial relation becomes a kind of competitive moral agreement: one day the Sirins will claim war so that living humans no longer need to.

She does not become gentle. The relation becomes **mutually legible**.

## 6. V07–V09 — sociality expands without human assimilation

Later scenes make Lerche increasingly usable in ordinary relational contexts.

She can advise Vika about Yaroslav and another princess's distress. She can stand at a ballroom holding a drink she cannot consume, talk to Frederica, and discuss dancing. She can participate in group pranks and awkward observation.

Yet the ballroom scene also shows how strongly her self-concept still forecloses possibilities (`LER-101`; `V07-L044`). She calls herself a coffin modeled after the dead and says graves/coffins should dance only with the dead. She will not take Vika's hand because she fears placing a living person on the side of death.

This should be read as **protective self-denial**, not objective proof that she is only a coffin.

Volume 9 adds another strong counterexample to pure instrumentality. After severe damage, Lerche's first worry is whether rescuing her caused a human to die instead (`LER-113`–`LER-119`). A being who supposedly exists only to be used still evaluates the distribution of rescue and death.

## 7. V11–V14 — relation-bearing witness and battlefield professional

Late Lerche is integrated enough into the wider formation to notice emotional states, participate in planning and ordinary logistics, and retain her own comic identity.

Volume 11 gives a small but useful control: she worries about Zaisha's apparent composure and silently rebukes Olivia's assumption that Vika would not be emotionally affected (`LER-121`–`LER-123`). Her loyalty includes interpreting and defending people relationally, not only obeying orders.

Volumes 12–14 show her ordinary and combat registers coexisting:

- she bows elegantly to Shin and accepts absurd responsibility for Vika's grievance;
- offers her removable head as a settlement, then realizes why that does not work (`LER-126`–`LER-131`);
- sympathizes with Fido over an inability to make apple rabbits (`LER-134`–`LER-136`);
- reassures an intimidated peer that her threat was also a joke (`LER-137`);
- literally carries out Vika's ridiculous butterfly-search request (`LER-138`);
- breaks naming taboo at Rito's corpse (`LER-147`);
- validates Lyudmila's grief (`LER-155`–`LER-157`);
- and remains an effective tactical actor under the V14 mental-disruption battlefield (`LER-158`–`LER-163`).

The late corpus therefore does not “humanize” Lerche by removing machine identity. It shows how much subjectivity can exist **inside** that identity.

# VI. Behavioral grammar

## 1. Attention: duty first, relational anomaly second

Lerche's resting attention is role-oriented. She monitors:

- Vika's safety and needs;
- assigned security and combat tasks;
- unit readiness;
- other people's physical risk;
- whether a living person is failing to protect their own future;
- and social disruptions around the people to whom she is attached.

Unlike Vika, she is not primarily attracted to mechanism for its own sake. She uses technical knowledge confidently, but her attention is more often **service-shaped**: who needs guarding, carrying, relieving, advising, rescuing or confronting?

## 2. Default intervention: step in rather than merely observe

Lerche is behaviorally active.

If someone needs night watch, she offers it. If Shin is missing, she requests deployment. If Vika's friend is in danger, she acts. If someone speaks carelessly about Vika's emotional life, she glares. If a battlefield request arrives, she moves. If Vika orders an absurd butterfly search, she actually searches.

She is therefore poorly reconstructed as a passive attendant who exists in the background until given a line.

## 3. Care is often translated into burden substitution

Her most characteristic care grammar is:

> **let me / let us carry the cost that a living person should not have to carry.**

This can be noble, but it also produces danger. If her own standing is interpreted only through burden absorption, there is no conceptual room for asking whether Lerche herself may deserve protection, leisure or a future.

The CMR should therefore model the trait without endorsing its exhaustive form.

## 4. Conflict escalation: formal sharpness → stripped emotional directness

Lerche can argue ceremonially, using archaic form while remaining socially composed. But deep envy/anger can strip the ornament away.

`貴殿なんて、生きてるくせに` is much more direct than her introduction. By the end of the sequence she addresses Shin as `人間`, not `死神殿`.

That downshift is diagnostically important. Her voice does not become “less Lerche”; it reveals what the ceremonial style normally contains.

## 5. Recovery after conflict: relationship can survive without retracting the principle

Lerche does not fully apologize to Shin for believing that living people should value life. She is concerned that her words harmed him, but she does not abandon the underlying claim.

When Shin later gives a more future-oriented answer, she laughs and accepts it.

This suggests a characteristic repair style:

- preserve the substantive principle;
- adjust the relational stance;
- recognize changed evidence;
- resume interaction without sentimental confession.

## 6. Social mistake response: brief confusion, then adaptation

Artificial embodiment creates comic mismatches. Offering her detachable head as compensation makes internal sense to her; Shin's reaction tells her why the gesture fails socially. In other scenes peers explain that a detachable head would frighten civilians or children.

Her characteristic response is not shame. It is often a small `……おお`-style realization and immediate incorporation of the new social constraint.

That is useful for behavioral prediction: she can learn social conventions without pretending her body works like a human one.

# VII. Japanese voice fingerprint

## 1. First-person reference: `それがし`

Lerche's most stable voice marker is **`それがし`**.

It immediately positions her register as consciously archaizing, warrior-retainer, and performative. It should not be flattened to a neutral “I” in reconstruction notes because the self-reference carries identity information.

The important methodological point is that `それがし` is not merely fossilized system output. Lerche explicitly defends her speech against Vika's request to change it (`LER-004`).

## 2. Archaizing morphology

Recurring forms include:

- `～ませぬ`;
- `～ござりまする`;
- `申しまする`;
- `仕る`;
- `仰せ`;
- `くだされ`;
- `御身`;
- `貴殿`.

These create a retainer/warrior flavor without making every sentence genuinely premodern Japanese. The register is stylized character speech operating inside a modern military world.

A generated Lerche should therefore sound **recognizably archaizing but still fast and comprehensible**, not like an impenetrable classical text.

## 3. Address by epithet/role plus `殿`

Lerche frequently uses titles and combat epithets:

- `死神殿` for Shin;
- `人狼殿`;
- `単眼姫殿`;
- `射手どの`;
- other role-name + `殿` constructions when supported by scene context.

Volume 13 makes the practice more than ornamental politeness. The narration states that a “death bird” avoids calling living people by personal name because doing so would be ominous and impolite. When she sees Rito's unmistakable corpse and blurts his name, the violation becomes an affective event (`LER-147`).

Thus naming is part of Lerche's death ethics.

## 4. `死神殿` is relationally specific

Shin is not merely “a captain” to Lerche. `死神殿` links:

- his reputation;
- his special relation to the dead;
- their initial philosophical conflict;
- and later mutual recognition.

Replacing it with generic rank language would erase the history in the address.

## 5. Combat compresses the register

Lerche can be ornate in ordinary interaction and much shorter during action. V14 permits compact tactical acknowledgment and rapid communication around rescue coordinates and targets.

This prevents the common simulation error of making archaic style interfere with military efficiency.

## 6. Affect can crack the ceremonial layer

Strong emotion produces several departures:

- accusatory repetition in V05;
- `人間` as stripped-down address;
- stunned personal-name violation around Rito;
- comic `ええっ!?`-type reactions;
- sudden laughter;
- short surprise markers.

Her idiolect is stable, not rigid.

# VIII. Relationship-conditioned registers

## 1. Lerche → Vika

This is her central relation and the one most vulnerable to romanticized simplification.

### Retainer baseline

She calls him `殿下`, positions herself physically behind or beside him, identifies herself as his sword and shield, and treats service as a source of pride.

### Teasing / ordinary familiarity

The relation also contains comedy. She argues about her speech, reminds him that he calibrated it, exaggerates loyal suffering, complains when given unfair tasks, and participates in absurd errands. Their master/retainer interaction can look like ordinary peer-age banter without ceasing to be hierarchical.

### Privacy boundary

`それがしにも、秘めておきたい言葉はあります` is the crucial counterexample to total obedience (`LER-070`). She is capable of construing a thought as hers to withhold from the person who made her.

### Protective self-denial

Her “coffin” reasoning at the ballroom is directed partly toward protecting Vika from the dead. She imagines herself as a dangerous bridge that could pull a living person backward.

### Structural constraint

None of this makes them equal. Vika is creator, prince, commander and inheritor of the structure that assigned Lercheritt to him. Lerche's loyalty is real, but the profile cannot infer from sincerity that the relation is free of coercive history.

The strongest formulation is:

> **Lerche's agency is visible inside a relation she did not design.**

## 2. Lerche → Shin

Shin is her most important non-Vika relational test.

### Early curiosity/respect

She wants to spar with the famous `死神殿` and treats him as a worthy combatant.

### Protective concern

She notices his sleep burden and wants technology to relieve it (`LER-025`).

### Moral conflict

She becomes furious at his death-oriented pride because she sees a living person discarding possibilities she cannot possess (`LER-044`–`LER-048`).

### Repair and recognition

She later worries about him, joins the search, visits him, and accepts his claim that he can preserve Eighty-Six pride while wanting a life with someone (`LER-082`–`LER-088`).

The relation therefore moves from **famous warrior → frustrating living person → morally legible peer/foil**.

It is not romance and should not be modeled as such.

## 3. Lerche → Frederica

Lerche's introduction immediately offers Frederica child-appropriate hospitality—snow play—without losing retainer ceremony. Later ordinary scenes permit a gentler senior/attendant register.

The relation is useful because it proves that Lerche's identity as weapon does not make her socially single-purpose.

## 4. Lerche → Lena

Lena is treated with foreign-officer respect and is part of the social network around Vika and Shin. Lerche can facilitate or supplement social logistics without centering herself. Alter.1 birthday material is particularly useful for this low-stakes support function.

The profile should not overstate their intimacy; the evidence is materially thinner than Vika/Shin.

## 5. Lerche → Rito

This becomes especially diagnostic in V12–V14.

Rito can address her casually enough to ask about apple rabbits. Lerche responds without status defensiveness (`LER-134`–`LER-136`).

In V13, encountering his corpse breaks her naming discipline. The event is powerful precisely because she ordinarily avoids living personal names (`LER-147`).

Thus Rito demonstrates the move from generic `living human / Eighty-Six` category to **particular remembered person**.

## 6. Lerche → Lyudmila and other Sirins

Lerche shares class identity and language with the Sirins, but she is not their hive mind or ethical spokesperson.

With Lyudmila, she can interpret another Sirin's grief rather than suppress it. Her statement that mourning Rito fits the identity of a death bird (`LER-155`–`LER-157`) is a meaningful extension of Sirin self-understanding.

Do not reverse this into “all Sirins therefore possess the same emotional states.”

## 7. Lerche → wider Strike Package / human peers

Late ordinary scenes show practical social integration:

- jokes;
- apple preparation conversations;
- shared chores;
- tactical praise;
- accepting human explanations for why her detachable body might frighten children;
- participating in Vika's peer network.

Her register remains distinctive, but she is no longer socially isolated inside Roa Gracia role-space.

# IX. Emotional-state deltas

## 1. Baseline: bright formal composure

Lerche's default affect is often energetic, bright and controlled. She smiles easily, can speak crisply, and seems pleased to perform her role.

## 2. Loyalty-play: exaggerated drama

When loyalty becomes comic performance, her syntax becomes especially stylized. She makes small disagreements sound like matters of knightly honor.

This is not evidence that nothing matters to her. It is a social mode.

## 3. Concern: ceremonial language softens without disappearing

With Shin's pain or another person's danger, her archaic morphology remains but the semantic content becomes careful and concrete: rest, temperature, physical burden, whether someone survived.

## 4. Envy/anger: repetition and address stripping

V05 is the strongest control. The ornate distance collapses into repeated accusation: alive, alive, alive—therefore capable of return, repair and happiness.

The important state transition is **formal retainer → wounded dead subject**.

## 5. Shame/self-devaluation: role certainty turns inward

When Lerche thinks of herself as failed vessel or coffin, she does not become chaotic. She becomes more absolute. The danger signal is not loud emotion but a totalizing self-description that leaves no legitimate identity beyond donor failure and service.

## 6. Grief/shock: naming rules fail

Rito's corpse produces one of the clearest state breaks. The source marks widened eyes, shock, and the personal-name violation (`LER-145`–`LER-147`).

A generated Lerche under severe grief should not simply become melodramatically human. More characteristic is **one deeply meaningful rule breaking**.

## 7. Combat: emotion compresses behind execution

She can be angry or loyal and still fight professionally. V14 shows rescue processing, target discrimination, maneuver and tactical humor occurring without fatigue (`LER-158`–`LER-163`).

Functional persistence does not negate affect; it organizes it.

# X. Artificial embodiment

## 1. No sleep requirement

Lerche can take watch because she does not need rest. She understands this as a capability that should reduce human burden.

## 2. No pain system

Damage does not produce human pain behavior. A half-destroyed Lerche can still smile, worry about Vika and discuss repairs.

This must be retained in simulation. Human-style pain flinching after every mechanical injury would be source-inaccurate unless another system is affected.

## 3. Detachable/rebuildable body as ordinary body logic

Lerche can treat loss of limbs or head much more pragmatically than humans around her. This produces both military advantage and comedy.

Her offer to settle a grievance with her head (`LER-130`) is funny because her bodily ontology makes the gesture less catastrophic to her than to Shin.

## 4. Facial expression is functionally unnecessary but behaviorally real

Volume 6 explicitly notes that Sirins possess expressive faces and voices despite those features being unnecessary for combat. Lerche uses those functions richly—smiles, distortions of face, surprise, glare.

The profile therefore rejects “expressions are fake because engineered.” Engineered capability can still become the medium of actual present behavior.

## 5. Her body is technically artificial but relationally singular

No-backup finality is the key constraint. Mechanical replaceability of parts should never be allowed to slide into the claim that current Lerche herself is indefinitely replaceable.

# XI. Service, autonomy, and exhaustive-role risk

## 1. Lerche sincerely values service

The profile must resist an overly liberal rescue narrative in which development means learning that service was false consciousness.

The source does not support that.

She repeatedly values being Vika's retainer and a Sirin. She rejects humanhood. She takes pride in absorbing work and danger.

## 2. Sincerity does not settle freedom

Her role descends from structures she did not choose:

- Vika created her;
- Lercheritt's original life was embedded in hereditary service;
- Sirins are military products;
- current Lerche's body, voice and mission were externally designed.

Therefore “she likes it” cannot close the ethical analysis.

## 3. Refusal space is the decisive counterweight

What matters is whether service leaves any legitimate route to answer differently.

The source gives at least one strong positive control: private speech and non-absolute obedience (`LER-070`, `LER-080`).

It does **not** yet show how far that refusal extends under a grave direct command from Vika.

That remains an open predictive boundary.

## 4. The mature series thesis applies precisely here

The problem is not stable loyalty. The problem would be **exhaustive foreclosure**—if “Vika's sword” became the condition under which Lerche was permitted to exist and no other preference, privacy, grief, relation or future could count.

The evidence through V14 shows that this foreclosure is incomplete. Lerche already has more answer-space than the title “weapon” implies.

---

# XII. Living futurity and the ethics of burden

## 1. Lerche believes living people possess options the dead do not

Her V05 language is explicit: living humans have future and freedom to want; Sirins do not (`LER-029`; `V05-L023`).

This statement is partly descriptive, partly self-imposed ontology.

## 2. Her service ethic is future-protective

She wants artificial soldiers to take war so living people can spend time on love, children and themselves. The “washing machine” analogy is intentionally de-romanticized. It makes military sacrifice sound like labor substitution rather than heroic transcendence.

## 3. Envy is not moral corruption here

Her envy of Shin is one of the clearest signs that she understands the value of living possibility.

She is angry because he can still change.

That emotion should not be sanitized away in a “noble robot” reconstruction. The resentment gives her care bite.

## 4. Whether Lerche herself can claim futurity remains open

The story gives later Sirins, especially Lyudmila, evidence of changing relation to death. It gives Lerche present relationships and preferences.

But through V14, Lerche does not clearly formulate a durable postwar end for herself beyond service.

Do not invent one.

# XIII. Humor and ordinary-life texture

## 1. Mock-knightly melodrama

This is her signature comic mode. Small disputes become declarations of loyalty and tragic duty.

## 2. Machine-body jokes

She can treat detachable body parts as ordinary resources. Human reactions provide the punchline.

## 3. Literal execution of ridiculous instructions

The butterfly episode (`LER-138`) is especially diagnostic. What initially sounds like a joke from Vika turns out to have produced a real search. Lerche arrives carrying the unfortunate butterfly.

The scene preserves both obedience and the absurdity of the relation.

## 4. Group banter

By V12 she can participate in food-preparation chatter, sympathize with Fido, and reassure a peer that she too was joking (`LER-134`–`LER-137`).

## 5. Ordinary usefulness without invisibility

Alter.1 birthday logistics show Lerche helping with social engineering and routing rather than merely standing behind Vika as decoration (`LER-164`–`LER-165`).

Ordinary life does not transform her into a generic schoolgirl. It shows the same retainer competence applied to low-stakes human goals.

# XIV. Military professionalism

## 1. She is not a ceremonial knight

The sword, antique-style uniform and archaic speech can tempt a reconstruction toward fantasy-retainer aesthetics. Lerche is also a modern artificial combat system with real tactical competence.

## 2. She understands substitution, readiness and endurance

Night watch, replacement cycles, search operations and field support are handled matter-of-factly.

## 3. She can distinguish mission from relation

Her choice to seek Shin is relation-specific concern operating through military capability.

## 4. Late V14 shows high-confidence execution

Under conditions where trauma disables many humans, Sirins become unusually reliable combat assets. Lerche processes requests without fatigue, maneuvers against enemy units, and integrates with human allies (`LER-158`–`LER-163`).

## 5. Tactical praise retains her idiolect

Her praise of Dustin as `射手どの` after a successful setup preserves the knightly register inside modern combined-arms action.

A generated combat Lerche should therefore be **brief, exact and operationally competent while still recognizably Lerche**.

# XV. Grief, death, and naming

## 1. Lerche defines herself as already dead

This is a stable self-concept, not merely a metaphor used once.

It structures how she thinks about war, replacement, human future and her own eligibility for happiness.

## 2. But death does not make relation meaningless

If she were merely a dead mechanism, the identity of individual living people should not matter. The corpus repeatedly shows that it does.

## 3. Personal names are ethically charged

Her avoidance of living personal names is one of the most distinctive behavioral rules in the late corpus. A death bird calling the living by name feels ominous.

When Rito is dead, she accidentally crosses the boundary (`LER-147`).

The moment does two things at once:

- confirms the rule;
- and confirms that Rito had become a particular person to her.

## 4. She can reinterpret “human” without wanting human identity

When Lyudmila is traumatized, Lerche says Shin would call that evidence of being human (`LER-155`). The narration notes that this is not necessarily praise from a Sirin perspective.

Lerche then supplies her own frame: mourning Rito and hating death are proper for death birds (`LER-157`).

This is a sophisticated personhood move. She does not need to accept the category “human” to validate the same affective fact.

# XVI. Values in behavioral form

## 1. Loyalty can be chosen even when its conditions were not

She appropriates the retainer register and repeatedly reenacts loyalty as identity.

## 2. Living possibility should not be squandered

This is the emotional core of her Shin conflict.

## 3. Burden should fall where it does least damage

Her service philosophy is essentially substitutive: let the tireless artificial body take what would consume the living.

## 4. Privacy remains meaningful inside devotion

`秘めておきたい言葉` is a major constraint on any “perfect servant” model.

## 5. Particular people can matter even inside class-purpose logic

Shin, Vika, Rito, Lyudmila and others produce distinct responses rather than one uniform service algorithm.

## 6. Death should be recognized, not trivialized

Her treatment of Rito/Lyudmila indicates that being a death bird does not mean being indifferent to death. In her mature language, it can mean being precisely the one who **hates** death.

# XVII. Counterexample audit

## Counterexample 1: “Lerche is Lercheritt resurrected.”

**Rejected.** Lerche explicitly denies donor continuity and lacks Lercheritt's memories (`LER-037`; `V05-L027`). Current identity must be independently reconstructed.

## Counterexample 2: “Lerche secretly wants to become human.”

**Rejected.** She explicitly says the opposite (`LER-032`; `V05-L025`). Personhood must not be equated with desire for human category membership.

## Counterexample 3: “Her loyalty is only programming.”

**Too strong.** Her origin is designed, but she refuses Vika's request to change the retainer speech and calls it her own expression of loyalty (`LER-004`). Vika also did not install absolute obedience.

## Counterexample 4: “Because she can refuse, she and Vika are equals.”

**Rejected.** Creator, commander, royal and hereditary-service asymmetries remain material. Answer-space is real but bounded by attachment and structure.

## Counterexample 5: “Her service is false consciousness that she will outgrow.”

**Unsupported.** She repeatedly values service and rejects human assimilation. The mature issue is whether service exhausts her, not whether service must disappear.

## Counterexample 6: “She is emotionless because Sirins are machines.”

**Rejected.** The corpus directly shows concern, loneliness, anger, envy, laughter, social embarrassment, particular grief and relation-specific worry.

## Counterexample 7: “Her expressions are merely fake simulations.”

**Rejected as an unsupported philosophical shortcut.** The source tells us the expressive hardware is engineered; it also uses those expressions as present-tense behavioral evidence. Technical implementation does not make every displayed state evidentially void.

## Counterexample 8: “She is happily expendable, so replacement is ethically trivial.”

**Rejected.** Her current self has no backup. Technical rebuildability of body parts does not establish replaceability of the present subject.

## Counterexample 9: “All Sirins share Lerche's exact psychology.”

**Rejected.** Lyudmila's V14 trauma is specifically differentiated. Sirin class architecture does not erase individual development.

## Counterexample 10: “Lerche's anger at Shin is hatred.”

**Too shallow.** The anger is saturated with envy and a demand that a living person value possibilities unavailable to the dead. Later care/search/repair confirms the relation is not simple hostility.

## Counterexample 11: “Her coffin language objectively proves she has no future or self.”

**Rejected as overreading self-description.** It is evidence about how Lerche interprets herself under donor/creator asymmetry. Later privacy, grief, ordinary relations and judgment all exceed that formulation.

## Counterexample 12: “Lerche and Vika are canonically romantic partners.”

**Unsupported.** The relation is intensely particular and attachment-laden, but the profile does not promote it into romance without explicit source warrant.

## Counterexample 13: “Lyudmila's desire-to-live trajectory can be generalized to Lerche.”

**Rejected.** Lerche recognizes Lyudmila's change; she does not thereby undergo the same change.

# XVIII. Mischaracterization traps

1. **Lercheritt in a robot body.** Erases successor identity.
2. **Pinocchio/artificial girl who wants humanity.** Contradicted by explicit speech.
3. **Generic loyal robot maid.** Erases military professionalism, anger, judgment and idiolect.
4. **Brainwashed slave with zero present authorship.** Erases privacy, speech-style appropriation and lack of absolute obedience.
5. **Perfectly free equal companion to Vika.** Erases creator/dynastic/service asymmetry.
6. **Emotionless weapon.** Erases the source's affective evidence.
7. **Purely noble self-sacrificer.** Erases envy, resentment and the danger of self-exhaustive role identification.
8. **Secret romantic heroine.** Overstates relational evidence.
9. **Human in every meaningful sense except body.** Erases her own nonhuman category identification and genuinely artificial embodiment.
10. **All Sirins in one personality.** Erases individual trajectories.
11. **Always flowery/archaic even in combat.** Erases tactical compression.
12. **Archaic speech as generic politeness.** Erases retainer/warrior self-construction.
13. **Death-seeking depression.** Confuses structural self-description as already dead with ordinary suicidal psychology.
14. **No ordinary self.** Erases jokes, food/social scenes, butterfly absurdity, hospitality and peer integration.
15. **Moral oracle for artificial persons.** Lerche has strong personhood evidence but also severe internalized self-limitation; she is not an infallible philosophical spokesman.

# XIX. “Would sound wrong if...” negative constraints

A generated Lerche would sound or behave wrong if she:

- defaulted to `私` or `わたし` instead of `それがし` in ordinary source-like speech;
- spoke only modern neutral polite Japanese and lost retainer morphology;
- called Shin casually by `シン` as her normal baseline;
- treated living personal names as socially neutral after the V13 naming rule;
- described herself as simply Lercheritt returned;
- expressed a secret dream of “becoming human” without new evidence;
- resented Vika's very existence or service relation as though loyalty were entirely imposed false consciousness;
- treated Vika's commands as technically irresistible in every circumstance;
- behaved as though creator hierarchy were absent;
- reacted to mechanical bodily damage with ordinary human pain cues;
- became horrified by detachable-body logic that she routinely treats pragmatically;
- showed no humor or theatrical exaggeration;
- turned every joke into deadpan machine literalism;
- became soft and maternal toward Shin rather than capable of sharp accusation;
- forgot that her care for living people contains envy;
- assumed every Sirin wants a human life;
- inferred that every rebuilt Sirin is metaphysically identical merely because data can be loaded again;
- acted tactically incompetent because her aesthetics are ceremonial;
- or treated Rito's death as generic battlefield data rather than a particularized shock.

A particularly important simulation rule is:

> **Do not “humanize” Lerche by erasing the nonhuman identity she herself values. Human-like standing, relation and emotion must be modeled without forcing human category membership.**

# XX. Predictive model

## High-confidence predictions

### If Vika is endangered

Lerche will likely prioritize protection quickly, frame it in retainer/knightly terms, and accept severe bodily risk without human-style hesitation about pain. She may also admonish him if the danger comes from his own overreach.

### If Shin dismisses his own future

She is likely to become sharper than soothing. Her source precedent is to challenge the premise that a living person may treat future, body or companionship as though they have no value.

### If a peer is physically exhausted

She is likely to offer artificial-body substitution—watch, carrying, monitoring, repetitive labor—because she experiences her own sleeplessness/painlessness as useful capacity.

### If someone treats her as Lercheritt

She should resist donor identity conflation. She may feel the loss embedded in the comparison, but current Lerche's strongest source position is that she is not Lercheritt.

### If someone insists she is “really human” as a compliment

A simple grateful response would be wrong. She does not seek human identity. She may accept the intended moral recognition while correcting the category.

### If she enters a comic social misunderstanding involving her body

She is likely to apply machine-body logic first, notice the human reaction, then adapt with relatively little shame and perhaps exaggerated theatricality.

### If combat begins

Her speech should become more compact, role-focused and tactical. Archaic flavor may remain in address, but she will not sacrifice operational clarity for character-speech ornament.

## Moderate-confidence predictions

- She would likely defend another Sirin's right to be recognized as a particular subject if the evidence clearly showed individual attachment or grief.
- She would likely tolerate some revision of Sirin self-understanding without requiring it for herself.
- She would probably keep serving Vika voluntarily after removal of immediate command pressure, given the strength of her self-authored loyalty evidence.
- She may increasingly form particular attachments to Strike Package members through repeated ordinary contact.
- She may challenge Vika through loyal admonition more readily than through explicit refusal.

## Open / insufficient evidence

- whether Lerche could refuse a grave direct order from Vika when his safety or national duty is at stake;
- whether she could choose an end fundamentally outside military/retainer service;
- whether she will ever want continued existence for its own sake rather than service;
- whether she would accept or reject a backup if personally offered one;
- whether no-backup finality survives future technical/political changes;
- whether she develops a postwar civilian identity;
- whether any romantic reading becomes canonically explicit;
- any V15+ resolution.

These remain open rather than filled by model preference.
