# T11 — Race, Othering, Disposability, and Sector Reproduction Retrieval

This dossier retrieves the evidence needed to answer one of the most methodologically dangerous questions in the V2 corpus: **when does comparison to San Magnolia's Eighty-Sixth Sector illuminate a repeated structure of exclusion and disposability, and when does the comparison erase the historical specificity of a racialized program of denaturalization, concentration, coerced military exposure, archival erasure, and planned extermination?**

The current answer is deliberately asymmetric:

> **The Sector is both a historically specific institution and a structurally reusable relation. Its historical specificity comes from the Republic's racial classification of Colorata, stripping of citizenship and human standing, forced concentration and property seizure, coerced labor and military service, euphemistic conversion of human soldiers into unmanned-machine components, suppression of casualty and identity records, systematic exposure to death, and finally a policy logic in which surviving witnesses themselves become a political liability to be eliminated. Later institutions or crises may reproduce some of those relations without reproducing that entire history. Structural analogy is therefore graded, not binary.**

The governing adversarial question is:

> **Which elements have to recur before “another Eighty-Sixth Sector” is an analytically defensible comparison, and which additional features make San Magnolia's original system historically and morally more specific than a generic case of prejudice, inequality, forced military service, or authoritarian rule?**

# 0. Retrieval method and lexical scope

The primary-source sweep was rerun against the locked original-Japanese EPUBs for Volumes 1-14 plus Alter.1 in its already-audited chronological position. Alter.2 remains excluded from mainline fact. The search was intentionally separated into racial classification, legal denaturalization, exclusion, dehumanizing language, blood hierarchy, disposability, blocked return, mass violence, representational othering, and Fawn weaponization. This prevents a frequent analytic failure: inferring that every use of `エイティシックス`, every insult, or every dangerous mission belongs to one undifferentiated phenomenon.

The machine pass produced **5,272 lexical-family hit rows across 4,422 unique primary-source paragraphs**. The human-facing ledger retains **211 context-reread items**. Frequency is not treated as moral weight. `エイティシックス` is frequent because it is both an imposed classification and a reclaimed identity; retreat vocabulary appears in ordinary military doctrine as well as no-return systems; hereditary language appears in Giad and the Alliance Kingdom without necessarily producing racial genocide.

# 1. Executive taxonomy — comparisons Phase 6 must keep separate

| Structure | Definition for T11 | Why it is not automatically “the Sector” |
|---|---|---|
| **Racial classification** | population sorted into inherited/phenotypic groups and assigned political meaning | classification alone need not entail denaturalization or extermination |
| **Denaturalization** | citizenship or legal membership removed from a targeted population | can exist without forced frontline exposure |
| **Dehumanization** | persons described or administered as animals, machines, components, monsters or subhuman categories | rhetoric may be socially ugly without having the original Sector's legal force |
| **Concentration / segregation** | targeted population forcibly spatially separated | may be punitive, protective, military, carceral or genocidal depending purpose and conditions |
| **Expropriation** | property/resources transferred from targeted group to beneficiaries of exclusion | important part of the Republic's material political economy |
| **Coerced service** | target required to labor or fight while exit/refusal is restricted | conscription alone is not equivalent to racialized extermination |
| **Disposability** | continued existence is institutionally valued less than the utility obtained by exposure or death | can target legally recognized people as V13 demonstrates |
| **Blocked return** | exposed population has no meaningful route back to ordinary membership/home | strongest structural marker shared by V01 and V13 |
| **Archival erasure** | names, deaths, records or causal responsibility systematically removed | makes extermination easier to deny after the fact |
| **Extermination logic** | survivor existence itself becomes a political liability or policy problem | one of the strongest features separating V01 from generic harsh military systems |
| **Scapegoating** | a wider crisis is displaced onto a weaker, legible target group | can emerge without stable racial doctrine |
| **Category substitution** | a society reproduces othering by transferring stigma to a new target | morally serious but not proof of historical equivalence |
| **Sector reproduction** | a human buffer is made to absorb danger while refusal and return are materially closed | structural relation; requires several features in conjunction |

T11 therefore adopts a **conjunctive threshold**. A strong structural Sector analogy normally needs at least: **expendability + constrained refusal + blocked return + social/political distancing**, with stronger analogies adding legal status degradation, targeted identity, segregation, archival erasure, or explicit elimination logic.

# 2. V01 — the historically specific sequence

V01 must remain the anchor against which every later analogy is tested. Its racism is not merely the existence of `白豚` insults or a population being hated. The original Japanese text narrates a sequence in which race becomes administration.

> `豚に人権を与えぬことを`

The memoir epigraph is deliberately compressed. It presents denial of rights through animal classification: once the target is called a pig, withholding human rights can be made to appear conceptually coherent. But V01 immediately makes clear that language matters because **law and institutions materialize it**.

The fresh direct-source evidence records the key legal move: Colorata residing in the Republic are designated enemy citizens, stripped of citizenship, and isolated in concentration camps. The law is explicitly described as inconsistent with the Republic's own constitution and as racially overinclusive: white people of Imperial origin are excluded from the measure while non-Imperial Colorata are included. That is strong primary evidence that “enemy ancestry” is pretext rather than adequate causal description.

The next step is material extraction. Colorata are sent to camps, subjected to military service and labor on the Gran Mur, while seized Colorata assets fund the arrangement and let the protected citizen population avoid military labor and some wartime burdens. **The system therefore generates beneficiaries.** Racial exclusion is not only belief; it redistributes risk, labor, property and death.

V01 then supplies the semantic conversion:

> `人間ではないのだから / 情報処理装置`

The target is not human; therefore a vehicle carrying the target can still be called unmanned. `プロセッサー` becomes an administrative technology for preserving the Republic's fiction that no human soldiers die at the front. This is why dehumanizing language cannot be separated from casualty accounting.

> `その戦場に、死者はいない`

The battlefield with “no dead” is made possible by deciding in advance which deaths are statistically incapable of counting.

# 3. V01 — promised return is part of the exterminatory architecture

The five-year citizenship promise deserves special emphasis because it prevents a simplistic model in which the Republic merely expels people and forgets them. The state actively **uses the possibility of restored membership** to extract service. Direct V01 evidence states that an Eighty-Six and their family are promised restored citizenship in exchange for service, and then makes clear that the promise was never intended to be honored for anomalous long-term survivors.

This creates a precise political technology:

1. citizenship is removed;
2. restoration is offered conditionally;
3. military exposure makes fulfillment statistically unlikely;
4. exceptional survivors become possible organizers, witnesses and political liabilities;
5. Special Recon resolves the anomaly by making survival itself the condition to eliminate.

> `守らせるためではなく、死なせるために`

The Special Reconnaissance mission is therefore not merely a high-risk assignment. Its purpose is to close the return path.

V01's later explanation makes the exterminatory logic explicit. If Eighty-Six survive the war, concentration, expropriation, coerced service and other abuses become objects of criticism and compensation. If surrounding Colorata societies survive, the Republic also faces international knowledge of persecution. The proposed solution is not integration, trial or demobilization. It is that these political problems disappear if the Eighty-Six disappear.

This is a crucial historical-specificity marker for T11:

> **The original Sector is not only a buffer system. It contains a survivor-elimination logic directed at erasing the evidence and political consequences of the preceding persecution.**

That feature should not be casually attributed to every later case of coercive military exposure.

# 4. V01 — archive destruction and the production of “people who never existed”

The Republic's policy also attacks evidentiary existence.

> `存在しなかったこと`

Direct-source material adds that graves and death records are not allowed to remain and Processor personnel files are destroyed. Once the targeted population is gone, official evidence can be made to imply that no persecution occurred because the persecuted population can no longer be shown to have existed in institutionally recognized form.

This strengthens the V1 interpretation of names. Names do not metaphysically create persons. They matter because they resist a state strategy in which administration first converts people to numbers/components and then destroys the files through which responsibility could later be reconstructed.

> `名前さえ、一回だって訊いたことがない`

Theo's rebuke to Lena about never asking names is therefore politically sharper than a generic lesson about empathy. **Particular knowledge resists the convenience of category.** Shin's dead-name fragments become a counter-archive because they preserve relations the official system intends to make unrecoverable.

T11 thus distinguishes:

- recognition of a generic group;
- recognition of a legal person;
- recognition of a particular named person;
- preservation of institutional evidence that this person lived and was acted upon.

The original Sector attacks all four at different points.

# 5. V01 — anti-essentialism is present from the beginning

The text simultaneously resists an inverse essentialism in which oppression morally purifies the oppressed or race perfectly predicts conduct.

> `より数が少なくて、弱い者に冷たい`

> `悪い人じゃない`

Kaie states that some Alba are good and some Eighty-Six are bad. V01 also supplies white individuals who resisted concentration and Colorata who reproduced internal hostility toward mixed or minority members. Direct-source evidence notes that people of white ancestry, Imperial noble ancestry, or minority eastern/southern origins could become targets **inside the camps** because they provided convenient outlets for resentment.

This does not create moral symmetry between the Republic and its victims. The institutional power and historical responsibility remain radically unequal.

The correct proposition is narrower:

> **Being subjected to racialized oppression does not make a person or group incapable of othering. It does not make the oppression less real, nor does later victim wrongdoing retroactively legitimate the original persecution.**

This control becomes indispensable in V13-V14, where Shin, Raiden and other Eighty-Six confront their own capacity to reproduce faceless “them” categories.

# 6. V02-V04 — legal recognition does not end othering

Giad changes the legal structure immediately.

> `今日から連邦市民`

The Five become Federacy citizens. That is a real change in standing, not symbolic paperwork. Yet V02 rapidly shows that legal personhood does not eliminate social flattening.

> `共和国の化物め`

> `戦い狂いのエイティシックス`

The former victims can be redescribed as Republic-made monsters or battle-mad Eighty-Six once their agency violates the expected script of the pitiful rescued child. V02 also gives the benevolent version: pity can position the observer above the subject even when the observer genuinely wants the subject protected.

> `一方的な同情はむしろ理解の放棄`

This matters for T11 because **vertical misrecognition is not morally equivalent to genocidal dehumanization**, even though both can flatten the subject. The dossier therefore rejects the earlier temptation to treat pity and contempt as interchangeable because both “look down.” They share a vertical structure; their institutional consequences and moral severity differ.

V03 sharpens the point:

> `区別も差別も、悪意だけじゃない`

Distinction and discrimination can arise from unequal protection, not only explicit hatred. `一番失っても惜しくない駒` then demonstrates how legally recognized citizens can still be selected as the socially cheapest risk-bearers.

V04 shows the Republic's categories surviving territorial collapse. People remain described as assets, machine inventories and sacrifice resources; racial supremacist politics persists through the `聖マグノリア純血純白憂国騎士団`.

The conclusion is important:

> **Legal personhood is necessary, but category hierarchy can persist socially and administratively after legal status changes.**

# 7. V05-V08 — nonracial functionalization is a comparator, not historical identity

V05-V08 are useful partly because they demonstrate ways of reducing subjects to function that are **not primarily racial**.

The Sirins are artificial entities assigned war purposes:

> `未来などない。課された役目しかない`

and the text is explicitly unwilling to make “not human” settle every moral question:

> `人間ではないと言い放つことは...できなかった`

This is a personhood/function comparison, not another racial genocide. The distinction matters because later synthesis should not call every system of functionalization “racialization” merely because it resembles the Sector in making a subject useful.

V06 makes the utility problem explicit through Sirin expendability while simultaneously showing the Eighty-Six reclaiming their own imposed name as identity. The category `エイティシックス` has therefore changed semantic ownership. It was imposed as a slur/status; survivors can later use it as a self-description carrying pride and shared history.

Reclamation does **not** validate the original category. It demonstrates that the historical target can reinterpret a term after escaping the institution that originally fixed its meaning.

V07 gives the strongest first-person anti-objectification statement:

> `おれは家畜でもなければ無人機の部品でもない`

Shin refuses both animal ontology and machine-component ontology while retaining Eighty-Six identity. This combination is central: **rejecting dehumanization does not require rejecting every identity formed under oppression.**

V08 then makes the institutional contrast concrete. The strike force is to be brought back alive:

> `機動打撃群は生還させる`

and the characters explicitly reject the old normalization that Eighty-Six death is simply expected. The difference from V01 is therefore not that military danger disappears. It is that danger is embedded in institutions that plan for return rather than systems that require nonreturn.

# 8. V09 — sacred functionalization and blood hierarchy as comparative controls

V09 provides two especially valuable controls because they are oppressive without reproducing San Magnolia's racial form exactly.

The Holy Theocracy says:

> `神戟は人ではないが、神の御使い`

Its sacred warriors are denied ordinary human category while being elevated rather than despised. They receive honor and education but lack meaningful role choice.

This is **sacred functionalization**. It proves that denial of ordinary personhood can operate through reverence as well as contempt. But it should not be called equivalent to the Republic's livestock ontology: the mechanisms of status, affect, benefit and violence differ.

Milmelkoleo supplies a closer blood-hierarchy comparison. Mixed-blood or otherwise devalued soldiers can acquire conditional worth through heroic military performance. Their social dignity becomes functionally purchased by service.

> `heroic proof as only value for mixed-blood soldiers`

The analytic commonality with the Sector is **utility-conditioned standing**. The historical difference is that V09 does not reproduce the Republic's total Colorata denaturalization/concentration/extermination program.

This is exactly why T11 uses graded analogy rather than binary labels.

# 9. V10 — retrospective evidence: names, medical utility, and hierarchy inside the oppressed

V10 turns the reader backward toward the Sector and makes several structures materially harder to romanticize.

> `名前もコールサインも、収容番号も`

Personal name, call sign and internment number can collapse together in Shin's self-understanding because the camp system has reorganized identity around administrative/military survival. Later recovery of names and family records is therefore not merely sentimental genealogy; it reverses a system that reduced people to manageable entries and then destroyed those entries.

The retrospective medical material also shows that an injury may be considered “unsalvageable” because the wounded Processor cannot rapidly return to combat. This is a functional death sentence produced institutionally, not biologically.

V10 further restores the existence of hierarchy inside the persecuted population. Mixed noble ancestry, national origin and internal differences can be weaponized among Eighty-Six themselves. Again, the conclusion is not symmetry. It is that **the social technology of finding a more vulnerable subgroup does not belong exclusively to the original persecutor.**

The V10 narrator's formulation that continuing to live can itself be resistance to persecution is consequently important:

> `迫害に対し人ができる最大にして最高の抵抗`

Survival denies the exterminatory system its desired closure.

# 10. V11 — the Republic collapse proves the inside/outside relation is reproducible

V11 is the first volume to make the structural claim unmistakable without requiring another Colorata genocide.

> `エイティシックスじゃなくてもよかったんだ`

The insight is that a society trained to solve danger by assigning an expendable outside can reproduce the structure when the original outside is absent. Direct-source material shows white-majority Republic society fragmenting by class, military status, aristocratic privilege and accusations of who is dying versus who is safe.

This is a **reproducibility argument**, not a claim that all groups become Eighty-Six in the historical sense.

The direct V11 evidence is especially useful. Battlefield dead are mostly white because the Republic population is mostly white; the apparent visual concentration of white casualties does not establish that Colorata are now institutionally protected. Yet another passage immediately shows the social reasoning that can follow: “those people” are not dying, they have Imperial blood, they are not us. The target category can move.

V11 also differentiates civilians from soldiers and individual conduct from race. Kurena protects Alba siblings while remaining unforgiving toward the Republic. That demonstrates a personhood floor independent of affection or collective innocence.

The broader V11 rule is:

> **If a society has learned to make vulnerability politically bearable by transferring it to a designated outside, removal of the original target does not necessarily remove the transfer mechanism.**

This is the beginning of the structural Sector concept later made explicit in V13.

# 11. V12 — private atrocity extends dehumanization beyond public law

V12 prevents the Republic's racial system from being understood only as military administration.

> `共和国の、壁の中に`

> `痛めつけたり強姦したり`

The recovered child evidence shows Eighty-Six infants purchased, abused, subjected to Para-RAID-mediated violence, and discarded. The direct-source formulation is even more explicit that perpetrators could believe almost anything was permissible because Eighty-Six were treated as rightless inferior beings and human-shaped livestock.

This matters because state racialization changes the **private permission structure** of society. Once the law says a population is outside ordinary human standing, abuse can migrate into households, black markets, experimentation and entertainment.

The rescued child's inability to conceptualize that the former master no longer has authority is also crucial:

> `今は主人の支配下にないのだと`

Legal rescue does not instantly restore usable refusal. Racial domination can survive inside learned bodily and relational expectations after the law changes.

This strengthens T11's historical specificity: the original Sector is not merely where people fight. It is an ecosystem in which public nonpersonhood enables private predation.

# 12. V12 — anti-Alba backlash is category substitution, not moral equivalence

After Republic atrocities become more visible, Giad public opinion turns sharply against Republic people and even against visibly white-haired people more generally. Direct-source evidence records slurs directed at white hair and a broader claim that the reputation of white racial categories is deteriorating by association.

This is analytically serious because it demonstrates **category substitution**: people can respond to a real atrocity by overextending culpability from perpetrators and institutions to phenotype and ancestry.

But T11 rejects the inference that Giad backlash is “the same as” San Magnolia's genocide. The structural and material differences remain enormous:

- Alba in Giad retain legal personhood;
- they are not denaturalized as a race;
- they are not concentrated into exterminatory camps;
- the state does not define them as nonhuman military components;
- there is no comparable policy of erasing their deaths and eliminating survivors.

The right conclusion is therefore:

> **Anti-Alba prejudice proves that the logic of inherited group blame can reverse direction. It does not erase the asymmetry between prejudice against members of a former dominant group and the Republic's state-organized racial persecution.**

This distinction is essential if the final synthesis is to remain morally precise rather than treating every ethnic insult as interchangeable.

# 13. Alter.1 — racial hierarchy lives in domestic practice as well as law

Alter.1 provides low-pressure evidence unavailable in crisis-heavy mainline scenes.

The kitten story preserves a small but revealing pollution logic:

> `エイティシックスが飼っていたモノなんて汚らわしい`

An object associated with the Eighty-Six becomes disgusting because of that association. The stakes are far lower than concentration or military disposal, but this is exactly why the scene is useful. It shows how racial hierarchy can reproduce itself in ordinary domestic judgment without an official present to issue an order.

Alter.1 also gives a separate caste comparator through Vika and Lercheritt:

> `ヴィーカは紫瑛種で王族で、レルヒェは……彼女の娘であったレルヒェリートは翠水種で隷民だ`

Affection does not erase structural difference between royal and serf origins. Again, this is **not** San Magnolia's racial system. It is evidence for the broader methodological point that hierarchy may be inherited, intimate and normalized without requiring hatred.

T11 therefore treats low-pressure material as evidence for **social reproduction mechanisms**, not as proof of equal severity.

# 14. V13 — the Fawn project is a refinement of disposal after recognition

V13 is the most important later test because the Fawn program deliberately preserves a relation to the old Sector while changing its technology.

> `「エイティシックスは幸いにも人型をしていて、犬猫よりはマシな知能もある。──あれらを直接、自走地雷に変えてしまえば〈ジャガー...`

The underlying designers do not need to deny that the targets are biologically human. They study their bodies precisely because those bodies are technically useful.

> `「私たちは、ユート。あなたたちエイティシックスの──〈レギオン〉戦争終結後まで生き残ったエイティシックスの、処分用の兵器とし...`

The project's postwar purpose is especially important: surviving Eighty-Six themselves are the disposal target. This means the Fawn system inherits the original Sector's survivor problem. The state imagines a future in which the persecuted population's continued existence is inconvenient and designs a weapon to solve that political inconvenience.

Yet the mechanism changes. The old Sector used walls, minefields, administrative segregation and externally imposed frontline exposure. Fawns weaponize social proximity and trust. A girl who looks like a comrade can be sent among survivors and detonated with them.

This makes the Fawn project one of the strongest **historical continuities** with the original Sector while simultaneously proving that dehumanization does not require explicit denial of biological humanity.

> **Recognition can coexist with disposal if the institution recognizes the subject primarily as a useful dangerous body.**

# 15. V13 — dangerous embodiment does not create a duty to disappear

The Fawn case is morally difficult because their danger is real. T11 therefore cannot solve the issue by arguing that the public is merely irrational to fear them.

Yuto's response instead attacks the inference from danger to disposable standing. If a body that might be captured or weaponized creates a duty to die preemptively, the same logic would condemn many Eighty-Six themselves.

Chitri then breaks the imposed category by articulating ordinary possible selves: daughter, student, friend, possible adult, traveler, loved person, person who wants to return.

The result is a major refinement of the anti-dehumanization thesis:

> **The personhood violation is not only false classification. It is forcing one dangerous or useful property of a person to become the complete political meaning of that person's existence.**

That principle extends beyond race while remaining historically connected to the Sector's original reduction of Colorata to military buffer components.

# 16. V13 — fear produces new target categories before doctrine fully stabilizes

V13's social collapse shows how quickly category boundaries can become self-justifying under pressure.

> `八六区を、エイティシックスを作り出した共和国だけが、特別におかしいわけじゃなかった。まともだと思っていた連邦でさえ、状況が変...`

The text does not say San Magnolia's history was irrelevant or ordinary. It says that the capacity to reproduce contempt and exclusion is not biologically or nationally unique to the Republic.

Direct-source evidence tracks the new targets multiplying: refugees, minorities, military families, wounded soldiers, returned captives, lower-status regions, and people merely suspected of Fawn or Eavesdropper association. Schools expel children; minority districts burn; hotels stop sheltering refugees after harassment.

This is where **scapegoating** becomes more analytically useful than “racism” alone. Some targets are ethnic; others are status groups. The common mechanism is that a frightened majority converts complex danger into a smaller category that can be punished, expelled, exposed or blamed.

T11 therefore distinguishes:

- racialization as one historically powerful form of othering;
- scapegoating as a broader transfer mechanism;
- disposability as the institutional conclusion that can follow either.

# 17. V13 — the reproduced Sector threshold

V13 itself authorizes the comparison in unusually strong language.

> `「連邦本土から戦場が切り離される。西部戦線が──連邦の全戦線が、八六区になります！」`

> `〈ジャガーノート〉とその処理装置に、一切の後退を許さぬために要塞壁と地雷原とを敷設した、共和国の戦場と同じように。`

> `征路を〈レギオン〉に、退路を人の悪意に鎖される。`

> `そのありかたは共和国で、八六区と呼ばれた戦場と何一つも変わらなかった。`

The key is conjunction. The strike force is separated from the interior, forbidden meaningful retreat, exposed to the Legion at the front, and closed off by human hostility at the rear. Lena is retained as leverage to prevent Eighty-Six refusal or rebellion.

This satisfies the mature structural threshold:

**expendability + constrained refusal + blocked return + social/political distancing.**

The narrator's statement that the arrangement is no different from the battlefield once called the Eighty-Sixth Sector is therefore not merely a reader-created analogy.

But historical equivalence still does not follow. The Federacy crisis emerges through war exhaustion, inherited class/ethnic fractures, refugee resentment, fear, information warfare, military breakdown and emergency coercion. It does not repeat the Republic's nine-year racial denaturalization and planned Colorata extermination in every respect.

The correct language is:

> **V13 reproduces the Sector relation without reproducing the complete historical genealogy of the original Sector.**

# 18. V13 — representation can erase personhood without fabricating the image

Rito's public-footage sequence adds a modern information dimension to othering.

The footage is factually real. The manipulation lies in cropping motive, chronology and context until a complex act becomes an identity claim about the person and group shown.

The public can then merge Fawn, Eavesdropper, Republic-born, Eighty-Six and Legion into one treacherous enemy category.

This is important because T11 should not define propaganda only as false images. **True visual evidence can be embedded in a false causal story.**

Representational othering works when:

1. an act is isolated from the conditions producing it;
2. the isolated act is treated as revelation of stable essence;
3. essence is transferred from individual to category;
4. category is used to interpret every later piece of evidence.

The result is a self-sealing enemy identity.

This mechanism is less materially complete than V01's legal nonpersonhood, but it can be an upstream step toward exclusion and disposability.

# 19. V13-V14 — victims can reproduce category erasure without becoming equivalent perpetrators

Shin himself becomes a critical adversarial control. V13 catches him using a faceless “them” category about people he experiences as weak, foolish and obstructive.

> `おろかものはエイティシックスではない。仲間ではない。愚かで無力なあいつらなど、いない方がいっそマシだ。`

The narrative then pushes him back toward individual differentiation.

V14 gives Raiden a related realization: rage against the Federacy can begin resembling the Republic's transfer of fear and inferiority onto a weaker target.

> `強大と膨大を極めた〈レギオン〉を相手には晴らせない劣等感と恐怖を、弱くて数も少ないエイティシックスに転てん嫁かした共和国の白ブタと、同じ振舞...`

The point is **not** that Raiden has become morally equivalent to the architects of San Magnolia's genocide. He has not created a racial state, stripped citizenship, built camps or planned extermination.

The point is that the **cognitive move**—taking diffuse pain or fear and locating it in a simplified group—is available to victims too.

T11 therefore freezes a crucial asymmetry rule:

> **Similarity of mechanism does not imply equality of power, scale, history, intent or consequence.**

This sentence should govern every later use of “the same logic” in the final synthesis.

# 20. V14 — ordinary particularity becomes an anti-othering practice

V14's pirate-radio and recipe material is politically significant because it offers a low-level response to categorical monsterization.

> `この投稿は、レシピもそれにまつわる笑い話も、全てがエイティシックスたちの抵抗だ。苦境でも笑うこと。悪意を笑い飛ばすこと。そして知らせること。...`

> `笑うし楽しむし好きな料理もあれば下手な物事もある、どこにでもいるただの人間なのだと。化物なんかじゃない、化物と無闇に恐れ忌いみ嫌きらわれれば...`

The point is not that cooking stories defeat racism by themselves. The mechanism is representational: a category described as monsters is reintroduced as particular people who laugh, cook, fail at things, remember old songs, possess family dishes and argue about mundane preferences.

This is a **counter-erasure practice**.

It does not replace law, citizenship, security or institutional reform. V01 has already shown that positive stories are powerless if the legal system still makes people disposable. But once the public sphere itself has been captured by enemy categories, particularity becomes one necessary ingredient of repair.

The recipe tied to Shin's mother is especially useful because it crosses imposed categories: an Eighty-Six family can also have been Republic citizens. The category history is politically constructed rather than an eternal separation of peoples.

# 21. V14 — shared victimhood does not authorize shared extermination

No Face attempts to recruit living Eighty-Six by invoking common persecution and exclusion.

> `「我々は君たちを歓迎する。何な故ぜなら我々〈レギオン〉はその多くが、君たちと同じエイティシックスだ。同胞たる君たちを、決して悪いようにはしな...`

The living reject the invitation.

> `「共和国も、この連邦も。──エイティシックスを排斥し、受け入れなかった国だからおれたちも嬉き々きとして殺さつ戮りくするだろうとでも思っている...`

and the narrative explicitly recalls their decision not to become as degraded as their persecutors:

> `迫害者と同じ下劣には堕おちないと決めた。憎ぞう悪おで矜きよう持じを穢けがさないと決めた。`

This is one of the strongest anti-essentialist conclusions in the current corpus. A victim identity can explain anger, solidarity and memory; it does **not** generate moral authority to exterminate outsiders.

That also prevents “Eighty-Six” from becoming a sacred moral category. It remains a historically meaningful and self-chosen identity, but its members are answerable for what they choose to do with it.

The series therefore rejects both:

- the perpetrator's claim that target category determines subhuman status;
- and the victim's possible claim that persecuted status automatically sanctifies later violence.

# 22. V14 — reactionary blood rule shows another route from crisis to inherited hierarchy

The V14 epilogue provides a different form of political regression. Its rhetoric does not reproduce Colorata racial genocide. Instead it argues that power should return to selected bloodlines and hereditary rulers.

> `「ギアーデは今度こそ、正当なる真紅の血統の下もとに統治されるべきだ」`

Direct-source material makes the theory even clearer: words and force are imagined as powers properly handled only by selected blood. This is **blood-legitimist guardianship**, not the same racial formation as San Magnolia.

The comparison nevertheless matters because both systems assign political capacity through inherited category rather than individual answerability.

The difference must remain visible:

- San Magnolia's racial regime creates a degraded outside to exploit and erase;
- the reactionary Imperial project creates an elevated hereditary inside authorized to rule.

These can coexist historically, but they are analytically different structures.

T11 therefore resists using “racism” as a catch-all for every inherited hierarchy. Blood caste, aristocracy, ethnic prejudice, racialization and genocide overlap but are not synonyms.

# 23. Graded Sector-comparison matrix

Use the following scale in Phase 5 and specialist synthesis.

| Comparison level | Required features | Example use |
|---|---|---|
| **Level 0 — thematic resemblance only** | insult, prejudice, unequal esteem, symbolic othering | Giad social pity or isolated ethnic slurs |
| **Level 1 — exclusionary structure** | targeted group boundary + differential burden or access | anti-Alba backlash; inherited caste exclusion |
| **Level 2 — functionalized other** | group/person valued primarily as role, weapon, sacred function or risk absorber | Holy Theocracy sacred warriors; utility-conditioned military castes |
| **Level 3 — disposability structure** | constrained refusal + institutionally unequal exposure + continued standing dependent on utility | selected Fawn-related or wartime buffer systems |
| **Level 4 — Sector relation** | expendability + blocked return + constrained refusal + social/political distancing | V13 final battlefield arrangement |
| **Level 5 — historically specific San Magnolia Sector system** | Level 4 plus racial denaturalization, concentration/expropriation, legal/statistical nonpersonhood, sustained coerced service, archive destruction and elimination of surviving witnesses | V01 Republic system |

This is an analytical device, not a taxonomy stated by the novels. It exists to prevent rhetorical escalation from replacing evidence.

# 24. Adversarial controls and counterevidence

## 24.1 Not every racial category is racial oppression

The novels use inherited color/ethnic descriptors in ordinary description. A racial taxonomy existing in the setting does not itself prove a hierarchy in every society or scene.

## 24.2 Not every unequal military burden is a Sector

Military organizations routinely assign unequal danger. The Sector threshold requires stronger evidence concerning refusal, return, status and the political distribution of danger.

## 24.3 Pity is not genocide

Benevolent misrecognition can be patronizing and agency-reducing. Treating it as morally equivalent to exterminatory dehumanization would destroy useful distinctions.

## 24.4 Later Alba suffering does not retroactively balance the historical account

Republic civilians can become victims of war, Legion massacre or later prejudice. Their victimization is real. It does not cancel the Republic's earlier racial program or convert history into a symmetrical ledger of group suffering.

## 24.5 Colorata are not a morally unified bloc

V01, V10, V13 and V14 repeatedly prevent this. Internal prejudice, disagreement and later wrongdoing must remain possible without becoming an argument against the reality of persecution.

## 24.6 Structural analogy does not require identical motive

V13 can reproduce a no-return human-buffer relation through fear, military crisis and social fragmentation even though the mechanism is not a replay of V01's racial doctrine. Structural comparison is precisely useful because different causes can produce related relations.

## 24.7 Historical specificity does not mean structural uniqueness

The reverse error is equally serious. Saying San Magnolia is historically specific must not become an excuse to refuse every later analogy. V13 explicitly authorizes one.

# 28. Final T11 judgment

The V01-V14 corpus supports a two-level conclusion.

First, **San Magnolia's Eighty-Sixth Sector is historically specific**. Its defining history is not exhausted by “some people were hated and sent to fight.” Colorata were racially classified, stripped of citizenship, concentrated outside ordinary society, expropriated, subjected to coerced labor and military service, linguistically converted into nonhuman machine components, removed from casualty statistics and public mourning, denied reliable routes back, stripped of durable official records, and finally treated as witnesses whose survival threatened the Republic's postwar legitimacy. That sequence gives the original Sector its distinctive genocidal architecture.

Second, **the Sector relation is structurally reproducible**. Later societies need not repeat the original racial doctrine to reproduce the political relation in which a recognizable population becomes the human buffer absorbing danger for others while refusal and return are closed. V13 explicitly reaches that point. Fawns demonstrate an even more disturbing variation: the state can recognize someone's biological humanity perfectly well and still design that person's future as disposal.

The strongest formulation is therefore:

> **Racial dehumanization is one route by which a society can make persons disposable; it is not the only route. The distinctive horror of San Magnolia is that racial classification, law, economy, military doctrine, record-keeping and exterminatory policy were integrated into one system. The broader warning of *86* is that the disposition to transfer danger onto a marked outside can survive the collapse of that system and find new categories, new justifications and new technologies.**

The methodological corollary is equally important:

> **Never call two structures “the same” merely because both are cruel. Specify what relation is reproduced, what historical machinery is absent, who has the power to refuse or return, and what the comparison gains that a more exact description would not.**
