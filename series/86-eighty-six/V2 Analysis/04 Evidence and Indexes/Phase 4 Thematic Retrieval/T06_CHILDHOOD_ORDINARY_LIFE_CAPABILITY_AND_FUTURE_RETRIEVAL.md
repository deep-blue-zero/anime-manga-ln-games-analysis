# T06 — Childhood, Ordinary Life, Capability, and Future Retrieval

This dossier retrieves the evidence behind a deceptively easy coming-of-age claim: that the Eighty-Six leave war, discover ordinary life, and thereby become capable of a future. The V01-V14 corpus does not support that sequence in such a simple form. Ordinary life exists inside the Eighty-Sixth Sector; military vocation can coexist with genuine futurity; leisure can coexist with trauma; education can enlarge agency without guaranteeing it; and extraordinary child-soldier competence can coexist with developmental deprivation.

The governing adversarial question is:

> **Does *86* treat ordinary life as the civilian opposite of war, or as a wider repertoire of human capabilities and repeatable future practices that may already exist under war but become more stable, diverse, recoverable, and projectable under better conditions?**

The retrieval result is: **ordinary life is not created by rescue. Giad changes its material reliability, breadth, repeatability, institutional support, and temporal horizon. The deepest future shift is therefore not soldier-to-civilian conversion but movement from terminal or one-use time toward repeatable time: another meal, another school term, another birthday, another trip, another return, another chance to learn, another year in which a desire can generate a later desire.**

At the same time, T06 rejects the reverse romanticization. Humor, food, hobbies and adolescent play inside atrocity do not prove that the Sector was livable. They prove that people continue producing ordinary human life even where institutions are organized to destroy it.

# 1. Executive taxonomy: ten concepts later synthesis must keep separate

| Concept | T06 meaning | Critical negative control |
|---|---|---|
| **Ordinary practice** | nonterminal activity valued beyond immediate survival | can occur even inside exterminatory conditions |
| **Pleasure** | enjoyment, humor, taste, play, beauty, affection | pleasure does not prove safety or recovery |
| **Capability** | a developed ability that expands what one can choose to do | possessing a skill does not require exercising it |
| **Option space** | materially and socially accessible possible actions | formal permission without transport, education or safety may be thin |
| **Repeatable time** | practices expected to recur: next meal, next birthday, next school term | a one-time pleasure under expected death is not the same temporal structure |
| **Future imagination** | ability to picture oneself continuing beyond current role/crisis | can be relational before it becomes self-directed |
| **Vocation** | role one may continue or newly pursue | military vocation can coexist with future; civilianization is not the metric |
| **Rest/recovery** | protected interruption that preserves future capacity | leisure without reduced burden is not necessarily recovery |
| **Developmental youth** | age-appropriate incompleteness, dependence, experimentation and growth | combat competence does not equal completed adulthood |
| **Adulthood/responsibility** | increasing responsibility for consequences affecting others | responsibility must not become total self-erasure or compulsory guardianship |

The central rule is:

> **Ordinary life is not defined by the absence of war. It is the existence of practices, relationships, skills and temporal expectations whose value is not exhausted by surviving the current emergency.**

# 2. V01-V05 — ordinary humanity exists before rescue; future breadth does not

## V01 — personhood already exceeds military function

V01 already contains sweets, coffee, basketball, guitar music, sexual curiosity, books, art, chores, domestic improvisation and social teasing. A direct-source row retains the hangar scene where maintenance noise coexists with a pickup basketball game and an old anime song played on guitar. These are not later Federacy habits projected backward. They are part of Sector life.

The line `人生には菓子とかコーヒーとか` makes the principle explicit enough: life contains things that are not reducible to combat function. This matters because later rescue does not manufacture ordinary humanity. It changes whether such practices are stable, materially supported, and expected to continue.

The limitation is temporal. These pleasures exist under a system where long-horizon planning is irrational. The Sector can sustain **moments** of ordinary life while destroying the expectation of an ordinary lifespan.

## V02 — Giad expands options faster than it creates belonging

V02 provides the first controlled experiment after rescue. The Five do not prove helpless outside combat. Within a month Raiden has courier work, Anju attends cooking classes, Theo sketches, Kurena window-shops, Shin visits libraries and museums, and they begin making acquaintances and friends.

That directly overturns the crude claim that they know nothing except war.

Yet Ernst's desire to make them `ただの子供に戻って` is also wrong. Childhood is not a state to which history can simply be reversed. Empty bedrooms, weak attachment to possessions, and reenlistment after a real civilian interval show that capability and preference are distinct. People may be able to perform civilian life without yet finding it their desired primary identity.

The strongest institutional line is `選べる道はなるべく多い方がいい`. The ethical goal is **more viable paths**, not forcing selection of the path outsiders consider normal.

## V03 — formal freedom can arrive as terrifying emptiness

V03 shows why opportunity must be distinguished from learned desire. Shin can now choose, but says `あの夜から本当は何も、望めてはいない`. A large legal option space does not instantly produce the internal capacity to imagine a long future.

Raiden provides an important positive control: `死にたくねえ。それだけだ` is enough. Futurity need not begin with a grand calling. Bare preference for continued life is already morally significant.

Shin's `見たいとは...思わない。でも、海を見せたい` establishes the first durable asymmetry between **self-directed desire** and **other-directed future care**. He cannot yet want the horizon for himself, but he can imagine surviving in order for another person to encounter it.

T06 therefore tracks future development along more than one axis. A person may acquire relational futurity before vocation, pleasure before planning, or survival desire before a positive life project.

## V04 — recovery is not restoration to childhood

The child Shin remembered in V04 is `よく笑う、明るい子だった`, but the volume refuses to make that earlier personality a recovery target. History happened. The adult observer cannot demand restoration of a pre-trauma child as proof of health.

More important, Shin himself says the Sector had its own `楽しみ`. Ordinary life is therefore retrospective evidence of resistance, not evidence that conditions were acceptable.

By the end of the volume `いつか` has become more schedulable: places and activities can be discussed as things that might actually occur. Yet `未来の幸福も思い描けない` remains a limit. Practical future talk can develop before a person possesses a rich image of personal happiness.

## V05 — Sirins provide the negative futurity contrast

The most useful V05 line for T06 is not about the Eighty-Six at all:

> `未来などない。課された役目しかない`

A role-only existence becomes a clean negative contrast. The problem is not military work. It is a future exhausted by one assigned purpose.

The human material moves in the opposite direction. `いつか幸せになりたいくせに` makes denied ordinary desire explicit, while the volume's fear and corpse-road material prevents future aspiration from becoming sentimental optimism.

This also creates an important personhood/capability boundary inherited from T02: lacking an open future does not erase moral standing, but an open future is increasingly central to **flourishing**.

# 3. V06-V09 — future becomes relational, then plural, then reconstructive

## V06 — from `見せたい` to shared life

V06 is the first major future breakthrough. The narrator still says Shin can access future primarily through Lena, but the content becomes concrete:

- survive;
- return;
- talk;
- apologize;
- see the sea together;
- live in a way that lets Lena smile;
- if possible, laugh together.

`まだそれしか、望めない` is methodologically important. The text itself refuses to call this complete development. The future is real but still concentrated.

Annette's thought that they are in some sense still children identifies the structural problem: they lacked ordinary models of adulthood. The missing thing is not intelligence. It is **developmental rehearsal**—seeing, trying and revising possible adult lives.

The epilogue then broadens future through grandparents, gifts, ordinary return, education and the possibility that friends may someday go to different homes without ceasing to belong to one another.

## V07 — ordinary life becomes a capability theory

V07 gives T06 its cleanest proposition:

> `できないよりもできた方が、楽しめるものは多い`

The point of learning dance, social convention, school habits or other ordinary skills is not that everyone must perform a standardized civilian identity. Capabilities matter because **having an ability expands the set of things one can meaningfully choose**.

The month's school/leave interval is therefore not assimilation. It is developmental option-expansion.

Shin's `実際皆、楽しんでいますから。おれを含めて` marks another threshold. He is no longer merely tolerating ordinary time for Lena or the others. He identifies himself as a participant in enjoyment.

And `貴女と共に、海を見たい` changes the sea grammar again: from wanting to show, to wanting to see together.

V07's explicit `まだ大人になりきらない少年` framing prevents the reader from turning competence into completed adulthood. Their capacity for command remains compatible with ordinary adolescent incompleteness.

## V08 — capabilities can be lost; future must be reconstructible

V08 complicates V07's option-expansion model. Theo's injury demonstrates that a capability once possessed can disappear irreversibly. The ethical future therefore cannot depend on indefinite preservation of one body or function.

Shin's counterfactual `研究にでも進んでいたかも` is especially useful. It does not reveal a secret authentic scientist hidden underneath the soldier. It proves that other life trajectories were imaginable under different history.

His statement that he no longer thinks the battlefield is his only place strengthens that plurality.

The sea also ceases to be a simple reward. It becomes military terrain and a site of bodily loss. Future-bearing symbols can be contaminated by reality without losing all meaning.

The durable V08 principle is:

> **A livable future requires not merely present capabilities but social arrangements that allow a person to reconstruct identity after involuntary subtraction.**

## V09 — small desires are enough

V09 gives perhaps the most anti-grandiose theory of futurity in the corpus. Theo can say `僕はもう、戦えない` and still reach `でも、死ななくていい`. Life does not require immediate replacement of the lost military identity with an equally grand vocation.

Annette's fried-bread example is decisive: hearing that a stall's fried bread is good can be enough reason to try something, and fulfilling one small desire can make room for another. A person does not need a master plan before being entitled to continue.

Rito's `楽しいですね、学生というのは` provides a school-positive control, while Kurena's fear of `戦場の外の、未来` shows that access to education does not automatically make future emotionally usable.

The V09 rule is:

> **Futurity can be generated iteratively. One small nonterminal desire can lead to another without requiring a complete theory of the good life in advance.**

# 4. V10-V12 — childhood history, repeatability, education, and supported self-government

## V10 — the Gentle World denies redemptive suffering

V10's genealogy of Undertaker makes childhood loss historically specific. The role did not reveal Shin's timeless essence; it was assembled through coercion, care, death, promise and repeated selection.

The Gentle World then says what the series must preserve ethically:

> `傷なんて、負わなくてよかった`

> `おれは死神でいなくてよかった`

The relationships that actually emerged after suffering remain real. The suffering is not thereby justified as necessary for producing them.

`会えない人がいる。知らない景色と言葉がある` is the important inverse. Rejecting the necessity of trauma does not mean wishing the actual present away, because the present now contains people and possibilities that also matter.

This is the cleanest anti-theodicy control for T06: **development after harm does not retroactively convert harm into beneficial education.**

## V11 — catastrophe does not cancel ordinary time

V11 repeatedly juxtaposes strategic catastrophe with deliberately ordinary future claims. The command that the adolescents should spend a month studying, reading foolish books, playing with friends and worrying about romance refuses to let military competence erase age.

Lena's insistence that she still wants the Revolution Festival fireworks matters for the same reason. The future is not only the end of war. It includes specific ordinary events the characters intend to reach.

Richard's `ただの子供に戻ったか` should remain bounded. No one literally returns to prewar childhood. What he recognizes is the reappearance of developmental behavior underneath a military identity that had made the children look older than they were.

V11 also shows ordinary feeling under morally ugly conditions: Rito can describe the dangerous exhilaration of power as `楽しかった`. T06 preserves this as a warning that pleasure is not itself ethical validation.

## V12 — capability becomes explicitly educational and political

V12 supplies the fullest capabilities argument in the corpus.

Shiden can say she does not understand something and therefore wants to know. Miarona treats `わからないから知りたい、学びたい` as a valuable quality and tells the students to proceed `君たちのペースでぜひ、楽しんでくれたまえ`.

The pedagogical structure matters:

1. make knowledge accessible;
2. allow a low-cost first encounter;
3. permit questions;
4. offer deeper material;
5. let the learner decide whether to continue.

Shiden can then decide the field is not for her. That is not educational failure. The capability did its work because the option could be explored and rejected without punishment.

The same volume provides the negative political control. Former serfs can experience literacy and choice as imposed burdens; Noelle's followers prefer outsourced judgment; Noelle herself closes around infallibility. Education therefore **enables** self-government but does not guarantee it.

Rest is also institutionalized. `休むのも仕事だと思って`; Lena is medically removed from duty; Shin is told to report overload. A direct-source passage explicitly says rest clears mental space that should otherwise contain future dreams, tomorrow's pleasant plans and love.

So T06's V12 formulation is:

> **A usable future requires not only rights but capacities, practice, recoverable error, material support, rest, and enough psychological space to form desires—while still leaving the person responsible for what they choose.**

# 5. Alter.1 — the decisive control against the rescue-as-humanization story

Alter.1 is indispensable because its low-pressure scenes sample what campaign plotting systematically underrepresents.

The afterword itself describes the collection as glimpses of Shin and his companions' `日常`. Pre-rescue stories contain birthdays, improvised celebration, food, humor, mending, animals, music, folklore, teasing, hanami memory, curiosity and play. `日常の中の些細なイベントを楽しむことに...素直で貪欲だ` makes the point almost explicit.

This does **not** mean the Sector contained enough ordinary life. It means ordinary human capacities persisted despite conditions designed to eliminate their future.

## 5.1 Birthdays as temporal technology

The birthday sequence is T06's strongest longitudinal object.

Inside the Sector, birthdays lose administrative and practical use. Shin forgets his. Raiden's remembered date becomes an excuse for the survivors to celebrate whoever is still alive.

Later, records recover lost dates. Lena wants celebration to become `当たり前`. The Eighty-Six begin giving birthday gifts because the practice looks fun. Theo's April birthday can be celebrated late in July. Lena can receive distributed personal gifts. Anju's birthday survives coinciding with a catastrophic offensive and becomes attached to a future flower-and-return ritual. Theo can participate in Annette's birthday after losing his hand and frontline role.

The progression is:

> **survivor excuse -> recovered calendar -> voluntary peer ritual -> repeatable expectation -> practice that survives disruption and bodily change.**

This supports one of the strongest T06 propositions:

> **A future is not merely believing one will survive. It is acquiring recurring practices whose meaning presupposes that there will be another occasion.**

## 5.2 Infrastructure changes what agency can do

The `徒歩圏内` story is an excellent capability control. Shin independently finds something interesting in a library and decides to investigate it. Curiosity and initiative already exist.

What does not exist in his behavioral repertoire is the assumption that he can simply use a bus or train. He contemplates a long walk because `公共交通機関を使おうという発想自体がない`.

That separates:

- **internal agency**: curiosity, decision, initiative;
- **external capability infrastructure**: transport, institutions, access, habit, money, safety.

Giad does not teach Shin to be curious. It makes curiosity able to travel farther.

## 5.3 Adolescence remains visible beneath competence

Frederica's first same-age meal, the Five's ongoing growth, adults seeing them asleep and recognizing `本当にただのガキどもだった`, and the later `まだ十代の少年少女` framing all reinforce the same rule:

> **specialized competence and developmental youth coexist.**

That blocks both infantilization and premature adultification.

# 6. V13-V14 — future becomes a political object that can be defended or closed

## V13 — ordinary tomorrow is what political violence destroys

Chitri's opening memory is deliberately banal: school every day, play afterward, go home, say see you tomorrow. The Fawn program destroys exactly this temporal structure.

Her late desire list is correspondingly ordinary: parents, teacher, friends, school, travel, family, adulthood, home. The extremity of the weaponization is measured partly by how many ordinary futures the assigned function removes.

V13's adulthood discussion adds another layer. Becoming adult means one's choices increasingly affect people for whom one bears responsibility. That is a genuine developmental constraint, but T06 rejects any inference that adulthood means ceasing to have personal desires. Responsibility should discipline choice, not annihilate selfhood.

The final minefield turns future capacity into geography. One may have plans, relationships and a desired home while institutions physically remove the route back. **Future is therefore partly infrastructural.**

## V14 — study becomes resistance to terminal time

V14 contains perhaps the clearest direct educational future command:

> `勉強をしましょう。こんな時だからこそ。──戦争が終わる日のために`

Study during crisis is not denial of crisis. It is refusal to let crisis monopolize time.

The volume also repeatedly recovers ordinary culture—recipes, jokes, anime songs, preferred foods, sweets, festivals and childhood memories—as evidence against monsterization. `笑うし楽しむし好きな料理もあれば下手な物事もある` makes ordinary particularity politically legible.

Rito becomes the most painful future counterfactual. He had learned to do things he previously could not, wanted to study sea life and whales, wanted to see more because `何をしてもいい`, and intended to bring materials back for others. His death matters not only as loss of life but as **closure of an actively expanding future**.

Shin's own case prevents triumphalism. He can choose future over past injury and still conceal serious sensory damage in order to preserve combat role. Future desire does not automatically eliminate learned self-instrumentalization.

Theo's response to that sensory loss—eat together and describe tastes—shows what capability reconstruction can look like relationally. The future does not require restoring the old body; another person can help make an altered body more usable without taking control of it.

The wartime recipes and old anime song also close a long loop back to V01/Alter.1. Ordinary culture is not an after-the-war reward. It is one of the means by which the Eighty-Six insist they were ordinary people all along.

# 7. The mature capability model

Across V01-V14, the corpus supports a layered capability model rather than a civilianization model.

A usable future requires some combination of:

1. **legal permission** — one is allowed to live, learn, travel, work, love and change role;
2. **material access** — transport, money, food, housing, healthcare, educational resources;
3. **developed skills** — literacy, social knowledge, professional training, ordinary practical competence;
4. **psychological room** — enough safety/rest that attention can extend beyond immediate threat;
5. **social scaffolding** — people who invite, teach, wait, correct and help without permanently deciding;
6. **recoverable error** — trying and failing does not erase belonging;
7. **repeatability** — experiences are not one-use miracles before expected death;
8. **exit and return** — one can leave a role/place and still have somewhere to come back to;
9. **plural identity** — no one option has to exhaust the self;
10. **participation by the person** — capabilities enable choice; they cannot substitute for choosing.

This is why neither pure autonomy nor paternalistic rescue adequately describes the series' positive model.

# 8. Childhood and adulthood: uneven development, not delayed humanity

The Eighty-Six are not "children trapped in adult bodies," nor are they adults simply because they command units and kill effectively.

Their development is **asymmetrical**.

War accelerates:

- tactical judgment;
- exposure to death;
- command responsibility;
- maintenance competence;
- emotional suppression;
- practical self-sufficiency.

It can simultaneously suppress or narrow:

- schooling;
- experimentation without lethal consequence;
- ordinary peer life;
- privacy;
- family continuity;
- vocation exploration;
- transport habits;
- consumer/social familiarity;
- long-horizon planning;
- safe dependence on adults.

Later development therefore cannot be measured by how successfully they imitate an imagined average teenager. The better question is:

> **Do they acquire more capacities and more permissible ways to be young, adult, soldier, student, partner, friend, relative, worker, artist or something not yet named?**

# 9. Negative controls: what ordinary-life evidence does not prove

## 9.1 Pleasure does not prove recovery

A person can laugh while frightened, enjoy power while behaving badly, or participate in leisure while carrying severe unprocessed injury. V11 and V14 are explicit about these coexistences.

## 9.2 School does not automatically produce autonomy

V12 makes this impossible to claim. Education can be resented, ignored, instrumentalized or paired with preference for obedience. It expands capability; it does not mechanically generate self-government.

## 9.3 Military vocation does not disprove future

Eugene, the Five's reenlistment, professional schooling and later military choices show that a positive future may still include military service. The diagnostic is not civilianity but whether the role is chosen, revisable, supported and nonexclusive.

## 9.4 Civilian leisure does not prove assimilation

Shopping, dancing, festivals, school and birthdays enlarge options. A character may enjoy them without abandoning Eighty-Six identity, military pride or inherited culture.

## 9.5 Future-orientation does not equal completed healing

V14 Shin is the clearest falsifier. He possesses multiple genuine reasons to live while still hiding bodily damage to preserve usefulness.

## 9.6 Child competence does not prove adulthood

The text repeatedly calls these soldiers children or teenagers even after demonstrating extraordinary competence. Any later synthesis that treats childhood as merely chronological will miss the developmental asymmetry.

# 13. Final retrieval judgment

T06's strongest formulation is:

> **The Eighty-Six do not become human when they reach ordinary life; they were already producing ordinary human life under conditions that denied its continuity. What better institutions add is the possibility that a joke, meal, lesson, birthday, trip, friendship, vocation, romance or period of rest can become part of a repeatable future rather than a temporary interruption before expected death.**

The maturation arc is therefore not war -> civilianity.

It is closer to:

> **terminal time -> intermittent ordinary time -> materially supported option space -> repeatable time -> plural future -> capacity to reconstruct after loss.**

And because the final arc remains open, even that sequence cannot be treated as irreversible. V13-V14 show how quickly institutions can once again close routes, turn adolescents into functions, and force future-oriented people back into emergency time.
