# T13 - Motifs, Objects, Spaces, and Visual Paratext Retrieval

T13 retrieves the material and formal evidence needed to answer a recurring interpretive danger in the V2 project: **when does recurrence become a meaningful motif, and when does the analyst turn visual familiarity into a symbolic code the novels themselves do not sustain?**

The governing adversarial question is:

> **When do recurring objects, places, colors, images, and publication-form devices accumulate longitudinal meaning through demonstrable reuse or transformation, and when should recurrence remain local, ambiguous, decorative, technical, or merely compositional?**

The principal result is restrictive. *86* does have a dense motif system, but its strongest motifs are usually **biographical rather than dictionary-like**. The scarf, misericorde, name fragments, Laughing Fox emblem, Stella Maris, Great Wall, photographs, Chitri's ribbon, birthdays, recipes, roads, and even the sea matter because their functions change while some material, spatial, or formal continuity remains. The series repeatedly asks the reader to remember **what an object used to mean and what a character can now make it mean**.

That is different from saying:

- red always means death;
- white always means innocence;
- butterflies always mean souls;
- the sea always means freedom;
- flowers always mean mourning;
- walls always mean oppression;
- or every visual echo is an authorial cipher.

T13 therefore treats **change of function under continuity** as stronger motif evidence than repetition alone.

# 0. Retrieval method and evidence hierarchy

The primary-source lexical pass scanned **48,723 non-empty Japanese EPUB paragraphs** across V01-V14 plus Alter.1. The selected families produced **7,391 raw family hits across 6,053 unique matching paragraphs**. The image inventory identified **227 distinct visual assets referenced by EPUB reading-order HTML**, including covers, color/front-matter plates, machine diagrams, maps, interior monochrome illustrations, divider-like graphics, and Alter.1 scrapbook/film-strip material.

The retained evidence ledger contains **300 items** in three layers:

1. **PROSE_LOCATOR** - verified canonical V2 locators whose wording or scene materially bears on a motif or spatial form;
2. **LONGITUDINAL_MOTIF** - entries from the V14-updated L10 motif/object ledger, retained as cross-volume routing rather than primary fact;
3. **VISUAL_PARATEXT** - direct EPUB image paths and the XHTML contexts that invoke them.

Search counts are retrieval aids, not symbolic weights. `COLOR_BLACK_WHITE_RED` is by far the largest family because ordinary prose constantly names hair, uniforms, blood, snow, machines, darkness, light, flags, and military markings. That frequency is itself a reason **not** to assign a fixed color dictionary.

## 0.1 T13 evidence weights

T13 distinguishes six levels of formal evidence.

| Evidence layer | What it can support | What it cannot establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| **Persistent physical object** | object biography, ownership, inheritance, changed use, repeated handling | universal symbolic meaning |
| **Repeated prose image / spatial relation** | thematic recurrence when contexts materially rhyme | authorial intention without contextual support |
| **Chapter-title / formal architecture** | editorially foregrounded structure, paired or repeated framing | character motive |
| **Interior illustration** | body, pose, juxtaposition, selected moment, visual rhyme | unillustrated internal states |
| **Cover / front-matter composition** | publication emphasis, visual pairing, role presentation | canonical chronology or exclusive thematic priority |
| **Decorative divider / promotional graphic** | formal atmosphere, editorial emphasis where clearly tied to text | hidden lore or a stable symbolic lexicon |

A visual reading becomes strongest when at least two layers agree. For example, V01's voice-before-body architecture is supported simultaneously by prose, Para-RAID structure, a front-matter Lena/Shin separation, and the final embodied reunion image. By contrast, a color recurring on several covers without an equivalent prose genealogy remains weak evidence.

# 1. Governing thesis - *86* uses object biographies more reliably than symbol dictionaries

The series' most robust motif structure can be represented as:

> **object / place / image -> inherited function -> repeated use -> altered context -> revised meaning**

The object is rarely purified of its past. Instead, old meaning remains available as pressure while a new use becomes possible.

This pattern is visible across very different domains:

- name fragments: prohibited graves -> portable memorial archive -> burden -> something that can eventually be shared or laid down;
- Alice's scarf: protection -> concealment -> carrying memory -> a retained object whose meaning no longer requires permanent wound identity;
- misericorde: absent medicine -> mercy killing -> inherited office -> burden -> object whose violent history does not have to dictate every later act;
- Laughing Fox: inherited admiration -> unresolved commander's request -> Theo's own mark -> post-injury continuity without restored combat function;
- Great Wall: segregation barrier -> failed defense -> first-meeting geography -> future memorial/return site;
- Stella Maris: national pride-object -> military vessel -> explicitly a ship for returning;
- photographs: proof of a social life -> mourning archive -> living-family continuity -> future-facing empty frame in Alter.1;
- Chitri's ribbon: ordinary personal possession -> voluntarily transferred memory-bind, sharply contrasted with the involuntary weaponization of her body;
- recipes: domestic habit -> family memory -> V14 civic counter-propaganda in which an abstract feared category becomes particular people with ordinary tastes and histories.

The critical formal rule is therefore:

> **Keeping an object does not prove psychic stasis, and discarding it is not required for change.**

V10 makes this almost explicit as a compositional principle. The anthology is built from fragments, and the same material inheritance can be reinterpreted rather than either worshipped or erased. T13 therefore routes V10 as the central object-biography volume even though many of its objects originate earlier.

# 2. Counter-archives - names, fragments, graves, photographs, records

The earliest major object system is archival.

V01's state attempts to make the Eighty-Six disappear administratively. Names, graves, photographs, marks, and fragments matter because they resist different layers of that disappearance. The relevant opposition is not simply **number = dehumanization / name = personhood**. Military organizations legitimately use numbers and identifiers. The deeper question is whether classification remains attached to a particular person or substitutes for that person.

The personal-mark fragments Shin carries are unusually strong motif objects because they combine:

- a physical remainder;
- a name/callsign relation;
- memorial custody;
- the absence of ordinary graves;
- and the burden of being the survivor who must transport the dead's existence forward.

The fragments can therefore function as a **portable grave and counter-archive** without implying that portable memorialization is always healthy. T03 and V10 have already shown why an inherited memorial office can become enclosing when preservation requires the bearer never to develop another identity.

Photographs provide an important control. V01's group image records ordinary social life that the state intends to erase. V02's Nina photographs link memory not only to death but to a living future and an expected return. Alter.1's empty photo frame then makes a subtle revision: the absence of recoverable old images is real, but the empty frame is also physically available for photographs that do not yet exist.

This yields a strong longitudinal proposition:

> **Archive in *86* is healthiest when it preserves the past without requiring the future to become a museum of the past.**

The same principle explains why recovered personnel records matter in Alter.1 without forcing Shin to restore an origin-house or family identity he no longer experiences as home.

# 3. Graves and memorial media - medium changes, particularity persists

V03 directly compares Eugene's formal national grave with the hundreds of fragments carried for Eighty-Six comrades. That comparison is methodologically important because it blocks fetishization of one memorial medium.

A grave can be:

- prohibited;
- formal and national;
- improvised;
- portable;
- private;
- revisited;
- refused;
- or even something the dead explicitly would not want survivors to center their lives around.

Richard's later refusal of sentimental grave pilgrimage reinforces that memorial ethics cannot be reduced to maintaining monuments.

T13 therefore treats **particularity and accountable memory**, not monumental form, as the stronger continuity.

This also explains why V14's War History Compilation Room matters in the same broad motif family even though it is not a grave. Archive becomes oriented toward future action: past failure is recorded so that later people can plan differently. Memorialization becomes politically useful when it preserves answerability rather than merely sanctifying the dead.

# 4. Roads, paths, walls, rails, ships, minefields - space becomes capability

T01 and T04 showed that return is not one Japanese word and home is not one category. T13 adds the spatial-form layer.

The recurring road is strongest when it stops being scenery and becomes a material answer to the question:

> **Can this person actually leave, return, retreat, or reach what they value?**

V01's Special Reconnaissance road is movement under a death sentence. V03's road can become a route that others expect the Five to return from. V06-V09 increasingly connect return to persons and repeatable home relations. V13 then converts the whole problem into military geography: the rear is mined, and the route that would connect exposure to ordinary life is physically closed.

The mature spatial principle is:

> **Political exclusion becomes materially complete when it can be built into geography.**

The Great Wall has an especially clear object biography. It begins as architecture separating protected citizens from those forced to absorb war. By V11 it is also failed fortification, first-meeting geography, and a place Lena can imagine returning to voluntarily. Its earlier meaning is not erased; the site can acquire a new relation without becoming innocent.

Rail performs a different double function. In V11 it is evacuation capacity and vulnerability at once. A railway can preserve bodies by moving them; destroying or controlling it can trap bodies. This makes infrastructure ethically legible rather than merely logistical.

Stella Maris supplies the complementary positive form. Fleet identity attaches pride to a ship, but the vessel is explicitly meaningful as a ship **for returning**. The institution becomes valuable because it carries persons and continuity rather than because the artifact itself must be preserved at all costs.

# 5. The sea - T13's strongest anti-dictionary motif

The sea is the clearest case where one recurring image supports **multiple incompatible local meanings**.

Across V03-V08 it becomes:

1. an unseen horizon of unnecessary, nonmilitary experience;
2. something Shin initially wants to show another person before he can fully want it for himself;
3. a jointly spoken future destination with Lena;
4. an actual place that cannot match an idealized fantasy;
5. an ecological world indifferent to the human/Legion conflict;
6. a battlefield;
7. the place where Theo's combat body is permanently altered.

V08 explicitly makes the contradiction visible. Theo reaches the sea, experiences anticlimax/emptiness, then sees its blue reality through the violence that ends his old combat role. The Musukura further prevents the sea from becoming a human moral stage: nonhuman life exceeds the categories through which the protagonists interpret war.

The correct motif claim is therefore not:

> **sea = freedom**

but:

> **the sea repeatedly tests whether an imagined future can survive contact with a world that is beautiful, material, indifferent, dangerous, and not obligated to confirm the meaning projected onto it.**

This is one of T13's strongest warnings against symbolic simplification.

# 6. Boundaries, lines, glass, mirrors, IFF - seeing and sorting

A second important spatial/formal family concerns boundaries and acts of classification.

V04 clusters IFF, `Front Toward Enemy`, scars, search, and a human-shaped Phonix. These motifs ask whether looking at a surface correctly tells the observer what relation exists behind it. Tactical systems classify friend/enemy; political systems classify citizen/nonperson; characters classify wound, machine, friend, stranger, and enemy.

Glass and acrylic recur as a useful but not universal form of separated contact. V07's conversation with Zelene across a barrier can support disclosure without implying restored equality of status or freedom of movement. Transparent material permits sight and speech while preserving separation.

Mirrors in V09 work differently. They enable **similarity without identity**. Hielna/Kurena and Milmelkoleo/Eighty-Six comparisons are useful because the reflected structure lets characters recognize something about themselves, but the text does not require the two histories to become morally identical.

V13 makes the boundary logic explicit:

> a line is drawn socially before it becomes territorial and eventually a minefield.

This is formal progression rather than a free-floating symbol. Language, public categorization, refused cooperation, military fragmentation, and finally geography occupy successive versions of the same boundary-making process.

# 7. Scars, scarf, misericorde, blade, ribbon - the body and portable inheritance

Portable objects are often strongest when they touch bodily history.

## 7.1 Scar

Scars provide visible evidence of history but do not grant interpretive ownership to the observer. V04's handling of Shin's neck scar is important precisely because Lena can see it without thereby acquiring the right to define its meaning.

Anju's later treatment of her scars adds the complementary correction:

> **historical truth does not require permanent preservation of injury on the body.**

This is a major T07/T13 bridge.

## 7.2 Scarf

V10 gives the scarf a complete object biography. It protects, conceals, carries the memory of Alice, participates in Shin's visual identity, and remains retainable even after its original survival function no longer governs his life.

The object therefore demonstrates that **reinterpretation need not require disposal**.

## 7.3 Misericorde

The mercy pistol similarly changes function through circumstances of absent medicine, mercy killing, inherited battlefield office, and Shin's later relation to Undertaker. Its persistence is morally uncomfortable by design. It carries care and violence together.

The key analytical caution is that a weapon inherited through trauma cannot be made into a clean emblem of either love or death. Its significance is the history of decisions attached to it.

## 7.4 Shin's blade

In V08, Shin's dropped blade becomes a relational continuity object after his disappearance. Theo and Fido recover/return it without treating possession of the object as proof that Shin is alive. The blade can support hope without becoming magical evidence.

## 7.5 Chitri's ribbon

V13's ribbon is perhaps the cleanest chosen-inheritance object. Chitri's body has been involuntarily converted into a weapon, while the ribbon is voluntarily given to Yuto as memory. The contrast is powerful precisely because the object is small and ordinary.

The ribbon says:

> **this is a burden/continuity I choose to place in your hands**

where the Fawn program says:

> **this is a function others have placed into my body.**

# 8. Emblems, flags, titles, uniforms, costumes - roles that can be inhabited or revised

The inverted Republic flag at Spearhead is a strong early political image, but its force is often misdescribed. It does not prove rejection of all republican ideals. It more directly shows that the Republic has inverted its own professed values.

Unit marks and callsign imagery then become more personal role-objects. Undertaker and Laughing Fox accumulate histories that exceed their original battlefield functions. Theo's later Laughing Fox/reaching-hand adaptation is especially important because symbolic continuity survives without restoration of his old body or Processor role.

Uniforms and formal clothing similarly operate as **inhabitable roles**, not essential selves. V07 social dress briefly makes noncombat identities experientially available. V11's Lena deliberately uses Republic uniform/cap/rifle visual grammar to compel civilians to move while internally refusing the total identity that image would imply. `Saint`, `Queen`, officer, and lover remain overlapping roles rather than one final icon.

This supports a general formal rule:

> **Costume in *86* often asks what a person can do while wearing a role, not what the costume reveals as the person's true essence.**

# 9. Flowers, red, white, black - recurrence without a fixed color code

Flowers recur extensively enough to be meaningful, but T13 rejects a fixed flower dictionary.

Early red/poppy imagery joins battlefield death, memory, and the emergence of Bloody Queen imagery. Later flower fields can become meeting spaces and sites of revised pride. White flowers can be stained by massacre; roses can appear in counterfactual gentle-world imagery; Alter.1 can redirect flowers toward birthdays and living social continuity.

The strongest pattern is **reuse**, not stable meaning.

Color requires even more caution. The lexical sweep found the black/white/red family vastly more often than any other family. The reason is obvious: the novels constantly describe hair, eyes, military uniforms, blood, snow, flags, darkness, machines, fire and light. A high count therefore proves that color is structurally important to description, not that each color has one code.

V14's `Paint It Black` architecture is unusually foregrounded, but even there black participates in multiple relations: concealment, butterfly swarms, visual targeting, political darkness, and the act of marking something so it can be seen. White can mean snow, Alba visual identity, flowers, purity rhetoric, blankness, or visible contrast. Crimson can mean blood, title, aristocratic lineage, warning, or military/political identity.

T13's rule is:

> **Color claims require local compositional evidence plus longitudinal recurrence; color alone never establishes a thematic proposition.**

# 10. Butterflies and wings - soul imagery is real, but not universal

Butterflies recur strongly enough to deserve a dedicated family. V10's Saiki material permits butterfly/soul/release associations without requiring literal cosmology. V14 weaponizes black butterfly imagery through Vaneseed, turning winged swarms into a delivery system for forced traumatic memory.

This is a useful reversal:

- butterfly can suggest release;
- butterfly-like form can also carry invasive control.

The visual similarity is therefore not a stable moral label. V14 makes connection itself dangerous when access to memory is nonconsensual.

The cover and internal illustration program reinforces the wing/butterfly field, but T13 keeps the cover at PT/VI weight. The strongest evidence comes from the combination of prose description, chapter architecture, weapon function and illustration—not the cover alone.

# 11. Photographs, cameras, footage, faces - the ethics of images

V13 provides the decisive negative control for a naive visual epistemology.

Rito's act is recorded. The image can be genuine and still tell a false causal story when cropped, narrated, or circulated without context. This is one of the clearest places where *86* explicitly states that **seeing is not equivalent to understanding**.

That principle retroactively disciplines the whole visual analysis.

- a photograph proves that a moment/image existed, not everything surrounding it;
- an illustration selects one instant and angle;
- a cover is editorial composition, not omniscient interpretation;
- a scar is visible history, not transparent meaning;
- a humanlike machine body can produce misleading recognition;
- No Face's V14 Rei-shaped appearance is mediated through Shin's expectation and should not be treated as literal proof of Rei identity.

The V14 glitch/fragment treatment of the Rei-shaped image is especially important. The illustration visualizes a familiar face as an interface assembled by perception/memory rather than stable bodily presence. Visual familiarity becomes evidence of **how Shin experiences the encounter**, not a metaphysical photograph of what No Face objectively is.

# 12. Maps, diagrams, technical plates - materiality before symbolism

The light-novel form gives machines and operations unusual visual weight. Juggernaut, Reginleif, Legion units, ships, maps, warning platforms, friendly-unit diagrams, and late-arc battlefield maps appear throughout the EPUBs.

These should first be read technically.

A machine diagram establishes:

- body plan;
- scale;
- armament;
- mobility;
- mechanical difference;
- or operational context.

Only afterward should interpretation ask whether the visual form participates in a broader argument.

V01's Juggernaut is a particularly good example. The machine's visual materiality helps expose the absurdity of calling the system “unmanned,” but the diagram itself does not make the political argument; the administrative language does.

Similarly, V02's Reginleif plate concretizes a better state's willingness to build a machine whose performance remains brutal for ordinary human bodies. The visual evidence supports embodiment and military-design analysis without proving that the Reginleif “symbolizes” the Five.

T13 therefore rejects **symbolizing technical diagrams by default**.

# 13. Calendars, birthdays, clocks, fireworks, food - ordinary recurrence as form

Some of the series' strongest future motifs are deliberately mundane.

Birthdays in Alter.1 move from forgotten/uncertain dates to reconstructed records, improvised survivor celebration, chosen gifts, repeatable ritual, and war-resistant plans for another year. The key formal property is recurrence.

Fireworks operate similarly. They can visually resemble battlefield light, but later peaceful repetition adds rather than erases meaning. A future festival matters because it creates another calendar point worth reaching.

V09's clock/crocodile title grammar and V11's D-DAY graphics use time differently. D-DAY imposes impersonal operational order over catastrophe; birthday/festival time creates social repetition. Both make time visible, but they organize it toward different purposes.

Food and recipes become especially important by V14. They are not merely comfort imagery. The radio/recipe material counters categorical demonization with ordinary particularity: people who have been made into symbols of danger are reintroduced as people who cook, inherit family tastes, improvise bad food, remember hospitality, and imagine sharing it.

Raiden's dandelion coffee is a particularly compact object of cross-racial memory and independent future desire. The item is ordinary enough that it resists heroic abstraction.

This produces a strong T06/T13 bridge:

> **A future can be materially represented by objects whose meaning requires another ordinary occasion, not only by grand destinations.**

# 14. V01 visual architecture - separation, voice, embodiment

The V01 EPUB gives T13 one of the clearest multi-layer formal structures.

Front matter presents Lena and Shin as paired but separated figures before they have seen one another. The Japanese caption explicitly emphasizes that they fight on separate battlefields without seeing each other's form. The volume then makes disembodied voice its primary relational medium through Para-RAID. A final image reverses that geometry through embodied meeting.

This is strong formal evidence because:

1. the prose relationship is voice-first;
2. the front matter visually stages separation;
3. the final illustration stages bodily co-presence;
4. later volumes repeatedly return to voice/body distance under changed conditions.

V13's physically detained Lena returning first through voice is therefore a genuine formal rhyme, but not a simple repetition. The relationship has changed from asymmetric Handler/Processor contact to reciprocal attachment and command trust.

# 15. V07-V09 visual form - alternative roles and fairy-tale frames

V07 uses social dress, dance, festival and fireworks to make alternative roles temporarily inhabitable. The dance is particularly useful as a relationship image because coordination does not require identical rhythm. Shin and Lena adjust, stumble and continue.

V08's paired `THE TOWER (UPRIGHT)` / `THE TOWER (REVERSE)` chapter architecture is stronger than most free-floating symbols because the form itself gives an explicit before/after logic. Upright movement strips away desire/feeling in order to survive; reversed movement permits pride to coexist with grief, desire and wish.

V09's fairy-tale grammar—mirror, blue bird, clock, piper, queen—repeatedly offers fixed story roles while the prose asks whether characters can leave the role without losing the relationships or meaning formed within it.

The lesson across these volumes is that **formal archetype is frequently something the character must learn to inhabit without becoming identical to it**.

# 16. V10 fragment form - the series' clearest formal self-commentary

V10 is organized as fragments, backward/forward numbered pieces, partial memories, inherited objects and reconstructed identity. This structure is not incidental to the content.

The volume asks what made Undertaker and then refuses a single origin story. It gives a network of objects and people instead.

The formal principle is:

> **continuity does not require seamlessness.**

A self can be historically real while assembled from partial inheritances whose meanings have changed. A memory object can remain; an earlier role can be retained; a past wound can remain legible; none has to function as the sole explanation of the present person.

This principle becomes one of the most portable formal findings in the V2 corpus.

# 17. V11 operational graphics and ruined national symbols

V11's D-DAY graphics perform an unusual formal operation: continent-scale catastrophe is made schedulable in the typography of military chronology. The effect is not emotional indifference so much as the command necessity to turn catastrophe into something on which action can be organized.

The Great Wall, Republic uniform, rifle, cap, Saint/Queen imagery and the final embrace all participate in a second movement: symbols of San Magnolia are not merely destroyed. Several are **stripped of sovereign interpretive authority and reassigned**.

Lena can wear/use Republic military visual grammar without accepting the Republic's ideology as her identity. The final private embrace then pulls the closing image away from the public saint/queen/military icon and toward grief held by another person.

# 18. Alter.1 scrapbook / film-strip form - ordinary memory as architecture

Alter.1 is formally distinct from the campaign volumes. Its cover/divider material uses scrapbook/film-strip imagery and snapshot-like organization. This matters because the collection is itself an archive of ordinary youth, side stories, gifts, birthdays, food, perfume, a kitten, records, and objects that recur outside decisive battles.

That paratext does not prove every short story is equally canonical or chronological; the collection contains ordering complications already controlled elsewhere. But it does make ordinary memory, rather than operational map progression, the collection's governing publication form.

The empty photo frame is an especially effective object within that architecture: the archive can contain a visible blank that remains available to future images.

# 19. V13 - symbolic boundary becomes battlefield geography

V13's formal sequence is one of the cleanest in the series:

> **social `we/them` line -> refused cooperation -> political fragmentation -> territorial separation -> minefield**

The final mined rear does not need symbolic embellishment because the political relation has become literal space.

The volume also makes visual evidence itself an ethical problem through Rito footage. A true image can be incorporated into a false narrative. That is one reason T13 ranks image context and provenance so highly.

Chitri's ribbon then provides the opposite kind of visual/material evidence: an ordinary object transferred voluntarily between particular people. It remains modest enough not to become a total explanation of Chitri, Yuto, grief or obligation.

# 20. V14 - paint, black butterflies, recipes, borrowed faces

V14's subtitle, chapter titles, prose and illustrations create the strongest color/visibility architecture in the current endpoint.

The volume repeatedly asks who can see what is actually present:

- hidden artillery and concealed battlefield structures;
- manipulated public narratives;
- trauma forcibly made present through Vaneseed;
- No Face appearing through a borrowed/familiar face;
- Shin's concealed sensory damage;
- political actors hiding contingency behind claims of justice;
- ordinary Eighty-Six lives hidden behind monster categories.

`Paint It Black` should therefore not be reduced to black = death. Paint and marking participate in the act of making a hidden target visible enough to act upon. Black butterfly imagery simultaneously turns a visually delicate form into an invasive memory weapon.

The V14 illustration of the Rei-shaped No Face encounter uses fragmentation/glitching around the familiar face. T13 reads that as strong VI evidence for **memory/interface instability**, while retaining the epistemic boundary established by the prose: the image does not prove literal Rei persistence.

The cover is paratextually confirmed by the afterword to depict Lena. That confirmation matters because visual identification should not be inferred solely from resemblance. The cover gives Lena unusual final-arc visual centrality but remains publication emphasis rather than proof that every V14 thematic axis belongs primarily to her.

Recipes/stew then supply the deliberately non-monumental counterpoint. Public hatred wants the Eighty-Six to remain monsters; recipe broadcasts make them particular, domestic and culturally continuous without claiming that ordinary charm resolves political conflict.

# 21. Overreading controls - what T13 explicitly refuses

T13 freezes the following restrictions for later specialist synthesis.

## 21.1 No one-to-one color dictionary

Red, white, black, blue and silver are too pervasive and context-dependent. Color may organize a scene or volume, but each claim must name the local relation.

## 21.2 No automatic soul-butterfly reading

Butterfly/soul association exists in specific contexts. V14 proves butterfly form can also carry invasive violence.

## 21.3 No universal sea = freedom equation

The sea is future, place, ecology, disappointment, battlefield and bodily rupture.

## 21.4 No object = psychological state shortcut

Possessing a scarf, gun, ribbon, photograph or emblem does not prove the bearer is trapped in its first meaning.

## 21.5 No illustration as omniscient camera

Illustrations select moments and may visualize subjective perception. V13 footage and V14 Rei/No Face are explicit warnings.

## 21.6 No cover as chronology

Covers establish marketing/editorial emphasis and visual comparison, not event order or exclusive causal priority.

## 21.7 No technical image as allegory by default

Maps and machine plates must first be read as technical/material information.

## 21.8 No symbolic analogy without historical differentiation

A wall, cage, line, collar or minefield can rhyme structurally without making two institutions historically identical. T11's graded-comparison rule still governs.

# 24. Final T13 proposition

The most defensible full-corpus formulation is:

> **In *86*, objects and spaces matter less as secret labels than as repositories of changing relations. A fragment can be a grave because ordinary burial is forbidden; a scarf can outlive the identity that first needed it; a wall can move from segregation to memory; a ship can matter because it returns people rather than because institutions must be immortal; a ribbon can turn memory into chosen inheritance; a recipe can make public categories particular again. The series repeatedly preserves material continuity while revising human meaning. Its visual form is therefore strongest when read historically: not "what does this image always mean?" but "what has this object, place or image been made to mean here, who is using it now, and what earlier meaning still exerts pressure on the new one?"**

That question should govern Document 13 and every later use of symbolic evidence.
