# T01 — Return, Refusal, Retreat, Waiting, and Coming Back Retrieval

This dossier is the first Phase-4 thematic retrieval pass. It does not attempt to prove a single master metaphor called “return.” Its purpose is to recover every major source pathway by which the series uses return, refusal, retreat, waiting, abandonment, catch-up, homeland, role exit, and coming back; reread those hits in scene context; separate meanings that later synthesis is tempted to collapse; and route the retained evidence toward the specialist documents that will be drafted only after the Phase-5 evidence lock.

The governing question is narrower than the Phase-3 checkpoint thesis: **Can the language of “return” legitimately connect the military, relational, political, psychological, and posthuman layers of the series, or does that connection disappear once the Japanese vocabulary and counterexamples are separated?** The answer at the V14 boundary is: return is a real longitudinal structure, but only if it remains a family of related problems rather than one universal metaphor.

# 0. Retrieval authority and evidence discipline

Primary authority is the locked original-Japanese prose of Volumes 1–14. Alter.1 is included only through its already-audited story-level chronology and is marked supplemental. Alter.2 is excluded because its AU/counterfactual events are not mainline facts. The canonical V2 volume readings and L01–L11 ledgers supply prior locator IDs and interpretation history, but the Phase-4 search was rerun against the EPUB text rather than merely searching our own prose.

The lexical sweep generated **2,656 family-hit rows across 2,443 unique primary-source paragraphs**. A paragraph may belong to more than one lexical family, so the family totals must not be summed as unique occurrences. Search frequency is not treated as thematic weight. V13, for example, contains unusually dense retreat/return vocabulary because its plot physically closes routes; V7 contains relatively little such vocabulary because its dramatic work concerns what characters do after a period of successful return and leave.

Every item retained in the human-facing ledger therefore passed a context reread. Exact volume locators inherited from Phase 2 remain traceable to the EPUB; newly retained V1 items were recovered directly from the source sweep. The machine appendices preserve both the raw search summary and the context-retained ledger so Phase 5 can audit inclusion and omission.

`帰る` and `戻る` overlap in English but do different work often enough that they must remain separate retrieval channels. `帰る` tends to foreground destination, homeward movement, or a relation that receives the returning person; `戻る` often foregrounds restoration, re-entry into a prior position/state, or movement back to an earlier point. This is a tendency, not a dictionary rule. V14 deliberately exploits both: Lena can say she cannot yet `戻る` to the front because her duty is elsewhere, while reactionaries demand that Giad `戻る` to empire.

Likewise, `待つ` is not uniformly romantic. It can mean waiting for a person, standing by on military order, passively waiting for destruction, or preserving a return-address. `退却`, `撤退`, `後退`, and `退路` belong first to military doctrine, not psychology. The dossier therefore prohibits translating “retreat” automatically into emotional withdrawal or treating emotional “return” as proof that tactical withdrawal is morally required.

# 2. Executive retrieval finding: eight distinct structures of return

The context-reread evidence supports at least eight structures that later specialist prose must keep distinct. They can interact, but no one of them should stand in for all the others.

| Structure | Definition | Representative locus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Kinship return | A person survives because someone specific should receive them again. | Rei -> Shin in V1; later family and ordinary relationships. |
| 2. Operational return | A military expects extraction, recovery, rest, and repeated redeployment rather than disappearance. | V3 onward; explicit retreat doctrine V5; normalized V14 maxim. |
| 3. Relational return-address | Someone waits, calls, or asks the combatant to come back. | V4-V6 Shin/Lena; then Anju/Dustin and wider peers. |
| 4. Role exit / return | A person may leave a role, temporarily or permanently, without ceasing to belong; returning to a role can also be chosen. | Theo V8-V14; Lena V12-V14; retirement contrasts. |
| 5. Home/homeland return | Origin, current home, and chosen memorial destination can diverge. | V6, V11, Alter.1, V13. |
| 6. Return after moral/psychological error | A person can become frightened, cruel, narrowed, or wrong and later revise course. | Prepared by V7-V13; directly stated in V14. |
| 7. Posthuman/continuity return | A subject may return after body destruction/recreation or through machine continuity, complicating identity. | Fido, Sirins, Shepherds; not reducible to human homecoming. |
| 8. Reactionary return | “Return” may mean regression to an earlier political order or identity enclosure. | V14 call to restore empire; critical negative control. |

The strongest cross-layer commonality is therefore not the word “return” itself. It is **whether a subject retains or regains a viable route back into relations, roles, institutions, places, and forms of life that are not exhausted by the crisis currently defining them**. The inverse is blocked return: no retreat, no homeward route, no role exit, no capacity to revise an end, or no person willing/able to receive the subject again.

# 3. Volume-by-volume retrieval

## V01

Volume 1 supplies both the positive seed and the institutional negation. Rei says `必ず生きて、帰らないと` (V01-L014; V01 / p-008.xhtml ¶489). The line is familial before it is romantic or political: survival has an addressee. The same volume then constructs Special Recon as an anti-return institution. A Processer who moves backward is classified as a deserter and can be executed; friendly registration is erased; the rear can fire upon retreat; and personal affairs are prepared on the assumption that the soldiers will never return. This makes the first series-level contrast surprisingly precise: **a person can understand life through return even while the state designs war around nonreturn.**

## V02

After legal rescue, return becomes dangerous evidence rather than an automatic good. Kurena says `帰ろう。あたし達のいるべきところに` (V02-L024; V02 / p-006.xhtml ¶336–343): the Five voluntarily “return” to military life. Ernst wants them `ただの子供に戻って` / empty rooms` (V02-L025; V02 / p-006.xhtml ¶352–358), imagining recovery as restoration to ordinary childhood. The text rejects both easy readings. The battlefield return is genuinely chosen but shaped by persecution, competence, pride, and lack of alternative identity; the childhood return is benevolent but impossible because there is no intact prior childhood to restore. V2 also says `退却という思考はそもそもなかった` (V02-L043; V02 / p-008.xhtml ¶282). The crucial institutional deficit is therefore not merely that the Republic ordered lethal missions; it trained soldiers for whom retreat did not exist as a trusted cognitive category.

## V03

Volume 3 turns return into explicit institutional and relational doctrine. Shin's abandonment language becomes direct: `置いていかれてばかりだ` / `おいていかないで` (V03-L008; V03 / p-005.xhtml ¶350–372), later `どうして今度も、連れていってくれなかった` / `おいていかないで` (V03-L040; V03 / p-011.xhtml ¶400–412). Frederica and Giad answer with a different norm: `必ず帰れ` (V03-L021; V03 / p-009.xhtml ¶089). Raiden then distinguishes survival instinct from return-intent through `生きて帰ってくるつもりもなかった` (V03-L022; V03 / p-009.xhtml ¶326). This is the first volume where “come back alive” becomes a social expectation rather than an improbable exception. Lena's `追いつきましたよ` / `一緒に戦います` (V03-L055; V03 / p-015.xhtml ¶028–037) completes the road/catch-up structure: she does not restore Shin to an earlier self; she reaches the road he is already on and joins it.

## V04

V4 makes restoration itself suspect. Frederica's `先に進めぬ、ではない。戻れぬ、であろ。` (V04-L015; V04 / p-006.xhtml ¶639) distinguishes being unable to advance from being unable to go back. The point is not that the past should be forgotten; it is that recovery cannot mean becoming the untouched person who existed before injury. Lena's battle request `必ず、ここへ。……帰ってきてください` (V04-L028; V04 / p-009.xhtml ¶474) moves the return motif toward a concrete person/place address. Yet V4 also shows that combat optimization can momentarily suppress that address from active awareness. Return is therefore an obligation that must be institutionally and relationally sustained, not a magical cure created by affection.

## V05

V5 gives the military vocabulary its cleanest doctrinal correction: `撤退は、れっきとした戦術行動です` (V05-L028; V05 / p-006.xhtml ¶759). Retreat is not cowardice and not a betrayal of pride; it is a legitimate tactical action. The volume simultaneously puts abandonment language into Vika's creation of Lerche: `傍にいてくれるか` / `おいて逝かないでくれ` (V05-L030; V05 / p-007.xhtml ¶59–60). The juxtaposition is useful for later synthesis. Militarily, a humane command system must preserve withdrawal. Relationally, a creator/guardian must confront the difference between wanting someone to remain and having authority to make continued presence compulsory.

## V06

Volume 6 is the densest relational transformation. Shin tells Lena `『帰ってこいと、貴女が命じたのでしょう。それなら待っていてください。帰る場所...` (V06-L036; V06 / p-006 ¶800) and then `『おいていかないでください』` (V06-L037; V06 / p-006 ¶804). Lena answers with `「だから、帰ってきなさい。絶対に。わたしはおいていきませんから、──あなたも...` (V06-L042; V06 / p-006 ¶871). Waiting, place, and return become bilateral: neither person is simply rescuer or destination. Importantly, Shin also gives Rito a legitimate nondeployment option `「……本部で、待機しているか」` (V06-L034; V06 / p-005 ¶850); the volume's interdependence ethic therefore includes refusal as well as attachment. The climax ties survival to unfinished ordinary obligations `必ず帰ると。話をしようと。傷つけたのを謝ることさえ自分はできていないのに、こ...` (V06-L047; V06 / p-007 ¶666), and the epilogue widens home again: the Five currently return to the same place `今は、同じ場所に帰ってくる。` (V06-L060; V06 / p-009 ¶93), but Shin can leave recovered blood family because `「今日は、帰ります。……待っている人がいますから」` (V06-L066; V06 / p-009 ¶139). Return has become plural rather than exclusive.

## V07

V7 has fewer literal return hits because its problem is how to inhabit time after return. Its relevant contribution is boundary correction. Shiden attacks Lena for repeatedly stepping backward and drawing an unnecessary line: `すぐ線引いて後ずさりしやがって` (V07-L022; V07 / p-008.xhtml ¶96). She also insists that the past includes not only error and helplessness but `間違いも無力も後悔も、──その先の決断も` (V07-L025; V07 / p-008.xhtml ¶120). This is an important precursor to V14 moral return: revision does not require pretending the earlier act or identity never existed. One crosses a line by adding later decisions, not by purifying history.

## V08

V8 stress-tests whether the V6-V7 future survives catastrophic subtraction. Ishmael's commitment `機動打撃群は生還させる` (V08-L017; V08 / p-005.xhtml ¶89) places return inside allied military doctrine. Theo then reaches `帰らないと` (V08-L050; V08 / p-011.xhtml ¶681); if the old home no longer exists, the narration continues with `どこかに見つけて。作って` (V08-L051; V08 / p-011.xhtml ¶682). This is one of the dossier's most important transitions: **return need not presuppose a preserved destination**. A return-place can be made after loss. Shin's new ability to imagine not fighting `戦わなくてもいいんだと思う` (V08-L032; V08 / p-008.xhtml ¶95) and not treating the battlefield as his only place `戦場以外に居場所がないとも、もう思わない` (V08-L034; V08 / p-008.xhtml ¶95) similarly converts role exit from betrayal into a live possibility.

## V09

V9 generalizes reciprocal return beyond the central romance. Anju promises `私も必ず、貴方のいる場所に帰ってくるから` (V09-L025; V09 / p-006 ¶506). At the same time, the volume repeatedly tests refusal of roles that become total: the hero expectation is explicitly rejected in `ヒーロー役まで、期待されんのはまっぴらだ` (V09-L037; V09 / p-010 ¶378–394), while Hielna's first positive movement is a refusal to abandon her living subordinates `わたくしだけは、わたくしの部下たちを見捨ててはいけない` (V09-L057; V09 / p-011 ¶533). The volume's “pride/wish/bond/prayer/curse” architecture therefore adds a necessary T01 rule: a commitment is not anti-freedom merely because it binds; the problem is whether the bond abolishes exit, revision, or the other person's separate future.

## V10

V10 pushes the motif backward through Shin's history. Young Shin asks Fido `それでも、一緒に帰るか` (V10-L015; V10 / p-012 ¶98), showing that companionship-as-return predates the mature Lena relationship. The Lethe material then gives return-language to death and release: `かえりたい` (V10-L047; V10 / p-027 ¶293) followed by `もう──かえっていい` (V10-L048; V10 / p-027 ¶328). These passages cannot simply be collapsed into ordinary human homecoming, but they reveal how deeply “going home” is entangled with release from role, burden, and continued compulsion. V10 also makes the Undertaker office genealogical: return to the past means reinterpretation of inherited fragments, not discovery of an immutable essence.

## V11

V11 is the strongest home/homeland differentiation. Rito says `EXACT_VERIFIED` (V11-L007; V11 / 人質なんか要らないから。ちゃんと帰ってくるから): hostage logic is unnecessary because return has been internalized. Anju says `EXACT_VERIFIED` (V11-L018; V11 / 帰るところは、ここじゃないから); birthplace remains historically real without remaining present home. Shin and Lena then convert devastated San Magnolia into a **voluntary future destination** through `EXACT_VERIFIED` (V11-L019; V11 / また来よう。次は、おれたちみんなで) and `EXACT_VERIFIED` (V11-L020; V11 / 必ず、帰ってきます。ここに). This is neither nationalist restoration nor repudiation of origin. It is memorial return under conditions where leaving again remains possible. The same volume's nonrevenge sequence `EXACT_VERIFIED` (V11-L010; V11 / 復讐なんて、おれは望まなかったし、選ばなかった) demonstrates refusal as self-definition without mandatory forgiveness.

## V12

V12 clarifies that temporary removal from role can itself protect future agency. Lena is told `一月ほどは軍務を離れて休養すべき` (V12-L008; V12 / p-004 ¶206); the point is not that she ceases to be a commander but that command cannot consume the body that makes continued command possible. Zelene's `わたくしは死なない。まだ死は選ばない` (V12-L073; V12 / p-008 ¶539) similarly makes death-refusal future-oriented without requiring biological humanity. The Fawn request `帰り道を、教えてほしいの` (V12-L078; V12 / p-009 ¶33) then turns “way home” into the next political problem: knowing where one came from is not enough if institutions, bodies, or violence make the route unusable.

## V13

V13 makes **blocked return** an explicit political technology. Chitri's `「這ってでも行くわ。……帰るんだもの」` (V13-L068; V13 / V13 / p-006.xhtml para 11) and her list of the ordinary life she wanted `「こんな知らない場所じゃなくて、こんな何もない場所じゃなくて故郷の街に帰りたかった。もう一度おとうさんに、おかあさんに、先生...` (V13-L091; V13 / V13 / p-007.xhtml para 177) make home concrete. Dustin's return is relationally scaffolded by Anju's voice: `「君の声が聞こえたんだ。帰ってきて、って。……願ってくれたんだろ？」` (V13-L101; V13 / V13 / p-007.xhtml para 587) and `「届いたよ、ちゃんと。帰らなきゃって、だから思えた。だから死なないでここまで、帰ってこられたんだ。ずるをした俺だけど、ずるを...` (V13-L102; V13 / V13 / p-007.xhtml para 589). The volume then closes the route. Lena is prevented from returning to Rustkammer `「ただし──リュストカマー基地への帰還だけは、許可できません。……ミリーゼ大佐、ペンローズ少佐はとりわけ、ご存じでしょう。全...` (V13-L115; V13 / V13 / p-007.xhtml para 885); the front is compared directly with the Republic no-retreat architecture `〈ジャガーノート〉とその処理装置に、一切の後退を許さぬために要塞壁と地雷原とを敷設した、共和国の戦場と同じように。` (V13-L122; V13 / V13 / p-007.xhtml para 980); withdrawal is prohibited `機動打撃群は、以降、リュストカマー戦区を死守。部隊の後退はこれを許可しない。` (V13-L123; V13 / V13 / p-007.xhtml para 983); peaceful-home return is explicitly denied `平和の故郷に帰ることも。` (V13-L124; V13 / V13 / p-007.xhtml para 985); and the final image is `征路を〈レギオン〉に、退路を人の悪意に鎖される。` (V13-L126; V13 / V13 / p-007.xhtml para 987). This confirms the checkpoint's narrow Sector definition: **expendability becomes Sector-like when refusal and return are materially closed.**

## V14

V14 elevates the motif from logistics and relationship into explicit moral anthropology, while also preserving negative controls. Lena says she cannot yet return because current duty is elsewhere `「わたしは戻れません。わたしには──今、中央にいるわたしには、こちらでやるべきことがある。だから……今はまだ、戻れません」` (V14-L024; V14 / p-005 para 66); Theo answers `「待ってるよ。僕もみんなも、──シンも」` (V14-L025; V14 / p-005 para 69). Annette wants to forbid Theo's departure but refuses to turn care into veto `行かないで、と言いたかった。けれどセオは、戻るつもりだ。アネットがいまさら何か言ってもそれはきっと変わらないし、それ以上に万一にも彼の意志を...` (V14-L027; V14 / p-005 para 85), asking instead that he return `「シンとレーナとアンジュとダスティンと、ついでに王子殿下にぶつけるんだから。一緒にやるって言ったんだから。だからちゃんと待っててよ。ちゃんと...` (V14-L028; V14 / p-005 para 95). Shin then states the central proposition: `「そうだ。だから、他の奴やつらだって変われる。戻ってこられる」` (V14-L044; V14 / p-005 para 308). Yet the epilogue warns that return is not intrinsically good: `「……我らギアーデは、再び帝国に戻るべきだ」` (V14-L209; V14 / p-009 para 15) calls for political regression to empire. V14 therefore licenses “coming back” as a model of human corrigibility only when the destination itself remains open to judgment.

## A1

Alter.1 supplies indispensable before-states. In the age-twelve winter parallel, Shin believes nobody waits for his return (`A1-E008`). During Special Recon he can imagine Lena as a future encounter without strongly valuing his own continued presence (`A1-E027`). Later, recovered records can restore an old address without making it home (`A1-E055`), coffee/tea can become a tiny repeatable future (`A1-E064`), old abandonment grammar can reverse into ordinary security (`A1-E075`), and Anju's birthday flowers can be preserved for a living person's return rather than only for the dead (`A1-E095`). The supplemental corpus therefore confirms that return is built through repeated low-pressure practices, not only climactic vows.

# 4. Lexical distinction: return is not one Japanese word

## 4.1 `帰る`: destination-bearing return

Across the retained scenes, `帰る` most often carries an implicit or explicit destination: younger brother, base, person waiting, home, homeland, or the ordinary place one resumes after an operation. This makes it especially important to Shin/Lena and home language. But V2 provides the essential falsifier: `帰る` can point **back to war** when war is the only place that feels inhabitable. Destination-bearing language therefore does not certify that the destination is healthy.

## 4.2 `戻る`: restoration, re-entry, and regression

`戻る` more often raises the question of returning to a prior state, position, or institutional order. That is why “become ordinary children again,” Lena returning to command/front, inability to return after irreversible change, and the V14 demand to return to empire belong in the same lexical family while carrying opposite normative values. The V2 synthesis should therefore be suspicious of any English sentence that says simply “return” without specifying whether the Japanese is `帰る` or `戻る` and what exactly is being restored.

## 4.3 `待つ`: waiting as address, standby, or passivity

Waiting becomes life-bearing when it preserves an address for voluntary return: V6 Lena, V14 Theo, ordinary family/peer scenes. But waiting can also mean military standby or condemned passivity. V3 Lena refuses merely waiting for Republic destruction. T01 therefore rejects the formula “waiting = love.” The ethically important form is **waiting that neither abandons nor imprisons**: the person can leave, act, fail, and still have somewhere to come back to.

## 4.4 `退却` / `撤退` / `後退` / `退路`: military withdrawal before metaphor

V1 Special Recon destroys retreat institutionally. V2 shows the resulting learned absence. V5 explicitly restores `撤退` as legitimate tactical doctrine. V11 demonstrates commanders ordering withdrawal from impossible rescues. V13 then deliberately recreates a battlefield where `後退` is forbidden and `退路` is chained by human malice. This is a direct military progression. Any psychological metaphor built from it must preserve the tactical substrate rather than replacing it.

## 4.5 `おいていかないで` and catch-up language

The left-behind phrase is one of the clearest emotional longitudinal markers because it moves from childhood/family trauma into explicit adult petition and then toward more reliable presence. `追いつく` supplies a different geometry: Lena does not demand that Shin go backward; she catches up. The relational ideal is therefore neither permanent fusion nor one person dragging the other home. It is **separate movement with recoverable distance**.

# 5. Return from battle versus return to battle

This distinction is essential. The Five voluntarily returning to military service in V2 is not the same phenomenon as Giad expecting soldiers to return **from** an operation. One concerns vocation/belonging; the other concerns military doctrine and the refusal of expendability. The series can affirm both at once: a person may choose soldierhood, and the institution may still be morally required to plan for that soldier to survive, withdraw, rest, receive treatment, and eventually stop.

The mature military standard emerging from T01 is therefore **recoverable service**. Professional risk is compatible with personhood when high-risk missions remain bounded by extraction plans, retreat options where tactically possible, medical care, replacement, rotation, and the presumption that the soldier continues to exist after usefulness in the immediate battle ends. The Republic's Sector violates this not because every order is dangerous but because the architecture turns nonreturn into the intended administrative outcome.

# 6. Waiting and the construction of a return-address

The most important relational change is not simply that Shin acquires people he loves. He acquires people who make **return behaviorally intelligible**. Early Shin can carry the dead forward while imagining nobody waits for him. V3 externalizes the abandonment grammar. V4-V6 then convert “come back” into repeated address, and V6 makes it bilateral. Later volumes distribute this beyond Shin/Lena: Anju and Dustin, Theo and Annette, Raiden and an old teacher, unit-level waiting for Lena, birthdays and flowers in Alter.1.

This makes “being awaited” materially different from being possessed. The healthy form does not say: because I wait, you owe me permanent conformity. It says: your departure does not erase your standing here, and your return will be received. V13's hostage logic is the political corruption of the same structure: a valued person can be held precisely because others are expected to return for her. Thus the existence of a return-address expands freedom only when the address is not controlled by someone who can turn attachment into coercion.

# 7. Refusal as a component of substantive freedom

T01 recovers refusal at several levels: refusal of passive defeat, refusal of death, refusal of revenge, refusal of total hero roles, refusal to deploy a frightened subordinate, refusal to make a creator's desire into a command, refusal to treat cruelty as final human truth, and refusal to let care become veto. These scenes support the checkpoint's substantive-freedom thesis, but they also constrain it. **Refusal is meaningful only if the subject has some viable way to survive the refusal.**

The Sector provides the negative control. A Processor may theoretically prefer not to fight, but the legal/material structure makes refusal lethal or impossible. V13's trapped front reproduces this locally: a formal person may still be denied meaningful refusal when rear routes, supplies, political standing, and return are closed. Conversely, V6 Rito can be offered headquarters, V12 Lena can be removed from duty without loss of personhood, and V14 Annette can fear Theo's choice without owning it. The series therefore treats refusal less as heroic defiance than as **institutional evidence that the person's answer still matters**.

# 8. Retreat as doctrine and the narrow definition of a Sector

The retreat sequence is unusually coherent across fourteen volumes. V1 makes retreat administratively criminal. V2 shows the soldiers no longer thinking in retreat categories. V5 explicitly restores retreat as legitimate tactics. V11 shows competent command accepting withdrawal from impossible rescue. V13 then removes retreat again and directly names the resulting structure as comparable to the old Sector.

This strongly supports the narrow structural definition generated in V13, but T01 adds an important limitation: **not every no-retreat order is an Eighty-Sixth Sector.** Military operations can legitimately require holding terrain or accepting extraordinary risk. The Sector analogy becomes robust when no-retreat combines with disposability, constrained refusal, blocked return, political distancing, and the expectation that the exposed group will absorb danger for those who excluded or abandoned it. Phase 5 should require these conjunctive features before routing a “Sector reproduction” claim into Documents 05–07.

# 9. Home, homeland, archive, and the right not to return

T01 confirms that home must remain plural. V6 allows blood history to return without displacing current belonging. Alter.1 shows that an archived address can be recovered and still feel like an unknown house. V11 separates birthplace, `祖国`, and `故郷`; the destroyed Republic can become somewhere characters voluntarily revisit without becoming the place they must resume living. V13 then demonstrates why the *ability* to return matters even when return is not obligatory: Chitri wants family, school, friends, adulthood, and familiar streets, while the final minefield converts those ordinary options into impossibility.

The mature principle is therefore two-sided: **freedom requires neither compulsory return nor compulsory departure.** A person may recover origin without being forced back into it, leave home without forfeiting belonging, make a new home after loss, or choose to revisit a former home as memorial practice. Blocked return is oppressive because it removes an option; compulsory restoration would be oppressive for the same reason.

# 10. Moral return: what V14 actually earns

V14's “other people can change and come back” is the strongest direct textual support for the checkpoint's revisability language, but T01 narrows what can legitimately be projected backward. Earlier volumes establish prerequisites: people can make later decisions without erasing past errors (V7); attachments can survive changed form (V8-V9); revenge can be refused without forgiveness (V11); legal freedom can coexist with impaired agency and agency can be rebuilt through scaffolding (V12); Shin himself can become contemptuous under stress and be interrupted back into judgment (V13). V14 then names a general proposition.

The dossier therefore permits **moral return** as a mature V14 formulation but forbids turning it into a claim that every wrongdoer will return, that institutions automatically recover, or that forgiveness is owed once someone changes. Return is possible, not guaranteed. Accountability may remain. Victims may remain unwilling to reconcile. Some damage is irreversible. The value lies in refusing to treat a bad state as metaphysical essence.

# 11. Posthuman return and the limits of the metaphor

Fido, Sirins, Shepherds, and No Face force another caution. A machine subject can “return” after body destruction, a Sirin can be recreated, and a Shepherd can retain memory while pursuing transformed ends. These are not simply metaphors for human trauma recovery. They are ontology questions about continuity, backup, embodiment, command architecture, and purpose. T01 therefore routes posthuman return evidence primarily to Documents 08 and 11 rather than using it as decorative confirmation of the human psychological thesis.

The important cross-layer question is narrower: **what can change while the subject remains the same enough to have standing?** Fido suggests continuity across body loss; Sirins complicate replacement and emergent desire; Shepherds demonstrate highly adaptive cognition whose ends may remain purpose-captured; No Face proposes peace by removing the capacity for open-ended human life. Return is useful here only when it names continuity-through-change, not when it erases technical distinctions.

- Preserve V14 return-to-empire as a standing falsifier against any celebratory master metaphor of return.

# 17. Open questions at the V14 boundary

- Can Giad politically “come back” from authoritarian regression without restoring an older hierarchy or relying on exceptional rulers?
- Will the no-retreat architecture introduced in V13 remain temporary emergency coercion, harden into a new institution, or be dismantled?
- Can Shin learn to treat medical removal and bodily limitation as forms of recoverable service rather than threats to usefulness?
- Does Raiden develop a postwar return destination beyond being the person who brings others back?
- Can Theo continue choosing forms of service without his return to comrades being interpreted as restoration of the old Processor identity?
- Will Lena remain able to defer physical return when responsibility requires distance, or will Queen/hostage symbolism narrow that choice?
- Can Lyudmila/Sirin emergent desire make “return safely” more than borrowed human language, and what would that imply for Sirin expendability doctrine?
- Does No Face possess any path of end-revision, or is his sophisticated agency permanently captured by Legion purpose?
- Will “return” remain a useful final-arc vocabulary once the series must decide what postwar political and personal life actually looks like?

# 18. Retrieval conclusion

T01 validates **return** as one of the strongest longitudinal structures in the current corpus, but only after refusing to treat it as one thing. The series begins with a brother who must live and return, while the state constructs a battlefield designed to make return impossible. Rescue then reveals a second problem: people who have never been permitted retreat or ordinary future do not automatically know how to inhabit either. Giad gradually supplies extraction, rest, medical care, choice, and the expectation of repeated return. Relationships supply people who wait. Ordinary life supplies reasons to come back. Home becomes plural. Disability demonstrates that one can return to belonging without returning to the old body or function. Nonrevenge demonstrates that one can leave a grievance without forgiving it. V13 demonstrates the political violence of closing the route back. V14 finally says that even people who become frightened and wrong can sometimes change and come back.

The corresponding negative principle is equally important: **nonreturn becomes dehumanizing when it is designed, expected, or politically useful**—when a soldier is a disposable buffer, a child has no route out of function, a body is useful only until damaged, a grievance has no exit, or an institution makes attachment into hostage leverage. But return itself is not salvation. One can return to war because no other life feels possible, return to an old role because it remains a prison, or return to empire because political regression calls itself restoration.

The specialist synthesis should therefore use a stricter formulation: **86 values recoverability.** A recoverable person or institution can be wounded without the wound becoming essence; can leave without ceasing to belong; can refuse without forfeiting standing; can retreat without becoming a coward; can return without being required to become the old self; and can sometimes be called back from cruelty without erasing accountability. Whether the unfinished final arc can preserve that proposition under the pressure of political collapse remains open.
