# T09 — Support, Trust, Protection, Decision, and Interruptibility Retrieval

This dossier retrieves the relationship-and-agency evidence behind one of the strongest claims to emerge from the V01–V14 V2 reread: that mature interdependence in *86* is neither radical self-sufficiency nor benevolent control, but a structure in which another person can help, disagree, interrupt, temporarily carry burden, or impose bounded emergency restraint **without permanently replacing the subject's own judgment**.

The governing adversarial question is:

> **When does dependence on another person enlarge agency, and when does care become guardianship, obedience, possession, or outsourced judgment?**

The current answer is deliberately procedural rather than sentimental:

> **Support is healthiest when it preserves or restores the other person's capacity to answer. Trust is not the same as obedience. Protection is not automatically legitimate because it is loving. Intervention is not domination merely because it overrides a momentary choice. The critical questions are who may decide, whether refusal remains meaningful, whether the intervention is proportionate and revisable, whether the relationship tolerates disagreement, and whether judgment is eventually returned rather than permanently seized.**

V13 gives this model its cleanest direct dramatic form: Raiden physically interrupts Shin's escalating exclusionary judgment, and Lena then asks Shin for his **own** tactical assessment rather than supplying one for him. V12 supplies the corresponding lexical formulation: `従うのではなく支えてくれた。求めるのではなく信じてくれた`. V14 extends the same logic to Raiden, Theo, Lena, and bodily care. But the pattern begins much earlier, and the corpus also contains powerful counterexamples in which care becomes a cage, trust becomes obedience, or love becomes leverage.

Alter.2 remains excluded from mainline fact because it is counterfactual/AU material. The sweep searched not only explicit relationship vocabulary but command and decision vocabulary, because *86* often stages relational ethics through **who gets to decide under pressure** rather than through emotional declarations.

# 1. Executive taxonomy — thirteen relational structures that Phase 6 must keep separate

| Structure | T09 definition | Critical negative control |
|---|---|---|
| **Support** | assistance that helps another continue or recover action without claiming ownership of the person's ends | can become covert guardianship if help determines the allowed future |
| **Trust** | accepting uncertainty about another person's judgment, capacity, or return without demanding total control | not identical with confidence that the person will do what one wants |
| **Obedience** | acting because another authority commands or expects it | can be legitimate in bounded roles; pathological when treated as permanent substitute for judgment |
| **Protection** | action intended to reduce danger to another | loving motive does not itself authorize overriding the protected person's agency |
| **Guardianship** | longer-term authority to decide because the protected subject is judged unable/unready to decide | legitimate only if capability-building, bounded, reviewable, and oriented toward eventual agency |
| **Paternalism** | overriding another's present choice for what one judges to be that person's good | ranges from proportionate emergency intervention to identity-enclosing control |
| **Delegation** | transferring a task, role, or authority while preserving accountability and possible return | differs from abandonment and from treating another as a disposable substitute |
| **Refusal** | meaningful ability to reject an order, role, interpretation, or offered form of care | refusal can be constrained by military necessity without becoming entirely fictive |
| **Correction** | another person contradicts a judgment or exposes self-deception | healthy correction leaves the corrected person able to think and answer |
| **Interruption** | bounded intervention that stops a self-justifying trajectory before irreversible action | becomes domination if interruption turns into permanent seizure of agency |
| **Co-bearing** | burden is shared rather than cured or transferred away | does not mean equal burden or perfect mutual understanding |
| **Saviorism** | the helper's identity or moral need begins to determine what counts as the other person's rescue | can erase the actual wishes of the person being “saved” |
| **Interruptible interdependence** | relation where each participant can matter enough to constrain the other's self-story while neither becomes permanent sovereign of the other | strongest current positive model; still vulnerable to possession, hostage leverage, and asymmetry |

The strongest procedural rule is:

> **A relationship becomes more person-respecting when help, command, expertise, or love can alter what someone does without making the helper the permanent author of who that person is allowed to become.**

# 2. The central thesis: support should return judgment, not abolish it

The V2 reread repeatedly produces situations where one person knows more, sees more clearly, possesses formal authority, or has stronger emotional leverage than another. *86* does not solve those asymmetries by pretending everyone is equally informed or equally capable at every moment. It instead asks what an asymmetry is **for**.

There are at least four possibilities.

1. **Exploitation:** the stronger party uses asymmetry to extract function.
2. **Guardianship:** the stronger party decides for the weaker party, sometimes benevolently.
3. **Scaffolding:** the stronger party supplies information, structure, protection, or time so the weaker party can increasingly judge for themselves.
4. **Interruptible reciprocity:** both parties are capable, but each recognizes that there will be moments when the other's correction or temporary burden-bearing is necessary.

San Magnolia often exemplifies the first. Ernst at his worst risks the second. V12 education and the better side of Giad often exemplify the third. Mature Spearhead and Shin/Lena increasingly approach the fourth.

This is why the V12 lexical distinction matters so much:

> `従うのではなく支えてくれた。求めるのではなく信じてくれた`

The contrast is not anti-leadership. Lena remains a commander. The Eighty-Six continue to accept orders. The point is that their relation does not require followers to **suspend judgment** so that the leader may become an unquestionable source of meaning.

Conversely, followers can also dominate leaders. Noelle's movement shows how the governed may demand certainty, protection, purity, or answers so insistently that the leader becomes trapped inside the role of savior. T09 therefore treats domination as potentially **bidirectional**. Dependency can control upward as well as downward.

# 3. Volumes 1–3 — from asymmetrical care to reciprocal objection

## V01 — recognition is not yet reciprocal agency

V01 begins with severe asymmetry. Lena recognizes the Eighty-Six as persons before she knows them relationally. That recognition matters, but it does not automatically give her authority to interpret their experience.

Theo's criticism that she had not even asked their names is therefore more than a social rebuke. It exposes a recurring problem in the series: **good intention without sufficient relational knowledge can become another way of speaking over the person one wants to help**.

The volume simultaneously gives early positive seeds. Rei's `必ず生きて、帰らないと` establishes survival as something another person may ask of you. Kurena's question about who carries Shin—`シンの心は、一体誰が預かって`—already identifies the asymmetry of a caretaker who has no recognized caretaker of his own. The issue is not that Shin must surrender judgment to someone else. It is that a relation in which one person is always the carrier and never the carried is structurally unstable.

San Magnolia supplies the institutional negative control. Processors are ostensibly sent out to “protect” the Republic, but the narration exposes Special Recon as `守らせるためではなく、死なせるために`. Protection language has been severed from reciprocal standing: those who do the protecting are denied the right to return.

**V01 result:** moral recognition is necessary but insufficient; protection can be structurally exploitative; durable care requires the cared-for person's voice rather than only the carer's intention.

## V02 — Ernst shows the difference between care and cage

V02 provides the first sustained paternalism test.

Ernst genuinely cares for the Five. He gives them citizenship, safety, household membership, education, food, legal recognition, and a future that San Magnolia denied them. None of that is fake.

But his desire to restore them to `ただの子供` and prevent reenlistment becomes what Raiden calls an `哀れみの檻`—a cage of pity. The metaphor is exact because the problem is not hatred. It is benevolent protection that defines a narrow acceptable future in advance.

The relationship does not collapse because Ernst is capable of revising his own paternalism. His officer-school condition matters precisely because it changes the structure from:

> **You may not choose this dangerous future**

into:

> **If you choose it, I will require preparation that increases the futures available afterward.**

`選べる道はなるべく多い方がいい` is therefore a major T09 line. It describes **capability-building support** rather than mere permission.

The same volume also uses Frederica to test interpretive authority. Shin recognizes that her guilt for the Empire exceeds what an infant could actually have caused. He can challenge her self-story without claiming ownership of her future. Their relation becomes useful because each can identify distortions in the other without reducing the other to a diagnostic object.

**V02 result:** protection becomes paternalistic when it uses care to settle the protected person's future; scaffolding is different because it increases later choice rather than replacing it.

## V03 — peers acquire standing to object to self-disposal

V03 gives the first fully reciprocal survival ethic.

Raiden's `一人で戦おうとすんじゃねえ` is not abstract emotional support. It is a direct claim that Shin's decision to carry lethal burden alone is **not solely his to make**, because other people are implicated in his survival and death.

Frederica makes the complementary argument in another register: when Shin cannot see a path, he may treat those walking with him as `支え`. This is not a command to outsource decision. It is permission to remain undecided without isolating himself.

The Kiriya climax provides the agency control. Shin recognizes that the final act concerning Kiriya belongs to Frederica: `引く覚悟はフレデリカ自身が決めるべき`. Care does not erase the harmed person's decision-authority merely because Shin possesses more combat power.

The volume also makes abandonment language explicit. `おいていかないで` reveals why dependence is emotionally difficult for Shin: asking someone to remain creates the possibility of being refused or left. Mature dependence therefore requires risk. A person cannot receive another as support while retaining total control over whether that person stays.

**V03 result:** interdependence begins becoming reciprocal when peers may object to self-destruction, support uncertainty, and leave final ownership of another person's morally central decision with that person.

# 4. Volumes 4–6 — delegation, disagreement, and bilateral protection

## V04 — command can be transferred without selfhood being transferred

V04 gives an unusually clean military version of healthy dependence.

When Shin is impaired, he says:

> `……悪い、指揮は任せていいか`

Raiden's response rests on actual delegated authority as vice-captain. This matters because the relationship is not “friend comforts distressed friend” alone. The organization has a structure in which Shin can cease temporarily to perform command and the unit does not become leaderless.

Delegation therefore functions as a personhood safeguard. Shin can be exhausted, injured, overwhelmed, or absent without having to prove worth by remaining the sole decision point.

The same volume deepens Shin/Lena through disagreement rather than perfect understanding. `一緒に戦う──のでしょう？` and the return requests do not establish fusion. They establish a relation in which each person's future decisions matter enough to the other that disagreement and negotiation become unavoidable.

**V04 result:** trust can mean handing another person real authority for a time; mature delegation protects the person precisely because it makes continuous personal indispensability unnecessary.

## V05 — affection does not automatically create authority

V05's artificial-life material gives T09 several useful counterexamples.

Vika's relation to Lerche is affectionate and morally serious, but it cannot be detached from creator authority, royal hierarchy, and the design of Sirins for military use. Lerche's attachment to Vika and Vika's love for her do not by themselves prove symmetry.

The volume repeatedly says, in different forms, that one person may not have the standing to decide another's deepest matter. `それは貴方には関係ない` and `口出しできる筋合いではない` preserve boundaries even inside emotionally charged conversations.

Most importantly, V05 does not equate refusal with abandonment. The fact that one person cannot decide for another does not mean they must become indifferent. The problem is not relational influence itself but **influence that silently converts itself into jurisdiction**.

**V05 result:** love, expertise, creation, or grief can all create reasons to speak; none automatically creates unlimited authority to decide.

## V06 — protection becomes explicitly bilateral

V06 is the largest early transformation of the Shin/Lena relation.

Shin gives frightened Rito an actual nondeployment option: `……本部で、待機しているか`. This is important because Shin's protective authority does not require Rito to prove courage by obeying the expected role.

With Lena, the language becomes reciprocal. Shin asks her not to order him to abandon her:

> `死ぬかもしれないのに、守るななんて言わないでください。……見捨てろなんて、よりにもよって貴方が、おれに命じないでください`

And the relation eventually settles into bilateral return and non-abandonment. The conceptual change is subtle but major:

- early Lena often wants to protect Shin from danger;
- mature Shin/Lena increasingly recognize that **each may object when the other converts protection into unilateral self-sacrifice**.

The same volume also shows Fido physically interrupting a death trajectory. This matters because not every ethically valid intervention must wait for articulate consent. Under immediate lethal conditions, stopping someone can be justified. The harder question is what happens **after** the interruption: whether judgment is returned or permanently seized.

**V06 result:** reciprocal care gives each person standing to resist the other's self-disposal, while still preserving meaningful refusal and role-specific command.

# 5. Volumes 7–9 — disagreement, trust under absence, and co-bearing

## V07 — healthy relation becomes more, not less, argumentative

V07's most important relational development is not the confession by itself. It is the normalization of disagreement.

Shiden's `すぐ線引いて後ずさりしやがって` is a direct challenge to Lena's habit of defining herself outside the Eighty-Six she serves. Shiden supports Lena by refusing Lena's own preferred self-description.

That becomes a key T09 principle:

> **Support that never contradicts can become another form of abandonment.**

Likewise Frederica tells Kurena that she may pursue `そなた自身の望み`. This is not a command to detach from Shin. It is a refusal to let Kurena's affection exhaust her identity.

The Shin/Lena confession also contains relational risk rather than certainty. The future of the relationship depends on asking, hearing, misunderstanding, clarifying, and potentially receiving an answer one cannot control. The relation becomes stronger because the parties increasingly accept that the other is not an extension of the self.

**V07 result:** a mature bond tolerates contradiction and does not require agreement as proof of loyalty.

## V08 — trust becomes possible in the other's absence

V08 provides an important negative test because Shin disappears from direct availability.

The response `あんたが信じてやらなくてどうする` is directed at Lena. Trust here is not certainty. No one actually knows Shin will return. The statement asks Lena to sustain a relation without converting uncertainty into collapse or total control.

At the same time, the volume exposes unhealthy dependence through `それほど長い間、頼りきっていた`. An organization and relationship network can lean so heavily on one person that his absence reveals how much judgment and stability had been outsourced to him.

Theo's injury then makes the same point bodily. If belonging requires continuing to perform the exact previous role, then support was conditional on function all along.

**V08 result:** trust must survive some uncertainty and absence; dependence becomes dangerous when the entire network loses its own capacity to act without the central person.

## V09 — support becomes explicit co-bearing

V09 contains the clearest pre-V12 language of co-bearing.

Shin says:

> `支えてもらってたのはおれも同じだ`

Lena answers:

> `頼ってください。つらいなら、寄りかかってください。...支えますから。共に、抱えますから`

The point is not that another person can cure grief, eliminate traumatic history, or make decision unnecessary. The language is about **distributing load without replacing the bearer**.

Theo's post-injury material supplies the agency control. `プロセッサー、やめたくない` matters because helpers cannot assume that disability should automatically redirect him into a future they regard as healthier. Yet the epilogue also says `新しい自分の形は決められる`. The person may retain a valued role, revise it, or choose another. Support is successful when those remain live possibilities.

The Holy Theocracy provides the institutional negative control through `選択の自由はない`. A role may be honored, loved, and socially supported while still being coercive if refusal is structurally unavailable.

**V09 result:** co-bearing differs from cure, and support must remain compatible with the supported person's continuing authority over role and identity.

# 6. Volumes 10–12 — isolation logic, guardianship, and supported self-government

## V10 — self-sufficiency is revealed as an adaptation, not an ideal

V10 gives the genealogy of Shin's refusal to rely on others.

`誰をも頼らずいる方がいい` is understandable under the logic of repeated loss: if everyone may die, dependence multiplies future pain. But the text presents this as a historically produced survival adaptation, not a final ethic.

The countervoice asks whether Shin actually wants to protect particular people. `その人を守ってやりたい` is significant because care requires recognizing another person as a source of claims one did not invent alone.

V10 therefore clarifies that radical independence can itself be an enclosure. A person may preserve decision-control by refusing dependence, but at the cost of relationships capable of correcting, helping, or carrying them.

**V10 result:** self-sufficiency can protect against abandonment while also preventing the relational structures required for a plural future.

## V11 — delegation spreads beyond command into memory and grief

V11 extends `任せる` into memorial authority. Shin gives Aldrecht's ending to Rito rather than treating Undertaker as the sole owner of every dead person's memory.

This is important because delegation is no longer merely tactical. It becomes an ethical admission that **another person's relationship may give them better standing to act**.

The same volume complicates rescue. Shin can say he does not especially want to save Republic civilians but does not wish them dead. Human standing does not depend upon emotional closeness.

And Lena's grief demonstrates another support limit. Shin can hold her, allow her to cry, and reject false guilt without claiming that he can solve the loss. Good support sometimes consists precisely in **not converting pain into a problem the helper must fix**.

**V11 result:** mature support includes knowing when to delegate, when to accompany grief, and when emotional distance does not erase duties toward others.

## V12 — the series states the distinction directly

V12 is T09's conceptual center.

The chapter's political problem is that some people have lived so long under inherited hierarchy that formal freedom arrives as abandonment. They know how to obey and do not know how to judge. `今なお従うことしか考えられない` is not evidence that they are naturally incapable of self-government; it is evidence that capabilities can atrophy when a system never requires their exercise.

The authoritarian answer is to restore permanent guardianship.

The series instead develops **supported self-government**.

Frederica's `己一人であっても己の王たらん` does not mean radical self-sufficiency. It means nobody else should permanently own the person's judgment.

The decisive relational line then states the positive model:

> `従うのではなく支えてくれた。求めるのではなく信じてくれた`

Support and trust keep leadership accountable without treating the leader as a savior.

Noelle and Hail Mary supply the negative control. Followers may outsource thought to a leader and then pressure that leader to continue supplying certainty. This reveals **upward coercion**: dependence can imprison the person depended upon.

V12 also gives temporary removal from command, mental-health rest, and education as forms of legitimate intervention. These are not automatically anti-autonomy because they can be bounded, reviewable, capability-preserving, and oriented toward later return to judgment.

The V12 rule should therefore be frozen as:

> **Scaffolding is legitimate when it enlarges eventual agency. Guardianship becomes domination when continued authority requires the governed never to become capable of answer, refusal, or exit.**

# 7. Alter.1 — low-pressure relations reveal the everyday infrastructure of trust

Alter.1 is especially useful because crisis scenes can make every relationship look more fused than ordinary life actually is.

The Claymore lesson—`誰にも頼らず戦えないような子は生き残れない`—is a clean negative control. It describes survival under a system where help is unreliable. The lesson may be tactically sound and still be socially tragic. **Forced self-reliance is not autonomy.**

Later low-pressure scenes show the opposite structure:

- Lena wishes she could share sight with Shin rather than simply direct him;
- the relationship grows through repeated calls, drinks, gifts, small jokes, and waiting;
- Annette can protect private information without demanding restored childhood intimacy;
- adults explicitly hope next time the Eighty-Six's exceptional hands will not be necessary;
- Shin can warn Lena not to overtrain without controlling her body;
- post-reunion trust becomes visible in the fact that Lena no longer simply disappears from Shin's world;
- Theo can be asked for ordinary practical help after amputation.

These scenes matter because they show that interdependence is sustainable only if it survives **outside emergency rescue**.

# 8. V13 — interruptibility becomes the mature relational criterion

V13 is where the concept becomes explicit enough to name.

## 8.1 Love as protection can still become exclusion

Primvere states the ugliest version of protective partiality: her own family must be kept safe, therefore someone else's children can be sacrificed.

This is not counterfeit love. That is precisely why the scene matters.

> **Protection of the beloved becomes morally dangerous when the beloved's safety is treated as sufficient authorization to dispose of outsiders.**

Ernst initially recognizes the point intellectually and then fails it behaviorally. He nearly kills Primvere in rage.

Frederica's intervention—`もうやめて。──おとうさん`—becomes a central T09 scene because family attachment now works in the opposite direction. The loved child does **not** ask Ernst to harm outsiders for her sake. She uses the bond to stop him from becoming the person he is becoming.

The ethical value of love is therefore not purity. It is partly **interruptibility**.

## 8.2 Raiden interrupts Shin; Lena returns judgment to him

Shin begins sliding into an exclusionary judgment under exhaustion and betrayal. Raiden physically stops him:

> `何してんだはお前だ、いまさらここにきて神様気取りか！`

The importance of the scene lies in what happens next.

Lena does **not** tell Shin what he is required to believe about weak or frightened people. Instead:

> `戦隊長としての判断を求めたレーナの、言外の信頼を正しく彼は受け取ってくれた。`

She asks him to resume his own professional judgment.

This yields T09's strongest positive definition:

> **Interruption is person-respecting when it stops a distorted trajectory long enough for the subject's own judgment to become usable again.**

It differs both from nonintervention and from permanent takeover.

Shin's `……ライデン、悪い。助かった` makes the relation explicitly reciprocal: being stopped can itself count as being helped.

## 8.3 Adulthood multiplies obligations without erasing desire

Olivier tells Shin that adulthood means having more people one can harm through one's choices. His inability to rush directly toward Lena is not evidence that love is unimportant. It means he has acquired command obligations as well as romantic ones.

This is a useful correction to simplistic autonomy language. Freedom is not maximal absence of constraint. Relationships and offices create legitimate claims.

The ethical question becomes whether those claims can be negotiated without any one of them becoming total.

## 8.4 Dustin demonstrates saviorism

Dustin's desire to save Chitri is compassionate, but his later question—`俺はいったい、チトリの何を救うつもりだったんだ？`—exposes how easily care can become helper-centered.

Chitri's own desires are mundane and particular. She is not merely a symbol through which Dustin can repair the Republic's sin.

So:

> **A rescue can be morally serious and still misunderstand what the person actually wants rescued.**

## 8.5 Hostage leverage is the dark mirror of relational return

V13 also shows that a relationship capable of giving someone a reason to return can be exploited by third parties. Lena becomes leverage precisely because Shin and the Eighty-Six care about her.

This is crucial counterevidence against romanticizing dependence.

The same bond can:

- sustain life;
- give a return-address;
- authorize interruption;
- and create hostage vulnerability.

Healthy interdependence therefore cannot mean eliminating vulnerability. It means refusing to let the existence of vulnerability justify ownership.

# 9. V14 — the stabilizer must also be stabilizable

V14 extends the V13 model in several directions.

## 9.1 Raiden discovers that borrowed restraint was still dependence

Raiden realizes that he could voice hatred so freely because Shin was there to stop the group from going too far. Once Raiden becomes acting leader, that external brake is gone.

This is not a revelation that Raiden was secretly weak. It is a revelation that **everyone's judgment had relational infrastructure**.

The person who stabilizes others also needs people capable of stabilizing him.

That is why Theo's intervention matters:

> `とりあえず、シンの代わりを務めようとかは考えなくていいよ、ライデン`

and:

> `別にシンだってライデンの代わりは務まらない`

Theo refuses both self-erasure and substitution. Raiden is not required to become Shin in order to carry temporary leadership.

The new Raiden thesis is therefore:

> **His distinctive burden is the ethical cost of being the person who stabilizes the stabilizer—and V14 finally gives him permission not to perform that function by erasing himself.**

## 9.2 Trust becomes explicitly reciprocal

The V14 line:

> `俺はあんたを信頼してるぜ、レーナ。だからあんたも信じてくれよ`

is almost a compact statement of nonpossessive trust.

The speaker does not say:

> trust me because I will obey you,

or:

> trust me because I cannot fail.

He asks for reciprocal confidence under uncertainty.

This is compatible with disagreement and anger. Trust does not require emotional smoothness.

## 9.3 Theo's response to Shin's sensory loss demonstrates boundary-respecting care

Shin tells Theo to stop testing him:

> `試すようなことはやめてくれ`

Theo accepts the limit and proposes accommodation instead: eat together, describe the taste first.

This is one of the cleanest everyday T09 examples because the alternative would be easy to rationalize as care. Theo could keep checking because Shin has hidden medically important information before. Instead he changes method when the cared-for person identifies the checking itself as harmful.

The lesson is:

> **Concern does not create unlimited investigative authority over another person's body.**

Support can respond to refusal without becoming withdrawal.

## 9.4 Lena's neural rescue is justified intervention with interpretive risk

Lena's V14 rescue of Shin is an acute emergency intervention. She uses extreme Para-RAID activation to break No Face's intrusion and return Shin to his own consciousness.

Behaviorally, the scene fits the positive interruption model: she removes coercive control and returns Shin to himself.

But the relationship immediately retains interpretive risk. In the later Rei conversation, Lena stops Shin before he completes a possible `hate` formulation and tells him what she thinks his real feeling is.

Her reading is narratively privileged and helps Shin finally say `痛かった`. But T09 must preserve the asymmetry:

> **A person can successfully interrupt self-punishment and still overstate their authority to name another person's interior state.**

Mature relation does not require perfect diagnostic access.

## 9.5 Care for Lena also becomes distributed

After Lena's maximum-activation rescue, others worry about her physical state and take over tasks she cannot presently perform. This is the same organizational ethic T05 identified militarily: temporary incapacity should trigger redistribution rather than shame or collapse.

The relational version is:

> **Being needed does not mean one must always remain the one doing the needed work.**

# 10. Comparative matrix — support versus control

| Case | What the helper/protector does | Agency outcome | T09 judgment |

# 11. Comparative matrix — when is paternal intervention legitimate?

T09 does not adopt a blanket rule that overriding another person is always wrong. The corpus itself contains situations where immediate intervention is plainly necessary. The more defensible test is multi-factor.

| Criterion | More legitimate intervention | More dangerous intervention |
|---|---|---|
| **Threat level** | immediate, severe, difficult to reverse | vague or merely disfavored future choice |
| **Capacity state** | temporary impairment, coercion, acute crisis | stable disagreement treated as incapacity |
| **Scope** | narrow action needed to stop imminent harm | broad control over identity, vocation, relationships |
| **Duration** | temporary | indefinite/permanent |
| **Reviewability** | reasons can be challenged later | authority treats itself as unquestionable |
| **Agency return** | judgment is returned once crisis passes | intervention becomes standing guardianship |
| **Refusal** | refusal is heard where practicable | refusal itself is proof that more control is needed |
| **Capability building** | intervention creates future ability to decide | intervention requires permanent dependence |
| **Relational interest** | helper can distinguish other's good from own fear/guilt | “protection” primarily protects helper from anxiety or remorse |

This matrix explains why several apparently similar actions receive different judgments.

Raiden stopping Shin in V13 is temporary, crisis-specific, and followed by restored judgment. Ernst trying to decide the Five's entire postwar identity in V2 is broad and identity-defining. Theo stopping a dangerous course may be justified; Theo permanently monitoring Shin against his explicit bodily boundary would be different. Removing Lena temporarily from command for recovery can preserve later agency; treating her as permanently incapable because she once needed rest would not.

# 12. Trust is not prediction, obedience, or certainty

The corpus supports at least four different meanings often flattened into “trust.”

## 12.1 Trust as confidence in competence

Military relationships repeatedly depend on knowing another person can perform a role. Shin can delegate command because Raiden actually possesses the competence and office to carry it.

## 12.2 Trust as acceptance of uncertainty

V8's demand that Lena believe Shin will return is not evidence-based certainty. It is a choice not to let uncertainty annihilate the relationship.

## 12.3 Trust as confidence in judgment rather than compliance

V13 is the clearest example. Lena asks for Shin's judgment precisely because she trusts him to think, not merely to obey.

## 12.4 Trust as reciprocal non-possession

V14's `だからあんたも信じてくれよ` asks Lena not to convert concern into control.

The stable principle is:

> **Trust becomes person-respecting when it leaves room for the trusted person to surprise, disagree, fail, return, or make a decision the truster would not have chosen.**

If “I trust you” really means “I trust you to do what I want,” then trust has collapsed into disguised obedience.

# 13. Protection is ethically double-edged

T09 strongly rejects any generic claim that *86* treats protection as unambiguously virtuous.

Protection can mean:

- a soldier physically shielding another;
- an adult preserving a child's future;
- an officer arranging extraction;
- a household offering refuge;
- a friend interrupting self-destruction;
- a government justifying paternal restriction;
- a citizen protecting family by sacrificing an out-group;
- a military retaining a uniquely useful person “for their own safety” while continuing to own their function.

The relevant question is therefore not:

> **Was the motive protective?**

but:

> **What claims did protection authorize against the protected person and against outsiders?**

V13's Primvere/Ernst sequence is the decisive negative control. Love can motivate genuine care and still become a justification for disposing of whoever is outside the circle.

V14's possible rear-retention of Shin supplies a different danger: protection of one person's life may coexist with appropriation of that person's strategic capacity.

# 14. Command does not disappear from the mature model

Because T09 emphasizes agency, it would be easy to overcorrect into anti-hierarchy.

The novels do not do that.

Military command remains real. Officers give orders. Subordinates obey under battlefield conditions. Grethe explicitly assumes responsibility for ordered fire. Lena retains command authority. Shin exercises battlefield judgment over others.

The ethical distinction is not command versus no command. It is whether command is:

- bounded by role;
- accountable;
- informed;
- capable of delegation;
- open to correction;
- compatible with reporting incapacity;
- capable of distinguishing emergency obedience from lifelong self-government.

A soldier may have to obey a tactical order without therefore surrendering the entire right to determine vocation, identity, belief, or postwar future.

This distinction will be essential in specialist Documents 06 and 07.

# 15. The strongest current model: interruptible interdependence

Across T01–T09, a broader pattern is now stable enough to state carefully.

The series does not idealize isolation.

It also does not idealize fusion.

The mature positive relation increasingly has five properties:

1. **Particularity** — the other person matters as a specific person, not merely a role.
2. **Reciprocity** — both parties may become giver and receiver of support.
3. **Refusal space** — disagreement does not automatically terminate belonging.
4. **Interruptibility** — another person has enough standing to stop self-justification or self-destruction.
5. **Returned judgment** — intervention ultimately aims to restore the person's own capacity to decide rather than permanently replacing it.

This gives a more exact formulation than “healthy dependence.”

> **Mature interdependence in *86* is a relationship in which another person's claims can genuinely constrain you without becoming sovereign over you.**

That formulation also explains why such relations remain dangerous. If the other matters enough to constrain action, then the other can become:

- hostage leverage;
- object of possessiveness;
- justification for exclusion;
- source of excessive self-sacrifice;
- or authority granted too much interpretive power.

The series does not solve those risks by making relationships less important. It tests whether the relationship itself remains answerable.

# 19. Japanese terminology notes

T09 should preserve several lexical distinctions that English "care," "support," and "trust" easily flatten.

## `支える`

`支える` is the strongest positive support verb in the mature corpus because it can mean holding up, sustaining, or assisting without necessarily replacing the supported subject's own action. V12's direct contrast with `従う` gives it unusually high analytical weight. It should not automatically be translated into therapeutic "support" where the context is military, political, or physical.

## `従う`

`従う` can mean ordinary following/obedience and is not intrinsically pathological. Its danger appears where following becomes the only practiced form of judgment, as in V12's people who can still think only in terms of obeying a master. T09 must therefore distinguish **role-bounded obedience** from **existentially outsourced judgment**.

## `信じる` / `信頼`

`信じる` often carries belief or faith under uncertainty; `信頼` more readily denotes trust/reliance placed in a person. The distinction is not absolute, but V13-V14 make both important. Trust is especially significant when it asks another person to exercise judgment rather than merely comply.

## `守る`

`守る` spans protect, defend, preserve, keep, and uphold. It may refer to people, promises, pride, institutions, borders, or moral principles. T09 should never infer paternalism from `守る` alone. The critical question is what protection authorizes and whose agency/cost it affects.

## `助ける` / `救う`

Both can map to "save/help," but scene function matters. `助ける` often describes concrete aid or rescue; `救う` can carry broader salvation/rescue force. Dustin/Chitri demonstrates why even sincere rescue language can become helper-centered if the actual subject's wishes disappear.

## `頼る` / `頼む`

`頼る` concerns reliance/dependence; `頼む` often means asking/requesting. Shin's development makes the first especially important because refusal to rely on others is historically produced rather than an ideal of autonomy. A request, meanwhile, differs from an order because it preserves answer-space to degrees that military context may constrain but not erase.

## `任せる`

`任せる` can mean entrust, leave to, delegate. V4 command transfer and V11 memorial delegation show why it is central. Entrusting another person is not abandonment when responsibility, competence, and continuing relation remain intact.

## `命令` / `指揮`

Military command vocabulary must remain role-specific. An order can legitimately constrain immediate action without settling a subordinate's whole personhood. Phase 6 should avoid turning all command into paternalism or all obedience into loss of self-government.

## `決める` / `決断` / `判断`

These words index different shades of deciding/judging, but T09 uses them together to ask who actually authors a course of action. V13's `戦隊長としての判断を求めた` is especially important because Lena restores Shin's professional judgment by asking for it.

## `止める` / `やめる`

Stopping another person or asking them to stop can be coercive, protective, corrective, or all three. T09 therefore treats these as **interruption evidence**, not automatically as positive care. Legitimacy depends on scope, urgency, duration, and whether agency is returned.

## `支配`

`支配` is stronger than ordinary influence. It belongs to control/domination and is especially valuable as a negative comparator for healthy support. V12's master/subject logic makes this distinction explicit.

## `求める`

`求める` can mean seek, demand, request, or expect depending on context. V12's `求めるのではなく信じてくれた` is important because followers are praised for not making Lena satisfy an unlimited savior-demand.

# 23. Final T09 judgment

The V01–V14 corpus does not support either of the two easiest relational philosophies.

It does not support:

> **good people become self-sufficient and no longer need others.**

Nor does it support:

> **love and trust are good because people surrender themselves safely to the right person.**

The stronger model is relationally demanding:

> **A mature bond gives another person enough standing to matter to your decisions without giving them permanent sovereignty over those decisions.**

That means another person may:

- ask you to return;
- disagree with your self-description;
- tell you to rest;
- take temporary command;
- stop you from killing someone;
- refuse the role you want them to accept;
- point out that your sacrifice is also their loss;
- carry part of a burden you cannot currently carry;
- insist that you think again;
- or protect you during a moment when your own judgment is unusable.

But the endpoint of the intervention is not obedience for its own sake.

It is the restoration or enlargement of answer-space.

The negative cases across the series all narrow that space in different ways:

- San Magnolia turns protection into exclusion and disposal;
- pity becomes a cage;
- sacred status becomes inherited function;
- followers turn leaders into answer-machines;
- love becomes a reason to sacrifice outsiders;
- rescue becomes a story about the rescuer;
- attachment becomes hostage leverage;
- medical concern can threaten surveillance;
- strategic protection can become functional ownership.

The positive cases remain positive not because they are free of dependence, asymmetry, hierarchy, or risk, but because they stay **interruptible themselves**.

The strongest current formulation is therefore:

> **Interruptible interdependence is the series' best-developed relational analogue to political self-government: a person remains embedded in ties strong enough to constrain, correct, protect, and sustain them, while those ties remain answerable to the person's continuing capacity for judgment, refusal, revision, and return.**

That formulation should be carried into Phase 5 as a high-confidence synthesis candidate, not yet as a closed final-series thesis.
