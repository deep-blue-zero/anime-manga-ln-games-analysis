# T10 — Institutions, Freedom, Paternalism, Capability, and Political Regression Retrieval

This dossier retrieves the institutional and political evidence behind a central V2 problem: **what does it take for a formally recognized person to possess freedom that is actually usable, and when does an institution turn the genuine difficulty of self-government into a justification for permanent rule over supposedly incapable people?**

The governing adversarial question is:

> **When does institutional support expand citizens' capacity for self-government, and when does a state use vulnerability, fear, incapacity, emergency, ignorance, or genuine human fallibility as justification for permanent guardianship?**

The current answer is deliberately two-sided:

> **Formal rights are necessary but not self-executing. People need knowledge, time, material security, transportation, health care, institutional memory, practice making decisions, opportunities to make recoverable mistakes, and relationships or offices that can help without permanently deciding for them. But the fact that freedom is difficult does not authorize a ruling class to make incapacity permanent. Capability support is freedom-enhancing only when it preserves eventual answer, refusal, revision, and exit.**

The V12-V14 sequence is the sharpest stress test. V12 directly confronts people who possess formal freedom but lack habits or confidence for using it. V13 shows a democratic polity under catastrophic pressure reproducing scapegoating, exclusion, coercion, and ultimately a battlefield relation textually compared with the Eighty-Sixth Sector. V14 then supplies two competing answers to that failure. One answer practices democracy as ongoing civic labor: look at unpleasant facts, study for the postwar future, preserve public records, communicate across social division, rebuild accountable judgment. The other declares that ordinary people never should have possessed `言葉も、思索も、自由も、意志も` and demands a return to hereditary empire.

The second answer is T10's decisive negative control. **Political regression does not prove that freedom was a mistake. It reveals how easily the burdens and failures of freedom can be redescribed as evidence for permanent guardianship.**

# 1. Executive taxonomy — institutional dimensions Phase 6 must keep separate

| Dimension | T10 question | Collapse error |
|---|---|---|
| **Formal status** | Is the person legally a citizen, subject, nonperson, soldier, refugee, royal, caste member? | treating emotional belonging as a substitute for rights |
| **Formal liberty** | Is the person legally free from ownership, hereditary assignment, or explicit coercion? | assuming non-domination alone creates usable freedom |
| **Usable capability** | Can the person realistically exercise options through knowledge, health, time, transport, money, education, safety, and information? | blaming individuals for options that exist only on paper |
| **Civic judgment** | Can citizens interpret information, accept uncertainty, make decisions, and revise them? | treating voting/legal equality as spontaneous competence |
| **Developmental scaffolding** | Does the institution teach, support, and create practice without fixing the final answer? | converting education into ideological guardianship |
| **Welfare / material floor** | Can weak, injured, displaced, or temporarily nonproductive persons continue to belong? | making productivity the hidden condition of citizenship |
| **Role-exit authority** | May a person leave or revise an inherited/assigned social function? | confusing social esteem with autonomy |
| **Information ecology** | Can bad news, disagreement, evidence, and minority testimony circulate? | treating propaganda or silence as merely rhetorical background |
| **Emergency authority** | What may institutions temporarily override under genuine catastrophe? | treating every restriction as tyranny or every emergency as unlimited license |
| **Accountability** | Can wrong acts be investigated, condemned, recorded, and corrected? | assuming institutional virtue because leaders mean well |
| **Return / exit capability** | Can exposed people retreat, return home, change role, or re-enter ordinary society? | treating nominal belonging as real while routes back are blocked |
| **Plural institutional memory** | Are records, names, histories, and mistakes preserved for later correction? | allowing institutions to erase the persons they used or failed |
| **Paternalism** | Does protection temporarily compensate for limited capacity or permanently substitute for agency? | assuming benevolence settles legitimacy |
| **Political recoverability** | Can a polity acknowledge failure and revise without defining itself by infallibility? | turning crisis into irreversible regime identity |
| **Justice grammar** | Is justice an external standard the polity must answer to, or a title it owns? | converting “we should be just” into “whatever we do is justice” |

The mature institutional question is therefore not “Is this state good?” but:

> **What happens to a person when they are weak, wrong, frightened, injured, unpopular, displaced, nonproductive, ignorant, or unwilling to perform the role the institution wants from them?**

That is where a political system's theory of persons becomes material.

# 2. Central thesis — formal freedom, usable freedom, and self-government are different layers

The current V2 corpus supports three related but nonidentical layers.

## 2.1 Formal non-domination

At the minimum, a person must not be legally classified as property, livestock, a weapon component, a hereditary function, or a body whose destruction is administratively desired. San Magnolia's stripping of Colorata citizenship and human standing establishes how foundational this layer is. V01's legal and administrative violence is not made less serious by later recognition that rights need material support.

## 2.2 Usable capability

A right that cannot realistically be exercised remains incomplete. Someone may formally possess freedom of movement while lacking transport. They may be free to choose a vocation while possessing no education or time to learn it. They may be citizens while lacking food, medical care, records, stable housing, reliable information, or a route home. Alter.1 and V09-V12 make this material layer increasingly explicit.

## 2.3 Practiced self-government

Even when law and material support exist, someone still has to interpret, choose, make mistakes, accept responsibility, and revise. V12's post-imperial subjects and Hail Mary material make this difficult layer impossible to ignore. Freedom is not purely something an institution gives; it is also something persons have to practice.

The danger is that these layers can be weaponized against one another.

- San Magnolia denies **formal personhood** while exploiting real human capacities.
- A purely formal liberalism can grant rights while underproviding the capability needed to use them.
- A paternalistic regime can correctly diagnose limited capability and use that diagnosis to justify indefinite domination.

T10 therefore locks the following rule:

> **Support is legitimate when it increases the governed person's future ability to answer, refuse, revise, and exit. It becomes guardianship when the institution's continued authority depends upon the subject remaining permanently unable to do those things.**

# 3. V01-V03 — legal exclusion, restored status, and the burden of open freedom

## V01 — San Magnolia demonstrates why formal status is foundational

The V01 epigraph gives the ideological compression immediately: `豚に人権を与えぬことを`. The Republic does not merely mistreat legally recognized citizens. It first constructs a category to which rights need not apply.

The primary-source sweep adds legal detail that the short canonical locator table did not fully preserve. Processor service is advertised as a route by which an Eighty-Six and their family can recover citizenship. The later text makes explicit that the Republic never intends to honor the promise. A person's political status is therefore used as a coercive incentive while the state simultaneously designs conditions ensuring that the promised restoration never becomes usable.

This matters for later V12-V14 analysis because it prevents “rights are insufficient” from becoming “rights are superficial.” In V01, citizenship determines whether a person can be:

- recognized by the law;
- recorded as a casualty;
- mourned publicly;
- treated as a member of the polity;
- expected to return;
- protected from state experimentation and disposal;
- and imagined as someone for whom the military exists rather than someone the military consumes.

The line `死んだ仲間を悼む自由も` makes even mourning a political allocation. Freedom is already materialized as who is allowed public relation to the dead.

V01 also places responsibility inside citizenship. Lena states that silently tolerating wrong is participation in it. Later volumes will narrow omnipotent guilt, but the earliest political premise remains: **being a citizen inside an unjust system can create duties that exceed merely avoiding personal cruelty.**

Finally, the Republic's claim to republican legitimacy is judged internally. Shin says the Republic died when its citizens chose to persecute fellow citizens. This is not a technical constitutional holding; it is a moral-political claim that a polity can preserve institutional shell while destroying the principle that made its name meaningful.

**V01 institutional result:** legal standing is a minimum condition of political personhood; majority procedure and constitutional vocabulary do not rescue a polity that uses them to place a population outside reciprocal membership.

## V02 — citizenship can be restored faster than a life can be rebuilt

Giad's grant of citizenship is real. `今日から連邦市民` is not presented as meaningless paperwork. It changes the Five's legal relation to the state immediately.

But V02 then runs the obvious test: does legal recognition automatically produce home, ordinary competence, or trusted institutions? It does not.

The Five encounter racism inside the better polity (`共和国の化物め`), showing that formal equality does not instantly erase social prejudice. Ernst's care is materially generous but can become an `哀れみの檻`: protection that decides in advance which future would count as healthy.

The officer-school compromise is therefore institutionally important. `選べる道はなるべく多い方がいい` does not force a civilian answer. It tries to make future options less path-dependent by adding education and qualification to combat experience.

This yields the first strong capability formulation:

> **A state can respect a choice while still insisting that the chooser acquire more future options.**

That is different from both laissez-faire abandonment and paternalistic prohibition.

V02 also refuses to idealize Giad. Sympathy can become `一方的な同情`, which the narration treats as a form of abandoning understanding. The Federacy's moral superiority to San Magnolia therefore remains conditional upon continued institutional self-criticism rather than national essence.

## V03 — freedom without developed wants can feel like emptiness

V03 gives a psychological control for political theory. Shin has obtained legal and practical freedoms unavailable in the Sector, yet `手に入れた“自由”` can register as `あまりにも大きな空虚` because the capacities for imagining and choosing a future have been damaged by years of terminal time.

This does not show that freedom was a mistake. It shows that release from coercion does not itself create desires, confidence, judgment, or practice choosing.

The Federacy's military institutions also demonstrate that freedom can coexist with binding roles. Shin says he is now on the battlefield by choice and that a road exists he can choose. At the same time, formal command remains real. Frederica's decisive role in Kiriya's end belongs to her: `引く覚悟はフレデリカ自身が決めるべき`. The better structure is therefore not absence of authority but differentiation of **who has standing to decide what**.

V03's Legion control (`人格と意志` yet unable to disobey a higher commander) further prevents the analysis from equating apparent cognition with political autonomy. One can possess mind and still lack reopening of the governing end.

**V01-V03 cumulative correction:** freedom has legal, material, and developmental layers. Removing coercion is indispensable; it is not the same process as learning how to use the resulting space.

# 4. V04-V06 — better institutions are not utopias, and freedom includes the option not to leave

## V04 — the Federacy remains a comparative polity, not a moral endpoint

V04's `連邦も別段、理想郷じゃない` must remain active throughout later synthesis. The Federacy can be better than San Magnolia without becoming morally pure.

The Republic's afterlife persists through racism, political categories, inherited habits, and the temptation to demand Eighty-Six sacrifice for Republic interests. The novel repeatedly forces characters to distinguish:

- Republic nationality from “white pig” ideology;
- military responsibility from Republic-style cruelty;
- solidarity with victims from appropriating their grievance;
- and the Federacy's real institutional improvements from the fantasy that geography removes human failure.

Raiden's invocation of his rights and responsibilities as vice-captain is a small but important institutional control. Office and delegated authority allow command to move without requiring Shin to remain the sole decision point. That is political personhood at organizational scale: the office exists beyond the individual.

V04's racism also clarifies that law does not erase social ontology by itself. A state may prohibit formal caste while citizens continue drawing racial lines. Phase 6 must therefore keep **legal equality** and **social equality** analytically separate.

## V05 — freedom can include remaining where one already is

`檻の扉は開いた` / `何処にも行かないことだって、自由` is one of the corpus's best defenses against a simplistic exit-only model of freedom.

If an institution or relationship becomes noncoercive, freedom does not require fleeing it. The critical change is that staying no longer has to be the only permitted action.

This matters for military vocation, citizenship, relationships, and later political membership. A person can remain a soldier, a citizen, or part of a inherited community and still be freer if refusal and revision have become meaningful.

Roa Gracia complicates the model through Sirins. Donor material is restricted to volunteers, an important consent boundary, yet the resulting artificial subjects exist within a monarchy that deploys them as military resources. The text therefore prevents “consent somewhere in the causal chain” from resolving all later institutional questions.

V05's `未来などない。課された役目しかない` gives the negative institutional form: a being may continue functioning indefinitely while possessing no socially recognized future outside assigned purpose.

The same volume normalizes military retreat (`撤退は、れっきとした戦術行動です`). That is relevant politically because a humane role includes institutionalized permission to leave an exposure without the act automatically becoming betrayal.

## V06 — capability and identity should remain non-exclusive

V06's comparative warrior-caste material broadens the analysis beyond San Magnolia. Historical or inherited military roles can become meaningful identities without being healthy merely because the bearer takes pride in them.

Dustin's warning against total freedom from identity and the volume's sustained pride discussion imply that a person need not become socially unbound. The problem is **exclusive assignment**.

Lena's `信じたいんです。この世界にはまだ、誰もが幸せに生きられる世界に、変われる可能性が` is not a policy program, but it supplies a political anthropology that becomes important in V14: people and institutions must remain capable of change rather than being treated as permanently exhausted by their worst state.

Vika's Sirin practice is another bounded positive control. He has creator, royal, and command authority, yet the text repeatedly notes that he does not reduce every choice to direct compulsion. This is not equality; it is a case where asymmetric authority deliberately preserves some answer-space.

**V04-V06 result:** freedom is increasingly best described not as the absence of every tie but as the ability to remain, leave, revise, or reinterpret a tie without losing one's standing as a person.

# 5. V07-V09 — capability expansion, institutional training, and the difference between honor and autonomy

## V07 — the Federacy's best institutions expand repertoire without prescribing the answer

V07 is especially important because it makes institutional support look ordinary.

The Eighty-Six are recognized as requiring particular care without that recognition automatically excluding them from demanding roles. They are taught formal social practices, given leave, exposed to civilian institutions, and encouraged to acquire capacities they may or may not use later.

The line `できないよりもできた方が、楽しめるものは多い` captures the capability logic precisely. The moral good is not dancing, school, civilian manners, or vacation in themselves. It is having more things one **can** do.

The volume also directly rejects political demands that patriotism require using other people up: `祖国のために他人を使い潰さねば愛国心がない`. Shin's reply `おれは家畜でもなければ無人機の部品でもない` keeps personhood separate from military utility even inside a legitimate military.

The anti-hero doctrine has institutional implications beyond T05. `一人の英雄で成り立つ軍など、在ってはならぬ` means the polity must not secure itself by creating a person whose inability to leave becomes a national vulnerability.

V07 also gives a useful negative institutional detail: some “care” is acknowledged as `アリバイ作り`, bureaucratic self-protection rather than purely altruistic support. The service can still benefit the recipients. Mixed motive therefore does not automatically nullify institutional function.

## V08 — training and sacrifice have to be distinguished from disposability

V08 explicitly states that combat organizations require `適切な訓練と教育`. Capability is not a moral abstraction; institutions must spend time and resources maintaining it.

The Fleet Countries then provide a hard comparative case. Small-state strategic survival can require terrible risk, yet the narrative distinguishes this from San Magnolia's exterminatory assignment by preserving agency, operational purpose, and the effort to return the Strike Package alive (`機動打撃群は生還させる`).

The volume's mature Eighty-Six language is institutional as well as psychological. `八六区をもう、……自分たちは出た` means the role once imposed by a genocidal structure no longer has exclusive claim simply because the same people continue fighting.

This supports a crucial distinction for later Documents 06-07:

> **Dangerous service is not identical with an Eighty-Sixth Sector. The stronger comparison requires an institution that converts danger into constrained refusal and blocked return for a marked population.**

## V09 — social honor can coexist with domination

The Holy Theocracy supplies the strongest non-Republic institutional falsifier of “high status = freedom.”

Its sacred fighters may be revered rather than animalized. Yet `選択の自由はない`: occupation, marriage, and role are substantially inherited. `神戟は人ではないが、神の御使い` elevates the role symbolically while still distinguishing the bearer from ordinary human self-direction.

This produces T10's category **sacralized functionalization**:

> **An institution can deny ordinary agency by declaring a person's role too holy to refuse.**

The later doctrinal reversal is equally important. An allegedly immutable sacred destiny becomes mutable when elite survival requires broader participation. That exposes “destiny” as selectively administered by authority rather than metaphysical necessity.

Milmelkoleo supplies a related class/blood case. Mixed-blood or aristocratically embarrassing children receive worth through exceptional military service and patron prestige. Their social value remains conditional upon heroic function.

V09 therefore freezes:

> **Role-exit authority must be tracked separately from prestige, honor, education, and material support.**

A well-treated caste can still be unfree.

# 6. V10-V11 — institutions distribute time, memory, logistics, and the right to remain human under collapse

## V10 — San Magnolia's violence includes the withdrawal of time

V10's retrospective material makes the Sector's institutional structure unusually concrete.

There are effectively no proper military physicians or field hospitals for Processors. Medical systems are configured around rapid return to combat. An injury that requires time can become administratively “unsalvageable” even if adequate medicine could preserve the person.

This reveals a political dimension of capability:

> **Institutions allocate time.**

They decide who may have time to:

- heal;
- learn;
- mature;
- mourn;
- communicate;
- fail and try again;
- keep records;
- and become something other than the current function.

V10's literacy and historical-education material shows oppressed communities repurposing whatever residual institutional space exists. That resistance should not be mistaken for evidence that the state provided adequate developmental freedom.

The clandestine memorial/name infrastructure likewise demonstrates that archival recognition is political. If official policy erases names and bodies because acknowledged graves would expose the unmanned-war fiction, the ability to record a person becomes itself resistance.

The communications prohibition between squads supplies another institutional point: administrative distance can make compassion operationally unusable. Help that must pass through an absent or hostile Handler may arrive too late. Formal goodwill does not substitute for functioning channels.

## V11 — state collapse reveals the politics inside humanitarian logistics

San Magnolia's evacuation reproduces hierarchy even at extinction. Preferred elites and phenotypes receive priority; the dying polity does not automatically become egalitarian when everyone faces a shared threat.

At the same time, Kurena's realization that `エイティシックスじゃなくてもよかったんだ` broadens the mechanism. Once a polity legitimates sacrificial categories, the boundary can move. Historical racism remains specific, but the political technology of expendability is portable.

Giad's response is more complicated than pure humanitarianism. The debate over whether to abandon Republic civilians is both strategic and moral. Richard explicitly invokes the Federacy as a `正義の国`, while another line exposes the possible `正義の美名`: humanitarian action can also create legitimacy and political capital.

T10 therefore rejects a purity test for institutions. Mixed motive does not make rescue fake. The relevant questions include what action is taken, who bears its cost, whether persons are actually protected, and whether the public justification remains answerable to facts.

`軍も軍人も、政のための道具だ` is similarly double-edged. Military institutions are instruments of politics. The ethical distinction is not whether roles are instrumental at all; every organized polity uses offices instrumentally. The question is whether **the whole person becomes owned by the role**.

V11's march and evacuation also show that moral commands have bodily preconditions. “Keep moving” means little if transport, food, rest, shoes, medicine, and shelter are absent. Political rescue requires logistics.

Finally, state failure does not homogenize citizens. Some Republic civilians apologize; others remain hateful or passive; some acted decently before collapse. Institution-level culpability and citizen-level culpability remain distinct.

**V10-V11 result:** institutions make political values real by allocating time, information, care, movement, records, and burdens—not merely by announcing ideals.

# 7. Alter.1 — infrastructure expands agency that already exists

Alter.1 is particularly useful because its low-pressure scenes make capability visible without crisis dominating every page.

The domestic Republic material shows racism reproduced below formal state level. A mother treats a kitten associated with Eighty-Six ownership as contaminated. Political ontology therefore survives through households and ordinary habits, not only laws.

The contrast in material abundance is equally important. Inside the Eighty-Five Sectors, citizens possess enough security and leisure for pets and ordinary domestic life while Eighty-Six children lack reliable medicine, graves, and secure survival. Capability inequality is thus not reducible to legal text.

The Giad library/transport example is perhaps the cleanest capability control in the whole corpus. Shin independently finds something interesting and chooses to pursue it. His problem is not lack of curiosity or internal agency. He simply lacks the learned assumption that buses and trains are normal tools for acting on that curiosity; a three-hour walk remains within his intuitive `徒歩圏内`.

This gives T10 a precise distinction:

> **Infrastructure does not create the desire. It changes the cost of acting upon the desire.**

Recovered personnel records similarly restore names, birthdays, family members, and old addresses. That expands biographical knowledge without compelling emotional homecoming. Institutional archives can enlarge a person's option set without dictating what the recovered information must mean.

Finally, Vika's note that a personal gift can become politically complicated because he is royal demonstrates that high status can reduce private freedom too. Office and heredity can make supposedly personal acts politically legible whether the actor wants that or not.

**Alter.1 correction:** better institutions do not manufacture humanity or agency in previously passive subjects. They reduce friction, restore information, stabilize recurrence, and enlarge the range over which existing agency can operate.

# 8. V12 — formal liberty becomes the explicit problem

V12 is the conceptual center of T10 because it directly stages the difference between possessing rights and being practiced in using them.

## 8.1 Formal equality is necessary and can still arrive faster than civic capacity

The post-imperial material presents people who experience education, judgment, and responsibility as burdens rather than gifts. `それが『自由』だと押しつけられた` captures the paradox: a person can experience being required to choose as coercive when their prior social world trained them to obey.

This does not prove that hereditary guardianship was humane. It proves that transition from domination to self-government has developmental costs.

The phrase `学ばない。考えない。決められない` should therefore not be quoted as neutral narration defining a permanent class of incapable people. It is a character's contemptuous judgment inside a contested political argument. The same social background also produces soldiers and citizens capable of responsibility when information and instruction are accessible.

This is why the line `市民全員が己の王だ` is aspirational as much as descriptive. Democracy distributes a form of responsibility previously concentrated in rulers. It does not guarantee that every citizen will exercise it well.

## 8.2 The aristocratic critique identifies a real problem and proposes a dangerous solution

The V12 critique that merely throwing `権利` at people is insufficient contains a real diagnosis: education can exist without learning, leisure without reflection, legal freedom without practiced judgment.

But the proposed conclusion—that some people are fundamentally sheep who ought to remain under stronger rulers—does not follow.

The corpus itself supplies the rebuttal. Capability varies, can be developed, and is context-sensitive. People who initially lack skill can learn. People who possess skill can fail under fear. Shin and Lena themselves repeatedly require correction. Human fallibility is therefore not a stable caste marker.

T10 locks the following distinction:

> **The fact that self-government requires capacities is an argument for building capacities, not for hereditary ownership of judgment.**

## 8.3 Support can become self-government infrastructure

V12's strongest positive institutional examples are mundane:

- adapted educational materials;
- public education;
- military training that meets learners where they are;
- mental-health removal from duty;
- protected rest;
- role reassignment;
- non-hereditary qualification pathways;
- explicit effort not to use refugee volunteers as expendable screens.

These are not proofs of a utopia. They are mechanisms through which formal membership becomes more usable.

## 8.4 Emergency power creates a difficult but necessary limit case

V12 also notes that even the Federacy's normally protected free reporting is temporarily suppressed during an operational deception/counterintelligence crisis: `自由報道さえしばらくは抑えてある`.

T10 does **not** infer that the series endorses unrestricted wartime censorship. Nor does the text treat any restriction as automatically identical with authoritarianism.

The useful institutional question is instead:

> **Is the restriction specific, purpose-bound, temporary, reviewable, and directed toward restoring normal political capacity—or does emergency become the permanent reason citizens may never be trusted with information and judgment?**

V13-V14 will make the second danger concrete.

**V12 result:** formal liberty is indispensable but incomplete; capability support is necessary but normatively dangerous if the institution starts treating temporary developmental dependence as permanent political incapacity.

# 9. V13 — democratic regression, scapegoating, and the reproducible Sector relation

V13 is the corpus's strongest demonstration that a comparatively decent polity can regress without having secretly been identical to San Magnolia all along.

## 9.1 Regression begins with real pressure, not cartoon evil

The Federacy is under catastrophic military strain. Casualties rise. Reserves, trainees, and volunteers are consumed. Refugees move through already stressed regions. Fear is materially reasonable.

This matters because the political argument would be much weaker if regression appeared only when obviously wicked actors decide to become wicked.

The novel instead asks what institutions do when:

- resources shrink;
- citizens are exhausted;
- information is manipulated;
- enemy attack exploits social fracture;
- old status cleavages remain unresolved;
- and people urgently want someone else to absorb risk.

Fear is therefore a **causal mechanism** of exclusion without becoming a moral excuse.

## 9.2 Refugee conscription reveals the slide from utility to desert

The parliamentary proposal to send refugees to the front is unusually revealing. It does not stop at a manpower calculation. The rhetoric says ordinary citizens should be spared because the displaced are `無駄飯ぐらいの役立たず` and therefore ought at least to become useful.

This is the political transition T10 needs to preserve:

> **“We need labor” → “you are less useful” → “therefore you deserve more exposure to death.”**

A capability deficit is transformed into moral desert.

This is one of the clearest signs that welfare and anti-disposability belong inside the freedom analysis. If citizenship protects a person only while they are productive, it is a conditional status rather than a stable floor.

## 9.3 Yatry's `救済` diagnosis is analytically useful but not the novel's normative program

Yatry argues that democracy is burdensome because everyone must be, at minimum, responsible for living their own life. He then suggests that if a society wants to preserve heavy freedom and equality, it needs `救済の仕組み` supplying belonging, achievement, enthusiasm, religion, patriotism, entertainment, or other substitutes for people who experience themselves as useless.

The passage is deliberately cynical. It explicitly describes `達成感の錯覚` and compares such mechanisms with ancient spectacle. T10 therefore must not promote it into an endorsed welfare blueprint.

Its diagnostic value is narrower:

> **A polity that celebrates autonomy only for the capable while allowing weakness to become humiliation creates a political vacuum that can be filled by resentment, spectacle, nationalism, scapegoating, or authoritarian belonging.**

Lena's answer takes a different route. She admits human foolishness rather than denying it.

## 9.4 Rights are “imaginary” in the sense that they require maintenance, not in the sense that they are disposable

Lena's V13 formulation is now central:

> `人権だとか、自由だとか平等だとか正義だとか。そういうものは、なるほど幻想だ。実在しない。だからこそ市民それぞれが、その幻想の価値を守らねばならない。`

The passage does not say rights are fake and therefore irrelevant. It says they lack automatic physical existence.

They persist only when people:

- treat them as binding;
- build institutions around them;
- act as though the weak still count;
- refuse to turn difference into grounds for erasure;
- and maintain them when doing so is costly.

This is a civic rather than metaphysical theory of rights.

The danger of wording it too broadly is obvious: if rights are “only fantasies,” an authoritarian can use the same premise to discard them. Lena instead derives the opposite obligation—**because they do not enforce themselves, people must enforce them against their own convenience.**

## 9.5 Information becomes battlefield infrastructure

No Face's social-systems strategy deliberately targets vulnerable formations and uses existing social divisions. The public revelation of the Eavesdropper betrayal becomes a decisive distrust accelerator.

The point is not that “misinformation caused everything.” Material pressure, prior hierarchy, refugee politics, military exhaustion, and human agency remain active.

The stronger proposition is:

> **Information warfare is most powerful when it can attach true or partly true evidence to an already available social fracture and supply a simple causal story.**

This is politically important because rights to speech and information exist inside adversarial environments. A democratic polity needs enough transparency to remain accountable without assuming that true information cannot be weaponized through context removal.

## 9.6 Giad's imperial inheritance remains politically active

No Face's history of the old empire—keeping social groups divided while aristocratic kinship bound the ruling class—comes from an antagonist and must remain `CS`, not omniscient political science. But the subsequent fragmentation corroborates at least part of the institutional concern.

The revolution removed aristocracy as the formal bonding structure without erasing all the social cleavages underneath. V13's phrase `名前だけの民主制` is also No Face's interpretation and therefore cannot simply be adopted as narrator fact.

The more defensible V2 conclusion is:

> **Democratization changed legal authority more quickly than it dissolved every inherited social hierarchy. Those unresolved cleavages remained potential fault lines under catastrophic stress.**

That explains regression without claiming the democratic transition was unreal.

## 9.7 Institutional goodness does not vanish uniformly during regression

V13 preserves counterevidence against “Giad becomes San Magnolia.”

Rito's killing is not institutionally endorsed. His murderer is investigated. The responsible command apologizes. Shin refuses to generalize one perpetrator into the entire Federacy military. Some officers continue resisting atrocity. Willem calls easy resort to nonhumanity a form of command laziness.

These residues matter because political regression is not instantaneous essence transformation. Institutions can contain mutually inconsistent practices while collapsing.

## 9.8 The Sector comparison becomes textually authorized—but narrow

The culmination is explicit.

The rear is closed by mines and human hostility. The front remains Legion. Orders prohibit withdrawal. Shin states that the Federacy's fronts are becoming `八六区`. The narrator describes advance and retreat as chained by enemy and human malice and says the resulting condition is unchanged from the battlefield San Magnolia called the Eighty-Sixth Sector.

This is the strongest basis for a structural use of “Sector.”

But T10 preserves the historical distinction:

- **San Magnolia:** racial denaturalization, citizenship stripping, deliberate exterminatory warfare, administrative erasure.
- **V13 Federacy regression:** military collapse, fear, unresolved social division, refugee politics, coercive emergency measures, rebellion, hostile information operations, and leadership failure.

The structures converge at:

> **frontline expendability + constrained refusal + blocked return + social/political distancing of the exposed population.**

Structural equivalence at that relation does not make the regimes historically identical.

# 10. V14 — two competing answers to democratic failure

V14 is the first chapter of the final arc, so T10 must preserve an open boundary. Nevertheless, it produces the sharpest political contrast in the current corpus.

## 10.1 Shin's political anthropology rejects permanent moral caste

Shin says:

> `人は愚かじゃない。ただ、いつでも賢くはいられない`

and earlier/later insists that people can change and `戻ってこられる`.

This is not a claim that people are naturally good. It is almost the opposite: people can become frightened, cruel, irrational, exclusionary, or dependent.

The political significance lies in refusing to make those states **essence**.

If foolishness is episodic and revisable, institutions should be built for:

- correction;
- recovery;
- accountability;
- education;
- information;
- and return from bad judgment.

A polity that assumes permanent moral purity is unrealistic. A polity that treats every failure as proof of permanent incapacity is authoritarian.

T10 therefore adds **recoverability after political error** to the concept of usable freedom.

## 10.2 War is identified as an environment that degrades judgment

Shin's claim that the real enemy includes `この戦争そのもの` because war corners people into fear, stupidity, and malice strengthens the anti-war thesis without producing pacifism.

Politically, this means institutional evaluation has to account for environment without erasing agency.

Fear can explain why citizens regress.

It does not make scapegoating correct.

Vaneseed adds another causal layer: V14 suggests that trauma-triggering technology may have amplified the synchronized collapse. But the text does not replace political responsibility with technological determinism. Existing cleavages, fear, institutions, and decisions remain necessary to the explanation.

## 10.3 Civic communication is rebuilt through particularity rather than abstraction alone

The unofficial broadcasts repeatedly address `連邦市民の同胞`. Recipes, jokes, music, household memories, food, and ordinary details circulate alongside political exhortation.

T10 deliberately does **not** call this a constitution.

Its institutional importance is smaller and more concrete:

> **Categories become harder to demonize when citizens can again imagine particular people living ordinary lives inside them.**

The reciprocal recipe exchange matters because it creates civic imagination at kitchen scale. It does not solve representation, law, welfare, or military command. It helps rebuild a social substrate upon which those institutions might operate.

## 10.4 Democracy is framed as an obligation not to close one's eyes

V14 states:

> `民主制を選び勝ち取った自分たち連邦市民には`

in the context of refusing the comfort of not looking at inconvenient facts.

This is an important correction to both paternalism and romantic democracy.

Democracy does not mean citizens are automatically wise.

It means they cannot permanently outsource the responsibility to know, judge, and answer.

This connects directly to V12's `市民全員が己の王だ`, but V14 is less triumphalist: the duty matters precisely because citizens have failed it.

## 10.5 Education continues during collapse because freedom needs a postwar user

`勉強をしましょう。こんな時だからこそ。──戦争が終わる日のために` is one of the clearest capability statements in the corpus.

Education is not deferred until peace as a luxury.

It is practiced under siege because the future polity will require people capable of inhabiting it.

This prevents emergency from consuming every developmental institution in the name of immediate survival.

The political implication is significant:

> **A state preserving bodies while abandoning the capacities needed after emergency is preserving subjects for continued emergency, not citizens for a future.**

## 10.6 Legal accountability becomes part of state reconstruction

Shin's insistence that Rito's murderer be `法的に裁かせる` and that the record remain public ties institutional survival to the ability to say that child-killing was wrong.

The desired Federacy is not merely one that wins the war. It is one capable of publicly judging its own member's wrongdoing and restoring the victim's name.

This is political recoverability:

> **the state must survive in a form capable of condemning what the state or its members did during collapse.**

That differs sharply from justice-as-national-self-identity.

## 10.7 `正義たらん` versus `正義である`

The motto `我ら、世界に誇る正義たらん` functions as an aspirational or volitional grammar: roughly, **let us / may we be worthy of being justice before the world**.

The V14 reactionary epilogue deliberately transforms it:

> `つまり、連邦人の我らはすなわち正義である。`

The next sentences make the danger explicit: if “we are justice,” then exclusion, violence, and persecution performed by “us” can be redescribed as just by definition.

T10 therefore freezes one of the series' strongest political-linguistic rules:

> **Justice remains corrigible while it is a standard placed upon the polity. Justice becomes dangerous when it becomes an identity owned by the polity.**

The difference is not merely stylistic. `正義たらん` leaves a gap between community and standard. `正義である` closes the gap.

## 10.8 Reactionary guardianship is V12's capabilities argument turned inside out

The reactionaries say:

> `言葉も、思索も、自由も、意志も、怠惰で惰弱なニワトリ風情が、手にして良いものではなかった`

and conclude:

> `我らギアーデは、再び帝国に戻るべきだ`

This is the most important T10 falsifier.

V12 established a real problem: some people struggle with self-government; formal rights can arrive faster than practiced judgment.

The reactionary solution is:

> because some citizens misuse freedom, ordinary people should never possess it; hereditary rulers should take judgment back permanently.

That is exactly where scaffolding becomes domination.

The governing distinction is now sharp enough for Phase 6:

> **A freedom-enhancing institution responds to incapacity by building capacity. An authoritarian guardian responds to incapacity by making the incapacity the permanent reason the subject must remain ruled.**

## 10.9 Aristocratic privilege itself receives two competing interpretations

V14's prologue gives a useful internal control. Willem and other privileged houses treat their inherited resources as a debt to be spent for soldiers and the polity. Even the destruction of a noble house can be acceptable if its resources can be rebuilt later.

The epilogue reactionaries treat bloodline as the reason the old order should return.

Thus inheritance can generate:

- **present responsibility**, because one has resources and historical protection;
- or **hereditary entitlement**, because one claims those resources prove a right to rule.

The series currently favors the first without pretending aristocratic history becomes innocent.

## 10.10 V14 remains open

The public positioning of Frederica on the throne and Shin beside her as `War Emperor` cannot yet be interpreted as consensual endorsement, successful restoration, parody, coercion, hostage politics, or final regime settlement. V14 itself explicitly points forward to the next volume for explanation.

T10 therefore leaves the exact institutional meaning of the New Imperial Court **OPEN**.

# 12. Formal rights are necessary but not sufficient

T10 now strongly supports four propositions simultaneously:

1. **Rights matter materially.** V01 makes denial of citizenship and human standing a mechanism of genocide.
2. **Rights do not execute themselves.** V12 shows that legal freedom can outpace practiced judgment; V13 shows rights collapsing under pressure when citizens cease maintaining them.
3. **Capability support cannot replace rights.** A benevolent guardian may provide food, education, safety, and status while denying role exit or independent judgment.
4. **Capability support is still necessary.** Transport, education, medicine, rest, information, records, and material security change whether a nominal choice can actually be exercised.

The mature formulation is therefore:

> **Substantive freedom requires both protected standing and usable capability, plus enough room to practice judgment and recover from mistakes. None of those layers can substitute completely for another.**

# 13. Paternalism continuum — not every protective override is the same

T09 established the relational version. T10 scales it institutionally.

| Institutional form | Typical justification | Agency effect | T10 status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Emergency protection** | immediate incapacity/danger | temporarily narrows choice to preserve later agency | potentially legitimate if bounded |
| **Developmental scaffolding** | learner lacks skills/information | adds capacities and future options | strongly compatible with substantive freedom |
| **Protective paternalism** | authority believes another's choice is self-harming | may protect while also narrowing self-authorship | requires strict review and exit criteria |
| **Guardianship** | subject judged unable to decide for extended period | substitutes authority for judgment | legitimate only if necessity and restoration path remain real |
| **Functional caste** | role said to be natural, sacred, inherited, or necessary | prevents role revision/exit | domination even when honored |
| **Authoritarian guardianship** | ordinary people judged permanently too weak/foolish for freedom | makes incapacity the reason for indefinite rule | V14 reactionary negative control |

The critical diagnostic is not simply whether the authority helps.

Ask:

> **What would count as successful support?**

If success means the supported person eventually gains more capacity to answer, the structure resembles scaffolding.

If success requires the supported person to remain dependent so that authority continues to be necessary, the structure has become domination.

# 14. Democracy in *86* is a fallibility-management system, not a claim of citizen perfection

The V12-V14 material should not be summarized as “democracy is good because ordinary people are wise.” The text says almost the opposite.

People are frequently:

- frightened;
- lazy;
- ignorant;
- dependent;
- traumatized;
- selfish;
- prone to scapegoating;
- susceptible to propaganda;
- and capable of catastrophic judgment.

Shin, Lena, Raiden, Ernst, and other highly competent characters also make serious mistakes.

The difference is therefore not wise masses versus wise rulers.

The stronger institutional principle is:

> **No class possesses stable entitlement to final judgment merely by claiming superior wisdom. Institutions should instead make error visible, contestable, correctable, and survivable.**

That requires:

- plural information;
- distributed offices;
- records;
- legal accountability;
- education;
- material support;
- limits on irreversible action;
- opportunities to revise;
- and political norms that permit citizens and leaders to say “we were wrong.”

V14's `戻ってこられる` is therefore political as well as psychological.

# 15. Emergency power — the institutional test is whether normal agency remains the destination

The series takes emergencies seriously enough that a simplistic absolutism would not fit the evidence.

Military commanders issue binding orders. Evacuations compel movement. Information can be temporarily restricted. Exhausted or impaired personnel can be removed from duty. Scarce resources require prioritization.

The question is not whether coercion ever occurs.

The useful Phase-6 test is:

1. **Necessity:** is there a real emergency rather than rhetorical convenience?
2. **Proportionality:** is the restriction narrower than the danger?
3. **Temporality:** is the restriction expected to end?
4. **Reviewability:** can facts or changing conditions reopen the decision?
5. **Standing:** does the constrained person remain someone whose interests count?
6. **Restoration:** is the purpose to restore ordinary agency or make emergency the new permanent normal?
7. **Distribution:** who repeatedly bears the cost?

San Magnolia fails especially on standing, restoration, and distribution.

The V13 Federacy crisis begins failing on distribution and restoration as rear routes close and politically weak populations are selected for greater exposure.

V14's reactionaries then attempt to convert emergency failure into a permanent constitutional anthropology: citizens were never fit for freedom in the first place.

# 16. Political regression mechanism — V13-V14 sequence

The current evidence supports a provisional regression chain. It should remain an analytical model rather than an asserted universal law.

1. **Material catastrophe:** casualties, displacement, logistics failure, exhaustion.
2. **Preexisting social fracture:** imperial class/blood cleavages, Republic/Federacy tension, refugee status, minority identities.
3. **Information shock:** true and manipulated information circulates in forms that intensify distrust.
4. **Fear seeks legible categories:** `we` and `they` become politically easier than differentiated responsibility.
5. **Utility becomes desert:** weak/nonproductive groups are redescribed as owing greater sacrifice.
6. **Protective institutions narrow:** rights and return routes become conditional under emergency.
7. **Coercion generates resistance:** reserve coercion, rebellion, hostage-taking, fragmentation.
8. **Organizations lose trust:** military and political identities fracture into smaller groups.
9. **Exceptional burdens reappear:** marked soldiers are ordered to absorb violence while rearward escape is blocked.
10. **Crisis becomes ideological proof:** reactionaries reinterpret failure as evidence that freedom itself was the mistake.
11. **Hereditary authority returns as promised relief:** rulers offer to remove the burden of judgment from ordinary people.

This model explains how regression can emerge from a better polity without claiming every democratic institution inevitably collapses.

# 17. `正義` as political grammar — standard versus possession

T10 should route the following distinction directly into Document 12 as well as Documents 05-06.

## `我ら、世界に誇る正義たらん`

Functionally aspirational/volitional. Justice stands ahead of the community as something it should strive to instantiate and be worthy of.

Analytical effect:

> **community remains answerable to standard.**

## `我らはすなわち正義である`

Declarative identity. The community and justice are collapsed.

Analytical effect:

> **the community becomes the standard.**

The V14 epilogue immediately demonstrates the political consequence: exclusion and violence become just because “we” perform them; criticism becomes evil because it opposes “justice.”

This is one of the clearest linguistic mechanisms by which corrigibility disappears.

# 21. Japanese terminology notes

## `自由`

Do not translate every occurrence into one political concept. Across the corpus it can mean legal freedom, freedom from ownership, option space, psychological release, role exit, action without command, or the burden of self-government.

## `平等`

Often paired with `自由` in V12-V13 political arguments. The text repeatedly tests equality when capacities and burdens are unequal. Equality therefore cannot be assumed to mean identical treatment.

## `権利` / `人権`

`人権` is especially politically charged by V01's dehumanizing epigraph and V13's civic-maintenance argument. `権利` in V12 appears in the critique of granting rights without developed practice. Neither usage licenses treating rights as dispensable.

## `市民`

Carries both legal and civic-duty meaning. `連邦市民の同胞` in V14 is not merely a demographic label; it attempts to reconstruct reciprocal membership.

## `臣民`

Important in post-imperial V12 discourse because it encodes the prior political relation of subjects to rulers. The transition from `臣民` to citizens is not simply a vocabulary change; it transfers judgment burden.

## `救済`

V13's Yatry passage is analytically dangerous. `救済の仕組み` there includes cynical substitute satisfactions and spectacle. Do not automatically translate it into a normatively approved social welfare program.

## `庇護` / `保護` / `支配`

Protection and domination are not synonyms, but the corpus repeatedly shows one becoming the other when the protected person's future answer-space disappears.

## `民主制`

V12-V14 increasingly treats democracy as practiced distributed responsibility, not merely institutional label. V13's antagonist phrase `名前だけの民主制` remains character judgment and should not be silently upgraded to narrator verdict.

## `正義たらん`

Aspirational/volitional political grammar. Preserve the gap between community and moral standard.

## `正義である`

Declarative identity. In V14 reactionary rhetoric the phrase collapses community and standard, making self-correction interpretable as betrayal.

## `戻る`

T01 already established that return is not inherently positive. T10 adds the political negative control `再び帝国に戻るべきだ`. Political recovery and restorationism must remain separate.

# 23. Phase-5 lock propositions

The following propositions are mature enough to carry into Phase 5 locator lock, subject to exact-source reopening:

### T10-P01 — formal status floor
A political community cannot meaningfully recognize persons while legally preserving categories whose members may be disposed of as nonpersons.

### T10-P02 — rights plus capability
Formal rights are necessary but become substantively usable only through material, epistemic, developmental, and institutional capability.

### T10-P03 — capability does not replace rights
A materially generous guardian can still dominate if the beneficiary lacks meaningful answer, refusal, revision, or exit.

### T10-P04 — scaffolding criterion
Support is freedom-enhancing when successful support makes continued guardianship less necessary rather than more necessary.

### T10-P05 — role-exit criterion
Social honor, prestige, holiness, or usefulness does not establish autonomy when the assigned role cannot be refused or revised.

### T10-P06 — error recoverability
Political freedom assumes fallible people and therefore requires institutions capable of correction, record, distributed judgment, and recovery after bad decisions.

### T10-P07 — no permanent-incapacity inference
Observed civic incompetence or dependence cannot by itself justify hereditary or permanent guardianship.

### T10-P08 — emergency restoration criterion
Emergency restriction is analytically distinct from authoritarian guardianship when it remains necessity-bound, proportionate, revisable, and directed toward restoring ordinary agency.

### T10-P09 — welfare anti-disposability floor
Weakness, displacement, injury, or low productivity cannot become sufficient reason that a person owes greater exposure to death.

### T10-P10 — Sector structural criterion
The strongest non-San-Magnolia Sector comparison requires the convergence of expendability, constrained refusal, and blocked return for an exposed population.

### T10-P11 — fear as cause, not excuse
Fear and war can explain political exclusion without justifying it or erasing individual/institutional responsibility.

### T10-P12 — political return is ambiguous
Return can mean recovery of judgment or reactionary restoration. The value lies in recoverability, not return as such.

### T10-P13 — justice externality
Justice is politically healthier when it remains a standard that can judge the community rather than an identity the community owns.

### T10-P14 — citizenship is material but non-total
Citizenship creates real rights and reciprocal obligations but does not by itself determine home, belonging, competence, or moral virtue.

### T10-P15 — democratization is developmental
Moving from subjecthood to citizenship transfers burdens of judgment that may require education, practice, support, and recoverable error; the difficulty of transition does not invalidate the destination.

### T10-P16 — institutional regression does not erase prior difference
A better institution can regress toward structures resembling a worse one without having always been morally or historically identical to it.

V14 contrasts privilege-as-debt with bloodline-as-entitlement but does not yet settle the long-term constitutional position of inherited status.

V13 demonstrates how quickly nominal citizenship becomes hollow when retreat and physical return are blocked. The final arc has not yet translated this into explicit institutional guarantees.

# 25. Final T10 judgment

The strongest institutional thesis available at the V14 boundary is:

> **86 increasingly treats political freedom as supported, corrigible self-government. Formal recognition is indispensable because people cannot be free while the law classifies them as livestock, weapons, hereditary functions, or disposable bodies. But recognition becomes usable only when institutions supply the material and developmental conditions through which people can learn, move, heal, know, decide, fail, revise, and return. Those supports remain legitimate only while they enlarge rather than permanently replace the subject's judgment.**

The series therefore rejects two symmetrical failures.

The first is **abandonment disguised as liberty**:

> you are formally free, so every failure is entirely yours even if the conditions needed to use that freedom were never built.

The second is **domination disguised as care**:

> because freedom is difficult and people sometimes fail, wiser guardians should decide permanently on their behalf.

V12 identifies the problem between them. V13 shows what happens when support, cohesion, information, and return-capability collapse. V14 then makes the choice explicit.

One answer says citizens must look, study, remember, judge, correct, communicate, and rebuild even after catastrophic failure.

The other says ordinary people should never have possessed `言葉も、思索も、自由も、意志も` and that society should `再び帝国に戻るべきだ`.

The current corpus does not romanticize the first answer. Self-government remains burdensome, fragile, and capable of terrible failure. Its defense is more demanding:

> **Human fallibility is not a reason to abolish freedom. It is a reason to build institutions in which error does not have to become destiny.**

That principle now links the political synthesis directly to T01's recoverability, T02's purpose-capture, T05's organizational redundancy, T06's repeatable future, T07's care after function loss, T08's bounded responsibility, and T09's interruptible interdependence.

A mature polity in *86* would therefore not be one whose citizens are always wise or whose leaders are always good. It would be one in which **status cannot be revoked for convenience, weakness does not create a duty to disappear, support does not become permanent ownership, offices survive individual failure, truth can re-enter public judgment, wrongdoing can be recorded and punished, roles can be exited, exposed people retain routes back, and neither state nor citizen is permitted to declare a temporary failure the final truth of who someone is.**
