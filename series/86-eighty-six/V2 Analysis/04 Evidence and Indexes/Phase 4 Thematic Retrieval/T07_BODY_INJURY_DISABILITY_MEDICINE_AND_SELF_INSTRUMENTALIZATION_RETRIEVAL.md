# T07 — Body, Injury, Disability, Medicine, and Self-Instrumentalization Retrieval

This dossier retrieves the bodily evidence needed to test a central V2 proposition: **does *86* separate a person's standing from bodily function while still taking bodily limits seriously enough to constrain what futures are actually usable?** The answer across V01-V14 is yes, but only if several concepts remain distinct. The novels repeatedly separate injury from disability, disability from social worth, treatment from restoration, accommodation from cure, physical danger from moral culpability, and protective medicine from paternalistic control. They also show that a person can genuinely desire a future while continuing to treat their own body as a military resource.

The governing adversarial question is:

> **When a body is wounded, changed, exhausted, medically limited, technologically altered, or made dangerous to others, does the series treat the change as a verdict on the person—or as a material constraint that institutions and relationships must answer without converting function into the condition of belonging?**

The retrieval result is: **bodily function matters because bodies make some actions possible and others impossible; it does not determine moral standing. A humane institution treats injury first as a claim to care and only afterward as a question of role. An exploitative institution reverses that sequence, asking whether the body can still perform before deciding whether it deserves treatment, protection, or continued membership.**

A second result complicates any recovery narrative centered on Shin. By V14 he has a genuine future, reciprocal love, ordinary desires, and a developed anti-expendability ethic; he nevertheless conceals major sensory loss because disclosure might remove him from his chosen combat role. **Future-orientation does not automatically produce bodily self-protection.**

# 1. Executive taxonomy — eleven distinctions later synthesis must preserve

| Dimension | T07 question | Failure mode if collapsed |
|---|---|---|
| **Body** | What material capacities and vulnerabilities does this embodiment create? | treating embodiment as mere metaphor |
| **Injury** | What damage occurred? Is it temporary, chronic, or irreversible? | assuming every injury produces the same disability |
| **Disability** | Which activities or roles become inaccessible or altered? | equating specific function loss with global incapacity |
| **Standing** | Does care, belonging, citizenship or respect survive function loss? | making usefulness the condition of personhood |
| **Treatment** | Is the aim survival, comfort, recovery, adaptation, or rapid return to duty? | treating medicine as synonymous with restoring old function |
| **Accommodation** | Can environment, role, tools or relationships adapt around changed capacity? | treating cure as the only successful response |
| **Bodily autonomy** | Who may expose, experiment on, modify, diagnose, or use the body? | benevolence becoming control |
| **Self-instrumentalization** | Does the subject conceal or override bodily limits to preserve usefulness? | assuming future desire automatically produces self-care |
| **Trauma embodiment** | How do memory and physiological response interact? | reducing trauma either to pure thought or pure biology |
| **Dangerous embodiment** | What if the body creates real risk to others? | converting risk management into a duty to disappear |
| **Artificial embodiment** | How do replaceability, pain, fatigue and continuity differ in Sirins/Legion? | making biological humanity the hidden moral criterion |

The central institutional rule is:

> **A person's body may determine what they can presently do. A humane institution does not let that answer determine whether the person remains worth saving, supporting, hearing, or allowing to choose another role.**

# 2. V01-V04 — the body is where political classification becomes material

## V01 — administrative nonpersonhood becomes medical and experimental permission

The Republic's dehumanization is not merely insulting language. It reorganizes what may be done to bodies. The Special Reconnaissance mission is prepared alongside `二度と帰らぬプロセッサー達の身辺整理`: nonreturn is administratively expected. Para-RAID development is even more explicit. The state that calls Eighty-Six nonhuman uses them as human experimental subjects precisely when their human nervous systems are useful, while disregarding subject safety.

This is the bodily form of V01's political contradiction: **denial of personhood does not mean the state stops recognizing human capacities; it means it feels freer to appropriate those capacities without reciprocal obligation.**

The Sector's medical problem therefore begins before any individual disability arc. Who receives anesthesia, treatment, shelter, nutrition, evacuation and a grave is already determined by political classification.

## V02 — rescue changes what happens after the body fails

Giad's difference becomes concrete before it becomes ideological. Shin survives an injury requiring emergency surgery that the narration explicitly contrasts with Republic abandonment. A clinician tells him not to overexert while postoperative fever remains. Eugene receives morphine even when his injury cannot be survived.

That last detail is especially important. Analgesia for a dying soldier demonstrates a form of care whose value cannot be reduced to restoring military productivity. **Pain relief still matters when the body will never fight again.**

V02 also establishes another durable theme: institutions persist as bodily history. The removed Raid Device leaves pain at the implant site. Shin's neck scar remains visible. The Five can tolerate Reginleif loads partly because years inside lethal Juggernauts trained their bodies under abusive conditions. A better institution inherits bodies already shaped by the worse one.

## V03 — pain and fatigue become alarms rather than virtues

The Federacy explicitly recognizes `戦闘神経症` and medically evacuates young soldiers. More revealingly, adults tell Shin that the thresholds he learned in the Sector are wrong for a force that intends its soldiers to survive. Fatigue and pain are described as **warning systems** whose dulling is itself a problem.

This is one of the earliest direct attacks on bodily self-instrumentalization. Shin does not have to be suicidal for his bodily habits to remain dangerous. He can sincerely want to accomplish the mission and still possess a learned tendency to treat pain as irrelevant until the body physically stops him.

The institutional countermeasure is not simply kindness. It is logistics: rotation, rest, medical evacuation, relief personnel and command authority willing to say that continuing is less professional than stopping.

## V04 — visible injury does not create interpretive ownership

V04 separates what can be seen from what outsiders are entitled to know. Lena notices Shin's neck scar and chooses not to demand its history. The body can make violence visible without giving another person automatic interpretive rights over the wound.

Taste also becomes analytically important. The text notes Shin's dulled taste long before V14 establishes anosmia/ageusia. T07 therefore treats the later sensory disability as a longitudinal development rather than a late-volume symbolic invention.

V04 additionally extends bodily exploitation into technology. Children died in Para-RAID human experimentation; human-farm bodies become neural resources; tactical decisions accept limited burns to prevent lethal outcomes. The volume's bodily ethics are therefore not reducible to “never harm.” They ask **who authorizes harm, for what end, under what alternatives, and whether the injured subject remains a person afterward rather than merely a resource.**

# 3. V05-V06 — artificial bodies expose the difference between vulnerability and moral standing

## V05 — pain, fatigue and replaceability are embodiment properties, not personhood tests

The Sirins are indispensable to T07 because their bodies violate assumptions built from human medicine. They lack pain; their artificial brains do not accumulate ordinary fatigue; damaged embodiments can be replaced or reconstructed to degrees impossible for humans. None of this automatically answers whether they matter morally.

The donor protocol also gives the series an explicit medical-consent contrast with San Magnolia. Vika states that donation is voluntary and that a person who could be saved is not sacrificed for a neural scan merely because they volunteered. Whatever else one thinks of Sirin ontology, the text itself marks a boundary between **using the dead/dying with consent** and **making a treatable person die because the military wants the resource.**

The Sirins therefore show why bodily irreplaceability cannot be the sole basis of moral standing. Their embodiments are more replaceable than human bodies. Particular Sirins can nevertheless acquire relational significance. Conversely, San Magnolia treats irreplaceable human bodies as disposable.

## V06 — replaceable embodiment still generates an anti-disposability problem

V06 strengthens that contradiction. Lerche can return in reconstructed embodiment, but the story refuses to say this makes her destruction ethically weightless. Vika's conduct preserves some answer-space and reveals discomfort with simply treating Sirins as `消耗品`.

At the human level, Shin's exhaustion becomes visible enough that Lena tells him to sleep. His face remains pale. V06's relationship work therefore has a bodily underside: learning to tell someone one's pain is not merely emotional vulnerability; it is also a condition for care before collapse.

The broader T07 rule emerging here is:

> **Vulnerability can increase claims to care, but invulnerability does not erase claims to moral consideration.**

# 4. V07 — scars can be altered without rewriting history

V07 is important because bodies stop appearing primarily as damaged military instruments. The resort, bathing, swimsuits, formalwear, dance, attraction and fatigue all make embodiment socially and erotically visible.

Anju's scar treatment is the central disability/body-ownership case. She begins treating the scars on her back. The act does not deny that the violence occurred; it does not betray Daiya or other dead; and the text does not require her to preserve visible damage as proof that the past mattered.

This gives T07 an essential distinction:

> **Remembering an injury does not require preserving every bodily trace of it.**

The same volume's explicit description of the group as still `十代のまだ子供` also protects the developmental analysis. Combat-trained bodies can be extraordinarily competent while still being adolescent bodies with growth, fatigue, attraction, embarrassment and incomplete development.

# 5. V08-V09 — Theo turns disability into an institutional test

## V08 — involuntary subtraction

Theo's left arm is amputated between wrist and elbow. At the V08 boundary the loss is not narratively solved. He immediately understands that he cannot fight beside the others in the same way. T07 preserves this prospective limit because later adaptation must not be imported backward as though the amputation were never catastrophic.

The volume simultaneously shows Shin cutting treatment short to return to the bridge because his unique hearing remains militarily valuable. He eventually collapses because he has pushed an untreated body past its limit. This is the clearest pre-V14 demonstration that **a person can want to survive while still spending the body as mission equipment.**

## V09 — care comes before replacement function

V09 supplies the institutional answer Theo did not yet possess in V08.

He is recovered and taken immediately into surgery. The Fleet Country's medical infrastructure prevents a major upper-limb injury from becoming lethal. No one waits to determine whether a sufficiently advanced prosthesis will restore Reginleif piloting before saving him.

That sequence is ethically decisive:

1. save the person;
2. stabilize and rehabilitate the body;
3. investigate prosthetic/adaptive possibilities;
4. discuss branch change or other roles;
5. let the person participate in deciding what comes next.

The available prosthetics cannot reproduce the response required for Reginleif combat. T07 therefore refuses both common disability simplifications. The injury is **not** secretly inconsequential, but it also does **not** erase Theo's future.

His movement from `僕はもう、戦えない` to `でも、死ななくていい` is among the series' strongest separations of function from standing. `まだ死んでないから、何かをしたい` then begins a future without requiring a new heroic identity to be immediately available.

The Republic comparison makes the political meaning explicit. An injury needing long recovery would have been abandoned even if medically survivable because a noncombat-capable Eighty-Six was not worth feeding. **Disability becomes lethal under the Republic not because the wound biologically demands death, but because the institution refuses noncombat standing.**

# 6. V10 — the retrospective medical anti-model

V10 turns that political contrast into direct historical evidence. The Sector's medical units are configured to treat only injuries compatible with quick return to combat. A body that needs time becomes administratively `救命不可` even where proper treatment could save it. There are no real military doctors or field hospitals because official doctrine insists that no human soldiers occupy the battlefield.

This is perhaps T07's clearest case of institutional meaning overriding biological fact:

> **“Unsavable” can be a political classification disguised as a medical one.**

The volume also restores child scale to the combat story. Eleven-year-old Shin and other growing children occupy cramped, violent machines. Differences in size and strength remain material even though later legend remembers exceptional combat skill. The body is therefore one of the places where V10 dismantles retrospective adultification.

The Gentle World supplies the anti-theodicy control: `傷なんて、負わなくてよかった`. Later development does not make the wound necessary. A person may value who they became without needing to claim the injury was good for them.

# 7. V11-V12 — medicine becomes part of professional responsibility

## V11 — logistics is bodily ethics

V11 repeatedly insists that military planning has to fit actual bodies. Marching speed has physiological limits. Tired people need breaks even when urgency is real. Medication use must be reported. Personnel who have been moving for more than a day are sent back earlier. Exhausted soldiers go to beds, showers and food.

The refugee march gives the civilian mirror. Someone saying `もう、歩けない。足が痛いの` is not disproved by the moral necessity of evacuation. Bodies can fail before obligations end. Good logistics is precisely the art of preventing ethical necessity from becoming a fantasy that flesh has no limits.

This expands T07 beyond hospitals. **Transport, sleep, food, footwear, rest cycles and evacuation vehicles are medical-personhood infrastructure because they determine whose body is expected to absorb the cost of policy.**

## V12 — reporting strain becomes a duty

V12 formalizes the medical lesson. Lena is ordered away from duty for a month. Kurena monitors nightmares and knows when she should consult medical staff. Dustin is withheld from the next operation after mental-health evaluation while remaining fully present in ordinary social life. Several Eighty-Six receive temporary rest after counseling despite elite combat experience.

Shin's ability produces `強制的な眠り`: the body simply shuts down. Grethe then tells him to report strain because hiding it transfers risk to subordinates. This reframes self-care from private preference into reciprocal responsibility.

T07 therefore distinguishes **medical authority that preserves agency** from paternalism. Temporary grounding, counseling or rest can narrow immediate choice while protecting the subject's longer-term ability to choose. But this legitimacy depends on proportionality, reviewability, and an actual route back or onward; V14 will show how easily the same vocabulary of protection can become another form of instrumental control.

# 8. Alter.1 — ordinary bodily need predates formal medical correction

Alter.1 is useful because its low-pressure chronology reveals the body outside climactic injury.

The adolescents are still growing and require unusually high food intake because of age and accumulated muscle. Lena's multi-day sleep deprivation appears before V12's formal medical removal, establishing overwork as a recurring trait rather than a one-volume crisis. Shin warns her not to overtrain because `体を壊したら元も子もありません`.

Theo's post-amputation participation in Annette's birthday logistics also gives a mundane disability control. He can be absent from frontline Reginleif combat while remaining socially useful, relationally active and capable of helping with ordinary tasks.

The supplemental lesson is therefore simple but important:

> **Health cannot be inferred from combat functionality, and disability cannot be inferred from absence of combat alone.**

# 9. V13 — the body itself becomes the weaponized institution

The Fawn project is the most extreme human-body instrumentalization in the current corpus. Adolescent girls are selected because female fat distribution and reproductive biology create useful substrate for engineered explosive cells, while the same designers classify girls as less useful than boys in ordinary combat.

This produces an unusually explicit conjunction of sexism, dehumanization and military optimization. The state does not merely put a weapon into a person's hands or place a person inside a weapon. It converts living tissue into the weapon system.

The intended postwar use is even more revealing. The Fawns can approach surviving Eighty-Six as recognizable comrades and later detonate among them. **Trust, resemblance and bodily proximity become delivery mechanisms.**

Yet V13 refuses the easy answer that these dangerous bodies therefore ought to die. Yuto explicitly rejects the claim that a person whose continued existence creates risk must accept death for humanity's sake. T07 therefore freezes a distinction needed by Document 08:

> **Danger can justify precautions, containment, treatment or negotiated restrictions. It does not by itself convert existence into culpability.**

This is the human control for the broader personhood architecture. A body can genuinely be dangerous without its bearer becoming morally equivalent to the danger.

# 10. V14 — future desire does not cure bodily self-instrumentalization

## 10.1 Shin's sensory loss is a disability, not merely symbolism

V14 establishes a long deterioration: Shin's taste had been dulled since the Sector, worsened as Shepherd burden increased, and by the post-October period both taste and smell are effectively absent. Theo notices the eating behavior because he has known another soldier with anosmia.

This matters materially. Food becomes harder to identify and trust; eating can provoke rejection. The disability affects ordinary life, not just tactical performance.

## 10.2 Shin hides the condition because treatment threatens role continuity

The decisive fact is not that Shin fails to notice the problem. He knows and conceals it.

`移ったら、それきり戻れなくなるだろ` reveals his fear that being shifted away from frontline status might become irreversible. He also understands that the military values his wide-area sensing even apart from Reginleif piloting. In the deteriorated political environment, a medical decision could therefore become a mechanism for preserving his usefulness while ignoring his own preferred role.

This is why V14 cannot be summarized as “Shin has learned to want to live, therefore the death wish is solved.” The older self-instrumentalizing habit survives in a new form:

> **I want a future, but I may conceal damage to preserve my chosen place in the present.**

## 10.3 Theo models accommodation without cure or surveillance

Theo's response is unusually mature. Shin asks him not to turn meals into tests. Theo accepts that boundary and instead offers to eat with him and describe the taste beforehand.

That is accommodation:

- it acknowledges the disability;
- it does not pretend the sense has returned;
- it preserves trust around food;
- it adds information rather than seizing control;
- it remains relational rather than clinicalizing every meal.

Theo's own combat return works similarly. He uses a slow adapted platform suitable to his present capacity, with another injured soldier taking the complementary role. The achievement is not restoration of his original body. It is **role redesign around changed bodies**.

## 10.4 Trauma becomes a bodily/cognitive attack surface

The Vaneseed makes trauma materially exploitable. It can induce forced flashback severe enough to incapacitate combatants. Vika explicitly links susceptibility to childhood violence and long periods without treatment, while also noting that non-Eighty-Six Federacy soldiers can be affected after sufficient loss.

This is important because it avoids two reductions. Trauma is not merely a mistaken belief that can be argued away, and it is not immutable essence. V14's later Shin/Rei scene gives the healthier formulation: a wound can still hurt even after moral reinterpretation. `傷を抉られたら、痛いのは当然です` separates persistent embodied vulnerability from failure to forgive.

# 12. Medicine versus paternalism

T07 does not treat all medical restriction as either benevolent or oppressive. The corpus supplies cases of both legitimate constraint and genuine paternalism risk.

A medical intervention is most compatible with the V2 freedom model when it is:

- responding to a real bodily or psychological condition;
- proportionate to the risk;
- aimed at preserving the person's longer-term agency rather than merely organizational convenience;
- temporary or reviewable when possible;
- accompanied by explanation and routes to return, adapt, or choose another role;
- not conditioned on whether the patient remains militarily useful.

This describes much of Giad's best practice: surgery, evacuation, counseling, rest, medication monitoring and rehabilitation.

The same vocabulary becomes dangerous when protection means:

- permanent exclusion decided without the subject;
- concealment of information “for their own good”;
- retaining a person as a strategic asset while ignoring their preferred role;
- defining the patient's good entirely through institutional readiness.

V14's narration explicitly recognizes that the weakened Federacy might medically relocate Shin and then continue exploiting his sensory ability while ignoring his wishes. **A humane goal does not immunize a medical institution against instrumentalization.**

# 13. Self-instrumentalization is not identical to suicidal intent

This is one of T07's most important character corrections.

Shin's bodily self-neglect appears in multiple forms:

1. Sector-trained high pain/fatigue thresholds;
2. V03 need for adults to redefine pain as warning;
3. V08 treatment interruption because his sensory ability remains mission-critical;
4. V12 involuntary shutdown from ability overload;
5. V14 concealment of sensory disability to avoid losing frontline role.

The later cases occur after Shin has clearly developed reasons to live.

Therefore the mature formulation is:

> **Self-instrumentalization can survive the end of a death wish. A person may value life while still valuing usefulness, role continuity, comrades' safety, or institutional contribution enough to hide or override bodily cost.**

This should prevent Document 02 from using medical self-protection as a simple proxy for psychological recovery.

# 14. Disability is neither pure tragedy nor automatic liberation

Theo supplies the decisive falsifier to both common narrative templates.

The amputation is unwanted. It closes a specific future. It changes balance, movement, training and career possibility. Available prostheses cannot simply make the loss disappear.

But the injury also does not reveal that Theo was “meant” to leave combat, cure him of pride, or automatically grant a healthier identity. At first he does not want to stop being a Processor.

The ethical achievement occurs later and institutionally:

- he survives;
- he receives rehabilitation;
- he remains part of the group;
- other military/civilian roles are possible;
- he can draw, help, travel and participate socially;
- he may later choose an adapted military role.

So the correct formula is:

> **Disability can involuntarily remove options. Freedom requires that the loss of one option not become the loss of every socially recognized future.**

# 15. Bodies, wounds, and memory

T03 established that an inherited wound need not remain sovereign over identity. T07 adds the bodily caution: reinterpretation does not erase tissue, reflex, sensory loss, scar, pain, nightmare or conditioned response.

That is why V14's Shin/Rei material is so important. Shin can understand Rei differently and still be physiologically vulnerable to reenacted strangulation. Lena's answer does not tell him to “move on” more successfully. It says the old event hurt because the person mattered.

The resulting distinction is:

> **A wound can lose authority over the future without losing causal force in the body.**

This allows later synthesis to discuss recovery without demanding disappearance of symptoms as proof that moral or relational reinterpretation was sincere.

# 20. T07 final retrieval rule

The strongest formulation T07 can safely pass forward is:

> **In *86*, the body is neither destiny nor disposable container. It is the material site through which politics, war, memory, technology, pleasure, injury and care become possible. Bodily loss can close real options without closing personhood; medicine can preserve agency without restoring the old body; and a society reveals its theory of persons most clearly when a body can no longer perform the function for which that society once valued it.**

Its institutional corollary is equally important:

> **Ask what happens after function fails. If the answer is abandonment, disposal, compulsory sacrifice, or conversion into another resource, the person was never fully protected from instrumentalization. If the answer is treatment, accommodation, rehabilitation, role revision, continued belonging, and meaningful participation in what comes next, then function has ceased to be the condition of standing.**
