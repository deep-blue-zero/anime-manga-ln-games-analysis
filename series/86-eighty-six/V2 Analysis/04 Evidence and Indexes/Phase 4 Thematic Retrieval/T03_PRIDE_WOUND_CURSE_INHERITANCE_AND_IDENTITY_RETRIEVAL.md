# T03 — Pride, Wound, Curse, Inheritance, and Identity Retrieval

This dossier retrieves the longitudinal evidence behind one of the easiest claims in *86* to state badly: that the Eighty-Six must somehow “let go of their pride and trauma.” The V01–V14 corpus does not support that simplification. Pride begins as an anti-dehumanization technology; wounds remain historical facts even after recovery; the dead can sustain the living without owning them; inherited names and roles can be accepted, revised, or declined; and the language of `呪い` can describe both coercive enclosure and a consciously accepted obligation that a character is unwilling to call meaningless.

The governing question is therefore: **When does an inheritance remain a meaningful part of a person, and when does preserving it become a demand that the person remain wounded in the same way forever?**

The retrieval answer is: **the problem is not inheritance, pride, memory, role, or binding in themselves. The strongest negative pattern is exclusivity plus nonrevisability: an inherited meaning becomes curse-like when belonging, worth, love, or identity depend upon continuing to inhabit one wound, role, grievance, function, or relation without a viable right to reinterpret, refuse, add alternatives, or leave.** Conversely, an inheritance can remain demanding and still be compatible with agency when its bearer knowingly accepts it, can reinterpret what it asks, can add other identities, and does not lose personhood or belonging by changing.

# 1. Executive taxonomy: six questions that later synthesis must keep separate

| Dimension | Healthy/compatible form | Curse-like/enclosing form |
|---|---|---|
| **Pride** | self-respect, refusal of dehumanization, chosen standard | sole positive identity: `誇りしか` |
| **Wound** | historically real injury that may remain painful | injury treated as destiny or qualification for belonging |
| **Inheritance** | memory/role/object received and reinterpreted | dead or institution dictating a fixed future |
| **Bond/obligation** | knowingly accepted, reciprocal, revisable commitment | binding whose refusal means abandonment, worthlessness, or betrayal |
| **Role/title** | one identity among several; can be delegated or revised | person valued only while remaining death god, Queen, hero, sacred soldier, weapon |
| **Memory of the dead** | additive archive that supports living futures | imitation, revenge, or self-destruction required in order to prove remembrance |

This matrix gives T03 its core rule: **identity continuity does not require semantic immobility.** A person may keep the same name, pride, dead, promise, unit, role, or object while changing what that inheritance authorizes.

# 2. Volume-by-volume inheritance map

## V01 — pride and naming as resistance to administrative erasure

V01 supplies the positive historical baseline that every later critique must preserve. The Republic does not merely exploit the Eighty-Six; it removes them from the category of persons whose names, deaths, families and graves matter. Against that system, pride is a form of retained authorship. The earliest identity problem is therefore not that the Eighty-Six cling too strongly to who they are. It is that the state is trying to tell them they are nothing but livestock and machine components.

The naming problem is already explicit in V01-L011: `名前さえ、一回だって訊いたことがない`. Lena's failure to ask names is not solved by adopting a more respectful abstract label. She has to encounter particular people. That becomes important later because T03's memory thesis depends upon particularity: inheritance is transmitted through *this* dead person, *this* promise, *this* object, *this* relationship—not through generalized reverence for “the fallen.”

The positive V01 rule is therefore: **pride and memory can be technologies of person-preservation when an institution is actively erasing persons.** Any later account that calls Eighty-Six pride “toxic” without this historical baseline is methodologically invalid.

## V02 — the first enclosure problem: benevolence can freeze the survivor too

V02 shifts the problem from hostile dehumanization to benevolent misrecognition. Ernst recognizes the Five as human beings and wants them protected, but Frederica's `哀れみの檻` establishes that care can become a cage when it decides in advance what a recovered person's life ought to look like. This is the first major precursor to V09's curse language: enclosure can arise from pity as well as hatred.

The volume also gives the strongest early statement of singular personhood, `人は常に、その人唯一つの存在`, which prevents role inheritance from becoming a theory of interchangeable bearers. An institution may need another soldier to fill a post; that does not mean the dead or injured person has been replaced as a person.

Shin's `名前、聞かなければよかったな` creates the opposite temptation from the Republic. The Republic avoids grief by refusing personhood. Shin briefly imagines avoiding grief by reducing attachment. Frederica's response and the larger series reject that route. **Knowing creates vulnerability, but the ethical answer is not to become less capable of knowing.**

## V03 — `誇りしか`: exclusivity becomes the explicit problem

V03 is the first volume where the corpus itself strongly marks pride as insufficient. Its epigraph—`それが誇りだと...それ以外の何も`—places the qualification before the later confrontation even begins. `戦い抜く誇りしか` then gives T03 its most important small word: `しか`.

Kiriya is not a warning against pride as such. He is the endpoint of identity exhaustion: no home, no meaningful future, no living relation capable of redirecting him, and battle as the only remaining positive content. Shin's `もうこれしかないからだ` deliberately rhymes with that structure. The danger is not intense commitment. It is **the absence of any legitimate self outside the commitment**.

V03 also gives memory a living answer through the refusal to forget. The point is not to remain forever at the red field, but to carry the dead forward without making death the destination. This anticipates V05's additive-memory formulation.

## V04 — the wound cannot be healed by another person's preferred story

V04's harmed-person-agency problem is vital to T03 even though its pride vocabulary is less dense. Lena's desire to help Shin does not make her the sovereign interpreter of what recovery must look like. The volume therefore prevents the later “curse” framework from becoming therapeutic paternalism: an outside observer cannot simply label an identity trauma-derived and order the bearer to abandon it.

The Legion memory-destruction material supplies a dark formal parallel. Identity can literally be stripped until cognition becomes more fungible. Human recovery must therefore not imitate the enemy's optimization principle by treating inconvenient memory, grief or attachment as noise to be removed.

V04's contribution to T03 is a methodological limit: **change must remain the subject's change.** The fact that a role or memory originated in trauma gives analysts evidence about causation, not authority to declare the role inauthentic.

## V05 — additive memory and the right to remain where one freely chooses

V05 contains two of T03's most important controls. First, `忘れないまま...好きになってもいい` establishes that preserving one attachment does not require excluding later attachments. The dead need not be erased for the living to love again. This is the series' clearest early model of **additive inheritance**.

Second, `檻の扉は開いた` is followed by the recognition that remaining in place can itself be free. That matters because T03 cannot define liberation as visible departure. A person who once had no exit may freely choose the same role, relationship or location after exit becomes real. What changes is the authority structure around the choice.

The Sirin statement `未来などない。課された役目しかない` supplies the clean negative control. The problem is not merely having a role; it is a role whose assigned character exhausts the future. That becomes the template against which human roles—death god, Queen, hero, sacred warrior—can be tested without asserting they are all equally coercive.

## V06 — wounds remain; bonds can survive divergence

V06 explicitly calls the Republic's damage to Shin a `傷`. The important clause is not merely that the wound exists but that recognition of it does not authorize Lena to make it disappear. The mature relationship must permit the wound to remain historically true while Shin changes what it means for his life.

The volume also introduces a key anti-curse relational structure: `結んだ絆が途切れたわけでもなく` even when people no longer share the same destination. A bond can remain valid without requiring identical trajectories. This is the direct opposite of the later Milmelkoleo problem, where shared wound becomes the condition for continued belonging.

Shin's defense of Eighty-Six pride at the climax confirms the same point. He is not healed by surrendering the ethic that helped him survive. He becomes more capable of choosing what that ethic does and does not require.

## V07 — titles become plural identities or cages depending on how they are inhabited

V07 is the key role-identity volume. `英雄など今の世に、あるべきではない` does not mean courageous acts should disappear. It means a military should not require a permanent hero as its structural support. `英雄であり続けなければならぬ部隊` names the institutional curse: the unit must continuously reproduce the identity because everyone else has organized around it.

Lena's identity provides the personal version. Shin insists she is both `共和国人` and `エイティシックスの女王`; plurality is the correction. But Lena's `わたしにはこれしかないんです` reproduces the old Eighty-Six logic from a different moral direction. Guilt makes command into the only identity she feels entitled to possess.

Shiden's shift from `女王陛下` to `レーナ` is therefore more than intimacy. It demonstrates that a title can be real and valued without exhausting the person carrying it.

## V08 — the explicit revision of `誇りしか`

V08 gives Shin's clearest self-correction: `誇りしかないとはもう言わない`. The statement is analytically superior to any paraphrase such as “Shin leaves pride behind,” because it preserves the object and rejects only exclusivity.

Reverse Tower then states the positive alternative: `誇り以外の懊悩も感情も欲も願いも`. Human life becomes more plural rather than less committed. Pride remains one content among grief, desire, fear, love and ordinary want.

Theo's `その役目を僕が継ぐ` is a critical inheritance test. He voluntarily inherits a dangerous function from Shin. The text does not automatically praise this as noble transmission; subsequent injury exposes the cost. Inheritance may be chosen and still reproduce risk. Phase 6 should therefore distinguish *voluntary* from *wise* rather than equating them.

Theo later converts a dead commander's `赦さないでくれ` into `幸福にならねばならないという呪い`. This is not simple obedience. He changes the dead man's meaning into an obligation to live. **Reinterpretation is itself an act of inheritance.**

## V09 — the taxonomy becomes explicit

V09 is the conceptual center of T03 because its epigraph places `誇り`, `願い`, `絆`, `祈り`, and `呪い` in one sequence. These are not separate moral species. They are different ways meaning can bind a person to a past, another person, or a future.

Theo supplies the clearest distinction. A dead person's wish may support him as a `願い` while he has not yet found another direction; it becomes `呪い` if he remains `縛られた`. This is why T03 uses **foreclosure** rather than emotional intensity as the principal diagnostic.

Hielna, Svenja and Gilwiese expand the model from individual trauma to group culture. `奴隷の首輪` names a heroic identity whose social value depends on continuing to perform. `たった一つの御役目` and `同じ傷` show how a group can become most intimate around the thing that prevents its members from changing. `誇りという名の、傷という名の呪い` is therefore not an attack on solidarity; it is an attack on solidarity that cannot survive recovery.

Lena's `貴方たちは英雄なんかじゃない` further protects ordinary humanity from imposed symbolic identity. Hero is no longer a flattering description once it functions as destiny.

## V10 — genealogy overturns essence

V10 reveals how many separate inheritances constitute Undertaker. The role is built through promises, objects, mercy killing, name fragments, squad need, personal decisions and repeated deaths. This retrospective genealogy makes the finished role look less like Shin's hidden essence and more like a sedimented social office.

The phrase `我らが死神` is especially important because it contains both dependence and affection. Shin is burdened because others trust him to carry them; the role also gives him reciprocal support. T03 therefore rejects any model in which a trauma-derived role is automatically unreal or purely harmful.

The Gentle World is the decisive counterfactual: `傷なんて、負わなくてよかった` and `おれは死神でいなくてよかった`. The actual relationships created after suffering remain meaningful, but the suffering is denied any claim of necessity. This is the dossier's strongest anti-redemptive-suffering control.

## V11 — refusing revenge without falsifying grievance

V11 moves inheritance into revenge and public memory. The dead Eighty-Six Shepherds embody a possible inheritance in which grievance becomes terminal purpose. The living Eighty-Six repeatedly decline that path without declaring the grievance false or forgiving the Republic.

This matters to T03 because “let go of hatred” would be an analytically poor description. Rito may still not forgive. Shin can understand the dead. The change lies in refusing to let the injury monopolize future action.

Richard's `墓参りなんて、絶対にされたくない` is another valuable counterexample to compulsory memorialization. The dead do not automatically acquire the right to govern how survivors remember them. Refusal of memorial ritual can itself be an ethical inheritance when it prevents the dead from becoming beautified symbols that recruit the living into repetition.

## V12 — inherited roles survive by becoming less grandiose

V12's `一人きりじゃ戦えない、弱い死神だから` is a model of role revision. Shin does not discard `死神`. He changes the content: from singular carrier of everyone to one combatant whose effectiveness depends on other people.

The institutional parallel is `英雄など連邦軍には必要ない`. A good organization can value extraordinary action while refusing the permanent identity structure of hero dependence.

Frederica's movement toward becoming `わらわ自身の王` belongs to the same family. Royal inheritance is not simply erased; its grammar is turned inward toward self-government. The mature question becomes: what can be retained from an inherited identity once the inherited claim to rule others is refused?

## V13 — chosen curse and weaponized attachment

V13 provides the strongest challenge to any freedom theory that equates liberation with lack of bonds. Chitri knowingly tells Yuto `あなたの呪いになるね`. He knowingly accepts the ribbon. The narration then states that words, wishes, prayers and feelings can all become curses because they redirect a person's path, but adds that a curse accepted anyway may be called love.

The passage preserves constraint. It does not say love is nonbinding. It says some binding is chosen as part of a valued relationship. This makes **consent, continued revisability, and right of exit** central variables rather than pretending healthy attachment has no force.

The same volume supplies the political inversion: Lena realizes her Queen relationship can make her a hostage used to force Eighty-Six obedience. A voluntarily meaningful bond becomes a coercive instrument when a third party controls the consequences of refusing. Thus the ethical character of inheritance depends not only on the original relationship but on the institutions surrounding it.

## V14 — wounds can recur while the person still chooses the future

V14 is the current frontier and provides the mature corrective to several therapeutic overclaims. Shin's history with Rei resurfaces violently. He interprets the recurrence as proof that he never truly forgave. Lena counters that renewed pain may simply mean the original injury mattered: `傷を抉られたら、痛いのは当然です`.

This makes V14 crucial for T03. A persistent wound is not necessarily a failed recovery, hidden moral defect, or secret desire to return to the old identity. The wound can remain while the person has genuinely changed.

Shin's line about comrades who chose the future over past wounds makes the positive model explicit. `過去の傷よりも未来を選んだ` is not historical amnesia. It is priority-setting under continued memory.

The memory of Rito likewise becomes future-facing: if another witness dies, one source of memories disappears, while Rito can no longer generate new memories himself. The living therefore preserve the dead not by freezing them at death but by continuing to create contexts in which their remembered lives mean something.

Finally, V14's reactionary movement offers the polity-level negative control. Societies can also become trapped by inherited identities, turning an imperial past or a self-image of justice into the only legitimate political future. T03 therefore routes institutional inheritance to Documents 05, 06 and 14 rather than treating “curse” as only psychological vocabulary.

## Alter.1 — low-pressure life proves the inherited warrior identity was never exhaustive

Alter.1 is supplemental rather than a mainline chronological step, but it is important evidence against retrospectively imagining the Eighty-Six as nothing except warriors even during the Sector years. Food, birthdays, teasing, objects, leisure, small gifts, jokes and curiosity coexist with pride and military identity before rescue.

That does not make the Sector healthy. It demonstrates that the later plural-self thesis does not require inventing a wholly new person after Giad. The ordinary capacities were already present in compressed, precarious forms. Rescue expands their material and temporal space.

For T03, Alter.1 therefore supplies an important falsifier: **`誇りしか` is a consciously articulated survival identity, not a literal empirical claim that the characters possessed no other traits, pleasures, relationships or desires.** The later development is partly a shift in what can safely count as identity, not the creation of human plurality from nothing.

The supplement's own title/afterword grammar reinforces this: `時々というかむしろしょっちゅう青春してる死神と鮮血女王` (A1-L005) places “Death God” and “Bloody Queen” beside frequent ordinary youth rather than treating the role names as exhaustive. The dream story preserves Shin's `兄には、言いたいことが沢山あったのに` (A1-L043) as memory evidence while explicitly refusing literal dead testimony. Recovered family records (`家族の名前とか元々住んでいた場所は、ある程度確認できてる`, A1-L054) demonstrate that archival inheritance can be recovered without automatically becoming present home. The recycled V6 plea `おいていかないでください` (A1-L070) becomes embarrassing social memory among living adults: even intensely vulnerable language can survive into later ordinary life without remaining frozen in its original crisis function.


# 3. Pride: from anti-dehumanization to `誇りしか`

V01 establishes the positive baseline. Sector pride is not adolescent macho posturing added to an otherwise healthy life. It is one of the few domains in which people administratively classified as nonhuman can still decide what kind of persons they will be. This is why later criticism of pride must never be projected backward into a claim that the Eighty-Six were wrong to possess it.

V03 then introduces the decisive lexical failure mode: {cite('V03-L001')}, followed by {cite('V03-L012')}. The danger is encoded by `しか`: not pride itself, but **pride as all that remains**. Kiriya is the externalized endpoint: {cite('V03-L016')}. Shin's parallel formulation appears in {cite('V03-L034')}.

V06 does not ask Shin to abandon pride. He actively defends it: {cite('V06-L053')}. The correction is authorship: outsiders do not get to decide what his pride means or where it must lead.

V08 provides the cleanest self-revision: {cite('V08-L033')}. The sentence does not say `誇りは間違いだった`. It says he will no longer say **there is only pride**. Reverse Tower then makes the additive structure explicit: {cite('V08-L046')}.

V09 closes the pride-and-wounds movement by separating pride from destiny: {cite('V09-L041')}. V14 proves the corpus has not abandoned pride at all: {cite('V14-L023')}. Pride remains a chosen ethical identity after exclusivity is rejected.

**Retrieval disposition:** `PRIDE_IS_DIGNITY_AND_POSSIBLE_PRISON` -> **STRONGLY STRENGTHENED / SPECIFIED**. The prison is `誇りしか`, not `誇り`.

# 4. Wounds: neither destiny nor evidence that recovery failed

V06 is the first mature warning against cure-as-erasure. Lena recognizes Shin's wound as something the Republic genuinely did to him: {cite('V06-L019')}. The ethical error would be to assume that loving him gives her authority to remove, reinterpret, or declare obsolete every part of that history.

V09 makes shared wound a social structure. Gilwiese's group possesses `たった一つの御役目` and `同じ傷`; the narration names the result {cite('V09-L053')}. The problem is not that their pain is fake. It is that common injury becomes the condition under which each member is recognizable to the others. Recovery begins to resemble desertion.

V10 supplies the strongest anti-theodicy control: {cite('V10-L059')}. The peaceful counterfactual does not say suffering was secretly necessary to create meaningful people. The characters and relationships formed afterward can be real without converting the wound into a good.

V14 then dismantles another therapeutic shortcut. Shin thinks renewed pain proves he failed to forgive. Lena answers {cite('V14-L195')}. Recurrence of pain is not proof that the person morally failed, remained secretly unchanged, or must return to the old interpretation. Wound persistence and identity enclosure are different phenomena.

V14 also states the positive alternative during the Legion confrontation: {cite('V14-L150')}. Choosing future over past wound does not mean the past ceased to hurt or ceased to be true.

**Retrieval disposition:** `HEALING_REQUIRES_ERASING_THE_WOUND` -> **OVERTURNED**. The mature model is historical continuity without compulsory reenactment.

# 5. Undertaker: inherited office rather than essential self

V10 is decisive because it reveals the genealogy of `Undertaker` after nine volumes of seeing the finished role. Shin accumulates the office from other people: Alice's promise, dead comrades' fragments, Iska's mercy role, squad dependence, and repeated acceptance. {cite('V10-L012')} captures explicit role inheritance; {cite('V10-L027')} shows the private name becoming communal office.

That office is neither purely imposed nor purely self-created. The squad genuinely relies upon Shin, and V09 later allows him to say that being relied upon was heavy but also supported him. This is precisely why “role = curse” is too crude.

The negative control is the Gentle World: {cite('V10-L060')}. Shin could have existed, mattered, loved, and been loved without ever becoming a death god. Undertaker is therefore historically constitutive without being metaphysically necessary.

V12's {cite('V12-L029')} demonstrates reinterpretation rather than abandonment. `弱い死神` retains the inherited name while stripping it of solitary grandeur. In V14, the organization can temporarily delegate the practical office while Shin is unavailable; role continuity becomes institutionally distributable.

**Retrieval disposition:** `UNDERTAKER_IS_SHINS_TRUE_ESSENCE` -> **OVERTURNED**. `UNDERTAKER_IS_A_REAL_INHERITED_IDENTITY_HE_CAN_REAUTHOR` -> **STRONGLY STRENGTHENED**.

# 6. Memory of the dead: archive, obligation, and the right not to imitate

The V01 baseline is the obscenity of not asking names: {cite('V01-L011')}. Memory begins as resistance to administrative erasure.

But V02 already shows the cost. Shin thinks {cite('V02-L034')}; knowing makes grief possible. The answer is not ignorance. The later corpus repeatedly chooses remembrance with pain over painless erasure.

V05 supplies one of the most important rules: {cite('V05-L019')}. Remembering one person does not prohibit loving another. Memory becomes **additive rather than exclusive**.

V11 refuses to make the dead automatically sacred. Richard says {cite('V11-L049')}; the living do not owe every dead person beautifying commemoration. Aldrecht and the revenge Shepherds likewise demonstrate that guilt or memory can become a demand to reproduce harm.

V14 gives memory a future-oriented justification: {cite('V14-L161')}. A living witness matters because memory can continue accumulating around the dead. Remembrance is not only preservation of a frozen final image; it is a living archive that changes through continued relation among survivors.

This matters to Chitri's request not to be viewed after weaponized death and to Yuto's accepted ribbon. Memory has ethics: what image is carried, who chooses it, and whether memory obliges imitation or simply continued recognition.

# 7. V09 taxonomy: pride, wish, bond, prayer, curse

V09 makes T03 possible because it supplies an explicit conceptual sequence: {cite('V09-L001')}.

The volume does **not** map these terms onto good/bad binaries. Theo says a dead commander's wish may be a `願い` for now and become a `呪い` only if he remains bound to it: {cite('V09-L012')}. This is the clearest support/scaffold criterion in the corpus.

Hielna and Milmelkoleo show the failure mode. A role may begin as proof, dignity, solidarity, or social survival and become a `奴隷の首輪`: {cite('V09-L051')}. The key is conditional belonging: if one member changes, heals, or leaves the role, the group experiences that departure as betrayal of the shared wound.

Hence the strongest T03 criterion:

> **An inheritance becomes curse-like when it claims exclusive authority over the bearer's future and makes reinterpretation, refusal, recovery, or alternative belonging morally illegible.**

# 8. Chitri and Yuto: chosen binding falsifies “freedom = no obligations”

V13 is the dossier's strongest negative control against an atomistic theory of freedom. Chitri says {cite('V13-L096')}. Yuto accepts the ribbon and the obligation knowingly. The narration then generalizes: {cite('V13-L113')}, followed immediately by {cite('V13-L114')}.

This is deliberately uncomfortable. The text does not say accepted bonds cease to constrain. It says some constraining obligations are knowingly accepted because relation itself matters.

Consent is therefore necessary but not magically sufficient. A freely accepted obligation can still become unhealthy later; people can change; power differences matter. But chosen binding is ethically distinct from a Fawn's assigned function, a Holy Theocracy soldier's inherited caste, Legion purpose-capture, or political hostage-taking.

T03 therefore adds three diagnostic questions for Phase 6:

1. **Who authored or accepted the binding?**
2. **Can its meaning be revised as circumstances change?**
3. **Does refusal/change terminate belonging or personhood?**

# 9. Titles and roles: Queen, hero, death god

The corpus repeatedly preserves roles while attacking their totalization.

Lena is both Republic citizen and Eighty-Six Queen: {cite('V07-L013')}. That is plural identity, not purification. But V07 also shows her guilt narrowing into {cite('V07-L024')}. The same title that can express trust can become enclosure when Lena imagines command as the only form in which she has a right to exist.

Likewise, `英雄` oscillates. V07 says {cite('V07-L002')} and warns against a unit that must remain heroic {cite('V07-L014')}. V09 tells the Eighty-Six {cite('V09-L040')}. V12 goes institutional: {cite('V12-L054')}.

Yet the story still admires courage and extraordinary skill. The target is not heroism as act; it is **hero as compulsory identity and organizational dependency**.

Roles therefore require a dual audit:
- what good does the role name or organize?
- what happens to the person if they cannot or do not want to perform it?

# 10. Inherited identity and institutional capture

Not every inheritance is interpersonal. Institutions inherit myths, political names and legitimating stories.

San Magnolia's `Saint Magnolia` and `Bloody Queen` language demonstrates that history can convert persons into usable symbols. The Holy Theocracy converts inherited military function into sacred destiny. V13 turns Lena's Queen identity into potential hostage infrastructure: {cite('V13-L119')}.

V14's reactionary politics intensify this at the polity level: historical empire becomes an inheritance offered as a cure for democratic crisis. T01 already established reactionary return as a negative control; T03 adds that **inheritance can capture institutions exactly as wounds capture individuals** when a remembered past becomes the only legitimate template for future identity.

This is why V14's `正義たらん -> 正義である` shift belongs downstream in political synthesis: identity claims become dangerous when they immunize inherited meaning from revision.

# 11. Counterevidence and prohibited overclaims

T03 preserves the following falsifiers:

- **Not all strong identity is enclosure.** Shin remains proudly Eighty-Six in V14.
- **Not all binding is coercive.** Chitri/Yuto deliberately accepts binding memory.
- **Not all inherited roles should be abandoned.** Shin can retain Undertaker in revised form; Theo can choose an adapted military role.
- **Not all wounds disappear with recovery.** V14 explicitly separates pain from failed forgiveness.
- **Not all refusal of forgetting is healthy.** Revenge Shepherds and Aldrecht show memory can monopolize action.
- **Not all leaving is growth.** Abandonment can itself reproduce harm; the right to leave must coexist with responsibility for promises and dependents.
- **Not all continuity with the dead is imitation.** Name fragments, stories, remembered tastes, photographs, graves and objects may support future life without dictating it.
- **Consent does not make every obligation permanently healthy.** Accepted bindings remain revisable because the accepter can change.

# 15. Final retrieval conclusion

T03's mature result is not that identity must be fluid and never binding. *86* repeatedly values persistence: pride, promises, names, the dead, unit membership, love, vocation and chosen responsibility. Its more exact concern is whether those inheritances can coexist with **additional identity, changed interpretation, refusal, recovery, and continued belonging**.

The strongest formulation at the V14 frontier is therefore:

> **A meaningful inheritance becomes dangerous when preserving it requires preserving the wound in the same form. A livable identity can remember, retain, and even deliberately bind itself to the past while still allowing the bearer to add other desires, reinterpret what the inheritance asks, refuse destructive reenactment, and remain recognizably themselves after change.**

That is why `誇りしか` is the problem while `誇り` survives; why Undertaker can remain after Shin learns he need not be a death god; why Theo can carry a dead commander's wish without making it permanent destiny; why Chitri can call herself a curse without making Yuto's acceptance equivalent to coercion; and why V14 can say that an old wound still hurts while the wounded person nevertheless chooses the future.
