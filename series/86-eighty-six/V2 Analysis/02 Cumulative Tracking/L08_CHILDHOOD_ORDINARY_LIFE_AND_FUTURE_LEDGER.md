## V01 correction to legacy ordinary-life thesis

Ordinary life is **not introduced by rescue**. It already exists as an improvised practice of personhood inside exterminatory conditions. Later safety should be analyzed as changing its **scope, reliability, and option space**.

# Volume 07 update

- V7 makes ordinary life a **capabilities problem**. Vika's `できないよりもできた方が、楽しめるものは多い` is the cleanest statement: social/dance/ordinary skills matter because having more capacities increases the set of experiences one can choose, not because every survivor must assimilate into a single civilian norm.
- The Strike Package's month of school and leave demonstrates that ordinary competence requires practice after years organized around combat. Rest is not simply absence of battle; environment and habit matter.
- Shin explicitly includes himself among those enjoying the leave. This is stronger than tolerating civilian life for Lena's sake.
- His future is now plural: school, comrades, grandparents, leisure, social participation, self-respect, possible postwar travel, and Lena. Lena remains the first explicitly long-duration relational future, but she is no longer the only positive content visible in his life.
- Fireworks/festival language makes futurity repeatable: `next time` and `again` replace the old logic in which beautiful experiences were exceptional interruptions before expected death.
- Ballroom participation reinforces that skills denied by childhood war can still be learned without pretending the characters were never soldiers.
- Olivia's `十代のまだ子供` framing keeps competence separate from completed adulthood.

# Alter.1 chronological supplemental update — ordinary life was already there

## Pre-rescue control sample

- The Sector contains birthdays, food improvisation, jokes, mending, music, folklore, cats, hanami recollection and small celebrations. `常闇のヘブンリー・ブルー` explicitly says the Eighty-Six are not fighting twenty-four hours a day and are `素直で貪欲` about small ordinary events.
- Therefore **ordinary life is not a Federacy invention**. Rescue changes stability, resources and expected repetition.

## Birthdays as temporal technology

The birthday sequence is now one of the strongest longitudinal future measures:

1. birthdays become useless/forgotten under Sector conditions;
2. survivors use them as excuses to make the living laugh;
3. Republic personnel archives restore lost birth dates;
4. Lena proposes future celebrations;
5. the Eighty-Six adopt gift culture because it looks fun;
6. celebrations survive delay and war disruption;
7. Anju's V11 birthday becomes a flower-and-return promise;
8. Theo can participate socially after amputation.

> **A future is not merely believing one might survive. It can be a repeatable occasion whose meaning presupposes there will be another year.**

## Capability / adolescence controls

- Shin's library curiosity plus nonexistent public-transport habit shows internal agency and external infrastructure as separate dimensions.
- Frederica's first same-age table and multiple explicitly teenage comic scenes strengthen C009: extreme competence coexists with developmental youth.
- The collection's scrapbook/film-strip paratext emphasizes remembered youth as an ongoing life layer rather than a postwar reward.

**Alter.1 future correction:** Giad makes ordinary practices more reliable, diverse and projectable. It does not create the people capable of wanting them.

**V13 future model:** a usable future includes the ability to imagine more than one role **and** a material path by which one may return, refuse, rest, or leave. Recognition and aspiration are not enough when geography/institutions close every exit.

# Volume 14 childhood / ordinary life / future update

1. **The unit studies because the war will end.** `こんな時だからこそ。──戦争が終わる日のために` explicitly makes education a practiced capacity for `幸せに、平和に生きる日` rather than postwar decoration (V14-L019-V14-L021).
2. **Christmas and play persist under siege.** Children remain children inside crisis; `楽しまないのは損だ` treats enjoyment as a legitimate present good, not merely therapy (V14-L013-V14-L018).
3. **`笑えなくなったら負け` returns as survival doctrine.** Laughter protects ordinary humanity without denying danger and links current Spearhead to Kujo-era Sector practice (V14-L017-V14-L018).
4. **Morale goods materially sustain future-bearing persons.** Coffee, sweets, magazines, clean living space and sensory rituals become part of operational sustainment (V14-L009-V14-L012).
5. **Shin's sensory loss complicates ordinary life.** The future has expanded, but eating itself is becoming less directly pleasurable; Theo's accompaniment prevents the loss from becoming isolation (V14-L055-V14-L061).
6. **Raiden independently imagines return.** His dandelion-coffee thought toward the Alba woman who once sheltered him gives him a future/home relation outside Spearhead and Shin (V14-L162).
7. **Postwar recipe exchange makes ordinary reciprocity imaginable.** A Federacy soldier imagines returning hospitality through food after the war (V14-L185-V14-L190).

**V14 future model:** future capacity is practiced through **education, repeated pleasure, recoverable social ritual, sensory accommodation, planned return, and the expectation that today's strangers may someday share a table**.
