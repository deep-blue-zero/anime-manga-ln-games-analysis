## V01 distinction

Memory is not automatically liberating.

- Shin's name-carrying protects dead persons from erasure.
- Rei's unresolved relation also constrains Shin's movement.

The future ledger must therefore distinguish **memory as recognition**, **memory as responsibility**, and **memory as enclosure** rather than assigning one moral value to remembrance.

# Volume 11 update — revenge, mourning, release, and return

- **Revenge:** dead Eighty-Six Shepherds preserve grievance as continuing purpose and generate new victims.
- **Nonforgiveness:** Rito explicitly may never forgive but relinquishes personal punishment as life project.
- **Release:** `だから──もういいや` marks withdrawal of attention/psychic occupation, not historical denial.
- **Distributed memory:** Rito, not Shin, ends Aldrecht; Undertaker no longer monopolizes the dead.
- **Aldrecht:** self-condemnation → revenge → Legion capture shows guilt can prevent the reunion with dead loved ones it claims to honor.
- **Mourning:** Lena accepts Republic collapse is not her sin while still crying for homeland/dead; Shin legitimizes grief as grief.
- **Return:** San Magnolia becomes future memorial destination rather than restored sole home.
- **Vaclav:** final coda opens a new dead-family continuity problem without resolving mechanism.

**V11 memory principle:** carrying, forgiving, avenging, forgetting, releasing, mourning, and returning are separate moral acts and must not be used as synonyms.

# Volume 12 update — inherited authority that expires versus authority that colonizes

- the stranded company commander's final instructions protect soldiers after death but are designed to end when rescue arrives: inheritance as **temporary scaffold**.
- Hail Mary converts local aristocratic memory and dead comrades into proof that Noelle must remain savior: inheritance as closed identity.
- Ishmael survives in order to preserve Fleet Countries memory and restrain future political misuse of his people; living remembrance becomes representative duty.
- Frederica chooses to witness failures she cannot prevent so later power may be better informed; memory becomes preparation rather than self-punishment.
- Zelene postpones death to witness/assist the living struggle, further separating “dead-derived existence” from compulsory deathward orientation.

**V12 inheritance principle:** the ethical value of inherited commands/bonds depends partly on whether they leave an exit, permit correction, and serve the living rather than demanding permanent repetition.

# Alter.1 chronological supplemental update — archive without compulsory restoration

- Twelve-year-old Shin's winter piece links Rei's burial world to a child who assumes no one waits for or desires his return.
- Kaie's birthday/hanami memory and later personnel-record recovery turn lost dates and names into reconstructable biography.
- The recovered archive includes family names, addresses and birthdays, but Shin's old home remains `覚えてない知らない家`: recovered data does not dictate belonging.
- Shin's empty photo frame becomes an object that can be filled with what comes **after**, not only reconstructed past.
- Anju's post-V11 birthday flowers are prepared for her return; memorial flower language is redirected from grave/death toward repeated future presence.
- Dream dialogue with Rei is excluded from literal posthumous evidence.

**Alter.1 memory principle:** the archive can restore optional knowledge without requiring restoration of identity, home or relationship. Memory becomes future-compatible when recovered fragments do not claim veto power over what comes next.

# Volume 13 death/memory/grief update

- **Chitri chooses the form of her inheritance.** She gives Yuto a ribbon, asks to become his `curse`, and asks him not to witness the explosive aftermath so that the remembered person is not replaced by the weapon image (V13-L096-V13-L098).
- **Yuto deliberately accepts memory rather than remaining invulnerably detached.** His keeping of the ribbon makes burden chosen, not compulsory (V13-L112-V13-L114).
- **Rito redirects the Aldrecht lesson.** He refuses to let another Eighty-Six turn revenge into posthumous purpose, then dies while performing mercy care (V13-L104-V13-L107).
- **Revenge Shepherds show memory untethered from original target.** `our turn` continues against Federacy people uninvolved in original persecution (V13-L077).
- **Dustin corrects symbolic memory.** Chitri had been remembered as Republic tragedy/salvation object rather than ordinary childhood friend; her mundane wish destabilizes that memorial use (V13-L039).

**V13 correction:** memory is ethically variable not by whether it binds, but by whether the living can remain responsive to the remembered person's particularity and whether inherited grievance can acquire limits.

# Volume 14 death / memory / grief / inheritance update

- **Rei-memory is finally separated from anesthesia.** Shin can love and forgive Rei and still remain physiologically/emotionally vulnerable; `痛かった` names an injury that interpretation did not erase (V14-L192-V14-L199).
- **Lyudmila turns memory into a reason to remain.** Rito's death does not command her to follow him; fear of losing one more bearer of his memory creates an emergent survival desire (V14-L113-V14-L118).
- **Raiden's Rito grief moves toward accountability without collective revenge.** Shin insists on individual legal/public condemnation while refusing to turn one killer into the Federacy's essence (V14-L043-V14-L051).
- **War history becomes future infrastructure.** The archive's moral relation to the dead is to build plans that allow future people to live (V14-L002-V14-L003).
- **Recipes carry memory without monumentalizing it.** Eighty-Six stories and local recipes let civilians encounter remembered soldiers as people who laughed, cooked badly, had preferences and imagined reciprocal meals (V14-L124-V14-L131, V14-L185-V14-L190).
- **No Face represents memory captured by a non-revisable end.** The preservation of memory/personality is not enough when grievance/program dictates the available future (V14-L139-V14-L151).

**V14 correction:** remembrance is healthiest when it can generate **new relation, new desire, new practice, or future protection** rather than demand replication of the dead person's endpoint.
