# L06 — Body, Injury, Disability, and Medical Ledger

Volume 1 contains no mature disability arc yet, but it establishes the body's political role.

| Figure / body | V01 evidence | Political / identity significance | Later watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anju** | scars from prejudice among Colorata/Eighty-Six | oppression is carried materially and is not reducible to Alba/Colorata binary | body image, intimacy, whether scars remain narratively active |
| **Shin** | constant exposure to Legion voices; fatigue; previous Handler driven to suicide after extreme synchronization | exceptional ability has a sensory/neurological cost; Para-RAID can transmit trauma | cumulative sensory damage, self-neglect, medical care |
| **All Processors** | adolescent bodies placed in underprotected Juggernauts | “unmanned” classification literally hides embodied risk | compare Reginleif/protection changes |
| **Dying Processors** | mercy killing after catastrophic wounds | body imposes battlefield ethical decisions not solved by abstract rules | later medical/rescue infrastructure |
| **Republic/Alba protected bodies** | Handler officers remain physically removed from battlefield | political privilege is spatial/embodied | how later command reduces/increases shared risk |
| **Material deprivation** | poor rations, scavenged equipment, lack of graves/medical security | bodily maintenance itself is institutionally rationed by status | later nutrition, treatment, rehabilitation |
| **Rei** | dead cognition exists without living body | embodiment becomes central to temporal-development question | later Legion/Sirin/personhood comparison |

## V01 caution

Do not use future disability themes to make every V01 injury symbolic. First record **what happens to bodies, who receives care, who is protected, and who is expected to absorb risk**.

## V02 body principle

Institutions survive in bodies after laws change. The Five carry Republic history as **reflex, scar, tolerance, sensory burden and tactical habit**. Recovery must therefore be more than formal emancipation.

# Volume 07 update

- V7's resort/formal-social setting makes bodies visible outside combat: scars, swimsuits, bathing, clothing, dance and attraction all detach embodiment from purely military injury/function.
- **Anju:** pursues treatment for the scars on her back. This directly supports the principle that altering a wound's bodily trace need not erase its history or betray the dead.
- **Shin:** formalwear and dance place a body trained for combat into an unfamiliar social skill domain. Competence is learned rather than assumed; bodily adaptation becomes ordinary-life capability rather than battlefield optimization.
- **Lena:** jealousy, attraction and the kiss make embodied desire an explicit part of her subjectivity rather than only command/mourning identity.
- **Adolescence:** Olivia's observation that the famous heroes are still `十代のまだ子供` remains an important corrective to competence-based premature adulthood.
- **Care:** scheduled leave and attempts to remove sensory/operational load treat accumulated strain as an institutional medical/readiness concern even when the soldiers themselves minimize it.

# Volume 10 body / injury / medical update

- **Eleven-year-old body:** cover and prose emphasize child scale against oversized uniform, Juggernaut, and Löwe.
- **Human factors:** Juggernaut cockpit ignores ergonomics, heat, vibration, and protection because the operator is administratively nonhuman.
- **Neck trauma:** Alice uses name/present focus and scarf; Glenn changes posture. Environmental accommodation precedes any “healing.”
- **Medical abandonment:** injuries requiring time are labeled unsalvageable even when treatable; mercy killing follows.
- **Scarf:** bodily concealment can protect from unwanted scrutiny while also enabling avoidance; later memorial use does not erase first function.
- **Child caregivers:** Alice/Iska/other adolescents perform triage and command, showing uneven development rather than completed adulthood.
- **Theo retrospective relevance:** V09's `でも、死ななくていい` depends on an institution that rejects precisely this V10 medical rule.

## V10 disability principle

A body does not become “unsalvageable” through injury alone. Institutional willingness to provide time, treatment, adaptation, and noncombat standing materially determines whether injury becomes a death sentence.

# Volume 11 update — bodies as limits, not verdicts

- Theo remains visibly amputated and outside former Reginleif combat function, yet exercises bureaucratic/civic protective agency by evacuating another child.
- Giad schedules food, rest, fatigue medication, rotation and extraction for Processors during sustained operations.
- Republic refugee marching failure is materially bodily: age, footwear, conditioning, terrain and exhaustion matter. Moral agency does not abolish physiological limits.
- The final evacuation adapts by sending trucks once rail fails; substantive survival requires infrastructure matched to bodies.
- Richard's rearguard dies in counted professional service rather than because injured bodies are discarded as valueless.

**V11 disability correction:** loss of a specific military function can be permanent while social, relational and civic agency remain open; accommodation and logistics are part of personhood-preserving institutions.

# Volume 12 update — bodies as limits on heroic will

- Lena: inadequate eating/sleep after V11 leads to formal one-month removal from duty; institution acts before role performance becomes the only criterion of worth.
- Kurena: nightmares emerge after safe return, demonstrating delayed distress despite intact combat performance.
- Multiple Eighty-Six receive temporary rest after counseling; habituation to death is not immunity.
- Shin: sensory overload causes `強制的な眠り`; Grethe orders him to report strain because hidden overload burdens subordinates.
- abused children: implanted devices and conditioned bodily fear show personhood violation through the body as well as law/status.
- radiation kills Hail Mary members regardless of ideological certainty; the body imposes nonnegotiable physical reality on magical-solution thinking.

**V12 body principle:** bodies can veto roles and intentions. Humane institutions preserve agency partly by treating recovery and reporting as duties rather than failures.

# Alter.1 chronological supplemental update — adolescent bodies and recoverable limits

- Early Sector/Giad shorts repeatedly foreground hunger, cooking, growth spurts and bodily adolescence. Military competence did not suspend developmental bodies.
- Lena's overwork appears before V12's formal removal: several-day sleep deprivation is already visible to peers, strengthening the longitudinal case that later medical leave answers a recurring pattern rather than a sudden one.
- Exercise/training scenes explicitly include `無理はしないでください`; bodily care is relationally negotiated rather than proof that Lena's appearance concerns are valid.
- Post-Morpho exhaustion and adult desire to make the children's exceptional combat unnecessary reinforce recovery as institutional responsibility.
- Theo's post-amputation participation in Annette's birthday task supplies further mundane evidence of social agency after loss of frontline function.

**Alter.1 body correction:** ordinary bodily need—sleep, food, growth, recuperation, disability accommodation—runs continuously beneath heroic roles; health is not inferred from combat functionality.

# Volume 13 body/injury/disability update

1. **Fawn bodies become engineered infrastructure.** Synthetic-cell nitroglycerin makes the body itself the munition; adolescent girls are selected through gendered biological and combat-utility assumptions (V13-L015-V13-L025).
2. **Chitri's deterioration remains materially bodily.** The analysis must not aestheticize her death into transcendence; her explicit repeated desire is not to die (V13-L090-V13-L092).
3. **Disabled/wounded personnel become scapegoating targets.** Rear-area soldiers are accused of escaping duty precisely because disability removes them from combat; V13 thus shows social recognition of injury can become resentment rather than care.
4. **Anju's moral injury is distinct from physical incapacity.** She can fight but recoils from retaliatory human killing; staff validates this ethical friction rather than treating it as combat uselessness (V13-L075-V13-L076).
5. **Rito's death is not a combat-performance failure.** A friendly soldier's misinterpretation kills him after a mercy act, emphasizing that bodies are vulnerable to institutional/social recognition failures as well as enemy fire (V13-L104-V13-L111).

**Continuity:** Theo's V8-V12 disability test remains separate. V13 broadens the bodily ledger from `can this injured person still belong?` to `what happens when societies convert bodily difference, injury, sex, or vulnerability into grounds for disposal?`

# Volume 14 body / injury / disability / medical update

1. **Shin's taste and smell loss is directly established.** Taste had been dulled since the Sector; by V14 he reports both taste and smell absent after the October offensive (V14-L055-V14-L057).
2. **He conceals the disability to avoid rear removal.** This strongly confirms that bodily self-protection lags behind psychological futurity (V14-L058-V14-L059).
3. **Theo provides accommodation rather than cure.** He offers to eat with Shin and describe taste first, while respecting Shin's refusal to make every meal a diagnostic test (V14-L060-V14-L061).
4. **Theo's own disability is integrated through role adaptation.** He returns with an adapted heavy engineering/combat platform rather than restoration of his former Processor body-function (V14-L023-V14-L029, V14-L109).
5. **Vaneseed turns trauma physiology/cognition into battlefield vulnerability.** Vika explicitly connects treatment/overcoming history to susceptibility and notes that Federacy soldiers after repeated losses can also be affected (V14-L093-V14-L102).
6. **Lena's maximum Para-RAID activation complicates her self-care development.** She knowingly risks neural injury to retrieve Shin while Annette attempts to stop her (V14-L153-V14-L161).
7. **Fear is not treated as bodily or moral defect.** Anju and others continue functioning while afraid; Grethe praises fighting the fear rather than not having it (V14-L111).

**V14 disability correction:** the text increasingly treats disability through **material limitation + accommodation + role redesign + continued standing**, while refusing both cure fantasy and the idea that usefulness is irrelevant. The ethical issue is whether function remains plural and negotiable rather than constitutive of worth.
