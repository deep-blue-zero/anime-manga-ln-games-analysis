# 『86―エイティシックス―』V2
# Phase 1 — Legacy Analysis / V1 Audit
## What the first analysis saw, where it compressed, and what the V2 reread must prove again

## 0. Purpose and governing rule

This document audits the previous *86* project before the V2 sequential reread begins.

It does **not** ask whether the prior analysis was “right” in a binary sense. Its purpose is to recover the intellectual work already performed while preventing inherited formulations from becoming invisible assumptions. Every legacy thesis is therefore treated as one of four things:

1. a strong hypothesis worth trying to falsify;
2. a useful but overcompressed hypothesis requiring narrower formulation;
3. a high-risk interpretation requiring adversarial testing;
4. a source-lineage problem that must be rebuilt from primary evidence.

The governing rule for Phase 2 is:

> **The old analysis may tell us where to look. It may not tell us what we are required to find.**

The Japanese V1–14 corpus locked in Phase 0 governs fact, language, chronology, and literary interpretation. `Alter.1` and the canonical-status-reviewed web short stories supplement that corpus. `Alter.2` and explicit `貴族if` material remain segregated counterfactual evidence. The previous transcript and full-series synthesis are never terminal evidence.

This distinction matters because the legacy work is conceptually strong. Its danger is therefore not obvious error so much as **premature stability**: elegant formulations can become hard to see around.

# 1. Legacy-source lineage and reliability

## 1.1 What the legacy corpus actually contains

The previous project proceeded in roughly four layers:

- an initial proposal for what *86* might contribute to the wider project;
- sequential volume analyses, originally V1–12 from Japanese EPUBs and later extended to V13 and V14;
- a full-series synthesis through Volume 12;
- follow-up thematic and linguistic questions, including Japanese character voice and cross-series comparisons.

The exported full transcript is useful but not archival-perfect. Its own metadata states that the selected branch was exported, that the top was reached, but that the exporter could not confirm turn 0 and did not confirm the bottom boundary. Therefore:

> **absence from the transcript is not evidence that an issue was never discussed.**

The transcript is a recovery instrument, not a complete legal record of the old conversation.

## 1.2 Language lineage

The strongest source-lineage distinction is:

- **V1–12:** old volume analyses were based on supplied Japanese EPUBs.
- **V13:** old analysis was also based on Japanese prose.
- **V14:** the old supplied EPUB falsely declared Japanese metadata while its narrative body was Simplified Chinese.

The previous V14 analysis noticed this and explicitly refrained from treating Chinese prose as evidence for Japanese wording, dialect, register, or sentence endings. That caution was correct. However, the old V14 thematic and characterization analysis still depended on a translated narrative body whose fidelity was not independently established.

The V2 corpus now contains a verified Japanese V14. Therefore:

> **Every substantive V14 legacy claim is reopened.**
>
> Plot-level claims are `REVALIDATE_FROM_JAPANESE`; Japanese-language claims are `SOURCE_LINEAGE_REDO`.

Nothing from the old V14 analysis is grandfathered into V2 merely because it is plausible.

## 1.3 Derived synthesis versus source evidence

`Deep Dive on 86 Novel.txt` is a valuable condensation of the V1–12 project. It is **derived analysis**, not a replacement for the novels. It may be cited internally as the origin of a legacy hypothesis, but a final V2 claim must terminate in:

> Japanese source → locator → evidence classification → volume analysis → specialist synthesis.

# 2. Executive judgment on the V1 analytical model

The prior analysis was substantially better than a superficial fandom reading. It already avoided several common mistakes:

- it did not reduce *86* to “racism bad”;
- it did not equate the Federacy with San Magnolia merely because both can instrumentalize soldiers;
- it did not treat Eighty-Six pride as either purely noble or purely pathological;
- it did not treat Shin as emotionally empty;
- it did not treat Lena as an uncomplicated savior;
- it did not treat romance as a cure;
- it did not assume artificial origin settles personhood;
- it did not read anti-war commitments as simple pacifism;
- it repeatedly noticed the difference between legal recognition and usable freedom;
- it noticed that ordinary life is not filler but part of the series’ positive moral content.

Those are significant strengths.

The V1 synthesis’s strongest durable proposition is:

> **The series progressively moves from demanding recognition of persons toward asking what material, institutional, relational, developmental, and temporal conditions allow recognized persons to keep changing.**

That is exactly the kind of thesis V2 should try to break rather than merely restate.

The principal weaknesses are methodological:

- locators were sparse;
- evidence classes were not explicit;
- retrospective conclusions sometimes bled backward into prospective volume readings;
- psychological interpretations were sometimes written with more certainty than the textual evidence warrants;
- military judgments sometimes mixed tactical, operational, strategic, and political levels;
- metaphor and literal mechanics sometimes merged, especially around Legion personhood and temporality;
- ordinary-life evidence was undersampled because `Alter.1` and the web-SS corpus were absent;
- secondary characters were compressed into thematic functions;
- Japanese-voice observations were perceptive but not systematically indexed;
- illustrations, maps, and light-novel form were not treated as a longitudinal evidence layer;
- V14 was necessarily compromised by source language.

Thus Phase 1 does **not** conclude “V1 was wrong.” It concludes:

> **V1 is a sophisticated hypothesis generator whose strongest claims require a new evidentiary spine.**

# 3. The inherited master thesis

## 3.1 Legacy formulation

The V1 synthesis described a movement approximately as follows:

> administrative denial of humanity  
> → names and remembrance  
> → rescue that proves insufficient  
> → pride as dignity and enclosure  
> → bodies and military usefulness  
> → the dead governing the living  
> → institutions and usable freedom  
> → ordinary life, love, home, and future  
> → the right to remain capable of change.

It ultimately proposed that the most important distinction in *86* is not simply:

- human / machine;
- citizen / foreigner;
- hero / coward;
- ruler / subject;
- living / dead;

but between arrangements that permit a person to revise the self and arrangements that freeze the self into a function.

## 3.2 Phase 1 assessment

**Assessment: `RETEST_STRONG`.**

This is the best candidate for a V2 master thesis because it explains why apparently disparate arcs—San Magnolia, Giad, Sirins, the Fleet Countries, Theo’s injury, V12’s political paternalism, and V13’s collapse—can belong to one argument without collapsing into one metaphor.

But it has three risks.

### Risk A — excessive unification

A sufficiently elastic “capacity to change” thesis can explain almost anything after the fact. V2 must identify scenes where the series values:

- continuity;
- obligation;
- refusal to change;
- inherited identity;
- military discipline;
- or fixed principle

without treating those as mere immature stages.

### Risk B — modern liberal vocabulary imposed on the text

“Revisable life,” “self-authorship,” and “usable freedom” are analytic terms. They are useful only if we keep them distinct from Asato’s actual vocabulary and narrative framing.

### Risk C — romance becoming the privileged proof

Shin/Lena provides exceptionally vivid future-oriented evidence. V2 must show whether the same model holds for:

- Raiden;
- Theo;
- Anju;
- Kurena;
- Shiden;
- Rito;
- Dustin;
- Frederica;
- noncombatants;
- Sirins;
- and institutions.

If the thesis only works perfectly for the protagonists, it is narrower than V1 claimed.

# 5. Domain audit: Shin

## 5.1 Strong legacy findings

The old project correctly rejected “emotionless super-soldier Shin.” It consistently read Shin’s restraint as a **form of disciplined care** expressed materially through:

- remembering names;
- carrying remains;
- mercy killing;
- planning routes;
- protecting younger Processors;
- returning for others;
- accepting command burdens;
- and eventually making ordinary requests.

It also identified an important longitudinal distinction:

> **carrying the dead ≠ following the dead.**

That distinction organizes V1–6 especially well and remains one of the strongest hypotheses in the corpus.

The legacy reading also tracked a convincing shift in his language of time:

- carry / fight / kill / remember / follow;
- then show / see / return / wait / meet / live.

The Japanese-voice pass adds a particularly promising linguistic hypothesis: Shin’s speech remains plain and direct, but the **semantic object of that directness changes** from tactical observation to voluntary emotional disclosure.

## 5.2 High-risk legacy findings

### “His deepest wound is abandonment”

The old synthesis sometimes states this as though the text settles it. That is too strong for Phase 1.

Abandonment is a powerful explanatory hypothesis because Shin’s biography contains repeated loss and because explicit pleas not to be left appear later. But V2 must separate:

- narrator description;
- Shin’s self-understanding;
- other characters’ interpretations;
- recurring behavior;
- and our psychological model.

Possible alternatives or coequal structures include:

- survivor guilt;
- traumatic guilt around Rei;
- role obligation;
- fear of purposelessness;
- developmental arrest;
- habituated fatalism;
- attachment avoidance;
- sensory burden from the dead.

V2 may still conclude that abandonment is central. It must earn the hierarchy.

### “Combat dissociation”

The old analysis sometimes uses clinically adjacent language for Shin’s combat state. V2 should prefer textual description unless a clinical category is explicitly justified. The safe formulation is:

> Shin can narrow or suppress awareness of pain, fear, exhaustion, and relational context to maximize combat performance.

Whether “dissociation” is the best psychological term is a separate interpretive claim.

### “Death wish”

The legacy project was right to identify self-endangering fatalism, but V2 must track its degrees. Shin can simultaneously:

- not seek immediate suicide;
- accept death too readily;
- privately assign himself sacrificial roles;
- and later develop explicit fear of dying.

The term should not flatten those states.

## 5.3 Phase 2 Shin questions

1. At what exact volume/scene does survival stop being merely tolerated and become desired?
2. How often does Shin distinguish duty from desire himself?
3. What does he voluntarily disclose without prompting?
4. Which relationships other than Lena materially alter his behavior?
5. When does he delegate?
6. When does he permit others to carry memory or responsibility?
7. Does his bodily self-neglect persist after his explicit future orientation changes?
8. How does V13–14 stress revise the earlier recovery model?
9. Does `Alter.1` show an ordinary Shin the main novels underrepresent?
10. Does the web-SS corpus complicate the “restrained because intimacy threatens Reaper identity” claim?

# 6. Domain audit: Lena

## 6.1 Strong legacy findings

The old project traced Lena more carefully than the common “naïve princess → badass commander” model.

Its strongest progression is:

> abstract moral recognition  
> → particular names  
> → technical competence  
> → disobedience with material cost  
> → command authority  
> → guilt and symbolic Queenhood  
> → increasing capacity to accept reciprocal support  
> → mourning and rest without total self-indictment.

The old analysis was also right to treat her command development as concrete. It repeatedly noticed:

- reserve allocation;
- information management;
- artillery and fire support;
- refusal/authorization decisions;
- retreats;
- force preservation;
- command under grief;
- and responsibility for ugly orders.

This is a strong basis for the V2 Lena specialist document.

## 6.2 High-risk legacy findings

### “Moral narcissism”

The V1 synthesis used this phrase to describe some of Lena’s guilt and desire to assume the Republic’s moral burden. The concept can be useful, but it is rhetorically stronger than the evidence classification warrants.

V2 should ask whether a given scene shows:

- self-centered guilt;
- command responsibility;
- survivor guilt;
- inherited political responsibility;
- privileged conscience;
- fear of rejection;
- or genuine solidarity.

Different scenes may support different answers.

### Lena as healer / savior

The old project deserves credit for repeatedly criticizing this possibility rather than endorsing it. However, V2 should not pre-load every act of concern as latent paternalism. The question is whether Lena:

- substitutes her judgment for Shin’s;
- denies information;
- manipulates consent;
- treats recovery as a command objective;
- or simply offers care and argument.

### “Queen” as danger

The V1 project reads Queenhood both as earned authority and risk of symbolic consumption. This is excellent but underlocated. V2 should track who uses:

- ハンドラー;
- ブラッディレジーナ / 鮮血の女王;
- 女王 / クイーン;
- レーナ;
- 少佐 / 大佐 / other rank forms;

and what each naming practice does relationally and institutionally.

## 6.3 Phase 2 Lena questions

1. Which early beliefs are naïve, and which are already politically sophisticated?
2. Does Theo’s rebuke primarily correct ignorance, privilege, sentimentality, command distance, or all four?
3. When does Lena start treating command competence as part of ethics?
4. When does guilt motivate responsibility, and when does it become self-erasure?
5. How often do subordinates challenge her successfully?
6. When does she delegate?
7. How does her private speech differ from her command register volume by volume?
8. Is “rest” in V12 primarily recovery, institutional correction, or narrative preparation?
9. How much does V13–14 test her constitutional beliefs versus her personal relationships?
10. What does the genuine Japanese V14 add to her relation with No Face and Václav?

# 7. Domain audit: Shin and Lena

The legacy relationship analysis is one of the strongest parts of V1 because it refuses several easy models.

It correctly rejects:

- “Lena saves Shin”;
- “Shin redeems Lena”;
- “they are two halves”;
- “romance cures trauma”;
- “love erases political asymmetry”;
- “healthy love means never needing another person.”

Its preferred formulation is **reciprocal dependence without possession**.

The old analysis also offers several useful sub-theses:

- they first know voices before bodies;
- idealization precedes ordinary knowledge;
- particular recognition works against racial categories without creating false historical symmetry;
- the relationship increasingly changes their access to future tense;
- waiting and return become reciprocal rather than one-directional;
- home can become relational without being reduced to one romantic person;
- mature intimacy requires curiosity about the other as a subject, not merely gratitude or rescue.

These are high-value hypotheses.

## Main V2 risks

### Risk 1 — romance as explanatory monopoly

Shin’s future cannot be attributed primarily to Lena if the text repeatedly shows contributions from:

- Raiden;
- Theo;
- Anju;
- Kurena;
- Frederica;
- Fido;
- Ernst’s household;
- Grethe;
- Vika;
- family;
- and functioning institutions.

Likewise, Lena’s maturity cannot be attributed primarily to Shin.

### Risk 2 — “anti-Legion temporality”

The old phrase is elegant: living love is future-oriented, while Shepherds can become frozen at final commands. But V2 must not confuse:

- literal Legion architecture;
- memory preservation;
- command persistence;
- character metaphor;
- and philosophical interpretation.

If the Legion can learn, adapt, or contain internally changing personlike structures, the metaphor requires qualification.

### Risk 3 — “home as person”

Useful at scene level, dangerous as full ontology. The V2 synthesis should preserve a plural model of home:

- people;
- household;
- unit;
- civic membership;
- remembered place;
- future destination;
- and practices of departure/return.

# 8. Domain audit: Spearhead and secondary characters

The V1 project noticed that *86* cannot finally be a Shin/Lena-only work, but it still compressed several secondary characters into thematic functions.

## Raiden

Legacy strength: stabilizing ordinary center, second-in-command, practical caretaker, translator of Shin, proof the unit is not tactically empty without Undertaker.

V2 risk: “ordinary” can become a synonym for underanalyzed. His positive desires, humor, anger, sexuality, relationships outside Shin, and political development need more attention. `Alter.1` and web SS may be disproportionately valuable here.

## Theo

Legacy strength: perhaps the best-developed secondary-character reading. His sensitivity to hypocrisy and symbolic appropriation, his inheritance of the fox mark, the failed revelation of the sea, and his injury create a strong personhood-beyond-function arc.

V2 risk: treating disability mainly as metaphor. The reread must attend to:

- pain;
- bodily adaptation;
- social accessibility;
- vocational change;
- stigma;
- dependence;
- prosthetic/medical choices;
- altered participation.

## Anju

Legacy strength: memory without enclosure; Daiya need not be forgotten for future intimacy to become legitimate.

V2 risk: reducing Anju to grief + Dustin. Her own forms of command, friendship, humor, body image, mixed-ancestry stigma, and self-presentation require independent tracking.

## Kurena

Legacy strength: movement from Shin-centered dependency toward allowing another person a future she does not control.

V2 risk: defining her mainly by failed romance. Her shooting craft, childhood history, relation to women, relation to Federacy civilianity, education, and positive future deserve separate attention.

## Shiden

Legacy strength: independent Eighty-Six command authority and fiercely personal loyalty to Lena; grief after Shana tests “die together” pride.

V2 risk: the prior project sometimes moved quickly from implication to claims about same-sex social norms in the Sector. The V2 rule should be:

> preserve erotic/romantic implication where textual; do not universalize social norms from a narrow sample.

## Rito

Legacy strength: younger cohort retains curiosity and therefore provides a generational contrast within “the Eighty-Six.”

V2 risk: using him principally as a thematic measuring device. His own relationships and decisions need chronology.

## Dustin

Legacy strength: demonstrates that Republic identity and Eighty-Six comradeship need not cancel one another; later savior impulses can mature into bounded responsibility.

V2 risk: evaluate how much narrative authority he has to represent a “constructive Alba response.”

## Annette

Legacy strength: guilt does not create entitlement to absolution; technical/engineering care is an important part of her character.

V2 risk: “first love” and “withdrawal of romantic claim” need exact textual support. Comedy should not be allowed to erase severity, but neither should the analysis refuse comedy’s genuine relational work.

## Frederica

Legacy strength: inherited monarchy becomes a language she can revise; she repeatedly resists adults turning Shin into a function; later must resist being reduced to shutdown key, bloodline, or legitimating symbol.

V2 risk: her role as analyst/commentator can make her sound like the novel’s omniscient ethical mouthpiece. Her youth, jealousy, manipulation, grief, desire, and error need equal weight.

# 9. Domain audit: institutions and political theory

## 9.1 San Magnolia

The strongest V1 political concept is **administrative dehumanization**.

The Republic does not merely hate the Eighty-Six. It creates legal, military, linguistic, geographical, archival, and statistical systems in which:

- the Eighty-Six are outside citizenship;
- “unmanned” weapons contain people;
- their deaths do not count as casualties;
- their territory is administratively absent;
- their graves and records can disappear.

This remains a high-confidence V2 hypothesis.

### V2 correction

The old phrase “administrative ontology” is analytic shorthand. V2 should not treat it as the novel’s own political vocabulary.

The reread must also preserve:

- variation among Alba;
- variation within military/civilian institutions;
- individual complicity;
- passive benefit;
- active resistance;
- historical development;
- and the limits of assigning one psychology to “Republic society.”

## 9.2 The Federacy

The old project repeatedly and correctly said:

> **The Federacy is morally better than San Magnolia and still capable of dehumanizing practices.**

That distinction is essential.

It provides:

- citizenship;
- law;
- medical care;
- education;
- public debate;
- military professionalism;
- institutional criticism;
- adult responsibility.

Its failures include:

- paternalistic rescue;
- sentimental consumption of Eighty-Six tragedy;
- strategic use of exceptional survivors;
- inherited Imperial hierarchy;
- uneven access to freedom;
- later fear and exclusion.

### V2 correction

V13’s structural recreation of an Eighty-Sixth Sector must not become the slogan “Giad was always the same as the Republic.” The point is more demanding:

> a better institution can regress under sufficient strain.

That requires a historical and causal account of regression.

## 9.3 Roa Gracia and comparative institutions

The legacy analysis uses Roa Gracia as evidence that regime label does not determine reciprocal responsibility. Its monarchy is unequal but its elite accepts visible military burden; the Sirin system then creates a different personhood problem.

This is promising, but V2 should avoid turning each country into a tidy philosophical laboratory. Countries are also:

- places;
- administrations;
- military systems;
- class structures;
- local cultures;
- individuals;
- and narrative arcs.

The comparative document should emerge after close reading rather than precede it.

## 9.4 V12 and “livable freedom”

This is among the most ambitious legacy claims:

> formal emancipation without education, social capital, practice in decision-making, or supportive institutions may feel like abandonment.

The old analysis connects former serfs, Noelle’s savior movement, and Ni’am’s paternalism into a theory of freedom requiring capacities.

**Assessment: `ADVERSARIAL_PRIORITY`.**

Why high priority? Because it may be one of the series’ strongest political achievements, but it also risks importing a highly contemporary liberal-capabilities framework and treating very different plot strands as one proof.

Phase 2 must test:

- whether these characters explicitly connect freedom to competence;
- whether the narrative endorses or criticizes their diagnoses;
- whether “support” becomes paternalism;
- whether dependence is chosen or assigned;
- and whether the series can imagine collective democratic agency rather than enlightened stewardship.

## 9.5 V13 and the Sector as political relation

The V1 formulation is powerful:

> **An Eighty-Sixth Sector exists where those behind the line compel another group to absorb the violence and close the road home.**

This is almost certainly important to V2, but its level must be specified.

- Structurally similar does not mean historically identical.
- Coerced soldiers are not automatically racially genocided Eighty-Six.
- A minefield does not by itself reproduce every aspect of San Magnolia.
- The moral significance lies in a particular **relationship of expendability and blocked return**.

This claim should be tested with the V13 Japanese language around equality, citizenship, fear, desertion, command, and mines.

# 10. Domain audit: military analysis

The legacy project repeatedly had strong military intuitions but lacked the V2 four-level discipline.

## Strong inherited military hypotheses

1. **The Republic’s “unmanned” model externalizes reciprocal risk.**
2. **The Eighty-Six’s inability to trust retreat/support is both adaptation and operational liability.**
3. **The Strike Package converts persecution-produced expertise into institutional capability while risking dependence on exceptional people.**
4. **A modern army should not require irreplaceable heroes.**
5. **Shin’s sensory ability becomes an institutional single point of failure.**
6. **Command responsibility includes owning morally ugly decisions rather than delegating guilt downward.**
7. **Logistics and rescue infrastructure can embody ethics: the system should enable return rather than merely spend bodies.**
8. ***86* admires military competence while asking what political failures make that competence necessary.**

## V2 military corrections

Every major military claim must be tagged:

- **Tactical:** immediate engagement and maneuver.
- **Operational:** campaign/theater orchestration, force generation, reserves, sustainment.
- **Strategic:** war aims, allocation among theaters, national military objectives.
- **Political-constitutional:** who may order, sacrifice, mobilize, conscript, retreat, or terminate war.

This is especially important for Shin and Lena. A tactical decision can be correct even if the operational system producing it is immoral. A morally admirable refusal can be operationally dangerous. A strategic necessity can coexist with political illegitimacy.

## Anti-war hypothesis

Legacy thesis:

> *86* is anti-war but not pacifist.

It admires courage, skill, discipline, tactics, machines, and comradeship. It also argues that war:

- colonizes identity;
- converts virtues into instruments of self-erasure;
- consumes childhood and developmental time;
- turns technology toward coercion;
- makes intolerable acts necessary;
- and encourages societies to build institutions around those acts.

**Assessment: `RETEST_STRONG`.**

The adversarial test must ask whether the novels’ aesthetic pleasure in combat sometimes exceeds or contradicts their critique. The prior analysis already noticed this tension in beautiful sacrifice and repeated heroic set pieces; V2 should not resolve it too quickly.

# 11. Domain audit: personhood, AI, the dead, and technology

The V1 analysis’s most promising philosophical move was refusing to define personhood by biological substrate alone.

Its recurring questions were:

- Can the entity revise purpose?
- Does it have memory?
- Does it form relationships?
- Can it refuse?
- Is its destruction treated as loss?
- Does it persist as more than utility?
- Is identity continuous or merely copied?
- Who owns the cognition?

This creates a useful comparative field:

## Legion

Human cognition is copied/preserved/used under inherited military command. But V2 must distinguish:

- Black Sheep;
- Shepherds;
- Sheepdogs;
- commander-level structures;
- No Face;
- individual persistence;
- learning/adaptation;
- original-person continuity;
- copied-person continuity.

The phrase “the dead frozen at their final command” is a **metaphysical interpretation**, not a mechanical description unless the text directly supports it in a given case.

## Sirins

The old project treats Sirins as personlike because they display individuality and relationships while institutions explicitly build them to be spent.

V2 must distinguish:

- legal/institutional status;
- technical construction;
- subjective self-conception;
- human characters’ treatment;
- narrative sympathy;
- philosophical moral status.

## Fido

The legacy “quiet ideal of personhood” reading is powerful:

> Legion preserves cognition for war; Fido preserves relationship.

But V2 should not imply Fido is morally ideal because it is friendly. Its technical autonomy, continuity, memory, and ability to choose must be established from source mechanics.

## Technology as ethical organization

The old project sometimes makes technology itself symbolic—bridge, Para-RAID, Cicada, Juggernaut, Reginleif, Sirin bodies, Phönix bodies.

V2 should preserve a double reading:

1. what the device literally does in the military world;
2. what formal or thematic work the narrative makes it perform.

Neither level should swallow the other.

# 12. Domain audit: ordinary life, childhood, and future

This is the area most likely to improve in V2 because the source corpus is now richer.

The legacy project correctly argued that:

- meals;
- games;
- bathing;
- travel;
- clothing;
- school;
- festivals;
- parties;
- drawing;
- hobbies;
- teasing;
- romance;
- repeated safe return

are not decorative pauses from the “real” war story. They are evidence of capacities the war interrupted.

It also correctly emphasized **fragmental neoteny / interrupted development**: children perform adult military functions while ordinary developmental needs remain unresolved.

But the main novels structurally privilege war and crisis. The new V2 corpus adds:

- `Alter.1`;
- 18 continuity-unclassified official web stories pending canon/chronology classification;
- additional low-pressure character interaction.

Therefore Phase 2 should deliberately test whether V1 overgeneralized “ordinary life as recovery” from a comparatively small set of high-salience leisure scenes.

Possible complications:

- ordinary life can also feel alienating;
- leisure can expose inequality or shame;
- hobbies can become duties;
- family can be coercive;
- romance can narrow rather than expand;
- “civilian” life is not one universal norm;
- some characters may genuinely prefer military vocation without being pathological.

The V2 positive thesis should be:

> **The right to a future includes the right to choose among forms of life, not a command to become civilian in one approved way.**

# 13. Domain audit: Japanese voice and translation-sensitive findings

The old Japanese-language pass is unusually valuable because it noticed that *86* has two different modes of voice characterization.

## Highly stylized voices

- **Lerche:** quasi-retainer / theatrical premodern forms such as `それがし`, `申しまする`.
- **Frederica:** archaic-imperial `わらわ / そなた / ～じゃ / ～ぬ`.
- **Vika:** aristocratic, analytical, sardonic.
- **Shiden:** rough, direct battlefield speech.
- **Noelle:** elevated declarative moral rhetoric.
- **Ni’am:** educated irony and managerial authority.

## Developmental voices

- **Shin:** minimal necessary statement → explanation → teasing → request → explicit desire.
- **Lena:** earnest institutional politeness → command authority → Queen rhetoric → private vulnerability.

## Understated differentiated voices

- Kurena’s youthful `あたし`, repetition, defensive endings;
- Annette’s modern, technical, sardonic `あたし`;
- Raiden’s comparatively unmarked masculine center;
- Anju’s restrained softness.

These are strong hypotheses.

### Phase 1 problem

They were not built into a primary-source locator index. V2 must therefore capture:

- exact Japanese phrase;
- volume;
- chapter/scene;
- addressee;
- relational context;
- register;
- pronoun/self-reference;
- sentence-final form;
- whether the construction is stable or context-specific.

The main V2 question is not only “who sounds distinctive?” It is:

> **What becomes sayable in a character’s established voice that was not sayable earlier?**

That is much harder—and much more useful—than collecting catchphrases.

These motifs are promising but were sometimes interpreted too quickly.

V2 requires a **motif ledger** that records appearances before assigning a unified meaning. A motif may:

- reverse meaning;
- accumulate contradictory meanings;
- be used ironically;
- belong more to one character than the whole work;
- operate visually rather than verbally.

Volume 10’s prequel structure also means chronology and publication order must remain distinct. A symbol’s **earlier fictional origin** can be a **later publication revelation**. V2 must preserve both.

- body positioning;
- machine scale;
- clothing;
- insignia;
- visual symmetry;
- injury visualization;
- landscape;
- map/geography.

- Anju as primarily grief-contained;
- Theo as primarily critical/ironic;
- Lena as primarily command/guilt;

The supplemental corpus is therefore not garnish. It is a **sampling correction**.

# 19. Phase 1 result

The old project supplies a mature conceptual map. V2 should preserve it as a set of falsifiable propositions, not as inherited truth.

The most important methodological transformation is:

### V1
> scene → interpretation → synthesis

often without an auditable trail.

### V2
> source object  
> → exact locator  
> → evidence class  
> → prospective reading  
> → retrospective correction  
> → ledger update  
> → specialist synthesis  
> → adversarial audit.

This means the V2 project may ultimately look similar to V1 at the highest level while being much stronger underneath.

It may also overturn some of V1’s cleanest sentences.

That is not a failure of continuity. It is the purpose of the second pass.

# 20. Handoff to Phase 2

Phase 1 closes with five governing instructions for the sequential reread.

1. **Do not prove the legacy thesis.** Record counterevidence as eagerly as confirmation.
2. **Preserve publication-boundary ambiguity.** Later revelations may revise early scenes, but may not erase what early readers/characters could reasonably infer.
3. **Give every character independent positive content.** Nobody exists only as evidence for Shin, Lena, romance, pride, or personhood.
4. **Separate levels.** Mechanics from metaphor; tactical from strategic; legal recognition from social belonging; care from control; loss from interpretation.
5. **Localize language.** Any important Japanese claim must have a recoverable textual anchor.

The paired `86_LEGACY_V1_VS_V2_REVISION_LEDGER.md` converts the hypotheses in this audit into claim-level test items for Phase 2.
