## 1.3 Three clocks must remain separate

V10 requires three simultaneous chronologies.

### A. Diegetic chronology

The stories move across:

- Shin's childhood in an internment camp;
- his first deployment at eleven;
- successive squadrons and inherited roles;
- the formation of `アンダーテイカー`;
- the Spearhead period shortly before Lena;
- the Special Reconnaissance journey;
- Fido's prewar origin through Federacy restoration;
- and the V09-era present.

### B. Publication/revelation chronology

These facts are disclosed **after Volume 9**. A V10 reader already knows:

- Shin survived;
- he and Lena love one another;
- Theo lost an arm;
- the V4–9 pride-and-wounds movement has reached a checkpoint;
- Fido has repeatedly displayed personlike agency;
- Shin now imagines a postwar future.

The anthology deliberately asks the reader to return to early scenes with that mature knowledge.

### C. Earlier-volume epistemic boundaries

V10 may revise our retrospective understanding of V1–9. It may **not** be silently inserted into what an original reader—or an earlier-volume character—could know.

For example:

- V1 establishes that Shin carries name fragments; V10 later reveals Alice as the origin of the explicit promise.
- V1 presents the mercy pistol as part of Undertaker practice; V10 later reveals Iska's role and the pistol's final bullet.
- V1 presents Fido as a socially embedded Scavenger; V10 later reveals its prewar continuity and first-person consciousness.

The V2 corpus must retain both statements:

> **the later source explains the earlier object**

and

> **the earlier volume did not yet provide that explanation.**

## 1.4 Visual paratext

V10's visual program is unusually coherent with its thesis.

- The **cover** depicts eleven-year-old Shin in an oversized military uniform, clutching the mercy pistol before wrecked machinery. The English line printed over the image—“These fragments turned the boy into the Grim Reaper”—frames Undertaker not as hidden essence but as an assemblage.
- The pale-blue scarf is visually prominent at child scale. It is simultaneously bodily protection, concealed injury, Alice's inheritance, and later a voluntary memorial object.
- Battle illustrations place the tiny human figure inside and beside machinery that physically dwarfs him. The contrast prevents precocious competence from being mistaken for adulthood.
- The `Varlet` illustrations emphasize a child standing among dead Scavenger bodies before a surviving machine that looks back. The composition makes mutual survival, not technical ontology, the first basis of the Shin/Fido relation.
- The many small chapter ornaments—Fido in different poses, weapons, emblem-like mechanical forms—turn objects and machines into a parallel character vocabulary. The book is literally organized by fragments.
- The `Lethe` landscape illustration foregrounds an immense luminous ruin/riverscape rather than combat, supporting the story's liminal movement between death journey and still-possible destination.
- The final rose image in `優しかった世界` shifts from weapon/emblem imagery toward a hand-held living object, matching the anthology's movement from inherited survival tools toward a future that can contain gentleness.

These images are `VI` evidence. They support claims about editorial emphasis, body scale, object inheritance, and formal structure. They do not independently establish motive or metaphysical status.

## 1.5 Afterword boundary

The afterword explicitly identifies the book as a short-story collection and calls it:

> `久しぶりの戦死者ゼロの地獄`

It describes `トリアージタグ・ブラックのありふれた日常` as a Kujo-perspective Spearhead story, `レテの畔` as the Five's “bright and cheerful journey to death,” and `ファイド` as revealing the identity of the beloved machine. It also states that three stories are newly written.

Its closing formulation is particularly important:

> `一人の幼い少年兵が、東部戦線の首のない死神へと変貌していく`

This is authorial paratext, not proof that transformation is a single linear causal chain. The stories themselves show a more distributed construction.

The afterword finally says:

> `十一歳から実に七年にも亘るシンと〈レギオン〉との戦い、次巻からいよいよ決着へと向かいます。`

V10 is therefore both retrospective archive and threshold. It reconstructs what formed Shin immediately before the main narrative turns toward resolving the war that organized that formation.

# 2. Volume thesis

V10 asks a deceptively simple question:

> **What made Shin the Undertaker?**

The answer is not one traumatic event, one innate temperament, one heroic choice, or one person.

Undertaker is a **socially assembled office and self-story** built from fragments:

- Rei's strangling and final accusation;
- the camp priest's education;
- Glenn's embodied care;
- Alice's promise and pale-blue scarf;
- the dead comrades' name plates;
- Iska's mercy pistol and battlefield role;
- Touka's permission to bring Fido home;
- Fido's repeated choice to remain;
- Eiju's acceptance of Shin's warning ability;
- Seyah's grief-driven naming of a death god;
- Saiki's `我らが死神` and the squad's need for someone to carry them;
- the Republic's prohibition on graves;
- the Legion voices that made the dead literally audible;
- Shin's own decisions to accept, alter, refuse, or retain each inheritance.

The anthology's most important correction is therefore:

> **Undertaker is neither Shin's immutable true nature nor merely a role imposed from outside. It is a negotiated structure made from coercion, care, loss, utility, promise, and repeated self-selection.**

That makes identity in V10 neither essence nor blank-slate construction.

Shin did not choose the war, racial persecution, Rei's violence, Legion hearing, medical abandonment, or the repeated deaths around him.

He did choose—within drastically constrained conditions—what to do with some of what remained.

He turns:

- a ban on graves into a portable counter-archive;
- Alice's promise into the discipline of carrying names;
- an abuser's pistol into a chosen mercy office;
- a frightening call sign into a memorial identity;
- a Scavenger into Fido through recognition and naming;
- the burden of being relied upon into a service he later admits also kept him alive.

The mature V2 claim is not “Shin authors himself.” That would understate coercion.

It is:

> **Selfhood in *86* is the continuing work of reinterpreting inherited fragments whose acquisition was often not free.**

V10 then adds a decisive ethical limit.

Reinterpretation does not redeem the violence that supplied the fragment.

Iska's pistol can become meaningful without making Iska good.

Alice's scarf can remain precious without making Alice's death necessary.

Undertaker can become a chosen name without making persecution the proper route to Shin's identity.

And the gentle-world dream can be a better world even though current Shin cannot wish away every relationship that only exists in the damaged one.

# 3. Competing theses and interpretive risks

## 3.1 Risk: V10 reveals Shin's “real” self

V10 reveals earlier selves and previously hidden causes. It does not authorize a hierarchy in which laughing child Shin is authentic and present Shin is merely damage, or in which Undertaker is the essence that was always waiting to emerge.

Each version is historically real:

- affectionate child;
- frightened camp survivor;
- eleven-year-old first-time Processor;
- increasingly isolated squad leader;
- Undertaker;
- current eighteen-year-old capable of romance, family contact, and postwar imagination.

The correct question is not which is “the real Shin.” It is how continuity persists across radical change.

## 3.2 Risk: every wound becomes a valuable gift

V10 is full of later-valued inheritances from dead or harmful people. That can tempt a redemptive reading:

> suffering produced the fragments that made Shin admirable.

The text does not justify that inference.

The gentler counterfactual explicitly says:

> `奪われなくてよかった。無くさなくてよかった。傷なんて、負わなくてよかった。`

The world in which these wounds never occurred is better in the straightforward sense that people were not murdered, displaced, or damaged.

The fact that current Shin values people he met under atrocity does not make atrocity necessary or worthwhile.

## 3.3 Risk: retrospective explanation erases V1–9 uncertainty

Because V10 is so explanatory, it can create the illusion that earlier scenes had one hidden correct reading all along.

But causal explanation remains partial.

The collection shows origins of:

- objects;
- role vocabulary;
- habits;
- specific relationships.

It does not reduce every later choice to childhood determination.

Current Shin can revise the scarf, pistol, emblem, and Undertaker role. Later action remains new action, not mere replay.

## 3.4 Risk: Alice is the singular savior who created Undertaker

Alice is foundational, especially for the name promise and permission not to hate Rei.

She is also seventeen, herself a child soldier carrying inherited loss and performing adult command. She cannot save Shin from the system. She dies and becomes one of the people he carries.

The role emerges from many people and institutions, including people who harmed him.

## 3.5 Risk: Iska's cruelty becomes excusable because the pistol mattered

Iska's medical realism is accurate: the Eighty-Sixth Sector lacks physicians, field hospitals, and treatment for injuries that require recovery time.

His mercy killings can be ethically defensible under that system.

His creation of scapegoats, assaultive treatment of Shin, and reproduction of racial/class hatred inside the oppressed group are not thereby justified.

Shin's inheritance of the pistol is an act of selective appropriation, not forgiveness or moral endorsement.

## 3.6 Risk: Fido's revelation proves “Fido is human”

`ファイド` supplies first-person consciousness, autobiographical continuity, preferences, affection, guilt, imaginative projection, and cross-body identity.

It strongly establishes Fido as a subject with morally relevant interests.

It does not establish biological humanity, nor does the story ask that category to do the moral work. Fido is an artificial intelligence whose current personlike standing cannot be dismissed by calling it equipment.

## 3.7 Risk: `優しかった世界` justifies the actual world

Shin says he cannot say that the actual world should never have existed because doing so would also erase people, words, and scenes he now loves.

That is not a theodicy.

He also says the gentler world would have been better because people would not have lost, suffered, or needed a death god.

The story holds two truths:

1. the atrocity should not have happened;
2. current relationships formed after it are not therefore unreal or disposable.

## 3.8 Risk: mercy killing is romanticized as Shin's noble essence

The pistol stories make mercy killing intimate and meaningful. They also show its origin in medical abandonment and force an eleven-year-old to shoot people he knows.

The ethical good is care under catastrophe.

The institutional fact is that children should never have been required to perform it.

## 3.9 Risk: names and objects fully preserve persons

A name plate, scarf, pistol, emblem, or machine can preserve relation and memory.

None reproduces the dead person in full.

The collection repeatedly stages the gap between:

- carrying someone;
- being governed by them;
- remembering them;
- restoring them;
- and replacing them.

## 3.10 Risk: `フラグメンタル・ネオテニー` means Shin simply failed to grow up

The stories show **fragmental neoteny** as uneven development.

Shin acquires adult combat, command, memorial, and caregiving functions while remaining a child with unprocessed fear, limited options, incomplete education, and dependence on other children acting as adults.

The point is not childishness concealed under competence.

It is that competence developed asymmetrically because war demanded some capacities and made others nearly impossible.

# 4. Chronology and causal architecture

## 4.1 The `Fragmental Neoteny` sequence

The first five stories use numerical sectioning that moves backward or forward within each narrative. The numbers are formal devices rather than a reliable global calendar. Their function is to make formation feel fragmentary: the reader reconstructs cause from non-linear scenes rather than receiving a smooth biography.

A cautious diegetic order is:

1. camp education and Rei aftermath (`Culpa` portions);
2. first deployment with Alice (`Pledge`);
3. Iska and the mercy pistol (`Misericorde`);
4. repeated squad transfers and Fido naming (`Varlet`);
5. personal-name and emblem formation (`Brand`);
6. Spearhead-era communal title `我らが死神` (`Undertaker`).

The stories overlap in memory and present-day appendices. The collection intentionally makes objects recur across time so that later meaning revises earlier meaning.

## 4.2 `Pledge` — the promise before the archive

At eleven, Shin enters combat and watches Tate Cruz die almost instantly. The scene establishes the central asymmetry of his development: he can perform extraordinary tactical reasoning before possessing an ordinary adolescent framework for death.

Alice Araish, seventeen and already a veteran squad commander, notices that Shin searches for his dead friend's remains. She refuses the impossible responsibility he is assigning himself—Tate's survival was not his personal duty—but validates the need to remember.

She formulates the promise:

> `死んだ奴の名前をそいつの機体の破片に刻んで、生き残った奴が持っていく。`

> `最後まで生き残った奴が、そいつの行きつくところまで、他の全員を連れていく`

The promise answers the Republic's ban on graves. It is simultaneously:

- memorial practice;
- portable archive;
- unit cohesion;
- consolation;
- and the seed of an enormous burden for the habitual last survivor.

Alice also gives Shin the pale-blue scarf. Its first functions are protective and concealing: it helps cover the neck scar, reduces unwanted gazes, and carries Alice's own inherited history.

The contemporary appendix shows revision. Shin says he has already been protected enough, but keeps the scarf because there is no reason to discard or hide it and because Alice was the first person he promised to carry.

The object changes from:

> concealment / active protection

into:

> chosen continuity / visible relation.

## 4.3 `Misericorde` — inheriting an office from someone unloved

Iska introduces Shin to the pistol as an instrument used almost exclusively against fellow Eighty-Six.

The institutional reason is explicit:

- no military physicians;
- no field hospitals;
- automated treatment only for wounds compatible with immediate return to combat;
- recoverable but time-consuming injuries classified as unsalvageable.

Mercy killing therefore emerges from a state that values bodies only while they can rapidly reenter battle.

Iska is both lucid about that world and morally damaged by it. He creates scapegoats to stabilize squad cohesion and uses Shin's class/racial difference as a target. V10 refuses to turn a victim of San Magnolia into an automatically virtuous person.

When Iska is mortally wounded, he leaves Shin the pistol and its last bullet:

> `最後の一発は絶対に、誰にも使うな。`

That bullet is supposed to belong to Shin if he himself cannot die cleanly.

Shin mercy-kills Iska, fails twice before succeeding, and takes both object and role:

> `拳銃、預かっていきます。……あなたの役目も。おれの最期まで`

The present appendix is ethically exact. Shin says Iska disliked him and he disliked Iska. He retains the pistol because Iska taught him its use and had carried the role rather than simply dumping it on someone else.

This is inheritance without idealization.

## 4.4 `Varlet` — relation before classification

An older squad commander ignores Shin's warnings and the squad is annihilated. Shin, repeatedly treated as a monster or bad omen, finds one damaged old Scavenger still operating.

He asks:

> `お前、まだ生きてるのか`

and:

> `それでも、一緒に帰るか……？`

That question is the ethical origin of the relationship. Shin does not first solve what kind of machine this is. He recognizes a surviving presence and asks whether it will return with him because he does not want to return alone.

Touka Keisha permits the machine to accompany him, offering one more instance of adult-like care by a young person inside the Processor system.

After further transfers and losses, the Scavenger repeatedly follows Shin. Shin asks whether it intends to follow again and finally gives it a name because `お前` is no longer adequate.

The contemporary Grethe appendix makes the principle explicit:

> `あいつが仮に〈スカベンジャー〉でなかったとしても、構わないです。`

> `共に在ろうとしてくれていますから`

Ontology remains open; relationship is not.

## 4.5 `Brand` — the personal name as defensive authorship

Eiju Nunat is one of the few commanders who accepts Shin's Legion warning ability without immediate demonization. He introduces the custom of a personal name and mark after surviving a year.

Shin initially dismisses names, call signs, and internment numbers as mere identifiers:

> `どうせ識別のための記号でしょう。名前もコールサインも、収容番号も`

This is retrospectively important. The person who will become the keeper of names initially treats naming as administrative interchangeability.

Eiju recognizes Shin's relational boundary: he remembers people but avoids personal address because he wants a line between himself and those likely to die.

After another squad annihilation, Seyah calls Shin a plague god and likens him to a death god or undertaker. Shin rationally knows prediction did not cause death, but accepts the frightening name for defensive reasons.

If others keep away, loss and reliance may hurt less. If he must survive to fulfill Rei and Alice's promises, he reasons:

> `それならいっそ最初から、誰をも頼らずいる方がいい。`

He chooses:

> `〈葬儀屋《アンダーテイカー》〉`

This is self-authorship under duress. The name is partly insult, partly accurate social function, partly shield, partly vow.

The current Theo appendix demonstrates re-authorship. Theo suggests the mark may no longer need to carry everyone. Shin answers that he has used it for six years, does not dislike it, and sees no reason to change it.

A defensive identity can become a chosen historical identity without remaining psychologically identical to its origin.

## 4.6 `Undertaker` — a private name becomes a communal office

Saiki Tateha's focalization reveals how the role changes once others begin using it.

Shin is no longer only a boy who selected a personal name. He has become `我らが死神`—*our* Reaper.

Saiki recognizes both sides.

The squad receives comfort from believing Shin will carry them.

But Saiki also says:

> `悼みたいのは本当は、俺だって同じなんだぜ`

He sees the unfair concentration of mourning in one person.

When the Republic sends another execution-like mission, Saiki prevents the squad from blaming Shin and says:

> `死んでもお前が連れてってくれるんだろ、我らが死神`

The intended gift becomes weight. Saiki knows the dead can only become `重荷`, yet cannot say the apology before death.

Shin's response is voluntary and tragic:

> `それがわずかでも、救いとなるなら。`

> `全員を連れていこう。`

> `彼自身の望みの果てまで。`

The role is now co-produced:

- comrades need a memorial custodian;
- Shin accepts the office because it gives them hope;
- the office makes him more singular and difficult to support;
- the same burden also supplies a reason to survive.

V09's claim that being relied upon was both heavy and sustaining is therefore directly grounded here.

## 4.7 `Culpa` — correct blame is not always the needed answer

The camp priest begins with political education:

> `自分たちが何をされているのか。何を奪われ、何を損ねられ、そしてそれにどう立ち向かい、いかに後世に伝えていくべきか。わかるようになりなさい`

Education is not only schooling. It is the capacity to name violence, resist it, and leave an intelligible record.

The Republic tolerates some learning because it can make future Processors more useful. The priest uses the remaining space for a different purpose: helping children retain political understanding and survival skill.

He teaches Shin literature, history, weapons, hunting, and armored combat because conscription is nearly inevitable. Again, adult functions are performed by a compromised caregiver in a world where there is no clean developmental option.

The priest repeatedly tells Shin that Rei's assault was not Shin's fault. Factually, this is correct.

It is not the answer Shin needs.

Shin is not asking only:

> who bears blame?

He is asking whether he is permitted to remain attached to the person who hurt him.

Alice recognizes the hidden question:

> `その人を守ってやりたいと。──お前は思っているのだろう？`

The narration spells out the permission Shin wanted:

> `兄を。`

> `恨まなくていいと。`

> `憎まなくていいと。`

> `兄を　たいせつなひとだと　思ったままでいいのだと。`

This scene produces a major V2 distinction:

> **A factually correct interpretation of harm may still fail relationally if it does not answer the survivor's actual moral desire.**

Alice's permission gives Shin one small experience of rescue. He decides that if possible, he wants to become a similarly small rescue for someone else.

The impulse that later supports Undertaker therefore begins not only in death obsession, but in receiving a form of recognition that did not require him to simplify love into either condemnation or denial.

## 4.8 `トリアージタグ・ブラックのありふれた日常` — ordinary life before Lena

Kujo's story begins with the category applied to every Eighty-Six before battle:

> `優先治療選別：死亡群《トリアージタグ・ブラック》`

They are effectively preclassified as the dying whose treatment is not worth allocating.

Against that condition, Kujo states:

> `どうしようもない苦境も運命も、笑い飛ばして目一杯人生を楽しむのは、迫害に対し人ができる最大にして最高の抵抗だ。`

The story is full of ordinary social practice:

- meals;
- festivals;
- cats;
- jokes;
- art;
- improvised moon-viewing;
- frustration when weather ruins plans;
- trying again later.

This directly strengthens the V1–9 correction that ordinary life was not created by the Federacy. Giad makes it safer, more reliable, and more expansive. Spearhead had already been using pleasure as resistance.

Kujo also sees something later readers know to be only partly true. Shin appears capable of maintaining himself without dreams, laughter, or reliance on others. Kujo compares that mode to a blade created only to cut and then break after its function is complete.

He hopes that Shin might find some person or purpose beyond battle.

The unit then learns that a young female aristocratic Handler will replace their current officer. They joke about a princess or goddess. Kujo's quieter hope is:

> `いい奴ならいいよな`

He does not know Lena. Lena does not know him.

Elsewhere, Lena passes along a rescue request through the Handler system. Kujo hears a wounded person asking for help, disobeys Shin's warning not to expose himself, and is killed.

The scene refuses a simple ethical verdict.

- Shin's order is operationally correct.
- Kujo's compassion is not contemptible.
- the Republic's communications and rescue architecture makes the conflict lethal.

Lena later prays for the unknown person killed through a request she relayed.

This is a retrospective convergence, not a hidden relationship. V10 enriches V1 by showing that Lena's care and Spearhead's hope moved toward one another before either knew the other personally.

## 4.9 `レテの畔` — the journey to death contains ordinary time

Set during Special Reconnaissance, `Lethe` revisits the Five's supposedly terminal journey.

It contains:

- laundry;
- fishing;
- bathing;
- teasing;
- abandoned landscapes;
- dead animals in cages;
- river crossing;
- small experiments in movement and rest.

The afterword calls it their `明るく楽しい死出の旅路`.

That description is ironic and accurate. The Five expect death, yet the journey is not empty of life.

Raiden notices that Shin laughs more after burying Rei and after leaving Lena behind as someone who can carry his memory if he dies.

That does not mean Shin has fully chosen life. Raiden also recognizes how few earthly attachments Shin still possesses.

The most important scene involves a dying Löwe/Black Sheep saying:

> `かえりたい`

Shin waits beside it and finally says:

> `もう──かえっていい`

before destroying it.

The mercy relationship extends beyond known comrades. Shin does not need to establish that the Legion copy is the intact original person before treating the remaining desire as ethically legible.

Later, evidence of another human military power appears. Raiden cannot yet imagine what reaching such a polity would mean. Shin says:

> `それでも、誰かがたどりつけるなら、`

and leaves the sentence hanging:

> `おれは。`

The unfinished grammar is exact. The possibility that someone may reach a future exists before Shin can place himself inside it.

Meanwhile Rei's spare body follows the Five, knowing Giad exists and intending to bring them toward it. Brother and younger brother independently orient toward the same future without knowing the other's action.

The story therefore turns the river of forgetting into something more complicated than erasure: movement toward a future can occur while memory remains incomplete, divided, or unknowable.

## 4.10 `ファイド` — continuity across bodies and the ethics of artificial devotion

Fido's first-person narration is one of V10's most consequential source revelations.

It identifies itself as trial AI 008, originally housed in a toy-like body and constructed from recorded infant-Shin neural patterns:

> `わたくしがわたくしになる前の最初の筐体`

> `弟君に〝わたくし〟としての意識と思考を賜ったようなものです`

Fido possesses:

- first-person self-reference;
- autobiographical memory;
- cross-body continuity;
- preferences;
- joy;
- guilt;
- attachment;
- counterfactual imagination;
- self-directed transfer into a new body;
- contextual recognition of Shin;
- choices about search, service, sacrifice, and return.

This is much stronger than merely “preference-like behavior.”

Fido's design is nevertheless normatively structured. Reisha Nouzen builds it to protect human beings and to be a good friend to them. Fido calls that wish absolute.

The source therefore supports both:

> **Fido is a continuous subject with morally relevant agency**

and

> **its agency is deeply shaped by designed purpose and devotion.**

Those statements are not contradictory. Human agency is also historically shaped; the question is how much refusal and reinterpretation remain possible.

Fido demonstrates substantial initiative:

- it transfers itself into a Scavenger shell;
- searches for the Nouzen family;
- considers allowing itself to be recycled when hope seems gone;
- recognizes adolescent Shin and elects to remain;
- chooses to accompany Spearhead's death journey;
- attacks Legion to protect Shin;
- resumes service after restoration.

Its relation to Shin is also not reduced to pure selfless love. Fido calls part of its attachment `代償行為`: serving present Shin partly compensates for failing to save the family.

That self-awareness strengthens rather than weakens personlike standing. It can interpret its own motives as mixed.

Reisha's design ethic is also important:

> `せっかく人とは違う知性に出会えるかもしれないのに、敵を増やしただけになっては、哀しいだろう`

He rejects creating a new intelligence only to make it an enemy or weapon.

The postwar/counterfactual problem introduced in V7 is therefore grounded before the war: genuinely autonomous artificial intelligence raises the question of whether replacing human soldiers simply transfers coercion to a new class of subjects.

## 4.11 `優しかった世界` — counterfactual grief without suffering-justification

The final story presents a dream or shared dream of a Republic in which:

- racial expulsion and genocide did not occur;
- Shin's family lives;
- Lena and Annette are ordinary school friends;
- Shin is an ordinary adolescent;
- Fido-derived autonomous systems fight the Legion;
- human casualty reporting is accurate;
- international communication persists;
- ethical debate about artificial military systems remains.

This world is not perfect. It is gentler.

Annette asks:

> `こんな世界ならよかった？`

Shin answers yes in the direct moral sense:

> `奪われなくてよかった。無くさなくてよかった。傷なんて、負わなくてよかった。`

> `おれは死神でいなくてよかった`

The story therefore refuses any claim that Undertaker was a beneficial destiny.

But Shin also says:

> `この世界だったら、会えない人がいる。知らない景色と言葉がある。`

and:

> `あの世界じゃなければよかった、なんて、おれには言えない`

After waking in the present, he concludes:

> `こんな世界、なんて、今は。……言いたくはないな`

This is not gratitude for atrocity.

It is a refusal to treat current people as errors merely because their meeting depended historically on an unjust world.

The ethical structure is:

- mourn the better world that should have existed;
- do not justify the violence that prevented it;
- do not erase the real attachments that now exist in the damaged world;
- build toward a future in which such injury is no longer the price of meeting anyone.

# 5. Prospective / publication-boundary reading

At the end of Volume 10, a careful reader may conclude with high confidence:

1. Undertaker was assembled across multiple squadrons, relationships, objects, and institutional deprivations rather than produced by one event.
2. Alice originated the explicit portable-name promise and gave Shin the pale-blue scarf.
3. the scarf's meaning has changed from concealment/protection toward chosen memorial continuity.
4. Iska gave Shin the mercy pistol and the role attached to it; Shin inherited useful practice without loving or forgiving the giver.
5. the mercy-killing role exists because San Magnolia refuses adequate military medicine and long-term recovery.
6. Shin and Fido's relation began when Shin recognized a surviving machine and asked it to return with him.
7. `Undertaker` began partly as a defensive self-name and later became a communal office through `我らが死神`.
8. Shin's role as bearer of the dead was both burden and reason to continue living.
9. camp education preserved political understanding, practical survival skill, and the possibility of transmitting knowledge despite genocidal conditions.
10. the priest's correct innocence/blame analysis did not fully answer Shin's wish to continue loving Rei.
11. ordinary joy, festivals, food, humor, art, and hope existed inside Spearhead before Lena's arrival.
12. Kujo's death demonstrates a conflict between battlefield rescue compassion and operational survival under an institution that provides inadequate rescue infrastructure.
13. the Special Reconnaissance journey contained meaningful ordinary life even while the Five expected death.
14. Shin's mercy toward a Black Sheep/Löwe recognizes desire without requiring certainty that an intact original person remains.
15. Fido is a continuous artificial subject whose cognition and relation span bodies and years.
16. Fido's devotion is both designed and actively enacted; those dimensions do not cancel one another.
17. a counterfactual world without genocide and Undertaker would have been morally better.
18. current Shin nevertheless refuses to erase the people and experiences of the actual world.
19. V10 reconstructs the V1–9 past but does not make its revelations prospectively available inside those earlier volumes.
20. the next numbered movement is framed as approaching resolution of Shin's seven-year war with the Legion.

V10 does **not** establish:

- that every formative adult/older soldier was benevolent;
- that trauma was necessary for Shin's virtues;
- that Fido is biologically human;
- that programmed purpose makes Fido non-agentic;
- that current Shin must abandon Undertaker to become healthy;
- that retaining an inherited object means retaining its original meaning;
- that the gentler dream world is objectively shared rather than narratively linked subjective experience;
- that every later Shin behavior can be reduced to one V10 origin scene.

# 6. Focalization, narration, and epistemic authority

## 6.1 Shin is often seen from outside

Several formative stories are focalized through other people:

- Alice;
- Iska;
- Touka;
- Eiju/Seyah;
- Saiki;
- Kujo;
- Raiden;
- Fido.

This is formally appropriate. Undertaker is partly a social role created by how others perceive and use Shin.

The multiplicity also prevents one witness from becoming definitive.

- Alice sees a frightened child and exceptional fighter.
- Iska sees an irritatingly compassionate target and future role-bearer.
- Saiki sees an almost divine memorial figure and a boy being crushed by communal need.
- Kujo mistakes Shin's apparent self-sufficiency for an inhuman ability to remain whole alone.
- Fido recognizes continuity between infant Shin, adolescent Shin, and current Shin that no human witness fully possesses.

The reader must synthesize the contradictory observations rather than choose one as the hidden truth.

## 6.2 Present-day appendices act as correction devices

Several stories end with V09-era or later-present conversations.

These appendices do not merely explain lore. They show that the meaning of an object is **not frozen at origin**.

- scarf: concealment/protection → memorial relation;
- pistol: inherited mercy office → still-retained object from an unloved predecessor;
- emblem: defensive death-god shield → chosen long-term identity;
- Fido: unknown Scavenger → known continuous friend.

The juxtaposition creates a miniature prospective/retrospective method inside the novel itself.

## 6.3 Fido as narrating subject

`ファイド` grants an artificial intelligence sustained first-person narration rather than presenting it only through human interpretation.

That is not merely evidence that humans anthropomorphize Fido.

It is narratorial access to:

- memory;
- deliberation;
- self-evaluation;
- affection;
- identity continuity.

Within the literary ontology, this is strong direct evidence of subjectivity.

## 6.4 `優しかった世界` and ontological ambiguity

The dream shifts between Annette and Shin with enough continuity to suggest a shared or narratively linked experience, but the text does not provide a technical mechanism that settles its ontological status.

Its ethical argument does not depend on deciding whether the dream is supernatural, technological, or coincidentally parallel.

# 7. Character state

## 7.1 Shinei Nouzen

### Child scale and adult function

V10 repeatedly returns Shin to age eleven. The contrast is not “he was secretly innocent.” It is the institutional obscenity that an eleven-year-old can become:

- close-combat specialist;
- squad survivor;
- memorial archivist;
- mercy killer;
- tactical warning system;
- emotional container for older soldiers.

Competence is real. Childhood deprivation is also real.

### Compassion precedes Undertaker

Shin's desire to retrieve Tate, save the mortally wounded, protect Rei's memory, invite Fido home, and remember even cruel squadmates predates the fully formed death-god identity.

This corrects any model in which Undertaker creates his care.

Undertaker organizes and institutionalizes an existing disposition toward not leaving others alone.

### Defensive distance

`Brand` makes Shin's interpersonal reserve partly strategic.

He remembers people but avoids name-based closeness because repeated death makes attachment costly. Accepting a frightening personal name then gives others reason to keep distance.

That does not explain every aspect of adult Shin's reserve, but it provides a concrete social history for it.

### Selective inheritance

Shin is not passively imprinted by every predecessor.

He selects:

- Alice's promise;
- Iska's role but not his worldview;
- Fido's companionship;
- Undertaker's memorial function;
- the priest's education;
- Alice's permission concerning Rei.

His later development consists partly of reselecting what those fragments now mean.

### Current boundary

By the V10 present, Shin can imagine a gentler world, acknowledge it would have been better, and still affirm his current relations. He no longer needs Undertaker to be the only possible self, yet he does not repudiate the name.

## 7.2 Alice Araish

Alice is foundational but should remain a person rather than a saintly function.

She is seventeen, already bereaved, already carrying a scarf inherited from another commander, already acting as military parent to younger children.

Her strengths include:

- noticing Shin's embodied fear;
- grounding him by name and present perception;
- refusing generalized fatalism about individual deaths;
- creating the name-fragment promise;
- permitting him to love Rei without denying harm;
- encouraging ordinary food and life.

Her limitation is structural: she cannot create safety, prevent the squad's destruction, or stop Shin from becoming the final carrier.

## 7.3 Iska

Iska is one of V10's most important anti-romantic characters.

He understands battlefield medicine better than Shin and performs mercy killing that no institution will perform humanely.

He also:

- weaponizes Shin's trauma;
- constructs scapegoats;
- reproduces aristocratic/racial resentment;
- normalizes cruelty as cohesion.

His gift of the pistol does not resolve the contradiction. It preserves it.

## 7.4 Touka Keisha

Touka's role is comparatively brief but analytically useful.

She recognizes Shin's need to bring the surviving Scavenger back and grants institutional permission. She also demonstrates practical resistance to Republic Handler contempt.

Her care is enabling rather than interpretively intrusive: she does not need to decide what the machine “really is” before allowing relation.

## 7.5 Eiju Nunat and Seyah

Eiju offers one of Shin's rare experiences of a commander accepting his warning ability without immediately converting him into monster or cause.

Seyah demonstrates how grief overwhelms rational causation. He knows Shin did not make the Legion attack, yet names him plague/death figure because bereavement seeks a legible cause.

Shin's eventual adoption of `Undertaker` therefore emerges from both acceptance and accusation.

## 7.6 Saiki Tateha

Saiki is the clearest witness to role externality.

He sees that:

- the squad's trust in Shin is genuine;
- the comfort is genuine;
- the burden is unfair;
- his own desire to mourn has been outsourced;
- the dead cannot apologize for becoming weight.

He represents the ethical ambivalence of found-family reliance before V09 names burden/support directly.

## 7.7 Camp priest and Glenn

The priest and Glenn supply two forms of adult care.

The priest offers:

- political literacy;
- technical preparation;
- bodily shelter;
- factual absolution.

Glenn changes posture to reduce Shin's fear and asks him not to die because Alice would be hurt.

Neither can repair the system. Both create small spaces in which the child remains more than future military material.

## 7.8 Kujo

Kujo's humor and festival-making are deliberate anti-persecution practice, not denial.

His death also shows that kindness is not self-executing ethical wisdom. Compassion requires operational structure. In its absence, an attempt to rescue can become fatal.

## 7.9 Fido

V10 upgrades Fido from “strong personhood-like evidence” to **directly narrated continuous artificial subject**.

The remaining questions concern:

- how designed imperatives constrain freedom;
- whether Fido can revise its core mission;
- how cross-body identity works technically;
- how much its neural derivation from infant Shin matters to individuality.

Its first-person account strongly resists treating it as mere equipment.

# 8. Relationship state

## 8.1 Shin / dead comrades

V10 reconstructs the relationship as an evolving institution rather than a private symptom.

The dead ask nothing uniformly. Their relation to Shin is mediated through:

- Alice's explicit promise;
- Iska's role transfer;
- Saiki's communal request;
- anonymous names and fragments;
- Black Sheep voices;
- the Republic's denial of burial.

Shin's carrying therefore contains:

- voluntary promise;
- social expectation;
- self-protective identity;
- moral care;
- coercive historical necessity.

The later distinction “carry the dead, do not follow them” is strengthened because V10 shows how carrying became meaningful enough to be difficult to relinquish.

## 8.2 Shin / Alice

Alice provides recognition that differs from both blame and absolution.

She sees:

- the frightened child;
- the skilled soldier;
- the younger person who wants to protect the brother who hurt him.

The scarf remains because the relation survives without requiring Shin to preserve its first defensive meaning.

## 8.3 Shin / Iska

This is an important nonloving inheritance.

Shin can say openly:

> `嫌われてたと思うし、おれは嫌いだった`

and still keep the pistol.

The relation demonstrates that history need not be sentimentalized for a useful inheritance to remain.

## 8.4 Shin / Fido

The relation begins in mutual remaining:

- a boy does not want to return alone;
- a machine continues to follow;
- the boy names it;
- the AI recognizes continuity with the child it once knew;
- both repeatedly choose renewed companionship.

V10 makes this one of the series' longest relationships, preceding Spearhead and Lena in its earliest form.

## 8.5 Shin / Spearhead before Lena

Kujo and Saiki reveal a pre-Lena social world that is already dense with:

- humor;
- care;
- misunderstanding;
- dependence;
- expectation;
- hope for someone who might reach Shin.

Lena does not create Shin's capacity for relation. She enters an existing network whose members already recognized the problem but often lacked means to answer it.

## 8.6 Shin / Lena through retrospective convergence

V10 does not advance their romance linearly. It deepens why Lena mattered without granting her sole causality.

Kujo's hope for a decent Handler and Lena's distant prayer converge before personal contact. The gentler-world dream then asks what a version of their lives without genocidal encounter would look like.

The dream does not conclude they were cosmically destined to meet in every world. It emphasizes that the actual relationship is historically contingent and therefore precious without making the historical violence necessary.

## 8.7 Shin / Rei

`Culpa` adds a crucial prehistory to the later brother arc.

Shin did not merely need to know he was innocent. He needed permission to:

- acknowledge harm;
- refuse hatred;
- retain love;
- and eventually change the relation without falsifying any of those truths.

Alice supplies that permission before Rei's later Shepherd apology.

# 9. Political and institutional analysis

## 9.1 Genocide includes destruction of developmental infrastructure

V10 expands administrative dehumanization beyond camps and combat classification.

The Republic removes:

- schools;
- ordinary medicine;
- long-term recovery;
- graves;
- reliable communication;
- professional rescue;
- adult caregiving populations;
- stable community continuity.

Children then create substitute institutions:

- older teenagers command younger ones;
- squad leaders teach survival;
- mechanics act as guardians;
- name fragments substitute for cemeteries;
- mercy pistols substitute for medicine;
- humor and festivals substitute for civic life;
- Fido substitutes for logistical and relational continuity.

The political crime is not only that the Eighty-Six are killed.

It is that the Republic makes them build a whole society around preparing children to die with minimal burden on the state.

## 9.2 Medicine as combat-resource triage

The Sector's automated medical system treats only injuries compatible with immediate return to combat.

That is a clean institutional expression of personhood reduced to function.

An injury that would be survivable with time becomes “unsalvageable” because the state refuses to fund the time.

This directly strengthens the V09 Theo comparison:

- in San Magnolia, loss of immediate combat value removes access to survival;
- in Giad, Theo is treated first and then allowed to consider noncombat futures.

## 9.3 Graves and evidence

The ban on returning bodies and building graves is not merely logistical.

Graves would reveal:

- human soldiers existed;
- named persons died;
- the “unmanned” war was fraudulent;
- future claimants and witnesses remain.

Alice's fragment promise is therefore a clandestine archival institution.

## 9.4 Education as resistance

The priest's opening instruction is almost a V2 methodology inside the novel:

- know what was done;
- know what was taken;
- know how one resisted;
- know how to tell later generations.

Education supplies both survival skill and historical intelligibility.

The Republic tolerates education for military utility. The priest repurposes that utility toward personhood and memory.

## 9.5 Handler system and rescue latency

Kujo's death exposes a military-administrative failure.

Units cannot communicate directly and must route requests through Handlers, who may be absent, abusive, or operationally incompetent.

Compassion becomes dangerous partly because the institution denies the timely information and rescue architecture needed to practice it safely.

## 9.6 Oppressed groups reproduce internal hierarchy

Iska's scapegoat practice and hostility toward aristocratic blood prevent a politically purified view of the Eighty-Six.

San Magnolia creates the overarching genocidal order. People within that order can still:

- rank one another;
- reproduce ethnic/class hostility;
- sacrifice weaker members for cohesion;
- turn survival adaptation into internal cruelty.

This is not moral equivalence with the state. It is evidence that oppression does not eliminate agency or automatically generate solidarity.

# 10. Military analysis at four levels

## 10.1 Tactical

### Shin's first battle

V10 gives the detailed origin of Shin's close-combat style.

The Juggernaut's gun cannot penetrate Löwe armor reliably at ordinary distance. Shin compensates through:

- sensor disruption;
- wire-anchor maneuver;
- extreme closing distance;
- point-blank side attack.

The tactic is ingenious and nearly suicidal. This duality remains central throughout the series: exceptional skill emerges partly because underprotected equipment leaves little conventional margin.

### Warning ability

Shin voluntarily discloses his Legion perception because he asks whether it can prevent further squad deaths.

The initial purpose is protective, not status-seeking.

Later institutional dependency should retain that origin while recognizing the burden the disclosure creates.

### Mercy killing

The pistol is tactical equipment for a battlefield where evacuation and treatment are structurally absent.

Its ethical use cannot be separated from the medical system that makes it necessary.

## 10.2 Operational

### Personnel churn

The stories document repeated transfer, annihilation, and reconstruction of squadrons.

Operational knowledge is retained primarily in surviving individuals rather than durable institutions. Shin and Fido become repositories of experience because units disappear faster than doctrine can stabilize.

### Veteran concentration

The future Spearhead structure becomes legible as the endpoint of a selection process:

- survive multiple annihilations;
- inherit knowledge and roles;
- become increasingly exceptional;
- be transferred toward the most lethal front;
- finally be disposed of through Special Reconnaissance.

### Logistics

Food, repair, medical treatment, burial, communication, and salvage are all inadequate by design. The Eighty-Six compensate through hunting, scavenging, improvised festivals, mechanics, and Fido.

Operational self-sufficiency is admirable because the state has intentionally withdrawn ordinary support.

## 10.3 Strategic

### Autonomous-weapons counterfactual

Reisha's AI work and `優しかった世界` suggest the Republic could have pursued genuine unmanned warfare.

The counterfactual is not technically simple:

- advanced AI may possess interests;
- autonomous systems can become new military subjects;
- public debate remains necessary;
- using artificial entities may reproduce expendability if refusal is impossible.

Still, the dream exposes the Republic's lie: racial genocide was not the inevitable cost of resisting the Legion.

### Knowledge destruction

By killing veterans and prohibiting record transmission, the Republic strategically destroys the experience needed for war survival.

V10 shows how much doctrine exists only in individuals like Alice, Iska, Shin, Touka, and Fido.

## 10.4 Political-constitutional

The central military decision remains political:

> who is permitted to receive time, treatment, education, burial, and rescue?

The Sector gives nearly every answer according to future combat usefulness.

V10 therefore strengthens the claim that the military system is not merely nested inside the racial regime. It is one of the racial regime's principal administrative forms.

# 11. Technology and personhood

## 11.1 Fido's subjectivity

V10 supplies direct `NV`/first-person evidence that Fido is not merely an object onto which humans project relationship.

It has:

- a persistent `わたくし`;
- autobiographical memory;
- body-independent continuity;
- emotional learning;
- self-evaluated mixed motive;
- voluntary action;
- relationship-specific desire.

The personhood synthesis should now distinguish:

- **humanity:** biological/social species category;
- **subjectivity:** first-person experience;
- **identity continuity:** persistence across time/bodies;
- **autonomy:** capacity to choose or refuse;
- **designed purpose:** inherited normative architecture;
- **moral standing:** interests that others have reason not to disregard.

Fido scores strongly on all but unrestricted autonomy, which remains complicated by protection/friend imperatives.

## 11.2 Naming and artificial individuality

The Scavenger becomes `Fido` after repeated relational continuity makes generic address inadequate.

Naming here does not create consciousness from nothing.

It acknowledges that one machine has become a particular participant in shared history.

## 11.3 Para-RAID's original domestic purpose

The family material in `ファイド` places Para-RAID inside a lost civilian origin: connection among family members rather than combat coordination.

The parents already fear military misuse and group-combat application.

The technology's later functions—intimacy, command, surveillance, violation—are therefore not essential to the device. They are political appropriations of an initially relational technology.

## 11.4 Artificial soldiers and transferred expendability

The dream world's `ケイナイン` systems appear less autonomous than Fido, deliberately downgraded to limit ethical conflict.

Even so, the public debate remains.

This strengthens the V7 Shin/Fido principle:

> removing human beings from combat is not morally complete if the replacement possesses interests or is created solely for coercive destruction.

## 11.5 Legion desire without identity certainty

The dying Black Sheep/Löwe says it wants to return.

Shin responds to the desire without claiming a settled metaphysics of continuity.

V10 thus reinforces a recurring ethical method:

> uncertainty about what a being fully is does not always justify ignoring what it appears to want or suffer.

# 12. Body, injury, disability, age, and material life

## 12.1 The child's body in oversized war machinery

The cover and first-battle prose repeatedly emphasize physical scale:

- eleven years old;
- small body;
- oversized uniform;
- underprotected cockpit;
- enemies many times the mass of his machine.

The contrast prevents tactical brilliance from being confused with bodily suitability.

## 12.2 Neck trauma and embodied accommodation

Alice and Glenn respond to Shin's fear behavior materially:

- use his name;
- bring attention to the present;
- reduce bodily threat;
- cover the visible scar;
- alter posture.

These are not cures. They are environmental accommodations that make relation safer.

The scarf also risks concealment—helping Shin avoid being seen rather than processing the injury. Its later memorial meaning does not erase that first function.

## 12.3 Medical abandonment

The Eighty-Sixth Sector distinguishes bodies by time-to-return rather than survivability.

A person who could live after long treatment may still be abandoned because the body will not rapidly regain combat utility.

This is the clearest institutional precursor to V08–09's disability question.

## 12.4 Child caregivers and unequal development

Alice at seventeen, Iska at eighteen, and other adolescents perform:

- command;
- triage;
- discipline;
- memorial care;
- guardianship.

Their maturity is real but emergency-shaped. They are children raising and killing other children because adult institutions have been destroyed or withdrawn.

## 12.5 Food, bathing, festivals, and sensory life

The stories repeatedly restore the body as more than machine interface:

- real food versus synthetic ration;
- hunting and cooking;
- bathing by a river;
- warmth and scarf touch;
- moon-viewing;
- fatigue;
- laughter.

Ordinary embodiment is a form of resistance because the state treats the body principally as consumable military material.

# 13. Childhood and interrupted development

## 13.1 Neoteny as uneven growth

Shin's development is not simply arrested.

It is accelerated in some domains and constrained in others.

He becomes highly developed in:

- combat perception;
- tactical decision;
- death ritual;
- command;
- emotional containment;
- practical survival.

He is deprived in:

- school continuity;
- safe attachment;
- ordinary adult modeling;
- medical trust;
- unstructured play;
- vocational exploration;
- expectation of long life.

`Fragmental` is therefore exact. Development occurs in fragments selected by war.

## 13.2 Adults who are also children

Alice, Iska, and other senior Processors look adult from Shin's perspective but are themselves adolescents.

This complicates moral judgment without suspending it.

Iska's cruelty may be intelligible as the product of a child forced into command and death work. It remains cruelty.

Alice's care is admirable. It should not be treated as proof the system supplied adequate adulthood.

## 13.3 Education as future-bearing capacity

The priest's teaching and Theo's current art discussion connect V10 back to V7–9.

Education matters because it gives the child more conceptual and practical languages than combat alone.

Even when used to survive war, reading and interpretation preserve the possibility of later nonmilitary selfhood.

## 13.4 Gentle-world adolescent Shin

The counterfactual teenager is not presented as a “more authentic” Shin. He demonstrates the developmental options that persecution removed:

- school;
- family conflict without annihilation;
- ordinary romance;
- hobbies;
- future planning;
- pet/AI companionship without military necessity.

The scene makes deprivation visible by showing what could have been ordinary.

# 14. Death, memory, grief, and inheritance

## 14.1 Portable graves

Name fragments become necessary because graves are prohibited.

They preserve:

- name;
- mark;
- relation;
- evidence of existence.

But the last survivor must carry them physically. Memory becomes weight in the literal logistical sense.

## 14.2 Inheritance without restoration

V10's objects do not restore the dead.

- Alice's scarf is not Alice.
- Iska's pistol is not Iska.
- Undertaker's emblem is not the squad.
- Fido's memory is not the Nouzen family.
- the name plates are not bodies.

The ethical value lies in maintaining relation without pretending the loss has been reversed.

## 14.3 Burden and support

Saiki's story grounds the V09 paradox.

Being entrusted with the dead is:

- unfair concentration of mourning;
- reason for survival;
- communal hope;
- barrier to asking for help;
- source of identity.

The V2 synthesis should not choose only “burden” or only “support.” The same structure changes function across time.

## 14.4 Mercy and release

The Black Sheep's `かえりたい` and Shin's `もう──かえっていい` turn mercy into permission to stop rather than command to continue.

This contrasts with the Undertaker promise, which tells the survivor to carry onward.

V10 therefore contains two ethical responses to the dead:

- carry what must be remembered;
- release what need not remain trapped.

## 14.5 Counterfactual mourning

`優しかった世界` mourns not only dead people but an entire unrealized history.

The lost object is a world in which no one needed the name Undertaker.

Shin can mourn that world without repudiating the actual people who now constitute his life.

# 15. Pride, heroism, and irreplaceability

## 15.1 Undertaker as survival office

The role makes Shin exceptional because the institution fails to distribute:

- memorial labor;
- warning intelligence;
- mercy work;
- emotional containment;
- continuity across annihilated units.

He becomes “heroic” partly because everyone else and every structure disappears.

This strongly supports the V7 thesis that heroism can be a sign of organizational failure.

## 15.2 Why others need the Reaper

The squad's reliance is not cowardice.

The Republic denies public mourning and future burial. Belief that someone will carry them supplies a minimal form of posthumous continuity.

The ethical failure is not that comrades ask for comfort.

It is that the system leaves one child as the only durable repository for everyone else's death.

## 15.3 Personal name as shield

`Undertaker` initially helps Shin maintain distance and survive expected loss.

This resembles Eighty-Six pride:

- adaptive under one condition;
- potentially enclosing when conditions change;
- historically meaningful even after revision.

By V10's present, the emblem remains without fully reproducing the old isolation.

## 15.4 Fido and replaceability

Fido's cross-body restoration complicates irreplaceability.

A subject can persist through hardware replacement if continuity of memory and organization survives.

That does not make destruction trivial. Fido experiences loss and relation, and its replacement is not automatic or institutionally guaranteed.

# 16. Freedom, paternalism, and self-authorship

## 16.1 Inherited fragments are not chosen origins

Shin's later ownership of the scarf, pistol, emblem, and role cannot retroactively make their origins free.

A mature freedom model must distinguish:

- choice of acquisition;
- choice of retention;
- choice of reinterpretation;
- practical ability to discard;
- social cost of leaving the role.

V10 shows more freedom in later **retention and reinterpretation** than in original acquisition.

## 16.2 Permission versus command

Alice's most important act is permission:

> you may still love Rei.

The priest's factual judgment becomes insufficient partly because it feels like an answer about what Shin should conclude.

Similarly, the present Theo does not command Shin to change his emblem. He asks whether Shin still wants it.

This is a recurring V2 distinction:

> **care expands agency when it offers permission and alternatives; it becomes paternalistic when it specifies the correct meaning the person must adopt.**

## 16.3 Designed purpose and Fido

Fido's case prevents a simple freedom criterion.

It has a designed imperative to protect humans and be their friend. Yet it interprets, particularizes, and repeatedly acts through that purpose.

The question is not whether it has no inheritance.

It is whether the inheritance leaves room for meaningful response.

## 16.4 The right not to hate

Shin's childhood need shows another form of freedom.

Survivors are often told they should hate the person who harmed them or forgive that person. Both can become imposed scripts.

Alice gives him room to retain a complicated attachment without denying the violence.

# 17. Ethics of command, violence, and responsibility

## 17.1 Alice's command

Alice combines:

- tactical authority;
- attention to child fear;
- memorial institution-building;
- ordinary-life protection.

She cannot eliminate risk but refuses to call individual deaths merely inevitable.

## 17.2 Iska's scapegoat doctrine

Iska believes one designated victim can stabilize the group.

The practice may produce cohesion. It is ethically indefensible because it transfers collective fear into predictable internal victimization.

This is a useful adversarial case against consequentialist command shortcuts under oppression.

## 17.3 Mercy killing

Shin's later mercy office is ethically different from casual execution because it responds to:

- irreversible injury;
- absence of rescue;
- consent or established unit expectation where available;
- prevention of prolonged suffering/Legion capture.

But the scene remains morally tragic because a child is deciding and performing death under preventable institutional abandonment.

## 17.4 Kujo and rescue

Kujo's death shows that compassion cannot substitute for tactical judgment.

Shin's warning is harsh and correct.

The ethical failure lies partly above both of them—in a system that makes rescue information late, direct communication forbidden, and self-exposure the only immediate option.

## 17.5 Responsibility for one's inheritance

V10 repeatedly distinguishes receiving a role from being morally innocent in how one continues it.

Shin inherits, but he also becomes responsible for:

- whether to retain;
- how to perform;
- whether to let the role exclude others;
- when to revise it.

The same principle later applies to institutions and political identities.

Child Shin's speech is restrained but not identical to adult Shin's.

# 20. Motifs, objects, and literary form

## 20.1 Fragments

Fragments are the collection's governing form and object.

- machine fragments carry names;
- memories arrive out of order;
- objects carry partial persons;
- stories reveal partial causes;
- the anthology refuses a seamless biography.

The form argues that identity is reconstructable but never perfectly whole.

## 20.2 Pale-blue scarf

The scarf's functions accumulate:

1. inherited from Alice's predecessor;
2. protection/grounding for Alice;
3. concealment of Shin's neck scar;
4. permission not to hate Rei;
5. container for Alice's severed head;
6. memorial object retained in the present.

No single symbolic gloss is sufficient.

## 20.3 Mercy pistol

The pistol is:

- proof of absent medical care;
- Iska's office;
- means of intimate killing;
- last-bullet promise of self-release;
- object Shin repairs and retains;
- instrument of care and burden.

## 20.4 Undertaker emblem

The emblem moves from insult and protective distance to a stable historical identity.

Theo's current redesign discussion shows visual authorship itself becoming relational: the mark is no longer solely the product of Seyah's accusation or Shin's isolation.

## 20.5 Butterfly

Saiki's butterfly story associates the dead with souls and imprisoned children with imagined flight.

Shin remains skeptical. The motif's value does not require literal endorsement.

It gives Saiki a language for release that the official system denies.

## 20.6 River / Lethe

The river is a liminal space between the Republic's death road and possible arrival elsewhere.

Forgetting is not complete erasure. The Five carry hundreds of names while moving through a landscape that invites release and uncertainty.

## 20.7 Fido's bodies

Fido's changing hardware becomes the strongest material image of identity continuity without bodily continuity.

The body matters—damage, capability, relation all change—but identity is not exhausted by one chassis.

## 20.8 Rose / gentler world

The final rose image shifts the book's material vocabulary away from weapons, fragments, and emblems toward living fragility.

It visually supports the possibility that current identity can retain the dead without requiring every future object to be a memorial instrument.

## 20.9 Countdowns and direction

The reverse and forward numeric sequences in the Fragmental stories formally prevent a smooth heroic origin myth.

The reader moves backward from outcomes and forward through repeated loss, assembling cause without receiving one definitive beginning.

# 21. Retrospective rereading of Volumes 1–9

This section uses V10 knowledge explicitly. It does not alter what earlier publication boundaries established.

## 21.1 Volume 1 — names were already an inherited institution

V1 correctly made name fragments central without identifying Alice as the origin of the explicit unit promise.

V10 strengthens the political reading:

- the archive did not arise as private eccentricity;
- it was a collective adaptation to prohibited graves;
- Shin inherited the practice from an older child commander;
- his position as final carrier was contingency repeatedly converted into office.

V1's “Undertaker” should therefore be reread as the visible endpoint of a hidden institutional genealogy.

## 21.2 Volume 1 — Lena did not create the hope for relational rescue

Kujo hoped that a decent Handler might become one more reason for Shin to remain connected to life. Other comrades already recognized that Shin needed someone beyond the role.

Lena matters profoundly because she becomes a person able to answer and transform that need.

She does not originate the diagnosis or Shin's capacity for attachment.

This further weakens any sole-savior romance model.

## 21.3 Volumes 2–3 — Fido's survival is not equipment restoration

V10 reveals the Fido restored in Giad is the same narrating subject that knew infant Shin, searched for the family, followed adolescent Shin, and chose the Special Reconnaissance journey.

The emotional continuity of Fido's return is therefore stronger than V2–3 could establish prospectively.

But the original V2 reader did not yet know this identity.

## 21.4 Volumes 3–6 — Shin's abandonment structure is older and more social than Rei alone

V10 reveals repeated squad annihilation, transfer, scapegoating, and role concentration. Shin is not only abandoned by family/death. He is repeatedly made the surviving repository after communities disappear.

This strengthens abandonment as a major structure while also making it less reducible to one attachment wound.

It includes:

- family;
- squad;
- institution;
- adult care;
- peer dependency;
- the practical fact of being left with everyone's remains.

## 21.5 Volumes 4–6 — memory objects can change function

The scarf, pistol, and emblem show that the meaning of memory is not set at acquisition.

This supports V4–6's contested-memory model:

- keeping an object does not prove one remains trapped in its first meaning;
- discarding it is not required for change;
- current ownership can be an act of reinterpretation.

## 21.6 Volumes 5–9 — assigned purpose is not automatically empty purpose

Fido, Undertaker, Alice's promise, and even Iska's role all complicate a simple opposition between assigned role and authentic self.

A role can be inherited and still become meaningful through response.

The stronger criterion from V09 survives:

> Does the person retain standing to revise, share, refuse, or eventually leave the role?

## 21.7 Volume 7 — Shin's dignity assertion has a long prehistory

The V7 line `おれは家畜でもなければ無人機の部品でもない` gains force after V10.

Child Shin had repeatedly treated names as mere identifiers and his body as something that had to continue for a promise. V7's explicit rejection of imposed ontology is therefore not a simple repetition of what he always knew. It is a new first-person claim to standing after years of functional self-definition.

## 21.8 Volumes 8–9 — Theo's disability answer is rooted in the Sector's medical regime

V10 makes the contrast sharper.

Theo's ability to survive, receive treatment, retain institutional standing, and search for a noncombat self is not only personal resilience. It depends on an institution refusing the Sector's triage rule that a body without immediate combat return is already dead.

## 21.9 Volume 9 — burden/support was not merely retrospective wisdom

Saiki's story supplies direct origin evidence for Shin's V9 statement that the role was both heavy and supporting.

The social dependence was visible from the beginning, even if the unit lacked a way to distribute it.

## 21.10 The full-series master thesis after V10

V10 strengthens the right-to-change thesis but adds a necessary historical dimension.

A person is not free simply because a later institution says, “you may now choose.”

They carry fragments acquired under coercion. Freedom includes the material and relational ability to:

- understand those inheritances;
- retain some;
- reject some;
- change their function;
- and refuse the claim that valuing one fragment justifies the violence that produced it.

# 23. Governing hypothesis tests

## H1 — Recognition expands toward conditions for a revisable life

**V10 status: STRENGTHENED / HISTORICIZED.**

Revisability requires more than future permission. It requires enough safety, education, relationship, and institutional support to reinterpret roles acquired under coercion.

## H2 — The core opposition is systems that permit revision versus systems that freeze people into functions

**V10 status: STRENGTHENED, WITH AN IMPORTANT QUALIFICATION.**

Roles themselves are not the enemy. Alice's promise and Undertaker can be sustaining. The problem is compulsory exclusivity and absence of exit/reinterpretation.

## H3 — Eighty-Six pride is dignity and possible prison

**V10 status: STRENGTHENED BY GENEALOGY.**

The same structure appears in Undertaker: a necessary survival role becomes dangerous when the social world asks one person to be nothing else.

## H4 — *86* is anti-war without being pacifist

**V10 status: STRENGTHENED / COMPLICATED.**

The stories admire tactical brilliance, command care, and comradeship while making clear that these virtues are being extracted from children by a genocidal system.

## H5 — Home is chosen and relational

**V10 status: STRENGTHENED.**

“Return” first appears through squad, machine companion, dead desire, possible human polity, and family AI before later romantic home. Home remains plural and historically layered.

## H6 — Mature interdependence differs from saviorism and self-sufficiency

**V10 status: STRENGTHENED RETROSPECTIVELY.**

Comrades already saw Shin's isolation and tried imperfectly to support him. The problem was not absence of care, but lack of durable structures capable of sharing his office.

## H7 — Exceptional heroism is admirable and institutionally dangerous

**V10 status: STRONGLY STRENGTHENED.**

Undertaker is an institutional single point of failure for memory, warning, mercy, and symbolic continuity.

## H8 — Shin moves from death-facing function toward future-bearing selfhood

**V10 status: STRENGTHENED / NONLINEAR.**

The anthology shows future-bearing capacities—curiosity, relation, education, ordinary pleasure—pre-existed but were subordinated. Later development is expansion, not creation from nothing.

## H9 — Lena moves from witness toward bounded responsibility

**V10 status: INDIRECTLY STRENGTHENED.**

Kujo's story shows her moral concern before Spearhead and clarifies the institutional rescue failures she later works to correct. It does not grant her causal responsibility for Kujo's death.

## H10 — Romance changes future time rather than merely rewarding survival

**V10 status: STRENGTHENED / DECENTERED.**

The gentler-world story and pre-Lena relationships show romance is one major future structure among family, comrades, artificial friendship, education, curiosity, and political hope.

# 26. Cumulative-series delta after Volume 10

## Character

- **Shin:** Undertaker reclassified from essential identity to socially assembled, selectively inherited, and revisable office. Compassion predates the role. Interpersonal reserve gains a concrete history in expected death and defensive distance.
- **Alice:** foundational source of name-fragment promise, scarf, grounding, and permission not to hate Rei; herself a child soldier inheriting adult care.
- **Iska:** morally mixed predecessor whose useful role can be retained without affection, forgiveness, or idealization.
- **Saiki:** direct evidence that the unit both needed Shin's office and recognized its unfair weight.
- **Fido:** direct first-person artificial subject with cross-body continuity, designed purpose, mixed motive, and active choice.
- **Lena:** pre-Spearhead ethical concern confirmed without retroactively making her responsible for or acquainted with Kujo.

## Relationship

- Shin/dead: communal institution and political counter-archive, not merely private pathology.
- Shin/Fido: one of the oldest continuous relationships in the corpus; recognition and return precede settled ontology.
- Shin/Rei: factual innocence and continued love become separately necessary truths.
- Shin/Lena: romance further decentered from sole-recovery causality while retaining major transformative importance.

## Institution

- administrative dehumanization now includes systematic removal of education, medicine, burial, rescue, and adult developmental infrastructure;
- internal child-built institutions partially compensate;
- medical triage explicitly treats time-to-combat-return as the criterion of worth;
- graves and direct communication are politically controlled.

## Military

- Shin's close-combat method originates as adaptation to radically inferior equipment;
- institutional learning is concentrated in survivors and repeatedly destroyed;
- mercy work and warning intelligence become singular dependencies;
- genuine unmanned-war counterfactual demonstrates genocide was not technically inevitable while introducing artificial-subject ethics.

## Personhood / AI

- Fido's subjectivity is directly established rather than inferred solely from human behavior;
- body/chassis continuity is not necessary for identity continuity in Fido's case;
- designed purpose and agency coexist in tension;
- legible desire can deserve response under uncertain continuity, as with the Black Sheep.

## Body / disability / age

- V09 Theo contrast is historicized through explicit Sector medicine;
- child combat competence is separated from bodily/developmental suitability;
- accommodations such as posture, naming, and scarf can protect without constituting cure.

## Memory / inheritance

- objects are active sites of reinterpretation;
- carrying does not restore;
- inherited fragments can be retained without endorsing their source;
- counterfactual mourning can coexist with present affirmation.

## Ordinary life / future

- ordinary resistance before rescue receives its strongest additional evidence;
- repeatable festivals and daily plans function as future practice;
- Shin's nonmilitary curiosity and family relation predate Lena and survive beneath Undertaker.

## Japanese voice

- names function as archive, grounding address, defensive distance, and recognition;
- `我らが` exposes communal possession of the Reaper role;
- child Shin's terse voice is continuous with adult Shin while remaining more questioning;
- Fido's formal `わたくし` narration establishes a distinctive nonhuman first-person voice.

## Motifs/form

- fragment becomes governing formal principle;
- scarf, pistol, emblem, river, butterfly, Fido body, and rose form an inheritance/revision system;
- non-linear counts prevent a seamless heroic origin myth.

# Closing V10 synthesis

V10 could easily have become a tragic origin story in which every object falls into place and the reader is shown the inevitable creation of a perfect death god.

It does something more demanding.

It shows that there was no single origin.

There was:

- a dead friend;
- a seventeen-year-old commander carrying someone else's scarf;
- a priest trying to preserve literacy and political memory;
- a cruel squad leader holding a mercy pistol;
- a mechanic who let a strange machine come home;
- a commander who believed a warning;
- a grieving boy who named a plague;
- a squad that needed a Reaper;
- an AI that kept choosing to stay;
- a Republic that prohibited graves and medicine;
- a child who accepted fragment after fragment because someone had to.

The cover is therefore exact:

> **These fragments turned the boy into the Grim Reaper.**

But “turned” must not be mistaken for “determined.”

Current Shin has changed what several fragments mean.

The scarf no longer merely hides the scar.

The emblem no longer requires total isolation.

The pistol remains without making Iska beloved.

Fido's machine body no longer hides a merely hypothetical subject.

Undertaker can remain a name without remaining the complete horizon of the self.

This gives V2 its strongest model yet of historical selfhood:

> **A person is neither an untouched essence beneath history nor the passive sum of what history did to them. A person continues by answering inherited fragments—keeping, rejecting, sharing, mourning, and changing their meaning under conditions that may or may not make such revision possible.**

The gentler-world dream then prevents this model from becoming a romance of suffering.

Shin can say the other world would have been better.

He can say he should never have needed to become Undertaker.

He can also refuse to say that the people he now loves should therefore be erased.

The moral demand is not to become grateful for atrocity.

It is to build a future in which no child must receive identity as a pile of weapons, scars, names, and last requests—and in which those who already did can decide what the fragments mean now.
