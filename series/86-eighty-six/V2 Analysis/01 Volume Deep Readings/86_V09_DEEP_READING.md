## 1.3 Visual paratext

V09's illustration program strongly reinforces the volume's concern with **girls and young women placed at the front of inherited roles**.

- A major color insert explicitly reads `最前線に立つ、少女たちの想いは──。` and groups several young female combatants/command figures against a military-base background. The editorial emphasis is not merely “girls in uniforms”; it links multiple forms of frontline obligation before the narrative compares their very different degrees of choice.
- A paired Shin/Lena color spread places them face-to-face while printing Lena's explicit confession, `わたしも──あなたが好きです`, beside language rejecting a merely death-bounded happiness. This gives the V09 verbal answer the status of a major publication event rather than a buried romantic beat.
- The illustration associated with Kurena's early breakdown emphasizes bodily distress and regression rather than glamorous combat competence.
- The illustration associated with Theo/Ishmael places Theo's grief and Ishmael's scarred veteran presence in a quiet, nonbattlefield frame; disability is treated as lived interruption rather than inspirational abstraction.
- Hielna's portrait presents an ornate, almost sacred visual role-body. Her costuming materially supports the political problem: a child can be honored, adorned, and revered while still being imprisoned inside an inherited function.
- Equipment diagrams continue to separate tactical technology from metaphor. The new airborne assault equipment and Holy Theocracy platforms are military objects first; their symbolic use must remain secondary.

These are `VI` evidence. They establish editorial emphasis, body/role presentation, and relational framing, not unspoken motives.

## 1.4 Afterword boundary

The afterword supplies two unusually valuable pieces of paratext.

First, Asato states that the `神戟` setting was one of the prototypes from which the Eighty-Six concept developed. This authorial genealogy explains the unusually strong structural rhyme but must not be converted into in-world moral identity: the Holy Theocracy is not simply “another San Magnolia.”

Second, Asato explicitly marks an architectural transition:

> `四巻からのエイティシックスたちの誇りと傷の物語は本巻にて一段落し`

and says what follows will be their battle toward the future.

This confirms V09 as a deliberate **checkpoint/end of the V4–9 pride-and-wounds movement**. It does not mean those themes disappear; it means the series considers enough groundwork established to change the primary dramatic question.

# 2. Volume thesis

The epigraph is the volume's most exact abstract statement:

> `誇り。願い。絆。祈り。あるいは──呪い。`

V09 is about **the moral instability of sustaining attachments**.

A pride, wish, bond, prayer, role, memory, promise, or love can keep someone alive.

The same thing can become a curse when it becomes a condition of identity that prohibits revision, departure, disagreement, or an alternative future.

The volume systematically tests this through several parallel cases.

### Theo

The dead commander's memory and the Eighty-Six pride once kept him moving. After losing his arm, Theo recognizes that he may temporarily need the dead man's wish as support. He explicitly calls it potentially a curse, but says that until he finds something of his own it can function as a `願い`.

His solution is not radical independence. It is **temporary scaffold with an exit condition**.

### Shin

The role `我らが死神` was genuinely heavy:

> `重かった。本当は、最初からずっと`

Yet Shin also says the weight prevented him from simply giving up:

> `支えてもらってたのはおれも同じだ`

The Undertaker role was therefore burden **and** support. It becomes pathological only if either side pretends the burden is weightless or makes it impossible for Shin to stop carrying it.

### Kurena

Her love for Shin, battlefield competence, and Eighty-Six pride all functioned as protection against a childhood conviction of helplessness. She fears a future outside battle not because she literally enjoys war, but because happiness can be taken away and she has learned to associate usefulness with the ability to prevent abandonment.

Hielna and Svenja let Kurena see what her attachments could become if she demanded that other people remain wounded and unchanged so she would not have to lose her place.

Her final confession therefore does not erase love. It changes its **claim structure**:

> `今でも好き。……きっとずっと、好きなまま`

but:

> `幸せになってね。必ず──幸せになってね`

She permits Shin's happiness to take a form that does not choose her.

### Hielna

Her entire identity is manufactured from injury. Her family is deliberately sacrificed so she can become a national sacred symbol. When the state later abandons the supposedly immutable “destiny” that justified generations of soldier-caste sacrifice, she concludes that the only remaining thing she owns—her wound and martial role—is being stolen.

Her response is the limit-case of curse:

> if my wound no longer has to determine the future, then the future itself should disappear.

### Svenja / Gilwiese / Milmelkoleo

Their shared wound is real. Their solidarity is real. Their military role gives them social value after aristocratic blood politics treated them as failed human products.

Precisely because the role was life-preserving, Svenja experiences Gilwiese's desire to reject it as abandonment. The group begins to reproduce its cage internally: **we are the same wounded people, therefore none of us may become different.**

### Shin / Lena

V09 makes their love fully verbal and reciprocal. The relationship is not automatically exempt from the volume's curse problem. Lena uses absolute language—`死にも二人を、分かたせない`—that could become possessive in another structure.

What keeps the current relation on the “wish/bond” rather than “curse” side is behavioral:

- Shin may be weak and ask support.
- Theo's independent choice is respected.
- Lena's answer is voluntarily given after Shin explicitly allowed her not to answer until ready.
- return is reciprocal rather than one person defining the other's life.
- neither is required to become identical to the other.

The central V09 proposition is therefore:

> **An attachment remains life-giving when it can support a person without demanding that the person—or everyone bound to them—remain the same in order for the attachment to stay valid.**

That is the strongest formulation yet of V2's `right to change` thesis.

# 3. Competing theses and interpretive risks

## 3.1 Risk: every commitment becomes “a curse”

The epigraph does not say that pride, wish, bond, and prayer are merely deceptive names for curse.

Theo explicitly distinguishes a temporary `願い` from a binding `呪い`. Shin explains that being relied upon was heavy but sustaining. Kurena refuses to call her love a mistake. The volume consistently protects the **positive historical truth** of these attachments.

The analytical test is not whether a commitment constrains at all. Every meaningful commitment constrains.

The better falsifier is:

> **Does the commitment preserve the person's standing to revise its meaning, loosen it, share it differently, or eventually leave it?**

## 3.2 Risk: Holy Theocracy = Republic

The structural rhyme is intentional and even confirmed by the afterword's production history.

But the institutions differ sharply.

San Magnolia:

- racializes former citizens;
- calls them subhuman livestock;
- strips legal status;
- forces military service;
- hides casualties;
- intends disappearance.

The Holy Theocracy:

- assigns social role largely by birth across society;
- regards `神戟` as nonhuman in a sacred rather than animal sense;
- treats them with ritual respect and education;
- links occupation and bloodline systematically;
- nevertheless denies occupational autonomy and historically concentrates military death in one hereditary caste.

Both systems subordinate individual revision to assigned ontology.

They do **not** do so through the same law, ideology, or social valuation.

## 3.3 Risk: Hielna is simply “Kurena gone bad”

Hielna is a mirror, and the text says so.

She is not Kurena's deterministic endpoint.

Hielna has undergone:

- deliberate state sacrifice of her family;
- transformation into a national grief-symbol;
- repeated ideological teaching that destiny is sacred;
- later discovery that the state treats destiny opportunistically.

Kurena recognizes a possible shared logic—wound as only remaining identity—not an inevitable personality outcome.

The ethical value of the mirror lies precisely in Kurena being able to **see and refuse** the possible endpoint.

## 3.4 Risk: Kurena's arc is finally “getting over Shin”

This remains too reductive.

V09 directly grounds Kurena's battlefield fixation in childhood powerlessness:

> `役に立てないのは嫌だ。無力なのは怖い。`

Her romantic attachment to Shin intersects with:

- rescue;
- protective competence;
- fear of being useless;
- fear that happiness will be stolen again;
- battlefield identity;
- desire for a stable place in the group.

Her final confession is important, but it is one consequence of a broader reconstruction of agency.

## 3.5 Risk: Theo's injury is secretly a gift

V09 partially answers the V08 personhood-beyond-function question, but it does not romanticize amputation.

Theo says plainly:

> `プロセッサー、やめたくない`

and:

> `戦いたいわけじゃないけど、みんなと一緒に最後まで戦いたかった`

His injury is an unwanted loss.

What becomes positive is **what the surrounding institution and relationships permit after loss**, not the loss itself.

## 3.6 Risk: Theo has now solved his future

Not yet.

The hospital interlude shows flatness, inability to enjoy even entertainment, and uncertainty about what to do.

Annette gives him a deliberately small model:

> `そんなものでいいでしょ`

and Theo reaches:

> `まだ死んでないから、何かをしたい。`

The first concrete result is not a vocation. It is fried bread and asking for his sketchbook.

This is **reactivated desire**, not completed identity reconstruction.

## 3.7 Risk: Shin/Lena has become a totalizing “until death and after death” bond

Lena's wording is deliberately absolute and should remain under scrutiny.

But V09's actual relation is not structured like Hielna's or Svenja's curse:

- neither needs the other to remain wounded;
- each can criticize the other;
- Shin can admit weakness without losing standing;
- Lena does not require Shin to die for her or remain Undertaker;
- Shin waited for her answer rather than treating the kiss as ownership.

The relationship is strongly committed, but V09 does not yet show the **prohibition of revision** that would make it structurally equivalent to the curse cases.

## 3.8 Risk: Shin's command in the betrayal proves he is simply “the hero who always saves everyone”

Shin explicitly refuses that framing.

He continues the mission because abandoning the offensive capability and intelligence objective could endanger Giad's future. He tells the angry Eighty-Six that their grievance is legitimate and that if risk becomes unacceptable, withdrawal must be reconsidered.

He is not saying:

> good soldiers must sacrifice themselves for ungrateful allies.

He is saying:

> **anger at an ally does not erase the strategic consequences of a mission that also protects your own future.**

This is professional judgment, not martyr ethics.

## 3.9 Risk: “warrior pride” naturally produces atrocity

Milmelkoleo's battlefield bloodlust is a real warning, but its causal structure is more specific.

They have been told they are failed mixed-blood products and receive social recognition only by demonstrating aristocratic warrior value. Victory becomes existential proof. Their laughter during killing is not “what pride always becomes”; it is pride fused to conditional human worth and an institution that rewards spectacular martial validation.

## 3.10 Risk: V09's ending means the pride/wounds problem is finished

The afterword says this movement reaches `一段落`, not that every person is healed or every institutional danger is resolved.

Theo has only begun rehabilitation. Kurena has only just restructured her love. Hielna and the `神戟` enter a future they did not choose. Shin still self-instrumentalizes bodily. The war continues.

V09 closes an **analytical movement**, not the consequences.

# 4. Causal and chapter map

## 4.1 Epigraph — five names for one structure

`誇り。願い。絆。祈り。あるいは──呪い。`

The epigraph instructs the reader to compare apparently different attachments by **function**, not sentimental valence.

It prepares the volume's central question:

> What lets a sustaining meaning remain sustaining rather than becoming compulsory identity?

## 4.2 Prologue — `暴食の獣`

The Legion's new assault-landship experiment asks whether integration is possible.

This creates the machine-side version of V09's bond problem before the human stories unfold: separate military systems can be joined into larger capability, but integration threatens the integrity of the parts.

The later human cases—wounded comrades, fused identity, role-castes, shared trauma—turn “integration” into a social rather than merely mechanical question.

## 4.3 Chapter 1 — `人魚の取引`

### Theo's injury becomes an institutional test

The immediate discussion is practical.

Theo probably cannot remain a Reginleif Processor. No available prosthetic can reproduce the reaction and precision needed for high-mobility Feldreß combat.

But Giad's first response is not disposal.

A special officer who cannot remain combat-capable may receive another military role. Medical treatment was given because he was injured, not because treatment would restore combat utility.

This contrast becomes explicit when Theo remembers the Sector:

> `僕はもう、戦えない`

but:

> `でも、死ななくていい`

That sentence is one of V09's most important institutional-personhood statements.

### Shin's improved self-assessment

When other group commanders ask whether he is all right, Shin begins to say `平気` and stops himself.

Instead:

> `ずいぶん動揺してると思う。無茶を言ったと思ったら、指摘してくれ`

This is a major behavioral improvement.

He no longer treats self-knowledge as sufficient to guarantee safe command. He creates an **external correction mechanism**.

### Other Eighty-Six and school

The other group commanders voluntarily cut leave short for operational training, but also say:

> `戦闘だけこなしていればいいって言うんじゃなくてさ`

and:

> `楽しいですね、学生というのは`

The important thing is heterogeneity.

The Eighty-Six can:

- choose military urgency;
- appreciate education;
- imagine finishing school after the war;
- joke about how much education remains.

The text no longer supports a singular group psychology in which “Eighty-Six cannot imagine anything but battle.”

### Kurena's collapse

Kurena isolates, refuses food, and tries to reactivate Sector survival logic.

Mika denies Kurena's desired guilt over Shana:

> `シャナが死んだのは、シャナが戦ったから`

and refuses to let Kurena appropriate Shana's choice as a way of punishing herself.

Kurena then states the crisis plainly:

> `戦うしかないんだよ。`

> `誇りしかない。`

> `その誇りさえも奪われる`

Theo's survival-without-combat terrifies her because it proves that an Eighty-Six can be forced to continue living after the role around which Eighty-Six identity was built has disappeared.

### Childhood embodiment

Kurena reacts strongly to a large silver-haired priest because his physical presence resembles the adults who dominated terrified children during forced relocation.

The priest recognizes the response and deliberately changes how he positions himself.

This is valuable body-level evidence: trauma is not only a proposition characters think. It exists in how adult scale, hair color, proximity, and posture can become threatening before conscious evaluation catches up.

### Future as threat

Kurena knows she could join Shin, Raiden, and Anju in study.

She cannot make herself enter.

The narration gives the reason:

> `戦場の外の、未来というものを意識するのが恐ろしくて、考えたくない`

and:

> `望めば幸福な明日が来るなんて──信じられない`

This is crucial.

Her future resistance is not mere love triangle or pride fetish.

Hope itself feels unsafe because anything desired can be taken.

## 4.4 Theo and Shin — burden becomes reciprocity

Theo contrasts his present treatment with a Sector comrade who died from an equivalent injury because a noncombat-capable Eighty-Six was not worth treating.

He says:

> `ここは本当に八六区じゃないんだな`

The Federacy's difference becomes bodily fact rather than political abstraction.

Theo then uses the volume's epigraph vocabulary with extraordinary precision. He will lean on the dead commander's wish for now:

> `縛られたら本当に呪いになっちゃうけど`

but until he finds something else:

> `願いみたいなものでしょ`

This is the V09 model of ethical scaffolding.

Then he apologizes for `我らが死神`.

Shin finally admits:

> `重かった。本当は、最初からずっと`

but immediately complicates that admission:

> `支えてもらってたのはおれも同じだ`

The dead-name burden worked because the people entrusting themselves to him also gave him a reason not to abandon himself.

The relationship was unequal in burden, but not one-way in psychological function.

## 4.5 Shin/Lena — explicit verbal reciprocity

After Theo's injury Shin admits:

> `何かできている、つもりだったんです`

and then that there is nothing he can do to reverse Theo's loss.

This is an important attack on savior identity.

Lena does not answer by telling him how to fix Theo.

She says:

> `頼ってください`

> `共に、抱えますから`

and then finally gives the verbal answer left pending since V7:

> `わたしも──あなたが好きです`

> `一緒に生きたい`

The sequence matters.

Love is spoken **after the limit of rescue has been acknowledged**.

The relationship is therefore not positioned as a technology for removing grief or disability.

It is a commitment to remain present while some things cannot be repaired.

## 4.6 Legion interlude — immortality of Shepherds

Vika and Zelene infer that the high-mobility type is research toward:

> `〈羊飼い〉の不死化`

Existing Shepherds are described as clinging to:

> `己の人格と存在の保持`

and survival.

This continues the collapse of the old “dead cannot change” thesis. Human-derived Legion minds may preserve personality, desire continuity, fear destruction, and seek technical means of persistence.

The philosophical boundary now moves from life/death to **what kinds of agency and revision remain possible within coercive machine architecture**.

## 4.7 Chapter 2 — `灰かぶりの戦場`

### Holy Theocracy

The Holy Theocracy's doctrine holds that divine role stands above individual choice. Occupation and marriage are substantially determined by house:

> `選択の自由はない`

The `神戟` are especially revealing. They are said to be **not human**, but not livestock:

> `神戟は人ではないが、神の御使いである`

They may receive respect and unusually high education while lacking basic authority to determine whether military function will define their lives.

This is the most important V09 institutional correction:

> **dehumanization is not always contempt. A system can honor a category while denying its members the standing to revise the category.**

### Frederica and the shutdown key

Shin now accepts Frederica's participation in the war-termination plan because Theo's injury makes further avoidable sacrifice harder to tolerate:

> `セオのような犠牲を、これ以上は増やせない`

Frederica insists:

> relying on her is relying on a comrade, not using a helpless child.

Shin retains a boundary:

> `お前を犠牲にはしない`

This is a meaningful compromise between protection and agency. It does not end the paternalism problem, but the relationship is no longer unilateral veto.

### Anju and Dustin

Anju corrects a belief about herself.

`ダイヤを今でも好き` remains true.

The inference that she therefore cannot want Dustin is false.

She asks Dustin to keep his promise not to die and leave her, while promising return herself.

This repeats the volume's general structure:

> **a prior bond can remain real without owning all subsequent attachments.**

## 4.8 Interlude — `青い鳥はどこにいたのか`

Theo tells Ishmael:

> `プロセッサー、やめたくない`

He does not experience loss of combat function as liberation.

He wanted to fight **with the others to the end**, even though he did not want war for its own sake.

Ishmael, who has lost his own inherited maritime role, does not hurry him toward a replacement identity.

Theo asks:

> `僕にも見つかるかな。次の何かって`

The answer is yes, but without timetable.

Theo then corrects Frederica's older blood-and-land theory of identity.

A person cannot preserve shape alone:

> `己一人では己の形を保てない`

His comrades are `縁`—relations that help form him—and the crucial fact is:

> even unable to fight, he can still return if he wants.

This is the first prospective partial answer to V08's disability/personhood question.

Belonging is shown to survive loss of combat function.

But material rehabilitation, role change, bodily adaptation, and vocational identity remain unfinished.

## 4.9 Chapter 3 — `彼女の首を刎ねよ`

Kurena's underlying fear becomes much clearer.

She remembers her parents being shot and her sister being taken to battle. The childhood logic is one of powerless witness.

The narration gives the central sentence:

> `役に立てないのは嫌だ。無力なのは怖い。`

and sharper still:

> `自分の無力を思い知らされることこそが、本当は一番怖い。`

This recasts much of Kurena's behavior.

Usefulness beside Shin has been one way to defend against returning to the helpless child who could not stop adults from taking everything.

The future is frightening because hope creates something that can again be stolen.

### Shin's command trust

Gilwiese wants Kurena removed from the operation because of her V08 freeze.

Shin refuses to define a soldier by one failure:

> `一度の過ちで兵を切り捨てては隊など成りたたない`

and:

> `彼女が今後も立ち直れはしないと、判断される謂れはありません`

If she fails:

> `その時にはおれが取り返します`

This is one of the clearest anti-disposability command principles in the series so far.

A reliable organization is not one in which humans never fail.

It is one with enough trust, redundancy, and leadership capacity to **recover from human failure without discarding the human**.

Kurena then remembers why she became a better shooter:

not to earn permission to stay beside Shin, but:

> `少しでも助けになるために`

This is a subtle shift from usefulness as price of belonging toward competence as chosen contribution.

## 4.10 Interlude — `ところでジークフリートの殺し方を知るには`

Theo returns to Giad and becomes emotionally flat.

He cannot even motivate himself toward entertainment.

A younger Eighty-Six child visits, and Theo later regrets being cold. This is important evidence that the future is already producing **younger people toward whom Theo can become an older survivor**, not merely a disabled ex-Processor.

Annette's future model is deliberately ordinary:

- clothes;
- good food;
- new films;
- throwing cream pies at friends.

When Theo asks whether such things are enough, Annette answers:

> `そんなものでいいでしょ`

and says that after one small desire is satisfied another will probably appear.

Theo's realization is one of V09's most important future formulations:

> `何かをしたいから死にたくない、じゃなくて。まだ死んでないから、何かをしたい。`

Life need not wait for a grand purpose to justify continuation.

Continued life can generate desire iteratively.

Theo chooses fried bread as a temporary goal and asks Annette to bring his sketchbook.

This is not trivial filler.

It is the reactivation of a noncombat capacity that existed before disability but had not previously needed to carry so much identity weight.

## 4.11 Chapter 4 — `鏡よ鏡、ただの鏡に映るのは？`

The Holy Theocracy betrays the Strike Package.

The immediate political plan is Hielna's, not an uncomplicated national consensus. Her goal is much darker than retaining the Eighty-Six as military assets.

She wants an atrocity whose consequences will poison trust among states:

- Eighty-Six die after being betrayed by a state they came to assist;
- Giad's public turns on its government;
- Holy Theocracy is disgraced and loses aid;
- Giad becomes more reluctant to help other states;
- isolated countries fall;
- perhaps eventually everyone loses the future Hielna was denied.

She calls it what the narration makes explicit:

> a **collective suicide** organized around preservation of the only role left to them.

### Shin's strategic refusal of martyr framing

Some Eighty-Six want to abandon the mission. Their anger is legitimate.

Shin continues because the Legion target and intelligence matter to **their own survival and war-termination prospects**.

He does not say their feelings are wrong:

> `不満はわかる`

He also says:

> `身を賭してやる必要はない`

and withdrawal should be reconsidered if danger crosses the line.

This is a mature version of V1 pride:

> do not fight because someone declares it your destiny;
> do not refuse strategically necessary action merely because the ally is contemptible;
> preserve both agency and long-horizon consequence.

### Lena's correction and overgeneralization

Lena declares:

> `貴方たちは英雄なんかじゃない`

and:

> `戦うのが運命ではない`

She then says the Eighty-Six fight to end the war.

Kurena immediately supplies the corrective: **not all of them do**.

This is exactly the kind of intratextual resistance V2 must preserve.

Lena correctly rejects assigned destiny.

She overgeneralizes Shin's increasingly future-oriented motive to the group.

## 4.12 Hielna as mirror

Hielna's state construction is horrifyingly precise.

Her family was intentionally abandoned so she could become the sacred symbol of national grief:

> `わたくしの家族は、聖教国と軍に見捨てられた`

When the religious leadership later decides to draft ordinary believers because the soldier caste is almost exhausted, Hielna interprets this as proof that “destiny” was never sacred enough to justify what was taken from her.

If destiny can be revised when elites themselves face danger, then the soldiers' centuries of sacrifice have been converted into an arbitrary theft.

Her response:

> `戦争が、終わらなければいい`

is the V09 curse in its clearest form.

The wound must remain necessary because if the wound no longer defines the future, she fears there is nothing left to validate the past.

Kurena sees her as a mirror.

The text does not say they are identical.

It says Kurena can recognize the possible endpoint of an identity that makes wound, pride, and belonging mutually dependent.

## 4.13 Human enemies

The Eighty-Six discover that their confidence in `戦いぬく` was partly enabled by the fact that their enemy had been Legion.

They have killed humans in mercy-killing contexts.

They have not ordinarily fought humans **as enemy combatants**.

Facing child `神戟` produces:

> `撃てない──戦えないだろうな。おれたちは……人とは`

This is an important complication of the anti-war/not-pacifist thesis.

Their warrior identity does not imply generalized willingness to kill.

The object of violence matters morally.

## 4.14 Milmelkoleo's mirror

The Federacy's aristocratic volunteer regiment initially looks like the opposite case: soldiers exhilarated by victory where the Eighty-Six freeze.

Then the narration supplies the institutional history.

They are mixed-blood “failures” produced by aristocratic breeding projects, socially despised and confined. Heroic military service is offered as the one way to become valuable.

Gilwiese finally names the role:

> `奴隷の首輪だ`

Svenja cannot accept that.

For her, the role and the other wounded people in the same “kennel” are all she possesses:

> `わたくしたちはこの御役目と、お互いしかいない`

and:

> `同じ傷しか持たない同じ犬舎の役立たずの仲間同士`

Kurena recognizes the mechanism:

> `誇りという名の、傷という名の呪いをかけあっている`

This is V09's clearest description of **mutual curse**.

Belonging becomes coercive when shared injury turns into a membership rule:

> if one person heals, changes, leaves the role, or wants something else, they are experienced as abandoning everyone whose identity still depends on sameness.

## 4.15 Hielna's defeat

Holy Theocracy's own command structure contains a literal coercive mechanism: a hidden sacred phrase capable of stopping `神戟` combat regardless of individual will.

The polity that calls their role sacred has therefore also encoded the possibility of externally suspending it.

That is an almost perfect institutional embodiment of V09's critique:

> destiny is absolute for the subordinate until authority decides otherwise.

Hielna's final turn is small but significant.

Lena tells her that if her wish was to prevent anything more being taken from her people, she must not make her own death one more thing taken from the soldiers who protected her.

Hielna finally says:

> `わたくしだけは、わたくしの部下たちを見捨ててはいけないですよね`

Her first movement out of martyr identity is not self-love.

It is responsibility to people whose attachment to her remains alive.

## 4.16 Shiden

After killing the Phönix/Shana-derived enemy, Shiden acknowledges that the battlefield had been a comfortable enough home once she accepted it.

Then she says:

> `戦場にいる限り、……こんなことになるんだよな。仲間が誰か、死ぬんだよな`

and:

> `戦争とかもう、たくさんだから`

Her desired future is refreshingly unsublime:

> `一生、気楽に楽しく生きていきてぇよな`

This matters because it provides another non-Shin, non-romantic route toward wanting the war to end.

## 4.17 Kurena's final transformation

On the return train, Kurena tells Lena:

> `どうなりたいかは結局は、あの子たちにしかわかんない`

and:

> `自由というなら、自分で決めたい`

She has moved from fearing others' change to defending their right to decide.

Then she seeks Shin out and says:

> `あたし、あなたのことが好きだった`

before correcting the tense emotionally:

> `今でも好き。……きっとずっと、好きなまま`

She imagines that she may someday love someone else and even make a family, without needing current love to become false.

This is perhaps V09's clearest answer to the volume's epigraph:

> **a bond stops being a curse not when it disappears, but when it ceases to demand exclusive possession of the future.**

Shin's answer is only:

> `──悪い`

That apparent severity is ethically important.

He does not offer counterfeit comfort that would blur his nonreciprocity.

Kurena can retain the meaning of loving him without requiring him to validate it as romance.

## 4.18 Epilogue — clock still moves inside the crocodile

Theo is discharged from the hospital but not automatically from military life.

He may:

- change branch;
- receive different education;
- perhaps eventually leave the military.

He says he will:

> `一足先に、戦場の外を見てくるよ`

The narration then states what V08 could not yet know:

> `戦場にはいられなくなっても、戦えなくなっても、新しい自分の形は決められる。`

This is the first clean prospective confirmation of the personhood-beyond-combat-function thesis.

At the same time, it remains a **capacity claim**, not proof of easy adjustment.

Finally, captured Legion data identifies likely command nodes and a probable site connected to the shutdown mechanism.

The pride-and-wounds movement closes precisely as the war-ending project becomes materially actionable.

# 5. Prospective / publication-boundary reading

At the end of V09, a careful reader can establish with high confidence:

1. Theo survives the amputation because Giad treats a noncombat-capable soldier as a life worth saving.
2. Theo cannot currently return to Reginleif Processor duty with available prosthetic technology.
3. Theo retains military/civilian options rather than being automatically discarded.
4. Theo experiences genuine grief over loss of combat role; disability is not liberation fantasy.
5. Theo has begun constructing a future through small repeatable desires and art, not yet a settled vocation.
6. Shin can now identify his own destabilization and explicitly ask peers to correct reckless command decisions.
7. Shin acknowledges Undertaker/death-god identity as genuinely burdensome and genuinely sustaining.
8. Lena gives a fully explicit verbal romantic answer to Shin.
9. Shin/Lena's relationship now includes explicit mutual love, future desire, bodily intimacy, and reciprocal return promises.
10. Kurena's battlefield fixation is deeply connected to childhood helplessness and fear of future loss.
11. Kurena's V7 nonpossessiveness develops further into a conscious restructuring of her love for Shin.
12. Unreturned love can remain meaningful without creating entitlement.
13. Anju can continue loving Daiya while allowing a new attachment to Dustin.
14. Hielna was deliberately manufactured into a national grief symbol through abandonment of her family.
15. Holy Theocracy's hereditary role system denies individual occupational/marital choice even where assigned groups are honored.
16. `神戟` sacred status is not equivalent to Republican livestock dehumanization.
17. Holy Theocracy's supposedly immutable destiny can be revised by political authority when elite survival is threatened.
18. Hielna tries to convert Eighty-Six deaths into a transnational political catastrophe and collective-suicide project.
19. not all Eighty-Six fight because they personally prioritize ending the war; Lena's V09 universalization is too broad.
20. the Eighty-Six can have difficulty fighting human enemies, especially child soldiers, despite extraordinary anti-Legion combat competence.
21. Milmelkoleo's heroic identity is partly produced by aristocratic abuse and conditional recognition.
22. shared wound can become a coercive bond when members require one another to remain unchanged.
23. Shin's command ethic explicitly rejects discarding Kurena after a single failure.
24. Shiden independently wants the war to end because permanent battlefield life guarantees further loss.
25. Shepherds may be pursuing technical immortality/preservation of personality.
26. the Legion war-ending strategy is becoming operationally concrete through captured command-node intelligence.
27. the afterword marks V4–9 as one completed movement concerning Eighty-Six pride and wounds.

V09 has **not** yet established:

- Theo's final postmilitary vocation;
- whether Theo permanently leaves combat/military service;
- how well a prosthesis restores ordinary bodily function;
- that every Eighty-Six has the same trauma structure as Kurena or Hielna;
- that Holy Theocracy reform succeeds;
- that assigned roles are inherently invalid merely because inherited;
- that Shin/Lena's absolute romantic language can never become constraining;
- that Hielna is psychologically “healed” by surrender;
- that Milmelkoleo escapes its political role;
- that Shepherd immortality research proves all Shepherds possess the same degree of agency;
- that the Legion shutdown operation will succeed.

# 6. Focalization and epistemic authority

## 6.1 Kurena becomes a principal interpretive lens

V09 gives Kurena some of her richest interiority in the series.

That matters because her earlier behavior could easily be read externally as jealousy or conservatism.

Inside her focalization, the chain is much more precise:

> childhood helplessness → usefulness as protection → battlefield competence → secure place beside comrades/Shin → fear of noncombat future → fear that future desire will be stolen → temptation to preserve war-state identity.

The book does not excuse harmful implications.

It makes them causally legible.

## 6.2 Hielna as self-aware unreliable theorist

Hielna often states truths about the Eighty-Six:

- they were forced into battle;
- they were stripped of alternatives;
- pride can become wound;
- other societies sentimentalize their suffering.

She converts those truths into a false normative conclusion:

> because injury shaped us, it must remain sovereign over the future.

The V2 reader must therefore resist the trap of treating an insightful antagonist as narratorial authority.

## 6.3 Lena's overgeneralization is immediately contested

Lena's statement that the Eighty-Six fight to end the war sounds like a heroic thematic summary.

Kurena's interior response immediately says: not all of us.

This is a high-value example of the novel correcting its own most rhetorically attractive formulation.

## 6.4 Narrator on Milmelkoleo

The narration gives their abusive institutional history in a way individual soldiers themselves partly naturalize.

This creates the opposite epistemic structure from Lena/Kurena:

- character pride says heroic proof;
- narration supplies the conditional social economy that makes heroic proof feel necessary.

## 6.5 Theo's self-report remains incomplete by design

Theo says he will be okay, then later becomes emotionally flat.

The difference is not contradiction in bad faith.

It shows that a person may sincerely formulate a viable theory of recovery before possessing the energy or desire required to live it.

V09 therefore distinguishes **conceptual permission** from **embodied capacity**.

# 7. Character state — Shinei Nouzen

## 7.1 Command self-awareness

The sentence:

> `無茶を言ったと思ったら、指摘してくれ`

should be treated as a significant command-state change.

Shin is no longer merely accepting help after others force it upon him. He proactively builds peer correction into command because he knows Theo's injury may distort his judgment.

This is mature interdependence at the organizational level.

## 7.2 Undertaker/death-god identity

V09 materially corrects the legacy tendency to treat Undertaker burden as pure imprisonment.

Shin says it was heavy **from the beginning**.

But weight gave his life constraint:

> because others relied on him, he could not casually abandon what had been entrusted.

This is exactly the epigraph's ambiguity.

The role was:

- pride;
- bond;
- burden;
- prayer from the dead;
- and potentially curse.

Its ethical problem was never that it mattered.

It was that Shin originally possessed too little else.

## 7.3 Limits of care

Theo's injury leaves Shin with no solution.

He experiences this as failure:

> `何もできている、つもりだったんです`

The important development is that Lena does not restore the fantasy of omnipotence.

Shin has to learn that caring for someone does not entail being able to restore every lost capability.

## 7.4 Kurena as subordinate

Shin's defense of Kurena is one of his strongest command scenes.

He does not deny V08 failure.

He rejects the inference:

> failed once → untrustworthy person → discard.

This is an institutionalized right to recover.

It directly opposes San Magnolia's logic of function-first expendability.

## 7.5 Strategy over wounded pride

During Holy Theocracy's betrayal, Shin acknowledges that the Eighty-Six have every right to be angry.

He nevertheless distinguishes:

- fighting *for Hielna*;
- fighting *because Hielna says it is destiny*;
- finishing an operation whose outcome materially affects Giad and the war.

This is the mature version of V1's scaffold ethics: agency includes the ability to distinguish **why** one accepts danger.

## 7.6 Relation to future

V09 does not add as much new vocational content as V08, but it consolidates futurity by making war termination operational rather than hypothetical.

His romantic return language and strategic war-ending work now reinforce rather than replace one another.

# 8. Character state — Vladilena Milizé

## 8.1 Explicit answer

The unresolved V7/V8 speech-act state ends.

Lena says:

> `わたしも──あなたが好きです`

and:

> `一緒に生きたい`

The relation is no longer merely internally reciprocal or physically reciprocal.

It is verbally reciprocal.

## 8.2 Care after the limit of rescue

Lena's best V09 relational act is not the confession itself.

It is the preceding offer:

> `頼ってください`

> `共に、抱えますから`

She does not claim she can fix Theo or remove Shin's grief.

She offers co-bearing.

That is much closer to the mature interdependence hypothesis than savior romance.

## 8.3 Command during betrayal

Lena makes several important distinctions:

- the Eighty-Six do not owe heroism to Holy Theocracy;
- their pride is not destiny;
- the operation still matters strategically;
- she, not Shin, must own the command decision and resulting resentment.

This is bounded responsibility.

She does, however, overgeneralize the Eighty-Six's reasons for fighting. Kurena's objection prevents Lena's commander empathy from becoming total epistemic authority.

## 8.4 Other people's autonomy

Near the end Kurena tells Lena not to absorb responsibility for what the Holy Theocracy's children should become:

> `どうなりたいかは結局は、あの子たちにしかわかんない`

This is a continuation of Lena's V5–6 correction.

She is increasingly surrounded by people who will stop her from turning care into ownership.

# 9. Character state — Theoto Rikka

V09 makes Theo the clearest early demonstration that **loss of military function does not remove social membership, life-value, or capacity for future**.

## 9.1 Body first

The missing arm has weight.

Balance changes.

Available prosthetics cannot restore the specific combat performance required.

The book refuses to leap directly to metaphor.

## 9.2 Institutional recognition

The contrast with the Sector is absolute:

> `僕はもう、戦えない`

> `でも、死ななくていい`

In V1, a body that stopped producing military value was abandoned.

In V9, treatment precedes any answer about future usefulness.

That is personhood made material.

## 9.3 Grief over role

Theo does not want to stop being a Processor.

This prevents the analysis from treating civilianity as inherently more authentic.

His military self was chosen, social, meaningful, and real.

## 9.4 Temporary external purpose

Theo admits that `be happy for the dead` may become a curse.

For now he needs it.

This is psychologically subtle:

> **dependence on an externally inherited reason is not automatically pathological if the reason is consciously treated as provisional and leaves room for a later self-authored object.**

## 9.5 Belonging after function

The breakthrough in `青い鳥はどこにいたのか` is not “I found a new job.”

It is:

> I can still return to the people who formed me even when I cannot perform the role through which we first belonged together.

This is personhood beyond military function **through relationship before vocation**.

## 9.6 Small-desire futurity

Annette's fried-bread model gives Theo perhaps the series' cleanest ordinary-life philosophy so far.

The future does not require finding one monumental purpose that justifies life.

> `まだ死んでないから、何かをしたい。`

Desire can be sequential, ordinary, replaceable, repeatable.

This is almost the conceptual inverse of `誇りしか`.

## 9.7 Art

Requesting the sketchbook matters because drawing predates the injury.

Recovery need not fabricate a new person from scratch.

It can **reactivate underused parts of an existing person** once the dominant function is no longer available.

# 10. Character state — Kurena Kukumila

## 10.1 Childhood powerlessness

V09 provides the missing causal depth behind her usefulness anxiety.

Her parents are killed before her; her sister is taken; Kurena is a child who cannot stop any of it.

The resulting fear is not merely death:

> `自分の無力を思い知らされることこそが、本当は一番怖い。`

## 10.2 Shin and usefulness

Kurena fears that if she cannot fight effectively, she loses the place beside Shin.

That is not simply manipulative romance.

It is an attachment system in which competence functions as insurance against abandonment.

V09 explicitly challenges it.

## 10.3 Future fear

She understands intellectually that she could study.

She cannot enter the room.

This is one of the clearest examples in V1–9 of the difference between **formal option** and **usable option**.

Giad has given her a school.

Her nervous system and expectation of future theft still prevent the school from functioning as an easy choice.

## 10.4 Hielna mirror

Hielna shows Kurena the endpoint of preserving wound as identity:

> if war ends, everything proving what was taken from me loses its governing role;
> therefore war must not end.

Kurena recognizes that she might have wanted something adjacent to this.

The recognition is frightening because it is not wholly external.

## 10.5 Svenja mirror

Svenja reveals the relational form:

> do not change, because your change means I am abandoned with the wound alone.

Kurena says:

> `いなくならないでほしかったの。でも──縛りたいわけじゃなかったの。呪いたいわけじゃなかったの。`

This is the decisive internal distinction.

## 10.6 Final love

Kurena does not “stop loving Shin.”

She explicitly refuses that false cleanup.

Her love changes from desired exclusive future to durable part of autobiographical identity compatible with other futures.

That is one of the strongest examples in the series of **revision without repudiation**.

# 11. Character state — Anju Emma

Anju's V09 development is quieter but philosophically aligned with Kurena's.

She realizes:

- loving Daiya remains true;
- believing that this truth forbids love for Dustin is false.

The distinction is crucial.

Memory does not have to be erased to stop functioning as exclusive prohibition.

Her new relation is then structured by reciprocal return:

> `私も必ず、貴方のいる場所に帰ってくるから`

That avoids replacement logic.

Dustin is not the person who makes Daiya irrelevant.

He is a new relationship that becomes possible while Daiya remains part of Anju's history.

# 12. Character state — Hiemelnaade Reze / Hielna

Hielna is perhaps the strongest single V09 example of **identity weaponized through grief**.

## 12.1 Manufactured symbol

Her family's death is not merely war misfortune.

They are deliberately left exposed so the state can construct:

- a lone surviving sacred girl;
- an icon of national resistance;
- a person whose private grief becomes public political capital.

This is a different kind of objectification from San Magnolia.

She is valued enormously.

She is valued **as a role**.

## 12.2 Betrayal of destiny

The Holy Theocracy taught that hereditary military destiny was sacred enough to justify centuries of unequal death.

When ordinary believers finally face extinction, the state revises the doctrine.

Hielna experiences the policy change as retroactive desecration:

> if destiny was revisable, what justified what was taken from us?

This is a real moral problem.

Her answer—to make everyone lose the future—is not therefore unintelligible. It is still wrong.

## 12.3 Collective suicide

Her plot is not strategic liberation.

It seeks a cascade of mistrust and isolation that could ultimately destroy human resistance.

The narration explicitly understands it as collective suicide.

The curse has become complete when the wound's validation is valued above the continuation of everyone who carries it.

## 12.4 First movement toward life

Lena's strongest argument is relational:

> do not make your death one more loss your soldiers must carry.

Hielna finally accepts that if her people refused to abandon her, she has an obligation not to abandon them.

This is not complete self-authorship.

It is a shift from wound-centered martyrdom toward living responsibility.

# 13. Character state — Gilwiese and Svenja

## 13.1 Gilwiese

Gilwiese understands the military role as coercive recognition:

> `奴隷の首輪だ`

The group has been offered conditional dignity:

> prove heroic value and you may cease being treated as failed stock.

His insight does not free him materially. Political dependence remains.

## 13.2 Svenja

Svenja has even less psychological room.

Her role, fellow “failures,” and Gilwiese are one interlocking identity system.

When he suggests leaving the role, she hears:

> leave me.

This is precisely the pattern Kurena recognizes in herself.

## 13.3 Mutual curse

The Milmelkoleo case is important because no singular villain needs to impose the final cage.

The abused group can reproduce it through mutual obligation:

> same wound → same identity → same role → same future.

This is not a reason to blame victims for their own oppression.

It is a reason to distinguish **solidarity** from **compulsory sameness**.

# 14. Relationship state

## 14.1 Shin / Lena

V09 completes the delayed V7 speech act.

The relationship state is now:

- explicit mutual love;
- mutual future desire;
- physical intimacy;
- reciprocal return language;
- permission for vulnerability;
- increasing bodily trust around Shin's scar;
- continued command partnership.

The most important developmental point is that Shin tells Lena his weakness around Theo and she does not try to become the solution.

The relation can now contain **unsolved pain**.

## 14.2 Shin / Theo

The old death-god relationship becomes more reciprocal.

Theo admits he relied on Shin.

Shin admits that being relied upon also kept him alive.

Theo then asks for a future in which both can say:

> if it is too much, rely on me.

This is one of the strongest direct texts for the interdependence thesis.

## 14.3 Shin / Kurena

The relationship passes through two different kinds of trust.

As commander, Shin refuses to discard Kurena after failure.

As romantic object, he refuses to falsify reciprocity he cannot give.

Both acts preserve her as a person rather than managing her emotion through convenient fiction.

## 14.4 Kurena / Lena

Kurena now actively pushes Lena toward the relationship with Shin.

This should not be read as self-erasure.

She retains the right to ask whether Lena actually answered him and frames Shin's happiness as something she genuinely wants.

The triangle changes because Kurena's love no longer needs Lena to disappear for Kurena's love to remain meaningful.

## 14.5 Anju / Dustin / Daiya

V09 creates an additive relational model:

> Daiya remains loved; Dustin can also become loved.

Memory does not require exclusivity across time.

## 14.6 Hielna / `神戟`

Their bond moves from symbolic martyrdom toward mutual responsibility.

Hielna initially wants others to die with her wound.

She ends by recognizing that her soldiers' refusal to abandon her gives her a reason not to abandon them.

# 15. Political and institutional analysis — Holy Theocracy

## 15.1 Destiny above choice

Holy Theocracy organizes social life around inherited role.

This includes:

- occupation;
- marriage;
- military caste;
- bloodline specialization;
- sacred authority.

The system's key political flaw is not simply hierarchy.

It is that **the person's right to reinterpret inherited status is institutionally weak**.

## 15.2 Sacred dehumanization

`神戟` are explicitly said not to be humans but divine instruments/messengers.

They may receive:

- respect;
- education;
- public honor;
- material care.

This creates a crucial comparative axis:

> dehumanization can operate through elevation as well as degradation if the category removes the person's authority to cease performing the assigned function.

The word “dehumanization” should still be used carefully here because in-world religious ontology differs from Republican racial animalization. The stronger institutional term is **sacralized functionalization**.

## 15.3 Selective mutability of doctrine

The system tells the subordinate that destiny is immutable.

When the cost reaches ordinary believers, leadership changes policy and introduces broader conscription.

This exposes an asymmetry:

> the role is metaphysically absolute when it disciplines the soldier caste and politically revisable when it threatens the governing population.

That is Hielna's strongest indictment.

## 15.4 Reform after betrayal

The ending suggests the Holy Theocracy will reconsider the institution.

V09 does not establish the outcome.

It also deliberately preserves a complication: some `神戟` may not experience compulsory removal from their inherited role as liberation.

This repeats Giad's V2 problem at a larger institutional scale.

Good reform must create alternatives **without assuming that everyone must choose the same alternative**.

# 16. Giad and comparative institutions

## 16.1 Medical personhood

Theo's treatment is one of Giad's strongest institutional contrasts with the Republic.

His life is preserved before anyone knows whether he can fight again.

That is more decisive than rhetoric about equality.

## 16.2 Military retention after disability

Giad offers possible noncombat retention because officers are understood as trained people whose value can persist across role change.

This is humane compared with San Magnolia.

It is not automatically benign: the military also has incentives to retain expensive human capital.

Again mixed motives do not erase real benefit.

## 16.3 Education

The commanders' reaction to school shows that Giad's educational investment is beginning to create **usable nonbattlefield identities**.

They can voluntarily prioritize urgent training without losing the expectation that education remains available later.

## 16.4 Aristocratic remnants

Milmelkoleo proves that the Federacy has not erased Imperial class and blood politics.

Former aristocratic structures can still:

- produce dependent private units;
- use marginalized mixed-race soldiers as prestige assets;
- convert military glory into factional power.

The Federacy remains genuinely better than San Magnolia while containing inherited coercive structures of its own.

# 17. Military analysis at four levels

## 17.1 Tactical

### Airborne assault

The new `アルメ・フュリウーズ` permits the Strike Package to bypass fronts rather than repeatedly break through them. This directly answers an operational problem that had become increasingly implausible to solve through ordinary penetration alone.

### Holy Theocracy equipment

The small `リャノ＝シュ` platforms are shaped by their child operators. Their limited initiative and dependence on older command vehicles become tactically exploitable.

This is a grotesque example of social organization becoming vehicle doctrine.

### Human enemies

The Eighty-Six's inability to fire easily on child human soldiers becomes a genuine tactical constraint.

Ethical history therefore affects immediate combat performance.

## 17.2 Operational

Giad conducts simultaneous deep strikes against several Legion production/command nodes.

The goals are not only destruction but:

- capture of control centers;
- component intelligence;
- railgun data;
- identification of command infrastructure.

This is a transition from reactive survival toward war-termination shaping operations.

## 17.3 Strategic

The war now has something it long lacked:

> a plausible route toward identifying and attacking the command architecture required for a shutdown.

The acquisition of probable hidden-command-node location in the epilogue is strategically more important than another spectacular local victory.

## 17.4 Political-constitutional

V09 repeatedly asks who may decide that another person's role is “destiny.”

- Holy Theocracy claims divine authority.
- Hielna attempts to impose wound-based destiny on Eighty-Six.
- aristocratic patrons impose heroic role on Milmelkoleo.
- Giad commanders can at least debate, refuse, withdraw, change branch, and continue education.

The contrast is fundamentally political:

> **military obligation becomes less dehumanizing when the soldier retains meaningful standing to renegotiate role, risk, and future.**

## 17.5 Command recovery from failure

Shin's Kurena judgment deserves doctrinal status:

> `一度の過ちで兵を切り捨てては隊など成りたたない`

Professional reliability means building a force in which imperfect people can fail and be recovered—not pretending soldiers are flawless components.

# 18. Technology, AI, and personhood

## 18.1 Shepherd immortality

V09 strengthens the evidence that Shepherds can possess continuity-oriented desire.

The Phönix/high-mobility program is interpreted as work toward:

> `〈羊飼い〉の不死化`

and Shepherds are described as clinging to preservation of personality/existence.

This is incompatible with the old static-dead-recording model.

## 18.2 Zelene

Zelene continues to make choices that appear personally motivated even within Legion compulsion. Her concern about causing Shin pain comes at cognitive cost.

The correct classification remains:

- personality continuity: strong evidence;
- human-origin continuity: strong evidence;
- autonomous moral agency: constrained;
- complete original-person identity: philosophically unresolved.

## 18.3 Integration and individuality

The prologue's machine integration problem rhymes with the volume's social problem.

A system gains capability by joining parts.

The ethical question for persons is whether integration preserves the member's independent agency.

This distinction will matter increasingly as Legion cognition becomes modular and human coalitions become more interconnected.

## 18.4 Prosthetics

Theo's case is technically important because a prosthesis can be excellent for ordinary human function and still fail to meet a specific military performance threshold.

“Disabled” and “combat-incapable” must therefore never be used as synonyms for “globally incapable.”

# 19. Body, injury, disability, and medical life

## 19.1 Theo's changed balance

V09 attends to the physical fact that an arm has weight.

Theo has to relearn balance.

The body is not merely symbolic proof of function loss.

It is a materially changed interface with the world.

## 19.2 Treatment versus utility

The Sector equivalent wound meant death because treatment was allocated according to return-to-combat value.

Giad treats Theo first.

The political difference is enacted through medicine.

## 19.3 Mental aftermath

Theo's later apathy means that physical survival and conceptual optimism do not guarantee immediate psychological energy.

Recovery must therefore be tracked longitudinally rather than inferred from one insightful speech.

## 19.4 Kurena's embodied fear

Adult size, silver hair, proximity, and looming posture trigger a child-conditioned fear response.

The priest's accommodation is an example of ordinary social environment changing to make another person's body feel safer.

This is a small but concrete “usable freedom” mechanism.

# 20. Childhood and interrupted development

V09 contains several distinct childhood structures.

### Kurena

The child who could not stop her family being taken turns competence into a defense against helplessness.

### Hielna

A child's family grief is deliberately staged as public political mythology.

### `神戟`

Children inherit military function before they can meaningfully choose it.

### Svenja

A young girl is trained to see a humiliating role as the sole proof that she and her companions deserve value.

### younger Eighty-Six visitor

Theo encounters a child too young to have served. This quietly proves that “Eighty-Six childhood” is already becoming historically differentiated.

Theo can potentially become an older person for children whose developmental trajectory will not replicate his own.

The series is moving from **interrupted childhood** toward **intergenerational consequence**.

# 22. Pride, wish, bond, prayer, curse

V09 finally supplies a more precise taxonomy than the V1 phrase “pride is dignity and prison.”

## Pride

A chosen or inherited commitment through which a person can preserve conduct and self-respect.

Healthy when it can coexist with other identities.

## Wish

A desired future that can orient action without being guaranteed.

Theo's temporary wish to become happy belongs here.

## Bond

A relation that helps preserve a person's shape when self-sufficiency fails.

Shin/Spearhead and Theo/Spearhead demonstrate this.

## Prayer

A desired good that may exceed one's power but can still give direction.

The dead commander's hoped-for happiness can function this way.

## Curse

A meaning that becomes coercive when abandoning or revising it is treated as annihilation, betrayal, or forfeiture of belonging.

Hielna and Svenja make this explicit.

### Working V09 criterion

> **The same commitment crosses toward curse when its validity begins to require foreclosure of alternative selves—either one's own or another person's.**

This is the best textual criterion the V2 project has yet found.

# 23. Freedom and paternalism

V09 deepens the freedom model in three ways.

## 23.1 Choice must be psychologically usable

Kurena technically has education available.

Fear prevents her from using it.

Formal option remains insufficient.

## 23.2 Inherited role may be retained freely

A person does not become unfree merely by choosing a role inherited from family or culture.

The problem is institutional and relational inability to **leave or revise** it.

This distinction is crucial for `神戟` reform.

## 23.3 Recovery cannot be imposed

Kurena tells Lena that the Holy Theocracy's children must decide what they want.

This continues V5's anti-paternalism:

> opening the cage is necessary;
> requiring the former captive to walk in the direction you approve is another form of control.

# 24. Ethics of command, violence, and responsibility

## 24.1 Humans as enemy

V09 sharply complicates the martial ethics of the Eighty-Six.

Their anti-Legion violence is not proof of generalized human-killing ease.

Facing child soldiers produces moral paralysis because they recognize their own coerced childhood selves.

## 24.2 Lena's burden

That is responsible ownership of authority.

## 24.3 Shin and withdrawal

Shin's response to mutinous frustration is not discipline through shame.

This is command through reasons rather than destiny.

## 24.4 Hielna's victimhood does not excuse victimization

being a wounded child explains Hielna.

## 24.5 Kurena and standing

Kurena's final request to Svenja—do not bind Gilwiese with your wound—is ethically meaningful because she speaks as someone who recognizes the temptation in herself.

It is not an outsider lecturing a victim to “move on.”

# 25. Ordinary life and future

V09 gives the strongest ordinary-life thesis so far because it strips “future” of grandeur.

Annette's answer is almost aggressively banal:

- clothes;
- food;
- movies;
- pranks.

Theo realizes that this is enough.

The mature V2 model should therefore distinguish:

### grand future

career, family, postwar polity, life mission.

### iterative future

one meal, one drawing, one conversation, one outing, then whatever becomes desirable afterward.

The second may be developmentally prior to the first.

This is important for people whose ability to imagine long horizons was repeatedly punished.

Rito and the other commanders independently support the same idea through school and curiosity.

Shiden supplies another version:

> end the war and live easily and happily.

No grand destiny required.

# 26. Japanese voice, address, and terminology

## 26.1 Shin

The most important V09 speech change is self-correction.

He starts to say he is fine and instead says:

> `ずいぶん動揺してると思う。無茶を言ったと思ったら、指摘してくれ`

Low-excess speech now admits **uncertainty about his own judgment**.

His final prebattle exchange with Lena also changes return language. He begins the formal military/socially polite `行ってきます` and revises to the more intimate:

> `──行ってくる`

This is a small but high-value relational-language shift: “I am going and coming back” moves from formal report toward household/intimate return speech.

## 26.2 Lena

V09 finally gives the explicit:

> `わたしも──あなたが好きです`

Her language around support is equally important:

> `頼ってください`

> `共に、抱えますから`

The grammar is not “I will remove this burden.” It is “share it.”

## 26.3 Theo

Theo's language becomes the volume's most philosophically self-aware without ceasing to sound colloquial:

- `僕はもう、戦えない`
- `でも、死ななくていい`
- `呪いだよね`
- `願いみたいなものでしょ`
- `まだ死んでないから、何かをしたい`

The lexical movement from curse to wish is explicit rather than imposed by analysis.

## 26.4 Kurena

The most important terms are exclusivity and fear:

- `戦うしかない`
- `誇りしかない`
- `みすてないで`
- `無力なのは怖い`

Her closing language removes exclusivity without erasing affection:

- `今でも好き`
- `幸せになってね`

This is a measurable linguistic revision.

## 26.5 Hielna

Her speech is saturated in:

- `運命`
- `御役目`
- `奪う / 奪われる`
- `傷`
- `赦さない`

The language exposes a self whose political ontology is built around assigned role and theft.

## 26.6 Key V09 terminology

- `神戟` — sacred hereditary military category; not equivalent to Republican `家畜` ontology.
- `御役目 / 役目` — assigned function; context determines whether chosen, inherited, or coercive.
- `運命` — destiny as religious-political legitimation.
- `誇り` — retained dignity/identity; dangerous when exclusive.
- `願い` — future-oriented support that can remain revisable.
- `絆` — relational form; can sustain or bind.
- `祈り` — hope directed beyond direct control.
- `呪い` — binding meaning that forecloses alternative self/future.
- `奴隷の首輪` — Gilwiese's name for heroic role imposed as conditional human worth.
- `我らが死神` — burden and reciprocal support, not one-way function.
- `退役 / 兵科転換` — institutional vocabulary demonstrating role change without person disappearance.

> **The series repeatedly asks whether persons and communities retain enough agency, material support, relationship, and institutional room to revise the meanings that once kept them alive—without requiring them to repudiate those meanings as false.**

V09 adds the clearest falsifier:

> a sustaining meaning becomes curse when it requires alternative selves to be foreclosed.

# Closing V09 synthesis

Volume 9 closes the V4–9 pride-and-wounds movement not by announcing that the Eighty-Six have healed, abandoned pride, found normal lives, or learned the one correct way to be free.

It does something more precise.

It teaches the series how to distinguish a **support** from a **curse**.

The difference is not whether something is heavy.

Shin's dead are heavy.

The difference is not whether something constrains.

Love, duty, friendship, command, family, and pride all constrain.

The difference is not whether something comes from the past.

Theo's commander, Anju's Daiya, Kurena's childhood, and Shin's Undertaker role all remain historically real.

The danger begins when a meaning says:

> **You may live only if you remain this.**

Hielna's state says it through destiny.

Milmelkoleo says it through heroic proof.

Svenja says it through shared wound.

Kurena nearly says it through usefulness and love.

The old Eighty-Six ethic could say it through `誇りしか`.

V09's constructive alternatives are deliberately less dramatic.

Theo can use a dead man's wish **until** he finds something else.

Shin can admit that being the death god was heavy **and** that the others' reliance helped him live.

Anju can love Daiya **and** begin to love Dustin.

Kurena can love Shin **and** want a future that does not choose her.

Lena can love Shin without curing Theo, curing Shin, or owning the future of Holy Theocracy's children.

A soldier can fail once and still be entrusted again.

A soldier can cease being able to fight and still be treated, welcomed home, taught another role, or leave to discover another life.

A future can begin with nothing grander than fried bread and a sketchbook.

That is why the afterword's transition matters.

The V4–9 movement begins by asking what wounds remain after the happy ending.

It ends by discovering that the opposite of a wound is not forgetting, purity, or restoration.

It is **room**:

room for the wound to mean something different;
room for the bond to change shape;
room for another desire to appear;
room for someone else to leave without becoming a traitor;
room to remain loved without being possessed;
room to be a soldier without soldierhood becoming destiny;
and room to be alive before one has discovered a sufficiently grand reason for being alive.

The war is not over.

But the characters finally possess enough conceptual room that ending it no longer necessarily means ending themselves.
