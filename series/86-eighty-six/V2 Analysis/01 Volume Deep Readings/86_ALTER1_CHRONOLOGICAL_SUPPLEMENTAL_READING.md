The checksum exactly matches the Phase-0 source lock. The collection is therefore admissible Tier-1 prose, subject to a stronger chronology discipline than an ordinary numbered volume.

Alter.1 cannot be treated as “Volume 12.5.” Its publication position is post-V12, but its prose reaches backward across roughly six in-world years. A detail first published here may revise our retrospective account of V1–V12, yet it **cannot be treated as information an earlier-volume reader already possessed**.

## 1.2 Collection-level paratext

Asato states that the book mainly gathers retailer/fair shorts associated with Volumes 1–6 and arranges scenes spanning **Shin at age twelve in winter through age eighteen in autumn**. The explicit purpose is everyday snapshots of Shin and his companions, not a hidden campaign arc. The afterword also calls them a record of the characters’ `成長の軌跡` and closes by inviting the reader into Spearhead’s evening gathering and Rustkammer’s daily bustle.

This authorial framing is useful but does not authorize us to flatten forty-seven pieces into one undifferentiated canon block. Each story still needs its own continuity classification.

## 1.3 Story-continuity classes

This pass uses five placement labels:

- **`SECURE_EXACT`** — an explicit date, age, named operation, or unique mainline event fixes the scene tightly.
- **`SECURE_WINDOW`** — the mainline interval is secure even if an exact day is not.
- **`SECURE_LAYERED`** — one short deliberately contains two or more time layers; each layer remains distinct.
- **`AMBIGUOUS`** — evidence permits more than one plausible narrow placement.
- **`NONMAINLINE_PARATEXT`** — authorial/comedic material intentionally incompatible with literal continuity.

Dreams, imagined conversations, and memorial reconstructions inside otherwise canonical stories remain evidence about the focal character, not literal testimony from the dead.

## 1.4 Two paratextual irregularities

The afterword says the collection is arranged in in-world chronological order. That is reliable **as a broad architecture**, but internal evidence proves it is not an infallible adjacency rule. `五月十九日（シン誕生日） ちょっとした仕掛け` says Annette has already ordered a choker that she will give Lena **two months later**. `七月十二日（レーナ誕生日）`, which appears *before* that May story in the table of contents, depicts Annette giving precisely that choker. Story-level internal causality therefore outranks neighboring TOC position.

A second inconsistency concerns Annette’s birthday. The story heading and TOC say `十一月十二日（アネット誕生日）`; the afterword calls the newly written piece `十一月十五日（アネット誕生日）`. V2 records this as a paratextual discrepancy rather than silently choosing the afterword date. The canonical locator title remains **November 12**, because that is the narrative item actually supplied by the EPUB.

## 1.5 Non-mainline exception: `オールスター香水バトル`

The perfume battle is explicitly excluded from literal continuity by the author. Asato says that including the full cast required bending space-time and asks readers to understand the event as something like a `集団幻覚`, explicitly pointing out that Rei, Kaie, Eugene and others are already dead.

Accordingly:

- no interaction in this story establishes mainline relationship state;
- no survival/death co-presence is canon evidence;
- fragrance, birthday, weapon, address, and comic-character associations may be indexed as **paratext** where useful;
- it cannot update longitudinal event causality.

This is a hard source boundary, not a matter of interpretive preference.

# 2. Primary thesis

Alter.1’s most important contribution is negative in the best sense: it prevents the mainline war plot from creating a false picture of the people living inside it.

The numbered novels necessarily concentrate on campaigns, strategic crises, political collapse, injury, and relationship turning points. If those scenes alone were treated as the whole evidentiary corpus, one could mistakenly infer that ordinary life enters the Eighty-Six’s world only **after Giad rescues them**, or that Shin becomes humorous, curious, affectionate, playful, and future-capable principally because Lena or Federacy civilian society teaches him how.

Alter.1 makes that reading untenable.

Inside the Eighty-Sixth Sector, before rescue, these children already:

- invent excuses to celebrate;
- cook badly and tease one another about it;
- rescue animals;
- tell ghost stories;
- mend clothes;
- explore ruins because something looks interesting;
- joke over Para-RAID;
- become jealous;
- notice beauty;
- hold impromptu hanami;
- drink, laugh, argue, and play;
- preserve practical care for one another;
- and want another person to see something beautiful with them.

The correct longitudinal distinction is therefore **not**:

> war-only existence → Giad teaches ordinary humanity.

It is:

> **ordinary humanity improvised under lethal scarcity → rescue supplies reliability, time, infrastructure, choice, repeatability, and the possibility that ordinary practices can accumulate into a future.**

That difference is central to the series’ ethics. Giad matters enormously, but not because it confers humanity on people who lacked it.

Alter.1 also reveals one concrete temporal technology through which that expansion occurs: **birthdays**.

In the Sector, exact birth dates become administratively and emotionally useless. Shin forgets his own. Raiden happens to remember his, and Shin turns it into a pretext for the surviving unit to make something sweet and be together before another reorganization. After the Republic falls, recovered personnel records restore exact dates. Lena proposes celebrating them. The Eighty-Six then imitate birthday-gift practices because they look fun. By V11 and V12, a birthday can resist the attempt of war to seize the meaning of a day, and Theo can participate in birthday reciprocity even after combat service has been taken from him.

The birthday sequence therefore enacts the series’ future thesis at human scale:

> **a future is not only the abstract conviction that one will survive; it is the emergence of repeatable occasions whose meaning depends on there being another year.**

# 3. Competing theses and interpretive risks

## 3.1 Risk: Alter.1 proves the Eighty-Six were basically fine before rescue

No. Their humor and ordinary activity coexist with extermination, hunger, racial exclusion, absent medicine, routine death, and the expectation that units will be annihilated and reconstituted. In `八月二十五日`, the birthday is meaningful partly because the squad is about to be reorganized and only the survivors can celebrate. In `クレイモア戦隊`, self-reliance is taught because anyone unable to read the battlefield without Shin’s warning is likely to die.

Ordinary life demonstrates personhood; it does not erase structural damage.

## 3.2 Risk: Giad adds nothing but comfort

Also false. `徒歩圏内` makes the difference wonderfully concrete. Shin is intellectually curious enough to use a library and war memorial on his own. What he lacks is the mundane habit of imagining a bus as part of movement through a city. He can walk three hours without considering that public transport is free and frequent.

Giad adds **convenience cognition, infrastructure, recoverable time, and alternative means**, not intelligence or dignity.

## 3.3 Risk: Sector competence is healthier than civilian dependence

Alter.1 supplies direct counterevidence. Anju’s Claymore lesson—`誰にも頼らず戦えないような子は生き残れない`—is tactically valid under the Sector’s conditions and morally terrible as a social norm. It describes a world where help cannot be assumed because helpers die.

V7’s later professional doctrine of organizational redundancy should therefore not be projected backward as though the Sector’s forced self-sufficiency were the same virtue. They are almost opposites:

- **Sector:** each child must function alone because the institution has abandoned them.
- **healthy force design:** no person should be irreplaceable because the institution builds enough capacity to support everyone.

## 3.4 Risk: Lena creates Shin’s relational life

Alter.1 weakens this further than any numbered volume. Thirteen-year-old Shin jokes with Raiden, wants an excuse to celebrate, teases him with deliberately terrible cooking, and is already capable of mutual care. Spearhead-era Shin rescues, repairs, reads, explores and participates in group ordinary life.

Lena’s significance survives the correction because she becomes something more specific: **a person toward whom Shin increasingly directs shared perception and future repetition**. In V1-window supplemental prose, he already thinks that if visual synchronization existed, he wishes she could see what he sees. On the Special Recon journey, he wonders whether she will someday catch up. Later this becomes `海を見せたい`, then `共に` seeing and living.

She does not create the capacity. She becomes one distinctive addressee of it.

## 3.5 Risk: romantic supplemental scenes can be treated as relationship milestones equivalent to explicit choices

No. Alter.1 contains deliberately comic bodily proximity. Lena wants to touch sleeping Shin but does not. Exhausted Shin accidentally pulls Lena into sleep and later remembers nothing. Drunk Lena pins Shin down and remembers nothing. These scenes may establish vulnerability, differentiated trust, attraction, embarrassment, or comic intimacy; they **cannot** be converted into consent-based romantic decisions.

By contrast, later scenes where Shin deliberately chooses a gift because Lena will like it, says he gave it because he wanted to, or experiences her nearby presence as safety are much stronger agency evidence.

## 3.6 Risk: the afterword’s “chronological order” settles every disputed date

The May/July choker sequence disproves that literal use. V2 therefore treats the afterword as strong collection-level chronology evidence, then resolves local order from internal anchors.

## 3.7 Risk: retrospective side stories can rewrite early publication knowledge

They cannot. `冬の日に、隻影二つ` is published after V12 and places twelve-year-old Lena and Shin under the same winter sky. That is a beautiful retrospective rhyme. It does **not** mean V1 readers possessed an established “fated parallel childhood” scene. The scene may revise our mature formal reading without being imported into the original prospective epistemic boundary.

## 4.1 Matrix-level conclusion

The collection’s four nominal sections—Republic, Federacy, Roa Gracia, and `死神ときどき青春`—are useful editorial groupings, but the **real longitudinal structure cuts across them**. Several motifs begin in the Sector, gain infrastructure in Giad, acquire reciprocal/romantic meaning around Roa and Rustkammer, then survive renewed catastrophe in V11–V12.

The strongest of these longitudinal lines are:

1. **ordinary life:** improvisation → repeatability → chosen expansion;
2. **birthday time:** forgotten date → survivor excuse → recovered archive → peer ritual → future-resistant celebration;
3. **Shin’s relational language:** shared sight → imagined catching-up → shared drink/meal/travel → explicit life together;
4. **home and archive:** erased origins → recovered data → optional return → future-fillable objects;
5. **care:** emergency mutual aid → bodily trust → institution-supported rest → post-role social participation.

# 5. Republic cluster: ordinary humanity under an exterminatory institution

## 5.1 Twelve-year-old parallelism without retroactive fate

`冬の日に、隻影二つ` is formally one of the strongest shorts in the collection. Twelve-year-old Lena walks alone through the Republic’s Atonement festival. Twelve-year-old Shin buries Rei’s bones in a frozen abandoned city with Fido. Lena wonders whether the Eighty-Six who once saved her ever returned to his little brother. Shin concludes that no one waits for his return and that even his survival is desired by no one.

The story closes by giving each the same tiny utterance beneath a sky neither knows is being shared: Lena’s `……寒い`, Shin’s `……寒いな`.

The mature reader can recognize an extraordinary formal parallel: before voice, before names, before romance, both are already lonely children whose respective institutions have accelerated them toward adult roles. But V2 should resist the easiest romantic formulation. The point is not that they were mystically destined. Their circumstances are profoundly unequal. Lena is lonely inside privilege and self-imposed acceleration; Shin is burying his murdered brother while his state works to kill him.

The formal rhyme establishes **possible recognition across distance**, not symmetry of suffering.

## 5.2 Birthday as improvised survivor technology

`八月二十五日（ライデン誕生日）` should become a load-bearing text for the ordinary-life synthesis.

Raiden has forgotten that it is his birthday because birthdays have almost no use in the Sector. Shin has forgotten his own date entirely. Yet Shin proposes celebrating anyway, eventually admitting his real motive: the squad is nearing reorganization and it would be useful to have an excuse for the remaining survivors to make noise together.

This is not conventional civilian birthday culture preserved intact.

It is **ritual reconstructed from scraps**.

The scene simultaneously gives us early Shin personality evidence. He deliberately threatens to cook despite being terrible at it, grins while tormenting Raiden, and gets called out for it. Raiden notes that the boy who once looked like an emotionless death god has learned to smile more—and has become annoyingly fond of teasing.

That scene occurs before Lena knows him.

## 5.3 Lena and Annette had ordinary adolescence too

`レーナ＋アネット` gives the complementary evidence. On the day before both officially enter the military, they go shopping because this is their `自由な最後の一日`. They compare clothes and cakes, drink their preferred beverages, and maintain the easy rhythm of two privileged adolescent friends.

This matters for Lena’s later Bloody Regina identity. Her militarization is not the only self she ever possessed. The collapse of the Republic and the deaths around her suppress an existing repertoire of ordinary femininity, friendship and leisure rather than creating a person who had never known them.

The contrast with the Eighty-Six remains crucial: Lena and Annette have department stores, money, parental homes, formal career assignments and the expectation of adulthood. The Eighty-Six improvise pleasures in a zone where adulthood is statistically unlikely.

## 5.4 Claymore: forced autonomy is not mature interdependence

`クレイモア戦隊` gives valuable pre-Spearhead evidence on Shin’s irreplaceability. Shin is sick. Raiden can take command because the current squad happens to contain enough veterans to absorb the absence. Anju warns younger soldiers that warnings and instructions must be prioritized and that anyone who cannot read the battlefield and fight without help will die.

Prospectively, this is not the healthy “no hero should be indispensable” doctrine V7 later articulates. The children are being trained to survive **institutional abandonment**.

The same scene shows why Shin’s individual exceptionalism grows: the Republic does not create a robust system around his gift. His peers have to learn to survive periods when even the boy with supernatural warning ability is sick, injured, overloaded, or dead.

The difference between **distributed competence** and **abandoned self-sufficiency** should remain explicit in Document 07.

## 5.5 Pets, stories, sewing, exploration

The remaining Spearhead shorts accumulate rather than announce their argument.

Daiya brings home a kitten from a bombed house. Theo, Kaie, Haruto and Fido exchange folklore around ordinary cleanup and camp life. Raiden and Shin mend clothing. Kurena turns mending into an excuse for proximity. Lena, who grew up with servants, does not even understand what `ツクロイモノ` means or how a needle and thread function in the relevant context.

This is not a hierarchy of better and worse people. It is evidence that **capability portfolios are historically produced**. Sector adolescents can sew, navigate ruins and improvise tools because they were forced to; Lena can handle academic/military abstraction but lacks banal household competencies because wealth insulated her from them.

V12’s freedom argument is strengthened by this supplemental evidence precisely because the skills involved are mundane. A person can be fully dignified while still lacking a practical option they were never taught to imagine.

## 5.6 `常闇のヘブンリー・ブルー`: curiosity before rescue

The narrator explicitly states that the Eighty-Six are not fighting twenty-four hours a day and are `素直で貪欲` about small events in daily life. Shin explores an abandoned underground environment because it interests him. He finds a strange blue, submerged battlefield image and wishes visual synchronization existed so Lena could see the same thing.

This is perhaps the strongest corrective to a simplistic V3 chronology of futurity.

The later `海を見せたい` does not emerge from nowhere. Its **relational grammar has an antecedent inside V1**:

> I wish you could see what I am seeing.

What changes later is not the existence of curiosity or sharing desire, but its durability, explicitness, and ability to support a long future.

## 5.7 Special Recon: relationship can point forward before survival is self-valued

`朽骨の剣尖` is darker. Shin sometimes cannot tell whether he is alive or dead. Yet he wonders whether Lena will someday reach the place where he currently stands and imagines flowers there.

This does not overturn V2’s conclusion that Shin lacks a strong self-authored desire to survive. It makes the model more precise: **future-oriented representation can exist before the self is treated as an object worth preserving for its own sake**.

He can imagine Lena’s future arrival while remaining frighteningly indifferent to whether he will still be there.

## 5.8 `仔猫`: ordinary care exposes Republic hierarchy

The kitten that passes into Lena’s care creates a small but brutal institutional comparison. The Republic can supply food and domestic space for a cat while claiming it cannot house human Eighty-Six inside the Eighty-Five Sectors. Lena’s mother refuses to let the animal roam because it belonged to Eighty-Six.

This is useful because it takes racial ontology out of command headquarters and into household life.

The story also gives Lena one of her most direct early statements of personal absence: `あなたがいなくて、わたしは──とてもさびしい`.

Again, the relationship is already particular before embodied reunion.

# 6. Early Giad: rescue as infrastructure, not manufacture of personhood

## 6.1 Food and growth

`成長` stages the first peer meal Frederica has ever experienced. Ironically, the imperial princess has known a form of social isolation different from the Eighty-Six’s. Meanwhile the Five’s appetite is narrated through continuing adolescent growth and unusually high muscle mass from war.

The scene refuses to let military iconography erase the body. These are growing teenagers who need food.

Federacy abundance matters because nutrition becomes reliable rather than scavenged, and because eating together can become routine rather than opportunistic.

## 6.2 Frederica asks the identity question before the series has an answer

`兄妹` is one of Alter.1’s highest-value retrospective additions.

Frederica asks how many constitutive features a person can discard and remain the same. She specifically treats her speech—her archaic vocabulary and royal register—as part of herself. She then recognizes the social pressure to adapt: `人は人の中でしか生きられぬ`.

Shin cannot answer.

This scene therefore becomes a genuine **prospective precursor** to the V6–V9 additive identity model. It prevents us from making later conclusions seem inevitable. Early Giad does not yet give Shin a polished doctrine of plural identity. It gives him the problem:

> How much can I change to live among others without treating survival as self-erasure?

Frederica’s own later `わらわ自身の王` development can now be read as one answer to the question she posed years earlier.

## 6.3 `徒歩圏内`: capabilities at the scale of a bus stop

Shin adapts to Zankt Tjeder quickly. He independently uses a library, finds historical material, visits a war memorial and talks with an older veteran. None of that resembles a boy cognitively incapable of civilian life.

What shocks Ernst is that Shin then walks for hours because the idea of taking a bus simply never occurs to him.

This is an ideal corrective to the potentially patronizing version of V12’s capabilities thesis.

Shin does not need a guardian to tell him what to value. He already has curiosity and judgment. What a functioning city can give him is **an option he does not yet know to include in the choice set**.

Capability expansion can be that small.

## 6.4 `哨戒任務`: uneven development made visible to Eugene

During Federacy officer training, Eugene experiences his first real battle while Shin responds with the practiced automaticity of someone who has already spent years at war. Eugene asks why he is so used to it.

Institutionally, both are cadets.

Developmentally, one is a new soldier and the other is a child veteran.

This makes V10’s uneven-development thesis visible without retrospective psychology. Shin can be undertrained in civilian conveniences and overtrained in death at the same time.

## 6.5 Adults who want children to become unnecessary

`怪物の殻の向こう` occurs immediately after the Morpho operation. Adult Federacy soldiers see the Five asleep near Fido and react not with hero worship but with recognition: `本当にただのガキどもだった`.

Most importantly, one says that next time they should be able to tell these children their hands are **not needed** because the adults have continued building the necessary capacity themselves.

This is one of the strongest supplemental confirmations of the anti-irreplaceability thesis.

Healthy admiration asks:

> how do we make it unnecessary to spend this child again?

It does not ask:

> how do we ensure the hero remains available forever?

## 6.6 Dream and humor are evidence, but of different kinds

`死神meetsバカ兄貴…` explicitly ends with Shin waking. Rei and Kiriya speaking through their Legion machine forms therefore cannot be used as new evidence about posthumous cognition. The story can support claims about Shin’s memory, unresolved family questions and the extent to which machine bodies have colonized his internal representation of the dead.

`這い飛ぶ大鳥`, by contrast, is straightforward behavioral evidence: Shin deliberately makes Frederica stumble over an absurd proposed aircraft name and finally laughs. This is not “healing complete.” It is simply another reason the global image of Shin as emotionally flat should never have survived the V2 reread.

# 7. Rustkammer and the Strike Package: embodied familiarity before explicit romance

## 7.1 Sleeping Shin and differentiated trust

In `いたずら（レーナ→シン）`, Lena sees sleeping Shin in person for the first time. The visual/embodied knowledge matters because their original relationship could never include unconscious presence: Para-RAID does not connect when one party is asleep.

Lena wants to touch his face, but does not. Shiden later notices that Shin would have woken much sooner had **she** approached and teases him for being unusually relaxed around Lena.

This is strong evidence for differentiated trust.

It is not a sexual or romantic action by either character.

## 7.2 Lena’s overwork is already a relational problem

`いたずら（シン→レーナ）` has Lena stay awake for days because electronic paperwork is unfamiliar and accumulated. Shin helps with the work, tells her to sleep, and eventually becomes the surface she falls asleep against.

He later idly braids her hair because he is stuck. Frederica observes that he looks pleased; he does not deny it. Lena later leaves the bad braid in place because undoing it feels `もったいない`.

The scene adds useful texture to V12. Lena’s self-reduction to function did not suddenly appear when medical staff finally removed her from duty. Earlier ordinary scenes already show the behavior and peers already try to interrupt it.

The difference in V12 is **institutional enforceability**: concern finally has a structure capable of making rest happen.

## 7.3 Body norms and practical care

`女王陛下、トレーニングをする` must be handled carefully. Lena worries that eating better has made her gain weight. That belief is hers; V2 should not turn it into narratorial fact or a health recommendation.

Shin’s response is more relevant: he thinks she remains too thin, accepts exercise as fine, and warns her not to overdo it and damage her body.

The recurrent form of Shin’s care remains behavioral and limit-oriented rather than elaborate emotional speech.

## 7.4 Accidental proximity is not consent evidence

Both `HELP!` stories are designed as comedy. An exhausted Shin unconsciously pulls Lena close for warmth and later remembers nothing. A drunk Lena later creates the opposite predicament.

These stories can support:

- bodily vulnerability;
- trust in the surrounding peer group;
- comic embarrassment;
- the transition from voice-only to embodied sociality.

They cannot support a claim that either unconscious/intoxicated character intentionally advanced the romance.

That distinction is retained explicitly in the relationship ledger.

# 8. `盃に射す影`: memory recovered without compulsory restoration

This is probably the single most analytically important short in Alter.1.

Its remembered Spearhead hanami establishes that, even in the Sector, the squad intentionally enjoyed `他愛もないやりとり` and `些細なイベント`. Kujo’s `笑えなくなったら負け` is not a doctrine of cheerful denial. It is an assertion that the exterminatory state should not be allowed to monopolize their affective life.

The present frame then changes the memory question through bureaucracy.

After the Republic falls, military personnel records become available. Shin can recover family names, former addresses and birth dates—including Kaie’s April 7 birthday. He even visits the address associated with his own family.

But the recovered house is simply `覚えてない知らない家`.

This produces one of the most important home/archive distinctions in the V2 corpus:

> **Recovered information can restore an option to know without requiring recovered emotional belonging.**

The archive does not command Shin to feel at home.

Nor does recovered memory require him to abandon Undertaker. He comes near Kaie’s birthday to restate `まだ覚えてる。忘れない`. The dead remain part of his identity.

What changes is the direction of ritual.

Lena asks for his birthday and proposes that they celebrate together. Administrative records originally designed to count personnel can become material for a **living calendar**.

The story’s Kaie is remembered/imagined within Shin’s consciousness. Her apparent approval of the survivors cannot be used as literal testimony from the dead. Its evidentiary function is internal: Shin can now imagine remembrance in a form that does not demand he join the remembered person.

# 9. Birthdays as longitudinal future technology

Alter.1 gives the series an unexpectedly coherent birthday architecture.

## 9.1 Stage 1 — birthday becomes useless

In the Sector, exact dates have little practical use. People die too quickly, records are stripped away, families are fragmented and the state does not treat the children as citizens whose biographies matter.

Shin forgets his own birthday.

## 9.2 Stage 2 — birthday becomes an excuse

Raiden remembers August 25. Shin uses it as a reason for surviving squadmates to eat something sweet and be noisy before another reorganization.

The point is not “traditional birthday culture survives.” It is that people can seize an otherwise useless date and make it socially valuable.

## 9.3 Stage 3 — archive restores dates

After the Republic falls, personnel records restore birthdays. The bureaucratic counter-archive now does something beyond memorializing the dead: it gives the living back pieces of personal time.

## 9.4 Stage 4 — Lena proposes repetition

`みんなで、お祝い。……やりましょうね` turns the recovered date into an anticipated future action.

Then `五月十九日（シン誕生日）` makes the logic explicit. Because birthdays were precisely the sort of thing people could not afford to care about, Lena wants celebration to become `当たり前`.

That is the recovery claim in miniature:

> ordinary does not mean trivial; ordinary means **reliably repeatable**.

## 9.5 Stage 5 — the Eighty-Six adopt the practice themselves

`四月二十日（セオ誕生日）` is decisive because the practice is no longer Lena’s civilizing project. The narration says birthday gifts have become a boom among the Eighty-Six. They watched Republic and Federacy peers do it and thought it looked fun.

They imitate, modify and prank with it.

Theo’s April birthday is celebrated late in July because operations intervened. The delay itself matters: unlike the Sector, missing the exact date does not make the person or celebration disappear. Friends can simply do it later.

Their real gift is an art set and a blackboard full of personal marks arranged around Theo’s own.

That transforms a military identity system into an ordinary relational artwork.

## 9.6 Stage 6 — birthday can resist war

Anju’s V11 birthday overlaps the Second Large-Scale Offensive. She explicitly refuses to let a Legion attack damage the meaning of her birth date. Dustin’s chaotic bouquet is dried so it will be waiting when they return, and Anju proposes that they make the bouquet again together.

The temporal structure is the opposite of a grave offering:

> preserve this now so it can participate in our return later.

## 9.7 Stage 7 — birthday survives loss of military role

During V12, Theo is no longer a Processor. Yet Lena and Shin use him to deliver Annette’s birthday gifts; Theo independently brings practical mugs; Annette invites him to stay for cake and coffee.

The social calendar does not eject him when military function disappears.

This is a tiny but powerful reinforcement of the disability/personhood thesis.

# 10. Roa Gracia cluster: ordinary peerhood inside extreme roles

## 10.1 Vika wants to play

`チェス` does more for Vika than its size suggests. The genius prince does not join because chess is a royal accomplishment; he joins because Shin and Raiden look like they are having fun. Lena then asks to join too.

`わたしも。入れてください` is banal language—and therefore analytically useful. A person defined by sovereign blood, impossible intellect, war research and death-derived artificial soldiers still has a peer-level desire to participate in play.

This does not absolve Vika’s ethics. It prevents his role from exhausting his characterization.

## 10.2 Coffee and tea make future time domestic

`コーヒーと紅茶と` is one of the strongest low-pressure Shin/Lena future scenes before explicit confession. Their current drinks are substitutes because war has cut access to real agricultural products. Lena says that someday, with the real things, she will ask which he actually likes—and plans to learn how to prepare both.

The future here is not victory, marriage or national reconstruction.

It is:

> someday there will be enough world, time, trade and ordinary proximity for us to compare beverages.

This is exactly what Document 10 means by love changing temporal grammar without monopolizing the whole future.

## 10.3 Martina/Lercheritt prevents easy moral appropriation

`かつて共に極光を見た` adds crucial social structure to Vika and Lerche. Original Lercheritt and her mother belong to a subordinated caste; Vika is royal. Their relationship cannot be treated as socially neutral private attachment.

Martina speaks of her daughter as a bird who flew away and hopes for her happiness. That can support a theme of parental relinquishment and the child’s independent path.

It cannot by itself legitimize Vika’s creation/use of Sirin Lerche. The caste asymmetry and the distinction between original Lercheritt and current Lerche remain mandatory.

# 11. Post-Roa youth: future objects and relational safety

## 11.1 The empty photo frame points forward

`五月十九日（シン誕生日）その二` links three generations and several times.

The childhood skeleton-knight storybook is restored to the genealogy of the Nouzen mark. Shin’s grandfather tries to reach a grandson who initially refuses contact. By the post-Roa present, Shin cannot yet say he wants the meeting—but can say he no longer wants to avoid it absolutely.

The birthday gift includes a photo frame with empty space.

Shin interprets the emptiness as:

> `これから得るもので埋めていけ`

This is one of the most compact visual-object formulations of the V2 future thesis.

An archive does not have to be completed only by recovering what was lost. Some empty spaces can be filled by **events that have not happened yet**.

## 11.2 Annette becomes a present-tense friend

Several supplemental pieces clarify Annette without resurrecting childhood.

She keeps Shin’s Para-RAID-source identity out of both Republic and Federacy reporting. She sorts preserved Nouzen books. Dustin assists. Later she gives Shin cufflinks as an apology without demanding a response. In the post-confession cookie short, she can joke that he “stole” her best friend.

The tone matters.

V4’s Annette wanted the childhood relationship to become legible again so her guilt could have an object. Alter.1’s later Annette can interact with **current Shin** as colleague/friend and tease him about **current Lena**.

That is evidence of successful relational revision without restoration.

## 11.3 `君がいるから`: safety as ordinary proximity

The paired first-leave stories are among the strongest supplemental Shin/Lena scenes.

Lena experiences Shin inside a domestic household rather than as commander, combatant or voice. Shin, after beginning to face his grandfather, recognizes that he had been too quick to tell Lena to forget or discard her lost homeland and that they had `ずいぶんすれ違っていた`.

Then the story makes the abandonment development bodily simple:

Lena is in the room next door.

She will not suddenly disappear.

`おいていかれない`.

This should not be converted into “Lena cures abandonment.” The causal network remains much broader: Raiden, Frederica, Giad, family recovery, ordinary leave, institutional safety and the absence of immediate battle all make this rest possible.

But it is extremely strong evidence that **relational safety can change what Shin’s body permits itself to do**.

# 12. Lena’s birthday and reciprocal objects

Lena’s July birthday is structurally the inverse of her earlier isolation.

The commander/Queen is repeatedly stopped by different people who give her small things chosen for her tastes or needs. The gift network includes Eighty-Six, Republic survivors, Federacy adults, subordinates, Fido and Shin. Her symbolic authority has not prevented others from seeing an ordinary person.

Shin’s gift—a music-box/kaleidoscope—is especially valuable because it is aesthetic, useless in the military sense, and selected to make her pause. Later he says simply:

> `おれが贈りたかったから、贈っただけです`

That sentence is a small self-authorship marker. He is not fulfilling debt or memorial obligation. He wants to give something.

The paired bookmark imagery adds reciprocity. Lena secretly ordered herself a version corresponding to the bookmark she gave Shin. Her object and his gift cast overlapping shadows.

Vika supplies an institutional contrast. Because he is royal, even a private birthday gift can become `下賜` or `褒賞`. Office changes what an apparently ordinary exchange means. He therefore gives Lena time/opportunity rather than an object.

This is a subtle but useful institutional principle:

> **formal status can constrain intimacy even when affection is genuine.**

# 13. Childhood, adolescence, and uneven maturity

Alter.1 strongly strengthens C009.

The text repeatedly refuses the choice between “these are children” and “these are competent soldiers.” Both are true.

- Twelve-year-old Shin can bury Rei and operate in a death zone.
- Thirteen-year-old Shin can command and also torment Raiden about bad cooking.
- Federacy-cadet Shin shocks Eugene with combat automaticity while lacking bus habits.
- Post-Morpho adults see sleeping heroes and conclude they are simply children.
- Later the supposedly elite Eighty-Six dump enormous quantities of candy into soda because someone said it would explode amusingly and soak everybody.

`スターシャワー・レモネード` says the proposition directly: their true faces are still those of teenage boys and girls, curious and reckless.

The developmental thesis should therefore remain:

> **War accelerates selected competencies while leaving age, curiosity, silliness, dependency needs, and unpracticed civilian capacities intact.**

That is not infantilization. It is a refusal to let survival competence retroactively make the conditions imposed on children legitimate.

# 14. Character corrections

## 14.1 Shin

Alter.1 strongly revises any remaining “emotionless Shin” residue.

Across the chronology he:

- teases;
- cooks badly;
- laughs;
- reads for pleasure;
- explores;
- repairs clothes;
- wants to share sights;
- chooses aesthetic gifts;
- feels bodily safety;
- embarrasses easily;
- remembers the dead;
- revises his judgments;
- and interprets an empty photo frame as space for future experience.

The major correction is not that Shin “was healthy all along.” It is that **war narrowed which parts of an already multidimensional person could reliably organize his future**.

## 14.2 Lena

Lena’s arc gains a stronger before-state. She has department-store leisure, a best friend, preferences in clothes and cake, and the expectation of a professional adult future. Bloody Regina later compresses this repertoire.

Alter.1 also gives repeated low-pressure evidence of private Lena:

- jealousy she does not yet understand;
- awkward bodily curiosity;
- exhaustion;
- gift enthusiasm;
- domestic flustering;
- pleasure in ordinary social exchange.

Queenhood is therefore even less defensible as her total identity.

## 14.3 Raiden

Raiden remains the practical stabilizer but is less “the normal one” than the legacy shorthand suggested. His birthday is one of the earliest sites where a social ritual is reconstructed inside the Sector. He cooks, sews, argues, reads Shin accurately and participates in teasing.

Alter.1 does not yet provide a clear long-term civilian vocation for him, so E003 remains open.

## 14.4 Theo

Theo’s art is present as ordinary identity long before amputation. Birthday gifts later center art rather than military equipment, and V12-period Theo can deliver presents and sit for cake after losing Processor function.

That strengthens the material—not merely symbolic—case that his personhood and social usefulness exceed combat.

## 14.5 Kurena

Kurena’s attraction to Shin is present early, but the supplementary corpus also keeps her inside ordinary peer practices: mending, birthdays, pranks, gifts and group life. The May-age short shows how much joy she can extract from something as banal as being the same age as Shin for thirteen days.

This further warns against defining her solely through failed romance.

## 14.6 Anju

The V11 birthday short is important because it gives Anju a future-facing Dustin scene not organized around replacing Daiya. She can accept Dustin’s bouquet, dry it, and plan to remake it after return without any implication that Daiya has been erased.

This supports the V5 principle that later attachment need not falsify earlier love.

## 14.7 Annette

Annette moves from childhood friend → estranged guilty survivor → colleague protecting Shin’s privacy → person capable of apologizing without demanding response → friend who can tease him about Lena.

Alter.1 therefore strongly confirms E012.

## 14.8 Frederica

Her early question about how much one may discard and remain oneself significantly improves her longitudinal arc. Later self-rule is no longer an isolated V12 insight; it answers an identity problem she had already articulated soon after the Five entered Giad.

## 14.9 Vika

Vika’s supplemental material preserves the ethical difficulties of caste and Lercheritt while also giving him peer desire. Wanting to join a chess game does not absolve him. It makes the synthesis more human and less schematic.

## 14.10 Fido

Fido is integrated into ordinary group life continuously—from helping Shin bury Rei, to Spearhead camp life, to birthday gifts. This strengthens the V10 conclusion that treating Fido as mere logistics equipment is not adequate to the series’ relational presentation.

# 15. Relationship-state corrections

## 15.1 Shin / Lena

Alter.1 adds several rungs to the relationship without replacing mainline milestones:

1. **pre-contact formal parallel:** age-twelve loneliness under shared sky;
2. **V1 shared-perception desire:** Shin wishes Lena could see the same blue ruin;
3. **Special Recon future representation:** he imagines her someday catching up;
4. **post-reunion bodily trust:** sleeping, proximity, scent, braiding, care;
5. **ordinary future micro-promises:** real coffee/tea someday;
6. **domestic safety:** she is nearby and will not disappear;
7. **deliberate gift self-authorship:** he gives because he wants to;
8. **reciprocal ordinary objects:** bookmark/music box/gifts;
9. **birthday repetition:** calendars make “again” materially thinkable.

This strongly supports R004 while narrowing any claim that romance alone produces Shin’s future.

## 15.2 Shin / Annette

The relationship becomes a model of non-restorative repair. They do not become five-year-olds again. Annette’s growth lies precisely in becoming able to relate to present Shin without requiring him to recreate the past so that her guilt can resolve neatly.

## 15.3 Eighty-Six peer group

Birthday culture, teasing, shared art, pet care and repeated meals demonstrate that the group is not only a military mutual-survival network. Their found family includes practices with no tactical utility.

The V09 warning still applies: found family can become a cage if it requires sameness. Alter.1 mostly supplies the healthier control—people adopting and changing practices together because they enjoy them.

# 16. Institutions, citizenship, and capability

Alter.1 strengthens the V12 capabilities thesis most effectively where it is least grandiose.

### Republic

- the state preserves Processor personnel records even while politically stripping people of citizenship, creating the possibility of later counter-archival recovery;
- civilian households reproduce racial disgust independently of military orders;
- resources sufficient for pets coexist with official claims that Eighty-Six humans cannot be accommodated;
- Lena/Annette’s privileged adolescence shows how class distributes practical and leisure capacities.

### Giad

- food is reliable;
- transport exists;
- libraries and memorial institutions are accessible;
- school and leave create low-pressure practice;
- adult soldiers can recognize child exploitation and set institutional aspiration toward making heroes unnecessary;
- archives can be recovered and used for living biography;
- relationships with officeholders still carry political constraints.

The key conclusion remains **supported self-government**, not guardianship.

Shin does not need Ernst to decide that history interests him. He needs a society where a library, memorial and bus can be used without lethal consequences.

# 17. Military doctrine, heroism, and irreplaceability

Alter.1 contributes three longitudinal military corrections.

First, the Sector’s distributed competence is born from abandonment. Claymore soldiers must fight independently because no one can promise help.

Second, Shin’s officer-school status demonstrates that formal training categories can badly underdescribe lived expertise. He is simultaneously a new Federacy cadet and a veteran with years of combat conditioning.

Third, the Morpho survivor’s `次こそは、お前たちの手は必要ない` gives the anti-hero thesis an adult ethical register. The correct response to extraordinary child competence is not endless admiration. It is institutional labor aimed at **making sacrifice unnecessary**.

These claims should remain separate from the later V7 doctrine of force redundancy, even though they converge retrospectively.

# 18. Personhood, AI, and relational standing

Alter.1 does not add a major new AI ontology comparable to V5 or V10, but it strengthens the relational layer.

Fido participates in burial, camp routine, transport, humor and gift exchange. People routinely address and interpret it as a participant. V10 already gave direct interiority; Alter.1 shows how that subjectivity is embedded in ordinary social life across years.

The Roa story involving Martina and Lercheritt adds a different caution. Biological parenthood, class status, Vika’s memory and Sirin continuity are not interchangeable. Martina’s willingness to let her daughter “fly away” cannot be turned into consent for every posthumous technological use associated with Lerche.

Personhood analysis must therefore preserve:

- original-person identity;
- current subject identity;
- social relation;
- designed role;
- class/power structure;
- and present capacity to refuse.

# 19. Body, illness, rest, and disability

The supplemental corpus repeatedly puts bodies back into roles that the mainline can make abstract.

- Shin gets ordinary illnesses.
- Unit capacity determines whether sickness can be rested or becomes lethal vulnerability.
- The Five eat enormous quantities because they are growing adolescents with unusually high muscle mass.
- Lena’s overwork produces visible sleep deprivation long before V12.
- Shin warns her not to exercise into injury.
- post-Morpho heroes collapse asleep like children.
- Theo’s post-amputation presence in V12 ordinary social exchange demonstrates continued participation outside the cockpit.

This strengthens the V2 distinction between **body as military resource** and **body as the material condition of the person’s continued life**.

# 20. Death, memory, grief, and inheritance

Alter.1’s memory contribution can be summarized as **archive without compulsion**.

Rei’s bones can be buried even though the Republic forbids markers. Kaie can be remembered through birthday and hanami. Records can restore names, dates and addresses. The old home can then fail to feel like home without invalidating the archive.

The empty photo frame extends the model still further:

> memory need not be a project of filling every blank with recovered past. Some blanks are legitimate space for future people and events.

Anju’s dried flowers make the same movement from another direction. Flowers cease to be only offerings to the dead; they can be preserved for the living person’s return.

The corpus therefore increasingly distinguishes:

- remembering the dead;
- reconstructing erased biography;
- returning to origin;
- continuing relationship;
- and allowing new experience.

None requires the others to become mandatory.

# 21. Japanese voice, address, and low-pressure register

Alter.1 is especially valuable for L09 because it samples characters when tactical command language is unnecessary.

## Shin

His core economy remains. He does not suddenly become verbose. But he jokes, needles Frederica, gives dry one-line answers, and later articulates quiet relational needs. The continuity matters: emotional development expands **what terse language carries** rather than replacing his voice.

## Lena

The gap between public officer and private young woman becomes much easier to hear. Her speech can be formal and commanding, then collapse into flustered fragments around sleeping Shin, gifts or domestic proximity.

## Frederica

Her archaic diction is not simply decorative characterization. `兄妹` explicitly makes speech choice part of her own identity theory.

## Shiden

Her slang-heavy teasing repeatedly functions as social diagnosis. She identifies Shin’s unusual trust around Lena without needing delicate psychological vocabulary.

## Vika

His royal position changes what even ordinary gift language can mean. The supplemental corpus is therefore useful for distinguishing linguistic intimacy from institutional speech acts.

# 22. Motifs and objects

## 22.1 Birthdays

The major new longitudinal motif: erased chronology becomes repeatable future.

## 22.2 Gifts

Gifts increasingly mark **desire without debt**. Shin’s strongest formulation is that he gave Lena something because he wanted to. Annette’s apology gift, by contrast, acknowledges responsibility without demanding absolution.

## 22.3 Food and drink

Bad custard, Republic recipes, coffee, tea, cake, lemonade and birthday meals repeatedly transform survival calories into shared taste. The future is often represented by the possibility of choosing what one likes.

Lena’s violet scent is a living bodily trace that can interrupt death-saturated spaces without erasing trauma. It should not be promoted into a cure metaphor.

## 22.6 Books and records

Libraries, personnel files, childhood books and preserved Nouzen collections turn text into continuity infrastructure. Crucially, recovered documents can be used without dictating what one must feel.

## 22.7 Photo frame

The empty frame is among the best V2 objects because its emptiness is not only lack. It is **capacity**.

## 22.8 Flowers

Flowers move between grave, birthday, gift and future return. V11 Anju explicitly wrests floral meaning away from war’s calendar.

# 23. Visual paratext

Alter.1’s visual apparatus is unusually self-conscious about memory and youth.

- The **cover** centers a broadly smiling Lena against film-strip imagery. This is publication-level framing, not a claim that Lena is the sole protagonist of the collection. The film-strip treatment encourages the reader to approach the shorts as snapshots or preserved moments rather than one continuous campaign.
- Section dividers resemble scrapbook/photo layouts. Republic, Federacy and Roa material is represented through collected images rather than battlefield maps, reinforcing the archival/everyday function.
- The contents art uses splashes of color and a playful Fido design. The book’s graphic identity is deliberately unlike the severe military apparatus of many numbered volumes.
- The `死神ときどき青春` divider pairs Shin and Lena in photographic framing, again emphasizing youth beside the role labels “Death God” and “Bloody Queen.”
- `オールスター香水バトル` receives a conspicuously comic graphic treatment, consistent with the afterword’s explicit non-mainline classification.

These are `VI` / `PT` observations. They support the book’s editorial positioning as memory/youth supplement; they do not independently establish character motives.

# 25. Hypothesis stress tests

## H1 — Recognition expands toward conditions for revisable life
**STRENGTHENED.**

Alter.1 supplies small-scale mechanisms: records, transport, leave, gifts, school, food, privacy and repeatable rituals. None confers humanity; each enlarges what already-human people can practically do.

## H2 — Pride is anti-dehumanization technology and possible prison
**STRENGTHENED WITH CONTROL EVIDENCE.**

Sector ordinary life shows pride never exhausted the Eighty-Six’s humanity. Later adoption of new rituals demonstrates that retaining pride need not prohibit cultural expansion.

## H3 — Anti-war but not pacifist
**STRENGTHENED.**

Supplemental military scenes admire tactical competence while foregrounding the obscenity of children needing such competence.

## H4 — Home becomes chosen/relational

# 29. Forward watchlist for Volume 13

Alter.1 changes the questions V13 must answer.

1. If ordinary life existed even in the Sector, what exactly is reproduced when later institutions begin making soldiers/exiles expendable? The analogy cannot merely be “they fight under pressure.”
2. Does V13 threaten **rights, return, institutional standing, material support, and ability to refuse**, or only comfort?
3. Can fear and exhaustion cause a formally better republic to reproduce the *relation* of the Eighty-Sixth Sector without reproducing San Magnolia’s genocide or racial ideology?
4. Does the birthday/ordinary-life infrastructure survive political regression?
5. How do Theo and other wounded/retired Eighty-Six fare when military usefulness is politically revalued?
6. Does Shin’s `弱い死神` interdependence survive demands for renewed heroic singularity?
7. Do refugees and citizens distinguish protection from permanent guardianship under emergency conditions?
8. Does Lena’s bounded guilt survive pressure to represent or redeem San Magnolia survivors?
9. Does archive recovery become politically weaponized—who gets to define “return,” homeland, debt, or citizenship?
10. Preserve the precise analogy boundary: structural rhyme does not equal moral equivalence.

# 30. Methodological conclusion

Alter.1 is not analytically secondary merely because much of it is light, comic, romantic, or domestic.

Precisely because the numbered novels foreground extreme situations, this supplemental corpus supplies a necessary control sample: **what do these people do when they are not currently being forced to answer the largest question in their lives?**

The answer materially changes the V2 synthesis.

The Eighty-Six were never creatures made only of pride and battle waiting for civilization to teach them ordinary humanity. They built ordinary life inside the very institution designed to erase them. Rescue matters because it changes the temporal and material conditions of those practices: an improvised sweet before a unit is dissolved can become a birthday next year; a remembered name can become a recovered birth date; a grave visit can become a celebration; an old address can be known without becoming home; an empty frame can wait for photographs that do not yet exist.

That gives Alter.1 a central place in the series’ future argument.

The deepest movement is not:

> death → normality.

It is:

> **precarious moments that do not assume tomorrow → practices that can be repeated because tomorrow has become a legitimate object of planning.**

And because those practices are chosen, borrowed, altered, joked about, delayed, rejected, and made personal, they also provide some of the clearest evidence that the series’ mature freedom is neither pure independence nor obedient normalization.

It is the ability to keep adding ways to live without being required to erase the person who survived before those ways were available.
