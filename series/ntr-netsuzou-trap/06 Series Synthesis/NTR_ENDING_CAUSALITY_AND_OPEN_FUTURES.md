# Ending causality and open futures: choosing to remain before learning how

The ending resolves a present choice more strongly than a durable relationship. Yuma names Hotaru as the person she loves, declines a restored romance she cannot responsibly offer Takeda, follows Hotaru's withdrawal and returns with her. Hotaru moves from category deflection to voiced temporal hurt, a trust bid, expressed fear of trust and chosen proximity. These are substantial developments. They do not establish complete disclosure, negotiated boundaries, admission to university, a secured shared home, sustained work or lifelong exclusivity. The endpoint is a shared return and an ordinary future proposed under conditions, with incompletely understood trust accepted in Yuma's interior account (V06 S0058–0084, S0157–0162; REL-0032–0036; CLM-0033–0035).

The causal question is how the story reaches that interval of rest. It does not move directly from public exposure to a successfully confessed couple. It passes through failed protection, differentiated male responses, bodily confrontation, a vulnerable exchange, renewed absence, conflicting family accounts, a costly unsuccessful search, chance recognition and another conflict over work and authority. Preserving that route prevents the final tenderness from swallowing the conditions that produced it.

## Exposure forces the inadequacy of the old account into public view

V05 closes with actual peers viewing a photograph after Fujiwara has been shown watching. V06 supplies a named account with a photography claim, comments and Takeda/Hotaru viewing. Publication agency is now much more strongly attributed, although no send panel, complete technical chain or total reach is depicted. The photograph does not objectively settle the girls' consent, mutual label or history; it gives an audience an extracted image on which to impose an account (V05 S0153–0156; V06 S0003–0004; INF-0028–0029; CLM-0036).

Hotaru publicly denies the relation and implicates herself and Fujiwara in teasing Yuma. Her later protection explanation is testimony about purpose. Yuma contests its benefit: the stigmatizing category would be preferable to the denial that erases the attachment she values. The original girl/friend exemption has become too costly to accept. Yet the source does not show a universally accepting school response. There are rumors, degrading comments, caring responses and friends' rejection of Hotaru/Fujiwara. The public world remains heterogeneous (V06 S0013–0021, S0034–0047, S0053–0055; REL-0031; INF-0030–0031; JPS-0025).

Post removal limits continued visible publication, while possible screenshots remain a voiced uncertainty. It is one practical change, not a universal restoration of privacy or Fujiwara's redemption. Takeda's punch and collar demand also belong to the causal route; anger at a violation explains the context without making the force disappear. The later generous recognition of Yuma's choice cannot be used to construct an entirely nonviolent male foil (V06 S0009–0012, S0031–0033; CON-0032–0033).

## Yuma's choice gains clarity without making her prior care fictitious

Yuma had privately recognized Hotaru in V04 and hedged that recognition in speech in V05. V06 therefore intensifies acknowledged choice rather than inventing attraction at the last moment. She tells Takeda the recipient is Hotaru and reports that she could not answer his earlier renewed approach. His response admits an interpretive limit while recognizing that love between people is not strange. Her subsequent report to Hotaru says she has properly refused; the exact refusal wording to Takeda is unshown. The inference must follow this sequence rather than score a prediction from the final partner alone (V04 S0083, S0129, S0144; V05 S0142; V06 S0058–0067; REL-0018, REL-0024, REL-0032; CLM-0029, CLM-0031).

Her explanation of prior confusion and irresponsibility gives the choice an ethical dimension. She distinguishes wishing she could have liked Takeda from having the affection needed to date him responsibly. This does not show that every earlier kindness or attraction involving him was fake. Nor does declining him repair all the earlier deceit. It supplies a concrete limit she now accepts: returning to the male couple cannot be used as a safe substitute for acknowledging the person she actually wants (V06 S0070–0071; CON-0039; JPS-0026–0027).

Takeda's recognition also changes the supposed competition. He is neither a successful normative destination nor merely an obstacle removed to free the girls. His practical care and capacity to reconsider create an alternative whose real value makes Yuma's refusal consequential. Fujiwara, by contrast, continues to present interference as a benefit to Takeda, which Takeda rejects in the parallel final conversation. These different male positions remain ethically and structurally important even after neither supplies the principal future (V03 S0093–0096, S0145; V06 S0061–0063, S0130–0132; REL-0017, REL-0032; INF-0035).

## Hotaru's vulnerable account is stronger than a retrospective total explanation

Before the vulnerable exchange, Yuma painfully drags Hotaru and releases the grip after her complaint. The confession confrontation includes another wrist grip, pain, a push and clothing/bag-area handling. A new named attachment does not authorize these acts. The direct hurt that follows must be read after this force, rather than used to transform the whole encounter into mutually permitted confession (V06 S0050–0053, S0073–0078; CON-0034–0035).

Hotaru then says that Yuma had failed to regard her for a long time and that disbelief now is intolerable; she asks whether Yuma will believe her. This partially resolves the earlier open defensive-attachment interpretation. It accords with withheld faces, jealousy, indirect love speech and fears of losing priority. Yuma's image of a guarded child provides a plausible synthesis, not omniscient psychology. Exact romantic onset, every early motive, the causes of older bruises and the permission status of the V01 room event remain open. Hotaru's current account cannot convert her earlier harmful conduct into universally protective intention (V01 S0028, S0055, S0136–0138; V02 S0099–0104; V06 S0078–0083; REL-0033; CLM-0006, CLM-0027, CLM-0033).

This is why the stronger defense of the ending need not deny harm. The source makes later receptive proximity and chosen attachment real enough to analyze independently. It does not make later desire evidence that earlier refusals were secretly invitations. Conversely, the earlier acts do not render the later exchange unreal. The mature account needs both findings because the plot now asks whether these subjects can stay together after the failed covers become explicit, not whether a final scene can retroactively give the covers a clean history (CON-0036, CON-0039).

## A morning smile does not solve approach and withdrawal

The shared morning and school plan are followed by Hotaru's absence, unanswered contact and relocation. The sequence rejects the inference that accepted closeness guarantees permanent availability. The reader sees a historical home with provision but little company, Hotaru's anti-burden statement to her father, and an empty new room. These images and words support a fear of dependence or burden, while a practical wish for independence remains compatible. Yuma does not hear the private anti-burden line and cannot operate with the reader's complete composite account (V06 S0085–0099, S0112; REL-0034; INF-0033; VIS-0032).

The father's claims require differentiated treatment. His belief in Hotaru's grades and domestic competence conflicts with Yuma's evidence. This narrows his authority to explain her everyday situation. It does not nullify the privacy condition he communicates: Hotaru asked that acquaintances not be helped to reach her. Likewise, the represented family absence does not justify inventing every detail of childhood deprivation or a clinical condition. The late recollection supplies a material circumstance and a voiced goal, not a complete origin story (V06 S0094–0105; CON-0037; JPS-0029).

Fujiwara's account of an arrangement “in form” and his conditional willingness to end it if Hotaru wants contributes another partial explanation. The historical terms tie his access to her time without company; Hotaru's own account places desired togetherness elsewhere. But no complete mutual breakup is depicted. The ending of the girls' denied relation therefore does not require a fabricated scene that tidies every acknowledged male couple into an officially concluded state (V06 S0109–0111; REL-0035; INF-0034).

## Pursuit matters, but effort is not entitlement or sufficient knowledge

Yuma returns to the father's space under a false textbook pretext and obtains rental clues. She misses school and goes to the city, repeatedly asking at marked possibilities. The search produces refusal, strangers and nonanswers; all marked places are checked, some without residents available. It does not demonstrate a reliable rescue technique. Her effort puts her where a chance encounter becomes possible, but chance rather than successful address certainty supplies actual access (V06 S0115–0129; AGY-0028; VIS-0033).

This distinction preserves agency without idealizing it. Yuma commits resources and accepts uncertainty instead of waiting passively for another approach. She also circumvents a communicated privacy boundary. The motive is understandable from attachment and fear of loss; the source does not thereby supply authority to recover Hotaru wherever she goes. Earlier Yuma already acknowledged that she had no right to direct Hotaru's work while inwardly refusing separation. V06 develops the same contradiction at a larger material scale (V03 S0101–0103, S0132–0133; V06 S0116–0118, S0140; AGY-0011–0012; CON-0037; CLM-0016, CLM-0034).

The childhood hand and city reunion then make the desired rescue emotionally legible, but the actual pulls meet resistance. Hotaru explains that she has work and has lost earnings; she asks Yuma to finish the tea and leave, and challenges how she found the address. No imminent emergency at the workplace is shown that would remove those costs. Being needed as a child does not create unlimited present access to the adult life Hotaru is trying to organize (V06 S0133–0140; CON-0038; VIS-0034).

## The trust scene accepts unfinished work rather than showing its completion

Yuma calls her behavior reliance on Hotaru and confronts leaving school and a shared future. Hotaru expresses fear of trusting someone. The source then juxtaposes recalled speech with reassurance, tears, a trust appeal, contact and quiet. This is not a comprehensive accountability conversation covering every earlier violation; it is a present encounter in which staying becomes possible despite fear. Old lines in the dark montage are remembered, not new current insults (V06 S0141–0155; JPS-0028; VIS-0035).

At the station Hotaru's coat grip and anxious eyes support chosen proximity. Yuma proposes living together if university admission succeeds. Her inward account accepts her inference that Hotaru's trust is incomplete, and her help vow is also inward. Hotaru does not independently say that the interpretation is wholly correct; neither girl speaks a durable protocol for withdrawal, disclosure or boundaries. The future is also material: fees and earnings have already constrained preparation, and the closing money/work exchange does not show a secured budget (V03 S0054–0058, S0080–0091; V05 S0072–0073; V06 S0157–0162; REL-0036; AGY-0031; JPS-0030).

The shared train rest therefore does real narrative work. It ends the mainline without another exposure cliffhanger and shows both present together after separation. The embedded body-cover aftermath separately confirms school return and adds caregiving/play. Hotaru's need bid and Yuma's inward mother joke preserve asymmetry within ordinary affection; Takeda explicitly distinguishes his heartbeat from romance. These gags are admitted aftermath, not university, cohabitation or a new couple certificate (V06 S0162, S0166–0167; REL-0037; CAST-0031).

## The strongest defense and the strongest objection

The strongest defense is that the ending replaces unattainable certainty with a choice to continue. The girls no longer need a perfectly understood identity, wholly trusted partner or completed independent life before acknowledging attachment. Takeda's recognition demonstrates that incomplete understanding need not require punitive rejection. Yuma's willingness to remain with uncertainty, Hotaru's staying gestures and the quiet return support this reading. The source permits meaningful reciprocity without staging a retrospective acquittal.

The strongest objection is that this resolution preserves a rescuing dependency rather than changing its governing behavior. Yuma's privacy circumvention and repeated resisted pulls show care imposing a preferred return; Hotaru's anti-burden retreat and later need bid keep independence and reliance unstable. The inward repeated-help vow can read as a renewed commitment to being uniquely needed, not evidence of learning to accept refusal. Work costs and lack of a durable boundary discussion make the objection substantial. It is weakened, but not eliminated, by Hotaru's voiced fear, chosen staying and practical future exchange: she is not depicted as simply unconscious of her wish or physically carried into a permanently settled life.

The undecidable question is not whether force or chosen return occurred; both are depicted. It is whether their future care can become reliably responsive to autonomy. The six-volume source stops before that test. The qualified ending is therefore both an attachment resolution and a preserved relational problem. External supplements, adaptation or retrospective hindsight cannot supply the missing outcome in this run.
