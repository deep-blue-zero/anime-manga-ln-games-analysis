# Agency, care and material constraint: the cost of being needed

The girls' relation is made from ordinary resources as well as desire: adjacent homes, parental provision and absence, school time, preparation fees, work shifts, food, housing deposits and the possibility of university. These conditions determine who can approach, leave, wait or provide help. They also expose a recurring contradiction. Yuma wants Hotaru safe and close, but being uniquely needed is part of what makes caring rewarding to her. Hotaru wants an autonomous life and claims that she should not burden anyone, but repeatedly seeks a particular person's proximity and sometimes represents that person's choices for her. Agency is therefore neither total freedom nor a fixed active/passive role. It is the use of available, costly options under unequal information and attachment.

The mature argument must preserve a double finding. The ending supplies meaningful chosen togetherness and renewed ordinary life. It also lets Yuma's rescue method cross privacy and bodily boundaries and impose a work cost. Present acceptance does not remove that cost. The question is whether care becomes answerable to the person's wishes and practical life, rather than whether caring is secretly sincere or merely false (AGY-0001–0031; CON-0016, CON-0037, CON-0038; CLM-0016, CLM-0034, CLM-0035).

## Childhood proximity is a resource and a claim to authority

Yuma's V01 childhood account establishes neighboring homes, bullying, help and satisfaction that Hotaru relied especially on her. The protective identity is not invented by the ending; it is present at the first boundary. Its positive side is actual attention and assistance. Its limiting side is the expectation that the unknown parts of Hotaru's life should remain accessible or subordinate to this familiar role. Her jealousy at a first boyfriend and displeasure at unfamiliar faces later make the possessive component explicit (V01 S0009–0010, S0057–0059; V02 S0135; REL-0001, REL-0008, AGY-0001, JPS-0006).

Hotaru's earlier loneliness is not initially a complete narrator-supplied explanation. V06 reveals an unreliable care context through an absent father, simple food and money notes, a prospective maternal partner's refusal and a missed parent conference. Material provision and emotional presence are contrasted. Yuma's accepted childhood hand appears as a distinct resource: someone remembers and accompanies the child. The pages support a history of loneliness without explaining every later harmful act or certifying a diagnosis (V06 S0093–0097; VIS-0032, CAST-0030, CLM-0033).

The asymmetry between these accounts matters. Yuma knows her own childhood care and Hotaru's current direct statements, but is not shown receiving the whole family flashback or the private anti-burden phone sentence. Her final understanding is an inference from what she knows, not a transcript of reader knowledge. Its compassion can be useful while its authority remains limited (V06 S0099–0109, S0148–0151; INF-0033, INF-0034).

## Mundane competence prevents a crisis-only character account

Yuma is athletic, can enjoy dates and rides, participates in basketball friendships, buys study books and sometimes chooses ordinary calm. Her mother helps with lunch and preparation arrangements; her cooking is imperfect. Hotaru supplies practical knowledge, hospitality, clothing assistance and workplace competence, while school and domestic evidence expose gaps. Takeda prepares lunches with frozen ingredients, assists applications and notes, discusses exams and likes ordinary shared activity. Fujiwara gives useful advanced-science advice and is reported to cook well. These differences matter because a person capable of harm is also present in unexceptional social life; useful competence is not redemption, and vulnerability is not general incapacity (V01 S0060–0066, S0082–0084, S0155–0162; V03 S0054–0059, S0080–0087, S0093–0098; V05 S0038–0047, S0081–0089, S0159–0162; CAST-0011–0014, AGY-0022, VIS-0028).

Cooking's comic asymmetry is particularly important. Yuma wants to help Hotaru eat while neither has an idealized household mastery. The heart-shaped food and newlywed joke make domestic possibility concrete; they do not establish a functioning future household. Takeda's ordinary meal practice and Fujiwara's reported skill disrupt a simplistic distribution in which moral character equals practical competence. The series gives the prospective couple material tasks it cannot solve by declaring love.

## Work enlarges Hotaru's life beyond the protector's map

Yuma assumes late homecomings indicate Fujiwara, then discovers Hotaru's hostess-bar work. Her original model of the friend's life is inadequate. A venue and costume establish represented labor and customer expectations, not paid sexual services. Hotaru's statement that it pays well and she needs savings supplies a goal, while its purpose remains undisclosed at V03. Yuma's wrist pull, refusal to leave and Hotaru's representation of her as a willing trial worker create competing claims over choice (V03 S0059–0079, S0092; INF-0013, CON-0014, AGY-0011).

Yuma's trial work is neither purely passive coercion nor a frictionless autonomous experiment. Her initial willingness is represented to the manager without her affirmative answer, and attachment makes departure costly. She later decides to persist and return. She requests practical assistance, resists customers and punches after the girls are drawn close; both receive workplace scolding. These consequences make resistance real and expensive. Subsequent participation does not authorize earlier recruitment or handling, while initial pressure does not erase later deliberate persistence (V03 S0075–0091, S0121–0129; CON-0014, CON-0015, CON-0017, AGY-0011, VIS-0015, VIS-0016).

Hotaru's authority objection has force. She asks why her work must be approved by Yuma and later rejects being treated as the bullied child. Yuma inwardly recognizes that she is neither guardian nor boyfriend. Yet she still resolves not to let Hotaru depart. Insight into an authority limit and a desire to override it coexist; self-recognition has not immediately transformed conduct (V03 S0099–0105, S0130–0133; CON-0016, AGY-0012, REL-0012, JPS-0009, CLM-0016).

Hotaru is contradictory too. She asserts her own working autonomy, publicly reserves Yuma from a customer, and admits she did not want Yuma in that same world. Her difference between the two girls can reflect care, possessiveness, experience, self-devaluation or several together. It is not a perfectly consistent autonomy philosophy. Nor does that inconsistency invalidate her narrower right to make work and departure decisions. The argument must be domain-specific rather than demanding a completely coherent person before acknowledging any legitimate boundary (V03 S0125, S0131, S0146–0147; AGY-0012, CON-0016, CON-0017).

## School produces time, cost and alternatives

School supplies more than scenery for secrecy. Preparatory classes require application, fees, attendance and study. Yuma's false cold excuse and absence become verifiable through her mother's call to the school; later fever is a genuine separate state, not a proven punishment mechanism. She recognizes debts to parents, Takeda, work and the manager. Care can be practical and nonromantic: Takeda's notes, application aid and illness arrangements remain useful after breakup (V03 S0054–0057, S0093–0097, S0134–0145; INF-0012, INF-0013, AGY-0013).

V04 and V05 future discussions differentiate the four people. Yuma's and Takeda's mock results affect university options, while Fujiwara's medical-faculty prospects are Takeda's testimony rather than achieved admission. Hotaru's earlier rich-marriage line is a voiced shield or possible wish, not a binding plan. The career survey's blankness is an observed nonanswer; the teacher authorizes a reminder, not Yuma's inspection. Yuma's attempt to obtain departure information turns an ordinary institution into another authority dispute (V03 S0049–0053; V04 S0043–0048; V05 S0013–0025; INF-0022, AGY-0020, CAST-0019).

Yuma's real summer distance, study and meals with Takeda also supply an alternative to constant crisis. She appreciates peace and wishes she could desire the honest kind person. The fact that she returns to Hotaru does not prove the calm had no value or that she never chose distance. Desire is not identical to evaluation of a life option, and the preferred ending has a cost because alternatives were real (V04 S0119–0129; REL-0020, AGY-0018, JPS-0014).

## Housing reveals that future proximity must be financed and defined

The rental brochure first supplies an alarming cue to Yuma, not an accomplished move. Later Hotaru says she plans to live alone after graduation and imagines sharing rooms and chores with her. Yuma's mother permits leaving under university-admission conditions. Publicly describing roomsharing with a close friend makes the wish legible without acknowledging its romantic content. Household geometry and joking divisions of labor give the future substance, while its tenancy, funding and mutual definition remain incomplete (V04 S0134–0144; V05 S0044–0047, S0072–0073, S0106–0109; REL-0030, AGY-0022, JPS-0019, JPS-0020, VIS-0025).

The eventual move changes the opportunity structure. After a tender confession exchange and shared morning, Hotaru misses school, stops answering and relocates. She asks her father for a rental guarantee, says she will work and does not want to burden anyone. The act is chosen and materially enabled; it is not simply another person's transfer of her. Her later trust fear supports an avoidance component, but a genuine independence goal remains compatible. The same behavior can serve autonomy and retreat at once (V06 S0085–0103, S0112, S0148; AGY-0027, REL-0034, JPS-0029).

Her father accepts an eighteen-year-old's work/living choice and believes she has good grades and domestic competence. The reader's prior school/cooking evidence and Yuma's lived knowledge contradict that specific assessment. His limited knowledge does not automatically invalidate Hotaru's communicated address restriction. Provision and permissiveness cannot by themselves establish adequate emotional understanding; neither can Yuma's better knowledge of certain weaknesses establish unlimited custodial authority (V06 S0100–0105; INF-0033, CON-0037).

## Pursuit has effort, deception, failure and chance

Yuma's search is active and costly. She returns under a textbook pretext, gains room access, finds marked rental clues, travels, misses school and asks strangers door by door. All marked places are checked, some residents absent, without locating Hotaru. She fears complete solitude and possible desire for someone else; these are her interpretations and worries. The reunion finally arrives by chance in a crowd. Persistence prepares the occasion but does not validate investigative certainty or prove that the intrusion was necessary in every possible alternative (V06 S0115–0129; AGY-0028, INF-0035, VIS-0033).

At reunion the material cost becomes explicit rather than sentimental inference. Hotaru breaks Yuma's grip, says she must work, then serves tea while complaining about lost income and asking her to leave. Yuma criticizes dropping school near graduation and invokes a lifelong future. Her concerns can be reasonable without making bodily insistence or address circumvention authorized. No shown imminent workplace emergency removes Hotaru's voiced choices (V06 S0133–0145; CON-0038, AGY-0029).

The scene repeats a childhood invitation while changing its terms. The child accepted the hand; the adult-seeming worker resists a pull. Later held hands and a coat grip add chosen proximity. A repeated image can link care across time while revealing that the original helper's authority has become contestable. The hand is not a universal permission symbol (VIS-0034).

## The boys are resources and decision-makers, not merely alternatives

Takeda offers ordinary help without an explicit dating price, then advice under incomplete information. His romantic desire remains; the relationship does not pay it automatically. His unauthorized following and mistaken rescue narrative show that caring concern can also overreach. His eventual recognition of Yuma's Hotaru attachment and rejection of Fujiwara's claimed benefit become meaningful because the source preserves his loss, hopes, error and force rather than making him a pure ethical device (AGY-0003, AGY-0006, AGY-0013, AGY-0017, AGY-0021, AGY-0030).

Fujiwara possesses knowledge the girls sometimes need. Yuma asks him for help despite his conduct; his location ignorance is not positively disproved. His partial relationship testimony usefully distinguishes form from emotional attachment. That utility does not redeem coercion or publication. The characters must sometimes act with information from an ethically compromised person, a condition different from endorsing his authority (V06 S0106–0111; INF-0034, REL-0035).

Friends, teachers, parents and workers widen the option space. Friends identify actual cruelty and offer concern; a teacher restores routine; parents provide or withhold money, permission and information; work imposes time and customer expectations. Their roles are bounded by thin independent characterization, but reducing them to obstructions would erase the social alternatives and resources that make the central choices costly (CAST-0030).

## Endpoint care accepts incompleteness while preserving asymmetry

The final future is conditional. Yuma proposes cohabitation if admitted to university and work after exams; Hotaru reports that moving consumed her funds. There is no completed lease or stable-income solution. Yuma inwardly accepts her inference of incomplete trust and vows repeated help. Hotaru's small staying gesture and shared rest support current agency toward closeness. They do not prove a sustainable equal distribution of care (V06 S0157–0162; AGY-0031, REL-0036, JPS-0030, CLM-0035).

The embedded post-return gag adds actual school return and then ordinary notes, study, food and a playful carrying request. Yuma's inward mother comparison makes care asymmetry comic, rather than supplying a diagnosis of permanent infantilization. It also prevents the tender train image from monopolizing the future: renewed daily life will require work, study and negotiation, not just rescue at a station (V06 S0166–0167; REL-0037, JPS-0031, VIS-0035).

The strongest affirmative reading is that the girls move from fantasy and denial to a practical future able to tolerate uncertainty. Conditions, finances and chosen gestures support it. The strongest opposing reading is that Yuma's protector gratification and Hotaru's dependence simply acquire romantic legitimacy. Pain, privacy circumvention, income loss and mother-like care support that alternative. The endpoint does not settle which pattern will dominate. Its achievement is a current return with newly speakable fear and material conditions; its limit is an unproved method of making care answerable and sustainable.
