# Direct attachment, independent preference, unshared hope

LateWinter extends the ending through ordinary disagreement rather than a distant completed future. Hotaru can refuse a group activity, defend not wanting friends and state her special interest in Yuma directly. Yuma can want a wider life for Hotaru while admitting that losing unique centrality would make her lonely. Their private closeness is real, but the hope remains unspoken. The supplement therefore strengthens attachment while leaving the practice of care, autonomy and shared understanding incomplete. Its new boundary is neither a mere replay of the reunion nor proof that love has solved the questions the mainline left open.

## A later institutional explanation is not a retroactively known successful plan

Yuma narrates that Hotaru asked her father to submit school withdrawal paperwork but he apparently forgot. Actual enrollment thus survives partly through another person's non-action. This retrospectively clarifies how a return remained institutionally available; it does not show that Hotaru deliberately kept a fallback or that the father intentionally protected her education. A neglectful omission can have a beneficial consequence without becoming care. The narrative's qualification and its report route remain necessary. (S0005; INF-0036, AGY-0032; CLM-0038.)

Hotaru's current account of her father's indifference and absence develops that constraint. The girlfriend's apparent dislike is her report; Yuma's thought connecting it to neglect is an inference. Hotaru's own distinction between childhood loneliness and present father indifference gives Yuma new direct access to a retrospective self-description. It strengthens the family/attachment reading already developed at V06 without assigning a sole cause to every earlier act, proving the father's mind or handing Yuma unrestricted guardian authority. Being more intelligible to the beloved does not make one's preferences subordinate to them. (S0005/0011–0012; REL-0040, INF-0037, JPS-0034; CLM-0040.)

The material result is modest but positive: school continues and graduation remains a near future constraint. Work, income and long-term home arrangements are not newly demonstrated. The S0012 coat reception/hanging positively depicts mundane H→Y hospitality, with a hanger and feasible resources. Care is not exclusively Yuma's role; this modest task nevertheless establishes no sustained household competence. Nothing in the private apartment sequence establishes which work Hotaru now performs, whether her spending has recovered or whether university conditions have been met. The mainline's economic and privacy conflicts retain their force; silence about them cannot count as solved sustainability. (S0005–0006/0011–0020; CLM-0046.)

## Ordinary refusal extends autonomy without certifying severe-conflict restraint

The meal invitation creates a social option beyond the closed dyad. Hotaru is included and declines with apology and later-contact language. The refusal preserves her preference while leaving contact with Yuma available. The later walk gives Yuma an opportunity to ask why she does not make friends or try taking interest. Hotaru's first reply contests the prescription; the next page compares unwanted interest to mathematics, politics and cricket. The joke makes a serious distinction concrete: advice about a good does not manufacture another person's desire for it. (S0007–0010; REL-0038, CON-0040–0041, AGY-0033–0034.)

This is a genuine independent-choice window for the original H-PC-04. It is also a different domain from the mainline's work, income and private-address costs. No forced attendance follows, which limits an always-compels account of Yuma. But the source supplies neither the immediate cafeteria response nor a repeated serious refusal/separation trial. The cut to coats and bags outside cannot be completed into Yuma abandoning lunch, eating happily with the friends or following at once. An unshown response supplies no evidence for either pursuit or secure accommodation. (S0008–0009; VIS-0036; [held-out adjudication](../07%20Audits%20and%20Handoffs/NTR_LATEWINTER_RECONSTRUCTION_VALIDATION_AUDIT.md).)

The broader ethical implication is that ordinary relationship practice has begun to include disagreement that remains a disagreement. That is an extension, not a proof of mature negotiation. Hotaru's valid choice objection survives her own other boundary harms. Yuma's concern can be real and still prescriptive. The source does not require a permanent autonomous-person/controlling-person division; it requires attention to what each does at this particular response opportunity. (CLM-0039.)

## Direct selective interest changes speech more clearly than it establishes a contract

Yuma generalizes the father/friend account into nobody interesting Hotaru. Hotaru explicitly corrects her: she has interest, in Yuma alone. Earlier practice and categorical defenses made closeness possible with a reduced relationship claim; this scene directly locates the claim after mutual naming. It strengthens present reciprocal attachment and gives H-LW a mode of private speech that the original undeclared-state model did not exclude. (S0012–0018; REL-0039, AGY-0035, JPS-0032; CLM-0040.)

Selective present investment differs from durable exclusivity. The phrase does not negotiate allowable other attachments, disagreement procedures, disclosure terms or continued availability. It names no fixed sexuality category. It is also a declaration of interest, not a new explicit request for a need to be met. Mainline fear/belief/hug bids retain their own evidence; LW cannot provide a fresh immediate-separation test merely by depicting familiar initiative. These distinctions keep the emotionally stronger statement from acquiring obligations it does not actually formulate.

Yuma's inward statement that she had long noticed somewhere in her heart is retrospective self-understanding. It makes latent recognition more plausible, but partial awareness is not earlier explicit knowledge or a completed bilateral definition. The old reader's uncertainty, Yuma's changing explanations and Hotaru's early motives remain time-indexed. Later understanding cannot turn earlier communicated objections into consent, erase Takeda's real pleasures or establish that every girlfriend act was knowingly fraudulent. (S0015–0016; REL-0041, JPS-0035; CLM-0040–0041.)

## The last inward wish is the ethical complication of the tender ending

Yuma wishes Hotaru might someday value others—family or friends—and admits that she herself would feel a little lonely. A welfare beyond the dyad becomes imaginable inside the dyad's pleasure. This is affirmative evidence against a wholly selfish-care reading. It is equally affirmative evidence that relinquishing exclusive centrality is costly to her. Hotaru's question could make the wish shared, but Yuma's nonanswer leaves it inward. The final scene supplies attention and closeness without a common account of the desired future. (S0019–0020; REL-0042, CON-0043, INF-0039, AGY-0036, VIS-0038.)

The strongest dependency/control counterreading accepts the attachment and asks how it is rewarded. Hotaru's narrowed investment gratifies Yuma; Yuma's broader hope is qualified by her own loneliness; the unspoken qualification preserves the comfortable present. The source thus adds evidence for rewarding closed dependence, while withholding the difficult later trial that might show more responsive care. It loses support for a total Hotaru passivity model, because she actively objects and chooses. It also loses support for the claim that Yuma cannot imagine welfare beyond herself. Neither limit defeats the stronger conditional counterreading. A relationship can be reciprocally desired and still depend on unequal authority or uncommunicated expectations. (CLM-0045.)

A favorable account sees modest restraint: Yuma does not enact a demand at the end, and Hotaru need not immediately cultivate others. That observation matters. It cannot independently establish the motive for silence or prove restraint when privacy, livelihood, credible loss or serious refusal is at stake. A harsher account that calls every pleasant moment fraudulent would also fail: present attraction and selective speech are directly represented. The source gives both pleasure and unresolved cost; the synthesis must retain both.

## A public afterlife differs from a voluntary shared public identity

Yuma's narration attributes photo spreading to Fujiwara and describes persistent unusual looks. This strengthens agency and social consequences beyond V06's already strong attribution. It does not show another upload, a technical dispatch chain, total reach, currently retained files or universal erasure. Removed public availability and continuing memory/inference are different tracks. The continued relation cannot redeem exposure merely because the resulting circumstances precede a happy private visit. (S0006; INF-0038, AGY-0037; CLM-0042.)

The peer extra gives a more differentiated public. 葉々 and 砂羽子, visually matching the invited school friends, infer the girls' relation from photos. 葉々 invokes individual freedom and says she will not interfere, then objects specifically to Hotaru as the partner. 砂羽子 identifies the interference. Category tolerance, partner approval and noninterference therefore do not coincide. These are particular peer positions, not all classmates' acceptance or a uniform homophobic explanation. The comic supplies reader knowledge the girls do not hear on the page. (S0022; REL-0043, INF-0040, JPS-0036, VIS-0039; CLM-0043.)

Public/private status has consequently changed through continuing consequences and named interpretations, without a shown jointly chosen disclosure account. Private clarity and publicly inferred relation can coexist. No common exclusivity terms, outward identity adoption or completed institutional response follows. Takeda's absent response and Fujiwara's retrospective mention supply no new dyad settlement, remorse or friendship end. (Whole witness; INF-0041, CAST-0038; CLM-0046.)

## Formal and linguistic changes carry independent analytical work

The source moves from institutional/peer frames to small comic invitation figures, to an extended interest argument, to concentrated private closeness. The spacious inward hope near the end then briefly imagines a world beyond that concentration, before the outward nonanswer restores proximity. The separately staged three-panel peer comic restores another viewpoint and ends in contradiction. This formal route explains why the after-story cannot be reduced to the girls being happier: it deliberately separates what the reader sees from what partners and peers share. (S0005–0022; VIS-0036–0039.)

The written semantic route is equally precise: friend noninterest, Yuma's nobody-interest inference and Hotaru's Y-only correction. Familiar 由真ちゃん and 蛍 coexist with polite 水科さん and evaluative 水科蛍 among peers. Present father indifference is separated from childhood loneliness by temporal language. Partial recognition and qualified loneliness are inward, while the final spoken answer withholds the hope. These are Japanese manga speech/address/attribution findings, not inferred vocal performance or imported localization. They substantiate changed relation and unequal knowledge independently of plot outcome. (JPS-0032–0036; CLM-0040/0043/0045.)
