# Information, infidelity and exposure: a visible fact can still misrepresent a relation

NTR does not simply hide the true girls' couple behind false male couples and then reveal it. It distributes conduct, feeling, labels and explanations differently. Someone can know that contact occurred without knowing what either participant wants. A person can correctly suspect concealment while assigning the wrong recipient or purpose. A public label can be accurate as social status and inadequate as an account of attachment. Exposure enlarges the audience of an image without supplying consent, negotiated commitment or a complete causal history. The central informational problem is therefore whose partial account gets to govern another person's choices.

Information has both enabling and coercive uses. Takeda's uncertainty leads to a boundary and later useful advice; Hotaru's partial reassurance changes his expectations; Fujiwara's knowledge supplies a price, a taunt or an imposed identity rather than straightforward clarification. Yuma's eventual direct naming improves the relation between knowledge and choice, but her investigative pretext shows that greater candor in one domain can coexist with deception in another. The six-volume history earns a stronger distinction than truth versus lies: access, accuracy, inference, interested testimony, withholding, authority over meaning and responsibility for circulation are separate variables (INF-0001–0035; CLM-0021, CLM-0027, CLM-0028, CLM-0036).

## Initial ignorance is produced by ordinary public appearances

The opening gives the reader an unusual informational position. Takeda sees close friends and receives a makeup explanation while the reader sees concealed intimacy. The mother interrupts a familiar balcony scene without sharing its private history. On the trip Yuma tells her mother only the girls will attend; Takeda's actual hesitation evidence can still plausibly fit nervousness. His ordinary slope question is misheard through Yuma's guilt. These are not demonstrations that every other person is foolish. Their interpretations follow incomplete or managed observations (V01 S0003–0005, S0017, S0031–0034, S0075, S0108–0110, S0123–0125; INF-0001, INF-0002).

The same volume splits violence knowledge. The reader sees Fujiwara's kick; Yuma and Takeda do not. An appealing skiing-couple image reassures Yuma under that absence. Her childhood protector self-conception does not give her the reader's access to current danger. Fujiwara, conversely, observes a particular kiss and later closeness but is not shown witnessing every encounter. His information advantage is consequential without being omniscient (V01 S0067–0068, S0082–0084, S0098, S0151–0152; INF-0003, INF-0004, VIS-0003).

This distribution makes early interpretation unstable in a productive way. Yuma can experience special jealousy without knowing its category. Hotaru can act through practice while withholding the relation she wants. Takeda can reasonably ask whether nerves explain hesitation. Fujiwara can exploit observations that undermine the public account. The governing problem cannot be solved merely by the analyst identifying a likely romantic pair, because that knowledge has not reached the people under the conditions they need.

## Infidelity depends on represented expectations, not a universal couple template

Yuma and Takeda publicly date. Her concealment, concern over discovery, later self-condemnation and admission of harm support an ordinary informal fidelity expectation. No complete explicit contract is depicted, so its exact terms should not be invented. Her real enjoyment, lunches and concern for his feelings coexist with misplaced priority and lies. In V02 saying nobody is present during a call converts the harmless-friend defense into an explicit falsehood. Hotaru's **浮気相手** category makes the contradiction nameable; it does not mean Yuma has agreed to become a second partner (V01 S0060–0066, S0101–0106; V02 S0031–0049, S0153–0155; REL-0003, REL-0006, INF-0005, JPS-0004, JPS-0006).

The breakup matters. V02 ends with requested distance and reconsideration; V03 newly supplies Yuma's “since the breakup” account. Later school care, meals and advice do not automatically restore exclusive dating. Calling every later Hotaru encounter cheating on Takeda would erase the recorded state transition and treat his continued desire as a commitment she has already granted. Calling every continued omission harmless friendship would instead lose the way incomplete truth sustains hope and limits informed choice. Those are distinct evaluations (V02 S0088–0092; V03 S0057, S0093–0097, S0105; V04 S0119–0121, S0135; V05 S0091–0095, S0104–0105; INF-0012, INF-0024, REL-0014, REL-0020, REL-0026).

Hotaru and Fujiwara have a different represented arrangement. She says she did not assume fidelity; later history supplies grammatically mutual no-complaint/no-prying/request-compliance terms under pressure, and V06 introduces his in-form account plus a physical-access/lonely-time explanation. These facts complicate conventional romantic exclusivity. They neither make every act consensual nor establish perfect equal bargaining. Nor does “in form” erase their actual public dating history or itself show a fully completed breakup (V02 S0029–0031, S0120–0122; V04 S0027–0031; V06 S0109–0111; REL-0010, REL-0022, REL-0035, INF-0018, INF-0034).

The girls' attachment becomes significant before it has an agreed exclusive definition. Yuma's V05 feeling of being two-timed by Hotaru is important interior evidence, especially as it prompts recognition of her earlier harm to Takeda. It is not independent proof that a mutually exclusive girls' couple already existed. Domestic imagination, shared jewelry, chosen contact and newlywed language make the desire substantial while leaving the agreement incomplete. Infidelity language therefore both exposes a real discrepancy and risks claiming terms the other person has not acknowledged (V05 S0039, S0045–0047, S0085–0088, S0141–0149; REL-0024, REL-0030, INF-0027, CLM-0027).

## A partial account can be sincere, strategic and causally effective

Hotaru's V03 account to Takeda explains a persistent admirer, lack of reciprocation, cessation and fear of misunderstanding. It changes his assurance before his next invitation. Her ensuing apology to Yuma acknowledges jealousy and special friendship. Then the reader receives the private nine-truth/one-lie tactic. This proves managed representation, not which exact clause is false or that every caring statement is fraudulent. The strongest inference is that she can tell consequential truth in a form that protects an unspoken position (V03 S0019–0035; INF-0011, REL-0013, JPS-0008).

Yuma's interpretations also require attribution. In V02 she inwardly assigns Hotaru's unnamed love speech to Fujiwara; Hotaru is not shown hearing or answering that thought. In V03 Fujiwara's suffocation report becomes Yuma's belief and drives pursuit, but remains his report of Hotaru's motive. In V04 her own inward Hotaru recognition and Takeda's explicit male-beloved assumption coexist. Useful advice about seeking the other person's answer does not require correct gender information to be useful; it also does not supply that information (V02 S0099–0104; V03 S0109–0116; V04 S0078–0085, S0118, S0123–0129; INF-0009, INF-0014, INF-0016, INF-0020).

V05's public denial of another beloved is different from earlier incomplete self-understanding. Yuma now privately knows the recipient and still says no one else is liked, while making roomsharing legible through friendship. Hotaru's “only for now” aside remains inward or faintly murmured and not mutually acknowledged. Growing private recognition does not ensure a truthful public account. Naming Hotaru with a hedge later changes the addressee's explicit information, though Hotaru's claim to have noticed supplies no reliable onset for earlier certainty (V05 S0100–0109, S0142–0143; INF-0025, INF-0027, JPS-0021, JPS-0022; CLM-0021 → CLM-0027).

The manga's delayed backstory is another form of asymmetry. Readers receive Hotaru's private anti-burden phone sentence and childhood household history; Yuma receives her father's current account, recalls her own care, and asks Fujiwara about the relationship. Her final psychological explanation is an inference from available fragments, not access to the entire reader dossier. This limit does not make her understanding worthless. It makes its risk and authority visible (V06 S0093–0109, S0148–0151; INF-0033, INF-0034).

## The photograph changes its function through six distinct stages

| Stage | What is represented | What does not follow | Source and interpreted home |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capture | Flash, phone-holder claim, surprised girls | Permission to record; exact later audience | V02 S0156–0158; INF-0008, CON-0012, VIS-0011 |
| Apparent deletion and copy | Phone deletion plus later revealed earlier forwarding and brother display | Complete erasure; girls knowing backup; Takeda receipt | V03 S0008, S0038–0040; INF-0010, VIS-0013 |
| Retention and contemplated exposure | Own computer image and tentative bomb thought | Completed sending/upload or fixed future plan | V04 S0145–0146; INF-0021, VIS-0023 |
| Actual peer audience | Multiple school peers display the image | Full chain/reach, actual uploader panel, agreed relationship or consent | V05 S0153–0156; INF-0028, CON-0031, VIS-0027 |
| Named attributed publication | FUJIWARA feed/origin claim, T/H screens, H attribution and F justification | Technical authentication or complete distribution history | V06 S0003, S0011, S0020; INF-0029, VIS-0029 |
| Visible-post removal and aftermath | F deletion report corroborated on a peer phone; rumors continue | Universal erasure, observed surviving screenshot, complete accountability | V06 S0031–0034, S0039–0047; INF-0031, VIS-0030 |

These stages are analytically distinct. The physical image is not a new proof of all previous conduct whenever it reappears. Its portability changes who can exert power over the girls' public appearance. Deletion's insufficiency first concerns a known third-party copy; final removal's insufficiency concerns the original violation and possible persistence, without current storage proof. The small difference between an observed retained copy and a merely possible screenshot matters: ethical seriousness does not authorize invented evidence.

Fujiwara's dissemination role becomes strongly attributable at V06. The named interface, origin claim, Hotaru's attribution and his beneficiary justification converge. There is no need to elevate an unsupported hacking hypothesis to equal weight simply because pressing upload is unshown. There is equally no need to claim technical authentication, exact reach or every recipient. The source supports a strong narrative agency conclusion with specified operational limits (INF-0029; CLM-0028 → CLM-0036).

The photograph also compresses context. It makes contact visible while removing the sequence of objections, reapproach, cover, assent and interpretation. A viewer can reasonably infer girl–girl involvement and still be wrong about an agreed romance or fully permitted act. Public visibility therefore intensifies the problem of meaning rather than automatically solving it. The form turns a secret into an externally usable object; the informational ethics concern who may publish and interpret another person's embodied moment.

## Protection through a false account is still answerable to its subject

Hotaru publicly denies the girls' relation and implicates herself and Fujiwara in teasing Yuma. That speech establishes self-complicity in a presented story, not a verified prior joint scheme. A later peer allegation that Hotaru ordered exposure is rumor. Privately she says she protected Yuma from being called a lesbian; Yuma prefers the category to an account that negates their attachment. The disagreement is not simply whether the story was beneficial. It is whether Hotaru has authority to define the cost Yuma should prefer (V06 S0017–0021, S0042, S0053–0056; INF-0030, REL-0031, JPS-0025).

The public response itself varies. Some peers taunt or degrade; some condemn Hotaru's apparent cruelty and demand an apology; friends advise cutting ties; a teacher restores routine; a peer suggests police action without an actual report. Neither universal acceptance nor universal hostility describes this sequence. The alternative sources of care matter: Yuma's choice to pursue Hotaru is made alongside friends who identify actual harm, not outside a social world with no other possible support (V06 S0021–0025, S0033–0047; CAST-0030, VIS-0030).

Yuma's explicit Hotaru naming to Takeda then produces a different disclosure route. It is a subject's account of her own attachment, not a captured artifact forcing a public category. Takeda recognizes her choice while acknowledging that he cannot fully grasp the friendship/romance distinction. He is not thereby told every hidden act. The refusal is later reported to Hotaru, with exact refusal wording unshown. This gives strong evidence of changed availability and a bounded evidential limit, rather than leaving the entire outcome indeterminate (V06 S0057–0067; INF-0032, REL-0032, JPS-0026, JPS-0027, CLM-0029, CLM-0031).

## A claimed beneficiary can reject the disclosure's moral story

Fujiwara's first account follows Takeda's punch and collar grip. His claim is that the girls concealed things and he meant to wake Takeda up. He acknowledges an intervention while minimizing culpability through the recipient's supposed interest. Later, unrestrained, he refers back to that account when directly asked about the photo-related act. Takeda asks whether this would make him happy and rejects it. The dismissive final line belongs to Takeda, with no further Fujiwara reply depicted (V06 S0009–0012, S0131–0132; JPS-0024, INF-0035, CLM-0032).

This rejection makes exposure more than a necessary plot mechanism for romantic revelation. The very person positioned as beneficiary refuses the ethical account that would justify it. Takeda's own punch remains a contradictory act; it does not invalidate the narrower rejection. Similarly, his prior ignorance does not make Fujiwara entitled to publish the image as corrective education. Useful consequences and violations remain independently assessable.

Fujiwara's later relationship testimony also deserves independent treatment. Calling the Hotaru arrangement in form and giving its terms supplies information useful to Yuma. Denying knowledge of her location is not positively contradicted. A pattern of prior withholding cannot prove that every subsequent ignorance statement is false. The source supports an information controller who can sometimes clarify; his clarity neither redeems previous harm nor authorizes an invented universal liar model (V06 S0107–0111; INF-0034).

## Counterreadings and scope of resolution

The strongest revelation-centered counterreading holds that greater visibility finally frees the authentic attachment from misleading couple appearances. It is supported by the image crisis, named choice, Takeda's recognition and joint return. Its limit is that visibility initially causes humiliation and further denial, while acknowledged love is followed by Hotaru's absence. Disclosure alone does not produce trust, equal authority or a sustainable future.

The stronger critical alternative is that all truth in the series remains another instrument of control: Hotaru protects through erasure, Fujiwara uses exposure, and Yuma uses a false pretext to pursue. Those acts support the critique. But it would lose meaningful differences to call Takeda's nontransactional advice, Yuma's cost-bearing admission or Hotaru's direct trust fear merely equivalent manipulation. Their later words and choices change information in ways the earlier categories could not, even while incomplete repair remains.

The bounded conclusion is that the manga advances toward an attachment whose people can name a recipient and accept an uncertain response, while leaving substantial authority and knowledge problems unresolved. The end is more explicit than the opening, not fully transparent. All claims here remain Japanese-mainline claims: no localization, reception, interview, adaptation or external supplement is used to turn a situated statement into exhaustive truth.
