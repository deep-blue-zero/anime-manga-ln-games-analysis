# A relation that becomes speakable before its terms become secure

## Governing argument

*NTR: Netsuzou Trap* begins by making secret girl–girl intimacy disturb the apparent stability of two publicly acknowledged male–female couples. Its developing question is larger than which attachment is genuine. It asks what happens when people obtain access to another person, or define that person's good, without supplying an account the other can adequately accept or contest. Practice, protection, friendship, sincerity, a limited arrangement and helpful revelation all provide plausible accounts of conduct. Their inadequacy becomes visible when the actual recipient's wishes, refusals or costs return. The completed manga moves toward named attachment and chosen continuation, but does not make that recognition a reliable method of respecting autonomy.

The central relation becomes more reciprocal and more explicit. Yuma learns whom she wants, admits some of the harm she has caused Takeda, and chooses Hotaru with greater candor. Hotaru's temporal hurt and fear of trust make her attachment more directly intelligible than early teasing and withheld faces could. Yet Yuma's clearer love still crosses privacy and work boundaries; Hotaru's direct vulnerability does not erase her earlier imposed acts or establish complete present trust. The ending secures a shared return more strongly than a repaired, sustainable relationship. This is a substantive achievement and a substantive limit: the girls can choose to remain before the manga demonstrates how their care will become reliably answerable to the person receiving it (V01 S0009–0016, S0130–0138; V03 S0099–0103, S0132–0133; V06 S0070–0084, S0137–0140, S0148–0162; CLM-0005, CLM-0016, CLM-0033–0035).

This is a retrospective interpretation of the six exact Japanese witnesses, not an omniscient account of every motive or a claim that the source forecasts an adult future. ## The apparent problem of inexperienced couples becomes a problem of adequate acknowledgment

The opening public couples give the reader familiar expectations before the girls possess a shared romantic definition. Yuma and Takeda have ordinary school familiarity, date enjoyment, nervousness and attempted couple progress. Hotaru and Fujiwara appear as an attractive pair. The first clandestine acts can consequently look like disruptive exceptions: teasing goes too far, a new girlfriend compares experiences, a childhood friend resists losing priority. But the source already makes the public image insufficient. Hotaru's practice explanation accompanies Yuma's objections; Fujiwara's kick occurs outside Yuma and Takeda's knowledge; later appealing couple staging reassures someone who lacks that observation. Social legibility and private conduct diverge before the manga has established any final preferred pair (V01 S0011–0024, S0060–0068, S0082–0097; REL-0003–0005; CON-0001, CON-0003–0004; INF-0003; VIS-0003).

Yuma's dream, in which Hotaru replaces Takeda, intensifies the discrepancy without completing recognition. The scene belongs to her internal staging, so it supplies neither Hotaru's actual proposition nor permission. Yuma's girls-and-friends account can explain why intimacy seems less serious while failing to explain why she wants it differently. Hotaru's practice account relocates purpose toward the male relationship while preserving private access. These explanations initially shelter an uncertain attachment. They also reduce the pressure to ask the other girl for an answer about what the relation is (V01 S0103, S0114–0117; JPS-0001–0002; VIS-0004; CLM-0002, CLM-0005).

The V02 room scene changes the novice-couple problem. Yuma insists on progress, but Takeda notices discomfort and stops, choosing separate bedding and naming his sense that she does not sufficiently like him. Performing the next couple act does not supply the reciprocal affection he wants. His boundary later withdraws a morning routine after suspicious contact, a missed date and an opaque account. Yuma's lunch and date efforts remain real care; they cannot make a misleading relationship adequate merely by restoring its rituals. The question is therefore no longer just how the girlfriend becomes ready. It is whether the couple's acknowledged form matches the affection actually available (V02 S0008–0013, S0048–0056, S0085–0092, S0138–0140; CON-0007; REL-0006–0007; INF-0007).

Hotaru's explicit infidelity language makes another mismatch unavoidable. Challenged to tell Takeda, Yuma says nobody is there during his call even while resisting Hotaru's continuing contact. The lie demonstrates the failure of the harmless-friend exemption. It establishes responsibility for concealment without establishing that every concealed act was freely chosen. The reader must separate the moral problem of a false account from the permission problem inside the situation it conceals. The manga's force lies partly in making both true at once (V02 S0031–0049; INF-0005; CON-0008; JPS-0004; CLM-0005, CLM-0011).

## Yuma's recognition changes her explanation faster than it changes her claim to access

Yuma's trajectory is not a simple passage from passivity to authentic initiative. She initially values the pleasure of being uniquely needed by Hotaru. Her jealousy at another boyfriend and unknown faces of Hotaru later becomes a privately admitted priority problem. That admission makes concern partly self-interested without making the concern false. The practical wish to protect and the wish to preserve a special place are mutually reinforcing until Hotaru's independent life makes them conflict (V01 S0009–0010, S0057–0059; V02 S0123–0135; REL-0001, REL-0008; JPS-0006).

Work discovery exposes the limits of her protector map. Hotaru has a paid role, skills, savings aims, customers and a manager that Yuma did not know. Yuma follows, persists, participates under a pressured trial arrangement and resists customers at real cost. Hotaru's objection to Yuma deciding her work is not invalidated by hazards in the setting. Yuma inwardly recognizes that she is neither guardian nor boyfriend, yet resolves not to let Hotaru separate even if she is hated. Knowledge of a right and compliance with it are different achievements. This contradiction survives the later confession (V03 S0063–0091, S0099–0105, S0125–0133; AGY-0011–0012; CON-0014–0017; INF-0013; CLM-0016).

V04 gives Yuma explicit private recipient recognition. Takeda's advice to ask the desired person's feelings provides a useful procedure without knowing whom she likes. The reader also sees his male-beloved assumption; Yuma later hears Fujiwara report his bad-man concern. That report is her available evidence, distinct from Takeda's private wording. Her summer distance, study and peaceful meals with Takeda then supply a real alternative. She wishes she could desire the honest, kind person, while attraction returns toward Hotaru. Quiet stability is valuable and still insufficient to determine affection (V04 S0075–0085, S0118–0129; REL-0018, REL-0020–0021; INF-0020; JPS-0014).

The renewed practice request is consequently a knowingly incomplete account. Yuma refuses to name the person she likes and asks for closeness under an unnamed-beloved purpose, while privately knowing that Hotaru is the recipient. Hotaru's affirmative response is local cooperation, not a plain mutual romantic definition. Yuma's private apology recognizes concealment; it does not give Hotaru the missing answer. Yuma has become a pursuer whose desired act may be accepted while her explanation remains evasive. This differs both from her first confusion and from Hotaru's earlier continuation over objections (V04 S0135–0144; CON-0023; INF-0019; JPS-0017; CLM-0021–0022).

V05 makes her initiative harder to idealize. She reaches for a private survey with a true but incomplete teacher-reminder explanation, reacts through contact and a slap, imagines domestic partnership, denies another beloved publicly and finally names Hotaru with a hedge. Her experience of betrayal leads to a meaningful comparison with Takeda's hurt, yet the apology he receives omits its cause. More self-knowledge produces more possible responsibility; it does not automatically produce full disclosure or restraint. In V06 her reported proper refusal and explicit ownership of irresponsible dating improve one domain. Her later deceptive search and resisted pulls preserve another domain's overreach (V05 S0019–0034, S0104–0108, S0138–0153; V06 S0066–0071, S0116–0118, S0137–0140; INF-0022, INF-0025, INF-0027, INF-0035; CON-0030, CON-0037–0038; CLM-0034).

## Hotaru's attachment becomes stronger evidence without becoming a universal excuse

Hotaru's early bright surface is repeatedly interrupted by withheld faces, redundancy worries and withdrawal around Yuma's boyfriend progress. The pattern weakens a wholly amusement-driven account without proving a named romantic intention at every act. V02's unnamed love description and comfort beside a self-described bad person add serious affect beneath the teasing. Yuma's assignment of that beloved to Fujiwara is inward and unconfirmed. Hotaru's ease account concerns the discomfort of goodness and expectations, not objective physical safety beside the person already shown to kick her (V01 S0028, S0055, S0069–0072, S0105–0107; V02 S0099–0104, S0120–0122; REL-0002, REL-0009–0010; JPS-0005; VIS-0002).

V03 supplies an actual apology, jealousy acknowledgment, special-friend pledge and cessation promise, then a private mixed-truth tactic. The tactic establishes managed presentation but identifies no false clause. Sincerity and strategy can coexist. Her workplace register adds another distinction: polite service, familiar possessive claim and autonomy confrontation are different audience-conditioned actions. She can defend her right to work while representing Yuma's trial willingness without an affirmative answer, claiming her against a client and being pleased when she quits. A valid autonomy objection does not require a perfectly coherent autonomy philosophy, and its validity does not acquit her contrary conduct (V03 S0019–0035, S0071–0076, S0085, S0101–0103, S0125–0131, S0146–0147; INF-0011; CON-0013–0017; JPS-0008, JPS-0010; CLM-0016).

Her later domestic possibilities and affirmative responses complicate an account of relentless imposition. She can accept a requested embrace, invite Yuma home, pause at a homework objection, imagine continued adult proximity and perform useful care. Those choices matter because they show that changing permission and agency are analytically available within a harmful history. Her no-count language after Yuma names her also causes a new relational injury: she uses the exemption that once made closeness possible to reject its significance when it becomes explicit. This is defensive or distancing conduct more strongly than unawareness, but her own desired relation is still not plainly stated at that boundary (V04 S0048–0051, S0135–0141; V05 S0044–0047, S0060–0068, S0097–0101, S0142–0144; REL-0019, REL-0025; CON-0026, CON-0028; JPS-0019–0022; CLM-0027).

V06 increases the authority of the attachment explanation through directly voiced temporal hurt and fear. Hotaru says Yuma did not regard her for a long time, asks whether she will be believed and later says trusting someone is frightening. Relocation after receptive closeness shows that being chosen does not settle the expectation of loss. The private anti-burden phone statement and childhood household absence support an analyst's defensive-attachment mechanism; Yuma does not receive every reader-visible part of that history. Her guarded-child interpretation is a plausible response to what she does know, not a diagnosis supplied by an omniscient narrator (V06 S0078–0083, S0085–0103, S0148–0151; REL-0033–0034; INF-0033; JPS-0028–0029; VIS-0032; CLM-0033).

This strengthens a retrospective motive account without dating exact onset, explaining every early initiative or settling the V01 room's unshown permission. It also leaves independence a real possible goal within retreat. Hotaru obtains a guarantee, moves, works, requests privacy and objects to lost earnings; she is not simply a child waiting to be recovered. The completed source makes her need more intelligible while preserving the authority of particular refusals. Sympathy adds explanation; it cannot replace another person's answer.

## The boys test sincerity and authority in different ways

Takeda's independent trajectory asks what believable reciprocity permits him to want and do. His first inquiry coexists with a sudden unrequested kiss; later stopping and distance supply stronger responsiveness under perceptible discomfort. After breakup he helps, hopes and gives a procedure for seeking another person's answer. He can be a genuinely valuable alternative whom Yuma does not sufficiently desire. Making him either ideal virtue or a disposable obstacle would erase why refusing him costs something and why gratitude cannot supply owed romance (V01 S0019–0022, S0060–0066; V02 S0011–0013, S0088–0092; V03 S0056–0057, S0093–0097, S0105; V04 S0078–0085, S0127–0129; REL-0004, REL-0007, REL-0015, REL-0020–0021).

His errors keep that ethical capacity particular. He follows Yuma secretly despite inwardly condemning the act and constructs a mistaken rescue account from the workplace glimpse. His vulnerable comfort precedes a renewal bid without an explicit care-for-dating price, making timing and initiated contact relevant rather than frictionless. He punches the publisher, then later recognizes Yuma's named Hotaru attachment while admitting incomplete understanding. The capacity to respect a choice does not become a guarantee against intrusion or violence. It is a concrete distinction he can make under new information (V05 S0050–0056, S0069–0070, S0149–0153; V06 S0009–0012, S0058–0063; CON-0029–0030, CON-0033; INF-0023, INF-0032; AGY-0030; JPS-0027).

Fujiwara's trajectory moves along another axis: partial knowledge becomes leverage, retained evidence, contemplated exposure, attributed publication and a beneficiary defense. His observation does not give him the complete consent history or every inner motive, yet he can demand access for silence and prescribe a total identity or intention. Some of his criticism identifies real concealment; its truth does not authorize the price, the imposed label or the public act. The eventual Takeda–Fujiwara exchange is decisive because the claimed beneficiary refuses the service. Fujiwara knows enough to intervene, but cannot establish the authority to decide what truth should cost its subjects or supposedly help its recipient (V02 S0081–0082, S0110–0117; V04 S0123–0124, S0145–0146; V06 S0003, S0011, S0131–0132; INF-0008, INF-0020–0021, INF-0029, INF-0035; CLM-0032, CLM-0036).

Ordinary usefulness makes that participant more intelligible without redeeming him. He supplies preparation advice, is reported to cook well, declines to forbid Hotaru's work and gives Yuma a useful in-form relationship account. There is no positive evidence that his location ignorance is false. A universal liar or villain-only dialogue model would fail these observations. An ethical redemption would fail the unchanged violence, leverage and publication. The source gives a person who can clarify some things and harm others, with sparse interior access and no complete remorse transition (V03 S0058, S0109–0112; V05 S0161–0162; V06 S0107–0111, S0166; REL-0017, REL-0030, REL-0035; INF-0034; VIS-0028).

## Different commitments produce different meanings of infidelity

The publicly dated Yuma–Takeda relation supports an ordinary informal fidelity expectation through concealment, guilt, distance and later ownership of harm. It supplies no exhaustive negotiated contract. V02's requested distance and V03's newly stated breakup matter: later friendship, notes and renewed desire cannot automatically restore exclusivity. Yuma can mislead hope after breakup without every subsequent Hotaru encounter being cheating on a still-current boyfriend. Equally, breakup does not make withholding a known recipient incapable of hurting someone who receives an incomplete account (V02 S0088–0092; V03 S0057, S0093–0105; V05 S0091–0095, S0104–0105; REL-0014–0015, REL-0026–0027; INF-0012, INF-0024).

Hotaru–Fujiwara has different represented terms: she says she did not expect fidelity; historical mutual no-complaint/no-prying/request language appears under pressure; the final account describes a limited arrangement with emotional company and physical access rather than securely mutual love. Mutual grammar does not establish equal enforcement or act-specific permission. Nor does an in-form account itself enact a complete mutual breakup. Their public label has real consequences while remaining inadequate as a description of attachment or freedom (V02 S0029–0031, S0120–0122; V04 S0028–0031; V06 S0109–0111; REL-0010, REL-0022, REL-0035; CON-0021; INF-0018, INF-0034).

The girls' intimacy becomes significant before agreed exclusivity. Yuma's V05 feeling of being two-timed is inward evidence of hurt and an ethical comparison with Takeda's pain, not proof that Hotaru violated a previously negotiated exclusive girls' couple. Matching necklaces, rooms, chores, newlywed play and local assent give the imagined relation substance; their failure to settle its definition is precisely the problem. The title's betrayal field therefore cannot be reduced to an uncomplicated switch from false male couples to a true female one. It concerns mismatches among conduct, expectation, acknowledged form and the accounts offered to people affected by them (V05 S0039–0047, S0081–0088, S0138–0149; REL-0024–0026, REL-0030; INF-0027; CLM-0027).

## Ordinary futures make attachment valuable and costly

The girls do not want only the continuation of clandestine scenes. Shopping, fireworks, food, notes, shared rooms and study make closeness desirable as daily life. Hotaru's married-neighbor fantasy initially locates continued proximity inside an ordinary public route; later roomsharing makes another route imaginable. These voiced and pictured futures are not fixed heterosexual identity, completed marriage or an agreed household. They show how available social forms can contain a desire before the participants know how to state its full meaning (V04 S0048–0051, S0062–0069; V05 S0038–0047, S0081–0085, S0106–0108; REL-0019, REL-0030; JPS-0013, JPS-0019–0020; VIS-0025–0026).

They also introduce costs that romance cannot simply dissolve. Preparation has fees and attendance obligations; pursuit disrupts school and paid classes. Work gives Hotaru savings and a larger social world but carries customer pressure and limits on time. Moving needs a guarantee and consumes money. Yuma's mother's admission condition returns in the closing conditional cohabitation proposal. A sustainable shared life therefore requires education, income, skill and negotiation as well as a chosen recipient. The domestic cooking joke is useful because neither girl possesses idealized household competence, while Takeda's self-made lunch and Fujiwara's reported meal disrupt any equation of moral worth with practical skill (V03 S0054–0058, S0080–0091, S0138–0144; V05 S0072–0073, S0159–0162; V06 S0098–0103, S0138–0140, S0158–0161; AGY-0011, AGY-0022, AGY-0027–0029, AGY-0031; VIS-0028).

Parents, friends, teachers and workers are consequently more than interruptions. They supply resources, warnings, routine, privacy limits and alternative care. The father's mistaken assessment of Hotaru's domestic and school competence does not cancel her communicated address restriction. Friends can identify actual cruelty while the public audience also includes degrading rumor and more sympathetic responses. The central choice is made within a heterogeneous social world, rather than a closed system where no one else could help or where every outside relation is merely conventional repression (V06 S0021–0047, S0094–0105; CAST-0030; INF-0030–0033; CON-0037).

## Changing permission changes the relation without cleansing its history

The manga's movement toward reciprocity is intelligible only if particular permissions remain particular. Yuma's early objections, Hotaru's continuations, the unshown V01 room completion and Fujiwara's violence cannot be resolved by later attraction. A later scene in which Yuma requests contact and Hotaru says yes is ethically different because it gives a local affirmative answer. It does not supply either girl with continuing permission for another occasion or a warrant to reinterpret every earlier encounter. The history becomes more complex rather than uniformly better or uniformly worse (V01 S0011–0016, S0130–0138; V02 S0034–0049; V04 S0135–0141; CON-0001, CON-0004, CON-0008, CON-0023).

V05's homework pause is a small but important example. Yuma says no in relation to doing homework; Hotaru pauses. The available boundary is contextual and recognizable, not a grand declaration that all future negotiation has been repaired. Yuma's initiative and Hotaru's receptivity elsewhere establish desire and participation while their practice account remains evasive. The relation can become more mutually desired before it becomes more honestly described. Consent, acknowledged attachment and a secure agreement are adjacent achievements rather than interchangeable names for one achievement (V05 S0060–0068, S0097–0101; CON-0026, CON-0028; JPS-0022).

The final volume prevents a one-directional innocence narrative. Yuma drags Hotaru, releases after a pain protest, later grips her wrists until pain and a push interrupt the approach, and pulls her away from work despite resistance. These acts occur as Yuma speaks more plainly and believes she is helping. A recipient can be loved, worried about and actually found through costly effort while still being overridden. Hotaru's eventual contact and return are real later choices; they do not turn the preceding resistance into a secret invitation. Conversely, those violations do not prove that her later trust fear, held hand or shared rest is merely counterfeit attachment (V06 S0050–0053, S0073–0084, S0133–0140, S0153–0162; CON-0034–0036, CON-0038–0039; AGY-0028–0031).

Repair is therefore distributed and incomplete. Yuma owns irresponsible dating and reports refusing Takeda properly; the exact refusal conversation is not drawn. Takeda receives a named recipient and gives a limited acknowledgment. Hotaru articulates hurt and fear more directly. These changes permit better answers in particular relations. Neither girl provides a comprehensive review of imposed contact, privacy invasion or the conditions of future conflict. Their developing affection has ethical substance because some conduct and acknowledgment change, but the ending offers no demonstrated system for stopping the next violation. That distinction supports the central argument more closely than either an absolution through love or a total dismissal through harm (V06 S0058–0071, S0078–0083, S0148–0160; INF-0032–0033; REL-0032–0034; CLM-0034–0035).

## The reader knows more than any single participant, and less than a complete motive history

Knowledge is not simply a secret withheld until a climactic revelation. Its uneven distribution creates competing rights to act. Yuma does not see the early kick, cannot identify Hotaru's unnamed beloved and does not hear the private anti-burden phone statement. Takeda lacks the girls' clandestine history while constructing a mistaken rescue account. Fujiwara sees compromising conduct without receiving its full permission history or every inner reason. Hotaru knows her own longer hurt while declining to tell Yuma enough to stabilize a mutual definition. The reader's wider access explains these clashes, but does not establish a fully dated interior history for any participant (V01 S0082–0097; V02 S0099–0104; V05 S0050–0056; V06 S0099, S0107–0111; INF-0003, INF-0023, INF-0033–0034).

The retained photograph makes that limitation public. Capture, possession, contemplated exposure, posting, circulation and deletion are distinct stages. A visible named feed supports publication where the earlier peer photograph alone did not establish its sender, audience or Takeda's receipt. The image can disclose clandestine closeness while suppressing the situation's permission, pressure and changing meaning. Public evidence is consequently powerful without being self-interpreting. Fujiwara's beneficiary defense attempts to turn an incomplete image and a true concealment problem into sufficient authority to expose; Takeda's later rejection defeats that justification more directly than a claim that nothing in the image was true (V04 S0145–0146; V05 S0154–0156; V06 S0003, S0011, S0031–0034, S0131–0132; INF-0021, INF-0028–0029, INF-0031, INF-0035; CLM-0028, CLM-0032, CLM-0036).

Audience changes also alter the girls' category language. Hotaru's V05 no-count response is a private dismissal after Yuma names her. In V06 a related category exemption becomes a public protective claim. The latter has a different immediate function because a degrading audience threatens Yuma as well as Hotaru. Yuma's own later preference for accepting the label if it means being together contests protection through erasure. This does not require imposing a settled, exhaustive identity on either girl. It shows a movement from using gender categories to reduce the significance of an act toward insisting that this particular attachment may be acknowledged despite the category's social cost (V05 S0142–0144; V06 S0017–0021, S0053–0058; REL-0031; INF-0030; JPS-0021, JPS-0025–0026; CLM-0027, CLM-0034).

Written voice carries that movement through use rather than an inventory of particles. Practice and friend language let Hotaru keep closeness familiar and deniable; Yuma later uses an unnamed-beloved purpose while privately recognizing Hotaru as its recipient. Takeda's questions increasingly seek an answer he can respect, whereas Fujiwara's totalizing labels and explanations attempt to settle other people's meanings. Hotaru's final fear of trust is harder to substitute with teasing than her earlier covers, and Yuma's voiced presence and inward repeated-help vow offer a response to dependence. Still, Yuma's final thought that Hotaru has not fully trusted her belongs to Yuma's interpretation, not to a confession she has received. Manga balloons, thought, quoted memory and reader-only asides supply different knowledge. The analysis must preserve those forms while reading the changing relation they make possible (V01 S0011–0016; V04 S0135–0144; V06 S0061–0063, S0142, S0148–0160; JPS-0001, JPS-0017, JPS-0027–0030).

## Recurrence turns apparent romantic progress into a test of how progress is read

The serial form repeats contact, interruption, concealment, injury, pursuit and renewed closeness. Those recurrences generate suspense, but they do not return every participant to an unchanged beginning. Early practice acts occur under Yuma's insufficient recognition and objections. A later requested embrace occurs with affirmative assent and privately known recipient concealment. The V05 admission meets dismissal; V06 direct hurt and trust fear finally provide different answers. The repetitions teach the reader to ask what information and permission have changed, rather than to count successive scenes as inevitable couple progress (V01 S0011–0016; V03 S0019–0035; V04 S0135–0144; V05 S0142–0153; V06 S0078–0084, S0148–0154; VIS-0002, VIS-0025; CLM-0021–0022, CLM-0027, CLM-0033–0034).

Withheld expressions and appealing couple images help keep that question unstable. Hotaru's sudden affect shifts give the reader reason to doubt her surface before supplying a settled motive. The dream makes Yuma's desire legible without staging an actual encounter. Familiar domestic and celebratory tableaux offer attractive futures that can briefly hide unresolved accounts. Erotic closeness likewise supplies affective intensity without independently proving consent or the absence of harm. The form invites attachment and then repeatedly returns an objection, a concealed person or a competing interpretation to the frame. Reading only the attractive image would miss the conduct that makes its appeal precarious (V01 S0028, S0069–0072, S0088–0097, S0114–0117; V05 S0081–0088; VIS-0002–0004, VIS-0025–0026).

The hand is an especially useful recurrence because its practical meaning changes. Childhood assistance gives an interpretive background to adult help; a clasp can promise presence, but a grip or pull can prevent withdrawal. The final wind-and-hand encounter makes recovery visually appealing while the ensuing work objection makes its cost audible. Held hands after trust fear and shared rest on the train are new receptive images, not evidence that the earlier resistant pull was acceptable. The manga makes the same general gesture capable of help, possession and chosen company. That ambiguity is part of its causal and ethical argument, rather than a decorative motif to be tallied separately (V06 S0073–0079, S0097–0099, S0133–0140, S0153–0162; CON-0035–0036, CON-0038–0039; VIS-0032–0035).

## The ending combines a choice with an uncompleted settlement

Publication does not mechanically reveal the true couple and end the conflict. It produces rumor, violence and a public protection strategy. Yuma contests that strategy, gives Takeda Hotaru's name and later claims responsibility for dating him while confused. Hotaru's hurt then makes the cost of Yuma's delayed regard more explicit. Receptive contact and a reassuring morning fail to prevent her disappearance. The ending is constructed through these mismatched responses, not through a single truth that every participant simply learns at once (V06 S0003–0021, S0053–0071, S0078–0088; REL-0031–0034; INF-0029–0033; CLM-0033–0034).

The absence acquires both household and material reasons. Hotaru's father has discussed a new marriage; the childhood scene shows missing parental company, while her private phone statement identifies not wanting to burden others. She also obtains a guarantee, moves, works and requests address privacy. Yuma receives the father's account, including mistaken competence judgments, and an address restriction. She then uses a textbook pretext to enter a room, gathers clues and travels. The marked-door search fails to find Hotaru and depends on a chance encounter to succeed. Effort demonstrates the intensity of pursuit; deception, unsuccessful investigation and chance prevent it from becoming a proof that the pursuer reliably knows the other's good (V06 S0094–0105, S0116–0129; INF-0033, INF-0035; AGY-0027–0029; CLM-0035).

The chance meeting is itself not the settlement. Hotaru objects that she is working and will lose earnings. Yuma persists, argues for staying through graduation and coming of age, and reassures Hotaru that she is not alone as Hotaru names fear of trust. The source finally supplies receptive hand contact and a shared return. It supplies no completed degree, secured income, successful university admission, established shared household, complete trust or detailed future agreement. Yuma's conditional university/cohabitation proposal and Yuma's plan to work after exams give the future concrete conditions. The closing smile and shared train rest show continuation under those conditions, rather than their achievement (V06 S0137–0162; AGY-0031; CON-0038–0039; REL-0036; JPS-0028–0030; VIS-0034–0035; CLM-0035).

The embedded school aftermath adds ordinary reintegration and play. A teacher asks Hotaru to be careful following her apology; study and food return. Takeda inwardly distinguishes a quickened heartbeat from romance, while Fujiwara supplies no demonstrated remorse transition. Hotaru's requested hold and Yuma's inward mother joke show resumed familiar play, not a clinical explanation of their bond or a warranty that all boundaries are repaired. The mainline therefore resolves named priority, some accountability and chosen continuation most strongly. It resolves formal status, practical sustainability, comprehensive repair and complete trust less strongly or not at all. External side stories cannot be recruited to fill those gaps in this six-volume argument (V06 S0166–0167; CAST-0031; REL-0036–0037; VIS-0035; CLM-0035–0036).

## Competing interpretations identify what the main argument must leave open

The strongest affirming reading treats the whole arc as latent mutual romance finally freed from inadequate public categories. Withheld early affect, Hotaru's prolonged hurt, Yuma's private recipient recognition, the shared return and imagined household all support it. It explains why the girls continue to matter more to one another than available alternatives do. Its weakness arises when an explanation of attachment becomes an explanation of permission or complete motive. The source includes objections, imposed contact, withdrawal, truth-mixed tactics and plainly resisted work interruption that the final choice cannot erase. Latent attachment is increasingly well supported; exact onset, a unified motive for every earlier act and retroactive authorization remain unavailable (V01 S0055, S0069–0072; V03 S0033–0035; V06 S0078–0083, S0137–0140, S0153–0162; CLM-0033–0035).

The strongest critical reading treats the ending as a consolidation of dependency through pursuit. Yuma refuses separation after recognizing that she lacks authority over Hotaru's work; she later violates address privacy and interrupts paid labor; her inward repeated-help vow can place her in the uniquely needed role she has long valued. Hotaru's non-burden strategy may make accepting that pursuit preferable to intolerable solitude without proving free, sustainable dependence. This reading has substantial force. It cannot, however, discard Hotaru's own direct hurt, stated trust fear and receptive endpoint contact, or Yuma's conditional future proposal. Nor can it infer that every act of care is only a deliberate control strategy. The completed corpus supports a chosen relation whose dependency and autonomy problems remain serious; it does not settle the balance of ordinary care and controlling persistence after the train returns (V03 S0132–0133; V06 S0099–0103, S0116–0118, S0137–0140, S0148–0162; AGY-0027–0031; CON-0037–0039; CLM-0035).

A further counterreading sees melodramatic contrivance as doing more causal work than the characters' development: an image appears publicly, an absence follows a promising night, a clue search fails and chance nevertheless reunites the girls. The source supports that formal concern. The resolution is neither the inevitable output of Yuma's skill nor a validated model forecast. Yet the aftermath of each contrivance differentiates decisions. Takeda rejects the claimed benefit of publication; Yuma chooses acknowledgment over erasure; Hotaru can voice trust fear after the recovery; the university future remains conditional. Contrivance supplies encounters and stakes without making every response semantically empty. How persuasive that form feels is an evaluative question the source cannot decide, while the changed responses are observable (V06 S0031–0034, S0053–0063, S0120–0132, S0148–0162; INF-0031–0032, INF-0035; VIS-0033–0035; CLM-0032, CLM-0034–0036).

The boys also defeat an easy counterreading in which conventional male partners exist only to validate the girls' escape. Takeda's stopping, food, help and limited recognition make loss of that possible relation meaningful, while his intrusive following, kiss and punch prevent idealization. Fujiwara's accurate partial criticism and practical competence prevent a universal falsehood model, while his kick, leverage and exposure defeat moral endorsement. The two are ethically differentiated participants in the knowledge system. Their difference makes the girls' choice a question about particular attachment and conduct, rather than a general verdict on the genders available to them (REL-0004, REL-0007, REL-0015, REL-0017, REL-0032, REL-0035–0036; CON-0003, CON-0029–0030, CON-0033; INF-0008, INF-0034–0035).

## Retrospective explanation remains distinct from demonstrated prediction

The completed corpus permits stronger time-indexed character explanations than the entering reader possessed. It does not permit the final models to inherit forecast credit from that hindsight. Yuma's earlier framing and request-cover rules failed in their stipulated later windows even when pursuit itself continued. Hotaru's V03 no-boyfriend-substitution prediction failed on the represented reapproach. Fujiwara's anticipated exposure or demand components were not demonstrated merely because later publication occurred. Takeda's ethical recognition at the end remains valuable observed conduct without validating a rule whose exact refusal window was not shown. Literal failures, component failures, bounded support and no-demonstrated-opportunity remain distinct in the claim ledger and local validation audit (CLM-0008, CLM-0012, CLM-0014, CLM-0017–0020, CLM-0029–0032).

The mature decision rules consequently have an explanatory rather than proven prospective status. They condition behavior on relationship, information and time-specific state, preserve counterexamples, and specify falsifiers for future testing. Native dialogue and mundane or relational behavior have different evidence strengths; materially novel transfer is not assessed. No post-corpus scenario has been run merely by proposing one. This restraint is integral to the literary argument: a story in which people misread reasons should not produce an analysis that claims complete access to motives or a universal recipe for predicting them.

The full series can thus be read as a movement toward a relation that its participants can finally acknowledge, while the question of how they will responsibly live it stays partly unanswered. Named desire makes new choices possible. Receiving an answer, accepting its limits and negotiating the cost of care are more demanding accomplishments than naming whom one loves. The final shared rest offers a present in which both girls remain. The unfinished work of trust, restraint and an ordinary future gives that rest its tenderness and its instability.
