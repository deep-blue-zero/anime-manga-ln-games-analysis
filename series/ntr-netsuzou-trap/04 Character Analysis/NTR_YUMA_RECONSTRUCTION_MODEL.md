# Yuma: care, self-image and access under changing knowledge

**Current scope note:** the original body below retains its V01–V06 source, authorship and untested-at-release statements. The final LW appendix is the current supplemental update after held-out scoring; old states, rule text and results are preserved.

This model explains bounded decisions rather than declaring a personality essence. Its working proposition is that Yuma repeatedly tries to preserve a special place with Hotaru while maintaining a morally intelligible account of herself. The account changes from friendship/protection to practice, then to named love and responsibility. The wish for access persists more consistently than the language used to justify it. Nevertheless, she can withdraw, value others, recognize limits and accept some uncertainty. Those contrary capacities prevent a rule that predicts pursuit regardless of circumstance.

A disciplined reconstruction first establishes time and available facts, then names the competing goals and actual options. It predicts a bounded **first eligible response**, with an inhibitor and a falsifier, rather than inferring an unseen motive from whichever action occurs. Later scenes do not move a failed speech window until a fitting line appears. Native interaction is often emotionally costly even when physically unforced; safety conditions must be stated, not assumed from conversational fluency.

## Relatively stable tendencies and changing state

Yuma's repeatedly supported tendencies are practical engagement, concern for close people, pleasure in a uniquely needed position, difficulty tolerating unknown Hotaru access, and self-critical comparison between conduct and a good-person image. These are tendencies, not identical intensity across volumes. Her sport/leisure competence, food efforts, school preparation and ability to value Takeda are evidence of ordinary activity outside Hotaru pursuit (YEL-0003,0008,0015,0017,0021).

What changes is decisive: whom she recognizes as beloved; whether she can name that recipient; whether she believes Hotaru needs protecting, may leave or has rejected her; whether Takeda is boyfriend, distant partner or friend; what secrecy costs; and which public categories are tolerable. Early anger at a suggestive room, late anger at categorical dismissal and final concern over relocation are different decisions under different knowledge. A stable access wish does not make their speech mechanisms the same. This is why Y-R04's protection framing and Y-R05's practice request both require preserved misses.

Her attachment need is not simply to give help. She wants to be seen as specially necessary, then to be wanted romantically, then to keep a life shared over time. Her fear is both loss and the discovery that her protective account contains self-interest. Self-condemnation can produce apology or more concealment; it is not a reliable behavioral brake. Her V03 no-right insight and V06 privacy circumvention are a particularly strong evidence-backed contradiction ([CLM-0016], [CLM-0034]).

## Directional conditioning

**Yuma → Hotaru.** Childhood familiarity makes direct concern easy and truthful recipient naming costly. Unknown H faces, competing partners/work and departure cues threaten a privileged place. She may confuse knowledge of Hotaru's practical limitations with authority over her future. Once love is explicit, protective justification changes but pursuit and force remain possible. Hotaru's objections are information Yuma actually receives, not simply symptoms to reinterpret away. Tender acceptance of incomplete trust supplies a competing capacity to bear uncertainty; it does not prove she can sustain negotiated separation.

**Yuma → Takeda.** Real pleasure, gratitude, guilt and affection coexist with displaced romantic attention. In V01–V02 she tries progress and repair under conflict; after breakup she accepts ordinary help, yet his mistaken recipient belief persists until V06. The mature decision to refuse renewal is supported through her report, not a directly drawn exact refusal. A model should predict different possibilities before and after recipient recognition. Calling him camouflage as a complete intention conflicts with her denial and genuine care; describing all concealment as innocent confusion conflicts with later inward knowledge (YEL-0003,0006–0008,0017,0023,0025).

**Yuma → Fujiwara.** She confronts perceived harm, directly resists an explicit access-for-silence demand and also depends on his useful information. His greater knowledge can wound or reorient her even when ethically discredited. Her slaps/grabs are not universal justified responses; his hostile report is not automatically false. No general rule about her behavior toward threatening unknown men is established by these native encounters ([CON-0010], [INF-0014], [INF-0034]).

**Yuma → family, peers and school.** Her mother's practical support and conditional permission, classmates' care and criticism, teacher reminders and university preparation constrain action. She can thank friends, deny trouble and use incomplete explanations. These are relationships with their own costs: paid prep absences, future choices and exposure concerns. H-directed urgency can outrank them, but independent competence and quiet T time show they are not absent. Unknown-person search requests give a narrow social sample, not a broad conversational repertoire.

## Mature conditional hypotheses — all POSTCORPUS_UNTESTED

Each `Y-PC-##` is **new retrospective model development** using the completed corpus. Source fitting is development support only. These rules have no held-out validation after their construction; they neither replace `Y-R##` nor improve those scores.

### Y-PC-01 — Threatened special access draws concrete pursuit before full clarification

**Condition:** at Y-V03 or later, she still wants H closeness, perceives credible access loss, lacks an intelligible negotiated alternative and has a real opportunity to approach. **Expected first response:** seek concrete contact/information before resolving all relationship meaning. **Inhibitors:** actual unavailability, clear practical barrier, chosen respite, or a credible agreed return/contact arrangement may redirect action; the last inhibitor is a hypothesis, not established competence. **Development evidence:** work following, brochure/practice, survey and search (YEL-0012,0018–0019,0028). **Counterexamples:** real summer withdrawal and her insight that she has no authority. **Falsifier:** sustained voluntary distance through a comparable available loss window, or candid negotiation before access-seeking. No fixed speech cover is predicted, because Y-R04/Y-R05 missed those components.

### Y-PC-02 — Self-image conflict delays public truth relative to inward recognition

**Condition:** Y recognizes an attachment/act incompatible with her current good-friend/girlfriend account, while candid disclosure risks rejection or social cost. **Expected response:** an incomplete explanation, denial or privately self-critical account may precede full disclosure. **Inhibitors:** explicitly choosing responsibility can change order; after V06 named disclosure, mere continued familiarity is not a fresh secrecy trigger. **Evidence:** phone lie, jealousy admission, practice cover and public no-beloved claim (YEL-0007,0010,0018,0021). **Counterexample:** V06 direct H naming to T and H. **Falsifier:** immediate complete disclosure in a genuinely comparable pre-disclosure conflict. This is not a claim that all courtesy or uncertainty is lying.

### Y-PC-03 — After recipient recognition, guilt alone is insufficient for romantic restoration

**Condition:** at Y-V04–V06 she knows H remains her beloved, T offers renewed couple access and no new T attraction or coercion changes the facts. **Expected response:** hesitate, clarify unavailable affection or decline rather than treat gratitude/remorse as restored love. **Inhibitors:** genuinely changed attachment or an interrupted/forced answer removes the antecedent. **Evidence:** wished-for rather than actual T attraction and the report-mediated Y-R06 outcome (YEL-0017,0023,0025). **Counterexample/limit:** earlier compensatory room progress occurred before clear recipient recognition; it cannot be ignored but belongs to another state. **Falsifier:** voluntary restored dating without addressing the known mismatch. It does not forecast every future partner choice or exactly replicate unshown refusal wording.

### Y-PC-04 — Anger at relational erasure can make intervention bodily

**Condition:** H-directed desire is active, Y experiences denial/humiliation or abrupt access frustration, the person is physically available and anger rises. **Expected response range:** forceful pursuit, grabbing or a retaliatory act becomes plausible alongside verbal confrontation. **Inhibitors:** physical distance, interruption, deliberation or respected exit can redirect; no reliable universal inhibitor is proved. **Evidence:** V05 classroom initiative/slap and V06 grips/pulls (YEL-0020,0023–0024,0026,0028). **Counterexamples:** chosen absence, ordinary requests and quiet T disclosure. **Falsifier of a stronger force tendency:** comparable accessible conflict handled consistently through nonphysical boundaries. The model describes risk, not justified action, inevitability or general violence toward strangers.

### Y-PC-05 — Practical knowledge can be converted into an overbroad care mandate

**Condition:** Y knows H's concrete weakness or hardship, believes waiting risks abandonment/solitude and sees herself as able to help. **Expected response:** provision, advice or intervention may proceed as if better knowledge authorizes deciding for H. **Inhibitors:** H's explicit objection supplies a reason to stop, but past evidence shows it does not reliably do so; emergency facts must be proved separately. **Evidence:** work-control/no-right contradiction, search pretext, work cost and endpoint care (YEL-0013,0027–0030). **Counterexample:** she can admit nonauthority and accept inferred incomplete trust. **Falsifier:** seeking and respecting H's decision before comparable nonemergency intervention. Knowing poor cooking/grades is not knowing every motive or possessing a guardian right.

### Y-PC-06 — Ordinary care is concrete, imperfect and vulnerable to competing priority

**Condition:** native school/domestic task with a valued person, feasible resources and no overriding attachment crisis. **Expected response:** offer practical help or shared activity—food, notes, study, planning—rather than only romantic rhetoric. **Inhibitors:** limited skill, school workload, money or H crisis can displace commitments. **Evidence:** mother-assisted lunch, study, notes/food gag and future savings (YEL-0008,0015,0017,0021,0030). **Counterexamples:** missed T date and prep absences; cooking is imperfect. **Falsifier:** repeated available-care refusal under comparable ordinary conditions, or evidence that material competence was consistently overestimated. No professional, nutrition or financial expertise is inferred.

### Y-PC-07 — Named commitment can coexist with tolerance of incomplete understanding

**Condition:** at Y-V06 after direct H hurt/trust speech and chosen proximity, immediate access is restored but H remains anxious. **Expected response:** accept some inferred uncertainty and orient toward repeated future care rather than demand an instant cure. **Evidence:** S0159–0160 interiority and conditional plans (YEL-0029). **Inhibitors/limits:** renewed disappearance, concrete refusal or practical crisis may reactivate pursuit; those changed scenarios are untested. **Counterreading:** repeated-help commitment may preserve unequal rescue authority rather than negotiated tolerance. **Falsifier:** demanding immediate full trust or punishing openly acknowledged uncertainty under comparable restored-access conditions. This is a weak endpoint-derived hypothesis, not proof of sustained behavioral restraint or a spoken repair agreement.

## Written-register constraints

Yuma's common self-reference in narration is **あたし**. Familiar address, short protests, comic mock-politeness and self-indictment differ from Hotaru's softer familiar presentation. Early **やめて** and later task/location objections remain actual boundaries even when awkward or followed by attraction. Self-critical interior narration becomes more specific before outward speech does: priority selfishness, no-right recognition, moral condemnation, then an explicitly named H recipient ([JPS-0001], [JPS-0002], [JPS-0006], [JPS-0009], [JPS-0014], [JPS-0026]).

Do not produce a constantly eloquent ethical spokesperson. She can know a problem without explaining it fully to the person harmed. V05 hedged recipient naming and V06 direct choice have different authority. Do not insert exclusive women-only identity labels into her mouth because F prescribed one or H/peers used a queer category. Her V06 preference for that category over erasure is situated recognition, not a complete taxonomy.

Public speech, thought boxes and retrospectively quoted montage must remain distinguishable. S0159 acceptance and S0160 repeated-help vow are Y interiority; they must not be scripted as something H heard and agreed to. The quiet or vivid lettering cannot establish timbre, breath, acoustic pacing or an anime voice. Written patterns constrain plausible phrasing under a declared relationship/state; they do not authorize unrestricted dialogue with unfamiliar people.

## Bounded scenario contracts and portability

The following are analysis contracts, **not new fictional facts, scenes or validated outcomes**. Every use must declare state, available information, interlocutor/history, current commitments, setting, leverage and safety before applying a rule.

1. **Native school privacy contract:** Y-V05 before H recipient disclosure; H is a familiar classmate/friend, future departure is uncertain, a teacher requests only a reminder, classmates are nearby. Y-PC-01/02 makes information-seeking and incomplete purpose disclosure plausible. The model cannot turn teacher authority into inspection permission or treat partial privacy as an automatically safe romance window. A plain reminder with no unauthorized reach would weaken the intervention expectation; an observed absence of a closeness request still misses historical Y-R05.
2. **Native post-return work-distance contract:** Y-V06, acknowledged H attachment, a stated nonemergency work/privacy wish and an explicitly negotiated later contact time. The new agreed time is an assumed scenario condition, absent from the manga's failed search. It may inhibit Y-PC-01/05, but whether Y would honor it is untested. Permitted output is competing response expectations and what would distinguish them, not certainty that love makes her respectful or that she must intrude.
3. **New person's ordinary study request:** Y-V06 with existing H commitments, an unfamiliar peer asking for notes, no H-access threat or romantic stake. Native practical-care evidence supports a modest willingness hypothesis if resources allow. Attachment-specific possessiveness and rescue rules do not transfer merely because another person needs help. Novel-person register, rapport and repeated reliability remain NOT_ASSESSED.
4. **Unfamiliar professional emergency:** changing occupation, legal responsibilities, institutional power or danger removes native assumptions. No rule here supplies expert judgment, clinical interpretation or a dependable rescue procedure. The responsible result is an evidence gap, not an invented competent scene.

An operational use must preserve adverse predictions and observed failures alongside appealing ones. Yuma's endpoint choice is more legible than her future boundary practice. The model can explain the cost-bearing change toward named love while still identifying the unresolved possibility that repeated care renews the same authority problem in a kinder register.
