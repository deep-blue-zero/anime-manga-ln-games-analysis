# Yuma: the cost of making love count

Yuma's development turns on a distinction between knowing that she wants Hotaru and allowing that want to change the account she gives of her life. Her earliest privilege is the pleasure of being needed. Her later achievement is a named, cost-bearing choice: she recognizes that gratitude to Takeda cannot make a renewed romance honest, tells him that Hotaru is the person she likes, and chooses to face Hotaru without an unnamed-other practice story. Yet that achievement leaves a second problem unresolved. A desire she can finally acknowledge still tempts her to decide what Hotaru needs, where Hotaru should be and which boundaries care may cross. The manga makes Yuma's attachment increasingly legible without proving that her authority over it becomes proportionately ethical.

This argument depends on keeping her changing agency in view. She is subject to unwanted initiatives, later seeks and initiates contact, lies, recognizes harms, resists coercive leverage, strikes in anger, provides ordinary care and conducts an intrusive search. Those actions are neither one uninterrupted victim position nor an exposure of a permanently false innocence. They become meaningful through the changes in what she knows, what she can admit and what she thinks closeness should entitle her to do. The central development is from a protective friendship explanation that cannot contain desire toward a chosen attachment that must learn to coexist with another person's uncertainty. The endpoint supports that coexistence as a present possibility; it does not certify a completed practice of mutual boundaries.

## A protective identity whose reward is special access

Yuma remembers childhood protection as something that gave her a cherished position. Hotaru's reliance is not merely a burden dutifully accepted; being uniquely needed makes Yuma happy. That reward matters because it makes the later protector account both credible and incomplete. She really can care about Hotaru's welfare, and she can also want the dependence that distinguishes her from everyone else. The first volume does not require her to know the romantic meaning of that investment. It establishes a prior attachment structure whose benefits become difficult to defend when Hotaru has other relationships and unfamiliar faces. ([REL-0001], [AGY-0001]; V01 S0009–0010,0057–0059.)

The second volume gives Yuma a more precise self-indictment. Remembering the growth of her own middle-school friendships and Hotaru's boy dating, she recognizes that she disliked other people receiving Hotaru's attention and seeing sides of her she did not know. This is stronger than a retrospective analyst calling childhood devotion possessive. Yuma herself sees self-interest inside her valued role. But the admission is inward before it becomes an effective limit on conduct. The story thereby distinguishes moral insight from moral discipline: recognizing that a desire is selfish does not immediately make it disappear or determine which action follows. ([REL-0008], [JPS-0006], [VIS-0009]; V02 S0123–0135.)

Her desire for knowledge is part of this privilege. Unknown Hotaru is alarming because it reveals a life not organized around Yuma's usefulness. Work, late homecomings, savings and housing plans make the problem increasingly ordinary and material. Yuma is not only jealous of a boyfriend; she is unsettled by another person's ability to have a reason, skill or destination that she has not been told. Fujiwara's suffocation report is especially painful because it proposes that her care could be the thing Hotaru wants to leave. The report is attributed and unconfirmed as a complete Hotaru motive. Its causal importance lies in what it makes Yuma believe and attempt, not in granting the reader an objective verdict on her. ([INF-0013], [INF-0014], [REL-0012]; V03 S0055–0060,0092,0109–0116.)

The sharper contradiction arrives when Yuma explicitly recognizes that she is neither guardian nor boyfriend and has no right to decide Hotaru's work. That recognition should matter. It prevents an interpretation in which she has never considered Hotaru's independent authority. Yet she later vows not to allow separation even if Hotaru hates her. The manga places an understood limit beside a competing resolve. Her eventual pursuit cannot therefore be explained only by ignorance of boundaries; it also shows the force of a wish that can outrank what she knows about her entitlement. The final rescue must be read against this early admitted contradiction. ([CON-0016], [JPS-0009], [CLM-0016]; V03 S0099–0105,0130–0133.)

## Takeda matters because he offers an actual alternative

The force of Yuma's Hotaru choice would be thinner if Takeda were only an empty public cover. Early nervousness, date laughter, rides, kissing and good times give the male relationship real content. Yuma's athletic and leisure confidence also appears in these ordinary settings; she is not constituted solely by intimate confusion. She can enjoy a day, act capably and anticipate a conventional couple future. The manga does not establish that all of this was false because her later retrospective account calls loving Hotaru different from what preceded it. Earlier pleasure and later revised self-understanding can both be true. ([REL-0003], [CAST-0001]; V01 S0019–0024,0060–0066,0082–0084; [JPS-0026], V06 S0059–0060.)

What fails is the assumption that deciding to advance a relationship will make her ready for its expected intimacy. Leaving Takeda's room is one available action; later choosing progress after the Hotaru/Fujiwara discovery is another. In V02 her insistence meets her own discomfort and Takeda's decision to stop. That sequence is important for Yuma's agency. She is capable of selecting a path that does not match her wants, and another person can notice the mismatch without obtaining the entire secret explanation. Socially intelligible progress does not automatically settle bodily or affective readiness. ([CON-0003], [CON-0007], [REL-0006]; V01 S0099–0103,0147–0149; V02 S0008–0013.)

Yuma also attempts ordinary care: a lunch with maternal help, plans to make pancakes, appreciation of Takeda's sport and later gratitude for notes, study support and kindness. These acts complicate both an innocent confusion account and Fujiwara's total camouflage accusation. Genuine care does not erase her lies or the missed date; concealment does not make every kindness instrumental. After breakup becomes stated in V03, shared study and peaceful meals remain meaningful without proving couple restoration. In V04 she wishes she could like the honest, kind person offering that peace, while her Hotaru-directed longing persists. The alternative is attractive in part because it could make her life coherent; wishing for that attraction is not the same as having it. ([REL-0006], [REL-0014], [REL-0020]; V02 S0050–0053,0136–0137; V03 S0056–0057,0093–0097; V04 S0119–0129.)

Her V06 response to renewal is consequently an ethical decision as well as a romantic one. She names Hotaru to Takeda, describes having been unable to answer and later tells Hotaru that she properly declined. She owns the unfairness of dating while confused and refuses to use remorse as a substitute for available affection. The exact refusal wording to Takeda is not drawn; the model's Y-R06 support is report-mediated. The literary change does not depend on pretending that missing dialogue is visible. Her represented account, actual recipient disclosure and absence of renewed couple status support a move away from compensatory dating. Takeda's recognition gives her an alternative to the proposition that a girl's love must be made harmless or invisible, while retaining the fact that she hurt him. ([REL-0032], [INF-0032], [AGY-0025], [CLM-0029]; V06 S0057–0063,0066–0071.)

Takeda's care is also not an unlimited moral contrast. His sudden kiss, covert following and later physical confrontation belong to his own trajectory. Those acts prevent Yuma's choice from being a simple rejection of a flawless safe alternative in favor of a wholly harmful one. The relevant point is narrower: she values him, can receive nontransactional care from him and finally recognizes that those benefits do not obligate romantic restoration. That recognition gives her choice a cost the childhood-priority account did not have to state so plainly. ([CON-0003], [CON-0029], [CON-0033]; V01 S0021–0022; V05 S0050; V06 S0009–0012.)

## Inward truth outruns the words she offers others

Yuma's concealment initially sits beside uncertainty. Girls and friends seem to explain why Hotaru contact differs from boyfriend contact, even as the dream substitution and favorable comparisons challenge that shelter. The dream is Yuma's own representation, not an actual Hotaru invitation. Her reluctance to call the intimacy consequential does not mean she already has an exhaustive concealed identity. It means the available friend category is being asked to do more work than it can bear. ([JPS-0002], [VIS-0004]; V01 S0103,0114–0117.)

The phone lie in V02 turns that categorical problem into conduct. Hotaru names outside-partner intimacy and asks whether it can be told to Takeda. Yuma claims nobody is present when Hotaru is there, then privately questions why saying her friend is present should be impossible. A real lie is now visible, without making the contact during the call consensual: Yuma is also protesting and pushing away. The manga places deception responsibility and unwanted initiative in the same sequence. Neither supplies a reason to delete the other. ([INF-0005], [CON-0008], [JPS-0004]; V02 S0041–0049.)

By V04 her inward account is more definite. She identifies Hotaru as the person she likes; after the housing cue, she corrects Takeda's status but refuses to name her current beloved and asks for practice for an unnamed other. The explanation is now knowingly inadequate. The change from protective friendship to practice is therefore not just a new euphemism chosen by an unchanged confused girl. It is an attempt to retain contact after recognition has already outrun what she will disclose. Hotaru's local yes makes this encounter a chosen request/response rather than an automatically unwanted act, but it does not make the recipient account truthful. ([CLM-0021], [CON-0023], [JPS-0017]; V04 S0083,0129,0134–0144.)

The information about Takeda's error has its own limit. Yuma hears Fujiwara report that Takeda worries about a bad man and inwardly recognizes the gender mismatch. Takeda's exact private male-beloved wording at S0118 is reader-visible, not something she directly hears. An attributed report gives her consequential information without making the parties share one omniscient account. Fujiwara's women-only prescription likewise exceeds what she adopts; her denial of intentional camouflage does not refute all the actual secrecy. The manga's sexuality problem is carried through a named recipient and contested relationship categories rather than a final universal identity certificate. ([INF-0016], [INF-0020], [JPS-0014]; V04 S0118,0123–0129.)

V05 extends the division between private knowledge and public language into ordinary future planning. Matching jewelry, shared-room geometry, chores, cooking and newlywed play provide real substance. Yuma's mother's condition that she first pass admission supplies a material limit. Yet a public friend-roomshare story and denial of another beloved keep the future legible without acknowledging its romantic meaning. The tutor couple's public disclosure makes this absence noticeable to Yuma herself. She begins to ask whether what she wants can be told to others, rather than only whether she can privately remain close. ([REL-0030], [INF-0025], [JPS-0020]; V05 S0039–0047,0072–0073,0081–0088,0102–0108.)

Her hedged naming at S0142 finally supplies Hotaru as recipient. Hotaru's no-count response injures her because a category they both helped sustain now deprives her attachment of a claim. Yuma has asked for practice, but that history does not require her to accept the explanation forever. At the same time, feeling two-timed does not prove that an exclusive girls couple had already been agreed. The confrontation makes relational pain legible without upgrading expectation into a contract. Her slap remains violence rather than justified proof of sincerity. ([REL-0024], [CON-0030], [INF-0027], [CLM-0027]; V05 S0138–0148.)

## Permission is not a single verdict on Yuma's desire

The manga's repeated intimate staging can invite a retrospective reading in which Yuma always wanted everything she protested. That reading loses the distinctions the sequence makes possible. Early stop words, self-sufficiency objections, pushes and slaps are evidence at the time of the act. Later favorable comparison, dream desire and voluntary approach are different evidence about attachment and participation. Desire can coexist with a refused setting, refused timing, refused act or inadequate information. A character's eventual love is not standing authorization for another person's initiative. ([CON-0001], [CON-0002], [CLM-0005]; V01 S0011–0016,0085–0097,0131–0135.)

Conversely, preserving those objections does not make Yuma's later chosen contact analytically unreal. V02's withheld anticipated kiss allows her desire to emerge before Hotaru again acts: Yuma grasps a hand and returns to the lap while refusing its name. V03's infirmary pull belongs to her, although exact mouth contact is obscured and illness limits interpretation. V04 provides an actual request with local affirmative reception. V05 gives Yuma unmistakable classroom and home initiative. These are developments in agency, not retroactive permissions. A good account must be able to describe a person who was wronged in one moment and acts, conceals or wrongs in another. ([CON-0011], [CON-0018], [CON-0023], [CON-0025], [CON-0026]; V02 S0143–0154; V03 S0149–0154; V04 S0138–0141; V05 S0026–0031,0060–0067.)

The ordinary help scenes make the distinction especially concrete. Asked fastening assistance in V03 differs from unwanted recruitment and customer handling; accepted swimwear help in V04 differs from added contact despite protest. A helpful result does not expand the scope of permission. The same logic applies to Yuma's own painful pulls and intrusive search. Knowing Hotaru needs care does not make every method of supplying it acceptable. The ending's shared comfort supports present reciprocity; it does not remove the need to assess the route by which that comfort became available. ([CON-0014], [CON-0015], [CON-0020], [CON-0037], [CON-0038].)

Historical reconstruction failures reinforce this literary caution. Y-R04 correctly catches some approach-before-naming but misses its predicted friendship/protection explanation; Y-R05 misses the covered continuing-closeness request at the primary first window, with privacy applicability separately uncertain. Those outcomes show that Yuma's conduct cannot be captured by simply relabeling any pursuit as the same predicted mechanism. The literary continuity lies in a problem of access and self-account; the actual speech and opportunity change. A monograph can recognize that continuity while preserving the model's failures. ([CLM-0012], [CLM-0017]; V04 S0134–0140; V05 S0023–0024.)

## Ordinary life gives the attachment both a future and a cost

Yuma's ordinary behavior keeps the attachment from being only a sequence of sensational reveals. Food, study books, matching necklaces, chores, family permission and money make a wanted shared life concrete. She can offer help imperfectly: the lunch depends on maternal assistance, cooking comedy does not make her an expert, and the endpoint notes/food advice is both useful and teasing. Her mock improvement and preparation show effort, while paid-class absences show its disruption. She is neither naturally competent at everything nor helpless outside romance. ([AGY-0022], [VIS-0028], [CAST-0026]; V02 S0050–0053; V03 S0134–0142; V05 S0013–0014,0038–0047,0089,0159–0162; V06 S0167.)

These details also expose the price of her priorities. Concern over Hotaru's bruising displaces an actual Takeda date; workplace pursuit costs preparation and brings unwanted labor expectations; school lies involve her mother's time and money. The story does not need to call Yuma heartless to show that caring for one person can impose costs on another. What she learns about infidelity and unfairness is therefore connected to mundane reliability. Choosing whom to love is only part of a life in which other people have claims on honesty, attendance and consideration. ([AGY-0005], [AGY-0011], [INF-0013]; V02 S0064–0087; V03 S0063–0091,0134–0144.)

The final finances matter for the same reason. Cohabitation is conditional on admission; saving and work after exams are plans; Hotaru reports the move exhausted her funds. Money does not spoil the romance by introducing an unrelated practical concern. It is the condition under which repeated availability could become a life rather than a promise. S0166 shows Hotaru's actual school return/apology/admonition; the separate S0167 gag has Yuma urge copying notes, studying enough to graduate and eating properly, then respond to a hug request. No graduation or shared household is complete. The comedy gives her care a daily form while preserving the asymmetry that the affectionate train ending might otherwise conceal. ([REL-0036], [REL-0037], [JPS-0030], [JPS-0031]; V06 S0158–0162,0166–0167.)

The chronology of extras must remain explicit. V01 pretrip shopping, V03 middle-school backstory, V04 earlier summer play and V05 earlier/undated cooking can enrich ordinary characterization without becoming later mainline outcomes. V02's adult parallel is another continuity and cannot prove Yuma's mainline marriage or future behavior. V06's embedded post-return gag is a bounded later school state within the admitted witness. Its usefulness comes from showing unfinished tasks after return, not from furnishing a new independent forecast sample. ([VIS-0012], [VIS-0017], [VIS-0023], [VIS-0028], [REL-0037].)

## Looking, naming and taking a hand

Yuma's visual role changes with access to her own recognition. Early concealment lets the reader see private action beside a public misreading. Dream substitution makes a desire available that her friend/girl account cannot yet state. The withheld kiss in V02 briefly separates anticipation from another person's initiative; the viewer can see that Yuma waits for what she denies wanting. Later the classroom curtain affords partial privacy for both an informational question and intimate contact, then a returning classmate makes the shelter unstable. These devices do not merely ornament feelings. They specify who can know what and when a wanted act becomes visible as Yuma's choice. ([VIS-0001], [VIS-0004], [VIS-0010], [VIS-0024].)

The public photograph reverses that control. It shows a moment while stripping away permission history, inward recognition and relationship negotiation. Hotaru tries to replace its meaning with a teasing story; Yuma prefers the stigmatized category to erasure of what matters to her. That preference is stronger than an unqualified statement that public disclosure solves self-recognition. Exposure hurts and does not become ethically justified because it helps make her choice explicit. The removed visible post does not undo humiliation or prove all copies destroyed; possible screenshots remain possible. ([CON-0031], [CON-0032], [REL-0031], [INF-0031]; V05 S0155–0156; V06 S0013–0021,0031–0033,0053–0055.)

Written Japanese preserves the differences between inner and public development. Yuma's common **あたし**, abrupt objections, mock-politeness and self-indictment do not make her an always candid speaker. Her inward recognition precedes the hedged named recipient; later **蛍が好きだから** makes a chosen cause explicit. Minimal language is enough to show the change. It should not be expanded into an orientation label the source has not supplied, a polished ethical manifesto she never delivers or an acoustic voice inferred from lettering. The final acceptance and repeated-help vow belong to interiority, while future money/work talk is spoken planning. ([JPS-0001], [JPS-0006], [JPS-0014], [JPS-0022], [JPS-0026], [JPS-0030].)

The hand motif is the sharpest test of the tender account. An accepted childhood hand offers company against isolation; the city reunion replays the reach. But Hotaru breaks a pull, objects that she must work, suffers lost income and asks Yuma to leave. Later held hands and coat-grip make chosen staying visible. Related compositions therefore carry different authority: invitation, control, acceptance and dependence. If the reader treats the motif as a single continuous permission, the very recurrence that enriches the ending also conceals its costs. If the reader treats every hand as only control, Hotaru's later modest initiative disappears. ([VIS-0034], [CON-0038], [AGY-0031]; V06 S0097,0133–0140,0157–0160.)

## The strongest counterreading: love gives the protector a stronger title

The strongest competing interpretation is that Yuma's development mainly replaces a shaky friendship entitlement with romantic rescue authority. On this account, naming love does not sufficiently change the structure in which she needs Hotaru to need her. Her V03 refusal of separation, V05 unauthorized survey reach and V06 false textbook pretext make an increasingly knowledgeable pursuer. Hotaru's requested privacy reaches her through the father; Yuma nevertheless uses clues to search. The search ends in chance, then resisted pulls and work loss accompany her insistence. Her inward repeated-help vow and maternal joke can be read as preserving dependence, with tender framing rewarding rather than correcting intrusion. This counterreading has substantial source support and should not be dismissed as hostility to their attachment. ([CLM-0016], [CLM-0034], [CLM-0035]; V03 S0133; V05 S0020–0022; V06 S0102,0116–0118,0137–0144,0160,0167.)

What limits it is not a blanket assertion that she means well. It is the specificity of the changes the source actually shows. She gives up a socially easier renewal rather than obtaining care under a false affective account. She hears Hotaru's long hurt and direct fear, recognizes a failure to look rather than merely a failure to receive gratitude, and can inwardly accept that trust is not complete. Hotaru contributes chosen proximity, reciprocal smiles and shared return. These facts support a relationship more real than a wholly imposed rescue. They also show that uncertainty need not be resolved before some care is chosen. The counterreading remains strongest about the methods and sustainability, not as proof that every later attachment is unreal. ([REL-0032], [REL-0033], [REL-0036]; V06 S0066–0084,0148–0162.)

Yuma's understanding is nonetheless bounded. The full family flashback and private anti-burden call are reader evidence; she is not shown receiving them all. She combines her childhood memory, present father's report and contradictions, Fujiwara's arrangement testimony, and Hotaru's direct hurt/trust speech. This makes her guarded-child interpretation source-supported without turning it into omniscient psychology. It can be responsive care and still risk subordinating current work/privacy wishes to the childhood person she believes she understands. The epistemic limit is part of the ethical problem, not a reason to discard the interpretation altogether. ([INF-0033], [INF-0034], [REL-0033]; V06 S0093–0111,0148–0151.)

Nor can the ending supply absent repair. Real earlier apologies, Yuma's responsibility toward Takeda, post removal, present receptivity and shared return are separate steps. The source does not show exhaustive Hotaru accountability, a durable negotiated boundary protocol, complete dissemination erasure, resolution of old injury causes or permission for the V01 room event. Later love does not authorize earlier acts; earlier wrongs do not make the later choice false. These are independent propositions the completed manga requires an account to sustain together. ([CON-0013], [CON-0024], [CON-0039], [CLM-0006], [CLM-0033].)

## A return that can bear incomplete trust, with its practice still unwritten

The ending's distinctive movement is from proving that attachment counts toward asking how it can continue when the beloved does not fully trust its durability. Yuma's **それでもいいよ** at S0159 is interior acceptance of her interpretation of Hotaru's anxious eyes; it is not a spoken mutual agreement or Hotaru's own endpoint confession. The repeated-help vow on S0160 is also inward. Its generosity and danger belong to the same unresolved structure: she can accept incompleteness, but she still imagines continued rescue as the route by which trust will be obtained. The text makes that commitment moving without demonstrating that it will always respect another person's refusal. ([JPS-0030], [REL-0036], [CLM-0035].)

Shared train sleep and smiles establish present comfort and joint return. Conditional housing and later school care leave ordinary life still to be built. Yuma is more able to name the person she wants and the unfairness of seeking affection elsewhere; she is not shown possessing complete knowledge, limitless competence or a settled right to intervene. The future question is therefore concrete: can the person who has learned to acknowledge love also learn to make care answerable to the beloved's current choices? The six-volume manga ends with evidence that they can stay together now, and with enough remembered costs to keep that question open.
