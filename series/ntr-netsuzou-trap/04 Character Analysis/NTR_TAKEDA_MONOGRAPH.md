# Takeda: learning that an affection can be legitimate without being returned

Takeda's independent trajectory concerns the difference between wanting a relationship, receiving credible affection and deciding what another person's choice permits him to do. At first he tries to make familiar school closeness recognizably romantic. When progress feels forced, he withdraws rather than accept performance as proof of love. Later he helps Yuma while continuing to hope for her, gives advice about an unknown beloved and eventually recognizes the person she actually chooses. This development gives him more than the role of a decent boy losing to a girl. It also gives him less than a moral ideal: sudden initiation, covert following and a punch remain acts he performs, not inconveniences explained away by his eventual insight.

**Canonical literary monograph.** Scope is the six Japanese numbered manga witnesses and their explicitly routed embedded material. No outside first name, adaptation, reception or supplement is used. ## Romantic distinction within an already familiar life

The early two-person outing does not place Takeda outside Yuma's everyday world. Their basketball/school familiarity gives the new couple an existing social relation to change. His worry that she replaced their date with a double date because she disliked being alone asks for the meaning of that change (V01/S0019–0021). Her nervousness explanation reassures him; he then initiates their sudden first kiss S0022. The sequence contains a real wish to understand and a gap in advance permission. His question is about relationship comfort, not an explicit request for that kiss. Calling him respectful in every respect would remove what the page actually shows.

The broader date scenes supply real enjoyment. Takeda can notice Hotaru's isolation, regret apparent pushiness and include her; Yuma and he laugh and enjoy rides (V01/S0045–0047,0060–0066). These ordinary pleasures matter because Yuma's later Hotaru-directed recognition need not retroactively make their first relationship an entirely false experience. A person can enjoy someone, care about hurting them and nonetheless discover that the relationship does not provide the affection they expected. Takeda is hurt by that divergence without becoming entitled to repair it through more couple activity.

The snowboard contrast also interrupts a simple competent-male/protected-girl arrangement. Yuma is better than he is, and his expectation of teaching does not organize the actual activity (V01/S0082–0084). His disappointment when she chooses early sleep is similarly ordinary and bounded. He lets her leave, then seeks a plausible nervousness explanation through his brother's advice S0101–0110. Neither clumsiness nor disappointment makes him a comic fool whose needs can be ignored. Neither makes his explanation true. At this stage he is navigating incomplete information through available ordinary scripts.

## The boundary is reciprocity, not successful progress

The V02 room scene makes that distinction sharper. Yuma tries to advance the relationship while comparing the situation with Hotaru. Takeda sees discomfort and stops; his choice of the floor and his statement that she seems not to like him are directly shown (V02/S0009–0013; CON-0007). He does not treat her prior insistence as sufficient evidence that the encounter should continue. The visible change is from wanting progress to wanting credible reciprocal participation.

This is one of his strongest ethically discriminating moments. It is also specific. The earlier sudden kiss prevents extending it into a universal advance-permission practice; later disappointment remains real. The point is not that a good boy never wants too much. It is that, when this discomfort becomes perceptible, he can allow the desired sequence to fail instead of forcing it to prove the relationship.

Suspicious phone contact, the failed lunch/pancake return to normality, a missed date and an opaque explanation deepen the sincerity concern (V02/S0048–0056,0085–0092). His request for distance and later refusal of the restored morning routine are acts of self-protection. Yuma's lost access is a consequence of his boundary, not evidence that he has coercively demanded performance. He asks her to reconsider actual affection rather than perform what a girlfriend should do. He still does not know the girls' acts. The reader's fuller view makes his uncertainty understandable while forbidding an invented confrontation with the complete truth.

The strongest alternative is that withdrawal itself pressures Yuma to choose or prove herself. Romantic boundaries can have that practical effect, and his waiting leaves a possibility of return. Yet the depicted alternative is reconsideration, not an explicit demand that she supply intimacy or lose basic care. Analysis should retain the possible pressure of disappointed hope while distinguishing it from Fujiwara's stated access-for-silence price. Otherwise every action that affects another person's options would become the same ethical mechanism.

## Changed assurance permits help but does not restore the couple

Hotaru's V03 café account changes the facts Takeda thinks he has. An admirer wanted Yuma, Yuma supposedly did not reciprocate, that person has stopped, and she likes Takeda (V03/S0023–0025). His invitation afterward has a causal informational basis. This does not show that Hotaru gives the whole truth or that Takeda verifies it. Her private truth/lie tactic leaves the exact false clause unidentified; his reliance on her cannot fill that gap.

The following school assistance makes their relation broader than a postponed date. Application help, notes and consultation offer concrete resources; fever assistance places him and Hotaru at the infirmary (V03/S0056–0057,0093–0098,0143–0145). He privately wants another chance S0105, but aid is not explicitly priced as that chance. Desire and care coexist without one exhausting the other. Yuma's gratitude and tears have meaning, yet they do not constitute renewed exclusivity or present romantic attraction.

This distinction is partly formal. The same practical care that supplies a drink sends him through the doorway to actual visual knowledge at V03/S0155–0156. He sees the girls close; he does not witness the entire approach, apology, response or earlier history. Help generates access to a painful fact without making him omniscient. V04 begins with his continued assistance before attempted dismissal, then brings his renewed questions to Fujiwara (S0007–0009,0034–0036). The oscillation is a represented attempt to understand, not an unchanged ignorance that a later commentator may silently restore.

The old T-R03 rule therefore cannot claim these scenes as a forecast hit. It excluded fresh contrary romantic evidence, and care cannot prove its own antecedent. This methodological limit preserves a literary fact: Takeda's generosity occurs while he has questions, not because the questions have vanished.

## A useful procedure from a mistaken map

At the beach Takeda describes an ordinary future of university, work and marrying someone he likes (V04/S0043–0048). That future is neither achieved nor intrinsically superior to Yuma's emerging alternative. Its literary force is that a familiar path once seemed available to her, while her present attachment makes that path difficult to inhabit honestly. Takeda gives the imagined peaceful life a human voice and ordinary uncertainty: his examinations are not triumphant, and he corrects the false current-couple label.

Their accepted night walk gives him another role. He notices preoccupation, calls it love and suggests asking the person's actual feelings (V04/S0075–0085). The procedure points away from himself. He can acknowledge failed first love and still want Yuma to obtain an answer. This is valuable assistance precisely because he does not need to be the recipient of her love to suggest better inquiry.

The recipient map remains mistaken. He worries that a bad man is playing with her; later he explicitly says the man Yuma likes (S0090–0091,0118). His category recognition helps Yuma privately name Hotaru, but her inner naming does not reach him. A familiar heterosexual assumption limits his understanding without making the procedure useless. The manga separates interpretive success from complete knowledge: attention may indeed be romantic even when the observer guesses the object incorrectly.

Written Japanese supports the distinction. His direct questions and practical offers differ from Fujiwara's insinuations; the formal school-acquaintance address to Hotaru differs from the girls' childhood familiarity. His male-beloved wording is evidentiary because it constrains what he can know, not because it certifies Yuma's orientation (JPS-0003/0007/0012/0014). The endpoint recognition will matter partly because he revises the recipient rather than requiring reality to conform to his first category.

## Care can become intrusive without becoming unreal

V05 gives the strongest counterexample to a frictionless care model. Takeda follows Yuma secretly, calls his own action low conduct and nevertheless continues (S0049–0056). The inward condemnation establishes ethical awareness; it is not a permission slip or a completed correction. His club view produces a mistaken account that a bad man may have coerced her into work. Reader-visible waiting and subsequent home closeness differ from the story he constructs.

This error is related to his earlier male-recipient assumption but is not merely its repetition. He now obtains information through an act that crosses privacy and then arranges the partial information into rescue. Genuine concern, curiosity, romantic hope and a need to resolve uncertainty may all be involved; the source does not provide a complete ranking. Defending Yuma against Fujiwara's disparagement S0069–0070 can be loyal and epistemically inadequate at once. The boy who objects to forced romance is capable of acting as if protective intention licenses observation.

When Yuma later reaches him tearful after the Hotaru confrontation, her apology omits its cause. Takeda sends Fujiwara ahead, asks, comforts and initiates an embrace before declaring continued love and requesting renewal (S0145–0153). The temporal order matters: there is no explicit price for care, but her vulnerability and his initiated contact prevent treating the scene as uncomplicated aid. Renewed dating is something he still wants. The fairest reading keeps both the care and the pressure risk without inventing a bargain the dialogue does not state.

Ordinary life supplies a second counterweight. Native study, sport, outings, fireworks, friendly meals and the campus invitation are activities he can share even after losing the boyfriend role. The embedded cooking extra makes him a provider of occasional lunch because his parents work; he modestly notes frozen ingredients and praises Fujiwara's cold pasta (V05/S0161–0162, original rechecked). That small competence is particularly revealing because it gives useful everyday action to both boys. It cannot redeem Fujiwara or prove Takeda's adult household capacity. It prevents either from existing only at the dramatic moments the central romance needs.

## Publication, violence and refusal of the supposed gift

The photograph gives Takeda both more knowledge and another ethical failure. V06/S0003 establishes receipt. His punch and collar control are concrete at S0009–0010, followed by deletion demand and peer intervention S0012. Anger at a privacy violation explains his action's object; it does not establish that violence is justified or that he is universally nonviolent. The form places bodily control beside image control, making an effective restraint part of the accountability exchange.

The later free conversation is different. At S0131 Fujiwara refers back to his concealment/wake-Takeda explanation; Takeda asks whether he thought this would make him happy and says アホ! at S0132. No subsequent Fujiwara reply is depicted. Takeda refuses the position of beneficiary. Knowledge of Yuma's attachment is useful to him, but exposing her cannot simply be declared a gift on that basis. The difference between truth as another person's admission and truth as someone else's imposed publication acquires a male participant who can state it from inside the network.

This refusal also exposes Fujiwara's limits. Their ordinary friendship and his useful advice did not give him authority to define what Takeda should want. Takeda need not become a wholly transformed ethical hero for that distinction to hold. His punch remains; permanent severance or future accountability is unshown. What the manga supplies is a particular rejection of harm presented as help.

## Recognition without ownership, and an endpoint with limits

Yuma's roof admission finally names Hotaru to him (V06/S0058). Her retrospective first-love account may hurt, but it does not independently prove she never valued him. Takeda says he cannot fully understand the difference between the girls' friendship and romance, while affirming that a person liking a person is not strange S0061–0062. Limited understanding and legitimacy are compatible. He does not demand complete comprehension as the condition of her right to choose.

The exact renewal-refusal words addressed to him are not printed; Yuma later reports proper refusal to Hotaru S0066. His good recognition therefore is new descriptive evidence, not a literal T-R05 forecast hit. The old rule expressly excluded new H-recipient discovery. Keeping NDO matters: the mature interpretation is allowed to learn something the earlier model had not predicted.

The embedded post-return school gag distinguishes his racing heart from romantic love (S0166). That single inner statement offers another way to respond to the girls' happiness, without supplying a new partner, permanent recovered friendship or a comprehensive orientation. It is after return within V06, not future external material. The teacher's line concerns absence; Yuma supplies the separate study-to-graduate/care joke at S0167. The ordinary school setting retains him in the social world after the central pair's train finish.

A strong counterreading is that Takeda remains structurally convenient: he gives the heroine a safe alternative, supplies the word love, briefly intrudes, then grants legitimacy so the main pair can close. The role economy supports that objection, and his interior access is thinner than theirs. Yet the decisions resisting convenience are substantial: he stops wanted progress, withdraws the old routine, helps outside a restored couple, follows despite guilt, renews at a vulnerable time, strikes the publisher and rejects publication's alleged benefit. These actions do not form a single obedient obstacle-removal script.

The endpoint resolves his knowledge of the chosen recipient and depicts nonpossessive recognition. It leaves full-history judgment, durable friendship, romantic recovery, future work and general conflict practice open. His literary significance lies in that narrower achieved distinction: desire can remain his own without becoming authority over the person desired, and newly acquired truth need not make the means of acquiring it acceptable. The separate model can carry that distinction into bounded hypotheses; it cannot turn the completed manga into proof of an ideal man or a tested unfamiliar-scenario personality.
