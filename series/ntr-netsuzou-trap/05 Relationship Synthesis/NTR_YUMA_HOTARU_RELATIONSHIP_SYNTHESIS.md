## The relation produces closeness faster than it produces a shared account

The pair's central mechanism is a persistent difference between **having access to someone** and **being answered by them**. Yuma initially wants Hotaru's unique reliance while treating their connection as friendship. Hotaru repeatedly obtains intimate access through help, practice, teasing, and concealed opportunity while avoiding a plain statement of her desired relation. The resulting proximity gives each evidence of specialness without requiring either to expose the full wish or grant the other an intelligible choice. It can sustain genuine attachment and produce harm simultaneously. Recognition eventually changes the terms of desire more clearly than it changes the terms of authority. The final return has meaning because the girls can now acknowledge an attachment that their earlier categories disqualified; it remains ethically unfinished because seeking or supplying care has not become a reliably negotiated practice. (NTR-JP-V01/S0009–0016/I01, S0103–0107/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0135–0144/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0148–0162/I01; REL-0001, REL-0002, REL-0018, REL-0033, REL-0036; CON-0001, CON-0038, CON-0039; CLM-0021, CLM-0034, CLM-0035.)

Neither direction begins as a transparent mirror of the other. Yuma narrates pleasure in being needed by the bullied child, resents Hotaru's first boyfriend, and later dislikes faces of Hotaru that she does not know. Her distinctive stake is privileged knowledge and indispensability. Hotaru says Yuma might no longer need her, falters at boyfriend progress, and eventually voices the wound of having gone unregarded for a long time. Her distinctive stake becomes credible continued regard. Those wants converge on proximity but are not identical: being someone's necessary protector can conflict with recognizing that person's autonomous life, while wanting sustained regard can conflict with permitting the beloved to refuse. The completed corpus strengthens an attachment interpretation without establishing its exact onset or the intention of every early act. (NTR-JP-V01/S0055–0059/I01, S0069–0072/I01; NTR-JP-V02/S0135/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0078–0083/I01; REL-0008, REL-0033; JPS-0006, JPS-0028; CLM-0006, CLM-0033.)

## The childhood privilege makes difference threatening in both directions

For Yuma, Hotaru's other relationships are disturbing partly because they disclose an unfamiliar Hotaru. The middle-school recollection shows Yuma acquiring basketball friends while Hotaru accepts a boy's invitation; Yuma's ensuing self-indictment identifies jealousy over priority, not just concern about a bad partner. The embedded middle-school makeup story gives the same problem a mundane form: accepting Hotaru's cosmetic help does not let Yuma share the adulthood she imagines Hotaru already possesses. The dependence she enjoyed cannot guarantee transparent development. Later work discovery repeats that experience in a concrete social world with clients, managers, money, and skills that Yuma did not know. Her protector map cannot simply absorb those worlds by declaring them dangerous. (NTR-JP-V02/S0123–0135/I01; NTR-JP-V03/S0061–0076/I01, S0159–0164/I01; REL-0008, REL-0012; INF-0013; AGY-0011; VIS-0009, VIS-0017.)

Hotaru's relation to that privilege is more conflicted than wanting to remain a helpless child. She rejects the premise that Yuma may decide which work she can do and says she is no longer the bullied child. She also intervenes possessively against a client, represents Yuma's willingness to try work without obtaining her affirmative answer, and is glad when Yuma quits while keeping her own work. These are domain-specific contradictions: Hotaru seeks freedom from Yuma's authority and still claims authority over Yuma in other circumstances. Her autonomy argument is valid without making all her conduct respecting of autonomy. Yuma herself recognizes that she is neither guardian nor boyfriend, yet inwardly resolves against separation. Their difficulty therefore cannot be explained by assigning one permanent protector and one permanent dependent. (NTR-JP-V03/S0073–0076/I01, S0099–0105/I01, S0125–0133/I01, S0146–0147/I01; REL-0012, REL-0013; CON-0014, CON-0016, CON-0017; AGY-0012; CLM-0016.)

V06 makes the cost of reliance more explicit for the reader. The private anti-burden phone statement, empty household, absent-parent history, and relocated empty room support a connection between reducing expectations and avoiding abandonment. But Yuma is not shown learning that exact phone sentence or every family flashback. Her final interpretation develops from her own childhood memories, the father's current account, Fujiwara's arrangement testimony, Hotaru's voiced hurt, and the direct fear of trust. Reading those routes as identical would give Yuma psychological authority she has not acquired. The narrative lets her respond to a more intelligible fear; it does not certify that she now knows Hotaru completely. (NTR-JP-V06/S0094–0105/I01, S0109–0112/I01, S0148–0151/I01; INF-0033, INF-0034; REL-0033, REL-0034, REL-0035; VIS-0032; CLM-0033.)

## Categories allow contact while postponing responsibility

Practice first assigns Hotaru's acts a beneficial destination outside the pair: Yuma supposedly becomes ready for Takeda. Yuma's girls-and-friends explanation makes differential comfort seem exempt from romance. The categories are complementary but unequal evasions. Yuma initially struggles to understand her desire; Hotaru acts over communicated objections. Neither a mutually convenient explanation nor unspoken attraction supplies the missing permission. When Hotaru names Yuma an affair partner and asks whether she can tell Takeda, the actual phone lie demonstrates that Yuma cannot preserve the harmless-friend story in practice. Her moral image is already implicated before she has a settled romantic theory. (NTR-JP-V01/S0011–0016/I01, S0103/I01; NTR-JP-V02/S0031–0049/I01, S0153–0155/I01; INF-0005; CON-0001, CON-0008; JPS-0002, JPS-0004, JPS-0006; CLM-0005, CLM-0011.)

Later the same practice word has another speaker and another knowledge state. Yuma now privately knows she likes Hotaru, refuses to name the recipient, and requests practice for an unnamed other. Hotaru's affirmative answer and embrace matter locally. The encounter is not reducible to the original imposed-contact pattern, but its cover is knowingly inadequate. Yuma gets access without asking for the answer that might threaten it. Hotaru can grant closeness without deciding aloud whether she wants the proposed romance. The system is stable only while neither insists that the category describe the attachment. The old Y-R04 protection-framing prediction accordingly missed a real component: practice is a different cover, not evidence that the frozen forecast was correct. (NTR-JP-V04/S0134–0144/I01; REL-0018, REL-0019; CON-0023; INF-0019; JPS-0017; CLM-0012/R-V04, CLM-0017, CLM-0021.)

V05 exposes the cost of that accommodation. A shared-home diagram and newlywed play make a couple future tangible, yet Yuma publicly denies another beloved and describes roomsharing with a close friend. When Hotaru reports possible pregnancy, the acknowledged male couple seems to carry consequences that girls' practice does not count against. The uncertain pregnancy and later negative report remain testimony, not clinical or paternity facts. Their relational function nevertheless is clear: Yuma discovers that she cannot insist on the significance of intimacy while preserving a language that denies it. Her experience of two-timing becomes understandable without proving a previously agreed exclusive Yuma–Hotaru couple. Hotaru's eventual no-count/identity question then uses that categorical shelter against the newly named desire. (NTR-JP-V05/S0045–0046/I01, S0085–0088/I01, S0104–0118/I01, S0129/I01, S0138–0147/I01; REL-0030; INF-0025, INF-0026, INF-0027; JPS-0020, JPS-0022; CLM-0027.)

Hotaru's humiliation story may shield Yuma from the queer label, as she later claims, but it destroys the meaning Yuma wants recognized. Yuma's preference for the stigmatized category over that lie is therefore a demand for representational standing: protection cannot consist of denying the attachment on her behalf. It is not an exhaustive orientation declaration. The confession gains force when Yuma also tells Takeda whom she likes and reports declining renewal. She relinquishes a male relationship alternative she cannot now sincerely offer before asking Hotaru to believe her choice. ## The changing initiator does not eliminate act-specific boundaries

The relation's development becomes distorted if every uncomfortable act is flattened into one kind of harm, or every later reciprocal act into proof that the early conduct was wanted. Yuma's objections, pushes, and slope slap are effective resistance at particular moments. Her later hand grasp, uniform pull, covered request, and home initiative are real choices at other moments. Hotaru's cessation announcement, broad apology, local affirmative responses, and later bids to stay are also real changes. Each complicates a fixed aggressor/passive-recipient story without authorizing the preceding violations. The distinguishing question is who acts, what was requested, what boundary was communicated, and how the other responds. (NTR-JP-V01/S0130–0138/I01; NTR-JP-V02/S0143–0154/I01; NTR-JP-V03/S0028–0035/I01, S0149–0154/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0060–0068/I01; CON-0002, CON-0011, CON-0013, CON-0018, CON-0026; CLM-0005, CLM-0011, CLM-0022.)

Practical help provides an especially strong comparison. Yuma asks for costume fastening at work and thanks Hotaru; at the beach she assents to fixing a loose fastening, but protests the added intimate acts. The useful service is not imaginary, and the permission remains specific. Similarly, an old practice request does not authorize contact at a newly objected-to classroom location. Yuma's reactive collar pull and kiss must then be separately assessed; it does not erase her earlier objection or prove Hotaru's advance assent to Yuma's initiative. Desire becomes bidirectional faster than a reliable permission practice does. (NTR-JP-V03/S0080–0084/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0053–0060/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0026–0034/I01; CON-0015, CON-0020, CON-0025; AGY-0020; JPS-0018.)

The apologies are neither absent nor comprehensive. Hotaru's V03 apology, jealous-friendship disclosure, and cessation promise acknowledge confusion and specialness; her private truth/lie thought confirms managed representation without identifying a false clause or making the apology wholly insincere. The aquarium apology acknowledges excessive teasing while leaving the work-money question unanswered. In V05 she actually pauses at Yuma's unfinished-work objection. These are partial repair capacities, not a fully negotiated account of earlier refusals. Yuma likewise becomes more accountable toward Takeda, but her slap after Hotaru's category denial and painful handling in V06 preserve responsibility toward Hotaru that confession alone cannot discharge. (NTR-JP-V03/S0028–0035/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0102–0110/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0096–0101/I01, S0142–0144/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0050–0053/I01, S0073–0075/I01; CON-0013, CON-0024, CON-0028, CON-0030, CON-0034, CON-0035, CON-0039.)

## Ordinary life both supports and tests the attachment

Shopping, matching necklaces, fireworks, study, poor cooking, meal jokes, imagined chores, and shared rest supply reasons to desire living with the other that cannot be reduced to secrecy or erotic escalation. They are activities with competence, humor, uneven labor, and future resources. Yuma can imagine something more couple-like with Hotaru than her former dating routine because those ordinary possibilities matter to her; that is her comparison, not an objective ranking of all relationships. Hotaru's rooms-and-chores proposal provides an intelligible alternative to Yuma's assumption that living alone necessarily means abandoning her. Their affection has practical content even while its terms remain unsettled. (NTR-JP-V04/S0062–0069/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0038–0048/I01, S0081–0088/I01, S0159–0162/I01; REL-0030; AGY-0022; VIS-0025, VIS-0026, VIS-0028.)

That content also prevents care from being a costless moral good. Work buys Hotaru options; Yuma's intervention can cost her income. School absence threatens an unfinished path; moving consumes money. A future shared home depends on Yuma's admission condition rather than on the emotional sufficiency of confession. The earlier cooking extra is undated/earlier domestic comedy, not a sequel, and the final school gags are separately post-return. Their humor preserves care asymmetry: Yuma offers notes and food, Hotaru asks to be held, and Yuma inwardly jokes about becoming her mother. Neither the comic mode nor the tender train ending answers how care will be reciprocated materially over time. (NTR-JP-V05/S0072–0073/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0138–0144/I01, S0158–0162/I01, S0166–0167/I01; REL-0036, REL-0037; CON-0038; AGY-0029, AGY-0031; JPS-0030, JPS-0031.)

## Confession makes withdrawal more, rather than less, intelligible

Hotaru's tears at Yuma's love claim reveal disbelief grounded in a stated temporal wound. Her claim to know techniques that attract boys can function defensively, but it is testimony rather than proof that every earlier approach was calculated manipulation. Yuma sees a less guarded face and interprets the performance through childhood. Hotaru asks for belief; Yuma promises to remain. This is a consequential reciprocal exchange. The following absence then blocks the easy narrative that being chosen has cured the fear. Relocation is both a decision toward work/housing independence and an avoidance of contact. Direct anti-burden speech to her father and later fear of trusting strengthen the avoidance interpretation, while leaving an authentic desire for independence available. (NTR-JP-V06/S0072–0088/I01, S0098–0103/I01, S0148/I01; REL-0033, REL-0034; AGY-0027; JPS-0028, JPS-0029; CLM-0033.)

Yuma's pursuit changes state too. She is no longer merely withholding an unnamed wish: she has chosen Hotaru and believes ordinary waiting risks permanent absence. The textbook pretext, rental clues, train journey, unsuccessful door requests, and eventual chance sighting make effort visible without making its method infallible or authorized. Hotaru breaks a pull, objects to losing work, serves tea, and tells Yuma to leave. The work/private-address boundary does not disappear because Yuma knows the father's flattering competence account is wrong. Nor does Hotaru's eventual staying prove each intervening objection secretly meant the opposite. Yuma's claim that flight is dependence and her invoked lifelong togetherness remain her judgments and wishes. (NTR-JP-V06/S0101–0108/I01, S0115–0129/I01, S0133–0147/I01; INF-0033, INF-0035; CON-0037, CON-0038; AGY-0028, AGY-0029; CLM-0034.)

The hand motif holds the contradiction rather than resolving it. Childhood invitation, city recognition, resisted pulling, held hands, and Hotaru's small coat grip use related images for different relational acts. Hotaru's staying gesture and joint return are more than Yuma's self-reported intention; they show participation. Yet the repeated-help vow belongs to Yuma's interiority. Her acceptance of incomplete trust at S0159 is likewise her inference from Hotaru's anxious eyes, not Hotaru stating an endpoint diagnosis of herself and not a spoken mutual repair contract. Yuma has acquired a capacity to remain with uncertainty, but whether that capacity becomes patience or repeated intrusive rescue is not tested beyond this endpoint. (NTR-JP-V06/S0097/I01, S0133–0138/I01, S0148–0162/I01; VIS-0034, VIS-0035; REL-0036; CON-0036; JPS-0030; CLM-0035.)

## Counterreading and endpoint adjudication

The strongest competing account is that the reunion restores an unequal protector/dependent arrangement, with romance beautifying intrusion rather than transforming it. Childhood indispensability, Yuma's refusal to allow separation, address circumvention, resisted pulling, lost income, inward repeated-rescue commitment, and the care gag make this reading substantial. It cannot be dismissed by pointing to a smile or by promising an adult future the source does not depict. It also cannot fully account for Hotaru's active early initiatives, work/privacy assertions, directly voiced fear, local acts of assent, and chosen staying as if they were all null. Dependency and autonomous attachment are not exclusive descriptions of every moment. The present relation can be reciprocally desired and still retain unequal authority. (NTR-JP-V03/S0099–0105/I01, S0132–0133/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0141/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0116–0118/I01, S0137–0148/I01, S0156–0167/I01; CON-0016, CON-0037, CON-0038; AGY-0012, AGY-0031; REL-0036, REL-0037; CLM-0016, CLM-0034, CLM-0035.)

A second counterreading treats the late backstory as retroactive explanation designed to absolve Hotaru's earlier manipulation. The formal delay does encourage sympathetic reinterpretation and Yuma's recalled-speech montage gives the reinterpretation emotional coherence. But the mature claim must stop where the evidence stops: current fear and long-unregarded testimony strengthen defensive attachment, not universal benevolence, exact romantic onset, or permission. The recorded H-R03 substitute-frame failure also remains a failure; later affection does not validate the earlier model by renaming every evasion. The Y-R04 framing miss and Y-R05 request/cover miss likewise resist a story of smoothly predicted romantic progress. (NTR-JP-V06/S0078/I01, S0094–0099/I01, S0149–0151/I01; VIS-0031, VIS-0032, VIS-0035; CLM-0008/R-V03, CLM-0012/R-V04, CLM-0017/R-V05, CLM-0033.)

At mainline **fin S0162**, Yuma's recipient and willingness to choose are explicit, Hotaru's attachment is strongly supported by voiced vulnerability and participation, and both return together in present comfort. S0166 separately establishes Hotaru's school return; S0167 gives bounded care/play. These resolve more than mere continued contact. They do not establish agreed lifelong exclusivity, a completed formal H/F breakup, graduation, university admission, completed cohabitation, stable finances, durable trust, or full accounting for past harm. The relationship's mature endpoint is a chosen continuation whose emotional reality is supported and whose ethical terms remain a task. That distinction is the analytical result, not a defect to be repaired with quarantined after-story material. (NTR-JP-V06/S0109–0111/I01, S0148–0162/I01, S0166–0167/I01; REL-0035, REL-0036, REL-0037; INF-0034; CON-0039; CLM-0033, CLM-0034, CLM-0035.)

### Yuma → Hotaru: care for a wider world still competes with indispensability

Yuma's friendship advice moves the autonomy problem into an ordinary social preference. Hotaru declines the cafeteria invitation and Yuma subsequently asks why she does not make friends or try taking interest. The advice is intelligible as concern for isolation, but its subject is something Hotaru has just declined. The first Hotaru answer questions Yuma's prescription and refuses that interest; the later mathematics/politics/cricket analogy forces Yuma to confront the difference between preferring a good for someone and producing their preference by instruction. No compelled attendance follows. Yuma is therefore neither depicted enacting another completed override nor shown mastering refusal across all domains. The important positive result is a preference disagreement that remains a conversation. (LW S0007–0010; REL-0038, CON-0040–0041, AGY-0033–0034; H-PC-04 SUPPORTED_BOUNDED.)

The closing wish complicates any simple claim that Yuma merely wants Hotaru isolated. She inwardly wants Hotaru someday to value family or friends, while acknowledging that she herself would feel lonely. Wider welfare and the reward of unique centrality occupy the same thought. This extends the mainline protector pleasure more precisely than either redemption or selfishness alone: she can recognize a good beyond being needed and remain attached to being needed. When Hotaru asks about her expression, Yuma says nothing. The wish is not an agreement that Hotaru will cultivate other bonds, and its qualification is not known to Hotaru. At most, nonstatement prevents an immediate new mandate. It leaves open whether future care becomes responsive negotiation or quietly retained authority. (LW S0019–0020; REL-0042, CON-0043, INF-0039, AGY-0036; CLM-0045.)

### Hotaru → Yuma: direct specialness does not require accepting the prescribed social life

Hotaru's group refusal includes apology and later-contact language. She can decline an activity while preserving the partner relation, rather than vanish from both. But the cut to their later outdoor walk hides Yuma's immediate response. It cannot prove that Yuma ate with her friends, followed Hotaru at once or sustained a mutually agreed separation. Refusal is observed; a complete two-person separation practice is not. The held-out audit accordingly gives no access-pursuit or ordinary-care score from that cut. (LW S0008–0009; AGY-0033, VIS-0036; Y-PC-01 NDO, Y-PC-06 INSUFFICIENT_VISIBILITY.)

At the apartment, Hotaru corrects Yuma's universal nobody-interest inference with plain interest in Yuma alone. This is a meaningful post-acknowledgment relation state. The familiar initiative no longer needs the local practice/unnamed-person account, and the spoken recipient is clear. Her refusal to manufacture unrelated friendship is compatible with wanting the person already chosen. It does not make the selective claim a full exclusivity contract, an orientation label, a new explicit need request, or proof that she can tolerate future disappointment without retreat. The first direct-interest exchange is new developmental evidence; H-LW-R01 cannot validate itself on it. (LW S0012–0018; REL-0039, AGY-0035, JPS-0032; CLM-0040/0044.)

H's father account and remembered childhood loneliness become more directly available to Yuma here than the mainline's private reader-facing family scenes. Yuma's partial-awareness narration similarly gives the reader her later interpretation of earlier knowledge; it does not date a complete earlier mutual agreement or give Hotaru every thought. ### The strongest control counterreading accepts the tenderness

The mainline counterreading does not depend on romance being false. LW adds a gratifying concentration of Hotaru's investment in Yuma and Yuma's admitted ambivalence about losing that concentration. The intimate ending can reward the closed dyad while withholding the negotiation that might make it less closed. That is additional evidence for dependency as an unresolved relationship structure. It is not a new depicted forced-isolation act: Hotaru's preference is her own speech, Yuma imagines a wider welfare, and no compelled meal follows. These limits weaken a total captive/wholly selfish account while leaving the stronger conditional claim intact. Future tested refusals would be needed to decide whether the relation develops reliable freedom inside attachment. The observed LW boundary is more direct selective closeness with an unshared concern, not completed ethical transformation. (LW S0009–0010/0014–0020; CLM-0045.)
