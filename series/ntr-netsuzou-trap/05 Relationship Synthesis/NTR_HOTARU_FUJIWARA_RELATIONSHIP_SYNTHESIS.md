## An acknowledged couple can exclude the relationship its label invites us to assume

Hotaru and Fujiwara begin as a publicly legible dating pair whose private terms are opaque. The final account calls their relation an arrangement in form, with physical access for him and filling lonely time for her. The resulting distinction is not that a publicly acknowledged male couple is automatically false while a denied girls' attachment is automatically true. It is that a shared social label cannot identify desire, emotional obligation, equal bargaining, or permission. Their arrangement can provide utility to both and be coercively enforced. Hotaru's continued presence can be a decision without establishing free agreement to every act; Fujiwara can possess useful information without becoming a trustworthy interpreter of all her motives. (NTR-JP-V01/S0067–0068/I01, S0142–0152/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0028–0031/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0107–0111/I01; REL-0005, REL-0022, REL-0035; CON-0004, CON-0005, CON-0021; INF-0034.)

The timing of the evidence matters. V01 supplies an actual kick after refusal and a suggestive room discovery with unshown act/permission. V02 adds Hotaru's self-bad comfort testimony and nonfaithfulness expectation. V04 reveals historical request-compliance language and a conditional genuine-love answer. V06 gives the in-form/lonely-time account. Those later disclosures revise the current explanation without making the V01 reader know the contract or retroactively settle the room event. Sparse interior access makes this dyad narrower in some psychological claims than Yuma–Hotaru; it does not make its structural and ethical responsibility smaller. (NTR-JP-V01/S0067–0068/I01, S0142–0146/I01; NTR-JP-V02/S0026–0028/I01, S0120–0122/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0028–0031/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0109–0111/I01; REL-0005, REL-0010, REL-0022, REL-0035; INF-0018; CLM-0006, CLM-0033.)

## Violence contradicts a free-compliance account before the arrangement is disclosed

Hotaru's refusal because she is not in the mood is followed by Fujiwara's kick. That action is directly depicted and occurs while Yuma and Takeda are absent. It is not an inference drawn from bruising or from a generally hostile personality. Its causal relationship to a voiced boundary makes it a punishment of refusal. Later attractive couple staging on the slopes reassures Yuma, who does not know the kick occurred; the visual mismatch demonstrates why neither outward dating coherence nor an uninformed witness's comfort can establish ethical conditions. (NTR-JP-V01/S0067–0068/I01, S0082–0084/I01; CON-0004; INF-0003; VIS-0003; AGY-0004.)

The later eye patch and uncovered bruising warrant concern, especially against that prior danger. They do not prove Fujiwara caused this particular injury. Hotaru says she fell; he denies knowing its cause. Those statements are not independently verified, but lack of verification is not permission to invent an off-page assault. The final room discovery similarly suggests an encounter without depicting the act or Hotaru's willingness. Takeda's assumption that dating intimacy is ordinary is itself a public-category inference. The mature account can establish actual violence and coercive conditions without manufacturing the unseen acts that would make every scene fit one complete story. (NTR-JP-V01/S0139–0146/I01; NTR-JP-V02/S0026–0028/I01, S0064–0080/I01; CON-0005; INF-0006; REL-0010; CLM-0006.)

The V04 historical montage deepens the enforcement question. Fujiwara rebukes Hotaru's reluctance; her hand at her cheek, his raised hand, and the sequence suggest violence, though no impact panel is supplied there. Her apology and recollection of the terms then precede his advance. Grammatically the promise binds both: no troublesome complaints, no unnecessary prying into each other, and response to the other's requests. That is an observation about the words. It is not a finding that both can refuse, complain, or invoke terms at equal cost. The represented pressure is precisely why broad compliance cannot stand in for affirmative permission at each act. (NTR-JP-V04/S0028–0029/I01; CON-0021; INF-0018; JPS-0015; VIS-0019.)

The prehistory also avoids a simple rescue-origin mythology. Hotaru's unnamed former boyfriend refuses her breakup and grabs her; Fujiwara interrupts, but his casual invitation to continue does not establish that Hotaru freely exchanged dependence on him for rescue. His own former girlfriend's begging apology belongs to another speaker, not present Hotaru. The new public pair appears without an exhaustive proposal scene. These distinctions preserve what the montage actually establishes: prior partners, interruption, public pairing, and subsequent terms. Neither invented names nor an inferred rescue debt may fill its omissions. (NTR-JP-V04/S0020–0027/I01; INF-0018; CON-0021; CAST-0020.)

## Hotaru's ease is a testimony about demand, not a certification of safety

Hotaru tells Yuma that a good person makes her uncomfortable and Fujiwara makes her feel at ease because she is not good either. The account gives remaining beside him an intelligible relation to self-image. A person who does not invite goodness or complete emotional exposure can seem less demanding than someone whose care raises expectations. This is an interpretation supported by her testimony and later emotional-exclusion account, not a clinical explanation or a proof that he is physically safe. The clenched hand and earlier kick prevent the word ease from becoming an objective measure of the arrangement's harmlessness. (NTR-JP-V02/S0120–0122/I01; REL-0010; JPS-0005; CLM-0006/R-V02.)

V06's historical lonely-time terms strengthen that reading. Fujiwara's physical-access account and Hotaru's statement that one cannot always stay with the person one wants distinguish company from the desired attachment. The earlier referent is not explicitly named in that statement. The complete Yuma-directed trajectory supports connecting it to Hotaru's attachment, but it cannot date exact onset or certify every earlier intention. The temporary companion can fill time while avoiding the expectations of being genuinely wanted by someone whose loss matters. Hotaru's private anti-burden goal and voiced fear of trust make that avoidance mechanism plausible; they do not show that Fujiwara knows her full family history or consciously operates as a prescribed emotional treatment. (NTR-JP-V06/S0098–0099/I01, S0109–0111/I01, S0148/I01; REL-0034, REL-0035; JPS-0028, JPS-0029; CLM-0033.)

Hotaru also acts within and against the terms. She contests Fujiwara's claim that silence toward Takeda is a favor and later invokes mutual request compliance to sustain the aquarium charade. This matters as evidence against total passivity: the arrangement offers language she can use for a preferred social situation. It does not negate violence, show symmetry in enforcement, or make every claimed favor legitimate. Her own concealment and harmful treatment of Yuma remain acts for which she is responsible. Her capacity to use a term is compatible with being pressured in another application of the same term. (NTR-JP-V04/S0019–0020/I01, S0092–0094/I01; REL-0022; INF-0017; JPS-0015; CLM-0016.)

## Fujiwara's nonpolicing is a real contrast, not general respect for autonomy

When Yuma discovers Hotaru's work, Fujiwara says he knew its contents from the beginning and disclaims an obligation to forbid it. This differs from Yuma's wish to make Hotaru do approved work. A partner need not possess the right to direct another's employment, and Hotaru's autonomy argument is not invalidated by choosing an ethically harmful companion. But Fujiwara's refusal to police one domain does not establish respect for boundaries in another. His kick, pressured contract enforcement, girls-directed nondisclosure price, retained photograph, and later physical restraint directly contradict a general autonomy-respecting model. The useful comparison is between particular domains and mechanisms, not between a wholly controlling Yuma and wholly permissive Fujiwara. (NTR-JP-V03/S0099–0103/I01, S0109–0112/I01; NTR-JP-V02/S0110–0117/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0074–0076/I01; CON-0010, CON-0016, CON-0027; REL-0016; INF-0014; CLM-0016.)

His suffocation/exit report gives Yuma a painful possibility: Hotaru might want to leave specifically because living beside her is oppressive. The report becomes Yuma's belief, not a directly confirmed exhaustive Hotaru motive. It is consistent with work and savings, and later relocation confirms an independent departure. V06 also gives anti-burden and trust-fear evidence that broadens the explanation beyond Yuma alone. A useful or partly accurate disclosure can still be strategically selected. Equally, there is no positive proof that Fujiwara withholds a known address when he later denies knowing where Hotaru lives. General distrust is insufficient to convert every unavailable answer into a lie. (NTR-JP-V03/S0111–0116/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0104–0108/I01; INF-0014, INF-0034; REL-0016; CLM-0033.)

Academic competence and reported cooking skill reinforce this differentiation. Fujiwara can be useful in ordinary school and friendship settings; Takeda's medical-faculty forecast is testimony, not attained professional status, and the comic food ranking is not an empirical capability grade. Mundane usefulness explains why a threatening figure can remain socially ordinary. It neither proves remorse nor balances out coercion. Treating utility as redemption would confuse the source's widening of repertoire with an ethical transition it does not depict. (NTR-JP-V03/S0058–0059/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0043–0044/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0159–0162/I01; REL-0017; JPS-0012; VIS-0028; CAST-0014, CAST-0019, CAST-0024.)

## Information access exceeds the dyad without making its holder omniscient

Fujiwara's observations of girls' closeness grow into an explicit access-for-silence demand, unexpected recording, and retained third-party copy. Hotaru takes the phone and depicts deletion, while the later forwarding flashback makes complete erasure false. Her knowing deception about the copy is not established; the reader's knowledge cannot be automatically assigned to her. The information advantage matters to the dyad because Hotaru's other attachment can be kept exposed to intervention, but it does not mean Fujiwara knows every act, private thought, or mutual definition. His later imposed sexuality account is stronger than his factual access warrants. (NTR-JP-V01/S0098/I01, S0151–0152/I01; NTR-JP-V02/S0110–0112/I01, S0156–0158/I01; NTR-JP-V03/S0006–0010/I01, S0038–0040/I01; INF-0004, INF-0008, INF-0010; CON-0012; REL-0011, REL-0017.)

Later mechanisms differ from that original bargain. Silence is presented as a favor without a new explicit access price; the phone scene exposes physical restraint without Fujiwara speech; a later truth-offer question to Yuma creates guilt pressure without an access-for-silence condition. The earlier F-R02 separate-opportunity and F-R03 conditional-demand components therefore remain absent. Public circulation is an additional event, not the private mechanism finally vindicated. The named account, Hotaru's attribution, and Fujiwara's own justification strongly support publication agency in V06, while the send action, complete chain, and reach remain unshown. The network and information specialist own that audience transition in full; the dyad must retain its consequences without treating all power as one interchangeable act. (NTR-JP-V04/S0019–0020/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0076/I01, S0095/I01, S0155–0156/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0003/I01, S0020/I01, S0131/I01; INF-0017, INF-0024, INF-0028, INF-0029; AGY-0024; CLM-0014/R-V04, CLM-0020/R-V05, CLM-0036.)

Hotaru's public claim of a joint teasing plan is observed self-complicity, not independent proof that she commissioned publication. Her later protection account cannot settle that past agency either. The actual image violation and her humiliating response remain separate decisions. Fujiwara's benefit-to-Takeda justification acknowledges purposeful intervention while minimizing culpability; Takeda rejects that supposed benefit. No portrayed mechanism converts another person's deception into permission to expose them. (NTR-JP-V06/S0018–0021/I01, S0053/I01, S0131–0132/I01; INF-0030, INF-0035; REL-0031; JPS-0024; CLM-0032.)

## Pregnancy reports reveal category power without proving a medical history

Hotaru's report of possible pregnancy gives the public male couple a kind of consequence that she says girls' contact cannot produce. Yuma's panic and imagined marriage show the fear's force even though no clinical diagnosis appears. Hotaru later reports that she was not pregnant. Fujiwara's statement that pregnancy would not necessarily be his responsibility establishes a callous conditional denial, not other actual partners or confirmed paternity facts. His push when Yuma challenges him remains visible regardless of those uncertainties. The relation's represented bodily stakes are therefore analyzable without filling unknowns from genre expectation. (NTR-JP-V05/S0110–0129/I01; INF-0026; REL-0028; CON-0029; JPS-0022, JPS-0023.)

This episode does not establish a manufactured scare designed by Hotaru to control Yuma, nor a secretly affectionate Fujiwara reaction behind the hostile words. It supports the narrower mechanism of category disparity: an acknowledged male couple seems able to claim a consequential future while girls' practice is dismissed as no-count. The V06 in-form account then complicates that hierarchy. Socially acknowledged relation and emotional centrality diverge. The contrast gives Yuma reason to question the categories, not reason to deny the material significance or unknown medical status of Hotaru's report. (NTR-JP-V05/S0116–0118/I01, S0138–0143/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0109–0111/I01; REL-0028, REL-0035; CLM-0027, CLM-0033.)

## Counterreadings and the unresolved formal end

The strongest functional-arrangement reading takes the stated terms seriously: both decline emotional demands, Hotaru gains companionship, Fujiwara gains physical access, and he does not forbid her work or insist on continuation if she wants breakup. This prevents the relation from being reduced to unmotivated captivity. The reading fails if it treats utility or mutual grammar as ethical sufficiency. Refusal punishment, pressured enforcement, explicit information leverage, restraint, and publication remain acts that the utility account does not authorize. Hotaru's own misconduct elsewhere is not a waiver of these boundaries. (NTR-JP-V01/S0067–0068/I01; NTR-JP-V02/S0110–0117/I01; NTR-JP-V03/S0109–0112/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0028–0029/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0076/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0003/I01, S0109–0111/I01; REL-0010, REL-0022, REL-0035; CON-0004, CON-0010, CON-0021, CON-0027, CON-0032.)

The strongest opposite reading sees only coercive dependence and treats every apparent Hotaru choice as empty. Actual pressure strongly supports concern, but that total account erases her terms invocation, work decision, privacy request, relocation, and later chosen proximity with Yuma. Those choices do not make coercion unreal; they show that constraint and agency coexist. A diagnosis, invented trauma for Fujiwara, or a hidden additional partner list would not resolve the contradiction. It would replace the source's bounded motives with an omniscient explanation. (NTR-JP-V03/S0099–0105/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0092–0094/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0098–0103/I01, S0137–0140/I01, S0156–0162/I01; AGY-0012, AGY-0027, AGY-0031; INF-0034; CAST-0027, CAST-0029.)

At the endpoint Fujiwara calls the relationship in form and conditionally says a breakup would be fine if Hotaru wanted it. No complete mutually performed formal breakup appears. Hotaru's return with Yuma strongly changes her present attachment state; it does not supply missing termination words or a detailed future agreement with Fujiwara. His silent presence in the school extra establishes neither approval nor remorse. The dyad's mature conclusion is therefore differentiated: its emotional exclusion becomes more intelligible, its coercive history remains, its present utility appears displaced, and its exact formal ending remains open. (NTR-JP-V06/S0109–0111/I01, S0157–0162/I01, S0166/I01; REL-0035, REL-0036; INF-0034; CLM-0033, CLM-0035.)
