## Their problem is reciprocity, not insufficient progress

Yuma and Takeda initially seem to face a novice couple's problem: how to become comfortable enough to advance a relationship that both have accepted. The repeated problem becomes whether that public acceptance describes Yuma's available affection. Takeda wants reciprocal liking rather than performance; Yuma wants to be able to want the kind, honest, ordinary future he represents, while her attention continues turning toward Hotaru. Enjoyment and gratitude remain real. Their inability to become sufficient romance does not make them fraudulent from the outset, and their reality does not create an obligation to resume dating. The decisive development is a move from compensating for an unnamed mismatch to acknowledging its recipient and cost. (NTR-JP-V01/S0019–0024/I01, S0060–0066/I01; NTR-JP-V02/S0006–0013/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0127–0129/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0058–0071/I01; REL-0003, REL-0004, REL-0006, REL-0007, REL-0020, REL-0032; CON-0007; CLM-0021, CLM-0029.)

## Real pleasure is evidence against the disposable-boyfriend account

The amusement-park outing gives Yuma rides, laughter, ordinary embarrassment, and a second kiss. Takeda includes Hotaru to keep her from being isolated and regrets sounding pushy about an empty-home visit. He also has his own nervousness and consults his brother. These observations make the opening relationship more than a false obstacle erected around an already declared girls couple. Yuma is participating in an attractive, socially recognizable possibility. Her displaced thoughts, special Hotaru jealousy, and eventual room withdrawal show that possibility's limits without erasing its pleasures. (NTR-JP-V01/S0045–0050/I01, S0060–0066/I01, S0101–0110/I01; REL-0003, REL-0004; AGY-0003; CON-0003; INF-0002.)

The first room scene reveals a different standard of success from simple escalation. Yuma insists on progress while looking desperate and privately comparing the experience with Hotaru. Takeda notices discomfort, stops, gives her the bedding and sleeps on the floor, then says he feels not especially liked. He does not take her insistence as reason to continue at any cost. His hurt indicates that consent to attempted progress and sincere reciprocal romance are separate questions. His response is a bounded demonstrated capacity under incomplete information, not universal exemplary permission: the earlier sudden first kiss had no depicted advance request. (NTR-JP-V02/S0006–0013/I01; CON-0003, CON-0007; REL-0006, REL-0007; AGY-0006; CLM-0004/R-V02.)

Ordinary couple rituals cannot automatically repair that mismatch. The handmade lunch, candid admission of maternal help, and pancake-date proposal show Yuma trying to care. Their inadequacy does not prove they were only manipulative camouflage. Takeda nevertheless has already noticed discomfort and later hears an unexplained companion during the call. He is evaluating whether the relation is wanted, not merely whether Yuma can produce the right gestures. The missed date adds real lost time and opaque explanation. Yuma has her own reasons, including care and jealousy concerning Hotaru; the reasons do not give Takeda the information he needs or make his hurt imaginary. (NTR-JP-V02/S0048–0056/I01, S0064–0092/I01; REL-0006, REL-0007; INF-0007; AGY-0005, AGY-0006.)

## Concealment is progressively more knowing, but not every hidden act is chosen

The opening infidelity problem has an informal expectation rather than a depicted exhaustive contract. Public dating, secrecy, Yuma's guilt, and Hotaru's explicit affair vocabulary support the expectation that concealed outside intimacy matters. Yet the permission record contains Hotaru's continued contact over Yuma's objections. Describing every hidden act as Yuma's freely chosen betrayal would substitute a moral category for the actual sequence. Conversely, those violations do not erase Yuma's later explicit lie that nobody is present, voluntary reapproach, or knowing denial of another beloved after she recognizes Hotaru. The relation requires chronological accountability rather than either comprehensive innocence or comprehensive guilt. (NTR-JP-V01/S0011–0017/I01; NTR-JP-V02/S0031–0049/I01, S0146–0155/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0104–0108/I01; INF-0001, INF-0005, INF-0025; CON-0001, CON-0008, CON-0011; CLM-0005, CLM-0011, CLM-0021.)

Yuma initially cannot explain what she does not understand; later she understands more than she reveals. That shift changes responsibility. Her V04 private Hotaru recognition and awareness, through Fujiwara's attributed report of Takeda's bad-man concern at S0124, that Takeda assumes a male beloved mean that preserving the assumption is no longer simply confusion. She does not hear Takeda's private S0118 wording to Fujiwara. Fujiwara's accusation of camouflage identifies the effect of concealment while overprescribing her entire sexuality and intention. Yuma denies deliberately using Takeda that way, and the denial is not automatically true or false. A present false public account can be criticized without claiming that her original acceptance was a fully planned cover. Her wish that she could love Takeda gives the alternative future emotional weight precisely because she cannot choose its affect into existence. (NTR-JP-V04/S0118/I01, S0123–0129/I01; REL-0020, REL-0023; INF-0020; JPS-0014; CLM-0021.)

When Yuma feels Hotaru has two-timed her, she inwardly recognizes a parallel to her own earlier treatment of Takeda. That is ethically consequential perspective-taking, but the spoken apology does not give Takeda its complete cause. He comforts someone visibly distressed without receiving her newly named Hotaru recipient. His renewal therefore emerges within another asymmetry, not after a fully informed clearing of the past. The scene permits sincere concern and a wish to regain romance together. It depicts no explicit care-for-dating price; vulnerable timing and his initiated embrace prevent a frictionless account of pure disinterested aid. (NTR-JP-V05/S0147–0153/I01; REL-0026, REL-0027; CON-0030; INF-0027; AGY-0023.)

## Distance protects a person without entitling them to affection

Takeda's request for distance is an exercise of his own boundary under the evidence available: Yuma seems to force herself and conceal something. He explicitly says accepting his confession before clubmates does not bind her to continue, asks for honest reconsideration, and offers to wait. His later refusal of their morning practice changes access to an old couple routine. It is not shown as an ultimatum requiring intimacy, and the disappointment is not itself punitive pressure. He has a right to reconsider his participation even without the secret's entire truth. (NTR-JP-V02/S0088–0092/I01, S0138–0140/I01; REL-0007; CON-0007; AGY-0006; JPS-0007.)

The changed Hotaru assurance matters causally. Her café story says the persistent admirer has given up and Yuma likes Takeda; his later invitations cannot be treated as decisions under unchanged suspicion. The source confirms managed truth/lie representation without naming the false clause. Yuma subsequently says they have broken up, and their ordinary conversation improves. Neither Takeda's renewed hope nor useful assistance establishes that the couple has resumed. The earlier T-R02 forecast has no diagnostic unchanged-assurance opportunity here. An analysis that rewards every later friendly exchange as successful reconciliation would reproduce the same confusion between a ritual and its relational meaning. (NTR-JP-V03/S0021–0035/I01, S0056–0057/I01, S0093–0107/I01; INF-0011, INF-0012; REL-0014, REL-0015; CLM-0010/R-V03.)

The infirmary encounter changes his knowledge again. He sees the girls' position, helps Yuma afterward, tries privately to dismiss its meaning, then asks Fujiwara about it and remains uncertain. He does not see every preceding act or receive a whole-history confession. Helping does not prove that his pre-discovery information state has somehow returned; the T-R03 school-care antecedent cannot be established by the helpful response it was intended to predict. The observed relation is more interesting than a rule hit: Takeda can act considerately while uncertain about information that may hurt him. His imperfect inference remains part of that conduct. (NTR-JP-V03/S0155–0156/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0007–0009/I01, S0034–0036/I01, S0117–0118/I01; INF-0015, INF-0016; REL-0021; AGY-0017; CLM-0015/R-V04.)

## Help can serve Yuma's choice without giving Takeda the choice he wants

Notes, preparatory-school application help, consultation, fever assistance, ordinary meals, and a walk give the postdating relation practical substance. Takeda's continuing desire does not make each helpful act covertly conditional, and his helpful acts do not entitle him to renewal. This distinction is particularly clear in the beach conversation. Yuma's worry leads him to name love and recommend asking the desired person's actual feelings. He admits he does not know the details. The advice gives her a procedure for escaping inference without requiring her to choose him. Ironically, he assists recognition of an attachment directed toward Hotaru while believing the beloved is another male. The advice is valuable because it concerns how another person can answer, not because he already possesses the answer. (NTR-JP-V03/S0056/I01, S0093–0097/I01, S0143–0145/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0075–0085/I01, S0090–0091/I01, S0118/I01; CON-0022; REL-0015, REL-0021; JPS-0012, JPS-0014; AGY-0013, AGY-0017.)

His male assumption is a source-backed limitation, not proof of stupidity or an omniscient denial of obvious love. The girls' public categories, Hotaru's selective assurance, Yuma's withholding, and Fujiwara's refusal of specific clarification supply a distorted but causally intelligible account. Takeda's worry about a bad man can be compassionate and mistaken simultaneously. In V05 it contributes to covert following that he himself calls wrong. The workplace glimpse becomes a false rescue premise: he has seen Yuma at the venue, not verified that she works there or was forced by a man. Self-condemnation shows awareness but does not reverse the intrusion. This counterevidence prevents earlier patience from becoming a universal ethic of respecting privacy. (NTR-JP-V04/S0090–0091/I01, S0117–0118/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0041–0056/I01, S0069–0070/I01; REL-0027; INF-0023; CON-0029; AGY-0021; CLM-0019/R-V05.)

Grades, university hopes, sports, meals, and cooking keep both people inside ordinary material lives. Takeda's declining mock result and hope for a shared university are represented concerns, while Fujiwara's total accusation that Yuma intentionally keeps him hoping is contested. Yuma's declaration that they properly broke up matters; his persistent hope cannot create an obligation that she never grants. Her care obligations concern truthful participation and responsibility for harm, not the production of his preferred feeling. The short domestic extra's self-made lunch supplies practical competence beside romantic disappointment; it is not an adult married future. V02's explicitly adult parallel roles are likewise excluded from the mainline outcome. (NTR-JP-V05/S0013–0014/I01, S0089–0095/I01, S0159–0162/I01; NTR-JP-V02/S0161–0166/I01; REL-0026, REL-0030; INF-0024; CAST-0010, CAST-0023; VIS-0012, VIS-0028.)

## Named recognition changes the account; it does not supply an unseen speech

V06 gives Takeda a photo and then Yuma's direct statement that Hotaru is the person she likes. She reports having been unable to answer the earlier renewal and describes this as her first genuine love. That is her retrospective self-understanding; it does not prove she never enjoyed or cared about Takeda. He acknowledges difficulty fully grasping the friendship/romance distinction while rejecting the suggestion that a person's love is strange. His recognition does not require universal identity expertise or disappearance of his own hurt. It is a response to the actual person and attachment he has now been told about. (NTR-JP-V06/S0057–0063/I01; REL-0032; INF-0032; JPS-0026, JPS-0027.)

Yuma later tells Hotaru she properly declined Takeda and will not again date irresponsibly while liking Hotaru. Together with the visible rooftop exchange and represented earlier delay, this supports a bounded refusal account and an explicit choice. The exact refusal wording to Takeda is not drawn. The analyst must not compose it or assign a precise reply sequence to him. Y-R06 gains only report-mediated bounded support; T-R05 gains no diagnostic opportunity because the Hotaru-recipient disclosure changes its literal information condition and the exact refusal-response ordering is not visible. Takeda's observed good recognition is important evidence in its own right, not a forecast success created by relaxing the freeze. (NTR-JP-V06/S0059/I01, S0066–0071/I01; AGY-0025; CLM-0029, CLM-0031.)

Takeda's rejection of Fujiwara's later beneficiary defense further clarifies what recognizing Yuma's choice is not. He does not accept humiliating image publication as a service that should make him happy. His rooftop punch and collar control remain actual violence, so his ethical role cannot be collapsed into impeccable behavior. Nevertheless, the rejection denies an instrumental view in which his heartbreak gives someone else permission to violate Yuma and Hotaru. It is compatible with letting a relationship end without treating another person's privacy as compensation for his loss. (NTR-JP-V06/S0009–0012/I01, S0130–0132/I01; CON-0033; AGY-0030; JPS-0024; CLM-0032.)

## Counterreadings and the final directional state

The strongest ideal-boy reading has considerable support: Takeda notices discomfort, permits reconsideration, offers practical aid, directs Yuma toward the other person's answer, recognizes Hotaru-directed love, and refuses Fujiwara's supposed benefit. Its limit is the leap from these acts to a person who never pressures, intrudes, or harms. The sudden kiss, secret following, initiated vulnerable comfort, renewal timing, and punch defeat that universality. The more bounded interpretation is a subject with demonstrated nontransactional care and recognition capacities whose anger, desire, and mistaken protection can still cross boundaries. (NTR-JP-V01/S0020–0024/I01; NTR-JP-V02/S0011–0013/I01, S0088–0092/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0078–0085/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0050–0056/I01, S0150–0153/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0009–0010/I01, S0061–0063/I01, S0132/I01; CON-0003, CON-0029, CON-0030, CON-0033; REL-0027; AGY-0030.)

The opposite reading makes Yuma an intentional user and Takeda a pathetic, disposable obstacle. Concealment and his recurring hope give that account force, especially after she inwardly knows Hotaru's identity. It fails to explain the actual pleasures, sincere wish to like him, gratitude, apology, chosen distance, and final refusal of irresponsible dating without redefining every contrary action as another fraud. Nor does it preserve the difference between his justified boundary and his continued aid. The corpus supports criticism of Yuma's misleading conduct and specific Takeda intrusions; it does not require erasing the relationship's real but insufficient affection. (NTR-JP-V01/S0045–0050/I01; NTR-JP-V02/S0050–0056/I01, S0088–0092/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0123–0129/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0148–0153/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0066–0071/I01; REL-0003, REL-0006, REL-0020, REL-0026, REL-0032; INF-0020, INF-0027; CLM-0021, CLM-0029.)

The embedded post-return school page gives Takeda another bounded state: he sees the girls happy and distinguishes his racing heart from romance. It cannot establish a new partner, fixed orientation, or complete future freedom from hurt. At the manga boundary there is no renewed Yuma–Takeda couple. Yuma has a more truthful recipient account and a reported refusal; Takeda recognizes that attachment without requiring care repayment. Their friendship's durable future remains unproved, but the ending no longer requires them to mistake appreciation for owed romance. (NTR-JP-V06/S0166/I01; REL-0037; JPS-0031; CAST-0028.)
