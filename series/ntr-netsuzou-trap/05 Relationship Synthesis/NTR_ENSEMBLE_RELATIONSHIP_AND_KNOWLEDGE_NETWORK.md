# Intimate knowledge, borrowed authority, and the audience

This canonical synthesis owns the ensemble's relational and epistemic topology, including the consequential Takeda–Fujiwara friendship. It does not substitute for the three major dyad studies or the information/exposure specialist. It integrates the complete six-volume readings, freezes, and eight ledgers against the committed role-gap audit. The coordinator inspected all 1,009 originals in strict order; this author has integrated that record and performed bounded original verification, without claiming an independent complete primary reread. Coordinates are exact Japanese-witness/one-based OPF spine/image routes. Local witnesses are verified; current Drive-byte equality remains unverified.

## The network turns unequal knowledge into disputed authority

The four-person arrangement is consequential because each relationship supplies a different route to knowledge, a different claim to act on another's behalf, and a different vulnerability to being misrepresented. Dating creates an ordinary expectation of sincerity without complete access. Friendship gives confidences and plausible advice without omniscience. Childhood intimacy gives practical knowledge and a compelling protector story without permanent investigative authority. A captured image gives transferable power without the depicted people's permission or an adequate account of their relationship. The governing conflict is therefore not simply which couple will replace which. It is who gets to define the relation, for whom, using what evidence, and with what cost to the subject whose choice is supposedly being clarified. (NTR-JP-V01/S0068–0084/I01; NTR-JP-V02/S0088–0092/I01, S0110–0117/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0117–0126/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0011/I01, S0131–0148/I01; REL-0005, REL-0007, REL-0011, REL-0023, REL-0036; INF-0003, INF-0007, INF-0020, INF-0035; AGY-0029, AGY-0030.)

Yuma and Hotaru are the central reciprocal attachment, but their secrecy is sustained inside other bonds rather than outside social life. Takeda's real dating pleasure and later practical care make the cost of Yuma's omission intelligible. Fujiwara's public position beside Hotaru and friendship with Takeda let him move information between audiences while retaining his own advantage. The two men accordingly do different work in the system. Takeda is a recipient of incomplete accounts who can offer useful recognition despite error; Fujiwara observes, retains, supplies selected truths, pressures, publishes, and defends his intervention. Neither is only a blockage to the girls, and neither can be described adequately by the other's function. (NTR-JP-V01/S0047–0054/I01; NTR-JP-V03/S0038–0040/I01, S0093–0107/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0078–0085/I01, S0123–0126/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0003–0011/I01, S0057–0063/I01; REL-0003, REL-0015, REL-0021, REL-0023, REL-0032; INF-0010, INF-0016, INF-0020, INF-0029, INF-0032; AGY-0013, AGY-0017, AGY-0030.)

## Takeda and Fujiwara: friendship as an uneven channel of advice

Takeda confides hurt and uncertainty to Fujiwara rather than possessing a complete case against Yuma. The missed date, unexplained phone companion, and inadequate explanation support his suspicion that she is forcing herself or hiding something; they do not tell him what occurred with Hotaru. Fujiwara's knowledge of that ignorance is explicit when he turns the girls' nondisclosure into a bargain. The friendship gives him both a report of Takeda's vulnerability and an audience to whom the girls fear disclosure. His power is not only photographic: the human route already exists before the transferable object is captured. (NTR-JP-V02/S0048–0049/I01, S0085–0092/I01, S0110–0117/I01, S0156–0158/I01; REL-0007, REL-0011; INF-0007, INF-0008; CON-0010, CON-0012; CLM-0003, CLM-0009.)

The bond also has an ordinary basis that makes advice plausible. School and prep routines, Fujiwara's academic reputation, and the cooking extra provide forms of competence and company beyond blackmail. These do not certify his ethical judgment. Rather, mundane credibility helps explain why Takeda can consult someone whose information management is harmful. The cooking extra is an undated/earlier domestic comedy, not a post-ending reconciliation; Takeda's educational forecast for Fujiwara remains Takeda's testimony. Neither competence nor familiarity supplies an unseen redeeming interior motive. (NTR-JP-V04/S0117–0122/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0159–0162/I01; REL-0021, REL-0030; CAST-0019; VIS-0028; CLM-0036.)

After seeing the girls in the infirmary, Takeda asks whether Hotaru was pressing Yuma. Later his explicit male-beloved assumption persists. Fujiwara's hinting and cynical interpretation can identify a real sincerity problem while withholding the fact needed to understand its actual recipient. Thus useful general advice and ethically inadequate disclosure coexist. Takeda, for his part, gives Yuma advice that is useful because it asks her to approach the person she likes; that procedure need not depend on a correct gender assumption. The network differentiates **a wrong premise that still permits another's choice** from **better information used to keep control of the audience**. (NTR-JP-V03/S0155–0156/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0034–0036/I01, S0078–0085/I01, S0117–0126/I01; INF-0015, INF-0016, INF-0020; REL-0021, REL-0023; AGY-0017; JPS-0014; CLM-0015/R-V04, CLM-0021.)

Their later disagreement is consequently more than Takeda learning that a friend has behaved badly. Fujiwara claims that revealing the girls will wake Takeda up. Takeda's opening punch and collar handling are coercive actions of his own; deletion following pressure cannot establish either man's general ethical disposition. But at the later free exchange, Takeda rejects the proposition that the act would make him happy. S0131 is Fujiwara's reference back to his earlier account, not a newly repeated full speech; S0132's happiness question and **アホ!** belong to Takeda, with no next Fujiwara reply. The supposed beneficiary refuses the authority claimed in his name. That refusal is independent evidence of changed recognition, while T-R05's old condition remains unestablished and F-R04's opening restraint is NDO before its later bounded support. Friendship repair, enduring estrangement, and a general conversion of either man are not shown. (NTR-JP-V06/S0009–0011/I01, S0031–0033/I01, S0131–0132/I01; CON-0033; INF-0031, INF-0035; AGY-0030; JPS-0024; CLM-0031, CLM-0032, CLM-0036.)

## Knowledge changes at different boundaries for different people

Possessing one relevant fact never means possessing the same story as the reader. Yuma knows her own omissions and attraction before either lover receives a named account. Hotaru knows actual shared acts and can direct explanations, but her private truth/lie thought does not identify which clause is false. Takeda sees increasingly consequential evidence without receiving the preceding history. Fujiwara knows more than he tells in particular situations, yet no source gives him all the girls' thoughts. The reader has a composite record that no subject inherits automatically. That distinction matters whenever someone explains, helps, punishes, or rescues another on the basis of apparent knowledge. ## The photograph changes the relation by changing its possible audience

The image matters as a portable fragment whose audience can be expanded without the girls' cooperation. Its depiction cannot establish a couple contract, every preceding act, or permission to publish. Fujiwara's control works first as private access pressure, then as retained possibility, and finally as public exposure. Those are separate mechanisms, with separate evidence thresholds and different effects on the network. Earlier failed conditional-demand forecasts cannot be repaired by treating eventual publication as simply the same predicted tactic. (NTR-JP-V02/S0110–0117/I01, S0156–0158/I01; NTR-JP-V04/S0019–0020/I01, S0145–0146/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0155–0156/I01; INF-0008, INF-0017, INF-0021, INF-0028; CON-0010, CON-0012, CON-0031; CLM-0009, CLM-0014/R-V04, CLM-0020/R-V05, CLM-0028.)

## Supporting nodes supply real conditions without becoming complete biographies

Parents connect care, housing, money, oversight, and partial understanding. Yuma's mother can provide meals and check school/prep claims, while the father-away account permits forms of Hotaru's everyday solitude. V06's father and prospective family changes make absence materially legible, but his confident assessment of Hotaru's grades and domestic competence conflicts with Yuma's lived knowledge. His knowledge is situated, not wholly worthless. Hotaru's anti-burden speech to him is also a claim addressed to a particular listener. Yuma's mother supplies a university-admission condition for proposed living arrangements; the ending does not show that condition fulfilled or shared housing completed. The parents therefore influence options and interpretations without supplying a final verdict on the girls' attachment. (NTR-JP-V02/S0051/I01, S0062–0063/I01; NTR-JP-V03/S0138–0142/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0072–0073/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0094–0105/I01, S0143–0144/I01; CAST-0010, CAST-0015, CAST-0030; INF-0006, INF-0013, INF-0033; AGY-0022, AGY-0029, AGY-0031.)

School peers are not one uniformly hostile public. Rumor and homophobic categorization occur, but classmates also defend Yuma against humiliating treatment and raise a police-response possibility. That proposal does not establish an actual police report or official intervention. Routine study, preparation, sports, notes, and subsequent welcomes preserve a social world beyond the exposure event. Teachers likewise have bounded functions: the future-survey reminder is not permission to read Hotaru's private answer; the final return brings an apology and admonition rather than a depicted graduation, credit settlement, or complete institutional response to exposure. Heterogeneous responses matter because public visibility changes risk without making all ordinary social support disappear. (NTR-JP-V05/S0019–0024/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0021/I01, S0033–0043/I01, S0166–0167/I01; CAST-0030, CAST-0031; INF-0022, INF-0030, INF-0031; AGY-0020, AGY-0031; REL-0037; JPS-0031.)

Workplaces widen the topology in another direction. Managers, clients, coworkers, promotional work, wages, and the relocated job provide interests that do not originate in romantic loyalty. Yuma can see things she interprets as danger or abandonment, and Takeda can misread her presence as coercion, while Hotaru has competence and a real economic motive for working. None of those facts grants a client, lover, or would-be rescuer blanket authority over a worker's body or schedule; no paid sexual-service conclusion follows. The final pull and missed work are material costs even though affection follows. Doorstep strangers can also refuse Yuma's questions without thereby becoming antagonists to the reunion. Social nodes have their own access limits, and persistence has costs borne by people beyond the pursuer. (NTR-JP-V03/S0069–0092/I01, S0125–0127/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0050–0056/I01, S0069–0076/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0120–0129/I01, S0137–0140/I01; CAST-0015; INF-0013, INF-0023, INF-0035; CON-0014, CON-0016, CON-0017, CON-0027, CON-0038; AGY-0011, AGY-0012, AGY-0028, AGY-0029.)

These supporting nodes justify social and material analysis, not invented independent diagnoses, biographies, intentions, or operational models. Maria remains an attributed contact/rumor; unidentified former partners remain unidentified; the brother's depicted receipt establishes audience access rather than a full storage practice. The four central people have enough repeated consequential decisions for differentiated treatment, but their thinner or thicker interior access must remain unequal. ## Recognition is a change in authority, not a full distribution of facts

A strong counterreading sees Fujiwara's publication as a necessary liberation: without exposure, evasive categories might have persisted, so the harmful informer seems to accomplish what kindness could not. The narrative gives the exposure real causal force. It moves private uncertainty into an audience crisis and precedes Yuma's clearer choice. But causal efficacy cannot establish the claimed beneficiary's desire for the service, nor consent to the method. Takeda explicitly refuses that benefit; Hotaru's humiliating defense then demonstrates that public pressure can produce another false account rather than immediate truth. The later confession has value because the girls must choose how to answer, not because publication has already chosen ethically for them. (NTR-JP-V05/S0155–0156/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0018–0021/I01, S0052–0088/I01, S0131–0132/I01; INF-0028, INF-0030, INF-0032, INF-0035; REL-0031, REL-0032, REL-0033; AGY-0025, AGY-0030; CLM-0028, CLM-0030, CLM-0036.)

The converse ideal-boy reading would make Takeda the network's unqualified ethical solution. His practical aid, useful advice under error, and recognition of a named Hotaru recipient oppose punitive ownership. His unauthorized following, false rescue belief, punch, and collar handling oppose a universal noncoercive characterization. The important achievement is narrower: he can know enough to respect Yuma's choice without acquiring her whole history or receiving romance as payment. Fujiwara can know more relevant facts and still use them inadequately. Their difference tests the assumption that greater knowledge alone produces a better relation. T-R05 remains NDO; the observed ethical change is not a retroactively validated forecast. (NTR-JP-V04/S0078–0085/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0050–0056/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0009–0010/I01, S0057–0063/I01; INF-0016, INF-0023, INF-0032; CON-0022, CON-0029, CON-0033; AGY-0017, AGY-0021, AGY-0030; JPS-0027; CLM-0019/R-V05, CLM-0031.)

Another objection is that a four-person map exaggerates a closed romantic system and neglects real social alternatives. The supporting-node evidence answers it without pretending to a comprehensive sociology. Housing conditions, parental availability, school expectations, labor, and ordinary friends repeatedly make particular choices possible or costly. They are not decorations. Yet the source does not warrant claiming every peer opinion, a complete institutional response, equal family support, or a stable future income. The network is deliberately bounded by observed and attributed access, not by the imagined completeness of the world outside its panels. (NTR-JP-V03/S0069–0092/I01, S0138–0142/I01; NTR-JP-V05/S0019–0024/I01; NTR-JP-V06/S0033–0043/I01, S0094–0105/I01, S0115–0140/I01, S0166–0167/I01; CAST-0015, CAST-0030, CAST-0031; INF-0013, INF-0022, INF-0033; AGY-0028, AGY-0029, AGY-0031; CLM-0035.)

## What the completed network actually resolves

At mainline **fin S0162**, the girls return together after reciprocal vulnerability and chosen staying gestures. The network has changed because Yuma has named the recipient to Takeda, Takeda recognizes that attachment, and the purported beneficiary has rejected Fujiwara's exposure justification. Present romance no longer needs the same male-recipient fiction. Hotaru's work/privacy objections still exist, Fujiwara's useful testimony does not redeem him, and Yuma's inward interpretation of trust does not become universally shared knowledge. The ending gives changed choice and present comfort rather than complete moral or informational settlement. (NTR-JP-V06/S0057–0066/I01, S0109–0111/I01, S0131–0140/I01, S0148–0162/I01; REL-0032, REL-0035, REL-0036; INF-0032, INF-0034, INF-0035; CON-0038, CON-0039; AGY-0030, AGY-0031; CLM-0033, CLM-0034, CLM-0035, CLM-0036.)

The embedded S0166 school return and S0167 notes/study/food/hug scenes are a distinct post-return lane. Takeda's heartbeat there is explicitly distinguished from romance; Fujiwara's silence is not a newly spoken redemption or counterargument. Their juxtaposition preserves the ordinary ensemble after the mainline choice without restoring the old couples or proving new ones. Graduation, university admission, completed cohabitation, full act-specific repair, lasting trust, formal H/F breakup, all-image erasure, and complete subject access to each other's history remain open. Those limits preserve the significance of a relation that can continue without becoming transparent or costless. (NTR-JP-V06/S0166–0167/I01; REL-0037; CAST-0031; JPS-0031; VIS-0035; CON-0039; CLM-0035.)
