## Thesis: an ordinary day tests what the investigation can see

The volume places Kazuya and Chizuru through three kinds of unusually close contact: a cramped room created by house-clearing, a public shopping trip with Mini, and a quiet unplanned walk at night. Kazuya repeatedly hopes each is a decisive romantic sign. The text supplies something narrower and more valuable: Chizuru asks Mini about his character, permits some company and care, sometimes blushes, and initiates a brief errand together. Her earlier account of her uncertain feelings is replayed through Kazuya's memory, not supplied as new access to her interior here. She still does not call the feeling love or agree that they are dating. The May 13 move supplies a practical endpoint that makes Kazuya's wish urgent without creating a deadline for her consent. The abrupt closing cut to a cafe shows Chizuru speaking with a pale-haired person about a belated White Day return; that person's identity and the conversation's consequences remain open at this boundary.

## Sequential account

### Testimony, moving, and the storeroom

The bath conversation continues as Mini tells Chizuru that Kazuya has always treated her seriously and that Mini wants her friend to be happy. Afterward Mini reports the exchange to Kazuya. His delight that Chizuru asked what sort of person he is is understandable but is his own hopeful inference. The next morning a moving truck arrives for the Ichinose house's accumulated family property. Chizuru enlists Kazuya in carrying boxes and storing items. Old appliances and clothes prompt practical decisions about what to keep, dispose of, or buy again; the objects make bereavement and the house's possible future concrete rather than only sentimental. When Chizuru asks where he went the previous day, Kazuya conceals an embarrassing adult-DVD errand. The exchange has awkwardness and a small information asymmetry, not an exposed romantic verdict.

Chizuru asks Kazuya to place boxes in her former room, surprising him with access to a personal space. The room contains school traces and belongings, but it is presently a storage site; access is purpose-bound. A neighbor opens the exterior storeroom door while they are inside; their efforts to avoid being seen, a toppled stack, and limited space bring Kazuya physically over Chizuru. He repeatedly worries about imposing or exploiting the moment. Chizuru tells him it is all right to keep still briefly until the neighbor leaves. This is a local, situational permission to avoid discovery and restore safety, not unlimited permission for a kiss or sexual contact. Both visibly blush; no kiss occurs. When they separate, Chizuru apologizes that the situation became awkward. Mini notices their odd state on return, but her quick suspicion is not a transcript of what happened. Chizuru later explains that her family and Kazuya's family are different people and that her earlier comments about his grandmother's generosity had been clumsy; Kazuya says he did not take offense. This small correction reveals self-scrutiny rather than proof of a settled feeling.

### Shopping: domestic resemblance, ordinary preferences, and the money limit

Chizuru proposes shopping for replacement goods and invites Kazuya and Mini. Mini calls the outing date-like; Kazuya feels that framing keenly. Chizuru's three-person invitation and practical errand are the stronger evidence. At an electronics store they compare televisions, room layout, screen size, cost, streaming, and whether to retain an old set. Chizuru sets a budget and explains why an expensive screen does not suit her resources. Mini pushes for upgrades, while Kazuya answers store questions and watches Chizuru's careful spending. Their movement through a shared living-room decision resembles couple shopping to him, but the household still includes Mini and remains temporary. Chizuru then proposes dining out together. She orders ordinary food, jokes, shares dishes, and thanks him when he fetches a drink. The point is an unpriced, pleasant mundane day, not a paid rental date or a declaration.

Mini's treatment of Chizuru's clothes, makeup, and chair-massage moment tests the observer's limits. Kazuya eroticizes Chizuru's appearance and blushes at body-conscious poses. Chizuru also directs choices: she changes posture, says no to impractical extras, chooses a smaller television, asks Kazuya to accompany or help at named points, and objects when Mini becomes intrusive. Body framing and Kazuya's heightened reaction cannot substitute for her speech. At lunch he thinks of the outing as their first ordinary shopping trip; by the end he notices how much he likes the Chizuru who is simply deciding what to buy and eat. He contrasts his earlier idealized picture with her unscripted comfort. This may indicate a more grounded attachment on his side, but it does not tell him her private classification.

The key interior passage is Kazuya's, not Chizuru's. He first wonders whether the easygoing Chizuru he sees is her real self, then corrects his earlier assumption that her reserve was an act. In a montage of earlier encounters, the page replays her previously spoken uncertainty about liking him and her concern about showing a complete self. Those embedded recollections are evidence of what she said earlier, not fresh V032 thoughts. His large declaration that he likes her is an unballooned internal conclusion as he watches her; she cannot be credited with hearing it. Her ordinary requests and reactions are still informative, but this passage mainly advances *his* interpretation of her. The restaurant and store remain public, with Mini often present; shopping does not become a private couple transaction.

### Spider, small labor, and a chosen night walk

Back at the family house, Chizuru asks Kazuya to deal with a spider. He initially mistakes the request for a grand romantic demand, then realizes it is a literal animal in a cupboard. He helps catch and release it, with both making a mess and bumping into each other in the cramped space. Chizuru's apology and Kazuya's assistance are specific care and practical cooperation. Her request is not made by presenting or redeeming the birthday service coupon. Kazuya is pleased that he could help, then checks his tendency to enlarge every moment into a romance signal.

Later that night Chizuru changes into casual clothes and glasses and asks Kazuya to accompany her to a convenience store. The walk is explicitly her initiative. She wants coffee and ordinary supplies; he is surprised by a nighttime invitation from her rather than Mini. They discuss small habits, including her grandmother's practice of sharing a sweet drink and the fear that some family routines may be lost. Chizuru offers him half of a small bottled treat. Kazuya thanks her and names his own preference without treating it as a claim on her. The conversation illustrates how familiar family memory can be carried into voluntary company. It is modest mutual pleasure. At the end they smile together under the night sky. The volume then cuts to a cafe conversation: Chizuru thanks a pale-haired companion for remembering a belated White Day return, and a nearby promotional image bears another person's name. The scene does not yet establish the companion's identity or how the meeting relates to Kazuya.

## Relationship, agency, information, and form deltas

The family house becomes a place of joint maintenance and shopping, not just granted shelter. Chizuru's old room and household goods carry family memory alongside disposal decisions. Mini provides testimony and organizes the outing, yet her labels and pressure remain fallible. Chizuru retains local authority over who helps, what to buy, how to handle the spider, and whether to invite Kazuya out. Kazuya's restraint in the storeroom and his willingness to perform small tasks are observable; his private hope remains distinct from Chizuru's unanswered inquiry. The closing cafe cut brings Chizuru into a separate conversation whose consequences are unresolved. Ruka and Mami do not appear or receive an update. The birthday coupon is not used.

The volume repeats cramped geometry: bath testimony gives way to stacked boxes, a storeroom where bodies cannot easily move, massage-chair frames, then a cupboard occupied by a spider. The repetition makes proximity comic and erotically charged while spoken limits keep its meaning local. A broader store floor and restaurant table offer the opposite scale, letting Chizuru's mundane preferences and budgeting become visible. The last moonlit walk returns to two-person space, but its quiet pace and family memory differ from the pressured storeroom. Kazuya's mid-volume recollection of Chizuru's earlier self-question puts his current interpretation in dialogue with her previously stated uncertainty, without giving him access to her present thoughts.

## Alternatives and uncertainty

Chizuru's embarrassment in the storeroom may indicate attraction; it also coexists with her stated uncertainty and practical reasons for the day. Her question about his character may be a serious method of inquiry and an attempt to gather social reassurance. The evidence supports the inquiry, not a result. The electronic purchase may become part of a future household, but V032 supplies no evidence that Kazuya stays beyond May 13. The night walk may be a trial of ordinary companionship, or simply an errand with someone she trusts; its initiative and enjoyment are certain, the motive is not. The cafe companion's identity and significance require the next volume; the closing panel alone cannot resolve them.
