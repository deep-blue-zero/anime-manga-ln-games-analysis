## Thesis: care becomes plural; classification remains singularly unresolved

The birthday does not simply provide a romantic trial for Kazuya. It places Chizuru among three distinguishable sources of recognition: the theater troupe acknowledges her professional presence, Sumi offers friendship and an undemanding private listener, and Kazuya and Mini make the inherited house a place in which someone waits for her return. These gestures contradict Kazuya's feared picture of complete isolation without proving that he alone answers Chizuru's loss. The volume gives Chizuru more voiced interior position than Kazuya's anxious inferences usually allow. With Sumi, she admits that Kazuya has supported her and that she cares about him, then explicitly withholds the word *love* because she does not know whether gratitude and being rescued are the same as romantic desire. Her investigation is thus serious but uncompleted. Mini later demands a simple progress reading, but Chizuru again distinguishes not disliking a person from liking them in the relevant sense. Meanwhile Kazuya treats an expiring month of residence as an urgent clock. The closing bath scene makes Chizuru's inquiry visible to Mini while leaving Kazuya outside that conversation.

## Sequential account

### Stage and birthday: Kazuya's focalization meets other witnesses

Kazuya attends Chizuru's stage performance on April 19. He sees her act among a troupe and admires her skill and professional force. The school-life role and ensemble setting prompt him to imagine the family and social world Chizuru lacks after Sayuri and Katsuhito, but that emotional interpretation is his, not a narrated report from Chizuru. A cast birthday surprise follows after the performance, with cake, colleagues, and photographs. Chizuru receives it warmly. Thus Kazuya's fear that she has no one to celebrate with is falsified in part even before the house gathering; the troupe's gesture is genuine, but neither a career outcome nor a substitute for her private grief. It also delays her return, creating a small timing problem for the people waiting at home.

Sumi arrives at the family house with a second cake. She had expected to visit Chizuru and is surprised to find Kazuya and Mini living there. Kazuya is acutely aware that a third party has learned a private housing fact, but her surprise is not Ruka's informed reaction or a public disclosure. The waiting residents store the cake, prepare a modest surprise, and make room for Sumi. When Chizuru returns shortly before midnight, she clarifies the earlier no-present request: she had told **Kazuya** not to buy a gift, not prohibited a birthday gathering or Sumi's care. This correction matters because Kazuya had hardened a practical request into a universal ban. Chizuru accepts the event, thanks them, and later eats Sumi's cake. The troupe and household celebrations are sequential and materially separate.

Sumi and Chizuru then speak alone. Sumi asks about her condition and the person who encouraged her after Sayuri's death, offers a hug, and does not force an answer. Chizuru acknowledges that Kazuya stayed with and helped her through loneliness, while she continues to ask whether what she feels is romantic liking. She says she cannot flee the question and intends to understand it. Her inability to name the feeling is not indifference, nor is the supportive history proof of a hidden settled confession. It is an explicit account of a genuine epistemic problem from the person whose feeling is under investigation. Sumi's listening is active care, not an authoritative diagnosis. Her own history of unreciprocated feeling is not converted into a license to decide for Chizuru.

Back at the table, Mini's improvised party sign and the four-person toast let Chizuru be a recipient rather than a performer or caregiver. A brief exchange about a birthday as a day to thank others connects her present life to the family photographs. She pauses at the household altar and the memory of her grandparents. Sumi gives her a fuzzy bear sweater, which Chizuru wears. Kazuya impulsively says she looks cute and becomes flustered; that exchange is not a dating agreement. Chizuru asks him about her performance and receives an earnest compliment that she stood out. This is meaningful support for her vocation, but the volume offers no external career evaluation or casting result.

Chizuru then explicitly makes space for a gift from Kazuya despite her earlier request. He offers improvised “I'll do anything for you” coupons. She chooses one sheet and keeps it. The gesture converts his anxious wish to give something into a token she can redeem later; it does not transfer unlimited control of him to her or demand emotional reciprocity. The exact service, if any, has not been selected. Sumi departs, and Chizuru and Kazuya exchange a quiet good night. The birthday resolves the local gift prohibition through Chizuru's own revised permission, while the larger relationship remains unsettled.

### After the birthday: Mini's metric versus the one-month clock

The following morning's ordinary greeting is enough to make Kazuya self-conscious. Alone, he counts roughly a week of cohabitation and fears time running out. His thoughts mix the real deadline with fantasies of intimacy, jealousy, and disappointment, none of which report Chizuru's choice. Mini asks whether there has been romantic “progress.” Kazuya says he cannot know what Chizuru's investigation has found and admits he has not demanded an answer. Mini's teasing reduction of progress to sexual and physical milestones is a character's intervention, not the analytical measure of their status. The house has generated expanded observation and a closing date; neither is a settled verdict. Kazuya names May 13 as the planned move to his next apartment, about three weeks away from the scene, while the provisional household may end before an answer is available.

Chizuru returns and Mini directly asks about progress. Chizuru explains that she has been busy with theater and daily life and has not yet reached a conclusion. Mini declares the shared house a rare opportunity, then suggests a bath together as an intrusive shortcut. The chapter moves from Kazuya's private panic to Chizuru's own viewpoint. At the bath, Mini enters while Chizuru is bathing. The unexpected nudity and confined space are deliberately comic and eroticized, but Mini's chosen entrance and Chizuru's objections keep consent and privacy legible. Their shared bath is **Mini and Chizuru**, not Kazuya and Chizuru. Mini's questions expose how eagerly an ally may press for an outcome on Kazuya's behalf.

Chizuru tells Mini that she appreciated Kazuya's kindness when she was bereaved and values the fact that he has thought seriously about her. She also refuses an easy equivalence: not hating someone is not identical to loving them. She says she understands the difficulty created by her imperfect state and is doing her best to find an answer. Mini presses whether there is any wish to kiss him again; Chizuru's response is embarrassed and bounded, not a declaration of couple status. Mini finally asks what Chizuru is investigating about a man who plainly values her. Chizuru's answer is not a numerical progress estimate. She wonders what sort of person he is from the person who has seen him — Mini — and invites a fuller account. The last narrative scene leaves that inquiry pending. No later-volume answer should be projected backward onto this exchange.

## Relationship, information, agency, and form deltas

Chizuru and Kazuya gain birthday and vocational intimacy, but neither uses the shared household to declare a partnership. Chizuru retains control over the meaning of her no-present request and over whether she receives a gift. Kazuya supplies support without a booking or payment, yet the service coupon creates an explicit future obligation rather than a covert claim on her answer. Sumi both learns a concealed housing fact and offers care to Chizuru independent of Kazuya's wishes. Mini is a household ally who can advocate and also overstep privacy; her interpretive confidence must not be confused with access to Chizuru's interior conclusion. Ruka and Mami are absent and cannot be assigned responses to information they have not been shown receiving.

The sequence is formally organized by **two birthdays and two kinds of spectatorship**: Chizuru is watched professionally onstage, then received as herself at home. Crescent moon and night-house panels connect the troupe celebration, Sumi's arrival, and the late toast; the altar reintroduces family absence without making the party a grief cure. The first-person exchange with Sumi interrupts Kazuya's fear-driven focalization and resists his binary answer. The later “progress” chapter deliberately juxtaposes a calendar deadline with Mini's provocative scoring of intimacy. Finally the comic bath framing risks sexualizing Chizuru's private inquiry, but the dialogue supplies a corrective: Chizuru names the difference between affection and love, refuses a shortcut, and seeks testimony about Kazuya's character.

## Alternatives and uncertainty

Chizuru's direct admission that Kazuya is important supports a more positive reading than mere politeness; it still does not support the stronger proposition that she has secretly reached an answer. Her request for Mini's perspective may be serious evidence gathering, a tactful deflection from an invasive conversation, or both. The pages support the request and the continuing uncertainty, not a settled motive. Kazuya's coupons may later facilitate care, flirtation, or conflict; V031 establishes only acceptance of one. May 13 gives a practical horizon, not a guaranteed day for confession, moving, or conclusion. The birthday party may comfort Chizuru, but her brief altar attention and her words to Sumi keep bereavement and desire analytically distinct.
