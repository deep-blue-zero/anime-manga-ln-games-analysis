## Volume thesis

Volume 1 builds its serial engine from failed endings. Kazuya repeatedly decides that the rental relationship is over, but each attempt to close it encounters an immediate social pressure or coincidence: his grandmother's hospitalization, the two grandmothers' friendship, a shared university, adjacent apartments, a home visit, and finally his friends and former girlfriend. His short-term lies prevent embarrassment or disappointment, then convert that avoided cost into a larger future obligation. The result is not simple stasis. Each recurrence adds an audience, a route of access, or a rule that did not exist before.

The transaction is equally not an authenticity switch. Payment gives Chizuru a script, a time limit, and grounds for refusal; it does not prove that every act inside the script is emotionally empty, and it does not grant Kazuya unlimited access. Her strongest through-line is controlled responsibility: she performs skillfully, states boundaries plainly, improvises when family welfare is implicated, and converts exceptional help into a bounded Wednesday arrangement. V001 does not establish romantic feeling on her side. It establishes chosen conduct whose motives remain partly professional, partly family-directed, and partly underdetermined.

Kazuya's interior access creates a complementary distortion. The manga shows his sexual fantasy, idealization, panic, and harsh self-description in detail, but his conduct is not exhausted by those accounts. He harasses Chizuru after blaming her for his own purchase, pressures her into renewed service, and lies reflexively; he also admits fault, tries to confess to Nagomi, and defends Chizuru from his friends even when doing so worsens his predicament. His recurrent error is not simply low confidence. Under shame, longing, or anticipated disappointment he substitutes an urgent story for a sustainable decision, then mistakes his own self-condemnation for a complete explanation of what he did.

## Causal and state deltas

### Kazuya

Kazuya moves from private heartbreak and paid substitution to a socially distributed false relationship. His knowledge improves in limited ways: he recognizes that Chizuru's labor has rules, that his first review was unfair, and that her university identity is vulnerable. His behavioral control does not keep pace. Anticipated disappointment repeatedly drives him to lie before he can tolerate a confession's immediate cost. His most consequential positive action is public defense of Chizuru, but even that act includes an unsupported claim about her feelings.

The gap between self-account and action is already diagnostic. He calls himself hopeless and assumes Chizuru's defense can only be pity, yet he can apologize, identify another person's mistreatment, and take a social risk. Conversely, his self-criticism does not reliably prevent repeated pressure on Chizuru. A model that treats low self-esteem as either moral innocence or total incapacity would miss both sides.

### Chizuru

Chizuru moves from one-off provider to a client-linked collaborator with overlapping university, family, hospital, and residential contexts. She repeatedly protects professional boundaries, but her chosen responses are sensitive to the beneficiary and audience. Nagomi's disclosed emotional stake changes her refusal into help; Sayuri's presence makes abrupt truth costly; Mami's public contempt brings direct intervention. The minimum warranted through-line is responsive responsibility plus strong compartmentalization. The volume provides too little private interior access to classify these acts as romance.

### Kazuya and Chizuru

The pair acquire candor, mutual identifying information, shared family stakes, a recurring Wednesday arrangement, and multiple public audiences. They do not acquire a mutually acknowledged private romance. Kazuya alternates between treating Chizuru as an ideal object and insisting her positive conduct is only paid performance. Chizuru alternates professional presentation with direct correction, but consistently denies unbounded access. Their relationship is closer in material opportunity and social consequence while remaining formally constrained and epistemically asymmetric.

### Mami

Mami re-enters after ending the relationship. She resumes intimacy-coded address and proximity, monitors the new girlfriend claim, and uses the drinking audience to narrate Kazuya as deficient. Her later approach prevents a simple reading of indifference, but V001 withholds whether the governing motive is jealousy, control, residual attachment, wounded status, amusement, or a mixture.

## Information, transaction, and agency findings

The girlfriend fiction expands through a specific chain: Kazuya's family, Sayuri, university friends, and Mami. Each new audience makes unilateral correction harder because disclosure would now revise several people's beliefs and expose Chizuru's work identity. The pair know the relationship is a service; the grandmothers and friends do not; Mami is shown only the public claim in V001.

Payment remains analytically decisive because it establishes duties and limits. It also coexists with conduct that is not reducible to a price line: listening through the wall, choosing to bring food, defending Kazuya's dignity, and calibrating the service to family need. Those actions cannot be priced into emotional proof. The correct conclusion is motive plurality and underdetermination, not that money makes care false or that care makes the transaction unreal.

Agency is distributed unevenly. Kazuya initiates the rentals, the first family lie, renewed requests, and the friends' disclosure. Chizuru initiates several boundary formulations, the apartment intervention, the Wednesday rules, and the public correction of Mami. Nagomi's expectations and Mami's social maneuvering exert pressure without eliminating either central character's choices.

## Manga form and written speech

The volume gives Kazuya dense interior narration, fantasies, sexual images, and comic deformation while rarely granting Chizuru comparable internal text. That asymmetry tempts an overconfident split between a fully known man and a mysterious woman. In fact, Kazuya's narration is itself evidence requiring correction against conduct, while Chizuru's observable actions remain substantial even when motive is not singularly accessible.

Persona shifts are drawn and written. Rental Chizuru uses inviting address, plans, handholding, and a soft social register; campus Chizuru uses subdued styling and sharper imperatives; private conflict brings direct accusation. These registers are contextually real. The manga does not authorize collapsing her into either the “perfect girlfriend” or the angry corrective voice.

Repeated accidental body contact and full-page beauty attention align the reader with Kazuya's arousal and with the reactions of male onlookers. The same pages often attach shame, panic, or comic punishment. That combination can make objectification part of the joke while continuing to provide the visual object. The effect is mixed rather than cleanly corrective.

The final DVD-rental page is a compact formal statement of seriality: the narrative has spent the volume trying to finish a purchased relation, then ends with the vocabulary of borrowing and “one more week.”

## Progress, recurrence, and counterreadings

The strongest progression is infrastructural rather than romantic. Compared with the first date, the pair now share secrets, physical proximity by residence, family obligations, a scheduled contact rule, and an expanding set of people who believe they are a couple. Those are durable constraints at the V001 boundary. Mutual romantic acknowledgment remains absent.

The repeated “this is over” movement is therefore an altered recurrence. The first closure leaves only a review; the next leaves hospital expectations; deleting the app leaves adjacent apartments; attempted confession at home leaves a weekly contract; the scheduled hospital visit leaves a friend-group fiction and contact with Mami. Each reset preserves an added dependency.

Three counterreadings remain live:

1. **Chizuru's care is entirely professional.** Her pride in satisfaction, payment rules, and girlfriend register support this. Against a reductive version, her response to overheard family meaning and her direct moral correction of Mami show active judgment rather than mechanical compliance. The evidence supports professionalism as a major motive, not a total explanation.
2. **Chizuru's exceptional actions already prove romance.** Beauty framing and Kazuya's reaction make this narratively available. Against it, the same actions have explicit professional, family, and fairness-based reasons, and she continues to narrow access. Romance remains possible but unestablished.
3. **Kazuya simply makes no progress.** He repeats lies and pressure, strongly supporting regression in honesty and boundary respect. Against a total no-change claim, he admits fault, learns the dual identity and service limits, attempts a confession, and protects Chizuru's dignity. The volume yields mixed domain-specific movement rather than one romance score.
