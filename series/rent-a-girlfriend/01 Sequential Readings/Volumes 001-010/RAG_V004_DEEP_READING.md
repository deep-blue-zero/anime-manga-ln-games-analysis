## Sequential account

### Satisfaction 24 — information symmetry does not remove leverage

Ruka explains that Kuribayashi hired her through a rental-girlfriend service. His apparent relationship therefore does not supply evidence of mutual private partnership. Kazuya and Ruka agree to monitor one another's handling of the central secret, but their positions are unequal: Ruka can expose Chizuru's work and Kazuya's public fiction.

Ruka appears at Kazuya's university, separates him from his friends, embraces him, and measures her pulse against his body. The reading remains below or near eighty. Kuribayashi discovers them together, and Chizuru later hears Ruka state her romantic interest. The episode shifts Ruka from an outside observer into an active participant who can invade the campus boundary.

### Satisfaction 25-27 — the real-girlfriend rule becomes a coerced trial relationship

Ruka learns that Kazuya and Chizuru live in adjacent apartments. She offers secrecy only on the condition that Kazuya date her, explaining that he is the first person who has driven her pulse above ninety and that she likes him. Chizuru initially doubts the declaration. Ruka enters Kazuya's room, treats Chizuru as a rival, and presses her claim in the very space that already joins the central pair's private lives.

Chizuru asks whether Kazuya likes Ruka. He says that Ruka is attractive but that he cannot honestly date someone whose feeling he does not share. Chizuru then applies the exit policy established in V003: a real girlfriend would let the pair end the rental arrangement and tell their grandmothers they broke up. Kazuya chooses to pursue Ruka because doing so may protect Chizuru and the secret. He apologizes and asks Ruka to date him.

Ruka correctly recognizes that Kazuya likes Chizuru. She cries when he denies it, then accepts a provisional or trial-girlfriend arrangement. Chizuru warns him that the arrangement remains provisional and that leaving Ruka uncontrolled at university would create further risk. A page fixes the transition at 17:29 on 2017-10-31: Kazuya acquires a provisional girlfriend.

This is neither an ordinary romantic beginning nor a wholly unilateral imposition. Ruka uses a disclosure threat to obtain access. Kazuya knowingly accepts her sincere feeling partly as an instrument for protecting Chizuru's privacy and preserving his own social fiction. Chizuru encourages the trial because it supplies an exit route and asks Kazuya to take Ruka seriously. Each has agency, and each choice is constrained by unequal information and incompatible goals.

### Satisfaction 28 — Ruka's pulse becomes a self-authentication system

At age nine, Ruka is told that her pulse is unusually low: adults average roughly seventy to eighty beats per minute, children around ninety, while hers is around sixty and does not rise above eighty. Exertion brings breathlessness, dizziness, and fainting. The represented medical response is medication and periodic examinations, with ordinary daily life otherwise permitted. The volume does not establish a more specific diagnosis.

Ruka experiences this condition as social and emotional estrangement. Other children become excited while she remains physiologically flat; she comes to describe herself as a robot. Hearing peers discuss first love and kissing gives her a possible test. She enters rental-girlfriend work in search of an event that will make her heart race.

Multiple clients produce readings in the mid-fifties. She endures objectifying attention and concludes that real love will not emerge from rental work, deciding to quit. Kazuya's desperate effort on behalf of Chizuru changes the result. His apology and willingness to abase himself over another rental girlfriend raise Ruka's pulse to ninety-one. She discloses her own rental status and cries with relief that she is not a robot.

The episode gives Ruka unusually direct interior history, but it also reveals a brittle inference: one bodily measurement becomes proof that Kazuya is her unique romantic answer. During the next month she messages frequently, takes him on weekly dates, and seeks repeated excitement. Kazuya loses sleep under the new demand. No further Chizuru meeting is shown before winter.

### Satisfaction 29 — reassurance and intrusion coexist

Ruka says she never intended to disclose the secret, understands that the relationship is a trial, and does not want to trouble him. She asks for a Saturday movie date before Christmas, and he agrees. Chizuru watches the encounter from a window.

Ruka's reassurance materially limits the harshest reading of her conduct: she does not simply seek public destruction. It does not erase the condition by which the relationship began or the ongoing campus pressure. Her words and conduct must remain in the same account.

### Satisfaction 29-31 — Kazuya's Christmas surveillance and revision

Chizuru's university friends discuss Christmas plans and partners; she says she has plans. Kazuya encounters a scarf associated with an earlier Christmas memory of Mami and notes that Mami's last message is already a month old. On December 24, he withdraws cash, recognizes the combined pressure of rental spending and living costs, and decides he needs a part-time job.

He sees Chizuru dressed for an outing and assumes she is working. When she meets an attractive young man, Umi, Kazuya follows them for hours without permission. He shadows their movie, meal, bookstore, and shopping. Each ambiguous action enters a romance narrative: a near embrace becomes intimacy, distance becomes emotional connection, and overheard praise appears to confirm that Chizuru is Umi's girlfriend.

The accumulation matters because the error is not a single mistaken glance. Kazuya repeatedly supplies the most threatening interpretation while withholding himself from direct inquiry. His surveillance violates privacy even though the conclusion later proves false.

Kazuya falls and exposes himself. Chizuru confronts him and asks why he is present and what he heard. Umi asks whether Kazuya is her boyfriend; she answers that he is an acquaintance from the same university. She then explains the day and a major withheld domain of her life. Umi is a colleague from acting school. Chizuru wants to become an actress; the school costs money, and rental work both funds her training and gives her performance practice. She helped Umi with shopping and script work, and his call concerned an acting connection and recommendation rather than a concealed relationship with her.

Kazuya revises his story. He apologizes for following her, accepts that she is free to have a boyfriend, recognizes that he is only a customer, and calls the acting ambition admirable. The apology does not undo the surveillance, but it demonstrates correction after disconfirming evidence.

Chizuru asks him to wait and gives him a Christmas present outside a booked date: a fish-patterned phone case chosen because his former case was damaged in Shimoda. She relates it to their shared secret and to help he has given her, then calls it a thank-you present. Kazuya cries because it is his first Christmas gift from a girl. The object is personalized through observed history and is not charged rental time. It creates a reciprocal, unpriced exchange while leaving private romantic motive unresolved.

### Satisfaction 32 — paid access carries an unpriced return gift

Kazuya studies the phone case and recognizes that it was chosen specifically for him. He decides to return the gesture and begins paid work at a karaoke venue, giving the project its first sustained employment evidence for him. He buys an aroma item as a return present and uses a rental booking to deliver it.

Chizuru resists treating the exchange as an ordinary expectation between provider and client and warns that personal gifts can look improper in the service context. She nevertheless turns to the unresolved trial relationship. She asks directly whether Kazuya likes Ruka. He says that he does not, while softening the answer by saying he does not dislike her. Chizuru explains that Ruka still treats her as a rival and that the trial exists for Kazuya, Chizuru, and their grandmothers. She urges him to take Ruka's feelings seriously, date with adult restraint, and remember that Chizuru herself is only a rental girlfriend.

The pair walk hand in hand under the booked service, drawing favorable public attention. The image resembles couple intimacy, but its governing condition remains a paid date. Kazuya privately realizes that Chizuru, rather than he, is directing the interaction.

After Chizuru leaves, Ruka appears and accuses Kazuya of dating Chizuru despite their promises. She claims his immediate time, cancels or displaces another plan, covers his eyes, and leads him into a private karaoke room. The volume ends as she removes her coat and stands before him. This is an escalation in setting and initiative; it does not establish consent to any sexual act, and no such act occurs within V004.
