## Sequential account

### Satisfaction 42-43 — Sumi's practice date requires active support

Sumi cannot conduct the booking as an ordinary self-directed provider. She struggles to speak, smile on cue, choose activities, and sustain conversation. Kazuya treats the encounter as practice rather than as a consumer evaluation. He changes the setting from conversation-heavy café time to bowling and other activities that reduce the need for speech, reassures her when attempts go badly, and notices her determination to change herself.

When adult men approach Sumi while Kazuya is away, he remembers that Chizuru entrusted the practice to him. He avoids a direct confrontation that could further frighten Sumi: he addresses her as though she has just returned to him, supplying a socially legible exit. Earlier, after an accidental fall exposes her skirt, he averts his gaze. These are bounded acts of protection and situational adaptation, not evidence that he owns or diagnoses her.

Sumi eventually takes Kazuya's hand, smiles, and audibly calls him by name when they part. The improvement is small but directly connected to the practice context and his cooperative conduct. The encounter therefore validates Chizuru's referral judgment and makes Kazuya a consequential training participant rather than an interchangeable customer.

Mami interrupts near the end of the date. Sumi immediately performs girlfriend closeness by holding Kazuya's arm, helping him protect the rental secret. Mami says she will keep quiet from the position of an ex-girlfriend, but she then watches Sumi and searches the rental site for “Sakurasawa Sumi.” The V005 observer cliffhanger becomes deliberate investigation.

### Satisfaction 44-45 — acting work creates a real endpoint

Kazuya again turns an ambiguous sight into a rival narrative when he sees Chizuru with a man and broods over his lack of standing. During the scheduled balcony conversation, however, Chizuru redirects attention to Sumi's progress and then raises a more consequential subject: acting work has advanced enough that her agency sees a path to further jobs, so she is considering leaving rental-girlfriend work.

The possible retirement makes the relationship's commercial support structure finite. Kazuya says she should pursue her dream and expresses gratitude for what she has already done. Internally, he cannot tell her that she is the person he wants. Chizuru says she will keep helping with his “girlfriend” problem until the end. The scene converts acting from a disclosed ambition into a scheduling, identity, and access constraint, a delayed practical consequence after the V005 short-horizon prediction failed.

The following day, a client using the name “MAYA” books Chizuru. The client is Mami. This turns Mami's investigation into platform-mediated access to Chizuru and places the former girlfriend inside the transactional frame she intends to interrogate.

### Satisfaction 46 — Kazuya nearly chooses Ruka as an answer to loss

Distressed by Chizuru's possible departure, Kazuya accepts an outing with Ruka and recognizes that she is sincere, attractive, and capable of making an ordinary boyfriend happy. His question is not whether Ruka has value but why he still cannot choose her. He concludes that he cannot keep depending on Chizuru and decides he must make an effort to like Ruka.

At the karaoke workplace, Kazuya brings Ruka into a private room and begins converting the trial relationship toward an official one. The action is partly responsible—he tries to stop leaving her in suspension—and partly avoidant because it is triggered by anticipated loss of Chizuru rather than newly discovered reciprocal feeling. Chizuru and Mami happen to occupy another room. Kazuya hears Chizuru singing, recognizes the collision, and abandons the conversation. Ruka finds him outside and resumes the unfinished question, but their manager calls them back to work. Her coworker position creates both private access and a monitoring opportunity, although she does not learn the full Mami-Chizuru context.

### Satisfaction 47-48 — Mami buys access, then attacks the underlying relation

Mami pays for Chizuru's time and initially keeps the exchange inside ordinary client procedure: she asks about songs, the work, and Kazuya. Chizuru follows the customer's preferences while pressing for the real reason she was booked. Mami eventually says that she does not intend to report or condemn the occupation itself. Her target is the continuing performance around Kazuya: taking his money, smiling, and calling herself his girlfriend despite not loving him.

Kazuya overhears enough to understand that Mami is blaming Chizuru for a deception he initiated and sustained. He leaves work under a false illness excuse and runs after them. The attempt is belated and still uses another lie, but it is directed toward accepting responsibility rather than leaving Chizuru to absorb the accusation.

Before he can intervene, Chizuru asks Mami what she thinks of Kazuya. She reports that Kazuya spoke of Mami as someone he sincerely loved and could not easily forget. When Mami tries to leave, Chizuru stops her by the wrist and argues that another person's romantic feeling is precious and difficult. She asks whether Mami ever seriously faced or tried to accept Kazuya's feelings, and whether she considered that he might make her happy. Mami is visibly affected but says that the matter belongs to her and Kazuya.

Chizuru's intervention exceeds neutral platform defense. It remains compatible with professional ethics, empathy, guilt about the false relation, and personal attachment. The volume does not give her a direct romantic self-report and does not authorize a single-motive reading.

### Satisfaction 49 — gratitude becomes an explicit preference

After Mami leaves, Kazuya tells Chizuru that he saw the exchange. She objects to his eavesdropping, then apologizes that she could not turn Mami back into his girlfriend. Kazuya rejects the apology and thanks her: the problem was never something rental work alone could solve, and she treated his feelings seriously even though he has repeatedly caused trouble.

This produces a clearer internal conclusion than the prior cycles of attraction and idealization. Kazuya acknowledges that Chizuru's appearance matters to him, but says that even after subtracting those features he still likes her. Her conduct toward other people's feelings, her work, and her repeated care have become part of the object of attachment.

When Chizuru begins to leave, Kazuya says that if she were his girlfriend he would be happy, then calls after her that she is the one he wants. Chizuru turns back in surprise. The volume ends before her interpretation or response. The act is an explicit preference statement, but the pronoun-centered phrasing, his earlier talk about Mami, and the interrupted endpoint leave the immediate relationship transition unresolved.

## Causal and state deltas

### Kazuya

Kazuya successfully adapts to Sumi's needs and later runs to accept responsibility when Mami blames Chizuru. His attempted formalization with Ruka shows a damaging version of responsibility: he tries to choose a willing partner because losing Chizuru seems inevitable, not because his feeling has changed. By the final chapter he can distinguish Chizuru's appearance from the ethical and interpersonal qualities he values, and he communicates an explicit preference. Whether Chizuru understands that statement as romantic and whether he can act consistently with it remain open.

### Chizuru

Chizuru's acting ambition becomes a practical exit condition for rental work. Her referral of Kazuya produces a successful practice context for Sumi, validating her trust judgment. When Mami attacks the relation, Chizuru defends the seriousness of Kazuya's feeling with unusual intensity and physical interruption, yet continues to frame herself as someone helping him obtain another girlfriend. Her public professionalism, private care, and self-protective indirection remain jointly necessary.

### Ruka

Ruka offers an affectionate ordinary date and receives Kazuya's near-attempt to make the relationship official. She does not coerce that attempt in the scene, but the proposal is driven by his fear of losing Chizuru and remains unfinished. Her workplace position creates access and lets her detect unexplained behavior, though it does not yet give her the Mami-Chizuru content. The provisional relationship is therefore neither ended nor honestly converted.

### Mami and Sumi

Mami converts a visual clue into platform research and then paid access to Chizuru. Her accusation correctly identifies the false girlfriend performance but assigns responsibility selectively. Chizuru's counterargument visibly affects her without revealing motive. Sumi demonstrates effort, responsive learning, and protective performance under severe communication difficulty; one date is insufficient for a stable reconstruction model.
