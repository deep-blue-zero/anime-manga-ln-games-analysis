## Sequential account

### Satisfaction 95-98 — Sumi turns a planned date into emotional support

Sumi deliberately chooses the dolphin-show splash zone, and both participants are soaked. Kazuya gives her his outer shirt to reduce embarrassment from wet clothing. He thinks that family circumstances prevented similar outings when he was young and experiences the aquarium as belated youthful pleasure. Sumi says that she likes him, but Kazuya does not receive the confession; when he later asks what she said, she redirects to fishing as the next activity.

The date continues through fishing, where Sumi catches a fish and treats a small cut on Kazuya's finger. She gives him a belated birthday present, a summer knit cap. Kazuya explicitly compares her current ability to say happy birthday with the girl who initially could not greet him. Sumi then uses a phone memo to ask whether something happened and asks him to tell her about the wall he faces.

Kazuya describes the crisis indirectly as an acquaintance whose ill grandmother may not have long. He explains that the woman has only that grandmother as blood family, that she works relentlessly toward the dream of appearing in a film for her, and that he feels useless because time may expire first. His account also describes Chizuru as someone who stayed near him through his own pain and became reassuring enough to resemble family.

Sumi cries with him, says she understands, tells him not to worry because she is there too, and holds his hand. The narration distinguishes the grip from a handshake and treats shared grief as possible even without kinship. At departure, Kazuya says that speaking made him feel lighter and calls the result the best comfort. A station advertisement for renting films gives him a practical association: relying on another person can matter, and he may have something he can do for Chizuru. Sumi does not prescribe the later film plan; her listening and shared affect make action thinkable.

### Satisfaction 99 — Chizuru's work system meets another rejection

Chizuru visits Sayuri at the hospital before acting school and asks her to use LINE if she needs anything. The chapter maps the labor behind Chizuru's composed surface: all acting classes, morning and evening hospital visits, troupe rehearsal, repeated auditions, weekend rental work, tuition and living costs, food management, and study during service downtime. Only a small fraction of students enter the agency, and auditions require skill, luck, and timing to align.

Sayuri's appetite has declined. Chizuru has understood since the diagnosis that gaining a film role in time would be difficult, so she has tried to leave no effort unfinished. She then receives another rejection from the film *Koi Kyun*, answers that she will try again, and privately asks what more she can do. The question leads into the history of her acting goal.

### Satisfaction 100-101 — Sayuri's screen legacy and Katsuhito's death make acting a promise

In Chizuru's first year of middle school, she lives with Sayuri and her grandfather Katsuhito. Katsuhito insists that dreams come true if a person strongly believes and continues acting. Chizuru challenges his optimism with historical counterexamples and calls it superstition. She already loves films and novels, but a rediscovered recording changes the abstraction: Sayuri had performed under the name Sayuri Otori and had been celebrated for commanding screen work before leaving the industry.

Chizuru's admiration becomes a declared ambition to become an actress like her grandmother. Sayuri cautions that the industry is difficult. Katsuhito immediately says that Chizuru can succeed, asks her to put the dream into words, promises full support, and says that he will see her on film beside Sayuri. Chizuru begins saving for acting classes and wants both grandparents to see her in a proper movie.

Katsuhito is later struck while driving his taxi when another vehicle enters against the light. Doctors warn that severe brain damage makes renewed consciousness unlikely. Chizuru runs an ohyakudo shrine circuit because Sayuri had told her that overflowing feeling could reach the gods. She insists that crying at the hospital cannot help him and tries to make the promise effective through action.

Katsuhito briefly repeats the assurance that a dream will come true if she wishes, then dies. The event joins encouragement to bereavement: Chizuru accuses him of lying, invokes her name's connection to the wish-granting thousand cranes, and continues to demand that he see her become an actress. In the present, after the new rejection, she asks his memorial photograph whether the promise was a lie. She rejects the conclusion that dreams do not come true and continues working.

### Satisfaction 102-103 — Kazuya proposes a crowdfunded film and Chizuru accepts

Kazuya knocks on Chizuru's door and asks her to hear a plan. He has researched crowdfunding as a way to raise money for an independently produced film starring her. He argues that a film can be made even now, that a modest independent production can be shot in roughly a month, and that a cinema screen can be rented so Sayuri can see Chizuru on it. He connects the plan to his business-administration background and volunteers to raise the funds.

Chizuru asks about cost and time. Kazuya continues researching rather than treating desire as sufficient: independent production costs, personnel and editing, the scale of public campaigns, a sample target of 2,618,500 yen, observed success rates, roughly a month and a half for fundraising, and another month for filming and editing. The figures make the plan risky but not obviously impossible.

Chizuru initially asks for time to think. She returns to ask whether he can really do it and says that she does not want to waste Sayuri's remaining time. Kazuya answers that abandoning the attempt because it might be impossible would make failure certain. Chizuru then says that if he will not give up midway, she wants the film now and asks him to make it.

Kazuya says he will do it. His closing promise is to continue to the end no matter what happens. The agreement converts previously stated vocational support into a joint, high-cost production project. It does not correct the family deception, settle romantic status, or prove that the film will be completed.
