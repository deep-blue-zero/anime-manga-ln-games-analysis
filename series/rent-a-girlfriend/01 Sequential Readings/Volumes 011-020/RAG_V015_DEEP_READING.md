## Sequential account

### Satisfaction 122 — disclosure produces response without settling classification

Mini tells Chizuru plainly that Kazuya likes her as a woman. Chizuru blushes but argues that the film can be explained by gratitude, acting, and Sayuri, and that the rental arrangement remains temporary and rule-bound. Mini asks what Chizuru herself thinks and argues that admiration alone does not explain Kazuya's effort. Her confidence is an interpretation, not Chizuru's report or a mutual agreement.

Kazuya arrives after the exchange. Chizuru treats him more coldly and directs him to pursue platform recommendation; alone, she continues handling one of the selected reward objects. Mini later confirms to Kazuya that she disclosed his preference. He fears the trust relation has been damaged, while Mini says Chizuru may not understand her own feelings. The disclosure therefore changes information and conduct, but the volume does not validate Mini's certainty as fact.

### Satisfaction 123-124 — final-day strategy joins reach, rivalry, and project solidarity

With the July 28 deadline approaching, Chizuru identifies Umi's roughly 230,000 social-media followers as a possible source of reach. An actor's cancellation has opened a place at a play; she can study the performance and may obtain campaign promotion from Umi. Kazuya worries that the invitation is conditional or romantic. Chizuru identifies Umi as an acting colleague of about a year and rejects the insinuation.

Ruka evaluates the campaign need and proposes that she, Kazuya, and Mini distribute flyers while Chizuru attends the play. She also suppresses Kazuya's jealous speculation and says the group should do what it can for Sayuri. Her rivalry remains active, but in this decision it is subordinated to project welfare. On the final day she performs the public labor rather than merely endorsing it.

### Satisfaction 125-127 — Umi's aid and romantic probe produce a qualified answer

After the play, Umi asks about the film. Chizuru describes Kazuya as puzzling but emphasizes that his belief and effort pulled her out of a low period and gave her a way to act for Sayuri. Umi praises Chizuru's sincerity and then reveals that he broke up with Nanaho about a week earlier. He invites Chizuru to dinner and turns the conversation toward Kazuya.

Chizuru refuses dinner because the campaign's final day matters and Kazuya has worked hard for her. Umi repeatedly asks whether she likes Kazuya. She first says `好きじゃない` and then qualifies it as `でも 好きじゃなくもない`: she does not like him, but it is not that she does not like him. The paired statements disallow both a flat declaration of love and a flat declaration of emotional absence. Umi's interaction combines useful promotion with romantic self-positioning after his breakup; the evidence supports mixed motives without establishing coercion or a bargain.

Umi promotes the campaign to his large audience. Visible responses identify the campaign as belonging to his acquaintance, and Chizuru returns to flyer work rather than accepting dinner. The promotion becomes one contributing reach event, although the volume does not isolate how much money came from Umi rather than other final-day tactics.

### Satisfaction 127-128 — the threshold funds production under exact monetary distinctions

The campaign reaches its 1,820,000-yen target and closes at 1,850,000 yen, 30,000 yen over target. After platform fees, the transfer is 1,535,500 yen. Kazuya's displayed bank balance becomes 1,849,829 yen because the transfer is added to a prior 314,329-yen balance; that account balance is not the campaign total. The revised strategy therefore yields measurable reach and an all-or-nothing funding result, while attribution among flyers, rewards, platform activity, Umi's post, and other support remains underdetermined.

Kazuya tells his university friends that the campaign succeeded. Their teasing gives way to practical advice that failure after serious effort can still matter and that he should try. Summer vacation begins, moving the project from fundraising into production.

### Satisfaction 129-130 — specialist-led filming makes the producer role operational

By August 17 roughly half a month of summer vacation and shooting has passed. A set of about twenty cast and crew operates under Tabuse's direction. Supporting performers come from the film club and acquaintances; locations, schedules, equipment, food, drinks, and numerous small tasks are coordinated. Chizuru introduces herself as lead Ichinose and commits to the role. Kazuya formally introduces himself as producer and promises to complete the film, receiving the crew's acknowledgment.

The role does not make him the principal artistic expert. He assembles tripods, carries supplies, fetches food and drinks, helps move equipment, and follows requests while Tabuse directs and the crew supplies specialist capacity. Chizuru studies a heavily marked script, responds to direction, and performs with an intensity that Kazuya experiences as distinct from her ordinary presence. She also insists that producer and actress maintain enough distance for her concentration. Kazuya accepts that limit and treats the set as an important stage for her rather than a private route to closeness.

Near sunset, cicadas repeatedly contaminate sound for a continuity-sensitive scene. With no alternate location and little light remaining, Kazuya climbs the bridge structure to drive one away. He nearly falls, is recovered, and returns wrapped in a towel; the crew immediately completes scene 138 while conditions hold. Chizuru's crying performance moves Kazuya and visibly affects crew members. Kazuya imagines that she may become a famous actress and privately wonders where he will be then. The ending joins a real production gain to unresolved status anxiety: his work helps create the performance, but it supplies no claim on her future.
