## Sequential account

### Satisfaction 113-115 — launch traction recruits a creator-strategist

Sayuri thinks about Chizuru's proposed film and invokes Katsuhito. The campaign meanwhile moves from zero to 118,000 yen from thirty-three backers, then to roughly 150,000 yen in its first day. Chizuru welcomes the result but warns that the script and the rest of production remain a mountain. Kazuya begins searching, while Chizuru supplies a web-novel submission route and says an acquaintance who writes may help.

Mini brings Kazuya into her creator workspace after an accidental fall. She explains her cosplay, streaming, and social-media practice. After hearing a bounded account of the rental relation and film, she insists that Kazuya plainly likes Chizuru, asks him to call her master, and commits to supporting him. She presents online reach as useful to crowdfunding rather than merely declaring sympathy. Chizuru overhears balcony conversation and privately wonders whether Kazuya and Mini are dating, showing that third-party relationship interpretation can move in both directions without establishing fact.

### Satisfaction 116-117 — text selection and persistent outreach secure specialists

Kazuya reads large numbers of web-novel submissions and selects Rie Itohara's `群青の星座`, a coming-of-age story whose nineteen-year-old protagonist reminds him of Chizuru. Chizuru independently reads it and agrees. Kazuya writes the author, identifies himself and the project, reports the campaign state and Sayuri deadline, and asks permission to adapt and discuss the work.

Permission is secured. Kazuya and Chizuru meet film-club contact Tabuse, who has assembled former colleagues after Kazuya contacted him repeatedly for roughly three weeks. Kazuya bows and says he must do something for Chizuru. Tabuse agrees to direct, explaining that he wanted to meet the actress who inspired such effort. Script and director are now assigned, but the campaign sits near 837,000 yen from 328 supporters with fourteen days remaining and no support in the latest twenty-four hours.

### Satisfaction 118-119 — a funding stall produces analysis and collective strategy

Kazuya first treats the gap as a personal cash problem, even calling home after rent leaves him unable to cover it. Mini diagnoses a common crowdfunding pattern: early support followed by a plateau. Drawing on earlier campaign experience, she requests analytics, studies the response, and convenes Kazuya, Chizuru, and Ruka. The all-or-nothing rule makes the stall decisive because missing the target by even one yen yields no funds.

Mini identifies sparse progress updates and a narrow reward structure as correctable causes. She proposes production reports, location and test-shoot news, more reward tiers, platform recommendation, broad promotion, and Chizuru-linked items such as costumes and signed scripts. Chizuru accepts the search for objects. Ruka says she will do anything for Kazuya, while Mini keeps rivalry subordinate to the shared goal. The resulting "203 strategy" distributes promotion, reward design, and flyer work across the group.

### Satisfaction 120 — public outreach turns attachment into costly labor

Kazuya distributes 500 color flyers in Shinjuku at a direct cost of 18,500 yen plus production expense and suffers repeated rejection. Ruka arrives with a sign explaining that the heroine is an acquaintance. Kazuya says he did not want to burden her with work for Chizuru, but also explains that for Chizuru he can continue even when exhausted and rejected. Ruka calls the statement embarrassing, then joins the work. Alone with strangers, she apologizes for the interruption and asks support for "our" project. Her rivalry remains active, yet it coexists with public, costly help.

### Satisfaction 121 — calibrated exposure meets a private limit and a direct romantic claim

In room 204, Chizuru, Mini, and Ruka search for campaign rewards. Mini treats each personal object as a distinct funding tier; the group gathers roughly fifty clothes, accessories, and ordinary items. Chizuru permits substantial project-linked exposure but physically stops Mini at the dresser area containing underwear. Project need expands access without dissolving her authority over intimate boundaries.

Mini asks whether Chizuru has a boyfriend and how she feels about Kazuya. Chizuru calls him kind and says she deeply appreciates the film, but classifies that gratitude through her work as an actress and says romance is an entirely different matter. Elsewhere, platform staff connect recommendation eligibility to page views and demonstrated results while Kazuya continues flyer distribution. During reward photography, Mini then tells Chizuru directly that Kazuya likes her. The volume ends on that disclosure before Chizuru's response.
