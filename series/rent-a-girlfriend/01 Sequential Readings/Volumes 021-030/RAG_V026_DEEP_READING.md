## Sequential account

### Mami converts the screen display into a staged public accusation

Ruka recognizes the device as her phone, and Mami says that she dropped it. Ruka first tries to explain the profile as a joke image made by friends. Mami uses the phone to surface the service page and asks whether Chizuru is a rental girlfriend. Kazuya recognizes that the visible site makes simple denial untenable and tries to move Chizuru away from the audience. Kazuo then raises the money exchange he previously witnessed, adding a financial-exploitation suspicion to the service evidence.

Mami kneels and apologizes to both principals, saying that she cannot keep hiding the situation. Nagomi stops the surrounding speculation and asks Kazuya directly whether Chizuru is a rental girlfriend and therefore not his real partner. Kazuya prepares to confess and accept responsibility while Mami's performed reluctance supplies the frame for the accusation that follows.

Mami's account mixes true facts, interested interpretation, and strategic omission. She says that she repeatedly warned the pair, frames Chizuru as treating harm as permissible because the arrangement was work, invokes Nagomi's wish for an heir, cites money and the inherited ring, and argues that Chizuru had no intention to marry. She then invokes Sayuri's death, saying that Chizuru deceived her own grandmother through the end. The performance casts Mami as a reluctant whistleblower while concealing her responsibility for the phone drop and her prior conditional bargain with Chizuru.

### Family and peer accountability divide over what the visible evidence proves

Chizuru remembers Sayuri's trust and future-oriented family hopes as the accusation lands. Kibe points to the inherited ring and the pair's repeated closeness as reasons the situation may not reduce to a simple paid encounter. He then punches Kazuya. Kuribayashi restrains him while resort guests watch. The violence is consequential conduct, not a valid truth test.

Nagomi says that she can bear her own disappointment but cannot ignore possible dishonor to Sayuri. She states that Sayuri believed in Chizuru and asks whether any explanation can make Mami's account a misunderstanding. Kazuya concludes that no truthful account can protect Chizuru from the accumulated deception. He therefore begins what the narration calls a final bluff: Chizuru is not a bad person, and he will protect her.

### Kazuya combines a fabricated dating timeline with a real public confession

Kazuya claims that the rental arrangement became a genuine relationship before Sayuri died. Kibe warns that a new lie would end everything between them. Kazuya continues anyway, takes all blame for the deception, and proposes that the pair can later present a breakup if necessary. Within that fabricated status account, he publicly says that he loves Chizuru Mizuhara.

The confession is emotionally sincere and directly identifies its object. It is also embedded inside a protective lie about when and whether a mutual relationship began. Mami answers by demanding that they kiss in front of everyone. The demand turns intimate conduct into compelled evidence for a public tribunal rather than a freely chosen private response.

### Ruka resists Mami's verification regime without relinquishing her own attachment

The proof demand prompts a concentrated return to Ruka's history: she experiences Kazuya as the person who made her emotionally and physically responsive, knows that he loves Chizuru, and remembers his promise to end the conflict without making anyone unhappy. In the present she recognizes the phone event and timing as deliberate Mami action. Letting the exposure destroy Kazuya and Chizuru would benefit her stated romantic objective, but she cannot accept that route without cost.

Ruka apologizes to Kazuya and tells the group that if the two say they are dating, that should be enough. She does not withdraw her own claim or become indifferent. Her intervention is a self-costly rejection of Mami's public proof regime, followed by continued attachment and an unresolved angry or stricken look at the volume endpoint.

### Kibe's trust ethic becomes another coercive demand before Kazuya gives up the cover

A Kuribayashi and Kibe memory supplies the principle behind Kibe's response: trusting someone includes accepting the possibility of being deceived, and Kibe wants friendship with a person capable of that trust. In the crisis, however, he tells Kuribayashi to stop defusing the scene and insists that a fifteen-month lie would destroy his understanding of Kazuya. He asks for proof and says that he has never thought Kazuya a liar.

Kazuya finally apologizes, calls the entire arrangement his fault, and relinquishes the bluff. Chizuru considers the harm to Nagomi, Kibe, Kuribayashi, Ruka, Mami, and Kazuya. She then steps forward, cups Kazuya's face, and initiates a prolonged kiss. Kazuya has just confessed love, but he gives no contemporaneous verbal assent and is visibly stunned. Chizuru chooses the act within intense public pressure created by Mami's demand and Kibe's enforcement.

### Chizuru discloses her identity and makes a public status claim

After the first kiss, Chizuru bows and tells the group that she is a rental girlfriend. She identifies Mizuhara Chizuru as a professional false name and gives her real name as Ichinose Chizuru. She says that Kazuya began as a customer, that he repeatedly helped her, and that she came to know his character and care about him. She then declares that she is now genuinely Kazuya's girlfriend.

The speech contains major truthful disclosures and an emotionally supported account of increasing personal importance. Its final status label remains part of the emergency cover. The volume later makes that limit explicit: when the pair privately explain the history to Nagomi, narration says that the one lie they preserve is that they are really dating.

### Mami demands repetition, and Chizuru supplies a second visible kiss

Mami claims that she could not see whether the first kiss was real and raises the possibility of performance or misdirection. She demands another visible act. Chizuru takes Kazuya by the hand, repositions him, and initiates a second kiss before the group. The repetition makes the physical contact unmistakable, while also intensifying the coercive context. Kazuya remains surprised rather than an active initiator.

The two kisses are real, deliberate conduct initiated by Chizuru. They show that she will use intimate action to protect Kazuya, preserve access to his family, and oppose Mami's framing. They do not establish a mutually agreed private relationship because both occur under crisis verification pressure and the subsequent narration identifies real dating as the remaining lie.

### Near-complete disclosure preserves one status lie and restores family access

Resort staff end the public disturbance, and Nagomi tells Kazuya and Chizuru to change and report to room 8504. Over roughly two hours, the pair explain almost the entire rental history, including the film and Sayuri's final period. Chizuru speaks as truthfully as the retained cover allows, while Kazuya sometimes struggles over which facts can be shared. The narration specifies the residual falsehood: they leave only the claim that they are actually dating.

Nagomi accepts the account, apologizes to Chizuru, and treats her as substantially exonerated. The acceptance follows sustained explanation, not the kiss alone. The inherited ring is discussed but not observably returned. Kazuo physically strikes Kazuya after the explanation; the family then plans further communication and dinner. The group leaves the room, while Ruka's hard stare at Kazuya and Chizuru closes the volume. No private romantic debrief, mutual reclassification, ring transfer, or workable Ruka separation occurs.
