## Sequential account

### A family portrait and an accidental close encounter

Chizuru identifies the second altar portrait as her mother, who died when Chizuru was four. She has few surviving photographs because her mother disliked being photographed. The physical photograph matters as a retained object and an opening into early family loss. Chizuru speaks warmly of what she did receive; Kazuya privately wishes to support and hold her, but he does not enact the imagined embrace. A later earthquake tremor destabilizes stored household material. Kazuya shields Chizuru, and they fall close. He imagines a possible kiss, but she notes a leaf on him and withdraws. This is safety-caused contact, not negotiated intimacy.

Outside, Chizuru says she would like a marriage in which the partners can laugh together soon after hardship. Kazuya enthusiastically agrees and imagines himself beside her. The ideal is hypothetical, and she makes no dating commitment. They then discover their old apartment building under emergency access restrictions following the quake. Its residents cannot simply return to the adjacent rooms. Essential objects can be retrieved, but ordinary neighborly access is lost.

### Displacement makes the childhood house a practical option

Chizuru returns to Sayuri's empty house, Kazuya to his family home, and Mini arranges temporary shelter. Kazuya's limited cash and the earliest prospective apartment availability around May 19 make an immediate independent replacement difficult. Chizuru's solitary time at the childhood house recalls Sayuri's living presence, making its vacancy emotionally and practically consequential. Mini proposes that the two principals share the house while Chizuru investigates her feeling. Kazuya worries about imposing on her and about Ruka's still-disputed claim. Mini's confident reading of Chizuru is an intermediary inference, not her answer.

Mini and Kazuya drink at an izakaya. He cries over the loss of proximity and Mini contacts Chizuru, who arrives and drinks with them. Mini raises cohabitation as both shelter and ordinary observation. Chizuru says that a man and woman living under one roof is no small matter; Mini suggests being a third housemate. Kazuya confesses love and support while visibly drunk. Chizuru acknowledges that she can feel lonely and miss him, yet refuses to reduce her still-uncertain feeling to a settled label. She worries about misleading him. Mini suggests another kiss as a test and offers herself as a demonstration; Chizuru stops that suggestion. Once Mini goes to call a taxi, Chizuru asks whether another kiss with Kazuya might help her understand. Kazuya says he likes her. They approach, but Mini and the taxi interrupt. No completed kiss is shown, and Chizuru later acknowledges that she drank too much. The drunken episode should be held apart from her subsequent sober decision.

### Sober consent creates a bounded three-person household

The taxi takes them to separate destinations. At his family home, Kazuya encounters Nagomi, whose wish to see the apparently genuine couple continues under the residual false dating premise. He wakes confused about the prior night. Chizuru then comes to him and invites him to her grandmother's house. There she offers a separate spare room for **one month**, citing housing scarcity, cost, and the available space. She discusses ordinary shared use and practical living expenses, while refusing to treat cohabitation as automatic romance. Kazuya remains anxious about Ruka, rejection, and the investigation. Chizuru also invites Mini, who accepts. This is a sober, limited offer of shelter and observation, not a mutual dating agreement or permanent residence. The volume ends as the household begins; actual operating rules, Ruka's knowledge, the answer to the investigation, the house's eventual sale, the ring, and full correction of the family lie remain open.
