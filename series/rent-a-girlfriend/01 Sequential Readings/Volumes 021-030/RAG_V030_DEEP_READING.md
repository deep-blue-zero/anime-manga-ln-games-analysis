## Sequential account

### Entering an actual household, with unequal knowledge outside it

Kazuya temporarily returns to his family home before moving into Chizuru's inherited house. Ruka learns that he left the damaged apartment, but he says he is staying with his family and conceals his destination. Her upset and affectionate insistence do not settle the provisional trial. Nagomi likewise treats Chizuru as his genuine girlfriend and wonders why they are not living together; he does not correct her. At university Kibe suggests sharing an apartment to halve rent, unaware that Kazuya has a different shared-house plan. These are distinct uninformed audiences. At the house Chizuru's ordinary welcome and Kazuya's reply initiate domestic access, not a new romantic status.

Chizuru shows him a separate room and bedding. She calls the arrangement one month and three people, discusses common areas, washing, meals, bath sequence, expenses, and consideration for each other, and gives him a key. Her practical generosity is real, but she does not offer unlimited access or a dating answer. Mini arrives as the third resident. The shared bath remains temporally separated: Chizuru, Kazuya, and Mini negotiate use, while much of the apparent erotic encounter is Kazuya's fantasy. At the sink, a brief phrase about liking conscientious people is not a confession of liking Kazuya. They brush their teeth in proximity without changing their official relationship. The house makes proximity routine and requires restraint as well as access.

Chizuru's interaction with a familiar neighborhood cat, Peter, exposes a quiet pattern of care associated with Sayuri's home. Kazuya observes and speculates about loneliness. The cat's behavior and his interpretation do not provide a direct answer from Chizuru about her feeling for him. Her stated need to observe him under ordinary conditions remains active, though this volume does not stage a completed sober kiss test or final classification.

### Birthday boundary and Mami's renewed access

Kazuya discovers that Chizuru's birthday is April 19, only three days after the current April 16. He wants to acknowledge it. Chizuru says he need not prepare a present, noting the forced move and his scarce funds; she does not bar all conversation, companionship, or care. Kazuya hears the request as a hard prohibition and worries about making her uncomfortable. The subsequent timing matters: he has a housing key, but not permission to turn every household occasion into romantic entitlement.

Mami sends a message and arranges a meeting in public. Kazuya expects possible anger about the Paradise exposure and their renewed proximity. They take tea, and he apologizes for the lies and trouble that affected her. Mami asks what he means and observes him, but cuts the encounter short rather than resolving her purpose or the history between them. Kazuya remains unsure how to interpret her gentler presentation. The reader cannot assign her a settled romantic, hostile, or altruistic endpoint from this meeting.

### Sumi's care gives the birthday question a second frame

Sumi arrives unexpectedly at the house, aware of Chizuru's impending birthday, while Chizuru is out. Kazuya cuts a finger during his room chores and apologizes for leaving Sumi waiting. She calmly treats and bandages the cut, then listens to his worry that Chizuru's refusal of presents makes any celebration improper. Sumi's quiet invitation to think about Chizuru's actual wishes reframes a modest acknowledgment as possible without overriding Chizuru's stated limit. Kazuya decides he wants to do something within it. The chapter does not show him giving a birthday present or Chizuru accepting one. Closing birthday imagery and the next-volume teaser cannot be treated as a completed event.
