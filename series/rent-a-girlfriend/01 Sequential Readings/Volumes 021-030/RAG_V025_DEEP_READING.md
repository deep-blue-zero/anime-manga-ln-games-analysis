## Sequential account

### The chapel attempt becomes an interruption rather than a completed confession

Mami tells Chizuru that she overheard the morning discussion that cast Chizuru as a victim and offers help if Chizuru cannot continue the lie. Chizuru tells Kazuya by message that she may be at her limit. When he reaches her near the chapel, he says that he does not expect forgiveness and asks for time to speak. Chizuru tries to leave, and Kazuya catches her wrist while insisting that their conversation is not finished. He begins to state what he wants to say, but Mami's message that she has arrived interrupts the attempt. Chizuru apologizes and runs before Kazuya completes a proposition or receives an answer.

The flight is an observable consequence to the V024 initiation, but its meaning is not mutually agreed. Chizuru is responding to Mami's arrival and disclosure pressure. Kazuya lacks that information and later treats her departure as romantic rejection.

### Mami converts rescue language into a coercive disclosure plan

Mami tells Chizuru that Nagomi appears to know about the morning lie and instructs her to bring the ring to room 8504. In the lobby, she repeatedly calls Chizuru a victim and Kazuya the wrongdoer. Chizuru raises money, booking, hotel, and concealment complications, but Mami treats those objections as delay rather than a basis for choice. She produces an envelope or document, says that she is not asking for Chizuru's opinion, and frames the proposed route as freedom. Her request to “rent” Chizuru for the disclosure recasts commercial language as control over Chizuru's participation.

The intervention is materially family-facing even before a completed disclosure: it specifies Nagomi as the audience, the inherited ring as evidence, a room destination, a written instrument, and an immediate timetable. The sequence supports concern and coercion at once; claimed rescue does not eliminate Chizuru's refusal rights.

### Mami's history explains a represented hostility toward romantic belief without excusing present conduct

The volume supplies a concentrated retrospective account of Mami's childhood. Her father controls possessions, schooling, and future marriage, and adults connect her to a wealthy family's son. A relationship with Tarou becomes a private source of hope and imagined independence. After her father orders the relation ended because she is expected to marry Hakuba, Tarou sends a breakup message saying that he likes someone else. Mami narrates herself as lacking the knowledge, ability, and courage to abandon the stable life her parents supply.

She describes the event as the death of a tiny “baby” inside her. In context this is a metaphor for an inner child, hope, or possible future; the volume does not establish a literal pregnancy. Her retrospective conclusion is that labels such as romance and love are social decoration and that she wants to destroy couples she sees performing them. This history supplies direct represented motives and beliefs, but it neither makes every public claim insincere nor absolves the coercive plan directed at Chizuru.

### Kuribayashi and Ruka replace an assumed grievance with direct clarification

While Mami leads Chizuru toward the planned meeting, Kuribayashi speaks directly with Ruka. Ruka anticipates blame or a complaint about their former rental arrangement. Kuribayashi instead says that seeing her made him happy, that he worried after she disappeared from the service, and that he is not angry. He says that he should be ashamed of trying to impress others without a real girlfriend. The exchange revises the grievance Ruka expected and gives their prior client-provider relation a bounded, unmediated clarification. It does not establish romance, reconciliation, or a continuing personal arrangement.

### Chizuru rejects Mami's method and defends agency inside an acknowledged deception

Mami displays the inherited ring, asks whether Chizuru has tried it, and insults Nagomi's family expectations. Chizuru first demands that Mami retract the insult. She then acknowledges her own failure to end the lie, but argues that Kazuya retains the freedom to choose when he lies and when he corrects it. Her defense recalls his attempts to tell the truth, his care for Sayuri's screening, and his effort to protect her feelings. She asks whether Nagomi's smile, although produced inside the deception, can still be real.

Mami answers that a lie is wrong in itself and argues that delayed truth will eventually hurt Nagomi. When Mami tries to continue toward the room, Chizuru pulls away, says that she cannot go, and repeatedly bows while asking for another chance to speak. The refusal is substantive: Chizuru rejects Mami's unilateral timing and method while accepting responsibility for the underlying concealment. It is not a complete defense of the lie or a completed correction.

### A fragile truce preserves the cover while Kazuya mistakes flight for rejection

Mami agrees to “forget” the matter after Chizuru's repeated request, but sets conditions. Chizuru must smooth over Nagomi's concern, send Mami the exact cover account so their stories align, and keep the exchange from Kazuya to avoid his resentment. Chizuru later tells Mami that she described the issue to Nagomi as a resolved financial dispute between friends. The bargain prevents the planned room confrontation but deepens coordinated concealment and Mami's information advantage.

Kazuya, unaware of that exchange, treats Chizuru's apology and flight as rejection. He decides that he should accept Ruka and leave the Chizuru route, then experiences an extended sequence of sexual and romantic jealousy images involving imagined other men. Those scenes are his intrusive fantasy, not events involving Chizuru. He breaks down crying in the pool. Ruka's physical affection and direct “like” occur during this state, but Kazuya supplies no reciprocal status decision.

When Chizuru arrives, Kazuya withdraws the chapel subject before she can explain, apologizes for bothering her, and renews his promise to protect her. Chizuru repeats his earlier wish that Nagomi be allowed to keep dreaming and accepts the promise. She asks whether the Mami matter is safe, then conceals their actual confrontation in accordance with the truce. Their apparent reconciliation therefore rests on reciprocal care and asymmetric information rather than a completed romantic answer.

### Ordinary group closure gives way to public profile exposure

The group resumes pool activity and prepares to leave. Chizuru and Mami present a friendly surface, and Kazuya takes Chizuru's calm appearance as evidence that the situation is safe. He reflects on the eighteen months since meeting her and describes the trip as a final rental-girlfriend memory. The narrative supplies an explicit November 24 afternoon anchor: a caption shows 15:37, while the exposed phone later shows 15:35, so the endpoint is best treated as approximately 15:35-15:37 rather than assigned false minute precision.

As the group departs, Nagomi drops her phone. Kibe, Kuribayashi, Ruka, Chizuru, Kazuya, and others are positioned around the visible screen. Chizuru reacts in shock, and Mami gives a small smile. A full-page image then shows Chizuru's Diamond rental-girlfriend profile on Nagomi's phone. The smile and prior intervention make orchestration a strong inference, but V025 does not directly show the precise action by which the profile was opened or the phone was dropped. The volume ends before any observer explains, denies, or processes the display.
