## The initial block's contribution

The first ten volumes build a relationship system whose central fact is not simply that a paid girlfriend arrangement persists. Each attempted ending adds a new audience, obligation, information route, or material debt. The first family lie creates weekly paid visits; campus recognition creates identity compartmentalization; the Izu disclosure plan becomes reciprocal rescue; the real-girlfriend exit rule helps produce Ruka's provisional trial; acting work turns Kazuya's spending into vocational support; university and workplace collisions give Mami new evidence; and direct LINE access becomes useful only after first producing a boundary objection. Continuation therefore accumulates consequences even when the public girlfriend fiction repeats.

The block also separates forms that the characters repeatedly confuse. Payment defines access without deciding whether every act is emotionally empty. Family recognition raises obligations without making the represented couple real. A provisional girlfriend label creates responsibility without supplying reciprocal love or blanket consent. Gifts and care can be voluntary without functioning as status grants. The evidence is strongest when these distinctions are tested in conduct: Kazuya refuses Ruka's sexual pressure, Chizuru corrects accidental bodily contact during a paid date, and both women perform girlfriend-like actions under sharply different terms.

Formally, the manga turns interfaces and objects into causal devices. Platform profiles expose birthdays and providers; invoices make extension costs visible; gifts carry gratitude and triangle information; glasses and uniforms make identity presentation unstable; photo-booth instructions manufacture reproducible romantic images; and LINE moves a campus contact route into a family crisis. Cliffhangers repeatedly convert a proposed resolution into a new access problem: a ferry rescue, a hot-spring room, an apartment doorway, a train sighting, and finally a hospital message.

## Cumulative interpretation through V010

### Transaction, performance, and authenticity

The commercial relationship is neither a decorative premise nor a reliable authenticity detector. It creates enforceable limits, price signals, scheduling rules, privacy risks, and skilled labor. Within that structure, Chizuru can improvise for families, protect a client, coordinate a repair for Kuribayashi, refer Sumi for practice, discuss acting, and lead an intimate-looking dream date. Some actions remain fully service-explainable; others occur outside paid time. Neither category independently reveals romantic motive. The strongest cumulative conclusion is RAG-CLM-002 and RAG-CLM-024: classify the terms and observable agency first, then leave underdetermined feeling underdetermined.

### Deception as a social network

The originating lie survives because different audiences hold different fragments. Nagomi and Kibe treat Chizuru as Kazuya's private girlfriend. University friends know Ichinose and a partial rescue history. Kuribayashi learns the rental truth through a bounded repair. Ruka knows the central secret but seeks status inside it. Mami builds her map through observation, platform research, paid access, workplace inquiry, a train sighting, direct confrontation, and the Kinoshita family account. The deception is therefore an information network rather than one false sentence. Local truth can repair one relationship without dissolving the system, as Kuribayashi's case shows.

### Progress inside repetition

The block supports cyclical development with retained changes. Repeated dates, family performances, interrupted confessions, and rivalry scenes can look static at the level of official couple status. Underneath them, several changes persist: Kazuya recognizes his preference for Chizuru; begins paid work; completes a friend repair; refuses sex; links support to acting; and gains a direct contact route. Chizuru names acting as a goal; survives casting loss; gives a first-person family account; articulates pride in rental labor; receives another stage opportunity; and chooses unbooked care and family access. Ruka's trial relationship gains workplace, campus, domestic, overnight, and family contexts while its nonreciprocity becomes clearer. Mami's observer role becomes deliberate investigation.

These changes do not equal mature romance. Kazuya still uses concealment under family pressure and sometimes converts protection into avoidable cost. Chizuru still provides no romantic self-report and repeatedly constrains interpretation. Ruka's sincere feeling increasingly coexists with factual overstatement and unilateral physical escalation. Progress is domain-specific and can intensify the same unresolved structure.

### Agency and responsibility

Kazuya is not adequately described as either passive or steadily maturing. He can dive after Chizuru, work to fund repair, support Sumi adaptively, protect privacy, choose a bounded gift, and encourage acting. He can also surveil, pressure, conceal, overdrink, and defer resolution of the Ruka trial. Chizuru's strongest through-line is controlled responsibility: she protects work identity, adapts to audiences, chooses bounded exceptions, persists vocationally, and restores legible limits after help or closeness. Ruka has consistently high initiative, but initiative is not the same as consent-sensitive competence; she can stop or apologize locally while preserving a conversion goal that produces later pressure.

### Gaze, comedy, and bodily limits

Sexual focalization and embarrassment often enlarge Kazuya's interior reaction or stage intimacy-like images. The block repeatedly supplies corrective evidence in dialogue and action: refusal of a peer-demanded kiss, explicit provider rules, no sex during the storm overnight, immediate correction after the Ferris-wheel accident, and recoil from Ruka's later kiss. Visual erotic intensity cannot be treated as a completed act, mutual desire, or relationship agreement. Comedy often represents dysregulation and audience pressure, but does not erase the material burden placed on another person.
