# What it takes to share a stage

## The promise makes several people answerable

At the end of *SHINE POST*, Kyoka Tamaki describes to an audience what it had been possible for that audience to do. The people in Nakano Sunplaza helped keep TINGS in existence by choosing to come. She distinguishes them from the president who devised the opportunity, the manager and staff who supported the work, and the seniors whose demands strengthened it. These are several forms of responsibility. In the same episode Kyoka later declares she wants to take the Budokan engagement and asks her four colleagues whether they will join her. The order leaves their answers available to them. Her own certainty about wanting the job has become a basis for asking instead of an excuse to answer for the group.

That order has a history. TiNgS begins by hoping the right man will see enough in three girls to agree to manage them. It ends with five people entitled to ask one another whether they want to take their next job, in an office where the manager still asks how much harder they are prepared to work. Something more particular than a gain in confidence has happened. The ability to see someone’s work, the power to assign a role, and the standing to decide what one will attempt have gradually come apart. The distinction makes success possible without requiring that everyone in the room share the same motive. It also leaves room for the freshly repeated threat of dissolution to be troubling after the doors of Nakano have been filled. Evidence begins at E01 OBS 14–20 and ends at E12 OBS 17–19 and CLM 17, 19, and 39.
