## Individual record contract

Assign a stable `SPV2-CH-…` record only when actual evidence is entered. Preserve verified names/aliases and distinguish the source person from analysis-subject or dyad identities. Every material state entry links evidence/claim IDs and its source boundary.

| Field group | Required content |

| Boundary | Episode/presentation cutoff, reconstructed story time, continuity/witness and analyst exposure |
| Personal history | What the source establishes, when it happened, when it is disclosed and who knows it |
| Stable disposition | Repeated tendencies, values and priorities, with exceptions and alternatives |
| Developmental state | Current goals, self-understanding, skills, constraints and changes; separate development from later revelation |
| Situation | Stakes, mood, fatigue/pressure when evidenced, options and immediate context; no medical or diagnostic inference from mannerism |
| Knowledge and cognition | Beliefs, uncertainty, misunderstandings, attention/appraisal and limits on what the person can know |

| Ordinary life | Habits, preferences, amusements, comforts, teasing, routine cooperation and low-pressure choices actually shown |
| Voice and register | Japanese wording/register by interlocutor and state; separately linked heard delivery and performed/public voice |
| Capability | What the person can do and under what conditions; distinguish capacity, confidence, motivation and willingness |
| Relationships | Links to directed edges; retain how this person appraises/responds to each other person |
| Operational hypotheses | Conditional response families with inhibitors, overrides, uncertainty and disconfirmation tests; never a validated grade |
| Revision/dependencies | Evidence/claim IDs, prior state, reason for update and affected episodes, monographs or other outputs |

Unknown information stays explicit. Do not fill preferences, romantic attitudes, private habits or diagnostic labels from a template, genre expectations or another adaptation. An isolated choice may reflect necessity, accommodation or publicity rather than disposition. Keep the observation and interpretation separate.

## State changes and ordinary-life controls

Maintain a compact current view per person plus recoverable changes: prior state → new formulation, source boundary, evidence, counterevidence and linked claim transition. Preserve the original earlier reading. Record whether apparent change is development, context variation, strategic presentation, a new disclosure or unresolved.

Whenever a major dramatic pattern is proposed, seek a low-pressure control and a counterexample within the admitted source. Record unavailable controls as gaps. Do not invent missing scenes or let crisis behavior determine every everyday response. Stage, professional, peer, private and unfamiliar-person behavior may differ without contradiction.

The [relationship ledger](SHINEPOST_V2_RELATIONSHIP_LEDGER.md) owns edges. The [performance ledger](SHINEPOST_V2_PERFORMANCE_AND_FORM_LEDGER.md) owns AV event detail. The [evidence ledger](SHINEPOST_V2_EVIDENCE_AND_CLAIM_LEDGER.md) owns claim formulations and revisions. Link them rather than maintaining competing copies of their facts.

### SPV2-CH-HARU · Haru Nabatame, 青天国春

**Role, history and attainable aim.** Production name card f514 gives なばためはる. The orange-haired girl holding a pink lightstick at Hotaru's 2018 finale tentatively wonders about joining the profession. Nine months into TiNgS, it is possible both to be someone’s favorite and to be commercially precarious. [OBS 01–03, 18; CLM 01–03, 11; PF-E01-01/02.]

**Ordinary and professional conduct.** Braids, glasses, a school uniform, ice-pop sticks with winning messages and favorable traffic timing introduce a morning on which she makes chance events encouraging. Her delight in imagining costumes, a CD or TV appearance makes an office message appear more promising than it is. She enters twice to test unwelcome news, guesses a stranger's identity, runs ahead and has to double back verbally to introductions. She can give a welcoming experience and miss a listener's attempt to object in the same encounter. One good day is an inadequate sample for a universal cheerful disposition. [OBS 03–04, 08, 22; CLM 03, 08, 15; REL-HARU-NAOKI, REL-HARU-KYOKA.]

**Development, knowledge and limit.** She does not know the history of Naoki's earlier refusal. She ends as a member who has recruited a provisional manager, with both attendance tasks ahead. Foresight, technique, vocal superiority and an inability ever to lie remain unestablished. Hypothesis for later comparison: she can lower the cost of another person's choice once a direct appeal fails; her earlier rush shows that this accommodation is context-dependent. [OBS 13–22, 25; CLM 08, 10–13, 15.]

**What the first five could see.** Haru welcomes Yukine's suggestions, participates in the study and practice, helps credit the girl who led, and wants audiences everywhere to be able to declare their love for an idol. She keeps the full extent of her skill from ordinary group rehearsal. The strongest new visual evidence is the difference between her public practice and the movement Yukine and Momiji privately witness. Yukine's early characterization of a beginner inhibited by dancing before others is superseded by this observation and by Yuki's closing account of earlier professional membership. Haru neither tells Yukine she had never danced publicly nor tells her she had belonged to a prior group. She can sincerely value the friendships and the group's achievements without giving a full explanation of her experience. [22, 25–27, 29.]

**Present cutoff.** Tells people directly that doing this would hurt everyone. Rejects the idea of ever giving full effort as something it would be better to avoid. FRM 001177 and 001180 show her visibly distressed face, and FRM 001181 at 1194.000 adds gold during the latter assurance (#1122). The earlier statement about harm has a different cue. The image puts the categorical prescription under pressure while leaving the fear intelligible. Her upcoming regular live and need to learn a new song supply the stated reason to leave. She goes before the president tells the others about her previous membership. The HY:RAIN poster in the adjacent street shots offers a strong connection, without saying whom she hurt, whether the feared outcome ever occurred, or why the earlier membership ended. [28–29.]
