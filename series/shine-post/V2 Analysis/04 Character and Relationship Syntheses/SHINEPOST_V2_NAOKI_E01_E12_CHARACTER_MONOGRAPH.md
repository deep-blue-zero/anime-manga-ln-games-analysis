# Naoki Hinase and the work of making a place to perform

Naoki Hinase (日生直輝) once wanted people everywhere to see what idols could do. At the beginning of *SHINE POST* that ambition is one reason he thinks he ought to stay away from managing them. Earning bookings and advancing an agency's business made him valuable. Working so intensely also hurt people around him. At Brightest Production he is prepared to do errands and administration. When three girls welcome him as their dedicated manager, he has to explain that nobody secured his agreement. The professional quality that survives his refusal is his ability to take someone else's desire seriously enough to ask what would let that person act on it. The danger, which survives his return to management, is that he can be so sure he has found the right opportunity that he imposes it.

## What the eyes give him

An ordinary walk through town supplies the first evidence for Naoki's peculiar sight. A close view of his eye and a localized golden treatment mark people telling lies. He leaves those strangers alone. Later his older cousin Yuki, Brightest's president, explicitly names his ability. What he sees can call a statement into question. It cannot supply the true proposition, say why the speaker withheld it, or tell him whether confronting the person would help. On the phone he tells Kei 輝いてるね, “you're shining.” No visible caller or corresponding street signal accompanies the remark. Their practiced teasing suffices to explain it. Conversely, a face without the effect can matter to him in a particular exchange without making every unmarked sentence reliable testimony about the future. The artist's power to shine in front of an audience is a different use of the word again. (SPV2-OBS-E01-06, SPV2-OBS-E01-07, SPV2-OBS-E01-20, E01-phone.) ## A working day and a professional voice

In E02 the order to try hard sounds painfully inadequate to three people who already work hard. Naoki stays to observe what they are doing. He recognizes a possible public for them, calls his former colleague Eiko Kikuchi and gets permission for the girls to approach the audience outside a larger show. The girls have to make the approaches and face the new customers the following day. Naoki supplies access, a more receptive setting, and advice to Haru about where to direct her attention. そっち, “that way,” asks her to look at the people already watching them. A short instruction can work because someone prepared the conditions that make it meaningful. To Kikuchi, with whom he can tease an acquired verbal tic, he redirects credit for finding the girls to Yuki. He claims the job of pointing the way. He can value this unglamorous labor even while his interventions onstage become increasingly ambitious. (SPV2-OBS-E02-04, SPV2-OBS-E02-06, SPV2-OBS-E02-08, SPV2-OBS-E02-11.) ## Two assignments, two kinds of pressure

With Kyoka Tamaki, whose steadiness and attention to the whole room he values, he recognizes a problem before he knows its history. He sees the encounter with her fan Tokka, asks questions, and receives Yukine's account of the missed opening solo that Haru covered. He has positive evidence of a discrepancy when Kyoka disavows wanting the center. He also has her repeated request to ease off. ## A bounded expectation

Presented with persistent work that produces a poor public result, Naoki is likely to inspect the setting, use his contacts or change an assignment so the effort can reach somebody new. Presented with a confident claim at odds with the evidence, he may ask a short pointed question, arrange a practical test, or wait for a private conversation. His authority makes a consequential mistake possible precisely when he believes an opportunity is good for the girl. Kyoka's refusal, Rio's insistence on distance, and Haru's right to choose show three different boundaries he negotiates with uneven care. With a trusted peer, an elliptical question such as 書けたの？　新曲, asking Kei about her new song, better fits the situation than a formal instruction. Her reply doesn't tell him when or whether the song was delivered. His recurring willingness to be corrected matters as much as his certainty when he has it. An instance of his accurately naming an unreported private history from the cue alone would challenge the limits above.
