# Momiji Ito and the terms of coming back

Momiji Ito has been offered a melting ice cream by a girl trying to recruit her. It is in no condition to be served. She says so and goes right on listening to Rio Seibu. Would she come back to TINGS? Yes, that would be fine. Within minutes there is a recording of that answer, three girls holding on to her, and an excellent reason to let Momiji finish what she was saying. Returning on her own would leave Yukine Gionji alone. She has made a commitment about doing that. Asked if she would come with Yukine, Momiji is quite willing to entertain the possibility. She reaches for a polite expression and says something about mounted archery. The error is hers. The much more consequential error belongs to the three girls who believed a recorded yes satisfied every condition of having her back. (SPV2-OBS-E07-10/11/27/36, 933–1057.97 s, recorder at 981.5 s · SPV2-CLM-E07-11/27.)

伊藤紅葉, the I in the original five-person TINGS, is a Brightest trainee and subsequently a performing member. The agency’s undated recruitment listing gives August 15 as her birthday and fourteen as her age and praises her sharp dancing and cheerful disposition. When the group first appears in the story, Haru Nabatame, Kyoka Tamaki and Rio perform as TiNgS. Momiji works with Yukine in the duo Yukimoji, which has yet to debut. We later learn that Momiji coined that name while all five were still practicing together. She is the girl who notices what is out of step, whether a group is waiting too late to move to the music or a formidable actress has been playing the person who always knows what to do. Her judgments can be incisive. Getting someone to accept the help she thinks they need is another matter. (SPV2-OBS-E01-12, 842.20–856.34 s · SPV2-OBS-E02-23 · SPV2-OBS-E07-02/19 · SPV2-OBS-E08-04/06.)

## What a dance teacher can see

A short shot in E11 establishes that Momiji was at Hotaru’s 2018 Budokan show. In the native picture at 598.35–599.51 s she holds a pink light close to her cheek. The shot fits between two pictures retained in the sampled set. Unlike Yukine she gives us no account of going there to study, no specific memory of deciding to become an idol and no established acquaintance with the other future TINGS members on that day. Her personal starting point as a practitioner is much clearer. When President Yuki Hinase gives the original five their song and choreography, Yukine asks Momiji, the outstanding dancer in her estimation, for advice. Momiji demonstrates how to count the movement. The group has been waiting for the sound and coming in late. Yukine helps put the diagnosis into words and calls for another try. Haru credits Momiji. Someone else’s skill at explaining the problem can coexist with Momiji supplying the thing the others can actually try. The five will use this number, *Be Your Light!!*, on the first public stage they share. (SPV2-OBS-E11-10/57 · SPV2-OBS-E08-02/03/04/22, 147.55–353.54 s · SPV2-PF-E08-03 · SPV2-OBS-E09-09/17.)

Momiji toils at the back of an outdoor run and Yukine encourages her. Later Yukine credits her improved stamina. We see her stretch by herself; a training montage under Nanami later includes work under loads and upside down. One well-controlled movement cannot measure how long a girl can run. It is easier to see what Momiji knows when she watches someone else. Her first dance lesson for Rio in the series’ presentation order is an offer that Rio has every reason to experience as an insult. She calls her a poor dancer, and her exchange about which of them is the fool gets the terms backward. She looks to Yukine for approval and gets it. After a show she can remember Rio being late, which is a rival’s appraisal with a professional basis. A claim about a specific late entrance carries less weight as an overall ranking than the teacher who made it would like. Momiji’s attempt to sharpen a claim by replacing “conviction” with “confidence” rather blunts it. In the original five she already taught Rio. What needs rebuilding in the present is permission between two people who have become public rivals. (SPV2-OBS-E02-03/23, 206.26–315.87 s · SPV2-OBS-E03-05/25, 471.83–619.63 s · SPV2-OBS-E07-03/21/27 · SPV2-OBS-E08-04/15/25.)

Her wording changes more than the broad impression of a nickname for everybody suggests. At about 250.67 s in E02 the scoped listener hears 理王さん, Rio-san. The babyish 理王たん is a later form. With it Momiji can attach 下手っぴ, poor at this, and offer tuition, call after Rio in real alarm or congratulate her when she has done something splendid. The ending alone cannot decide between mockery and affection. Momiji likes expressions with a satisfying finish and reaches for them faster than she can check their meanings. E01 has the out-of-place いとおかし in the trainees’ exchange. E07 gives mounted archery, やぶさめ, for a form of willingness, やぶさか. E12 substitutes the old capital 平城京 for keeping one’s composure, 平常心. By contrast, the pupil who actually asks for dance lessons receives two entirely adequate syllables, いいよ. Momiji can be simple when the question has become simple enough to answer. These scenes establish recurring habits of address and comic phrasing. They leave the exact sound of unsampled private speech open. (E02-jp · SPV2-OBS-E01-12 · SPV2-OBS-E05-32 · SPV2-OBS-E06-32/39 · SPV2-OBS-E07-36 · SPV2-OBS-E12-06/33.)

## With Yukine before the rivalry

In an early rehearsal break Yukine is caught eating an ice cream by herself. Momiji does indeed want ice cream. She also wants to thank the person who has been coping with other people’s accidents and bad jokes as well as running rehearsals. It takes Yukine a moment to understand what she is being thanked for. Momiji recognizes the performance of coolness under the act of care. When Yukine admits she doubts she can offer the sort of cuteness she sees in the others, Momiji says the less guarded girl is cute too. Yukine entrusts her with the confidence and asks for help leading. Momiji makes the name ゆきもじ out of the two of them. She sees an opportunity to be useful to *this* girl in particular before there is a public duo or a rival to investigate. That is firmer evidence for the origin of their partnership than an explanation that makes Momiji a follower by disposition. A sweet reward and an excuse to sit together recur. What food she usually buys, how she dresses away from work and whom she lives with are largely unknown. (SPV2-OBS-E08-05/06/24, 359.17–561.74 s · SPV2-CLM-E08-06.)

Momiji sees something else she would prefer not to have noticed. In the locker room she starts, backs out, admits she actually has something important to say and tells Yukine that Haru has been holding back. Yukine doubts her and Momiji even suggests turning back before they have reached the place where they can check. The two see Haru dance alone through a doorway. They have a common sight there; the suspicion that led them to it came from Momiji. The earlier glance she gives along the line of faces during fireworks leaves the particular thing that tipped her off unknown. The observer who can notice a disparity before Yukine can is still a friend who would prefer to spare Yukine its consequences. The office discussion that follows is between President Yuki and these two girls. The president attributes Haru’s restraint to concern for them, a wording confirmed by scoped listening. Yukine voices resentment and a desire to see Haru give her best. Momiji participates in the plan without delivering a second copy of Yukine’s declaration. (SPV2-OBS-E08-10/11/13/26/27, 806.09–1032.97 s · SPV2-CLM-E08-10/14 · SPV2-XREV-E08-01, 987.5–988.6 s.)

Yuki proposes that the two split off and become rivals capable of making Haru believe TiNgS needs her full strength. The five could fail to reunite even if the gamble succeeds. Both go into it with that warning. Both undertake the training Yuki has prepared Nanami to give them, look on as the trio debuts and attend its shows. Momiji and Yukine had first learned to share a practice room crowded with other people’s needs. In E03 they have it to themselves and it feels too large. Momiji begins a question to Yukine that the returning trio interrupts. We never hear its end. When Yukine wants to help Kyoka in secret, Momiji intuits that part accurately and then repeats Yukine’s private laundry-tissue comparison in front of Kyoka herself. Keeping an important confidence at the bench and exposing a friend’s private joke in a crowd are both things she has done. The difference is a reason to ask how *she* sees the stakes of a confidence. (SPV2-OBS-E08-14/15, 1033.33–1152.07 s · SPV2-OBS-E03-05/06/25, especially 471.83–597.30 s and 620.46 s onward.)

## What Rio asks her for

E05 gives this young teacher time off the dance floor. Momiji is curious enough about Rio’s first television shoot to go to the farm. She says she lost sleep over it, traces her concern back to Rio’s ability and eventually admits she worries. She follows Rio toward the crew, sees her bow and finds an occasion to praise what Rio can do. At the break Momiji offers water. When the green-pepper tasting goes badly, she sits near Rio. Later she sees Rio distribute flyers away from the ticket table and make a case for coming to hear the other two girls. A rival can be there to see who succeeds and care about the person who does badly. The cost of the trip, her practical help and her willingness to stay for the discouraging part give care more support than a reading of simple professional surveillance. Rio never tells her the entire rule by which she has come to regard asking for help as selfish. Tracking somebody through a difficult day gives a friend opportunities to intervene and many opportunities to be wrong. (SPV2-OBS-E05-05/07/10/11/24/25/28/29/32, 549.84–704, 853.53–1010.41 and 1146–1281 s.)

When manager Naoki Hinase names Rio to center a nearly danceless song, Rio is horrified at what the others seem to have given up for her. Momiji runs after her. She takes Rio’s arm and urges her to return because the others will worry and 一人はダメ, being alone is no good. She tries to say why Rio is capable. Rio interrupts. Momiji proposes teaching her so they can surprise everyone together. The offer gives Rio a future she might actually want. The grip and the instruction to return push her toward a decision she has just refused. Rio gets free, leaving Momiji visibly distressed. Later that evening Rio goes to practice alone and finds Naoki waiting. That private conversation belongs to the two of them. The next meeting between the girls takes a different form. Rio holds out a Kantendō custard pudding with both hands, bows and asks whether Momiji will teach her to dance if the next live goes well. Momiji accepts. She had been willing to teach on the street without any such test. Rio is the one who brings the gift and needs the condition. Yukine can see the meeting from the doorway. (SPV2-OBS-E06-01/02/06/07/08/22/27/32, 202.13–291.65, 586.36–877.44 and 888–930.93 s · SPV2-CLM-E06-08.)

At the mall Momiji watches *Yellow Rose* from the gallery, urges Rio on and calls to her after the number. The performance places Rio’s lead before people who had expected another center; the documented hearing supports Rio’s dominance and leaves the source of the backing layer open. Momiji never finished explaining her faith in Rio on the street, so it would be an invention to say she predicted precisely what the singing would do. In E07 she watches Rio dance, awards a qualified あり, and explains the improvement through an elaborately unflattering scale made of varieties of yes and no. Rio objects and asks for another lesson. Momiji has become the person to whom Rio can bring work for criticism. It is a different achievement from having guessed a latent vocal gift. The work still costs time. Yukine reminds her they have a practice of their own. In private that same day Yukine thanks her for saving her from brooding alone. Neither obligation is incidental. (SPV2-OBS-E06-28/29/39 · E06-D06, lead from 1124.13 s · SPV2-OBS-E07-01/03/21/32, 0–40.31 and 194.027–295 s.)

## The difference between a partner and a contract

The rooftop dessert has traveled through an extraordinary number of hands. Yukine paid for the ice Haru could not afford. Haru first tried offering it back to the buyer as an inducement to return, then entrusted it to Rio to use with Momiji. The members actually miss something by treating her isolated answer as a commitment. Haru holds Momiji from behind, Rio and Kyoka hold her legs, Kyoka produces the recorder, and Haru later covers her mouth. The emphatic person in the middle has already given enough of her answer to explain how their argument could succeed and why this approach cannot. She wants to be with them. She refuses to do it by abandoning the person with whom she prepared and who has thanked her for staying. One could read this as dependence on Yukine or obedience to a senior. Neither explains how freely Momiji gives her first answer before she thinks through whom it would leave behind, or how readily she contemplates a return by both. She offers her own reason. Yukine arrives after the recording and learns the two parts of the answer from Haru. It is Momiji who gives the first flustered denial of concern, and Yukine who follows with the explanation about researching rivals. (SPV2-OBS-E07-24/26/27/28/36, 933–1175.36 s, especially 981.5 s · SPV2-XREV-E07-01.)

Haru refuses to give her best if it will hurt the others and leaves. Yukine goes down on hands and knees and apologizes to the president for failing. Momiji kneels beside her, puts her hands on her, and recalls what the two of them did manage. She can stay loyal while disagreeing about the worth of work that missed its aim. Kyoka says the pair were conspicuously poor at being rivals. Momiji even grants its truth while objecting to saying it so baldly right then. In the account Yuki gives after Haru has gone, they learn about the exceptional former member of HY:RAIN and the condition on which she joined Brightest. Momiji’s answer is an apology to the absent Haru for what she has borne alone. Her principle acquires a painful second application. The gamble undertaken beside Yukine had done little to end another friend’s isolation. The account on the roof does not give Momiji access to Haru’s private memories or to what the members of HY:RAIN later said among themselves. (SPV2-OBS-E08-16/17/18/28/29, 1153.96–1278.90 s · SPV2-OBS-E09-07/24, 433.57–480.30 s.)

Kyoka summons the pair back, ends their rival arrangement and proposes that four teammates show Haru in public what they are willing to risk. Yukine takes it up. The change addresses the condition that stopped Momiji before. She responds to the choice of their old five-person *Be Your Light!!* with a playful どんとこい, bring it on. Naoki goes to Haru; Haru still has to come herself. At their first shared regular live the five perform, then four of them have to catch their breath. Hearing centrally placed panting after the song does not identify Momiji as its solo source. Her own account that her heart stopped belongs with her taste for emphatic speech. Yukine vows to catch Haru. Momiji proposes to go *past* her. Beside the same partner, the same performance yields a different measure of ambition. She apologizes alongside Yukine when Kyoka rebukes them for leaving. They now have grounds for saying the five have chosen to work together which were unavailable at the time of the recording or the rooftop invitation. (SPV2-OBS-E09-08/09/17/18/27/28/29/35, 481.63–602.06 and 1149.79–1267.99 s · SPV2-CLM-E09-24/28 · SPV2-XREV-E09-01.)

## When someone else gives her a nickname

At the ticket counter one young woman addresses Yukine and a second praises Momiji’s dancing. Being somebody’s もみたん, Momi-tan, catches the habitual giver of diminutives by surprise. It pleases her to have become this person’s favorite. The two women later share a screen in the publicity campaign. Their family relationship and who authored every word of a post remain unknown. Momiji also speaks to viewers she cannot see. During a recorded dance demonstration Naoki handles the camera and Momiji breaks a sequence down into sounds she invites others to imitate. The scoped hearing gives キュッ、キュッ、キューッ, short, short, long, and a separate breathy sound on landing. An upside-down balance and a later physical TV challenge expand what a fan can see of her. Individual ticket conversions, athletic rankings and her training before Brightest remain outside the evidence. (SPV2-OBS-E10-02/06/10/21/24/31, 51.20–109.94 and 367.20–455.01 s · SPV2-PF-E10-05 · SPV2-ADJ-E10-012.)

Ren Kurogane arrives asking Haru to rejoin HY:RAIN and uses Momiji’s dancing to reduce her to someone who can *only* dance. Momiji challenges the “only.” She is the same person who has taught a colleague, worried about leaving another friend alone and objected that a truth about a defeated person may be badly timed. Later she calls Rio back to her senses when pudding in the visiting group’s gift to Naoki briefly improves Rio’s estimate of Ren. At the office discussion of Ren’s old promise, Momiji recognizes the affection involved and sees a familiar danger in Kyoka’s renewed claim to have nothing of her own. She calls up the stuck-paper expression Yukine once used in private, こびりペーパー. Kyoka sees an ability to astonish people through dancing in the girl who notices her retreat. Before the Nakano show, when Yukine offers Kyoka company if anything goes wrong, Momiji tells her she is not alone. It costs no one a decision this time. (SPV2-OBS-E10-13/16/26/28 · SPV2-OBS-E11-18/29, 1128.419–1232.022 s · SPV2-ADJ-E11-039 · SPV2-OBS-E12-21, 6.66–111.46 s.)

At Nakano, *On Your Mark!!* gives Momiji a first-verse handoff between Rio and Kyoka’s joint phrase and Yukine’s entry, independently supported by published parts and an aligned recording. It is an answer in practice to the visiting critic’s “only.” It establishes a part, without ranking her voice against her friends’ or resolving overdub layers. She can still turn a tense backstage reminder about composure into an excursion to the old capital. In the final office meeting Kyoka accepts the prospect of a competitive appearance with HY:RAIN at Budokan and asks for the others’ assent. Momiji gives hers as 合点小吉, another borrowed expression made unmistakably her own. A group booking with room for her individual yes is a different prospect from joining on the authority of somebody’s recorder. The season ends before that concert. (SPV2-ADJ-E12-013 · SPV2-OBS-E12-06/24/29/39, 330.39–458.8 and 1146.64–1283.92 s.)

## How far her rule about company goes

Consider Momiji after Nakano, in rehearsal, when a known teammate is late to an entry and asks her to watch it again. There is strong support for showing a count or giving a blunt verdict on whether the step has improved, possibly a playful one if the pupil is Rio. Change the scene to a friend leaving after a public failure, declining to explain. Momiji is likely to follow and offer company. The street encounter supports a real risk of insisting too soon. A clear request for help can turn the same impulse into a useful lesson, and a failure the two have actually shared may call for kneeling beside the other person and saying they did what they could. The occasion, the friend’s wishes and whether Momiji shares responsibility distinguish these responses. Her experience with the recorder gives a good reason to avoid treating an invitation from a third party as proof that the person concerned wants a public confrontation. It does not establish that she has formulated a general rule about privacy.

If the new decision required choosing an enduring solo career over remaining beside Yukine, we would need to know what Yukine wanted, what the job offered and whether any person would actually be left alone. The closeness on the bench, the partnership and the public jokes establish an attachment with real consequences. They give no adequate evidence of romantic attraction, dating history or an exclusive claim on either girl’s future. There is also scant evidence for how Momiji speaks alone at home, or how she would spend a day entirely outside the agency. Her silent stretch before the rooftop interruption and her brief childhood appearance as a spectator are better allowed their modest size.
