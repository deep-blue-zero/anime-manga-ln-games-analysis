# Homare’s place beside the stage

When Ren asks Homare to become an idol, Homare already has a shirt for the tour whose star inspired the invitation. She can repeat the star’s advertisement at school, follow her friends to the Budokan and remember how a performance makes the three of them feel. She tells Ren that the idea of auditioning together sounds rather like Ren is afraid to go on her own. Then she gives her answer. For Homare, idols are something to watch and enjoy. If the other two decide to become idols, she will support them with everything she has. At Nakano, after one career has become two and Ren has asked Haru to come back, Homare is still willing to sit across from Ren with a bag of merchandise for Haru’s show. She has turned down one obligation and lived up to another for years. That choice helps explain why she can question an old friend’s claim and still come to hear what the friend has to say. (E11, 10:38.77–11:23.74, SPV2-OBS-E11-11 and SPV2-CLM-E11-23. E12, 04:49.43–05:21.81, SPV2-OBS-E12-05.)

Homare, credited 虎渡誉, is a childhood friend of Haru Nabatame and Ren Kurogane. Haru calls her 誉ちゃん. School and shared leisure connect her to both girls before either takes a professional role. In the present she is still Haru’s schoolfriend. Ren introduces her at Nakano to people who have never needed to know that there were once three girls doing everything together. Nothing in this introduction establishes that Homare herself has joined an agency or knows how the industry makes its choices. It recovers the person who saw two friends decide on a course that was never her own. (E02, 00:32.04; E11, SPV2-OBS-E11-08/09/11 and credit; E12, 04:51.61, SPV2-CH-HOMARE.)

## How the three spent their time

E11 turns Ren’s account of their closeness into a series of quite ordinary comparisons. Ren says she charged toward the things she found interesting. Haru happily came along. Homare complained and then offered sound advice. That is one friend’s appraisal remembered years later. The pictures give it independent texture. All three can sit with bread and arithmetic before a television, and all have something they want to do when an idol appears there. Homare can copy some of the dancing from her place at the table. They look together at cookies in shapes the camera has time to show. Their friendship affords play and unremarkable time in one another’s company, including room to disagree. The episode does not trace a beginning of that friendship beyond the children’s own sense that they had simply got used to being together. (E11, 07:08.62–07:48.59, SPV2-OBS-E11-08 and SPV2-CLM-E11-08.)

The doughnut scene gives Homare an unusually good example of the kind of objection Ren remembers. All three have their own doughnuts. Haru and Ren enjoy saying that repeating Hotaru’s jingle makes the flavor rounder. Homare points out that they heard it in a commercial. She can distinguish a sales pitch from a taste and still own the pleasure of the ritual when the other two tease her about liking Hotaru just as much as they do. Three tour shirts follow the question of what they plan to wear. In Ren’s recalled Budokan audience the three sit near one another. Familiar company, a favorite artist, things to buy and discuss afterwards, and the liberty of disagreeing about a doughnut are already enough for Homare. The fact that the friends eat doughnuts again is evidence of a shared pleasure. It tells us little about her personal ranking of flavors, cooking habits or what she would eat alone. (E09, SPV2-OBS-E09-21; E11, 08:19.92–09:08.48, SPV2-OBS-E11-09/10 and SPV2-CLM-E11-44. E12, 08:44.72–09:01.69, SPV2-OBS-E12-09.)

The concert prompts a specific distinction in what they want. Ren proposes the Rainbow Management audition by describing what Haru and Homare could give a group. Homare already knows a great deal about idols, according to Ren. Homare calls attention to what Ren might need from them first. Her question about doing it alone need not disclose a motive Ren lacks the courage to admit. It could be a genial challenge to a friend already building her plan around the three of them. Homare follows it with a direct and personally attributable account of what she wants. The context allows her to choose the companion’s role without treating it as a lesser place in their friendship. Haru joins hands with Ren. Homare offers help without entering the pact. Ren and Haru’s later hurt is therefore a history Homare can observe only in part. E12 shows the three leaving the earlier show together. Haru returns alone for her bag. Their plan to compare impressions does not put Homare in the emptied arena while Haru speaks to Hotaru and meets Naoki. Homare is likewise outside the locker-room exchange that Ren recalls in E11. (E11, 10:49.76–11:23.74, SPV2-OBS-E11-11/12. E12, SPV2-OBS-E12-09–11.)

## Haru’s friend at school and in the audience

The present-day school scenes keep the two relationships separate. In E02 Homare asks Haru how she is doing over the green box of doughnuts, says Haru is dependable and listens as she starts to credit someone else. Haru leaves a ticket and goes. Homare says **頑張れ　春** after Haru has departed. The audience later sees the characteristic blonde curls and colored ties above a cyan light at regular-live frame f1013. It is possible to support someone without that person seeing the moment of support. The drawings and the desk invitation make the attendance strongly identifiable. They leave the use of that particular piece of paper and Haru’s view of the seats open. (E02, 00:09.81–00:39.69 and 1039.340 s, SPV2-OBS-E02-01/21 and SPV2-REL-HOMARE-HARU.)

Haru is visibly happier at the start of their next school conversation. Her quiz lets Homare name the success and win a smile. Homare adds that people have been talking about Haru, and Haru’s expression changes. Homare makes room for the less comfortable answer. Haru wishes that people had fallen for Kyoka and Rio too. Homare can praise the other two without taking back what she said about Haru; the friends turn to the old jingle again. This gives Haru a place where she can unpack a successful night, something she is reluctant to do with the people whose feelings she is trying to protect. Homare enjoys Haru’s success for herself and can take Haru’s wish for attention to go elsewhere seriously. What every other audience member said to Homare and how much of their discussion she heard in person are unshown. (E03, 02:56.40–03:53.29 and surrounding exchange, SPV2-OBS-E03-02/22 and SPV2-REL-HARU-HOMARE.)

In the Nakano campaign Homare reaches to Haru’s stack of flyers and stands beside her with some in hand. Compare E10 at 719.2–720.219 s. There is real pedestrian work to be done whether a stranger buys a ticket or simply keeps walking. Kyoka’s classmate 池柴朱, who produces tickets she already has at a restaurant, belongs to a different sequence. Homare’s help is a good consequence of the choice she made when Ren presented the audition. She can make public work lighter for her friend without needing to claim the stage or set its artistic terms. At the anniversary she has spent effort and money of her own. When Ren says she bought too much, Homare explains that it is Haru’s big day. The spending supports the importance she gives to this occasion. A budget, the source of her own ticket or what she routinely buys in other settings remain unavailable. (E10, SPV2-OBS-E10-10 and SPV2-REL-HOMARE-HARU. E12, 04:56.07–04:59.81, SPV2-OBS-E12-05.)

## Ren’s friend across the stairs

Ren herself has introduced Homare as a childhood friend. Homare knows about her uninvited visit to Haru. The route of this knowledge is unstaged. She recognizes the habit of charging off when an idea occurs and asks whether Ren just wants the old pair back. The question comes from someone who knew them when talk of being inseparable sounded reasonable. It also comes from someone who could see the difference between cheering for a friend’s work and joining it herself. Ren protests. It is possible that the wish for a lost intimacy and a real professional standard both matter to her. E11 gives Ren substantial evidence for the second. She has endured training to catch up to Haru and has seen the trio at earlier shows. She herself says she could not accept the level of the stage or seeing Haru work with other people. Homare’s observation gives shape to one part of this explanation. Her years of seeing the friends together do not settle which part ultimately brought Ren to the sales table. (E12, 05:02.05–05:21.81, SPV2-OBS and CLM E12-05; E11, SPV2-OBS-E11-13–15.)

The setting keeps the advice contestable. Concert steps and a rail are between the women when the camera finds a way to include them. Homare can ask her questions in the space available to a fellow spectator. She cannot order Ren to make up with Haru, and does not try. The later edit lets Homare look across the steps when Ren recognizes the Haru Nabatame of TINGS. This is a meaningful juxtaposition. We cannot specify what words Homare hears, how she interprets a visible expression or how she and Ren would discuss the matter on a later day. What happened to their friendship during the time their professional roles diverged is likewise only partially recoverable. Both being there tells us where each finally looked, and nothing requires them to have reached the house for the same reason. (E12, SPV2-OBS-E12-05/16, SPV2-CLM-E12-16 and SPV2-REL-HOMARE-REN.)

## How she talks and how far the account can travel

The friend who asks **春　調子はどう** over food can also cheer **頑張れ　春** once Haru is gone. These short attested utterances use a name and assume close company. With Ren over the audition she starts with **そんなこと言って** and asks **１人でやるのが不安なんでしょ**. The question presses an interpretation into the conversation without a formal accusation. Homare’s own answer starts with **だって**, gives the pleasure of watching as reason enough, and offers **全力でサポートするよ** if they decide to go ahead. The punchline about a commercial and the offer of active support belong to the same young woman. At Nakano, **ほんとは戻りたいだけなんじゃない** is a far more serious use of a familiar question, followed by the first-person warrant that she has been watching for years. The early question about going alone establishes a register, not proof that the later explanation is right. The selected written Japanese tracks support this analysis. The documents supply no independent basis for generalizing her speaking voice to industry strangers, or claiming exact delivery from punctuation. (E02, SPV2-W-E02-JP 00:15.97 and 00:39.69; E11, SPV2-W-E11-JP 08:31.04–08:46.75 and 11:02.66–11:20.34; E12, SPV2-W-E12-JP 05:12.09–05:21.81.)

A plausible Homare@E12 response to another friend who mistakes loyalty for assent would be to ask what the friend actually wants from her, decline work that takes her onto the stage, and consider a practical way to help from the audience’s side. This short-distance extrapolation rests on the audition, the handbills and the two different conversations about Haru’s efforts. A friend’s explicit request to keep a matter private would alter the case. At Nakano firsthand familiarity appears to make Homare unusually bold, and Ren’s protest is a contrary example to any rule that tact always succeeds. A scene in which Homare puts a paid assignment or the chance to perform above spectator independence would require a revised account. A chance to listen to one familiar idol sheds little light on whether she would mediate a contractual dispute or forgive a serious betrayal. The source gives neither verified romantic or intimate ties nor a basis for reconstructing those decisions.
