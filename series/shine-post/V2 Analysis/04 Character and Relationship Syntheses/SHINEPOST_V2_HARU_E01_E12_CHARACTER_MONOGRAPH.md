# Haru Nabatame through the anniversary concert

When Haru Nabatame (青天国春, なばためはる) tells her schoolfriend Homare about her first full house, Homare has a compliment for her. People were talking about Haru’s performance. Haru thanks her. Then her smile changes. She would like people to have gone away wanting to see Kyoka and Rio, too. It is an ambitious wish for a girl who can draw eyes just by dancing. Haru wants to be seen, and she wants what someone sees in her to give that person a reason to look at somebody else. Her most consequential mistake comes from deciding on her friends’ behalf how much of her talent they can bear to see. The hardest thing she does onstage is to give them the chance to answer. (E03, school desks, 71–237 s, SPV2-OBS-E03-02. E09, dressing-room challenge and its answer, 898.63–949.62 and 1202–1270.60 s, SPV2-OBS-E09-14, SPV2-OBS-E09-18, SPV2-OBS-E09-19.)

Haru is a sixteen-year-old student and idol at Brightest Production. Fans call her Harurun. At the start she usually stands in the middle of the public trio TiNgS, alongside Kyoka Tamaki and Rio Seibu. Its regular theater seats a hundred. Nine months into their work, its best attendance has been thirty-seven. President Yuki Hinase gives the three until their anniversary, three months away, to fill the 2,000-seat Nakano Sunplaza. The difference between someone’s favorite girl and a girl who can sell two thousand tickets is palpable to Haru. After publicly thanking the regulars she wonders quietly whether she is suited to this profession at all. The close, hushed line accompanies moving lips. A murmur to herself and a stylized interior voice both fit what the scene lets us hear. (E01, 136.345–179.284 and 337.65–428.28 s, SPV2-OBS-E01-02, SPV2-OBS-E01-05, SPV2-OBS-E01-14, case E01-club. E02, Rin at the ticket table, SPV2-OBS-E02-04. E10, radio introduction, cues 877–909, SPV2-OBS-E10-06.)

## What she carries into the practice room

Haru, Ren Kurogane and Homare grew up eating at the same table, joining in the movements of an idol on television and looking at what they had baked together. Ren remembers herself running ahead with Haru smiling and following, and Homare supplying an objection and some advice. The picture has room for all three to play at being performers. In middle school they enjoy Hotaru’s doughnut advertisement enough to try its claim about roundness for themselves. They wear matching shirts to her 2018 Budokan concert. Years later, when the two girls who did become idols have lost the ability to speak easily to each other, there is still a bag of doughnuts Ren can give Haru. Haru’s taste for sharing them recurs with Homare across ordinary schooldays. The changing flavors, the sweets the girls promote for work and the gifts they bring people do not establish a favorite flavor or a general diet. (E02, opening 0–52.594 s, SPV2-OBS-E02-01. E03, after the full house, SPV2-OBS-E03-02. E11, childhood and commercial, cues 888–925, and lakeside farewell, cues 1016–1031, SPV2-OBS-E11-08, SPV2-OBS-E11-09, SPV2-OBS-E11-16.)

At Budokan Haru sees a possibility for herself in someone else’s work. E01 shows the girl with the pink lightstick wondering if she could do this too. Only E12 shows what happened after the show. She turned back alone for a little brown bag and met Hotaru. Haru had already felt the discomfort of being looked at strangely for loving idols. Hotaru’s bearing made her feel she could own that pleasure. What Haru wants is a world where people anywhere can say aloud whom they love. Trying to describe Hotaru to Hotaru, she reaches for a mark, an arrow. Hotaru suggests a guidepost. Haru adds that it shines brightly enough to be found from anywhere. Hotaru offers the name *shine post* and the possibility of working toward that world as an idol herself, with the help of an excellent manager. This is a conversation between an articulate fan and a performer who listens closely enough to help her finish a thought. Ren and Homare, waiting elsewhere, had no access to it. Naoki Hinase arrived partway through. Years later he needs Haru’s account to recognize her as that girl. E12 reopens the meaning of Haru getting his name right when she approached him in E01. Their old connection mattered to her. The series leaves the precise route by which she identified his full name at the agency indistinct. (E01, concert and first approach, SPV2-OBS-E01-01, SPV2-OBS-E01-08. E12, bag, guidepost and dressing-room recollection, cues 904–985, especially 644.7 and 728.561 s, SPV2-OBS-E12-09, SPV2-OBS-E12-10, SPV2-OBS-E12-11, SPV2-OBS-E12-12.)

Ren made the next step practical. When she produced the Rainbow Management audition notice, Homare chose to stay a fan. Haru wondered whether she could manage it and reached across the desk to take Ren’s hand. The two were accepted. With Yawara, Itoha and Aoba, they trained for what became HY:RAIN. Haru loved getting a piece of choreography today that they could not do yesterday. She and Ren could fall asleep against each other on the train home from their long days. When Haru learned a new dance in one try, she called it lucky. Ren praised her and wanted a turn at beating her. The first competitive remark in this friendship sounded like a reason to go on working together. (E09, practice, commute and Ren’s raised fists at 223.014 s, SPV2-OBS-E09-02, SPV2-OBS-E09-03. E11, audition notice and notification, cues 937–960, SPV2-OBS-E11-11, SPV2-OBS-E11-59.)

During the trial to select a center, Haru stood out among the five. Later she saw Ren crying by the lockers through a partly open door and heard Ren wish she could become as good as Haru. Haru’s conclusion was much larger than what she had heard. Giving her best could hurt the people dearest to her. She quit, and the girls passed each other at school without the conversation that might have changed her mind. E09 gives the audience Haru’s recollection, including her wish never to feel that pain herself again. E10 lets Ren tell Haru that the old disparity gave Ren a reason to train. E11 shows that Ren saw Haru at the door too. The apology Ren made for falling behind came later, at Haru’s empty locker. Haru could see the tears and hear the first wish. She could not hear an apology addressed to an absent friend. No vote awarding the center, order to leave or request by Ren for Haru to diminish herself appears. (E09, evaluation and locker-room door, 209.13–337.45 s, SPV2-OBS-E09-03, SPV2-OBS-E09-04, SPV2-OBS-E09-05. E10, Ren at the ticket table, SPV2-OBS-E10-12. ## The girl who says to leave it to her

At school Haru wears glasses and braids. She slips out of them and into training clothes at the agency. In E01 she savors winning ice-pop sticks and well-timed crossings, works herself into a happy anticipation of a costume, a CD or television, and enters the president’s office ready for a treat. Told TiNgS may be dissolved, she tries coming through the door again. By E12 she has stood on the Nakano stage and still takes pleasure in reaching a crossing just as the light turns. At her desk with Homare she can run off with her mouth full, turn good news into a quiz and offer her own smile as the prize. She can also let Homare see her reservation about a successful show. Good luck gives her something enjoyable to do with attention. It also lets her play down expertise when a compliment about a particular achievement makes her uncomfortable. Having fun is a daily practice for Haru, and its results are fallible. (E01, 182.95–336.69 s, SPV2-OBS-E01-03, SPV2-OBS-E01-04. E02, 0–52.594 s, SPV2-OBS-E02-01. E03, SPV2-OBS-E03-02. E09, SPV2-OBS-E09-03. E12, lights and change of clothes, 1145–1168 s, SPV2-OBS-E12-18.)

Her cheerful manner takes work. The diminutive Haru-chan that she uses to announce her own dependability in a team message recurs in her theatrical sign-off. At the end of E01 she pauses, presses her cheeks and forms a smile, a V sign and a wink. A measured picture and hearing check found the gold and shimmer associated with Naoki’s perception of a lie on other girls’ assurances, and neither marker on Haru’s boast. The best inference from her hands is that encouragement can be something she makes for herself. The source leaves open what combination of strain, sincere excitement and practiced presentation she feels. E07 will give that same onlooker reason to doubt a different, smiling assurance. Each occasion has to be read for what she is promising. (E01, chat at frame 275 and closing address at 1364.029–1370.202 s, SPV2-OBS-E01-03, SPV2-OBS-E01-22, case E01-motive. E07, SPV2-OBS-E07-14.)

Naoki first experiences the cost of Haru’s enthusiasm. She welcomes him to the agency as though he has taken the manager’s job and brings him along fast enough that he cannot finish objecting. Told explicitly that he refuses, she eventually hears a distinction in his wording. Saying he will not do it leaves room to ask whether he wants to. The audience has heard his private conviction that he ought not manage idols. Haru has heard only what he says to her. She proposes the smaller commitment of watching a lesson. Her account of the world she wants helps draw him back to the profession. He offers a provisional appointment tied to their filling their own theater. She can listen to why a refusal leaves an opening, and she can also be the last person in the room to notice that she has neglected to ask. Later Naoki can tell her that she helped him when she imagined she was simply asking for help. (E01, 540.77–616, 959.65–1049.78 and 1130.53–1212.69 s, SPV2-OBS-E01-08, SPV2-OBS-E01-14, SPV2-OBS-E01-15, SPV2-CLM-E01-08, SPV2-CLM-E01-11. Haru’s ability often consists in seeing where somebody else is. Naoki describes her adjusting to Kyoka’s and Rio’s different movements, then invites her to direct her attention outward to the audience. In the E02 number she already faces front before the featured passage. A literal moment at which she suddenly discovers the crowd would be a false precision. Naoki sees an existing skill applied where spectators can notice it. Haru’s work away from the stage is more laborious. She politely invites passersby, gets a lukewarm assent, knows some regulars by name and thanks the group whose concert gives the trio access to other listeners. In E05 a shop appearance puts her face to face with customers she can recognize later at ticket sales. She praises Momiji’s dancing in Rio’s hearing, wonders about Rio at work and calls after her when she leaves. The praise and concern are both credible. Neither lets her see the private comparisons tormenting Rio. (E02, street and hosts, 317.12–441.61 and 696.26–841 s, and concert, 1198.38–1279.75 s, SPV2-OBS-E02-04, SPV2-OBS-E02-07, SPV2-CLM-E02-03. Kyoka has experienced the power and the cost of being seen by Haru. When Kyoka missed the entrance to her song, Haru crossed to cover it and kept the number going. The E03 remembered passage shows the cover under reflective music, with no identifiable stage vocal in the checked gaps. Its meaning for Kyoka is bound up with knowing Haru could rescue the show. In E04 Kyoka recalls Haru’s readiness to adjust and encourage her colleagues, calls her special, and uses comparison with her as a reason to expect less of herself. Haru has no access to all that narration. She wonders whether she did too much in covering. What she wants now is precise and generous. Let people hear the version of this song that Kyoka sings. She has also tried a dangling charm and improvised hypnosis to cheer Kyoka up, with no practical plan for what happens next. Her regard for Kyoka is reliable. Her solutions vary greatly in their sensitivity. (E03, 620.46–824.72 and 917.05–963.28 s, SPV2-OBS-E03-06, SPV2-OBS-E03-08, case E03-mute. For Rio, Haru can be a rival for the center and a welcome witness to what happens when Rio gets it. In E06 Haru puts herself forward too. When Rio objects to the almost danceless new number, Haru’s criterion is whether the live show will be enjoyable. Removing a specialty of Haru’s can serve that criterion. She gives Rio room to introduce the song, then puts an arm around her after the crowd calls back and Rio can scarcely finish her customary grand introduction. On another day Rio will tell Haru to her face that she intends to beat her at singing. Haru can receive both the tears and the challenge. Rio’s need for help did not equip Haru to diagnose her beforehand, or give Haru an exclusive claim to her recovery. ## Who knows why Haru holds back?

Haru’s delight in the little group predates the trio the public knows. Yukine Gionji and Momiji Ito had practiced with Haru, Kyoka and Rio as the original five TINGS. To Haru their sudden departure was a loss she could neither explain nor reconcile with the fact that the two still came to the trio’s shows. E07 puts her back among people who could, in principle, tell her what happened. With glasses, a pointer and a whiteboard, she treats their reunion as a problem a committee could solve. She remembers who led them and who taught whom. She also turns a necessary condition for success at Nakano into a promise that gathering all five will guarantee it. Trying to buy Yukine’s cooperation with half of an ice cream that Yukine has just paid for is typical Haru improvisation. Holding Momiji from behind and covering her mouth when Yukine reappears is more troubling. Momiji has a reason of her own to stay with Yukine and needs to be allowed to finish saying it. Haru’s affection is clearest when she finally puts out an empty hand and names the four people she wants beside her. None is interchangeable. None owes her a yes. (E07, whiteboard at 497.038 s, shop and courtyard at 793.10–1057.97 s, invitation at 1057.97–1187.12 s, SPV2-OBS-E07-06, SPV2-OBS-E07-09, SPV2-OBS-E07-11, SPV2-OBS-E07-12.)

The explanation reaches each person differently. In E07 Yukine says Haru has made no progress and that she herself still falls far short of Haru. Seen from Yukine’s position, an evidently capable performer who refuses to show her best makes it hard to know what working alongside her would mean. Naoki remembers seeing how quickly Haru can find and answer a spectator’s interest. He offers a paradox about the amount of effort it costs her to hold back. Haru repeats でも as she tries to meet the objections, smiles and claims she always gives her all. The image picks her out in gold on that assurance. The gold gives the viewer reason to doubt her account. Kyoka and Rio hear the challenge. Neither has been shown to share Naoki’s faculty, and Haru has still given them no account of her reasons. (E07, cues 1141–1213, especially 1222.012–1244.118 and 1277.526–1279.820 s, SPV2-OBS-E07-12, SPV2-OBS-E07-13, SPV2-OBS-E07-14, SPV2-OBS-E07-38.)

E08 goes back to what Yukine and Momiji knew before leaving. In rehearsal Yukine had taken Haru for a competent beginner who felt inhibited about dancing in front of others. Haru cooperated in learning as a group and credited Yukine’s leadership. Haru had never told her this was her first public dance. Momiji noticed discrepancies. Then the two actually saw Haru dancing by herself through the rooftop doorway. There is no sign Haru knew they were watching. They now had evidence of a difference between her group performance and her solo dancing. The reason came from a third person. President Yuki, who already knew the terms of Haru’s recruitment, told them that it was for their sake. Yuki also discussed their present level and set the two the risky task of becoming rivals capable of forcing a change. Haru experienced their departure without the private explanation that gives the viewer a different view of their apparent hostility. (E08, rehearsal, cues 833–854, rooftop and president’s office, 894.36–1086.16 s, SPV2-OBS-E08-03, SPV2-OBS-E08-09, SPV2-OBS-E08-11, SPV2-OBS-E08-13, SPV2-OBS-E08-14, SPV2-XREV-E08-01.)

The case register records two independent hearings of ため at 987.5–988.6 s. The selected fan subtitle prints ダメ. (SPV2-XREV-E08-01.)

Back in the present, Haru says giving her whole effort will hurt everyone. The gold occurs on the subsequent categorical claim that it would be better never to do it. This distinction matters. The danger is sincerely imaginable to a girl who once saw Ren on her knees. The anime puts pressure on the rule Haru has made out of that experience. She leaves with a real show to prepare for and a real conversation she can no longer bear. Only after she goes does Yuki tell the others that Haru had belonged to an earlier idol group. The poster suggests HY:RAIN to the viewer at E08’s close. Rio supplies the name aloud in E09. (E08, cues 1110–1122 and 1143–1147, gold at the end of the first interval near 1194 s, SPV2-OBS-E08-16, SPV2-OBS-E08-18. E09, 433.57–480.30 s, SPV2-OBS-E09-07.)

E09 finally shows the agreement itself. Yuki had seen Haru at the center trial. When Haru repeatedly refused her approach, Yuki asked about the world the girl once looked toward. Haru chose to return on condition that she could restrain herself until the others became stronger. Yuki accepted. Haru has known the terms since she joined Brightest, long before the viewer or the rest of the group learns them. She has deliberately limited her work with these colleagues, with the president’s knowledge. Calling it ordinary beginner anxiety is untenable after this scene. Calling it simple self-sacrifice also omits her own account of the experience she wanted never to repeat. She had good reason to believe comparison could hurt a friend. She had much less reason to conclude that she could predict everybody’s response, judge privately when they were strong enough and spare them injury by keeping them uninformed. Haru’s account and ringing success as a public colleague can coexist because she values the very things restraint imperils. She likes learning with these people and wants to continue showing up and singing with them. The other four and Naoki hear Yuki’s account at the rooftop. The viewer has additionally had access to Haru’s recollection and thoughts. (E09, departure and recruitment, 306.95–432.62 s, rooftop at 426.009 s, SPV2-OBS-E09-05, SPV2-OBS-E09-06, SPV2-OBS-E09-07, SPV2-CLM-E09-06.)

There are serious alternatives to an account that makes concealment Haru’s entire personality. On an ordinary trio number, watching Rio and Kyoka can be valuable ensemble work. The old center cover accomplished something nobody else in the formation then did. A spotlight is also an imperfect measure of how hard somebody is trying. What changes the seasonal judgment is the conjunction of Haru’s own bargain, the two friends’ rooftop observation, Naoki’s diagnosis and Haru’s expressed fear. None requires every adjustment, kind remark or unforced smile to have been calculated. Equally, her friends’ disagreement leaves the original pain intact. Ren’s tears were real. A musician who chooses to restrain herself once for the sake of a particular partner can have made a sound decision. What fails in Haru’s general rule is that her partners cannot answer it.

## What the other people can say back

Rio wants to know why Haru never consulted them. Momiji thinks about the loneliness of keeping the secret. Kyoka thinks Haru did not trust them *yet*. She asks Yukine and Momiji to return, and the four undertake a public performance of *Be Your Light!!*, a number the original five had already learned together. The old formation gives them something practical they can offer besides reassurance. Haru asks Naoki not to look for her. He finds her anyway using a clue in the park announcement on the telephone. He tells her about the harm he thinks his own earlier work caused, reminds her that her request to him once mattered, and asks her to trust her colleagues this once. Then he leaves her on the bench to think. Both the pressure of the visit and the freedom of the final decision matter. Alone on her walk, Haru takes account of Kyoka’s growth, Rio’s ability to move people with her voice and the risk Yukine and Momiji took in challenging her. The thought the viewer hears goes no further than “maybe.” Her exact moment of decision remains private. (E09, song chosen at 547.58–602.06 s, park and subsequent walk at 610.68–885.69 s, SPV2-OBS-E09-07, SPV2-OBS-E09-09, SPV2-OBS-E09-10, SPV2-OBS-E09-12, SPV2-OBS-E09-13.)

Haru turns up in costume after missing rehearsals. The girls are entitled to be angry about that. Kyoka gives the underlying offense a name. Deciding how much the others could bear had been Haru’s decision alone. Back onstage Kyoka’s greeting to the whole house turns into a request addressed to one person to trust them. Haru answers by performing. Five hands and five positions make a shape none could make alone. When the music recedes at about 1123.25 s and panting becomes audible around 1128.28 s, the mix lets us hear effort without assigning individual breaths. The show continues. Only later, backstage, are the other four bent over and Haru still upright. At first that looks like the outcome she always feared. What she does differently is **stay until they finish speaking**. Kyoka wants to grow by keeping up. Rio wants to best her at singing. Yukine wants to reach her level, and Momiji wants to pass it. Their bodies show a cost. Their answers show that the cost is theirs to choose. Haru apologizes, thanks them and throws her arms around Kyoka. Finding that four particular friends can meet her this way is a change in trust. It leaves her earlier friendship with Ren unresolved. (E09, dressing room through public address, 898.63–1028.88 s, SPV2-OBS-E09-14, SPV2-OBS-E09-15. That trust has an everyday expression. Haru introduces the group on radio, carries flyers to school where Homare shares the work, and joins the other girls in taking pudding to the overworked Naoki. It also has an inconvenient test. At the ticket table Ren has come to claim Haru for the now-successful HY:RAIN. Ren’s account of training after the old split changes what Haru can know about her. Kyoka promises to show what TINGS can offer. Ren attaches a condition under which Haru would be returned to her, and Kyoka accepts it on Haru’s behalf. When Haru objects, the two tell her in audible unison to stay quiet. Haru asks why, when this is her own problem. An observer could see guilt or uncertainty in her hesitation, or see two forceful friends giving her barely any space in which to speak. The voiced objection is stronger evidence than any one of these diagnoses. Seeing HY:RAIN’s impressive Budokan performance does not commit her to a transfer. ## How Haru sounds to different people

The girl who jokes at a desk and the idol who takes a microphone share a facility for finding an addressee. With Homare she offers a game, lets herself be teased, credits somebody else, and then qualifies a compliment once she feels safe enough to say what the evening lacked for her. Her self-question in the theater begins with ordinary 私. The comic Haru-chan she uses in the chat and her sign-off is a way of making a small promise into an occasion. With Naoki, マネージャーくん makes his job title into an affectionate everyday address. She can still listen minutely to his choice between refusing an act and not wanting it. With a stranger on the street she asks 来てくれませんか, a polite invitation one can turn down or brush past. On radio she gives the full group name followed by よろしくお願いします. The later source hearing corrects the truncated name on the fan subtitle. These forms describe attested situations. The one hushed self-question does not supply a pitch, speed or timbre to assign to her voice everywhere. (E01, SPV2-OBS-E01-02, SPV2-OBS-E01-03, SPV2-OBS-E01-14, SPV2-OBS-E01-20, case E01-club. ## Decisions a reader can anticipate

These are candidate tendencies within the television story. Each has a failure condition.

- **An invitation that somebody can try.** Where Haru cares about a shared undertaking but a listener hesitates, expect her to move from enthusiasm to something the listener can experience, such as watching the lesson or coming to see the five. Support is strong in E01 and E11. In a hurry she may assume agreement, or turn persuasion into a stunt, as the E03 hypnosis and E07 courtyard show. A clear refusal she continues to override after hearing the reasons would count against predicting a smaller, voluntary invitation in that setting. (SPV2-OBS-E01-08, SPV2-OBS-E01-15, SPV2-OBS-E03-06, SPV2-OBS-E07-11, SPV2-OBS-E11-15.)
- **When someone else has a contribution she can name.** Expect Haru to credit a particular act, invite the person to show it, and participate in the performance that makes it possible. This is strongly supported by her advocacy of Kyoka’s version and response to Rio’s solo. She may still bid for the same opportunity herself. If the person has expressed reluctance, do not assume Haru understands it or that increased attention is welcome. A missed entry in front of a live audience can trigger an immediate cover instead. (SPV2-OBS-E03-08, SPV2-OBS-E04-01, SPV2-OBS-E06-01, SPV2-OBS-E06-72.)
- **When a disparity threatens a valued friendship.** Before the five’s first public performance together, there is strong evidence that remembered distress can prompt Haru to limit herself, explain little and let others underestimate her. A competitive remark alone carries little predictive weight. Ren’s first challenge delighted her. After the others demonstrate both their ability to stand with her and their wish to try, expect more willingness to risk comparison and stay for their answers. Confidence in transfer to an unfamiliar troupe is low. Choosing secret restraint again under equivalent, explicitly accepted conditions would challenge the extent of this change. (SPV2-OBS-E09-03, SPV2-OBS-E09-06, SPV2-OBS-E09-14, SPV2-OBS-E09-18, SPV2-OBS-E11-18.)
- **When friends presume to choose for her.** At Haru@E10–E12 she may protest in the moment, seek a conversation where she can hear the other person out, and refuse a request without withdrawing affection. Joining a bill with Ren poses a different question from transferring to Ren’s group. This distinction is well supported in these relationships. A conflict in which listening would place her livelihood or physical safety immediately at risk has no comparable test here. Agreement to transfer merely because somebody won the wager would have contradicted the reading. (SPV2-OBS-E10-15, SPV2-ADJ-E10-064, SPV2-OBS-E11-15, SPV2-OBS-E12-19.)

Haru’s hugs, her intensity toward Ren, Ren’s sense of loss and the cheek pinch with Kyoka support friendship, shared ambitions and differing expectations about privacy. Romantic attraction, experience, jealousy in a romantic sense and intimate preferences remain unknown. Her family life, a favorite food or flavor, an upper limit to her stamina or singing, and her choices after the proposed joint bill are also unknown. The owner heard the finale’s feather phrase at 887.7–889.7 s as full ensemble. The exact vocal doubles in the mix remain open. (E11, SPV2-OBS-E11-08, SPV2-OBS-E11-16, SPV2-OBS-E11-17. E12, SPV2-ADJ-E12-013, SPV2-ADJ-E12-015.)
