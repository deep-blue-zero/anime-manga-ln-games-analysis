# What the five can ask of one another

Yukine buys Haru an ice cream while refusing to return to TINGS. The dessert then goes to Momiji, who says she would come back. A recording catches that answer. By the time the ice cream is melting between the girls, Momiji has made clear that she will stay out if the others are asking her to leave Yukine behind. The two answers can coexist. So can Yukine’s refusal and the purchase Haru hoped would soften it. Each favor establishes what one person is willing to do on this occasion. A request to share the work and risk of being a group still needs an answer of its own. [E07, OBS 08–11 and 27, CLM 11 and 27][e07]

After Itō Momiji and Gionji Yukine leave TINGS, the remaining three perform as TiNgS. The lowercase *i* and *g* keep the absent members’ initials in the name, and their influence survives in the work. The lesson Rio can take, the criticism Kyoka can hear, the loyalties Momiji names, and the disclosures Haru can refuse all have different terms. The five become a viable group when they can bring these terms into the same room and answer for them. The experience helps explain a later surprise. A friend who held Haru while she cried can silence her in the next confrontation and help her speak in the one after that. Trust changes what the two can try to do for each other. It leaves both responsible for what they do next. [E07, OBS 01–05 and 08–14][e07] · [E09, OBS 18–19][e09] · [E10, ticket-counter encounter][e10] · [E11, OBS 06–07 and 38][e11]

## The pair formed to help five

Brightest president Hinase Yuki gives the five trainees equipment and free rein. They need someone to lead the practice and Yukine steps forward with the others’ agreement. She makes an immediate difficulty manageable for Momiji by lowering the running target. Momiji subsequently gives a count the others can move to, while Yukine puts technical corrections into words. These are different kinds of instruction. Splitting them makes the girls more capable than either could be as a lone leader. When Yukine asks Momiji to help with the whole group and Momiji names them Yukimoji, the partnership has a beneficiary beyond either partner. Its first success gives the pair a reason to expect their presence to matter to the other three. [E08, OBS 02–06 and 09][e08] · [Yukine and Momiji’s directed edges][edges]

That arrangement becomes harder to sustain when Momiji wonders whether Haru has ever performed at the level available to her. The two see Haru dance by herself. They have reason to question the effort they have been evaluating together, and a prior history gives the discovery its particular force. Yukine had helped a visibly hesitant Haru as a trainee. The stronger performance reopens her understanding of that earlier help. Seeing the discrepancy is enough for her to question the relationship. Haru’s reasons remain outside the two observers’ view. The series subsequently supplies Haru’s account of seeing Ren cry. The delay matters because it lets Yukine’s injury have its own history before a spectator can explain Haru to her. [E08, OBS 06 and 11–14][e08] · [E09, OBS 03–06][e09]

Yuki’s proposal sends the pair out of the group together. Her plan assumes a common motive will survive a change in membership. Haru will use the ability she withholds to protect TINGS if protecting it requires meeting a threat. Yukine and Momiji take on real risk and receive no promise of restoration. Kyoka and Rio are left to supply reasons for their own abandonment. Haru still has to respond to a threat made by people she wants alongside her. The split makes a previously useful partnership carry two purposes simultaneously. Momiji can understand staying with Yukine as solidarity, while the pair’s departure leaves three colleagues without the account that might help them respond. That contradiction belongs to the work they chose together. Treating it as evidence that they ceased to care would miss the extent to which they keep doing ordinary things for their former teammates. [E08, OBS and CLM 13–15][e08] · [E07, OBS 01, 07–14 and 27][e07]

## What can cross the split

Momiji worries about how to greet Rio at the location shoot and offers water when Rio is tired. Rio can refuse that immediate help and still be the person Momiji sees struggling with the work. By the end of the mall episode, Momiji has also run after Rio and caught her arm to keep her from leaving alone. There is a cost to receiving that kind of care on somebody else’s timetable. The later meeting gives Rio a different way to proceed. She comes with custard pudding and makes a request for instruction, after having set a condition for herself about succeeding onstage. Momiji accepts the request. By E07 the two are practising with the other trio members in the room. Rio can register that the latest estimate of her dancing sounds discouraging and ask for more work anyway. Momiji also has practice with Yukine to attend. The two units now share a concrete working relationship through Rio and Momiji while their membership remains formally divided. [E05, OBS 22, 24–25 and 27–29][e05] · [E06, OBS 22 and 26–29][e06] · [E07, OBS 03 and PF 04][e07]

Yukine’s ordinary help in E07 takes forms a recipient could reasonably accept as affection, professional respect or simple courtesy. She wipes sauce from Rio’s cheek and tells her the split was not her fault. She ties Kyoka’s loose shoelace after turning down an offer of useful shared practice. She pays 130 yen for the ice cream Haru cannot buy. These acts differ in how much they explain. The assurance settles one suspicion for Rio and leaves the cause of the split out of the conversation. The working knot solves the immediate problem for Kyoka. Taking pity on Haru’s budget leaves the proposed reunion exactly where it was. The refusals make the help analytically valuable. They show which parts of a relationship are already active when its members disagree about a future together. [E07, OBS 08–10 and 23–26][e07]

Yukine’s earlier handling of Kyoka’s secret shows why the kind of aid matters. Haru remembers Kyoka’s explicit instruction to keep her failed center performance private. Yukine makes herself the informant so Haru can avoid breaking her own promise. Knowing Kyoka well makes the disclosure possible. It also lets Yukine reinterpret a prohibition as a comedian’s setup. Kyoka also tells Naoki directly that she wants him to stop intruding. The friend who can locate Kyoka and encourage her in E04 has access a manager lacks. That access can help get somebody to the venue and can expose an experience its owner asked to keep private. Both uses belong to the same ongoing friendship. Each calls for judgment about who is affected and who gets to answer. [E03, OBS and CLM on the disclosure and center assignment][e03] · [E04, OBS 21 and 27][e04] · [Yukine → Kyoka][edges]

The ice cream meeting repeats the risk in miniature. Rio approaches Momiji and receives a willing answer in the scope of the question as Momiji understands it. Haru and Kyoka have been listening out of view. The Japanese captions repeat Momiji’s earlier assent while the pink phone displays its recorder. The three treat the captured answer as if it could settle the question. The condition that follows changes the decision the listeners are trying to extract. Kyoka finally asks the question that includes Yukine. Worry about losing the assent has already made the three surround Momiji, and Haru puts an arm across her mouth. The scramble remains a comedy. Its concrete mistake is to place Momiji’s ability to state the terms of her agreement in competition with the others’ desire to secure it. Momiji goes to Yukine and reports her refusal. Haru then tells Yukine both parts of the answer. Momiji’s willingness to work with the three and her commitment to Yukine can finally enter the same conversation. [E07, OBS 10–12 and 27, CLM 11 and 27][e07]

## A promise the other people can test

Kyoka’s return proposal in E09 begins as an order to Yukine and Momiji. Yuki has explained the split after Haru leaves, and the existing Nakano condition still gives everybody reasons to agree. The group does something more specific than give Haru a welcome. Rio points out that adapting the trio’s material would require choreography and vocal parts for five. Kyoka proposes *Be Your Light!!*, a song these five already have experience rehearsing together. Their manager Naoki tells Haru where to come and what is being proposed, then leaves the bench without an answer. Haru goes to the venue and meets the colleagues who can speak to her directly about what they intend to do. Putting four people beside her on the same stage exposes their own preparation and confidence to the difficulty they are asking Haru to risk. Their willingness can now be tested by what happens to all five. [E09, OBS 06–09 and 12–17, CLM 08, 12 and 14][e09]

The decisive replies come after the number. Kyoka is bent over, Yukine and Momiji are leaning forward, and Rio is flushed. Haru has already seen a friend brought to her knees after she performed at full strength. This time she stays to receive an answer. Kyoka considers how much she could grow by keeping up. Rio names singing as the field in which she intends to win at the next regular live. Yukine talks about catching up soon and Momiji goes further, saying she will pass Haru. Each can acknowledge the effect of Haru’s performance and choose a response of her own. Kyoka makes the returning two answer for leaving, receives their apologies, and tells Haru to stop carrying everything alone. She names tomorrow’s training for Nakano as the next thing all five will do. Haru’s thanks and embrace follow that return to specific obligations. The cost of the performance and the warmth of the embrace belong to an arrangement that has just made more room for disagreement. [E09, OBS 17–19, CLM 18–19][e09]

Common career need helps explain why a group under a deadline would try again. It gives a thinner account of the help Yukine and Momiji continue to give on days when they still refuse membership. Rivalry can also generate exertion. In E08 its point was for a threat to make somebody act on an inferred desire to protect. In E09 the girls tell Haru what they will do alongside her, experience the result, and claim the right to judge and compete again individually. Keeping their two responses distinct makes the change visible even if one is skeptical about how free a choice the anniversary deadline allows. [E07, OBS 08–14][e07] · [E08, CLM 14–15][e08] · [E09, OBS 12 and 18, OPEN 02][e09]

## Help after a refusal

Haru and Ren offer a comparison that keeps the five from looking inevitable. There is a real history of shared pleasure, mutual ambition and a friend recruiting a friend. At the original evaluation, Haru sees Ren’s tears and hears her wish to be able to keep up. Ren’s separate account in E11 adds what Haru missed, including an apology spoken to an empty locker. Haru makes decisions in response to a scene she knows only in part. Ren builds a career and a case for trying again. The doughnuts she brings are an intelligible gesture even before her old friend can decide how to answer. Homare’s earlier choice to support her friends without auditioning had already shown how an offer to share an interest could be accepted on different terms by the third person. [E09, OBS 02–06][e09] · [E11, OBS and CLM 15–17][e11]

At the ticket counter Ren and Kyoka both tell Haru to be quiet. Their purposes are opposed, and a person standing between them loses a turn either way. In E11 Kyoka arrives when public attention has interrupted the meeting and makes it possible to get out of the crowd. She remains with Haru through the conversation. Haru can keep the gift and tell Ren she is TINGS’s Haru Nabatame. The refusal belongs to Haru, and it precedes the anniversary performance. Any influence that show has on Ren is accordingly a change in what the recipient of the refusal can recognize. It supplies neither the original answer nor the right to make it. [E10, ticket-counter encounter][e10] · [E10-064, synchronized dismissal][adj] · [E11, OBS 06–07 and 12][e11] · [E12, OBS and CLM 16][e12]

There is unfinished business on both sides. Haru asks Kyoka to keep the meeting private. At Brightest the others already know the gist, and Haru asks Kyoka why she told them. The scene gives Haru’s attribution and cuts across the disclosure itself. Claiming exact contents or a motive would require a different witness. Kyoka, who can name what Rio, Momiji and Yukine accomplish for a spectator, still says she has nothing of her own. The others have expressed their wish to keep Haru and have an immediate difficulty hearing the colleague who helped her stay. In E12 Homare suggests what Ren wants from the past, and Ren disputes her authority to say it. Ren later recognizes Haru’s affiliation to herself in the hall. The story gives that acknowledgment its own moment. The Budokan proposal arrives separately. Yuki attributes it to HY:RAIN’s agency. The earlier relationship can continue to matter in a future rivalry even though the series closes with Haru inside TINGS and Ren inside HY:RAIN. [E11, OBS 38–39 and CLM 39][e11] · [E12, OBS and CLM 05, 16 and 18–19][e12]

## Counting who has undertaken what

On the empty Nakano stage Haru recalls Kyoka’s support at the earlier live and offers it back. Rio corrects the count. All four are making the offer. Yukine and Momiji tell Kyoka they will be with her even if it goes wrong. They can now promise a kind of company they helped deprive the original trio of when they left. The support also makes room for the performer to decide how far to go. Kyoka’s mock tears give the others a brief chance to misjudge her, followed by a laugh and a small gesture at her eye that leaves room for actual feeling. She says she will do what Naoki asked, then revises that to doing more than he asked. Haru’s initial account would have left this occasion as the return of one favor between two friends. Rio’s intervention gives the other three a share of the undertaking, and Kyoka’s revision remains hers. [E12, OBS 01–02, CLM 02, 21 and 31][e12]

The office at the end makes a smaller but harder test of the same distinction. Facing a new condition, Kyoka takes the first turn and then checks her companions’ answers. All four are present and have a chance to answer for themselves. The proposed contest lies beyond the broadcast. What the story supplies is a change in how the five authorize a next step. There is a history behind every answer, and the person making the request still has to hear each of them. [E12, OBS and CLM 18–19][e12]

Gemini’s frozen interpersonal study §2.1 recognizes both the cross-unit relationships and the apparent reversal from Kyoka embracing Haru in E09 to silencing her in E10. Its notion of horizontal parity describes the restored group at a useful scale. One way to test it is to ask what changes in each transaction. The names and intentions of the four supporters can be recovered in E12 when Rio corrects Haru, even though the assignment and who decides how to perform it remain asymmetrical. The account of recovery gains precision if the ability to dissent and the ability to give a particular kind of help can vary independently. The service Yukine offers in E07 and the voice Haru recovers in E11 are consequential even during disagreement. Gemini §2.2 usefully preserves the difference between Haru’s and Ren’s views of the locker-room rupture. It reads Homare’s challenge and the performance as part of three-person de-escalation. The order of the scenes limits what can be assigned to that mechanism. Haru has already refused. Homare’s account of Ren’s desire is contested in the conversation in which she offers it. Ren’s later recognition is private, and the agency-level proposal has a separately reported source. Together they show how a refusal can be restated, witnessed and eventually acknowledged. The stronger causal claim that Ren personally converts the old friendship into the specific Budokan event would need another scene.
