# Hotaru and the warmth left in the seats

Hotaru likes an auditorium after the audience has gone home. She tells one child why. The seats still hold something of the people who sat in them. That is where Haru Nabatame finds her after Hotaru’s 2018 Budokan concert, with a little brown bag Haru lost. Hotaru returns it, asks what Haru thought of the show and requests that Haru keep this habit and their meeting to herself. Then she is about to go. Haru has to call her back in order to tell her the thing that will matter to both of them later. A girl on a stage can hold thousands of people’s attention. The same girl off the stage can give one stranger her attention, hear an ambition neither came prepared to discuss, and let the stranger leave without taking down her name. (SPV2-OBS-E12-09/10/11/25/26, about 529–710 s · SPV2-CLM-E12-09/11.)

The performer billed as 松口螢 is the person who appears on Naoki Hinase’s phone as ケイ, Kei, and calls him ナオ, Nao. E01 supplies the caller’s display name and an established star called Hotaru. E11 gives them associated casting and gives Kei a strong interest in the promising girl Naoki manages. E12 binds the identities across scenes. In the old hall Naoki starts with a cut-off け, then says ほたる at about 669–670 s and receives the familiar Nao. In the new hall he addresses the pink-haired visitor as けい at about 964 s. Both completed addresses are written 螢 in the subtitles. The visitor recognizes Haru, shares Kei’s unfinished lyric business and uses their familiar mock punishment. What was disclosed late is the connection between an already active private life and an already active public one. It was reasonable for someone watching earlier episodes to keep the two records apart. SPV2-CH-KEI remains a route to those early encounters. The incomplete syllable, shared cast credit and a subtitle note taken individually would be weak evidence for this identification. The precise legal status and reading of every form of her name remain open. (SPV2-OBS-E01-07, 483.858–533.55 s · SPV2-OBS-E11-01/19/21 · SPV2-OBS-E12-11/26, native billing at the finale · SPV2-ADJ-E12-018.)

## What different people found at one concert

A shop broadcast in the series’ 2022 setting presents Hotaru as eighteen and credits her with enormous attendance and sales. The figures are publicity within the story. Her 2018 concert gives a closer look at the work the fame attaches to. In *Sweet Surrender* she extends an invitation to shine and performs a lyric whose speaker claims to see through the addressee. Dancers use their arms and bodies to frame her, screens carry her image, and the show gives spectators expressions and gestures they can answer. Her ability to command attention has witnesses. The lyric’s lover and its extraordinary powers have no comparable standing as evidence about Hotaru in private. A scoped comparison of E01 and E11 identifies shared performance audio with different crowd layers near the edges. A sustained final pitch takes on vibrato late, then Ren’s reflection is carried by a different orchestral passage. In Rio’s E06 recollection a listener found Hotaru’s spoken さあ、輝こう！ and instrumental music with Rio’s speech; no identifiable sung Hotaru syllable was established in that 392–438 s return. A remembered concert can illustrate what Rio says about hearing her without the edit replaying the thing Rio heard. (SPV2-OBS-E01-01/03/24, 0–136 and 182.95–275.34 s · SPV2-PF-E01-01 · SPV2-OBS-E06-04 · E06-D06 · SPV2-ADJ-E11-018.)

Kyoka Tamaki attends because a classmate is paying her back for help with homework. She resents the wait, sees no use for the merchandise, then goes looking for the lightstick she did not buy. Outside the hall she announces that she will become an idol. Rio Seibu won tickets for her friend Aoi, initially feared getting lost in a crowd and leaves with the wish to give people strength. Yukine Gionji is a child actor sent to study what a real smile might mean. Her account identifies other spectators’ smiles as something she wants to be able to bring about. A native E11 cut at 598.35–599.51 s also places Momiji Ito among the spectators, a light against her cheek. Momiji gives no parallel account of deciding anything there. None of those encounters establishes a personal response to the girl concerned from Hotaru. (SPV2-OBS-E04-22, 226.25–442.95 s · SPV2-OBS-E06-03/04/23/24 · SPV2-OBS-E08-01/08 · SPV2-OBS-E11-10/57.)

Ren Kurogane changes the terms of her own explanation as she watches. The audience is absorbed, yes, but it is Hotaru who is actively drawing them in. Voice, gaze, heat and radiance give Ren a concrete ambition. Haru and their longtime friend Homare are with her. In ordinary life the three repeat and debate a Hotaru doughnut advertisement. Homare can love the performer, dispute the claim the others make for the food and decide she would prefer to stay on the audience side. Ren later invites both girls to audition. That action belongs to Ren. The direct conversation Hotaru has with Haru is possible because Haru turns back for the bag and the other two do not. Even Haru’s subsequent career depends on decisions and friends that Hotaru did not arrange. We know much more about what it was like for these spectators to discover her than about her internal experience of singing to them. Her liking for the hall afterward is one precious first-person exception. (SPV2-OBS-E09-21 · SPV2-OBS-E11-23/24, especially cues 926–960 · SPV2-OBS-E12-09/25.)

Haru tells her it hurts to get strange looks for loving an idol. She would like anyone to be able to say which idol they love. She tries out a marker or arrow for the visible guide that might point the way. Hotaru supplies 道標. Haru finds the image of a guide so bright that people could find it from anywhere. When Hotaru later returns to the image, the source hearing gives 輝くしるべ, a shining guide; the written 道標 alone leaves its precise pronunciation unstated. Hotaru helps name it *shine post*, calls this girl a 金の卵, a promising prospect, and asks whether Haru might become an idol herself. The moral wish comes from Haru. The vocabulary, vocational question and suggestion of an excellent manager come in answer to it. Hotaru does not put her through a test, make an offer on behalf of an agency or enroll her. Naoki arrives partway through and later asks who the girl was. Years later it is Haru who has recognized him and who tells him about meeting Hotaru. The person best placed to see promise in a stranger cannot decide whether that stranger wants the work. Hotaru’s question makes it possible for Haru to imagine choosing it. (SPV2-OBS-E12-08/09/10/11/12, 469–765.014 s · SPV2-CLM-E12-10/11 · SPV2-ADJ-E12-025/027.)

## What she can ask of Nao

The earlier messages on Naoki’s phone include an invitation to lunch and an extravagant sentence of 切腹, seppuku, when he has already eaten. The same joke later does duty for an unanswered call, a ticket, a refused errand and an awkwardly timed question about her work. This is an established privilege between two people who know how to answer each other. Some occasions are quite serious. When he leaves his earlier work because he thinks staying will cause harm, she objects that leaving can hurt people too. She invokes an older brother’s obligations. He calls them childhood friends. She threatens to give up her own activity and asks whether this is the end. His apology does not give her the reassurance she sought. There is real hurt here alongside an appeal that may be calculated to change his mind. Her threat gives no certain account of what she would have done. When he says she is shining during the phone call, the picture supplies no image of the remote speaker and the listening check found no associated chime. Familiar teasing accounts for their exchange without supposing he can inspect her face over the phone. (SPV2-OBS-E01-07, 483.858–533.55 s · E01-phone.)

Once Naoki accepts the provisional Brightest post, Kei wants him to return. When he refuses, she asks for a call and tells him to do the work properly. When he accepts the full job she objects again. He assumes she resents the job; she says the trouble is that he did not invite her. Later she establishes she is free on the day of a regular performance, asks for special admission and accepts his promise of a different occasion after he turns her down. She can seek a personal privilege and live with a professional boundary. The distinction is often where the wit lies. To someone else’s audience she offers a public invitation. To one old friend she can state an absurd consequence and wait for him to discover the quite practicable thing she wants. With Haru alone, by contrast, she asks about Haru’s experience, volunteers something that might make it easier to answer, then listens long enough for a child to find her own words. A mock death sentence in a prospective fan’s mouth would have very little basis in these scenes. (SPV2-OBS-E02-27, 850.02–903.79 s · SPV2-OBS-E03-28, 427.71–462.65 s · SPV2-OBS-E04-28, 1060.28–1098.78 s · SPV2-OBS-E12-25.)

The tower of Kantendō roll cakes would make an excellent photograph. She wants Naoki to get it, is short of time, says there is no mail order and reminds him a delivery would mean seeing her. He finds the listing. In E07 there is a price and an order button. In E10 he says he sent it. By then it is plain from her response that receiving an object did not satisfy the request for his company. It need not follow that the cake meant nothing to her. A missed lunch and this highly particular sweet give us some knowledge of leisure she would like to share and the use she makes of an errand. They give no dependable account of a favorite food, how much she actually ate or whether a photograph was taken. Her private circumstances, romantic history and sexual or romantic attraction remain undisclosed. Sibling language and childhood intimacy can carry great weight in a friendship without settling any of those questions. (SPV2-OBS-E01-07 · SPV2-OBS-E07-04/16/26, product listing 332.207 s · SPV2-OBS-E10-23.)

## The unfinished words

Kei has a career to attend to as well as a friend to chase. In E05 she mentions a single in three months and asks whether Naoki will write for her. He cites his position at Brightest. It is a request made to him, neither a credit on a past release nor an agreement to supply the new one. In E10 he asks how the new song is going. Her ギリギリ leaves room for a tight deadline already met or one still pressing. She identifies a need for stimulus and wants to see the girls who have made him work so hard. She tells him his work is reaching people while he has little to show the five beyond the lag between promotion and sales. Her confidence encourages him. The story has not shown her an individual sale or the evidence the five will see later. In E11 she has failed to buy a ticket, revives the issue of a seat, calls Haru a promising discovery and teases him for something he has missed. He offers to find a seat and asks about the lyrics she has put out of mind. She can notice a friend’s unfinished business and be disconcerted by a question about her own in the same call. (SPV2-OBS-E05-26, 1011.34–1048.48 s · SPV2-OBS-E10-23 · SPV2-ADJ-E10-016/027 · SPV2-OBS-E11-01/21.)

At Nakano she comes in a cap to watch with Naoki. She recognizes Haru and Kyoka occupying the two front positions and thinks the solution interesting. Naoki tells her what he sees in Kyoka and why going beyond accuracy would depend on what Kyoka herself wanted. By now he has made the connection between Haru and the girl with the bag. Hotaru has grounds for affectionate exasperation at how long it took. Kyoka thanks the people who got TINGS here and the five undertake to get up again after being hurt. Hotaru says she thinks she can write good lyrics and is going home. Naoki asks about meeting everybody. She leaves him with a charge to support the five and an allusion to their old penalty should he fail. She has selected the terms on which she will participate. Someone who spent months pressing for an invitation can use it fully without needing a meeting backstage. Haru’s idea returns in Hotaru’s own language, and the possibility of a new lyric emerges from something she has received as an audience member. There is no completed text, title, recipient or transfer. The real insert-song credits and the next stage Brightest offers its five members answer different questions. (SPV2-OBS-E12-14/15/16/17/20/26/27/28, especially native 964.630, 992.700 and 1117.033 s · SPV2-ADJ-E12-026/027.)

## A comparable invitation

Suppose this performer, after Nakano, meets another young audience member returning for a lost item. If time and privacy permit, there is moderate support for her asking what the child saw and letting the child speak. The single strong precedent depends on what Haru then volunteered. A practical thank-you and a prompt departure would be entirely consistent if the visitor wanted only the object. If instead an invitation from Naoki offers a close look at a show and a compulsory public appearance of her own, distinguish the two attractions. A chance to see a performance, see him and stimulate unfinished writing has repeated support. Agreement to appear publicly on someone else’s terms has little. Even an enthusiastic answer to one invitation would be poor evidence that she will write a lyric for a named person by a particular date. With Naoki her mock authority is well attested. How she handles bullying from a stranger, teaches technique during rehearsal, negotiates contracts or sustains a romantic relationship lies beyond the people and circumstances we have seen.
