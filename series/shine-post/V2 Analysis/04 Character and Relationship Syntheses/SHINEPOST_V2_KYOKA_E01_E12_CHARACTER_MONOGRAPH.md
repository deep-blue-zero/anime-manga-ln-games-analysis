# Kyoka Tamaki and the work of standing in front

On the day before TINGS plays Nakano Sunplaza, Kyoka Tamaki looks out at two thousand empty seats. She believes she can do what her manager asked without making a mistake. She wants to know whether doing it will be enough. Haru Nabatame answers from experience. Kyoka once trusted Haru enough to ask for a performance the other girls might struggle to match. Haru asks to return that trust. Rio Seibu, Yukine Gionji and Momiji Ito each give Kyoka a reason to expect company if something goes wrong. Kyoka lowers her head long enough for her friends to worry she is crying, then announces that her joke has succeeded. There is moisture at her eyes. After the laugh she raises the promise she has just made. She will do more than she was told. Here the professional who knows how to follow directions and the girl who loves to put one over on her friends make the same decision. (SPV2-OBS-E12-01, SPV2-OBS-E12-02, SPV2-OBS-E12-21, 6.66–111.46 s, particularly 67.025 s.)

玉城杏夏, introduced as たまききょうか, is a schoolgirl and a performer with Brightest Production. Some fans call her O-kyon. She helps host the regular lives at Bright Station, sells tickets in person and performs in the three-member TiNgS with Haru and Rio. Yukine and Momiji share an earlier five-person practice history with them and later return to form TINGS onstage. Kyoka’s initial professional wish is a CD debut. Her more difficult wish is to be special to someone. She can spot the difference between a good group and a group strangers have had a chance to hear. She finds it much harder to decide what a stranger might come specifically to hear from her. A single fan has an answer several episodes before Kyoka can use it. (SPV2-OBS-E01-08, E01 name card f0517, 540.77–616 s and the ensuing meeting, cues 177–249 · SPV2-OBS-E03-23, E03-27 · SPV2-OBS-E09-24, E09-27.)

## A girl who checks the time

Before the missed solo, even before the auditions, Kyoka was the pupil other people could rely on. Her red-haired classmate 池柴朱 asks for homework help again. Kyoka obliges. In her own recollection, doing what parents and teachers prescribed earned her good results and made her useful to other people. Nobody could give her an assignment whose reward would be to matter uniquely to somebody. Her classmate repays the homework favor with a trip to Hotaru’s 2018 Budokan concert. Kyoka would rather go home than shop for merchandise. The lightstick seems unnecessary. She checks the 18:00 ticket against 18:10 on her phone. Then Hotaru appears. Kyoka looks for the lightstick and lifts her empty hand among all the pink ones. By the time she is outside, she is holding a lit stick and telling the very friend who had to drag her along that she will become an idol. Kyoka knows how it feels to enter a room unconvinced and leave with a decision nobody made for her. On this occasion we know what the concert did to *this* spectator. We have no account of Hotaru noticing her individually. (SPV2-OBS-E04-22, E04-43, E04-44, E04-54, E04-55, especially 236–276, 338–435 s, JP cues 834–887.)

Her first response to having an ambition of her own is to find out how one does the work. She researches entry routes, submits applications, receives rejections and submits again. An audition panel credits her meticulous work and struggles to name what distinguishes her. Yuki Hinase sees promise and brings her to Brightest. Once there, work does not translate into marks the way it did at school. Flyering, rehearsing and playing to a few people continue after the apparent victory of selection. In Haru, Kyoka finds a second person she considers special. What she envies and admires is quite exact. Haru can adjust when something changes and still smile. That is Kyoka’s account from beside her, before any question of an anniversary wager and before she knows what smiling and adjusting cost Haru. Kyoka has a ready measure of her own ability to reproduce what she has learned. Comparing that with what she sees Haru do in an emergency leaves out everything a particular listener might want to hear from Kyoka. (SPV2-OBS-E04-23, E04-24, E04-46, 452.59–696.90 s · SPV2-OBS-E08-22, E08-25, JP cues 797–875, 948–1019.)

The series lets her remain funny, and sometimes very unfunny, alongside this preoccupation. She asks the newly arrived manager, Naoki Hinase, to sit on the meeting-room floor despite the available chairs. She explains how the tension she created and the release of her reveal ought to have produced a great laugh. Haru gets on with the meeting. In a memory of helping at an FFF show, a customer takes Kyoka’s mock refusal of entry seriously. Yukine has to apologize and invite the visitor inside. In an established live the same taste for a false ending works differently. Kyoka bids the audience a premature goodbye and two pictured spectators with pink lights call back 帰りませーん. Other people turn toward them. At Nakano her friends know her well and still fall for the apparent tears. What she enjoys is constructing and springing a joke. Its reception depends on a listener’s ability to tell when it is safe to play along. She misreads that distinction often enough for Yukine to recognize the habit even when Kyoka is being earnest. (SPV2-OBS-E01-09, 620.18–652 s · SPV2-OBS-E02-28, E02-33 and E02-reply, 17:21–17:36, JP cues 1081–1085 · SPV2-OBS-E08-23, cues 876–892 · SPV2-OBS-E12-21, cues 1–36.)

Much of Kyoka’s reliability consists of small judgments made while other things are happening. She gives her ticket subtotal as seven beside Haru’s approximate fifteen. She reminds Haru of the customers still waiting when Rio steps away from a discouraging sales line. Earlier she also turned Haru back to ticket sales just as Haru was about to explain their history with Yukine and Momiji. Both interruptions have a stated practical purpose. The second purpose a friend might see in either, sparing Rio observation or protecting a group confidence, remains an inference. In the five’s study session she stands with a book while the other four work at the table. She notices how much work they have left Yukine and can point out a thing Rio’s newest spinning maneuver actually does. Yukine later ties Kyoka’s own loose shoelace for her. Tenderness between these girls can amount to letting somebody kneel and finish a knot in the middle of a disagreement. (SPV2-OBS-E02-03, E02-04, E02-05, E02-24, 194.98–315.87 and 352.96–545.35 s · SPV2-OBS-E03-05, 608.50–618.38 s · SPV2-OBS-E07-25, cues 1011–1034 · SPV2-OBS-E08-08, E08-22, E08-23, study board at 725.308 s.)

Kyoka goes out in a cap and glasses and stops for a hamburger on her own. In the fast-food shop in E04 she takes off her glasses while nearby students discuss HY:RAIN. We briefly see the warm exchange she imagines having with girls who recognize O-kyon. Liking cheeseburgers is the answer she has ready in that fantasy. The real girls leave for karaoke. Later, over a real meal, Kyoka tells Naoki to eat the pickle he has removed. In E11 she has a bitten hamburger in one hand when she spots two friends who need a way out of public view. Food here gives her the pleasures of a small indulgence, a private disappointment, the upper hand over an adult, and an interruption worth dropping everything for. It establishes a repeated choice of venue and meal. Her favorite food, visual acuity, home arrangements and a larger leisure routine remain unknown. The soft toys and Hotaru poster at her bedside are visible possessions. The anime gives no comparable access to her romantic history, attraction or intimate preferences. (SPV2-OBS-E03-21, 1.64–70.57 s · SPV2-OBS-E04-25, E04-26, E04-59, 704.29–830.02 and 1028–1055 s · SPV2-OBS-E11-04, E11-07, especially 348.72–356.52 s.)

## What Tokka came back to hear

At their first meeting with Naoki the three idols have an immediate reason to seek help. President Yuki has made the group’s continuation depend on filling an anniversary hall vastly larger than their regular house. Kyoka first asks why they should entrust their future to a man unwilling to take the job. She joins Haru because the three girls will share the consequences. When Naoki later asks what they want to become, Kyoka says it would be enough for the group to be special even if she were special to nobody. Gold and a heard shimmer accompany her answer. The visual and auditory convention tells us that Naoki has grounds to doubt the answer as given. It does not identify the false clause. Her work for the group and her own ambition both have independent histories. (SPV2-OBS-E01-13, 857.84–953.72 s · SPV2-OBS-E01-17, 1101.46–1129.69 s, E01-motive · SPV2-OBS-E04-22, E04-26.)

E03 opens inside Kyoka’s nightmare. She forgets the opening of *一歩前ノセカイ*, cannot reach her partners and looks out at a distressed Tokka. When the episode later shows the remembered concert, Haru covers the lost opening. At 940.75–944.50 s Tokka holds her light, dry-eyed. Neither scene gives us an actual spectator condemning Kyoka. Yukine is the person who tells the manager what happened and that Kyoka subsequently gave up taking center. Haru wonders if covering made things worse. Kyoka herself knows she forgot the words. E04 later reveals to us that Yuki offered her the center on the strength of her MC work. Yuki began to give her a way out when she saw her apprehension. Kyoka interrupted to ask for the part. She personally told Tokka to wait for the song. Her enthusiasm preceded the public lapse. Its later disclosure changes the meaning of her reluctance for a viewer. The new event in the present is having to decide what to do when the same wish comes back to her from across a ticket table. (SPV2-OBS-E03-21, E03-23, E03-26, 1.64–70.57, 338.07–377.57 and 917.05–961.87 s · E03-fan · SPV2-OBS-E04-05, E04-46, 584.15–681.30 s.)

Tokka buys a ticket. She asks whether she may keep believing. At the handshake event she remembers seeing the look in Kyoka’s eyes when she was out with flyers. That persistence gave her courage. Tokka manages to name the song and say she believes Kyoka can sing it as the staff moves her along. Kyoka recognizes her by name, thanks her politely and initially answers as though the question were whether she still sings at all. Later she remembers the fan’s words while resisting Naoki. A wish meant as encouragement can weigh on the person who once asked for it. Kyoka’s professional argument is sound on its terms. People need to know a group exists before they can fill a hall. She can work as its main MC, hold the flank and support Haru. Her more doubtful test is whether *she* could give a better performance at center than Haru. Tokka already has a reason of her own to want Kyoka there, and she has not asked her to defeat anybody. Pressure, comparative shame and loyalty to work Kyoka does well all fit what we see. The show gives no basis for ranking shame or guilt as her sole motive. (SPV2-OBS-E03-23, E03-27, E03-48, 338.07–377.57, 757.37–876.56, 1018.62–1073.16 and 1082.28–1170.70 s · E03-belief and E03-fan.)

Kyoka knows how to draw a boundary even with someone whose work she values. She asks Naoki to stop pressing at about 847.5 s and again at 1162.8 s. Her instruction about keeping the earlier episode private goes to Haru and Yukine. Yukine interprets the repetition as a comic invitation to do the opposite and tells him herself. Kyoka subsequently complains. A gold cue accompanies the earlier denial of wanting center and a positive control occurs at about 1085 s. The later conversation about singing *this* song has no matching cue, even through sustained views of her refusal. A listener hears strain there. Naoki assigns Rio the MC and Kyoka the final song’s center at a six-person studio meeting. The episode cuts on Kyoka’s face before she consents. In E04 he says the assignment stands whatever her answer and she acknowledges the instruction. That is a very different speech act from her earlier request to Yuki. (SPV2-OBS-E03-07, E03-10, E03-11, E03-26, E03-27, E03-29 · E03-authority and E03-belief · SPV2-OBS-E04-21, 90.47–212.69 s, cues 823–827.)

Haru wants an audience to hear the song with Kyoka singing it, and Rio agrees. Yukine, who herself has yet to debut at this point, challenges a colleague who has a stage and is ready to leave the middle of it. She also tells Naoki where to find her. At the shop he borrows Kyoka’s own prank flourish, receives a withering review and stays to hear her out. She can finally say that she loves the work, wants a bigger stage and would like to become someone like Hotaru. Naoki hears something wanting in the claim that supporting the group is sufficient. The gold around Kyoka at about 942 s supports that reading in context. He offers to take responsibility for helping when an idol makes a mistake. For somebody whose last mistake seemed to invalidate an entire ambition, knowing *what happens afterwards* offers more than praise. She thanks him and can still scold him about the pickle. Yuki had given her an entrance into the profession. Yukine confronts her as a fellow worker. Naoki both imposed this assignment and gave her a way to make it a choice she wanted to carry out. (SPV2-OBS-E04-01, E04-07, E04-13, E04-26, E04-27 · 176.08–194.34, 846.33–859.37, 861.19–1057.91 s.)

When Rio introduces the last number at Bright Station, Kyoka steps into the exposed opening and stays there. Haru and Rio continue beside her and the first line is hers to deliver this time. Other pink lights come up, the house is reflected in her pupil, and the closing wink cuts to Tokka smiling and tipping down among concerned neighbors. That edit lets the viewer connect the two women. It cannot establish that Kyoka saw exactly what happened to Tokka from the stage. Tokka’s response is one person’s response. (SPV2-OBS-E04-29, E04-49, E04-65, E04-66, E04-68 · E04-D04, owner’s second pass · 1222–1245, 1267.77–1282.57 and 1354.56–1369.58 s.)

At about 1223.525 s her lead on ワタシノ… is identifiable. The owner tentatively heard Tokka join around 1:17–1:18 in a clip beginning at episode 1149 s, or 1226–1227 s in the episode. A separately reported fuller texture begins around 1226.2 s. The picture still shows Kyoka singing then, with the cut to open-mouthed Tokka between 1229.437 and 1229.478 s. An audience singalong is plausible. Backing or doubling onstage also fits. Ensemble sound is distinguishable by 1234.8–1240.3 s, and the later テーブル line has no secure individual assignment. (E04-D04.)

## What she asks other people to risk

The immediate sequel to retaking the center is ordinary work. Kyoka thanks Yukine for helping her keep her dream and admits she is frightened about filling Nakano. She makes the invitation less direct than the thanks. There are “people” she would very much like to have come and help. Yukine calls this one of her bad jokes. Kyoka accepts a day working in a sweetshop with Haru when she learns the manager became her fan at the recent live. She encourages patience about sales. She and Haru both think they know how Rio will decide between the shop and a television appearance. They are surprised by Rio’s choice. When Rio stands on the street recommending Haru and Kyoka to a passerby and putting herself down in the process, the two friends she is promoting are absent. Knowing how to invite Yukine to help has hardly given Kyoka access to what Rio is doing on their behalf. (SPV2-OBS-E05-22, E05-23, E05-27, E05-29, especially 03:38–04:13, 07:21–08:29 and 20:35.81–21:18.95.)

When a *new* song becomes available in E06, Kyoka puts her hand up first. 私、やりたいです is an uncomplicated “I want to do it.” Haru competes for it too. Naoki gives the part to Rio and proposes a number with almost no dancing. Kyoka can say why she supports that decision. Dancing is one way of giving people an enjoyable show. She can also be remarkably rough on a girl who fears the same exposure she used to dread. Who would turn down center? Haru calls attention to the irony. At the mall Kyoka enjoys her preceding performance, publicly promises the crowd another reason to enjoy the group and turns the introduction over to Rio. Rio has had reason to call her reliable since Kyoka encouraged her after an early fall. Rio also gets to meet the limit of that reliability when her friend’s hard-won conviction sounds like a demand. The change in Kyoka is observable in two actions with different costs. She asks for a part to which another girl has an equal claim and she helps that girl hold the attention of the room when the choice goes against her. Whether she would have volunteered first for the *old* exposed solo under exactly its former conditions remains untested. (SPV2-OBS-E06-21, 3.18–49.28 and 141.41–215.32 s · SPV2-OBS-E06-25, 509.10–582.14 s · SPV2-OBS-E06-28, E06-29, 941.97–1115.82 and 1289.50–1368.26 s.)

With Yukine, Kyoka can be grateful, combative and empirically quite shrewd. She argues for five-person practice because they could cover one another and learn more together. Yukine already has a partner in Momiji and declines to explain their departure. Kyoka adds daily access to her jokes as a recruitment benefit. After the loose-lace intervention she complains that Yukine is stubborn and supplies the more flattering “strength of will” when Rio applies the same description to her. Later, with evidence of repeated care to set beside the two girls’ claim to be rivals, Kyoka remarks how poor they were at playing enemies. With Momiji, persistence can cross a line. In the effort to recruit her the trio physically holds Momiji while Kyoka plays back a recording and asks the much more precise question of whether *both* would return. The question distinguishes willingness to rejoin from willingness to leave Yukine. The pressure makes agreement an unsafe inference. Momiji, in turn, keeps a familiar joke about Kyoka’s stubbornness alive even when Kyoka is too discouraged to answer it. Kyoka can infer an allegiance from behavior and still overstep in trying to change what her friends do. (SPV2-OBS-E07-25, E07-27, JP cues 1011–1034, 1078–1140 · SPV2-OBS-E08-29, cues 1133–1147 · SPV2-OBS-E11-39 and SPV2-ADJ-E11-039, 1222–1223.5 s.)

E09 changes what her trust in Haru asks of *her*. In Haru’s absence Yuki tells the four that Haru had previously left HY:RAIN and asked to hold back at Brightest until her partners grew stronger. Kyoka takes the delay in telling them to mean Haru did not trust them *yet*. This is the excluded friend’s inference. What Kyoka decides is open to everyone in the room. She tells Yukine and Momiji to return, treats failure to secure every member’s full effort as fatal under the existing Nakano condition, and answers Rio’s very practical objection about redistributing parts with *Be Your Light!!*, the song they once managed to bring together as five. The two returning performers object to being ordered around. Naoki carries the decision to Haru and leaves her to come of her own accord. Onstage Kyoka introduces the five and asks for trust with the public listening. She takes Haru’s full ability seriously enough to be left bent over afterwards. Her first account of that gap is that they have room to improve. Then she tells Haru to stop carrying everything alone and calls for practice tomorrow. Haru embraces her. The promise Kyoka can keep concerns her response to being outperformed. It requires neither equal ability nor immunity to a painful comparison. (SPV2-OBS-E09-23, E09-24, cues 838–925 · SPV2-OBS-E09-25, cues 926–972 · SPV2-OBS-E09-27, E09-28, E09-29, cues 989–1080.)

Ren Kurogane forces a harder question. What right does a girl acquire by standing up for someone else? At the ticket campaign Kyoka thanks a named online contact, reminds her anxious colleagues that Naoki warned results could take time and offers an anniversary flyer to her old schoolfriend. Her friend, interrupted over a burger, fans out tickets she already has. Tokka, who once spoke to Kyoka across a table, now publicly recommends O-kyon. These are concrete effects of repeated contact. Against them stands Ren, Haru’s former groupmate and childhood friend, come to recruit her back to HY:RAIN. Ren appraises each of the four as inadequate company for Haru. What she can find to say of Kyoka is that she gets things right. Kyoka steps in front of Haru and proposes Nakano as the place to see what TINGS can do for her. Ren adds Haru’s return as the price of failure. Kyoka accepts without consulting Haru. The two tell Haru 春は黙ってて together. Their shared timing and Haru’s objection that this directly concerns her give the moment its sting. The girl who asked Haru to trust her has briefly made a decision for Haru as readily as Ren has. The later replay of Ren’s assessment in Kyoka’s hearing supports taking personal hurt seriously alongside loyalty. There is little warrant for making either the whole reason for the wager. (SPV2-OBS-E10-22, E10-24, E10-25, especially 735.8–736.15 s · SPV2-OBS-E10-15, E10-17, E10-28, cues 1081–1116 · SPV2-ADJ-E10-048 and SPV2-ADJ-E10-064.)

The next day people who recognize Ren want her picture. A mouthful of hamburger does not stop Kyoka from beckoning *both* Haru and Ren out of the way. Once clear of the crowd, she criticizes the inadequacy of Ren’s disguise. At the park she discovers that the old promise the two girls mean predates her own wager. Ren’s account of starting again with her group earns Kyoka’s admiration. She begins to consider whether Haru’s future might look different alongside people who worked this hard. Haru is the one who puts that possibility into words, explains why she has chosen TINGS and asks Kyoka for discretion. Back at Brightest the others know of the old promise and Haru blames Kyoka for telling. The transfer itself falls across a cut. Kyoka has left a friend’s confidence vulnerable, just as she once minded having her own history told. The scene leaves both her precise words and her reason for sharing unknown. (SPV2-OBS-E11-07, E11-17, E11-18, E11-22, E11-28, especially 348.72–414.83, 1016.6–1106.81 and 1128.42–1232.02 s.)

Folded over the meeting-room table, Kyoka can name what the other women do *to somebody*. Rio’s voice reaches a listener, Momiji’s dance surprises its observer and Yukine’s expression holds attention. About herself she can name a procedure. She does not make mistakes with what she has learned. The adjudicated hearing at 1215.4–1217.7 s gives 私は、覚えたことは間違えません, while the Japanese transcription has が after こと. That restriction to learned material matters. Yukine earlier credited her quick pickup, observed stiffness and later saw progress in expression. E03 showed someone who knew a lyric and nevertheless lost an entrance. Tokka’s repeated testimony, the returning sweetshop manager and the schoolfriend with tickets supply counterexamples to “I have nothing.” None is a like-for-like technical comparison with HY:RAIN. Naoki agrees that accuracy alone may fall short for the two thousand people they expect, then offers a way Kyoka in particular can contribute. E11 ends with her looking up. Her friends know more about the plan by the next episode than an audience did at that cut. (SPV2-OBS-E08-03, E08-25, JP cues 797–875 and 948–1019 · SPV2-OBS-E11-18, E11-20, E11-29, 1128.42–1280.82 s · SPV2-ADJ-E11-049.)

## Two places at the front

At Nakano, an event that was once meant to prove the group could sell two thousand tickets acquires a harder audience test. President Yuki asks that people who came go away satisfied. Kyoka puts fear and immaturity among the things the five can take onstage. She opens the concert and later speaks from the center for all five. Between those addresses she and Haru share the front of *On Your Mark!!*. At 846.012 s the two are in the foreground and at 850.016 s the overhead view gives the five a different figure. Rio, Momiji and Yukine assume distinct positions within the number. Tokka recognizes Haru and O-kyon in front. Kyoka’s place alongside someone whose adaptability she still admires lets the audience look at either woman without requiring a mistake by one to give the other a turn. (SPV2-OBS-E12-13, E12-14, E12-21, E12-24, E12-27, especially cues 843–884 and 986–1025.)

There is a corresponding distinction between whom we see and whom we hear. The commercial parts and the owner-supplied color-coded guide agree and align closely with the episode recording. In the first verse, Rio takes the first unit at about 851.3–855.7 s, Kyoka the next at 855.7–858.8, Rio and Kyoka share 858.8–862.1, Momiji takes 862.1–866.3 and Yukine 866.3–872.0. Haru enters at the following pre-chorus. The recording grows fuller toward Yukine’s unit. The named assignments identify parts, while the TV stereo leaves room for doubles and backing. When Haru and Kyoka face each other in the feather-and-hand passage, the owner heard “Full ensemble” through the momentary tightening at 887.7–889.7 s. The guide highlights all five and the published chorus assignment agrees. The principal clasp comes around 897.48 s. The intimacy of that image consequently belongs to an ensemble refrain in which Kyoka also has a distinct voice. Counting two pictured faces would get both her relationship with Rio in the verse and the sound of this refrain wrong. (SPV2-OBS-E12-14, E12-63, E12-64 · SPV2-ADJ-E12-013 and SPV2-ADJ-E12-015.)

Naoki tells Hotaru, from the audience, that if Kyoka wishes it she can see beyond the limit she has set herself. His heard 間違いない at 977.4–978.3 s expresses *his certainty*. The transcription’s 間違えない would suggest not making a mistake. The claim belongs to the manager watching her. Ren comes away recognizing Haru as TINGS’ Haru. Tokka closes her eyes as hands settle on her shoulders. What can be attributed to Kyoka herself is different again. Later she thanks the president for the chance, the manager and staff for support, exacting and kind seniors, longstanding supporters and everyone who came today. She has learned to address people who can do different things for her without needing them to give a unanimous verdict. The full house, the two leads visible onstage, the performance the five complete, Ren’s response and each spectator’s satisfaction answer different questions. The missing verdict is how all two thousand felt. Her next encounter with a mistake would tell us more about the persistence of that fear than a count of occupied seats can. (SPV2-OBS-E12-15, E12-16, E12-17, E12-27, E12-28, 912.75–946.03, 957.05–1010.52 and 1016.69–1133.70 s · SPV2-ADJ-E12-026.)

At the office HY:RAIN’s agency proposes a competitive joint live at Budokan. Yuki attaches a new dissolution condition to losing. Kyoka raises her fist and is first to say they should accept. Then she asks the other four. Rio, Yukine, Momiji and Haru get to answer, and their reasons differ. This is a small, consequential change from speaking for Haru in front of Ren. It also reaches much further back. The girl who first went to Budokan as repayment for somebody’s homework can now own the wish to go there again and give the others room to say what taking the stage would mean to them. The concert and its result lie beyond the television ending. (SPV2-OBS-E12-19, E12-29, especially 1234.23 s and cues 1055–1101.)

## Who gets which words

Kyoka ordinarily sounds more formal on the page than the force of what she is doing might suggest. The same courteous request construction can introduce a song, ask for a chance or close a conversation. Words addressed to a crowd can also have a second recipient standing right beside her. These are recoverable distinctions of wording, occasion and intent. The episode transcripts are derivative Japanese text checked against the specified picture or targeted hearing where available. They do not support a general impression of her pitch, tempo, breath or level of confidence.

## What the portrait predicts at close range

The following are **candidate tendencies**, retrospective and unvalidated. Each changes if a material condition changes.

- **A stage opening after a remembered error.** At the E03 boundary, a public instruction to retry the same exposed line is likely to draw a comparison with Haru and an argument for the MC work Kyoka already does well. A private opportunity to say what she wants, with someone willing to help if she misses, makes assent more plausible. Moderate support for the mechanism, high for those observed differences in response. A new song after the E04 return yields a different prediction, as her first-raised hand in E06 illustrates. Repeated refusals of unrelated, low-risk solos even with support would count against specificity to that remembered failure. (SPV2-OBS-E03-27, E04-21, E04-26, E06-21.)
- **A familiar audience offered a joke.** A planned feint and an explanation of its success are plausible even when everybody else wants to get on with work. The prediction concerns her attempt. For the invited theater response or her close friends there is support for playing along. For a first-time customer at the door, expect a real risk of distress and abstain about whether Kyoka could repair the mistake unaided. A pattern in which she consistently checked strangers’ comfort before springing the gag would weaken the latter inference. (SPV2-OBS-E01-09, E02-28, E08-23, E12-21 · E02-reply.)
- **Someone proposes to decide for her group.** By E09–E10 she is likely to ask for grounds and to suggest a concrete test spectators or colleagues could witness. Under immediate challenge she may even agree to terms affecting somebody else before asking her. Moderate support. Change the premise from a rival publicly claiming Haru to an office invitation addressed to all five, and taking the initiative can include a prompt request for their assent. A comparable public challenge in which she first sought the affected friend’s terms would narrow this reading. TINGS’ actual capacities at a future Budokan concert and Kyoka’s decisions in a private family dispute are outside its reach. (SPV2-OBS-E09-24, E10-15, E10-28, E11-17, E12-29.)

Kyoka’s remaining difficulty is one a successful performance cannot settle on its own. She can remember what another person does to a room and still lack a reliable account of what *she* does there. Her audience has seen an entrance she missed and another she delivered, a joke that scared somebody and another that drew a reply. Tokka returns across all those changes. At the end of the season Kyoka has acquired enough room in her own estimate to name a wish first and then listen to what her friends want. How she would answer another missed first line, on a larger stage or without these particular companions, stays open.
