# Rio Seibu and the right to ask

When a stranger finally stops for Rio Seibu’s flyer, Rio has a good pitch ready. Haru can do remarkable things onstage. Kyoka has worked so hard. Please come and see those two. Rio’s first accomplishment here is getting someone to listen to a girl who has spent the afternoon saying she cannot sell. Her difficulty is counting herself among the people worth coming to see. A few weeks of selling tickets will give her admirers by name, an audience chanting her own name and a fellow performer who tells her to her face exactly where she falls short. The admirers are there even before she learns how to answer them. (SPV2-OBS-E02-24, 352.96–545.35 s · SPV2-OBS-E05-10/11, 1146–1281 s · SPV2-OBS-E06-12 · SPV2-OBS-E10-13.)

聖舞理王, introduced as せいぶりお, performs for Brightest Production with Haru Nabatame and Kyoka Tamaki in the three-member TiNgS. The anniversary booking makes a particular demand on the small group. Its regular crowd numbers around thirty. Its future depends on filling two thousand seats at Nakano Sunplaza. Rio announces that Budokan would suit her better. She unpacks the characters of her name as a claim to royal distinction, and Miku already knows how to meet her on those terms. There is a private courtesy beneath this ceremony that strangers sometimes get to see first. After the other girls hurry away Rio bows properly to their prospective manager, Naoki Hinase. When the attempt to recruit him falters, she holds out her pudding. Her face shows what it costs her. She is delighted when he tells her to eat it. She can want someone’s help, enjoy the part she plays in recruiting him and want her pudding back all at once. (SPV2-OBS-E01-05, E01-08, E01-13, 337.65–428.28, 540.77–616 and 857.84–953.72 s · SPV2-OBS-E02-24.)

## The girl who got the tickets

Before her first stage, Rio expects another sort of disappearance. In a crowd she feels as though she might vanish. Music and video are available elsewhere. Why put yourself in a crush of strangers? Her schoolfriend Aoi Tanaka is a Hotaru fan. When Rio forgets her Japanese textbook, Aoi arranges to borrow one from the next class and another pupil actually hands it over. Rio enters the concert lottery for Aoi and wins tickets. Aoi thanks her. Rio credits Hotaru for the smile on Aoi’s face, overlooking the one thing only a friend paying attention to Aoi could have done. At Hotaru’s 2018 Budokan show, Rio sees other people responding to the same person. A crowd can enlarge what happens to you. She wants to give other people that feeling of strength. We learn this history in E06, after hearing Rio say in E01 that what she wants is universal praise. The later disclosure gives us her older wish. It leaves room for a young performer to want praise too. Aoi appears at Nakano. Their lives between the two concerts are largely private. (SPV2-OBS-E06-03/04, E06-23/24, 293.79–437.30 s · SPV2-CLM-E06-03/04 · SPV2-OBS-E11-10 · SPV2-OBS-E12-04.)

It takes repeated applications and rejections to enter Brightest. Rio falls at the audition. President Yuki Hinase sees a gentle shine in her. When Rio falls at an early live, Kyoka tells her they can recover and Haru tells her they still have something to give the people watching. Rio remembers Kyoka as dependable, Haru as bright and herself as ダメダメな私, hopeless me. That is Rio’s division of the credit. Neither friend asks her to conceal her distress. She decides that if she projects sufficient confidence, even at the price of being laughed at, nobody else will have to look after her feelings on top of everything else. Saying 理王様, Rio-sama, can relieve her partners of an obligation she imagines they feel. It can also be a joke, a pleasure or a way to let a regular greet her. E06 dates the decision to play this part and shows her using it again after an honest admission. It would take evidence from the particular occasion to say exactly what she hides behind it. Gold outlines and chimes sometimes give Naoki a reason to question an assurance. They supply neither its secret wording nor a measure of Rio’s dance skill. (SPV2-OBS-E01-17, E01-21 · SPV2-OBS-E06-05, E06-25, 440–582.14 s · SPV2-CLM-E06-05/06 · E02-confidence.)

At the ticket table Rio recognizes Miku and approves of her good judgment. When the customers cluster around the others, Rio looks down, complains of being the one left out again, announces boredom and leaves. She comes back with a ticket in hand. At the next venue a stranger specifically asks to buy one from her. Rio’s immediate response to the completed sale is to call her two partners and hug them. E05 later shows what she does on one announced break. It even repeats images from the earlier afternoon. Only the later excursion actually follows her far enough to show the cold approaches. There are other checks on an account of Rio as permanently demoralized. She notices Kyoka’s face in the middle of celebrating a sellout and tries to cheer her with an extravagantly named move. She nearly leaks the Nakano booking during a handshake. She distinguishes a Cyclone from a Typhoon by the direction of rotation. When Kyoka has to risk the opening of a song she once missed, Rio handles the MC position and calls both partners into the number. An appetite for being seen can put other people exactly where they need to be. Her early sale, joke and bow belong to the girl on the yellow name card; the forgotten solo and the main MC job before Rio’s turn belong to Kyoka. (SPV2-OBS-E02-24/26, 352.96–545.35 and 747.35–853.33 s · SPV2-OBS-E03-04/09/24 · SPV2-OBS-E04-09, 1100–1223.45 s · SPV2-OBS-E05-03/13.)

## What the farm camera catches

Rio’s partners recognize her habit of arriving first. Alone she checks a phone on which people praise Haru and Kyoka and encourage Rio to keep working at dance. The greetings turn grand when the others arrive. A little later she practices balance, falls and dismisses the fall. She genuinely likes the custard pudding from Kantendō. When the shop wants two guest managers the others think they know which assignment she will choose. Naoki thinks face-to-face contact at the shop offers a better chance of winning fans than the regional television shoot booked for the same day. Rio nevertheless volunteers for television, saying she dislikes being Kyoka’s extra. The choice comes *before* she knows Nanami will join her. Fear of costing the group the more important appearance fits her later behavior especially well. Wishing for a job of her own and wanting to escape a conspicuous comparison both fit the choice too. Her words never settle their relative importance. (SPV2-OBS-E05-01/03/04, E05-21/23, 0.25–32.29 and 346.96–539.58 s.)

At the farm Rio gives the crew her professional greeting and deep bow. In front of the camera she echoes the ends of Nanami’s lines and stumbles over words. Director Sato sees something worth keeping in Rio’s unguarded reactions to harvesting. In the break Rio privately asks Naoki how she did. Taking him at his word about being frank, she tastes a green pepper and keeps saying it is bitter. That part goes on the cutting-room floor. Naoki acknowledges his own failure to check in advance. Rio thinks she has caused trouble *again*. The difference matters professionally. The same director makes two judgments about two pieces of footage on one day. Rio makes one mistake into a judgment about the person her partners have to work around. Later she can smile with a pepper in another camera setup and turn a game win into a plug for TINGS. Her private taste after the second shoot, and the finished edit of either appearance, are unknown. Her repeated delight in pudding has a much better claim to being a preference. (SPV2-OBS-E05-05/06/07, E05-24/25, 549.84–1010.41 s · SPV2-OBS-E10-06, E10-22/25 · SPV2-ADJ-E10-014.)

In public Rio gives orders, confers honors and treats perseverance as a fitting undertaking for a sovereign. With Momiji Ito she can be sharply competitive, especially when unsolicited instruction comes wrapped in criticism. Alone on the street she apologizes to people she stops. Faced with an actual listener, she can say just what she admires about her partners and ask openly for a favor on their behalf. The score recedes close to the final appeal, with a tonal residue still audible in a brief gap around 1275.5 s. The impression of being exposed has a particular sound basis there. Rio does her hardest work with no assurance her partners will ever see it. Even Naoki only happens on the later part. The woman takes a flyer. Her attendance and any resultant sale remain unknown. (SPV2-OBS-E05-09/10/11/29 · SPV2-CLM-E05-09/10 · E05-D03, 1176–1284 s.)

## Whose decision the solo is

Kyoka volunteers first when Naoki asks about center. Haru wants a turn. Rio says the choice could only be herself, then demands an explanation when Naoki actually names her. He has already made the choice and stripped nearly all the dancing from the new number. Rio thinks everybody has had to give up something important to accommodate her. That is a real artistic objection as well as a fear about her place. Her partners can speak for themselves. Haru cares what the audience experiences and Kyoka understands dance as one means to that end. Rio tells all three she hates them and runs. Momiji catches her arm, tries to bring her back and offers to teach her. Rio insists on improving alone. Alone on a swing she regrets how she treated Momiji and acknowledges that she would like help. In the evening she checks the room before going in to practice privately. Naoki has waited. His theatrical entrance is embarrassing enough that she tells him so. This is already a different conversation from a fan exchange. She can tease him before she can tell him the truth. (SPV2-OBS-E06-01/02/06/07, 3.18–49.28, 141.41–291.65 and 586.36–877.44 s · SPV2-CLM-E06-02/07.)

As Rio explains it, someone who already depends on other people ought to forgo selfish requests. She calls them わがまま. Naoki grants some of the comparison with Haru and Kyoka. He disputes the conclusion that she has nothing to offer. He wants her to find out in front of listeners and asks her to take the stage. Being told that only she can do it also bears hard on someone who already feels indebted. Her eventual yes matters precisely because the afternoon contained a genuine refusal. She names Haru and Kyoka as the people she will help, then broadens the promise to everyone. Next she offers Momiji a Kantendō pudding and asks her, politely and on her own initiative, for dance lessons **if** the live succeeds. Momiji accepts both gift and request. Rio supplies the condition. Yukine Gionji sees them from the door. The scene gives Yukine no access to what Naoki and Rio said the night before. E07 subsequently shows Rio seeking Momiji’s evaluation and asking for more coaching. The pudding buys neither a grade nor a friendship. (SPV2-OBS-E06-06/07/08, 586.36–930.93 s · SPV2-CLM-E06-07/08 · SPV2-OBS-E07-21.)

At the mall Haru and Kyoka give the new song a public introduction. Rio names *Yellow Rose*. Three microphone stands make the unexpected reduction of dance visible even to a passerby. A later scoped listener hears Rio’s exposed lead from about 1124.13 s, still prominent as the chorus thickens. The mix supports a soft backing layer especially at sustained endings. It leaves the individual backing singers, self-doubling and the contribution of other sounds unresolved. The edit interleaves the girl in front of the crowd with the child, applicant and struggling colleague we now recognize. Those memories belong to the television audience’s view. On the gallery Sato, whom Naoki invited, voices her own approval. Fumu, previously especially interested in Haru, says she was drawn in. Someone asks what group the girl belongs to. When the crowd takes up her name, Rio tries her customary flourish, cries and tells Haru and Kyoka she did it. Sato’s words need never have reached her for the answer at the rail to count. Rio can at last give herself some of the credit she once assigned entirely to Hotaru. Success here proves neither that she has mastered dancing nor that strangers bought anniversary tickets. (SPV2-OBS-E06-09/10/12/28/29, 932.431 s through the final stage exchange · SPV2-CLM-E06-38/49 · E06-D06.)

## A place in a group of five

Rio still knows how to reason herself into a debt. When Naoki proposes bringing former practice partners Yukine and Momiji back into the performing group, she asks Yukine over a meal whether her poor work drove them away. Yukine wipes sauce from her face and tells her it did not. Rio gets a true answer to her own question alongside a continuing refusal to return. Later, entrusted with Haru’s melting ice cream, she starts to tell Momiji she too would like to be with her. Rio also helps hold Momiji’s leg while others play back a recording to press the point. The scheme hurts the trio’s case for freely chosen company. It gives particular force to Rio’s later objection to Haru deciding *for them* what they can bear. E08 reveals the departure as a gamble undertaken by Yukine and Momiji with Yuki to draw out Haru’s exceptional performance. In the old five Yukine once gave Rio a literal hand up and recognized when she learned the steps. Rio remembered the help long before she knew the reason for losing her helper. (SPV2-OBS-E07-24/26/27, especially 641.72–715.85 s and cues 1078–1140 · SPV2-OBS-E08-03/09/14/17.)

When the four learn about Haru’s previous group, Rio asks why Haru suffered alone. A return to five raises an immediate question she is particularly well qualified to spot. Who will sing which parts, where will they stand, and how can two first-time participants in this lineup prepare in time? Kyoka chooses *Be Your Light!!*, a song the original five practiced together. The stage goes ahead and Haru returns by her own decision. Afterward four people are bent over while Haru remains upright. Rio singles out something on which she intends to compete with her friend at the next live. She wants to beat Haru at **singing**. It is a modestly delimited claim from someone with a famously immodest stage name. It also allows Haru to excel without making Rio’s own achievement a gift from her. (SPV2-OBS-E09-07/09/24/27/28, 433.57–480.30, 547.58–602.06 and 1202–1267.99 s · SPV2-CLM-E09-24/28.)

Ren Kurogane of HY:RAIN faults Rio’s dancing while she sings and invites Haru to return to her former group. Rio notices how much Ren knows about an act she discounts. The discovery of Kantendō pudding in the visitor’s gift briefly improves Rio’s view of the giver, to Momiji’s evident exasperation. The gift passes from Ren to Naoki at the visit. Later doughnuts belong to Ren and Haru’s separate meeting. Rio can understand why Ren pursued an old promise when it is discussed in the office, eat one of the doughnuts and still want Haru to stay. What she learns in the office does not make her a witness to either private park exchange. Kyoka in turn identifies singing that moves people as Rio’s individual strength. In the empty Nakano hall Rio makes Haru’s pledge of trust in Kyoka plural. During the show she has both an individual first-verse part and a part shared with Kyoka; the assignments draw on matched recordings and published parts. After the MC she invites everyone to follow Rio-sama. In the final office meeting she answers the possibility of a joint competitive Budokan show with another boast, after Kyoka asks the others for their assent. The invitation returns to the scale of her first extravagant claim. Its result lies beyond E12. (SPV2-OBS-E10-13/16/17, E10-26/28 · SPV2-ADJ-E10-020 · SPV2-OBS-E11-18/28/29 · SPV2-OBS-E12-21/28/29 · SPV2-ADJ-E12-013.)

## Where this account travels

In a close variant after Nakano, suppose an existing fan asks Rio at the ticket table to show a new move, while there are other buyers waiting. Recognition, a familiar role and an identifiable audience give strong support to a grand answer and a playful claim of authorship. The queue and her near-disclosure of the booking leave the length and good judgment of the display uncertain. Change one premise and make this an unfamiliar director questioning the usefulness of a take. A formal greeting and a private request for an assessment have better support. If her mistake might cost two teammates their footage, self-blame could recur. The changed history with Momiji makes a specific, privately negotiated request for instruction more plausible than it was before the mall. Whether Rio would make it without an earning condition remains open. These are conditional inferences from the table, farm and practice room.

Her showy vocabulary travels best when its addressee and its purpose are known. The series gives us little ground for writing a casual evening conversation with Aoi as though all Rio’s sentences were royal proclamations. Nor does it establish her household routine, romantic attractions or intimate history. Tournament rankings, vocal range, solo recording technique, changing taste in vegetables and a future result against Haru all require a different kind of witness from the ones available here.
