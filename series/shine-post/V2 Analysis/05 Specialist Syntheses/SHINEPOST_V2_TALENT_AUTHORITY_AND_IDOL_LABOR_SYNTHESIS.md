# Who pays for a wrong call?

At a restaurant counter, Kyoka Tamaki asks her manager what will happen if she gets it wrong. Naoki Hinase has already assigned her the center of a song whose opening she once forgot. He can put it at the end of the next show. He cannot stand in the light and get her through the first line. His best answer comes when he tells her what he will do if his judgment proves mistaken. Helping an idol through a mistake is part of his job. A few minutes earlier, in the rehearsal room, he told her the casting would stand whatever she said. Both remarks come from the same capable manager. Only one gives the performer something she can count on him to do. [E04, OBS 01, 07, 13, 26][E04]

In *SHINE POST*, a proposal often reaches the person who has to carry it out after someone else has decided it is good for her. Sometimes that is ordinary direction. A show with a running order needs somebody to cast it. Sometimes the decision surrenders a friend’s place in a group or commits a colleague whom nobody has consulted. The distinction is easiest to see when a judgment proves right. Kyoka does sing well. The original five do recover a stage together. Those results tell us what the performers were able to do under pressure. To understand the practice that produced each result, we also need to know what the person making the call could reasonably know beforehand, who had a chance to answer and who would have to put things right afterward. [E03, OBS 06–11][E03] · [E08, OBS 13–17][E08] · [E09, OBS 07–18][E09]

## A president can offer a venue. Who supplies the manager?

President Yuki Hinase has a real problem. Nine months into the trio’s life, TiNgS has yet to fill Bright Station, its hundred-person theater. She gives Haru Nabatame, Kyoka and Rio Seibu three months to fill the two-thousand-seat Nakano Sunplaza at their anniversary, with dissolution in prospect if they fail. The performers can act on the news precisely because the president is still considering their future. Yet they also receive what sounds like a commitment of excellent professional help. They welcome Naoki on his way in. He has agreed to work at Brightest Production and expressly ruled out managing idols. The work of reconciling those two different understandings falls to the three people whose future Yuki has just put in doubt. Her affection for them, later expressed in private, was unavailable to them across her desk. [E01, OBS 04–05, 08, 14–19][E01]

Haru finds something smaller she can ask him to do. He can come to a lesson and watch. Afterward he still has grounds for judging that they cannot reach the larger target in their present form. Hearing what Haru wants this work to make possible for people who love idols matters to him. He offers to manage them provisionally, with his own continued participation riding on whether they fill their home theater. He has been able to say no, to watch, and to choose the terms on which he will try. Haru has done consequential recruiting work herself. She would have had a much harder time declining Yuki’s terms and retaining the group she wants a manager for. [E01, OBS 14–19][E01]

E02 gives Naoki a chance to earn that job. Hours of selling outside Bright Station have brought the trio to thirty-two tickets. Naoki calls Kikuchi, the manager of the Yura Yura Sisters. He takes the performers to her group’s concert, where Kikuchi welcomes them and authorizes them to approach the departing audience. The girls thank their hosts and sell the rest in about seven and a half minutes. Their suitability for idol work appears to have changed extraordinarily fast. What has actually changed includes access to a crowd, the time of approach and the hosts’ permission. Naoki has asked the person in a position to offer him a place to work. The performers still have to interest strangers, and buyers will make their own judgments in the theater. This is a managerial success with an identifiable limit. It would make poor sense to treat every unsold ticket at the first site as a defect in one of the girls. [E02, OBS 05–07, 11–12][E02]

## Two offers of the same song

Yuki once invited Kyoka to lead *一歩前ノセカイ* on the strength of her work as an MC. When Yuki began to give her an opening to back out, Kyoka broke in with やらせてください, “please let me.” She told Tokka, the fan who had taken courage from meeting her, to wait for the song. Then she lost the words in front of an audience, and Haru covered. E04 reconstructs an illuminating professional failure. An offer can take a performer’s wish seriously and end in a missed entrance. That wish expressed what Kyoka wanted to attempt on that occasion. By E03 she knows what it costs to lose the opening, and she has developed other work she values doing. [E03, OBS 08–10][E03] · [E04, OBS 04–05, 23–24][E04]

Naoki has good reasons to reopen the question. He has watched Tokka return, heard Yukine Gionji’s account of the missed entrance and seen gold around an earlier disavowal of a wish for center. His unusual sight can register a discrepancy. It cannot specify which part of an account to disbelieve. The sound and picture checks distinguish the marked earlier denial from a longer refusal to sing this particular number for which they find no matching cue. Kyoka asks Naoki to ease off twice, at about 847.5 and 1162.8 seconds. An intervening request for secrecy goes to Haru and Yukine. Yukine takes its repetition as a cue to tell him anyway. Naoki acquires information from someone who knows what happened. He also acquires it after the person concerned asked her colleagues to leave the matter alone. [E03, OBS 06–10, 23, 26–27][E03] · [E03-belief and E03-authority][j1]

At the studio meeting Naoki assigns Rio the next live’s MC work, then gives Kyoka the center of its last song. Those are two jobs with two recipients. Taking away the first deprives Kyoka of the concrete thing she has offered to contribute in place of the second. She is good at carrying a show from one number to the next. Her fear that she will disappoint the audience gives that offer a serious professional basis. In E04 Haru asks Kyoka to go through the difficulty with her, Rio backs the choice, and Yukine reproaches a debuted performer for wasting an opportunity. Naoki stops the public rebuke but leaves the decision binding. Kyoka’s わかりました, “I understand,” is an exact answer to the instruction as given. Her earlier “please let me” answered a different person under different conditions. [E03, OBS 10–11, 29][E03] · [E04, OBS 01, 05 and “Asking, agreeing and daring to want”][E04]

The meal gives Kyoka room to say that dependable work can leave her feeling interchangeable. Naoki grants the reality of competition and takes on a duty to help if she fails. Backstage she tells herself to do what she wants. Onstage Rio carries the MC, Haru and Rio take the flanking places, Kyoka enters the song, and Tokka is there to hear her. Each supplies something the manager could neither deliver nor guarantee. One reason to call the casting benevolently coercive is that it preserves both parts of that history. Naoki judged that Kyoka could do meaningful work there, then used his power over the programme to override her explicit objection. Later she claimed the opportunity as her own. At the next chance in E06 she volunteers at once. Her changing answers deserve as much weight as the consistency of his judgment. [E04, OBS 07, 09–10, 13, 26][E04] · [E06, OBS 01–02][E06]

## What a director can use

Naoki makes a different kind of mistake on a regional television shoot. Rio chooses that job herself while Haru and Kyoka go to a sweetshop. She introduces herself properly to the crew. Presenter Nanami Usazuka gets the farm interview moving, picks up Rio’s verbal stumbles, and gives her a partner to play off. Director Sato values Rio’s unguarded reactions during the harvest. Encouraged by this first part of the job, Naoki advises Rio to say honestly what she thinks. Then a grower’s dish containing green pepper is put in front of her. Rio can tell the waiting crew quite sincerely that it tastes bitter. Nanami has already supplied a usable food report. Naoki proposes dropping Rio’s tasting segment and Sato agrees. The programme that would eventually air is unavailable. The decision we see concerns this report, and the harvest has earned a different judgment from the same director. [E05, OBS 04–07, 24–25][E05] · [Sato, “What a camera can use”][sato]

Rio brought her unpracticed candor to both halves of the shoot. The work had changed. A spontaneous reaction while harvesting can interest a viewer. A presenter introducing somebody else’s produce has to find something to say about it that the show can use. Nanami has that skill. The instruction Rio followed was Naoki’s, and it was Rio who had to sit at the table and taste the dish. He bows and acknowledges inadequate preparation. That acknowledgment assigns a share of the failure where it belongs, even as Rio hears the incident as one more time she has troubled her friends. Later Naoki sees her away from the sales table, pressing a flyer on a passerby so that somebody will come and see Haru and Kyoka. The effort is easily missed if the manager measures contribution solely by who is still standing at the point of sale. He gains more information by following her than he could extract from her boasts. [E05, OBS 05–07, 09–11, 14][E05]

When Naoki subsequently casts Rio at the center of *Yellow Rose* and removes almost all the dancing, she understands the adjustment as further evidence that others must work around her. Haru and Kyoka speak for their own willingness to take different parts. The three cannot settle Rio’s answer in that room. Naoki later asks her to learn from trying the song in front of people and withholds his own name for what she can offer. Telling her that she alone can do the job still places considerable pressure on someone who fears letting her colleagues down. This time we hear Rio undertake the part after she has objected and left. She also goes to Momiji Ito with a pudding and a conditional request for dance lessons. At the mall Sato is able to admire the singer whose tasting segment she agreed to cut. In E07 Momiji is actually teaching and Rio asks her to continue. The performance has given Rio grounds to reassess her contribution without cancelling the need to train or reversing Sato’s judgment about the tasting footage. [E06, OBS 01–02, 06–12][E06] · [E07, OBS 03][E07]

## The performers enlisted to manage one another

Before there was a trio, Yuki gave the original five a player with their song and choreography and left them to start without naming an instructor. Yukine, an experienced child actor and a novice idol, proposed a warm-up, and the others followed her lead. When Momiji struggled on a run, Yukine adjusted the task to getting her across the finish. Back indoors Momiji could show the others a count that would bring their bodies into time. Yukine put the correction into words and learned from it herself. Expertise moved to the performer in a position to correct this particular difficulty. Nobody had to be the best at everything to lead a few minutes of useful rehearsal. Yukine and Momiji even made the name *Yukimoji* for their partnership in helping the five. [E08, OBS 02–06][E08]

Yukine faced a much more difficult teaching problem with Haru. Haru looked like a competent beginner. Her instructor had explanations for the hesitation she saw, few of which had much to do with its cause. In the previous group Haru had seen her friend Ren Kurogane crying after a center selection session and heard her wish to become as skilled as Haru. Haru left. When Yuki recruited her, Haru made holding back until the new group grew stronger a condition of coming back. Yuki had seen Haru at the selection session. The locker-room account belongs to Haru, and the apology Ren would later speak beside an empty locker was outside Haru’s hearing. Haru could choose whether to continue in that profession. The open-ended part of her new arrangement gave her a further decision about four other people. How would anyone besides Haru know when they were strong enough? Yukine even offered to change practice if she had devised the wrong exercises. A conscientious instructor could go on correcting her own work indefinitely on the evidence Haru allowed her to see. [E08, OBS 03, 07][E08] · [E09, OBS 03–07][E09] · [E11, OBS 12 and CLM 25][E11]

Seeing Haru dance alone through a rooftop doorway finally gives Yukine and Momiji evidence of the gap. Its meaning comes from Yuki. Two listeners hear 君たちのためだよ, “it’s for your sake,” at about 987.5–988.6 seconds, where the matched subtitle prints 君たちのダメだよ. Yuki is offering the pair an explanation of Haru’s restraint. Her judgment that the pair still lack the strength to draw out Haru’s best follows separately. Yuki proposes that they leave TINGS together and become rivals formidable enough to threaten it. Haru might then exert herself to protect the colleagues for whose sake she has been holding back. Yuki asks about Yukine’s frustration and makes the stakes explicit. The pair could lose everything, and success carries no promise of reunion. Yukine wants to stand beside a Haru who gives her best. Momiji has made keeping Yukine from facing things alone a reason for what she herself will do. Their participation matters. It gives neither of them the opportunity to accept the test for the three who remain. [E07, OBS 10–14][E07] · [E08, OBS 11–17][E08] · [SPV2-XREV-E08-01][j1]

The two have to practice, wait and make their public distance convincing. Kyoka and Rio have to work beside apparent rivals without knowing whether some deficiency of their own brought about the split. Haru experiences as a threat something explicitly designed to exploit her wish to protect them. Offering all three Haru’s private history would itself have raised the question of whose story it was to tell. The narrower alternative the series gives us is Yukine’s earlier question about the exercises. She knew how to ask Haru about work they shared. It hadn’t elicited a disclosure then, so an open conversation promises no easy rescue. Yet the present rooftop confrontation actually ends with Haru going home and Yukine apologizing for the failure of the scheme. What sustains the later reunion includes the help the supposed rivals kept giving the trio, the very conduct that made them such unconvincing enemies. Calling the eventual group Yuki’s success alone would credit the plan with the result of people departing from it. [E07, OBS 03, 08–14][E07] · [E08, OBS 07, 14–18][E08] · [E09, OBS 07–09][E09]

## A public trial that the performers design

With Haru absent, Yuki tells the others about the earlier arrangement. Rio wants to know why Haru never consulted them. Momiji thinks of the company Haru lacked. Kyoka calls Yukine and Momiji back in terms that sound like an order, then proposes a piece of practical repertory. Rio has just pointed out what rearranging a trio number for five would cost in new parts and choreography. All five already know *Be Your Light!!* because they worked on it before the split. The four ask Naoki to bring Haru. Credit for this professional solution belongs in several places. The critical objection to the workload is Rio’s. The song choice is Kyoka’s. Their earlier rehearsals supply what none could manufacture at short notice. They give the manager a job to do for them. [E09, OBS 07–11][E09]

The earlier V1 study of Naoki identifies a real skill in his ability to arrange the conditions for a performer to succeed. He does exactly that when he secures access to Kikuchi’s audience. Applied to *Be Your Light!!*, however, the same account credits him with a design he receives from the performers. Its description of Kyoka’s supporting role as an “alibi” misses a related point. She can use her work as an MC to deflect a question about ambition precisely because hosting is a valuable job that someone else will have to do. Once she draws on her knowledge of the five to choose a song for Haru, it becomes particularly hard to tell the history of the group’s best professional decisions from the manager’s side of the desk alone. [V1, “Naoki’s actual philosophy”][v1] · [E02, OBS 06–07][E02] · [E03, OBS 10–11][E03] · [E09, OBS 08–11][E09]

Haru asks him not to look for her. He comes to the park anyway. On the bench he can give her something more useful than a guarantee about her friends. He tells her of his own dedication to idols and of discovering too late that he had hurt people. He tells her what her earlier words did for his decision to manage again. He brings the proposal and leaves her sitting there to make up her own mind. There is time in which she could choose otherwise, even though the others have staked a regular live on her being there. The practical cost of a refusal has risen. What has changed in the quality of the invitation is that the four can undertake the risk they are asking her to trust them with. Afterward they bend over spent while Haru is still upright. Rio wants to beat her at singing, Kyoka to keep up and learn, Yukine to catch her and Momiji to pass her. Their fatigue gives her friends something more substantial to reply with than cheerful reassurance. How much of the exertion the gap in ability caused remains uncertain. Her colleagues can experience that cost, interpret it differently from one another and ask her to come back to practice tomorrow. [E09, OBS 10–12, 14–18][E09] · [E10, OBS 01][E10]

## Buying a seat and bargaining over its performer

The Nakano campaign supplies a measure for an urgent professional decision. A week in which the remaining seats fall from 995 to 992 tells Naoki that a successful regular live has reached only so far. He puts together a schedule. Momiji demonstrates a dance in front of a camera he holds. Rio wins a game and uses her chance to plug the show. Fans including Sato and Tokka pass on reasons of their own for seeing the group. FFF gives TINGS an opening-act slot. A later sales figure can tell Naoki that fewer tickets remain. It cannot allocate those purchases among his schedule, one girl’s efforts, a colleague’s invitation or what a previous spectator told a friend. The professional virtue of the campaign is partly that it puts work worth passing on within reach of people free to pass it on. The group’s eventual success depends on decisions that its manager cannot direct. [E10, OBS 03–11][E10]

The visitor who takes a ticket from Haru has a very different transaction in mind. Ren wants her back in HY:RAIN. Ren has trained to keep up and can name strengths and shortcomings in TINGS. E11 confirms that she has watched some of the trio’s work. A friend can take those grounds seriously and decline the invitation. When Ren first proposed trying out, Homare chose to support her friends as a spectator. Years later she takes a share of the leaflets Haru is distributing for Nakano. Refusing the work Ren once imagined for her has left her able to choose work another friend needs. Haru’s earlier agreement deserves comparable care when she wants to answer differently now. Once Kyoka offers to prove TINGS’s worth at Nakano, Ren demands Haru back if she finds the proof wanting. Kyoka accepts that condition herself. The other TINGS members supported Kyoka’s defense before hearing its price. HY:RAIN’s other members have come to fetch their own leader and rebuke the trouble she is causing. Naoki’s presence supplies no additional assent. When Haru tries to speak, Ren and Kyoka tell her together, 春は黙ってて, “Haru, keep quiet.” The doubled voices and synchronized mouths matter. The person defending Haru’s place has joined the person seeking to take her away in excluding her from the decision. Haru objects immediately. [E10, OBS 12–16][E10] · [SPV2-ADJ-E10-064][j2] · [E11, OBS 11, 13–15][E11]

Kyoka’s most effective intervention the next day requires no promise about what she can prove on a stage. When strangers recognize Ren in public, Kyoka gets Ren and Haru clear of the crowd. At the waterside Ren can explain the promise she once made with Haru, and Haru can answer the person to whom she made it. Haru refuses to return and asks Ren to watch her at Nakano as a member of TINGS. The professional engagement now has an entirely different purpose for Ren. She can see what Haru’s choice means in practice. She has already heard the choice itself. At the show Ren acknowledges it from her seat. The series gives Haru no opportunity to hear that acknowledgment, and her decision has already taken effect without it. [E11, OBS 06–07, 11–16][E11] · [E12, OBS 16][E12]

## Accepting the next job

At Nakano, Yuki asks the five to satisfy the people who have come. In the few remaining minutes the girls can decide what they are prepared to give. Satisfaction will take an audience as well. Haru and Kyoka share the front of the opening number. Watching beside Hotaru, Naoki speaks of the possibility that Kyoka can go beyond her present limit if she wishes. The adjudicated spoken form is 間違いない, expressing his confidence. The subtitle prints 間違えない, a wording that recalls how accurately Kyoka performs. Her technical accuracy has ample evidence elsewhere. Here a manager’s statement of confidence preserves the importance of what Kyoka may choose. Her address to the house gives it a practical form. She thanks Yuki, Naoki, staff, senior performers and fans for different contributions. She can promise to get up again when she loses her way. Tokka, who sat through that missed entrance, has come to hear this promise too. [E12, OBS 06, 13–17][E12] · [SPV2-ADJ-E12-026][j2]

Back at Brightest, Haru has every reason to be startled by another announcement about dissolution. The five have just done what Yuki required. HY:RAIN’s agency has proposed a joint bill at the Budokan where the groups would compete. Yuki attaches the consequence of disbanding TINGS if they lose. The gold around her at roughly 1217.5 seconds gives Naoki grounds to doubt the threat as spoken. The frozen Gemini study of interpersonal relations reads a bluff here and contrasts the president’s ability to draw out talent with the cost of her control over information. The players’ positions give the second reading particular force. Naoki can see the cue. The five hear their president set a condition they would have to live with. How Yuki would act after a loss remains open, as do the identity of the individual who devised the bill and the outcome of the contest. The five must answer a president who can still make the threat. Kyoka says she wants to accept and then asks the others. They have room to give different reasons, from showing a rival what their stage has become to Haru’s pleasure at the prospect of performing with old friends. [E12, OBS 18–19, CLM 19, 39][E12] · [SPV2-ADJ-E12-003][j2] · [V2 comparison of Gemini 05 §3][comp]

Yuki still controls terms the performers cannot set for themselves. What has grown is their ability to decide together what to do within those terms. One can tell the difference by remembering what Kyoka offered Ren at the end of the ticket sale. She offered to put a change of group at stake in the coming show. Haru was the one who could accept such a change for herself. In the office Kyoka puts her own desire on the table first. The others may answer in their own names. That is a small change in the way a group accepts work, and a considerable change in what one colleague is prepared to promise on behalf of another.
