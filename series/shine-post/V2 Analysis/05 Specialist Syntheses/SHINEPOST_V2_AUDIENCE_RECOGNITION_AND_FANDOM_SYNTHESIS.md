# What the people in the seats can see

Kyoka Tamaki makes good on a promise in a small theater. Tokka has waited to hear her sing the opening of *一歩前ノセカイ* again. In E04 we see the performance in the company of someone to whom it matters very much that *this* girl gets through it. Seven episodes later, a woman in a green hood remembers standing in that audience. Ren Kurogane had come to see what sort of stage Haru Nabatame had found with her new companions. The shot of Kyoka in front of the trio belongs to both accounts. Tokka can experience the recovery of something she once feared she might never hear. Ren can go away convinced that this is too little for Haru. Nobody has to have attended the wrong night. To understand how the same concert can sustain those judgments, one has to know what each woman came there hoping to see. *SHINE POST* regularly lets us distinguish three things that can happen across the distance between a stage and a seat. A performer can catch somebody’s eye. A spectator can understand something particular about the work. What the spectator took from it can come to matter enough to change what she does. These need not happen in order. Tokka had already taken courage from Kyoka before she could have judged her singing. A professional can understand exactly how hard a group has worked and be deeply uneasy about the performance to come. People can also take something away from an encounter that the performer never learns she has given. The interesting question for this series is how a performer sometimes gets to find out.

## The same song reaches different people

A mall lets *Yellow Rose* reach listeners who never had to buy admission to a TiNgS show. The arrangement of people matters as much as their number. Some familiar fans at floor level expect Haru or Kyoka to take center. Miku’s earlier visit to buy from Rio and a call of encouragement before the song establish that Rio had admirers already. Fumu considers either of the expected leads suitable, worries about the choice of Rio, then says afterward how intently she listened. This is a change of mind we can attribute to one woman across a single performance. Another listener wants to know the group’s name. That question measures how far Rio’s voice has reached beyond people who already know her, and also how little its listener needed to know to be moved to ask. In E07 a post shown at the agency explicitly says that the singing made its writer a fan on the spot. The series has supplied the declaration that the question alone could never give us.
