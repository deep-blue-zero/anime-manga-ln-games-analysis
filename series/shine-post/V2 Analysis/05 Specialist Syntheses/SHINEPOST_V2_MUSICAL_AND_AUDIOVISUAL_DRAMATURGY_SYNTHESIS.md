# What happens on the next phrase

When Kyoka Tamaki misses the first words of her song, Haru Nabatame can cross in front of her and keep the concert going. When Kyoka returns to that song in E04, Haru passes through the foreground again and leaves her in view. At Nakano the two women turn to face each other, join hands on an accent, then turn back to the house. These are three different answers to a question that can only be put so precisely in performance. What can one girl do beside another in the time a phrase allows? A promise of support might describe all three actions. In the first, the concert continues because someone else can take the opening Kyoka cannot sing. [E03, the earlier live][e03] · [E04, the foreground pass][x04] · [E12, the pair at Nakano][e12]

The distinction the [full-series synthesis][full] draws among recognizing talent, assigning work and deciding what to attempt has a musical consequence. There is an interval even after everyone has agreed to try. In it a singer must enter, an arm must clear someone else's face, a dancer has to occupy the space their partners have left, or a room of listeners may withhold its reply longer than a performer can bear. The audience in the anime and the audience watching it on television do not always get to see or hear the same thing in that interval. Much of the drama lies in the difference.

## Which light can a performer give?

In E01 Haru might reasonably take *you don't shine* as a dreadful assessment from a prospective manager. We have just seen a crowd bathed in pink light and Hotaru asking everyone to shine. When Naoki watches someone speak, the programme can do something much narrower with light. A gold outline gathers around particular people while others near them retain their ordinary colors. At the end of E01 Rio and Kyoka can be gold together with Haru beside them in ordinary light. In E02 Rio and Kyoka acquire the effect in succession in the same rearview mirror. By contrast, Naoki uses related language on a telephone in E01 with no corresponding evidence that he can see the other speaker. The local effect makes the discrepancy worth attending to and leaves the person's account of it to be discovered. It also leaves a spectator free to admire a performer in a deliberate pose. Haru's final wink requires considerable self-presentation from a girl whose dream Naoki believes. [E01][e01] · [E01-phone and E02-confidence][a1] · [Naoki’s account of his eyes][NAOKI]

The editing keeps testing how large a statement the light qualifies. In E03 a reprise of Kyoka's E01 answer interrupts the current conversation. Her present denial of wanting center has a gold cue. It also lets Yukine give directions that actually bring Naoki to Kyoka while the story she tells about finding her attracts the effect. E05 uses it during Nanami's assured professional commentary on a tasting. Whatever else Naoki learns, he can still recognize that she knows how to make usable television.
