## The friend on the other side of the door

Haru and Ren wanted to be idols together. They passed the same audition, trained alongside three other girls and took delight in getting better. When their agency set out to select a center, Haru discovered what it was like to give a performance that drew the evaluators’ eyes. She also discovered her closest friend crying in the locker room, wishing she could become as good as Haru. Haru took those tears as evidence that giving her best had hurt the person she loved. She left the group. Later, President Hinase Yuki, who had watched the center test, persuaded her to try another group on a condition Haru set. Until her new group could bear what she could do, Haru would hold back. Now the people she meant to protect have learned about that bargain. Tamaki Kyoka brings Gionji Yukine and Itō Momiji back into TINGS and proposes “Be Your Light!!”, the first song all five had managed to put together. Rio, Kyoka, Yukine and Momiji ask Naoki to bring Haru. On a park bench he tells her about the people he hurt through his devotion to his work and how her own words once gave him the courage to manage idols again. The four can offer her an evening on which to test her fear. Only Haru can decide to come. [SPV2-OBS-E09-02, SPV2-OBS-E09-04, SPV2-OBS-E09-06, SPV2-OBS-E09-09, SPV2-OBS-E09-11]

At the regular live, performing together does indeed expose a difference in ability. Four girls bend over in the dressing room while the fifth stands upright. Rio intends to beat Haru at singing. Kyoka wants to learn by keeping up. Yukine promises to catch her and Momiji to pass her. Haru apologizes, thanks her friends and embraces Kyoka. A friend can feel the distance between herself and Haru without wanting Haru to go away. At this regular live the present five get to test that possibility in front of the people who have come to see them. The voice from the old locker room remains a wish to become better. Whether Ren ever wanted Haru to leave, how she understood the departure and what she would say to Haru now are questions the embrace backstage cannot answer. [SPV2-OBS-E09-04, SPV2-OBS-E09-17, SPV2-OBS-E09-18, SPV2-OPEN-E09-01]

The opening credits already know a picture Haru does not yet believe she can help make. Five costumed girls occupy an enormous stage in the first fourteen seconds. Later the same sequence can make space for Haru alone at night, an audience on the other side of the lights, and finally a framed picture in which the five fit together as easily as close friends posing for a camera. The opening bills “Wonder Starter” to TINGS. Its arena has a scale entirely different from the regular live later in E09. The opening’s five promise an image of companionship; the episode proper will ask how much work it takes to have five performers together in a considerably smaller room. The first picture the story gives us after the credit sequence is a city at sunset and a title that fastens the problem on one person: 青天国春は《信じない》. The sentence leaves its object empty. It takes the rest of the episode to discover who wants to be trusted and what that trust would look like onstage. [SPV2-PF-E09-01, SPV2-OBS-E09-01, SPV2-OBS-E09-15]

### A girl in the audience for her former group

On a city street Haru has the same view of HY:RAIN as a passerby. The giant screen makes the group’s achievements public property. Their latest single has topped the chart. Their music videos have passed one hundred million views in total. The presenter cites a Budokan success only a year after the group formed and promises a look at the people behind that success. The smaller screen within the broadcast starts to occupy the programme’s own picture. A neon “Get you back” flashes among coloured silhouettes while Japanese feature copy stays under the presenter. Then a close profile in glasses puts Haru back among the onlookers. She possesses a history that would make for a very different feature from the one being advertised. The picture does not give the reporter that knowledge. Nor is the music industry’s measure of what HY:RAIN has become a measure of how well its members recovered from losing a friend. [SPV2-OBS-E09-01]

The serious relationship begins with a ridiculous claim about a doughnut. At a school desk three girls repeat a Hotaru commercial and argue whether saying the slogan as Hotaru does can make something taste rounder. The box sits between them. Each is holding a different pastry. To take the argument entirely literally would miss the pleasure of being able to reproduce an idol’s line among friends. To call it mere make-believe would miss what the friends go on to do together. Haru identifies Ren as her childhood friend, her best friend and another girl who loves idols. Later it is Ren who shows a phone and Haru who looks up at her. The two succeeded in the audition they entered together. The third girl is Homare. Her pale ring curls with coloured beads match the schoolfriend Haru invited by name in E02, where the two shared doughnuts after Haru had joined TiNgS. E09 holds the school friendship and the subsequent training group apart. It carries Haru and Ren from the desk into the studio and gives Homare no role in their audition. [SPV2-OBS-E09-02]

The arrangement on the yellow practice-room floor tells the story of that beginning especially well. At 171.630 Haru and Ren bow toward the other three trainees. Haru names Yawara, Itoha and Aoba. The five later credited to HY:RAIN’s song include Ren and those three, and name a fifth person, 氷海菜花. Haru was the fifth member of the earlier lineup. E09 gives no date or procedure for that change. Lessons bring them onto the treadmills and into a shared line. The train at the end of a day, the five gathered over a tablet showing dancers in different practice clothes, and the long shadows of five people walking down one street make a life around the classes. Some days they go to school as well. What Haru remembers enjoying is specific. Some dances defeated her and some songs were beyond her, until she could do them. Remembering herself at a time when it was possible to learn something is important. Later she will speak as though what she does to other people is the unavoidable result of an ability given to her in advance. Her first account of this ability gives both her and her friend room to improve. The pleasure belongs as much to the imagined debut of this particular five as to the prospect of becoming an idol at all. [SPV2-OBS-E09-02, SPV2-OBS-E09-03]

When Ren praises a new piece of choreography Haru has got in one try, Haru says she got lucky. Ren can grant a role to luck and still call the achievement remarkable. Her next sentiment is competitive. She wants a turn and declares that she will not lose to Haru. In the two-shot at 223.014 her fists are raised in front of her chest as she looks at her friend. A promise to compete is among the earliest expressions of affection the episode gives Ren. The remembered Haru smiles at that point. It is the narrating Haru who now says she didn’t understand. The change in tense allows a viewer to do something she could no longer easily do after she quit. We can hold on to the first, encouraging exchange while considering the very different one she overhears later. [SPV2-OBS-E09-03, SPV2-CLM-E09-04]

The professional occasion that puts a value on the difference between them is the selection of a center. A line of people with papers sits and stands opposite the dancers. Haru’s hair, the bend of her arms and the small points of light on her dark sleeves receive a succession of close views. We get much more of her than of the other girls, until a wide picture restores all five with one hand raised to the head. Haru is second from the left at 251.001. Looking back from the end of that shot, what seemed an extended solo portrait has happened in a room where four other people were working. The evaluators have visibly taken notice. Three teammates stare across the room. Haru’s smile gives way to a searching expression. “I did it” in this recollection has the unhappy sense that she has done something she should have avoided. The episode lets us see why Haru might feel she had already been singled out, without showing a ballot or recording the award of the center position to anyone. [SPV2-OBS-E09-03]

Later, the door stands slightly open. From a bright passage Haru can see Ren on her knees against a bank of lockers in a dark room. There are tears on her face. Ren says she wants to become skilled like Haru. Haru’s face fills the gap between the open door and the jamb. We see her from inside as well as seeing what she can glimpse from outside. The light along the corridor makes Haru visible to us but does not establish that Ren knows she has a listener. Haru does not go in for a conversation we can witness. In the wider view Haru is still outside. A dark opening and the sill remain visible through 303.34. The next cut shows her luggage. Neither girl is shown shutting that door. Her account supplies a much more extensive conclusion than the words she overheard. Her talent, exercised in earnest, has hurt someone precious to her. As evidence of Ren’s distress the tears are unusually direct. As evidence that Ren blamed Haru, judged their friendship over or wanted the result to be Haru’s departure, they barely begin to answer the question. [SPV2-OBS-E09-04, SPV2-OPEN-E09-01]

Haru’s decision has a definite subject. She says she quit being an idol. A school uniform has replaced the exercise clothes, and she takes a wheeled case down the pavement at night. At school she and Ren occupy the same corridor, then pass. The illustrated moment between their faces would have been a place to exchange a line. No such exchange appears in the Japanese dialogue. A wide view across a station platform puts Haru almost directly in front of an idol advertisement. She is still living in a world saturated with the work she walked away from. She tells herself this is for the best because she never wants to go through a feeling like that again. This last reason matters alongside her concern for Ren. She wants to spare other people, and she knows how intolerable it was for *her* to see what giving her best might do. Nobody in the earlier group gets to answer that decision here. [SPV2-OBS-E09-05, SPV2-CLM-E09-05]

President Yuki makes contact on that platform. The station bench places her cane in the frame before a later café conversation sets the two across a table. Haru says she turned the invitation down repeatedly. Yuki brings two things to the eventual discussion. In the selection-room picture at 231.148 she is identifiable at the far right in her pale suit, hand on the cane, behind the table where others sit with evaluation papers. She speaks as a witness to Haru’s performance that day, and as someone willing to take a chance on the girls she now hopes to recruit. Seeing Haru perform gives weight to her judgment about what Haru can do. It gives her no special access to the conversation missing on the other side of that locker-room door. Her question about the destination Haru once looked straight toward allows Haru to picture continuing without first deciding that what happened to Ren did not matter. She asks for one concession. For the time being she will withhold her full effort, until everyone in TINGS has grown stronger. The president later calls this the condition on which Haru agreed to come back. “Until” leaves an opening that might disappear entirely if Haru remains the only person who decides what counts as strong enough. [SPV2-OBS-E09-06, SPV2-CLM-E09-06]

### A promise made by four in the absence of the fifth

Pictures of a new troupe enter Haru’s condition before the programme returns to the people hearing about it. Yukine introduces herself with her grand first-person pronoun. The girls are shoulder to shoulder in the studio, a music player is propped against the wall, and Rio appears in one of her precarious balancing poses. Haru has asked for time for these people to improve. The images give the request an uncomfortable practical meaning. A beginner who lacks that information about her colleague will draw mistaken conclusions about her own progress alongside her. That was a cost the first bargain placed particularly heavily on Yukine. In E08 she tried to help someone whom she took to be inexperienced, then watched with Momiji as Haru danced alone on the rooftop. The bargain also helps explain something about Yuki’s readiness in the earlier episode to let the pair try an unlikely course of action. The girl who made the condition has declined to lift it. The people who were its object have had no opportunity to say how much longer they need its protection. [SPV2-OBS-E09-06, SPV2-CLM-E08-03, SPV2-CLM-E08-14]

The return to the rooftop defines who gets to hear this explanation. At 426.009 Yuki faces Naoki and the four in their practice clothes. Haru has gone. The audience for the television episode has just had Haru’s own first-person account, the view through her door and the company of her private thoughts. The group on the roof has what Yuki chooses to tell it. Both accounts traverse the same history, and at their boundary Yuki says that from here on everyone knows what happened. That framing still leaves a real difference between the viewer’s access and the other girls’. Nobody there can put a question to the Ren who said she wanted to get better, and even Haru never heard her say what a friend ought to do about it. This time Rio supplies the name HY:RAIN (C866–867). Her profile is the one articulating the name before Yukine takes up what it explains about Haru. Yuki’s disclosure at the end of E08 and the passing poster are now easier to identify, although the personnel of the old training group and of the group whose record is on the charts still need to be kept distinct. [SPV2-OBS-E09-01, SPV2-OBS-E09-07, SPV2-REV-E09-01]

For Yukine the new fact explains the scale of the ability she and Momiji tried to provoke. Momiji turns quickly to what it must have been like to live alone with that secret and apologizes to the absent Haru. Rio’s question points the other way. Why did Haru keep everything to herself instead of consulting any of them? Kyoka offers a painful answer, qualified by one small word. Haru did not trust them *yet*. Knowing more makes continued ignorance impossible, but it does not put them all immediately on the same emotional footing. The different responses give the girls something to do. Momiji can recognize suffering in a friend she once confronted. Rio can say there was a cost to being treated as an object of care rather than a person to talk to. Kyoka can ask what it would take to earn or demand that missing trust. Each still has to meet Haru face to face. [SPV2-OBS-E09-07]

Kyoka first turns to Yukine and Momiji. Come back to TINGS, she says. It is an order. Their work as Yukimoji is over because they failed to get Haru to give her all. The two short reactions to the decision register how one-sided it sounds. Earlier, the president had asked the two to leave as rivals in hopes of producing exactly that change in Haru, with no promise that their place would be waiting for them. It takes somebody now willing to exercise authority to end the pretence. The risk Kyoka attaches to failure has two parts. TINGS has a real obligation to make the Nakano live succeed. Kyoka also believes it will take the full effort of every member to do it. The conditional conclusion that failing to reach Haru will mean the end of TINGS follows from Kyoka’s reasoning here. It is important to distinguish the underlying survival condition from a new management order requiring Haru to dance at any specified intensity. [SPV2-OBS-E09-08, SPV2-CLM-E09-08]

What can the four actually bring Haru that two rivals could not? An idol’s most fitting place to show what she means, Naoki says, is the stage where the fans wait. Taking somebody’s word for it has proved remarkably difficult during the preceding conversation. The proposed stage makes the promise costly. Haru will see what her present companions are willing to undertake, and other people besides Haru will see whether the evening works. Rio immediately locates a problem of craft. A song for three needs different choreography and vocal allocation for five, on a first public outing for Yukine and Momiji with this lineup. It is a particularly good objection from the performer whose newly established claim to sing the center of another song mattered so much at the end of E06. Redistributing parts is no longer an abstraction to her. She also puts the two newcomers’ burden into words, even when she is worried about the outcome herself. [SPV2-OBS-E09-08, SPV2-OBS-E09-09]

Kyoka proposes “Be Your Light!!”. These five first put choreography together to this song when they were teammates. A practice image at 565.023 supplies an almost diagrammatic reason for her confidence. Kyoka and Rio reach up behind the other three. Momiji and Yukine open their arms to either side with Haru low in the middle. Take one person away and a space in the design stays vacant. Return the two who were missing and there is already work in their bodies from a time before anyone met Yukimoji as a rival act. Such a solution could fail. It assumes the former members remember something useful, the current three can accommodate their return, and Haru will come to the live. The title and the group's eventual address give the choice a further aptness. They intend to do for someone who wants to be a light for other people what she has always offered to do for them. Importantly, the practical solution came before anybody could know how she would receive that offer. [SPV2-OBS-E09-09, SPV2-OBS-E09-16]

Kyoka’s group cannot finish this work by itself. She asks Naoki to bring Haru. He calls and asks where she is. “Don’t look for me” overlaps a park announcement identified as such in the second subtitle lane. The announcement names Inokashira twice. At 621.913 Naoki holds the phone out and his mouth opens during the first identification of the park. A second caption repeats the name. Kyoka is shown making her request to the manager at 626.668, and Naoki answers her. The girls and the manager now have an intelligible lead. A sign at 638.012 independently locates the subsequent scene at the entrance to Inokashira Park. The manager’s skill looks almost magical to Haru when she asks how he found her. For once a viewer can reconstruct how anybody on the near end of that telephone call could have done it. The source permits that deduction without assigning an exact volume, listening distance or recognition threshold to an unheard voice. It also preserves the contradiction in the practical request. He comes despite being asked not to search, then gives the decision about performing back to the person he found. [SPV2-OBS-E09-10, SPV2-OBS-E09-11]

On the bench Naoki says he too has discovered, too late, that people were hurt. He talks about idols who sacrificed so much that wearing himself out in their service felt reasonable. He worked without looking to the side and discovered he had lost everything. The harm and loss are his account of his own experience. There is no retrospective roll call of victims, no reason to attach this confession to one specific older idol, and no guarantee that the harm he remembers has the same cause as Ren’s distress. He can offer the shape of his mistake and the choice it led him to make. It is why he once ruled out managing idols. And Haru, without yet trusting him with her old story, helped undo that rule. A returning image of the practice room answers why. Haru wanted people everywhere to be able to say aloud which idols they loved. He saw a chance to try once more with this group because somebody meant those words. His explanation is also a delayed acknowledgment of work Haru has already done for him while thinking she was the one being recruited. [SPV2-OBS-E09-11, SPV2-OBS-E09-12]

Naoki tells Haru the decision the others have made, which stage they will use, which song they have chosen and the promise he carries from Kyoka and the rest. Then he asks one favor of his own. He honored her request to become their manager. Would she trust TINGS this once? He tells her he has said everything and that she must think and give her own answer. The long path at 786.035 puts him at a distance while Haru still sits in the foreground. If this were simply an order, getting the reluctant girl off the bench and escorting her directly back would have made a tidier ending. Instead, the invitation leaves an interval during which she might still do something he does not want. It matters that her public return can follow an independent decision even though the members have already announced their new number, and that her eventual participation does not make every pressure brought to bear on her disappear. [SPV2-OBS-E09-12, SPV2-CLM-E09-12]

That change of audience is already at work in a joke about the plan. Yukine says the song is もってこい, just the thing for their purpose. Momiji comes back with どんとこい, bring it on. Each short expression makes the other girl's bravado a little easier to bear. The joke also shows how much of the two-person act can survive when nobody is obliged to behave like an enemy. Yuki once made their knowledge of Haru a reason for the two of them to leave, concealing from the remaining three why they had gone. Haru’s phone now shows an EnteRoid headline announcing that TiNgS is becoming TINGS with new members. The photograph identifies Yukine and Momiji visually. Naoki has named them to Haru in C958, and Fumu will name them at the venue in C987. The headline itself gives neither personal name. The news outlet can call them new members of the publicly known trio. Kyoka can call them returning members and produce a song commissioned for them all. There are two different histories of who constitutes a group because there have been two different audiences for it. [SPV2-OBS-E09-08, SPV2-OBS-E09-09, SPV2-OBS-E09-13, SPV2-OBS-E09-17]

### The wait at the crossing and the first regular live of five

On her way through the city Haru takes stock of what could be lost. Practising in the company of people she loves, sharing a stage and singing before the audience are the things she treasures. They give her a positive reason for having held back. They also give her reasons for thinking she may have been wrong. Kyoka has begun to discover work only she can do. Rio has begun to captivate people with her voice. A phone in Haru’s hand carries the news that two new girls have joined and the group’s name is changing. Her problem with that word “new” is partly the opposite of the public’s. She knows how long Yukine and Momiji have been there, how accurately they observed her and how prepared they were to stand in her way. They knew what she had tried so hard to spare them. Their decision to challenge her is itself a kind of evidence about how much they need that protection. [SPV2-OBS-E09-13]

The pictures during this walk are generous with knowledge. We see the two peeking at Haru on the roof, for instance, as Haru reaches the conclusion that they knew. That gives the viewer a way of placing the thought. It need not mean Haru has a first-person memory of looking at herself through their eyes. E08 gave the sight of the performance to them. Later episodes of work also enter by way of the group’s earlier public images, including the three at microphones punctuated by shots of Rio. In the present, Haru has to wait on a street corner. The traffic signal changes while Haru’s uncertainty plays out. Finally a solitary figure recedes down the pavement and the next exterior is in daylight. The last thought the viewer receives here trails off at “Maybe …”. The cut leaves space between having an idea and being able to act on it. A later reproach that she missed practice gives that space a cost to other people, but does not specify how long she spent making up her mind. [SPV2-OBS-E09-13, SPV2-CLM-E09-13]

At the venue, three spectators put different questions to the news. Tokka registers the change to five. Fumu names the two additions, Yukine and Momiji. Sato wonders what this will mean for Rio’s vocal parts. Sato, the television director at E06’s food shoot and a spectator at the mall live, reappears in her orange shirt. Her face and green eyes match E06 f1039 at E09 f0947 and f1225. Later, her hands are together and she is smiling. Her question about Rio picks up the interest she first showed at the food shoot and the mall. That was precisely the kind of practical question Rio raised privately. The equation between more performers and a better show cannot be taken for granted even in a room full of fans. A skirt and legs in the passage give way to Haru at the door. Waiting on the other side are four already dressed in the same group colours. Haru has made it this far on her own, which answers the simplest question about the invitation. The others now press the more difficult question of what she intends to do once she is inside. Four friends standing shoulder to shoulder can be as intimidating as a panel of evaluating adults. The difference is that these people have their own performance at stake and are ready to answer in person. [SPV2-OBS-E09-13, SPV2-OBS-E09-14, SPV2-OBS-E09-26]

A hard greeting makes the friendly invitation at the park sound almost naive. Haru has not come to a single practice for their first stage as five, Kyoka observes. The suggestion that Haru thinks she could fit in without ever going all out draws an immediate denial. But the other argument is harder to deny. To decide all by herself that full effort would hurt them was to settle on their behalf how they would meet it. Had she trusted them with the situation, they could have told her before now. Yukine can make an outrageously confident claim about the skills Nanami has taught them. Kyoka can take on the more modest and essentially emotional promise that seeing the real extent of Haru’s ability will no longer harm them in the way she expects. The two claims are different enough that the first can later turn out to have exaggerated their position without discrediting the second. The invitation that finally reaches Haru is to bring it on, offered by people liable to suffer the embarrassment and effort of keeping their word. [SPV2-OBS-E09-14, SPV2-CLM-E09-14]

Onstage the public introduction also gives the members a way of speaking to one another. Kyoka presents the two additions, Yukine and Momiji introduce themselves, and the group’s five capital letters become part of the MC. Kyoka explains the wish in the name: to get past falsehood and difficulty, reach the truth and one day become girls who sing it. Haru’s face is lowered while the words have an apparently general meaning. Then a single “you” makes the address much more intimate. You may stop pretending. We will receive you as you are. Trust us. Kyoka moves from 皆様, everyone here, to singular あなた, and begins だから twice before she gets out 私たちを信じて. The last phrase finally supplies the title’s missing object, the friends now asking for Haru’s trust. For the public these are words an idol offers to a listener. For Haru they repeat a promise the same people have just made at the dressing-room door. The editing visits both her companions and her face as the address becomes harder to mistake. Haru lowers her lids and gathers a hand to her chest. Her mouth is closed when the invitation to listen begins. At 1027.902 the picture is on Kyoka with her mouth open for the song title. By 1029.820 Haru has opened her eyes and is smiling. Her reply takes the form of a performance. [SPV2-OBS-E09-15, SPV2-CLM-E09-15]

The five start “Be Your Light!!”. A single row of five under the sign makes an initial wide picture, with Haru now in the middle. The opening portraits pass in order from Haru’s raised palm to Kyoka, Rio, Momiji and Yukine (native 1036.827–1040.539). Each performer gets a distinct angle on the same outward gesture. At one point Yukine and Haru share the foreground. At another the two outside bodies have come low while three remain standing. The words move from not having been able to be oneself, through the discovery of company, to a promise to bring light to another person. At the level of two simultaneous printed parts, a count of three crosses the bound between recognizing light in another’s eyes and wanting the message to reach them. Placing those words in an ensemble allows the people speaking the promise and the people receiving it to change places from one phrase to the next. No one singer’s ownership of that count can be recovered from which face the editor gives us. [SPV2-OBS-E09-16]

At 1103.019 all five appear from behind against a dark auditorium pricked by lights. At 1113.029 the view is close enough for Rio to dominate it with a hand to her chest. At 1123.039 arms reach out from the low foreground and rise at the back. Haru’s low place between Momiji and Yukine gives the two taller figures room to join above her. The midday practice tableau seen during the conversation about choosing this song has a recognizable counterpart here. In E08 the girls could celebrate having got through a whole run and immediately complain of having to do it again from the top. They could also give away nothing of the apparent gap between their abilities. In E09, after seeing what Haru can do, they can still arrive at a common picture. The space around her has been composed for other bodies. Even a spectator convinced of her special ability has to look at those bodies to see how the last shape works. The evidence for an artistic achievement here includes a visible shared composition and what the performers and audience do afterwards. It cannot yet supply a comparative technical rating of who kept time, how breath was managed or which voice dominated. [SPV2-OBS-E09-16, SPV2-OBS-E09-17, SPV2-OPEN-E09-03]

The held formation lasts beyond the last indexed lyric. The pictures go out to spectators. Sato smiles and claps. At 1149.690 the five are side by side, bent forward from the waist in a bow. Kyoka’s question begins at 1149.79 in the Japanese captions. By 1150.316 the five are upright. Kyoka asks how the audience found the song and explains that it was originally made for the five to sing, and that today is the first day they could finally sing it before everyone. That sentence changes the status of an existing song. Neither an anonymous new commission nor a showcase borrowed from another act, it was theirs before it had ever been public in this configuration. The next title she announces is “TOKYO WATASHI COLLECTION”. Two pictures of lamps and a different ceiling shortly afterward carry the story to the dressing room. Giving the next title is a way to continue the show while letting the programme omit what it does not need to show. We have no comparable stretch of five-person choreography, indexed lyrics and audience reaction for this second number. And because the show has continued, the exhaustion we are about to see may reflect more than the effort of its first song. [SPV2-OBS-E09-17, SPV2-OPEN-E09-03]

### How the five hold their places

“Be Your Light!!” makes it possible to watch the group do what it has just asked Haru to believe. The oval sign at the back keeps the changes of position intelligible. Rio occupies the front below it. Kyoka folds down and Haru comes into view directly behind her. Arms spread almost horizontally across the picture, then the palms turn upward and the fists draw in below the chin. Rio and Haru exchange places in the same narrow strip beneath the sign. When they move apart again, a wider view restores the gaps needed to travel around each other. From behind, white lights in the crowd reach almost to the edge of the stage. From the front, Haru's open hand comes close to the lens. The changes of scale make a spectator’s point of view possible even on a small screen. We can recognize who occupies the foreground in one picture, then see what the same action looks like to someone several rows back. Haru can be the person we see first without becoming the only person worth seeing.

The song lets its pronouns travel further. 君 is there for the singer. A few lines on, it is the singer who will light the way for 君. Haru can stand on either side of that exchange, and so can a fan. At 18:29 two lines occupy the screen together. The upper line reads “Shinin’ Stars.” Below it the Japanese gives the purpose, to make one dream come true. Keeping both lets the starry plural and the singular dream occupy the same interval in the subtitles. Afterward Kyoka’s 作ってもらった remembers the people who made the number for them. Even having a song of their own was an act of trust by someone outside the five. (17:16–18:39, C1027–1047 and lower lyric beside C1045; 19:09–19:26, C1048–1052.)

### What the other four do with seeing Haru at her best

The stage lights give way to four ordinary ceiling lights. Naoki stands to the left of the room with the five. Kyoka has bent almost double, Yukine and Momiji lean forward, Rio’s face is flushed. Haru stands among them looking from one to another. If this arrangement ended the episode, it would make a plausible confirmation of Haru’s worst fear. Her companions have indeed been shaken. Their language, too, is a mixture of astonishment and self-deprecation, including the extravagance of saying a heart stopped. For a moment the point of view returns to a girl who once saw another girl brought to her knees by what she had done in a studio. The difference in physical positions is real in both scenes. This time the edit stays, and the people in the lower position can finish speaking to her. [SPV2-OBS-E09-04, SPV2-OBS-E09-17, SPV2-OBS-E09-18]

The next answers are strikingly unalike. Kyoka sees a prospect for further growth if she can keep up with Haru. Rio narrows the field in which she intends to win at the next regular live: singing. Yukine claims it will take very little time to reach Haru’s level, Momiji amends that to passing her. One might smile at their optimism a few seconds after their evident distress, but Ren too once told Haru she would not lose to her. The resemblance brings back the earlier challenge, whose warmth it was easy to forget after the tears. These replies cannot clear up what happened to Ren. They do establish what these four want *now*, after the event they had promised to withstand. Their newly restored group has space for wanting to beat one another. That is different from simulating a rivalry between groups in the hope that a common friend will end it for them. [SPV2-OBS-E09-03, SPV2-OBS-E09-18, SPV2-CLM-E09-18]

Kyoka is still quite capable of getting cross. The two who chose to leave TINGS have barely begun to boast before she calls them on it. One hand comes down on each of their heads (f1277–f1279). They apologize. She turns to Haru next. They are five in one group and she is to stop bearing things by herself. Nor has the deadline vanished in a flood of good feeling. Training for Nakano Sunplaza starts again tomorrow. Both Japanese subtitle editions print the Chinese form 明天 in C1076. The intended schedule is intelligible, but the two editions cannot establish which Japanese word Kyoka spoke. Kyoka can rebuke three of her colleagues in quick succession because the content of the rebuke is that each has obligations to all the others. She has used the authority of someone willing to get up from the floor and practise again, with no immunity for herself when Haru outperforms her. Earlier it felt alarming that she issued an order to the returning pair. Here the order comes with the modest work of naming what everybody, including Kyoka, will have to do the next day. This gives a concrete meaning to Haru’s freedom to be herself. She has responsibilities within the group, among them letting its members judge their own capacity for risk. [SPV2-OBS-E09-18]

Haru apologizes, starts a sentence with “and yet”, then thanks them. The source does not complete that little connective on her behalf. There has been injury in keeping things to herself and a welcome from the people who have just named it. Words nearly hold these things together. Bodies do the rest. At 1270.603 Haru has thrown her arms around Kyoka. The later wide picture leaves room for Yukine, Rio, Momiji and the manager to look on. Tears, which earlier reached Haru from across the width of a partially open door, are now on her own face against a friend’s shoulder. They change their possible meaning accordingly. The person crying is the one who outperformed the others. She can let Kyoka see how afraid she has been of hurting the others and losing the life they have built together. In this particular case the preceding apology and thanks make relief a stronger reading than rejection. They leave plenty about the past still painful. [SPV2-OBS-E09-18, SPV2-OBS-E09-19, SPV2-CLM-E09-19]

The audience has its own unfinished business. An encore is written into the secondary dialogue lane while the performers are backstage. The same plea is still on the episode clock when the credits have begun at 1282.031. For the fans who have waited for a new five, a program that could go on is a reasonable thing to ask for. For the girl in tears, being asked back out into that room is a future she once refused to imagine. The episode stops short of depicting an encore number or even a reply to that request. The closing pictures return instead to Haru braiding her hair, the three school friends with their doughnuts, the five current girls in rehearsal clothes, and the solitary figure against the sunset on the roof. These images collect people who occupied quite different lives into the span of one ending. The ending theme is credited to TINGS as パレットガールズ, “Palette Girls.” Its ten timed lines (C1081–1090) begin with someone who has a little time before the others arrive and can take the long way under a blue sky. Only later do the girls make one picture together. Each is a different type, each can bring her own colour to the palette, and the last line asks the people the singer loves to stay as they are. That wish has a new inflection after Kyoka, Rio, Yukine and Momiji have just promised to change. They can preserve what they like about each other while wanting to become better performers. The late backstage group illustration changes how closely the performers stand together compared with the scene from which it draws. The image can picture a belonging larger than the arms we saw around a particular girl. It cannot enroll Ren in the reconciliation or tell us that five musicians sang particular lines in unison. [SPV2-OBS-E09-19, SPV2-OBS-E09-20]

One credit detail makes the limits of the last pictures especially clear. The same rooftop image at 1344.927 has an insert credit for HY:RAIN’s “GYB!!”; at 1349.014 it has a different insert credit for TINGS’ “Be Your Light!!”. The two cards identify two works and their respective credited performers, with a different composer/arranger on each. Neither time locates a second concert by the group printed at the right of the picture. Earlier the broadcast contained lettering corresponding to the refrain and the programme’s MC named TINGS’ song. Those are independent reasons to connect a title with a particular in-story event. It remains possible to audit the Japanese release directly for the audible transitions, voice distribution and actual relation between the two groups’ performance styles. The episode’s present more secure comparison is between what success looks like on a city billboard and what it costs five people to get through one evening in a small room. [SPV2-OBS-E09-01, SPV2-OBS-E09-16, SPV2-OBS-E09-20]

### Two ways of protecting a group

It is tempting to read the episode as a moral against anyone who hangs back. Taken that way, the cure would have been simple: persuade Haru to dance as well as she can, and everybody wins. This leaves too much out. Ren really cries. The four who have made a promise to withstand Haru’s ability are genuinely startled by it. Yuki’s invitation would have been refused if Haru had been required to risk everything again at once. There was a time when a concession allowed a damaged friendship to be followed by the beginnings of a new one. The concession becomes a problem when its original parties continue deciding for people whose skills have grown and who have acquired commitments of their own. E09 does not give us a numerical threshold above which it becomes safe to share a stage with a gifted teammate. It shows what changes when the people affected can say what they are willing to do. [SPV2-CLM-E09-04, SPV2-CLM-E09-06, SPV2-CLM-E09-14, SPV2-CLM-E09-18]

There is another plausible objection. Kyoka’s order to the pair, the already announced reorganization and a live in front of fans put pressure on Haru. The others greet her arrival by accusing her of thinking too little of them. One could reasonably wonder how much choice this arrangement leaves a friend who knows that everyone might lose their career. There is counterevidence to treating the evening simply as an ambush. She knows the place, song, membership and purpose before she attends. Naoki leaves the bench without an answer. His last words there tell Haru he will wait for her at the regular live. The girls stand next to her when the test arrives. They pay in public for any failure to keep their promises, and privately accept responsibilities that go well beyond inducing one impressive performance. A skeptical account of the means is compatible with accepting the sincerity of their welcome. A longer-term test would be what they do when a member needs something harder to give than applause, or when a new disparity emerges and they cannot immediately turn it into a contest. [SPV2-CLM-E09-08, SPV2-CLM-E09-12, SPV2-CLM-E09-18, SPV2-OPEN-E09-02]

Haru’s wish to help fans speak openly gives the trust problem another turn. She wants strangers to be free to say what matters most to them. When the subject is the gap between herself and a close friend, however, she has long believed it kinder to decide in advance what her friend can bear to hear or see. E09 has offered an actual case in which a listener needed her candour. Naoki says her words at rehearsal helped him try again. The repeated picture, E09 f0780 and E01 f1000, gives considerable precision to the memory. Haru, Rio and Kyoka face Naoki across a stretch of studio floor, his chair turned toward them. E08 f0712 places her saying much the same thing outdoors to Yukine at a different moment in her life. That distinction preserves two kinds of constancy. A continuing ambition can be shared independently with more than one person. Different listeners can also discover different obligations in it. Neither occasion proves that the person speaking was already able to take the same advice herself. [SPV2-OBS-E09-11, SPV2-OBS-E08-08, SPV2-CLM-E01-11]

### What changed after the president spoke

The principal’s entering expectation about who could supply an account of the past was only partly borne out. The episode supplies a more direct answer than the poster and Yuki’s testimony in E08. Haru narrates her friendship with Ren, the difference in their abilities and the fact that she chose to quit. In the place where the other members learn about this, Haru herself is absent. The first-person history reaches viewers without giving the four the opportunity to question her about the locker-room encounter. When the chance for confrontation comes, what they ask of Haru concerns their own relationship with her. It is a material correction to the earlier expectation that finding another person who had been there would be the central event. Yuki can corroborate the evaluation, Naoki can speak for his old workplace experience and Ren’s own words can attest to a painful ambition. The decisive new witnesses are the people who have just stood onstage with Haru and can tell her what that costs them and why they want to do it again. [SPV2-OBS-E09-03, SPV2-OBS-E09-04, SPV2-OBS-E09-07, SPV2-OBS-E09-11, SPV2-OBS-E09-18]

### Source and evidence boundary

The principal read 310 lexical rows of the E09 Japanese index and checked the original subtitle script, including the simultaneous line about making one dream come true that is missing from the index at 1109.30–1114.19. The index also records 780 events from the drawn opening graphics. The principal inspected 856 retained images on 43 sheets. Nineteen additional native-picture sets supplied 312 selections, including the phone call, the group introductions and the concert bow. Twenty-one side-by-side comparisons included two within E09. Thirteen selected intervals in the Japanese main stereo measure energy and quiet portions of the mix. For example, the interval from the start of the final appeal through the start of the song introduction (1007.92–1026.18) has a mean level of −39.62 dBFS and 16.76 seconds below −45 dBFS. The main indexed lyric span (1036.64–1119.47) measures −19.63 dBFS, with no 10 ms bin below that threshold. This distinguishes the two intervals acoustically. It does not tell us who takes a breath, sings a line or lands a step. The witnesses are SPV2-W-E09-SRC, SPV2-W-E09-ZIP, SPV2-W-E09-JP and SPV2-W-E09-AO. Continuous movement, individual vocal assignments, actual transitions in the mix and performance judgments that depend on hearing remain open for the later audiovisual reading.
