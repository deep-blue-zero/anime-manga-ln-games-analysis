# SHINE POST E04 — What the girl in the middle wants

## A promise reaches its audience

The opening first puts five costumed performers before an arena, then cuts to street handbills, a springtime run, intercut portraits and a photograph. Its song, ワンダー・スターター, credits the five-person TINGS. The story resumes in the rehearsal room with three members of TiNgS facing their manager. The E03 title called Kyoka 引き立て役, the person who makes someone else stand out. The E04 title reads 玉城杏夏は《目立たない》. A job she can do for someone else has become a statement about how visible she is herself. Naoki has given her the center of the final song at the next regular live. The girl who once stood there and could not begin is furious. What does he think will happen if she fails again? Haru wants people to hear the version of *一歩前ノセカイ* that Kyoka sings. Her insistence that this is the “real” song can be heard in light of Haru's own doubt about the first attempt. In E03 she wondered whether coming to her friend's rescue at the first performance had been the right thing to do. Rio backs the decision. It is Haru who asks Kyoka to overcome the difficulty with them. Kyoka gives a professional reason for turning it down. She expects the audience to enjoy the concert more with someone else in front. She can do the supporting work. [SPV2-OBS-E04-01]

Yukine overhears and challenges that division of labor. Kyoka says the matter has nothing to do with her. Yukine supplies the distinction between their groups herself, then argues that being idols gives them a common stake in what either does with a chance. Kyoka has debuted. There is a stage she can stand on and an opportunity being offered to her. Yukine hears her talk of supporting the group as a young performer abandoning her dream under pressure. Kyoka begins a rejoinder about Yukine herself and never gets to finish it. Naoki calls a halt. Yukine concedes that she has intervened too much and leaves with Momiji. Naoki gives a brief thanks after the two turn to leave. Whether Yukine receives it is left unstated. For all his care in putting an end to a public reprimand, Naoki also makes the decision binding. Nothing Kyoka says will change the assignment. She acknowledges the instruction and the others prepare for practice. Her ability to conduct herself professionally in a room of people who have just overruled her will prove entirely consistent with a strong private desire for the very thing she refused. [SPV2-OBS-E04-01, SPV2-CLM-E04-01]

Out alone in a cap and glasses, Kyoka stops at a poster of Hotaru. The advertisement for Hotaru's 前奏曲 calls the performer a 特別な存在, a special presence. Kyoka adds a relationship the poster leaves out. She wants to be special *to someone*. The sight of the poster brings back the time before she knew that being special could mean something quite specific to another person. A girl at school asks for help with her homework. It has happened before. Kyoka good-naturedly resigns herself to helping and quietly gives her friend no account of what has been bothering her. She has done what her parents and teachers taught her, so well that other people depend on her. Nobody has taught her how to find one remarkable thing to do. In a particularly apt repayment her friend, credited as 池柴 朱 and addressed as 朱, takes the decision out of her hands. They go to see Hotaru at the Budokan. Her friend calls this a favor returned. Kyoka thinks she remembers being given very little choice. [SPV2-OBS-E04-02]

She would like to go home. First she has to get through the merchandise and the enthusiasm of someone who knows the place and the equipment. The stick in particular strikes her as unnecessary. The ticket identifies Hotaru’s Shining Tour Final 2018 at the Budokan, with a six o’clock start. The sign at 3.086 seconds in E01 named the same engagement. E01 let us watch the possibility of becoming an idol dawn on Haru in the audience. Kyoka’s first-person account gives the same evening a second history, without suggesting that the two future groupmates met there. The phone already reads ten past. Punctuality is something Kyoka knows how to judge, and she is ready to deliver a lecture afterward. Even in the dark she can supply a reasonable use for that stick as a flashlight. Then Hotaru appears. Her invitation to shine leads to one indexed line of the insert *Sweet Surrender* at 376.400–389.080. In that line the singer addresses someone who wants her love and asks for a pledge. The source gives us more of the concert than those few captioned words. What follows in Kyoka's account is the greatest shock of her life. The irritation goes. She searches for the stick. The friend who understood the point of bringing it holds a lit one up near her. Once the show is over Kyoka tells her what she has decided. She is going to be an idol. Between the ticket and the street outside the same object acquires an entirely different use. Kyoka has found someone on stage she would like to answer. [SPV2-OBS-E04-02, SPV2-OBS-E04-03]

She researches possible routes on a computer and chooses auditions. She starts applying, keeps being rejected and starts again. A judge recognizes the care she puts into her work but cannot see a quality that distinguishes her from other candidates. Outside, Yuki, the president of Brightest, says she came on someone else's business. She sees something she wants to cultivate in Kyoka. Kyoka joins Brightest by scouting. She remembers the offer as a reward from God. She soon learns that getting into the business does little to fix the exchange rate between effort and reward. School had seemed to pay back the work she put in. Flyers, a sparse house and repeated practice can coexist. She urges Rio to make up the loss next time. Haru believes they can bring energy to many people. Hearing that confidence, Kyoka locates the shadow that has fallen across the work in a second meeting with someone remarkable. [SPV2-OBS-E04-04]

Haru can adjust to situations as they arise. A smile stays on her face in circumstances where Kyoka would have to work to produce one. These are Kyoka's impressions of a partner she admires. E02 gave the audience grounds to understand how demanding Haru's adjustment to an ensemble can be. Kyoka lives on the receiving side of one especially painful example. The president gives her the center of a new song because her hosting has been so well received. At first her apprehension is visible enough for the president to give her an opening to reconsider. She has a ready, personable answer about liking cheeseburgers. The film returns to the real customers. They have finished and are off to sing karaoke. None has approached her. A fry is in her hand just before she says it tastes salty. Her face is wet. She can only ask for ‘just a little’. She has just complained of ordinary talent and imagined being recognized. The unfinished request can carry either wish. [SPV2-OBS-E04-06]

## Who can ask Kyoka to try again?

The girl who brought her there has a better grasp of Kyoka's everyday generosity than Kyoka does. In the classroom, she asks for homework help again, hands together in supplication. Kyoka protests and then sits down with her. The trip to Budokan is the friend's way of paying back a debt. Kyoka experiences the invitation as something closer to coercion, with some justice. Her companion has taken a liberty and guessed right about the pleasure it will give her. That history matters when another person later decides he knows what she should try. Being susceptible to pressure can open an unexpected door. It gives nobody a standing invitation to push her through it.

The most illuminating thing Kyoka does after the show is go home and look up how to become an idol. She says that if nobody has taught her what to do, she doesn't know where to start. Yet she finds an application route, tries it repeatedly, and continues after judges tell her they can't see what distinguishes her. Classwork has taught her to expect a relationship between effort and results. The auditions break that relationship while giving her a form of work she knows how to sustain. When Yuki finally approaches her, it is after another disappointment. What one panel experiences as undifferentiated thoroughness, a woman who happened to come along reads as the makings of an unusually steady light. Neither verdict involves measuring a fixed quantity of talent.

Haru is a harder case because Kyoka has stood beside her and watched her adjust to circumstances, smile through setbacks and bring the others with her. Kyoka calls her the second person to become special to her after Hotaru. The first encounter gave her a profession to pursue. The second gives her daily evidence for a theory that could keep her at the side of the stage forever. She can identify a quality she admires in a friend and still make a terrible deduction about her own prospects. Haru, meanwhile, wants people to hear the song as Kyoka sings it. The friend Kyoka has made her standard is asking for something Kyoka alone can give her.

The earlier offer of center exposed just how much she wanted the chance. Yuki starts from something audiences already like, her work as MC, and asks her to carry that ability into the new number. When Yuki notices fear and begins to offer a way out, Kyoka interrupts to ask for the part. She even tells Tokka to wait for her. Losing the words in the opening solo undoes a promise to a particular person as well as a bid for public recognition. Haru's cover saves the performance at the moment Kyoka feels her own song slipping away. It makes sense that she can be grateful to a colleague and unwilling to put herself in that position again.

Yukine has a different claim on Kyoka's attention. She sees a girl who has already debuted, has somewhere to stand and is prepared to abandon an opportunity that another idol can only want. Their common occupation is precisely what gives the dispute its edge. Her later interventions are smaller. She tells Naoki where to find Kyoka and, backstage, tells Kyoka that fifteen is an age for growing. She doesn't stay to deliver another speech. Rio makes room in her own way, taking on the MC work in her exuberantly regal manner before calling both girls into the last number. [SPV2-OBS-E04-21, SPV2-OBS-E04-22, SPV2-OBS-E04-23, SPV2-OBS-E04-24, SPV2-OBS-E04-27]

## Asking, agreeing and daring to want

Kyoka has two answers to being offered the center. When Yuki first asks, she says やります and then やらせてください, moving from “I’ll do it” to “please let me.” When Naoki assigns her the same song again, she says わかりました, “I understand.” The present-day answer comes after he has called it a settled decision regardless of what she says. The earlier answer comes when Yuki is ready to let her back out. Between the two lies the missed opening solo. The difference matters because it gives Kyoka a history of wanting the position before anyone thought she needed persuading. [TXT 823–827, 930–944. FRM 000318, 000766–000788.]

That desire started with an apparently useless stick. A school friend asks Kyoka for homework help, closing her appeals with the breezy っす. She insists on repaying the favor with a trip to see Hotaru. Kyoka, contemplating the merchandise queue, has a more searching description of the outing. It feels as though she has been press-ganged. At the concert she calls the light stick 棒 and wonders whether it serves as a flashlight. Once Hotaru appears, she urgently wants to know where her wonderful “stick” is. She still calls it by the same plain, descriptive word. The reversal belongs entirely to the person speaking. That evening she tells the same friend what she has decided to do with her life. [TXT 834–858, 862–887. FRM 000453, 000528–000544.]

Naoki has borrowed her joke too. His triumphant little flourish is Kyoka’s signal that one of her playful jokes has succeeded. She has every reason to tell him it has failed. Yet once seated beside her he can listen to something she has persistently withheld. Being dependable feels like a poor substitute for being wanted. She wants to remain an idol, stand on a larger stage and someday be like Hotaru. He grants her account of competition and different roles within a group considerable force. Then he asks which part of that account tells her what she wants. Calling her 野心家 gives her ambition a name she can use alongside “cool-headed” and “dependable.” When she asks what follows if her choice is a mistake, his answer puts an obligation on himself. Helping an idol through a mistake is her manager’s work. Soon she is instructing him to eat his pickles and asking which of them is the child. [TXT 1013–1061. FRM 000996, 001011–001028, 001085–001091.]

Backstage, Kyoka repeats やりたいことをやる, “do what you want to do,” and recasts the phrase in her customary polite form. She also has to tell herself to calm down. Permission to want something has hardly made it easy. Yukine has made a comparable change of register. Earlier she accused Kyoka of having no intention of growing and told her to hold on to her dream. Now she offers the peculiar observation that fifteen is a growing age. Kyoka says she knows. The line is awkward enough to be funny and leaves Kyoka room to receive encouragement without discussing her feelings again. The Japanese moves from 成長, development, to 育ち盛り, a young person’s growing years. [TXT 802–816, 1089–1093. FRM 000285, 001160–001173.]

Onstage Rio, working as the MC, orders everybody to keep enjoying themselves right to the end. Kyoka takes over with her own courteous 聴いてください. People in the hall remember that the group attempted the song once and left it out ever since. This time a first person makes it through the opening. The song places the singer one seat to the side and gives her a childhood habit of swallowing her own wishes so that others can be pleased. It also credits everyone who gave her courage. The public assertion of a private wish is something other people helped her accomplish. [TXT 1085–1088, 1094–1115, 1117–1132. FRM 001144, 001227, 001262–001299.]

Kyoka's objection and Yukine's reply make different claims even on paper. Kyoka says 私では盛り上がりません. The proposition concerns what the room will do *with me in the part*. This leaves room for an artist who does want the part to refuse it on the audience's behalf. Yukine picks up the words about getting people excited and sticking to support, and throws both back as questions. When Kyoka says the matter has nothing to do with her, Yukine makes a claim of professional jurisdiction. Her 我々 includes the two girls as fellow idols across the division between their groups. She can grant that division and still claim the right to speak. Kyoka's あなただって, roughly “you yourself, too…,” has a person in it and no finished accusation. We can see her turning the judgment back on the challenger. Supplying the charge would require evidence the interruption has denied us. [SPV2-OBS-E04-01, SPV2-OBS-E04-36]

Kyoka also exercises a small, sharp authority of her own through the way she addresses Naoki. When she calls his joke the greatest disappointment of her life, the superlative recalls her earlier narration about Hotaru as the greatest shock of her life. An idol's entrance and a manager's entrance have produced wonderfully different reviews from the same person. Later she uses a polite request to tell him to eat the pickles he removed. His すみません closes the exchange. The phone call that follows opens another demand for an apology, this time because he promised to call and didn't. Kei can jokingly sentence him to seppuku, ask him for an exception and take back her sentence when he offers another meeting. His ダメ, the little refusal between those bigger gestures, does what it says. There is no ticket. These two short sequences put some limits on the power we have just seen him exercise. He can be teased and held to account by people who know him, and his warm relations with a caller still allow him to decline a favor. [SPV2-OBS-E04-07, SPV2-OBS-E04-08, SPV2-OBS-E04-35, SPV2-OBS-E04-37]

## Two answers to the same question

The most damaging judgment Kyoka hears at an audition may be the most useful one for understanding her. Nobody there mistakes her for somebody who has not practiced. The problem is that the people deciding whom to pass cannot see whose work it is.
