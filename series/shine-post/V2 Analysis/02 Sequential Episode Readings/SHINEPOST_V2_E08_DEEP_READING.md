## How five girls became three and two

Before the split, TINGS had no assigned teacher. Gionji Yukine, an actress new to idol work, got five uncertain trainees moving, and Itō Momiji became the person who could both correct a dance and relieve the leader of feeling she had to do everything alone. The name Yukimoji first described their decision to help all five together. Their first complete rehearsal seemed to vindicate that promise. Then Momiji told Yukine she thought Haru had never given them her best. Watching Haru dance alone on the roof, Yukine saw an altogether different performer from the girl she had advised on overcoming stage fright. Hurt that Haru might have deceived them, Yukine accepted President Hinase Yuki’s proposal that the two leave TINGS and threaten it as rivals. The president thought Haru might uncover her strength to protect the people she had been holding back to spare. The pair risked their place in the group without any promise of getting it back. [SPV2-OBS-E08-02, SPV2-OBS-E08-06, SPV2-OBS-E08-11, SPV2-OBS-E08-13, SPV2-OBS-E08-14]

The recollection returns us to the conversation left unfinished in E07. Yukine has worked for the right to stand beside Haru. Haru says that giving her best would hurt everybody, invokes the approaching regular concert and a new song, and leaves. Momiji puts a hand on the defeated Yukine. Kyoka can list the things the two kept doing to help their supposed rivals. Rio still wants to know what Haru’s “real effort” could mean. The president tells the people left on the rooftop that Haru belonged to an idol group before joining Brightest. The picture supplies the name HY:RAIN on a poster that the school-uniformed Haru walks past. E08 has explained why Yukine and Momiji left. It ends before anyone explains why Haru once did. ## The part Yukine cannot play yet

A girl rehearsing at home speaks of making a world of real smiles. She can say the line in the first person of the boy who sings. What catches her is the adjective. Yukine will remember the evening when somebody asks what she wants from her second profession. ## What a leader can see from beside someone

Haru’s own speech here gives that interpretation some footing, but it also shows how much one loses by reducing all her conduct to one trait. She wants enough public prominence to make a great many strangers bolder. She insists in the present that her friends would be hurt by what she could do. The relation between those ambitions is a difficulty Haru lives with, and this retrospective episode gives us her companion’s first encounter with it. ## How an act of loyalty became a threat

Yuki catches them outside the roof. In the office Yukine can finally say what the episode title has withheld. She cannot forgive this. She had asked five people to use their strength together. Has Haru been deceiving them the whole time? Yukine has more reason to feel foolish than she has grounds to conclude she was a fool. She has been teaching someone better qualified than she is, congratulating herself on helping a girl reluctant to face an audience, worrying a bad training plan was keeping that girl back. Yet at least three other members improved under that plan, and all four thanked her before the fireworks. What she mistook was her pupil's need. What she has lost is the confidence that her friends and she meant the same thing by trying their best. Watching a stranger draw smiles at Budokan was the beginning of a vocation. Finding herself made a spectator without her knowledge threatens the terms on which she practices it. [SPV2-OBS-E08-13, SPV2-CLM-E08-13]

The president offers an account which has the force of an explanation while remaining conditional in the crucial place. Haru's dancing, singing and expression are in a different class. If she demonstrated her full ability, the difference might hurt the others and leave them in despair. Yuki can tell the pair what she believes Haru intends. She has not brought Haru into the room to tell them herself. In the subtitle witness the short phrase at C1054 is difficult, but the following lines explicitly name a disparity and a possible injury. In the present Haru will herself say she would hurt everyone. That later corroboration raises the confidence we can place in the broad protective account. It still supplies neither the event that taught her to expect harm nor the identity of someone she previously hurt. It is especially important to preserve that distinction in a story in which people repeatedly discover that a performance they observed correctly had a history they did not know. [SPV2-OBS-E08-13, SPV2-CLM-E08-13]

One line of the president's explanation resists a clean translation. At 16:27.44 both Japanese subtitle editions read 君たちのダメだよ. The remedy is also the president's idea. She asks first if Yukine is frustrated, then whether she wants Haru to give her best. Give Haru something she must use her strength to protect. Someone who withholds her ability to guard her colleagues may use it for exactly the same reason under different conditions. It is a gamble on the consistency of Haru's character. Its price falls on both the girl being tested and the people enlisted to test her. Failure might leave them hated and cost them everything. Even success carries no promise that the original five can come back. The episode goes directly from Yuki asking whether they will do it to the moment E07 had given us from the abandoned three's side. The spectators can now answer a question they could not have answered at the time. Rio and Kyoka, whose own inadequacies seemed to each of them a plausible reason for the split, are still going to suffer the consequences of not being told. [SPV2-OBS-E08-14, SPV2-CLM-E08-14]

### The gamble and its terms

What else might the pair have done? The story contains one untried alternative in Yukine’s own recent past. On the bench she asked Haru whether her lessons suited her. Haru deflected the question, but the exchange that followed did give them a dream each could describe in her own words. When the president learns that the girls have seen the private dance, the three have another possible occasion for asking Haru. Instead the president asks whether Yukine wants to *make* Haru show what she can do. The change of verb makes room for the plan that follows. It does not require us to assume that an honest conversation would have worked. Haru's response on the present-day roof gives ample reason to doubt that it would have brought a quick resolution. But she has never consented to this test, and the people asked to conduct it have interests of their own. Yukine wants a place beside the strongest version of her friend, is ashamed at what she has misunderstood and is angry at what she regards as deceit. Momiji has just pledged herself to the person now hurt most visibly by what they saw. The president's account is perceptive enough to predict the fear Haru will name and partial enough to leave the most important condition of success outside her control. [SPV2-CLM-E08-06, SPV2-CLM-E08-07, SPV2-CLM-E08-13, SPV2-CLM-E08-14, SPV2-CLM-E08-16]

### Hands in the foreground
