# SHINE POST E05 — Who gets people through the door?

Rio gives up a day at a sweetshop whose pudding delights her and takes the television assignment, leaving Haru and Kyoka to meet prospective fans in person. ## What counts as doing the job?

The first images on Rio's phone pose a question the episode keeps transferring from one working environment to another. How do you know whether you have done well? Somebody has typed a message for each of these girls. Rio can count the likes. She cannot read from six little thumbs-up marks what the people behind them thought she was capable of. Even the encouragement about dancing permits two readings. A fan might mean it as a vote of confidence in someone who is visibly trying. In the company of two messages about the appeal her partners already have, the comment may land as a reminder that she has yet to arrive. When the others come in they greet her perfectly sincerely as the first of them at work. Only the viewer gets to see just how strange a measure of precedence that is for the girl who has been reading those comments. [SPV2-OBS-E05-01, SPV2-CLM-E05-01]

At the farm Rio has more success than she can recognize with a measure that a television director is qualified to use. A vegetable can come out of the ground awkwardly in a way that gives an editor quite a lot to work with. A lizard on a hand, the position of the camera when somebody loses her balance, a friend ready to pick up a mispronunciation: none of this requires Rio to have become the mixture of grace and speed that she admires in her partners. The harvest gives her a chance to be funny while still being taken seriously as a person who has come to do a day's work. The trouble with applying that lesson wholesale to the next scene is that Sato no longer needs a funny thing to happen in front of the camera. A grower's produce is on the table. An honest personal judgment about a vegetable can work at cross purposes with the ordinary courtesies of introducing someone else's food to a television audience.
