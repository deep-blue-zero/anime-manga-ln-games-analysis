# SHINE POST E03 — Whom a fan comes back to hear

## A song that keeps coming back

Kyoka stands between Haru and Rio at Bright Station. She thanks the people who have come and introduces a new number, *一歩前ノセカイ*. A young woman with a pink light looks toward the stage. The welcome has gone perfectly well. It is when the audience expects the song to begin that Kyoka cannot find the words. She looks for her partners. They are still standing on either side of her, but even at close quarters she cannot get a readable face from either. At 40.040 seconds the young woman in the audience has an extraordinarily legible face by comparison. Kyoka wakes in her bedroom. She has been dreaming. Her conclusion as she looks toward the idol poster is that she is no one special. It takes almost the whole episode to recover the history behind that conclusion, and an encounter with this same fan to learn what the history looks like from the floor. [SPV2-OBS-E03-01]

The previous regular live has left Haru in an altogether different mood. Homare can tell just by looking across their school desks. Haru makes a quiz of it and awards her friend a Haru smile for the right answer. Haru remembers their shows as roughly half full and celebrates that this one was full. Her informal comparison leaves E01's reported previous high of thirty-seven at its own boundary. Haru credits the manager. Homare says people at the performance were talking about Haru herself. Haru is grateful but her expression changes. She wants people to have come away wanting to see Kyoka and Rio, too. Homare can both name the attention Haru drew and agree with her about the other two. For Haru this is what the ambition to be a signpost means in practice. She wants to direct someone's gaze beyond herself. They can discuss a second sellout, encourage each other and then turn with equal enthusiasm to the round doughnuts. School gives Haru a place to admit a reservation about a good night without having to manage anyone else's disappointment. [SPV2-OBS-E03-02]

Before the next sale, Rio trusts her imperial persona, Haru volunteers cheerfully, and Kyoka offers to sell every ticket by next month. The concert is tomorrow. Kyoka announces that her little joke has succeeded while the manager admits to some distress. He calls the coming show his first regular live as their dedicated manager in the presence of all three girls. E02 ended with evidence of his decision addressed to Kikuchi. E03 supplies evidence for what the performers themselves can now know. The three step out carrying flyers and discover customers already waiting. Naoki estimates ninety and expects that this time he can leave the selling to the performers. Fans greet particular members, exchange words and take tickets. The sellout takes an hour according to Haru's account in the car. Three different things have happened here. People came to the right place without being collected from another venue, someone estimated the size of a queue, and the tickets for this show sold out. In E02 a timer covered the sale of the remainder after thirty-two tickets had already sold. This hour belongs to the new sale. None of those is an independent head count at either show. [SPV2-OBS-E03-03]

Kyoka recognizes Tokka in the line. Tokka asks for a ticket. Part of the exchange could take place with any returning customer. Then she asks whether she can believe, and says she will be waiting. Kyoka starts to speak after her. Another buyer has to get her attention. That interruption registers the cost to a competent seller of a question she understands better than we do. Naoki sees one problem solved and a second one emerging. Rio sees an odd expression in the car. She gives her attention a name and a physical form, her Rio the Spiral, and tells Kyoka they have to make Nakano Sunplaza a success. The girls can be very close together in that back seat and still have different accounts of what just happened. Elsewhere Kei makes Naoki defend himself over the phone. He starts explaining why the girls impressed him enough to accept the dedicated post. The complaint she corrects him about is that he didn't invite her. For someone with an extraordinary faculty for spotting discrepancies in other people, he is entirely capable of misunderstanding an ordinary reproach. [SPV2-OBS-E03-03, SPV2-OBS-E03-04]

Yukine and Momiji are together in the lesson room. On a day when the agency does not need their help, the two of them can use all this space. Momiji finds it too large. She begins a thought which the scene leaves unfinished. The others' arrival turns the room back into a place of competitive familiarity. The sellout seems incredible against the number of tickets that would ordinarily have gone by this hour. Momiji also claims that Rio was late in the dancing last time. She thinks she dances better, and gets herself into a semantic muddle trying to express how sure she is. Across the room Yukine has a better way of testing an impression. She makes the usual cutting remark about Kyoka's lack of growth. Kyoka agrees. Yukine expected one of her bad jokes. An acquaintance who recognizes the failure to make a joke has learned a great deal about the person making them. Haru tells Yukine about the longer-than-usual conversation with Tokka. She thinks Kyoka has remembered something. She enlists Yukine's help, which Yukine agrees to give this once. [SPV2-OBS-E03-05]

Rio has a turn she wants to promote to a signature performance. Kyoka's verdict is that she is going round in a circle. ## The job Kyoka knows how to do

When Kyoka tells her manager a joke, a misunderstanding is half the fun. She promises to sell all the tickets by next month. When he reminds her that the concert is tomorrow, she declares the joke a success, then wonders why he hasn't found it funnier. There is plenty of daring in someone prepared to keep doing this to her colleagues. Ask her to stand at the center of the same stage where she can address a roomful of people, though, and she has an elaborate account of why the proposal makes no sense. It would be easy to call one of these versions of Kyoka an act. Both call on an ability she has worked hard to acquire. She knows who the audience is, what the job requires, and exactly what she might lose.

Seeing what a friend needs and knowing how to give it to her are different skills. Haru persuades Yukine to help, throws her arms around her, then hurries away before they have agreed what the plan entails. It turns out to involve dangling a charm in front of Kyoka and hoping that Yukine will somehow solve the problem afterward. Kyoka's dry observation that a person with something difficult to disclose might choose to keep it to herself deserves to survive the joke. Momiji makes much the same diagnosis and promptly demonstrates its downside by telling everyone the unflattering comparison Yukine once made in private. Even Kyoka finds the laundry-tissue image interesting. Its public airing still puts two would-be helpers within reach of her formidable hands.

Yukine knows Kyoka well enough for an absent joke to alarm her. She has just accused Kyoka of making no progress. The unexpected response is agreement. Yukine goes after an explanation and later tells Naoki what happened at the old regular live. Haru remembers Kyoka's express prohibition and objects. Yukine promises to take responsibility herself. There is tenderness in giving Haru a way out of betraying her friend, and presumption in deciding what another girl's repeated “absolutely” must mean. When Kyoka later accuses the manager of hearing it from both girls, we know something she doesn't. Haru was present. Yukine made the decision. Knowing more about the wound than Kyoka wanted him to know may make him a better coach, but it does nothing by itself to make him easier to trust.

Rio's idea of intervention scarcely requires a private confidence. When somebody in the car looks miserable after a sellout, Rio launches her “Spiral” at Kyoka and forbids her to wear that face. The threat to the group's future is real enough. She can be genuinely worried and still turn that worry into another chance to perform as Rio-sama. At the handshake table a fan praises how hard she has been working. She waves that praise away and nearly gives out the as-yet unannounced Nakano Sunplaza plan. The girl who tells others to watch themselves is capable of a glorious failure to watch her own mouth. She does catch herself. It is hard to imagine a more interesting test of the manager's judgment than asking her to do Kyoka's work as main MC. [SPV2-OBS-E03-21, SPV2-OBS-E03-24, SPV2-OBS-E03-25, SPV2-OBS-E03-26]

## Which performance does Tokka want to hear?

The first invitation to hear “一歩前ノセカイ” comes from the stage. The second comes from the floor. At the beginning Kyoka announces the new song and says 聴いてください, the courteous imperative of a performer who has the floor. Before long, the only words the transcript can find for her are repeated あれ and an unfinished “the lyrics…” At the handshake event the invitation comes back the other way. Tokka asks Kyoka to let *her* hear the song, 聴かせてね. The fan has the title and the words to make the request. The idol who introduced it can barely acknowledge what she means. [TXT 1–10, 1107–1121, 00:01–01:10 and 17:03–17:55. FRM 000014, 000046, 001077.]

Politeness can make a refusal unmistakable. Kyoka’s これ以上踏み込まないでください, “please don’t intrude any further,” comes after she calls Naoki indispensable. Both statements can hold. She has also answered his question about the group with a sound account of public awareness. He first asked how the present difficulty looks from her perspective, and agrees with her diagnosis. Only then does he ask what problem is hers to solve. She would put that question off and give the group priority. Yukine calls Kyoka’s repeated insistence on secrecy a フリ, a comic setup inviting someone to do what was expressly forbidden. Haru initially objects. Yukine takes responsibility for telling Naoki, and Kyoka later complains that she specifically warned them. Translating フリ as genuine permission would let the listener decide what the speaker meant without preserving the speaker’s objection. [TXT 1031–1058, 1065–1086, 1122–1130, 12:56–14:27, 14:41–15:36 and 18:02–18:22.]

## Rehearsing a public identity in the rehearsal room

Rio the Typhoon has its own miniature audience and critic. At 608.500–618.380 Rio presents it to Kyoka as her ultimate invention. Kyoka's literal description of turning lets Rio insist twice that this is something different. A turn accompanied by a name and an attitude is different to the person trying to sell it. The others can find it ordinary and still pay attention. Momiji has her own way of explaining how Rio's dancing at the last show struck her. Her conviction has changed into confidence. The target of the criticism catches the accidental downgrade. It is a small measure of how much Rio can do with an audience of one. She can hear an insult to her prowess, spot a hole in its wording and keep up her own royal act. None of those skills settles the reported lateness or guarantees the control needed to introduce a whole concert. When she nearly tells a customer what is planned for Nakano, the danger comes from too much eagerness to take that customer into her confidence. ## Who gets to say what counts as success?
