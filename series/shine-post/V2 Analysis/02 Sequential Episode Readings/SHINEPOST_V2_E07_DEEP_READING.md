## Why five names make a difficult invitation

Rio’s performance at the mall has brought TiNgS new fans. The president thinks the gamble on the mall performance has paid off and begins to look ahead to Nakano Sunplaza, two months away. Naoki takes her back to the condition they had set for that concert. Everyone in the group must be able to perform at full strength. The three idols he manages once belonged to a five-member TINGS. The two lowercase letters in their current name stand for the two who left, Itō Momiji and Gionji Yukine. Using material the president has shown him, he asks them to return. All three present members welcome the proposal. The two former members refuse. Momiji has been teaching Rio to dance, and Yukine has just thanked Momiji for understanding her. Affection crosses the line between the units considerably more easily than the manager’s invitation does. [SPV2-OBS-E07-01, SPV2-OBS-E07-02, SPV2-OBS-E07-03, SPV2-OBS-E07-05]

Haru organizes the attempt to persuade them. Rio and Kyoka each ask Yukine, who helps both with more immediate problems and maintains her refusal. Haru tries to buy her cooperation with an ice cream Yukine herself has paid for. The same ice cream reaches Momiji, who says quite readily that she would return. There is a condition the others have missed. She will never leave anybody alone, and returning without Yukine would do just that. Faced with Yukine again, Haru asks why someone who calls herself their rival makes a point of watching all their performances. Haru wants to pursue her dream with these particular four girls. Yukine answers that she still comes nowhere near Haru, who has yet to show them what she can really do. Naoki asks the question too. He has seen how quickly Haru can register what a spectator needs and change what reaches the floor. Why is she holding back? Haru insists she always gives it everything. At the end she is the one surrounded by the gold that Naoki sees when a person’s words conflict with what they believe. Nobody has rejoined the group. The dispute over two absent members has exposed a question about one who was there all along. [SPV2-OBS-E07-08, SPV2-OBS-E07-10, SPV2-OBS-E07-11, SPV2-OBS-E07-12, SPV2-OBS-E07-13, SPV2-OBS-E07-14]

## Two people who think together

Before a manager explains what an agency is missing, the episode shows what the smaller unit already has. Yukine stands at a locker in practice clothes. A brief image of Rio singing at her standing microphone interrupts the view of Yukine’s face. Momiji recognizes something newly apparent about “Rio-tan” and how much TiNgS is improving. There is barely a sentence between them when they get to their reservation. One starts a “but” and the other can supply the thought that something is still missing. A little later Yukine will use the same words about how far she herself has to go. In this first exchange the object is still unstated. What Yukine does name is her debt to Momiji. Thanks to her she needn’t turn things over in her mind all by herself. By 31.031 seconds she has turned toward the smaller girl and is smiling. Momiji calls them YukiMoji, as though saying their joint name were enough of an explanation. Two dresses hang together on a rack as the scene ends. At the other end of this episode, the girls who want to perform with them will find out how much goes into keeping that promise to be two. [SPV2-OBS-E07-01, SPV2-CLM-E07-01]

In the president's office it is possible to see the same evening as an uncomplicated success. A post has a close view of Rio at the microphone. Another shows all three from behind people with yellow lights. One of the worthwhile things the last episode left undecided has an answer. The performance has travelled beyond the people who stood at the mall. The president says that event videos are still being shared and the group has become better known. When the friends later recall the increased interest, the picture moves to the pavement outside Bright Station, where a little crowd gathers around the girls. The signs and spatial arrangements distinguish the two sites. Neither scene gives a running count of tickets for Nakano, and a person who sees a shared clip can intend any number of things by passing it on. The president has reason to be pleased. It is her move from success at the mall to the likelihood of success at Nakano that interests Naoki. [SPV2-OBS-E07-02, SPV2-CLM-E07-02]

Yuki’s pleasure is worth looking at twice. She is still at the desk, in her ordinary blue jacket at 43.627 seconds. At 44.211 the gold surrounds her as she says that TiNgS has exceeded expectations. The pictures of posts are evidence of a different kind. One shows Rio at the microphone. Another writer says the quality of her singing made them a fan on the spot, 一発でファンになった, above a picture of the three at the mall. The publicity survives scrutiny even if its supposed power to surprise the president does not. Perhaps she had hoped for just such a result and is trying to make her manager admit that their gamble has paid off. Perhaps the exaggeration lies in the comparison of actual growth with expectations that nobody has specified. The source localizes the discrepancy better than it identifies the private thought behind it. The president can see the words of someone publicly professing a new interest in the group because of Rio’s singing. The yellow lights in E06 suggested interest, as did the lingering picture of a woman who resembled a flyer recipient. Neither witness could tell us how a particular spectator understood the experience. Somebody who calls themselves a new fan has still made a different commitment from somebody who buys a ticket. [SPV2-OBS-E07-15, SPV2-CLM-E07-15, SPV2-OPEN-E06-03]

The group name has been in front of the audience for six episodes. Now the film takes it apart. Tamaki Kyoka supplies the T, Nabatame Haru the N and Seibu Rio the S. From the spectator's side the two little letters have always looked like a graphic choice. In the older logo the I of Itō and the G of Gionji occupy the same scale as the other three. The tablet seen at 403.111 seconds carries both designs, one above the other. Yuki has given Naoki access to footage of the five and documents reaching back to their formation. His discovery is new to the audience in a different sense from its novelty for the girls. Haru already knows who left. She is surprised that her manager knows. The five performers in the opening sequence can now be recognized as a historically possible group by a viewer who reaches this episode for the first time. A song credit in E01 had supplied the two names. Only now do the conversation and records reveal what those names meant to the people at Brightest. ### What the credit cards can tell us

The opening begins with five performers, after the office conversation has accounted for every letter in their name. The Brightest trainee listing supplies two different professional histories. Yukine brings experience as a child actor to her expressive work, while Momiji’s entry draws attention to the sharpness of her dancing and her cheerful disposition. The undated page lists Yukine as sixteen, born August 8, and Momiji as fourteen, born August 15. These are profiles the agency chose to show prospective trainees. They establish how the two were presented to visitors at the time of the listing. [SPV2-OBS-E07-19, SPV2-CLM-E07-19] Those profiles complicate the assumption that the pair could be hired back simply to give the trio two more bodies for the same routine. Rio already knows what this particular teacher can do for her. Yukine, who is particularly qualified to recognize a performed identity, is about to ask Haru whether she will show them who she is. The parallel is interpretive. Yukine herself describes the basis of her judgment as having worked beside Haru since they met. [SPV2-OBS-E07-42, SPV2-OBS-E07-52, SPV2-OBS-E07-12]

## Holding a meeting is easier than changing someone’s mind

“Holding the first emergency meeting to restore the five-member TINGS” is quite a production for three girls, one interested manager and a whiteboard. Haru has equipped herself to run it. She points to the already-written agenda, adjusts a pair of glasses and asks whether Naoki has noticed anything that might help. It's an appealing reversal of their usual working arrangements, and an opportunity to watch an argument slip a gear. The president and Naoki regard everybody reaching their potential as a condition of success at Nakano. Haru tries to “put that another way” and arrives at a prediction. Once the five are together, the concert will succeed. The other two accept the inviting conclusion. Even beating HY:RAIN is thinkable with those two on their side. The two months of preparation, the work involved in selling seats and the consent of the absent members have vanished inside a neat proposition. Naoki tells Haru he still cannot see the answer. She will have to supply a means of persuasion for an end about which her colleagues already agree. [SPV2-OBS-E07-06, SPV2-CLM-E07-06]

What she has particularly good access to is the past. Yukine led the five and sustained the other girls. Momiji repeatedly taught Rio to dance. The tablet shows the strenuous work behind the fond recollection, with Rio’s leg raised and her face registering the stretch. The memory of the split has an equally particular geography. Yukine and Momiji, carrying their things, walk away along the pavement while the other three stand outside Brightest in practice clothes. Haru begs them to say they're kidding. Yukine announces a new unit, says she has nothing to tell them and asks them to stop addressing her so freely. The departure was a fact the others did not get to negotiate, accompanied by an explanation too slight to let them know what to change. Rio and Kyoka are consequently each able to imagine being the reason. Rio thinks she held Yukine back. Kyoka remembers giving others trouble. Haru is generous enough to reject both accounts and honest enough to admit she has no alternative to offer. She repeatedly says they must “do their best” until someone points out that she keeps saying the same thing. Even from behind the pointer she is better at telling friends what outcome she wants than working out why another friend might want something else. [SPV2-OBS-E07-07, SPV2-CLM-E07-07]

The three subsequent attempts to enlist Yukine are comedies with different costs. Rio finds her eating a hamburger and joins her at the counter with a tray. School uniforms replace practice clothes, both girls face a window and there is time to go on eating even when the conversation has stalled. Rio’s first offer is a public stage. When will the two have one if they insist on reaching it under their own name? Her second proposal is the familiar plan to leave everything to Rio-sama. Both make it possible to avoid asking the question that has followed her out of the meeting. When she finally asks if they left because she kept dragging them down, Yukine gives her face some close attention. She has sauce on it. An original picture at 697.739 shows Yukine raising a cloth to Rio while supporting her chin with her other hand. The cloth is pale blue, and Rio raises her own hand only after Yukine draws away. Yukine tells Rio the sauce is gone, accepts her thanks and answers her question kindly. Of course Rio wasn’t the reason. There is an important personal victory here for somebody who recently had to learn that she could ask for lessons. Rio has expressed what she was afraid of and received an answer from the person best placed to give it. When she tries to get back to the question of rejoining, Yukine gives the same refusal as before and walks away. Knowing it wasn't her fault is something Rio can take back from a failed mission. [SPV2-OBS-E07-08, SPV2-CLM-E07-08]

Kyoka tries something more like her own account of why groups are worth joining. Practicing with five people improves the quality of the work and lets people cover for each other. She makes the case while jogging abreast of someone who says she already has the partner she needs. Could Yukine at least give her the reason they left? No information is owed to a rival. Kyoka offers another inducement, the chance to hear her charming jokes every day. Yukine has told her before what she thinks of them. She can be this blunt because they have known each other long enough to repeat an old disagreement in the middle of a new one. A sudden instruction to stop cuts across the exchange. Kyoka has a loose shoelace. The pictures take us from the trailing lace and Yukine bending down to the fastening in order. Yukine calls it an Ian knot, the sort that will stay put during a hard run. Here is one of those quite specific cases of someone covering for somebody else. Rejoining is unnecessary for Yukine to do it. Kyoka thanks her, and her interlocutor gets away running before the thanks can do the work of a new argument. [SPV2-OBS-E07-08, SPV2-CLM-E07-08]

Haru does not even manage to stage her attempt before she needs Yukine’s help. She is in Family Martha worrying that it is up to her now. The clerk needs 130 yen. That amount recurs five times in the two subtitle lanes as Haru tries to think what to do about her friends. One demand occupies the main lane. Four more run across the upper lane while her private difficulties occupy the lower one. It takes an inspection of her purse to discover that acquiring the ice cream she brought to the till presents a problem of its own. Yukine pays. The picture isolates one silver-colored and three copper-colored coins beside the twin pack. Outside the shop she teases Haru for wanting an ice so badly. Haru offers it to her if she will come back. It takes a special sort of ingenuity to try to buy someone’s cooperation with something that person bought in the first place, as Yukine promptly points out. Her sentence gives the buyer and the prospective beneficiary the same grand pronoun, 私様. The failure has a durable prop, a little red and white package containing two pieces meant for sharing. It goes back to the committee. Kyoka remarks that she has never met someone so stubborn. Rio spots the resemblance to Kyoka, who protests on the second text lane that what she has is strength of will. Those lines are still occupying the conversation when Haru says “two?” They've been treating a joint refusal as a decision that only one member of the pair can explain. [SPV2-OBS-E07-09, SPV2-CLM-E07-09]

Turning first to Yukine made sense if she was the leader who had spoken for both of them in the street. It is less adequate to the two working friendships in front of the girls now. Rio spends enough time with Momiji for Haru and Kyoka to send her as their representative. Why, Rio wants to know, does that mean it has to be her? Her friends have the answer, the confidence to supply it for her and the ice cream. It is worth remembering how a sweet changed hands in E06. Rio brought a Kantendō pudding to the changing room, gave it to Momiji and made her own difficult request. This second gift is someone else’s idea, has passed through several hands and is expected to soften up its recipient for a request to which three people want the same answer. With all their attention on whether Momiji is in a nice mood, the organizers have allowed the ice to melt. An especially good joke is the way its failure makes an ordinary invitation possible again. Rio is so discouraged that she simply asks whether Momiji might come back, with a negative ending as if preparing for a no. Momiji says yes, quite readily, and then says it again. [SPV2-OBS-E07-09, SPV2-OBS-E07-10, SPV2-CLM-E07-10]

We can trace their plan in the changing shape of the frozen sweet. On the roof two white rounds sit side by side in the open tray. Momiji lifts a long, soft strand with the little pick as she talks. Haru and Kyoka have been stacked one above the other behind a tree, peering around the trunk. When Momiji realizes what she has agreed to, she raises a hand to her mouth. Now the two watchers tower over her, arms folded. Rio points. The picture cuts up into the leaves and stays there. We come down to find Haru already around Momiji’s shoulders, Rio at one leg and Kyoka at the other, holding up a pink phone with a recorder on its screen. The camera leaves the change between those arrangements out of sight. [SPV2-OBS-E07-60, SPV2-OBS-E07-61, SPV2-OBS-E07-62]

No one has been prepared for that answer either. At 16:21.5 the phone reads VOICE RECORDER and 録音１. The Japanese transcription repeats Momiji’s earlier assent while the device is on screen. For a moment the meeting operates as if a recording could secure a member. What the little recording preserves is a sentence. It leaves out the condition that makes the sentence a reliable indication of what Momiji wants. She will never leave anybody alone. If she returned by herself she would do precisely that to Yukine. The girl who was able to take a stand against Rio leaving by herself in E06 means this enough to inconvenience several other people she genuinely likes. It would be too simple to conclude that she is obeying an order from Yukine, and just as simple to take her first “fine by me” as final consent. Kyoka, who is good at finding the missing term in an arrangement once she stops trying to win it, asks whether the answer changes if Yukine comes too. Momiji reaches for a decorous expression of willingness and comes out with やぶさめ, horseback archery. The correction replaces the last め with か, yielding やぶさか. Its phrasing on the page suggests a continuation of Kyoka’s polite question, but the shot stays on Momiji’s eye and the subtitle names no speaker. A friend can be exact about another girl’s idiom without having been remotely accurate about what kept her away. [SPV2-OBS-E07-10, SPV2-OBS-E07-11, SPV2-CLM-E07-11]

## Who is asking whom to change?

What Yukine sees when she comes looking for her partner would test the explanatory powers of an innocent party. The dessert is collapsing in its tray. Haru puts a bare arm across Momiji’s mouth, and Momiji takes hold of her forearm. At 17:16.369 Kyoka has her hands at her own chest, with Rio down on all fours. Momiji soon runs over to Yukine herself. The three would-be recruiters can no longer speak for her even by accident. Rio asked about returning and Momiji freely answered yes. The physical scramble and the recording have since made the others look much more effective at laying a trap than they actually were. When Momiji warns of the enemy's cunning and says she very nearly finished nodding her assent, Yukine has reason to wonder whether that isn't a little late. Momiji can assure her of one important fact. She did say she wouldn't return. Here the apparent about-face takes on another value. The answer she can sincerely give this particular friend includes what she has decided to do about the friend's situation. Her solo preference and her decision about leaving have different objects. A clip of her voice, like the gold around her earlier answer, can expose a gap. Neither can fill in her reason. She had to be allowed to finish a sentence. [SPV2-OBS-E07-10, SPV2-OBS-E07-11, SPV2-OBS-E07-63]

Yukine arrived after the recorded exchange. Haru now tells her both parts of Momiji’s answer. She would return, but she will not leave Yukine alone. For the first time the former leader has the terms Momiji explained to the trio beside the bench. Haru’s last attempt at persuasion grows out of something she has noticed herself. Every time her trio has a live, these supposed rivals make a point of coming. What are they doing there, if their resolve to stand apart means everything Yukine says it means? It's a question a reader can now take back to the two figures on the mall balcony. We already knew they were watching Rio. We can now place their position in the audience next to the history of a five-person practice room and the refusal to make it five again. Haru's interpretation is that they worry about the group. Yukine begins to call it research into a rival. Watching a competitor closely could certainly be part of it. In the last episode Momiji was also calling to someone she had promised to teach, and these women have demonstrated over and over how immediately practical their concern for a colleague can be. More than one motive can bring someone to a show. Haru can ask this question because the performer has been paying attention to the occupants of the gallery in her turn. The very act that makes her a formidable advocate for getting the five back together prepares the charge that she is wasting a professional gift. [SPV2-OBS-E07-12, SPV2-CLM-E07-12, SPV2-OBS-E06-12]

Haru extends a bare hand across the gap. This time she doesn't offer a stage, a joke or an ice cream. She names Kyoka, Rio, Yukine and Momiji, and tells them exactly whom she wants beside her when she becomes a Shine Post. Her ambition in E01 had room for anybody who loved an idol. The list makes clear that the company in which she pursues it is far from interchangeable. Yukine looks at her and recognizes the girl she met in the first place. To Haru, constancy has the force of a promise. To Yukine the same thing is becoming intolerable. The earlier accusation that Haru's singing and dancing have stayed the same had sounded like a reproach to someone whose teammates were surpassing her. Yukine now says she herself still doesn't come close to Haru. “Still not enough” has come back in the first person. Someone who aspires to stand beside a much stronger performer can have reason to resent being asked to go back to a version of the group in which that difference is never acknowledged. It remains a reading of her present complaint. The flashback to the day of the split does not give us a private conversation or prove that this was the entire reason either one of them left. [SPV2-OBS-E07-01, SPV2-OBS-E07-07, SPV2-OBS-E07-12]

### A pronoun that cannot speak for two

Yukine can make a unit of two sound like an institution. 我々ゆきもじ gives her a formal collective “we” for her own side, while 君たちTiNgS puts the girls who want her help on the other side of the sentence. She speaks for the two even in recalling her decision to leave. That is one reason the committee never thinks of interviewing Momiji until it has failed three times with Yukine. Once it does, the person with the smaller share of the original announcement can answer for herself. On the roof the two girls agree on what they will do today, but they give reasons in the first person. Momiji can contemplate dancing with her former partners again. Yukine thinks about what it would take for *her* to stand on a stage beside Haru. [SPV2-OBS-E07-31, SPV2-OBS-E07-34, SPV2-OBS-E07-36, SPV2-OBS-E07-37]

It makes a difference that Yukine drops the self-important 私様 for a plain 私 when she gestures towards her own chest at 19:21.8. She can tell Haru she still falls short of her without specifying exactly what she finds unforgivable in the other's conduct. There are two uses of “return” to set beside each other. In the practice room she denies ever wanting to return to *those days*. Here 戻れるわけない denies the possibility of going back while things stand as they are. That leaves open a future with new terms. Haru repeats でも four times across two subtitle entries as she tries to frame a reply. Naoki addresses her by her full name when he asks why she hides what she can do. We have had an account of a restraint from somebody who watches her, and an account of its effect on somebody who works with her. Her own language still gives us an assertion about her effort where a reason for her conduct is wanted. [SPV2-OBS-E07-33, SPV2-OBS-E07-37, SPV2-OBS-E07-38]

Rio asked the first question with Momiji beside her and their two friends listening from their hiding place. Now all three are in plain view. When Rio begins to tell Momiji that she too wants to be together, Momiji cuts her off. Rejoining on her own would leave Yukine alone.
