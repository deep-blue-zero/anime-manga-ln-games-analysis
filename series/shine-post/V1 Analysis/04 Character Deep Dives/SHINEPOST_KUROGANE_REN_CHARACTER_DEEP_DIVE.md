# Kurogane Ren deep dive: the girl who trained herself into a promise Haru could no longer return to

Ren is a late-arriving character, but she is not a minor one. She is the living proof that Haru’s past did not disappear just because Haru found TINGS. In narrative terms, Ren is the person who forces the series to ask whether Haru’s new home is a resolution or an evasion.

Her core thesis is:

> **Ren is a girl who turned abandonment into training, love into proof, and an unfinished childhood promise into a claim over Haru’s future.**

That is why she feels so possessive. She is not merely jealous that Haru joined another group. She has built her post-Haru self around becoming the one person who can receive Haru’s full brilliance. When Haru chooses TINGS, Ren does not experience it as a simple career decision. She experiences it as the collapse of the meaning she gave to her own suffering.

The anime does not give Ren as much time as the TINGS girls, but what it gives is dense. The broader *SHINE POST* synthesis identifies Ren as “the possessive wound of an unfinished promise,” and that is exactly right: her relationship with Haru begins in ordinary intimacy, becomes an idol promise, breaks under talent inequality, and returns as a possessive demand to “get Haru back.”[

# Ren’s function in the story

Ren enters after TINGS has already solved its internal problem.

By Episode 9, Haru has finally gone all out. TINGS has accepted her full power. The lowercase TiNgS has become uppercase TINGS. In a more conventional idol anime, that could be the final emotional climax: the group becomes whole, sells out the venue, performs successfully, and the dream continues.

Ren prevents that from being enough.

She says, in effect:

> You may have accepted Haru.
> But I knew Haru first.
> I made the promise first.
> I trained for Haru.
> I can receive her full power better than you can.
> So why does she belong with you?

That is why Ren matters so much. She is not just a rival from a stronger group. She is a rival claim on Haru’s identity.

TINGS’ internal arc answers the question:

> Can Haru go all out without destroying the people beside her?

Ren’s arc asks the next question:

> Even if TINGS can receive Haru, do they have the right to call her theirs when someone from her past rebuilt herself around the same promise?

That makes Ren the final test of Haru’s agency.

# Ren before the wound: childhood intimacy, not rivalry

Ren’s relationship with Haru does not begin as rivalry. It begins as shared ordinary life.

The flashbacks are important because they are warm, casual, and domestic: donuts, Hotaru fandom, imitating idol language, talking about dreams as children do before the world has hardened those dreams into stakes. Ren is not introduced as someone who wants to defeat Haru. She is introduced as someone who wants to go forward with Haru.

That matters because Ren’s later possessiveness is not built from abstract ambition. It is built from the memory of a life where Haru’s future and Ren’s future felt naturally joined.

Ren is also the person who helps Haru step toward idolhood. Haru wonders if she can become an idol too. Ren answers with confidence. Ren’s faith precedes Haru’s self-assurance.

That makes Ren’s later wound sharper. She was not simply “left behind” by a random prodigy. She helped Haru walk toward the stage, then discovered that Haru could reach a place she herself could not.

There is a quiet cruelty in that structure:

> Ren helped open the door.
> Haru walked through it too brilliantly.
> Ren could not follow at the same speed.

# The original injury: Haru’s talent makes Ren fail a promise

The key to Ren is not jealousy by itself. It is failure.

When Haru’s talent reveals itself, Ren does not merely think, “Haru is better than me.” She realizes that the future she imagined — Haru and Ren shining together — may not be possible on equal terms.

Her apology is central: she is sorry she cannot keep up; sorry she cannot keep the promise. That means Ren experiences the talent gap as a moral failure on her own part. Haru may feel she hurt Ren, but Ren also feels she failed Haru.

This is what makes the relationship so tangled. Both girls internalize the same event as guilt.

Haru’s version:

> My full power hurt someone precious to me.
> Therefore I should not go all out.

Ren’s version:

> I could not receive Haru’s full power.
> Therefore I must become strong enough to do so.

The same wound produces opposite coping strategies.

Haru suppresses herself.

Ren reconstructs herself.

That is the core symmetry.

# Ren’s reconstruction: training as grief-work

Ren does not simply move on after Haru leaves HY:RAIN. She turns the absence into a training program.

She rebuilds voice, dance, expression, body, and professionalism. She becomes a top idol inside one of the most successful groups in the series. HY:RAIN becomes a public triumph, but psychologically it is also a monument to a private absence.

This is one of the more interesting things about Ren: success does not heal her. In fact, success makes her fixation more credible to herself.

If HY:RAIN had failed, Ren might have had to admit that the old promise was unrecoverable. But HY:RAIN succeeds spectacularly. They reach a level far beyond TINGS’ early scale. From Ren’s point of view, that success proves her argument:

> I did become strong.
> I did reach the level needed.
> I did build a group worthy of Haru.
> So now Haru should return.

That is why her demand is not random possessiveness. It is the conclusion of years of disciplined emotional logic.

The tragedy is that Ren trained herself to answer a Haru who no longer exists in the same way. Haru is not only the girl from the promise anymore. She is TINGS’ Haru.

# HY:RAIN as Ren’s successful scar

HY:RAIN is not just a rival group. For Ren, HY:RAIN is the proof that she survived Haru’s departure.

This makes HY:RAIN very different from TINGS. TINGS is a group formed through the correction of lies: Kyoka’s support-role lie, Rio’s Rio-sama lie, Yukine’s rival lie, Haru’s restraint lie. HY:RAIN, at least as seen through Ren, is a group formed around discipline, polish, and the desire to become worthy of the person who left.

That does not mean HY:RAIN is fake. The other members clearly have their own rhythm, affection, and group identity. They are not merely Ren’s props. But in the anime’s Haru/Ren drama, HY:RAIN functions as Ren’s answer to abandonment:

> You left because we could not receive you.
> Look at us now.
> Look at me now.
> Come back.

That is why her arrival after TINGS sells out Nakano Sunplaza is so narratively effective. TINGS has just become socially real. Then HY:RAIN appears as the far larger, more polished, more successful road Haru might have taken.

Ren’s challenge is not only emotional. It is also industry-scale pressure.

# “Get you back!!” as Ren’s emotional thesis

The song **Get you back!!** is basically Ren’s inner monologue in idol-pop form. The series synthesis notes that *SHINE POST* uses songs as dramatic arguments, and that **Get you back!!** functions narratively as Ren’s possessive love song toward Haru.[

That matters because the song collapses professional rivalry and personal longing. HY:RAIN performs as a top idol group, but inside the episode’s dramatic structure, the performance says:

> I cannot forgive.
> I want to take you back.
> Why did you leave?
> No one can love you more than I can.
> The future we chose together is still shining.

Even if one avoids reading the relationship romantically, the song’s emotional grammar is possessive. It is about reclamation, not merely competition.

And because it is an idol song, Ren’s private fixation becomes public spectacle. That is very *SHINE POST*: the private wound becomes a performance form. The difference is that Ren’s performance has not yet become truth in the healthy sense. It is still trying to pull the past into the present by force.

# Why Ren calls TINGS “backup dancers”

Ren’s cruelty toward TINGS is not ignorance. It is defensive precision.

She calls them Haru’s backup dancers. She critiques Rio, Momiji, Yukine, and Kyoka in specific ways. Rio can sing but struggles to combine singing and dancing. Momiji is dance-heavy. Yukine has expression but not enough. Kyoka does not make mistakes, but that is all.

The important thing is that Ren is not completely wrong at the level of surface evaluation. Her critique is technically grounded. That is what makes it hurt.

But she is emotionally blind to what those weaknesses have become.

Rio’s weakness became vulnerability that reaches people.

Momiji’s dance exists alongside loyalty and emotional perception.

Yukine’s expression is tied to her desire to create real smiles and to face Haru honestly.

Kyoka’s precision becomes a foundation for presence under pressure.

Ren evaluates TINGS as if idol value were simply a competitive capacity to keep up with Haru. Naoki and the anime itself evaluate them differently: each girl’s apparent limitation has become a particular form of truth. The full synthesis notes that *SHINE POST* is fundamentally about finding “what performance form will make that truth visible,” not merely about ranking technical strength.[

So Ren’s error is not that she lacks discernment. It is that her fixation on Haru narrows her discernment. She sees the members of TINGS mainly by asking:

> Can this girl make Haru shine?

She does not initially ask:

> What truth does this girl bring that Haru could not bring alone?

# Ren and Haru as opposite failures of trust

This is probably the sharpest way to read the pair.

Haru’s failure of trust:

> I cannot show my full power because others may break.

Ren’s failure of trust:

> Only I can receive your full power, so you should return to me.

Both are wrong in mirrored ways.

Haru underestimates others by protecting them from her brilliance.

Ren underestimates Haru by deciding where Haru’s brilliance belongs.

Haru’s love controls by withholding.

Ren’s love controls by reclaiming.

TINGS’ answer is different:

> Haru’s light does not have to be hidden.
> Haru’s light does not belong to Ren.
> Haru’s light can exist inside a group that answers it in plural form.

That pluralism is crucial. Ren imagines compatibility as something almost dyadic: Haru and Ren, or Haru and HY:RAIN as the group Ren has built to receive her. TINGS proves compatibility does not require one person to match Haru one-to-one. It requires a group where each member has a distinct claim to the stage.

# Ren’s love: real, but possessive

The hardest thing about Ren is that the anime does not present her love as fake.

She really loves Haru. She really treasured their promise. She really trained. She really became stronger. She really can understand parts of Haru that TINGS did not originally know. She really does have a legitimate wound.

That is why she is compelling. If Ren were simply delusional or selfish, Haru’s refusal would be easy. But Ren’s claim has emotional weight.

The problem is that Ren’s love has become organized around restoration.

She does not merely want Haru happy. She wants the world where Haru and Ren’s promise can resume. She wants Haru’s return to prove that the past was not lost, that her training was not merely self-salvation, that the wound could still be reversed.

That is where love becomes possessive. Ren’s feeling for Haru is genuine, but it has attached itself to an outcome:

> Haru must return.

Once love requires return to be validated, it becomes a claim.

# The yuri coding: high, but not simple romance

Ren/Haru is one of the strongest yuri-coded relationships in *SHINE POST*, but the texture matters.

At explicit canon level, the relationship is childhood friendship, idol partnership, shared dream, broken promise, and rival-group conflict. There is no direct romantic confession.

At emotional-coding level, it is intense enough that a yuri reading is not a stretch. It has many of the familiar markers:

-   childhood intimacy;

-   shared private dream;

-   abandonment wound;

-   “I came to get you” framing;

-   possessive jealousy toward Haru’s new group;

-   Ren rebuilding herself to stand beside Haru;

-   the claim that no one can give Haru more love than Ren/HY:RAIN can;

-   a song that functions like a public reclamation cry;

-   the pain of seeing the beloved belong elsewhere.


The series synthesis explicitly identifies Ren/Haru as strongly romantic-adjacent, while also noting that the relationship’s coding is possessive, wounded, and unresolved rather than soft or fluffy.[

That is the key distinction. This is not “cute girls who obviously like each other.” It is closer to tragic-yuri structure:

> I loved you before they did.
> I built myself for you.
> You left.
> Now you are shining somewhere I did not create.
> How can that be allowed?

As yuri subtext, Ren/Haru is much closer to **obsessive rival-partner attachment** than to ordinary romantic sweetness. That is part of why it works. It has bite.

# Why Haru does not reciprocate in the same register

Haru cares deeply about Ren. She feels guilt. She knows Ren was hurt. She knows HY:RAIN is strong. She does not dismiss the old promise.

But Haru’s emotional center has moved.

That is the point Ren has to accept.

Haru’s line, **“TINGS’ Nabatame Haru,”** is not a rejection of Ren as a person. It is a boundary against being absorbed back into Ren’s narrative.

Haru’s love for Ren is tied to gratitude, guilt, childhood memory, and the pain of having hurt someone precious.

Ren’s love for Haru is tied to restoration, proof, and the desire to reclaim a lost future.

Those are not symmetrical.

This asymmetry is why Ren feels possessive while Haru feels sorrowful. Ren wants to reopen the road they once shared. Haru wants to honor that road without walking back down it.

# Ren versus Yukine: two girls wounded by Haru’s talent

Ren and Yukine are useful mirrors.

Both are wounded by Haru’s hidden or overwhelming talent.

Yukine’s wound is ethical and group-structural:

> We were supposed to be building TINGS together, but Haru was holding back. That makes our shared labor false.

Ren’s wound is intimate and promissory:

> We were supposed to shine together, but Haru went somewhere I could not follow, then left before I could become strong enough.

Yukine responds by becoming a rival from inside the TINGS wound. She wants Haru to stop lying and stand honestly with the group.

Ren responds by becoming a top idol who can reclaim Haru. She wants to restore the future Haru abandoned.

That is why Yukine’s anger ultimately reintegrates into TINGS, while Ren’s anger must become external rivalry. Yukine belongs inside Haru’s present group. Ren belongs as the rival who preserves the old promise in transformed form.

# Ren’s defeat is not humiliation

One thing I like about the finale is that Ren is not “defeated” in a cheap way.

TINGS does not prove HY:RAIN is fake. HY:RAIN remains powerful. Ren remains talented. Her love for Haru is not mocked. Her training was not meaningless.

What changes is her claim.

Before watching TINGS, Ren’s emotional premise is:

> Haru’s true place is with me/us.

After watching TINGS, she can say:

> Haru is already TINGS’ Nabatame Haru.

That is not a total release of feeling. It is recognition.

Ren’s fixation does not vanish. It matures into rivalry. The emotional vector changes:

> come back to me
> becomes
> stand there, and I will face you.

That is a healthier form because it acknowledges Haru’s agency. Ren can still love Haru, challenge Haru, compete with Haru, and want to prove herself. But she can no longer demand that Haru’s future validate Ren’s past.

The synthesis captures this transformation well: Ren’s final development is not that she stops caring, but that her desire changes from “come back to me” into “stand there as TINGS, and I will face you as HY:RAIN.”[

# Ren’s limitation as a character

Ren is effective, but not fully explored.

The anime gives us enough to understand her fixation on Haru, but not enough to fully develop her life inside HY:RAIN. The other HY:RAIN members clearly know her, manage her excesses, and care about her in their own way, but the series does not give them the same depth as TINGS. That leaves Ren’s present-day group somewhat underwritten.

This is partly structural. The anime is twelve episodes and primarily about TINGS. Ren’s job is not to become a second protagonist; her job is to make Haru’s past walk onstage and challenge Haru’s present.

But one can feel the missing material. A longer version could explore:

> What does HY:RAIN mean to Ren apart from Haru?
> Do the other members resent being part of Ren’s unresolved Haru-project?
> How much of Ren’s top-idol discipline is healthy ambition versus sublimated abandonment?
> Does Ren know how to be loved by people who are not Haru?
> Can HY:RAIN become more than the group that proves Ren can receive Haru?

Those are unresolved, and they are interesting.

# Final thesis

Ren is the girl who made herself strong enough for a future that Haru no longer inhabits.

That is the tragedy.

She is not wrong to love Haru. She is not wrong to value the promise. She is not wrong that Haru’s full power requires someone strong enough to receive it. She is not wrong that HY:RAIN is extraordinary.

Her mistake is turning all of that into ownership.

Ren looks at Haru and sees the lost partner of a promised future. TINGS looks at Haru and sees a present member whose full light they are willing to face. Haru’s choice is not that Ren meant nothing. Haru’s choice is that the past cannot decide where her truth belongs now.

So Ren’s arc is not “possessive girl learns to stop caring.”

It is:

> **Ren learns that love cannot resurrect an old promise by taking someone back.
> It can only face the person she has become.**

That is why her final form as a rival is satisfying. Rivalry lets Ren preserve intensity without possession. It lets her keep loving Haru in the only way that can now respect Haru’s agency:

> not by pulling her back,
> but by standing across from her and demanding that both of them shine.
