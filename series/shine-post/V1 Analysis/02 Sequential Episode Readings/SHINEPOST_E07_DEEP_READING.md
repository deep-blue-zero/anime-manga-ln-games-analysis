> **第7話「伊藤紅葉は《戻らない》」**
> **“Ito Momiji Will Not Go Back.”**

The official synopsis frames the episode around Naoki’s next strategy: TiNgS can only become their “true” form if the lowercase **i** and **g** — **Ito Momiji** and **Gionji Yukine** — return to the group. # Episode 7 thesis

Episode 7 is a major structural pivot. The first six episodes corrected **Haru, Kyoka, and Rio as individuals**. Episode 7 says: that is not enough.

TiNgS’ problem is not only that its three current members had distorted self-images. The group itself is built around an absence. The name **TiNgS** has been telling us this all along: the **T**, **N**, and **S** are present as Kyoka, Haru, and Rio, while the **i** and **g** are lowercase, visually diminished, as if Momiji and Yukine are still there but reduced to traces.

Naoki makes this explicit:

> **そう 大文字で「TINGS」
> それが彼女たちの本当のグループ名**
> “Yes — in all caps, TINGS. That is their true group name.”

That is a very good piece of idol-story symbolism. The group’s typography is its trauma. TiNgS is not merely a trio; it is a five-member group living in a three-member form.

But the episode’s deeper turn is sharper:

> **TINGS cannot be restored simply by bringing Momiji and Yukine back. Haru herself is still hiding something.**

That is why an episode titled after Momiji ends by indicting Haru.

# “Going back” is the wrong desire

Haru’s instinct is simple and emotionally pure: TINGS began with five people, so they should return to being five people. She says she loved the time when all five trained together. She wants them to become a shine post together.

But the word **戻る / modoru** — “to return” — becomes the trap.

Haru keeps treating the old five-member TINGS as a lost happy state that can be restored through enough sincerity. Yukine rejects that premise. She does not merely say, “I don’t want to be friends.” She says:

> **私様はあのころに戻りたいと思ったことなど 一度たりともない**
> “I have never once wanted to go back to those days.”

That line is crucial. Haru is trying to recover a cherished past. Yukine remembers that same past as structurally unbearable. The episode does not yet show all the reasons, but by the end we know the key: Yukine cannot return to a TINGS where Haru has not shown her true self.

So the real problem is not:

> Can they go back to five members?

It is:

> Can they become five members again without repeating the lie that broke them?

# Momiji: “I will never leave anyone alone”

Momiji initially looks like the easier target. She is literal, soft, slightly odd, food-motivated, and emotionally much less guarded than Yukine. The comedy scheme with melted ice cream and the recorded “戻っていいよ” is funny because Momiji is genuinely almost persuadable.

But her refusal is not shallow. Her line is one of the episode’s emotional cores:

> **私は絶対に誰も一人にしない**
> “I will absolutely never leave anyone alone.”

That is Momiji’s character thesis.

She is not refusing because she hates TiNgS. She is not refusing because she lacks affection for Haru, Kyoka, or Rio. She is refusing because if she returns alone, Yukine becomes alone. Momiji’s loyalty is not ideological; it is relational. She stays with the person who would otherwise be left behind.

That gives the title its real meaning. **“Momiji will not go back”** does not mean “Momiji rejects TINGS.” It means:

> Momiji will not return to a group by abandoning the person she has chosen to stay beside.

This makes her much more interesting than a comic satellite to Yukine. She has an ethic. It is simple, but not weak.

# Yukine: care disguised as rivalry

Yukine’s surface position is rigid:

> **我々ゆきもじと君たちTiNgSはライバル**
> “We YukiMoji and you TiNgS are rivals.”

She repeats this so often it becomes almost comical. But the episode keeps showing that the line is a defense.

When Rio worries that YukiMoji left because Rio dragged them down, Yukine wipes sauce from Rio’s face and tells her that is not the reason. When Kyoka runs after her, Yukine notices her shoelace, stops her, ties it properly, and even teaches her the Ian knot. When Haru has no money for ice cream, Yukine pays.

These are not grand gestures. They are practical, bodily, immediate acts of care.

Yukine refuses to rejoin TiNgS while still caring for the girls with almost reflexive precision. That is the contradiction. She does not want to be their member, but she has not stopped being responsible for them.

This is why her claim to be a rival is only partly false. She probably does want to compete. She does want to grow. She does want YukiMoji to stand through its own power. But “rival” also gives her a socially acceptable language for remaining close without admitting attachment.

# Yukine’s anger is aimed at Haru, not TiNgS as a whole

The final confrontation clarifies everything. Haru says she wants to become a shine post with Kyoka, Rio, Yukine, and Momiji. Yukine finally breaks:

> **ホントに 君は出会ったときから何も変わらないな**
> “You really haven’t changed at all since we met.”

Then:

> **だからこそ許せないの**
> “That’s exactly why I can’t forgive you.”

This is not the complaint of someone who simply hates Haru. It is the complaint of someone who has been measuring herself against Haru for a long time.

Yukine’s next lines are the real wound:

> **まだ足りないの
> まだ私じゃ春の足元にも及ばないの**
> “I’m still not enough. I still don’t even reach Haru’s feet.”

That changes her entire character. Yukine is not just the strict former leader. She is a girl living under Haru’s overwhelming potential. She has been working, sharpening herself, leading Momiji, and calling herself a rival because she believes she is still nowhere near Haru.

So when Haru says “let’s go back,” Yukine hears something insulting:

> You can ask us to return because you still don’t understand how far above us you are.
> You can smile and say “let’s do our best together” because you are not showing your real strength.
> You want the warmth of five members without facing the inequality at the center of the group.

That is why Yukine says they cannot return while Haru has not shown her real self.

# Haru: sincerity is not the same as honesty

This episode is the first real destabilization of Haru.

Until now, Haru has been the show’s sincere center. Her impossible dream did not register as a lie to Naoki in Episode 1. Her ability to read and move the audience in Episode 2 seemed like the purest version of idol radiance. She helped Kyoka and Rio move forward. She has consistently been kind, warm, and outward-facing.

Episode 7 does not undo that. Haru is still sincere.

But it reveals that sincerity and honesty are not identical.

Haru sincerely wants the five of them together. Haru sincerely wants to become a shine post. Haru sincerely loves TiNgS. But she may also be hiding her true capacity, and perhaps hiding it so deeply that she has convinced herself it is not concealment.

Naoki’s line is devastating:

> **春って 一度も本気出してないでしょ**
> “Haru, you’ve never once gone all out, have you?”

Haru tries to deny it:

> **私はいつでも精一杯…**
> “I’m always doing my best…”

Naoki cuts in:

> **精一杯我慢してるよね**
> “You’re doing your best to hold back, right?”

That is a brilliant inversion. Haru is giving maximum effort — but toward restraint. Her effort is real. The direction of that effort is the problem.

# Naoki’s diagnosis: Haru is not merely good, she is abnormal

Naoki’s description of Haru’s ability is more extreme than anything he has said about Kyoka or Rio.

He cites:

-   her wide field of vision;

-   her instant understanding of what the audience wants;

-   her expressive output;

-   her industry-level potential;

-   her ability as something exceptional even across the entire idol industry.


This reframes Episode 2. Haru’s “instability” was not merely a clever adaptive center trait. It was the visible edge of a much larger gift. She has apparently been using a fraction of herself while the others struggled to reach her.

That creates a difficult moral problem. If Haru goes all out, does she inspire the group, or erase it? If she holds back, is she protecting the group, or lying to it? If she wants all five members to shine together, can she do that while refusing to reveal the scale of her own shine?

The show is now pushing past the safe idol-message version of teamwork. It is asking what happens when one member’s talent is so large that equality becomes impossible unless everyone agrees to pretend.

# Naoki’s role changes

In the first six episodes, Naoki worked mostly as a precise individual diagnostician. He found the hidden truth beneath each girl’s label:

Haru was not unstable; she was over-adjusting.
Kyoka was not merely a supporting role; she was hiding ambition.
Rio was not merely selfish; she wanted praise because she wanted to help.

Episode 7 moves him into a more dangerous role. He is no longer just helping girls reveal their strengths. He is exposing the structural lie beneath the group.

His initial strategy — bring back Momiji and Yukine — is managerial and almost mechanical. Full TINGS has five members; therefore bring back the missing members. But the persuasion attempts fail because the missing members are not just personnel. They are witnesses to the original wound.

By the end, Naoki aligns with Yukine:

> **僕も雪音と同じことを聞きたかったんだ 春に**
> “I wanted to ask Haru the same thing Yukine did.”

That is a major moment. Naoki does not simply oppose the difficult rival. He recognizes that the rival has seen the problem more clearly than the current members.

# Yuuki’s long game

Yuuki’s role also becomes more interesting. Naoki says Yuuki showed him everything: old footage, TINGS formation materials, all of it. The “stalker footage” joke aside, this tells us Yuuki preserved the history. She did not simply form a new trio and forget the old five-member group.

That matters because Yuuki’s pressure campaign now looks more deliberate. The Nakano Sunplaza challenge is not merely about ticket numbers. It is a forcing mechanism designed to make the incomplete group confront its missing form.

Yuuki knows TiNgS is not truly complete. Naoki is the instrument she brings in to expose why.

# The comedy is doing real work

The middle section is structurally comic: the “First Emergency Meeting for the Revival of Five-Person TINGS,” Rio’s blustering argument, Kyoka’s rational pitch and bad joke, Haru’s ice cream bribe, the recording trap, Momiji’s malapropism, and the “Momiji secured” gag.

But the comedy is not filler. Each failed persuasion reveals the speaker’s current mode.

Rio tries force and ego: “leave it to Rio-sama.”
Kyoka tries logic, mutual benefit, and awkward humor.
Haru tries warmth, nostalgia, and sheer sincerity.
Momiji responds to food and companionship.
Yukine responds to none of it because the issue is deeper than persuasion.

The comedy exhausts the shallow explanations. By the time Yukine finally speaks the truth, the episode has shown that no ordinary appeal will solve this.

# Updated character map

**Momiji** is the loyal companion who refuses restoration if restoration means abandoning Yukine. Her simplicity hides a very firm relational ethic.

**Yukine** is the ex-leader/rival whose harshness protects a wound: she cannot accept Haru’s invitation while Haru remains untrue to her own power.

**Haru** is now far more complicated. Her dream may be sincere, but her performance self is partially concealed. She may be less a simple sun-type protagonist and more a terrifyingly gifted girl who has chosen “togetherness” as a way to avoid confronting the loneliness of overwhelming talent.

**Naoki** is beginning to see that producing TINGS means not only revealing each girl’s light, but also confronting the lie that kept the original light arrangement from working.

**Yuuki** continues to read as a quiet architect. She found these girls, preserved their history, and created a crisis that would force the buried structure back into view.

# Series thesis after Episode 7

The thesis now needs revision.

Episode 1: dreams can be true even when they look impossible.
Episode 2: effort needs the right audience and staging.
Episode 3: a useful role can become a hiding place.
Episode 4: correctness can bury desire.
Episode 5: arrogance can hide shame.
Episode 6: wanting praise can be the wounded form of wanting to help.
Episode 7: togetherness itself can become a hiding place.

That last point is the new one.

Haru’s wish to restore five-person TINGS is beautiful. It is also incomplete. She wants everyone together, but Yukine is forcing a harsher question:

> Together as what?
> Equal friends?
> Rivals?
> People orbiting Haru’s hidden power?
> A group pretending the strongest member is not holding back?

The episode title says Momiji will not go back. But the real answer is larger:

> **None of them can go back. They can only become TINGS again if Haru stops pretending that “doing her best” and “holding back for everyone’s sake” are the same thing.**
