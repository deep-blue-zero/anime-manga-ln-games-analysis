## 3. Volume orientation

V07 separated power, legitimacy, credit, justice, and service. V08 attacks the same system from a more difficult direction: what happens when public recognition is not merely inaccurate but **attached to a person who knows he cannot perform the role being attributed to him**?

King is therefore not just another joke about mistaken rankings. He exposes three different realities that have become causally entangled:

1. **objective history** — King did not perform the victories assigned to him;
2. **public/institutional belief** — society treats King as its strongest hero;
3. **current social role** — monsters target him, civilians depend on his reputation, and the Association routes expectations through that reputation.

Saitama's reaction refuses the obvious binary. He neither declares the false history harmless nor treats restoration of his own stolen credit as the central moral task. His question is whether King enjoys living inside the lie; his practical answer is that if King cannot yet perform the role, he can become stronger. He then asks to come back and play games.

The result is a new early-series principle:

**False attribution can create a real present responsibility without making the false attribution true.**

V08 then widens that problem institutionally. Faced with rising disaster rates and a prophecy window that appears to be closing, the Hero Association recruits people from the criminal underworld because recognized heroes may be numerically insufficient. The institution separates fighting capacity from moral/legal qualification in the name of survival. Garou enters through exactly that opening and offers a self-authored antagonist identity rather than the service the Association wants.

The extras return the same question to mundane scale. Saitama can defeat monsters effortlessly and still be badly matched to finding a lost cat. Genos can perform high-speed combat analysis and then become intensely serious about cabbage, hotpot preparation, and dojo etiquette. Bang can be an S-class martial master and still be a lonely old man with too many lobsters and almost no disciples.

V08 therefore deepens the series' non-equivalence architecture:

**Capability does not determine role fit; reputation does not determine history; role expectation does not automatically determine motive; and institutional need does not guarantee that recruited power will accept institutional purpose.**

## 4. Central thesis

**V08 converts recognition from a reward problem into an obligation problem.**

Earlier volumes established that Saitama wants accurate recognition but can sacrifice it. King supplies the inverse condition: a person who receives immense recognition without the causal achievement. The accumulated myth gives King comfort and material benefit, but it also generates fear, shame, target selection, and expectations he cannot meet.

Saitama's response is diagnostic because the stolen credit includes achievements that may have belonged to him. Yet he says:

> `手柄とか そういう問題じゃないだろ`

He is not claiming that attribution is factually irrelevant. He is ranking problems. The immediate moral problem, for him, is not possession of prestige but King's relation to a socially operative hero role.

The same distinction appears in `迷い猫`. Saitama's hero status does not make every assigned task a good fit. He performs the task, protects the child, captures the cat, and participates in the larger monster incident, but concludes that there are different roles and that cat-searching may not suit him. Competence is plural and domain-conditioned.

Finally, Garou's entrance demonstrates that the institution cannot solve a capacity shortage simply by collecting powerful bodies. When the Association says it will ask skilled fighters for cooperation regardless of good or evil personality, it is explicitly treating combat power as separable from moral alignment. Garou proves the separation immediately.

## 5. Sequential close reading

### 5.1 38撃目 `キング` — reputation becomes a danger magnet

King's public presence is already saturated with interpretation. Ordinary people recognize him as the strongest hero; hostile actors treat him as a premier target. The body that King privately experiences as frightened is publicly read as evidence of overwhelming menace.

G4 approaches him specifically because of this reputation. King does not reveal hidden combat mastery. He manufactures a bathroom/stomach excuse and escapes. The scene is important because the deception is defensive rather than dominance-seeking: King lies to create distance from an opponent he believes will expose or kill him.

Public observers, however, possess a model strong enough to absorb anomalous behavior. King's retreat does not easily collapse the myth; surrounding expectations reinterpret what they see through the established reputation.

#### Saitama enters the private layer

Saitama reaches King's twenty-second-floor apartment through an open window. He already knows the name and S-class identity, but this produces essentially no ceremonial deference. Instead he sees the private King: games, clutter, embarrassment over a dating simulation, and real skill at action games.

This is the first genuinely ordinary peer activity Saitama shares with a non-Genos adult at meaningful length. The interaction matters before the confession because Saitama meets King as a person in a domain where King actually **is** exceptional. Saitama tells him he is extremely good at games; King reports tournament success.

The contrast is structural rather than merely comic:

`public combat mastery = false`  
`private game mastery = real`.

King is therefore not modeled as globally incompetent. He possesses genuine confidence and skill in a domain society does not reward with the identity imposed on him.

#### Saitama's social inference is active but self-conditioned

After gaming, Saitama abruptly asks why King fled from the robot Genos is now fighting. He does not accept S-class reputation as sufficient explanation of behavior. He tries to infer motive and explicitly proposes a hypothesis from his own experience: perhaps King became so strong that he lost interest in fighting.

This is valuable because the hypothesis is wrong.

Saitama's social cognition is neither absent nor omniscient. He can detect inconsistency, generate a causal model, and ask for correction, but his available model is shaped by his own abnormal experience. The correct reconstruction is:

`salient behavioral contradiction -> direct inquiry -> self-conditioned causal hypothesis -> request for explanation`.

This is stronger than the fandom shorthand “Saitama cannot read people” and safer than the opposite claim that he possesses unusually accurate social intuition.

#### G4 and Genos — mechanism while acting

Genos takes over the G4 confrontation. During the fight he does not merely escalate output. When vapor fills the battlefield, he identifies the interaction directly:

> `無駄だ 湯気は光を拡散する`

This is the clean held-out test V07 lacked. Genos is simultaneously engaged in combat and reasoning about the mechanism governing an opponent's attack environment. Checkpoint G-A-02 therefore receives `CONFIRM`.

The G4 sequence also continues a recurring Genos pattern: defeating an opponent is not the endpoint if the opponent contains useful capability information. V08 soon turns the defeated machine into a source of upgrade material.

### 5.2 39撃目 `あの人` — King confesses, Saitama refuses the prestige frame

King's retrospective account resolves the V06 reputation/self-report contradiction prospectively.

He says that he was repeatedly **present** at monster incidents in which someone else defeated the threat. Others inferred that he had done it. He did not need to win fights or even fight at all; the mistaken attribution generated money and reverence. Most importantly:

> `現場に居合わせただけで 俺が倒したと思ってる`
>
> `俺は喧嘩もした事がない`

The origin of the public King is therefore **accidental causal misattribution amplified into durable reputation**. This does not eliminate King's later responsibility for allowing the myth to persist, but it prevents the model from being flattened into “King invented a heroic career by claiming victories.”

His private self-assessment is severe:

> `何もかも間違ってやがる`
>
> `俺は嘘の塊のような男!`
>
> `世間が憧れるキングは俺ではない`

He even distinguishes the fictional public King from a hypothetical real person somewhere in the world who actually performed those feats. The false identity is therefore not merely useful to him; it has split self-conception from public persona.

#### Recognition of Saitama repairs one causal chain

King recognizes Saitama as a rescuer from an earlier incident, when Saitama still had hair and described himself as someone aiming to be a hero as a hobby. The flashback is important for both characters.

For King, it supplies a concrete answer to at least one piece of the attribution mystery: the anonymous person who saved him exists in front of him.

For Saitama, it provides retrospective continuity between pre-registration hero activity and his current professional status. The younger Saitama can be injured, call the victory a big success, and continue pursuing heroism before Association recognition exists.

#### Saitama learns King may have received his achievements

After King confesses, Saitama checks the proposition bluntly rather than responding with theatrical outrage. King eventually says that some achievements credited to him may actually have belonged to Saitama.

Saitama's answer is the most important recognition line in V08:

> `手柄とか そういう問題じゃないだろ`

This is a direct held-out confirmation of S-A-03. Recognition still matters to Saitama; the first seven volumes already exclude global indifference. But even when the misplaced recognition may be **his own**, he does not make restitution of prestige the controlling concern.

He instead tells King:

> `お前は皆のヒーローなんだぜ`
>
> `お前が何と言おうと皆は最強のヒーローだと思ってる`

This must not be misread as “the lie has become truth.” Saitama is distinguishing historical truth from current social position. People do in fact rely on the King identity whether or not the underlying combat record is real.

King asks whether he should maintain the lie or resign. Saitama does not pretend to solve that institutional/ethical dilemma. His bounded suggestion is:

> `だったら 強くなればいいんじゃね?`

The advice is radically competence-oriented: reduce the gap between role and ability. It is not a complete solution to false records, public deception, or institutional responsibility, and Saitama does not present it as one.

#### Friendship rather than punishment

King asks why Saitama is not angry despite having seriously pursued heroism while King benefited from false attribution. Saitama leaves with:

> `たまにゲームやりに来るから ヨロシク`

This is a major relationship-state transition. Saitama has just learned that King is frightened, falsely credited, and possibly the recipient of Saitama's own public achievements. His chosen next interaction is not interrogation, denunciation, blackmail, or moral rehabilitation. It is ordinary recurring social contact in a domain where King is authentically skilled.

V08 therefore creates Saitama's first strong peer-friendship channel outside the Genos household: **games as low-stakes reciprocity**.

### 5.3 40撃目 `アウトロー` — capacity shortage opens the institution to uncontrolled power

#### Genos salvages G4

Genos brings pieces of the destroyed G4 to Dr. Kuseno and describes the machine as possessing enormous power and advanced artificial intelligence. His request is practical:

- determine whether usable components remain;
- incorporate them if possible;
- become stronger.

This continues the capability-acquisition model but changes its material basis. Enemy technology becomes upgrade input.

Genos also names a concrete institutional target: S-class top ten. Professional ranking therefore has motivational reality for him even though his deepest orientation remains independent of rank for its own sake.

#### Hero work has displaced, not erased, revenge

Kuseno asks about the rampaging cyborg that destroyed Genos's hometown. Genos acknowledges that recent hero activity has occupied him and his search has not progressed, but states that he will find and completely destroy the cyborg no matter what.

Kuseno summarizes the critical tension: becoming a hero and acquiring a master have **not weathered away the hatred**.

This is an important correction to an overly developmental reading. Saitama has broadened Genos's cognition, civic duty, relationships, and ordinary life; V08 does not support the claim that revenge has been transcended. The accurate state is coexistence:

`expanded hero/social life + continuing absolute revenge commitment`.

Kuseno's response also deepens their relationship. He has pursued the same cyborg longer, knows its danger, worries that Genos's youth will produce ruin, and explicitly asks Genos not to fight alone if contact occurs. This is protective technical mentorship, not merely maintenance service.

#### The Association's emergency logic

The Hero Association calls a large group of criminals, wanted fighters, and other underworld figures to headquarters. Sitch explicitly brackets their prior crimes for the purpose of the meeting. The problem is capacity.

The Association's reasoning is clear:

- powerful monsters/disasters are appearing more frequently;
- repeated deployment will exhaust heroes;
- even S-class cannot cover every incident;
- Shibabawa's forecast places an unprecedented crisis within a half-year window;
- therefore the Association asks experienced fighters to cooperate in humanity's defense **regardless of whether their personalities are good or evil**.

The current registered force is stated as:

- C-class: 390
- B-class: 101
- A-class: 38
- S-class: 17

The important point is not the exact total alone. The Association now admits that its formal hero registry may not contain enough usable force. Institutional legitimacy and combat capacity are separated deliberately.

#### Garou enters through that separation

Garou appears within this recruitment experiment and refuses the Association's intended role. His opening language is not passive criminality. He reacts enthusiastically to the prospect of an unprecedented God-level disaster, identifies himself as someone who admired monsters, trained, and destroyed martial-arts dojos, and claims the assembled group cannot kill him even collectively.

The final page of the chapter supplies the direct formulation:

> `俺はガロウ`
>
> `怪人に憧れて修行し 数々の武術道場を潰してきた男だ`

At V08, Garou is a **human martial antagonist with monster aspiration**, not evidence for a later biological/ontological monster state. This distinction is essential.

His entrance also demonstrates a failure mode in emergency institutional reasoning:

`capacity shortage -> lower membership/moral gate -> invite uncontrolled power -> power may reject service frame and turn the gathering into a target set`.

The Association's reasoning is not irrational; the danger is that combat capacity is not equivalent to willingness to protect.

### 5.4 番外編1 `迷い猫` — mundane role mismatch is a real competence test

This extra is unusually valuable for character reconstruction because it strips away scale.

Saitama is asked to find a lost cat. He agrees initially because he assumes the Association wants help with the kind of problem he can obviously solve—monster extermination. Once he sees the crude drawing and realizes the task includes escorting the owner's child, his engagement becomes compressed and irritable rather than heroic-performance oriented.

He says being fired would not matter much because heroism was originally his hobby. Yet when the child begins to cry, he immediately backs off the dismissal and says he will properly search because he has time.

This is a direct held-out confirmation of S-A-01:

`physically trivial/routine problem -> low ceremony, complaints, compressed engagement`.

But the prediction would be wrong if interpreted as “therefore he will not care or act.” He does the task.

#### Rank boundary does not become deference

The cat enters a sealed monster area guarded by Tanktop Tiger and Tanktop Black Hole. The guards say only specially appointed heroes may enter and emphasize that they dislike lower-ranked heroes whose advancement seems insufficiently earned. Saitama recognizes that description as applying to him.

He does not perform status submission. When the practical objective requires entry, he simply creates a route through the barrier. This supplies another independent `CONFIRM` for S-A-06: rank/status determines institutional friction but does not itself generate deference.

The scene also broadens Tanktop Tiger and Tanktop Black Hole. They are not only rank-threat antagonists from V03–V04; they are presently performing a real quarantine/guard assignment. Their status sensitivity must coexist with genuine duty behavior.

#### Grizz-Meow and domain-specific hero work

Genos has separately been asked to eliminate an escaped creature secretly raised by an Association employee. He follows blood and incident evidence, encounters injured heroes, identifies the threat, and engages the Demon-level `グリズニャー`.

The extra deliberately lets Saitama's trivial task and Genos's official monster task intersect. The missing cat has given birth; the giant creature creates a real danger; lower-ranked heroes protect a child; Genos fights the larger threat; Saitama retrieves the cat while moving through the same space.

Afterward Saitama tells Genos:

> `俺プロヒーロー向いてないかもしれないな`

and develops the thought that people have different roles. The final gag—being scratched by the cat—ends with:

> `猫探しは向いてないね`

This supplies a second major held-out result: S-A-05 receives `CONFIRM`. Saitama does not pretend that overwhelming combat power makes him naturally competent at every service task. He completes the task, helps where he can, and explicitly acknowledges mismatch.

The result sharpens the V06 `鮭` rule. Universal physical superiority does not produce universal social/occupational competence.

### 5.5 番外編2 `海老` — food, loneliness, domestic machinery, and fragile dojo sociality

Bang's dojo is physically grand but socially depleted. He has almost no students left after the earlier Garou incident. A large shipment of spiny lobster from his brother becomes a mundane reason to ask Saitama and Genos for company/help eating it.

Saitama immediately notices the high-value food dimension. He outwardly frames attendance as helping Bang, but the page gives direct access to his excitement over expensive-looking lobster. This continues the mundane material/food salience that persists beside cosmic capability.

His later formulation is more socially revealing:

> `こういう時は助け合いだ`
>
> `孤独なジイさんとメシくらい付き合ってやろうぜ`

Saitama's practical care can therefore appear as shared food rather than explicit emotional consolation.

#### Genos's low-stakes intensity

Genos treats hotpot composition and dojo etiquette with disproportionate seriousness. The absence of napa cabbage produces a sharp rebuke; Charanko's disrespect toward guests produces near-combat escalation. At the same time, Genos explains that Kuseno deliberately restored taste and allows ingested organic matter to serve as biofuel.

This is valuable character evidence because it is structurally opposite the G4 battle. The analytical/motivational intensity does not disappear when stakes fall; it is redirected into concrete household rules, master dignity, preparation, and usefulness.

Saitama, not Genos, is the one who simply goes to buy the missing cabbage.

#### Charanko and Bang

Charanko's V07 ordinary-status model continues. He is sensitive to his standing as Bang's remaining student, resents the casual presence of Saitama/Genos in the dojo, and is simultaneously much weaker than the martial intruders who arrive.

Bang's relation to the dojo remains understated but meaningful. He is lonely enough to create social occasion through food, still bears the consequence of Garou's earlier destruction of the school, and treats the current intruders more as a problem to be dismissed than a crisis requiring self-dramatization.

#### Food crosses the seriousness boundary

When intruders step on the lobster box, Saitama internally treats them as having crossed a line. The manga is playing the scene for comedy, but the behavioral evidence is real: ordinary food/material interests can activate Saitama more sharply than generic martial posturing.

The extra closes by returning Garou to the `悪役` frame. The placement matters prospectively: V08 does not let Garou remain merely an Association-meeting disturbance. His self-authored adversarial position is carried into the volume's final character beat.

### 5.6 Omake — King attribution has become self-propagating

The prisoner-transport omake shows monsters discussing which heroes defeated them. One prisoner attributes his defeat to S-class King even though the volume has just established how false King's record can be.

The gag is analytically sharp: attribution error is no longer a one-time misunderstanding. King's reputation acts as an **explanatory attractor**. Events can be assimilated into the myth because the myth already exists.

This is the public-narrative mechanism behind King's trap: reputation generates expectations, expectations generate interpretations, and those interpretations reinforce reputation.

## 6. Character-state transitions

### Saitama — STRENGTHEN within `strong`
V08 adds several high-value domains:
- detects contradiction between King reputation and behavior;
- generates a wrong but intelligible self-conditioned hypothesis and asks for correction;
- learns King may possess Saitama's own credit and refuses to make prestige restitution the central moral issue;

## 8. Institutional and thematic transitions

### Recognition — from absent credit to false identity
King proves that misattribution can become durable enough to create a public person distinct from the private individual. The public narrative is causally false but socially operative.

### Heroism — social role can outlive factual origin
Saitama's response to King suggests that once people treat someone as a hero, the resulting expectation is a real social fact. This does not erase truth; it changes what future responsibility exists.

### Association — capability emergency
The institution explicitly reaches outside lawful/professional membership because it predicts hero exhaustion and insufficient coverage. This is a rational capacity response with a major governance risk.

### Garou — human monster aspiration
At V08, monster aspiration is ideological/social and martial, not bodily transformation. This is further evidence that `怪人`, antagonist, criminal, and transformed body must not be collapsed.

### Technology — defeated enemy as upgrade resource
G4 introduces a new technological pipeline: hostile machine -> recovered components -> Kuseno evaluation -> potential Genos incorporation. Origin remains open.

### Ordinary labor — hero work contains trivial service
The lost-cat task demonstrates that registered hero labor includes tasks whose difficulty is not scalar combat difficulty. Saitama can be best-in-world at violence and mismatched to ordinary retrieval/service work.

## 12. Final V08 ruling

V08 is canonical and materially state-changing.

Its deepest contribution is not the revelation that King is weak. It is the demonstration that **reputation can become socially real even when its causal history is false**, forcing a distinction between correcting the record and deciding what responsibility exists now. Saitama's response is unusually revealing: he does not defend prestige property, does not excuse factual falsehood, does not demand punitive confession, and does not pretend to possess a complete solution. He offers a competence path and ordinary friendship.

At the same time, `迷い猫` places Saitama on the other side of the role problem. His real power does not make every hero task a natural fit. He complains, helps, succeeds, and admits the mismatch. King has role without capability; Saitama has capability without universal role fit. The pairing is structurally deliberate.

The Association's underworld recruitment then scales the same problem upward: it has an urgent role to fill and seeks capability wherever it can find it. Garou demonstrates why capability alone cannot fill a moral/institutional role.
