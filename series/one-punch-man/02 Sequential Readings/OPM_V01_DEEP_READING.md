## 3. Publication-boundary thesis

Volume 1 establishes a protagonist whose central problem is **not inability to become strong, but the experiential consequences of having already achieved the thing that once gave his life direction**.

The decisive structural move is the placement of `蟹と就活` immediately after the opening one-punch gag. Current Saitama presents himself with the famously unceremonious `趣味でヒーローをやっている者だ` (`OPM|V01|chapter:1|image:0020`). The line removes career, institution, prestige, destiny, and external authorization from his self-definition. He is a hero because he elected to do heroism.

The flashback then shows that this self-authorship originated at a point of social failure. Job-hunting Saitama is battered, unemployed, and directionless. Facing Crablante revives a childhood desire he thought he had abandoned: `また思い出した 俺 小さい頃ヒーローになりたかったんだよ` (`image:0042`). He explicitly abandons job hunting and chooses the fight. The key emotional fact is not that he was secretly invincible; he is visibly frightened, injured, and physically outmatched. What returns is **wanting something enough to accept risk**.

Three years later, the outcome is paradoxical. He succeeded too completely. The dream was not false, but the completed project no longer generates the affective intensity that made it meaningful. Volume 1 therefore begins with an achievement crisis rather than a growth quest.

### Counterreading

It would be too strong at V01 to say Saitama has lost all purpose or all emotion. He displays irritation, pleasure, embarrassment, impatience, curiosity, and social responsiveness. The narrower claim is that **combat no longer reliably produces urgency, fear, exhilaration, or self-transcending effort**. The text repeatedly contrasts this combat-specific flattening with ordinary emotional reactivity.

# 4. Sequential close reading

## 4.1 `一撃` — identity before institution

The opening spends lavish visual resources on catastrophe before allowing Saitama to puncture it. City destruction, Vaccine Man's grotesque rendering, and the monster's planetary rhetoric imply a conventional escalation structure. Vaccine Man casts humanity as ecological corruption and himself as the Earth's corrective will. Saitama answers not with counter-ideology but with the minimally self-dramatizing statement that he is a hero as a hobby.

This is already a formal joke about scale: enormous environmental and ideological claims meet a protagonist whose speech refuses matching grandeur.

The one-punch kill is therefore doing two things simultaneously:

1. establishing an apparently absolute capability gap; and
2. demonstrating that capability no longer guarantees meaningful experience.

The chapter closes on Saitama's dissatisfaction: the victory is mechanically successful and emotionally defective. The series' central suspense is displaced away from *can he win?*.

### Visual/formal note

Saitama's simplified face is not merely comedic shorthand. Against Murata's densely modeled monster anatomy and destruction, simplification visually codes **under-engagement**. When the world insists on spectacle and Saitama does not experience the event as spectacular, the page lets his rendering violate the scene's expected register.

## 4.2 `蟹と就活` — the missing variable is risked desire

The origin chapter should govern interpretation of later boredom.

Pre-hero Saitama is socially weak but emotionally much more legible. He fears death, gets hurt, improvises, shouts, and commits himself. His rejection of job hunting is not a rational career plan. It is a reassertion of agency after a social route has become empty to him.

This makes the later bald Saitama's problem sharper. The young man fighting Crablante possesses very little power but a high density of desire. Present Saitama possesses absurd power but comparatively little access to that intensity.

The series thus refuses the normal shonen assumption that more power simply enlarges meaningful agency. In Saitama's case power can **decrease the number of situations capable of demanding his whole self**.

## 4.3 `災害存在` — power as absurd scale

Marugori is a useful mirror because his transformation literalizes the fantasy of becoming the strongest. Fukegao treats strength as an engineered solution to human limitation. The result is colossal power with catastrophic externalities and almost no corresponding wisdom.

Saitama defeats Marugori with trivial effort, but the encounter also turns power into a problem of scale and collateral damage. OPM's physical world is not abstract arena space: cities disappear, structures break, and the framing repeatedly reminds the reader that strength has social geography.

At this boundary the series has not yet built a mature institutional theory, but it already distinguishes **having power** from **knowing what power is for**.

## 4.4 `闇の地底人` — the dream supplies what reality withholds

The Subterranean dream is the clearest V01 evidence that Saitama does not simply dislike fighting. In the dream he encounters opponents who can hurt him, force adaptation, and generate exhilaration. His rendering becomes more intense; his affect rises with the perceived challenge.

The waking punchline matters because the real subterraneans immediately surrender once confronted with him. The disappointment is not that violence failed to happen; it is that reality once again cannot supply reciprocal resistance.

This supports a precise model:

> Saitama's combat boredom is partly a **reciprocity deficit**. Action becomes meaningful to him when the world can push back enough to require genuine participation.

That should not yet be generalized into a claim that he requires danger in every domain. The later mundane chapters already show other sources of salience.

## 4.5 `かゆさ爆発` — low stakes are behaviorally diagnostic

The mosquito sequence is crucial for character modeling because it separates physical danger from frustration. Saitama can destroy apocalyptic threats without effort, yet an ordinary mosquito provokes escalating irritation precisely because it refuses his control.

This is not a throwaway contradiction. It demonstrates that emotional activation depends on the **kind of friction**, not objective threat magnitude.

The chapter also introduces Genos as a completely different behavioral system. Genos is analytical, purpose-driven, technically precise, verbally explicit, and willing to accept extreme bodily risk. His seriousness makes Saitama's flattened register more visible.

## 4.6 `サイタマ` — Genos creates a persistent witness

Genos's fight with Mosquito Girl establishes both impressive competence and dangerous self-sacrificial commitment. He is prepared to destroy himself rather than permit the threat to continue. Saitama's intervention resolves the combat absurdly, but the structural consequence is relational: Genos has now witnessed a level of power he cannot assimilate into his existing model.

This matters because most early antagonists disappear after Saitama defeats them. Genos does the opposite: he **stays**.

A protagonist whose problem includes the emptiness of unreciprocated power gains a character whose defining response is sustained attention to him.

## 4.7 `謎の襲撃` — reluctant master, self-serious disciple

Genos addresses Saitama as `先生` and asks: `先生のように強くなる方法 教えてください` (`image:0146`). The honorific and request establish the relationship from Genos's side before Saitama has chosen it.

Their speech rhythms are already strongly differentiated. Genos delivers dense, information-heavy self-explanation. Saitama becomes impatient with discursive overload and demands compression. This is useful voice evidence because it is not a catchphrase distinction; it is a difference in **information-processing and conversational tolerance**.

Saitama's acceptance of the teacher role is therefore provisional and socially improvised. He does not present himself as a doctrinal master. Genos creates a social expectation that Saitama begins to inhabit without having sought it.

That is an important developmental avenue: even if combat cannot challenge Saitama, **relationships can assign him obligations he did not generate alone**.

## 4.8 `それコイツ` — the narrative becomes dyadic

By the House of Evolution attack, Saitama and Genos already function as a loose pair. Genos categorizes targets, deploys force, and seeks information. Saitama's actions are more intuitive and less procedural. Their difference is productive rather than redundant.

Armored Gorilla's surrender also introduces a recurring OPM mechanism: characters revise their behavior when their model of relative power is corrected. This is the beginning of the series' broader misrecognition architecture—what characters think strength is, who possesses it, and what follows from learning they were wrong.

## 4.9 `200円` — childhood does not reduce Saitama to post-training flatness

The bonus story gives a twelve-year-old Saitama who is socially vulnerable, not prestigious, and capable of being intimidated or humiliated. It prevents a retrospective reading in which his adult detachment is treated as timeless essence.

The useful V01 modeling conclusion is modest: adult Saitama's flat combat affect is a **developed state**, not evidence that he was always affectively blank. Earlier Saitama exists within ordinary peer hierarchy and ordinary vulnerability.

# 5. Saitama state at V01

## Desire

- explicit hero desire originated before the current timeline;
- heroism is self-authored rather than institutionally granted;
- present problem: the achieved condition no longer supplies sufficient resistance or emotional intensity;
- he still seeks situations capable of making him feel genuinely engaged.

## Self-conception

Saitama does not frame himself as a savior, elite, chosen figure, or public moral authority. `趣味でヒーローをやっている者だ` is deliberately deflationary. His self-conception is behavior-first: he does hero work because that is what he chose to do.

## Recognition

V01 does not yet establish recognition as his dominant conscious need. He is not introduced pursuing fame. What matters more immediately is **felt meaning**. Later recognition claims must therefore be checked against this early boundary rather than imported backward.

## Ethics

His intervention pattern is direct and largely non-bureaucratic. He acts against beings threatening others without requiring authorization. At the same time, V01 does not yet provide enough evidence for a complete moral philosophy.

## Ordinary life

The mosquito gag, apartment scenes, conversational impatience, clothing, and domestic proximity to Genos are first-class evidence. Saitama is not globally numb. Minor inconvenience can activate him more readily than spectacular physical threat.

## Combat

The stable V01 tendency is not "careless" in a generic sense. It is **low perceived need for effort because his observed capability gap is enormous**. The Subterranean dream shows that if resistance were credible, his engagement profile could change dramatically.

# 6. Genos state at V01

Genos enters as the first major character whose seriousness persists beyond a single encounter.

Observed tendencies:

- rapid threat assessment and technical response;
- high willingness to accept bodily destruction for mission success;
- formal, information-dense speech;
- strong causal curiosity about superior capability;
- immediate readiness to reclassify another person upward when evidence warrants it;
- deference to Saitama once convinced of the capability gap;
- active pursuit of apprenticeship rather than waiting for invitation.

A key caution: "rational" would be an incomplete label. His willingness to self-destruct indicates that instrumental calculation is subordinated to a very strong mission/vengeance orientation. Later volumes must determine how flexible that orientation is.

# 7. Relationship state: Saitama and Genos

## Genos -> Saitama

Transition in one volume:

`unknown civilian -> anomalous superior fighter -> object of investigation -> chosen master`

The movement is evidence-responsive. Genos does not defer because of social rank; he defers after directly observing capability he cannot explain.

## Saitama -> Genos

Transition:

`stranger -> persistent talkative cyborg -> tolerated follower -> reluctant/provisional disciple relationship`

Saitama's side is less ideologically committed. The asymmetry is therefore foundational: Genos attaches much more meaning to `先生` than Saitama initially attaches to being one.

This directional difference must remain explicit in the relationship ledger.

# 8. Japanese voice baseline

## Saitama

V01 establishes a **context-sensitive colloquial masculine system**, not a single deadpan register:

- default adult speech is plain, direct, and low-formality;
- minimal self-mythologizing and short answers persist under spectacular pressure;
- familiar `お前` can occur in casual praise rather than only hostility (`image:0130`);
- irritation produces rough contractions and evaluative forms such as `マジで来やがったか`, `使ってねーよ`, and `変わってんなお前` (`images:0140-0141`);
- stronger irritation escalates to `ハゲてんだようるせーな!!` and `何なんだテメーは!!` (`image:0142`);
- excessive explanation triggers imperative compression: `20文字以内で簡潔にまとめて出直してこい!` (`image:0143`);
- twelve-year-old Saitama uses ordinary teacher-directed polite forms (`完全に忘れていたので今からやります`, `わかりません`, `image:0185`), so adult roughness is contextual/developed rather than a timeless inability to use politeness;
- comic mismatch remains between linguistically/visually plain delivery and enormous physical outcome.

Representative anchors:

- `趣味でヒーローをやっている者だ` — `image:0020`
- `就活はやめだ / かかって来いコラ!` — `image:0042`
- `マジで来やがったか` — `image:0140`
- `ハゲてんだようるせーな!!` — `image:0142`

The diachronic contrast matters: present-day deadpan is a dominant adult mode under many conditions, not Saitama's entire linguistic capacity.

## Genos

V01 establishes a **partner-conditioned mixed register**:

- masculine self-reference with `俺`;
- plain/rough tactical and enemy-directed language (`所詮は虫か`, `image:0129`);
- polite/deferential request morphology toward Saitama after capability reclassification (`弟子にしていただきたい`, `image:0138`);
- `先生` as relationship-defining address, followed by an immediate attempted substitution `師匠` when Saitama asks him to stop using `先生` (`image:0140`);
- long-form, causally ordered explanatory speech that exceeds Saitama's conversational tolerance (`images:0142-0143`);
- seriousness with little ironic self-distancing;
- task-oriented questioning.

Representative anchor:

- `先生のように強くなる方法 教えてください` — `image:0146`

It is therefore more precise to model Genos as **masculine and register-flexible, but strongly deferential toward a recognized superior**, rather than globally formal.

# 9. Monsterhood, ideology, and disposable grandeur

V01 repeatedly gives antagonists expansive explanatory frames:

- Vaccine Man: planetary/ecological judgment;
- Fukegao/Marugori: technologically pursued supremacy;
- Subterraneans: conquest fantasy;
- Mosquito Girl: predatory collective biology;
- House of Evolution agents: engineered hierarchy and instrumental classification.

Saitama can erase the physical threat before the ideological speech has time to become a conventional battle-manga dialectic. This is not equivalent to the manga saying ideology is irrelevant. Instead, it creates a recurring question: **what kinds of meaning survive when power can terminate the conventional argument by force instantly?**

That question should remain open rather than answered from later arcs prematurely.

# 10. Visual-form ledger contributions

V01 establishes several formal grammars worth tracking longitudinally:

1. **rendering mismatch** — highly detailed monster/spectacle against simplified Saitama;
2. **scale inflation then puncture** — multiple pages build destructive scale before a compressed resolution;
3. **page-turn asymmetry** — conventional anticipation is often answered by anticlimax;
4. **expression as engagement index** — Saitama's face changes when a situation finally produces friction;
5. **dream/reality contrast** — the Subterranean sequence uses conventional battle intensity sincerely enough that waking anticlimax becomes psychologically informative, not only comic;
6. **body as argument** — Genos's mechanical destruction and Saitama's untouched body create different relations between effort, damage, and power.

# 11. V1 legacy claim audit — initial transitions

This V01 pass does not yet adjudicate the entire legacy corpus. It does allow several early transitions:

- **STRENGTHEN:** Saitama's problem is post-achievement meaning rather than simple laziness.
- **STRENGTHEN:** supporting characters inherit conventional struggle because Saitama cannot reliably supply it through combat.
- **STRENGTHEN:** visual register is constitutive of characterization, especially Saitama's under-rendering against spectacle.
- **REVISE:** "Saitama is bored" is too broad. His boredom is strongly domain- and friction-dependent; ordinary irritants and social demands still activate him.
- **OPEN:** recognition as a central need. V01 gives insufficient evidence to make it primary.
- **OPEN:** mature philosophy of heroism. V01 establishes self-authorship and intervention behavior, but not a complete doctrine.

# 13. Open questions after V01

1. Can Saitama regain meaningful affect through relationships rather than danger?
2. Does being treated as a teacher create obligations capable of changing his self-conception?
3. Will public/institutional recognition matter to him once heroism becomes socially organized?
4. Is Genos's self-sacrificial risk tolerance stable, trauma-driven, role-specific, or revisable?
5. How often will the series use misrecognition of power as comedy versus institutional critique?
6. Will monster ideological claims remain disposable speeches, or later become sustained challenges to human/hero categories?
7. How durable is the visual rule that Saitama's simplification indexes under-engagement?

## 14. Current conclusion

Volume 1 is much more than a premise delivery system. Its deepest structural claim is that **achieving power can solve the external problem while creating an internal one**. Saitama's origin shows that what once made heroism meaningful was not victory itself but choosing, risking, and straining toward something he wanted. His current invincibility has removed much of that reciprocal pressure.

Genos arrives as the first serious alternative source of structure: a person who watches Saitama, asks something of him, assigns him a role, and persists after the fight is over. That makes the master-disciple relationship—not the next monster—the most important forward-looking change introduced by the end of V01.

V01 passed the line-level Japanese dialogue/register audit and ledger-propagation readback on 2026-08-24. The artifact is therefore `canonical` at the V01 collected boundary. Later volumes may revise longitudinal claims through the normal revision vocabulary without changing what V01 established at its own boundary.
