## 2. Volume thesis

V22 repeatedly tests the relation between **the person inside a role and the shell through which that role is made legible**.

The relevant shells are different in kind:

- Phoenix Man's costume has become identity, body, doctrine, and recruitment model;
- Child Emperor's hero title and Brave Giant are roles/tools that risk becoming substitutes for recognition of the child using them;
- Pureblood treats inherited vampire lineage as proof of authentic monster essence;
- Zombieman's artificial abnormality and improvised tool use refuse that purity grammar by winning through persistence;
- Do-S converts people into externally controlled extensions of her will;
- Amai Mask treats heroic legitimacy as a purity obligation while hiding a body state that destabilizes his own public human image.

The volume's strongest answer is not that shells, institutions, equipment, categories, or appearances are meaningless. Child Emperor's science works; hero institutions coordinate real rescue; rank and reputation alter behavior; costumes and bodies have causal force. The stronger claim is:

> **A role, body, organization, technology, lineage, or public image becomes normatively dangerous when it is treated as self-validating—when the shell is allowed to define the person and override conduct, relationship, agency, or responsibility.**

V22 repeatedly returns evaluation to the actor's conduct under pressure: whom they protect, whether they coerce, whether they can revise a failed model, what they sacrifice, and what they do when recognition or identity is threatened.

## 3. `102撃目 少年ヒーロー` — Phoenix Man finds the recognition wound

### 3.1 Brave Giant is a tool and a hard commitment, not Child Emperor's self

Child Emperor begins from a technically explicit relation to Brave Giant. He calls it a robot weapon concentrating the essence of his scientific capability:

`BGは僕の科学力の粋を集めたロボット兵器だ`

He also rejects Phoenix Man's effort to collapse that machine into the same category as Phoenix Man's costume. This distinction becomes crucial later. Child Emperor is deeply invested in technology, but his identity is not yet fused to any one machine.

The machine had been retained as an extreme contingency, and Child Emperor remains conscious that Waganma's safe extraction is the governing mission. V21's mission-over-victory ethic therefore persists into V22.

### 3.2 Phoenix Man recruits through organization, recognition, and alienation

Phoenix Man does not merely offer greater combat power. He explicitly attempts to recruit Child Emperor:

`お前をスカウトしたいのだ`

He claims Child Emperor has the disposition to become a costume monster and says the child carries greater darkness/stress than the surrounding heroes. These are **Phoenix Man's diagnoses**, not narrator-certified psychological facts.

The more diagnostic move is organizational. Phoenix tells Child Emperor that what he really wants is:

`お前は心から信頼できる仲間と 自分を正しく導かせる組織を欲している`

He proposes that the two of them create an ideal organization surpassing the Monster Association, free of distrust, internal conflict, and corruption. He imagines costume monsters sharing a mental/spiritual connection strong enough to collapse the boundary between self and other. His invocation of a possible `災害レベル “神”` belongs to this aspirational ideology; it is not evidence that such a disaster classification has been achieved.

Phoenix's organization is therefore offered as a cure for institutional alienation: perfect unity in place of frustrating pluralism. The cost of that cure is increasingly explicit—the reduction of separateness and individual agency.

### 3.3 Child Emperor rejects the ideology but still hesitates

Child Emperor recognizes the pitch as psychological manipulation and never states an intention to defect. After resisting Phoenix Man, however, he makes the volume's most important self-observation:

`僕は一瞬 戸惑ったのか……？ 敵に寝返るつもりなんて さらさらない だけど`

He then remembers Phoenix Man saying:

`童帝 お前の力が必要なんだ`

and thinks:

`ヒーローになってから どんなに我慢して頑張っても あんな風に言って貰えた事 あったかな…`

This must not be flattened into “Child Emperor almost became a monster.” The text separates two things:

1. **ideological allegiance** — he says he had no intention of changing sides;
2. **recognition vulnerability** — explicit acknowledgment that somebody needs his power reaches a deprivation produced by sustained responsibility without equivalent affirmation.

The hesitation is therefore evidence of an institutional/relational wound, not evidence that Phoenix Man's worldview was accepted.

## 4. `103撃目 光` — coercive belonging versus recognition without absorption

### 4.1 Phoenix Man's unity doctrine

Inside the mental space, Phoenix Man projects the danger of organizational fragmentation. He claims the Hero Association and Monster Association both risk collapse because members possess different interests and philosophies:

`ヒーロー協会も怪人協会も いつでもああなる危険性を孕んでいる 利害や哲学がバラバラの集団だからな`

This is a coherent critique of plural organizations, but the proposed solution is totalized identity: costume monsters supposedly share a unity unavailable to ordinary heterogeneous groups.

Phoenix also shows Child Emperor alleged wrongdoing by Hero Association leadership and says:

`お前は何も知らんまま 悪事の片棒をかつがされてたんだ`

Within V22, this material is under Phoenix Man's control and lacks independent corroboration. It can destabilize Child Emperor; it cannot be promoted to narrator-certified institutional fact.

### 4.2 Monsterization as Phoenix Man's salvation narrative

Phoenix Man's own origin makes his recruitment philosophy intelligible. He says he had been a suit performer portraying `ドンカンバード`, was abruptly dismissed despite his devotion, could not accept taking the costume off, and eventually found it impossible to remove. Monsterization followed obsessive identity fusion after rejection and role loss.

He explicitly frames monsterization as liberation and later as cure:

`怪人化は救いだよ`

and:

`怪人化は救済 ヒトという病からの回復なんだ`

This is **Phoenix Man's ideology**. The volume does not make “humanity is a disease” a narrator proposition.

What matters psychologically is that Phoenix asks Child Emperor whether the hero role imposed on him ever reciprocated his devotion—whether it gave him power, freedom, or recognition. Phoenix says `俺は認めるぞ`: *I acknowledge you*. The recruitment offer is thus built from a real need and an authoritarian solution.

### 4.3 Saitama: autonomy without recruitment

Saitama's intrusion into the mental space supplies a radically different response. He rejects the equation of monsterization/evil with glory:

`怪人化して悪さすんのが栄光な訳ないだろ!`

His broader rule is characteristically simple:

`やりたい事があるんだったら 趣味として自分で勝手にやってろよ 悪事だったらぶっとばすけど 子供巻き込んで困らすな!`

The key distinction is between **self-directed eccentricity** and **coercive externalization**. Saitama does not demand Phoenix join the correct institution or embrace the correct prestige hierarchy. Do what you want yourself; if it becomes wrongdoing, he will stop it; do not drag a child into it.

Saitama also categorizes Child Emperor first as a child who should not be coerced. That does not deny Child Emperor's competence. Later recognition explicitly adds “hero” rather than replacing “child.”

### 4.4 Zombieman supplies the recognition Phoenix could only weaponize

A communication from Zombieman becomes the structural answer to image 0045. Child Emperor's earlier technical prediction about enemy equipment weak points helped Zombieman. Zombieman then says:

`大の大人が揃いも揃って お前に頼りっぱなしですまん`

and:

`皆に代わって礼を言うぜ ありがとう お前がいてくれてよかったよ`

This is unusually important relationship evidence. Zombieman does four things Phoenix's pitch only imitates:

- acknowledges that adults have relied too heavily on a child;
- apologizes rather than naturalizing that burden;
- identifies Child Emperor's concrete contribution;
- expresses gratitude for his presence rather than merely demanding his power.

The exchange then becomes reciprocal. Zombieman proposes sharing a milkshake after both return safely; Child Emperor warns him to take care. Recognition therefore occurs inside a relationship that preserves separateness, reciprocity, and freedom.

The scene strongly supports the interpretation that Phoenix Man found a **real** wound but prescribed the wrong cure.

### 4.5 `中身なんだ` — the inside, not anti-technology

Child Emperor's resolution is explicit:

`中身なんだ`

He tells Phoenix Man:

`着ぐるみみたいな借りものの力に振り回されて 怪人化したのがあんただ`

and:

`僕は僕のなりたいヒーローの背中を追うよ`

The claim is not “technology is fake.” Child Emperor remains a scientist, continues using small robots, and later warmly reunites with his robot dog. The contrast is between **using an external capability** and **allowing the external capability/role to become the definition of the self**.

The visual composition places Saitama's caped back beside Child Emperor's statement about following the hero he wants to become. This strongly associates Saitama with the renewed orientation, but V22 does not require the stronger claim that Child Emperor verbally names Saitama as his sole or exclusive model.

Child Emperor then deliberately spends Brave Giant's remaining energy:

`残存エネルギー 全解放`

`さよなら ブレイブジャイアント`

The machine is valuable enough to mourn and expend, but not sacred enough to preserve at the expense of self or mission.

### 4.6 Phoenix Man is defeated through his dependency on the shell

Phoenix Man mocks Child Emperor after Brave Giant is gone. Child Emperor instead identifies the opposite weakness:

`あんたはガワに依存し過ぎてるから…`

His small `コチョコチョ虫3号` attacks through the costume opening. The strategy works precisely because Phoenix Man refuses to remove the suit:

`ドンカンバードは俺の魂!! 全存在!!`

`金輪際 脱ぐもんか`

Identity fusion becomes a mechanical vulnerability. Phoenix Man collapses into:

`災害レベル:狼未満 怪人ヒヨコ男`

V22 therefore demonstrates a dramatic loss of threat level tied to the costume/identity mechanism. It does **not** establish a universal biological theory of de-monsterization.

### 4.7 Saitama's recognition joins child and hero

After the fight, Saitama says:

`すげーなお前 子供かと思ったらヒーローなんだな`

Child Emperor answers:

`ありがとう……`

The line is significant because Saitama does not require Child Emperor to stop being a child in order to be recognized as a hero. The volume has spent many pages showing how “child genius” can become an exploitative role. Saitama's recognition is comparatively non-institutional and unpossessive.

Child Emperor also recognizes Saitama as `ハゲマント`, then notes the irregularity of his presence in the operation. This reactivates a suspicion/information problem. The exact identity of any suspected spy or institutional traitor remains OPEN at the V22 boundary; V22 does not justify converting Child Emperor's uncertainty into fact.

The revived Hellfire Flame and Gale Wind are conscious enough to remember fighting Flash and express confusion before Saitama knocks them out again. Phoenix Man's revival effect therefore produces at least temporary conscious return; it should not be simplified into mere corpse puppetry.

Child Emperor later asks Saitama to accompany him and Waganma toward the surface. His robot dog `カマセイヌマン` rejoins him; Child Emperor apologizes for having treated/called it like disposable fodder. This is important negative evidence against an “anti-external-tool” reading of `中身`: a tool can also be a known, cared-for extension/partner without replacing the user's identity.

## 5. `104撃目 不死身の泥仕合` — purity claims meet attritional reality

### 5.1 Pureblood's essentialist hierarchy

Pureblood frames the encounter as one between innate superiority and compensatory tricks. He describes jealousy as the misunderstanding of the talentless:

`嫉妬だよ`

`才能のない者が よくする勘違いだ`

`小細工をすれば 私との差が埋まると思ってる`

Later he explicitly claims his vampire line is the Monster Association's sole authentic monster category:

`このヴァンパイア種は怪人協会の中で唯一の… 本物の怪人`

and identifies himself with inherited supernatural blood:

`先祖代々 異能の血を引く 吸血鬼だ`

This is a monster-aristocratic ontology: legitimacy through inherited essence. As with Garou's earlier `本物の怪人` qualification, the volume gives us another actor using “real monster” as a contested status claim rather than a neutral biological label.

Pureblood also tells Zombieman that his final stand and `不屈の正義` will not be rewarded. The fight then becomes a practical test of whether lineage superiority predicts operational victory.

### 5.2 Zombieman refuses the purity grammar

Zombieman does not answer with a superior essence claim. His combat model is deliberately inelegant:

- firearms and edged tools;
- repeated destruction of his body;
- tactical use of regeneration;
- waiting for opportunities;
- willingness to accept a “muddy fight” (`泥仕合`);
- continuing when the opponent expects accumulated victories to settle the matter.

His body damage is narrated with dry matter-of-fact humor. Broken bones and extreme injuries are both real and incorporated into the work process.

At the decisive point he tells Pureblood:

`おい吸血鬼お前… 泥仕合だが覚悟しろよ`

The chapter then jumps **30 minutes**. Pureblood has apparently killed or decisively incapacitated Zombieman again and again. Zombieman's summary is the cleanest statement of his model:

`ああ200回くらい お前が勝ってるよ`

`ようやく俺の1勝だな`

Local exchanges and final operational outcome are different metrics. Pureblood can dominate repeatedly and still lose the engagement because his kill model is insufficient for Zombieman's regeneration/endurance.

This does not make Zombieman literally inexhaustible. He later notes that ordinary wounds still take minutes to heal, says he wants to finish quickly, and acknowledges physiological inconvenience. “Immortal” remains an operational description with constraints, not permission to ignore damage.

### 5.3 Heroism as continuing after the ugly victory

After Pureblood falls, surrounding monsters expect Zombieman to be exhausted. He nevertheless closes himself in with the next group. Later he notes that he is not the only one working hard and, after recovering enough, goes on to another rampage.

The chapter gives heroism an anti-glamorous form: not purity, public image, or decisive spectacle, but continuing necessary work after a prolonged, ugly, physically costly win.

## 6. `105撃目 アマイマスク` — rescuing controlled humans versus optimizing the fight

### 6.1 Narinki's private squad returns as controlled persons

The Atomic Samurai disciples encounter the private rescue squad Narinki had sent in earlier. The squad is now being controlled by Do-S. Iaian explicitly recognizes that these are not ordinary monster enemies but human participants who entered for the same hostage-rescue objective and have been transformed into a tactical obstacle through external control.

That recognition changes the disciples' combat constraints. They attempt to fight without simply treating the controlled people as disposable enemy bodies.

Do-S's power is therefore another “shell/control” mechanism: people retain bodies and combat capability while agency has been externally captured.

### 6.2 Amai Mask correctly identifies the mechanism—and proposes a more dangerous solution

When Amai Mask arrives, he rapidly reads the battlefield. He understands that Do-S is controlling the squad and that pain/conditioning can compromise selfhood. He also correctly sees that the disciples' concern for the victims gives Do-S leverage.

He orders them away because their current relationship to the controlled humans makes the matchup unfavorable. This is tactically intelligible.

The problem is what follows. The disciples infer from his murderous intent that his “simple” solution is to eliminate the controlled people along with the monster rather than preserve them as rescue subjects.

This makes Amai Mask an important contrast with Child Emperor in the same tankobon. Child Emperor repeatedly constrains victory by the requirement to return a hostage alive. Amai Mask is tempted to remove ambiguity by purifying the battlefield of compromised persons.

## 7. `106撃目 見てはいけないもの` — purity logic turns inward

### 7.1 The disciples contest Amai's command doctrine

The disciples explicitly distinguish the current humans from monsters they had seen Amai kill before. These people infiltrated for the same rescue mission and are under external control. They therefore cannot understand why a hero would treat them as killable enemies.

Amai interprets the disagreement not merely as tactical dissent but as obstruction of his justice. The conflict exposes a deeper doctrine: command, moral classification, and heroic image are fused in his self-conception.

### 7.2 Amai's categorical purity rule

Amai's internal reasoning is unusually explicit:

`ヒーローでいる為には…`

`正義の味方でいる為には悪を討たねばならない—`

`悪に与した者も……`

He then follows the logic toward the disciples themselves: if helping people who “sided with evil” is itself wrong, perhaps the rescuers are also evil.

This is a classic contamination rule. Context, coercion, and recoverability are at risk of disappearing once a person is assigned to the “evil” side of the boundary.

The manga does not endorse this logic. The scene places it against the rescued squad's gratitude and the disciples' insistence that externally controlled humans remain rescue subjects.

### 7.3 A body that contradicts the public image

During the fight, Amai's face is struck and visibly exposes a cracked/scaled, non-ordinary structure. Do-S begins:

`お お前… 人間じゃ…`

before the exchange is cut short. Amai then attacks her while saying:

`怪人は死ね`

V22 provides direct visual evidence that Amai's body can display a strongly nonhuman-looking state and that Do-S recognizes the discrepancy. It does **not**, at this boundary, explain the mechanism or license a final ontology label. “Amai Mask is biologically a monster” would outrun the source unless and until later canon establishes it.

Immediately afterward his public-facing appearance is normal enough that the recovered rescue squad recognizes the celebrity hero, asks for photographs/autographs, and interprets the visible injury as a minor scrape. The visual contrast is deliberate: hidden body anomaly and restored social image occupy adjacent pages.

This makes the chapter title `見てはいけないもの`—“something that must not be seen”—analytically load-bearing. Amai's heroic purity doctrine is maintained alongside an intensely policed visible self whose underlying body threatens the category he enforces on others.

The safe V22 formulation is **contradiction**, not yet explanation.

### 7.4 Controlled humans survive the encounter

The Narinki squad is shown conscious again after Do-S's control is broken. Their gratitude confirms that the disciples' refusal to collapse “controlled by evil” into “evil person” had practical rescue value.

Amai's willingness to use lethal logic against compromised humans therefore cannot be defended inside V22 as necessary because no alternative existed. A nonfatal outcome is visibly achieved.

## 8. `番外編 コーヒー` — Zombieman's ordinary professional register

The two-page extra returns Zombieman to a coffee shop after the volume's extreme attrition. Nearby civilians recognize his pallor and public hero identity. A child wants coffee; an adult redirects the child toward a sweet milk drink, keeping the scene deliberately mundane.

Zombieman receives a work call about another threat. His logistical response is practical and bodily: he asks for replacement clothing and specifies ordinary details rather than mythologizing his durability. The joke is that his willingness to finish a `泥仕合` can repeatedly destroy clothing and dignity as well as flesh.

The extra therefore preserves a useful modeling baseline: Zombieman's extraordinary body does not produce an extraordinary conversational register. He remains terse, practical, professionally available, and dryly aware of the material consequences of his work.

## 9. Cross-volume character-state transitions at the V22 boundary

### 9.1 Child Emperor — major strengthening; promotion candidate

V22 supplies breadth that V21 deliberately did not yet have:

- high-stress psychological manipulation;
- explicit self-observation of a recognition wound;
- distrust/institutional uncertainty without automatic defection;
- relationship evidence with Zombieman;
- interaction with Saitama outside formal hierarchy;
- protection-first mission ethics;
- sacrifice of his largest machine;
- recovery after model failure;
- a precise philosophy of tool/self distinction;
- warm relation to a lesser robot companion;
- continued information-seeking after victory.

Cumulatively with V19-V21, this is sufficient evidence to consider `moderate -> strong` under the character-modeling schema, subject to readiness-index consistency audit after the prospective document is frozen. The promotion, if made, must be attributed to cumulative breadth rather than to one speech.

### 9.2 Zombieman — substantial strengthening

V22 adds sustained combat, pain/damage register, strategic patience, dry humor, threat persistence, professional duty, comparison to an essentialist monster opponent, and a mundane extra. It also gives him one of his best relationship scenes through Child Emperor.

His model should be strengthened substantially; tier promotion should depend on the existing readiness record rather than be forced from this volume alone.

### 9.3 Amai Mask — substantial strengthening with an ontology OPEN

V22 gives a high-value contradiction cluster:

- fast tactical reading;
- command confidence;
- concern with heroic exemplarity;
- willingness to classify controlled humans as killable through contamination logic;
- explicit `正義` language;
- anger when subordinates/other heroes interfere with that justice;
- hidden nonhuman-looking body state;
- immediate restoration of public celebrity image;
- violent policing of the hero/monster boundary while his own body destabilizes it.

The body mechanism remains OPEN.

### 9.4 Phoenix Man — bounded but coherent model

Phoenix Man now has enough evidence for a coherent model of:

- identity fusion with performed role;
- injury from rejection/status loss;
- monsterization interpreted as reciprocal recognition from the role;
- authoritarian ideal of perfect organizational unity;
- recruitment through alienation and recognition deprivation;
- strategic psychological reading of an opponent;
- refusal to remove the costume even when it becomes the fight's exploitable weakness.

His claims about Hero Association wrongdoing, Watchdog Man, costume-monster unity, and future `神` scale remain speaker claims unless independently established.

### 9.5 Pureblood — bounded essentialist antagonist model

Pureblood's model is coherent around lineage, innate superiority, contempt for compensatory technique, and confidence that inherited essence determines outcome. His loss to Zombieman exposes the operational limits of that model without proving his lineage claims false in every biological detail.

## 10. Relationship updates

### Child Emperor ↔ Zombieman

V22 upgrades this from operational coordination to meaningful reciprocal recognition. Zombieman notices the burden placed on the child, apologizes on behalf of adults, thanks him for concrete contributions, and proposes a future ordinary social interaction. Child Emperor visibly receives this and returns concern.

This is one of the best current examples of **recognition without absorption into hierarchy**.

### Child Emperor ↔ Saitama

Their first substantial direct interaction is unusually clean for character modeling. Saitama opposes coercive recruitment, recognizes Child Emperor as both child and hero, helps because he is visibly battered, and accepts Child Emperor's local navigation/mission guidance without ceremony. Child Emperor initially processes Saitama through rank/intelligence anomalies rather than instant trust.

### Child Emperor ↔ Phoenix Man

The relationship is adversarial but psychologically diagnostic. Phoenix Man sees and exploits something real; his inference is not therefore wholly correct, and his proposed solution is coercive. Child Emperor's rejection becomes stronger precisely because the temptation was not empty.

### Atomic Samurai disciples ↔ Amai Mask

V22 creates an overt ethical fault line. The disciples accept the rescue status of compromised humans; Amai's purity logic makes those same people candidates for elimination. Command authority does not erase the disagreement.

## 11. Institutional and thematic ledger deltas

### Hero Association / child responsibility

V22 strengthens the critique already latent in Child Emperor's role: adults rely heavily on a child genius. Crucially, the manga now includes an adult hero explicitly recognizing and apologizing for that burden. The institution is therefore not represented as one undifferentiated exploitative consciousness; different actors can perceive and respond to the problem differently.

### Organizational pluralism

Phoenix Man correctly identifies that heterogeneous organizations contain disagreement, competing interests, and trust problems. His remedy—erase separateness through totalized identity—is not validated. V22 therefore makes **coordination without annihilating difference** a positive problem rather than assuming perfect unity is desirable.

### Monsterhood

V22 supplies several incompatible monsterhood grammars:

- Phoenix Man: monsterization as salvation, identity fulfillment, and cure from humanity;
- Pureblood: authentic monsterhood as inherited supernatural lineage;
- Do-S: domination of agency without requiring bodily monsterization of victims;
- Amai Mask: a hero who enforces a strict monster/evil boundary while his own body visibly destabilizes the human side of it.

These should remain distinct until the manga itself connects them.

### Technology

Child Emperor's `中身` formulation does not downgrade technology. V22 instead differentiates:

- technology as instrument;
- technology as cherished creation/partner;
- technology as replaceable mission resource;
- external shell as identity substitute.

The first three can coexist healthily. The fourth is Phoenix Man's pathology in this volume.

## 12. Power-system deductions, with epistemic limits

1. **Phoenix Man resurrection/growth:** strong local evidence that his costume-linked monster mechanism can resurrect and alter threat state. Do not universalize.
2. **Phoenix Man downgrade:** the `コチョコチョ虫3号` strategy leads to `災害レベル:狼未満 怪人ヒヨコ男`. Mechanism is tied to costume dependence; do not call this universal de-monsterization.
3. **Revived ninja pair:** conscious revival occurs at least temporarily. Do not reduce Phoenix's ability to simple corpse puppetry.
4. **Zombieman regeneration:** extreme but not instantaneous in every case; he explicitly mentions minutes for ordinary wound recovery and experiences fatigue/material cost.
5. **Pureblood lineage claims:** speaker-attributed. Operational superiority is disproven by the final engagement outcome, not every underlying biological statement.
6. **Amai Mask body:** direct anomaly evidence, explanation OPEN.

## 13. Visual/form reading

### 13.1 Child versus hero is resolved compositionally, not by erasing either

Child Emperor's small body remains visually embedded in oversized machines and weapons. Saitama's recognition does not transform him into an adult-coded figure; it adds “hero” to the already visible child. The visual contradiction survives the emotional resolution.

### 13.2 Phoenix Man's shell consumes the frame

Phoenix's transformations repeatedly enlarge and elaborate the costume body. His final refusal to remove it makes the visual excess itself a trap: the more total the shell becomes, the more exploitable his dependency is.

### 13.3 Zombieman's fight degrades spectacle into persistence

The Pureblood fight moves from clean aristocratic confidence into accumulated gore, repeated black impact fields, a thirty-minute cut, and exhausted continuation. Form follows thesis: the “beautiful” superiority model is dragged into a `泥仕合` that Zombieman is structurally better equipped to survive.

### 13.4 Amai Mask: public face / hidden face

The most important visual transition of the volume may be Amai's damaged face exposing a nonhuman-looking structure, immediately followed by the restored celebrity face being greeted by admirers. The manga places ontology uncertainty beside public legibility rather than resolving it verbally.

## 14. Open questions after V22

1. What exactly is Amai Mask's body/ontology, and how does he restore his public appearance?
2. How far does Amai's contamination logic extend when “evil-aligned” people are coerced rather than willing?
3. Will the Atomic Samurai disciples report or otherwise confront Amai's conduct?
4. What precisely did Child Emperor infer about a possible internal spy/unauthorized operative, and will later evidence validate or reject it?
5. Are Phoenix Man's claims about Hero Association wrongdoing independently true?
6. Does Phoenix Man's costume-monster mental connection apply to anyone beyond himself as claimed?
7. Is his Watchdog Man classification meaningful or merely speculative recruitment rhetoric?
8. What is the stable mechanism/status of `怪人ヒヨコ男` after the V22 downgrade?
9. What are the actual upper limits and failure conditions of Zombieman's regeneration?
