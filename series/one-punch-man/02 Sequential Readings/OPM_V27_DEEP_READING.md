## 2. Volume orientation

V27 is the volume in which **strength becomes inseparable from dependency**.

V26 established that strong models can fail when systems change, distribute themselves, or adapt faster than the observer. V27 asks a harder question: **when power is relational rather than isolated, what distinguishes cooperation from dependence, dependence from exploitation, and protection from domination?**

The volume supplies several competing answers.

- Orochi and Psykos begin their fusion by trying to consume or subordinate one another.
- Psykos interprets external cosmic power as selection, mandate, and transcendence; Tatsumaki interprets the same structure as being seduced into another entity's service.
- Darkshine's enormous body turns out to have supported confidence partly because it made uncertain defeat psychologically unnecessary.
- Garou wants to embody “evil” but recoils when the relationship in front of him looks like straightforward bullying.
- Tatsumaki's “full power” becomes available only after she has spent substantial effort locating, shielding, and extracting people who would be endangered by it.
- Wounded heroes refuse to equate prior defeat with the end of useful action; Mumen Rider explicitly chooses civilian rescue because he knows he is not the best anti-monster combat asset.
- Genos and Tatsumaki cooperate despite abrasive speech because the threat requires complementary roles.
- Saitama asks Manako her name, introduces himself, and treats a monster-classified stranger as a concrete individual rather than a category.

The volume's grandest antagonist ideology imagines all life absorbed into one total organism. Its quietest relational counterexample is three mismatched people trapped underground arguing about names, routes, and what to eat when they get home.

## 3. Central volume thesis and strongest counter-reading

### Thesis — V27 distinguishes power that absorbs from power that coordinates

**V27 tests whether strength can coexist with other people without converting them into fuel, proof, property, or dependence.** The volume repeatedly contrasts **assimilation** with **bounded interdependence**.

Psykos/Orochi fusion is the clearest negative form. Orochi thanks Psykos for bringing sacrifices and announces that she is next. Psykos answers that Orochi is only a vessel for amplifying her power and declares herself master. The resulting organism gains scale by absorbing flesh, blood, energy, and eventually—if Psykos's stated plan succeeds—all living things. The system's ideal endpoint is not partnership but incorporation.

Hero-side power behaves differently. Tatsumaki's strongest work is not merely the later spectacle of twisting City Z or hurling mountains of rock. It includes the invisible labor required before spectacle is safe: tiny psychic signals that Psykos will not notice, spherical barriers, rescue accounting, mass extraction, King/Tareo status checks, and finally environmental mitigation after Psykos's attack generates a tsunami. Her maximum output is constrained by the continued existence of other people.

Darkshine provides the psychological version. He built real strength, but the confidence attached to it had become dependent on the expectation of a `気持ちの良い勝利`—a pleasant victory. The moment the encounter becomes reciprocal and genuinely uncertain, old fear returns. Garou, conversely, thrives on pressure, yet the text immediately prevents “growth through adversity” from becoming a clean heroic slogan: he notices that Darkshine's frightened face makes Garou look like a bully and finds that role inconsistent with the “evil” he thinks he ought to enact.

The volume therefore does not simply praise independence over dependence. Tatsumaki explicitly voices a severe self-reliance doctrine, but her own behavior is highly relational. The more precise distinction is:

> **Power becomes ethically and psychologically unstable when another person exists only as fuel, proof, shield, property, or audience. Power becomes socially durable when roles remain distinct enough for protection, coordination, refusal, and mutual revision.**

### Strongest counter-reading

A narrower reading would treat V27 primarily as the Psykos-Orochi/Tatsumaki escalation volume: the base is lifted, the fight reaches continental/planetary visual scale, God enters the power system more explicitly, and Genos adds his strongest current weapon. That is descriptively true.

The limitation is that the volume spends unusual space explaining **why** Tatsumaki did not simply use maximum force earlier, **why** Darkshine's confidence fails even before catastrophic bodily injury, **why** Mumen Rider remains useful despite combat inferiority, **why** Garou dislikes appearing to bully, **why** Psykos calls external empowerment “selection,” and **why** Tatsumaki calls it servitude. Those scenes turn escalation into an argument about the structure surrounding power, not merely its magnitude.

## 4. Sequential close reading

### 4.1 `132撃目 侵蝕`: fusion begins as a civil war inside one body

V26 ended with a fusion initiated by Psykos. V27 immediately prevents “fusion” from meaning peaceful or settled unity.

At `OPM|V27|132|image:0015-0017`, regenerated Orochi flesh grips Psykos. Psykos realizes that her mind is being invaded (`精神が侵蝕される…`). Orochi then speaks as an actor with memory, gratitude, appetite, and intent:

- `これまで私の成長を促すためにせっせと生贄を運んでくれたな`
- `感謝するぞサイコス`
- `次はお前だ`

The conservative meaning is decisive: Orochi remembers Psykos feeding his growth and now treats Psykos herself as the next sacrifice.

Psykos answers with an incompatible sovereignty claim:

- `お前は私の力を増幅するための器に過ぎないんだ…!!!`
- `主人は私だ!!!`

This is not yet “Psykos and Orochi share a single will.” It is **mutual instrumentalization**. Each actor understands the other as a means to its own expansion.

By `0019`, Psykos claims to have won the control contest and says `オロチの肉体は私のものだ`. That supports a current self-report of domination. It does not retroactively erase Orochi's agency, nor does it prove that no residual contest can return. V27's first correction to a simplistic fusion model is therefore:

> **The fused organism is born through contested sovereignty.**

The chapter title `侵蝕` works at several scales:

1. Orochi invades Psykos mentally/biologically.
2. Psykos attempts to occupy and govern Orochi's body.
3. the roots/flesh invade the underground environment;
4. Psykos attacks Tatsumaki by reversing the functional inside/outside of Tatsumaki's own barrier;
5. Saitama's local contact damages the distributed organism in the opposite direction, sending a small event back through the network.

#### Barrier inversion — mechanism match over raw output

At `0025-0026`, Psykos turns Tatsumaki's defensive geometry against her. She says the barrier's front/back orientation has been reversed (`バリアの表裏を反転させて貰った`) and explains that Tatsumaki is trapped in a “bug cage” whose internal pressure can be raised as long as the barrier stays active.

This is an unusually clean V27 example of the V26 power-function revision. Psykos wins a local exchange not because the manga has already proven a globally larger psychic number, but because **mechanism knowledge changes what the same barrier does**.

Tatsumaki coughs blood. Psykos's response—`お前の血肉も無駄にはしない`—extends the fusion's logic: enemy body is not merely something to defeat; it is future feedstock.

#### Saitama touches the distributed system from the wrong scale

At `0027-0031`, Saitama, Flash, and Manako encounter the living roots underground. A strand touches Saitama's bald head. His response is not cosmic revelation but irritation:

- `アタマに触んな!!`

He casually destroys the nearby flesh. The central fused organism then registers that a large number of cells died at once and, more comically, the sensory trace as a strangely smooth sensation (`今の……ツルッツルの感触は—`).

This is a high-value formal/character beat. A distributed body makes the microscopic and macroscopic causally continuous. Saitama's mundane local annoyance becomes a system-wide injury signal without Saitama ever adopting the narrative scale of the enemy.

#### King/Tareo communication releases Tatsumaki's constraint

At `0031-0032`, King reports that he has protected an ordinary child and is heading for the surface. Tatsumaki's reaction is immediate. Once the child-accounting problem is resolved, she says in substance that she no longer has to worry/hold back.

This makes V26's constraint reading explicit. The coming “full power” is not simply a new form the plot delayed. **Tatsumaki was operating under hostage/rescue uncertainty.** V27 converts new information into permission for a larger action envelope.

### 4.2 `133撃目 ひっくり返す!`: Darkshine did not want uncertainty

V27 gives Darkshine one of the most psychologically explicit chapters in the corpus so far.

Garou remains standing after an attack Darkshine expected to settle the encounter. At `0040`, Garou says `俺はまだ生きてるぞ`. Darkshine's visible reaction is fear, and the chapter immediately supplies the developmental history that makes that fear legible.

#### The strength project was real

At `0041-0044`, Darkshine is described as a physically weak child with poor exercise performance and little confidence. At fifteen he receives 3 kg dumbbells and begins lifting every day. The weights climb—50 kg one-handed, 200 kg, 500 kg, one ton, two tons. The text treats this as enormous persistence, not fraud. His body becomes a genuine achievement, produces repeated overwhelming victories, and eventually carries him into S-Class.

This matters because the chapter does not say “muscles are fake confidence.” Training transformed him materially and psychologically.

#### But the desired opponent was still supposed to be safe

The decisive distinction arrives at `0045-0049`.

The narration asks whether Darkshine was truly seeking `戦い`—battle/struggle—and answers no. What he wanted was:

- `気持ちの良い勝利`

A pleasant/good-feeling victory.

The chapter then states that he had no intention of throwing himself into a struggle where the outcome could not be grasped in advance (`勝敗の掴めない闘争に身を投じるつもりはなく`). His ideal “rival” was someone strong enough to receive his 100 percent and thereby validate him, not someone who might return an uncertain result.

The triggering cognition is equally explicit: `この戦いは俺も無事では済まないかもしれない`—he may not come through the fight unharmed. The long-forgotten `恐怖` grows.

Darkshine's vulnerability is therefore not adequately described as simple cowardice or low pain tolerance. It is a **confidence architecture built around removal of reciprocal uncertainty**. His training solved the childhood problem so thoroughly that he had little practice metabolizing a world in which the body might not guarantee the answer.

#### Garou rejects the bully position

V27 then complicates Garou at the moment he has the psychological upper hand.

At `0057`, seeing Darkshine's frightened face, Garou thinks:

- `チッ 情けねえツラしやがって`
- `まるで俺がいじめてるみたいじゃねーか`
- `俺の為すべき悪は…もっとこう…`

Garou is irritated because the encounter now looks like bullying. His self-authored “evil” apparently requires something other than frightening and dominating a psychologically weaker person.

This is not proof that Garou is secretly a conventional hero. It is stronger evidence for a narrower claim: **his monster/evil project contains normative exclusions he has not yet reconciled with the label he uses for himself.** The kind of evil he wants to embody is selective and theatrical/structural rather than indiscriminate cruelty.

#### Darkshine remains prosocial while afraid

At `0058-0061`, Tatsumaki's barrier begins extracting Darkshine. He recognizes the environmental danger and warns Garou:

- `おいガロウ ここはもうダメだ`
- `そこに居たら圧しつぶされるぞ`
- `逃げろ!!`

The scene blocks another overcorrection. Fear breaches Darkshine's invulnerability self-model, but it does not erase his capacity for concern toward an opponent. The same person can be terrified and still warn Garou to survive.

**Prospective V27 disposition:** Darkshine's psychological breach has become source-explicit. A later claim that his confidence problem is only fan inference would now be untenable. But the chapter still does not prove the final durability, social consequences, or recovery trajectory of the breach.

### 4.3 `134撃目 タツマキ全開`: maximum power is a rescue-state transition

The title promises maximum Tatsumaki. The chapter's first move is to define what that means relationally.

#### Fubuki knows she is a protected exception

At `0064`, Fubuki tells Bang/Bomb to stay close and explains:

- `私の傍にいれば大丈夫なはずだから…`
- `お姉ちゃんが力を使う時は必ず私に危害が及ばないようになってるの`

Whenever Tatsumaki uses her power, Fubuki says, Tatsumaki makes sure Fubuki will not be harmed.

The statement is highly diagnostic because it converts the sisters' relationship into an operational rule. Tatsumaki's protection of Fubuki is not merely a sentiment expressed after danger. Fubuki expects it to shape the geometry of large-scale psychic force.

#### Protection expands beyond Fubuki

Pages `0066-0076` visually multiply spherical rescue barriers around heroes, mercenaries, civilians/hostages, and other trapped actors. The chapter repeatedly checks who is inside a barrier, who can move, and who still needs extraction.

At `0067`, a hero explicitly calls attention to people who still need rescue. The sequence therefore makes the ethical constraint visible before the base-scale spectacle begins.

Several details matter:

- extraction is distributed rather than one giant undifferentiated shield;
- actors inside barriers continue to communicate and help account for others;
- Pig God and other heroes remain useful because they physically contain/carry people who then become extractable units;
- the protection network converts individual survival states into information Tatsumaki can use.

#### City Z becomes a spatial proof of psychic force

Once the extraction state is sufficiently advanced, Tatsumaki lifts/twists the Monster Association base and surrounding urban mass (`0077-0083`). The manga gives the event several pages of city-scale perspective, including civilians watching an urban region deform and rise.

The point is not merely that Tatsumaki is powerful. The sequence is the payoff to the prior information problem. **The same force that would be intolerably indiscriminate under unresolved hostage conditions becomes tactically usable after rescue geometry is established.**

Psykos is surprised that Tatsumaki had kept this level of output in reserve. The correct reading is not that Tatsumaki was pretending to be weaker. She was solving a different problem first.

#### King/Tareo and Genos enter the surface network

King and Tareo are physically carried toward safety amid the restructuring. Their rescue remains meaningful even though King is not the source of the psychic barriers. The role is social and positional: Tareo follows King as the trusted rescuer while Tatsumaki's power changes the environment around them.

Genos reaches the surface (`0087-0089`), observes the biological structure, and identifies a special biological response near the top of the protrusion. This is a clean transition from rescue/route chaos to mechanism-oriented battlefield entry. He does not merely see “a big monster”; he tries to locate the relevant biological node.

The chapter ends with the fused organism emerging into a more coherent aerial/dragon-like surface form (`0091-0094`) while Tatsumaki faces it above a radically altered City Z. “Full power” has produced a new battlefield, not a resolved fight.

### 4.4 `135撃目 負けない!`: not losing is not the same as never being defeated

The chapter leaves the central battle and moves through wounded people who decide that prior defeat or injury does not exhaust what heroism can still demand.

#### Metal Bat — defeat is not final identity

At `0096-0098`, news reports the abnormal City Z event and says S-Class heroes are handling it. Metal Bat is hospitalized and still injured. Zenko worries. Metal Bat tells her:

- `心配すんな`
- `ヒーローは負けねえよ`

He then leaves with his bat.

The line is easy to misread as factual denial—heroes literally never lose. The chapter itself refutes that reading because almost everyone it follows has already been injured, overwhelmed, or hospitalized. `負けない` functions here as **refusal to treat a setback as the final definition of agency**.

#### Mumen Rider chooses the domain where he can help

The Mumen Rider/Tanktop Master sequence (`0101-0104`) makes the point more analytically explicit.

Mumen says he may not be useful as anti-monster combat power, but civilians around City Z may still be waiting for help; he asks to assist there. This is not self-erasure. It is accurate domain recognition followed by action.

Tanktop Master answers by refusing to treat Mumen as negligible. He tells him, in Tanktop Master's comic idiom, that he too is a proper hero/tanktop wearer. The humor is ridiculous; the mutual validation is sincere.

Their paired model is:

> **Comparative weakness can change role assignment without canceling heroic standing.**

That is a more mature institutional/power claim than “everyone should attack the strongest monster.”

#### Other wounded heroes re-enter the response

Pages `0105-0109` show additional injured heroes/Fubuki-group members reacting to the crisis and leaving or preparing to help. The chapter treats recovery wards as staging grounds rather than narrative retirement. The comic energy comes partly from bandages, hospital restrictions, and people refusing to remain passive.

The correct thematic claim is not that recklessness is always admirable. It is that the series distinguishes **being beaten** from **accepting defeat as a complete identity**.

#### Suiryu's hero aspiration survives the tournament crisis

At `0110-0111`, another martial artist argues that people like them have fighting ability comparable to nearby heroes. Suiryu's response moves away from combat power as the definition. He says the true value of a hero is strength of heart: the capacity to leap into danger to help someone even in an extreme situation.

He ends:

- `俺 ヒーローになりたいんだ`

This is a major V27 continuity point. Suiryu's post-rescue hero aspiration is not a transient V14/V21 emotional aftereffect. At the V27 boundary he states it again while injured and while watching other heroes move toward danger.

#### Saitama and Manako become named acquaintances

The chapter then returns underground. Saitama, Flash, and Manako are still dealing with debris and route failure rather than the spectacular surface fight.

At `0117`, Saitama asks Manako's name, learns/remarks that she is female, then gives his own introduction:

- `俺はサイタマだ`
- `よろしくな マナコ`

This is small but unusually clean personhood evidence. Saitama has already cooperated with Manako behaviorally. V27 now makes the relation **name-bearing and reciprocal at the level of acquaintance**. Monster category has not disappeared from the world; it simply does not override the immediate individual relation for Saitama.

### 4.5 `136撃目 大いなる何か`: divine selection or predatory recruitment?

This chapter produces the largest power spectacle in the volume and immediately makes its causal origin a philosophical dispute.

#### Tatsumaki identifies the root-energy system

At `0121-0123`, Tatsumaki observes that the fused organism has grown again and identifies energy being drawn upward from the roots. She prepares to sever/weaken that feed. Psykos responds that Tatsumaki still does not understand and claims that she has already surpassed Tatsumaki even in power.

The resulting beam (`0124-0131`) is depicted at a scale that slices/lifts a vast curved section of the Earth's surface/ocean and produces a catastrophic returning water mass. The manga deliberately uses outer-space curvature panels to make the scale visually legible.

V27 therefore establishes a new demonstrated attack scale for the fusion. It still does not authorize a simple permanent “Psykos > Tatsumaki” hierarchy, because the remainder of the fight is dominated by mechanism, information, rescue constraints, deception, and external empowerment.

#### Tatsumaki recognizes that fusion alone does not explain the output

At `0133`, Tatsumaki explicitly reasons that this is not the level of power one should obtain merely because several monsters fused. Her question is causal: **where did this power come from?**

Genos independently registers the anomaly from below, with the energy scale exceeding what he can straightforwardly measure/read.

The chapter is therefore not content to let “fusion” function as a magical explanation word. The new output itself becomes evidence for another layer.

#### Psykos's `神の如き何か` encounter

Pages `0134-0138` give Psykos's account.

At the moment of fusion, she says, something appeared. The visual sequence compares what she saw to brain cells, the structure of the universe, and the surface of Jupiter. The text labels it:

- `神の如き何か`

Something like God.

Psykos says it looked into her **within stopped time** and that she simultaneously felt awe/fear and mysterious power flowing into her. At that instant:

- `私は天命を悟った`

She understood/realized a heavenly mandate.

Evidence classification must remain disciplined:

- **TF:** Psykos says she experienced a God-like something, stopped time, power inflow, and mandate.
- **VF/SF:** the manga supplies an enormous nonhuman cosmic visual rather than leaving the experience purely off-panel.
- **IR:** an external agent/entity is now a strong explanation for the fusion's otherwise anomalous increase.
- **OPEN:** the entity's identity, motives, exact mechanism, relation to prior `神` evidence, reliability of Psykos's interpretation, and cost/conditions of the grant.

The text supports “external empowerment is real enough to change the causal model.” It does not yet support “Psykos correctly understands the benefactor's purpose.”

#### Psykos turns power into an assimilation teleology

At `0139-0141`, Psykos describes the roots absorbing living things as fuel. She imagines the fused psychic/organic system growing until the distinction between organism and planet collapses. The Adam/Eve-like visual and her diction push the claim into teleology:

- `これははじめから定められた大きな流れ`
- `知性体の終着点だ`
- `私はその最終走者に選ばれた`

For Psykos, total assimilation is the ordained endpoint of intelligent life, and she is the selected final runner.

This is speaker ideology, but it matters because it converts monster expansion into a theory of history. Psykos does not merely want to win. She understands incorporation of all life as **progress**.

Tatsumaki's reply is devastatingly simple. She reduces the supposed sublime endpoint to an experiential one: use enormous power, eat everyone, end up alone in the universe—`バッカみたい`.

The objection is not scientific. It is value-based. **A total organism that abolishes all other life also abolishes relationship, difference, rescue, rivalry, recognition, and society.** Tatsumaki rejects the endpoint as stupid rather than transcendent.

#### Tatsumaki reads divine selection as exploitation

At `0141-0143`, Psykos contrasts herself with Tatsumaki and refers to Fubuki. She says Tatsumaki/Fubuki failed to seriously consider the future possibilities of psychic power, spent themselves on hero activity, and ignored her warning. She asks why she—not Tatsumaki, the overwhelmingly stronger esper—was chosen.

Tatsumaki refuses the premise:

- `バカバカしい`
- espers attract people who want to use them;
- because Psykos lacks the right mental posture, `妙なのに口説かれて手先にされるのよ`—she gets sweet-talked by something strange and turned into its pawn.

This is one of the most important Tatsumaki ideology lines to date. Her harsh self-reliance is not presented only as temperament. She explicitly links psychic exceptionalism to **predatory social recruitment**.

She follows with:

- `いざという時 誰かが助けてくれると思っちゃいけないの`

In a crisis, do not assume someone will save you.

That doctrine remains morally complicated because Tatsumaki herself spends the volume saving others. The contradiction is real and should be preserved rather than solved too early.

Psykos reacts as though this refusal is another recognition injury:

- `半端者だと…この力を見てまだ…`
- `お前も私を認めないのか`

A Fubuki image accompanies the line. The safest V27 reading is that Psykos experiences Tatsumaki's dismissal within an already emotionally charged recognition history involving Fubuki. The exact past relationship/“warning” remains open at this boundary.

### 4.6 `137撃目 究極の叡智`: the strongest moves are information moves

The final chapter pays off V27's central distinction between output and control.

#### Tatsumaki is still solving the civilian problem while fighting

After Psykos's planetary-scale beam displaces a vast mass of water, Tatsumaki checks that the surface team is evacuated and that King is leaving with the hostage (`0150`). She interprets the others' withdrawal as a sensible matchup decision: they have left the battlefield to the person suited to this opponent.

She then raises enormous masses of rock to impede/redirect the oncoming water (`0151-0154`). The psychic battle and disaster-response layer are simultaneous.

This is a direct rebuttal to a “maximum power = unconstrained destruction” reading. Tatsumaki's maximum power remains **multi-tasked toward damage containment**.

#### Psykos and Tatsumaki fight through deception, sensing, and interior geometry

The combat from `0155-0172` repeatedly refuses a pure beam contest.

Psykos uses projectile/beam systems and attempts to sense Tatsumaki's movement through psychic energy. Tatsumaki lets Psykos commit to an apparent target, then identifies that the fused body is unexpectedly hollow/empty in important regions:

- `案外中はスカスカなんだ`
- `じゃあね ハリボテさん`

More importantly, the narration explains Tatsumaki's earlier hidden operation. The fused organism had wrapped itself in a powerful barrier, but one functional opening remained: the roots that absorbed energy.

Tatsumaki camouflaged herself in the incoming energy, traced the flow upstream through the flesh roots, and infiltrated Psykos's body (`0170-0171`). Until then, she had deliberately used psychic waves fine enough to avoid Psykos's detection when extracting heroes because she feared the roots would otherwise seize the protected bodies/barriers too.

This is one of the strongest demonstrations in the manga so far that Tatsumaki's combat intelligence cannot be reduced to “bigger telekinesis.” She:

1. identifies a compulsory system input;
2. hides within it;
3. exploits the input as an infiltration route;
4. keeps rescue traffic below enemy detection threshold;
5. delays battlefield-scale destruction until extraction is complete.

The chapter's title `究極の叡智` is therefore not monopolized by Psykos's grandiose claims. Tatsumaki's actual tactical performance is a competing form of intelligence: bounded, local, mechanism-sensitive, and protection-aware.

#### Psykos attacks the sister relationship

After Tatsumaki's intrusion hurts the fused organism, Psykos threatens to send Fubuki after Tatsumaki and invokes her rage (`0173-0175`). This is not random taunting. V27 has already established Fubuki as a predictable protected exception in Tatsumaki's power geometry.

The enemy now tries to turn a relationship into leverage—exactly the kind of dependency/exploitation problem Tatsumaki warned about.

#### Genos enters as complementary force, not subordinate spectator

At `0181-0188`, Genos intervenes against the fusion's beam. Tatsumaki initially objects that King entrusted the opponent to her and tells the “Demon Cyborg” not to interfere.

Genos answers in strongly assertive register:

- `キングはサイタマ先生が来るまでのつなぎを貴様に任せただけだ`
- `先生のフォローは本来弟子の仕事だ`
- `光線は俺がさばいてやるから さっさとこのバケモノを片付けろ タツマキ!!`

He frames his role through disciple identity and task decomposition: he will handle/deflect the beam; Tatsumaki should eliminate the monster.

Kuseno's established hardware warning returns:

- `使えるフルパワーは10秒まで`

Genos asks whether that window will be enough and commits it. His `穿天雷光砲` can oppose/deflect the fusion's attack, but he explicitly experiences the task as near the edge of the current system.

This scene strengthens several current models simultaneously:

- Genos remains mechanism/constraint-aware during action.
- His Saitama disciple role is not passive admiration; it structures intervention duty.
- Tatsumaki's authority/status does not automatically produce Genos deference.
- The hero side becomes stronger through complementary role allocation even when personalities clash.

#### Fubuki senses multiple impossible powers and refuses easy extraction

As Tatsumaki begins moving the remaining heroes, Fubuki senses several absurdly large power sources on the surface and asks what is happening. Her alarm is not simple fear of Tatsumaki; she is detecting a field with multiple exceptional signatures.

This keeps Fubuki relevant to the psychic conflict without pretending she is operating at Tatsumaki's output scale.

#### Final anti-grandiose beat: “ultimate wisdom” becomes curry

The surface chapter ends with Psykos declaring herself the culmination of human evolution and telling the opponents to know the power of `究極の叡智`.

The very next pages return underground.

Saitama, Flash, and Manako are still stuck under rubble. Saitama jokes that perhaps they have somehow obtained “ultimate wisdom” down there. Manako riffs on the group. Flash, proud and miserable, is reduced to hoping nobody sees him in this state. Saitama's actual next concern is ordinary:

- `家に帰ったら何食おっかな`
- `カレーとか?`

The formal joke is not merely “Saitama likes food.” It collapses a chapter-length cosmic-teleological claim into the ordinary problem of getting home and eating dinner.

Psykos's “wisdom” tries to abolish the difference between individual lives by absorbing them into one destiny. Saitama's underground world is trivial, plural, inconvenient, and relational. The manga ends the volume there.

## 5. Saitama state through V27

Saitama is physically peripheral to the headline battle and therefore highly diagnostic of his stable post-growth mode.

### 5.1 Objective scale still fails to govern subjective salience

The fused organism becomes a distributed planetary-threat system. Saitama's first meaningful contact with it is that a root touches his head. He destroys the local flesh because it annoys him, not because he has understood the global system.

This preserves the long-running distinction:

> objective danger != Saitama's subjective engagement scale.

### 5.2 Local action can exceed local awareness

His irritated strike kills enough connected cells that the central fusion notices the mass loss. Saitama himself receives no corresponding revelation. He is causally central and epistemically local at the same time.

### 5.3 Manako becomes a named individual

V27 upgrades the Saitama/Manako relation from pragmatic co-travel to minimal personal acquaintance. He asks her name and gives his own, ending `よろしくな マナコ`.

### 5.4 Ordinary future orientation remains intact

The final curry thought matters because it occurs while the surface conflict is reaching unprecedented scale. Saitama is not dissociated from life in the sense of having no ordinary desires. Food, home, route-finding, and the people immediately around him continue to supply concrete near-term salience.

## 7. Heroism, rank, recognition, and institutions

### 7.1 S-Class responsibility remains real

The public broadcast says S-Class heroes are handling the City Z event. V27 does not treat that claim as pure propaganda. Tatsumaki, Genos, King, Tanktop Master, Darkshine, and others materially shape rescue/combat conditions.

Mumen Rider supplies the clearest corrective. He does not need to pretend he can defeat the strongest monster to remain a hero. Civilian rescue is a legitimate task domain.

Tatsumaki's battlefield delegation, King escorting Tareo, Genos choosing beam interception, Mumen choosing civilian rescue, and heroes helping identify/extract others show a system that works through distributed initiative as much as top-down dispatch.

Civilians can see City Z deforming, gigantic energy structures, and apparent end-of-world phenomena. They cannot see the fine psychic waves, hostage accounting, root infiltration, or barrier-placement logic that make Tatsumaki's later force possible.

This preserves the series' long-running distinction between **visible spectacle** and **causal work**.

## 8. Monsterhood, personhood, body, and transformation

### 8.1 Fusion is not identity resolution

Psykos/Orochi fusion demonstrates that shared body can coexist with conflicting wills and ownership claims. Body unity is therefore not sufficient evidence for personhood unity.

### 8.2 Distributed body creates new vulnerability as well as scale

The flesh-root network lets the fusion absorb energy/material across space. It also lets local damage and sensory information propagate back to a center. Saitama's tiny local contact becomes centrally felt.

### 8.3 Assimilation is the fusion's explicit growth model

Psykos imagines all life becoming fuel/root material and eventually a planetary organism. This is a biological, social, and ideological collapse of boundaries at once.

### 8.4 Manako remains a monster-classified individual

Saitama's behavior supplies a clean counterexample to treating “monster” as an exhaustive moral/personhood category. Manako is addressed by name and included in practical social exchange.

### 8.5 Garou's monster identity remains self-authored and normatively unstable

His aversion to looking like a bully is difficult to reconcile with indiscriminate monsterhood. V27 does not solve the contradiction; it sharpens it.

V27 supplies unusually strong evidence for nearly every term.

The Kuseno limit `使えるフルパワーは10秒まで` becomes active rather than merely preparatory. Genos can contest/deflect a beam from the fusion for a bounded window, but the scene emphasizes constraint rather than declaring equal total power.

The volume's final gag is a formal thesis. The cosmic phrase “ultimate wisdom” survives only long enough to be stolen by Saitama/Manako banter and replaced with dinner planning. The manga enjoys world-ending spectacle and refuses to let spectacle monopolize meaning.

One of the chapter's most consequential actions is not the largest panel. Tatsumaki hides as a tiny psychic presence inside an energy/root pathway. Formal scale separates **importance** from **size**.

### 12.8 The final cave sequence is an anti-splash-page ending

After planetary and city-scale imagery, V27 ends in cramped rubble, three bodies, ordinary speech, and Flash's embarrassment. The visual contraction is the joke and the thematic release valve.

## 17. Retrospective recontextualization after V1 reopen

### 17.1 V1's “crisis of scale” thesis survives, but V27 shows that scale is relational architecture

V1 correctly saw V25-V27 as the point where the Monster Association raid stops behaving like a sequence of contained matchups. V27 strongly confirms that. What the tankobon-native pass adds is that the largest powers are repeatedly governed by relations that cannot be summarized as output:

- Tatsumaki's scale is constrained by rescue information;
- Psykos's scale depends on Orochi, roots, absorbed energy, and an external grant;
- Genos's scale is bounded by a ten-second hardware window;
- Darkshine's bodily scale depends psychologically on legible safety;
- Garou's growth depends on the adversity supplied by another actor;
- King's weak body still changes the battlefield because reputation changes others' behavior and Tatsumaki's information state.

The V27 refinement of “scale” is therefore **who or what the power needs, consumes, protects, trusts, or misreads.**

### 17.2 Darkshine and Tatsumaki become an illuminating contrast in self-reliance

Darkshine built a body intended to make vulnerability disappear. V27 shows that the strategy fails when reciprocal uncertainty returns.

Tatsumaki voices a different but related doctrine: in a crisis one must not assume somebody else will save you. Yet the same volume shows her creating safety for many others and eventually operating alongside Genos's support. Her doctrine is not disproved, but it is behaviorally incomplete.

Both characters therefore make self-sufficiency analytically unstable. Darkshine's **psychological dependence on invulnerability** and Tatsumaki's **ideological rejection of dependence despite practiced protection/coordination** are opposite ways of revealing that no useful model of strength can ignore relational conditions.

### 17.3 Psykos's “chosenness” and Tatsumaki's “recruitment” are competing interpretations of the same event

Psykos experiences the God-like encounter as `天命` and election. Tatsumaki redescribes it as being seduced by something strange and made into its pawn.

This is more important than simply adding “God” to a power taxonomy. V27 places the epistemic question inside the empowerment event itself:

`gift / revelation / election` **versus** `recruitment / exploitation / subordination`.

Neither interpretation is narrator-certified as complete at this boundary. The tension should govern later God-power analysis.

### 17.4 Garou's monster project remains moralized from inside

V1's emphasis on Garou's body becoming a growth engine is correct but insufficient. V27 proves that his self-authored monster/evil role still contains judgments about what kind of relation counts as meaningful evil. He dislikes the appearance that he is simply bullying a frightened man.

That does not redeem his violence. It prevents “monsterization” from being modeled as a linear erasure of normative distinctions.

### 17.5 The quiet counter-model to assimilation is ordinary acquaintance

Psykos's grand endpoint is a world in which difference is absorbed into one system. The volume's smallest counter-image is Saitama learning Manako's name and giving his own. Nothing is merged; neither person must become the other; no institution certifies the relation.

That scene should not be inflated into a total moral thesis, but it makes the volume's dependence problem visible at human scale: **coordination can begin by recognizing another actor as distinct rather than converting them into category, subordinate, resource, or proof.**
