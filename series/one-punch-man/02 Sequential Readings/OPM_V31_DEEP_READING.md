## 2. Orientation and central thesis

King's standoff continues while the heroes try to coordinate, Bang reaches Garou through a losing fight, and Zombieman attempts to turn restraint into information. The volume then exposes two different forms of apparently overwhelming power: Platinum Sperm's irreversible fusion is materially demonstrated and defeated; King's supposed ultimate attack is supplied by other characters' imaginations and Garou's separate action. Saitama returns through the portal but the anticipated encounter with Garou becomes attention to Genos. A three-way speed contest narrows to two and ends; deceptive power solicitation and newly declared divine punishment open a further conflict at the boundary.

**IR/TH — Recognition can sustain another person, but its reliability depends on what it attends to.** Characters repeatedly turn partial signs into a total account of someone else's power. Homeless Emperor reads mundane caution as tactical omniscience; Child Emperor builds a real plan around an imaginary King technique; Genos locates energy accurately while assigning its source incorrectly; the public sees a light lattice and receives the wrong explanation. These errors are consequential without becoming actual King output or psychic causation.

Alongside those projections, more specific recognition remains possible. Bang stays with his chosen teaching purpose despite defeat. King reports the actual protection Genos performed, and Saitama answers the disciple's failed self-destruction criterion by noticing survival and touching the core. Manako runs toward the defeated Flash. Tatsumaki's memory of a particular relationship exposes a counterfeit appearance; Blast recognizes her resistance and relies on others to care and fight. These are bounded acts of recognition, not proof that anyone has perfect insight. Saitama's modest question-form response is valuable without becoming a technical diagnosis.

The volume's scale therefore runs along two partly independent axes: bodily output escalates through fusion, acceleration and earth/sea threats, while achievement is also found in protecting, withholding a destructive act, refusing an offer and accepting a helper's contribution. It does not award every caring person combat victory, abolish differences of strength, or identify every use of `神` with one mechanism.

## 3. Strongest counter-reading and limits

The escalating battles can be read primarily as action spectacle with King's misunderstandings providing comic delay and Saitama/Genos providing emotional relief. King's survival repeatedly benefits from timing and other fighters; it is not necessary to explain that timing as moral reward. Bang loses despite his sincere purpose. Garou's tactical supremacy does not stop him proclaiming a program of terror. Tatsumaki's recognition can also be a personal-memory test rather than a general philosophy of identity. The lunar imagery and divine declarations may deliberately invite a stronger cosmic unity than the current evidence can establish.

Those qualifications narrow the thesis. The source explicitly marks expectation and cause through interior speech, a decomposed timer sequence, a normal-speed replay, false observer reports, and the completed cover design. It also gives protection its own report and response, separate from winning the fight. Platinum's categorical denial that heroes want to save the weak is not validated simply because he defeats one proud hero: Genos, Bang and King have supplied counterexamples within this volume, and Manako complicates the categorical division from the other side. The reading claims a sustained distinction between kinds of recognition and achievement, not a universal virtue-to-victory law or a single explanation of every scene.
