## 2. Volume orientation

V23 continues the Monster Association raid but shifts the analytical burden away from simple room-clearing. Across very different matchups, the volume asks how anyone knows whether an actor, technique, body, role, or moral claim is the "real thing."

The repeated tests are operational rather than nominal:

- Atomic Samurai's swordsmanship is copied as combat data by G5, but copied trajectory is not treated as equivalent to mastery;
- the surface team and Atomic's disciples show that rank and individual prestige do not exhaust useful combat specialization;
- Black Sperm makes a decisive cutting technique counterproductive by turning damage into proliferation;
- Homeless Emperor presents a cosmic authorization narrative whose reported encounter is real evidence but whose divine interpretation remains speaker-attributed;
- Puri-Puri Prisoner sincerely tries to save monsterized prisoners while persistently refusing their explicit rejection of his relationship claims;
- Nyan treats autonomy from imposed affection as a core preference and chooses his own opponents;
- Pig God turns grotesque appetite into threat handling and later quietly subsidizes an ordinary stranger's eating challenge;
- Darkshine's polished body is still a source of near-total confidence rather than fear at this boundary;
- Amai Mask argues that heroes must be human exemplars while his own ranking practice remains heavily image- and norm-centered.

The volume ends without resolving several of the mechanisms it exposes. Black Sperm remains active; Pig God versus Gums is unresolved; the ontological status of the entity Homeless Emperor calls 神 remains OPEN; and Darkshine has not yet encountered a source-supported crisis of bodily confidence.

## 3. Central volume thesis and strongest counter-reading

### Thesis

**V23 repeatedly distinguishes representation from operational truth.** A copied technique, an S-Class label, an inherited body, a self-declared moral ideal, an alleged divine mandate, a monstrous appearance, and a beautiful or grotesque public image all carry information, but none proves itself. The volume tests claims against mechanism, conduct, constraint, relationship, and consequence.

`107` and `108` establish the pattern through teamwork and `shingan` - discrimination between genuine mastery and imitation. `109` expands it into social and cosmological domains. `110-112` apply it to body, personhood, appetite, autonomy, and self-confidence. The extra `han` closes the volume by asking what it means for a hero to be a moral model, then showing Pig God performing a quiet practical kindness outside Amai Mask's polished model of heroic exemplarity.

### Strongest counter-reading

The volume is not arguing that all categories are fake or that only hidden inner virtue matters. G5 really does copy useful combat data. Hero ranks correlate with meaningful capability and deployment. Homeless Emperor really possesses lethal light attacks. Monsterization visibly changes bodies. Darkshine's confidence is backed by extraordinary physical performance. Pig God's appetite is not merely symbolic; it is a concrete power mechanism.

The safer formulation is therefore: **categories and representations are partial models whose adequacy is determined by what they predict and enable under actual conditions.**

## 4. Sequential close reading

### 4.1 `107` - 背中: support is a form of professional strength

The Narinki mercenaries and Atomic Samurai's disciples move through a battlefield in which ordinary human-war assumptions do not transfer cleanly. A veteran mercenary explicitly contrasts human conflict, where bullets kill soldiers and civilians alike, with the abnormal threat environment of monster combat. His experience matters, but it does not automatically make him the best model for hero work.

The mercenaries also reveal moral scar tissue. One acknowledges that they may once have treated comrades instrumentally and tells a younger fighter that survival and extraction take priority over pride. This is not a narrator declaration that mercenary realism is superior to heroic idealism. The chapter instead lets different forms of practical experience interrogate each other.

Atomic Samurai's disciples supply the cleaner positive model. Iaian says their real support duty is to reduce their master's burden, so they must complete the rescue efficiently and return. Their loyalty is not passive hero worship. It is reciprocal task ownership.

Against Devil Long Hair, the three disciples discover that direct cutting pressure is insufficient. The enemy's hair functions as a distributed weapon system, eventually framed at roughly one hundred thousand strands. They respond through coordination: reading the attack geometry, creating an opening, and treating the impossible-looking quantity as a shared technical problem. The victory belongs to a team process rather than to one disciple proving individual supremacy.

This sequence strengthens a recurring V2 distinction: **good specialization plus coordination can be more important than ordinal rank, but coordination itself must be adapted to the target mechanism.**

### 4.2 `108` - 真贋: copied data is not the same thing as embodied mastery

Machine God G5 tells Atomic Samurai that it has completely copied his sword trajectory and obtained his battle data (`OPM|V23|chapter:108|image:0055`). This is a major technology/Organization datum because G5 is not merely observing a hero; it is explicitly converting observation into a reusable combat model.

Atomic Samurai's answer is ridicule rather than anxiety. He treats G5 as an advanced toy and jokes that, if useful, he might order one online as a practice dummy for his disciples (`image:0056`). The line is characteristically arrogant, but the fight does support the narrow technical point: access to recorded trajectory/data does not make G5 equivalent to the swordsman whose practice generated it.

This should not be inflated into "machines cannot master technique." G5 is dangerous, adaptive enough to preserve an escape option, and possesses mechanisms Atomic does not immediately reduce to simple swordsmanship. The title's genuine/counterfeit problem is local: **representation of technique is not identical to the tacit timing, judgment, target reading, and improvisation of mastery.**

Atomic later notices that one component resisted his cut and acknowledges G5's cleverness. His contempt therefore coexists with mechanism-sensitive observation. He is not merely a traditionalist refusing to see technological competence.

The surrounding mercenary dialogue keeps the chapter from becoming a narrow man-versus-machine story. A veteran argues that justice and ideals cannot be discussed without knowing human filth and real hell. The heroes do not simply accept that claim as superior wisdom. The exchange instead exposes a reciprocal gap: mercenary experience sees what heroic rhetoric can omit, while the mercenaries themselves recognize a kind of courage in people who willingly step in front of monsters that their own professional history did not require of them.

### 4.3 `109` - 増えるヤバイ奴: rescue truth, mechanism failure, and cosmic authorization

#### 4.3.1 Saitama arrives through inconvenience, not institutional mobilization

Child Emperor identifies Saitama to the surface personnel as a hero he happened to encounter/protect underground. The officials are shocked because Saitama was not part of the formal raid. Saitama's own explanation is aggressively mundane: the repeated crashing/noise annoyed him, so he came to complain (`image:0087`).

The scene is important because it preserves two simultaneous facts:

1. Saitama is physically embedded in an operation whose institutional scale is enormous;
2. his subjective entry point remains an ordinary-life nuisance rather than the prestige or drama of the raid.

When officials warn him about the danger of becoming another hostage and tell him to evacuate, he does not perform rank-based deference. He simply continues according to his own practical objective. This is consistent with his established low-ceremony baseline and status-insensitivity, but the prospective document does not yet score the frozen Checkpoint-A registry.

#### 4.3.2 Waganma finally supplies the missing Tareo truth

Once Waganma is safe, a report of another child forces a new problem. Waganma recognizes the description as Tareo and admits that Tareo had been confined with him before a monster took him away.

The important moral fact is why Waganma withheld this earlier. He says that when he was asked whether there were other hostages, he feared being scolded and could not say it (`image:0094`). This is a more precise developmental claim than "Waganma was selfish." Fear, shame avoidance, and privileged expectations produced a consequential omission.

Child Emperor's response is equally diagnostic. He does not punish the confession. He thanks Waganma for telling him and wishes him luck (`image:0095`). That response converts truth-telling into useful rescue information rather than turning confession into another source of shame.

Child Emperor then refuses the idea that exhausting his secret weapon excuses him from the new rescue. He says his technical information and route/enemy analysis still give him a role that only he can perform, while the surface team should preserve its own defensive role. The governing line is role completion, not heroic omnipotence: everyone should fulfill their respective function.

This strengthens the V22 `strong` model without requiring another readiness promotion. Child Emperor increasingly separates **responsibility** from possession of a trump card.

#### 4.3.3 Black Sperm defeats the assumption behind the decisive cut

Atomic Samurai initially treats Black Sperm as an easy opponent. Black Sperm then states the mechanism explicitly: cutting, tearing, hitting, crushing, or twisting him causes continued multiplication (`image:0103`).

This is a clean mechanism mismatch. Atomic's technique has not become bad; the target changes what "successful damage" means. The fight therefore strengthens the project's power rule that effectiveness is relational:

`output x target mechanism x information x geometry x endurance x coordination`

A technically excellent action can worsen the problem if the target converts that action into proliferation.

#### 4.3.4 Homeless Emperor: grievance becomes revelation, but revelation remains attributed

Homeless Emperor's first substantial manga-boundary model appears here. He confronts Zombieman with Dragon-level light attacks and then gives an autobiographical explanation after Zombieman asks what the power actually is.

The account begins in social collapse: job loss, debt, park sleeping, and detachment from ordinary human society. His memory of dismissal is bound to humiliation at a company event. Looking at the sky then shifts his scale of attention: buildings and artificial systems seem small against Earth and sky. For a moment this resembles ecological decentering.

The next step is what matters. The ecological insight becomes fused with resentment. He condemns human beings as egoistic violators of the ecosystem and cannot tolerate having been treated as inferior by such creatures. He decides that escape from the human world and return to nature requires death.

At that point, **within Homeless Emperor's narrated memory**, an entity appears. The quoted speech does four things (`image:0144`): it says he is right; calls humans foolish; says he need not die; and offers him power, adding that he should know what to do with it.

Epistemic boundary:

- TF: Homeless Emperor reports an encounter and quotes the entity.
- TF/VF: Homeless Emperor demonstrably possesses extraordinary light attacks.
- TF: Homeless Emperor calls the power God-granted supernatural/divine power.
- IR: the encounter validates and redirects his suicidal grievance into outward violence.
- OPEN: whether the entity is literally what the manga's cosmology would finally define as 神.
- OPEN: the exact mechanism of the power grant.
- IMPORTANT QUALIFICATION: the quoted entity does not explicitly say "kill humanity." Homeless Emperor supplies the exterminatory conclusion from the validation and grant.

Zombieman immediately asks why Homeless Emperor believed that "thing" was God (`image:0145`). That skepticism is load-bearing. V23 introduces much stronger God evidence than Dr. Genus's earlier language, but it also embeds ontological doubt inside the scene itself.

Homeless Emperor dismisses the need for Zombieman to believe and resumes attack. His rhetoric frames killing as repayment for human arrogance and return to the planet. The ecological language is therefore not an independent environmental ethic; it has become moral authorization for a grievance-centered exterminatory program.

The chapter then cuts from cosmic rhetoric to Saitama casually wondering whether the ground/ceiling is all right. The juxtaposition is not a refutation of the God mystery. It is OPM's familiar formal compression: enormous cosmological self-importance and mundane practical attention occupy adjacent panels.

### 4.4 `110` - ラブエボリューション: bodily adaptation does not repair relational blindness

The chapter begins with lower-ranked/support heroes displaying a dense array of specialized techniques. Electrical, thermal, freezing, acoustic, toxic, mechanical, and other attack modes matter because no one hero needs to contain the whole toolkit. The scene extends V20's argument that rank does not exhaust capability.

Puri-Puri Prisoner's fight then makes the body itself adaptive. An absorbing monster explains that incorporated special abilities can be used until they are digested/excreted. PPP responds with an unusually realistic comparative self-assessment: relative to stronger S-Class heroes he is close to an ordinary adult man, so the monster should first deal with the "ordinary" man in front of it. This is not global humility; it is comparative power calibration.

PPP develops `Vibration Angel` and later a dense-body-hair defensive state. Nyan explicitly hypothesizes that accumulated damage awakened a defensive instinct and rapidly evolved PPP's body (`image:0181`). This is useful evidence of stress-linked adaptation, but Nyan's explanation remains an in-scene inference, not a universal biological law.

The chapter's relationship material is more important than the power-up. PPP recognizes a group of prisoners who have become monsters and insists he can save them. They repeatedly reject his description of them as his "boyfriends" and attack him. His own recollection of prison "affection" remains coercive and self-serving. The scene therefore gives genuine rescue intent without erasing the long-established consent problem.

Nyan makes the contrast explicit. He says PPP's forced affection reminds him of a former owner who pushed affection onto him (`image:0184`). Nyan then states that he has decided to live as he pleases and choose for himself whom he fights (`image:0188`). His monster model is therefore unusually autonomy-centered: avoidance of imposed intimacy and preservation of self-directed choice are more important than proving courage by accepting every fight.

PPP, meanwhile, rejects Nyan's claim that irreversible bodily monsterization would settle personhood. His position is that if their hearts remain human, they are already proper humans (`image:0180`). This is a valuable speaker-level personhood claim. It is not narrator proof that these particular prisoners remain psychologically unchanged; in fact, they are actively telling PPP that his relationship story is false.

The chapter ends by refusing to reward PPP's certainty cleanly. In pursuing Nyan he catches the prisoners in his destructive movement and then interprets the resulting new technique/body development as a "present" from them. This preserves the central contradiction:

> PPP can be sincerely protective and operationally heroic while still filtering other people's agency through a fantasy of reciprocal affection they explicitly deny.

Any character model that keeps only the sincerity or only the coercion would be incomplete.

### 4.5 `111` - 食い意地: appetite has no built-in moral direction

Pig God encounters monsters who assume his huge body makes him easy food. The relation immediately reverses: Pig God eats them instead. The sequence then introduces Dragon-level Gums, another body organized almost entirely around mouth, consumption, and digestive force.

The visual rhyme is deliberate: both hero and monster weaponize appetite, but shared bodily grammar does not establish shared moral direction. Pig God's earlier raid behavior has used consumption to neutralize threats and protect others; Gums is presented as a predatory monster.

V23 does **not** resolve their confrontation. Gums closes its mouth around Pig God at the chapter boundary. No later-volume survival or victory result may be imported into this reading.

The safe V23 claim is therefore structural: **body form and appetite do not determine moral role, and matchup between superficially similar mechanisms remains an open operational question.**

### 4.6 `112` - 超合金クロビカリ: Darkshine is still confidence, not collapse

V23's Darkshine material is especially important because later knowledge could easily contaminate it.

At this exact boundary, Darkshine is not frightened by Bug God. He deliberately allows the monster to attack because Bug God's insect identity touches one of Darkshine's own bodily ideals. Darkshine says his ultimate aspiration was precisely insect-like strength and admits he wanted to feel the power, speed, and frightening strength of a monster confident enough to call itself Bug God (`images:0209-0210`).

The result disappoints him. His phrase is effectively "more than imagined, less than imagined": Bug God exceeded ordinary expectations but fell below the idealized insect-strength model Darkshine carried. He even complains that an insect monster is not black-shining.

Darkshine then identifies three heroes said to be especially dangerous when angered: King, Tatsumaki, and himself. The King description is public-reputation evidence from Darkshine's perspective, not proof of King's physical capability. Tatsumaki's killing power is treated as real. Darkshine's self-entry is comic and sincere: when angered, his muscles move on their own.

Bug God escalates by claiming Garou's fists had not affected his shell and by transforming into a more weaponized insect body. Darkshine simply calls the attack reckless and destroys the transformed body.

The chapter ends with his strongest V23 self-model:

> he says he does not know any way to put a wound on his own body.

He hears another large vibration and expresses interest in finding a cadre-level or greater opponent capable of making his muscles "motivated." This is **pre-crisis invulnerability confidence**. Do not import a later Darkshine/Garou fear collapse backward into V23.

### 4.7 Extra `han`: who actually serves as an example?

The extra begins with Amai Mask reviewing Pig God's S-Class profile. Amai focuses on the ugliness of Pig God's fighting style and asks whether heroes should expose children to such an image. Pig God explains that eating/digestion is his weapon.

Amai describes his ranking practice as analogous to elite restaurant evaluation and says heroes should always exist as objects of civilian admiration. More importantly, he says he continually studies what kind of human being is appropriate for hero status and argues that providing a human model/example is an important heroic role (`image:0223`).

That statement is consistent with Amai's established image-centered hero philosophy. The extra then quietly tests it.

Pig God goes to a ramen shop, undertakes the shop's huge eating challenge, and notices Saitama/Genos nearby. When leaving, Pig God secretly gives the shopkeeper money and asks that it be kept quiet so that the other challenger will not worry if he finishes (`image:0225`). The act is materially generous and non-performative: Pig God does not use it to improve reputation or demand recognition.

The extra therefore creates a productive irony. Amai speaks explicitly about heroes serving as human examples; Pig God, the visually "unpresentable" hero Amai is judging, performs a quiet act that more directly fits ordinary prosocial exemplarity.

This should not be flattened into "Amai is fake, Pig God is the real hero." Amai's concern with public trust is not meaningless. The scene instead shows the blind spot in treating exemplary heroism primarily as a legible image that can be scored from above.

## 5. Saitama state through V23

V23 adds a compact but useful Saitama delta.

### Ordinary inconvenience remains a legitimate action trigger

Saitama enters the Monster Association operation without institutional mobilization because the repeated underground crashing is noisy enough to annoy him. His language is plain, compressed, and domestic in scale relative to the raid.

### Status/institution still fails to define his behavior

Surface personnel treat his unplanned presence as irresponsible because they do not know his actual capability. Their institutional model is rational given their information; Saitama's refusal to orient around that model is equally consistent with his own knowledge of himself. The scene therefore dramatizes knowledge asymmetry rather than simply mocking administrators as stupid.

### Cosmic rhetoric remains outside his chosen register

The cut from Homeless Emperor's planetary/cosmic condemnation to Saitama's practical concern about structural damage again places his plain attention against grand metaphysical rhetoric. This is formal contrast, not evidence that Saitama has assessed or refuted the claimed God entity.

No readiness change is warranted. Saitama remains `strong`.

## 7. Heroism, rank, recognition, and institutions

V23 adds four institutional findings.

1. **Support is not subordinate in analytical value.** Atomic's disciples and surface heroes repeatedly matter because they own tasks elite heroes cannot perform simultaneously.
2. **Role allocation should track current capability and information.** Child Emperor refuses both omnipotent hero fantasy and indiscriminate reinforcement; he keeps the surface defense intact while returning underground because his own information role is unusually valuable.
3. **Professional experience is domain-specific.** Mercenary realism and heroic idealism each expose blind spots in the other.
4. **Exemplarity is contested.** Amai treats heroes as public moral/aesthetic models; Pig God's quiet generosity shows that exemplary conduct may be socially illegible and aesthetically unglamorous.

## 8. Monsterhood, personhood, and body

V23 makes one of the project's strongest body/personhood distinctions so far.

- Black Sperm's body makes ordinary damage reproduce the threat.
- Puri-Puri Prisoner's body develops new defense under accumulated damage.
- Nyan claims monsterization is physically irreversible; treat this as Nyan's proposition, not universal narrator law.
- PPP counters that a human heart is enough for human personhood; treat this as PPP's ethical proposition, not proof of the prisoners' actual inner state.
- The prisoners' explicit rejection of PPP's relationship claims demonstrates why bodily rescue intent cannot substitute for listening to agency.
- Pig God and Gums share consumption/body grammar while occupying opposite operational roles.
- Darkshine's body is both actual defense and a psychological ideal of invulnerability.

V23 therefore continues to resist any single equation among body form, institutional label, self-identification, moral conduct, and personhood.

## 9. Power, technique, God, and explanatory systems

### 9.1 Copying

G5 establishes that the external Organization can collect hero combat data and reproduce at least parts of technique form. The result is meaningful but incomplete: copied trajectory does not automatically reproduce Atomic Samurai's full situational mastery.

### 9.2 Proliferation

Black Sperm supplies a target mechanism in which cutting is locally successful but strategically counterproductive. Future scaling must preserve mechanism.

### 9.3 Stress-linked adaptation

Nyan interprets PPP's new body-hair defense as rapid evolution after accumulated damage triggers defensive instinct. Preserve the attribution. Do not silently equate this with Gyoro Gyoro's near-death research, monster-cell transformation, or Dr. Genus's limiter theory.

### 9.4 God-granted power

V23 creates a major new node in the God ledger. Homeless Emperor reports direct speech from an entity he calls God and attributes his light power to that entity. The manga simultaneously supplies skepticism through Zombieman's question about why he believed the entity was God.

Current V23 ontology:

- `entity encounter`: reported by Homeless Emperor - high-confidence character testimony, not independent omniscient narration;
- `quoted validating speech`: reported within that memory;
- `light power`: directly demonstrated;
- `power granted by entity`: Homeless Emperor attribution, strongly linked narratively but not independently mechanistically verified;
- `entity literally God`: OPEN;
- `God directly ordered human extermination`: NOT ESTABLISHED by the quoted speech;
- `Homeless Emperor interprets the grant as authorization to destroy humanity`: established.

### 9.5 Darkshine body model

V23 establishes a hard subjective claim - Darkshine himself does not know how his body could be wounded - plus direct Bug God failure. This should be stored as **observed invulnerability confidence through V23**, not an unconditional future scaling rule.

## 10. Humor, satire, and genre mechanics

V23's humor repeatedly attacks overconfident classification without denying the pleasure of battle-manga spectacle.

- Atomic Samurai converts G5's boast about copied combat data into an online-shopping joke.
- Saitama enters a massive raid as a noise complaint.
- PPP names involuntary bodily adaptations with increasingly grand `Angel` terminology while other characters observe the mechanics more neutrally.
- Darkshine's idealization of insects culminates in disappointment that Bug God is not even properly black-shining.
- Amai compares hero ranking to elite restaurant grading; Pig God immediately relocates "exemplarity" to a ramen shop.

The jokes do not necessarily destroy the underlying ideas. Atomic's mastery is real, PPP's adaptation is real, Darkshine is extraordinarily durable, and public hero exemplarity really matters. Satire targets the self-mythologizing frameworks wrapped around those facts.

## 12. Visual and formal analysis

### 12.1 Distributed bodies versus singular mastery

Devil Long Hair and Black Sperm fill panels with multiplicity. The page resists clean one-target geometry. By contrast, Atomic Samurai and Darkshine are repeatedly rendered as highly coherent singular bodies. The formal conflict mirrors the mechanism conflict: concentrated mastery must solve threats whose ontology is distributed.

### 12.2 G5 and Atomic Samurai

G5's segmented mechanical armor and multiple sword forms make copied technique visually dense and reproducible. Atomic Samurai's comparatively simple human silhouette keeps attention on timing and line. The imagery reinforces the difference between stored form and embodied judgment without making technology visually weak.

### 12.3 Homeless Emperor

The reported God figure is rendered as a sketch-like, unstable human outline rather than a clean divine icon.
