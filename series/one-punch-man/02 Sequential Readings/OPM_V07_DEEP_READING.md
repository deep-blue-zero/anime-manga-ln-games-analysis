## 3. Volume orientation

V06 ended by placing Saitama against an opponent whose experiential problem resembled his own: overwhelming strength had made ordinary combat unrewarding. V07 resolves that encounter and then refuses to let its cosmic scale dictate the rest of the volume. The manga moves rapidly from a moon-to-Earth battle into disputes about professional legitimacy, reconstruction contracts, police authority, media credit, managers, family recitals, dojo attendance, and the resale value of a lunar rock.

That compression is not merely tonal whiplash. It clarifies the early-series system.

**Power, legitimacy, credit, justice, and service are separate currencies.**

Boros can provide Saitama with the strongest resistance so far without becoming an equal. Amai Mask can possess extraordinary professional legitimacy and still articulate a narrow eradication-centered moral framework. The Hero Association can be indispensable in monster response while one of its operations officials uses a police emergency as a publicity opportunity. Police can be physically unable to defeat a Demon-level threat while still performing socially necessary work Saitama respects. Metal Knight can be absent from the interpersonal coalition while becoming indispensable to post-disaster infrastructure. Saitama can want recognition and deliberately leave a scene so another institution receives it.

V07 therefore closes one power problem and immediately broadens the problem of **who is authorized to count as a protector and who receives the story afterward**.

## 4. Central thesis

**V07 establishes that effective service cannot be inferred from prestige, victory, publicity, or even moral rhetoric alone.** The volume repeatedly forces those dimensions apart.

The Boros conclusion shows the first distinction. Boros is legitimately powerful relative to every prior opponent, and Saitama says so. Yet the closing exchange also establishes that Saitama retained substantial reserve. “Strongest so far” is therefore not “reciprocal equal.” Saitama's brief consoling agreement with Boros is knowingly softer than strict factual description.

The Amai sequence supplies the second distinction. Amai explicitly reveals that remaining A-class rank 1 is a deliberate gatekeeping position: he wants to control who may rise into S-class. He then kills surviving alien combatants while naming them evil and describing the act as rapid execution of justice. This does not make his hero status fraudulent; it demonstrates that hero status does not settle the content of justice.

`カツ丼` supplies the third and strongest distinction. Police and Hero Association are rival institutions with overlapping protective claims. McCoy attempts to transform police failure into Hero Association publicity. Saitama instead defeats the threat without waiting for the Association's formal dispatch and disappears before media arrival, allowing the police to receive public credit. He does this even though earlier volumes have established that recognition matters to him.

The gesture is not anti-recognition. It is **recognition subordinated to a higher judgment about deserved institutional dignity**.

## 5. Sequential close reading

### 5.1 35撃目 `戦い` — heterogeneous elites learn through comparison

Tanktop Master begins with confidence that his own anti-air technique can contribute despite Tatsumaki's rank. He throws large structural debris upward; Tatsumaki appropriates the same material and demonstrates a radically higher-output version of the action. His response is not face-protective denial. He simply registers the comparison and says, in effect, that he has lost.

That matters because V06 froze the S-class as a heterogeneous coalition rather than a common personality type. V07 immediately confirms that heterogeneity and adds a useful Tanktop Master policy: **comparative defeat can become input for later self-modification rather than merely humiliation**.

The ground battle against Melzargard continues the V06 mechanism-learning structure. Once the embedded spheres are identified as meaningful vulnerabilities, the team can convert apparently futile damage into targeted destruction. The resolution is therefore not reducible to a single hero's scalar superiority. Information changes what counts as effective force.

#### Drive Knight's warning

Drive Knight approaches Genos despite noting that they do not know one another and gives him a terse warning:

> `メタルナイトは お前の敵だ`

When Genos asks for the meaning, Drive Knight refuses full explanation and tells him to avoid Metal Knight for now.

**Boundary ruling:** this is evidence that Drive Knight believes, claims, or strategically communicates that Metal Knight is dangerous to Genos. It is **not evidence that the claim is true**. V07 later supplies enormous constructive capacity from Metal Knight, but constructive capacity also does not prove benign motive. The correct state is unresolved actor-to-actor suspicion.

### 5.2 36撃目 `ボロスの本領` — challenge without equality

Saitama's engagement is unusually explicit. After landing a meaningful hit he notes that he felt impact; when Boros survives and regenerates, Saitama asks whether the battle is already over.

The frozen Checkpoint-A prediction was that credible resistance or novelty would increase Saitama's engagement sharply. V07 provides a direct confirmation. His attention is not merely higher because the stakes are cosmic; the line is specifically about whether the **fight** continues.

Boros explains that his species has exceptional survival traits and that his own healing, physical function, and latent energy are extraordinary even by that standard. Concentrated energy allows rapid regeneration of catastrophic damage. This is direct V07 body-mechanism evidence; it should not be expanded into later biological terminology.

Boros then escalates through Meteoric Burst. The sequence produces the manga's largest scale discontinuity so far: Saitama is kicked to the moon, pauses long enough to orient himself, and returns to Earth. Yet the emotional logic remains continuous with V01. Saitama finally has a combat encounter that does not terminate instantly—but even this degree of resistance fails to become equality.

The final exchange is decisive. Saitama tells the dying Boros:

> `やっぱ強ぇーよ お前`

Boros says the prophecy was right and that it was an equal, good fight. Saitama agrees briefly. Boros immediately rejects the agreement as false: Saitama still had room to spare, and Boros never truly reached him.

Two character consequences follow.

First, Saitama can recognize real relative excellence. His boredom does not erase discrimination among opponents.

Second, the agreement is another case of **intentional nonliteral speech**, but unlike V05's crowd manipulation it is not strategic public narrative management. It is closer to a dying-adversary concession: Saitama gives Boros the kinder version of the encounter without insisting on humiliating factual precision. This broadens the prior rule from “Saitama can lie to control public narrative” to “Saitama can use consciously softened/nonliteral wording when strict truth would serve no important value.” It remains context-dependent and must not be generalized into habitual dishonesty.

### 5.3 37撃目 `墜落` — justice, gatekeeping, self-recognition, and the post-disaster state

#### Amai Mask's gatekeeping becomes explicit

V05 established that Amai's concern centered on the social meaning and presentation of influential heroes, but did **not** justify the claim that he personally controls every promotion. V07 sharpens the model with direct wording:

> `僕がなぜS級に上がらないかわかるか?`
>
> `キミみたいな弱くて何の取り柄もない雑魚をS級にさせないためにA級1位を守っているのさ!`

The prospective correction is now precise. Amai intentionally occupies A1 as a **barrier to S-class entry**. The earlier refusal to overclaim was correct; V07 supplies the stronger proposition only now.

#### Eradication-centered justice

Surviving Dark Matter aliens attempt to survive after the invasion. Amai kills them and frames the act through `悪` and `正義` language: they lack value to keep alive, and he has executed justice rapidly.

The scene should not be reduced to a verdict about whether the act is legally justified. Its modeling value lies in Amai's cognitive compression:

`identified evil -> low tolerance for ambiguity/delay -> eradication framed as justice`.

Genos's reaction is even more important than his speculation about Amai's history. He thinks Amai resembles **himself before meeting Saitama**: a person without emotional room, urgently trying to eradicate evil. This is the first strong evidence that Genos can model his own developmental change by observing another person.

His subsequent guess that Amai may have experienced some past catastrophe is only a hypothesis. It must not be converted into Amai biography.

#### Genos's trust receives a boundary probe, not a contradiction

When the alien ship collapses, Genos worries because Saitama was inside. His cognition still contains the assumption that Saitama is probably fine. The moment therefore does not supply the serious contradiction Checkpoint A identified as especially diagnostic. It is a **non-diagnostic/weak boundary probe** showing attachment can generate worry even inside near-axiomatic confidence.

When Saitama returns, Genos immediately checks the blood on him. Saitama explains it is only blood from others and reports the enemy leader as genuinely strong—possibly the strongest he has fought so far.

This preserves the distinction established in the Boros conclusion: “strongest so far” can be true without “equal” being true.

#### Tatsumaki conflict confirms rank insensitivity

Tatsumaki rebukes Saitama for acting independently despite being B-class. Saitama does not increase deference because she is S-class rank 2. Genos escalates much more aggressively, and Bang intervenes as regulator.

This directly confirms another Checkpoint-A prediction: **status alone does not produce Saitama deference**. Relationship, practical relevance, and his own judgment matter more.

Bang's intervention also strengthens his V06 role as a coalition regulator who is willing to criticize another S-class hero's conduct rather than defer to rank.

#### Catastrophe produces centralization

After A-City disappears, the Hero Association does not simply rebuild the previous city. Its headquarters is reinforced into a fortress; transport routes are constructed from headquarters toward the other cities; and A-class-and-above heroes can relocate into the headquarters complex.

The institutional consequence of planetary attack is therefore **spatial centralization of professional hero infrastructure**. The Association becomes more capable of rapid dispatch and more physically concentrated at the same time.

### 5.4 Moon-rock omake — cosmic achievement becomes material ordinariness

Genos identifies a stone Saitama brought back as lunar material worth roughly `1g 60万円`. Saitama's response is immediate practical excitement about selling it.

The gag is diagnostically useful because it refuses the expected prestige framing. Saitama has accomplished an impossible cosmic transit, but his salient interpretation of the residue is: **this may be worth money**. The moment preserves the mundane financial baseline established through sales, rent/food scarcity, and the V05 drink bonus.

Cosmic power does not erase ordinary material desire.

### 5.5 番外編1 `大工事` — Metal Knight as infrastructure power

Two days after A-City's destruction, planners discuss transforming the devastated land into an expanded Hero Association complex and transport network. Conventional estimates put the project on roughly a ten-year horizon.

Metal Knight's construction system arrives and turns the wasteland into a pipeline of automated demolition, material handling, fabrication, and construction. Tanktop Master initially interprets the machine as another opponent, tries to stop it, and is overwhelmed by the scale of the machinery. He then learns that the system belongs to Metal Knight and that the operation is construction rather than attack.

The decisive endpoint is explicit:

> `メタルナイトは7日で竣工させた`

The analytically important ratio is not merely “Metal Knight is technologically advanced.” It is **institutional time compression**: a project spoken about on a decade-scale is completed in seven days.

This moves Metal Knight from one-disaster test evidence toward a recurring technological actor with at least three separate functions by V07: experimental weapons deployment, hardened Association infrastructure, and post-catastrophe salvage/reconstruction. Motive remains open, but reconstructive breadth now justifies `emerging` rather than `insufficient`.

Tanktop Master also carries his earlier Tatsumaki comparison into training. He treats psychic power and now mechanical power as deficits to work against. His policy is not graceful philosophical acceptance; it is competitive adaptation:

`encounter superior capability -> acknowledge loss -> train against the revealed category`.

### 5.6 番外編2 `一番弟子の回想` — Charanko's ordinary motive and accidental status

Charanko recalls joining Bang's school because he wanted to be popular, not because he possessed an austere martial philosophy. After a year he does not feel substantially stronger; he skips training for two weeks and contemplates quitting partly because even reaching the dojo is burdensome.

On returning, he finds the school empty. Bang tells him the other disciples quit after the earlier catastrophe involving Garou. Charanko realizes that being the last remaining student makes him, effectively, the top disciple. His motivation rises again.

This is valuable precisely because it is undignified. Charanko's persistence is not pure discipline; status framing can reactivate him. Bang sees the sudden enthusiasm with mild skepticism rather than grand mentorship rhetoric.

The recurrence across V06–V07 now justifies a populated independent/civilian state ledger for Charanko. It does not yet justify `moderate` readiness.

### 5.7 番外編3 `カツ丼` — rival institutions and recognition sacrificed again

`カツ丼` is the most important V07 material for the early heroism model because it removes the issue from Hero Association internal hierarchy.

#### Police legitimacy is real, not a straw man

Police detain Saitama as a suspicious, blood-stained, apparently unemployed man without an obvious conventional address. The police chief expresses distrust of the Hero Association, but his argument is not simply resentment. Police already perform public-order functions without heroic branding: traffic enforcement, juvenile protection, and maintenance of ordinary civic order.

When asked why he became a hero, Saitama gives an uncertain memory that it was because being a hero seemed cool. The chief contrasts that with police work that is necessary even when it is not publicly glamorous.

Saitama does not ridicule the point. This matters because he has no institutional reason to flatter the police. His eventual behavior shows that he regards their work as possessing real social value independent of rank or fame.

#### Failure does not erase service

A monster later identified as `奇襲梅`, estimated disaster level Demon, attacks police headquarters. Police use the tools available to them and discover that ordinary weapons are ineffective. The chief reluctantly requests Hero Association support.

The Association's McCoy turns the request into a status contest. He reminds police of their hostility to the Association, pushes the chief into an explicit `お願いします`, and later arranges incentives/media presence so professional heroes can visibly demonstrate superiority over police.

This is not evidence that “the Hero Association” has one motive. It is evidence of **an official exploiting an emergency for institutional narrative advantage**.

#### Saitama's authorization principle

Saitama acts before the formal publicity operation arrives. His formulation is direct:

> `「お願い」されて動くもんじゃない`
>
> `ヒーローも ケーサツも な?`

The line extends the self-authorship model beyond Saitama himself. In his view, the normative basis of protective work cannot be reduced to being asked, thanked, ranked, or publicly validated.

He then undercuts ceremony by framing the intervention as repayment for a bowl of katsudon.

#### Held-out confirmation: recognition can be surrendered to preserve deserved legitimacy

Saitama defeats the monster and leaves. By the time Hero Association responders and television arrive, the visible evidence makes it appear that police handled the incident. Media immediately credits police. McCoy's publicity plan collapses.

The police chief eventually understands enough to identify Saitama as the person who acted and sees him on a distant rooftop.

This is a clean independent recurrence of the V05 recognition-sacrifice pattern, now under a different social structure:

`Saitama wants recognition` remains true;

`police performed real service but lacked the physical capacity to finish the threat` is also true;

`visible Saitama credit would participate in a publicity narrative humiliating police`;

therefore `Saitama removes himself from the credit chain`.

Checkpoint A predicted that if recognition conflicted with a higher ethical/social value, Saitama might sacrifice recognition. **V07 confirms it.**

The recurrence also refines the reason. Saitama protects not only individual heroes' standing, as in V05, but the legitimacy of a different public-service institution whose ordinary work he judges meaningful.

### 5.8 Final omake — Amai, Metal Bat, managers, and family

The post-battle omake prevents both Amai and Metal Bat from remaining combat-only silhouettes.

Amai has professional obligations beyond immediate hero fighting, including scheduled performance work and a manager who calls him. He can disengage from a confrontation because he has somewhere else to be. This does not erase his eradication rhetoric; it adds an ordinary professional-state channel.

Metal Bat receives a call from his younger sister after her piano recital. She tells him she placed third and practiced for him. His emotional response is direct. He is simultaneously happy for her and angry that hero duty caused him to miss the event. He then asks Amai for an autograph for her.

The scene strongly confirms that his rough/aggressive register is not emotional poverty. Family attachment is now repeated, behaviorally consequential, and expressed without ironic distance.

## 6. Character-model deltas

### Saitama — remains `strong`, validation confidence increases

New V07 coverage:
- best-so-far opponent recognition without false equality;
- intentional softening/nonliteral consolation toward a dying opponent;
- cross-institution legitimacy judgment;
- repeated voluntary credit sacrifice;
- police/public-service respect not based on rank;
- low-stakes money salience through moon-rock resale;
- unchanged rank insensitivity toward Tatsumaki.

The model should now distinguish three nonliteral-speech functions:
1. strategic crowd narrative manipulation (V05);
2. compassionate softening to a dying adversary (V07);
3. ordinary joking/compression.

Do not collapse these into “Saitama lies casually.”

### Genos — remains `strong`, metacognition strengthened

The most important V07 addition is his explicit self-comparison to Amai: he recognizes a rigid, hurried anti-evil psychology as resembling his pre-Saitama self. That is evidence that the Saitama relationship has become part of how Genos narrates his own development.

His worry during the ship collapse is attachment evidence but does not contradict his strong confidence in Saitama.

### Amai Mask — `emerging -> moderate`

V07 adds:
- explicit A1-as-S-class-gatekeeper motive;
- eradication-centered justice language;
- confrontation with Genos;
- ordinary professional/manager obligations;
- flexible withdrawal from a low-value fight because of scheduled work.

This clears `moderate` while leaving private intimacy, failure/recovery, and sustained non-public relationships underdeveloped.

### Metal Bat — `emerging -> moderate`

Family evidence recurs and becomes direct emotional behavior. Combat roughness, tactical observation, sister salience, professional interruption, disappointment, pride, and an ordinary favor request now form a meaningful conditional range.

### Bang — remains `moderate`, strengthened

V07 reinforces:
- coalition-regulator behavior with Tatsumaki;
- Charanko relationship continuity;
- low-ceremony handling of dojo collapse.

### Tanktop Master — enters `emerging`

Comparative loss to Tatsumaki and later mechanical construction produce a recurring adaptation policy: acknowledge defeat and train against the revealed capability category.

### Metal Knight / Bofoi — `insufficient -> emerging`

Across V04/V06/V07, directly observed effects now span weapons testing, hardened HQ construction, alien-ship recovery, and an automated seven-day reconstruction/infrastructure project. Motive and social psychology remain poorly observed; readiness should not exceed `emerging`.

### Drive Knight — `insufficient`

The warning to Genos is consequential but one cryptic interaction is not broad character evidence.

### Charanko — enters `emerging`

V07 supplies motive, dropout temptation, burden sensitivity, status-reactivated persistence, and an ongoing Bang relation. A dedicated independent/civilian ledger is now justified.

## 7. Relationship deltas

### Saitama ↔ Genos
Genos's concern after the ship fall strengthens attachment without overturning his epistemic confidence. Saitama returns and immediately speaks to Genos in ordinary post-work terms (`終わったから帰ろうぜ`), compressing planetary combat back into domestic continuity.

### Bang → Charanko
Bang provides factual information about the school's collapse and observes Charanko's sudden renewed enthusiasm without manufacturing a grand lesson. The relation is teacher/student, but the student's motivation remains imperfect and ordinary.

### Metal Bat → younger sister
Family attachment is now repeated enough to be a stable relationship-conditioned behavior channel. Hero work competes with, rather than erases, family obligation.

### Amai Mask ↔ Metal Bat
The omake shows mutual provocation can be interrupted by ordinary work/family realities. Their hostility should not be generated as an always-on death match.

## 8. Thematic deltas

### Heroism / legitimacy
V07 expands the question from “who counts as a hero inside the Association?” to “what kinds of institutions perform socially necessary protective work?” Police and professional heroes can overlap, compete, and misrecognize each other while both possess legitimate functions.

### Recognition
Saitama's credit sacrifice now repeats independently. The current rule is stronger than humility: he is willing to lose desired public legibility when he thinks the credit structure would unjustly erase another actor's meaningful contribution or legitimacy.

### Institution
Post-A-City reconstruction centralizes the Association spatially and logistically. `カツ丼` simultaneously shows inter-institution rivalry and publicity incentives. “Institution” therefore includes both infrastructure and narrative competition.

### Justice
Amai's language makes `正義` a contested term rather than a series-provided verdict. His justice favors rapid eradication of identified evil; Saitama's service model is much less dependent on categorical moral rhetoric.

### Technology
Metal Knight now has enough recurrence to be treated as an institutional-scale technology actor rather than only a remote weapons tester. Drive Knight's warning opens, but does not resolve, the question of hidden technological actors and mutual suspicion.

### Visual form
The volume oscillates between extreme-scale spectacle and anticlimactic ordinary framing: moon impact/return → pawnshop rock; planetary ship ruin → seven-day construction; hero/police institutional dispute → katsudon debt. Scale compression is a recurring formal device for keeping Saitama's ordinary life analytically adjacent to cosmic capability.

## 10. Claim transitions at V07

- **STRENGTHEN — Saitama challenge model:** Boros confirms activation by credible resistance but also shows activation is not the same as satisfaction or equality.
- **STRENGTHEN / broaden — strategic/nonliteral speech:** Saitama's consoling agreement with Boros is a distinct function from V05 public manipulation.
- **STRENGTHEN — recognition hierarchy:** `カツ丼` independently repeats voluntary credit sacrifice outside Hero Association internal politics.
- **STRENGTHEN — internally authorized service:** `お願い` is explicitly rejected as the thing that makes either heroes or police act.
- **REVISE / sharpen — Amai Mask:** V07 now directly establishes deliberate A1 gatekeeping of S-class entry and a rapid eradication conception of justice.
- **STRENGTHEN — Genos self-model:** Amai becomes a mirror for Genos's pre-Saitama rigidity; speculation about Amai's past remains non-authoritative.
- **STRENGTHEN — S-class heterogeneity:** Tanktop Master comparison and Tatsumaki/Genos/Bang conflict continue divergent elite styles.
- **STRENGTHEN — Metal Knight institutional power:** post-catastrophe technology now spans hardened infrastructure, salvage, and seven-day reconstruction.
- **OPEN — Drive Knight / Metal Knight relation:** warning is direct evidence of suspicion, not proof of target guilt.
- **CLOSE / REVISE — Boros encounter:** strongest relative opponent so far, but not an equal; encounter and V06 upper-bound question are resolved at this boundary.

## 11. Open questions carried forward

- What is the cause of Saitama's strength?
- What form of reciprocal challenge, if any, could produce sustained satisfaction rather than brief activation?
- How durable is Saitama's willingness to surrender recognition across still other institutions/relationships?
- What is the true basis and motive behind Drive Knight's warning about Metal Knight?
- What are Metal Knight's larger motives despite clearly demonstrated institutional utility?
- How does Amai's eradication-centered justice behave under moral ambiguity or personal failure?
- Does Genos's near-axiomatic Saitama belief ever face a genuinely disconfirming case?
- What exactly is the scope/referent of Shibabawa's `地球がヤバい` prophecy? V07 character opinion does not close it.
- How does Hero Association centralization alter later dispatch, residence, politics, and dependence?
- Will the police/HA legitimacy conflict recur, or is `カツ丼` a bounded institutional episode?

## 12. Final V07 ruling

**PASS — suitable for canonical promotion after Japanese-language audit, cumulative propagation, and Drive readback.**

V07 closes the first cosmic opponent without closing Saitama's experiential problem. More importantly, it expands the early-series ethical architecture beyond the Hero Association. The strongest protector can recognize another institution's worth, act without formal authorization, and then deliberately step out of the credit chain. At the same time, Amai, McCoy, Metal Knight, Tanktop Master, and the police demonstrate that power, status, publicity, infrastructure, and justice each operate through different logics.

The first held-out checkpoint result is encouraging but appropriately modest: **3 confirm, 2 partial, 0 contradict, 6 non-diagnostic**. V08 must continue validation without treating V07 success as proof of future behavior.
