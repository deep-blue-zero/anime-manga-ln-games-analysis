## 2. Volume orientation

V04 asked what professional heroism means when disaster, institutional rank, actual contribution, and public judgment diverge. V05 continues the same crisis but changes the governing question.

The Deep Sea King cannot initially be defeated by the heroes available at the shelter. The volume therefore spends most of its emotional energy not on victory but on what different people do **after the probability of victory collapses**. Sonic withdraws when the situation becomes irrational to continue. Genos calculates, commits, and then destroys his own combat viability to shield a child. Mumen Rider knows in explicit language that he cannot win and attacks anyway because someone must stand between the threat and the civilians. The crowd's hope becomes detached from realistic capability. Only after that sequence does Saitama arrive and end the physical conflict instantly.

That does not end the volume's real problem. A spectator immediately tries to reinterpret the victory in a way that devalues every hero who lost before Saitama arrived. Saitama responds by deliberately damaging **his own** reputation so that the defeated heroes retain social credit. The same protagonist who has repeatedly wanted recognition now chooses to lose it when recognition becomes a zero-sum weapon against other people's heroism.

The bonus chapter then makes the professional system material. Hero rank controls monthly pay; activity points are opaque; lower-class heroes can remain poor; and Saitama is poor enough to count coins in front of a vending machine. The volume closes not with cosmic abstraction but with Genos noticing an unspoken consumer desire and trying, somewhat excessively, to satisfy it.

V05 therefore links three apparently different forms of scarcity:

- insufficient **power** against the Deep Sea King;
- insufficient **social credit** after the victory;
- insufficient **money** in ordinary life.

In each case the characters reveal what they value when there is not enough to distribute cleanly.

# 3. Central thesis

**V05 asks what heroism is worth when power, victory, reputation, and reward point in different directions.**

Its strongest answer is not that winning is irrelevant. The civilians still need the monster stopped. Rather, V05 separates at least four dimensions that the story's institutions and spectators are tempted to collapse:

1. **capacity** — what a hero can physically accomplish;
2. **duty** — whether a hero acts when action is required;
3. **recognition** — who receives social credit for what happened;
4. **reward** — what institutional status and money follow afterward.

Mumen Rider has almost no useful capacity against the Deep Sea King but extraordinary duty commitment. Saitama has overwhelming capacity and ultimately wins, but then voluntarily gives away recognition. Genos has enough capacity to matter but chooses civilian protection over preserving his own body. The Hero Association converts heterogeneous action into ranks and pay despite admitting through narration that its point criteria are opaque and imperfectly reflected in standings.

That means V05 is not simply an underdog chapter followed by a gag victory. Its formal structure shifts the moral climax **after** the physical climax. Saitama's punch answers “who can stop the monster?” His subsequent lie answers the harder social question: “what should happen to the meaning of everyone else's failed resistance once the unbeatable person arrives?”

### Strongest counter-reading

A reductive reading would call Saitama's post-battle performance pure humility. That loses the strategic and ethically costly element. He is not merely refusing praise. He knowingly makes himself look worse so that the public will not convert his effortless success into contempt for the people who fought before him. This costs him exactly the kind of social legibility V02-V04 established that he does care about.

A second reductive reading would say Mumen Rider proves that trying is all that matters. The chapter does not erase capability. The narration and Mumen himself both acknowledge that he cannot win. His action matters because the civilians still need time, hope, and a defender, **not because belief magically makes the power gap disappear**.

A third reductive reading would treat the Hero Association's compensation system as pure nonsense. V05 instead presents a legible but lossy system: rank determines monthly payment; activity points contribute to ranking; reports and eyewitness testimony feed evaluation. The satire comes from opacity, imperfect attribution, and the mismatch between a scalar ranking and heterogeneous heroic value.

# 4. Sequential close reading

## 4.1 25撃目 `深海王・2` — rational withdrawal, public image, and repeated courage

### Sonic can retreat

The continuation of Sonic's encounter with the Deep Sea King is important precisely because it occurs outside his Saitama fixation. Sonic reads the environment and realizes the rain is restoring or increasing the Sea King's effective condition. Without the weapon/equipment state he considers necessary, he does not convert pride into suicidal persistence. He withdraws.

This strengthens the V04 conclusion that Sonic's obsessive escalation around Saitama is **relationship-conditioned**. Baseline pride and superiority remain, but survival cognition is intact. Novel-situation modeling should not make him fight every stronger opponent to the death merely because humiliation can make him reckless around Saitama.

### Amai Mask defines the hero as an image-management institution

Amai Mask's media appearance gives the first direct ideological sample broad enough to matter. He says:

> `人々の不安を取り除くためにも ヒーローというものは 常にタフで力強く そして美しく`
>
> `速やかにスマートに悪を排除できる存在でなくてはなりません`
>
> (`OPM|V05|chapter:25|image:0057`)

The claim is not merely aesthetic vanity. Its premise is explicitly public affect: heroes should remove people's anxiety. Amai therefore treats hero presentation, power, speed, and beauty as part of the **social technology of reassurance**.

That is a genuine philosophy, but V05 immediately supplies counterexamples. The shelter heroes are not uniformly beautiful, dominant, or successful. Their resistance still has moral and social value. The volume thus opens a durable tension between:

`hero as reassuring ideal image` and `hero as person who acts under ugly failure`.

### Shelter defense makes defeat cumulative rather than meaningless

The Deep Sea King moves into the shelter and repeatedly overwhelms registered heroes. The chapter's rhythm matters: new defenders arrive or stand up, hope rises, and the power gap reasserts itself.

The point is not that the lesser heroes are secretly strong enough. They are not. But their defeats create a cumulative civic action history. They expose themselves to harm while civilians remain behind them. This becomes crucial later, because Saitama's reputational maneuver only makes sense if those failed actions genuinely deserve preservation rather than mockery.

**V05 claim:** failed resistance can be heroically meaningful without being physically sufficient.

## 4.2 26撃目 `不安定な希望` — Genos chooses the civilian over his own combat body

### Independent S-class response

Genos enters the Deep Sea King crisis as an independently functioning S-class hero. Saitama is not directing him. This matters for the character model because early Genos evidence can otherwise overfit every serious decision to the master-disciple relationship.

He locates the threat, assesses it, and commits to combat on his own professional/civic authority.

### Power remains useful even when it is not decisive

Genos can hurt, pressure, and tactically contest the Deep Sea King. The fight is not presented as meaningless because Genos ultimately loses. It demonstrates an intermediate capability state between “wins” and “does nothing.”

This supports the power ledger's broader anti-scalar rule: combat contribution cannot be represented only as final victory.

### The child-shield moment is a major character-model atom

When the Deep Sea King directs corrosive fluid toward a child, Genos physically interposes himself. The action catastrophically damages his body and sharply reduces his remaining ability to fight.

This is among the strongest V01-V05 pieces of evidence for Genos because the protected person is not Saitama, Dr. Kuseno, or someone tied to his revenge history. The beneficiary is an unknown civilian child.

The behavioral sequence is:

`civilian target identified -> immediate bodily interception -> accept catastrophic self-damage -> preserve civilian`.

This strengthens several independent model fields at once:

- civilian protectiveness;
- high bodily-cost tolerance;
- rescue priority over combat-preservation;
- hero-duty internalization independent of Saitama;
- ability to act prosocially without needing explicit institutional command.

It also prevents a shallow reconstruction in which Genos's self-sacrifice is treated only as revenge-driven recklessness. V05 shows the same willingness to spend his body for anonymous others.

### Unstable hope

The chapter title `不安定な希望` describes more than the crowd's emotions. Hope repeatedly attaches itself to new bodies: another hero arrives, another capability becomes visible, and then that body is broken. The visual rhythm deliberately makes hope temporary.

This prepares the volume's key distinction: hope need not be epistemically justified to have social force, but a hero should not confuse the crowd's hope with a truthful estimate of victory.

## 4.3 27撃目 `ズタボロに輝く` — Mumen Rider knows he cannot win

Mumen Rider's scene is the clearest V01-V05 rejection of the idea that courage requires optimistic self-deception.

He says:

> `期待されてないのはわかってるんだ`
>
> (`OPM|V05|chapter:27|image:0125`)

Then:

> `俺じゃB級で通用しない`
>
> `自分が弱いって事はちゃんとわかってるんだ!`
>
> (`image:0126`)

And finally:

> `俺が一番よくわかってるんだよォッ…!!`
>
> `それでもやるしかないんだ`
>
> `俺しかいないんだ`
>
> (`image:0127`)

The sequence culminates in:

> `勝てる勝てないじゃなく`
>
> `ここで俺は お前に立ち向かわなくちゃいけないんだ!`
>
> (`images:0128-0129`)

### What this does and does not establish

It establishes:

- accurate self-assessment;
- knowledge of the class boundary he believes he cannot clear;
- awareness that spectators do not rationally expect victory;
- willingness to act anyway;
- a duty model based on **who is present and able to stand**, not on expected success.

It does **not** establish:

- indifference to survival;
- belief that effort guarantees victory;
- contempt for strength;
- a universal rule that every hopeless fight must always be taken regardless of alternatives.

At this scene's knowledge boundary, Mumen sees civilians behind him, other heroes incapacitated, and himself as the available defender. `俺しかいないんだ` is situationally important.

### Crowd response

The crowd begins to chant for Mumen Rider even though the narration makes his lack of victory probability explicit. Their hope is no longer a rational capability assessment. It becomes recognition of willingness.

This is a decisive extension of the heroism ledger:

`public recognition can be wrong about capability` — already established;

but now also:

`public recognition can correctly perceive moral courage even when it cannot produce victory`.

That keeps the project from treating “public narrative” as automatically foolish. OPM's crowds can misattribute causality, but they can also recognize meaningful effort.

## 4.4 28撃目 `雨降ってるから` — victory is physically trivial; credit becomes the real conflict

### Saitama's arrival

Saitama arrives after the volume has spent dozens of pages making defeat costly. His physical solution is almost offensively simple. That disparity is the point.

The Deep Sea King dies to one punch. The power question closes instantly.

But the story refuses to end the chapter there.

### The cynical reinterpretation

A civilian begins arguing that Saitama's victory was easy because the preceding heroes had already weakened the monster. The claim has an important double structure:

- it tries to preserve the defeated heroes by denying Saitama's superiority;
- in doing so, it creates a new false account of causal contribution.

Saitama could correct the record. Everything from V02 onward tells us that correct attribution matters to him.

Instead, he chooses the opposite.

### Deliberate reputational self-sabotage

Saitama loudly embraces a false opportunist narrative. He says, in effect, that the other heroes drained the monster's strength, that arriving late made his job easy, and that he has managed to monopolize the credit despite doing little. He even tells the crowd to spread the story of the cowardly rookie who stole the benefit of others' work (`images:0151-0152`).

He then points toward the fallen heroes:

> `おいお前ら 倒れたヒーロー達をちゃんと看てやれよ`
>
> `死なれたら困るんだよ俺が利用できなくなるだろ`
>
> (`image:0154`)

The second clause is another lie layered over a real directive. He frames concern as exploitation so the audience can preserve the contemptuous picture he has just built of himself.

The social effect follows immediately: attention returns to the injured heroes and gratitude is reallocated toward them.

### Recognition hierarchy — V05 revision

V02 established that Saitama wants recognition. V04 established that public evaluation does not constitute his motive for hero work. V05 adds a stronger ordering rule:

**Saitama can intentionally sacrifice recognition when receiving it would erase or devalue other people's meaningful hero action.**

This is not evidence that he never wants praise. The sacrifice matters precisely because the resource has value to him.

A useful current hierarchy is:

`self-authored hero action > preservation of others' deserved heroic standing > Saitama's own immediate public credit`.

That hierarchy remains provisional to V05 and should not be expanded beyond the observed conflict without later evidence.

### Social intelligence — major correction to a shallow Saitama model

The maneuver also matters for cognition. Saitama is often socially blunt, but here he:

1. detects how the crowd is beginning to reinterpret the defeated heroes;
2. predicts that a particular persona will redirect that interpretation;
3. performs the persona convincingly;
4. accepts reputational cost;
5. keeps the actual ethical motive hidden.

Therefore:

**“Saitama is socially oblivious” cannot be a global trait.**

He can be inattentive to ordinary conventions and still display sharp situational social strategy when a value he cares about is threatened.

### Genos understands the hidden action

Genos asks:

> `サイタマ先生 それでいいのですね?`
>
> `それが先生の進む道だというのなら 俺は何も口を出しません`
>
> (`image:0156`)

This is a relationship transition. Genos is not merely admiring Saitama's strength. He reads the ethical/social intent behind a false outward persona and then chooses not to expose or correct it.

His deference becomes **interpretive trust**: he can understand that Saitama is choosing a cost and respect the choice without needing to imitate or rationalize it aloud.

### Shibabawa opens a new threat horizon

The chapter closes its local disaster and then opens a radically different scale of uncertainty through Shibabawa. Her prophecy is simply:

> `地球がヤバい`
>
> (`image:0162`)

At V05 this should remain an open signal. The manga establishes a respected fortune-teller, unusual alarm, and a planetary-threat statement. It does not yet establish the cause, timing, actor, or mechanism.

## 4.5 29撃目 `B級` — advancement does not resolve institutional ambiguity

### Saitama accepts promotion for practical reasons

Saitama becomes eligible to leave C-class. His own discussion is characteristically mundane rather than prestige-driven. He notes the practical benefit of no longer dealing with C-class reporting/activity pressure and also finds the S-class/Genos versus C-class/Saitama mismatch increasingly awkward.

This does not mean rank has become his core identity. It means he can instrumentally use institutions when they reduce friction.

### Physical measurement remains anomalous

Association staff still discuss the impossibility of Saitama's entrance-exam physical results and the difficulty of reconciling them with ordinary evaluation. The institutional information problem therefore persists after several real incidents.

### Amai Mask's actual V05 institutional role

The volume must be precise here. When staff consult Amai Mask about Saitama's B-class promotion, Amai says:

> `B級昇格か…そんなの僕を通さなくたっていいよ`
>
> `C級やB級なんてどうなったって構わない`
>
> (`image:0177`)

He then clarifies that the management he wants influence over concerns the more socially influential **A-class and above** heroes. His stated concern is that heroes in the same organization behaving poorly create reputational harm.

Therefore V05 does **not** yet justify describing him as the active gatekeeper of every lower-class promotion. What it establishes is:

- A-class rank 1 status;
- formal/informal consultation by the Association;
- concern with public-facing quality control;
- desire for influence over A-class-and-above hero management;
- near indifference to C/B-class composition.

This aligns with his earlier ideology: heroism is partly an institution of public reassurance and image management.

### Saitama enters B-class at rank 101

The promotion changes Saitama's institutional location but not the accumulated evidence about self-authorship. Rank is now useful, real, and materially consequential; it still does not define his underlying hero motive.

## 4.6 番外編 `買えないモノ` — heroism has a monthly paycheck and Saitama is still broke

This bonus is first-class modeling evidence, not disposable comedy.

### The compensation system becomes explicit

The narrator explains:

- Hero Association compensation is simple and rank-linked;
- a rank-corresponding amount is paid monthly;
- activity uses a point system;
- the explicit point criteria are not publicly disclosed and are understood by Association assessors;
- self-reports and civilian eyewitness testimony are considered;
- achievements often fail to map cleanly into rank;
- C-class heroes can therefore be poorly paid.

The text explicitly says that even if the lowest C-class heroes receive low wages, they are not in a strong position to complain because the Association is framed as a nonprofit organization founded around justice and charity (`images:0189-0190`).

This is the most concrete institutional-economy evidence so far.

### Poverty is not an abstract running joke

Saitama's account is nearly empty. He counts coins in front of a vending machine and cannot buy the drink that catches his attention. The bonus makes previous sale/food/money activation materially grounded: scarcity affects his ordinary choices.

This should be modeled as **real financial constraint at the V05 boundary**, not merely a comic preference for bargains.

### Refusing the child's money

After Saitama helps a child, the child offers his allowance in thanks. Saitama gives it back:

> `お前にやる`
>
> `もっと大事に使え`
>
> (`image:0206`)

His private reaction is essentially disbelief that he looks poor enough to invite charity.

This is a valuable low-stakes ethics atom. He is actually short of money and actually wanted a cheap drink, yet he refuses to monetize a child's gratitude.

The action is consistent with V05's recognition hierarchy: desired resources do not automatically override what he considers an inappropriate way of obtaining them.

### Genos notices without being told

The final relationship beat is exceptionally useful for reconstruction. Genos has noticed Saitama looking at the vending-machine drink on an earlier outing. He later says he ordered it because he saw Saitama paying attention to it. He buys a whole box.

This demonstrates a specific care style:

`observe small preference -> remember it -> solve practically -> overprovide slightly`.

Saitama is pleased to see the desired product and then discovers that it tastes bad. Genos calmly notes the long shelf life and the problem of finishing the box.

The joke preserves rather than cancels the evidence. Their domestic relationship now includes **attentive mundane preference tracking**, not only combat apprenticeship and cohabitation.

# 5. Character-modeling synthesis at V05

## Saitama

### STRENGTHEN — prosocial reputation management
V05 proves he can manipulate public narrative intentionally and accept personal reputational cost to preserve the perceived value of other heroes' actions.

### REVISE / STRENGTHEN — recognition hierarchy
Recognition remains wanted. It is now demonstrably subordinate to at least one other value: preventing his own overwhelming success from erasing the meaning of others' costly resistance.

### STRENGTHEN — social cognition
He is not globally socially oblivious. Situational social strategy can become acute when he detects a morally salient narrative distortion.

### STRENGTHEN — ordinary material constraint
Saitama's bargain sensitivity sits inside genuine scarcity. The bonus supplies direct money evidence.

### STRENGTHEN — refusal to exploit gratitude
Even under scarcity, he refuses a child's allowance after helping him.

### Readiness
**Remain `strong`, substantially strengthened within tier.** V05 adds reputation sacrifice, strategic self-presentation, money scarcity, child interaction, and mundane desire. Major remaining gaps still include grief/loss, durable peer friendship breadth, and failure in a domain where his physical power cannot solve the central problem.

## Genos

### STRENGTHEN strongly — civilian protectiveness
The child-shield sequence is decisive independent-duty evidence.

### STRENGTHEN — bodily sacrifice
Catastrophic damage is accepted to preserve a civilian, not merely to pursue revenge or impress Saitama.

### STRENGTHEN — interpretive trust in Saitama
Genos recognizes Saitama's deliberate reputational loss and chooses not to interfere.

### STRENGTHEN — mundane attentiveness
He notices an unspoken trivial preference, remembers it, and purchases the item later.

### Readiness
**Remain `moderate`, now very near the strong threshold.** Stress, voice, ethical action, and Saitama-relationship evidence are excellent. Independent ordinary-life and repeated durable non-Saitama relationships remain thinner than the Saitama-centered domain.

## Mumen Rider

### Transition: `insufficient -> emerging`
V05 supplies a sustained internal policy rather than a single courageous image:

- accurate weakness assessment;
- explicit knowledge that victory is impossible;
- situational duty logic;
- repeated continued action;
- crowd relationship;
- stable earnest voice under severe stress.

Do not promote to `moderate` yet. Ordinary life, relationships, and longitudinal recurrence remain limited.

## Speed-o'-Sound Sonic

**Remain `moderate`, strengthened.** The Deep Sea King encounter adds rational retreat/environmental analysis outside Saitama rivalry, further proving that rivalry obsession is conditional rather than global.

## Amai Mask

### Transition: `insufficient -> emerging`
V03 supplied only mediated public image. V05 supplies direct normative speech and institutional behavior. His hero model emphasizes public reassurance, strength, beauty, rapid removal of evil, and management of socially influential A-class-and-above heroes. Low-stakes and relational breadth remain absent.

## Deep Sea King

**Remain `emerging`, strengthened but narratively concluded at this boundary.** V05 adds adaptation to rain, shelter terror, deliberate civilian targeting, and serial dominance over multiple heroes. No need to inflate readiness because breadth remains almost entirely domination/combat.

# 6. Relationship synthesis

## Saitama -> Genos
V05 adds no grand declaration. Instead, the relationship is increasingly legible through practical continuity after crisis. Saitama accepts Genos's presence and later the mundane gift without ceremony.

## Genos -> Saitama
The important new state is not stronger worship. It is **interpretive respect**. Genos recognizes that Saitama's public dishonesty is deliberate and ethically motivated, asks for confirmation, and then refrains from correcting the record.

## Saitama <-> Mumen Rider
Still too thin for a mature relationship. Saitama arrives after Mumen's stand and acts; no broad reciprocal state should be inferred yet.

## Hero <-> public
V05 complicates “the crowd” as an actor:

- crowd hope can overestimate capability;
- crowd gratitude can accurately recognize sacrifice;
- a single cynical speaker can redirect interpretation;
- Saitama can strategically manipulate that interpretation;
- public recognition is therefore neither reliable truth nor meaningless noise.

# 7. Longitudinal thematic consequences

## Heroism / recognition / rank / institution
V05 is a major update. Heroic value now explicitly survives certain failure states. Recognition can become a resource deliberately redistributed by a hero. Rank also becomes income and mobility, while its input criteria remain partly opaque.

## Monsterhood / personhood / body
The Deep Sea King continues the V04 Sea Folk route rather than adding a wholly new ontology. Rain-mediated body change/recovery is observed, but no later biological theory should be imported.

## Power / technique
Genos and Mumen Rider make the distinction between contribution and victory unavoidable. Power remains real and unequal; ethical value is not reducible to it.

## Technology / organization
The Association's assessment apparatus becomes more concrete: self-reports, eyewitness testimony, undisclosed point criteria, rank-linked pay, promotion processes, and consultation with Amai Mask over high-visibility hero management.

## Satire / public narrative
The physical climax is displaced by reputational interpretation. The strongest person in the scene wins instantly and then has to **lie downward** about himself to keep the social narrative from flattening everyone else.

## Visual form
The sustained rain is a functional environment:

- it materially strengthens/restores the Deep Sea King;
- it creates visual continuity across hero after hero being damaged;
- it makes Mumen Rider's small human body against the Sea King especially exposed;
- it continues through Saitama's arrival and reputational aftermath, refusing a clean visual reset at victory.

The chapter architecture repeatedly cycles `arrival -> hope -> overmatch -> damage`, then makes Saitama's one-punch victory intentionally compressed. The moral climax arrives afterward in dialogue and crowd reaction.

## Open questions

At the V05 boundary, preserve:

- What exactly will Shibabawa's `地球がヤバい` prophecy refer to?
- How durable is Saitama's willingness to sacrifice recognition when it conflicts with others' deserved standing?
- Does Genos's child-shield policy generalize across future civilian-protection situations?
- How will Amai Mask's public-image ideology shape his judgments of other heroes?
- How much does opaque assessment distort long-term rank and compensation?
- Will Mumen Rider's impossible-duty policy recur outside this exact crisis?

# 10. V05 closure judgment

V05 is analytically high-value because it gives the project something action manga often underprovide: **behavior under known failure and behavior after victory when social credit becomes morally contested**.

Mumen Rider becomes reconstructible not because he acquires power but because the text gives him explicit self-knowledge and a decision rule. Genos becomes more reconstructible because his self-sacrifice is shown protecting an anonymous child. Saitama becomes more reconstructible because his desire for recognition is tested against another value and loses—and because he proves capable of intentional public deception for a prosocial end.

The bonus then grounds all of those elevated questions in monthly pay, coins, a vending machine, a child's allowance, and a badly chosen drink.

**Canonical V05 thesis:** heroism in V05 is neither victory nor reputation. It is the set of choices characters make when those rewards are unavailable, insufficient, or ethically expensive.
