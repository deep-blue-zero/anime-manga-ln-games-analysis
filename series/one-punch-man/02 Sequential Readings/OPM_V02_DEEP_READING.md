# 2. Volume orientation

V01 established Saitama as a self-authored hero whose overwhelming success has damaged combat's ability to provide meaningful resistance. V02 asks what happens when that private identity encounters competing theories of **how people change** and then encounters a society that has created an official definition of **who counts as a hero**.

Three structures dominate the volume:

1. **Dr. Genus / House of Evolution:** improvement through engineered biological control;
2. **Hammerhead / the Paradisers:** liberation imagined as changing social distribution while refusing the discipline of self-change;
3. **Saitama:** improvement narrated as repeated chosen practice, followed by the discovery that private conviction alone does not make one's heroism socially legible.

The volume ends by introducing the Hero Association, which converts `ヒーロー` from Saitama's personal identity into a registry, occupation, ranking system, public reputation economy, and paid social institution.

The bonus story then folds the two halves together. Training-year Saitama remembers that gratitude from people he helped made otherwise empty days feel productive; repeated heroic action also becomes bodily habit. Recognition therefore enters the series not simply as vanity but as **feedback that one's chosen activity has reached another person**.

# 3. Central thesis

**V02 transforms the problem of strength into a problem of self-change and the problem of heroism into a problem of social legibility.**

Saitama's training speech is often structurally presented as a joke because its content is absurdly ordinary relative to the power it supposedly produced. But at the character level he is not joking. His governing proposition is explicit:

> `自分で変われるのが人間の強さだ!` (`OPM|V02|chapter:11|image:0075`)

The line is aimed at beings whose entire project is called the House of Evolution. The conflict is therefore not merely "exercise versus science." Saitama places value on **self-directed persistence** against a system that treats superior life as something designed, produced, ranked, and controlled from above.

The volume's second half reveals the limit of purely private self-definition. Saitama can sincerely say `趣味でヒーローをやってる者` and still be mistaken for a terrorist or Paradiser because society has no category through which to recognize his work. He eventually states the problem with unusual directness:

> `知名度が低い` (`image:0181`)

This does not cancel V01's finding that fame was not his opening motive. V02 **revises the recognition model**: Saitama does care about being known, having his actions correctly attributed, not being mistaken for a villain, and even having fans. The extra story clarifies why that can matter without reducing him to celebrity hunger: gratitude once gave him the clearest proof that his actions were useful.

## Strongest counter-reading

It would be too simple to read V02 as "hard work beats shortcuts" or "society should merely recognize Saitama." The manga itself destabilizes both formulations.

First, Genos explicitly notices that ordinary training cannot adequately explain the observed magnitude of Saitama's power. Saitama's training account is **his sincere causal model**, not automatically the final ontology of the manga. The proper V02 claim is that persistence and self-transformation define Saitama's understanding of strength, not that the text has scientifically settled the origin of his absolute capability.

Second, institutional recognition brings its own distortions. The Hero Association links legitimate hero identity to registration, ranking, popularity, donation-funded professional labor, and public visibility. V02 therefore does not simply solve Saitama's recognition problem by announcing that bureaucracy is correct. It creates a new longitudinal question: **what happens when actual heroic action and institutionally legible heroism diverge?**

# 4. Sequential close reading

## 4.1 9撃目 `進化の家` — evolution as an administered project

The volume begins by expanding Dr. Genus from distant mastermind into an ideological figure. His intelligence isolates him from ordinary humanity, and his response is not to enter human limitation but to redesign it. The House of Evolution treats life as a hierarchy to be engineered.

That matters because Saitama's origin has already presented another route out of dissatisfaction. Job-hunting Saitama did not solve his social failure by being selected, modified, or recognized by an institution. He chose an activity and repeatedly remade himself through it.

The House of Evolution therefore provides a conceptual foil before Saitama ever explains his regimen.

Genos and Saitama also arrive as a functioning pair rather than recreating their V01 introduction. Genos behaves procedurally: identify the objective, destroy the visible base, eliminate tactical uncertainty. Saitama's response to Genos's enormous blast is characteristically mundane and interpersonal rather than awed. Their asymmetry is becoming useful coordination rather than merely a joke about an unwanted disciple.

## 4.2 10撃目 `現代アート` — when explanatory hierarchy meets unclassifiable power

Beast King and Armored Gorilla arrive inside a carefully designed hierarchy of artificial beings. Their confidence depends on that system being predictive. Saitama breaks the prediction.

The result is not only physical defeat. Armored Gorilla changes behavior when the power information changes. This strengthens a V01 pattern: *One Punch Man* repeatedly asks what people do when the world falsifies their model of relative strength.

The chapter title's `現代アート` joke converts Beast King's spectacular destruction into grotesque aesthetic residue. Murata's high-detail anatomy and violent motion are not abandoned; they are made to coexist with Saitama's evaluative flatness. The formal system is already teaching the reader that visual grandeur does not guarantee equivalent subjective importance.

## 4.3 11撃目 `強さの秘訣` — sincerity inside the joke

Saitama's explanation to Genos, Dr. Genus, and Carnage Kabuto deserves to be separated into two questions.

### What Saitama believes

He tells Genos that the important thing is whether one can continue the hard training:

- `続ける事だ どんなに辛くてもな` (`image:0071`)
- 100 push-ups;
- 100 sit-ups;
- 100 squats;
- 10 km running every day (`image:0072`);
- no air conditioning in summer or winter as mental discipline (`image:0073`).

He remembers wanting to stop, continuing through pain, and eventually noticing both baldness and extraordinary strength. His climactic formulation is not a numerical power claim but a humanist one: `自分で変われるのが人間の強さだ!`.

Nothing in the scene supports a model in which Saitama is deliberately concealing a secret from Genos. When Genos insists that mere bodily training cannot explain the observed result, Saitama replies in effect that there is nothing else to tell him: `んな事言われても 他に何もねーぞ` (`image:0078`).

For character modeling, this is a strong **epistemic boundary**. Saitama's autobiographical explanation is sincere even if the reader remains free to doubt whether he correctly understands the full causal mechanism.

### What Genos believes

Genos does not become intellectually submissive merely because he calls Saitama `先生`. His response is analytical disagreement:

> `サイタマ先生の強さは明らかに身体を鍛えた程度のものではない！俺はそれが知りたいのです` (`image:0078`)

This is a major refinement to the disciple model. Deference and idealization coexist with epistemic independence. Genos is willing to tell his chosen master that the explanation is inadequate.

### Carnage Kabuto and instinctive perception

Carnage Kabuto's threat perception creates another distinction: formal scientific hierarchy does not fully register Saitama, but Kabuto's instinct detects something wrong with the apparent weakling. The volume is increasingly interested in the difference among **objective capability, institutional/scientific model, public reputation, and individual perception**.

### The supermarket sale

The fight's emotional climax for Saitama is not Kabuto's transformation. It is the realization:

> `今日がスーパーの特売日じゃねーか` (`image:0088`)

The gag strongly **STRENGTHENS** the V01 low-stakes model. Objective threat magnitude remains a poor predictor of Saitama's affect. Ordinary consumer life contains deadlines and scarcity that his combat life usually cannot impose. Kabuto can be ended at once; a missed sale cannot be punched back into existence.

## 4.4 12撃目 `桃源団` — grievance, freedom, and the first registered hero

Hammerhead's speech begins with questions that are not inherently absurd:

- why must people work;
- why must money mediate food;
- whether people are genuinely free when bound to labor;
- why the wealthy flourish while the poor suffer.

The satire becomes sharper because the critique is attached to a movement whose practical program is `我々は断固働きたくない` and whose proposed utopia is a society where only those who want to work do so while everyone else is supported. Stolen battle suits then turn critique into coercive destruction.

The manga does not need the reader to conclude that every complaint about labor is false. Its target is the distance between universal rhetoric and Hammerhead's self-serving behavioral policy.

### Mumen Rider

Mumen Rider's entrance is the first explicit collision between **heroic legitimacy and capability insufficiency**. Civilians respond to his arrival with relief because they recognize him as a hero. He announces himself as `正義の自転車乗り 無免ライダー参上!` (`image:0100`) and charges despite a severe power mismatch.

He loses immediately.

That failure is analytically productive. V02 is already separating at least three variables:

- willingness to confront danger;
- actual combat capability;
- public/institutional recognition as a hero.

Mumen Rider possesses the first and third while lacking enough of the second for this encounter. Saitama possesses overwhelming capability and a strong self-authored hero practice while lacking the third.

### Saitama's first public-image trigger

Watching the news, Saitama initially treats the Paradisers as an uninteresting incident. That changes when their shaved heads cause public warnings about bald men. He realizes:

> `これじゃ俺とかぶってんじゃねーか`
>
> `せっかくヒーローやってても こんな真似されたら俺も悪者扱いされるじゃないか` (`image:0106`)

This is the first explicit V02 recognition delta. His concern is initially not abstract fame; it is **misclassification of his chosen identity**. Public narrative can erase the ethical difference between him and the Paradisers simply because the visible sign is wrong.

## 4.5 13撃目 `スピード` — Sonic and professionalized violence

Speed-o'-Sound Sonic introduces another relation among skill, occupation, and self-conception. He is not a monster and does not need a moral crusade. He treats lethal force as professional competence and takes pleasure in the opportunity to encounter stronger opposition.

Against the Paradisers, his speed is overwhelming and his affect is controlled. He speculates about accidentally leaving a pile of corpses because the suits may make restraint difficult. Hammerhead attracts interest because he might offer resistance.

This is an important future character-model baseline:

- high confidence in specialized skill;
- low hesitation around killing;
- pride in being able to read/control combat;
- stimulation from worthy opposition;
- occupational self-conception that normalizes lethal technique.

The battle suits simultaneously introduce an external power-augmentation system. Ordinary humans become dangerous through equipment, but the equipment belongs to an unseen organization capable of tracking performance data. Technological power already implies institutional ownership and surveillance.

## 4.6 14撃目 `お前など知らん` — alternate selves, mercy, and misrecognition

This chapter is one of V02's densest character sections.

### Saitama openly admits a personal motive

When Hammerhead assumes Saitama wants to join the Paradisers, Saitama answers:

> `俺は趣味でヒーローをやってる者だが 一身上の都合によりお前らをぶっ潰すわ` (`image:0149`)

The line is ethically revealing because he does not beautify his motive. He is stopping dangerous people, but the trigger is also personal: they are damaging the public legibility of his hero identity.

### Curiosity survives overwhelming strength

The battle suit briefly interests him. After discovering Hammerhead survives a punch, Saitama says he had been a little excited by the suit (`image:0151`). The same volume therefore preserves the V01 distinction between generalized numbness and **conditional anticipation of friction**.

### Mercy and self-recognition

After defeat, Hammerhead pleads that he only did this because he did not want to work and promises to stop. Saitama simply tells him:

> `行け` (`image:0158`)

The next page gives the reason more depth than simple softness:

> `社会への失望か？ 俺も一歩間違えばアイツみたいになってたのかな` (`image:0159`)

This directly links Hammerhead to job-hunting Saitama. V01's origin can now be read as a branch point. Both men experience alienation from ordinary work and social routes; Saitama converts that failure into a difficult self-authored project, while Hammerhead converts grievance into entitlement and coercion. Saitama can recognize enough of himself in Hammerhead to refuse annihilation after surrender.

This is strong evidence against modeling Saitama's ethics as indiscriminate elimination of anyone labeled hostile.

### Sonic misclassifies Saitama

Sonic initially interprets the bald hero through the visible Paradiser category. Saitama's attempt to correct him becomes more revealing than the mistake itself. He protests that he is the one who has been doing hero work and even says he has been `割と活躍してる` (`image:0164`). Sonic's response supplies the chapter title:

> `お前など知らん`

The line hits an already developing insecurity: achievement without public attribution.

### Saitama reads Sonic correctly

Sonic dresses his decision to kill Saitama in professional pride and secrecy. Saitama replies:

> `嘘つけ お前はただ自分の技を試したいだけだろ？ 無邪気な笑顔みりゃわかるぜ` (`image:0167`)

Saitama can therefore be socially perceptive when the motive is behaviorally legible. He is not simply obtuse about people. Here he strips self-mythologizing language from Sonic in the same way the manga strips grandeur from battles.

### Restraint is intentional

The notorious accidental `チェックメイト` collision matters behaviorally. Saitama immediately apologizes and explains that he was trying to stop short: `寸止めしようと思ったら勢いでぶつかっただけ` (`image:0176`). The joke requires the reader to understand that Saitama **was attempting nonlethal restraint** against a human opponent.

When Sonic leaves vowing to return and kill him, Saitama's response is not reciprocal hatred:

> `ああ! 頑張れよ!` (`image:0179`)

The mismatch establishes an early relationship asymmetry. Sonic interprets humiliation as a rivalry requiring restoration; Saitama interprets the vow almost like an eccentric personal project.

### Hammerhead's coda

The battle-suit organization intercepts Hammerhead after Saitama releases him. He survives again and concludes:

> `母ちゃん 俺…働くよ` (`image:0170`)

The joke is blunt, but the state transition matters. Hammerhead changes behavioral policy after the collapse of both ideological theater and technological borrowed power. Like Saitama, he is allowed to **change himself** rather than being frozen as the role in which he entered the volume.

## 4.7 15撃目 `趣味と仕事` — private hero becomes prospective professional

After Sonic, Saitama finally verbalizes the recognition problem as a major concern.

He has been doing hero work for roughly three years, has defeated many monsters, and yet has rarely seen other heroes and has not become known. He explicitly wonders why more people do not know his existence and says it would not be strange to have more fans (`images:0181-0183`).

This requires a revision to the V01 state:

**V01:** recognition was not established as the dominant motive for hero activity.

**V02:** recognition is now established as a real desire, especially accurate attribution, ordinary acknowledgment, and social visibility. It still does not replace the evidence that heroism began as self-authored meaning rather than a fame project.

### The Hero Association

Genos provides the institutional explanation. The `ヒーロー名簿` is not just a list. Registration authorizes the official title `ヒーロー`; registered heroes can be paid from donated funds according to activity; they are ranked by ability/popularity; some have fan clubs; and unregistered independent activity is not socially recognized as legitimate hero work (`image:0184`).

This creates a major ontological distinction for the project:

> **heroic action != self-identification as hero != registered professional hero != public reputation**

Saitama has possessed the first two but not the latter two.

The origin story of the Association intensifies the irony. Agoni founded it after his grandson was saved from a monster by a passing man about three years earlier (`image:0185`). V01 has already shown pre-hero Saitama saving a visually distinctive child during the Crablante incident three years earlier. At the V02 boundary, the text strongly invites the inference that **Saitama's unrecognized act helped produce the institution that now defines him as unrecognized**. This is an `IR`, not merely a later retrospective claim.

Saitama's final decision is pragmatic and relational:

> `登録しようぜ! 一緒に登録してくれたら弟子にしてやるから!` (`image:0186`)

The master-disciple relation becomes more reciprocal. Saitama now uses the role Genos wanted as leverage to recruit Genos into an institutional step Saitama wants. Genos immediately agrees: `いきましょう!` (`image:0187`).

The chapter title `趣味と仕事` therefore names a genuine transition. Saitama's heroism is not ceasing to be self-authored; it is about to enter a professional social system.

## 4.8 番外編 `じぶんみがき` — practice turns chosen identity into embodied habit

The bonus story is not optional character fluff. It supplies some of the strongest evidence in the volume about why early hero activity mattered to Saitama.

Training-year Saitama has already defeated more than ten monsters. Receiving a bag of sweets from an elderly shopkeeper prompts reflection. He says he is not doing this *in order to be thanked*, but gratitude makes it easy to feel that he has been useful:

> `人の役に立ったと分かりやすく実感できるのは 感謝された時だけだ` (`image:0189`)

He further reflects that the happiness/stimulation of that recognition gives him fulfillment and becomes fuel for the next fight.

This is the cleanest bridge between V01's meaning problem and V02's recognition problem. **Recognition is valuable when it verifies relational consequence.** The evidence does not require reducing Saitama to a desire for applause.

The story then shows day 300 of training. His body is exhausted, but repeated movements have become more efficient and reflexive (`images:0190-0194`). He saves a child and dog from an oncoming vehicle almost automatically. The narration observes that he has become able to move in accordance with his image of a hero (`image:0194`).

This is an important model of identity formation:

> **Saitama first chooses "hero" as a project; repeated action then trains the role into perception, reflex, and body.**

His later power cannot be reduced to a moral allegory, but the text makes habitual practice part of his character formation independently of the unresolved power mechanism.

The final tooth gag literalizes the title's metaphor. Saitama exceeds his limit in battle, loses a bad tooth, and the coda states `磨かなければ すぐ腐る` (`image:0201`). `じぶんみがき` therefore means both self-improvement and literal polishing/brushing. The joke does not cancel the preceding discipline; it compresses it into the volume's governing principle that maintenance and practice are continuous rather than one-time transformations.

# 5. Saitama state — V02 delta

## Meaning and desire

**STRENGTHEN:** meaningful engagement remains central.

**REVISE:** recognition is no longer merely OPEN. V02 establishes a genuine desire for social legibility and acknowledgment. The evidence separates at least three components:

1. not being mistaken for a villain (`image:0106`);
2. having one's actual hero activity known/credited (`images:0164, 0181-0183`);
3. gratitude/fans as evidence that one's actions matter to other people (`images:0182, 0189`).

This is not yet evidence that rank, wealth, or celebrity prestige is his dominant motive.

## Self-conception

His self-authored formula remains stable: `趣味でヒーローをやってる者`.

V02 adds the proposition that human strength lies in the capacity for self-change. His own history is understood as deliberate persistence rather than special selection.

## Ethics

New evidence:

- spares Hammerhead after surrender;
- recognizes social alienation as something that could have redirected his own life;
- attempts nonlethal restraint against Sonic;
- does not answer Sonic's death vow with equivalent hostility;
- early training extra shows gratitude and helping others as meaningful reinforcement.

**STRENGTHEN:** direct intervention is not identical to indiscriminate violence. Human antagonists are evaluated through current behavior and surrender state.

## Perception

New evidence:

- accurately identifies Sonic's concealed desire to test his techniques;
- sees Hammerhead as a plausible failed-alternate self;
- remains ignorant of major public institutions surrounding heroism;
- underestimates how completely lack of registration shapes public interpretation.

## Ordinary life

**STRENGTHEN:** the supermarket sale is one of his strongest emotional activators in the volume. Consumer deadlines can matter more to him than extreme physical danger because they impose consequences his strength cannot retroactively erase.

## Teacher role

Saitama gives Genos the training explanation sincerely, tolerates disagreement, and eventually makes apprenticeship explicit as part of a bargain to register together. The role is moving from accidental label to lightly instrumentalized social commitment.

## Japanese voice

V02 preserves the context-sensitive colloquial system established in V01 and expands it:

- didactic but still plain speech when explaining training;
- self-deprecating/comic directness in ordinary concerns;
- rough identity-defense when Paradisers threaten his reputation;
- strikingly economical mercy (`行け`);
- reflective internal language around Hammerhead;
- casual encouragement toward Sonic after a hostile encounter.

He can be serious without becoming formally grandiose.

# 6. Ensemble character deltas

## Genos

### STRENGTHEN — analytical independence

Genos's deference to Saitama does not erase skepticism. He openly rejects the training regimen as an adequate causal explanation for Saitama's observed power. This is important protection against a simplistic "worshipful disciple" model.

### STRENGTHEN — lethal instrumentalism

His willingness to solve threats by destruction remains high. When Saitama mentions Sonic, Genos casually offers to eliminate him if he is troubling his teacher (`image:0180`). The offer is framed as practical assistance, not emotional rage.

### New institutional role

Genos knows about the Hero Association and becomes the information source that converts Saitama's recognition problem into an actionable plan. He agrees to register together.

### Relationship movement

The disciple relation becomes reciprocally acknowledged enough for Saitama to offer it explicitly. Genos remains more invested in the hierarchy, but Saitama is no longer merely tolerating an unwanted label.

## Mumen Rider

First strong baseline:

- self-defines explicitly through justice;
- recognized by civilians as a legitimate hero;
- approaches a superior threat despite obvious danger;
- loses immediately, making courage and capability analytically separable.

Readiness remains low because evidence is confined to a single high-stakes context.

## Dr. Genus

V02 gives a coherent ideological arc:

- extraordinary intelligence produces alienation and a hierarchical view of humanity;
- chooses artificial evolution as solution;
- interprets living beings through designed advancement;
- Saitama's inexplicable result destabilizes the framework;
- after the House of Evolution collapses, he is capable of abandoning/reconsidering the project rather than preserving it at any cost.

This is enough for an **emerging**, still narrow model.

## Carnage Kabuto

- strong predatory confidence;
- unusually sensitive instinctive detection of Saitama's danger;
- accepts violence as proof/contest;
- mania escalates after rational caution fails to resolve the anomaly.

Still too encounter-bound for substantial reconstruction.

## Hammerhead

V02 provides a compact but unusually complete one-volume transition:

`anti-work grievance leader -> empowered coercive revolutionary -> defeated fugitive -> surrendering human -> target of the Organization -> resolution to work`

His rhetoric alternates between collective `我々` and plainly personal motive. The ideological program is partially a grand language for an individual aversion to labor. Defeat strips away the collective persona.

His final state should not be treated as a mature conversion yet, but it demonstrates behavioral plasticity.

## Speed-o'-Sound Sonic

Initial model:

- professionalized lethal violence;
- pride in specialized skill and secrecy;
- stimulation from worthy opponents;
- attempts to convert desire to test himself into a professional/pride justification;
- severe status response when Saitama reads/neutralizes him;
- humiliation becomes self-declared rivalry.

His rivalry is strongly asymmetric at this boundary.

# 7. Heroism, rank, recognition, and institutions

V02 is the first major institutional foundation volume.

## Hero ontology now has four layers

1. **heroic behavior** — acts that protect/help others;
2. **self-authored hero identity** — Saitama before registration;
3. **registered professional identity** — Hero Association list;
4. **public reputation** — what civilians actually know/believe.

These layers can diverge sharply.

## Mumen Rider / Saitama inversion

Mumen Rider has public legitimacy and limited power in this encounter. Saitama has overwhelming power and little public legitimacy. Neither fact alone settles what heroism means.

## Association design

The Association introduces:

- qualification testing;
- registry-based official hero status;
- donation-funded compensation;
- performance/ranking systems;
- popularity ranking;
- fan culture;
- public dependence on registry status for credibility.

This immediately makes future rank evidence ethically ambiguous. Rank may measure some combination of ability, institutional record, public narrative, and organizational procedures rather than transparent moral worth.

## Founding irony

At the V02 publication boundary, the Agoni-grandson story strongly points back to V01's Crablante rescue. If so, the institution that now excludes unregistered Saitama from public recognition was indirectly caused by Saitama's own pre-institutional heroism. That is a high-confidence structural inference, not yet an excuse to import later institutional judgments backward.

# 8. Monsterhood, personhood, and body

V02 broadens the source categories.

### Scientific modification

The House of Evolution creates artificial beings through scientific intervention. Monster-like body does not itself supply a complete moral or ontological category; Armored Gorilla can surrender, communicate, and revise behavior.

### Human technological amplification

The Paradisers remain human while battle suits give them destructive capability. Power enhancement therefore does not automatically equal monster status.

### Obsession/habit transformation

The bonus story's `電気のヒモの化身` is explicitly a former human produced through absurdly excessive shadowboxing with a room-light pull cord. Even as comedy, it establishes that "becoming a monster" can emerge from a human practice/obsession rather than only species biology or laboratory engineering.

### Saitama's body

The training extra treats adaptation as gradual and embodied: repetition changes reflex, performance, and the ease with which his chosen hero image becomes action. This evidence belongs to character formation even if the final source of his extreme power remains unresolved.

# 9. Power, technique, and explanatory systems

## Saitama's regimen: TF about belief, OPEN about ultimate causation

The regimen is explicit textual fact. Saitama's belief in its causal importance is explicit textual fact. The proposition "this ordinary regimen fully explains his current power" remains an open explanatory hypothesis at V02 because Genos explicitly contests it and the capability gap is intentionally disproportionate.

This distinction is essential for later modeling:

- **what Saitama believes:** high confidence;
- **what objectively caused his power:** OPEN at V02.

## Self-directed versus administered evolution

Saitama's `自分で変われる` statement is philosophically stronger than a power-scaling claim. It opposes Genus's hierarchy of designed life with the capacity of ordinary humans to transform through agency.

## Battle suits

The Paradiser suits demonstrate rapid external amplification and connect strength to a hidden technological supplier. The unseen organization records performance data, indicating that distributed equipment can also be a surveillance/testing system.

## Sonic

Sonic supplies a specialized technique model: extreme speed, afterimage-based perception disruption, and high pride in trained skill. Saitama's inability to value the spectacle at Sonic's intended scale repeats the series' power/meaning mismatch.

# 10. Humor, satire, and genre mechanics

V02 repeatedly pairs sincere thematic material with comic puncture.

### The training joke

The regimen is funny because it is recognizable exercise presented as the secret behind impossible power. But the discipline and Saitama's belief in self-change are sincere. Treating the entire sequence as "just a gag" discards character evidence.

### Supermarket sale

Carnage Kabuto represents maximum laboratory spectacle; the sale provides the actual deadline. The joke does not mean Saitama is shallow. It demonstrates that ordinary life contains real constraints even for a person physically beyond conventional threat.

### Hammerhead

Political-economic language is attached to an actor who finally admits `俺はただ働きたくなかっただけ`. The satire targets the gap between universalized rhetoric and personal motive, not necessarily every premise of the labor critique.

### Sonic

A highly stylized ninja speed sequence ends in an accidental groin collision because Saitama was trying to stop short. Genre prestige and bodily slapstick occupy the same formal space.

### Hero Association

## 12.5 Training montage as bodily continuity

The bonus story gives repeated images of exhaustion, running, reflex, rescue, injury, and continued movement. This makes "training" visually cumulative rather than a single explanatory speech.
