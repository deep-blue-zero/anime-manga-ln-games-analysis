## 2. Orientation and prospective thesis

V37 independently establishes a volume about appraisal under institutional strain. The Hero Association recruits replacements because it cannot control or retain exceptional heroes; Axel and Raiden judge the institution rather than accept its judgment. Genos seeks measurable progress but abandons a superior offer when Saitama will not accompany him. A gambling scheme falsifies ranks, threat levels and outcomes, while Mumen Rider correctly recognizes courage without relying on any of those labels. Amai Mask is investigated through body, history and conduct without a settled verdict. Nichirin demonstrates precision that ordinary perception misses, and Atomic Samurai learns that strength has more than one scale—only to misapply that openness by treating King's every failure as proof of invisible mastery.

The stable prospective thesis is therefore bounded: **value in V37 is real but no single ranking, reputation, bodily sign, institutional narrative or expert test can reliably contain it. Recognition becomes ethical when it remains answerable to conduct and revisable evidence; it becomes exploitative or comic when the evaluator's prior model makes every result self-confirming.** The volume does not argue that measurement is useless. Genos's upgrade, Nichirin's technique, Mumen's judgment and Child Emperor's investigation all require careful discrimination. It argues that measurement fails when power, money, fear or reputation pre-decides what observers are allowed to see.

## 3. Strongest counter-reading

The strongest counter-reading is that V37 is primarily a transitional ensemble inventory before the Neo Heroes arc: the Association recruits, familiar heroes receive updates, unresolved characters re-enter, and the King/Atomic sequence is an extended gag. On this account, `値打ち` and `試し斬り` are local chapter motifs rather than a volume-wide argument.

That reading captures the volume's bridge function and guards against turning every scene into a single abstraction. It is insufficient, however, because independent scenes repeatedly hinge on who is permitted to appraise whom and by what evidence: scouts evaluate outsiders who reverse the evaluation; Saitama cannot give Genos useful feedback; gambling personnel manufacture classifications; Mumen disregards false labels; investigators withhold judgment on Amai Mask; Nichirin contrasts exact cutting with spectacle; Atomic tests King and produces a false conclusion. The volume is transitional **through** a repeated crisis of evaluation, not merely adjacent to it.

## 4. Sequential close reading — incremental primary observations

### Images 0001–0008 — cover and frontmatter

The frontmatter makes recruitment and institutional presentation explicit before the story begins, but its visual emphasis remains character-specific. Genos's cover lunge points toward an untroubled Saitama, while the title art pairs Atomic Samurai with the apple that will likely organize a later test or evaluation. The cast/recap pages preserve the entering V36 asymmetry: Saitama's local importance is visible to the reader while the institution remains compromised by concealed conduct. ONE's note about accumulating information and the difficulty of discarding it is recorded as paratext only; it is not used as evidence for an in-story mental state.

### Images 0009–0012 — chapter 189 opens with a recruitment commercial

- 0009 (TF/VF/SF): chapter 189 opens under `スカウト`, with a low-angle Saitama title image.
- 0010 (TF/IR): Tatsumaki receives or considers a request connected to a commercial and new-hero recruitment, dismissing it as dull. The page also recalls the recent proposition that someone unable to beat even a Wolf-level threat can change; the exact speaker/time relation will be checked in the language audit rather than overclaimed now.
- 0011 (TF/VF): at the commercial shoot, the director frames Tatsumaki's appearance as an opportunity to communicate what a hero is to applicants. The intended pitch stresses a harsh profession lived beside death.
- 0012 (TF/VF/IR): asked to display the strength expected of an S-Class representative, Tatsumaki produces a massive tornado and alarms the staff. The gag converts a controlled recruitment message into an uncontrolled proof of force: she answers the institution's demand literally, but at a scale its publicity apparatus cannot comfortably absorb.

This opening independently establishes recruitment, evaluation and image-management concerns without relying on the quarantined V1 thesis.

### Images 0013–0020 — a successful advertisement masks an internal control crisis

Tatsumaki's finished commercial turns her into flirtatious recruitment spectacle (0013–0015), to the delight of the producer and to the visible attention of Axel. An Association scout then approaches him directly (0015–0017). The executive-room cross-cut explains why. Managers praise the commercial's image benefit and McCoy's handling of incident reporting, yet their governing concern is that Metal Knight and Tatsumaki are too independently powerful to control (0018–0020). Metal Knight's trust has fallen after robot failure and questioned construction integrity; Tatsumaki's conduct likewise cannot be restrained. The executives therefore discuss tighter surveillance and replacement personnel. Recruitment is not simply growth: it is an institutional hedge against dependence on exceptional agents.

### Images 0021–0028 — Axel refuses incorporation

The scout argues that constant monster incidents, unstable S-Class availability and increasing casualties below A-Class require urgent reinforcement (0021–0022). Axel identifies the premise beneath that language: people are being treated as replenishable consumables. He leads the 53-member civilian volunteer group Hunters, which has sustained public support and real disaster-response losses; five members died in a prior Demon-level engagement, leaving 48 (0023–0025). The Association offers him an S-Class position, lower ranks for his people, equipment, medical support and greater rewards (0025–0026). He refuses because the institution surrounds valuable people while serving moneyed interests, devotes resources to its wealthy shelter, stations numerous heroes there, and no longer acts for ordinary people (0026–0028). His rejection distinguishes material capacity from legitimate purpose: the Association can equip heroism without earning its allegiance.

Before the scout leaves, Axel says the Association will not last and advises her to find another employer while she can (0028). His warning also establishes that outside actors possess information about the institution's deterioration.

### Images 0029–0036 — Raiden's prior commitment and the rival organization

The second recruitment target is undefeated super-sumo yokozuna Raiden, introduced while training after roughly seven hundred consecutive wins (0029–0030). A recruiter argues that an age of monsters requires strength beyond ordinary human intelligence and that police and military can preserve order but few people can confront monsters directly; installing Raiden as a new S-Class hero would lift morale (0031). Raiden refuses even a special reward because he has already made a commitment: a new hero organization will soon raise its banner (0032). The Association's leaders panic on learning both that the Hunters have been recruited and that sponsor companies intend to transfer; Axel reports that several S-Class heroes have also been approached (0033–0034).

The chapter ends on recruitment as an impending institutional schism. A separate scene places Genos with Dr. Kuseno: Kuseno notes Genos's stated distrust of the Hero Association and asks what he will do, while Genos begins an answer (0034–0035). The cut to Saitama spitting out his drink at Tatsumaki's commercial postpones that answer and restores comic scale without dissolving the choice (0035). The sparse final page's insurance gag treats Tatsumaki's commercial damage as a consumer problem (0036), converting the Association's idealized appeal into collateral-cost satire.

### Images 0037–0041 — Saitama's uncredentialed appraisal

Chapter 190 opens on Genos (0037), then cuts to a long-haired combatant interrogating a surviving Monster Association remnant about unusual beings and drawing a crude image of Manako before killing the target (0038–0040). The hunter treats rare contact with the unknown entity as urgent and worries that other heroes may find it first. In a thought-image, however, Saitama evaluates the hunter without rank language: the person's physical basis is poor, but a capable teacher might still make use of him (0040–0041). The sequence juxtaposes violent expertise with teachability. Saitama's appraisal is neither awe nor condemnation; he notices a possible student where the hunter is preoccupied with specimens and competition.

### Images 0042–0046 — Tanktop Master's group rebuilds by reciprocity

Tanktop Master's rehabilitation has restored and further strengthened him, although the special compression/extension effect associated with his hellish treatment has disappeared (0042–0043). His regular meeting is thin because many members quit after seeing the scale of the Monster Association battle. Those who remain reject the idea that their master fought without purpose: they stay out of gratitude and describe a new `tanktop breath` (0043–0044). Three very low-ranked C-Class applicants then ask to join after being moved by his battlefield conduct (0044–0045). The group grows through witnessed service rather than advertising, a direct countermodel to the Association's top-down recruitment campaign.

### Images 0046–0048 — Child Emperor buys truth from a transformed enemy

A chick-costumed Phoenix Man encounters Child Emperor and explains his reduced form and tentative social rehabilitation through charitable activity (0046–0047). Child Emperor does not treat that claim as absolution. He identifies the meeting's purpose: Phoenix Man previously exposed the Association's dark side, so the boy demands every source and fact he knows (0047–0048). The scene makes adversarial testimony usable without confusing it with trust.

### Images 0049–0050 — Darkshine's body is ahead of his will

Superalloy Darkshine can again move enormous weight, but the next-battle prospect revives fear. He concludes that he can no longer face forward (0049–0050). Physical restoration is therefore explicitly non-equivalent to psychological readiness.

### Images 0051–0064 — low-rank collective action and Genos's decisive force

The Forest Tribe overruns Y-City, seals roads and buildings, and regenerates lost greenery from human corpses; the mass is initially rated Tiger while its king later receives Dragon (0051–0052, 0058–0059). A cluster of low-ranked heroes attacks despite poor individual matchup: Dynamite Man (C221), Techno? Man (A11), Ecolo Grandpa (C179), Hikikomori Samurai (B39) and others combine explosives, smoke, restraints, blades and a Tanktop tackle (0053–0057). They are quickly entangled, yet their coordinated resistance creates time and visibly resists passivity.

Forest King advances by treating people as fertilizer (0058–0059). Genos arrives, burns away the mass, judges his firepower usable even against Dragon-level opposition, and asks to spar with Saitama before confronting the king (0060–0062). His declaration that he will eliminate the target is followed by his upgraded form (0062–0063). Back at Saitama's replacement room, Saitama casually notices the new parts (0064). The chapter thus closes its `update` motif on unequal kinds of revision: hardware, institutions, social roles, teams and damaged resolve do not improve by the same mechanism.

### Images 0065–0072 — chapter 191 opens on renewed master-disciple testing

Saitama has chosen another very small room after his prior home was destroyed. Genos suggests the Association provide a larger one and immediately proposes bringing his own belongings the next day; Saitama repeatedly stops him and says cohabitation is impossible because the space is even smaller than before (0065–0067). Genos insists that a place to sit suffices because, as disciple, observing Saitama's daily life should reveal training—an assumption Saitama doubts (0067).

Genos then asks for another bout. Saitama agrees to go somewhere wide, and they travel through the ruined outskirts where monsters are now easier to find than wolves (0068–0070). Genos formally requests the match and activates a luminous electrical core identified as `雷光核` (0069–0072). The visual escalation is Genos's; Saitama's role remains permissive and observant rather than performative.

### Images 0073–0084 — a power test becomes an attention crisis

Genos rushes Saitama with the new core blazing. Saitama first shades his eyes and calls it dazzling, then stops or diverts Genos's punch with a tiny hand movement and casually avoids the ensuing barrage (0073–0078). His question `やらないのか?` after the dust clears implies that Genos's spectacular attack did not register to him as the anticipated exchange (0078). An animal on a rock provides a scale-breaking visual beat amid the devastation (0079).

Genos abandons the exercise, saying it will only dirty Saitama's clothes, and asks for advice instead (0080). Asked whether he has become stronger, Saitama notices that parts have changed but cannot produce the masterly encouragement he thinks the moment demands; he falls back on saying that, whatever the answer about strength, Genos was dazzling (0080–0082). The remark is truthful at the sensory level but evades the evaluative question.

Genos then supplies a harsher meaning: Saitama has no interest in his `strength`, was not even looking at him and does not hold him within his field of concern (0083–0084). That is Genos's pessimistic internal attribution, not an objective narrative statement about Saitama's care. Saitama did observe the changed parts, agree to the bout and try to respond, but his overwhelming power and poor emotional articulation leave Genos without usable recognition. The sequence creates a real relational failure without requiring indifference as its cause.

### Images 0085–0096 — Genos turns stagnation into an institutional choice

Two months after becoming Saitama's disciple, Genos feels that neither his own gradual improvement nor Saitama's perspective supplies evidence that he is closing the gap; he cannot ignore the resulting stagnation (0085). He recalls Saitama's earlier task of reaching the Hero Association's top ten but now judges that route a low-level dead end. His proposed remedy is environmental change: the soon-to-launch Neo Heroes (0086).

Genos outlines the rival institution's case. The Hero Association, founded four years earlier through Agoni's advocacy, faces growing doubt about disaster response and management; excessive collateral damage, donation misuse and other scandals are beginning to leak (0087). Its image campaign may delay reputational collapse, but an expected scandal could be fatal (0088). The Neo Heroes offer aggressive headhunting, stronger anti-monster systems and better treatment; their spokesman is named Scoutman (0088–0089). Against the Association's roughly six hundred registered professionals, Neo Heroes candidates already exceed two thousand, and they are courting strong professionals up through S-Class while potentially bringing previously unknown capable people into public action (0089–0090).

Saitama asks whether Genos is really seeking stronger opponents for revenge (0090). Genos names the Mad Cyborg that took everything from him four years earlier. The Hero Association possesses little information, although the incident remains recorded and the target is designated a mysterious evil being; Saitama raises the possibility that someone, perhaps even he, already destroyed it (0091). Genos reasons that an opponent on that scale would have remained in Saitama's memory, while Saitama admits that he hardly remembers the monsters he has defeated (0092). The exchange does not prove the target lives; it exposes an evidentiary mismatch between Genos's identity-defining search and Saitama's undifferentiated victories.

If he finds the Mad Cyborg, Genos says, he wants to win with certainty; that aim is why he is considering transfer (0092–0093). Saitama answers `そうか まあ頑張れよ`—an acknowledgment and loose encouragement, not a substantive evaluation (0094). Genos then asks whether Saitama would transfer too, reporting that the Neo Heroes want him and would likely offer high treatment because the Association has failed to value him adequately (0094–0095). Saitama declines: `俺はいいわ 面倒くせえから`—roughly, “I'm good; it'd be a hassle” (0096). He does not oppose Genos's move, but he refuses to make institutional recognition his own project. Genos seeks a setting that can measure, test and value strength; Saitama remains largely uninterested in organizational correction even when the complaint about his undervaluation is accurate.

### Images 0097–0098 — Genos chooses the relationship over the transfer

Genos immediately answers `じゃあ俺もやめときます`—“Then I'll drop it too” (0097). His proposed institutional turn was not independent of Saitama: once Saitama declines to move, Genos abandons the idea rather than accept the promised tests, status and treatment alone. This does not erase the attention crisis or his desire for measurable growth. It establishes that continued proximity to Saitama currently outweighs both. A tiny reflected-glare gag closes the chapter (0098), returning Genos's consequential declaration to the spar's visual motif of unbearable brightness.

### Images 0099–0108 — chapter 192 exposes the Association's hero-gambling operation

Chapter 192, `蝶と背中`, opens with a bicycle detail and an epigraph: life's failure begins in admiring a person unlike oneself (0099–0100). The target of that proposition is withheld while a butterfly-themed hero, Papillon 800, prepares for a controlled deployment (0100–0102). Operators release a trembling canine monster from a truck, connect a live feed to a private gathering and announce a presumed Demon-level threat in M-City (0101–0103).

The gathering is a covert `ヒーロー賭博`, or hero-gambling event, for wealthy Association patrons (0103–0104). Viewers wager on whether selected heroes can defeat a target and on related outcomes. The operation supplies a secret revenue stream and sometimes arranges elaborate interventions so favored large sponsors can win (0104–0105). This is not merely unauthorized spectatorship: Association personnel choose the encounter, set the information environment and manipulate the apparent competitors.

Papillon 800 is himself part of the fix. He is presented as C-Class rank 333 but is actually the much stronger B-Class rank 4 hero provisionally romanized `Maruido Yankee`; the exact established English rendering is deferred to the language audit (0106). A stronger hero masked as a weak one creates long odds and the intended upset. He is told to stage a struggle with what he believes is a fake or Wolf-level opponent before winning (0105–0107). The creature, however, lands a genuinely overwhelming blow and throws him into rubbish bags (0107–0109). The patrons applaud because authentic danger initially looks like successful theater.

### Images 0109–0120 — coercion, misclassification and the return of unscripted heroism

The small opponent itself is surprised by its strength while the disguised hero loses consciousness (0109–0110). A flashback shows how Association management secured `Maruido Yankee`'s participation. An executive calls him trustworthy and offers payment; he refuses to take part in hero gambling and objects to being made the fall guy (0110–0111). The executive then invokes the hero's hospitalized mother, her imminent surgery and the difficulty of obtaining advanced care amid medical strain (0112). Material support for hero activity is recast as leverage over a family member. The executive rationalizes the resulting arrangement as mutually beneficial and as necessary funding for the Association's costly hero-support system (0112–0113).

Back in the encounter, the hero revives and recognizes that the supposedly manageable opponent is far too strong. In the gambling room, the handler praises his acting and prepares to use him again, still reading his distress as performance (0114). A narrator identifies the actual creature as Demon-level Gachiwawa; extreme miniaturization has distorted a once-domestic animal's nature, and fear and anxiety outside its enclosure make it violent (0115). The organization has therefore converted a real public danger into an incorrectly controlled prop. Staff who have repeatedly tested the creature's strength begin to suspect an error only after the bout becomes visibly lethal (0116).

`Maruido Yankee` nevertheless rises. He first frames defeat as job loss and recalls his mother's treatment (0117), but the deeper image is aspirational: he wants to stand facing danger like “that person” (0118). The chapter's opening bicycle and the silhouetted figure indicate Mumen Rider as the admired model. The epigraph's warning is not allowed the last word. Imitation can be naïve or identity-erasing, yet here admiration recalls a coerced performer to genuine protective conduct.

The live crowd cheers as he puts himself between Gachiwawa and others (0118–0119). The monster finds it absurd that someone so strong does not flee and associates violence with the ability to live in peace; it resumes its assault (0119–0120). Institutional manipulation, financial coercion and false classification remain causal conditions, but the hero's decision is no longer reducible to the wager. The Association can script the costume and matchup; it cannot fully own the ethical reason he stands back up.

### Images 0121–0132 — Saitama interrupts the production; Child Emperor names the fix

Spectators watching locally beg Papillon 800 not to die while wealthy gamblers abuse him for jeopardizing their bets (0121). Believing he cannot win, the injured hero asks that headquarters—or ultimately his mother—be told he quit hero work and found honest employment in a distant city (0122). Gachiwawa attacks again as McCoy's operators congratulate themselves on the apparent realism of the performance (0123).

Saitama arrives and asks who let such a creature roam free, then destroys it and checks the injured hero (0124–0126). Genos is with him. Saitama's intervention resolves the actual danger without knowing the betting script and without performing for the audience. The injured hero understands that he was saved, while the gamblers and handlers are stunned (0126–0127).

McCoy reacts not with relief but with fury that another hero ruined the match and imperiled his money (0128). The result screen nevertheless identifies Saitama, now A-Class rank 39 `ハゲマント` (Caped Baldy), as the winner. McCoy confronts Saitama for acting independently; Saitama simply asks why defeating the monster made him angry, and Genos notes that a Demon-level kill should be a major achievement (0129). McCoy tries to deny the level and minimize the intervention.

Child Emperor then addresses McCoy by name and asks whether he is angry that the fixed match was interrupted (0130). He displays the underground betting site and its listed odds (0130–0131). Genos infers that unnatural threat classifications and hero ranks may have been manipulated to control those odds. Child Emperor says he had already suspected wrongdoing and will keep investigating the Association's still-deep roots; he warns them not to become involved (0131–0132). This extends his V36 inquiry from concealed prisoner abuse into a monetized system that falsifies both danger and evaluation.

### Images 0133–0140 — admired conduct receives recognition and Saitama rejects a cash motive

Mumen Rider arrives by bicycle, takes the injured hero behind him and says the hospital is close (0133). He watched the broadcast and tells the shocked hero that he is strong (0134). When the hero protests that the monster level and his own displayed rank were lies, Mumen Rider answers that labels do not matter: he did not retreat from someone stronger than himself and that was admirable (0134). The person whose back inspired him now recognizes precisely the ethical act the betting system could neither price nor fake. He accepts the praise, and Mumen carries him away (0135). The chapter's opening warning about admiration is therefore qualified rather than simply confirmed: imitation becomes failure when it replaces the self, but an admired example can also call forth and recognize genuine courage.

That night Saitama remains irritated by the site's twenty-to-one odds against him and asks whether he could bet on himself and then defeat a monster (0136). Genos rejects hero gambling as foolish, illegal and liable to severe punishment; Saitama laughs off the idea when Genos asks whether he truly intends to recover his finances that way (0136–0137). On a subsequent monster alert they run toward the threat, passing a racing vehicle in the confusion (0137–0138). After Saitama resolves the incident, a civilian offers a share of a recovered bag's contents as thanks (0139). He is briefly tempted, realizes he has started treating rescue as a money-making objective, and returns the reward while saying that was not his motive (0139). The final page quietly shows the injured hero visiting or assisting his hospitalized mother (0140). The chapter closes on material need without permitting material leverage to define heroism.

### Images 0141–0147 — Amai Mask is investigated through competing measures of personhood

Chapter 193, `値打ち`, opens on Amai Mask changing his shirt, then moves to a mountain temple where four people examine the uncertainty around his identity (0141–0142). His pre-debut history is unknown, his face has appeared to change and no cosmetic-surgery record explains the difference. The investigators consider whether he once lived under another face or identity and whether visible facial distortion could be a special ability or a sign of approaching monsterization (0142–0145). One participant recalls troubling battlefield conduct, including Amai Mask's willingness to attack in a hostage or captive situation, but the exact attribution and register remain deferred to the language audit (0143).

The discussion does not settle into exposure or acquittal. An investigator says inquiry can continue while also reporting a sensed sincerity; another warns against trusting too quickly (0144). The woman then suggests that the day may come when Amai Mask is glad he did not stain the hand of the person under discussion (0145–0146). That line frames restraint as potentially protective rather than merely evidentiary. At the monument to successive generations of the Council of Swordmasters, the investigator says stronger evidence will be reported to the master while noting that the present generation has its own burdens (0147). Amai Mask's `worth` is therefore posed as an unresolved personhood question: body, history and violent behavior matter, but none alone exhausts the judgment.

### Images 0148–0160 — inherited grief, Nichirin's precision and Atomic Samurai's wider scale

The active swordmasters pray at the memorial (0148). Yuta, identified as Amahare's daughter, vows to eradicate the remaining Monster Association members and make them regret her father's death (0149). Shidou, Zanbai's son, voices grief and insufficiency; the elders answer through memory of the fallen and Nichirin's survival rather than promising that vengeance will repair the loss (0150). The exact kinship-era phrasing will be audited before finalization.

Nichirin explains his restored body by saying he can circulate ki into a weapon and make it part of himself, applying the same principle to a blade and a prosthesis (0151–0152). His apple demonstration appears at first to spare the fruit while splitting the cliff behind it (0153–0155). The explanation corrects that first impression: the apple was cut too, but the opening is so fine that it does not separate; the object has not yet “noticed” it was cut (0156–0157). The feat makes precision, not visible destruction, the measure of mastery.

Nichirin then asks Atomic Samurai, Kamikaze, whether he will remain a hero now that Bang has retired. Atomic says competing with Bang may once have drawn him to the Association, but he now finds something there unavailable elsewhere (0157–0158). Among the top heroes is a man whose full ability he still cannot see; whether Atomic continues depends on that man's worth (0159). His companions call him changed. Nichirin interprets that change as greater tolerance and broader vision acquired by living among a mixed crowd of strong people and learning that strength has multiple scales (0159–0160). He concludes that being a hero may not be so bad. The sequence converts `worth` from a single ranked capacity into an education in incommensurable strengths.

### Images 0161–0170 — domestic proximity and mistaken recognition lead Atomic Samurai to King

Saitama and Genos return to Hero Association headquarters (0161). Forte, A-Class rank 31, has built a doghouse for Rover while Black Sperm impatiently manages the food; the latter says eating is presently his only route to increasing his cells (0162–0163). Seeing Genos beside Saitama, Forte recognizes Demon Cyborg. Genos identifies Forte and immediately demands the neighboring room, ordering him to transfer his belongings that day; Saitama's only positive credential for Forte is that he built Rover's house (0163–0165). The gag is relationally exact: after Saitama refused cohabitation, Genos preserves maximal proximity by displacing the neighbor rather than loosening his attachment.

At an M-City conveyor-belt sushi restaurant, Atomic Samurai and other swordsmen discuss testing a blade's handling while treating an ordinary meal as a field of miniature demonstrations (0165–0167). Atomic cuts or takes sushi with such control that even his companions struggle to follow the action. Their attention then shifts to a solitary diner: King (0168). Atomic approaches him as `地上最強`, the strongest man on Earth, says he came to meet him and asks to hear him out (0169). The swordsman's broadened scale of strength is real, but the scene immediately subjects it to the series' central recognition error: the man he selects as unreadably powerful is socially overvalued, while Saitama remains nearby and institutionally undervalued. Image 0170 returns to Rover and Black Sperm squeezed into the doghouse, labeled the newborn Monster Association headquarters. The joke miniaturizes organizational rebirth and closes a chapter about worth on two beings surviving beneath nearly everyone's notice.

### Images 0171–0177 — Atomic Samurai asks King to make reputed strength visible

Chapter 194, `試し斬り`, opens with Yuta as a multi-blade title figure (0171). Back in the sushi restaurant, King's engine-like heartbeat fills the room. Atomic tells him that conversation is too quick a route and asks him to show a combat stance; he directly asks whether King is truly strong and says strength cannot be confirmed until seen with his own eyes (0172–0173). King chokes while trying to respond. The companions interpret even his distress as ominous pressure, while Atomic treats a wooden or disposable implement as enough if his own sword is the opponent (0173–0174).

Atomic first demonstrates his own control with an apple (0174–0175). His movement is too fast for King to follow, and the fruit appears transformed into a dense lattice of cuts while remaining assembled (0175–0176). King blurts that the apple has become something extraordinary. Atomic then asks him to display his own ability and leads everyone outside (0176). When explanation fails, Atomic explicitly tells King to regard him as a monster and attack; King internally admits that he cannot reveal his weakness or even draw the sword, but his fear and the audible King Engine are read as escalating intent (0177).

### Images 0178–0188 — fear becomes an expert-authored proof of mastery

King's internal plea for help produces an externally terrifying posture (0178). Iaian and the others see no opening; another observer says that looking carefully reveals the whole body trembling with murderous energy (0178). They describe feints layered on feints and infer that Atomic also understands them (0179). Atomic, sweating, draws and announces that he will go first (0179–0180). King interrupts with an improvised reference to Tuesday. The observers supply arcane meanings involving air composition, radiation, a full moon and a remembered event, converting evasion into coded strategic warning (0181–0184). King continues trying to avoid a tragic fight; Atomic treats the increasingly opaque statements as evidence that something consequential has already begun (0184–0186).

Atomic returns to the apple and makes the request minimal: cut this fruit, even with no audience and no concern for sword ownership or special technique (0187–0188). He explains that watching another person cut reveals what kind of life that person has walked (0188). The proposition is sincere but epistemically dangerous: it assumes that any visible result or hesitation can be read through a preloaded theory of the person.

### Images 0189–0200 — inability to draw the sword compounds King's legend and redirects Atomic

Atomic's companions say that even the three disciples behind him were accepted because of their interest in how a prior man cut an apple (0189). King agrees to try. He kneels beside the apple, grips the sword and strains for more than two minutes without drawing it; finally he puts the still-sheathed weapon down and walks away (0190–0195). The literal event is simple: King cannot unsheathe the blade. No cut occurs.

Atomic nevertheless cannot perceive the non-event as failure. He says he saw neither the line of the blade nor any part of the sequence and imagines that the apple, like Nichirin's apple, has not noticed it was cut (0196). He goes further: even the sword must not have noticed being drawn (0197). King's statement that he is a sword novice is reclassified as the modesty of an extraordinary monster. The disciples laugh at the idea that he merely could not draw it and name Atomic, Okamaitachi, Bushidrill and Iaian as inadequate observers (0198).

The misrecognition has constructive consequences for the mistaken observer. Atomic decides to begin his training again from the ground up, warning that endless hardship may await, and asks whether his students will still follow (0198–0199). They answer yes. He tells them that among the top heroes are people who overcome their own defects through blood and tears and that they must not be mocked by such people; Iaian accepts the demand (0200). King's reputation thus grows through an unfalsifiable interpretive loop, but that false belief humbles Atomic and renews genuine discipline. King, unseen by them, only wants stomach medicine and hopes they will not follow him (0199).

## 5. Saitama state

Saitama remains institutionally underrecognized and largely uninterested in correcting the condition. Genos accurately notes that the Neo Heroes would likely offer him better treatment; Saitama refuses because transfer would be a hassle, not because he defends the Hero Association (0094–0097). This is inertia and disinterest in organizational validation, not proof of loyalty.

His relational conduct is more consequential than his stated evaluation. He agrees to Genos's bout, notices changed parts and tries to answer, but cannot supply meaningful feedback across the power gap; Genos experiences that failure as lack of interest (0065–0084). When Genos drops his transfer because Saitama will not go, Saitama does not explicitly recognize the sacrifice. Yet Genos's later move into the neighboring room and continued presence confirm that proximity remains structurally important (0097–0098,0161–0165).

Saitama's hero conduct remains unscripted and self-correcting. He destroys Gachiwawa without knowing the betting production, asks why victory makes McCoy angry and receives formal credit only inside the rigged result screen (0124–0131). He is briefly attracted to profiting from intervention, then notices that money has begun to replace rescue as his objective and returns the offered reward (0136–0139). V37 therefore adds neither purified selflessness nor cynical corruption; it shows an ordinary temptation caught by his own motive check.

## 6. Ensemble and readiness

**Hero Association and rivals:** executives anticipate defection, court outside strength and discuss surveillance or replacement for uncontrollable S-Class assets. Axel's Hunters, Raiden, sponsors and unnamed heroes are already aligned or in negotiation with the Neo Heroes. The Association remains operational but faces a legitimacy, retention and control crisis (0010–0034).

**Genos:** hardware and confidence increase through `雷光核`, and he judges his firepower potentially usable against Dragon-level opposition. His strategic readiness remains unsettled: he cannot measure progress against Saitama, still organizes advancement around the Mad Cyborg and subordinates an institutional move to staying near his teacher (0060–0098).

**Tanktop Master and group:** Tanktop Master is physically restored and stronger; his group shrank after battlefield trauma but receives low-ranked applicants moved by his conduct. Reciprocity and example improve cohesion without proving matchup readiness (0042–0046).

**Child Emperor:** he is actively building an evidence case from adversarial sources and catches the hero-gambling operation. His investigative readiness strengthens while his institutional protection and ability to expose the result remain open (0046–0048,0130–0132).

**Darkshine:** his body can again perform exceptional work, but battle anticipation triggers fear and he says he cannot face forward. Physical return is not psychological combat readiness (0049–0050).

**Atomic Samurai and swordmasters:** Nichirin has adapted sword-body principles to prosthetic restoration. Atomic's judgment broadens after exposure to varied heroes, then fails spectacularly on King; the false conclusion nevertheless produces humility and a declared return to training accepted by his disciples (0151–0160,0168–0200).

**Amai Mask, Black Sperm and Rover:** Amai's personhood and bodily state remain under investigation. Black Sperm and Rover survive concealed in reduced domestic circumstances; survival is clear, recovery scale and public risk are not (0141–0147,0162–0170).

## 7. Directional relationship changes

**Saitama ↔ Genos:** Genos experiences a genuine attention failure, considers leaving for a more measurable environment and then immediately cancels when Saitama refuses to transfer. The bond strengthens behaviorally through chosen proximity while its communication deficit remains unresolved (0080–0098,0161–0165).

**Mumen Rider → Maruido Yankee/Papillon 800:** admiration becomes reciprocal recognition. The coerced hero stands because Mumen's example matters; Mumen later names his refusal to retreat as strength without endorsing the false rank or gambling script (0117–0135).

**Atomic Samurai ↔ disciples:** their shared misreading of King lowers Atomic's self-estimate and produces a renewed apprenticeship-like discipline. The students choose to follow into open-ended hardship, strengthening commitment around a false premise but a real practice (0196–0200).

**Swordmaster successors ↔ the dead:** Yuta and Shidou inherit grief, identity and expectation from Amahare and Zanbai. Vengeance is stated, not resolved; the memorial sequence gives their loss a collective lineage rather than proof of readiness (0148–0150).

**Hero Association ↔ prospective talent:** recruitment reverses direction. Axel judges the Association illegitimate, Raiden reveals prior commitment elsewhere and Genos treats transfer as negotiable rather than sacred. Institutional membership becomes conditional and comparative (0015–0034,0085–0097).

## 8. Heroism, recognition and institutional implications

The volume distinguishes four recognition systems. Commercial recruitment turns exceptional heroes into controlled images; executive planning treats recruits as substitutes for people it cannot govern (0010–0020). The Neo Heroes compete through scale, treatment and infrastructure, but V37 supplies promises and recruitment success rather than operational proof (0021–0034,0085–0090). Hero gambling directly corrupts appraisal by manipulating ranks, threat levels, opponents and information for patrons (0099–0132). Mumen Rider supplies the ethical countermodel: he judges the act of standing before a stronger danger, not the false classification attached to it (0133–0135).

Child Emperor's evidence links V36's hidden wrongdoing to a monetized apparatus rather than an isolated abuse. McCoy's defense—that revenue supports heroes—does not erase coercion, sponsor favoritism or public risk. The Association's resources can materially sustain care, as the injured hero's mother demonstrates, while the same dependency becomes leverage. Institutional support and institutional exploitation are therefore joined rather than cleanly opposed (0110–0116,0130–0132).

Saitama and King form the volume's recognition asymmetry. Saitama performs real, unscripted rescue while remaining undervalued; King supplies no demonstrated skill yet receives escalating expert confirmation. The contrast is not that all public reputation is false. It shows that causal contribution can be invisible while a reputation protected by interpretive bias becomes unfalsifiable (0124–0139,0168–0200).

## 9. Body, personhood and power mechanisms

**Cyborg modification:** Genos's visible parts, electrical core and firepower are concrete changes, but V37 provides no independent numerical ceiling or proof against the Mad Cyborg (0060–0093).

**Psychological readiness:** Darkshine demonstrates that restored muscular performance does not restore willingness to fight. Bodily capacity and accessible action remain separable (0049–0050).

**Regeneration and altered organisms:** the Forest Tribe regenerates vegetation through human bodies; Gachiwawa's extreme miniaturization changes a domestic animal's nature and makes fear outside containment violent; Black Sperm associates eating with rebuilding cell count (0051–0064,0115–0120,0162–0163). These are distinct mechanisms and should not be collapsed into one monster rule.

**Amai Mask:** unknown history, changing face, missing surgery records and battle conduct support investigation of a possible special ability or monsterization. They do not independently prove that he is a monster or that visible facial change has one cause (0141–0147).

**Nichirin:** circulating ki through a weapon so it becomes part of the body explains both sword use and prosthetic adaptation. His apple remains assembled after a real cut because the opening is too fine to separate, not because no cut occurred (0151–0157).

**King:** heartbeat, fear, sweat, tremor and inability to draw the sword are ordinary bodily facts misread through reputation. No invisible slash occurs. The chapter's power mechanism is social inference rather than an unrevealed combat feat (0172–0200).

## 10. Satire and genre

Recruitment satire begins with Tatsumaki turning a controlled advertisement into dangerous literal proof, then lets the polished commercial succeed while executives privately confess dependence and fear (0010–0020). The Neo Heroes plot is introduced less as a clean heroic alternative than as a labor-market shock exposing the Association's treatment failures.

The hero-gambling chapter shifts superhero deployment into sports fixing, production control and patron entertainment. Its black comedy depends on handlers treating authentic terror as good acting until Saitama ruins the show by actually resolving the danger (0099–0132). Mumen's arrival returns genre seriousness without erasing the absurd machinery.

Domestic scale repeatedly punctures power mythology: Saitama's tiny apartment determines Genos's relocation strategy; Rover receives a doghouse; Black Sperm becomes its feeding attendant; a “new Monster Association headquarters” fits inside it (0065–0067,0161–0170). Survival and threat persist inside household comedy.

The King/Atomic sequence is an escalating epistemic farce. Every falsifying sign—choking, evasion, trembling, random weekday talk, delay, failure to unsheathe and retreat—is reclassified as deeper mastery. The gag works because Atomic is sincere, observant and highly skilled; expertise without a falsifiable interpretive rule amplifies error rather than preventing it (0172–0200).

## 12. Visual and formal analysis

**Commercial and monitor frames:** cameras, advertisements, executive screens, betting feeds and result displays repeatedly separate what happens from what an institution presents. The same event acquires different value depending on who controls the frame (0010–0020,0099–0132).

**Backs and models:** chapter 192's bicycle and silhouetted back delay Mumen Rider's identification, then make the admired person's arrival a reciprocal recognition. The visual back is not merely imitation; it becomes an orientation the injured hero can choose under pressure (0099–0100,0117–0135).

**Apples and unreadable cuts:** Nichirin's intact-looking apple establishes a real technique whose effect appears late. Chapter 194 repeats the object but removes the cut entirely; Atomic imports the valid earlier model into a false case. Visual sameness becomes the trap by which prior knowledge overrides present evidence (0153–0157,0174–0200).

**Glare, light and attention:** Genos's new core overwhelms Saitama's eyes without producing evaluative recognition. The visual spectacle is real, but the chapter distinguishes being dazzling from being seen in the relational sense Genos needs (0072–0084,0097–0098).

**Scale reversals:** Dragon-level threats, institutional collapse and sword mastery alternate with insurance, cramped rooms, sushi, a doghouse and stomach medicine. Large systems are repeatedly made legible through small objects whose use exposes motive and relation (0036,0064–0067,0161–0170,0165–0201).
