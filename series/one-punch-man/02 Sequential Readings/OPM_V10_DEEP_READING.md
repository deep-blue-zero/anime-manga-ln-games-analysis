## 2. Volume orientation

V09 asked what characters do when hierarchy assigns them a position. V10 asks a related but more technical question: **how do people know what kind of strength they are looking at?**

The volume repeatedly introduces a proxy for strength, competence, heroism, or social value and then tests its domain:

- Mumen Rider distinguishes Garou's power from raw force and names its organizing principle `技`.
- Saitama becomes interested enough in that unfamiliar category to seek direct martial experience.
- Garou demonstrates that technique includes prediction, geometry, timing, and adaptation, not simply named moves.
- Metal Bat describes another power logic through `気合い`, where damage and emotional activation can increase rather than simply deplete performance.
- the Hero Association uses rank, threat levels, and group organization as operational approximations;
- a hero-suit contest treats visual design as a public-facing proxy for heroic appeal;
- Child Emperor's `オカメちゃん` attempts to reduce physical strength to a number and immediately encounters domain errors, transformation, psychic power, technological augmentation, and Saitama.

The result is not a claim that measurement is useless. Golden Ball's trajectories can be inferred. Hero rankings carry some information. Disaster levels matter operationally. The strength meter can distinguish ordinary physical output from exceptional physical output inside its design range. Fubuki's organization genuinely coordinates many heroes. The volume's stronger point is narrower: **a useful proxy becomes misleading when its domain is forgotten.**

This is also a character volume. Garou's self-authored “monster” identity becomes harder to equate with simple antisociality once he tells Tareo to run home during a real monster emergency. Tatsumaki's “day off” reveals that being needed is not merely an occupational burden for her; inactivity itself is psychologically uncomfortable. Fubuki, already established as rank-anxious in V09, confronts several numbers that fail to capture what she values and ends the volume by asserting `私は私のやり方でいく` while still comically calculating calories.

### Central volume thesis

**V10 distinguishes reality from the systems used to make reality legible. Technique, fighting spirit, rank, reputation, costume, threat level, group size, and numerical strength can each reveal something; none is sufficient by itself. The most important character changes occur when someone mistakes a proxy for the thing itself—or learns not to.**

Saitama sits at the center of this not because he rejects all classification, but because he repeatedly exposes classification limits accidentally. He is curious about `技` because it may name something he does not understand. He is simultaneously misread as a nobody, a mugging victim, an ugly dresser, a weak B-Class hero, and the leader of an elite “Saitama Group.” None of those descriptions captures the same person completely.

### Strongest limiting reading

V10 is also transparently a transitional volume. The Super Fight tournament has barely begun; Elder Centipede is unresolved; Garou's Watchdog Man intention is only a prospective target; the monster incidents are expanding faster than the volume can integrate them. It would therefore be over-systematic to treat every number or category as part of a single authorial manifesto. The three extras are especially playful and can sustain local joke logic without every gag carrying equal thematic weight.

The safer formulation is that **legibility and proxy failure recur densely enough to be analytically meaningful**, while later volumes must determine which of these mechanisms become durable series-level structures.

# 3. Sequential close reading

## 3.1 `48撃目 バナナ` — aftermath makes `技` legible

### 3.1.1 Garou's victories become other people's injuries

V09 ended with Garou's assault on Tanktop Master and multiple heroes. V10 begins in the Hero Association hospital rather than giving Garou an uninterrupted victory lap. That structural choice turns “hero hunting” into consequences borne by bodies and institutions.

Saitama arrives with bananas and his usual low ceremony. The comedy does not erase the informational importance of the hospital scene: injured heroes are now witnesses who can describe what kind of opponent Garou is.

**SF/IR:** Garou's character arc is reframed through its externalities before it advances through his own point of view. `OPM|V10|chapter:48|images:0009-0018|anchor:hospital aftermath before renewed Garou action`

### 3.1.2 Mumen Rider refuses the ontology shortcut

Mumen Rider's language is unusually careful. He says:

- `彼は人間だったよ`
- `人間だった…怪人を名乗る人間だ…`
- `あるいはまだ人間と表現するべきか`

The first-order fact at the V10 boundary is therefore not “Garou is a monster.” A badly injured hero who has every reason to fear him still distinguishes **human body/status from self-applied monster identity**.

**TF:** Garou is described as a human who calls himself a monster. `OPM|V10|chapter:48|images:0014-0015|anchor:Mumen distinguishes human Garou from monster label`

This strengthens the ontology control from V08-V09. `怪人` is currently a role, aspiration, social label, and ideological self-positioning for Garou; biological monster transformation remains unestablished.

### 3.1.3 `強さの種類が違う` -> `技だ`

Mumen is unable to reduce the encounter to “Garou was simply stronger.” He tells Saitama that the **kind** of strength felt different:

`強さの種類が違う…なんて言えばいいのか`

Then the page isolates the answer:

`「技」だ`

`奴は高度な武術を使う`

This is a major power-system clarification because it comes from a defeated observer rather than from a martial artist advertising a school. `技` is presented as an explanatory category for how capability can differ from raw physical superiority.

**TF/SF:** V10 explicitly names technique as a distinct organization of strength. `OPM|V10|chapter:48|image:0016|anchor:技 identified as different strength category`

### 3.1.4 Tanktop Master's humility is evidence, not humiliation alone

Tanktop Master follows with an unusually useful self-correction. He notes that he has handled many monstrous opponents through overwhelming force and admits that being beaten by a younger martial artist demonstrates how troublesome `武術` can be.

This does not turn Tanktop Master into a martial theorist. It does show a learning policy: **defeat can revise his model of what counts as dangerous**. The earlier V07 pattern of recognizing a gap and training rather than protecting ego survives another adverse comparison.

Saitama, who had been casually eating bananas, asks for more detail. The change in attention matters more than any claim that he suddenly reveres martial arts.

## 3.2 `49撃目 どうせ暇だから` — curiosity becomes embodied inquiry

### 3.2.1 Tareo gives Garou an ordinary social register

Garou's encounter with Tareo is the first V10 scene where he is not primarily performing for an enemy, institution, or martial audience. Tareo treats him as an older stranger with a shared interest in heroes. Garou borrows the Hero Almanac, notices its monster section, and says that frightening monsters are preferable because fear is what makes them cool:

`怖い方がいい それがかっこいいと思わないか?`

The line is consistent with his monster self-authorship, but the interaction is not menacing. Garou can joke, borrow something, answer a child's questions, and show sustained attention without needing dominance as the immediate social mode.

**TF/VF:** ordinary Garou/Tareo contact broadens Garou beyond battle rhetoric. `OPM|V10|chapter:49|images:0022-0025|anchor:Garou borrows Hero Almanac and talks monsters with Tareo`

### 3.2.2 Saitama wants to test a category, not worship a tradition

At the hospital, the idea that martial arts might represent a different kind of strength catches Saitama's attention. With Charanko he makes the interest explicit:

`ちょっと興味が湧いたんだよ 何かツテとかない?`

He wants a match with a strong martial artist. Charanko responds with the ordinary infrastructure of martial training: basic forms, conditioning, stairs, dojo work, time. Saitama answers with the destabilizing reminder that he too was a beginner only three years earlier.

This is important for Saitama's boredom model. He is not categorically incapable of curiosity after becoming overwhelmingly strong. **A concept he recognizes as genuinely new can generate initiative.** He asks questions, seeks a partner, and changes plans.

The interest remains highly Saitama-like: practical, compressed, and embodied. He does not ask for a treatise on martial history. He wants to experience the thing.

**TF/IR:** recognized novelty produces concrete engagement. `OPM|V10|chapter:49|images:0029-0031|anchor:Saitama asks Charanko for access to strong martial artist`

### 3.2.3 Prize money and impersonation keep the inquiry morally untidy

Charanko's unused Super Fight entry gives Saitama a shortcut. The `賞金300万` becomes salient immediately. The tournament therefore combines at least three motives:

1. curiosity about martial technique;
2. the possibility of fighting strong specialists;
3. ordinary financial incentive.

Saitama also knows the entry is under Charanko's name and begins moving toward impersonation rather than formal registration. The volume should not sanitize this because Saitama is heroic elsewhere. His ethics are strong in protection contexts; his attitude toward low-stakes bureaucratic rules can be opportunistic and comic.

**TF/IR:** Saitama's tournament entry is curiosity + money + casual rule circumvention, not a pure spiritual quest. `OPM|V10|chapter:49|images:0032-0033|anchor:Charanko ticket and 3 million prize`

## 3.3 `50撃目 調子に乗る!` — Garou's strength is information processing

### 3.3.1 Golden Ball turns the city into a trajectory problem

Garou deliberately selects A-Class Golden Ball as prey. Golden Ball's ricocheting projectiles force Garou to read angles, surfaces, timing, and blind trajectories rather than simply endure a frontal attack.

Murata's form does the analytical work: repeated lines cross alley architecture, turning urban space into a geometric diagram. Garou's body is repeatedly framed relative to the projectile path rather than alone in a heroic pose.

**VF/SF:** combat space becomes an inference problem. `OPM|V10|chapter:50|images:0037-0050|anchor:Golden Ball ricochet geometry pressures Garou`

### 3.3.2 Spring Mustachio changes the problem by combining ranges

Spring Mustachio adds close-range thrusts while Golden Ball maintains projectile pressure. The pair therefore tests Garou under coordination rather than serial one-on-one dominance. He is injured and surprised.

The important result is not “Garou wins again.” It is that he has to **update**. V09 established adaptation against Tanktop Master's raw force; V10 shows adaptation to geometry and coordinated attack. Martial competence includes rapid opponent modeling.

**IR:** Garou's combat policy is `receive new constraint -> infer mechanism/timing -> adapt technique -> reverse`. `OPM|V10|chapter:50|images:0050-0058|anchor:Garou adapts to coordinated Golden Ball/Spring Mustachio pressure`

### 3.3.3 Hero hunting remains risky

Because both heroes can meaningfully wound or pressure him, V10 prevents “Hero Hunter” from becoming a retrospective label for effortless omnipotence. Garou is strong enough to choose dangerous tests and skilled enough to recover, but he is still learnable, woundable, and matchup-sensitive.

That matters for character modeling: the hunt is partly **self-development through escalating exposure**, not merely a fixed-strength predator collecting weaker targets.

## 3.4 `51撃目 被り物` — Saitama and Garou meet without meeting

### 3.4.1 Garou's reputation model fails on Saitama

Garou sees an unfamiliar man and interprets him through his hero-hunting script: probably a low-level hero seeking promotion by catching the now-famous hunter. He attacks from behind.

Saitama does not recognize the script at all.

His responses are mundane:

- `何すんだ 誰だテメー`
- `カツアゲする気か?`
- `買い物の邪魔すんな`

He incapacitates Garou and continues the shopping task.

**TF/VF:** the first Saitama/Garou contact is an extreme knowledge asymmetry. `OPM|V10|chapter:51|images:0064-0069|anchor:Garou attacks; Saitama interprets him as street nuisance`

This is one of the volume's cleanest legibility jokes. Garou is investing enormous effort in becoming socially legible as the `ヒーロー狩り`. Saitama, who is now actively curious about martial arts, encounters the very martial antagonist driving that discourse and files him as a mugger because the necessary context is absent.

**Important qualification:** this does not contradict Saitama's renewed interest in martial novelty. The novelty must first be recognized as relevant. Saitama cannot become engaged by information he does not possess.

### 3.4.2 The cheap wig punctures martial prestige before the tournament begins

Saitama then buys a party wig so he can pass as Charanko. The disguise is transparently absurd. The Super Fight tournament is being framed as a prestige space organized around schools, styles, reputation, and sanctioned comparison; Saitama enters it through a cheap consumer object and administrative fraud.

The joke is not that martial art itself is fake. V10 has already shown technique to be real. The joke targets **prestige machinery around technique** and the inability of formal appearance to identify actual capability.

## 3.5 `52撃目 戻すな!` — institutions react while crises multiply

The Hero Association is now trying to interpret Garou as a distinct operational threat while monster incidents are rising. The volume refuses to let the human “monster” storyline replace literal monsters. Multiple hazard streams coexist.

This matters for institutional analysis. The Association must classify:

- a human who calls himself a monster and hunts heroes;
- ordinary monster incidents of varying disaster levels;
- S-Class allocation problems;
- public safety around scheduled events such as Super Fight.

The chapter's function is largely connective, but it strengthens a recurring V2 claim: **classification is necessary under scarcity precisely because classification is imperfect.**

Saitama's own institutional behavior remains opportunistic. He is preparing to enter a formally structured tournament under another man's identity while the Association separately struggles to make Garou legible through official threat categories.

## 3.6 `53撃目 控え室` — martial prestige and the memory of a real fight

### 3.6.1 The waiting room is a social taxonomy of martial arts

The tournament waiting area collects named schools, styles, physiques, reputations, and prior winners. Fighters understand one another through martial credentials in much the same way heroes use rank.

Saitama is almost uniquely illegible inside that culture. He has the wrong name, the wrong hair, no legitimate school identity, and overwhelming capability that the social field cannot see.

**SF/IR:** Super Fight becomes another classification environment in which visible credential and underlying capacity can diverge. `OPM|V10|chapter:53|images:0091-0102|anchor:martial contestants and Saitama-as-Charanko waiting room`

### 3.6.2 `本当の戦いか…覚えてねぇな その感覚`

A veteran contestant promises to teach “Charanko” what a real fight is. Saitama's internal response is not ridicule:

`本当の戦いか……覚えてねぇな その感覚`

This is one of V10's best compact statements of his post-growth condition. “Real fight” is not merely something he wants to win. It names a **felt condition he no longer remembers**.

**TF/IR:** Saitama's martial curiosity connects to experiential loss without proving the tournament can repair it. `OPM|V10|chapter:53|image:0103|anchor:Saitama cannot remember the feeling of a real fight`

Do not convert this into “Saitama is clinically depressed” or “martial arts will restore him.” The source supports lost experiential access and curiosity, not a diagnosis or outcome.

## 3.7 `54撃目 百足` — Garou's monster project meets a child's safety

### 3.7.1 Tareo's almanac becomes Garou's target-selection tool

Garou uses Tareo's Hero Almanac to learn about Watchdog Man, including the fixed `番犬広場` location and S-Class position. The same child-facing guide that supports fandom therefore becomes intelligence for hero hunting.

Garou's thought process remains strength-oriented: Watchdog Man looks like a costume gimmick, so Garou wants to verify whether he is actually strong.

This continues the V10 motif: appearance is a poor capability proxy, and Garou knows it. He is skeptical of the costume rather than automatically dismissing the opponent.

### 3.7.2 `お前もダッシュで家に帰れよ`

A real monster emergency interrupts the exchange. Tareo hears the evacuation warning. Garou tells him directly:

`今の聞いただろ お前もダッシュで家に帰れよ`

Tareo answers that he understands and leaves.

**TF:** Garou gives immediate practical safety direction to a child during an actual monster emergency. `OPM|V10|chapter:54|images:0113-0115|anchor:Garou tells Tareo to evacuate home`

This is high-value contradiction evidence. Garou's chosen `怪人` identity is not yet a complete moral policy. He can celebrate fear, attack heroes, and cultivate a monster persona while also displaying selective ordinary concern for Tareo's safety.

The correct inference is not “Garou is secretly harmless.” His victims disprove that. It is that **monster self-authorship and concrete care coexist**, making simple moral ontology inadequate.

### 3.7.3 Literal monster pressure raises the contrast

The surrounding incidents include physically monstrous bodies and escalating disaster response. Garou's human body and selective concern are therefore staged beside creatures for whom `怪人` is not merely a chosen social persona.

The contrast strengthens the monsterhood ledger without resolving where Garou will go next.

## 3.8 `55撃目 気合い` — Metal Bat's strength runs on a different variable

### 3.8.1 `気合い` is not just macho dialogue

Metal Bat's combat initially looks like straightforward blunt-force heroism. The important change arrives after he has taken damage and his output rises. His own explanation is:

`気合いがありゃ大抵なんとかなるんだよ`

Later:

`久々に気合いが入ったぜ`

Within the V10 observational boundary, `気合い` is therefore not safe to translate analytically as mere “attitude.” The manga shows a **performance state** associated with taking punishment, emotional activation, persistence, and increased offensive effectiveness.

**TF/VF:** fighting spirit is behaviorally coupled to Metal Bat's rising combat performance. `OPM|V10|chapter:55|images:0124-0130|anchor:Metal Bat damage-to-fighting-spirit escalation`

Do not yet infer a universal formal power equation. V10 establishes Metal Bat's mechanism phenomenologically, not a general energy system.

### 3.8.2 Abrasiveness and protective duty coexist

When support heroes arrive, Metal Bat does not use them as spectators to his toughness. He tells them to escort the endangered parent and child away. When Elder Centipede emerges, he identifies the monster's target and gives the same basic instruction:

`奴の狙いはそこの親子だ`

`俺が相手してる間に連れて逃げろ`

**TF:** Metal Bat prioritizes extraction while accepting the high-risk delaying role. `OPM|V10|chapter:55|images:0131-0135|anchor:Metal Bat orders evacuation and remains against Elder Centipede`

This strengthens his hero model: loudness and aggression do not predict low protective reliability.

### 3.8.3 Elder Centipede exceeds the chapter's local scale

The Association identifies Elder Centipede as a previously uneliminated dangerous monster associated with catastrophic city damage and `災害レベル“竜”`. The volume ends the main story on Metal Bat facing that larger threat.

The unresolved endpoint matters. V10 establishes the commitment and the mismatch; it does not establish the battle outcome.

# 4. Extra 1 — `タツマキの休日`: being needed as self-structure

## 4.1 “I have things to do” is immediately contradicted by inactivity

After Tatsumaki resolves a monster incident without casualties, an Association attendant praises her and explains that her power is exceptional enough that the organization wants to reserve her for especially dangerous threats.

Tatsumaki complains that it is a holiday and says she is busy, then immediately qualifies her willingness:

`まぁどうしても私の力が必要っていうなら仕方ないけど 仕方なくよ`

The next day, no call arrives. At home in ordinary clothes, she asks why there is no monster assignment. When told another S-Class hero handled the available threat and she should enjoy a day off, her response is:

`は? 別に休みたいなんて言ってないでしょ`

The scene then lands on the simple admission:

`暇だわ…`

**TF/VF:** Tatsumaki's “busy” self-presentation and her behavior under nondeployment diverge. `OPM|V10|extra:タツマキの休日|images:0140-0143|anchor:no dispatch produces boredom and self-initiated monster search`

## 4.2 Usefulness is not merely an imposed burden

Tatsumaki begins seeking monster incidents herself. She is repeatedly frustrated to find enemies already defeated. Her thought is revealing:

`私が戦わなくても平和だなんて…何なのよ`

This is not a simple desire for violence. The extra supports a stronger but still bounded inference: **being uniquely useful is part of Tatsumaki's preferred self-position**. Peace that does not require her is emotionally unsatisfying because it removes the role through which she ordinarily demonstrates competence and necessity.

That fills a major readiness gap from V07: private/ordinary state now exists, and it is structurally consistent with her public imperiousness without being identical to it.

## 4.3 Saitama accidentally competes without knowing there is a competition

The monsters Tatsumaki keeps finding dead bear the trace of a single overwhelming blow. Saitama is nearby on an idle walk, commenting that he happens to be running into many monsters.

Neither character is intentionally competing. Tatsumaki nevertheless experiences the sequence as frustrated access to work and eventually races toward another incident as `私の獲物`.

The asymmetry is comic but character-relevant: Tatsumaki experiences strength relationally—as something whose use and comparative visibility matter—while Saitama can destroy monsters incidentally during a walk and then nap.

## 4.4 Association manpower shortage versus allocation failure

The extra begins by saying monster incidents are increasing and the Hero Association is troubled by manpower shortage. Yet its strongest hero is bored enough to hunt for work herself.

This is not proof that the Association could solve all scarcity by calling Tatsumaki more often. It **is** evidence that scarcity and allocation are distinct problems. The institution can simultaneously have too much demand overall and fail to route a high-capacity actor efficiently at a specific moment.

# 5. Extra 2 — `センス`: heroic value survives bad clothes

## 5.1 The contest creates a deliberately artificial evaluation domain

A hero-suit contest rewards visual design and public presentation. Saitama is attracted in part by prize money and, once again, treats a prestige system as an ordinary opportunity rather than sacred judgment.

The contest itself is legitimate on its own terms: costume design and presentation can matter to a public hero. The analytical mistake would be to treat those terms as a complete measure of heroism.

## 5.2 Saitama gives away the thing being judged

Before competing, Saitama encounters a young man whose clothes have been taken by classmates who mock his fashion sense. The youth cannot enter without clothing. Saitama removes his own hero suit and says:

`格好良いだろ 貸してやる`

`今日はこれ着て帰れ`

Genos reacts with `先生!?` because the gift directly interferes with the contest Saitama came to enter.

**TF/VF:** Saitama voluntarily gives his hero suit to a humiliated stranger despite its immediate instrumental value to himself. `OPM|V10|extra:センス|image:0154|anchor:Saitama lends hero suit to bullied youth`

This is one of the cleanest V10 statements of value hierarchy. He does not make a speech about compassion. He solves the concrete problem and improvises around the cost.

## 5.3 `ヒーローの服装に決まりなんてないしな`

Saitama's backup is a sushi-shop promotional T-shirt and floral shorts. He tells Genos:

`ヒーローの服装に決まりなんてないしな`

The statement fits his wider anti-prestige pattern but should not be inflated into “appearance never matters.” Saitama has already shown that `ハゲマント` annoys him. The distinction is between **appearance as self/public expression** and appearance as proof of underlying heroic worth.

## 5.4 Emergency response breaks into the aesthetic contest

A monster attack interrupts judging. Costumed participants respond with real capabilities. Saitama also acts. The judge later insists that defeating a monster is not what the hero-suit contest is evaluating.

That position is absurd in context but technically coherent: the event has a declared domain. V10's satire again comes from **domain mismatch**. The person who looks worst by the costume rubric can be overwhelmingly better by the life-protection rubric.

Saitama receives only eight points and finishes last. The audience, however, has seen what happened. The bullied youth returns the suit after drawing a lesson less about Saitama's clothing than about self-authorship: whether something is lame is not wholly for other people to dictate.

The judge experiences his own small correction, realizing that obsessive attention to a fixed aesthetic perspective can miss what matters to someone else—before Saitama immediately jokes that the youth's shirt really is lame. The extra refuses sentimental purity even while allowing the lesson to land.

# 6. Extra 3 — `数字`: numbers are useful until they become ontology

## 6.1 Fubuki begins with a real organizational fact

The extra opens by surveying hero collectives: Tanktop Master's followers, Atomic Samurai's disciples, C-Class alliances, Amai Mask's fan/institutional reach, Metal Knight's machines. It then presents the Fubuki Group as numerically the largest organized professional-hero faction.

The claim is not merely vanity. The group is shown as structured and efficient under Fubuki's command. V09's correction therefore survives: Fubuki's faction is coercive **and** organizationally real.

## 6.2 Fubuki has already updated on Saitama's combat value

When subordinates consider overwhelming Saitama collectively, Fubuki says:

`無理よ`

`強いのよ 彼は`

`その戦力は おそらくB級で一番`

She wants him incorporated because strength she cannot easily suppress is better placed inside her network than outside it.

**TF:** Fubuki's model updates after V09 defeat; she does not preserve the convenient fiction that Saitama is an ordinary low-ranked subordinate. `OPM|V10|extra:数字|images:0175-0176|anchor:Fubuki acknowledges Saitama likely strongest in B-Class`

This is important epistemically. Her rank obsession distorts strategy, but she is not unable to learn contrary evidence.

## 6.3 “Saitama Group” exists socially before Saitama thinks it exists

Fubuki proposes a team contest and tells Saitama to bring members. Saitama objects:

`ちょっと待て サイタマ組なんてねーぞ`

The next day he arrives with Genos, Bang, and King:

- S-Class Rank 14 Genos;
- S-Class Rank 3 Silver Fang;
- S-Class Rank 7 King;
- B-Class Rank 7 Saitama.

Fubuki is visibly shocked. Saitama explains that they are simply people he knows and could call.

**TF/SF:** institutional prestige has accumulated around Saitama through organic relationships he does not conceptualize as a faction. `OPM|V10|extra:数字|images:0177-0179|anchor:Fubuki sees Saitama arrive with Genos Bang King`

This is a major relational distinction:

- Fubuki deliberately constructs a hierarchical group to stabilize position.
- Saitama accidentally develops a small high-capacity network through repeated direct relationships.

The point is not “small groups are better.” The two networks are produced by different social mechanisms.

## 6.4 Fubuki exploits specialization correctly—and King breaks the intended numbers advantage

Fubuki's team contest is not direct combat. A former professional gamer inside her large organization becomes the specialist representative. That is a smart use of group breadth: many members create access to noncombat skills that a small combat-heavy team may lack.

Saitama, Bang, and Genos are poor or ordinary fits for the selected domain. King is not. His gaming mastery overturns the numerical advantage of the group in the exact domain Fubuki selected.

This preserves a subtle point: **specialization is valuable; headcount does not guarantee possession of the best specialist.**

## 6.5 `オカメちゃん` turns strength into a number

The narrative then shifts to Child Emperor, who has built `オカメちゃん`, a device that reads bodily/neural information to assign a physical-strength number.

The experiment looks promising:

- an ordinary C-Class hero is normalized around 100;
- stronger heroes receive larger values;
- a bear exceeds many ordinary humans;
- Child Emperor himself measures high despite his age.

The device is not fraudulent. It measures a real dimension.

**TF:** Child Emperor explicitly treats it as `肉体強度` measurement. `OPM|V10|extra:数字|images:0183-0185|anchor:Okame-chan physical-strength calibration`

## 6.6 A changing monster breaks static confidence

The spider monster `ハエトリノ` initially produces a value around 420, low enough to tempt a downgrade in intuitive threat. Its body then changes and the reading jumps to 6999, corresponding to a much more dangerous state.

The episode demonstrates two distinct limits:

1. **state dependence:** a measurement can be accurate for the body state sampled and still become obsolete seconds later;
2. **dimensional incompleteness:** physical output alone does not capture every tactical ability, weapon, coordination effect, or threat mechanism.

Child Emperor does not simply throw the device away. He tries to interpret the error. This is good scientific behavior inside a comic failure scene.

## 6.7 Saitama makes upper-bound and lower-bound failure observationally identical

When Child Emperor tests Saitama, the machine returns `測定不能`. Child Emperor initially interprets that as output too low to register, places Saitama near an exercise-deprived civilian, and advises him to train more.

The later explanation reveals the instrument's other failure mode: strength beyond its upper bound likewise produces no number.

**TF/IR:** a single output state—“unmeasurable”—maps to opposite realities. `OPM|V10|extra:数字|images:0197-0199|anchor:Saitama measurement impossible and misread as weak`

This is almost a miniature model of Saitama's whole institutional problem. An observation is not only data; it is data **plus an interpretive model**. When the model lacks the relevant possibility, correct sensor behavior can still support a false conclusion.

## 6.8 Fubuki's `19` exposes domain blindness

Fubuki then receives a physical-strength value of `19`, below the nominal C-Class physical baseline. The reading is not evidence that she is weaker than an ordinary C-Class hero in the only sense that matters. It is evidence that psychic capability is not being measured by the physical-strength instrument.

Her emotional response matters because numbers already occupy too much of her self-model. She connects the episode back to rank anxiety and the fear that Saitama will erase the B1 position she carefully built.

Saitama's critique becomes explicit:

`お前は順位とか数値とか上っ面の部分を気にしすぎなんだよ`

He follows with:

`それじゃ素の本気を出せないだろ`

`本当に強くなりたいんなら…`

and then the blunt:

`周りを気にすんな!!`

**TF:** V10 converts Saitama's V09 anti-subordination critique into a broader anti-proxy critique: rank and numerical position can become obstacles when treated as identity rather than information. `OPM|V10|extra:数字|images:0200-0202|anchor:Saitama tells Fubuki not to fixate on rank and numbers`

## 6.9 Fubuki's answer is not surrender to Saitama

Fubuki does not become a disciple. After helping address the spider offspring crisis, she says in effect that it does not matter if Saitama or her sister is stronger than she is:

`あなたや姉が私より強くたって関係ない`

`私は私のやり方でいく!`

She also says:

`今回の負けは認めるわ`

and explicitly refuses to give up on her broader aim.

**TF/IR:** the immediate movement is from externally comparative identity toward a more self-authored formulation, not from hierarchy to submission. `OPM|V10|extra:数字|images:0203-0205|anchor:Fubuki admits loss but insists on her own way`

The closing calorie-counting joke prevents a falsely clean transformation. Fubuki may loosen one kind of numerical obsession while continuing to quantify ordinary life. The character has moved; she has not become a symbolic anti-number convert.

# 7. Saitama state through V10

## 7.1 Boredom is permeable to recognized novelty

V10 provides one of the cleanest post-growth curiosity sequences yet. `技` is presented as a kind of strength he may not understand. Saitama:

1. asks for detail;
2. asks Charanko for access to a strong martial artist;
3. accepts a tournament route;
4. tolerates disguise and event bureaucracy to get firsthand exposure;
5. internally notices that he no longer remembers the feeling of a `本当の戦い`.

This strengthens the model that boredom is not emotional flatness. It is partly **low expected informational/experiential value**. When expected novelty rises, effort can rise with it.

## 7.2 Recognition requires legibility in both directions

The Garou first contact is a useful negative test. Saitama has martial-arts curiosity, but he does not recognize Garou as the relevant source. The “Hero Hunter” is processed as a street nuisance. Thus:

`novel object exists` ≠ `Saitama experiences novelty`.

A cue must enter his model as meaningful.

## 7.3 Public image matters less than a person in front of him

`センス` strengthens Saitama's value hierarchy. He came to a contest where his hero suit is the object being evaluated, yet he gives the suit to a bullied stranger because the stranger has an immediate need.

This does not prove that Saitama never wants prizes or approval; V09 already disproved that simplistic reading. It shows that those wants are **defeasible**.

## 7.4 Social network without faction consciousness

When asked to produce a `サイタマ組`, Saitama insists no such group exists and then arrives with Genos, Bang, and King. This is extremely useful for relational modeling. Saitama's social world can become substantial without his narrating it as status, faction, or charisma.

He does not cultivate followers to prove importance. Other people nevertheless become callable relationships.

## 7.5 Advice is plain, sometimes underqualified, but not empty

Saitama tells Fubuki not to obsess over rank, numbers, or what others think. The advice emerges from his own unusual position and can be too simple for another person's institutional reality. Yet V10 gives it immediate explanatory force because Fubuki's `19` and her panic demonstrate exactly how external metrics can colonize self-evaluation.

**Model caution:** Saitama can identify the trap without necessarily providing a complete social strategy for escaping it.

## 7.6 Rule adherence remains context-dependent

Saitama protects strangers at personal inconvenience while also impersonating Charanko to enter a tournament. This is not a contradiction requiring harmonization. His moral seriousness is strongly domain-sensitive: protection and cruelty matter greatly; low-stakes formal procedure often does not command the same reverence.

# 9. Heroism, rank, recognition, and institutions

## 9.1 Rank is information, not essence

V10 repeatedly places formal rank beside contradictory capability evidence:

- Saitama is B-Class but unmeasurably strong;
- Fubuki is B1 but physically scores below a nominal C-Class baseline because the device excludes psychic power;
- Child Emperor's physical score does not capture his technological arsenal;
- King is S-Class for false combat reasons yet genuinely elite in the game domain;
- group headcount fails to predict which side has the strongest game specialist.

The correct institutional conclusion is not “abolish rank.” It is that **rank is a lossy aggregation over multiple domains and histories**.

## 9.2 Hero Association scarcity includes allocation

`タツマキの休日` directly juxtaposes manpower shortage with Tatsumaki's inability to find an assignment. Scarcity can coexist with routing inefficiency, scheduling mismatch, and over-specialization of an exceptional resource.

## 9.3 Threat levels are operational and state-sensitive

The spider-monster measurement sequence demonstrates that a threat can change materially after an initial observation. Disaster-level classification is not a timeless essence stamped onto a creature independently of body state, location, collateral capacity, and available responders.

## 9.4 Hero branding can evaluate a different problem from heroism

The suit contest is not “wrong” to judge clothes. It becomes satirical when the heroic act that saves the event is excluded from the score because it lies outside the rubric. V10 thereby distinguishes **valid narrow evaluation** from **invalid totalizing interpretation**.

## 9.5 Factions and organic networks should not be collapsed

Fubuki's group is deliberately recruited, ranked, and managed. Saitama's callable set is emergent and relational. Both can coordinate action, but they should be tracked as different social structures rather than both labeled “teams.”

# 10. Monsterhood, personhood, body, and transformation

## 10.1 Garou remains human at V10

The strongest direct ontology line is Mumen Rider's `怪人を名乗る人間`. Garou is dangerous enough that the Association may operationally treat him like a monster threat, but the manga continues to distinguish body, social category, self-identification, and moral conduct.

## 10.2 Selective care is evidence against moral-category collapse

Garou's evacuation instruction to Tareo matters because it is voluntary and immediate. It does not cancel hero hunting. It shows that `怪人` as Garou uses it is not yet equivalent to “will maximize harm to all humans.”

## 10.3 Literal monsters remain heterogeneous

V10 includes plant/insect/centipede and other monster forms with different bodies and threat mechanisms. The spider's changing body state directly changes measured physical strength. Elder Centipede is introduced as a recurrent catastrophic threat rather than a generic disposable creature.

No single biological rule should be inferred from this volume.

# 11. Power, technique, and explanatory systems

## 11.1 `技` becomes a first-class explanatory category

The volume begins by explicitly distinguishing technique from raw force and then demonstrates why through Garou's opponent-reading, trajectory inference, timing, and redirection.

## 11.2 `気合い` becomes a Metal Bat-specific performance mechanism

Metal Bat's output is not modeled as a fixed health bar. Injury and activation can increase effectiveness. Preserve the phenomenological rule without inventing a universal equation.

## 11.3 Physical-strength numbers are dimension-specific

`オカメちゃん` measures something real and still fails as a total power scale. Relevant exclusions visible in V10 include:

- psychic ability;
- cybernetic/technological capability;
- weapon systems;
- technique;
- body-state transformation;
- coordination;
- upper-bound saturation.

This is unusually direct textual evidence against treating one scalar as complete combat ontology.

## 11.4 Saitama's “unmeasurable” result is epistemically important

The device does not merely fail; the failure is **misinterpreted**. Child Emperor initially maps “no number” to “too weak.” The later discovery that “too strong” produces the same output demonstrates that capability inference depends on hypothesis space, not just measurement quality.

## 11.5 Martial arts and tournament prestige must remain separable

V10 sincerely establishes that technique matters while simultaneously mocking the cultural machinery around martial expertise. Satire of prestige is not evidence that the underlying technique is fake.

# 12. Humor, satire, and genre mechanics

## 12.1 The Hero Hunter gets mistaken for a mugger

Garou has built an identity around terrorizing heroes. Saitama's `カツアゲする気か?` collapses that grand narrative into ordinary street nuisance. The joke works because both interpretations are locally rational from each character's knowledge state.

## 12.2 The tournament is entered with a party wig

A serious martial hierarchy is penetrated by Saitama through a consumer gag disguise. Formally visible identity becomes maximally unreliable before the tournament can even compare styles.

## 12.3 Tatsumaki suffers from successful peace

A hero complains about work, receives a day off, then becomes unhappy because monsters are being defeated without her. The joke sharpens rather than erases her psychological investment in usefulness.

## 12.4 Costume judging survives a monster attack

The judge's insistence that monster defeat is outside the fashion rubric is bureaucratically coherent and socially absurd. This is classic *One Punch Man* satire: **a system can follow its own rule correctly and still miss the event's morally obvious center.**

## 12.5 `数字` turns power-scaling into an in-world measurement comedy

The extra gives literal numbers to heroes and a monster, then breaks the fantasy that the numbers can settle “who is stronger.” Saitama's missing number and Fubuki's `19` are not just punch lines; they show two opposite domain errors.

## 12.6 The calorie-counting coda prevents ideological overclosure

Fubuki's movement away from rank/strength-number fixation ends with `カロリー計算中`. People do not become clean philosophical symbols because one lesson landed.

# 22. Canonical V10 synthesis

Volume 10 is about **classification under pressure**.

The hospital begins with a classification problem. Garou is strong, but what kind of strong? Mumen Rider answers `技`. Garou is called a monster, but what kind of monster? Mumen answers: a human who calls himself one. Saitama then pursues martial arts because that category might contain something he has not felt or understood in years.

The manga immediately complicates the pursuit. Garou demonstrates genuine technique against Golden Ball and Spring Mustachio, yet when he finally attacks Saitama, Saitama does not recognize him as either Garou or martial novelty. He is just a guy interrupting shopping. The Hero Hunter's symbolic project therefore fails at the level of transmission: **an identity is not socially real merely because its author intends it.**

The widening monster crisis adds another kind of test. Garou, who wants to become the monster that defeats heroes, tells Tareo to run home when an actual monster emergency sounds. That does not absolve his violence. It shows that self-label, body category, and practical moral behavior do not line up automatically.

Metal Bat introduces a second strength vocabulary, `気合い`. His body looks more damaged while his fighting effectiveness rises. Technique and fighting spirit therefore join raw force, psychic power, machines, and other mechanisms already in the corpus. There is no single visible attribute from which combat reality can safely be inferred.

The extras make the same problem social and epistemic. Tatsumaki is so useful that the Association tries to reserve her, then leaves her bored while complaining about manpower shortage. A hero-suit contest correctly judges clothes and thereby places the worst-dressed man who saves the event last. Fubuki's huge faction gives her real organizational depth, but a tiny accidental network around Saitama contains King, Bang, and Genos. Child Emperor builds a machine that really does measure physical strength and then watches the interpretive framework fail on transformation, nonphysical power, and Saitama's upper-bound overflow.

Fubuki is the character most directly forced to confront the lesson. Her B1 rank, group size, and strength numbers are not imaginary. They are simply incomplete. Her panic at a physical score of `19` demonstrates how easily a narrow measure can become self-ontology when status is already a wound. Saitama's answer—stop fixating on `順位`, `数値`, and `上っ面`—is blunt and under-socialized, but the volume has spent hundreds of pages making the diagnosis legible.

Fubuki's response is therefore more important than simple agreement. She says it does not matter if Saitama or her sister is stronger: `私は私のやり方でいく`. The line does not cure her. The calorie-counting coda guarantees that. It marks a small but real shift from **being located by numbers** toward **choosing what to do despite them**.

That is V10's strongest contribution to the V2 corpus: not “numbers are bad,” not “rank is fake,” and not “real strength cannot be measured.” It is that every measure has a domain, every label has an observer, and characters become distorted when they mistake the representation for the whole person.
