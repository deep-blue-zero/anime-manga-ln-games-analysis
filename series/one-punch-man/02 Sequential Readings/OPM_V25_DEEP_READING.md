## 2. Volume orientation

V25 continues the Monster Association raid, but its most important development is epistemic and operational rather than merely escalatory. The volume repeatedly places characters in situations where a first model of the problem is wrong, incomplete, or morally misleading, then asks whether they can update before the error becomes fatal.

The repeated pattern is unusually explicit:

- Flashy Flash correctly reads contextual danger but incorrectly classifies Saitama as a humanoid monster, then as a low-ranked liability, and must repeatedly revise after direct contact;
- Saitama reciprocally classifies Flash as a monster because Flash attacked him, making the misrecognition bilateral rather than one-sided;
- Nyan's V23 language of autonomy is clarified by V25: freedom from the Monster Association does not make him prosocial; he chooses to follow predatory desire without organizational restraint;
- Gearsper forms an image of Tatsumaki-level psychic action from observation, but his control and endurance do not match the borrowed model;
- Drive Knight defeats Nyan by collecting target-specific data, allowing Nyan to form the wrong model of `戦術変形「金」`, and turning Nyan's gap-infiltration habit into the trap;
- Drive Knight then offers an elaborate hidden-actor model of Metal Knight/Bofoi, but the scene itself supplies only speaker-attributed inference, not narrator certification;
- Sekingar updates operational priorities and transfers Drive Knight's data to Genos rather than treating the original raid plan as sacred;
- Genos recognizes an Organization-like energy signature, destroys G5, compares his own upgrade rate favorably, and then returns to the main battlefield once Saitama's participation and the wounded assault team's need are known;
- Child Emperor initially thinks Evil Natural Water may be a more manageable Dragon-level threat than Phoenix Man, then survives by rapidly identifying its water-pressure mechanism and deploying a matchup-specific freezing countermeasure;
- Darkshine enters Garou's path still believing his body to be effectively unwoundable, while PPP's catastrophic failure supplies explicit warning that the opponent model has changed.

V25 therefore advances the raid from **representation versus operational truth** into **model revision under contact**. Raw strength matters constantly, but it becomes useful only through information, mechanism, role fit, and the ability to stop believing an obsolete description.

## 3. Central volume thesis and strongest counter-reading

### Thesis

**V25 treats strength as conditional rather than self-certifying.** Power becomes effective when an actor can correctly model the target, recognize the limits of a role or technique, route information to the person who can use it, and revise assumptions when contact disproves them.

The tankobon's own title `必要なのは強さだけ` is therefore productively unstable. V25 contains spectacular physical and technical strength, but the rescue operation succeeds through much more than output: Food Battler Futoshi's body mechanics, wounded lower-ranked heroes carrying each other, Sekingar's judgment, Drive Knight's intelligence package, Genos's mobility and firepower, and Child Emperor's stored contingency engineering all matter. Even Drive Knight's Nyan victory is explicitly attributed to enemy complacency, prior observation, and conditions aligning rather than to simple rank superiority.

The volume's strongest positive model is not “the strongest wins.” It is closer to:

> **Capability x information x target mechanism x role fit x adaptation x coordination.**

### Strongest counter-reading

V25 should not be converted into an anti-strength argument. Several scenes are possible only because exceptional output exists. Gearsper's desperate telekinesis buys space because it is genuinely powerful; Drive Knight's analysis would be useless without the forms and heat output needed to execute his trap; Genos rescues the evacuation group because his new body can erase pursuing monsters; Child Emperor's engineering still requires a functioning freezing system; and Darkshine's confidence is grounded in a body that has in fact survived extraordinary violence.

The safer formulation is: **strength is necessary in many V25 problems, but no single scalar notion of strength is sufficient to predict success.**

## 4. Sequential close reading

### 4.1 `119` - 遭遇: encounter forces classification to answer to contact

The chapter opens with Puri-Puri Prisoner pursuing Nyan deeper underground. He finds a chained figure and initially frames the situation as rescue: someone is bound, therefore he should free him. The body in front of him is Garou. The chain breaks, Garou attacks, and PPP's new defensive bristle adaptation is insufficient to prevent severe injury.

The scene is brief but structurally important. V24 ended with characters increasingly solving targets by mechanism. V25 immediately reminds us that the first mechanism is often **classification**: who is this person, what state are they in, and what does the visible situation imply? PPP's rescue-oriented default is morally intelligible, but the chained-victim model does not survive contact.

The larger chapter then stages the same problem comedically through Flashy Flash and Saitama.

Flash hears an approaching person and infers an approximately adult-human body mass. Given the location, the lack of a known recruited ally, and the Monster Association context, he concludes that the approaching figure is probably a humanoid monster and attacks before establishing identity (`OPM|V25|chapter:119|image:0021|anchor:Flash classifies footsteps as humanoid monster`). The inference is not stupid. It is a high-threat contextual model with incomplete data. It is still wrong.

Saitama evades. Flash recognizes him as the lower-ranked hero previously seen around Demon Cyborg and begins a second model: perhaps the first evasion was accidental. He tests again. Saitama stops `閃光斬`, warns him, and forces another revision.

The exchange then becomes reciprocal. Saitama asks whether Flash is a monster. Flash protests that he is obviously a hero and identifies himself as S-Class Flashy Flash. Saitama's practical answer is more important than the rank declaration: Flash attacked him. S-Class status therefore does not automatically restore moral or causal legibility.

This is a useful Saitama distinction. He is not status-blind in the sense of being unable to understand categories; he simply refuses to let rank settle a concrete behavioral question. Flash's explanation that it was a mistake is relevant. The prestige label by itself is not.

Flash continues to underestimate him. He warns Saitama that Demon- and Dragon-level monsters make this battlefield unsuitable for him. Saitama reveals that the entire complex is beneath the abandoned district where he lives and gives the mundane reason the location suits him: above all, the rent is free (`image:0031`). Catastrophic underground warfare and household economics coexist without contradiction in his subjective frame.

When Flash names Monster King Orochi, he does not know that Saitama has already defeated Orochi. Saitama's mild interest in whether someone with that title might be strong is misread by Flash as evidence of apprehension. The chapter thus gives us a particularly clean reputation/reality irony: Flash possesses excellent battlefield speed and contextual competence while being radically behind the operational truth of the very opponent hierarchy he is explaining.

Saitama chooses to accompany Flash because Flash expects to encounter strong enemies (`image:0034`). This is not institutional obedience. It is practical route selection motivated by the possibility of worthwhile opposition. Flash warns that he will not protect Saitama; Saitama accepts the arrangement.

By the next movement Flash is already noticing that Saitama's leg strength is far beyond what his rank-based model predicted. The chapter does not fully correct Flash's model yet. It shows the correction beginning.

**V25 delta:** Saitama's low ceremony, status-insensitivity, ordinary-life grounding, and strong-opponent seeking all recur in one scene, while Flash gains an epistemic limitation that is not reducible to arrogance: he updates from evidence, but his institutional priors remain sticky.

### 4.2 `120` - 遊び: autonomy without restraint becomes predation

Nyan reaches the surface support group and makes a declaration that materially revises the ethical valence of his V23 autonomy language. He identifies himself as a Monster Association cadre and then says that he has already quit. From here, he will act as an individual monster according to his own desires (`OPM|V25|chapter:120|image:0046-0048|anchor:Nyan quits organization and follows desire`).

That could superficially look like liberation. V25 immediately refuses the flattering reading.

Nyan explains that he likes playing with “toys” until they break. The lower-ranked heroes become those toys. He injures them while treating their fear, effort, and bodies as entertainment. When their commitment to Waganma gives the resistance a recognizable purpose, Nyan selects a “theme” for the game: tear the child apart in front of them.

This is one of the cleanest ethical controls in the V2 corpus so far:

> **Autonomy is a property of agency, not a morality score.**

V23 established that Nyan rejects forced affection and values self-direction. V25 strengthens the self-direction claim while sharply revising any implication that anti-coercive personal history makes him generally respectful of others' agency. He wants jurisdiction over himself and treats weaker others as objects.

The support heroes provide the opposite structure. They are constrained by duty, wounded, outclassed, and organizationally assigned to evacuation. Yet those external commitments produce protective action rather than dehumanization. They do not become morally better because they obey an institution; they become diagnostic because the duty they accept is directed toward another person's survival.

The chapter therefore separates three things that are easy to collapse:

- freedom from authority;
- possession of agency;
- ethical regard for other agents.

Nyan has the first two and catastrophically lacks the third.

### 4.3 `121` - 窮鼠: an aspirational model can exceed the body that carries it

With the support group near collapse, Gearsper attempts an extreme psychic intervention. He explains that seeing Tatsumaki's power at close range gave him a new `vision` of what psychic force could be. He cannot properly control it, but says he will manifest that vision even at the cost of his life (`OPM|V25|chapter:121|image:0063|anchor:Gearsper Tatsumaki-derived vision`).

The resulting attack is genuinely large. Its page design deliberately borrows some of the scale grammar associated with Tatsumaki: debris, wide psychic control, environmental restructuring. But the visual rhyme must not be confused with equality of capability.

Gearsper's problem is a gap among:

- conceptual model;
- peak output;
- control;
- endurance;
- repeatability.

Observation lets him imagine beyond his old ceiling. It does not instantly give him the embodied system necessary to sustain or reliably reproduce what he imagines.

Nyan survives. Gearsper burns himself out. The other lower-ranked heroes continue trying to protect Waganma anyway. Their resistance matters operationally despite not producing a clean victory: it consumes time, creates movement, and preserves the rescue long enough for Drive Knight to arrive.

The chapter title `窮鼠` is therefore more than a simple “cornered heroes get stronger” slogan. Desperation can produce extraordinary action, but V25 is careful about cost and controllability. Gearsper's act is heroic because of what he risks for others, not because the manga secretly reveals him to be a stable Tatsumaki-class esper.

### 4.4 `122` - 駆動騎士: combat as adversarial modeling

Drive Knight versus Nyan is the volume's clearest technical demonstration of strength mediated by information.

Drive Knight cycles through tactical transformations named for shogi pieces. The point is not a catalogue of forms for its own sake. Each form changes what Nyan thinks the cyborg can do.

Nyan initially enjoys the novelty and treats Drive Knight as another toy. Drive Knight tests chemical/physiological options, discovers that an anesthetic approach is ineffective, and explicitly abandons the live-capture objective. That is a small but important command decision: the goal changes when the constraint changes.

Nyan's most distinctive mechanism is gap infiltration. He can flatten/slip through narrow openings and attack vulnerable internal structure. Drive Knight experiences that threat, disengages a transformation rather than letting Nyan destroy him from within, and gives Nyan reason to believe the gap tactic has been understood but not solved.

The decisive trap comes with `戦術変形「金」`. Nyan and Drive Knight each say, in effect, that they have decided how to kill the other. Nyan believes Drive Knight has repeated an exploitable configuration. Drive Knight lets the belief mature.

When Nyan penetrates the supposed weakness, Drive Knight reveals that the performance increase of `金` is only incidental; its real function is heat (`OPM|V25|chapter:122|image:0109|anchor:Gold form true function is heat`). The target-specific logic is elegant:

1. observe the enemy's favored mechanism;
2. let the enemy learn a partial truth;
3. present the same access route again;
4. make the familiar route the kill zone.

Nyan's last insult calls Drive Knight a mere toy. That is a formal reversal of Nyan's own ontology. He spent the surface battle reducing people to toys; he dies after treating a system he did not understand as a toy.

Drive Knight closes with `目標の排除完了` and orders recovery of the Dragon-level corpse. Even the victory is immediately converted into information/material value.

### 4.5 `123` - 裏の気配: a good threat model is not the same thing as proven truth

The aftermath makes Drive Knight unusually explicit about method.

Sekingar initially interprets Nyan's defeat as evidence that the cadre was not as formidable as feared. Drive Knight rejects that inference. Nyan was strong; a straightforward fight would have made landing a decisive blow extremely difficult. Drive Knight instead attributes the result to enemy complacency, prior collection of Nyan's combat data, and conditions aligning (`image:0114-0115`).

This is one of V25's clearest anti-power-scaling controls. Outcome alone does not identify the relative value of two abstract combat numbers.

Sekingar then raises the ethical cost. If Drive Knight had intervened earlier, some of the support heroes' injuries might have been avoided. Drive Knight answers that fighting before analysis and losing would have worsened the situation. His governing line is explicit: he uses everything available to improve the probability of victory, and assumes the fallen heroes have accepted that level of risk (`image:0116`).

The statement should not be softened. It reveals a real instrumental tendency. Drive Knight treats other heroes' suffering as usable information and partially justifies doing so by imputing consent from professional commitment. That may be strategically coherent; it is also morally contestable. The manga does not supply the injured heroes' consent to his exact decision rule.

Drive Knight says he stayed hidden to gather information. He repeatedly tortured captured monsters for intelligence and delayed while doing so. He avoided transmitting because a traitor could intercept the communication.

The chapter then turns from demonstrated method to **speaker-attributed hidden-actor theory**. Drive Knight asks about the Hero Association's depleted defenses, Watchdog Man, Blast's availability, and the Metal Knight-built headquarters system. He explicitly names Bofoi/Metal Knight as the suspected traitor.

His case is internally coherent:

- a Metal Knight machine was defeated and dissected inside the Monster Association;
- Bofoi's robot systems share data;
- losing such a machine could leak critical technical information;
- the loss may have been intentional, or an enemy may have hacked the system;
- either route could compromise Hero Association defenses;
- Bofoi has been accumulating private military power;
- Drive Knight interprets that accumulation as preparation for eventual domination.

But the epistemic boundary is essential:

- **TF:** Drive Knight makes the accusation.
- **TF:** he supplies these observations and hypotheses as his reasons.
- **IR:** the threat model is coherent enough that Sekingar takes it seriously.
- **OPEN:** whether Bofoi intentionally sacrificed the machine.
- **OPEN:** whether Monster Association actors actually gained command access to Metal Knight systems.
- **OPEN:** whether Bofoi is a traitor.
- **OPEN:** whether Bofoi intends world domination.

Sekingar himself notes that the evidence remains circumstantial. V25 therefore models a distinction especially important for the mystery ledger: **operationally serious suspicion is not narrator-certified ontology.**

When Drive Knight asks whether Blast would respond if headquarters were attacked, Sekingar says that, in his view, Blast is effectively retired/unavailable. He immediately tries to strengthen headquarters security. The absence of reliable top-rank response becomes part of the threat model.

The discussion is interrupted by Machine God G5, already damaged but still dangerous. Drive Knight reports that his energy reserves are nearly exhausted after prolonged operation. Sekingar briefly considers fighting himself with the beam weapon despite his injuries.

Genos arrives because he recognizes the energy signature. He destroys G5 and remarks that his own growth/upgrade speed appears faster than G5's (`image:0140`). This is both a power delta and an epistemic one: Genos has enough prior machine-combat experience to classify the energy source before visual confirmation.

Drive Knight then asks for the situation. The chapter leaves their relationship deliberately cool. They possess overlapping machine-war competence but not established trust.

#### `Engage` omake: Sekingar's body is provenance, not decoration

The attached omake reveals that Sekingar lost his right eye during his own period of hero activity roughly around his engagement. His fiancee wanted him to leave the dangerous path; he promised to return alive and later entered the Association.

The detail materially revises his surface bureaucrat image. His willingness to consider fighting G5 while wounded is not sudden cosplay as a field hero. It comes from a history in which bodily injury, institutional service, and a promise to survive are already connected.

### 4.6 `124` - 必要なのは強さだけ: the rescue succeeds through distributed capability

The chapter title can be read straightforwardly: the battlefield now needs strength. But the chapter itself broadens what “strength” consists of.

Food Battler Futoshi carries Waganma despite having been slashed. His body mechanics turn a gag into a real survival mechanism: the cut followed a fold rather than opening the abdomen catastrophically, and he explains that nutrients can be converted rapidly into fat and burned to raise metabolism/recovery. The body that looks comic supplies endurance and evacuation capacity.

A cluster of wounded support heroes carries other wounded heroes toward the extraction point. Pursuing monsters mock the fact that their hands are full and try to reduce the group further. Genos erases the pursuers and escorts the evacuation.

The sequence matters because the raid's “weaker” layer is not merely dead weight. Lower-ranked heroes preserve people long enough for an S-Class intervention to matter. Medical vehicles, food stores, field command, and route control convert survival into an actual outcome.

Sekingar reports that Waganma's rescue is the operation's highest priority. Food Battler then asks that Genos be released from escort duty to help the S-Class heroes still inside the Monster Association. The request is strikingly non-possessive: someone whose own survival just depended on Genos argues that the stronger asset should go where the remaining need is greatest.

Genos has already told Shicchi that he intended to return to the battlefield regardless of Association orders. The crucial reason is not prestige. `サイタマ先生は既に参戦している`. Saitama is already fighting, and Genos's own operational orientation points back toward the center of danger.

Sekingar reasons rather than simply invoking hierarchy. If S-Class combat strength can reconnect, cadre win probability improves; finishing the raid reduces the chance that powerful enemies can pursue the evacuation. He then transfers Drive Knight's collected main-enemy data to Genos. Genos answers `情報提供 感謝する` and departs.

This is a strong institutional scene precisely because no one actor owns the success. Information moves across distrust boundaries. A wounded commander changes his plan. A lower-ranked hero volunteers to absorb escort risk. An excluded S-Class hero re-enters. Drive Knight's morally compromised intelligence gathering becomes useful to someone who does not share his full worldview.

V25's answer to the chapter title is therefore not “strength alone.” It is that **strength is distributed across bodies, knowledge, logistics, judgment, and specialized roles, then concentrated where the causal bottleneck requires it.**

### 4.7 `125` - 壊れている: breaking a body does not tell you which model has failed

The chapter begins by returning to the consequences of Garou's reactivation. PPP has been catastrophically beaten. His body is damaged enough that he cannot stand and he expects Garou to return before recovery. He apologizes to the people he still calls his boyfriends, preserving the problematic relational register even in sincere concern.

Superalloy Darkshine arrives. PPP warns him that Garou is no longer the opponent described by older Hero Hunter reports. He explicitly notes that Garou has grown far beyond expectation and that PPP could not even scratch him. Darkshine accepts the warning at the level of target identification but not yet at the level of self-model revision.

His answer remains confident: he will smash Garou in PPP's place. He says there is no need to worry because he does not have even a scratch and wonders whether Garou is worthy of receiving his full power (`image:0176-0178`).

This is the correct V25 boundary for Darkshine. The volume **begins** the Garou confrontation; it does not yet establish the later psychological collapse. V23's invulnerability self-model remains active here, now under a much more diagnostic test.

The title `壊れている` is therefore deliberately ambiguous at this boundary. PPP's body is visibly broken. The surrounding arc also suggests that assumptions are breaking. But V25 does not yet authorize a retrospective statement that Darkshine himself has already psychologically broken.

The chapter crosscuts to Child Emperor finding Iaian, Okamaitachi, and Bushidrill down after Evil Natural Water. Iaian warns that the cadre remains nearby and tells him to flee.

Child Emperor initially asks what kind of organism he is seeing and, comparing it with Phoenix Man, briefly thinks the target may be more manageable than its Dragon classification implies. That thought is not treated as settled. A water jet nearly kills him.

He studies the evidence instead. Cuts through metal and the geometry of the attacks suggest an extremely high-pressure water cutter. He separates the fish from the water entity rather than assuming psychic control: the fish appear to coexist with it. He concludes that contact with the cutting stream can be fatal and that ordinary fighting would be extremely dangerous.

Then he identifies a domain-specific countermeasure. If the body is water and cannot autonomously regulate temperature, rapid freezing may neutralize the mechanism. He deploys a stored freezing system and produces an enormous ice formation (`image:0189-0190`).

His closing thought is almost a manifesto for his engineering style: he had not specifically expected an enemy like this, but it is worth building weapons for ideas that might become useful.

The scene strengthens a long-running Child Emperor distinction:

> **Preparedness is not omniscience. It is the ability to carry a sufficiently diverse tool/model library that an unpredicted problem can still be mapped onto an available mechanism.**

The tankobon ends the main narrative with Evil Natural Water frozen. This is a **local observed neutralization state**, not proof at V25 that the entity is permanently destroyed.

### 4.8 Extra `後頭部`: pre-bald Saitama's body can still become an ordinary problem

The extra moves roughly a year and a half into the past, during Saitama's training period. He completes the familiar hundred-push-up routine and showers. Hair is already falling out.

The joke is not only “Saitama is becoming bald.” He becomes preoccupied with whether his crown/back of head is visibly thinning. With only one mirror, he cannot easily inspect the spot. He tries to turn fast enough to see his own rear reflection, escalating an ordinary grooming anxiety into a physically absurd problem.

The ending claims, with comic uncertainty, that for one instant Saitama really did see the back of his own head.

This supplies unusually good low-stakes evidence:

- the body transformation toward baldness was perceptible to him during training;
- he cared enough about appearance to become distracted by it;
- extraordinary physical speed can be recruited to a trivial self-image problem;
- his later flat presentation should not be back-projected into an assumption that he never had ordinary vanity or bodily self-consciousness.

It is also an excellent formal miniature of the series: superhuman capability does not abolish mundane problems; it gives mundane problems ridiculous solutions.

## 5. Saitama state at V25

V25 does not contain a long Saitama arc, but the evidence is high quality because it places several durable rules in one compact encounter and one low-stakes extra.

### 5.1 Status remains causally weak

Flash's S-Class identity does not make Saitama defer to him. Saitama's evaluation starts from behavior: Flash attacked him. Once the mistake is explained, Saitama moves on without converting the status difference into hierarchy.

### 5.2 Recognized route to strong opponents activates engagement

When Flash provides a credible route toward stronger enemies, Saitama chooses to accompany him. This is not the same as generalized excitement about the raid. The activation occurs when the possibility is legible in practical terms.

### 5.3 Home remains an ordinary anchor inside catastrophe

Saitama's explanation of why he lives in the abandoned district includes free rent. The Monster Association's underground headquarters is literally beneath the domestic space he treats through cost-of-living logic.

### 5.4 Pre-bald Saitama was bodily self-conscious

`後頭部` strengthens the distinction between current flatness and historical total indifference. Training-era Saitama worries about hair loss and uses absurd speed in an attempt to inspect it.

**Readiness:** remains `strong`; V25 strengthens low-stakes bodily/ordinary-life coverage and recognized-novelty activation rather than requiring a tier change.

## 6. Ensemble character deltas

### Flashy Flash

- **Goal:** continue the Monster Association operation and eliminate threats efficiently.
- **Strengthened trait:** rapid evidence updating once contact contradicts an inference.
- **Limitation:** institutional/rank priors remain sticky enough that he repeatedly underestimates Saitama after multiple anomalous observations.
- **Relationship delta:** Saitama changes from possible enemy -> low-rank liability -> anomalous companion whose capacity Flash has not yet explained.
- **Readiness:** `moderate` remains appropriate; V25 materially strengthens epistemic and interpersonal behavior.

### Puri-Puri Prisoner

- Garou overwhelms his new defensive adaptation.
- He honestly warns Darkshine rather than protecting S-Class prestige.
- His sincere concern still uses coercively romantic `彼氏達` framing; V23's moral contradiction remains intact.
- `moderate` preserved.

### Nyan

- **STRENGTHEN:** self-direction is explicit enough that he quits the Monster Association.
- **REVISE moral interpretation:** autonomy is used to pursue predatory play and the deliberate suffering of weaker targets.
- **Mechanism:** gap infiltration remains central and becomes the basis of Drive Knight's trap.
- `emerging` remains bounded but substantially stronger.

### Gearsper

- Observation of Tatsumaki creates an aspirational psychic model beyond his normal control.
- Willingness to spend his life for the rescue is explicit.
- High peak does not equal stable capability.
- Candidate `emerging` model: altruistic desperation + unstable psychic output + external-model transfer.

### Drive Knight

V25 provides enough cross-context material for a substantial bounded model:

- treats combat as information acquisition;
- changes objectives when constraints change;
- willingly allows allies' exposure to generate data when he judges early intervention too risky;
- uses torture for intelligence;
- assumes professional heroes accept instrumental risk;
- distinguishes outcome from opponent strength;
- preserves and transfers intelligence;
- builds a coherent but unproven insider-threat theory;
- treats monster bodies as research material for future weaponization;
- is willing to carry his own burden after depletion rather than consume evacuation capacity.

His principal danger as a model is epistemic overconfidence: excellent analytical skill can make a coherent hypothesis feel more certain than the evidence warrants.

### Sekingar

V25 materially deepens him beyond “operations official”:

- challenges Drive Knight's cost-imposition rather than passively admiring results;
- takes circumstantial insider-threat evidence seriously without certifying it;
- attempts to strengthen headquarters security;
- considers fighting personally when no other force is available;
- has a prior hero history and right-eye loss;
- reasons explicitly about changing priorities and combat-force routing;
- transfers intelligence to Genos and permits plan revision.

This is enough for an `emerging` command/field model if the readiness index does not already contain one.

### Genos

- recognizes G5's energy signature before visual confirmation;
- destroys the damaged G5 and explicitly compares upgrade trajectories;
- treats enemy intelligence as useful and thanks Sekingar for it;
- intends to return to the battlefield independent of Association orders;
- Saitama's presence remains a powerful directional anchor;
- accepts rescue/coordination logic rather than reducing the choice to personal rivalry.

`strong` preserved and strengthened.

### Food Battler Futoshi

V25 gives unusually concrete low-ranked-hero evidence:

- carries Waganma while injured;
- explains a real body/metabolic mechanism rather than pure gag invulnerability;
- volunteers to keep handling evacuation so Genos can reinforce the interior;
- frames limited personal capacity as a role contribution, not a reason to abandon responsibility.

This is a useful bounded support-hero model even if monograph readiness remains low.

### Waganma

His response to Food Battler's injuries shows continued movement away from pure entitlement. He notices that the “fat guy” who protected him really was cut despite earlier reassurance. This is not enough for a tier promotion, but it adds another small consequence-awareness datum after V23's Tareo disclosure.

### Superalloy Darkshine

- enters Garou confrontation with confidence fully intact;
- accepts PPP's information without visibly internalizing its implications for his own invulnerability model;
- expects full-power physical force to settle the question.
- V25 is the start of a clean stress test, not yet the result.

`emerging` preserved.

### Child Emperor

- continues prioritizing Tareo as the still-unrecovered child;
- after losing his “ultimate” system, shifts toward regrouping rather than treating himself as useless;

## 7. Heroism, rank, recognition, and institution

V25 is unusually rich in evidence that heroism is a **distributed labor system** rather than an S-Class spectacle.

The surface sequence requires:

- lower-ranked heroes willing to absorb risk;
- a child extraction objective;
- a commander who can revise deployment;
- medical transport;
- food/fuel for biological recovery;
- information gathered by a specialist;
- an S-Class responder who can exploit that information;
- wounded heroes who explicitly release stronger help toward the greater bottleneck.

At the same time, institutional classification continues to misrecognize reality. Flash knows Saitama's low rank and therefore repeatedly underweights direct evidence of anomalous performance. Rank contains information; it is not a complete causal model.

Drive Knight adds another institutional problem: professional commitment is used to justify exposure to instrumental risk. V25 does not settle whether that is legitimate. It makes the assumption visible.

## 8. Monsterhood, personhood, body, and transformation

### Nyan

Monster autonomy is not equivalent to moral autonomy. He exits an institution while preserving a predatory relation to weaker persons.

### Garou

His body/state has advanced enough that PPP's cumulative adaptations fail catastrophically. At this boundary the exact ontological status of the darkened/changed form remains open; do not silently label every visual transformation as completed biological monsterization.

### Evil Natural Water

The entity forces the ontology ledger to preserve a mechanism-first description. V25 establishes:

- water-like body;
- embedded/coexisting fish;
- lethal high-pressure cutting output;
- no demonstrated autonomous temperature regulation before freezing;

V25 does **not** establish ordinary human-like cognition, speech, or permanent death.

### Saitama

The extra gives a benign bodily transformation datum: hair loss during training is visible and psychologically noticed. Body change does not imply monsterhood; the broader corpus must continue distinguishing transformation mechanisms rather than using “changed body” as an ontology shortcut.
