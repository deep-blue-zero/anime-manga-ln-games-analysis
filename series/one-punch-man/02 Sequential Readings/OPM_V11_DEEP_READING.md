# 2. Volume orientation

V10 asked how characters and institutions make strength legible through techniques, ranks, numerical scores, costumes, threat levels, and other bounded proxies. V11 shifts one level upward. Its recurring question is:

> **What happens when strength has to pass through a system that tries to coordinate, constrain, recruit, deploy, or exploit it?**

The answer is not simply “teams are good” or “institutions fail.” V11 supplies several distinct coordination structures and gives each a different failure mode.

- **Metal Bat** can keep escalating through `気合い`, but his sister Zenko can terminate the fight through a relationship-specific promise that Garou cannot override with force.
- **Garou** calls himself a monster yet rejects `怪人協会`; his self-authored monster role is therefore not equivalent to organizational monster membership.
- **Metal Knight** participates remotely and treats Elder Centipede as a weapons/materials research opportunity, demonstrating how technological capability can be operationally useful while motivationally detached from rescue.
- **The Hero Association** can allocate heroes, coordinate emergency calls, and assemble specialist teams, but simultaneous incidents overload response capacity and private incentives can corrupt mission design.
- **The Monster Association** is introduced not merely as a population of monsters but as an organization capable of surveillance, recruitment, discipline, synchronized multi-city attacks, strategic withdrawal, and hostage selection.
- **Bang and Bomb** demonstrate voluntary coordination grounded in kinship and shared purpose rather than bureaucratic assignment.
- **`戦隊`** contrasts fictional team mythology with a real B-class team whose specialized roles are genuinely sensible but whose intelligence is obsolete and whose fifth member is badly matched to the assigned support role.

The result is a more precise institutional thesis than “individuals beat systems.” **Coordination can create real capability, but it is only as good as its information, incentives, role assignment, and relational legitimacy.** V11 repeatedly separates those variables.

## 2.1 Central volume thesis

**V11 treats power as something that must be organized. Strength alone does not determine outcome: relationships can constrain it, institutions can misallocate it, organizations can multiply it, incentives can distort it, and badly assigned roles can make competent individuals interfere with one another. The strongest structures are those whose authority is legible and whose information matches reality.**

That thesis also clarifies Saitama. The volume does not establish a categorical anti-team doctrine. In `戦隊`, Saitama initially becomes interested in team roles, accepts a support assignment, notices evidence that the others miss, rescues every swallowed teammate, and jokingly invokes `連帯責任`. What he rejects is the **formalized role structure as a personal fit**, especially after being poorly used and after seeing the gap between a television team fantasy and real field coordination. The final gaming gag with King immediately prevents “Saitama cannot cooperate” from becoming a stable rule.

## 2.2 Strongest limiting reading

V11 remains transitional. Elder Centipede is not resolved here. The Monster Association’s full scale, leadership, aims, and internal hierarchy remain unknown. Super Fight has only begun, and Suiryu has not yet supplied enough longitudinal context for a mature character model. Garou has rejected recruitment, but whether that rejection persists under different conditions is open. The child-hostage strategy is visible before its downstream consequences are.

Therefore V11 supports a durable **coordination/constraint problem**, but not yet a complete theory of either the Hero Association or Monster Association.

# 3. Sequential close reading

## 3.1 `56撃目 真正面` — Metal Bat as direct protective force

V11 opens where the Elder Centipede incident left off. The monster’s scale exceeds the ordinary “fight the human-sized target in front of you” grammar. Metal Bat recognizes the problem immediately: `このサイズは初めてだな…さて…どうしたもんかね`.

The key evidence is not a clever explanatory monologue. It is role behavior under a threat too large for him to solve cleanly. Civilians are being moved out; when Elder Centipede turns toward them, Metal Bat warns that the charge is coming and puts himself between the monster and the escape route.

**TF:** Metal Bat continues to orient toward civilian evacuation rather than treating the Dragon-level encounter as merely a prestige duel. `OPM|V11|chapter:56|images:0009-0018|anchor:Metal Bat holds Elder Centipede during evacuation`

This strengthens the V10 reading of `気合い`: fighting spirit is embedded in duty rather than floating as pure aggression. V11 will later show that the same activation state can be redirected into a personal fight with Garou, which is precisely why the relational constraint supplied by Zenko matters.

### Visual form

Murata repeatedly scales Metal Bat against segments of Elder Centipede rather than the whole creature. The visual field communicates a mismatch of **human action radius versus disaster scale**. This is an important precursor to the institutional overload later in the volume: the problem is not only “can this hero win?” but “how much of the event can one responder physically cover?”

## 3.2 `57撃目 横槍` — Garou interrupts a disaster response

Garou arrives while the area is already in emergency mode. This matters morally because his hero hunt is no longer abstracted from competing social need. He sees an active Dragon-level incident and nevertheless treats the presence of Metal Bat as an opportunity.

When he confirms Metal Bat is still alive, his response is exuberant:

`生きてんなら話は別だ!`

`S級2匹目ヒット♪`

`こっから先は俺が相手だ テメェを狩るぜ`

**TF:** Garou knowingly redirects Metal Bat from an active large-scale monster emergency into a hero-hunting fight. `OPM|V11|chapter:57|images:0029-0035|anchor:Garou prioritizes S-class hunt during Dragon emergency`

This is a strong qualification to the selective-care evidence introduced by Tareo. Garou is not indiscriminately homicidal toward civilians, but neither does concern for civilian safety function as a general priority hierarchy. His anti-hero project can consume responder capacity during a public emergency.

### Revision control — Garou grievance versus weak-person solidarity

V09 already warned against translating Garou’s childhood identification with the designated loser into broad solidarity with weak people. V11 strengthens that warning. He is capable of selective protection and ordinary rapport, but his chosen project can make other people more vulnerable by incapacitating those actively responding to disasters.

**STRENGTHEN:** “Garou cares about some vulnerable individuals” and “Garou’s hero hunt imposes public-safety externalities” must remain simultaneously true.

## 3.3 `58撃目 大怪蟲` — three coordination systems collide

This chapter is structurally dense because it crosscuts three different ways of organizing force:

1. the Hero Association trying to route responders;
2. Metal Knight using remote technology as a weapons experiment;
3. Garou and Metal Bat turning an emergency zone into a bilateral martial contest.

### 3.3.1 Hero Association capacity is finite and visible

At headquarters, Elder Centipede is discussed as a threat beyond ordinary monsters. Metal Bat is unreachable, A-Class-and-up support has been requested, and personnel ask whether King or Tatsumaki could handle the event. The familiar King myth reappears in the claim that he is apparently already “fighting something” alone.

The scene does two things at once:

- it confirms that the Association has real information-routing and escalation procedures;
- it shows that high-end response capacity is scarce enough that several simultaneous absences become a strategic problem.

**SF:** the institution is not nonfunctional, but its operational model depends heavily on a small number of exceptional assets. `OPM|V11|chapter:58|images:0037-0039|anchor:HQ searches for S-class capacity during Elder Centipede emergency`

Lower-ranked heroes simultaneously carry injured people and perform evacuation work. This is another reason not to model rank as “useful hero / useless hero.” High-end combat and field rescue are different operational functions.

### 3.3.2 Metal Knight: useful intervention, divergent objective

Metal Knight’s machine attacks Elder Centipede from range. The experiment yields information: missile attacks do not meaningfully damage the shell, and the shell’s hardness becomes a materials-research opportunity. The robot explicitly tells nearby heroes:

`邪魔ダ 兵器実験ハ見世物ジャナイ サッサトコノ場カラ立チ去レ`

The language is important. He is not presented as a hero who happens to gather data while rescuing. The **experiment is foregrounded as the purpose** of the deployment.

**TF/SF:** Bofoi’s remote system can contribute meaningful combat pressure while treating the disaster as a test and acquisition opportunity. `OPM|V11|chapter:58|images:0041-0050|anchor:Metal Knight weapon test and shell-material interest`

This strengthens the technology ledger’s separation between capability, deployment, and allegiance. A useful weapon platform does not establish a rescue-centered motive.

### 3.3.3 Garou versus Metal Bat — `技` meets `気合い`

The fight now tests two power logics V10 made legible.

Garou uses reading, redirection, timing, and technique to neutralize attacks that would be catastrophic if they landed cleanly. Metal Bat, however, does not behave like a resource pool that simply drains with injury. As damage accumulates, his attacks become more dangerous. Garou has to update rather than merely repeat his first counter.

**TF/IR:** V11 confirms `気合い` as a real activated combat state with observable performance effects, while preserving a hard limit: Metal Bat is accumulating injury underneath that state. `OPM|V11|chapter:58|images:0051-0082|anchor:Metal Bat escalation under damage vs Garou technique`

The post-fight collapse later makes that distinction explicit. `気合い` sustains action; it does not erase injury.

### 3.3.4 Zenko is a stronger constraint than Garou

The most important character beat in the fight is not another technique. Zenko arrives.

Metal Bat immediately shifts from combat aggression to protective family speech. When Garou prepares to continue, Zenko invokes a promise:

`お兄ちゃんは私の前で暴力見せないって約束したの!`

`だからもう終わり!`

Metal Bat’s response is immediate:

`終わり!! ケンカ終わり!!!`

Garou reacts with disbelief that he is expected to accommodate somebody else’s household rule.

**TF:** Metal Bat possesses a relationship-specific inhibition strong enough to terminate a fight while his `気合い` state is still active. `OPM|V11|chapter:58|images:0083-0087|anchor:Zenko promise ends Metal Bat/Garou fight`

This is high-value modeling evidence because it demonstrates that Metal Bat’s aggressive surface style is not globally dominant. **Zenko has real normative authority over him.** Her authority is not based on rank, physical force, or institutional command. It comes from a prior promise and the sibling relationship.

### 3.3.5 Post-activation crash

Once the fight is actually over, Metal Bat collapses. That is a critical boundary condition on `気合い`.

**STRENGTHEN / LIMIT:** `気合い` can maintain or increase performance despite injury; V11 does not support interpreting it as limitless power, regeneration, or immunity to accumulated damage. `OPM|V11|chapter:58|image:0092|anchor:Metal Bat collapses after fight termination`

### 3.3.6 Monster Association surveillance begins before recruitment

Phoenix Man and Sludge Jellyfish have been watching Garou. Phoenix Man judges a human capable of this much combat performance as potentially useful.

**NEW:** Garou has become an object of organizational evaluation by monsters before he has any relationship to them. `OPM|V11|chapter:58|image:0088|anchor:Monster Association agents evaluate Garou as recruitable`

That asymmetry matters in the next chapter: Garou’s self-definition and the organization’s attempt to classify/use him are not the same process.

## 3.4 `59撃目 お前だけ` — Garou refuses organizational monsterhood

### 3.4.1 Garou dislikes being observed

Garou detects the surveillance and attacks its source. His wording is direct:

`ずっと感じてた気持ちの悪い視線の正体はテメェだな`

`俺は観察されんのが大嫌いなんだよ`

This adds a useful autonomy variable to his behavioral model. Garou is willing to make himself publicly notorious, yet he dislikes becoming somebody else’s object of study or recruitment.

### 3.4.2 `怪人協会?` -> `興味ねぇ 失せろ`

Phoenix Man offers Garou an invitation to `怪人協会`. Garou looks at it and tears it up:

`興味ねぇ 失せろ`

This is one of V11’s most important ontology/identity controls.

**TF:** Garou’s self-authored claim to be a `怪人` does **not** imply identification with an organized community of actual monsters. `OPM|V11|chapter:59|images:0094-0098|anchor:Garou rejects Monster Association invitation`

The distinction can now be stated strongly:

- Garou uses `怪人` as a chosen oppositional role and identity project.
- The Monster Association is an external organization attempting to classify and recruit him for strategic use.
- Garou rejects that organizational capture.

**REVISE / STRENGTHEN:** V08-V10 “self-authored monster aspiration” becomes more precise at V11: **self-authored and anti-affiliative** at this boundary. It is not yet biological transformation and not institutional membership.

### 3.4.3 The Monster Association reveals strategic patience

Phoenix Man does not immediately turn recruitment failure into a death match. He says the destroyed invitation will be overlooked and implies that continued hero hunting will create another meeting.

This is the first clear sign that the Monster Association can subordinate immediate insult to longer-term strategic interest.

**NEW:** organization-level patience and recruitment policy enter the model. `OPM|V11|chapter:59|images:0096-0098|anchor:Phoenix Man preserves future recruitment option`

### 3.4.4 Rhino Wrestler and scored combat

Rhino Wrestler evaluates hero attacks numerically. His scoring is comic, but the behavior connects to V10’s bounded-proxy problem. The numbers measure how much the attacks impress or affect **him**; they do not measure total hero worth, rescue competence, or matchup-independent capability.

The important new layer is organizational discipline. Rhino Wrestler wants to keep fighting; Phoenix Man orders withdrawal because the operation has a larger timetable. He also explains that Metal Knight’s machine is remotely operated and not worth wasting resources on.

**SF:** the Monster Association is already doing cost/benefit analysis rather than simply unleashing aggression. `OPM|V11|chapter:59|images:0099-0102|anchor:Phoenix Man disciplines Rhino Wrestler and avoids remote robot attrition`

### 3.4.5 Hostage strategy

The monsters have captured a parent and child. Phoenix Man orders that the child alone be taken because the human response will be more interesting/useful that way.

**NEW:** hostage-taking is deliberate strategic communication, not incidental predation. `OPM|V11|chapter:59|images:0102-0103|anchor:child selected as hostage to provoke response`

This is the point where “Monster Association” becomes more than a name. It is an actor that can plan around the psychology and institutional incentives of its enemy.

### 3.4.6 Simultaneous attacks create systemic overload

The chapter widens across cities. Heroes who would normally be adequate local responders are isolated, outmatched, or face poor matchups. Headquarters recognizes the pattern:

`各市で同時多発的に`

`災害レベル鬼以上の怪人が一斉に出現するなんて…!`

`対応が追いつかない!`

This is a major institutional transition.

**STRENGTHEN:** Hero Association weakness is no longer only recruitment quality, rank politics, or isolated allocation failure. V11 demonstrates **simultaneous-demand overload** against coordinated adversarial timing. `OPM|V11|chapter:59|images:0107-0114|anchor:multi-city simultaneous incidents exceed response capacity`

Saitama’s tournament calm is juxtaposed with this crisis. The joke should not be converted into moral indifference: V11 does not show him receiving a complete briefing and refusing to help. He is operating inside a different information environment.

## 3.5 `60撃目 入場` — prestige field versus actual capability

The Super Fight arena is a controlled social system with seedings, past champions, schools, reputations, names, uniforms, and audience expectations. V11 deliberately intercuts this legible prestige field with the much less controllable monster emergency outside.

### 3.5.1 Suiryu enters as a coherent but still narrow model

Suiryu is introduced as a returning high-level competitor who has been absent for years and is currently motivated by prize money rather than institutional honor. He recognizes killing intent from Bakuzan without visible anxiety and treats the tournament as entertainment and a source of strong opponents.

**NEW / EMERGING:** Suiryu enters as an independent martial actor whose current visible policy combines confidence, pleasure-seeking, money motive, and interest in strong opponents. `OPM|V11|chapter:60|images:0126-0129|anchor:Suiryu tournament motive and calm response to Bakuzan hostility`

One volume of tournament framing is not enough for `moderate` readiness.

### 3.5.2 Saitama remains socially illegible as “Charanko”

Other contestants evaluate Saitama through martial legitimacy: how he ties his uniform, what school he belongs to, whether his master is respectable, and whether he deserves to be here. Saitama’s attention often lands elsewhere. When threatened by a younger-looking martial artist, his salient reaction is surprise that the man is younger than he expected.

This is another status-insensitivity case, but with a useful qualification. Saitama **does care about whether martial arts contain something new**. He just does not translate prestige claims into deference.

### 3.5.3 Bang’s absence is legible differently inside and outside the dojo

Competitors complain that Bang abandoned tournament/judging responsibilities and is too absorbed in hero activity. Their framing is not authoritative on Bang’s motives. V11 then shows Bang and Bomb actively searching for Garou.

This is a strong example of public/peer inference being incomplete rather than wholly false. Martial-world observers correctly detect a priority shift but not the full reason.

### 3.5.4 Bang and Bomb coordinate by choice

Bang’s older brother Bomb is now directly active beside him. Bang first tries to manage the danger, but the pair proceed together. Against a monster, their combined action rapidly ends the encounter; the aftermath is immediately reframed around the actual task:

`ガロウを捜そう`

`奴がこうなっちまう前にな`

**NEW / STRENGTHEN:** Bang/Bomb is a kinship-based cooperative system with shared concern over Garou, not a rank-assigned Hero Association team. `OPM|V11|chapter:60|images:0134-0143|anchor:Bang and Bomb search for Garou after joint monster defeat`

The phrase about acting before Garou “becomes like this” should remain prospective. At V11 it expresses fear about Garou’s trajectory, not proof of any later transformation state.

## 3.6 `61撃目 ダークホース` — controlled tournament, uncontrolled cities

The chapter is built on crosscutting asymmetry. Inside the arena, strength is bracketed, named, announced, and judged. Outside it, multiple cities are entering a coordinated emergency with uncertain matchups and incomplete information.

### 3.6.1 First-round tournament outcomes are rapid classification events

Some competitors dominate immediately. Others who enter with reputations or violent confidence are exposed in seconds. The tournament therefore becomes another proxy machine: seed, school, past record, and announcer framing are useful but not exhaustive.

Saitama’s first opponent Zakos is dispatched in roughly four seconds. The visual and narrative treatment is compressed. Saitama does not “perform martial arts” for prestige; a physically trivial opponent receives a physically trivial response.

**TF:** Saitama’s sought-after martial novelty does not force ceremonial engagement with an opponent who cannot supply it. `OPM|V11|chapter:61|images:0172-0175|anchor:Saitama ends first Super Fight match almost immediately`

Suiryu notices the result. His final expression and `これは…決勝の相手が決まったかな?`-type evaluation mark Saitama as newly interesting to him, but V11 stops before a substantive relationship exists.

### 3.6.2 Crisis montage defeats single-number thinking again

Outside, different monsters present different matchups and different threat profiles. Some heroes are badly mismatched; other local forces are simply too thin. One field observer’s thought captures the operational problem: dispersed heroes are being defeated sequentially because there are too many monsters and the individual danger levels vary.

The Monster Association’s strength is therefore not reducible to “every monster is stronger than every hero.” Its advantage includes **distribution and synchronization**.

**SF:** adversarial coordination converts heterogeneous monsters into a system-level threat. `OPM|V11|chapter:61|images:0160-0171|anchor:dispersed heroes lose across simultaneous city attacks`

# 4. `番外編 戦隊` — the volume’s coordination thesis made explicit

This extra is not disposable parody. It functions as a controlled laboratory for V11’s central question.

## 4.1 Fictional team mythology first

Saitama and King watch the team-hero show `ZOOMEN`. King explains the appeal: each member has an individual role, but the group’s final strength comes from combining those roles through bonds and trust. Saitama recognizes that team play is interesting to watch because the members have distinct techniques and functions.

Genos arrives and calls team heroes a `弱者の発想`, reasoning that a group with insufficient total power could simply be annihilated together. His critique is not wholly wrong as arithmetic, but the episode immediately gives a real-world case where team specialization is operationally rational.

This is important for Genos modeling: he initially interprets team value primarily through **aggregate combat power**, while King’s description foregrounds differentiated roles and trust.

## 4.2 Hero Association Z-branch specialist team

The Association assembles five B-Class heroes to capture the escaped monster Urokodon alive:

- Wild Horn acts as field leader/frontline protector;
- Pink Hornet contributes flexible martial response and route denial;
- Smell Master handles scent/lure work;
- Trap Tengu specializes in capture traps;
- Saitama / `ハゲマント` is added as extra manpower.

The plan is not absurd. The four specialists have complementary functions, and the mission requires **capture rather than killing** because the monster’s blood has potential medical value.

**SF:** V11 provides direct evidence that lower-ranked hero teams can be organized around specialization rather than merely headcount. `OPM|V11|extra:戦隊|images:0181-0187|anchor:Z-branch B-class specialist capture team`

This strengthens V09’s Fubuki Group correction: organizational hero labor can be real even when rank politics or incentives are ugly.

## 4.3 Saitama is willing to accept a support role

Saitama asks what his role is. Wild Horn effectively gives him map duty. Saitama accepts it. He even starts to enjoy the sentai-like feeling:

`…チームっぽくなってきたな`

He proposes color/codename logic after watching `ZOOMEN`, only to be reminded that his real hero name is `ハゲマント`. The embarrassment punctures the fantasy immediately.

**REVISE:** Saitama’s anti-faction stance does not imply inability or refusal to operate under somebody else’s practical plan. He can accept a low-status support task when the task is concrete. `OPM|V11|extra:戦隊|images:0183-0186|anchor:Saitama accepts map/support role and briefly enjoys team framing`

## 4.4 Team failure begins with information and role mismatch

The operation has two hidden defects.

First, the Association’s intelligence is stale. Urokodon was classified/assessed when smaller and weaker. Poor containment and stress have allowed it to grow into a form whose escape implies at least Demon-level physical output.

Second, the team’s fifth member is the strongest person present but is assigned to map support because the institution does not know what he is.

This is almost a pure demonstration of V10’s bounded-proxy thesis extended into V11’s coordination thesis:

> **A team can be well designed relative to the wrong model and still fail.**

The Association has valid role logic but invalid state information.

## 4.5 Saitama is bad at the assigned role—but notices a useful anomaly

Saitama turns the map incorrectly and drifts off route. Wild Horn criticizes him for lacking team discipline and stresses that in emergencies individual action is dangerous because failure becomes `連帯責任`.

Saitama’s deviation is real; the text does not make him secretly perfect at teamwork. Yet the deviation also puts him near a vending machine with damage matching the escaped target. He notices the clue.

**TF/IR:** Saitama is genuinely poor at formal map/team procedure while still capable of useful concrete observation outside the plan. `OPM|V11|extra:戦隊|images:0188-0190|anchor:map failure and vending-machine clue`

This is precisely why “Saitama is bad at teamwork” is too coarse. The failure is **role- and procedure-specific**.

## 4.6 Institutional incentive corruption is real

The extra then reveals why Urokodon must be captured alive. An Association-side executive expects its blood to have profitable regenerative/medical value. Another report notes that the reinforced containment glass should have required at least Demon-level power to break. The monster’s growth was accelerated by bad management and stress.

The mismatch is therefore not random bad luck. It is partly produced by the institution’s own handling of a living asset.

**NEW / STRENGTHEN:** Hero Association dysfunction now includes **private/organizational incentive distortion and information withholding**, not merely rank politics or insufficient manpower. `OPM|V11|extra:戦隊|images:0191-0192|anchor:medical-profit motive and concealed monster escalation`

The B-Class team is placed into a capture mission without being adequately informed that the target has become far more dangerous.

## 4.7 The specialist plan works—until the premise breaks

The team’s tools initially function. Traps bind the target, scents and route control shape its movement, and members cover one another. The extra therefore refuses the simplistic interpretation that teamwork itself is incompetent.

But Urokodon’s upgraded physical power exceeds the plan’s assumed envelope. The monster eventually swallows the team.

**SF:** failure comes from a **model-to-reality mismatch**, not from specialization being useless. `OPM|V11|extra:戦隊|images:0193-0197|anchor:specialist capture plan works locally but fails against upgraded target`

## 4.8 Saitama rescues the team through `連帯責任`

Saitama encounters Urokodon separately. When he realizes his teammates are in the monster’s stomach, he does not simply leave because the team operation went badly. He demands that they be released, is swallowed, finds the others, and exits explosively with everyone in tow.

His line during the rescue is the extra’s punchline and its ethical hinge:

`連帯責任ってことで!`

The phrase was introduced by Wild Horn as a disciplinary principle. Saitama repurposes it as **collective rescue responsibility**.

**TF:** Saitama can internalize a team norm selectively even while rejecting the formal team structure as a personal fit. `OPM|V11|extra:戦隊|images:0198-0203|anchor:Saitama rescues swallowed team under 連帯責任 framing`

## 4.9 “I am not suited to this” is not “teams are false”

Inside the monster, Saitama remarks that the team operation failed and that he has learned it does not suit him. Afterward he reflects on the gap between television-team ideal and field reality and concludes that working alone fits him better.

This should be modeled as a **self-fit judgment**, not a universal doctrine.

Evidence against universalizing it appears inside the same extra:

- he was interested in team roles;
- he accepted the support assignment;
- he noticed a useful clue;
- he rescued the others as a collective;
- the specialist plan itself had real functional logic;
- the mission was sabotaged by stale intelligence and incentive distortion;
- the closing game scene with King immediately makes Saitama participate in a practical team context again.

**REVISE:** Saitama is **not faction-minded and is poorly suited to rigid assigned-role field teams**, but he remains capable of cooperation, collective responsibility, and organic coordination.

# 5. `巻末おまけ 武器` — Metal Bat’s weapon identity

A short flashback shows Metal Bat before/around S-Class recognition. The Association offers to prepare whatever weapon or equipment he wants as a special benefit. The expected answer would be an advanced custom weapon.

Metal Bat asks for:

`折れないバットをくれ`

and then clarifies:

`とびきり頑丈な金属バットをくれ`

The request is revealing because it compresses his fighting identity into one requirement: preserve the familiar form, eliminate the failure mode.

**TF:** Metal Bat does not seek technological complexity for its own sake; his preferred equipment policy is continuity of embodied fighting style plus extreme durability. `OPM|V11|extra:武器|images:0208-0209|anchor:request for exceptionally durable unbreakable metal bat`

# 6. Character-state transitions

## 6.1 Saitama — `strong`, strengthened without tier change

### New evidence

1. **Recognized martial novelty remains motivationally active**, but weak tournament opposition is processed with minimal ceremony.
2. **Prestige remains low-salience**: martial credentials, intimidation, seniority, and tournament reputation do not produce deference.
3. **Formal teamwork is a real domain limitation.** Map duty, route discipline, and role-bound coordination do not fit him naturally.
4. **He can accept subordinate/support responsibility** when a practical mission gives him a role.
5. **He notices concrete anomalies even while failing formal procedure.**
6. **He rescues the entire team and repurposes `連帯責任` as a rescue principle.**
7. **His conclusion is self-specific:** team operation does not suit him; it is not a philosophical denial of cooperation.
8. **Organic cooperation remains present** through King/Genos and the closing game gag.

### Model transition

**STRENGTHEN / REVISE:** “Saitama is anti-team” is too strong. Better:

> Saitama resists faction identity and rigid role structures, especially when they substitute procedure for direct problem solving. He can nevertheless accept practical authority, cooperate, rescue collectively, and participate in emergent teams. His strongest difficulty is not selfishness but mismatch between overwhelming general combat capability and narrowly assigned institutional roles.

Genos’s main V11 contribution is conceptual rather than combat-driven. This is consistent with his power-maximizing frame but incomplete relative to the extra’s demonstration of specialization.

**STRENGTHEN / QUALIFY:** Genos understands cooperation tactically but can default to aggregate combat-power evaluation when discussing team value abstractly. No V11 evidence justifies lowering his already strong reconstructive readiness.

## 6.3 Garou — `moderate`, strongly strengthened

- aversion to surveillance;

# 8. Heroism, rank, institution, and organizational structure

## 8.1 Lower-ranked heroes remain operationally real

V11 repeatedly shows lower-ranked heroes evacuating injured people, fighting local incidents, and carrying out specialist capture roles. This directly resists any synthesis that turns rank hierarchy into moral worth or operational usefulness.

## 8.2 Hero Association failure now has four separable mechanisms

By V11, institutional weakness should no longer be written as one undifferentiated “corruption” claim.

1. **capacity shortage** — simultaneous incidents exceed available responders;
2. **information lag** — Urokodon’s actual condition exceeds mission intelligence;
3. **misclassification / role assignment** — Saitama is used as generic B-Class manpower because the institution does not know his capability;
4. **incentive distortion** — live capture is prioritized because a decision-maker expects medical/profit value from the monster.

These mechanisms can interact but should remain analytically distinct.

## 8.3 Monster Association becomes an organization, not merely a label

V11 supports the following organization-level functions directly:

- intelligence collection;
- surveillance;
- recruitment;
- member discipline;
- operational timing;
- strategic withdrawal;
- matchup/resource reasoning;
- hostage selection;
- synchronized multi-city action.

Its leadership and deeper hierarchy remain open.

## 8.4 Rank remains a proxy, not nothing

B-Class specialists are genuinely useful. S-Class responders are genuinely scarce high-end assets. A-Class heroes can be strong enough to matter locally and weak enough to fail badly under a poor matchup. V11 continues V10’s bounded-proxy rule rather than overturning it.

# 9. Power and technique

## 9.1 `気合い` receives its strongest boundary condition yet

Metal Bat’s performance can rise through injury, but his body still accumulates damage and collapses once the fight terminates.

**Canonical V11 formulation:**

> `気合い` is a combat activation/amplification state with demonstrated performance effects under injury; V11 does not establish limitless growth, regeneration, or immunity to physiological cost.

## 9.2 Garou’s `技` remains adaptive rather than static

Against Metal Bat, Garou cannot rely on a one-time read because the opponent’s effective output changes during the encounter. He has to continually update.

**STRENGTHEN:** Garou’s martial cognition is recursive: opponent model -> counter -> observe changed state -> revise.

## 9.3 Team strength is not simple addition

`戦隊` shows three distinct meanings of “team power”:

- fictional synergy through complementary roles and trust;
- Genos’s aggregate-power critique;
- real specialist coordination whose success depends on correct intelligence and task fit.

No single scalar “team strength” captures all three.

# 11. Satire, genre, and public narrative

## 11.1 Sentai parody is analytically functional

`ZOOMEN` is not only a genre joke. Its clean color-coded team logic becomes a contrast model for messy field organization. The real team has no matching costumes, no stable narrative roles, no omniscient script, and incomplete intelligence.

The parody therefore exposes a gap between **genre legibility** and **operational reality**.

## 11.2 Tournament prestige and emergency reality coexist without knowing each other

The Super Fight announcer can confidently produce histories, schools, seeds, and expectations while the outside world is undergoing multi-city monster attacks. This crosscutting is a formal satire of systems that are highly legible internally yet locally blind to larger context.

## 11.3 King’s public myth continues to absorb unseen activity

Association staff still assume King is engaged in some high-level battle because his ordinary life is not available to them. V11 therefore preserves the public-King phenomenon as an ongoing social fact even after Saitama privately knows the truth.
