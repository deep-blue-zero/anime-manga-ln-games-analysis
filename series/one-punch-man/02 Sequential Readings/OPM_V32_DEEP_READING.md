## 2. Orientation and central thesis

**Prospective synthesis frozen after complete sequential review and Japanese/register PASS.**

V32 tests the difference between making force effective and making its meaning final. Garou and Metal Bat keep quarreling while protecting helicopters; their attacks accidentally resonate, but rescue also requires evacuees' marksmanship, psychic assistance, a landing net and later boats. Saitama breaks the sea threat, then still has to manage the ship, wave and endangered shore. Physical victories repeatedly create or expose further duties. The question is not only who can destroy the largest body, but who notices the people left inside the consequences (`162–165 / 0010–0150`).

Garou turns the centipede's destruction into a personal graduation: the remembered unbroken tile is finally answered, and what was called punishment becomes his “blessing.” Yet the emphatic first-person completion is immediately qualified by a forgotten uncertainty, the mistaken belief that absent heroes are finished, and a stranger who refuses his monster identity. Saitama recognizes protective conduct without accurately diagnosing every bodily change. Tareo recognizes a continuing person through fear and repeated care, then refuses the victory demonstration Garou wants to give him. Their refusal exposes the dependence of “absolute evil” on other people's recognition (`164–167 / 0112–0126,0157–0179,0201–0213`).

The main ending makes the task more precise. Saitama asks whether Tareo wants Garou stopped rather than defeated, receives a nod and accepts responsibility. This is an undertaking with another person's desired outcome, not a completed moral cure. Garou's will and transformation remain active beyond the boundary (`167 / 0222–0223`). The bonus compresses grand precautions into a reflected-beam gag, extending the gap between impressive self-description and effective understanding without solving the main confrontation (`反射 / 0224–0225`).

## 3. Strongest counter-reading

The strongest limiting reading is that this remains an action spectacle that resolves immediate danger through exceptional force and familiar rescue conventions. Garou's protection does not cancel hero hunting, unprovoked assault, coercion or his threat to eliminate rescuers alongside attackers. His self-described nonlethal opening against Saitama still aims to break another person's spirit. Tareo's personal “best hero” is meaningful testimony, not a universal acquittal, and his return to danger is courageous but unsafe. Saitama's dismissive cosplay language overlooks actual bodily change; his earlier counterattack drives danger toward evacuees, and his provocation happens beside the child before he moves the fight. Neither possesses complete information or immaculate practice.

The volume supports this counter-reading in concrete details rather than inviting their omission. What exceeds it is the persistent separation of strength, intention, attribution and outcome: private protective thoughts contradict Garou's exclusive witness rationale; explicit narration makes accidental resonance distinct from chosen rescue cooperation; a remembered personal shortfall does not establish absolute mastery; and the final question differentiates stopping from defeating. The thesis therefore concerns an unresolved test of purpose and recognition, not an achieved reconciliation, an anti-force doctrine, or proof that every apparent monster is secretly good.

## 5. Saitama state

Saitama's main additions are **practical adaptation under enormous power**, **specific rather than omniscient recognition**, and **care that specifies an outcome**.
