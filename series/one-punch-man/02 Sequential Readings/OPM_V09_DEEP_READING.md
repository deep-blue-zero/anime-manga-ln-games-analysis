## 2. Volume orientation

V08 introduced Garou as an unusually strong human who called himself a would-be monster. V09 makes that claim legible enough to model without importing later ideology. It also gives Fubuki her first sustained characterization and places her institutional strategy directly against Saitama's hero ethic. The result is a volume unusually concentrated on **rank, winner/loser assignment, group protection, and the difference between becoming strong and arranging the field so that one cannot lose**.

Three structures repeatedly rhyme:

1. **Garou** remembers a childhood justice narrative in which the popular hero is structurally guaranteed to defeat the hated monster. He chooses the monster side and vows to alter the script by becoming strong enough that the designated loser wins.
2. **Fubuki** occupies B-Class Rank 1, recruits lower-ranked heroes into a faction, and suppresses people who threaten to pass her. Her security depends partly on controlling the ranking environment rather than simply surpassing everyone above her.
3. **Saitama** rejects both the status pressure and the premise that strength consists in accumulating weaker subordinates. His answer to Fubuki is not an anti-social declaration that groups are inherently contemptible; it is that heroism requires facing the genuinely strong and dangerous rather than using hierarchy to protect one's own position.

The volume then tests the same problem physically. Genos and Sonic escalate a contest of speed and technique until Saitama's intervention makes the entire prestige contest look small. Garou, by contrast, proves that **technique can overturn a raw-power hierarchy** when he uses Bang's martial art against Tanktop Master.

### Central volume thesis

**V09 treats hierarchy as something characters can obey, manipulate, reject, or invert. Its strongest contrast is not simply hero versus monster; it is between strength pursued as an answer to vulnerability and status systems used to preassign who is allowed to win.**

Garou's childhood grievance, Fubuki's B-Class fortress, hero-name bureaucracy, Sonic's speed pride, Genos's adaptive escalation, Tanktop Master's raw-force confidence, and Saitama's indifference to nominal status all participate in that argument.

### Strongest limiting reading

The volume does **not** yet prove that Garou's monster project is a coherent emancipatory philosophy. His sympathy for the designated loser coexists immediately with sadistic provocation, indiscriminate assault, and delight in becoming feared. Likewise, Fubuki's faction cannot yet be reduced to pure cowardice or pure predation: she provides organization, identity, resources, and mutual activity to subordinates, and the bonus chapter shows a functioning social/economic unit rather than disposable drones. V09 establishes contradictions that later material must resolve; it does not license an idealized Garou or demonized Fubuki.

# 3. Sequential close reading

## 3.1 `41撃目 怪人になりたい男` — Garou chooses the assigned loser

### 3.1.1 The recruitment hall becomes Garou's demonstration stage

The Hero Association has invited criminals and fighters into an emergency recruitment structure because conventional capacity is inadequate. Garou uses the meeting for the opposite purpose. Instead of accepting a path into legitimate service, he turns the room into a public demonstration that the Association cannot convert raw combat capacity into aligned heroism merely by opening a recruitment channel.

**TF/VF:** Garou openly antagonizes the assembled criminals and attacks them rather than negotiating a place in the Association. His grin, relaxed stance, and escalating verbal provocation make the violence self-authored rather than defensive. `OPM|V09|chapter:41|image:0012-0036|anchor:Garou turns recruitment hall into a massacre/demonstration`

The scene strengthens V08's institutional warning. The Association's problem is not just insufficient manpower. It is that **combat capability and prosocial alignment are separable variables**. A policy that suspends ordinary character filters under crisis can recruit useful outsiders, but it can also deliver an audience and concentrated targets to someone like Garou.

### 3.1.2 The childhood flashback supplies the first real ideological kernel

The load-bearing V09 evidence is the short childhood memory around the `Justice Man` hero narrative.

Garou is disturbed by the automatic moral/narrative asymmetry: the popular side wins, the hated side loses. The concise formulation `人気者が勝って 嫌われ者が負けるなんて悲劇だ` is not a mature theory of justice, but it is an unusually clean statement of his emotional premise. `OPM|V09|chapter:41|image:0014-0016|anchor:popular winner and hated loser as tragedy`

Two distinctions are necessary.

First, Garou is not yet arguing that every monster is morally innocent. He is reacting to **preassigned narrative status**. His grievance is with a world in which popularity and the hero label seem to guarantee legitimacy and victory before the contest begins.

Second, the response is not to make the system more impartial. He chooses a far more personal solution: `だったら俺が誰にも負けない` and then a vow to become the strongest monster and change the scenario. `OPM|V09|chapter:41|image:0016-0018|anchor:Garou vows to become the unbeatable monster and alter the scenario`

That move is diagnostic for his early model. Garou converts an insight about structural asymmetry into **self-mythologizing combat vocation**. He does not initially seek procedure, persuasion, institutional reform, or protection of weak outsiders. He intends to become the exception so overwhelming that the script breaks around him.

### 3.1.3 Named technique and anti-prestige combat judgment

Garou's behavior toward the elderly fighter and other criminals also establishes a martial evaluation style. He is unimpressed by labels, names, or reputation detached from actual execution. His challenge about whether a punch contains conviction and his contempt for flashy named techniques without sufficient strength make him a useful contrast with the series' constant technique inflation. `OPM|V09|chapter:41|image:0018-0028|anchor:Garou evaluates actual force and commitment rather than labels`

This does **not** mean Garou is anti-technique. Chapter 47 will prove the opposite. He is anti-empty prestige. A technique matters when embodied in competent motion.

### 3.1.4 Violence, theatricality, and the problem with romanticizing the underdog claim

The chapter actively prevents a clean heroic reading of Garou. Once he controls the room he continues the assault and announces the beginning of his campaign. He enjoys fear and domination. His willingness to treat other marginal/criminal people as expendable bodies also undermines any simple claim that he fights *for* the socially despised.

**IR:** At V09, the safest formulation is therefore: **Garou identifies with the role of the designated loser, but he has not demonstrated solidarity with losers as persons.** He wants to seize and weaponize the monster position.

## 3.2 `42撃目 フブキ組` — security by ranked incorporation

### 3.2.1 Fubuki enters as an organization, not just an esper

Fubuki's first sustained appearance is deliberately collective. She arrives with B-Class Rank 2 `マツゲ` (Eyelashes) and Rank 3 `山猿` (Mountain Ape). The chapter title is not simply `フブキ`; it is `フブキ組`. `OPM|V09|chapter:42|image:0037-0041|anchor:Fubuki introduced through her ranked group`

That framing matters. Her power is psychic, but her social strategy is organizational. She maintains B-Class Rank 1 and makes herself a gatekeeper for new B-Class heroes. The group offers affiliation and protection while also functioning as rank control.

### 3.2.2 Saitama's first response strips away aura

The group arrives with rehearsed prestige, but Saitama initially does not know who Fubuki is. His plain `誰?` response is not a deliberate insult; it is a genuine absence of status recognition. `OPM|V09|chapter:42|image:0049-0052|anchor:Saitama does not recognize Hellish Blizzard`

This is important for Saitama because his status insensitivity is not merely rebellious rhetoric after he learns someone's rank. He often fails to internalize the prestige map in the first place.

### 3.2.3 Recruitment becomes coercion

Fubuki offers Saitama entry into her faction and protection of his B-Class position, but the offer is backed by a threat: people who do not enter the group can be obstructed or broken so that they cannot climb past her. The coercive logic becomes explicit when refusal produces violence. `OPM|V09|chapter:42|image:0052-0059|anchor:Fubuki offers protection and threatens rank suppression`

The scene therefore establishes a mixed institution:

- **real benefit:** membership, coordination, information, identity, pooled resources;
- **real coercion:** suppression of autonomous competitors;
- **real insecurity:** Fubuki's need to hold Rank 1;
- **real competence:** she is not bluffing about being unusually powerful by B-Class standards.

The mistake would be to select only one side.

## 3.3 `43撃目 なめんな!` — Saitama's hero ethic becomes explicit

### 3.3.1 Fubuki's attack and Saitama's refusal of the ranking premise

Fubuki escalates from recruitment pressure to psychic violence. Saitama survives with almost comical physical stability while the built environment is torn apart. The visual asymmetry matters: her attack is rendered with debris, spiraling environmental motion, and spectacular force, while his body remains narratively unpersuaded. `OPM|V09|chapter:43|image:0060-0076|anchor:Fubuki psychic assault fails to impose hierarchy on Saitama`

The scene is more than another power gag because Saitama tells her why he objects.

A particularly important sequence occurs around images `0068-0069`. Saitama argues that there are extraordinarily strong and evil people in the world and that **facing them is what heroes do**. He then attacks the logic of surrounding oneself with weaker subordinates and mistaking that arrangement for personal strength. `OPM|V09|chapter:43|image:0068-0069|anchor:Saitama says heroes confront powerful evil and rejects strength-by-subordinates`

This is one of the clearest publication-boundary statements of Saitama's heroic self-concept so far.

He is not saying:

- heroes must work alone;
- friendship is weakness;
- teams are illegitimate;
- ranks must not exist.

He is saying that **rank-protection through domination of weaker people is the opposite of the courage the hero role demands**.

### 3.3.2 Fubuki's insecurity is visible before Tatsumaki is fully explained

Fubuki's anger is disproportionate to the practical threat Saitama poses in the moment. His refusal threatens a structure by which she stabilizes her identity. Her ranking strategy therefore already reads as compensatory before Chapter 45 supplies its most explicit comparative cause.

**IR:** V09 supports describing Fubuki's control as *defensive domination*: she exerts downward control partly because upward comparison feels unwinnable.

That formulation remains provisional until the volume itself connects the ceiling to Tatsumaki.

### 3.3.3 Genos and Sonic interrupt the social conflict with a prestige duel

Sonic's arrival redirects the volume into a second form of hierarchy: individual combat reputation. Genos refuses to let Sonic simply reach Saitama, and Sonic's pride is organized around speed and the desire to settle his prior humiliation. Their conflict differs from Fubuki's rank politics, but it runs on the same anxiety about relative position.

Fubuki becomes an observer to a combat stratum outside the ranking logic she expected to control. Her uncertainty is not just “Saitama is secretly strong.” She is seeing that the nominal B-Class position is a poor description of the actual network of power around him.

## 3.4 `44撃目 加速` — technique, adaptation, and escalation

### 3.4.1 Genos treats Sonic as a mechanism to solve

The Genos/Sonic fight is built around iterative adaptation. Sonic changes his movement, Genos increases pursuit speed and attempts to track actual body position through afterimages and attack trajectories. The sequence repeatedly alternates close perceptual panels with decompressed high-speed motion. `OPM|V09|chapter:44|image:0089-0108|anchor:Genos analyzes and adapts to Sonic speed/afterimages`

**Character delta:** Genos's investigative cognition is not confined to post-fight reflection. He reasons during action and immediately changes tactics.

The strongest clean example is not a long explanation but the sequence in which he notices changed movement, tracks the face/body line, believes he has captured the real target, and is then forced to update when Sonic produces more afterimages. This is good evidence for **mechanism-seeking under pressure**, even though he can still overcommit tactically.

### 3.4.2 Escalation exposes Genos's collateral-risk weakness

When direct tracking ceases to be sufficient, Genos prepares a maximum-output incineration to erase the surrounding area and deny Sonic space. `OPM|V09|chapter:44|image:0109-0113|anchor:Genos prepares maximum-output area incineration`

Saitama stops the escalation. This matters because the scene prevents “analytical Genos” from being confused with “perfectly calibrated Genos.” His reasoning can be technically competent while his operational objective becomes too narrow. Saitama's intervention restores proportionality.

At this boundary there is no basis to say Genos deliberately intends civilian casualties. The relevant fact is narrower: **competitive pursuit can drive him toward excessive environmental force unless an external check interrupts him.**

### 3.4.3 Saitama ends a speed hierarchy with a deliberately ridiculous technique

Saitama takes over and gives Sonic the confrontation he wants. Sonic's culmination is an advanced afterimage technique; Saitama answers with `マジ反復横跳び` — Serious Side Hops. `OPM|V09|chapter:44|image:0117-0126|anchor:Serious Side Hops overwhelm Sonic`

The joke works at several levels:

- `マジ` borrows the formal gravity of Saitama's “serious” register;
- `反復横跳び` is a mundane school-fitness drill rather than a martial secret;
- the visual rendering grants that banal movement the density and grandeur of a finishing technique;
- Sonic's elaborate speed identity collapses under something that barely deserves to be called combat technique.

This is not merely anti-shonen parody. Murata still gives speed genuine visual pleasure. The manga mocks technique prestige while luxuriating in technique spectacle.

### 3.4.4 Fubuki's ranking map becomes untenable

Fubuki watches the exchange and reaches the obvious local conclusion: Saitama is far too strong to fit the meaning she assigned to B-Class. `OPM|V09|chapter:44|image:0124-0128|anchor:Fubuki recognizes Saitama as abnormally strong`

The institution is not shown to have false data in a simple bureaucratic sense. Rather, rank is revealed as an aggregate social measure that can badly lag causal reality.

## 3.5 `45撃目 ヒーローネーム` — ceiling, identity, and bureaucratic naming

### 3.5.1 Fubuki's Rank 1 fortress is explicitly connected to Tatsumaki

Fubuki's internal account turns the earlier insecurity into a structured model. She expected her intelligence, physical qualities, social skill, and psychic ability to make her exceptional, but her older sister Tatsumaki is an incomparably stronger esper. `OPM|V09|chapter:45|image:0129-0133|anchor:Fubuki narrates Tatsumaki as the unreachable ceiling`

She therefore states that she cannot become number one and remains B-Class Rank 1 rather than simply moving upward. The A-Class ladder also contains a hard bottleneck at Rank 1, Sweet Mask, with the Atomic Samurai disciples occupying Ranks 2-4. `OPM|V09|chapter:45|image:0132-0136|anchor:Fubuki explains A-Class bottleneck and refusal to abandon B1 position`

This is a crucial qualification to a simple “Fubuki is lazy” reading. Her behavior is strategic under a prestige system she believes she cannot conquer. But strategy does not absolve coercion: she preserves her local maximum partly by controlling other people.

### 3.5.2 The world contains many abnormal powers; Fubuki's model begins to expand

The chapter juxtaposes A-Class fighters, monsters, Tatsumaki, Genos, Sonic, and Saitama. Fubuki's worldview is forced outward. She has treated the ranking system as a sufficiently complete map of relevant power, but Saitama's existence shows that important strength may sit outside the status map.

Saitama does not reciprocate her hierarchy. When she asks about joining or continuing around him, he is not hostile to her presence as a person, but he explicitly refuses becoming her subordinate. `OPM|V09|chapter:45|image:0141-0144|anchor:Saitama accepts Fubuki's presence but refuses subordinate status`

That is an important relational distinction: **rejection of hierarchy is not rejection of relationship**.

### 3.5.3 King provides a low-stakes counterexample to status obsession

The chapter briefly returns to Saitama and King gaming. King casually possesses competence and wins in the game domain; Saitama becomes irritated and competitive, but the S-Class/King public myth is irrelevant to the interaction. `OPM|V09|chapter:45|image:0145-0146|anchor:King and Saitama gaming remains peer-coded`

V08's low-stakes peer channel therefore immediately receives longitudinal follow-through.

### 3.5.4 Hero names: bureaucracy produces public identity from visual shorthand

The Hero Association assigns names to registered heroes. Genos becomes `鬼サイボーグ`; Saitama becomes `ハゲマント`. `OPM|V09|chapter:45|image:0147-0151|anchor:Association assigns Demon Cyborg and Caped Baldy hero names`

The contrast is satirical and character-relevant. Genos receives an aggressive, competence-congruent brand. Saitama receives a reductive physical description that ignores the reality the institution still cannot recognize.

The naming process does not create power; it creates **publicly searchable identity**. It is another layer in the series' persistent separation among causal achievement, institutional classification, and social narrative.

### 3.5.5 The Association recognizes Garou as a new threat category

The final movement shifts to emergency administration: Garou has begun attacking heroes, and his activity becomes legible as `ヒーロー狩り`. The institution is now responding not to a conventional monster but to a human martial actor who deliberately targets the hero category. `OPM|V09|chapter:45|image:0151-0152|anchor:Association frames Garou as Hero Hunter threat`

That classification is operationally useful but ontologically incomplete. Garou wants the monster identity; the body available at V09 remains human.

## 3.6 `46撃目 ヒーロー狩り` — Garou makes the category war literal

### 3.6.1 Hunting heroes is not incidental violence; it is identity production

Garou attacks registered heroes and explicitly treats the campaign as his route toward becoming a monster. He is not simply selecting strong opponents at random. The hero label itself is part of the target. `OPM|V09|chapter:46|image:0153-0164|anchor:Garou begins systematic Hero Hunter campaign`

He tells himself that defeating heroes will let him grow `怪人として`. That phrasing is important at this boundary. Monsterhood is still an **aspirational social/ideological identity enacted through behavior**, not a demonstrated biological transformation.

### 3.6.2 Bang's dojo becomes the hidden causal bridge

Charanko identifies Garou as Bang's former top disciple. Saitama had not known the connection; Genos begins reasoning from it. `OPM|V09|chapter:46|image:0156-0161|anchor:Garou identified as Bang former disciple`

This revises the martial map. Garou's competence is not an inexplicable “villain power.” It comes from a lineage already present in the hero world. The same martial infrastructure can produce a hero, a teacher, or a self-declared monster depending on the person using it.

### 3.6.3 Mumen Rider chooses intervention despite obvious power disadvantage

Mumen Rider encounters the conflict and still approaches. The volume again preserves his established pattern: heroism is not defined by believing one will win. `OPM|V09|chapter:46|image:0161-0164|anchor:Mumen Rider approaches Garou despite mismatch`

This creates a quiet ideological contradiction with Garou. Garou's childhood grievance centers on a story in which heroes are preassigned winners. Mumen Rider is a hero precisely because he repeatedly acts without any guarantee of winning.

V09 does not verbalize that comparison for the reader. Structurally, however, it is one of the strongest counterexamples to Garou's simplified childhood model already available within his own target category.

### 3.6.4 Tanktop Master changes the scale of the hunt

Tanktop Master enters as the first S-Class target shown engaging Garou seriously. His raw force initially surprises and injures Garou. `OPM|V09|chapter:46|image:0165-0174|anchor:Tanktop Master raw power pressures Garou`

Garou's reaction is diagnostically important: genuine difficulty does not immediately break his self-concept. It activates analysis, aggression, and eventually adaptation. He recognizes that S-Class can exceed his easy assumptions.

The chapter ends with Bang and Bomb preparing to act. Garou's private rebellion is becoming a relational and institutional crisis.

## 3.7 `47撃目 技` — technique overturns raw-power expectation

### 3.7.1 Tanktop Master's mercy exposes the human/monster ambiguity

Tanktop Master can hit Garou with enormous force, but Garou remains recognizably human. The other heroes' language repeatedly oscillates between treating him as a monster-like threat and acknowledging the human body in front of them. `OPM|V09|chapter:47|image:0179-0187|anchor:heroes debate Garou as human while attempting to stop him`

Mumen Rider intervenes when the fight risks becoming an execution. This is not naïveté in the sense of believing Garou harmless; it is a normative boundary about what heroes should do to a human opponent.

Garou immediately exploits the opening. That consequence matters. V09 does not guarantee that ethical restraint will be rewarded.

### 3.7.2 Garou learns during pain rather than merely enduring it

Tanktop Master's power forces Garou into a losing position, but Garou does not only “power up.” He observes rhythm, trajectory, timing, and openings. Then he uses `流水岩砕拳`, the martial art learned from Bang. `OPM|V09|chapter:47|image:0188-0201|anchor:Garou uses Flowing Water Rock Smashing Fist to reverse Tanktop Master fight`

This establishes the early Garou combat model more precisely:

- high pain tolerance;
- rapid situational reading;
- willingness to fight from disadvantage;
- embodied technical memory;
- ability to turn an opponent's committed force into an exploitable pattern;
- strong preference for proving hierarchy through direct combat.

The contrast with the earlier contempt for empty named attacks now resolves cleanly. Garou does not reject technique names; he rejects **technique without substance**.

### 3.7.3 The crowd turns a duel into a moral stress test

Once Tanktop Master's group joins the confrontation, Garou is attacked by multiple heroes. He frames this as confirmation of his role, but the scene also shows his willingness to brutalize opponents who attempt to protect one another. The collective hero side is not uniformly noble—some are aggressive and status-conscious—but neither is it the simple bullying machine of Garou's childhood fiction.

The strongest counterexample is again Mumen Rider. He attempts to restrain excessive force against Garou and then is attacked by Garou anyway. `OPM|V09|chapter:47|image:0183-0196|anchor:Mumen Rider protects Garou from excessive force then becomes victim of Garou assault`

**IR:** V09 begins testing Garou's ideology from inside the narrative. The category “hero” already contains people who do not behave like guaranteed popular victors.

### 3.7.4 Bang and Bomb arrive after the damage

Bang and Bomb find the aftermath and recognize Garou's progression. `OPM|V09|chapter:47|image:0201-0202|anchor:Bang and Bomb reach Garou aftermath`

The volume closes the main narrative with the hunt now reciprocal: Garou hunts heroes; his former master begins hunting him.

## 3.8 Bonus: `フブキ組の奮闘` — coercive hierarchy also has mundane economics

The bonus chapter is analytically useful rather than disposable comedy. Fubuki's group needs money, status objects, transportation, and collective labor. Fubuki's desire for an expensive car produces a minor economic campaign involving part-time work, bounty chasing, and group frustration. `OPM|V09|extra:FubukiGroup|image:0203-0210|anchor:Fubuki Group tries to finance vehicle and chase bounty`

Several qualifications follow:

- Fubuki's followers are not visually framed as mindless slaves; they complain, strategize, work, and share group identity.
- Fubuki can impose priorities selfishly.
- The group has real solidarity and real hierarchy at the same time.
- Their resource scarcity punctures the glamorous visual image of Fubuki's entrance.
- Saitama's practical solution—running rather than being trapped in traffic—again collapses prestige consumption into functional utility.

The joke therefore strengthens rather than cancels the main chapter's institutional reading.

# 4. Saitama state through V09

## 4.1 Heroic self-concept: unusually explicit

V09 provides one of the cleanest direct statements of Saitama's hero ethic so far. Heroes exist to confront genuinely powerful evil, not to preserve their rank by dominating weaker people. `OPM|V09|chapter:43|image:0068-0069|anchor:Saitama defines hero direction toward stronger evil`

This is not a grand doctrine. His language remains blunt and practical. That plainness is itself consistent: Saitama's ethical cognition tends to appear as obvious-action judgment rather than abstract moral system.

**Transition: STRENGTHEN** — Saitama's heroism is less recognition-centered than role-centered. He can dislike humiliating labels and still reject status games when they conflict with what a hero should actually do.

## 4.2 Status and deference

Fubuki's B1 rank produces no deference. Her faction produces no deference. Sonic's prestige produces no deference. King remains a game-night peer despite his S-Class public image.

**Transition: STRENGTHEN** — status is information at most; it is not a command over Saitama's interpersonal posture.

## 4.3 Recognition is not irrelevant

The hero-name sequence is important because Saitama does care about public presentation enough to be unhappy with `ハゲマント`. The correct model is therefore not “Saitama has transcended recognition.” He wants a respectable public identity, but he does not let that desire determine whom he obeys or what he thinks heroism is.

**Transition: REVISE / PRECISION** — recognition desire exists, but it remains subordinate and selectively activated.

## 4.4 Relationships: King continuity and Fubuki's first route inward

King returns as a stable low-stakes peer through gaming. Fubuki, meanwhile, fails to become Saitama's superior but does not simply disappear after defeat. Saitama can reject subordination without demanding total relational exclusion.

**New relational distinction:** `拒否 of hierarchy != refusal of contact`.

## 4.5 Combat affect

Neither Fubuki nor Sonic supplies credible resistance. Saitama remains physically untroubled, and the Sonic resolution becomes a gag technique rather than a meaningful struggle. V09 therefore adds little diagnostic evidence about Saitama's response to a truly worthy opponent.

# 5. Ensemble character deltas

## 5.1 Garou — `emerging -> moderate` readiness candidate

V09 materially expands every model axis introduced in V08.

**Goal:** become the monster who overturns the expected hero-victory script; begin active hero hunting.

**Self-concept:** designated adversary to the hero category; strong enough to rewrite the scenario rather than seek permission from it.

**Wound / grievance:** childhood identification with hated/guaranteed-loser figures and resentment of popularity-backed moral certainty.

**Institutional position:** hostile outsider generated partly from within hero-world martial infrastructure; former Bang disciple.

**Combat cognition:** adaptive, technical, pain-tolerant, skeptical of empty prestige; can reverse raw-power disadvantage through learned technique.

**Moral contradiction:** objects to one-sided narrative domination while personally dominating weak and marginal opponents; identifies with losers without yet protecting losers.

**Unresolved:** whether `怪人` is metaphorical identity, moral vocation, bodily trajectory, social label, or some combination.

V09 is broad enough that **moderate** readiness is justified: he now has motive evidence, speech, childhood framing, institutional relation, multiple combat contexts, and contradictions. What remains absent is sufficient longitudinal follow-through to call the model strong.

## 5.2 Fubuki — enters `moderate`

Fubuki receives an unusually dense debut.

**Goal:** preserve B-Class Rank 1, expand/maintain the Fubuki Group, recruit anomalously useful people.

**Self-concept:** exceptional, organizationally competent, and entitled to command within her tier; simultaneously conscious of an unreachable superior ceiling.

**Fear/wound:** being permanently second-order relative to Tatsumaki and unable to occupy the absolute top position she imagined for herself.

**Institutional strategy:** convert local rank into network power; make subordination valuable enough to attract members and coercive enough to suppress refusal.

**Relationship to Saitama:** attempted superior -> defeated evaluator -> interested would-be incorporator/associate; hierarchy rejected, contact not fully rejected.

**Contradiction:** seeks security through a group yet interprets strength partly as rank control; provides real organization while reproducing insecurity downward.

**Readiness:** `moderate` is warranted immediately because V09 supplies private motivation, public leadership, coercion, vulnerability, power use, ordinary economic behavior, and cross-context speech.

## 5.3 Genos — `strong`, materially refined

- adaptive tracking against Sonic strengthens mechanism-under-action cognition;
- pursuit pride can narrow proportionality, culminating in an excessive area-incineration plan;
- Saitama remains the operational override he obeys;
- hero-name assignment gives him a public brand more congruent with his visible capabilities than Saitama receives.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN analytical competence; REVISE upward the importance of competitive tunnel vision as a tactical failure mode.

## 5.4 Sonic — `moderate`, strengthened

Sonic's identity remains heavily performance- and rivalry-centered. He has trained specifically to erase humiliation by Saitama and treats speed as proof of superiority. Against Genos he adapts and reveals additional technique; against Saitama his self-authored prestige hierarchy is destroyed by `マジ反復横跳び`.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN — rivalry is not incidental recurrence; it organizes training and return behavior.

## 5.5 Tanktop Master — enters `emerging` / strong first combat sample

Tanktop Master is not merely a “big strong S-Class.” V09 shows:

- direct responsibility for subordinates;
- willingness to confront an emergent human threat;
- immense raw-force orientation;
- restraint affected by Garou's human status;
- vulnerability to a technically superior counter-system.

More cross-context evidence is required before `moderate`.

## 5.6 Mumen Rider — `moderate`, ethically strengthened

Mumen Rider again acts without a plausible expectation of victory and intervenes against excessive violence toward a dangerous human opponent. The immediate consequence is that Garou exploits the restraint and attacks him.

**Transition: STRENGTHEN** — Mumen Rider's ethics are not merely rhetoric rewarded by narrative convenience; he applies them under conditions where they make him vulnerable.

## 5.7 Bang — `moderate`, relationship state changes

**Transition: NEW / STRENGTHEN** — Bang↔Garou becomes a major unresolved master/former-disciple relation.

# 6. Heroism, rank, recognition, and institutions

V09 is the most concentrated institutional-rank volume of the V2 pass so far.

## 6.1 Rank as incentive can distort hero behavior

Fubuki demonstrates a direct failure mode: when rank is scarce and identity is tied to being first, a hero can rationally invest in **blocking competitors** rather than simply increasing public-good output.

The manga does not therefore prove that ranks are useless. Rank conveys useful rough information and organizes professional labor. The finding is narrower: **rank is an incentive system, and incentive systems can be gamed.**

## 6.2 Group power can supplement individual rank

Fubuki turns B1 into organizational capital. Her followers extend surveillance, recruitment, labor, and social presence beyond what individual psychic force could do.

This is the first clear V2 case where a hero builds a quasi-political faction inside the Association.

## 6.3 A-Class contains its own bottleneck

Sweet Mask at A1 and Atomic Samurai's disciples immediately below him make advancement structurally nontrivial. Fubuki's decision to remain B1 therefore has more context than fear alone. She prefers a guaranteed local crown to a higher tier where she may be structurally trapped.

## 6.4 Hero names institutionalize simplified public narratives

`鬼サイボーグ` and `ハゲマント` demonstrate that bureaucratic naming compresses people into memorable visible traits. The mismatch between Saitama's causal importance and the banality of his assigned name is an institutional satire of recognition lag.

## 6.5 Garou creates a category the institution cannot treat as ordinary monster response

A human hunter of heroes complicates legal, ethical, and operational assumptions. Tanktop Master's and Mumen Rider's behavior shows why. Force sufficient for monsters may become morally problematic against a human even when that human is extremely dangerous.

# 7. Monsterhood, personhood, body, and self-labeling

Garou is the volume's central ontological problem.

At V09:

- **biological body:** human;
- **social identity:** increasingly treated as a criminal / Hero Hunter / monster-like threat;
- **self-identification:** explicitly seeks to become `怪人`;
- **moral behavior:** violent, predatory toward heroes and criminals, not reducible to biological category;
- **transformational evidence:** none sufficient to claim bodily monsterization.

Therefore:

> **Garou V09 is a human using monsterhood as a self-authored antagonistic identity and developmental project.**

Do not import later bodily states into this boundary.

Fubuki and Tatsumaki provide another useful distinction. Psychic abnormality and enormous power are not treated as monsterhood by default. The series therefore continues separating abnormal capacity from monster ontology.

# 8. Power, technique, and explanatory systems

## 8.1 Garou demonstrates martial lineage and adaptive technique

Garou's reversal against Tanktop Master makes power multidimensional. Raw force can dominate until technique changes the geometry of exchange. `流水岩砕拳` establishes a clear lineage from Bang to Garou without implying shared ethics.

## 8.2 Genos demonstrates adaptive machine cognition plus escalation risk

Genos updates against Sonic in real time, but when precision fails he moves toward area destruction. Intelligence and proportionality are distinct.

## 8.3 Sonic turns speed into identity

Sonic does not merely possess speed; he narrates himself through it. Additional afterimages are simultaneously technique and self-validation.

## 8.4 Saitama breaks technique prestige rather than technique physics

`マジ反復横跳び` is funny because it obeys the physical premise—move absurdly fast—while refusing the cultural prestige of a secret martial technique. His power makes nomenclature optional.

## 8.5 Psychic force remains spectacular but insufficient against Saitama

Fubuki's environmental destruction is visually real. Saitama's immunity does not make her weak in the volume's ordinary scale; it makes him an invalid comparator for normal ranking.

# 9. Humor, satire, and genre mechanics

## 9.1 Prestige systems are the main satirical target

V09 mocks multiple ways of turning relative strength into identity:

- rank protection through faction politics;
- cool hero names;
- elaborate speed techniques;
- martial named attacks;
- luxury cars as leadership image;
- public labels that poorly track causal reality.

The satire is not nihilistic. Bang's technique works. Fubuki's group works in some ways. Hero names have administrative use. Sonic really is fast. The series mocks **overinvestment in the sign of competence**, not competence itself.

## 9.2 Saitama's flat language punctures institutional grandeur

Fubuki arrives with visual glamour and ranked entourage; Saitama says `誰?`. Sonic offers a high-speed rival performance; Saitama answers with side hops. The Association crafts public identities; Saitama receives `ハゲマント`.

The recurrent mechanism is deflation through plainness.

## 9.3 Garou is played less as a joke than the surrounding systems

Garou has comic edges, but the childhood grievance and violent commitment receive enough seriousness that the volume does not reduce him to parody. He is beginning to inherit the conventional battle-manga development engine that Saitama disrupts.

# 10. Japanese language and voice findings

A separate `OPM_V09_JAPANESE_DIALOGUE_AND_REGISTER_AUDIT.md` owns the detailed language audit. The most analytically load-bearing observations are summarized here.

## 10.1 Garou: scenario, popularity, and self-authored monsterhood

The lexicon around `人気者`, `嫌われ者`, `悲劇`, `怪人`, and `シナリオ` makes his grievance explicitly narrative/social rather than merely biological. He imagines monsterhood as a role capable of rewriting an imposed scenario.

His ordinary battle speech is rough, insulting, and direct. The style repeatedly converts evaluation into challenge: strength must be demonstrated, not announced.

## 10.2 Fubuki: command register and incorporation

Fubuki uses imperatives and confident first-person positioning appropriate to someone accustomed to command. `私の傘下に入りなさい` frames membership as incorporation under her umbrella, not peer affiliation.

When her certainty breaks, her speech becomes more internally comparative and less imperious, especially around Tatsumaki and Saitama.

## 10.3 Saitama: blunt colloquial ethical language

Saitama's hero statement is linguistically important precisely because it is not elevated. He uses ordinary, confrontational language to state what the role demands. The contrast between moral seriousness and non-grandiose diction remains stable.

## 10.4 Genos: analytical compression under combat

Genos's combat voice remains concise and mechanism-directed. He names observed changes, updates target models, and then acts. His register toward Saitama remains `先生` even when the practical situation is chaotic.

## 10.5 Hero names versus personal identity

`鬼サイボーグ` and `ハゲマント` are externally imposed public labels. They should not be used as if they were self-chosen identities.

# 11. Visual and formal analysis

## 11.1 Garou's first chapter uses crowds to create a self-isolating protagonist frame

Garou is often centered against a field of opponents. As the crowd falls, the negative social space around him increases. The composition visually enacts his self-authorship: he creates individuality by making everyone else into defeated background.

## 11.2 Fubuki is introduced through glamour, then visually stripped of control

Her chapter-title image, fur, jewelry, entourage, and low-angle entrance construct high-status visual authority. The subsequent apartment doorway, Saitama's blank face, and the destruction that still fails to move him progressively detach visual prestige from causal control.

## 11.3 Psychic force is environmental; Saitama's resistance is bodily stillness

Fubuki's attacks move buildings, debris, and perspective lines. Saitama's body becomes the stable visual axis. The contrast is formal argument: spectacle can fill the page without changing the hierarchy that matters.

## 11.4 Genos/Sonic acceleration is rendered through perceptual failure

The sequence shifts from readable bodies to streaks, partial frames, afterimages, and near-abstract motion. Fubuki's inability to visually parse portions of the exchange lets the page itself model a witness whose ranking assumptions are failing.

## 11.5 Serious Side Hops is visual parody by over-rendering

A mundane exercise is drawn with the visual grammar of apocalyptic high-speed combat. The joke requires Murata to take the motion *more* seriously, not less.

## 11.6 Tanktop Master versus Garou changes from mass to flow

Tanktop Master's attacks emphasize muscular bulk and impact craters. Garou's `流水岩砕拳` shifts the motion grammar toward curved redirection and continuous flow. The art makes the conceptual distinction between raw force and technique immediately visible.

## 11.7 Bonus chapter re-scales Fubuki from psychic glamour to traffic

The luxury-car plot ends in mundane congestion and practical inconvenience. Social prestige is again subordinated to ordinary function.

# 19. Canonical V09 synthesis

Volume 9 turns *One Punch Man*'s recurring rank jokes into a serious character problem.

Garou begins from the belief that the social story has already assigned heroes the role of popular winner and monsters the role of hated loser. He does not answer that unfairness by protecting the powerless. He decides to become the monster strong enough to reverse the script by force. Fubuki answers a different vulnerability in almost the opposite way: unable to imagine herself surpassing the absolute ceiling represented by Tatsumaki, she creates a local order in which she can remain first by organizing and, when necessary, suppressing weaker heroes. Both characters therefore try to solve vulnerability through control of the winner/loser relation.

Saitama's answer is almost aggressively uncomplicated. Rank does not make Fubuki his superior. Weak followers do not make her strong. A hero's direction of travel should be toward dangerous strength, not downward toward people whose subordination makes the hero feel secure. That statement does not make Saitama anti-social: King remains a peer, Genos remains close, and Fubuki is not expelled from his orbit merely because he refuses her hierarchy.

The combat chapters then materialize the same argument. Genos and Sonic compete through increasingly refined speed. Garou discovers that Tanktop Master's raw power can overwhelm him and answers with technique learned from Bang. Saitama, uniquely, makes prestige itself optional: his “serious” answer to Sonic is a school-fitness movement executed at impossible scale.

V09 therefore does not flatten hierarchy into “rankings bad.” It asks **what a person does when hierarchy says they are below someone else**. Fubuki entrenches a domain she can rule. Sonic trains to erase humiliation. Genos analyzes and escalates. Garou vows to rewrite the whole scenario. Mumen Rider acts despite knowing hierarchy is against him. Saitama largely refuses to let hierarchy tell him who deserves obedience, respect, fear, or heroic responsibility.

That is the volume's most durable contribution to the V2 model.
