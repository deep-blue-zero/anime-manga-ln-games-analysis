# 2. Volume orientation

V16 ended with Garou under extreme bodily pressure, his self-declared monster role increasingly contradicted by his protection of Tareo, and Bang's arrival converting the hero hunt into a master-disciple reckoning. V17 does not resolve those contradictions. It finally supplies the **social memory and ideological reasoning from which Garou built them**, then places him inside an organization that offers to recognize him as a monster only if he accepts its own definition of monsterhood.

The volume simultaneously broadens this problem beyond Garou.

- The Hero Association can rank Blast first but cannot simply command him.
- A donor can finance and pressure the Association without thereby acquiring reliable operational judgment.
- Narinki can bypass public command by deploying a privately equipped rescue unit, only for the unit's apparently superior hardware to fail inside an adversarial environment.
- Metal Knight advises Child Emperor to distrust even nearby humans and to accumulate enough independent strength to act alone.
- Fubuki attempts to read Saitama, Genos, Bang, Bomb, and King as something faction-like, only to discover that their actual social coordination is too loose and self-authored for her to command.
- King has no combat force but can convert reputation into operational leverage against Elder Centipede.
- Saitama has overwhelming force but remains almost absent from formal command and public causal legibility.
- The Monster Association possesses hundreds of members and high-end combat assets yet explicitly identifies **cohesion and leadership** as deficits.
- An external actor calling itself `組織` sends Machine God G5 to cooperate with the Monster Association, making inter-organizational alignment visible without proving identity with every earlier hidden-actor hypothesis.

V17 therefore makes **role assignment, command, and legitimacy** as important as raw power.

## Central V17 thesis

**V17 turns the assignment of “hero,” “monster,” “leader,” “strongest,” and “legitimate actor” into an explicit political and behavioral problem. Social consensus, institutional rank, self-identification, reputation, and organizational recruitment can all assign roles, but none is treated as self-validating. The volume repeatedly tests labels against what bodies can do, what people actually choose, whom they protect, whom they can command, and what costs they accept.**

Garou is the volume's clearest test case. His childhood grievance is not merely that he personally lost fights. He remembers a game in which the popular child automatically occupies the hero role, the disliked child is assigned the monster role, the surrounding group validates that distribution, and the monster's victory is structurally excluded. Adult Garou generalizes that experience into anger at majority-defined justice and evil. But the Monster Association immediately exposes a problem in his solution: it too claims authority to decide what a “real monster” is, and its proof condition is not social exclusion but **unhesitating hero murder**.

The resulting question is sharper than “is Garou secretly good?”:

> **Can Garou reject socially predetermined moral roles without allowing another institution—or his own grievance—to predetermine his behavior in the opposite direction?**

V17 leaves that open.

## Strongest limiting / counter-reading

V17 must not be flattened into “hero and monster are only labels” or “the Hero Association and Monster Association are morally equivalent institutions.”

The volume itself supplies strong counterevidence to that move:

- Elder Centipede is a materially catastrophic threat whose destructive capacity does not disappear because classification systems are imperfect.
- Garou's childhood grievance explains his ideology but does not retroactively justify his deliberate assaults on heroes.
- Garou is no longer the powerless child in his own origin story; by V17 he is an extraordinarily dangerous adult who often has more immediate physical power than those he attacks.
- The Hero Association is vulnerable to status, donor, and command problems, yet it is still mobilizing to rescue a hostage and defend civilians.
- The Monster Association explicitly conditions Garou's acceptance on murder and is using kidnapping, terror, and predation as operational tools.
- Narinki's impatience with the Association may be emotionally understandable, but his private alternative is neither better informed nor safer merely because it is more direct.

The safe V17 claim is therefore not that moral categories are fictitious. It is that **the authority to assign a category and the evidence that warrants the category must be kept distinct**.

# 3. Sequential close reading

## 3.1 `85撃目 パワー` — Garou's origin story turns popularity into a theory of moral scripting

### 3.1.1 Childhood “hero play” is not remembered as harmless imitation

V17 opens not with a new opponent but with Garou's childhood memory. Other children invite him into `ヒーローごっこ`. The socially central child, Tatchan, occupies the heroic `ジャスティスマン` role. Garou is assigned the monster role and is physically hit as part of the game.

`OPM|V17|chapter:85|images:0010-0012|anchor:child Garou assigned monster role in hero play`

When Garou resists, the surrounding children and later the teacher read **his resistance** as the disruptive aggression. He objects that the play is painful and unfair; Tatchan can answer that it is only hero play. Garou eventually states that he no longer wants to be the monster role, demands an actual contest, and asks to be left alone if he wins.

`OPM|V17|chapter:85|images:0014-0015|anchor:Garou refuses predetermined monster role and challenges Tatchan`

The scene matters because the social process has at least four layers:

1. **role allocation** — the popular child becomes hero, Garou becomes monster;
2. **script allocation** — the hero is supposed to win and the monster is supposed to lose;
3. **group validation** — peers defend the popular child and restrain Garou;
4. **adult interpretation** — the teacher receives the conflict through the group's framing and treats Garou as the problem.

Nothing here proves that all later heroic institutions reproduce this childhood structure. It does establish the experiential model through which Garou is predisposed to interpret them.

### 3.1.2 Garou's retrospective portrait of Tatchan is deliberately contradictory

Adult Garou's memory does not simply say “Tatchan was evil.” He lists properties that coexist:

- athletic ability;
- popularity;
- romantic/social success;
- enjoyment of bullying weaker children;
- being, in Garou's judgment, an unpleasant person.

He contrasts this with his own isolation and lack of friends.

`OPM|V17|chapter:85|image:0016|anchor:Garou contrasts Tatchan popularity with bullying and his own isolation`

This is important because Garou's grievance targets an inference he thinks society makes too easily:

`popular / publicly approved -> righteous / hero`

and its inverse:

`disliked / isolated -> wrong / monster`

V17 gives real evidence for why **he** distrusts those mappings. It does not grant that the mappings explain every later hero-monster conflict.

### 3.1.3 The load-bearing phrase is `圧倒的理不尽さ`

Garou explicitly says he felt an overwhelming unfairness through hero play. His formulation is unusually analytical for him: a popular person liked by everyone unilaterally beats a weak person hated by everyone; the surrounding group's approval stabilizes the hero role; the monster role has no scenario in which it is allowed to win.

`OPM|V17|chapter:85|image:0017|anchor:Garou formulates majority-approved hero-villain script`

This substantially **strengthens but also narrows** the earlier V09 winner/loser and `人気者`/`嫌われ者` model. Garou is not objecting only to outcome inequality. He is objecting to what he perceives as **pre-authored legitimacy**: the result and its moral meaning are decided before the contest.

That makes his monster identification intelligible as an attempt to seize the losing role and force a result the script prohibits.

### 3.1.4 Garou admits his theory is affectively stronger than analytically complete

The next page prevents us from treating the childhood monologue as a finished political philosophy. Garou explodes against fixed justice/evil positions and majority will, then concedes in substance that he cannot cleanly analyze the basis of his anger. What he can state is the desire to make others understand—to land the blow of the weak and deny fixed positions of good and evil.

`OPM|V17|chapter:85|image:0018|anchor:Garou admits under-theorized anger and wants the weak person's blow`

This self-admission is diagnostic. Garou possesses:

- a genuine autobiographical grievance;
- a recurring interpretive model;
- strong moral affect;
- a coherent target in predetermined moral legitimacy;

but not yet a stable demonstration that his current hero-hunting practice follows from those premises without contradiction.

Indeed, his present power already complicates the language of `弱者`. He identifies with the weak-role position even while physically overpowering many people he targets. V17 therefore asks us to distinguish **remembered structural weakness / outsider identification** from **current tactical weakness**.

### 3.1.5 Bang's intervention restores the personal cost of the ideology

Bang's blow returns the chapter from theory to relationship. Garou's body is failing after the V16 gauntlet, yet he refuses to accept the confrontation as his endpoint.

This is not just another strong hero striking him. Bang is the teacher whose martial inheritance Garou has transformed into an anti-hero weapon. V16 activated that relation; V17 makes the collision physical.

Garou's ideology may be addressed to society, but Bang's presence keeps a more intimate question open: **what does a master owe when a gifted student uses transmitted discipline destructively, and what does the student still owe to a lineage he rejects?**

## 3.2 Garou is removed from the master-disciple confrontation by a larger institutional war

The Bang/Bomb/Genos pressure on Garou is interrupted by a threat whose scale changes the action grammar: Elder Centipede, explicitly marked `災害レベル：竜`.

`OPM|V17|chapter:85|image:0041|anchor:Elder Centipede enters as Dragon-level disaster`

Phoenix Man extracts Garou while Garou objects that the heroes are his prey. The Monster Association is already treating him as a recoverable strategic asset before he has consented to membership.

This matters for the later recruitment scene. Garou wants monsterhood as a **self-authored role against hero society**. The Monster Association begins converting that self-authorship into an institutional asset: rescue him, house him, evaluate him, recruit him, test him.

---

## 3.3 Elder Centipede makes matchup and domain limits explicit

Phoenix Man's assessment of the defenders is one of the volume's cleanest anti-scalar power passages. Bang is exceptionally dangerous in close martial exchange, but Elder Centipede's body size and form reduce the applicability of ordinary martial targets. Genos is technologically advanced, but a cyborg's available hardware still places ceilings on what can be brought to a giant target.

`OPM|V17|chapter:85|images:0051-0053|anchor:Phoenix Man evaluates Blast revenge and matchup limits against Elder Centipede`

The volume thereby strengthens a recurrent V2 rule:

> **“Strong” is not a universal operation. Capability is mechanism-, body-, environment-, information-, and target-dependent.**

The Elder Centipede sequence proves this repeatedly rather than merely stating it.

### 3.3.1 Bang and Bomb can break the shell without solving the organism

Bang and Bomb's combined martial technique fractures Elder Centipede's exterior. Genos explicitly recognizes the achievement as a kind of technical extremity or culmination.

`OPM|V17|chapter:85|images:0057-0061|anchor:Bang and Bomb combined technique fractures Elder Centipede shell`

The apparent success is immediately revised when Elder Centipede molts, regenerates, and returns larger. Technique was effective against the observed surface; the biological system contained a recovery mechanism not captured by the initial model.

`OPM|V17|chapter:85|images:0062-0069|anchor:Elder Centipede molts and invalidates apparent victory`

The sequence echoes V15's institutional lesson about revisable disaster models, but at the combat level: **correct local evidence can still support an incomplete victory model**.

### 3.3.2 Genos converts that failure into protective self-expenditure

Genos volunteers to hold Elder Centipede in place while Bang and Bomb move the injured heroes away.

`OPM|V17|chapter:85|image:0071|anchor:Genos volunteers to hold Elder Centipede while wounded heroes escape`

Bang objects to the younger hero taking the fight alone. The exchange broadens Genos's protective policy: he is willing to spend a newly rebuilt body almost immediately after Kuseno's work when a concrete group faces annihilation.

This is not a clean repetition of the earlier **civilian** shield case—the threatened people here are injured heroes—so it should not be collapsed into an identical prediction test. As character evidence, however, it is strongly consistent with a low threshold for accepting bodily cost in defense of others.

### 3.3.3 Genos's upgrade does not erase mechanism-seeking

Genos's V17 combat is not “new upgrade = bigger number.” He continues to observe, select vectors, exploit opening and geometry, and change attack mode. He enters Elder Centipede's body and uses maximum heat/output from inside, seeking a different target relation after exterior attacks fail.

`OPM|V17|chapter:85|images:0081-0096|anchor:Genos changes attack geometry and burns Elder Centipede internally`

The manga gives the attack enormous visual prestige. It also refuses to let spectacle stand in for success. Elder Centipede survives. Genos's internal conclusion is not that effort was meaningless; it is that the present power set remains insufficient.

This is a key Genos continuity: **failure is information and motive for iteration**, but the iteration loop carries severe bodily and psychological cost.

## 3.4 Bang prepares to spend what remains of himself

After Genos fails to finish Elder Centipede, Bang prepares to exert his remaining strength while others can still escape. His language frames it as what may be the final full output of his life.

`OPM|V17|chapter:85|image:0105|anchor:Bang prepares final full-force stand`

The ethical structure resembles several earlier OPM hero moments without making them identical: capability assessment can be pessimistic while duty remains active. Bang does not need confidence of victory to identify a role he can still perform.

That is interrupted not by a stronger institutional plan, but by King.

---

## 3.5 King turns false reputation into real operational leverage

King arrives with a megaphone and loudly claims he has brought Blast, specifically exploiting Elder Centipede's desire for revenge.

`OPM|V17|chapter:85|images:0106-0109|anchor:King invokes Blast to bait Elder Centipede`

This is a major King development because the sequence cannot be reduced to “coward accidentally gets credit.” He intentionally enters the danger zone and deploys what he actually possesses: **public identity, enemy expectation, and the willingness to be the visible target long enough for a plan to work**.

King's reputation remains false as a claim about his physical strength. It becomes true as a causal resource.

The distinction is essential:

- `King is physically the strongest human` — false on the evidence available to us;
- `others believe King is extraordinarily dangerous` — true;
- `that belief can alter enemy behavior` — true;
- `King can sometimes choose to weaponize that belief deliberately` — now strongly established.

This is not retroactive validation of the myth. It is **second-order competence in using the myth**.

### 3.5.1 The Association itself can observe the trajectory without possessing the cause

Hero Association monitoring notices the movement of the threat and the destruction pattern but does not thereby gain full causal visibility. King is doing something operationally meaningful outside the institution's command structure; Saitama is about to provide the decisive force outside its predictive model.

The system sees effects more readily than causal agency—a recurring recognition problem in the series.

### 3.5.2 King and Saitama form a complementary two-person solution

King's task is to make Elder Centipede commit to the right direction and target. Saitama's task is to end the threat in a single hit once that geometry is created.

`OPM|V17|chapter:85|images:0112-0123|anchor:King fixes Elder Centipede trajectory and Saitama destroys it`

The formal construction matters. The manga spends pages on a massive, elongated organism, movement through terrain, layered pursuit, and the physical limits of Bang/Bomb/Genos. Saitama's intervention then collapses all that accumulated scale into one strike. Yet the strike is **not sufficient as a plan by itself**: King helps place the threat so the decisive force can be used without simply letting it continue through populated space.

This is one of the clearest examples so far of Saitama's power functioning **inside another person's tactical design**.

It also provides a useful correction to simple “Saitama makes everyone irrelevant” readings. Saitama makes the final force comparison absurd. He does not erase the informational, positional, protective, or social work that makes the intervention usable.

### 3.5.3 After the kill, Saitama remains characteristically low-ceremony

The aftermath rapidly compresses cosmic-scale spectacle back into ordinary interaction. Saitama identifies Genos, checks the scene, and returns to mundane concerns. The chapter even allows a brief comedic beat in which destructive output and ordinary clothing/consumer concerns coexist.

`OPM|V17|chapter:85|images:0124-0130|anchor:post-Elder Centipede decompression into ordinary Saitama register`

The visual and tonal decompression is not disrespect for the danger; it is a core formal mechanism by which OPM refuses to let epic scale permanently colonize ordinary life.

# 4. `86撃目 ハゲマントだからか？` — the strongest assets are the least commandable

## 4.1 Hero Association knowledge remains effect-first and attribution-poor

The Association knows Elder Centipede has been destroyed and that citywide catastrophe was avoided. It does not thereby obtain a clean map of who actually did what.

`OPM|V17|chapter:86|images:0131-0133|anchor:Hero Association evaluates Elder Centipede aftermath`

This continues the series' distinction between **monitoring an outcome** and **knowing its causal production**. The Association is not blind; it is epistemically incomplete.

## 4.2 Blast's rank does not create command authority over Blast

Under pressure to call Blast, Association personnel state that he cannot be moved at their convenience and that he conducts hero activity by his own free will.

`OPM|V17|chapter:86|images:0134-0135|anchor:Hero Association states Blast cannot be commanded at convenience`

This is one of the cleanest institution/capability distinctions in the corpus:

`highest formal hero rank != most commandable institutional subordinate`.

The Association depends on abnormal individuals precisely because ordinary systems cannot solve every crisis, yet those individuals may not behave like ordinary employees or military assets. V17 therefore sharpens a structural contradiction already visible in S-Class heterogeneity: **the institution's strongest resources can be the hardest to integrate into centralized command**.

Do not infer from this that Blast is irresponsible. At V17 we have an institutional statement about autonomy, not enough direct Blast behavior to judge his full duty policy.

## 4.3 Narinki converts grief/fear into donor pressure

Narinki demands that his abducted son Waganma receive priority and treats the present hostage crisis as the moment that should compel top-level response.

`OPM|V17|chapter:86|images:0135-0137|anchor:Narinki demands priority rescue for Waganma`

His urgency is emotionally intelligible. It is also politically diagnostic. A public protection institution is being pressed by a stakeholder whose money/status gives him direct access to decision-makers.

The scene should not be simplified into “the Association only cares about donors.” The organization is already mounting rescue activity. The stronger claim is:

> **private wealth can exert visible pressure over what a nominally public hero institution treats as urgent and how its leadership is spoken to.**

That matters because V17 later lets Narinki substitute private force for public process when he loses patience.

# 5. S-Class mobilization shows coordination without common psychology

V17's rapid S-Class cuts reinforce a longstanding V2 rule: `S級` is a capability/recognition tier, not a shared personality or command culture.

Tatsumaki pushes for faster mobilization despite the nominal three-day window. Atomic Samurai appears with disciples and professional pride. Puri-Puri Prisoner responds from prison. Watchdog Man remains territorially fixed. Child Emperor is actively coordinating and trying to contact assets. Metal Knight refuses the requested framing and instead supplies a strategic warning.

`OPM|V17|chapter:86|images:0138-0157|anchor:heterogeneous S-Class mobilization and availability`

The institution must therefore coordinate not a conventional unit but a set of highly asymmetric autonomous actors.

# 6. Zombieman follows a hidden-actor thread instead of joining the obvious mobilization

Zombieman is shown tracing a lead to a small establishment and treating it as the endpoint of a pursuit.

`OPM|V17|chapter:86|images:0144-0145|anchor:Zombieman follows hidden-actor lead to small establishment`

At the V17 boundary this strengthens the fact that he is conducting an independent investigation. The exact identity and relevance of the target should remain bounded by what is visibly established here and prior source-locked material. It would be methodologically wrong to fill the scene with later exposition merely because the continuity eventually clarifies it.

This is a useful example of V17's decentralized structure: while the Association is centralizing a raid response, important information work is occurring outside that command channel.

---

# 7. Metal Knight gives Child Emperor an explicitly anti-coalitional security doctrine

Metal Knight's conversation with Child Emperor is one of the volume's strongest ideological contrasts to the cooperative hero material around it.

He states that what he fears is not only the Monster Association itself. Other evils may be waiting for humanity's defenses to become thin. His direct warning is to distrust even the human beside you; if one intends to enact justice, one should accumulate enough power to do it alone.

`OPM|V17|chapter:86|images:0155-0157|anchor:Metal Knight warns Child Emperor not to trust nearby humans`

This is **Metal Knight's worldview**, not narrator-certified truth.

Its structure is internally coherent:

1. simultaneous threats may exploit concentration of defenses;
2. organizations create insider/coordination vulnerabilities;
3. reliance on partners can therefore become strategic exposure;
4. independent force is treated as the safest guarantor of justice.

But V17 itself supplies counterevidence to the absolutized version of this doctrine. King and Saitama solve Elder Centipede through complementary capacities. Bang/Bomb/Genos coordinate across different mechanisms. The Association, despite flaws, is organizing a rescue that no ordinary individual could easily perform alone.

The tension should remain live:

`security through independent overwhelming capacity`
versus
`security through distributed specialization and trust`.

Child Emperor is placed directly between them.

# 8. Fubuki encounters a social orbit that refuses to become the Fubuki Group

## 8.1 The scene begins with a category error

Fubuki arrives expecting an emergency-response conversation. Saitama is using his phone, wounded heroes are recovering, King is present, and the atmosphere does not resemble formal crisis command.

When Saitama asks why she is there, Fubuki answers that it is an emergency.

`OPM|V17|chapter:86|images:0158-0163|anchor:Fubuki enters Saitama's informal post-battle gathering`

The comedy rests on a real institutional contrast. Fubuki understands group action through hierarchy, mobilization, and visible organizational behavior. Saitama's orbit has developed through repeated relationships, practical contact, coincidence, mentorship, and individual choice. It has **social density without formal membership**.

## 8.2 Fubuki does useful operational reasoning even while defaulting to status logic

Fubuki asks what can be done and reasons about the Monster Association as an unusual aggregation of normally independent monsters. She recognizes that some stronger organizing force or structure must explain the level of coordination.

`OPM|V17|chapter:86|images:0166-0169|anchor:Fubuki reasons about Monster Association coordination`

This is real analytical competence. It should not be erased by the joke that follows.

At the same time, she immediately considers how B-Class and lower heroes' action during the emergency might affect later evaluation. The old rank/visibility heuristic remains active even after previous challenges to it.

That is a useful character-model result: **Fubuki updates, but she does not become a different person after one corrective conversation.** Her strategic and social instincts remain path-dependent.

## 8.3 The others' injuries expose her attempt to convert proximity into a task force

Genos notes that Kuseno worked through the previous night to rebuild him and that less than half a day has passed. Bang and Bomb acknowledge that age/injury leave them unable to move normally. King is not a conventional fighter. Fubuki concludes that she and Saitama are the immediately mobile pair.

`OPM|V17|chapter:86|image:0167|anchor:Fubuki inventories who can act immediately`

That is a reasonable local conclusion. The problem is what she assumes it means socially.

## 8.4 Saitama acts on his own reason, not the faction plan

When King tells Saitama that the hero hunter he had wanted to meet was present earlier and Saitama hears that Garou has attacked more heroes, Saitama becomes angry. He also adds a characteristically petty-recognition layer: Garou has not come to hunt **him**, and he wonders whether that is because he is `ハゲマント`.

`OPM|V17|chapter:86|images:0169-0170|anchor:Saitama anger at Garou plus Caped Baldy recognition irritation`

The combination is important:

- he is genuinely angry that heroes are being attacked;
- he also dislikes being omitted from the threat's attention;
- the disliked hero name remains emotionally live;
- ethical concern and ego irritation coexist without being interchangeable.

He then decides to search for Garou and hit him. He casually adds that he will buy napa cabbage on the way.

`OPM|V17|chapter:86|image:0171|anchor:Saitama combines Garou search with cabbage errand`

This is quintessential Saitama domain compression: a dangerous hero-hunter search and an ordinary household errand can occupy the same practical sentence. The mundane layer has not been displaced by escalating plot stakes.

## 8.5 Fubuki interprets exclusion through the only group grammar she knows well

Fubuki thinks that she had tried to take charge but was left outside the action again. Her next thought is not “perhaps these people are not a faction.” It is that getting Saitama and the others into the Fubuki Group will be difficult.

`OPM|V17|chapter:86|image:0171|anchor:Fubuki treats Saitama orbit as difficult recruitment target`

This strengthens her reconstruction model. Fubuki's group-building is not a superficial gimmick abandoned when evidence contradicts it. It is a durable social strategy through which she translates ambiguous relationships into hierarchy and affiliation.

The volume simultaneously shows why the strategy misreads Saitama: **he relates to people without needing the relationship to become an organization**.

# 9. Narinki's private substitute for the Association reproduces the same epistemic problem at smaller scale

Narinki declares the Hero Association unreliable and sends his own private unit into Z-City. The team wears battle suits obtained through a special route, and Narinki describes their performance as exceeding ordinary professional heroes.

`OPM|V17|chapter:86|images:0172-0174|anchor:Narinki deploys private squad in special-route battle suits`

The squad is named and presented with confidence. That framing invites a familiar OPM question: **what exactly does a measured equipment advantage tell us about performance inside an unknown adversarial environment?**

V17 answers in the next chapter: not enough.

Private procurement increases capacity. It does not automatically supply reconnaissance, threat modeling, institutional intelligence, escape routes, or matchup knowledge. Narinki's frustration with bureaucracy creates **faster action**, but faster action is not identical to better action.

# 10. `87撃目 アジト` — Garou enters an institution that wants to own the monster role

## 10.1 The Monster Association begins with hospitality and asymmetry of control

Garou wakes heavily bandaged in a bed. A written message welcomes him to the Monster Association base, tells him he can use the room and provided clothing, and frames his presence as something arranged for him.

`OPM|V17|chapter:87|images:0175-0178|anchor:Garou wakes treated inside Monster Association base`

The scene is not benevolence in a neutral sense. Garou did not independently choose to check into the organization. He was extracted from battle, treated, and placed inside a controlled environment because the Association sees potential strategic value.

The hospitality is therefore simultaneously:

- material care;
- recruitment preparation;
- containment;
- information asymmetry.

Garou's damaged body makes the asymmetry especially visible.

## 10.2 Garou remains observant and resistant rather than grateful-submissive

He inventories injuries, hunger, mobility, sounds, routes, and surrounding actors. He does not take treatment as establishing authority over him.

This continues a core Garou policy: **he wants the monster role without wanting to become another actor's subordinate monster**.

That distinction becomes decisive by the end of the chapter.

# 11. Machine God G5 makes `組織` an explicit inter-organizational actor

A mechanical combatant identifies itself as `機神G5`, disaster level Demon, and states:

`怪人協会に加勢すべく “組織”から派遣されてきました`

`OPM|V17|chapter:87|image:0183|anchor:Machine God G5 says Organization dispatched it to assist Monster Association`

This is a significant hidden-actor advancement. At V17 we can safely state:

- an external actor named `組織` exists in the dialogue as G5's sender;
- that actor can deploy a high-capability mechanical agent;
- the deployment is intended to support the Monster Association;
- therefore Monster Association operations are not fully self-contained.

We **cannot yet safely collapse every earlier reference, robot, laboratory, battle suit, or Zombieman hypothesis into this same organization** without explicit connective evidence. The new fact strengthens a candidate network; it does not solve the whole network.

G5's own speech is formal, declarative, and mission-oriented. Its introduction immediately establishes institutional provenance rather than personal appetite, distinguishing its antagonist register from many biologically monstrous actors around it.

# 12. The Narinki private squad demonstrates hardware advantage without environment mastery

The captured private unit's suits are noticed for their high performance. That high performance does not save the group from being overwhelmed by the number, unfamiliar abilities, and tactical environment of the base.

`OPM|V17|chapter:87|images:0182-0194|anchor:Narinki private squad's advanced suits fail inside Monster Association base`

This is not an argument that technology is worthless. It is almost the opposite: the suits are sufficiently valuable that other actors comment on them. The failure shows that **equipment performance is one variable inside an adversarial system**.

Their deployment lacked the broader structure needed to convert capacity into mission success.

This sequence should be read beside Metal Knight's warning, but not as a simple endorsement of him. Metal Knight favors independent overwhelming strength; Narinki's squad shows that **not being embedded in the Hero Association** does not magically produce that strength or the intelligence required to use it.

# 13. Garou and the Monster Association negotiate competing definitions of monsterhood

## 13.1 Gyoro Gyoro treats Garou as a strategic personnel problem

Garou is brought before Gyoro Gyoro and the much larger figure behind it. He immediately asks why he has received special treatment and what the Association wants from him.

`OPM|V17|chapter:87|images:0196-0198|anchor:Garou questions Gyoro Gyoro about special treatment and desired role`

This is consistent with Garou's autonomy. He does not interpret care as loyalty debt.

## 13.2 The Association's numbers reveal that raw headcount is not its stated deficit

Gyoro Gyoro says the Monster Association has roughly **500 members**, a number likened to the professional hero population. It also states that after the recent conflict, the remaining estimated Demon-level-or-higher group is under thirty.

`OPM|V17|chapter:87|image:0199|anchor:Monster Association states approximate membership and high-threat survivor count`

The crucial organizational claim follows: even if present force may be sufficient to win, what the organization lacks is **`団結力` and `先導者`**—cohesion and leadership.

That is an important correction to “Monster Association = collection of strong monsters.” The group itself recognizes that aggregation does not automatically produce organization.

Power here has at least four distinct dimensions:

1. individual combat capacity;
2. number of available actors;
3. willingness to coordinate;
4. command/leadership capable of directing coordination.

The Monster Association has a great deal of the first two and explicitly identifies weakness in the latter two.

## 13.3 Garou is offered institutional promotion before his identity has been accepted

Gyoro Gyoro asks Garou to become an executive and assist it. His anti-hero disposition, survival capacity, and willingness to challenge S-Class heroes make him attractive as personnel.

`OPM|V17|chapter:87|image:0199|anchor:Gyoro Gyoro offers Garou executive role`

This is deeply ironic relative to the volume opening. Garou's monster role began as rebellion against a socially predetermined hierarchy. Now an actual monster institution offers him **rank** inside its hierarchy because his rebellion is useful.

The offer creates a new danger for his self-authorship: becoming institutionally legible as a monster may require accepting someone else's monster script.

## 13.4 The Association explicitly says Garou has not yet proved he is a monster

Gyoro Gyoro tells him that they still cannot judge whether he is truly a monster and demands proof.

`OPM|V17|chapter:87|image:0200|anchor:Gyoro Gyoro says Garou has not proved true monsterhood`

Garou jokes about growing horns, exposing the body-category assumption. The organization immediately answers with a behavioral criterion instead.

## 13.5 The proof condition is a hero's severed head

The instruction is direct: bring the head of any hero. Another monster glosses the condition: if Garou can kill a hero without hesitation, that makes him a proper monster.

`OPM|V17|chapter:87|image:0201|anchor:Monster Association defines proof as unhesitating hero killing`

This is perhaps V17's most important monsterhood evidence because it splits four layers that Garou has often tried to collapse:

- **self-label** — Garou calls himself a monster;
- **social assignment** — others call or treat him as monster/criminal;
- **body state** — he remains human-bodied at this boundary despite extreme injury and adaptation;
- **ethical action threshold** — the Monster Association says “real monster” requires willingness to murder a hero.

The organization does not validate Garou merely because he hates heroes. It demands conduct.

Garou's silence matters, but silence is not yet a refusal. The V17 boundary must remain open on whether he will accept, evade, reinterpret, or reject the test.

# 16. Saitama state at the V17 boundary

V17 is not primarily a Saitama interiority volume, but it adds several useful constraints.

## 16.1 Overwhelming force can be embedded inside someone else's plan

Saitama's Elder Centipede kill is physically decisive but tactically situated by King. This strengthens a non-solipsistic model of his power: the most effective use of overwhelming capability can depend on information and positioning supplied by someone weaker.

He does not need to dominate command in order to be useful.

Saitama is angry that Garou has attacked heroes. He also finds it personally irritating that Garou has not come after him and wonders whether `ハゲマント` is the reason.

## Garou — major strengthen; strong-tier candidacy

### Goal / self-concept

### Relationship

### Institution
Monster Association attempts to convert his self-authored monsterhood into executive membership and tests him through hero murder. Garou does not submit cleanly.

### Power / learning
He survives extreme cumulative damage, but V17's biggest advancement is ideological and relational rather than a new clean technique claim.

### Readiness implication
Across V09–V17 Garou now has repeated evidence in combat, ordinary interaction, child relationship, master relationship, self-narration, ideological origin, defeat response, learning policy, institutional coercion/recruitment, bodily limits, and contradiction. Promotion from `moderate` to **`strong` is warranted** if the readiness index confirms no missing gating dimension.

## Narinki — emerging stakeholder model

# 19. Heroism, rank, recognition, and institution

### 19.1 Rank can indicate extraordinary capability without creating obedience

Blast is the clearest case. Formal rank does not equal availability to command.

### 19.2 Public reputation can become real causal power without becoming true description

King's myth causes Elder Centipede to behave differently. Reputation functions as a strategic asset while remaining epistemically false about physical ability.

The Monster Association also has rank, recruitment, executive loss, coordination problems, and heterogeneous elite actors. That parallel is organizational. Its explicit murder test preserves a major ethical difference.

# 26. Prospective synthesis at the V17 boundary

V17 begins with Garou arguing that a social group can make morality feel predetermined by assigning the admired child the hero role and the disliked child the monster role. It ends with a literal Monster Association telling Garou that his own self-label is insufficient and prescribing the act that will make the role real in its eyes.

That bookending is the volume's strongest construction.

The first institution in Garou's memory says, in effect:

`you are the monster, therefore you lose`.

The present Monster Association says:

`you say you are the monster; prove it by killing`.

Garou's entire project sits between those two forms of external authorship.

The rest of the volume multiplies the same problem across other characters. Blast's rank cannot force his action. Fubuki cannot turn friendship into command merely by deciding she should lead. Narinki cannot buy perfect competence. Metal Knight thinks justice should become independent enough to need no trust. King has a false reputation that becomes strategically real because others believe it. Saitama has real power that repeatedly escapes institutional recognition. The Monster Association has hundreds of bodies but lacks the cohesion it wants.

The volume's title `パワー` therefore expands far beyond attack output. V17 asks what kinds of power actually move a situation:

- physical force;
- technique;
- regeneration;
- reputation;
- information;
- money;
- rank;
- autonomy;
- coordination;
- leadership;
- social consensus;
- narrative legitimacy;
- willingness to kill;
- the ability to refuse someone else's assigned role.

No single dimension dominates every domain.

The safest V17 cumulative formulation is:

> **Power becomes politically meaningful when it can assign roles, compel behavior, or make others accept a story about who someone is. V17's deepest characters are those who expose the gap between that assigned story and observable conduct.**

At the boundary, Garou remains the most unstable case. His childhood grievance is now much better grounded. His current moral status is not thereby cleansed. The Monster Association's murder test is valuable precisely because it forces the contradiction out of rhetoric and into future behavior.
