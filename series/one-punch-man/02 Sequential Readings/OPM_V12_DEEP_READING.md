# 2. Volume orientation

V10 distinguished **reality from the proxies used to make it legible**. V11 then asked how strength is **coordinated, constrained, recruited, deployed, or misassigned**. V12 moves the inquiry again. Its most persistent question is not simply “who is stronger?” but:

> **What is strength for?**

The answer is different for nearly every major participant.

- Lightning Max and Snek connect strength to the public vocation of hero work.
- Suiryu treats strength as personal freedom: sufficient ability lets him live comfortably, avoid burdens, enjoy himself, and refuse moral obligation.
- Saitama wants strength to provide meaningful experience, but his overwhelming capability now obstructs the very experience he is seeking.
- Genos treats strength as an iterative technical problem and explicitly accepts that growth requires acknowledging domains in which an opponent surpasses him.
- Fubuki accepts a tactical handicap in order not to injure her subordinates, making strength answerable to relationship.
- Tatsumaki sees weaker allies as liabilities that stronger power should render unnecessary, even while protecting them.
- Snek begins asking whether institutionalized heroism has meaning if much stronger people exist outside the institution.
- the Monster Association treats strength as organized strategic capital.
- Drive Knight treats strength as an instrument for acquiring information.
- Watchdog Man's strength has become a real public protection infrastructure for Q City.
- King's nonexistent combat strength nevertheless produces a genuine deterrence effect because other people act on the reputation.

The volume therefore refuses to let “strength” remain a single scalar. Physical output, technique, mobility, information, willingness to serve, institutional legitimacy, social deterrence, relationship, and motivation can all matter independently.

## Central V12 thesis

**V12 separates capability from the purpose, role, and relationship that give capability meaning. Strength can protect, liberate, isolate, obligate, intimidate, entertain, mislead, or become an obstacle to experience. The volume's decisive contrasts arise not merely from who is strongest, but from what each character believes strength permits or requires and what they do when strength fails to settle the problem by itself.**

This formulation is stronger than “power levels do not matter” and safer than “heroism is unrelated to power.” Power plainly matters: Suiryu effortlessly defeats professional heroes, the Monster Association counts depleted hero assets, Watchdog Man's actual strength makes Q City safer, and Saitama ends matches instantly. The distinction is that **power does not determine its own telos**.

## Strongest limiting reading

V12 remains a transitional volume. The Monster Association offensive is still developing. Garou's Watchdog Man fight has barely begun. The Super Fight tournament is not resolved. Genos has identified an opponent as `規格外` without yet explaining the broader consequence. Several new heroes receive only one bounded sequence. Accordingly:

- Suiryu's fatalistic speech is a character position, not a narrator-certified theory of society;
- Snek's professional crisis is genuine but not yet a settled resignation;
- `怪人王` establishes an observed Monster Association title/hierarchy, not the final hidden truth of command;
- Drive Knight's interrogation scene establishes a ruthless operational policy, not his ultimate allegiance;
- Garou's belief that he has `見切った` Watchdog Man's movements is an observed judgment immediately stress-tested by the next impact, not yet a complete fight outcome.

# 3. Sequential close reading

## 3.1 `62撃目 求める理由` — strength acquires motives

### 3.1.1 Lightning Max: strength as professional aspiration

The Super Fight tournament opens V12 by continuing to place recognized martial specialists beside professional heroes. Lightning Max's participation is not merely a sports cameo. His self-understanding is organized around the need to become stronger because his hero work has exposed him to opponents beyond his current level.

The chapter title `求める理由` therefore matters. It frames strength as something that requires a reason, not as a self-explanatory good.

Max's professional orientation is legible in the contrast that follows: he may be weaker than Suiryu, but his desire for improvement is tied to a public role and prior exposure to danger.

**Interpretive consequence:** a lower power level can coexist with a more externally answerable strength ethic. V12 is already separating **capacity** from **purpose**.

### 3.1.2 Suiryu: strength as low-burden autonomy

Suiryu's answer is almost the inverse. After demonstrating superior ability, he states:

`テキトーに楽して生きてくためだけど 俺の方が強くてごめんな`

The important phrase is not merely the taunt `俺の方が強くてごめんな`. His reason for pursuing strength is to live casually and comfortably. Strength functions as **freedom from demands**.

This does not yet make Suiryu a coward or a sadist. His position is closer to hedonistic autonomy: if he is strong enough, he can enjoy the world without accepting the burdens other people attach to strength.

`OPM|V12|chapter:62|image:0025|anchor:Suiryu strength sought to live easily`

### 3.1.3 Genos recognizes strong people outside the hero profession

Genos observes that many powerful people exist outside formal hero work and notes Suiryu's prize-centered reason for entering. That observation matters after V10's critique of measurement and V11's institutional coordination problems. The Hero Association does not monopolize strength.

Yet the sequence does not imply that professional hero classification is meaningless. Rather, it creates a new separation:

`strong person` ≠ `professional hero` ≠ `heroic purpose`.

Genos also wonders whether Saitama will obtain the satisfaction he is looking for from martial arts. He is watching not merely power output but his teacher's **motivational problem**.

### 3.1.4 Saitama sees Suiryu without sharing the prestige response

Snek and the crowd treat Suiryu as a high-salience martial figure. Saitama's response remains characteristically flat. He has seen Suiryu, but he does not automatically inherit the room's excitement.

This should not be scored as “Saitama is uninterested in martial arts.” V10 already established genuine curiosity. The distinction is between:

- interest in an unfamiliar **domain** (`武術`, `技`), and
- automatic investment in a person's **reputation** inside that domain.

Saitama's curiosity remains experiential rather than prestige-mediated.

### 3.1.5 Genos chooses independent crisis duty rather than interrupt Saitama

When simultaneous monster reports spread across the city, Genos considers whether to alert Saitama. He instead judges that Saitama is focused on the tournament and that the current problem is something Genos can handle himself.

The important behavioral structure is:

1. monitor large-scale threat information;
2. consider teacher routing;
3. infer Saitama's immediate activity/priority;
4. decide not to interrupt;
5. assume independent duty.

This strengthens Genos's autonomy. It also demonstrates attentiveness to Saitama without making the stronger claim that Genos has infallible insight into Saitama's unspoken needs.

`OPM|V12|chapter:62|images:0026-0031|anchor:Genos leaves Saitama at tournament and routes himself to incidents`

## 3.2 `63撃目 試合と戦闘` — identical kinetic grammar, different moral stakes

### 3.2.1 The chapter title is a formal instruction

`試合と戦闘` announces the chapter's construction. Tournament matches and city combat are intercut, and Murata repeatedly gives both the same kinetic dignity: named techniques, impact panels, acceleration, pose, tactical movement.

But the contexts are different.

A tournament match is bounded by rules, spectators, declared winners, and a prize. Genos's city fights involve active disaster reports, lethal monsters, unknown civilians, incomplete intelligence, and continuing incident queues.

The manga therefore refuses an easy formal equation:

> looking like combat does not make two combats ethically or institutionally equivalent.

### 3.2.2 Genos treats a city-wide crisis as a queue of solvable mechanisms

Genos moves among multiple incidents using his familiar combination of rapid violence and analysis. His tools include incineration, electrical output, machine-gun blows, mobility, and target sequencing. After eliminating one threat, he registers that multiple incidents remain and keeps routing himself rather than treating a local victory as completion.

This strengthens a durable Genos policy:

`threat report -> classify -> intervene -> confirm local result -> update remaining problem set -> move`.

The chapter makes this cognition visible by juxtaposing him with tournament brackets. Both systems generate “next opponents,” but Genos's sequence is a civic workload rather than an entertainment ladder.

### 3.2.3 Electric technique becomes a cross-domain visual rhyme

One tournament competitor's electric-body technique is intercut with Genos's electrical combat. The rhyme matters because it prevents the power system from belonging to one institutional genre. Similar mechanisms can appear as sport, style, profession, or survival tool.

**Visual-form implication:** V12 repeatedly asks the reader to distinguish the *function* of spectacular technique from the spectacle itself.

### 3.2.4 Saitama's tournament interest remains narrow and practical

Saitama continues participating under Charanko's identity. His fraud is not morally elevated into a spiritual martial quest. Prize money, curiosity, and direct experience remain mixed motives. The narrative permits his large ethical reflexes and his casual low-stakes rule-breaking to coexist.

That coexistence is important for modeling. “Heroic” does not mean uniformly scrupulous about every bureaucratic rule.

## 3.3 `64撃目 限界` — power itself can become a limit

### 3.3.1 Bakuzan reads stance; Saitama asks for experience

Bakuzan initially interprets Saitama's posture as incompetence. Saitama does not answer with school prestige or boast. He says:

`ああ 実はこういうの初めてなんだ`

and then:

`アンタの武術を体験させてくれ`

The request is highly characteristic of Saitama's V10-V12 curiosity. He wants to **experience** the martial art. He is not asking to be initiated into Bakuzan's hierarchy, memorizing a doctrine, or deferring to a reputation.

`OPM|V12|chapter:64|image:0074|anchor:Saitama asks to experience Bakuzan martial art`

### 3.3.2 Saitama permits technique to happen to him

Bakuzan interprets the request as insulting and applies technique. Saitama does not instantly retaliate at the first contact. This is not physical vulnerability. It is a deliberate experiential opening: he is permitting an unfamiliar system to act long enough to learn what it feels like.

When Bakuzan handles his head, however, Saitama's ordinary boundary appears: `頭さわんなよ`. The line keeps the scene grounded in bodily irritation rather than mystical martial reverence.

### 3.3.3 The paradox of overwhelming strength

Saitama's eventual response sends Bakuzan out immediately. Saitama then realizes:

`しまった また試合できなかった`

This is one of the most important Saitama observations since the Boros material because it identifies a nontraditional **limit created by excess capability**.

Saitama is not unable to win. He is unable to sustain the experiential conditions under which he hoped to learn.

His strength therefore has a paradoxical cost:

`overwhelming physical solution capacity -> premature closure -> loss of desired experience`.

The word `限界` need not be reduced to “Saitama has found his combat limit.” The volume instead creates several different limits, including a limit of **meaningful access**.

`OPM|V12|chapter:64|images:0074-0078|anchor:Saitama experiential inquiry defeated by own overwhelming finish`

### 3.3.4 Metal Bat's hospitalization bounds `気合い`

The wider crisis report states that Metal Bat is unconscious and seriously injured. This is important continuity from V11. `気合い` allowed extraordinary continued combat performance under accumulated damage; it did not erase damage, regenerate his body, or make him limitless.

**Revision control:**
- PRESERVE: fighting spirit can materially elevate Metal Bat's combat performance under injury.
- STRENGTHEN: the state has real post-combat cost.
- REJECT: any interpretation of `気合い` as costless regeneration or indefinite invulnerability.

### 3.3.5 Hero Association uncertainty is not one failure mechanism

Association leadership is confronted with simultaneous monster activity and the kidnapping of Narinki's son Waganma. The discussion explicitly states that the relation between the mass monster incidents and the kidnapping is not yet established.

At the same time, Narinki provides **7% of the Association's total funding**. His son's rescue therefore receives extraordinary priority pressure.

The chapter makes at least three vulnerabilities distinct:

1. **information uncertainty** — the Association does not yet know how the events relate;
2. **capacity shortage** — `ヒーローが足りない`;
3. **funding leverage** — a major donor can distort prioritization pressure.

This is much more precise than “the Hero Association is corrupt.” The organization has real public-safety work, real shortages, and a financing structure that can create incentive distortion simultaneously.

`OPM|V12|chapter:64|image:0083|anchor:Association uncertainty, Narinki seven-percent funding, hero shortage`

### 3.3.6 Awakened Cockroach defeats Genos in a domain before Genos defeats the local problem

Against Awakened Cockroach, Genos first admits that he cannot capture the opponent's movement. He reasons that a large-output attack is likely to miss and create a counterattack opening. The enemy further claims that his body automatically reads hostile intent and evades before a strike lands.

Genos's crucial response is not denial. He remembers Dr. Kuseno's advice and states:

`どうしても相手が上回る分野については受け入れなければ成長することはできない`

The wording is load-bearing. The point is **domain-specific inferiority**: `相手が上回る分野`. Growth requires accepting that fact.

This is not humility as self-abasement. Genos remains irritated by what the concession means. He disliked implementing a device that implicitly admitted he could not match an opponent's speed.

The resulting model is more precise:

> Genos can experience pride at a capability gap, explicitly acknowledge the gap, and still use an inelegant countermeasure rather than protect his self-image at the expense of the mission.

### 3.3.7 Prepared adhesive turns prior weakness into engineering memory

Genos uses `超強力な瞬間接着剤`, a prepared counter for an exceptionally fast opponent. The device is particularly important after earlier speed problems with Sonic.

He does not “become faster than speed.” He changes the problem:

`cannot reliably win speed domain -> restrict opponent's movement -> restore attackability`.

This is one of the cleanest demonstrations of Genos's learning architecture in the V2 pass.

`OPM|V12|chapter:64|images:0089-0095|anchor:Genos accepts speed-domain inferiority and uses prepared adhesive`

### 3.3.8 Local adaptation does not prevent global surprise

Awakened Cockroach escapes through bodily sacrifice. Soon afterward, another fast/high-level attacker strikes Genos and defeats him badly.

The sequence is important because it refuses to convert “Genos learns” into “Genos cannot be surprised.” His adaptation policy is real, but the environment can still contain an opponent outside the model he prepared for.

This becomes explicit later in `67撃目`, when Genos calls the attacker `規格外` and recognizes that an enemy of clearly different level is present.

## 3.4 `65撃目 姉妹` — relationship makes power costly

### 3.4.1 Fubuki's group becomes a liability she chooses not to discard

Do-S controls members of the Fubuki Group and turns them against Fubuki. The tactical structure is diagnostic precisely because V09 established that Fubuki's group is both coercive hierarchy **and** genuine organization.

Fubuki has a simple high-output option only if she treats her subordinates as expendable. Instead she tries to neutralize them without injuring them.

Do-S identifies the consequence: stopping this many controlled people without hurting them requires Fubuki to divide her concentration.

This is a concrete behavioral cost. Fubuki's attachment to her organization is not merely symbolic or self-serving when the group becomes inconvenient.

`OPM|V12|chapter:65|image:0104|anchor:Fubuki divides concentration to avoid injuring controlled subordinates`

### 3.4.2 Saitama's warning returns without dictating Fubuki's answer

Fubuki recalls Saitama's earlier warning that weaker followers will not save her from an opponent stronger than she is. The memory is now operationally relevant: her followers are actively being used to weaken her.

But the scene does **not** vindicate the strongest possible reading of Saitama's warning. Fubuki does not conclude “therefore relationships are worthless” or abandon them.

She continues trying to protect the same people who have become a tactical burden.

Thus the V09/V12 transition is:

- PRESERVE: Fubuki's reliance on hierarchy creates vulnerability.
- STRENGTHEN: the group also carries genuine attachment/responsibility.
- REVISE: “Fubuki Group = purely instrumental entourage” is no longer tenable.

### 3.4.3 Tatsumaki's sister connection is protective and controlling at once

Fubuki says Tatsumaki will come even without being called and later infers that Tatsumaki sensed she had taken damage. When Tatsumaki arrives, she tells Fubuki to go home and says she will handle everything.

The relational signal is not simply affection. It combines:

- remote/sensory vigilance toward Fubuki;
- immediate protective intervention;
- confidence that Tatsumaki alone is sufficient;
- infantilization of Fubuki's autonomy;
- low tolerance for Fubuki's chosen network.

`全部 私が片付けるから` is protective reassurance and hierarchical displacement in the same sentence.

### 3.4.4 Fubuki calls the controlled group allies; Tatsumaki calls them burdens

When Do-S orders the controlled heroes to sacrifice themselves against Tatsumaki, Fubuki protests that they cannot win. She then explicitly tells Tatsumaki:

`待って お姉ちゃん！彼らは味方よ!!`

and asks her not to attack them because they have only temporarily lost themselves.

Tatsumaki does in fact neutralize them without killing them. That point matters: she is not indifferent to their lives.

But she rejects Fubuki's category. She calls them `足手まとい` rather than useful allies and frames her own intervention as so far below a serious attack that Fubuki's concern is almost absurd.

This is the cleanest sister contrast yet:

| Fubuki | Tatsumaki |
|---|---|
| strength is partly organized through people | strength should make weaker people unnecessary |
| followers can be liability and still be `味方` | weaker allies become `足手まとい` under crisis |
| accepts concentration cost to preserve group | eliminates immediate liability through unilateral control |
| hierarchy plus dependency | protection plus radical self-sufficiency |

Neither side is flattened into moral simplicity. Fubuki's earlier coercion remains canon. Tatsumaki actually protects Fubuki and spares the group.

### 3.4.5 Tatsumaki's protection model remains operational rather than dialogic

Before leaving, Tatsumaki tells Fubuki to have the others evacuate during disasters because they are in the way. Her instinct is not to coordinate their contribution. It is to remove them from the operational field.

This strengthens the V10 private-state finding that being needed is central to Tatsumaki while adding a sharper relational implication: **the role of indispensable protector is maintained partly by treating dependence on weaker actors as a problem to eliminate.**

`OPM|V12|chapter:65|images:0121-0134|anchor:Tatsumaki senses Fubuki, intervenes, neutralizes group, calls them burdens`

## 3.5 `66撃目 強い奴ら` — capability and heroism separate explicitly

### 3.5.1 Snek begins from remembered vocational pride

Snek confronts a monster while civilians identify him as an A-Class professional hero. His internal narration reaches backward to a time when he felt fully legitimate:

`あの頃の俺は最高に輝いていた`

He understood himself as one of the limited people who possessed the power to save the public. That belief gave him psychological fulfillment and a sense that he was a `本物のヒーロー`.

This is important because V03 mainly showed Snek under status threat. V12 reveals what status had been supporting: not only ego, but a professional identity organized around **being unusually capable of rescue**.

### 3.5.2 Stronger people destabilize the basis of that professional identity

Repeated encounters with S-Class power and with extremely strong people outside the Hero Association destabilize Snek's premise. He begins asking:

`プロヒーローとは本当に必要なのだろうか?`

He even imagines that if he were absent—or if the Hero Association disappeared—someone else might still kill monsters.

The question is not yet settled. Snek explicitly resists self-negation: `自己否定はしたくない`.

This makes the scene substantially richer than simple envy. His crisis is:

`professional uniqueness claim -> evidence of stronger outsiders -> uncertainty about institutional necessity -> resistance to self-erasure`.

`OPM|V12|chapter:66|images:0136-0142|anchor:Snek questions whether professional heroes are necessary`

### 3.5.3 Suiryu attacks the moral premise rather than merely Snek's power

Suiryu asks why Snek is a hero at all given his level of strength. He describes his own power as largely a matter of `才能`, notes that he has not treated training as an all-consuming duty, and then asks whether Snek's hero work has meaning.

This is the volume's clearest confrontation between **ability** and **obligation**.

Suiryu's argument continues: strong monsters are increasing; one hero's effort is like trying to cool a burning stone with water; people who survive will survive; weaker creatures being selected out is `自然の理`.

When Snek challenges the implication—should people under monster attack simply be abandoned?—Suiryu clarifies that he is not forbidding rescue:

`助けたければ勝手にどうぞ`

His stronger claim is that temporary rescue merely stages an appearance of peace without changing the world very much:

`その場しのぎで平和を演出したって 世界は大して変わらないだろう`

This is best classified as **detached fatalism / hedonistic anti-obligation**, not a demonstrated commitment to maximizing suffering. He permits other people to rescue; he denies that their local action creates deeper meaning.

### 3.5.4 The montage refuses to let Suiryu become narrator

The speech is intercut with a world still full of people acting: heroes, injured people, monsters, public figures, institutions. The form therefore matters. Suiryu is not given an empty philosophical stage in which his conclusion becomes authorial exposition.

The manga immediately preserves the empirical counterpressure:

- people still call for heroes;
- people are still injured when heroes fail;
- some heroes continue fighting despite being weaker than Suiryu;
- institutions may be flawed yet still route real protection.

The question remains open rather than resolved by power.

### 3.5.5 Suiryu proves the power claim while leaving the value claim unresolved

Suiryu defeats Snek easily. This validates a narrow proposition: Suiryu is much stronger in the tournament.

It does **not** logically validate his philosophy of public obligation.

V12 is unusually careful about this distinction. In a conventional power fantasy, philosophical authority often follows victory. Here the physical verdict is decisive and the moral verdict remains contested.

### 3.5.6 `強い奴ら` widens beyond the tournament

The chapter title pluralizes strength across the wider crisis. The visual field includes hero professionals, Suiryu, Saitama in a mundane bodily setting, and Monster Association leadership. “Strong ones” exist inside radically different moral, social, and institutional configurations.

Saitama's inclusion in a toilet gag is especially functional: the objectively strongest known figure at the V12 boundary can occupy the least prestigious frame. Power and presentation remain separable.

## 3.6 Monster Association strategy becomes hierarchical

### 3.6.1 Gyoro Gyoro evaluates the campaign as an organization, not a swarm

The Monster Association's direct planning becomes clearer than in V11. Gyoro Gyoro reviews the simultaneous-city offensive and concludes that the monster side is broadly ahead despite losses.

The analysis includes hero casualties and reduced hero strength. Metal Bat, Genos, Metal Knight assets, and other high-value defenders are treated as variables in a campaign model.

This is organization-level cognition:

`distributed attacks -> observe defender depletion -> preserve main force -> compare remaining strategic reserves -> forecast victory`.

### 3.6.2 Hero losses are converted into strategic resource accounting

The statement that the Hero Association has suffered a major `戦力ダウン` does not simply celebrate individual monster wins. It treats hero capacity as a system resource.

That continues V11's coordination thesis but adds a sharper asymmetry: the Monster Association appears able to intentionally **produce** overload while the Hero Association is still reconstructing what is happening.

### 3.6.3 `怪人王` / `オロチ様` establishes an observed hierarchy, not ultimate metaphysics

Gyoro Gyoro says that above all, they have “you,” and the next pages identify the figure through:

`怪人王`

and

`オロチ様`

At the V12 boundary, the safe conclusions are:

- Orochi carries the title **Monster King**;
- Gyoro Gyoro treats Orochi as a decisive strategic asset and uses honorific `様`;
- the Monster Association has a visible hierarchy rather than purely decentralized membership;
- Gyoro Gyoro is acting as planner/analyst in the observed scene.

OPEN:
- whether title equals ultimate authority in every sense;
- the true creation/history of the organization;
- any hidden layer above/beside this structure;
- Orochi's own full motives and decision style.

`OPM|V12|chapter:66|images:0157-0161|anchor:Gyoro Gyoro campaign accounting and Orochi Monster King title`

## 3.7 `67撃目 規格外` — limits become epistemic and organizational

### 3.7.1 Genos names the fact of being outclassed

After recovering awareness from the surprise attack, Genos states:

`やられた…`

He cannot move and needs recovery/support from Dr. Kuseno's system. He then classifies the attacker:

`規格外だった…`

and recognizes that the enemy side contains a monster of clearly different level.

`規格外` should be read relationally: outside the expected standard/model. It is not yet a universal formal tier.

This is a crucial Genos state because it combines:

- physical incapacity;
- no rhetorical denial of defeat;
- immediate threat-model revision;
- reliance on technical support.

V12 repeatedly shows that growth requires accurately naming what one's current model cannot handle.

### 3.7.2 Drive Knight moves from cryptic warning to independent operational policy

Drive Knight's V07 appearance was a narrow warning to Genos. V12 finally supplies an independent field sequence.

He defeats a monster group and comments that numbers cannot simply be ignored even when individual enemies are weak. He then criticizes the Hero Association's information shortage and decides that killing endless “flies” is inefficient if the source can be found.

His language is explicitly instrumental:

`そこで相談がある`

followed by a demand to know where the monsters came from. One surviving informant is enough.

This establishes a decision policy:

`local extermination insufficient -> identify generating/source system -> preserve one information source -> coerce intelligence`.

### 3.7.3 Drive Knight's coercion is not sanitized by analytical competence

His final interrogation language makes the method morally explicit. He asks what torture the captive would like to experience, boasts that his tactical transformations can supply many kinds of pain, and offers the alternative of giving up all known information.

This is not merely “efficient intelligence collection.” It is threatened torture.

For character modeling, the scene supports:

- instrumental cognition;
- strong information priority;
- low expressed concern for enemy suffering;
- tactical transformation as both combat and interrogation infrastructure;
- calm, controlled menace rather than emotional rage.

It does **not** establish Drive Knight's ultimate alignment.

`OPM|V12|chapter:67|images:0169-0175|anchor:Drive Knight source-seeking and coercive interrogation policy`

### 3.7.4 Child Emperor turns defeat of a tool into useful information

Against the poison monster, Child Emperor's equipment does not simply solve the whole encounter. He obtains poison information, concludes an antiserum can be prepared for the relevant toxin, and routes the data to the Association even as the immediate fight becomes dangerous.

This extends the V10 `オカメちゃん` epistemic model:

`instrument -> measurement -> partial success/failure -> extract useful information -> update wider system`.

He then recognizes a biological sensing mechanism after the monster detects him and admits he is in a bad position without his backpack equipment.

The scene is valuable because the child prodigy is allowed to have **real equipment dependency and situational vulnerability** without making his analytical competence fake.

### 3.7.5 Pig God provides a radically embodied rescue solution

Pig God arrives and resolves the immediate poison-monster threat by swallowing it. He checks his own bodily condition and says he is fine to continue elsewhere.

Child Emperor is visibly surprised by the poison tolerance and remains concerned. Pig God does not turn that concern into a prestige argument; he simply self-assesses and resumes duty.

A bounded Pig God policy appears:

`ally in immediate danger -> bodily intervention -> internalize hazard -> check condition -> continue deployment`.

The evidence is coherent but still narrow.

### 3.7.6 Watchdog Man is a real version of public deterrence

Q City supplies an important counterpoint to King. Watchdog Man sits on an enormous pile of defeated monsters. Citizens say he eliminated around thirty monsters alone. Residents from surrounding areas are moving **toward** Q City because they believe his presence makes it safer.

He is called the city's `守り神`.

Unlike King's reputation, the public belief is grounded in directly observed local monster suppression at the V12 boundary.

This distinction matters for the public-narrative ledger:

- King: false causal attribution produces real deterrence;
- Watchdog Man: real capability produces real localized protective reputation.

Both can change civilian behavior, but the evidence bases are opposite.

### 3.7.7 Garou's strongest cognition becomes a source of overconfidence

Garou arrives after watching Watchdog Man fight monsters. He notes Watchdog Man's speed and power but concludes that there is no special technique or weapon and that he has already seen through the movement:

`もう番犬マンの動きは見切った`

This is not arbitrary arrogance. It emerges from a genuine strength established across V09-V11: Garou learns by observation and adapts rapidly.

V12 makes the next analytical move: **a reliable heuristic can become a blind spot when the observer assumes the available sample exhausts an unfamiliar domain.**

Garou thinks prior observation has converted the fight into a solved problem. The very next impact immediately stress-tests that confidence.

The volume ends the sequence there. V12 cannot say whether Garou wins, loses, escapes, or fully understands Watchdog Man.

# 4. `番外編 キングの休日なようで平日` — reputation manufactures action

## 4.1 King has an ordinary creative gaming life

King sits in a family restaurant with food and a laptop. He is not conducting hero work. By the end, he tells Saitama he was making a script for a self-produced game:

`自主制作ゲームの脚本作り`

This is important low-stakes character evidence. King is not merely “the guy who plays games.” He is engaged enough with games to create/write one.

That gives the private model another genuine competence/hobby domain that has nothing to do with his false heroic reputation.

## 4.2 King's public image produces real criminal deterrence

Narration states that King is said to deter crime merely by existing. A professional assassination team has accepted a lucrative contract to kill him and approaches the restaurant with a coordinated plan.

The irony is structural. King lacks the combat capability attributed to him, but the belief in that capability is itself causally powerful.

The assassins interpret ordinary behavior through an adversarial model in which every action by “the strongest man on Earth” must be intentional.

### Misrecognition loop

1. King sits normally.
2. assassins assume his stillness is calculated awareness.
3. King moves casually.
4. they infer a baited opening.
5. King changes location / attends to restaurant routine.
6. they infer civilian-protection positioning and trap design.
7. fear of his supposed hidden plan makes them less able to act.

This is not passive comedy alone. Incorrect belief creates a **real deterrence equilibrium**.

## 4.3 King partially recognizes the social problem and sets a boundary

The extra should not be flattened into “King has no idea anything is happening.” By the time he speaks directly, he clearly addresses the men as a category whose interaction he does not want during his private time.

He says:

`今はプライベートな時間だ`

and clarifies:

`俺の仕事は怪人退治`

`キミ達を相手にすることじゃない`

The statement is an attempt to decline engagement and define his hero-role boundary. The assassins hear something else: terrifying mercy from an omnipotent opponent.

King then worries:

`ちょっと言い方きつかったかな`

The private concern is ordinary social harshness, not domination.

## 4.4 The assassins author moral greatness onto him

The killers conclude that King understands everything and is nevertheless sparing them because even assassins are humans whom a hero protects from monsters. They interpret his refusal to fight as extraordinary ethical magnanimity.

They finally judge themselves defeated without combat:

`戦わずして完敗した`

The key analytical move is that **King's reputation now manufactures not only power feats but moral character**.

Earlier misattribution centered on strength and heroic accomplishments. Here observers infer:

- total tactical awareness;
- absolute control;
- mercy;
- equal regard for human life;
- large moral “capacity.”

The public King is becoming a collaboratively written fictional person whose supposed virtue can nonetheless change real behavior.

## 4.5 Saitama supplies the uncredited physical cleanup

Saitama arrives because King is waiting for him for games. When another hostile man obstructs him, Saitama responds with characteristic bluntness. The remaining hostile actors are physically neutralized quickly enough that the extra ends with bodies lying around while King and Saitama game.

This repeats the series' reputation inversion in a new configuration:

- King's mythical deterrence resolves one threat socially;
- Saitama's actual physical ability resolves another threat with almost no prestige;
- the two men then return to the low-stakes relationship that neither public narrative captures well.

## 4.6 The Saitama–King friendship is now longitudinally ordinary

Saitama asks what King was doing on the computer. King answers `自主制作ゲームの脚本作り`. They play together. Saitama still cannot beat him even with a handicap:

`くそ～ やっぱ強いなキング ハンデあっても勝てねぇ…`

King tells him to practice and come back next week.

That last point is especially valuable. The relationship has progressed beyond one confession and one gaming visit into **planned recurring leisure**.

At the V12 boundary, King is one of Saitama's clearest reciprocal low-stakes peer relationships.

# 17. V12 closure thesis

Volume 12 is not principally a ranking exercise even though it contains a tournament, S-Class heroes, monster campaign accounting, and repeated one-sided fights. Its deeper structure is **teleological**: strength becomes intelligible only through what a person does with it and what they think it entitles or obligates them to do.

Suiryu and Snek make the conflict explicit. Suiryu can dominate Snek physically while failing to prove that rescue is meaningless. Genos can lose a speed domain without losing his growth orientation. Fubuki can be endangered by her followers and still choose not to harm them. Tatsumaki can protect those same people while denying that they count as useful allies. Saitama can be physically unlimited in a match and still be blocked from the experience he wanted. King can be physically weak and nevertheless exert real social deterrence through a false reputation. Watchdog Man can have a similarly strong reputation grounded in real local defense.

The V12 boundary therefore supports a durable distinction:

> **Capability answers “what can this actor do?” Purpose, relationship, institution, and belief answer “what will that capability mean?”**

Those questions remain coupled but nonidentical.
