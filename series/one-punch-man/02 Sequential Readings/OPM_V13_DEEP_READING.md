# 2. Volume orientation

V10 distinguished reality from the proxies used to make it legible. V11 examined how strength is coordinated, constrained, recruited, and misassigned. V12 then asked what strength is *for*: protection, freedom, recognition, information, relation, duty, experience, or organized domination.

V13 pushes that inquiry across a threshold. Strength no longer merely has purposes; people and institutions begin to **change themselves, their roles, or their relation to others in order to obtain, display, preserve, or answer for it**.

The volume repeatedly asks variants of four linked questions:

1. **What must a person become to obtain more strength?**  
   Monster Cells literalize this question as biological conversion. Haragiri chooses to `人間を辞める` in exchange for greater power and then declares `ワシはもう人間じゃない`.

2. **What does strength commit its possessor to?**  
   Suiryu says very little: strength should make life freer and more fun. Mumen Rider, Tanktop Master, Max, and other heroes embody the opposite direction: even damaged or weaker professionals treat strength as a reason to remain between danger and people who cannot move.

3. **Can overwhelming strength make a practice unintelligible?**  
   Saitama deliberately receives Suiryu's martial techniques in order to understand them, yet his body never experiences the necessity that gives those techniques their functional meaning. He eventually reduces martial arts to `なんか動きがかっこいいもの`—not because the art has no function for everyone, but because his own capability removes most of the constraints the art solves.

4. **How do public roles and actual conduct coexist?**  
   Amai Mask can sing love and peace to an arena, preserve the performance while fighting, kill monsters brutally, and return immediately to an idol smile that lets the crowd assimilate lethal hero work back into show business.

## Central V13 thesis

**V13 treats strength as a threshold that can transform identity, role, knowledge, and consequence. The manga distinguishes the possession of power from the bodily, social, institutional, and moral changes made in its pursuit or use. A stronger body can alter ontology; a professional role can turn weakness into obligation; overwhelming capability can erase access to technique's practical meaning; and public identity can make radically different modes of conduct appear continuous from the outside.**

This is not the claim that strength itself is corrupting, nor that technique is cosmetic, nor that heroes are morally superior to nonheroes by definition. V13 supplies counterexamples to each simplification:

- Atomic Samurai's technique and judgment defeat a biologically enhanced Haragiri before the latter can exploit his new speed.
- Suiryu is extraordinarily strong without being a monster or member of either Association.
- weaker heroes can act with courage while stronger heroes can still be arrogant, competitive, or poor collaborators.
- Amai Mask's image management coexists with real combat effectiveness rather than replacing it.
- Monster Association organization explicitly aims beyond the monsters' `根源的欲求` for violence.

## Strongest limiting reading

V13 leaves several large questions unresolved and must not borrow answers from later canon:

- `怪人細胞` establishes an observed conversion mechanism and claimed effects, but not its ultimate origin, production chain, full reliability, or complete metaphysics.
- Haragiri converges biological monsterization, monster self-identification, anti-human conduct, and Monster Association affiliation; his case does **not** prove those dimensions are always identical.
- Orochi's ability to make monsters and visible `怪人王` status do not settle the complete hidden authority structure of the Monster Association.
- Suiryu's first defeat clearly destabilizes his self-conception, but V13 does not yet establish a mature post-defeat ethic.
- Saitama's shallow-sounding conclusion about martial arts should not be converted into narrator judgment that martial arts are merely aesthetic; the surrounding volume repeatedly shows technique solving problems for bodies that do not have Saitama's extreme physical surplus.
- Amai Mask's idol/hero split is strongly observed, but the source does not yet authorize deeper claims about hidden body ontology or ultimate psychological origin.

# 3. Sequential close reading

## 3.1 `68撃目 大戦力` — concentrated force does not equal coherent force

### 3.1.1 Bone: comic mechanism, serious professional orientation

The chapter opens amid distributed monster incidents rather than returning immediately to the tournament. B-class rank 77 Bone (`骨`) receives a deliberately comic bodily gimmick: drinking milk to strengthen his skeleton. Yet the scene around the gag matters more than the mechanism's plausibility. He remains in the field, protects/supports a lower or injured hero, and confronts a giant octopus even when the mismatch is obvious.

The volume repeatedly preserves this OPM pattern: ridiculous presentation does not cancel the underlying behavioral evidence. Bone's sample is too narrow for a broad model, but it is still evidence that low-ranked hero work can include active protective duty rather than mere status-seeking.

### 3.1.2 Death Gatling defines survival as cognition under extremity

A-class rank 8 Death Gatling gives one of V13's clearest statements about professional combat competence. The decisive requirement for surviving hard battle is not merely arm strength but:

`どんなピンチや極限状態でも正常に働く思考・判断力だ`

He has hidden in the rubble, observed the situation, and waited for sufficient combat assets to assemble. This is not cowardice concealed by rhetoric. His next action is to coordinate six A-class heroes into a joint counterattack.

The sequence defines a reusable policy:

`overmatch recognized -> preserve self as information-processing asset -> aggregate complementary force -> communicate target/weakness -> attack collectively`.

It is a different model of hero competence from Saitama's direct solution, Tatsumaki's overwhelming psychokinesis, or Flashy Flash's precision specialization.

`OPM|V13|chapter:68|image:0017|anchor:Death Gatling normal cognition and judgment under extremity`

### 3.1.3 The A-class coalition is rational even though it fails

Death Gatling gathers:

- Death Gatling — A8;
- Butterfly DX — A16;
- Smile Man — A27;
- Narcisstoic — A29;
- Peach Terry — A30;
- Chain'n'Toad / 鎖ガマ — A36.

The group exchanges information and attacks as a unit. V13 therefore should not be summarized as “teamwork is useless.” The team is a rational response to limited individual force.

The problem is that coordination has a capability ceiling. An observer warns that half-measures may simply make the giant octopus rampage more violently and increase collateral damage. V13 separates **good coordination** from **sufficient force**.

### 3.1.4 Flashy Flash: precision specialization and low tolerance for interference

Flashy Flash enters as the person warning that the A-class effort may be counterproductive. When challenged about handling the monster alone, he responds with low-ceremony confidence: `黙って見ていろ`.

His subsequent assault makes the basis for the confidence visible. The page grammar emphasizes extreme acceleration and precision: eyes and limbs are cut faster than lower-ranked observers can meaningfully process. Yet the octopus's scale and distributed anatomy keep the fight from becoming a trivial single-point elimination.

This gives a bounded first behavioral model:

- high confidence grounded in demonstrable specialty;
- precision/speed rather than blunt area force;
- direct contempt for lower-grade interference;
- weak collaborative instinct even when his threat assessment is correct.

He is not yet broad enough for a mature model, but this is substantially more than a rank cameo.

### 3.1.5 Tatsumaki changes the scale of the problem

Tatsumaki arrives and effectively changes the problem from “how do we cut enough vulnerable surfaces?” to “how much of the target can be spatially dominated at once?” She suspends, compresses, and crushes the giant monster into a compact mass.

Her immediate response is not celebration. She attacks the surrounding heroes' allocation of effort:

`人手が足りないっていうときに こんな所で雁首そろえて何遊んでんの!?`

She then singles out Flashy Flash as S-class and demands work more commensurate with that status.

This strengthens the V12 Tatsumaki model. Her harshness is not only interpersonal impatience; it is tied to a genuine **resource-utilization standard**. Strong people should solve hard problems efficiently and move on.

### 3.1.6 Flash and Tatsumaki: competence without cooperation

Flash responds that Tatsumaki stole prey he had already cornered: `追い詰めた獲物を横取りして何を言っている馬鹿め`.

The bystanders' reaction matters. Two elite heroes are not just strong; their competing self-conceptions create a secondary social hazard. Tatsumaki leaves complaining about useless people; Flash complains that people keep doing unnecessary things.

This is V11's coordination problem at a higher power level:

- A-class coalition: cooperative but insufficient.
- Flash: sufficient specialization, low tolerance for interference.
- Tatsumaki: overwhelming solution, low tolerance for slower or redundant actors.

**Inference:** capability can reduce the *need* to coordinate without creating the *ability* to coordinate well.

### 3.1.7 Death Gatling feels rank/class humiliation rather than denying the result

After Tatsumaki solves the incident, Death Gatling says:

`悔しいが俺達では退治できなかった`

and:

`くそ…A級の立つ瀬がないな`

This is useful because it combines honest model updating with rank/class pride. He does not pretend the coalition won. He accurately concedes failure while experiencing it as a blow to A-class standing.

Status sensitivity therefore need not imply epistemic dishonesty.

### 3.1.8 The Monster Association learns from the same battle

The enemy surveillance layer turns the hero encounter into force accounting. Gyoro Gyoro judges that even Demon-level monsters require favorable one-on-one matching against S-class heroes, and Tatsumaki is treated as a particularly serious problem.

Orochi's reply is terse: `問題ない`.

He then states:

`怪人など いくらでも作れる`

The line transforms the strategic frame. Individual monsters are not merely autonomous threats; monster *production* is entering the organization's resource model.

`OPM|V13|chapter:68|image:0052|anchor:Orochi monsters can be made in any number`

## 3.2 `69撃目 怪人細胞` — power acquisition becomes literal transformation

### 3.2.1 Atomic Samurai's network broadens hero society beyond the Association office

Atomic Samurai meets the Sword Saint Council in the mountains with Iaian, Okamaitachi, and Bushidrill. The meeting is prompted by Garou, whose martial lineage makes the Hero Hunter problem legible through professional networks that predate or exceed ordinary Association bureaucracy.

Garou is explicitly recognized as Bang's former top disciple. Atomic Samurai respects Bang's skill enough that this lineage materially raises the threat assessment.

The scene is important for institutional analysis because “hero response” is not one centralized information path. Martial schools, teachers, disciples, and professional peer councils carry intelligence and obligation alongside the Hero Association.

### 3.2.2 The disciples demonstrate different evidentiary discipline

The discussion of Garou does not consist entirely of hype. His victory over Tanktop Master is treated as important, but Iaian does not simply convert reputation into omniscience. The group reasons from observed accomplishments, lineage, and victim reports.

That evidentiary caution contrasts with the exaggerated public misreadings elsewhere in OPM.

### 3.2.3 Bushidrill rejects honorable competition with an outlaw

Bushidrill states in effect that there is no need to compete fairly in strength with someone who has left the proper path:

`道を外れた者と実力で競い合う必要などない`

The priority is to prevent additional victims, not to stage a satisfying duel.

This materially broadens the project's ethics of combat. V12 already contrasted Suiryu's joy-in-combat with professional rescue. V13 adds a martial professional who explicitly subordinates fair contest to public-risk reduction.

### 3.2.4 Atomic Samurai's willingness to kill Garou is not universal hero ethics

The Sword Saint conversation raises Bang's relation to his former disciple and whether attachment could create fatal hesitation. Atomic Samurai concludes that hesitation in a deathmatch can kill the hesitating party and says he will eliminate Garou before Bang has to confront that problem.

This must remain character- and context-specific. Mumen Rider's V09 attempt to stop excessive force against a dangerous human remains valid evidence in another ethical register. OPM's hero system contains disagreement over lethal policy toward human antagonists.

### 3.2.5 Haragiri reframes the gathering around strength itself

Haragiri enters as a peer from the swordmaster world and addresses Atomic Samurai by `カミカゼ`, mocking the public hero title `アトミック侍`. He complains that master swordsmen should refine one another rather than hold social gatherings.

His grievance is not yet monsterhood. It is a martial-strength ideology in which elite fellowship is justified by mutual sharpening.

That makes the next reveal structurally coherent rather than random: Monster Cells offer a route to the greater strength he has prioritized.

### 3.2.6 Monster Cells establish a repeatable human-to-monster conversion mechanism

Haragiri reveals `怪人細胞` and explains:

`これを体内に取り入れると 肉体が活性化し…人間の限界を超えた筋力や寿命を手に入れることができる`

Then the proposition is stated plainly:

`つまりは怪人になる`

This is a major ontology update. Before V13, “monster” could refer to radically different bodies, self-authored social roles, Association categories, or species-like enemies. V13 now adds an explicit **conversion technology/biological mechanism**:

`human body + Monster Cell ingestion -> activated bodily transformation -> claimed beyond-human strength/lifespan -> monster body state`.

The mechanism is observed strongly enough to be canonical at V13 because Haragiri visibly transforms after taking it. The complete origin and reliability remain open.

### 3.2.7 Haragiri voluntarily aligns four dimensions that must remain analytically separable

Haragiri says a `怪人協会` exists as an organization opposing the Hero Association and that it invited him. He accepted because he was willing to exchange humanity for more strength:

`人間を辞めるのと引き換えにこれまで以上の強さを手に入れられるのならと入会した`

After transforming, he says:

`ワシはもう人間じゃない`

His case brings four dimensions together:

1. **body:** visible Monster Cell transformation;
2. **identity:** explicit rejection of human identity;
3. **affiliation:** entry into the Monster Association;
4. **conduct:** threat to kill peers who refuse conversion.

The analytical value is precisely that earlier Garou evidence did **not** align these dimensions. Through V12 Garou remained biologically human, self-labeled as monster, refused Monster Association affiliation, and showed selective civilian concern alongside severe hero violence.

V13 therefore strengthens a decomposition rather than collapsing all “monster” cases into one type.

### 3.2.8 Monsterization does not abolish technique hierarchy

Haragiri exults in transformed strength and speed, claiming his draw now exceeds sound and that he can defeat all four masters. Atomic Samurai does not debate the metaphysics. When Haragiri draws, Atomic cuts him apart before the transformed swordmaster can exploit his boast.

This is a useful limiting case against a simplistic “biological upgrade supersedes training” reading. The Monster Cell provides an enormous bodily enhancement. It does not automatically erase timing, judgment, technique, experience, or first-action advantage.

### 3.2.9 Atomic Samurai immediately reprioritizes the threat model

After Haragiri's death, Atomic Samurai tells Iaian that the Hero Hunter is no longer the only problem and states:

`まず先に潰すは“怪人協会”だ!`

This is one of his strongest modeling scenes so far. He does not remain locked onto the task he arrived with. New evidence reveals a scalable conversion organization, so he changes target priority immediately.

The sequence supports promotion of Atomic Samurai's readiness beyond the earlier bounded crisis/mentor sample: mentor hierarchy, professional peer network, lethal policy, threat-model updating, decisive action, and strong Japanese voice now recur across separate contexts.

### 3.2.10 Gyoro Gyoro separates monster impulse from organizational purpose

The Monster Association side then states:

`暴力は怪人にとって根源的欲求として備わっているものではあるが…我々の目的はそれではない`

and says the organization is trying to advance to another stage.

This distinction is essential. Even within the source's own monster discourse:

`monster violent impulse` ≠ `Monster Association strategic purpose`.

The organization is not merely a crowd of creatures whose shared goal is indiscriminate destruction. It possesses planning, force accounting, recruitment, conversion, targeting, and an objective not yet fully revealed.

`OPM|V13|chapter:69|image:0084|anchor:violent impulse is not Monster Association purpose`

### 3.2.11 Choze: inherited superiority rhetoric punctured by Saitama's mundane analogy

The tournament resumes with Choze, whose `選民血脈格闘術` and self-description turn inherited selection into a superiority ideology. He frames himself as the result of elite-lineage combination and speaks toward species-level superiority and domination.

Saitama answers not with an alternative genetic theory but with a grocery-level analogy:

`トウモロコシの品種改良みたいなもんだろ`

and, whatever the taste:

`トウモロコシには変わらねぇ`

Then he defeats Choze in roughly two seconds.

The scene is classic Saitama deflation: a grandiose identity claim is translated into an ordinary category. It should not be elevated into a complete biological doctrine. Its character value is Saitama's refusal to inherit another person's prestige language merely because the language is ornate.

## 3.3 `70撃目 強いは楽しい` — Suiryu sees Saitama more clearly than he understands him

### 3.3.1 The crowd again invents emotion from Saitama's face

Before the final, spectators interpret Saitama's blank expression as a goofy face concealing tension. Saitama internally notes that it is simply his normal expression.

This small gag belongs in the public-narrative ledger because it repeats the series' attribution problem at microscopic scale: observers need a story, so neutral evidence acquires motive and emotion.

### 3.3.2 Suiryu correctly detects strength without borrowing formal status

Unlike the crowd, Suiryu makes a genuinely useful inference. He tells Saitama:

`キミ…かなり強いんだろ? わかるよ 俺も強いからね`

He reads Saitama as someone not especially motivated by `地位や名誉`, but looking for something more enjoyable.

This is one of the first people in the tournament to infer something meaningful from Saitama without relying on hero rank or public reputation. Yet Suiryu's next error is equally important: because their boredom and strength appear similar, he assumes their **values** are similar too.

### 3.3.3 `強いのは楽しい` is Suiryu's positive philosophy of strength

Suiryu admits that prize money is not his entire motive. Sometimes he simply wants to move his body, compare strength with a good opponent, and enjoy the rare experience of competition.

He says plainly:

`強いのは楽しい`

This is not identical to V12's fatalism. V13 exposes the affirmative center beneath it. Strength is valuable to Suiryu because it generates:

- freedom from imposed burdens;
- access to pleasure;
- social ease;
- sexual/romantic confidence;
- the possibility of occasional stimulating competition.

The fatalism about weaker people being selected out is therefore not only abstract ideology. It helps protect a life in which his own power is not converted into obligation.

### 3.3.4 Suiryu identifies Saitama's boredom but misidentifies its solution

Suiryu asks whether Saitama is bored and craving excitement. This is partly right. The V01–V12 model already establishes Saitama's deprivation of meaningful challenge and V10–V12 martial curiosity.

Suiryu's mistake is treating “be strong and pursue fun” as advice Saitama has not already exceeded. For Suiryu, strength still opens experiences. For Saitama, too much strength has closed many of them.

Thus the same phrase `強いのは楽しい` sits on opposite sides of a threshold:

- Suiryu: strength enables fun.
- Saitama: extreme strength can destroy access to the fun sought from contest.

### 3.3.5 Saitama deliberately restrains himself to create a learning environment

Saitama explains that he entered to experience martial arts and that if he counterattacks even a little the match will probably end. He has therefore been letting Suiryu act.

This is stronger than passive invulnerability. It is an intentional experimental policy:

`recognized unfamiliar domain -> solicit exposure -> suppress normal solution -> preserve test duration -> observe`.

That matters for Saitama's cognition. His learning style is often embodied and direct rather than doctrinal. He wants to feel what martial arts *does*.

### 3.3.6 Suiryu accepts the experiment as a reciprocal entertainment contract

Suiryu proposes:

`俺が武術を体験させてやる`

and asks Saitama to make the match fun in return. Saitama accepts, explicitly hoping finally to experience martial arts' frightening side.

For a short span, their aims align:

- Saitama wants an unfamiliar experience;
- Suiryu wants an entertaining opponent;
- both willingly prolong the exchange.

This is why the match is more character-diagnostic than a simple power comparison.

### 3.3.7 Saitama's “danger” remains local to the wig

Suiryu escalates technique and eventually thinks he may have finished the opponent. Saitama rises and says:

`今のは流石にヒヤッとしたぜ`

but immediately clarifies that he thought the wig might come off.

The language matters. The scene visually borrows the rhythm of a danger reveal and then relocates danger to the mundane disguise constraint.

Saitama is genuinely more engaged than in a routine one-punch encounter, but the engagement comes from **novelty, experiment, and the rules of his disguise**, not credible bodily risk.

### 3.3.8 Suiryu infers martial defense where Saitama is protecting a costume constraint

When Saitama guards his head, Suiryu interprets the behavior as part of the fight. In reality, Saitama is protecting the wig required for his Charanko disguise.

This is another clean OPM attribution problem:

`observable action` = real;

`observer's causal explanation` = plausible but wrong.

The manga does not need a cosmic reputation inversion to produce epistemic comedy; a wig is enough.

### 3.3.9 Saitama's Charanko cover story must not be misfiled as literal self-report

Asked what he ordinarily does, Saitama begins with walking around and defeating monsters, then remembers that he is currently impersonating Charanko. He shifts into a cover statement about perhaps aiming to become a hero someday.

This is a knowledge-state/source-control trap. The later phrase is tactical disguise maintenance, not a canonical statement that current Saitama merely *aspires* to hero work.

Any dialogue index must retain the audience/role condition.

### 3.3.10 Suiryu: `ヒーローなんて名前だけさ`

Suiryu says the hero industry is no good and, after beating A-class fighters who seemed weak to him, concludes:

`“ヒーロー”なんて名前だけさ 現実にはそんなもの存在しない`

The wording is intentionally sweeping. It is a **character thesis**, not narrative voice.

The manga immediately cuts away to test it against action.

### 3.3.11 Mumen Rider and Tanktop Master answer with bodies, not speeches

Monsters attack the hospital where injured heroes and immobile patients are located. Mumen Rider refuses to retreat because the intensive-care area contains patients who cannot be moved:

`俺が退く訳にはいかない…!!!`

He is already injured and physically mismatched.

When a monster seizes him, Tanktop Master—also badly damaged, hospitalized, and without his tanktop—launches himself into the threat and protects the space. The two injured heroes then check on one another.

The construction is too deliberate to ignore. Suiryu's claim that heroes exist only in name is followed by two heroes whose bodies are already broken continuing to act because other people cannot move.

The scene does **not** prove every registered hero is morally admirable. It demonstrates that the category Suiryu dismisses contains conduct his model does not explain.

### 3.3.12 Suiryu turns ethical commitments into “impurities” in a fun life

Suiryu lists the kinds of things hero work takes too seriously:

- `立派な志`;
- `崇高な思想`;
- `高尚な倫理観`;
- `ヒーロー的な正義感`.

For his desired life, these become `退屈のもとになる不純物`.

This is sharper than simply “Suiryu is lazy.” He experiences moral obligation as contamination of unencumbered enjoyment.

He advises Saitama, whom he recognizes as strong, not to become trapped in boring responsibility.

### 3.3.13 Saitama's counterargument comes from experience, not heroic doctrine

When the wig finally comes off, Saitama rejects the category of “impurities” and gives Suiryu one piece of advice:

`楽しむのが目的だったら`

`それ以上強くならない方がいいぜ`

This is one of the volume's most important lines. Saitama does not answer with a sermon about sacred duty. He answers Suiryu **inside Suiryu's own telos**.

If enjoyment is the goal, unlimited strength can be self-defeating.

This directly synthesizes V12's experiential-limit evidence. Saitama knows something Suiryu does not: at the far end of strength, contest can stop supplying the stimulation strength was supposed to secure.

### 3.3.14 The restrained punch reveals a capability gap without ending the experiment by accident

Saitama throws an obviously restrained response whose shock tears apart Suiryu's clothing. He remarks that he almost ended the match again.

The line preserves Saitama's experimental goal. He is not trying to maximize victory speed. He is actively fighting his own default capability in order to keep the encounter informative.

This is a rare case where Saitama's main obstacle is **self-calibration downward**.

## 3.4 `71撃目 武術とは…!!` — formal victory, actual defeat, and a practice made inaccessible by excess power

### 3.4.1 The tournament declares a winner without answering who is stronger

The Charanko impersonation collides with revised Super Fight rules introduced after an earlier masked-entry problem. Coverings are prohibited, and a wig requires prior application. Saitama's wig is treated as a disqualifying covering.

The important institutional distinction is:

`official result` ≠ `comparative capability result`.

Suiryu becomes the formal champion because Saitama is disqualified. This does not mean the rules are meaningless; they are doing a different job. They determine tournament legitimacy, not metaphysical power rank.

### 3.4.2 Saitama flees administrative consequence rather than seeking recognition

Saitama leaves rather than remain for identity/registration consequences. The scene continues his low-stakes willingness to break or evade formal rules when he has already entered under false pretenses.

It should not be romanticized as ethical recognition sacrifice. His behavior here is practical evasion of a problem he created.

### 3.4.3 Suiryu refuses to let the official win settle his personal loss state

Suiryu pursues Saitama and says:

`遊びは終わり ここから本番だ`

He later explains:

`悪いけど俺も 負けたままじゃ楽しくないんでね`

This line revises the simple “fun above status” picture. Suiryu still rejects conventional institutional prestige, but his enjoyment depends on a self-conception that includes not being left in a defeated state.

**Revision:** he is not status-indifferent so much as differently status-sensitive. Formal titles matter less than the experiential hierarchy between himself and another strong person.

### 3.4.4 The first defeat exposes the conditional structure beneath `強いのは楽しい`

Suiryu escalates into named martial techniques, destroying the arena and chaining high-speed attacks. The visual grammar gives his martial art spectacular legitimacy even as none of it harms Saitama.

The problem is not that Suiryu's technique is fake. The problem is that Saitama's body is an extreme counterfactual environment in which the usual costs and constraints fail to register.

### 3.4.5 Saitama finally gives a definition of martial arts—and it is shallow for a structural reason

After receiving Suiryu's attacks, Saitama says:

`武術…これが武術か…`

`なるほど…少しわかってきたぞ`

Suiryu expects a meaningful insight. Saitama concludes:

`「なんか動きがかっこいいもの」だな…?`

Suiryu's reaction—`あ…浅い!!!`—is justified from his own perspective.

But the manga has already supplied the reason for the shallowness. Saitama wanted *functional* experience, yet:

- he did not need Suiryu's defensive structures;
- he did not need efficient force transfer;
- he did not need timing to overcome a superior body;
- he did not need technique to survive the incoming techniques;
- he did not need to manage stamina, injury, or positional disadvantage.

He can perceive the **form**—movement looks cool—while the **necessity** that gives the form meaning is largely unavailable to his body.

This is an epistemic limit produced by capability surplus.

### 3.4.6 Imitation converts martial art into visual style

Saitama says he might try the spinning movement because it would look cool while defeating monsters. He imitates the external form, and the resulting improvised motion overwhelms Suiryu.

The gag is not evidence that the original technique has no content. It shows that Saitama can reproduce a spectacular external pattern without needing the internal problem-solving system that made the art valuable to its practitioner.

### 3.4.7 `負けた……初めて…` — Suiryu's first defeat

After being blown away, Suiryu recognizes:

`負けた……初めて…`

This is his largest character-state transition so far. The man whose strength made life easy and fun now encounters a person who renders his own strongest practice insufficient.

Importantly, the formal tournament immediately produces the opposite statement: Suiryu is still announced as champion.

V13 therefore splits two truths:

- **institutional truth:** Suiryu won Super Fight under the rules;
- **experiential truth:** Suiryu knows he lost to Saitama.

### 3.4.8 Suiryu correctly infers restraint after the encounter

He later thinks:

`…あの一発が寸止めじゃなかったら 俺はどうなっていたか…`

This shows meaningful post-event cognition. Suiryu does not merely blame a fluke. He understands that Saitama stopped short and that the unstopped consequence is frighteningly uncertain.

The experience punctures invincibility without yet rewriting Suiryu's broader ethics.

### 3.4.9 The rapid return to ease is coping evidence, not proof nothing changed

Once the official award ceremony restores him as champion, Suiryu tells himself that a win is a win and tries to think casually. He quickly returns toward flirtation and ordinary ease.

This should not be read as total psychological reset. The private defeat recognition and stopped-punch thought remain explicit. V13 shows an immediate coping strategy: reinstate the familiar low-burden social mode where possible.

Whether that strategy survives later stress remains open.

### 3.4.10 Monster Association recruitment arrives exactly where Suiryu's philosophy has been destabilized

Three Crows enter carrying captured martial artists, followed by the much larger Gouketsu. The venue's social meaning shifts instantly from tournament entertainment to forced recruitment site.

Lightning Max tells civilians to stay back and invokes professional hero responsibility:

`ここは俺達プロヒーローが…`

The chapter then ends with Gouketsu's direct order:

`お前ら怪人になれ`

This is an exceptionally tight structural closure. A chapter spent debating what martial strength is *for* ends with an organization offering/forcing a literal transformation of martial strength into monster strength.

The next volume must determine what the martial artists do with that proposition. V13 cannot.

# 4. `番外編 スター` — Amai Mask's two professions occupy one body but not one register

## 4.1 The idol performance is a genuine part of the evidence surface

The extra opens at the final day of Amai Mask's new-album concert tour. He performs before an enormous crowd. The public language is maximally expansive: love, gratitude, peace, embrace, happiness for life across the universe.

Representative performance language includes the desire to embrace everyone, `愛と平和を歌おう`, `全てに感謝!!!`, and `この宇宙の全生命に幸あれ!!!`.

This is evidence for **public performer register**. It is not automatically evidence that every sentiment is a transparent private self-report. Performance context must remain attached.

## 4.2 The Monster Association targets symbolic infrastructure, not only bodies

Monsters crash the concert and identify Amai Mask as a priority target not merely because he is strong. His value to the Hero Association's public image makes killing him strategically useful; a bloodbath in front of fans would damage institutional legitimacy and morale.

This is an important organization-ledger update. The Monster Association is attacking:

- physical defenders;
- civilians;
- donor/family leverage;
- professional responders;
- and now **symbolic/media assets**.

Hero image is therefore not merely satire. It is operational infrastructure because enemies recognize its social effects.

## 4.3 Amai Mask preserves the show while fighting

When the attack becomes real, Amai does not immediately collapse the performance frame. He continues singing while evading and attacking monsters.

Fans initially interpret the violence as choreography or special effects. The page sequence makes role continuity visible:

`idol performance surface` + `hero combat underneath`.

The strongest supported claim is behavioral: Amai can sustain public performance while executing high-speed lethal work. It is plausible that this prevents panic and protects the show, but the crowd's interpretations should not be substituted for his unspoken motive unless the source states it.

## 4.4 “Love and peace” does not mean pacifism

Amai says conflict is wasteful and asks the monsters to listen to his feelings. Then he kills one and states:

`よくもコンサートの邪魔をしてくれたな 死んで償え`

This is a decisive voice-register switch.

The public performer can sing universal love and peace; the hero mode can answer hostile monsters with lethal punitive justice. V13 does not resolve the philosophical contradiction into hypocrisy or sincerity. It establishes that both registers are behaviorally real and context-conditioned.

## 4.5 Appearance remains morally and professionally salient to Amai

Amai worries about facial damage partly because fans would be saddened. He also describes the monster's eyes as ugly.

This continues his earlier hero-image evidence. Beauty is not incidental decoration in his model. Appearance, public feeling, professional brand, and normative judgment remain entangled.

## 4.6 The crowd repairs reality into performance

After the monsters are killed, Amai returns to his smile. Fans conclude that what they saw must have been part of the concert production.

This is another high-value public-narrative case:

- the lethal event is real;
- the idol frame is already dominant;
- the audience assimilates incompatible evidence into the prior story.

Amai's smile does not merely conceal; it helps make the preferred interpretation available.

## 4.7 Staff explicitly describe a role-boundary they cannot cross

Backstage staff worry about what happened to the monsters. An older staff member warns against prying:

`俺達が知ってる彼はアイドルの部分だけ…それだけでいい`

and describes the hero side as difficult to approach.

This is unusually explicit source support for role separation. People professionally close to Amai Mask understand that their knowledge is partial.

That evidence should constrain later character reconstruction: idol familiarity does not equal access to his hero-state motives, and public hero knowledge does not automatically grant private-performer knowledge.

## 4.8 Amai Mask moves from targeted symbol to active Monster Association responder

The extra closes with Amai connecting the incident to the Monster Association and deciding:

`僕が出る必要がありそうだね`

This strengthens his professional agency. He is not merely a celebrity whom the organization targets; he identifies the organization as a problem requiring his direct intervention.

# 5. Character-state transitions through V13

## 5.1 Saitama — strong; model substantially strengthened

### PRESERVE
- status and reputation do not automatically produce deference;
- curiosity activates around recognized novelty rather than every objectively unusual opponent;
- physical danger remains a poor proxy for emotional engagement;
- mundane constraints can matter more to him than bodily threat.

### STRENGTHEN — experiential learning policy

V12 established Saitama's desire to `体験` martial arts. V13 shows the full procedure: he deliberately suppresses counterattack, lets Suiryu act, and preserves the encounter because normal retaliation would end the test.

This is stronger evidence for an embodied learning style than “Saitama likes new things.”

### STRENGTHEN — capability surplus as epistemic barrier

Saitama can see martial movement but cannot naturally access the problems martial technique solves for ordinary bodies. His conclusion that martial arts is “cool-looking movement” is therefore a model limitation created by extreme strength.

This is a new kind of domain limitation:

`cannot be meaningfully constrained -> cannot directly experience why constraint-solving technique matters`.

### STRENGTHEN — argument style

His counter to Suiryu does not begin from institutional hero doctrine. He takes Suiryu's stated goal—fun—and argues that further strength may undermine that very goal.

This is Saitama reasoning from personal consequence rather than abstract moral prestige.

### Counterexample control

Do not claim he has become a serious martial-arts student. V13 ends with him treating external form aesthetically and imitating the spin because it looks cool.

## 5.2 Suiryu — `emerging -> moderate`

V13 supplies enough breadth to promote the model.

Evidence now includes:

- V11 introduction and tournament orientation;
- V12 explicit low-burden autonomy/fatalism;
- V13 strong-person perception of Saitama;
- desire for rare stimulation and reciprocal fun;
- philosophical argument against hero obligation;
- contempt for institutional hero prestige after beating A-class participants;
- active attempt to persuade Saitama;
- willingness to continue fighting outside the official match because remaining “defeated” is not fun;
- extensive technique under escalating competitive stress;
- first-defeat cognition;
- recognition that Saitama restrained himself;
- immediate attempt to restore casual social mode afterward.

### REVISE — “status indifferent”

Suiryu is not well modeled as simply indifferent to hierarchy. He is relatively indifferent to **formal/institutional prestige**, but highly sensitive to **experienced comparative superiority**. The official championship cannot erase his private knowledge that Saitama defeated him.

### OPEN

How first defeat changes his heroism/freedom philosophy remains unresolved at the V13 endpoint.

## 5.3 Atomic Samurai — `emerging -> moderate`

V13 materially broadens an earlier high-stakes combat/mentor sample.

New dimensions:

- durable disciple structure;
- professional peer/swordmaster network;
- respect for Bang without submission;
- explicit lethal policy toward dangerous human Garou;
- willingness to protect Bang from a former-disciple deathmatch by acting first;
- rapid action against transformed Haragiri;
- immediate reprioritization from Garou to the Monster Association after new evidence;
- stable rough/confident register across contexts.

This is enough conditional breadth for moderate readiness, while ordinary/private life remains a major gap.

## 5.4 Amai Mask — moderate; strongly broadened

`スター` adds the most substantial private-professional role contrast so far:

- concert performer;
- public love/peace register;
- high control under attack;
- lethal hero violence;
- appearance/fan concern;
- rapid return to idol smile;
- staff knowledge boundary;
- self-directed Monster Association response.

The evidence does **not** yet justify a hidden-ontology conclusion.

## 5.5 Mumen Rider — moderate; duty model replicated

The hospital scene replicates his impossible-duty behavior under a new context: already injured, civilians/patients unable to move, and no expectation of easy victory. He remains because retreat would expose them.

The important longitudinal point is not simply courage. It is a recurring prioritization policy:

`someone else cannot withdraw -> own weakness/injury does not remove duty to remain`.

## 5.6 Tanktop Master — `emerging -> moderate`

V13 supplies the missing longitudinal rescue/failure recurrence after his Garou defeat and group-leadership evidence.

He is hospitalized and physically damaged, yet intervenes when Mumen Rider and immobile patients are threatened. His behavior is direct, physical, and low-rhetoric; afterward he checks Mumen's condition.

Combined with earlier subordinate protection, Garou restraint, defeat, and recovery context, the model now crosses the moderate threshold. Ordinary life remains sparse.

## 5.7 Lightning Max — `insufficient -> emerging`

V04 supplied severe-overmatch behavior; V12 supplied tournament aspiration and defeat; V13 supplies an explicit professional-duty boundary when monsters enter the arena and he tells civilians to stay back because this is work for `プロヒーロー`.

The sample is still narrow, but it is now longitudinal and coherent enough for emerging readiness.

## 5.8 Flashy Flash — enters `emerging`

A bounded but coherent model now exists:

- extraordinary speed/precision;
- accurate recognition that insufficient attacks can worsen collateral;
- confidence in independent solution;
- contempt for interference;
- immediate irritation when Tatsumaki finishes his prey;
- weak collaborative orientation despite valid competence.

More ordinary/relational evidence is required before further promotion.

## 5.9 Death Gatling — enters `emerging`

The giant-octopus sequence provides a coherent professional policy:

- cognition/judgment under extreme stress;
- concealment for information and survival;
- coalition formation;
- information sharing;
- honest concession after failure;
- rank/class pride and humiliation.

Longitudinal recurrence remains absent.

## 5.10 Haragiri — enters `emerging` as a bounded transformation/strength model

One chapter supplies unusually dense evidence:

- swordmaster peer identity;
- strength-refinement ideology;
- voluntary Monster Cell ingestion;
- willingness to exchange humanity for power;
- Monster Association affiliation;
- explicit nonhuman self-identification after transformation;
- coercion of peers;
- overconfidence after bodily enhancement;
- death against Atomic Samurai's preemptive technique.

This is enough for a bounded emerging antagonist model, not a broad personality reconstruction.

## 5.11 Tatsumaki — moderate; role-efficiency model strengthened

V13 adds a large-scale field case in which she evaluates powerful heroes as misallocated resources and solves the target through overwhelming compression. Her harshness is linked to an efficiency standard, not merely generalized irritability.

The Flash conflict also strengthens the proposition that elite capability does not produce easy peer collaboration.

## 5.12 Garou — moderate; no broad promotion

Garou's Watchdog Man encounter from the V12 endpoint remains a useful open boundary rather than a V13 centerpiece. The surrounding V13 institutional material strengthens the stakes around him—Atomic Samurai and the Sword Saint Council now explicitly plan around the Hero Hunter—but the volume does not supply enough additional direct Garou state to justify tier movement.

# 6. Relationship-state deltas

## 6.1 Saitama ↔ Suiryu

### Prior state
Tournament opponent with philosophical contrast beginning to form.

**STRENGTHEN — reciprocal recognition without symmetry.**

# 7. Heroism, rank, and institutional implications

## 7.1 A-class failure is not evidence that class has no meaning

Death Gatling's coalition fails where Tatsumaki succeeds. The correct inference is not “A-class is fake.” The chapter shows:

- A-class heroes can possess strong professional cognition and coordination;
- threat-scale mismatch can still make their aggregate force insufficient;
- rank/class remains socially meaningful enough that failure produces humiliation;
- S-class capability itself is heterogeneous and can create coordination friction.

## 7.2 Professional heroism gains an action-based rebuttal to Suiryu

Suiryu's `“ヒーロー”なんて名前だけさ` is not answered with institutional propaganda. It is juxtaposed with injured heroes protecting immobile patients and Max asserting professional responsibility when civilians face monsters.

The strongest source-grounded claim is:

> V13 structurally contests the equation “weak hero = hero only in name” by showing professional obligation persisting where comparative strength is unfavorable.

## 7.3 Official rules create legitimate outcomes that are not capability rankings

Suiryu's formal championship is real under the tournament rules even though Saitama is stronger. This is the same proxy-domain discipline strengthened since V10:

- tournament result measures rule-governed contest legitimacy;
- it does not necessarily measure absolute combat capability.

## 7.4 Hero media value becomes an enemy target

Amai Mask is selected partly because damaging him in front of fans can damage Hero Association image. Reputation therefore has strategic value even when it is socially constructed.

Public narrative is not epiphenomenal; people organize action around it.

## 8.1 V13's largest ontology update: Monster Cells

Haragiri's upgrade is real, but Atomic Samurai kills him before transformed speed can decide the fight. V13 therefore keeps “power increase” distinct from “complete combat superiority.”

## 8.4 Monster desire and organization remain distinct

Gyoro Gyoro explicitly distinguishes monster violence as a root impulse from the Association's larger objective. This blocks a purely instinctual theory of the organization.

# 9. Power, technique, and explanatory systems

## 9.1 Technique remains real even when Saitama cannot access its need

V13's most important technique claim is paradoxical:

- martial technique is demonstrably meaningful throughout the volume;
- Saitama's own experience reduces it to cool movement.

This is not contradiction. It is **observer-relative access**.

The form solves constraints Saitama does not share.

## 9.2 Atomic Samurai versus Haragiri: timing can dominate raw enhancement

Haragiri's transformed speed claim matters; Atomic's response matters more. An enhancement can move the body's ceiling without rewriting the entire decision/technique hierarchy.

## 9.3 Flash versus Tatsumaki: different solution spaces

Flash decomposes the octopus through precision and speed. Tatsumaki changes the entire spatial scale. Both are “strong,” but the mechanism and resource profile differ.

Power analysis should therefore preserve dimensions rather than compress all feats into a single number.

## 9.4 Saitama's self-restraint is an active technical problem

Against Suiryu, Saitama repeatedly calibrates *down* to avoid terminating the experiment. That is a practical problem not captured by maximum-output scaling.

# 17. V13 close

V13's central achievement is not simply that stronger monsters and stronger martial artists appear. It makes **conversion** visible at several levels:

- Haragiri converts human bodily identity into monster power.
- the Monster Association converts human specialists and public symbols into strategic resources or targets.
- Suiryu tries to convert strength into unrestricted enjoyment, then discovers that comparative defeat can invade that freedom.
- Saitama tries to convert exposure into understanding, but excess strength strips martial technique of much of the bodily necessity that would make it legible.
- Amai Mask converts a real monster attack back into public spectacle without ceasing to act as a lethal hero.

The volume therefore leaves the V13 boundary with a more differentiated model of strength than the project had at V12. **Power can change what a body can do, but the consequential question is what else changes with it—and what refuses to change.**
