# 2. Volume orientation

V02 moved Saitama's heroism from private hobby into an institution. V03 then showed that the Hero Association can measure his body accurately while still ranking, observing, and crediting him poorly. V04 asks what happens when that already-imperfect information system is placed under genuine mass-casualty pressure.

The result is not merely a larger action volume. V04 builds a sequence around **responsibility under uncertainty**:

1. an extinction-scale meteor approaches City Z;
2. the Association issues an S-class summons, but almost nobody answers;
3. Genos attempts an analytically responsible but nearly hopeless intervention;
4. Metal Knight treats the event as a weapons test and refuses collaboration;
5. Bang remains despite admitting the problem may exceed him;
6. Saitama destroys the meteor but cannot make physical consequence disappear;
7. institutional rank distributes credit afterward;
8. public narrative then converts the same intervention into blame;
9. Saitama explicitly separates his desire for recognition from the reason he acts as a hero.

The second half broadens the same question. Stinger is theatrical but genuinely brave. Puri-Puri Prisoner is institutionally an S-class hero while also being a convicted predator. Sonic is a criminal and prisoner yet has a coherent personal code and extraordinary skill. Deep Sea King's biological/political identity does not fit the earlier human-transformation model. Hero rank, public image, legality, moral worth, courage, and combat capability are therefore increasingly **non-identical dimensions**.

# 3. Central thesis

**V04 turns professional heroism into a test of what a person does when no available institution, reputation system, or public audience can guarantee a fair reading of the result.**

Saitama has now entered the Hero Association partly because he wanted his work to become legible. V04 does not erase that desire. It subjects it to the strongest contradiction so far: he saves City Z from total destruction and is then publicly blamed for the destruction that remains.

His response is unusually explicit:

> `俺はテメェらの評価が欲しくてヒーローやってんじゃねぇからな`
>
> `俺がやりたくてやってんだ`
>
> (`OPM|V04|chapter:22|image:0126`)

This requires a **revision, not a reversal**, of the V02 recognition model.

V02 established that Saitama wants to be known, wants correct attribution, can imagine fans, and cares when his hero identity is socially erased or misclassified. V04 establishes that **public approval is not the authority that makes the activity worth doing**. Recognition functions as desired feedback and social legibility; it is not the constitutive source of his hero identity.

The meteor also makes responsibility materially imperfect. Saitama's intervention prevents the central impact but breaks the meteor into destructive fragments. The manga therefore refuses a clean fantasy in which sufficient strength automatically produces a consequence-free rescue. Physical salvation, collateral harm, institutional accounting, and public interpretation separate into different layers.

## Strongest counter-reading

It would be too simple to read V04 as “Saitama does not care what anyone thinks.” That contradicts V02 and even V04's own rank/public-image context. The crowd scene is not indifference. Saitama is angry enough to confront the accusation, rejects the causal claim, and makes his motive explicit. The more precise model is:

- he **does care** about acknowledgment, reputation, and correct attribution;
- he **does not require approval** to authorize his heroism;
- under hostile public scrutiny, he prefers blunt causal confrontation over reputational appeasement;
- he does not perform remorse for damage he does not accept as morally attributable to his intervention.

A second oversimplification would be “the Hero Association is useless.” The Association issues evacuation and summons, gathers event information, updates ranks, and creates a response network that puts multiple heroes into motion. Its failure is more interesting: its measurements and records are **partial sensors**. It cannot force S-class compliance, cannot observe every decisive act, cannot perfectly attribute causal contribution, and cannot control public interpretation after the fact.

# 4. Sequential close reading

## 4.1 21撃目 `巨大隕石` — responsibility before success is knowable

### The S-class summons does not guarantee S-class presence

V04 opens by showing the organizational limits of the Association under a Dragon-level emergency. Genos reaches the City Z branch expecting coordination. Instead, personnel have evacuated and the building is nearly empty. Bang tells him:

> `召集かけられたS級ヒーローも ワシとキミ以外は誰も来とらん`
>
> (`OPM|V04|chapter:21|image:0017`)

Bang does not reduce every absentee to cowardice. Some may be elsewhere or occupied. But he also states the institutional reality: summons of this scale often mean impossible, troublesome work, and people sometimes do not come. He concludes that this event may simply be beyond them.

This is the first volume in which professional rank and response obligation visibly detach at the top of the system. `S級` is not a magical enforcement mechanism. It identifies elite heroes but does not abolish autonomy.

### Bang's crisis cognition

Bang's response to impossibility is neither despair nor heroic grandstanding. He remains physically present, reads Genos's agitation, and intervenes in the younger hero's cognition rather than pretending to possess a solution.

When Genos begins recursively modeling failure—whether an attack will work, whether breaking the meteor could produce worse consequences—he tells himself:

> `まあ 落ち着け`
>
> (`image:0053`)

Bang then observes:

> `心に乱れが見える`
>
> `おぬしは失敗を考えるにはまだ若すぎるのう`
>
> (`image:0054`)

Bang's advice is easy to caricature as “don't think.” The scene supports something narrower. Genos is already at the edge of the available action window. Further recursive failure-modeling is becoming paralysis rather than useful analysis. Bang's language functions as **crisis regulation**: when consequence cannot be made certain, act on the best available option rather than demanding perfect foresight.

Genos himself jokingly/compressively parses this as `適当がベスト?`, which should not be promoted into Bang's universal philosophy.

### Genos's responsibility is analytical and self-sacrificial

Genos's meteor sequence broadens him beyond the Saitama-centered disciple role. He is summoned independently as S-class. His first concern is not simply “can I destroy it?” He immediately asks what happens after destruction:

> `攻撃が命中したとして その後はどうする`
>
> `隕石が破裂して大惨事が起きるのではないか`
>
> (`image:0053`)

This matters. Genos's technical cognition includes **secondary-effect responsibility**. He does not equate the visible target with the whole problem.

When ordinary output appears insufficient, he is willing to use extraordinary internal energy despite uncertainty and bodily cost. V01-V03 established very high risk tolerance. V04 shows that the risk is not merely aggression or revenge fixation: he is willing to spend himself on a civic disaster he did not create and may not be able to solve.

### Metal Knight: intervention without rescue-centered cooperation

Metal Knight/Bofoi enters as a different S-class policy. His remote weapons system appears at the disaster, but when Genos asks for help he refuses to coordinate. His purpose is the field test of new armaments.

The key analytical distinction is not “Metal Knight does nothing.” He does act. He deploys technology and fires on the meteor. But his action is **instrumental and experimental** rather than organized around the cooperative rescue norm Genos assumes.

This gives the technology/organization ledger an important new axis:

`response presence != rescue-centered motive != collaborative obligation`.

The Association's rank category contains people whose reasons for appearing at the same emergency differ radically.

### Saitama's entrance compresses the institutional crisis back into self-authored action

Saitama arrives after Genos has exhausted conventional options. He does not request command authority or ask how many rank points are available. He first handles the interpersonal situation:

> `じいさん こいつ任せるぞ`
>
> (`image:0065`)

Bang asks who he is. Saitama's answer is strikingly close to, but not identical with, his earlier self-description:

> `俺はヒーローをやっている者だ`
>
> (`image:0066`)

The omission of `趣味で` matters contextually without proving a wholesale identity change. He is now a registered professional hero, but the grammar still defines heroism as something **he is doing**, not a prestige essence granted by rank. He then tells Bang to evacuate:

> `避難してな じいさん`
>
> (`image:0067`)

and acts.

### The meteor is defeated, but consequence remains

Saitama's punch destroys the main meteor body. The page-scale spectacle makes his physical superiority unmistakable. Yet fragments rain over City Z. Buildings are destroyed. The intervention changes the disaster from direct annihilation into widespread collateral devastation; it does not cancel physics.

This is important for both ethics and formal analysis. Saitama's power is absurd, but V04 refuses to make it omnipotent in the narrative sense of retroactively removing every consequence.

His own immediate framing—essentially `一件落着`—is much more compressed than the city's visible aftermath. That does not prove callousness. It does show a recurring cognitive asymmetry: when the existential threat is removed, Saitama can move very quickly to “problem solved” even while other people remain inside the secondary consequences.

## 4.2 22撃目 `声` — the same act becomes rank credit and public blame

### Institutional accounting distributes credit imperfectly

Three days later the Hero Association updates rankings. Saitama's rise from the bottom of C-class to rank 5 is extraordinary. Genos and Metal Knight also rise within S-class.

The system therefore does register that something important happened. But it cannot cleanly represent causal contribution:

- Saitama delivered the decisive physical intervention;
- Genos independently answered the summons, coordinated, analyzed secondary effects, and risked total output;
- Metal Knight deployed because he wanted weapons data and refused collaboration;
- Bang answered the summons and remained on site but did not attack the meteor.

All four behaviors are hero-relevant in different ways, yet rank movement compresses them into a single ladder.

The problem is not that ranking contains zero information. It is that **one scalar status variable cannot preserve the full structure of action, motive, contribution, and consequence**.

### Public narrative reverses causality

Genos informs Saitama that some people now blame him for the city's damage: if he had not broken the meteor, they reason, the fragments would not have destroyed their homes.

This is a causal inversion produced by counterfactual blindness. The direct-impact alternative—citywide annihilation—is less psychologically present than the debris people can see around them.

Tanktop Tiger and Tanktop Black Hole exploit that vulnerability. Their concern is not simply truth. Saitama's rapid rank rise threatens status. They weaponize disaster victims' anger to produce a reputational attack.

This is the first strong example of **peer heroes manipulating public narrative as a competitive resource**.

### Saitama's violence remains bounded

Saitama incapacitates the Tanktop brothers quickly rather than escalating lethal force. V02-V03 already established that human opponents do not automatically receive monster-level force. V04 repeats the distinction in a public humiliation/status context.

This matters because Saitama is genuinely angry. Restraint is therefore not merely a consequence of emotional flatness.

### The crowd speech clarifies recognition

Saitama's first move toward the crowd is confrontational openness:

> `文句がありゃ言ってみろ 聞いてやる`
>
> (`image:0125`)

When the accusations continue, he rejects them violently in register:

> `うるせぇ だまれ`
>
> `そんなもん隕石に言えボケ`
>
> `俺はテメェらの評価が欲しくてヒーローやってんじゃねぇからな`
>
> `俺がやりたくてやってんだ`
>
> (`image:0126`)

The speech is central because it distinguishes **recognition desire** from **motive authority**.

Saitama does not say that reputation never matters. He says it does not define why he is a hero. This is consistent with the entire V01-V04 trajectory:

- V01: he becomes a hero through self-authored desire and action;
- V02: he discovers that invisibility/misattribution frustrates him and that gratitude once gave meaningful feedback;
- V03: institutional rank and public visibility become real pressures;
- V04: when approval becomes coercive—“accept our blame or your hero identity is illegitimate”—he refuses the premise.

### Ethical caution: self-authorship is not the same as perfect victim attunement

The scene should not be idealized into a generic “true hero ignores criticism” message.

The civilians' causal accusation is wrong, and the Tanktop brothers are manipulating them. But the civilians have genuinely lost homes and property. Saitama does not spend the scene acknowledging that grief. His response is about causal responsibility and motive, not pastoral care.

For character modeling, this is useful. Under hostile public blame Saitama is likely to:

- reject false premises;
- speak bluntly or aggressively;
- refuse reputational performance;
- return to self-authored action;
- show relatively low patience for emotionally mediated collective attribution.

That is more specific—and more predictive—than simply calling him “humble” or “unbothered.”

## 4.3 23撃目 `海からの脅威` — performance does not cancel courage

The J-City invasion introduces a different kind of threat. The Sea Folk are not an isolated transformed human or engineered specimen. They arrive as a collective and speak in terms of surface domination. The monsterhood model therefore expands from individual abnormality toward **nonhuman political/social identity**.

### Stinger: theatrical heroism with real exposure to danger

Stinger enters with a conspicuous hero persona. He is visually theatrical, publicly legible, and responsive to attention. Yet V04 immediately prevents the reader from equating performance with fraud.

He fights multiple Sea Folk alone, takes the field while civilians flee, and remains engaged until the scale of the threat exceeds what he can handle.

This creates an important counterexample for the satire ledger:

`public-facing style / self-promotion` does **not** imply `cowardice / false heroism`.

The manga can satirize branding while still treating branded heroes as brave.

### Domestic Saitama/Genos continues beneath the crisis system

The sea-threat news enters Saitama and Genos's ordinary domestic space. Genos follows Association/rank/threat information with far more procedural intensity than Saitama. This preserves their V03 contrast: Genos internalizes the professional information system; Saitama uses it instrumentally but does not let it occupy the center of his identity.

## 4.4 24撃目 `深海王` — rank does not guarantee parity, and hero status does not certify morality

### Deep Sea King: threat hierarchy overwhelms hero hierarchy

Deep Sea King reframes the earlier Sea Folk attacks as subordinate action. His self-presentation is sovereign rather than merely predatory: he is a ruler, and the surface is a territory to be taken.

Registered heroes engage him and are badly overmatched. The sequence strengthens a point already visible with Mumen Rider and Saitama: Association class and rank are useful social/organizational descriptors, but they are not a direct combat equation.

### Puri-Puri Prisoner produces the strongest institutional contradiction yet

Puri-Puri Prisoner's entrance is deliberately absurd and ethically sharp. He is an S-class hero, appears in prison stripes, and announces:

> `S級ヒーロー ぷりぷりプリズナー`
>
> `あなたに会いに脱獄成功!`
>
> (`image:0176`)

Another hero is stunned that an S-class hero was serving a prison sentence. The contradiction is not repaired by explaining that his imprisonment is a misunderstanding. The following page makes it explicit that he was actually incarcerated.

Sonic, also imprisoned, emerges and says:

> `礼をいう`
>
> (`image:0178`)

Puri identifies him as prisoner 418 and states that he has checked all the men he likes and is repeatedly imprisoned because he cannot restrain himself from attacking men. Sonic's response is essentially that Puri belongs in prison.

The analytical conclusion must remain unsanitized:

**Hero Association status is not a moral certification.**

A person can be institutionally ranked as an elite hero and still commit coercive/predatory crimes serious enough to produce repeated imprisonment. This is a major limit on any later inference of “hero = morally approved person.”

# 5. Bonus reading: `ぷりズン` — hierarchy outside the Hero Association and Sonic beyond Saitama

The bonus chapter is unusually important for character reconstruction because it places Sonic in a sustained environment where Saitama is not the organizing stimulus.

### Prison as a second hierarchy system

The prison contains its own status structure: bosses, intimidation, surveillance, physical power, rules, and enforcement. Sonic enters this structure without accepting its legitimacy. Larger prisoners read his body as vulnerable; he reads them as obstacles.

His response is not the dramatic rivalry register used around Saitama. He is cooler, contemptuous, and efficient. This helps separate **stable Sonic tendencies** from **Saitama-specific humiliation activation**.

### Sonic's non-rival baseline

The bonus strengthens several model components:

- extremely high confidence in technical superiority;
- contempt for crude strength and institutional intimidation;
- strong autonomy / refusal of imposed hierarchy;
- willingness to use violence rapidly when physically challenged;
- less rhetorical fixation when Saitama is absent.

His V02-V03 rivalry remains real, but it should no longer consume the whole character model.

### Puri-Puri Prisoner in the prison ecosystem

The bonus also contextualizes Puri as both extraordinarily powerful and structurally embedded in prison life. His hero identity does not erase the penal institution; his power does not make the prison sentence conceptually meaningless, even if containment is physically unstable.

His breakout for the Sea Folk crisis creates a morally mixed state:

- he is responding to a genuine hero emergency;
- he is simultaneously escaping lawful confinement;
- his predatory behavior remains part of the record.

OPM is therefore already producing characters whose institutional roles cannot be collapsed into a single moral label.

# 6. Short omake — ordinary urgency survives catastrophe

The post-prison omake returns to Saitama and Genos's mundane life. Saitama notices shopping/food pricing and a missed or relevant sale context with immediate attention.

This is small but high-value character evidence because the first four volumes now repeat the same structural asymmetry:

- existential combat threats often produce limited emotional activation;
- ordinary scarcity, timing, food, shopping, and household logistics produce immediate salience.

The pattern is no longer a one-volume joke. It is a stable low-stakes baseline candidate.

# 7. Saitama state through V04

## Hero identity — STRENGTHEN strongly

The meteor scene supplies a post-registration formulation:

> `俺はヒーローをやっている者だ`

Saitama's status has changed, but the core grammar of self-authorship persists. Rank does not replace action as the basis of identity.

## Recognition — REVISE / differentiate

Earlier state:

`recognition, correct attribution, fans, and social legibility matter`.

V04 correction:

`recognition matters as desired feedback, but public evaluation is explicitly non-governing motive`.

This rejects two opposite simplifications:

- **REJECT:** Saitama is indifferent to recognition.
- **REJECT:** Saitama is a hero because he wants public approval.

Current formulation:

> Saitama wants his work and identity to be legible, but he retains an internal source of authorization that survives hostile or inaccurate public judgment.

## Public scrutiny response — NEW / high confidence

When collectively blamed:

- first offers a confrontational hearing (`文句がありゃ言ってみろ 聞いてやる`);
- rejects the crowd's causal premise;
- escalates to rough/aggressive speech;
- refuses apology-for-approval performance;
- states his motive directly.

This is distinct from ordinary low-affect behavior and should be modeled as a **public-scrutiny stress regime**.

## Force restraint — STRENGTHEN

Tanktop Tiger/Black Hole are human peer heroes behaving maliciously. Saitama incapacitates rather than kills. Emotional anger does not abolish human-opponent restraint.

## Responsibility toward Genos — STRENGTHEN

`じいさん こいつ任せるぞ` is a small but meaningful behavioral delta. Saitama does not verbalize sentimental concern; he notices Genos's depleted state, secures another competent adult/hero as caretaker, then takes the dangerous task himself.

Care is often operational rather than verbally affective.

## Catastrophe cognition — NEW nuance

Saitama acts decisively on the existential threat but does not demonstrate Genos's pre-action secondary-effects analysis. After the meteor is fragmented he rapidly frames the main problem as resolved.

Do not infer irresponsibility from one case, but preserve the contrast:

- Genos models downstream physical consequences before acting;
- Saitama prioritizes removing the immediate catastrophic object and cognitively compresses the aftermath afterward.

## Low-stakes baseline — STRENGTHEN

Shopping/food/sale salience recurs again. This is now repeated enough to serve as a stable ordinary-life activation pattern.

### Readiness

**strong; substantially strengthened within tier.** V04 adds mass-casualty decision context, hostile public scrutiny, false blame, rank reward, caretaker behavior toward Genos, large-scale collateral consequence, and another mundane consumer baseline.

# 8. Genos state through V04

## Independent hero duty — STRENGTHEN strongly

Genos responds to the S-class meteor summons independently. His action is no longer merely attached to Saitama's presence or revenge mission.

## Secondary-effect cognition — NEW

The meteor is the cleanest evidence so far that Genos analyzes not only target destruction but consequence chains. He asks whether successful fragmentation itself could cause mass harm.

## Self-regulation — NEW

`まあ 落ち着け` is an explicit internal attempt to regulate escalating cognition under disaster pressure. Genos is not simply impulsive; he can recognize his own destabilization and attempt a corrective.

## Cooperation policy — NEW

Genos asks Metal Knight for cooperation rather than treating all high-level actors as rivals. He is willing to coordinate instrumentally toward civilian protection.

## Self-sacrificial threshold — STRENGTHEN

When success probability is poor, Genos considers spending his deepest available power reserve anyway. High bodily risk tolerance is now repeated outside his origin revenge context.

## Bang interaction — NEW relationship breadth

Genos listens to Bang's crisis advice without simply replacing his own model. He parses it, tests it, and continues acting. This is useful evidence that respectful reception of senior input can coexist with analytical independence.

## Saitama arrival — STRENGTHEN trust/epistemic asymmetry

Once Saitama enters, Genos's impossible problem becomes one he can relinquish. That confidence is not blind faith in the abstract; it follows repeated direct observation of Saitama's capability.

### Readiness

**moderate, near strong; substantially strengthened.** V04 materially broadens Genos beyond the Saitama dyad through civic duty, Bang, Metal Knight, disaster cognition, and public-rank analysis. A move to `strong` is withheld until further independent ordinary-life/relationship recurrence and recovery/failure contexts accumulate.

# 9. Bang — V04 baseline

Bang enters at **emerging** readiness rather than being overpromoted from one disaster.

### Role / conduct
- S-class rank 3 at this boundary;
- responds personally to a near-impossible summons;
- remains after Association personnel evacuate;
- neither guarantees success nor abandons duty because success is doubtful.

### Perception
- reads Genos's mental disorder quickly (`心に乱れが見える`);
- distinguishes useful action from excessive preoccupation with failure.

### Speech / register
- older masculine register with `ワシ`, `おぬし`, sentence endings such as `～のう`;
- advisory without bureaucratic officiousness;
- can speak lightly while acknowledging catastrophe.

### Relationship seeds
- Bang -> Genos: situational senior guidance, direct observation of a younger S-class hero under strain.
- Bang -> Saitama: first contact as an unknown low-ranked/unknown hero, followed immediately by direct witness of impossible capability.
- Saitama -> Bang: addresses him generically as `じいさん`, but entrusts Genos to him in the emergency.

Readiness remains **emerging**: one chapter gives unusually diagnostic crisis behavior but little ordinary-life breadth or longitudinal recurrence.

# 10. Other V04 character-state baselines and transitions

## Metal Knight / Bofoi — insufficient

- appears through remote weapons platform;
- explicitly prioritizes testing new weapons;
- refuses Genos's request for collaboration;
- attacks the meteor anyway;
- does not yet have enough direct interpersonal/ordinary-life evidence for `emerging` readiness.

The policy is analytically important even though the character model remains insufficient.

## Tanktop Tiger — insufficient -> emerging

V03 supplied status-threat behavior. V04 repeats it in a more deliberate form: rapid Saitama advancement becomes something to suppress through public narrative manipulation. The recurrence supports an `emerging` model of rank insecurity and opportunistic peer aggression.

## Tanktop Black Hole — emerging, narrow

His V04 behavior is coherent enough to seed an `emerging` status/manipulation profile, but evidence is tightly bounded to one public confrontation.

## Stinger — emerging

Stinger's first substantial sample combines:
- overt hero branding / theatricality;
- attention to public recognition;
- willingness to enter genuine danger;
- sustained solo engagement against multiple monsters;
- persistence through injury/overmatch.

Do not infer that performative self-presentation makes his heroism fake.

## Lightning Max — insufficient

Heroic intervention under severe overmatch is valuable but too narrow for reconstruction readiness.

## Deep Sea King — emerging

Several scenes support a coherent dominance policy:
- sovereign self-conception;
- contempt for surface humans;
- delight in overwhelming weaker opponents;
- hierarchical relation to Sea Folk.

Later behavior is not imported. V04 alone justifies `emerging`, not more.

## Puri-Puri Prisoner — emerging

V04 supplies both main-story and bonus evidence:
- S-class hero status;
- recurring imprisonment;
- predatory sexual conduct stated in-text;
- physical dominance within prison ecology;
- willingness to break confinement to answer a monster emergency;
- theatrical/flirtatious self-presentation.

Heroic response and serious misconduct must both remain in the model. Neither cancels the other.

## Sonic — moderate, strengthened

The prison bonus is high-value because it broadens Sonic beyond Saitama:
- refuses prison hierarchy;
- treats larger inmates with contempt rather than fear;
- rapidly establishes technical superiority;
- remains autonomy-centered;
- voice is less humiliation-activated when Saitama is absent.

His V02-V03 rivalry therefore becomes a **relationship-specific intensifier**, not the total character.

# 11. Relationship-state updates

## Saitama -> Genos — STRENGTHEN protective responsibility

V03 already established domestic tolerance, teacher-role obligation, and protected sparring. V04 adds operational care under mass danger:

`notices depleted Genos -> entrusts him to Bang -> assumes catastrophic task`.

The relationship remains emotionally understated, but behavior increasingly carries responsibility.

## Genos -> Saitama — STRENGTHEN trust grounded in witnessed capability

Saitama's arrival changes Genos's state because Genos has repeated direct evidence that ordinary power models fail around him. The trust is idealizing but not evidence-free.

## Bang <-> Genos — NEW, provisional

Directional records should remain separate:

- **Bang -> Genos:** younger elite hero whose cognition needs regulation under impossible stakes;
- **Genos -> Bang:** senior hero worth hearing, but not an epistemic authority who replaces analysis.

## Bang <-> Saitama — NEW, provisional

- **Bang -> Saitama:** unknown hero -> direct witness to impossible capability;
- **Saitama -> Bang:** generic older hero/stranger -> trusted enough for immediate caretaker delegation.

No friendship or durable mentorship should be inferred yet.

## Puri-Puri Prisoner -> Sonic / Sonic -> Puri-Puri Prisoner — NEW and strongly asymmetric

- **Puri -> Sonic:** objectifying/sexualized attention toward a fellow prisoner;
- **Sonic -> Puri:** hostility/disgust and explicit judgment that Puri belongs in prison.

Do not normalize this into reciprocal banter or friendship.

# 12. Japanese dialogue / voice findings

## Saitama

V04 extends the V01-V03 conditional-register model.

### Crisis-to-stranger register
With Bang:
- `じいさん こいつ任せるぞ`
- `俺はヒーローをやっている者だ`
- `避難してな じいさん`

Plain, colloquial, directive, non-deferential. He does not adopt formal S-class-facing speech despite Bang's rank/age.

### Public-hostility register
With the crowd:
- `文句がありゃ言ってみろ 聞いてやる`
- `うるせぇ だまれ`
- `そんなもん隕石に言えボケ`
- `テメェら`
- `～じゃねぇ`

This is the strongest roughening yet. Public false blame activates openly aggressive masculine colloquial speech.

### Modeling consequence
Do not generate a uniformly monotone Saitama. Under moral/status irritation his lexical aggression and contraction density rise dramatically even though his substantive position remains concise.

## Genos

Genos's internal crisis language is structured and causal. He enumerates outcomes, asks what follows target destruction, and then explicitly self-regulates with `まあ 落ち着け`.

With Bang/Metal Knight he remains functional and information-oriented rather than becoming deferential in the same way he is with `サイタマ先生`.

## Bang

Strong elderly-masculine voice markers:

# 17. Satire, genre, and public narrative

V04's satire is not “heroes are fake.” It repeatedly breaks that easy binary.

- Bang is old, understated, and genuinely responsible.
- Genos is intensely professional and nearly destroys himself trying to help.
- Metal Knight is useful enough to participate but treats disaster instrumentally.
- Tanktop brothers are registered heroes who weaponize victim anger for status.
- Stinger is flashy and still courageous.
- Puri-Puri Prisoner is an elite hero and a criminal predator.
- Saitama is the decisive savior and becomes the target of public blame.

The object of satire is therefore often the **social machinery that wants one clean label to summarize a person or event**.

Public narrative after the meteor is especially important. The visible aftermath has more narrative power than the invisible counterfactual catastrophe. People judge what remains, not what was prevented.

# 22. V04 prospective synthesis

Through four tankobon volumes, *One Punch Man* has not merely contrasted “real heroes” with “fake heroes.” It is building a multi-variable social ontology in which:

- **power** determines what one can physically do;
- **courage** determines whether one enters danger;
- **motive** determines why one acts;
- **institution** determines how one is classified;
- **rank** compresses performance into a social ladder;
- **public narrative** determines what outsiders think happened;
- **law** determines another axis of legitimacy;
- **relationships** determine what particular people know about one another;
- **self-authorship** determines whether identity survives when those external systems disagree.

Saitama is becoming analytically useful precisely because he does not fit only one side of those binaries. He wants recognition but rejects approval as motive. He is registered but does not derive hero identity from registration. He is overwhelmingly capable but does not always model aftermath in the way Genos does. He is emotionally flat toward many physical threats yet highly activated by false blame, sales, and ordinary material concerns. He can be blunt to the point of social insensitivity while repeatedly exercising restraint and practical care.

V04 therefore strengthens the central reconstruction principle of this reread: **predictive character modeling requires conditional systems, not adjectives**.
