## 2. Orientation and central thesis

V30 carries the surface battle through partial treatment, the swordmasters' intervention and losses, Garou's confrontation with Bang, Genos's progressive dismantling, and a standoff around King. It adds an extended history of the brothers and an undated extra about King's public image. The ending withholds the major combat resolutions. Saitama remains a remembered or represented figure within the main battle; the extra gives him a small direct ordinary-life reaction.

**IR/TH:** V30 asks what remains of strength when superior force stops being an adequate account of a person's conduct. Bang's reform consists in changing the purpose of his art and maintaining that choice under pressure; Genos doubts his progress while refusing to turn surviving allies into acceptable collateral; King steps into danger because of Genos while knowing he cannot supply the force attributed to him. The three cases separate protective commitment from assured victory. They do not make victory unnecessary or ethical resolve sufficient to survive (0145–0159, 0163–0167, 0187–0195).

Strength also becomes intelligible through particular witnesses, and witnesses can be wrong. Bomb recognizes the difference between Bang's old violence and his present teaching; Atomic retracts his earlier judgment and finds a model for concentration in his peer. Conversely, Darkshine's comparative bodily ideal turns hostile when a shinier opponent appears, and King's allies and enemies organize their actions around feats and capacities misattributed to him. Recognition can support reform, destabilize confidence or produce dangerous expectations; it cannot be treated as either empty decoration or reliable measurement (0034–0036, 0108–0113, 0147–0154, 0173–0176, 0189–0201).

The mechanisms enforce that distinction materially. Healing consumes a donor's and healer's resources, sword deployment depends on safe geometry and others' protection, corrosive digestion defeats a previously effective body/blade matchup, and the inherited Sun Blade supplies a local breakthrough followed by exhaustion. Fuhrer Ugly's wish to make others experience his humiliation and Homeless Emperor's wish to break an ally into a useful slave show alternative uses of other people. This is a conflict over conduct and interpretation within a battle whose actual forces remain lethal (0023–0030, 0057–0065, 0107, 0170–0186).

## 3. Strongest counter-reading

The strongest limiting reading is that this is still a serial escalation volume: new attacks invalidate old advantages, aid arrives and is destroyed, hidden weapons briefly reverse the situation, and King supplies an unresolved comic cliffhanger. Much of the ethical formulation comes from Bang's confession, Bomb's interpretation and Genos's internal question rather than completed reparative outcomes. Garou has not accepted the invitation, Genos has not rescued Tatsumaki to safety, and King has not defeated anyone. The older brother's remembered nonlethal victory may be accurate within the account without proving that restraint always beats killing intent.

These limits constrain the thesis instead of dissolving the observed changes. Genos explicitly says he cannot now take the surviving heroes with him; Bang continues his protective art despite the perceived offensive alternative; King identifies concern for Genos as the reason he exposed himself. The choices are already present even where their practical success is not. Nor does the volume offer a clean heroic monopoly on cooperation: Golden Sperm saves Homeless Emperor, and enemies can coordinate after competing. Conversely, helpful heroes misread King or override Child Emperor's wishes. Darkshine's collapse must retain actual acid injury and prior protective contributions alongside dependence on comparative shine. Saitama's silent irritation at a magazine cannot bear an entire new theory of envy or recognition.
