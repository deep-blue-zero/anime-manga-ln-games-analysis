# 2. Volume orientation

V14 ended by making power asymmetry ethical: superior strength could become license to dominate or responsibility toward someone weaker. V15 shifts the question from **what strength is for** to **what a person or institution does when its present model stops working**.

Saitama has solved physical growth so completely that he mistakes the end of one progression axis for the end of development itself. King attacks that assumption from the domain he actually understands: games. A maxed level is not a completed game; one can pursue records, collect everything, explore, interact with other players, or discover different forms of mastery. King then translates that logic into heroism. Being the strongest hero does not make Saitama the `最高のヒーロー`; helping people and being useful to the world remain open-ended tasks.

Garou supplies the opposite growth trajectory. Defeat does not produce resignation. Watchdog Man's unfamiliar movement becomes data. Garou studies, imitates, adapts, and explicitly enjoys the fact that he can still become stronger. His development is real, but it is not moral improvement: he immediately redirects that growth toward continued hero hunting.

Sonic chooses a third response. Faced with a ceiling embodied by Saitama, he accepts the idea of a Monster Cell shortcut. Yet intention, procedure, and bodily outcome diverge. His mundane insistence on cooking unknown raw meat appears to prevent the intended monsterization and instead produces gastrointestinal collapse. The joke is ontologically useful: wanting to cease being human is not identical to successfully becoming biologically nonhuman.

Genos routes defeat through repair and upgrade. The Hero Association routes uncertainty through threat classification, witness reports, mobilization, and revision. The Monster Association routes scattered monster violence through communication, recruitment, discipline, and organized war. King can defeat Saitama inside a fighting game because game rules make mechanical knowledge and technique causally relevant in a way raw physical power is not.

## Central V15 thesis

**V15 is about growth after model failure. It distinguishes being strong from knowing how to continue, and it repeatedly shows actors responding to limits by learning, revising, upgrading, shortcutting, organizing, or misattributing. A model becomes dangerous not because it is incomplete, but because its user mistakes a successful local rule for a complete account of reality.**

That thesis extends the bounded-proxy discipline established in V10 without repeating it. V15 supplies explicit processes for what should happen after a proxy fails:

1. **Saitama / King:** redefine the progression axis rather than assume completion;
2. **Garou:** treat defeat as information and adapt;
3. **Genos / Kuseno:** diagnose failure and alter hardware;
4. **Hero Association:** revise threat level when new field information arrives;
5. **Monster Association:** convert dispersed strength into strategy and command;
6. **Sonic:** seek a radical shortcut but discover that procedure mediates transformation;
7. **public narrative:** fail to revise when an eyewitness scene is causally misread, allowing King's false legend to grow.

## Strongest limiting reading

V15 does **not** prove that growth is always virtuous, that institutions become accurate merely by revising, or that Saitama has accepted King's philosophy.

- Garou's learning makes him more dangerous while leaving his hero-hunting project active.
- Sonic's failed transformation is not a moral refusal; it is a procedural/comedic failure after genuine intent to become a monster.
- Genos's upgrade loop is adaptive but may also preserve a self-concept in which every defeat must be solved by becoming stronger.
- Hero Association classifications are useful enough to organize response while remaining vulnerable to hidden information and mismeasured actors.
- King's advice is unusually penetrating, but V15 does not yet show Saitama reorganizing his life around it.
- King's public legend remains false even though his private conceptual value to Saitama is becoming increasingly real.

The safest V15 claim is therefore not “everyone grows.” It is that **V15 exposes multiple growth mechanisms and asks whether each actor can recognize the domain in which change is actually needed.**

# 3. Sequential close reading

## 3.1 `76撃目 いつも通りの退屈` — overwhelming strength cannot solve absence

### 3.1.1 Puri-Puri Prisoner: pain reframed through character-specific meaning

The opening fight with `フリーハガー` preserves the series' ability to combine sincere hero endurance with comic character register. Puri-Puri Prisoner is physically punished by an opponent whose body makes ordinary contact hazardous, yet he converts pain into his idiosyncratic love-language rather than abandoning the fight.

This does not provide a general moral theory of pain. It does strengthen a character-specific rule: Puri-Puri Prisoner can absorb humiliation or injury by narratively transforming it into affective meaning. The comedy and hero labor are simultaneous rather than mutually cancelling.

### 3.1.2 Saitama arrives after the important work has already happened

The most consequential early scene is not a fight. Saitama encounters the injured C-class hero `ミズデッポウ`, learns that the monster was overwhelmingly strong, and hears that support was requested but never arrived.

`OPM|V15|chapter:76|images:0020-0021|anchor:Saitama learns he missed citywide monster emergency`

Saitama reconstructs that a major incident rose and disappeared within only a few hours. His complaint is not simply that the enemy was weak or that combat is boring:

`そんな一大事を見逃すなんて 空回りしてばっかりだな`

The phrase `空回り` is important. He is not describing a lack of power. He is describing effort or activity that fails to connect with a meaningful target.

That distinction materially advances the Saitama model. His heroic dissatisfaction now includes **temporal and informational misalignment**. A hero can possess more than enough capacity and still fail to be useful because he is not present when the need occurs.

### 3.1.3 “Always the same boredom” is therefore not only opponent deprivation

The chapter title `いつも通りの退屈` initially invites the familiar reading that Saitama is bored because no enemy can challenge him. V15 complicates that interpretation. He has also missed a crisis in which other heroes were hurt while he pursued the novelty of Super Fight.

The problem has at least three layers:

- physical challenge remains absent;
- novelty-seeking can misallocate attention;
- hero usefulness depends on discovering where intervention is needed.

This is an important correction to any model that treats Saitama's existential problem as a single-variable search for a stronger opponent.

## 3.2 `77撃目 停滞と成長` — King distinguishes strength completion from hero completion

### 3.2.1 The game analogy is not decorative

King begins from his actual competence domain. A game is not exhausted because the avatar has reached maximum level. He lists alternative progression systems:

`アイテムを全部集めたり 最速クリア記録に挑戦したり 他のプレーヤーと交流したり やりこみ要素はレベル上げ以外にも見つけられるよ`

`OPM|V15|chapter:77|image:0033|anchor:King lists progression beyond level grinding`

This is one of V15's most important conceptual statements because it does not deny Saitama's premise that his physical growth may be effectively finished. Instead, it **changes the axis on which completion is measured**.

King's game literacy becomes useful precisely because Saitama's problem is structurally game-like: one progression system has been maxed, but the larger activity still contains unexplored goals.

### 3.2.2 King converts game logic into an ethical theory of heroism

King then shifts from games to the hero role:

`サイタマ氏は強くなっただけで目的地に辿り着いたと勘違いしてるんじゃないかな?`

and:

`ヒーローとして進む道ってそんなに簡単にゴールできるものじゃないと思うんだ`

`OPM|V15|chapter:77|image:0037|anchor:King separates becoming strong from completing hero path`

The next page is even more explicit:

`強いの中に充実を得ようとするのはヒーローの本質的に間違っている`

`人助けや世の役に立つことにこそヒーローの存在意義があるのではないか`

The exact grammar is colloquial and King's formulation is his own philosophy rather than narrator law, but the semantic direction is unambiguous: deriving fulfillment from strength alone mistakes strength for the purpose of heroism.

King therefore articulates something V14 showed through action. Snek, Max, and Saitama made strength answerable to another person's need; V15 gives Saitama a peer who can formulate why strength is not the final metric.

### 3.2.3 `最強` and `最高` are deliberately separated

King says Saitama may be the `最強のヒーロー` while still not having become the `最高のヒーロー`.

`OPM|V15|chapter:77|images:0038-0039|anchor:King separates strongest hero from highest/best hero`

This is a compact lexical distinction with large modeling consequences. `最強` is a capability superlative. `最高` is a value-laden evaluative superlative. V15 refuses to let the former automatically entail the latter.

King continues that the question `最高のヒーローとは何か?` should keep a hero occupied rather than be prematurely closed. The counsel is unusually serious and relationally important because King is one of the few people who knows Saitama's actual strength and can therefore address the real problem rather than the public fiction.

### 3.2.4 Saitama does not simply accept the lesson

Saitama pushes back by noting that King himself has little standing to lecture about hero work. That objection is fair. King has not suddenly become an experienced moral authority merely because he can formulate a useful analogy.

Yet Saitama continues listening, and his later expression shows the argument has reached him enough to produce reflection. The safe claim is **receptivity to an unexpectedly useful peer model**, not completed philosophical conversion.

This also deepens the Saitama/King relationship. V08 established confession and gaming as a low-stakes peer channel. V15 shows that the same channel can support serious conceptual counsel without requiring formal mentorship.

### 3.2.5 Garou provides the chapter's opposite growth image

The chapter then turns to a badly injured Garou. Unlike Saitama, Garou does not experience strength as completed. He retrospectively analyzes Watchdog Man's strange movement and recognizes that unfamiliarity itself was instructive.

His language is explicit:

`勉強になったぜ 興奮が冷めねぇ`

and:

`楽しくてしょうがねぇ! 俺はもっともっと強くなれるんだ!!`

`OPM|V15|chapter:77|images:0048-0049|anchor:Garou treats defeat as learning and enjoys future growth`

This gives Garou one of the clearest growth policies in the corpus so far:

**defeat / unfamiliar mechanism → analysis → incorporation → excitement → renewed challenge-seeking.**

The policy is developmentally powerful and ethically neutral. Garou's ability to learn does not make his targets legitimate.

### 3.2.6 Saitama and Garou remain radically asymmetric in mutual recognition

Garou sees King and attempts to initiate a meaningful hunt. Saitama is present and once again removes him almost incidentally before grasping his significance. Garou's carefully accumulated growth is real; Saitama's overwhelming capability makes that growth illegible at the moment of contact.

The sequence reinforces the V10 first-contact result: the Saitama/Garou relation is still **asymmetric non-recognition**, not yet an ordinary rivalry.

## 3.3 `78撃目 暗躍する者たち` — shortcuts, hidden organizations, and a failed ontology transition

### 3.3.1 Gale Wind and Hellfire Flame convert ninja skill into Monster Association assets

The appearance of `疾風のウィンド` and `業火のフレイム` expands both the ninja-world and Monster Association models. They retain recognizable technical identity from human ninja training while presenting monsterized bodies and affiliating with the Association.

This matters because V13–V14 had already shown that Monster Cells can recruit outside specialists. V15 supplies another high-skill intake channel and makes the attraction legible to Sonic: monsterization promises an immediate way past a human ceiling.

### 3.3.2 Sonic's intent to cease being human is genuine

Sonic is not tricked into misunderstanding what is being offered. The Monster Cell is explained as a route to monster power. He thinks through the implication:

`人間をやめることになるが…`

and then frames his prior defeat as a kind of death already suffered. His intent culminates in the decision to become a monster in order to surpass Saitama.

`OPM|V15|chapter:78|images:0082-0083|anchor:Sonic accepts monsterization intent as Saitama shortcut`

This is important character evidence. Sonic's Saitama fixation can override attachment to human categorical identity. But V15 does not make him an actual biological monster.

### 3.3.3 Cooking the Monster Cell separates intention from bodily outcome

Sonic later cooks the unknown flesh. His reasoning is mundane:

`これ…火を通して良かったんだよな?`

`常識的に考えて こんな得体の知れない生肉をそのまま食える訳がないしな…`

`OPM|V15|chapter:78|image:0098|anchor:Sonic cooks Monster Cell from ordinary food-safety logic`

The result is severe stomach trouble rather than visible successful monsterization.

This is one of the volume's best ontology controls. Four dimensions separate cleanly:

- **intent:** Sonic intends to become a monster;
- **procedure:** he alters the prescribed raw-ingestion method;
- **body:** no successful monster body transition is established;
- **conduct/identity afterward:** V15 ends before any stable post-event reclassification is warranted.

Do not promote “cooking chemically neutralizes Monster Cells” into a universal law. V15 provides a comic causal implication, not an experimentally controlled biochemical model.

### 3.3.4 Zombieman is investigating a hidden causal model, not announcing a fact

Zombieman says:

`俺の読みが正しければ…今回の騒動を裏で操っている黒幕はあの組織…`

`OPM|V15|chapter:78|image:0087|anchor:Zombieman hypothesizes an organization behind recent incident`

The conditional `俺の読みが正しければ` is essential. V15 establishes an investigation hypothesis. It does **not** establish that the unspecified `あの組織` is definitely the causal author of the Monster Association crisis.

This belongs in the hidden-actors ledger as OPEN evidence, not as solved conspiracy structure.

### 3.3.5 Armored Gorilla is a control case against body = current enemy

The encounter between Martial Gorilla and Armored Gorilla is comic but analytically important. Armored Gorilla, once an enemy asset of the House of Evolution, is now simply engaged in ordinary shopping/ingredient acquisition. He rebuffs local trouble rather than initiating it.

Longitudinally, this is the strongest evidence yet that a visibly nonhuman/artificial body and prior antagonist history do not fix present conduct. It reinforces the project's separation of body ontology, affiliation, and moral behavior.

It also materially improves Armored Gorilla's modeling readiness because he now recurs across radically different contexts: hostile organizational asset, defeated survivor, and ordinary integrated civilian-like actor.

## 3.4 `79撃目 ハメ技` — organizations and games both make rules matter

### 3.4.1 Monster Association communication turns violence into strategic signaling

The Hero Association receives a deliberate communication through a monster-mediated messenger. The hostage is not merely a victim; it is leverage. The Association is invited into a controlled confrontation under enemy-defined conditions.

This strengthens the V14 operational model. Terror publicity, hostage value, and recruitment are now joined by **formal adversarial communication**. The Monster Association is attempting to shape the Hero Association's behavior, not merely inflict casualties.

### 3.4.2 Gyoro Gyoro and Orochi convert a monster population into an army

Inside the Monster Association, the story emphasizes hierarchy, discipline, and strategic direction. `怪人王` Orochi is not merely a symbol of power. His violence enforces organizational obedience; withdrawal or disobedience can itself become lethal.

Gyoro Gyoro's planning logic further distinguishes centralized war-making from spontaneous monster violence. The emerging conflict is explicitly institutional: Hero Association versus Monster Association.

The analytical consequence is that “monster” can no longer be treated as a sufficient organizational category. There are independent monsters, recruited monsters, coerced converts, disciplined members, commanders, and actors whose affiliations remain uncertain.

### 3.4.3 Garou remains outside that organizational logic

The volume's organization material continues to matter for Garou even when he is not physically inside the Monster Association. His self-authored `怪人` identity remains anti-affiliative at this boundary. He is pursuing his own hero-hunting project while the formal monster institution is building a war machine.

The two trajectories should not be merged merely because both are socially labeled monstrous.

### 3.4.4 The chapter title pays off through King defeating Saitama in a rule-bound domain

After the large organizational material, the chapter ends with a smaller but conceptually exact example. King traps Saitama in a fighting-game loop / `ハメ技` and drives the counter to an absurd hit count.

`OPM|V15|chapter:79|images:0142-0148|anchor:King locks Saitama in fighting-game infinite combo`

Saitama's physical supremacy has no causal relevance inside the game's input rules. King's technical literacy is what matters.

This is not a gag that “really” proves King is stronger. It is a clean domain-specific competence demonstration:

**when the rules of the domain exclude raw bodily power, mastery of mechanics can dominate Saitama.**

That principle rhymes with King’s earlier counsel. Saitama's mistake is repeatedly to imagine that one maxed dimension may exhaust the activity.

## 3.5 `80撃目 包囲` — repair, mobilization, and Garou's narrowing freedom

### 3.5.1 Genos treats defeat as an engineering problem

At Kuseno's secret laboratory, Genos emerges from another rapid reconstruction. Kuseno asks about operation/function; Genos thanks him for working through the night at his urgent request.

Kuseno frames the emergency as serious enough that sleep is secondary. He also remarks that Genos's requests continue to generate improvement ideas. Genos ends the exchange with a categorical commitment:

`もう…負けません`

`OPM|V15|chapter:80|images:0150-0151|anchor:Genos repair upgrade after defeat`

This strengthens, rather than transforms, Genos's established policy:

**defeat → diagnosis → technical revision → immediate return intent.**

The limitation remains important. V15 does not yet show whether the new configuration solves the conditions that produced the prior defeat, and determination should not be mistaken for proof of calibration.

### 3.5.2 Hero Association mobilization makes uncertainty operational

The institutional scenes show an organization attempting to act under incomplete information: public alarm, hostage pressure, unknown enemy numbers, uncertain base structure, wounded heroes, and disagreement about response scale.

Child Emperor's analytic role strengthens. Metal Knight/Bofoi contributes strategic judgment from a distance rather than simply entering the battlefield. The institution is neither omniscient nor inert; it is a constrained information-processing system.

V15 therefore avoids the easy institutional caricature “bureaucracy is useless.” The more precise problem is **how incomplete evidence, elite availability, donor/hostage pressure, and responder specialization distort or delay decisions.**

### 3.5.3 Saitama remains outside the institutional emotional temperature

The manga cuts from institutional war framing to Saitama casually moving through a landscape of monster corpses and remarking on the frequency of monsters / ordinary cleanup implications.

The tonal discontinuity is structurally important. The same external crisis is experienced at radically different scales depending on capability, information, and social role. Saitama's low ceremony is not evidence that the crisis is unreal; it is evidence that threat magnitude is observer-relative.

### 3.5.4 Tareo returns to Garou as an actual relational complication

Tareo finds Garou badly injured in the abandoned shed. The interaction is characteristically abrasive:

- Garou recognizes him from the hero-almanac encounter;
- Tareo calls him the monster-loving older guy;
- Garou rejects the age label;
- when Tareo cries after other children exclude him, Garou says `泣くな 鬱陶しい`;
- Garou nonetheless tells him not to worry and indicates he will move once recovered.

`OPM|V15|chapter:80|images:0170-0172|anchor:Garou responds gruffly to Tareo exclusion distress`

This is not warm nurturance, but neither is it indifference. Garou gives attention, remembers Tareo, tries to regulate the crying, and becomes interested in the bullying problem.

### 3.5.5 Garou begins to offer Tareo an anti-humiliation lesson

Garou asks Tareo to bring the other children and says:

`お前に良いこと教えてやろうか? 周りにバカにされない方法さ`

`OPM|V15|chapter:80|image:0173|anchor:Garou offers Tareo method not to be mocked`

The volume cuts away before that method is delivered. This is a crucial prospective boundary. It is fair to infer that Garou recognizes Tareo's social humiliation as personally resonant. It is **not** fair to specify what lesson he would teach or whether it would help Tareo.

The scene further complicates Garou's “loser” identification. He can react to a bullied child with recognition and proto-mentoring while simultaneously maintaining a project built around assaulting heroes.

### 3.5.6 The hero coalition removes Garou's control over encounter conditions

Death Gatling and a mixed hero squad surround the shed while Garou is injured. V15 stops at the encirclement setup.

This boundary matters because the volume has spent multiple chapters distinguishing learning from invulnerability. Garou has grown, but he is physically depleted, relationally entangled with Tareo, and now faces a deliberately assembled team rather than a self-selected one-on-one hunt.

No V16 outcome should be imported. At V15 the question is simply OPEN: **what happens when Garou's defeat-learning system is tested under exhaustion, mixed techniques, constrained space, and a nearby child?**

# 4. `番外編.1 災害レベル` — the institution explicitly models uncertainty

## 4.1 Threat level is defined as a composite estimate, not an essence

The extra provides the clearest formal description yet of the Hero Association's disaster-level system. Classification is assigned from evidence such as strength, aggression, extermination difficulty, and broader danger. It is used to guide deployment.

This is important because the manga itself explains what the metric is for. Threat level is an operational estimate.

## 4.2 Hero-rank equivalence is only approximate

The extra supplies rough combat heuristics:

- Wolf ≈ several C-class heroes / roughly one B-class hero;
- Tiger ≈ several B-class heroes / roughly one A-class hero;
- Demon ≈ several A-class heroes / roughly one S-class hero.

But the surrounding narration explicitly qualifies the model. Hero ranking is not identical to pure combat evaluation, and context, hidden capabilities, matchups, and incomplete information can invalidate simple correspondence.

`OPM|V15|extra:災害レベル|images:0185-0188|anchor:formal disaster-level and hero-rank heuristic with caveats`

This strongly confirms the longitudinal bounded-proxy model: the institution itself knows its categories are references, not complete ontologies.

## 4.3 `やせ細りモヤシ` demonstrates evidence-driven reclassification

A seemingly weak monster is initially treated as low threat. Once its actual ice capability is exposed, the estimate jumps to `推定災害レベル・鬼`.

The correction matters more than whether the first estimate was embarrassing. The institutional process contains a mechanism for updating when field evidence changes.

This is the clearest V15 example of productive model failure:

**initial classification → contradictory observation → revised classification.**

## 4.4 Saitama remains a catastrophic outlier for rank-based inference

The monster then reads Saitama through his public position: `B級7位 ハゲマント`. It concludes that such a rank cannot represent the displayed capability and tries to explain the contradiction through disguise/feigned weakness.

Saitama's reply `誰が擬態だ` punctures that narrative, but the institutional point remains. When rank is used outside its reliable domain, an observer can rationalize away direct evidence rather than revise the model.

## 4.5 Genos performs unglamorous maintenance around Saitama

At the end, Genos explains that Saitama's wandering has again left monster corpses along the streets and that he planned to incinerate them together. Saitama is instead using fire for roasted sweet potatoes.

The sequence is small but highly useful for character modeling. Genos notices recurring practical consequences of Saitama's ordinary hero activity and independently undertakes cleanup/maintenance. His `先生` relationship includes infrastructural labor, not only admiration and combat learning.

# 5. `番外編.2 目撃` — eyewitnessing is not causal knowledge

## 5.1 The title is deliberately ironic

A giant threat overwhelms the available response and is then eliminated by Saitama. The institutional/public evidence afterward is fragmentary.

The extra is titled `目撃` — witnessing. Yet what witnesses see is not enough to reconstruct what happened correctly.

## 5.2 King becomes the visible residue of Saitama's invisible action

After the monster is destroyed, King is present covered in debris/blood-like residue while searching for a dropped game disc. His posture and reputation make the scene visually legible as a heroic victory even though he did not perform it.

Injured heroes report that the responsible party was “that man again,” and the visual reveal supplies King as the obvious referent.

`OPM|V15|extra:目撃|images:0207-0209|anchor:King receives eyewitness causal attribution for Saitama kill`

V08 established that King's legend can grow through accidental proximity to Saitama's victories. V15 strengthens the mechanism: **eyewitness perception itself can be causally wrong when observers enter the scene after the decisive action and fill missing sequence with reputation.**

## 5.3 Reputation is functioning as a prior

Observers do not invent King from nothing. They already possess a model of him as an overwhelming S-class hero. Ambiguous evidence is therefore interpreted through that prior.

The extra is a near-perfect satire of evidence processing:

- genuine event: monster destroyed;
- genuine observation: King at scene;
- missing observation: Saitama's decisive action;
- prior belief: King is extraordinarily powerful;
- inferred cause: King defeated monster;
- institutional/public update: King's legend grows stronger.

The evidence is real. The causal inference is wrong.

This makes V15's concern with model revision sharper. Some systems revise when contradictory information appears. King's reputation system instead often absorbs ambiguity as further confirmation.

# 6. Saitama state through V15

## 6.1 Affect and boredom

**STRENGTHEN / REVISE.** Boredom remains real, but V15 makes it inadequate to define it solely as lack of worthy combat. Missing a major incident produces frustration because he was not useful where needed. The problem now includes challenge deprivation, timing, information, and purpose.

## 6.2 Heroic self-concept

**STRENGTHEN.** King's `最強` versus `最高` distinction places explicit pressure on Saitama's hero self-model. Saitama has physical completion without a settled account of ideal herohood.

## 6.3 Growth model

**REVISE.** “No room to grow” is too broad. The source only strongly supports physical/combat completion. King articulates other possible axes: usefulness, helping people, social interaction, pursuit of an ideal, and forms of mastery not reducible to level.

## 6.4 Recognition

**PRESERVE.** No clean new scene changes the recognition hierarchy. `目撃` again deprives him of credit, but V15 does not show him knowingly choosing that outcome.

## 6.5 Relationship with King

**STRENGTHEN.** King is now demonstrably more than an accidental confidant/game opponent. He can provide conceptual counsel rooted in a domain where he possesses actual expertise. Their relationship supports both trivial competition and serious existential conversation.

## 6.6 Domain limits

**STRENGTHEN.** King's fighting-game `ハメ技` supplies a concrete nonphysical domain where Saitama's body does not determine success. Saitama remains capable of frustration and defeat when a system's rules make other skills causally relevant.

# 7. Ensemble character deltas

## 7.1 Garou — defeat as data, growth as pleasure

**Readiness: remains `moderate`, substantially strengthened.**

V15 gives Garou a more explicit learning policy than any prior volume. He reconstructs Watchdog Man's unfamiliar movement, treats the encounter as study, and expresses joy at future growth. This is not mere durability. It is metacognitive adaptation.

Relationship evidence with Tareo also deepens. Garou remains abrasive but recognizes Tareo's humiliation, tolerates repeated contact while injured, and begins offering a lesson about avoiding ridicule. The relationship is now strong enough to function as a meaningful constraint variable in future Garou modeling.

Open contradiction: his sensitivity to a bullied child coexists with continued willingness to hunt heroes, including while physically depleted.

## 7.2 King — from confidant to conceptual peer

**Readiness: remains `moderate`, strengthened.**

# 8. Heroism, recognition, rank, and institutional state

V15 produces four major institutional results.

### 8.1 Heroism is not exhausted by strength

King formulates a source-internal challenge to strength-as-hero-completion. Helping people and being useful remain distinct axes of hero value.

### 8.2 Disaster levels are operational estimates

The Association's own explanatory extra frames threat level as a composite, revisable estimate. Rankings and threat levels are neither meaningless nor ontologically complete.

### 8.3 Institutional response depends on information latency

Saitama's missed crisis, delayed support, the Monster Association's hostage message, and threat-level reclassification all show timing/information as first-order variables.

### 8.4 Recognition can update in the wrong direction

`目撃` shows an institution/public audience receiving genuine observations but inferring the wrong causal actor. King's reputation then becomes even more resistant to correction because each ambiguous event is interpreted as new confirming evidence.

V15 adds one of the cleanest ontology test cases in the project.

### 9.1 Sonic: self-identification intent without successful body conversion

Sonic genuinely decides to become a monster. Yet V15 does not establish successful biological transformation after he cooks the cell. ### 9.3 Armored Gorilla: abnormal body without current antagonist conduct

Longitudinal ordinary-life evidence further blocks any equation of visible nonhuman body with present hostility.

### 9.4 Monster Association membership remains organizational, not merely ontological

Membership entails command, communication, recruitment, discipline, and strategic objectives. Garou's self-authored monster identity remains distinct from that structure.

# 10. Power, technique, and explanatory systems

## 10.1 Garou's adaptation is a genuine power system

Garou does not only get tougher. He observes unfamiliar movement, extracts principles, and attempts incorporation. This is learning-driven combat development.

## 10.2 King's game technique demonstrates domain dependence

The `ハメ技` sequence is a formal power-system joke with analytical force. Capability must be evaluated relative to rules. Saitama's physical power cannot substitute for controller knowledge/timing inside the game's mechanics.

## 10.3 Genos treats hardware as revisable explanatory machinery

Upgrade follows observed insufficiency. V15 strengthens the technical-modification pathway without proving that every new upgrade solves strategic/calibration problems.

## 10.4 Disaster classification is explicitly Bayesian-like, not absolute

The Association does not use that vocabulary, but structurally the process is evidence-sensitive: prior estimate, new observation, posterior correction. Hidden abilities remain a known failure mode.

## 10.5 Monster Cell procedure matters

V15 supplies procedural sensitivity without yet supplying a full mechanism. Raw ingestion is prescribed; Sonic alters the procedure; intended transformation does not visibly occur. Mechanism remains OPEN.

# 11. Humor, satire, and genre mechanics

V15 repeatedly lets comedy expose analytical structure rather than interrupt it.

# 17. V15 closure synthesis

Volume 15 does not merely bridge Super Fight into the Monster Association conflict. It places **models of growth and correction** beside one another.

Saitama's body has nowhere obvious to go, so King asks him to stop treating the body as the whole game. Garou is almost the inverse: every injury tells him there is still something to learn, and that prospect thrills him. Sonic tries to jump across development through monsterization, only for mundane procedure to interrupt the shortcut. Genos converts defeat into reconstruction. The Hero Association formalizes uncertainty into revisable danger levels. The Monster Association formalizes scattered monsters into a strategic organization. King's legend demonstrates the darker possibility: a model can become stronger through repeated misinterpretation rather than correction.

The result is a volume where **growth is less about accumulating power than about correctly identifying what failed**.

That insight also sharpens the series' treatment of Saitama. His problem is not that nothing in the world can change him. It is that the most obvious shonen development axis—becoming stronger—has become useless for explaining what he still lacks. V15 gives him a friend capable of saying that aloud in terms he can understand, while leaving completely open whether he can live the answer.
