## 2. Volume orientation

V01-V05 gradually professionalized Saitama's private hero identity. The Hero Association became a registry, a ranking system, a source of pay, a producer of reputation, and an imperfect information network. V06 scales that apparatus up to a problem its ordinary categories barely contain: a prophecy that the Earth itself is in danger and an alien attack capable of erasing A-City almost instantly.

The escalation does not make the earlier institutional questions disappear. It intensifies them.

The Association can summon elite heroes but cannot guarantee that all will answer. It possesses prophetic intelligence but must decide whether and how to disclose it. It offers the assembled S-class a real chance to leave **before** revealing a secret, then announces coercive confinement **after** disclosure to prevent panic. Its strongest available heroes do not become a harmonious super-team merely because the threat is planetary. They arrive with incompatible temperaments, priorities, speech styles, expertise, and motives.

At the same time, V06 gives Saitama the closest thematic mirror so far. Boros also lives with a problem of insufficient challenge. He crossed space because an alien seer promised a worthy opponent who might restore stimulation to his life. The resemblance is real enough that the manga does not need to manufacture it retrospectively: Saitama has spent five volumes searching, consciously or otherwise, for resistant experience.

But the resemblance is not moral equivalence. Boros converts boredom into entitlement over other worlds. Saitama rejects that conversion immediately. The volume therefore separates **shared experiential deprivation** from **shared ethics**.

The bonus `鮭` then miniaturizes the same problem of limits. Saitama encounters a man whose job, family, dignity, and desire to live have collapsed. Physical power cannot solve those causes. Saitama explicitly says so. He does not invent a motivational speech or claim authority over the man's life. Yet when the man actually falls, Saitama catches him, and afterward he offers a characteristically indirect form of availability: if the man ever feels like jumping again, do it from a building where Saitama is eating.

V06 therefore links planetary and ordinary crisis through a common question:

> **What remains of heroism when power cannot make another person's problem simple?**

# 3. Central thesis

**V06 is the point where One Punch Man most clearly separates overwhelming power from moral authority, elite status from shared ethos, and existential boredom from permission to dominate others.**

Three structures carry that argument.

First, the S-class assembly shows that `S級` is not a personality category. It contains Bang's calm elder authority, Tatsumaki's hostile confidence, Metal Bat's family attachment and rough threat language, Child Emperor's childlike schedule and tactical intelligence, Puri-Puri Prisoner's theatrical criminal contradiction, Genos's analytical seriousness, King's opaque reputation, and multiple absent or unavailable members. Their cooperation is situational rather than ideological.

Second, Saitama/Boros makes the series' boredom problem ethically comparative. Boros says he came because a prophecy promised a being who could give his life stimulation. Saitama understands the premise and calls the conclusion stupid: being bored does not justify attacking other planets. The same felt deprivation can produce self-directed searching or predatory externalization.

Third, `鮭` demonstrates a limit that cannot be punched. Saitama cannot restore a stranger's employment, marriage, or subjective reasons for living. His help is therefore modest and conditional: honest acknowledgment of limits, refusal to sermonize, intervention when the body is actually in danger, and an indirect invitation to future contact.

### Strongest counter-readings

A reductive reading might say V06 proves the Hero Association is authoritarian. That is incomplete. Sicchi's secrecy regime is coercive, but the scene deliberately places a genuine informed opt-out before disclosure: anyone who wishes to leave may do so, may retain S-class registration, and is told that death is possible. The ethical problem begins **after** the secret is accepted. The institution is not purely benevolent or purely arbitrary; it is attempting crisis governance under uncertainty with real coercive costs.

A second reductive reading might treat Boros as “evil Saitama.” That collapses the distinction the volume works to establish. Their experiential problems overlap, but their action policies do not. Saitama's boredom has never licensed mass victimization. Boros's choice to invade and destroy is exactly what Saitama condemns.

A third reductive reading might interpret `鮭` as Saitama not caring whether a suicidal stranger lives. The complete sequence contradicts that. He does not pretend to solve the man's life, but he catches him immediately when he falls and leaves a route for future presence. The useful character distinction is between **non-paternalistic affective style** and **absence of care**.

# 4. Sequential close reading

## 4.1 30撃目 `S級` — elite status is not a common personality

### Bang's invitation and Saitama's interest threshold

Bang invites Saitama and Genos to his dojo with the promise of showing them something interesting. Saitama quickly realizes the real purpose is martial-arts recruitment:

> `面白いもん見せてくれるっつーから わざわざ来たのに 勧誘かよジイサン`
>
> `興味ねーよ ジェノスやっとけお前`

Genos refuses as well:

> `俺が求めるものは護身術ではなく 絶対的な破壊力です`

This short exchange is modeling-rich. Saitama will expend effort for possible novelty, but institutionalized training doctrine has little attraction once it presents itself as ordinary instruction. Genos, by contrast, states his goal directly as **absolute destructive power**, not merely self-defense.

The line should not be generalized into “Genos does not care about protection.” V05 has already shown the opposite through catastrophic self-sacrifice for a child. V06 instead distinguishes his desired **means** from his ethical **uses**: he seeks overwhelming destructive capacity even while deploying himself protectively.

### Garou exists at V06 only as Bang's mediated past

Bang explains that a former top disciple, Garou, attacked the dojo, incapacitated skilled students, caused others to quit in fear, and was then beaten and expelled by Bang.

This is high-value **mediated** evidence about Bang:

- his dojo once possessed a larger social structure;
- he will use overwhelming force to expel a dangerous disciple;
- the event materially damaged his school;
- he does not conceal his own role in the expulsion.

It is **not** yet direct behavioral evidence for present Garou. V06 supplies a name, a historical action set, and Bang's account. Later motives, categories, relationships, and ideology remain outside the boundary.

### Bang's ego-free recognition of Saitama

When Charanko boasts about Silver Fang, Bang rebukes him and says:

> `ワシよりサイタマ君の方が何倍も強いわ!`

The important point is not power ranking for its own sake. Bang possesses S-class status, a martial-arts school, a disciple audience, and an obvious opportunity to protect face. He instead states observed superiority bluntly.

**Bang model consequence:** status pride does not require denial of stronger evidence. At this boundary he can separate personal/institutional prestige from capability recognition.

### Genos invites Saitama into an S-class problem

When the Association summons all S-class heroes, Genos says:

> `S級が呼ばれるという事は先生の力も必要になるかもしれない 一緒に来てくれますか?`

Saitama answers:

> `いいぜ 暇だから`

This is a useful relationship atom. Genos does not hide Saitama from the Association to preserve his own status; he actively brings him into a problem because he believes Saitama may be necessary. Saitama's stated motive is not heroic ceremony. He is free and interested enough to go.

### Tatsumaki and rank-boundary hostility

Tatsumaki immediately challenges Saitama's presence. Her language assumes the S-class meeting is a privileged space and imputes status-seeking motive to him:

> `S級とお近づきになりたいとか 浅い考えでここに来たんでしょ!?`
>
> `不愉快`
>
> `消えて`

Saitama's first interpretation is appearance-based rather than status-aware:

> `なんだこの生意気な……迷子?`

Genos identifies her as S-class rank 2 and an esper. Saitama then follows Genos instead of escalating the quarrel.

At V06 the safe Tatsumaki formulation is not “she hates weak people” or any later psychologized thesis. What is directly supported is:

`uninvited lower-ranked outsider in elite space -> status-boundary policing + motive attribution + contemptuous dismissal`.

For Saitama, the scene reinforces two rules:

- he does not automatically raise politeness because someone is S-class;
- he is willing to disengage from a low-value status conflict rather than convert every insult into a contest.

## 4.2 31撃目 `大予言` — emergency governance, uncertainty, and a real pre-disclosure choice

### Sicchi's opt-out matters

The Association official Sicchi tells the assembled S-class that the coming information may involve death. Before disclosing it, he offers withdrawal without loss of S-class registration and explicitly frames withdrawal as possible courage.

Only after the group stays does he state the secrecy consequence: once they hear the information, they may be prevented from leaving until resolution because uncontrolled disclosure could panic the public.

This creates a more precise institutional model than “the Association controls heroes.” The sequence is:

`high-risk briefing -> informed exit opportunity -> voluntary remaining -> classified disclosure -> coercive information containment`.

The coercion remains ethically important. So does the fact that the institution tries to locate consent **before** crossing the information boundary.

### S-class absence remains a structural limit

Blast and Metal Knight are absent/unavailable, and the Association cannot simply summon capability into existence. V04 already showed that S-class status does not guarantee response. V06 strengthens that at planetary scale.

The Association therefore functions less like a military chain of command than a network of highly autonomous elite actors whose participation must often be induced, requested, or worked around.

### Metal Bat's little sister is real competing salience

Metal Bat says:

> `こっちはわざわざ妹の大事なピアノ演奏会を抜け出して来たんだ`

and threatens to destroy headquarters if the meeting proves trivial.

The joke structure should not obscure the character evidence. A little sister's piano recital is important enough to him that an S-class planetary summons is evaluated partly against what it made him leave. That gives him a high-value noncombat attachment before the manga supplies a broad domestic model.

### Child Emperor is simultaneously an elite analyst and a child with school

When the prophecy first sounds vague, Child Emperor says:

> `なにそれ くだらないなぁ ボク塾あるから帰っていいかな?`

This is not evidence of stupidity. Later in the same crisis he performs some of the clearest tactical role partitioning among the heroes. V06 instead establishes **dual state**:

- cognitively elite enough to operate among S-class;
- socially/developmentally still a child whose schedule contains cram school.

Future reconstruction should preserve both rather than turning him into a miniature adult.

### Shibabawa's prediction is specific in wording but vague in mechanism

Bang says Shibabawa's predictions were completely accurate. Sicchi distinguishes her final note from earlier earthquake, flood, and monster forecasts because she had never used the word `ヤバい` before.

The note itself is only:

> `地球がヤバい`

and the Association concludes that something worse than their previous disaster expectations may occur within six months.

**Boundary control:** V06 does not prove that the alien attack exhausts, fulfills, or is identical to the full meaning of Shibabawa's prophecy. The attack's immediate timing makes the association natural, but the exact referent remains open.

### Saitama's response to existential danger is interest

After hearing the threat, Saitama says in substance that coming was worthwhile: `来て良かった`.

This strongly supports the V01 reciprocity/engagement model. Where ordinary danger often fails to activate fear, the possibility of a threat beyond known scale produces interest. The correct inference is not that Saitama wants civilians endangered. It is that **the prospect of genuine resistance is affectively salient to someone deprived of challenge**.

The attack beginning almost immediately converts speculative excitement into real catastrophe before the meeting can formulate a plan.

## 4.3 32撃目 `宇宙からの…` — A-City disappears and the heroes become a coalition of partial solutions

### Catastrophe outpaces the institution

A-City is annihilated almost instantly by the alien ship's bombardment. Association headquarters survives largely because its construction had been entrusted to Metal Knight and made dramatically stronger than ordinary structures.

The sequence continues V03-V05's information-architecture theme at a new scale:

- the Association successfully gathered elite responders;
- it possessed a warning horizon measured in months;
- the attack still arrives before meaningful countermeasure planning;
- institutional survival depends on unusually robust physical infrastructure rather than predictive mastery.

### The Sky King establishes alien-force discontinuity

The Sky King and aerial followers arrive as an already-existing nonhuman threat and are destroyed almost immediately by the alien ship. This is useful comparative evidence without reducing the scene to a numeric power ladder: the invaders can eliminate an indigenous aerial threat with negligible interruption.

### Iaian makes failure cognitively useful

Iaian repeatedly uses his sword against Melzargard, loses an arm, and realizes ordinary lethal sword damage is being nullified by regeneration:

> `必殺の居合いを何太刀浴びせても倒れん`
>
> `不死身かこいつは…`
>
> `こんな事は初めてだ`

When Atomic Samurai arrives, Iaian does not protect his pride by pretending the problem is solved. He warns his master that slashing has not worked and even urges retreat rather than making himself the priority.

This is a strong bounded model of a disciple who can:

`observe -> update -> communicate failure -> prioritize information over face`.

### Atomic Samurai / Iaian establishes a real mentor relationship

Atomic's first concern is `イアイ 無事だったか`. Iaian addresses him as `師匠` and gives battlefield information. The relationship is not yet broad enough for a mature model, but it is sufficiently explicit to enter the relationship ledger as an emerging directional state.

### Saitama does not wait for the coalition

While the S-class discuss how to identify and reach the enemy, Genos notices Saitama has already left and boarded the ship.

His action is self-directed rather than commanded. He identifies the giant vessel as the platform responsible for destroying A-City and attacks the source directly.

Even inside the catastrophe, mundane embodiment remains present: he remarks that a beam weapon would be worse than physical projectiles because it could burn his clothes. The line is comic but valuable—world-threatening violence does not erase his ordinary material concerns.

### Ground heroes cooperate without becoming harmonious

Atomic Samurai, Bang, Metal Bat, Puri-Puri Prisoner, and others converge on Melzargard. Their styles are incompatible, but the external threat produces enough temporary alignment for useful coordination.

This is the best current model of S-class collective action:

> **coordination under common threat without interpersonal or ideological unity.**

### Puri-Puri Prisoner's moral contradiction remains intact

Puri says he will avenge the men killed by the enemy and describes putting killing intent into each blow. He is genuinely participating in defense. V04's evidence of his predatory criminal conduct remains equally canonical.

The correct longitudinal rule is not to resolve the contradiction by choosing one label. He is both an emergency responder and a repeat sexual predator at this boundary.

### Child Emperor performs tactical decomposition

Child Emperor distinguishes the ground target from the ship and identifies the latter as the harder strategic problem because most heroes cannot reach it.

This sharply qualifies the earlier `塾あるから帰っていいかな?` beat. His child state and analytical state coexist.

### King's statement remains ambiguous

Asked what he can do against the huge airborne ship, King says:

> `俺は何もできん`

At V06 this cannot safely be converted into any later global explanation. In context it can mean that he has no applicable means to attack the vessel. Preserve it as **situational incapacity statement under enormous reputation**, not as a resolved identity reveal.

### Genos offers cooperation and then tolerates rejection

Tatsumaki declares she will deal with the ship. Genos says:

> `待て 俺も行く`

and offers to try attacking from the ground even though success is uncertain. Tatsumaki refuses help. Genos privately concludes that he does not need to socialize with the S-class and redirects attention to the fact that Saitama has already boarded the ship.

The sequence prevents a false global inference from his irritated internal line. He **did** offer cooperation first. The more precise model is:

`shared external threat -> cooperation attempt`; `contemptuous rejection -> disengagement rather than status contest`.

### Genos's trust in Saitama approaches an existential axiom

Genos thinks:

> `先生に倒せぬ悪などない`
>
> `もし先生でも勝てない悪が存在したなら……人類などとうに滅んでいるだろう`

This is among the strongest relationship/epistemic lines in the first six volumes.

It strengthens attachment and confidence, but it must also be recorded as a **possible blind spot**. Genos is no longer merely saying Saitama is much stronger than observed alternatives. He is using Saitama as a premise in his model of existential threat.

Future reconstruction should therefore distinguish:

- evidence-grounded confidence produced by repeated observation;
- extrapolation beyond direct evidence;
- whether that extrapolation ever affects Genos's own decision quality.

V06 does not yet prove maladaptive consequence. It establishes the belief.

### Dark Matter's command structure is not omniscient

Inside the ship, Geryuganshoop knows Groribas has been killed and tries to contact Melzargard. A subordinate reports that within roughly four minutes a single intruder has caused 23% ship damage and killed interceptors in seconds.

Boros initially orders the top combatants to eliminate the intruder without yet knowing Groribas is already dead; the correction arrives afterward.

This is a useful organizational counterpoint to the Hero Association. The alien force has centralized command, telepathic communication, weapons infrastructure, and elite combatants, but **information latency and compartmentalization still exist**.

### Melzargard is a distributed person/body system

Melzargard's heads speak to one another, divide tasks, relay ship information, and later refer to one destroyed head as a companion of more than a hundred years. The body is therefore not adequately modeled as a single ordinary consciousness with cosmetic heads.

At V06 the safe ontology is:

`one regenerative combat body + multiple voiced/cognitively distinct head units + internal coordination + vulnerable embedded spheres`.

Do not force later biological explanation beyond what the pages establish.

## 4.4 33撃目 `話を聞かない男たち` — stubbornness, adversarial inference, and discovering a rule by refusing retreat

### Saitama turns enemy guidance into navigation data

Lost inside the ship, Saitama receives Geryuganshoop's telepathic order to leave. He asks for directions. Once Gery gives him an exit route, Saitama deliberately chooses the opposite direction and watches the enemy panic:

> `じゃあ左に進もうっと`

The tactic is simple and highly diagnostic. Saitama is often inattentive to convention, media, and status signals, but he can perform rapid **adversarial social inference**:

`enemy urgently wants me to go direction X -> direction not-X probably protects something important`.

This strengthens the V05 correction against global “social obliviousness.” His cognition is selective, not absent.

### The title describes more than one kind of non-listening

On the ground, Iaian proposes withdrawing to reconsider because direct physical attacks appear ineffective. The senior fighters largely reject retreat.

Metal Bat decides to keep tearing the enemy apart. Atomic Samurai asks, effectively, whether Iaian does not trust his sword and asserts there is nothing he cannot cut. Bang continues with the tools he has.

The manga plays their stubborn confidence for comedy, but the scene produces two simultaneous truths:

1. Iaian's caution is rational based on current evidence;
2. continued physical pressure accidentally exposes the hidden spheres that make Melzargard vulnerable.

This is not a general endorsement of refusing advice. It is a case where persistence plus observation discovers a rule that pure retreat would not have revealed immediately.

### Metal Bat discovers the sphere through concrete attention

When a sphere drops from the damaged body, Metal Bat notices the incongruous object, calls it a `ビー玉?`, and destroys it. One of Melzargard's heads dies.

Bang immediately infers the broader rule: spheres somewhere inside the body are the weak points. Atomic Samurai converts that into a new attack policy—keep cutting until the vulnerable cores are exposed.

The episode therefore contains a clean team-learning sequence:

`brute-force persistence -> anomalous object noticed -> test/destruction -> enemy state change -> rule inference -> shared tactical update`.

Metal Bat's value here is not abstract intelligence scoring. He is concretely attentive enough to exploit an unexpected battlefield object while continuing high-intensity combat.

### Alien coordination responds in parallel

Melzargard's internal heads exchange information with Geryuganshoop while fighting. One head learns that more than half the ship's combatants have been killed and is told concentrated bombardment is imminent.

The enemy is also adapting. V06's battle structure is therefore less “heroes hit until win” than two distributed organizations updating partial information under time pressure.

## 4.5 34撃目 `馬鹿かお前` — psychic spectacle, Boros as experiential mirror, and the ethical boundary of boredom

### Geryuganshoop mistakes spectacle for decisive difference

Geryuganshoop uses telekinesis to accelerate debris and assumes Saitama has been defeated. Saitama emerges and dismisses the display as using superpower merely to throw small stones:

> `念動力? 超能力か……`
>
> `小石飛ばすだけか……超能力の無駄遣いだぜ`

He then returns a projectile by ordinary physical flicking and kills Geryuganshoop.

This scene should not be generalized into a theory that psychic power is weak. Tatsumaki's parallel battlefield action demonstrates the opposite. The chapter's point is local: **technique category does not guarantee meaningful effect against Saitama, and spectacular framing can be defeated by crude mechanics when the capability gap is overwhelming.**

### Boros recognizes Saitama as extraordinary before demanding submission

Boros praises Saitama for eliminating his subordinates and introduces himself as leader of the Dark Matter thieves and conqueror of the universe. Saitama begins with an old self-description and corrects himself:

> `俺は趣味で…じゃなくてプロのヒーローをやってるサイタマという者だ`

This tiny self-repair is one of the best identity-transition lines in the early corpus. `趣味でヒーロー` remains cognitively habitual, but institutional professionalization has become real enough that he consciously corrects the label.

The hero identity is therefore not replaced. It has acquired an additional professional state.

### Saitama's moral accusation is concrete

Saitama tells Boros that whatever brought him to Earth, the destruction of an entire city means he cannot simply be allowed to leave.

This is important after V04-V05's complicated public-responsibility material. Against a clear aggressor, Saitama's attribution is direct. He does not require institutional authorization, rank jurisdiction, or public opinion to decide that mass destructive aggression warrants intervention.

### Boros explains the same kind of deprivation Saitama knows

Boros recounts a prophecy made twenty years earlier: on a distant planet there would be someone capable of fighting him evenly and restoring stimulation to his life. He crossed the universe to find that opponent.

He says, in substance, that he believed because he came for something that would give his life stimulation.

This is the strongest early mirror to Saitama's own problem:

- extraordinary power reduces ordinary opponents' ability to produce reciprocal challenge;
- life becomes experientially impoverished;
- the possibility of a worthy opponent becomes unusually salient.

But Saitama's response defines the ethical difference:

> `馬鹿か お前`
>
> `退屈な人生に刺激が欲しくて他の星を襲うなんてOLでも考えねぇぞ`

The comic `OL` comparison does not erase the moral content. Saitama understands boredom and still treats **victimizing strangers to solve it** as absurd.

### Current boredom ethic

Through V06:

`lack of meaningful resistance` can explain a desire for stimulation;

but

`desire for stimulation` does **not** justify coercing or destroying others to provide it.

This is a major refinement of the Saitama model. His own challenge-seeking remains bounded by a non-predatory default.

### Boros's armor and first real acknowledgment of strength

Saitama's blow breaks the armor Boros says was sealing his excessive power. Boros releases more energy and expresses satisfaction that Saitama has pushed him this far.

Saitama answers:

> `ああ 強いよ お前は`

At the V06 boundary this proves only that Saitama recognizes Boros as strong relative to prior experience. The volume ends before the fight is resolved. Do not import later judgments about how satisfying, serious, or close the complete battle becomes.

### Tatsumaki turns the ship's own artillery back on it

On the ground, the alien vessel launches concentrated bombardment against the heroes. Tatsumaki arrests the shells in the air and says:

> `砲弾お返しするわ`

before sending them back into the ship.

This materially broadens her model:

- she can rapidly recognize and reverse a large-scale threat;
- her confidence is backed by decisive performance, not merely status rhetoric;
- she works in proximity to other S-class without becoming collaborative in interpersonal style.

Her V06 state is therefore better described as **high-autonomy battlefield protection** than simple arrogance.

# 5. 番外編 `鮭` — Saitama cannot fix a life, but he can remain available

`鮭` is one of the most important low-stakes Saitama artifacts in the first checkpoint precisely because it removes the usual action-series solution.

### The stranger's problems are not punchable

A salaryman stands on a building preparing to jump after restructuring, family separation, and cumulative loss. Saitama is already on the roof eating lunch.

The man's first assumption is that a hero has been dispatched to stop him. Saitama corrects it: he was there first, eating.

The bluntness is deliberately anti-ceremonial. Saitama does not instantly transform into a counselor persona simply because the situation is socially coded as one requiring solemnity.

### Saitama explicitly acknowledges limits

The key line is:

> `確かに俺がオッサンを止める事は出来ないな`
>
> `リストラも家庭問題も解決できねぇし`
>
> `そもそも飛び降りるのはオッサンの自由だ`

This is not a claim that death is desirable. It is a claim about **authority and competence**. Saitama cannot honestly promise to restore the causes of the man's despair, and he does not claim ownership of the man's choice.

That gives the character model an important noncombat rule:

> Saitama may refuse to manufacture control where he lacks causal competence.

### Physical rescue remains immediate

When the man actually slips/falls, Saitama catches him by the tie. The action happens without a moral speech.

The contrast matters:

`I cannot solve your life / I do not own your choices` does not become `I will passively watch your body fall`.

Saitama distinguishes between respecting another person's agency and responding to immediate physical danger.

### The man discovers his own ambivalence

After being caught, the man thinks that he reflexively thanked Saitama and that, in truth, he had not possessed the courage to jump. He begins to wonder whether the public act itself contained a wish to be seen or helped.

The manga therefore does not retroactively make Saitama's words a cure. The stranger's own interpretation changes through the encounter.

### Saitama offers availability in his own register

Saitama tells him:

> `オッサンのスキにすればいいけどさ`
>
> `また飛び降りたくなったら俺がメシ食ってるビルからにしろよ`

This is an unusually precise Saitama care pattern:

- no sentimental self-display;
- no promise to fix everything;
- explicit respect for agency;
- practical willingness to be physically present again;
- concern disguised as casual logistics.

The line should not be universalized into a mental-health doctrine. It is character evidence for **indirect availability**.

### Self-authorship echoes the main volume

The salaryman eventually leaves the roof and wonders whether he can live in a more self-directed way. Saitama observes that he seems better and immediately returns to hero work when a monster appears.

This gives `鮭` a structural relationship to the main chapters. Boros solves boredom by making other worlds pay for it. The salaryman initially experiences his life as something done to him by company, family, and circumstance. Saitama's intervention is not a theory lecture; it is a lived example of self-authored action without demanding that others become instruments for it.

# 6. Character-state propagation

## Saitama — remains `strong`, major within-tier strengthening

### STRENGTHEN — engagement deficit / challenge salience
The prospect of an unprecedented planetary threat produces explicit interest (`来て良かった`). Boros then becomes the first opponent in the collected sequence whom Saitama directly calls `強い` at the current boundary.

### NEW — boredom ethics

# 8. Thematic propagation

## 8.1 Heroism / recognition / institution

V06 adds the S-class as a **heterogeneous emergency coalition**, not a unified moral order. The Association's secrecy procedure combines a genuine informed exit with post-disclosure coercion. Elite status does not guarantee attendance, common motive, common manner, or common judgment.

## 8.2 Monsterhood / personhood / body

The alien invaders introduce another hostile category that should not automatically be collapsed into the existing `怪人` taxonomy. Melzargard additionally provides a distributed-consciousness body case: multiple voiced head units share one regenerative body and vulnerable embedded spheres.

## 8.3 Power / technique / cosmic systems

V06 adds:

- large-scale psychic manipulation from Tatsumaki;
- alien telepathy/telekinesis from Geryuganshoop;
- Boros's sealed/released power state;
- Melzargard core-based regeneration;
- ship bombardment and power-sphere infrastructure.

None of this establishes later `limiter`, `God`, or broader cosmic mechanics. Keep those closed.

## 8.4 Technology / organization / hidden actors

The Hero Association and Dark Matter ship become parallel information/command systems with different failure modes. HA cannot guarantee elite attendance and receives vague prophecy. Dark Matter has central command and telepathic communication but suffers combatant attrition and information lag about Groribas.

## 8.5 Satire / genre / public narrative

The volume repeatedly punctures epic status with ordinary life:

- a planetary briefing competes with Metal Bat's sister's piano recital and Child Emperor's cram school;
- Saitama worries about clothes and gets lost in the alien ship;
- the universe's conqueror is briefly heard by Saitama as a `歯医者`-like term;
- the volume's bonus leaves cosmic invasion for salmon lunch and employment despair.

The satire does not nullify seriousness. It insists that cosmic and mundane realities coexist.

## 8.6 Visual form

V06 sharply expands spatial scale:

- A-City disappears under an aerial vessel whose full body exceeds ordinary panel comprehension;
- tiny human figures are repeatedly placed against the ship to make institutional power physically small;
- the interior maze becomes a literal navigation/comprehension problem;
- Tatsumaki's arrested artillery changes downward vector into upward retaliation;
- Boros/Saitama face-offs use increasingly stripped backgrounds to isolate experiential confrontation;
- `鮭` returns to ordinary roofs, crowds, food, elevators, and street scale.

The volume's formal movement is therefore `city -> planet -> ship interior -> duel -> rooftop lunch`, a deliberate compression from cosmic threat back into ordinary human vulnerability.

## 8.7 Open questions

- Does the alien invasion fully satisfy Shibabawa's `地球がヤバい` prediction, or only appear to?
- What is the relation, if any, between Shibabawa's prophecy and the alien seer's prophecy to Boros? **OPEN; do not equate.**
- What is Garou's present state after expulsion? Only Bang's historical account exists.
- Why are Blast and Metal Knight unavailable, and what does absence mean institutionally?
- How far does Genos's Saitama axiom generalize, and can it become a decision blind spot?
- What are Tatsumaki's practical limits beyond the feats observed here?
- What does `俺は何もできん` mean for King beyond this particular airborne problem? **OPEN.**
- How much challenge will Boros actually provide Saitama? V06 ends before resolution.
- What precisely are the alien species/personhood categories relative to `怪人`?

# 12. V06 final synthesis

V06 does not merely escalate One Punch Man from city threats to aliens. It reveals what escalation does **not** unify.

The S-class do not become one heroic personality. They become a temporary coalition. The Association does not become omniscient because it possesses prophecy. It becomes an institution trying to govern uncertainty. Saitama does not become morally equivalent to Boros because both suffer from insufficient challenge. Their shared deprivation makes their ethical divergence clearer. And Saitama's physical omnipotence does not become social or existential omnipotence: on the rooftop in `鮭`, he openly admits that another person's job and family crisis are beyond what he can repair.

The most important early-series Saitama model after six volumes is therefore no longer “a bored overpowered hero.” It is closer to:

> **a materially ordinary, socially selective, internally authorized person whose overwhelming physical capacity has deprived combat of reciprocity, but whose conduct remains constrained by non-predatory ethics, situational concern, practical restraint, and an increasing willingness to participate in relationships and institutions without letting them define his reasons for acting.**

V06 gives that model its first explicit negative mirror in Boros and its first noncombat limit case in the salaryman.
