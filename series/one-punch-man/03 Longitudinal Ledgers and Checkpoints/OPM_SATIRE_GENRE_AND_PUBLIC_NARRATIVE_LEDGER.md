# One Punch Man — Satire, Genre, and Public Narrative Ledger


## Governing rule


Record both the **target** of a joke and what the manga continues to value sincerely. Comic puncture does not automatically negate the underlying theme.


## V01 baseline


- catastrophic/villain grandeur meets `趣味でヒーロー` understatement;
- elaborate monster anatomy meets simplified Saitama;
- conventional escalation is punctured by one-punch resolution;
- Subterranean dream shows the series can sincerely desire battle intensity even while mocking battle structure;
- mosquito gag relocates difficulty from apocalypse to mundane frustration.


## V02 additions


### Ordinary training as impossible secret
The regimen is comic because it is familiar exercise presented as the cause of impossible power. Saitama's discipline and self-change philosophy remain sincere.


### Supermarket sale
Carnage Kabuto supplies maximal engineered spectacle; a consumer sale supplies Saitama's meaningful deadline. Target: shonen threat hierarchy as measure of subjective stakes.


### Hammerhead / Paradisers
Political-economic critique is attached to a leader whose private motive finally collapses to not wanting to work. Target: grand collective rhetoric used to dignify self-serving coercion. Do not infer that every stated labor critique is thereby textually refuted.


### Mumen Rider
The costume/name can be comic while his willingness to face a superior threat is treated as real. Early example of satire coexisting with admiration.


### Sonic
Ninja technique, speed lines, and professional self-mythology culminate in accidental bodily slapstick. Saitama's `頑張れよ` refuses to enter the rivalry at Sonic's dramatic register.


### Hero Association
Heroism becomes tests, registry, rankings, salary, popularity, donors, and fan clubs. Target is not necessarily the existence of coordination itself but the absurdity introduced when moral action becomes a public classification/celebrity system.


### `じぶんみがき`
A sincere training/identity story closes with a literal tooth/brushing gag: `磨かなければ すぐ腐る`. Self-improvement and anti-solemn comedy remain simultaneous.


## Public narrative state


V02 establishes that television/crowd categories can overwrite actual behavior:
- bald Paradisers cause Saitama to fear villain misidentification;
- registered Mumen Rider is immediately recognized;
- unregistered Saitama's actions remain socially uncredited;
- Hero Registry makes public narrative structurally dependent on institutional classification.


## V03 additions


### Perfect physical test / bottom class
Target: confidence that formal composite scoring automatically captures relevant heroic capability. The institution sees the impossible physical score and still produces C-class placement.


### Professional ethics / status self-interest
Sneck lectures new heroes about discipline and then attacks Saitama through `新人潰し`; Tanktop Tiger mixes legitimate collective-reputation concern with rank assertion. Hero rhetoric and motive are deliberately non-identical.


### One-sided rivalry as employment
Sonic experiences a defining rematch; Saitama ends the confrontation wondering whether it counts as work. Genre rivalry is subordinated to C-class quota anxiety.


### Ghost-town rumor and kombu dinner
A-class investigators lose to a monster; Saitama resolves it unrecorded and cooks the body. Public threat hierarchy collapses into household food.


### `夏`
A disaster shelter capable of withstanding extreme external danger becomes intolerable because of mundane bodily need. Professional responders then discover they were confronting a discarded shell while the actual adult monster exists elsewhere.


### `吹き込む新風`
A salaryman escapes company life by becoming a hero and discovers faction/reward politics that feel like a company again. Saitama's anti-limit intervention is followed immediately by supermarket-sale priority. The joke punctures solemnity without negating the recruit's genuine change.


### Public narrative state through V03
Public and institutional knowledge can lag behind actual capability in structurally predictable ways. Rumor is not merely false information: it can attract both investigators and monsters, thereby changing the environment it describes.

## V04 additions

### Prevented catastrophe is narratively invisible
After the meteor, civilians experience visible debris and ruined homes rather than the counterfactual direct impact Saitama prevented. Public causality attaches to what remains visible.

### Scapegoating as hero competition
Tanktop Tiger/Black Hole exploit that asymmetry to protect status, converting disaster narrative into a rank weapon.

### Performance does not map cleanly to authenticity
Stinger is showy and still courageous. Bang is understated and responsible. Metal Knight acts but from experimental motive. V04 rejects a simple spectacle=fake / humility=true-hero binary.

### Institutional labels do not summarize persons
Puri-Puri Prisoner is S-class and criminal; Saitama is decisive rescuer and public target; Sonic is prisoner and extraordinarily skilled. Satire increasingly targets systems that seek one clean label for morally/causally multidimensional people.

## V05 additions

### Physical climax displaced by reputational climax
Deep Sea King dies instantly once Saitama arrives, but the chapter continues into the harder social conflict: whether effortless victory will erase the value of everyone who lost before him. The ethical climax occurs after the battle climax.

### Saitama performs downward
Instead of advertising heroism, Saitama deliberately performs an uglier public self — lucky, late, opportunistic, credit-stealing — to preserve the defeated heroes' social standing. Satire reverses the usual superhero publicity problem: the strongest hero must make himself look worse to protect the meaning of weaker heroism.

### Crowd judgment is not uniformly stupid
The crowd's cheering for Mumen Rider recognizes courage despite an obvious power gap. Later, one cynical interpretation destabilizes credit. Public narrative is socially contagious but morally heterogeneous.

### Professional heroism becomes wage labor without fully becoming ordinary labor
The bonus reveals monthly rank-linked pay, opaque points, self-reporting, witness inputs, and low C-class income. Heroism is institutionalized enough to recreate wage scarcity and evaluation bureaucracy while still claiming charity/justice rather than ordinary employment as its principle.

### `買えないモノ`
The title's joke is structural: Saitama can buy/obtain overwhelming physical solutions but cannot casually buy a cheap desired drink. The sequence grounds cosmic-power satire in ordinary scarcity.

## V06 additions

### The super-team refuses to become one archetype
The S-class assembly visually/narratively resembles an elite superhero-team convention but is populated by incompatible speech styles, priorities, ages, family obligations, status attitudes, and motives. Genre grandeur is preserved while team uniformity is punctured.

### Planetary prophecy becomes meeting procedure
`地球がヤバい` is routed through schedules, disclosure rules, opt-out, secrecy, and attendance problems. The satire targets institutional mediation without making the threat unreal.

### Saitama / Boros — genre mirror without moral symmetry
The conventional “worthy rival” setup is real, but the manga makes the ethical premise explicit: boredom can explain why Boros wants a challenge without excusing conquest. Saitama's blunt `馬鹿かお前` punctures antagonist grandeur while preserving the seriousness of the moral distinction.

### Cosmic scale -> salmon rooftop
After city annihilation, alien command, psychic bombardment, and Boros's conquest narrative, `鮭` relocates heroism to a rooftop lunch and a stranger's job/family despair. The joke is not that social suffering is trivial. It demonstrates that the physically strongest hero lacks total social/existential competence.

### Saitama's anti-sermon care
The bonus subverts motivational-hero rhetoric. Saitama does not produce a grand life lesson; he admits limits, catches the man, and offers an odd practical route to future contact. Comic awkwardness and sincere care coexist.

### Public narrative status
V06 is less focused on crowds than V04-V05. That absence matters: the alien crisis temporarily shifts emphasis from public attribution to elite coordination and hidden information, leaving prior recognition questions unresolved rather than superseded.

## V07 boundary update

### Cosmic spectacle -> pawnshop value
The moon battle leaves Saitama with lunar material; once Genos identifies its resale value, the impossible cosmic artifact becomes an ordinary money problem. This is a concentrated example of OPM's scale-compression satire: extraordinary capability is returned to everyday material salience rather than preserved as prestige.

### `カツ丼` — publicity machinery versus service
McCoy tries to convert a police emergency into a televised Hero Association superiority demonstration. Special incentives and media arrival create a performative rescue economy. Saitama resolves the threat before that spectacle and leaves, causing the visible narrative to credit police instead.

The satire is not “media always lies.” Media reports the evidence available to it. The distortion is produced by incomplete causal visibility and strategic staging.

### Amai / Metal Bat omake
The same people who occupy severe justice/combat roles are immediately returned to managers, television work, family recitals, tears, and autograph requests. Character contradiction is used as anti-archetype structure rather than a reset.

### Post-catastrophe institutional comedy with real consequence
A-City's destruction becomes a giant reconstruction/centralization project completed with absurd speed. The joke about construction scale also materially changes the world-state; comedy and institutional consequence are not separate layers.

## V08 boundary update — the strongest man is real only as a public story

### King — reputation becomes self-reinforcing explanatory infrastructure
King's record is causally false, but public belief is strong enough to reinterpret his behavior and attract threats. The transport omake repeats the loop when a captured monster attributes defeat to S-class King.

**Satirical mechanism:** prior reputation narrows later interpretation, and later interpretation feeds prior reputation.

### Private mastery / public mastery inversion
King is not globally incompetent: he is genuinely excellent at games. The series assigns authentic mastery to a socially low-prestige private domain while assigning false mastery to the public hero domain.

### Saitama/King reveal refuses expected moral melodrama
After an enormous fraud/reputation revelation, Saitama's endpoint is future game visits. The anti-climax does not erase the ethical issue; it reallocates attention from prestige punishment to role mismatch and ordinary relationship.

### `迷い猫` scale inversion
A B-class hero capable of absurd force is professionally occupied by a badly drawn lost-cat notice while a Demon-level creature crisis unfolds nearby. Hero labor cannot be ordered by spectacle alone.

### `海老` food seriousness
Dojo challengers become morally/comically salient to Saitama when they damage expensive lobster. Mundane attachment repeatedly hijacks genre seriousness without becoming fake character evidence.

## V09 update

- **Rank prestige -> local politics:** Fubuki turns a numerical rank into factional dominance, allowing the manga to criticize ranking culture through behavior rather than through a narrator declaring the system meaningless.
- **Hero naming -> public compression:** `ハゲマント` and `鬼サイボーグ` show institutional/public language reducing people to highly legible traits. The joke coexists with real reputational consequences.
- **Technique prestige -> mundane deflation:** Saitama's `マジ反復横跳び` is formally treated as overwhelming combat spectacle while remaining a school-exercise phrase.
- **Garou's `シナリオ` grievance — serious/satirical overlap:** his childhood complaint explicitly treats hero-monster conflict as a popularity script. The series gives that critique genuine emotional causality while immediately complicating it through adult Garou's violence toward people weaker than himself.
- **Luxury prestige -> practical inconvenience:** the Fubuki Group bonus lets expensive-car/status imagery collapse into traffic, money, work, and bounty logistics.

## V10 update — labels, rubrics, power numbers, and failed dramatic self-presentation

### Hero Hunter becomes “mugger”
Garou approaches Saitama carrying a dramatic antagonist identity; Saitama hears none of it and reads the encounter as street harassment interfering with shopping. The gag collapses villain self-mythology by refusing the intended genre frame.

### Super Fight wig
Saitama enters a prestige martial tournament under Charanko's identity using a visibly cheap wig. Battle-manga seriousness and mundane bureaucratic opportunism occupy the same page space.

### Tatsumaki allocation paradox
The Association complains of manpower shortage while its strongest psychic is bored because no one calls. The comedy targets allocation/communication and Tatsumaki's need to be indispensable at once.

### Hero-suit contest
The contest continues judging clothes even after Saitama acts heroically during the monster interruption. The institution is funny because its rubric remains internally coherent while obviously failing to capture the value the surrounding story cares about.

### `数字` and power-scaling satire
The manga supplies detailed scores and then engineers failures:
- a transformed monster jumps numerically;
- Fubuki scores `19` despite obvious psychic power;
- Saitama returns `測定不能` and is initially interpreted as weak;
- Fubuki's large faction loses a game contest to King's specialist competence.

The target is not quantification itself so much as **totalizing confidence in a narrow proxy**.

### `カロリー計算中`
Fubuki's self-authorship declaration is immediately followed by calorie counting. The joke resists a clean therapeutic conversion in which she has transcended all measurement; context determines whether a number is useful, neutral, or identity-distorting.

## V11 - team genre, hero prestige, and public-information satire

### Sentai grammar - NEW / STRENGTHEN
The extra begins with the clean genre image of differentiated heroes whose bonds and roles create combined power, then translates that logic into an awkward real Hero Association operation. The joke is not simply "teams are bad": specialization works until bad information and role mismatch overwhelm it.

### Hero-name deflation - STRENGTHEN
Saitama briefly enjoys the fantasy of color-coded team identities before the official name `Caped Baldy` punctures the performance. Public branding remains a comic mechanism that can be socially real while poorly representing the person.

### Tournament versus emergency - NEW juxtaposition
Controlled martial prestige and entertainment continue while the city network experiences simultaneous monster incidents. The bracketed spectacle and distributed emergency expose different ways society packages strength.

### King rumor - STRENGTHEN public-narrative autonomy
The Association interprets King's unavailability through his legendary public persona, while prior canon has established a very different private reality. Reputation continues to operate institutionally even when causal knowledge is weak.

## V12 update — spectacle, role, and King moral misattribution

### `試合と戦闘` — shared spectacle grammar, divergent stakes
V12 crosscuts the Super Fight tournament with Genos's lethal city combat. Speed lines, impact frames, audience anticipation, and named techniques can make the two spaces visually rhyme, while the consequences do not: one is regulated entertainment, the other emergency protection. Genre spectacle therefore cannot by itself identify moral stakes.

### `強い奴ら` — prestige deflation
The volume repeatedly punctures martial prestige through Saitama's mundane attention, including the toilet interruption. The joke does not erase Suiryu's genuine strength; it refuses to let arena ceremony automatically dictate Saitama's emotional scale.

### Suiryu — physical victory is not argumentative proof
Suiryu defeats Snek while arguing that weak creatures are naturally eliminated and that temporary rescue does not change the world. The manga demonstrates his physical superiority but does not convert the win into narrator endorsement of the philosophy.

### King extra — reputation expands from combat myth to moral myth
`キングの休日なようで平日` extends the King mechanism substantially. Narration says his existence itself functions as `犯罪抑止力`. Professional assassins interpret mundane motions as anti-assassination tactics and his refusal to fight them as extraordinary mercy, concluding `戦わずして完敗した`.

King's own interior correction—`ちょっと言い方きつかったかな`—shows a much smaller ordinary concern than the grand moral character attributed to him. The public myth can therefore manufacture **virtue and magnanimity**, not only combat feats.

The extra also records genuine competence/interest that should not be swallowed by the joke: King is writing the script for a self-produced game (`自主制作ゲームの脚本作り`).

### Saitama as control case
Saitama later physically handles hostile men with low ceremony while King remains the mythic interpretive center. Actual intervention and attributed intervention can occupy different people.

### Watchdog Man — accurate reputation control
Q-City citizens move toward the city because Watchdog Man is there and call him a `守り神`; V12 simultaneously shows a large pile of monsters he has defeated. Unlike King, the public reputation has direct observed-capability support at this boundary. This prevents the series' reputation satire from becoming a blanket claim that public hero stories are always false.

## V13 update — attribution, official truth, and role-mediated reality

### Saitama's face — micro-attribution
Tournament spectators interpret Saitama's ordinary blank face as a goofy expression hiding tension; Saitama internally notes that it is simply his normal expression. Ambiguous cue + expected narrative again generates confident false psychology.

### Wig defense — causal misattribution through visual form
Suiryu interprets Saitama guarding his head as combat defense. The actual cause is preservation of the Charanko wig. Observable behavior can be accurately seen while its cause is confidently misread.

### Choze — prestige translated into corn
Saitama reduces elite-lineage rhetoric to `トウモロコシの品種改良`. The satire attacks grandiose framing without establishing a narrator-level scientific claim that inherited traits never matter.

### Official champion versus private defeat
Suiryu remains the legitimate tournament champion after Saitama's disqualification, while privately knowing `負けた……初めて…`. OPM again distinguishes an institutionally valid label from exhaustive reality.

### Amai Mask concert — public frame absorbs lethal reality
Fans initially read the monster attack as stagecraft and, after Amai returns smiling, conclude the violence must have been part of the show. A prior role narrative can assimilate incompatible real evidence when the performer supplies continuity cues.

### Amai Mask staff — explicit partial-knowledge warning
Staff say they know only his `アイドルの部分`; the hero side is `近寄りがたい`. The manga directly warns against treating occupational proximity as total-person knowledge.

### Public image as material social force
Because Monster Association attackers explicitly value the reputational damage of killing Amai in front of fans, constructed image produces real strategic consequences. “Narrative” is not the opposite of material effect.

## V14 update — publicity, tournament reality, and `foul` power

### Monster Association `publicity` — STRENGTHEN institutional satire
Calling synchronized terror attacks `宣伝活動` converts marketing language into violent threat display. The joke does not make the operation unserious; bureaucratic/promotional vocabulary is being used to organize mass fear.

### Tournament championship — STRENGTHEN official-reality split
Suiryu retains the public championship while privately knowing Saitama defeated him. Formal record, audience narrative, and experienced reality continue to diverge without making the formal event nonexistent.

### `反則` — STRENGTHEN double-use genre language
The chapter title links literal tournament rule-breaking with Suiryu's description of Gouketsu-level strength as effectively unfair. The series uses sports-rule language to make extreme battle-manga asymmetry intuitively legible.

### Saitama/Gouketsu anti-spectacle — STRENGTHEN genre inversion
After an entire volume builds Gouketsu through fear, conversion authority, hierarchy, and Suiryu's warning, the decisive fight is withheld. Shockwaves and the returned head replace a climactic exchange. The formal absence is itself the gag and the genre argument.

### Tournament secrecy coda — PRESERVE mundane deflation
Immediately after the rescue and off-panel Dragon-level victory, Saitama worries about his Charanko impersonation becoming known. Social/administrative embarrassment punctures battle escalation without erasing the rescue stakes.

## V15 update — model failure as comedy

- King's game analogy parodies battle-manga “leveling” while seriously proposing non-level growth.
- King's `ハメ技` lets the reputed strongest physical actor lose inside a rule system where physical power is irrelevant.
- Sonic's transhuman shortcut collapses into ordinary cooking and gastrointestinal failure; mundane common sense interrupts grand transformation rhetoric.
- Armored Gorilla's grocery shopping punctures body-based antagonist assumptions.
- `災害レベル` satirizes rigid power scaling while explicitly preserving the utility of approximate/revisable classification.
- Saitama's monster-corpse aftermath becomes a roasted-sweet-potato gag, linking civic cleanup and consumer routine.
- `目撃` is a reputation satire built from real evidence plus missing causality: King gains another feat precisely because observers saw the wrong part of the event.

**STRENGTHEN:** OPM's satire is not “all metrics are fake” or “all public belief is foolish.” It repeatedly shows useful local models becoming comic when treated as total accounts.

## V16 update — category satire under real competence

### Hero profile-card grammar versus field composition
V16 presents ranks and specialities in stat-card form, then makes the fight depend on interactions among roles. The joke/critique is not that the cards are false; it is that locally useful descriptors become inadequate as total performance models.

### Saitama/King 81-0
A battle-manga invincibility premise is moved into a fighting-game rule system where bodily power has no jurisdiction. King can honestly dominate Saitama and even manage match closeness. Satire and sincere domain competence coexist.

### `お手`
Genos answers Garou's Watchdog-derived movement with an ordinary dog command. The joke punctures Garou's self-mythologizing without negating the technique's tactical value.

### Death Gatling recognition loop
The hero who correctly diagnoses public/rank distortion tries to escape it by winning a prestige-rich fight legible through the same status system. V16 satirizes institutional internalization more than simple bureaucratic stupidity.

## V17 update -- role scripts and reputation as theater

### Hero play as antagonist-origin genre criticism
Garou's childhood grievance begins in literal hero play. The popular child is assigned the heroic role, the isolated child the monster role, and the expected victory is socially prewritten. V17 turns genre imitation into an origin mechanism without endorsing Garou's adult generalization.

### King
The strongest-man myth is still false as physical description, but King deliberately performs inside it to bait Elder Centipede. Public narrative can therefore be epistemically wrong and strategically useful at the same time.

### Saitama
Garou hunting and cabbage shopping share one practical outing. Epic plot and household routine remain formally co-present rather than hierarchical.

### Fubuki
A room full of relationships becomes, in her cognition, a difficult recruitment problem. The joke targets the conversion of social life into faction logic while preserving her genuine organizational competence.

### Sonic
A power-up transformation sequence ends with a two-kilogram weight-loss caption. V17 satirizes shortcut escalation and confirmation bias simultaneously.


## V18 update — reputation, mundane scale, and explanatory parody

### Epic/mundane compression — STRENGTHEN
V18 repeatedly collapses high genre stakes into ordinary material life: Garou's monster identity becomes a dine-and-dash problem; Saitama's post-crisis emergency is a 6,000-yen wallet and forgotten cabbage; Kuseno's premium beef changes Saitama's social register.

### Limiter exposition / baldness — STRENGTHEN genre double game
A serious in-world theory of biological growth limits is paired with Genus treating Saitama's baldness as a `代償`. The scene functions simultaneously as explanatory worldbuilding and battle-transformation parody.

### Reputation as imagined power — STRENGTHEN
Waganma and Tareo invoke hero names to stabilize rescue hope; a captive monster counters with cadre myths. King's name produces real fear despite false combat content. Public narrative is causally powerful even when its factual basis is weak.

### Private autograph — STRENGTHEN recognition comedy
Saitama signs Tareo's hero almanac without invitation and asks secrecy. The joke depends on Saitama wanting some legibility while remaining largely unrecognized.

## V19 satire update - cabbage, main-force myth, dog logic, and `Real Punch`

### Cabbage annihilation / hotpot combat grammar - STRENGTHEN
A chapter titled around annihilation applies battle-manga visual and verbal intensity to hotpot ingredients and chopstick competition while institutional war planning accelerates elsewhere. Mundane stakes and epic grammar remain deliberately interchangeable.

### King as `main force` - STRENGTHEN reputation satire
The strongest-man myth is no longer only a public joke or tactical bluff. Child Emperor imports it into formal raid planning. Satire becomes institutional risk: narrative reputation can be operationally actionable while factually false.

### Rover command gag - STRENGTHEN category mismatch
Garou tries ordinary dog-command logic on a Dragon-level monster. The gag depends on Rover being both legible as dog-like and catastrophically outside ordinary pet scale.

### `Real Punch` - major genre self-analysis
Saitama reads the progression grammar of battle manga and explicitly envies struggle, allies, rivals, training, techniques and uncertainty. Reality then supplies actual monsters that are too trivial to reproduce the fiction's process. The joke is not that Saitama dislikes battle manga; it is that his own premise deprives him of the genre experience he can recognize and desire.


## V20 satire / public-narrative delta

### King: reputational dirt becomes battle evidence — STRENGTHEN
King's dirty/bloodied appearance and absence are narrativized by others as proof of extraordinary solo action. Public myth again converts ambiguous surface evidence into a false causal story with real institutional consequences.

### Rhino Wrestler as power-scaling satire — NEW
A monster literally assigns point values to attacks and solicits ever higher scores. The gag works because numerical evaluation can be locally informative while still reflecting the evaluator's ego, chosen test and blind spots—an exaggerated mirror of both ranking institutions and battle-manga quantification.

### `所持アイテム` — STRENGTHEN embodied/toolkit comedy
The extra treats carried items as character ontology: weapons, support gear, clothing, reassurance objects and Futoshi's huge food supply all index how different heroes materially inhabit the job.

## V21 satire / genre delta

### `判定は？` — STRENGTHEN power-scaling satire
Rhino Wrestler turns combat evaluation into literal points. The sequence works because the numbers can register local differences while still reflecting the evaluator's chosen test and ego. V21 answers the score not with “numbers mean nothing” but with capability domains that exceed the score's authority.

### `修行不足だな` — transformation spectacle puncture
After Dragon-class transformation and pages of hyper-speed combat, Flash reduces the defeated duo's deficiency to “insufficient training.” The bathos preserves the reality of their upgrade while denying that escalation automatically equals mastery.

### `ランドセル` — NEW childhood/military-object collision
A school backpack becomes an arsenal, sensor suite and command platform. Child-coded gadgets, lollipops and toy-like names coexist with lethal rescue work, making Child Emperor's role contradiction formally comic and uncomfortable at once.

### `ドンカンバード` — low-status media knowledge becomes tactical evidence
A cancelled black-comedy mascot show supplies the clue to a Dragon-level resurrection hypothesis. Cultural prestige is again decoupled from practical usefulness.


## V22 satire / genre delta

### Giant-mecha climax defeated by tickling — NEW genre inversion
Brave Giant's spectacular reserve system is exhausted, yet the decisive solution is `コチョコチョ虫3号`: a tiny robot exploiting a costume opening and Phoenix Man's refusal to undress. The joke does not say “big weapons are useless”; it says correct mechanism knowledge can make a ridiculous small tool decisive.

### `中身` against costume escalation — STRENGTHEN role/image satire
Phoenix's increasingly magnificent transformed shell culminates in literal dependency on the costume. Child Emperor's “inside” formulation makes the battle a satire of equating spectacular presentation with authentic selfhood.

### Amai Mask public/private image cut — NEW
A nonhuman-looking damaged face is followed almost immediately by celebrity recognition, selfie/autograph requests and a restored public face. The sequence makes public heroic legibility an active visual performance rather than a transparent report of ontology.

### `コーヒー` — extraordinary body / ordinary logistics
Zombieman's horror-grade regeneration is closed by coffee-shop recognition and replacement-clothing logistics. The extra returns S-Class abnormality to mundane professional inconvenience.

## V23 satire / genre / public-narrative delta

### G5's `complete copy` becomes an online-shopping joke
Atomic answers the machine's mastery claim by treating a copy of himself as something that might be ordered online as a practice dummy. The joke targets boastful equivalence claims without denying that copied combat data is dangerous.

### Saitama enters the raid as a noise complaint
The surrounding plot is hostage rescue and Dragon-level warfare; Saitama's subjective entry point is repeated `ドッカンドッカン` noise. Genre scale and ordinary annoyance occupy the same causal register.

### PPP's `Angel` naming — spectacle over involuntary adaptation
PPP repeatedly gives grand affectionate names to new body states while Nyan supplies a colder damage/adaptation interpretation. Humor comes partly from the mismatch between mechanism and self-narration.

### Darkshine's insect ideal — bodybuilding myth meets disappointed calibration
Darkshine's grand ideal of insect strength culminates in the joke `想像以上に想像以下だった` and disappointment that Bug God is not even properly black-shining. The scene simultaneously admires and mocks physique mythology.

### `範` — public scoring versus invisible prosocial conduct
Amai compares hero evaluation to elite restaurant grading and emphasizes citizen-facing exemplarity. Pig God's subsequent secret payment occurs after the evaluator has left. The formal joke makes morally relevant behavior literally unavailable to the scoring system.

## V24 delta — prestige collapse and false narratives with real effects

- **Orochi/Saitama:** the manga sincerely renders Monster King grandeur, sacrifice mythology, and geological attack scale, then answers with a Serious-Series water-squirting technique. The satire targets prestige inflation without refusing spectacle.
- **Rover:** disaster-scale monsterhood is compressed into pet-management grammar because Saitama's asymmetry makes the mundane category survivable.
- **King:** public narrative is false as biography but real as causal environment; fear generated by reputation changes behavior.
- **`強一般人`:** the extra restores strength to ordinary exercise/comparison culture after apocalyptic escalation, preventing S-Class/monster scale from monopolizing the concept.

## V25 delta — epic systems reduced to ordinary classification problems

- **Free rent beneath apocalypse:** Saitama explains residence over the Monster Association through household economics, preserving ordinary-life scale inside catastrophic plot scale.
- **Mutual monster accusation:** Flash attacks on a contextual inference; Saitama then asks whether Flash is a monster because Flash attacked him. Epistemic comedy comes from confident categories colliding with behavior.
- **Nyan's “freedom”:** organizational exit becomes freedom to act on sadistic desire, undercutting any easy heroic rhetoric of autonomy without denying that the autonomy itself is real.
- **Food Battler:** body-design comedy and sincere rescue labor are simultaneous; fat folds/metabolism become actual survival infrastructure rather than making the character's heroism a joke.
- **`後頭部`:** battle-manga speed grammar is redirected toward bathroom vanity and hair-loss anxiety. Formal grandeur survives while the objective becomes aggressively ordinary.
- **Drive Knight outcome irony:** Nyan repeatedly calls others toys and is ultimately trapped after treating Drive Knight's visible form as a comprehensible toy/system.

## V26 delta — prestige, interpretation, and institutional purpose drift

- **S-Class reputation montage:** V26 preserves genuinely earned capability while showing that elite reputation remains incomplete and can coexist with compromised or misleading public narratives. The satire targets reputation treated as omniscience, not the proposition that every famous hero is fraudulent.
- **Bang's `おすわり`:** disaster-scale Rover is again compressed into dog-training grammar. Saitama supplies the earlier conditioning history; Bang supplies the current command, preserving both the joke and the causal distinction.
- **King and Tareo:** King's false combat biography remains causally real. Fear, sweat, and rigid posture are read outwardly as terrifying composure, letting public myth continue to change other people's behavior even when the biography underneath it is false.
- **Saitama / Flash / Manako:** Flash's working model of Saitama lags the reader's operational knowledge, while Saitama does little to narrate or advertise his own history. Institutional ignorance is therefore not produced by one foolish observer alone; missing self-report and fragmented witness structure help sustain it.
- **Garou / Darkshine:** Darkshine's finished-body rhetoric meets Garou's explicitly processual, pressure-driven growth. The battle genre's ideal of the completed powerful body is placed against a protagonist-antagonist body that keeps becoming something else under opposition.
- **`大抜擢`:** the Hero Association accurately identifies staff competence and then redirects that competence toward sponsor-facing, idolized publicity work. The joke is purpose drift: merit recognition can be locally correct while institutional incentives redirect what the recognized person is used for.
