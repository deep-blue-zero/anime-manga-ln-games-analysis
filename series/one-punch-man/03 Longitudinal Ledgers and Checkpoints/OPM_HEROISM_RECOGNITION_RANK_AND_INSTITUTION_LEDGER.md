# One Punch Man — Heroism, Recognition, Rank, and Institution Ledger


## Governing distinction


Track separately:


1. heroic behavior;
2. self-identification as hero;
3. institutionally registered/professional hero status;
4. public reputation/attribution;
5. rank/popularity;
6. moral worth.


Never treat these as synonyms unless the source makes them coincide.


## V01 baseline


### Saitama
- self-definition: `趣味でヒーローをやっている者だ` (`OPM|V01|chapter:1|image:0020`);
- hero identity predates any demonstrated institution and is grounded in personal choice/action;
- recognition is not established as the motive that created heroism;
- direct intervention does not require authorization.


### Structural state
V01 supplies hero behavior but no mature public/professional taxonomy. The project should therefore preserve the historical fact that Saitama's hero identity begins outside the later institutional category.

## V02 transition


### Mumen Rider: legitimacy without sufficient power


Civilians recognize his arrival as reassuring, and he declares `正義の自転車乗り 無免ライダー参上!` before confronting the Paradisers (`OPM|V02|chapter:12|images:0100-0101`). He is rapidly defeated.


**Ledger consequence:** public/institutional hero legitimacy and combat sufficiency are distinct variables.


### Saitama: power/heroic activity without public legitimacy


The Paradisers' bald appearance makes Saitama fear being treated as a villain despite his hero activity (`image:0106`). Sonic later says `お前など知らん`, and Saitama finally names `知名度が低い` as a serious problem (`images:0164, 0181-0183`).


**REVISE:** recognition is a real Saitama concern by V02, especially accurate identity, attribution, and social feedback. Do not retroactively make fame the origin of his hero project.


### Hero Association / Hero Registry


`image:0184` establishes:


- qualification testing;
- official permission to use registered hero status;
- professional hero occupation;
- donation-funded compensation tied to work;
- ability/performance ranking;
- popularity ranking;
- public attention/fan clubs;
- severe social discounting of unregistered self-declared heroes.


The Association therefore creates a **social ontology of heroism**, not merely administrative bookkeeping.


### Founding irony — IR, high confidence


Agoni is said to have founded the Association after his grandson was saved from a monster by a passing man around three years earlier (`image:0185`). V01's three-years-earlier Crablante story shows Saitama saving the distinctive child who triggered his hero decision.


At the V02 boundary the structure strongly invites:


> unregistered Saitama's heroic act helped create the institution that later renders unregistered Saitama socially invisible.


Keep this as `IR` until explicit confirmation if the text has not yet named the rescuer.


### Hobby -> work


Saitama responds to the registry information by proposing joint registration and explicitly offering Genos disciple status (`images:0186-0187`).


**OPEN:** professionalization may increase legibility while adding incentives/distortions of rank, popularity, and bureaucracy.

## V03 additions


### Measurement versus classification
- Genos scores 100 and enters S-class directly;
- Saitama nearly fails overall despite perfect/record-breaking physical performance;
- exam officials explicitly recognize the physical anomaly;
- institutional failure therefore occurs in **aggregation/classification**, not total inability to see evidence.


### Rank as incentive
- Sneck's `新人潰し` converts fear of being passed into aggression;
- Tanktop Tiger combines public-reputation norms with explicit C-class rank assertion;
- rank is now demonstrated as both coordination/status information and a source of peer competition.


### Professional survival
C-class inactivity rules force Saitama to seek visible/reportable work. Heroism acquires quota/sales logic that partially recreates the labor world Saitama abandoned.


### Public legibility
Tanktop Tiger is recognized by the crowd while Saitama is not. Public hero identity is institution-mediated rather than automatically generated by actual rescue history.


### City Z sensor limits
Association headquarters detects abnormal City Z patterns and dispatches A-class investigators. Golden Ball/Spring Mustachio are defeated; Saitama resolves Kombu Infinity outside institutional observation. Add distinction:


`real event -> witnessed/reported event -> institutional record -> rank/reputation consequence`.


### Factional topology
`吹き込む新風` explicitly states that factions exist inside the Hero Association and that the Fubuki Group largely controls B-class by scale. Reward distribution and conformity make an unnamed recruit think `これじゃ会社と何も変わらない`.


### Heroism as four non-identical states at V03
1. self-authored heroic identity/practice;
2. registered profession;
3. ranked institutional status;
4. publicly recognized symbolic role.


Future claims must specify which sense of `ヒーロー` is intended.

## V04 additions

### Meteor response separates rank from obligation
The City Z S-class summons produces only Bang and Genos at the branch. Elite classification does not compel response. Bang explicitly notes that impossible/troublesome summons can go unanswered.

### Contribution cannot be reduced to one rank variable
Saitama, Genos, Metal Knight, and Bang occupy different positions across presence, motive, cooperation, risk, direct effect, and collateral consequence. Post-event rank movement compresses those differences.

### Recognition model — REVISE
Saitama's V02 desire for fame/attribution remains valid, but `俺はテメェらの評価が欲しくてヒーローやってんじゃねぇ` establishes that public evaluation is not the motive authority for his heroism. Current rule: **desired social feedback != approval-dependent identity**.

### Public narrative as competitive weapon
Tanktop Tiger and Tanktop Black Hole exploit disaster victims' anger to suppress a rapidly rising peer hero. Public reputation is now an arena of intra-hero competition.

### Showmanship is not false heroism
Stinger's theatrical public persona coexists with real danger exposure and persistence. Do not map performance directly onto cowardice/fraud.

### Hero rank is not moral certification
Puri-Puri Prisoner is simultaneously S-class and a repeat imprisoned predator. Institutional hero classification records a role/capability function; it does not certify the whole person's morality or legality.

## V05 additions

### Failed resistance remains heroically legible
The Deep Sea King shelter sequence shows repeated heroes acting despite severe or obvious mismatch. Physical failure does not by itself negate courage, protective function, or public meaning.

### Mumen Rider formalizes duty without victory expectation
Mumen explicitly knows he is weak, knows he cannot win, and acts because `俺しかいないんだ` / `立ち向かわなくちゃいけない`. Add a fifth non-identical hero variable: **duty commitment under known insufficiency**.

### Recognition — REVISE / STRENGTHEN
Saitama deliberately sacrifices his own credit after the Deep Sea King fight so the crowd will not conclude that the defeated heroes were worthless. Recognition is therefore not only desired feedback; it is a social resource Saitama can redistribute at personal cost.

### Public narrative is heterogeneous
V04 showed scapegoating and causal inversion. V05 shows the crowd can also recognize Mumen Rider's courage despite knowing he is unlikely to win. Public judgment is fallible, manipulable, and sometimes morally perceptive; it is not uniformly foolish.

### Rank becomes money
`買えないモノ` states that ranking position determines monthly Association compensation. Activity points, self-reports, civilian eyewitness evidence, and nonpublic assessment criteria feed the system, yet achievements may fail to map cleanly into rank. Institutional legibility now has direct material consequences.

### C-class precarity
The bonus explicitly links low C-class status to low pay. Saitama's near-empty finances provide a concrete outcome of the professional hierarchy.

### Amai Mask — public-image governance
Amai Mask's direct ideology links hero function to removing public anxiety through strength, beauty, speed, and clean victory. In chapter 29 he explicitly does **not** demand control of B/C promotions; his management concern is A-class and above because those heroes have stronger social influence. Preserve that boundary.

### Current heroism dimensions through V05
At minimum distinguish:
1. self-authored hero identity/practice;
2. registered profession;
3. physical capability;
4. duty under uncertainty/known failure;
5. ranked institutional status;
6. public symbolic recognition;
7. economic reward;
8. legality/morality.
No one variable reliably entails the others.

## V06 additions

### S-class status is capability recognition, not common ethos
The emergency assembly makes heterogeneity explicit. S-class contains radically different motives, speech registers, family obligations, institutional loyalties, tactical styles, and moral histories. Rank is useful as a capability/status signal but cannot be converted into a shared personality or hero philosophy.

### Emergency governance — coercion with a pre-disclosure opt-out
Sicchi tells the assembled heroes that death is possible and offers a real chance to leave **before** the classified prophecy is disclosed, without stripping S-class registration (`逃げるのも勇気だ`). After disclosure, confinement/secrecy is threatened to prevent panic. Current institutional model: meaningful consent at one stage can coexist with coercive information control at the next.

### Professional identity — STRENGTHEN layered model
Saitama's `趣味で…じゃなくてプロのヒーロー` self-correction shows professional hero status has entered his own explicit identity language. This does not reverse V01-V05 self-authorship; it adds an institutional layer to an internally authorized practice.

### Elite coalition learning
The Melzargard fight demonstrates distributed value: Iaian reports failure, senior heroes persist, Metal Bat discovers the sphere anomaly, Bang infers the rule, and Atomic adapts. Hero contribution remains multidimensional and can emerge from information production rather than final victory alone.

### `鮭` — heroism without false omnipotence
Saitama explicitly refuses to claim competence over employment, family breakdown, or another adult's ultimate choice. He still rescues immediate physical danger and leaves an indirect route to future presence. Heroism therefore includes **accurate scope limits**, not only intervention.

### Recognition continuity
V06 does not materially overturn V05's recognition hierarchy. Saitama's primary issue in the alien crisis is challenge/action, not public credit. The recognition question remains open rather than solved by cosmic escalation.

### Current heroism dimensions through V07
Keep separately: capability; willingness to respond; duty; accuracy of self-assessment; institutional status; information contribution; tactical cooperation; public recognition; compensation; legality; moral worth; and honest limits of competence.

## V07 boundary update

### Recognition sacrifice generalizes across institutions — STRENGTHEN
`カツ丼` independently repeats the V05 pattern outside Hero Association internal politics. Saitama defeats a threat, leaves before the media/publicity response, and allows police to receive public credit after police performed genuine but insufficient protective work.

Current formulation: recognition/attribution matters to Saitama, but he can surrender it when the credit structure would unjustly erase another actor's meaningful service or legitimacy.

### Service is not authorized by supplication — STRENGTHEN
Saitama's `「お願い」されて動くもんじゃない / ヒーローも ケーサツも な?` places hero and police duty in parallel. Requests can coordinate action, but are not the moral source of the duty.

### Police / Hero Association legitimacy conflict — NEW
The police chief gives a substantive public-order case for police legitimacy. McCoy, by contrast, tries to turn the police emergency into a Hero Association publicity victory. V07 therefore adds **inter-institutional competition over protective legitimacy**, not merely internal hero rank conflict.

Do not flatten McCoy's opportunism into a universal Association motive.

### Amai Mask — S-class gatekeeping becomes explicit
V07 directly establishes that Amai preserves A-class rank 1 to block unworthy candidates from S-class. This sharpens V05's image/management ideology without retroactively changing what was known earlier.

### Post-A-City centralization
The Association response to catastrophic loss is infrastructural centralization: fortress-like HQ, roads toward other cities, and residence rights for willing A-class-and-above heroes. Disaster increases institutional dispatch capacity and spatial concentration simultaneously.

### Heroism / capacity distinction continues
Police resistance to `奇襲梅` fails physically but remains socially meaningful to Saitama. The volume again rejects `failed to win -> worthless service` as an inference.

## V08 boundary update — false credit becomes operative role

### King — misattribution can become an institutionally real identity
V08 resolves the V06 reputation contradiction: King did not perform the victories attributed to him. Repeated coincidence/misattribution produced money, S-class standing, civilian expectation and monster targeting.

**Key distinction:** `false causal attribution != socially unreal role`.

Saitama's `手柄とか そういう問題じゃないだろ` after learning King may hold Saitama's own achievements strengthens the recognition hierarchy. He does not erase factual truth; he treats current responsibility as more important than prestige recovery.

### `皆のヒーロー` — expectation as real social fact
`お前は皆のヒーローなんだぜ / お前が何と言おうと皆は最強のヒーローだと思ってる` describes the present expectation structure. Public belief can create obligations and danger even when the historical record is false.

### Association capacity shortage -> outsider recruitment
Sitch explicitly warns that repeated strong disasters will exhaust heroes and says the half-year unprecedented-crisis window is not waiting. The Association requests combat cooperation from underworld fighters `人格の善悪を問わず`.

Current registered counts are stated as C390 / B101 / A38 / S17. The important transition is institutional: **the formal hero registry is admitted to be an insufficient monopoly on usable force**.

### Governance risk — Garou
Garou immediately demonstrates that combat capacity is not equivalent to willingness to serve. Emergency recruitment lowers the moral/legal gate but cannot make the recruited actor accept protective purpose.

### `迷い猫` — registered hero labor includes trivial service
A lost-cat assignment makes role fit visible. Saitama's overwhelming combat ability does not make every Association task a natural competence domain. Hero status therefore cannot be read as universal service competence.

### Tanktop Tiger / Black Hole correction
Both perform real perimeter duty in the sealed monster zone while retaining rank resentment. Professional duty and status pathology coexist.

## V09 institutional update

- **STRENGTHEN — rank as behavioral incentive:** Fubuki's B1 position becomes organizational capital. She does not merely occupy a rank; she builds a faction around controlling the local hierarchy and suppressing rival advancement.
- **NEW — perceived promotion ceilings:** Fubuki's account identifies Sweet Mask at A1 and Atomic Samurai's disciples immediately below as barriers within the ranking ecology. The claim is evidence of how actors perceive and strategize around the institution, not a proof that promotion is objectively impossible.
- **NEW — hero-name bureaucracy:** Saitama receives `ハゲマント`; Genos receives `鬼サイボーグ`. Institutional naming converts complex persons into memorable public brands and can reproduce misrecognition even when technically functioning as identification.
- **REVISE — Saitama and recognition:** his dislike of `ハゲマント` shows that public naming can matter affectively even though rank/credit does not govern his ethical behavior.
- **NEW — human Hero Hunter problem:** Garou destabilizes the simple institutional binary of hero versus monster. A dangerous human attacking heroes creates restraint and classification problems visible in the Tanktop Master/Mumen sequence.
- **STRENGTHEN — heroism versus rank security:** Saitama's criticism of Fubuki distinguishes actual heroic exposure to stronger threats from status maintained by dominating weaker followers.

Do not reduce V09 to `rankings are bad`: rankings retain informational/coordination functions, but actors can convert them into local political power and distorted incentives.

## V10 update — classification, resource allocation, public image, factions, and bounded metrics

### `技` exposes a capability dimension rank does not summarize
The hospital discussion after Garou's attacks isolates martial `技` as a distinct kind of strength. Hero rank remains institutionally real, but the system does not encode every matchup-relevant capability.

### Garou plus widening monster incidents — STRENGTHEN scarcity problem
The Association must route resources across a dangerous human hero-hunter and increasing literal monster incidents. V10 reinforces the institution as a triage/classification system operating under incomplete information rather than as a simple ranking board.

### Tatsumaki holiday — NEW allocation mismatch
The Association explicitly faces manpower shortage yet tries to preserve Tatsumaki's availability and gives her time off. Tatsumaki then becomes idle while ordinary monsters are being handled elsewhere. This is not evidence that scarcity is fake; it is evidence that **capacity allocation and local demand can misalign even under real scarcity**.

### `センス` — rubric-domain mismatch
A hero-suit contest legitimately evaluates design and still ranks Saitama last after he helps the bullied youth and defeats the monster interrupting the event. The judges are not necessarily “wrong” at costume judging. The comic mismatch demonstrates that a narrow institutional/public rubric can be valid in-domain while being useless as a total measure of heroism.

### Fubuki Group versus Saitama's network
Fubuki Group's headcount, organization, and specialist selection are genuine advantages. Saitama denies having any group, yet can call King, Bang, and Genos. The contrast separates **formal faction capacity** from **organic relational capacity**.

### `オカメちゃん` — metric validity and domain limits
Child Emperor's device returns useful physical-strength comparisons inside its measurement domain. It also:
- gives Fubuki a low number because psychic power is not physical strength;
- cannot distinguish below-range from above-range failure in Saitama's `測定不能` output;
- changes sharply when a monster's bodily state changes;
- does not encode technique, weapons, technology, psychic ability, coordination, or other relevant combat variables.

Institutional lesson: do not infer “rank/measurement is meaningless.” Infer that every classification surface has a domain and failure modes.

### Threat levels
Elder Centipede's `災害レベル“竜”` classification remains operationally meaningful even though individual outcomes are not reducible to the label. V10 continues the distinction between useful triage category and complete predictive ontology.

## V11 - coordination, capacity, role assignment, and incentive failure

### Simultaneous overload - STRENGTHEN
The Hero Association faces multiple Demon-or-higher incidents across cities while high-end heroes are unavailable, unreachable, or already committed. V11 makes finite responder capacity a direct institutional problem rather than a background assumption.

### Lower-ranked civic value - STRENGTHEN
Non-S-class heroes evacuate civilians, carry injured people, and provide specialized capture capabilities. Rank retains operational meaning but remains an incomplete measure of civic usefulness.

### Specialist teamwork - REVISE simplistic weak-team model
`Sentai` shows a B-class capture team with differentiated functions that initially work: leadership/frontline control, scent tracking, traps, and support. Failure occurs when the target has changed beyond the intelligence/model used for assignment, not because specialization itself is worthless.

### Saitama and formal role fit - NEW institutional boundary
Saitama can accept practical field authority while remaining poor at map discipline and fixed role procedure. His eventual rescue of the team separates institutional fit from heroic responsibility.

### Urokodon operation - NEW information/incentive failure
The live-capture mission is shaped by the target's supposed regenerative/medical value and by stale threat information after poor containment/management. V11 therefore distinguishes capacity shortage from incentive distortion and information lag; it does not justify a blanket claim that the Association or scientific research is uniformly corrupt.

### Recognition and prestige - STRENGTHEN
Super Fight school prestige and tournament presentation do not produce Saitama deference. Practical competence remains separable from social hierarchy.

## V12 update — strength, vocation, scarcity, and donor leverage

### Snek / Suiryu — NEW explicit strength-versus-heroism argument
Snek remembers professional heroism as proof that he possessed rare capacity to save the public, then questions `プロヒーローとは本当に必要なのだろうか?` after encountering much stronger actors outside his role. Suiryu attacks the premise more directly: his superior strength serves easy living, and he treats rescue as optional personal choice rather than obligation.

V12 therefore separates three variables that rank discourse can conflate: **combat capacity, professional role, and public obligation**.

### Snek institutional identity — REVISE status model
Earlier status sensitivity remains valid but is no longer adequately described as vanity alone. Rank/professional recognition had supported Snek's belief that he was a legitimately useful protector. Strong outsiders destabilize that usefulness narrative.

### Hero Association crisis governance — STRENGTHEN / DECOMPOSE
The Waganma discussion establishes three simultaneous pressures:
1. the relation between mass monster activity and kidnapping is not yet known;
2. `ヒーローが足りない` — responder capacity is genuinely scarce;
3. Narinki supplies **7%** of total Association funding, creating powerful donor pressure to prioritize his son.

The supported institutional claim is **funding concentration can distort priority under scarcity and uncertainty**, not that every Association decision is purchased.

Evidence: `64撃目`, image `0083`.

### Watchdog Man — NEW successful local institutional trust case
Citizens treat Q City as safer specifically because Watchdog Man is there and call him `守り神`; the observed pile of defeated monsters supplies a real capability basis. This is a useful control against King: public reputation can be accurate and socially protective, not only false.

### King — STRENGTHEN deterrence without capability correspondence
The V12 extra states that King's presence is said to be a `犯罪抑止力`. Hired assassins withdraw without combat because they interpret ordinary actions as omniscient threat control and moral mercy. Institution/public narrative can therefore produce real safety effects even when the underlying power attribution is false.

### Rank and external strength — STRENGTHEN bounded-proxy model
Suiryu's tournament dominance again proves that Hero Association rank is not a complete census of human combat capability. It remains meaningful as a professional/institutional category rather than a total ontology of strength.

## V13 update — professional duty, specialization, formal outcomes, and symbolic assets

### A-class coalition — STRENGTHEN institution as differentiated competence
Death Gatling organizes six A-class heroes after preserving cognition/information under extreme conditions. The coalition is rational and coordinated but cannot finish the giant octopus before S-class intervention.

Current institutional claim: rank does not reduce to raw power, and competent coordination does not guarantee sufficient force. A-class failure here is not evidence that the class or profession is meaningless.

### Flashy Flash / Tatsumaki — STRENGTHEN elite-capability coordination problem
Flash supplies precise high-speed specialization; Tatsumaki supplies whole-target spatial domination. Both solve dimensions the A-class group cannot. Their mutual hostility shows that elite capability can reduce dependence on others without improving cooperative behavior.

### Death Gatling — rank pride without denial
`悔しいが俺達では退治できなかった` plus `A級の立つ瀬がない` demonstrates honest result recognition combined with class/status humiliation. Status sensitivity and epistemic accuracy coexist.

### Suiryu thesis versus hospital action — STRENGTHEN role/heroism distinction
Suiryu says `“ヒーロー”なんて名前だけさ 現実にはそんなもの存在しない`. The source immediately juxtaposes injured Mumen Rider refusing retreat because immobile ICU patients remain behind, followed by injured Tanktop Master intervening to protect the space.

V13 therefore structurally contests `weak registered hero -> hero only in name`. It does not certify every registered hero as virtuous.

### Lightning Max — STRENGTHEN professional-duty category
When monsters enter Super Fight, Max tells civilians to withdraw and begins `ここは俺達プロヒーローが…`. The professional role is asserted most clearly precisely when the tournament's civilian/martial entertainment frame becomes lethal crisis.

### Suiryu championship — STRENGTHEN domain-bound legitimacy
Saitama is disqualified under tournament rules and Suiryu remains the official champion even though both participants privately know Saitama is stronger. The official result is not “fake”; it is authoritative for the rule-governed contest and non-authoritative for absolute capability.

### Amai Mask — public image as strategic infrastructure
Monster Association attackers identify Amai not only as a combatant but as a high-value Hero Association image asset. Killing him in front of fans would damage institutional public legitimacy. Reputation/image is therefore an operational resource that enemies can target.

### Current V13 formulation
Institutional labels and roles remain **domain-valid but incomplete**. Hero rank, tournament victory, celebrity image, and professional status can organize real obligations and social effects without exhaustively describing capability or character.

## V14 update — hero value, institutional overload, and coercive asymmetry

### Hero value versus hero power — STRENGTHEN
Snek and Lightning Max are weaker than Suiryu in direct combat terms, yet they choose to remain behind against Gouketsu to create an escape window. V14 therefore supplies a clean counterexample to any model that equates hero value with rank or combat superiority. Professional heroism can be expressed through assuming risk on behalf of someone stronger.

### Suiryu — REVISE hero-legibility model
Suiryu's earlier dismissal of professional obligation breaks only after he experiences overwhelming force from the weaker side. Under torture and despair he explicitly calls for heroes, reclassifies the fallen professionals' renewed stand as kindness, and later identifies Saitama's conduct as the authentic image of a hero.

The endpoint goal of becoming a hero is canonical but still provisional as a durable vocational identity.

### Distributed crisis — STRENGTHEN institutional-overload model
Suiryu's calls occur while heroes are deployed across multiple cities. The montage of ongoing hero labor should not be read as literal hearing or as institutional indifference. V14 reinforces that delayed rescue can arise from distributed scarcity and simultaneous incidents.

### Monster Association hostage leverage — NEW explicit institutional pressure
The Association states that one operational objective is abducting a person with hostage value to the Hero Association. This makes coercion of the institution through connected elites a deliberate strategic mechanism rather than incidental kidnapping.

### Official tournament outcome versus experienced reality — STRENGTHEN
Suiryu remains the official champion while privately recognizing Saitama as the actual superior. Institutional record and experienced comparative truth remain separate domains.

## V15 update — growth, classification, and causal recognition

### `最強` is not `最高`
King explicitly separates strongest hero from best/highest hero and proposes `人助けや世の役に立つこと` as remaining hero purpose. Record as serious in-character ethical argument, not narrator-issued doctrine.

### Missed intervention as institutional/temporal failure
Saitama's `空回り` after missing a major monster event shows that sufficient power does not guarantee useful hero labor. Information, timing and deployment remain causal variables.

### Disaster level as bounded operational proxy
`番外編.1 災害レベル` formally explains threat classification, supplies approximate hero-rank response heuristics, and demonstrates evidence-driven reclassification from a low estimate to `推定災害レベル・鬼`. Strengthen: rankings/levels are useful for deployment but not complete power ontologies.

### Extreme outlier problem
The monster misreads `B級7位 ハゲマント` because Saitama's rank prior conflicts with direct observed capability. This is not evidence that all ranks are useless; it is an outlier/calibration failure.

### `目撃`: genuine observation -> false causal recognition
Witnesses see King at the aftermath of a Saitama kill and infer that King caused the victory. V15 therefore strengthens the King reputation mechanism from rumor to **post-event eyewitness causal completion**. The institution/public can update in the wrong direction when sequence information is missing.

## V16 update — rank, role, recognition, and self-worth

### Death Gatling makes ranking distortion explicit
V16 states rather than merely implies that rankings produce perceived `優劣`, can deny capable heroes `正当な評価`, and that increased monster disasters concentrate `脚光` on combat-specialized S-class heroes. This is the strongest direct institutional critique of rank-recognition coupling so far.

### Mixed-rank coordination demonstrates compositional value
Glasses' `役割` as support/pursuit and the eight-hero formation show that field value can emerge from complementary functions rather than individual rank. Preserve conditions: preparation, numbers, and an already-depleted Garou are crucial.

### Recognition critique is internally trapped in rank logic
Death Gatling selects Garou partly because defeating a target known for beating S-class heroes would publicly demonstrate non-S `存在価値`. **REVISE:** the rank system is not merely externally unfair; actors can internalize its prestige logic even while criticizing it.

### Glasses separates growth from rank authorization
`成長過程` supplies a healthier response to comparative hierarchy: Glasses remains modest about current rank but makes `自分の限界を決めない` his development policy and continues hero work. This does not make rank irrelevant; it prevents rank from becoming an endpoint verdict.

## V17 update -- authority without command

### Blast: rank is not commandability
The Association explicitly states that Blast cannot be moved for its convenience and acts by his own will. V17 therefore supplies a clean institutional distinction between formal No.1 rank and ordinary subordinate availability.

### King: false description, real leverage
King deliberately uses the belief in his strength and Elder Centipede's revenge motive to fix the monster's approach. Public reputation remains wrong as physical description but becomes a real strategic resource when the reputed person can manipulate expectation.

### Narinki: donor pressure and private substitution
Narinki's demand that Waganma be prioritized makes stakeholder pressure visible inside the Hero Association. His later decision to bypass the Association with a privately equipped squad shows that loss of confidence in public process can produce parallel security action, not necessarily better operational knowledge.

### Fubuki / informal organization
Fubuki's attempt to organize Saitama, Genos, Bang, Bomb, and King fails because their actual orbit is relationship-driven rather than factional. V17 adds a durable institutional category: high-coordination social networks can exist without formal membership or a command chain.

### Monster Association mirror -- qualified
The Monster Association also has personnel accounting, executives, recruitment, leadership deficits, and coordination problems. Preserve structural mirroring while rejecting moral equivalence: its explicit hero-killing qualification test is a material ethical difference.


## V18 update — capitulation, elite rescue entitlement, and reputation as operational information

### Human response to monster threat — STRENGTHEN heterogeneity
The `怪人崇拝党` advocates accommodation with monsters rather than resistance, showing that human society's response includes ideological capitulation as well as hero/police defense.

### Waganma / donor privilege — STRENGTHEN
Waganma says his father's Hero Association importance guarantees professional rescue and explicitly assumes his own priority over Tareo. This is strong evidence of perceived/internalized elite rescue entitlement, but child speech does not by itself prove every Association responder shares that priority.

### King reputation — STRENGTHEN dual-use/risk model
King's name frightens a captive monster, preserving deterrent value. Genos also sends King toward an apparent high-energy threat because he believes the combat myth. Reputation can therefore produce both useful social leverage and dangerous task misallocation.

### Saitama recognition — STRENGTHEN
`やっちまった・2` shows Saitama privately signing Tareo's hero almanac without being asked. Recognition/legibility remains desirable when no higher value conflicts with it.

### Fubuki institution/relationship boundary — STRENGTHEN
Fubuki still reads cooperation as possible Blizzard Group membership, but V18 demonstrates that practical reciprocity and ordinary accountability are more effective relational routes than rank or organizational pressure.

## V19 institution update - raid roster epistemology and rescue visibility

### Raid roster as an epistemic model - STRENGTHEN
The Hero Association must plan with incomplete internal maps and uncertain enemy capability. Child Emperor turns relationship history, availability, rank and public reputation into a deployable roster. Bang is excluded because of inferred attachment risk; Genos is explicitly excluded; lower-ranked heroes are considered as replacements; King is treated as a main force. Institutional planning is therefore necessary but only as good as its inputs.

### King reputation - STRENGTHEN from public myth to command error
The false strongest-man narrative now affects real mission planning. This is more consequential than public admiration: a mistaken causal model can allocate operational responsibility to a person who lacks the assumed capability.

### Professional-risk ethics - REVISE
Child Emperor accepts that lower-ranked entrants may not all return unharmed, while Sekingar resists treating them as disposable. Preserve the professional-risk premise and the moral dispute over how institutions may spend that risk.

### Donor influence versus individual hero motive - DECOMPOSE
Narinki can pressure the Association and offer money because his son is the hostage, but S-Class dialogue rejects money as a reason to fight. Donor leverage at the institution level must not be projected onto every hero's personal motive.

### Tareo - NEW institutional invisibility case
The official rescue model knows about the donor's child but does not know Tareo exists. The resulting exclusion is an information failure, not evidence of a conscious decision that Tareo is unworthy of rescue.

### Informal Saitama orbit - STRENGTHEN alternative coordination form
The hotpot network is dense with high-capability actors but remains non-factional and outside the Association's formal deployment structure. Social coordination and institutional membership remain distinct.


## V20 institutional delta — responsibility, rank and distributed competence

### Hostage-first mission ethics — STRENGTHEN
Child Emperor explicitly defines the operation as hostage rescue first and monster extermination second. Unknown hostage location constrains indiscriminate base destruction; encountered monsters remain each hero's responsibility to finish. This is a concrete case where operational power is subordinated to mission ethics.

### Sweet Mask's responsibility criterion — NEW high-value doctrine / speaker-qualified
Sweet Mask argues that the S-Class were seated there because of combat power, but power without fulfilled responsibility can leave them as merely dangerous people. He then claims management/commands—specifically his own rational direction—are what would make such power properly heroic. Preserve the **diagnosis** separately from the **control solution**.

### Rank as incomplete but real professional infrastructure — STRENGTHEN
Needle Star treats ranking advancement as compensation for entering an unknown enemy stronghold; Sekingar agrees. Later Sekingar calls selected B/C heroes highly capable and Double Hole states that combat expertise is not limited to high-ranked heroes. Rank therefore remains a career/deployment signal while demonstrably failing as a complete capability ontology.

### Support is a real role, not a synonym for deadweight — STRENGTHEN
The lower-ranked surface team protects evacuation/reception functions and then demonstrates specialist combat value after Tatsumaki questions why she should protect them. Extreme power asymmetry does not erase distinct professional contribution.

### S-Class autonomy — STRENGTHEN command-friction model
Tatsumaki self-assigns the boss, Flashy Flash resists unilateral allocation, and Sweet Mask demands control. The Hero Association possesses a formal hierarchy but not a simple command chain over its strongest assets.

### King reputation — STRENGTHEN operational misassignment
King's false combat image continues to enter planning as if it were physical fact. The error is not merely public fame; it can allocate real battlefield expectations.

## V21 institutional / heroism delta

### Mission purpose outranks optional local victory — STRENGTHEN
Child Emperor explicitly says `勝つことは重要じゃない` while refusing to stop the hostage extraction to fight a strong machine opponent. The line is mission-relative: delivering Waganma to the surface is his `使命`, and a local win that leaves him unable to walk would undermine the protective objective. This is one of the clearest professional-hero cases where enemy elimination is subordinated to mission purpose.

### S-Class as capability signal and expectation burden — STRENGTHEN
Waganma repeatedly treats Child Emperor's S-Class status as assurance that he should simply win. V21 demonstrates why the inference is incomplete: Child Emperor is exceptional, but tools deplete, contingencies fail, and Brave Giant has a hard operating limit. Rank remains informative while social expectation strips away conditionality.

### Evaluation systems remain local — STRENGTHEN
Rhino Wrestler's scoring, ninja cohort ranking (`44期の元一位`), explicit disaster levels, and S-Class titles all measure different things. V21 does not abolish classification; it shows that each system has a scope and can fail when treated as a complete ontology.

### Child-to-child institutional asymmetry — NEW
Waganma is the child protected by the rescue institution; Child Emperor is the child expected to provide adult-grade strategic and combat protection. Record as a structural asymmetry visible in V21, not as a total claim that the institution consciously intends “child exploitation.”


## V22 institutional / heroism delta

### Adult reliance on Child Emperor becomes textually recognized — STRENGTHEN
Zombieman explicitly apologizes that a group of grown adults has been relying on Child Emperor and thanks him for being there. The institution-level problem visible since V21 is therefore no longer only analyst inference. Equally important, the manga gives an adult hero the capacity to recognize and partially repair the burden; “the institution” must not be flattened into one consciousness.

### Recognition without possession — NEW
Phoenix Man says `お前の力が必要なんだ` as part of recruitment into a totalizing organization. Zombieman later says effectively “we relied on you too much; thank you; I'm glad you're here” without demanding institutional absorption. V22 distinguishes recognition as interpersonal acknowledgment from recognition as leverage.

### Mission/rescue category versus contamination justice — NEW conflict
The Atomic Samurai disciples treat Do-S-controlled humans as rescue subjects whose agency has been captured. Amai's `正義` logic tends to treat those aligned with evil as legitimate elimination targets even under coercion. V22 therefore makes “who counts as rescueable?” an explicit hero-institution ethics problem.

### Child and hero are compatible categories — STRENGTHEN
Saitama's `子供かと思ったらヒーローなんだな` recognizes Child Emperor as a hero without denying his child status. This is a useful counter to institutional expectations that tacitly make competence erase childhood.

## V23 heroism / recognition / institution delta

### Role after equipment loss — STRENGTHEN
Child Emperor explicitly rejects the idea that losing a trump card eliminates usefulness. Technical knowledge, enemy analysis, route knowledge, and coordination remain role-specific capability. `お互いの役割を全うしましょう` strengthens the project's recurring rule that **professional value is multidimensional and situational**, not reducible to rank or maximum attack output.

### Lower-ranked / subordinate specialization — STRENGTHEN
Atomic Samurai's disciples defeat Devil Long Hair through coordination while framing their success as reducing their master's burden. The raid continues to distribute legitimate professional value below S-Class rather than treating lower rank as decorative manpower.

### Incomplete institutional knowledge of Saitama — STRENGTHEN
Association personnel fear that an unauthorized B-class hero underground could become another hostage. Their conclusion is wrong about Saitama but rational relative to recorded/public knowledge. This is an institutional **misrecognition problem**, not evidence that professional caution itself is irrational.

### Amai's `範` doctrine versus uncredited exemplarity — NEW
Amai says heroes should be admired and should provide a human model/example. The extra then places a quiet Pig God kindness outside Amai's evaluative gaze. Heroic exemplarity can therefore be socially valuable while remaining only partially legible to formal/public evaluators.

### Waganma disclosure — STRENGTHEN rescue ethics under imperfect testimony
The donor's son becomes a fallible child witness whose shame delayed crucial hostage information. Child Emperor's response shows that rescue institutions depend not only on authority and technology but on creating conditions in which frightened people can disclose mistakes.

## V24 delta — reputation, leadership, and hidden authority

- **King:** false attribution can become real operational power. The public King model is descriptively wrong but behaviorally causal in enemies. Recognition must therefore track truth-value and causal effect separately.
- **Fubuki / Do-S:** organizational membership is ethically underdetermined by hierarchy alone. V24 makes agency preservation/restoration a necessary discriminator between flawed leadership and outright possession.
- **Gyoro Gyoro:** visible command surfaces can be proxies. Institutional/organizational analysis must distinguish public/apparent node from causal controller.
- **Raid labor:** specialist observation, restraint, warning, and support remain operationally important when Dragon-level mechanisms punish naive direct attack.

## V25 delta — distributed hero labor and rank/model mismatch

- **Distributed heroism — STRENGTHEN:** the surface rescue succeeds through lower-ranked resistance, wounded transport, food/recovery, field command, intelligence transfer, and force routing. S-Class output is necessary in places but not a complete account of institutional success.

- **Professional-risk assumption — OPEN / ethically load-bearing:** Drive Knight treats fallen heroes' exposure as usable data and assumes professional resolve licenses such risk. V25 makes the assumption explicit without narratively certifying it as legitimate consent.

- **Child Emperor/Tareo — STRENGTHEN:** successful extraction of the donor's son does not erase the second child's rescue claim.

## V26 delta — earned rank, missing records, and competent bureaucracy with bad routing

- **S-Class reputation — STRENGTHEN / qualify:** V26 explicitly treats S-Class awe as historically earned among people who once sought the strongest position. Their later damage is therefore evidence of a changed threat environment, not proof that rank was meaningless.
- **Saitama — STRENGTHEN record-production problem:** narration states that his comparable prior battle history remains unknown while he is indifferent to telling it. Under-recognition is partly an information-production failure, not bureaucracy alone.
- **Tatsumaki — STRENGTHEN final-weapon role:** because Blast rarely appears, the Association often treats Tatsumaki as its decisive final weapon. Preserve as institutional/reputation state rather than universal metaphysical proof.
- **Distributed rescue — STRENGTHEN:** hostage/second-child accounting and hero-status communication directly constrain force application.
- **King — STRENGTHEN causal reputation:** public falsehood about physical strength still produces protective deterrence for Tareo.
- **`大抜擢` — NEW institutional control case:** personnel review contains real evidence, competing criteria, and recognition of Sitch's crisis value, yet promotion can redirect that competence into idol-hero publicity production. Institution-level dysfunction can coexist with competent evaluators and workers.
- **Governing revision:** recognition quality depends on evidence availability, evaluation quality, and organizational use after evaluation. These are separate failure points.
