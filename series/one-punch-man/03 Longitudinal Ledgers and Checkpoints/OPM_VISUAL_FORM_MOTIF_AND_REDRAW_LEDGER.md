# One Punch Man — Visual Form, Motif, and Redraw Ledger


## V01 formal grammar


1. **rendering mismatch:** hyper-detailed monsters/destruction versus simplified Saitama;
2. **scale inflation -> compressed resolution:** page space builds danger that one strike punctures;
3. **affect through rendering:** Saitama becomes more intensely rendered when genuinely activated;
4. **reaction timing:** other characters' attempts to assimilate impossible power become part of the comedy;
5. **ordinary/combat register collision:** mundane concern can occupy visual emphasis normally reserved for battle stakes.


## V02 additions


### Designed complexity versus blank anomaly
House of Evolution bodies, architecture, and hierarchy are rendered as elaborate engineered systems. Saitama's visually simplified body/face repeatedly refuses the assumption that visible complexity predicts power.


### Facial salience map
- Carnage Kabuto threat: comparatively flat Saitama;
- supermarket-sale realization: extreme panic/activation (`image:0088`);
- `知名度が低い` realization: concentrated dark facial rendering (`image:0181`).


**Formal claim:** changes in Saitama's visual intensity are evidence about subjective salience, not objective danger.


### Sonic motion architecture
Long speed traces, afterimages, decompressed movement, and tight eye/reaction panels construct Sonic's self-understanding as untouchably fast. Saitama remains compositionally stable enough to make the speed system visually impressive but narratively insufficient.


### Crowd/public framing
Mumen Rider's entrance is staged through civilians' recognition; Paradiser news is mediated by television; Hero Association exposition turns heroism into a graphic/institutional information system. Public meaning increasingly enters the panel through audiences and media.


### Training continuity
`じぶんみがき` uses repeated exhaustion/rescue/fight images to give bodily continuity to Saitama's transformation rather than treating the regimen as only a spoken gag.

Rain is not background texture. It restores/strengthens Deep Sea King's effective state, visually binds successive hero defeats, saturates the crowd/Mumen sequence, and continues through Saitama's arrival and the reputational aftermath.

Mumen Rider's comparatively small, battered human body is held against Deep Sea King's massive form while crowd speech grows around him. Moral/social scale rises as physical scale remains hopelessly unequal.

After extended struggle, Saitama's punch is compressed. Formal emphasis then moves to faces, speech balloons, crowd interpretation, and injured bodies. The manga reallocates climax from impact to credit.

### Mundane reset
`買えないモノ` shifts visual attention to bank balance, coins, vending machine, trash bin, ordinary streets, and apartment life. The reset is not analytically empty; it makes material scarcity and domestic observation visible.

V13 frames Suiryu through upright control, mobility, and competitive pleasure. V14 progressively re-stages the same body as injured, crawling, unable to rise, and finally immobilized by fear. Power hierarchy is communicated through loss of posture and agency as much as through stated rankings.

Close facial distortion, tears, compressed posture, and halted movement replace Suiryu's prior relaxed competitive body language. V14 makes fear a bodily state rather than a dialogue label.

### Darkness / light — NEW conceptual visual motif
Suiryu's language about seeking light after being thrown into darkness receives literalized visual treatment; Bakuzan then attacks the remaining hope. The motif frames hero arrival as an experiential structure without establishing a universal metaphysics.

### Hero montage — STRENGTHEN distributed-labor form
Suiryu's private cries are intercut with heroes already fighting elsewhere. The montage widens heroism from the absent rescuer he wants to a distributed social labor system. It does not prove those heroes literally hear him.

The decisive encounter is represented by distant impacts, environmental shock, silence, and the return of Gouketsu's head rather than by exchanged techniques. The omission itself measures Saitama's mismatch with conventional climax form.
