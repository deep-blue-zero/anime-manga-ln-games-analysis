# One Punch Man — Volume 17 Japanese Dialogue and Register Audit

## Audit result

**PASS.** The load-bearing Japanese-language claims in `OPM_V17_DEEP_READING.md` are supportable from the Japanese V17 tankobon at the V16 observational boundary.

This audit is selective. It does not translate every speech balloon. Its function is to verify places where lexical choice, register, address, or grammatical framing materially changes character/institutional interpretation.

The audit was stabilized before the historical V1 `OPM_V16-V18_DEEP_READING.md`, V18+, or Checkpoint-A validation ledger was consulted for V17 adjudication.

## 1. Garou childhood role language — PASS

### Locator
`OPM|V17|chapter:85|images:0010-0018|anchor:Garou childhood hero-play origin`

### Finding
The deep reading is correct to treat Garou's childhood memory as **role assignment plus majority validation**, not merely generic bullying.

The scene repeatedly uses role/game language:

- `ヒーローごっこ`
- `怪人`
- `ジャスティスマン`
- “you be the monster” framing
- Garou's explicit refusal to continue the assigned monster role

Garou says he no longer wants to play the monster role because it is not enjoyable and proposes an actual contest whose result should determine whether the others leave him alone. The surrounding children still interpret his resistance as the disruptive act.

### Analytical implication
**PASS:** V17 source-groundedly explains why Garou associates moral role with social popularity and predetermined outcome.

Do **not** upgrade this into narrator confirmation that every hero/monster distinction is socially arbitrary.

## 2. `人気者` / `嫌われている弱者` — PASS

### Locator
`OPM|V17|chapter:85|images:0016-0017|anchor:Garou popular hero versus disliked weak person formulation`

### Finding
Garou's vocabulary is specifically social-evaluative. His memory contrasts Tatchan's popularity with the weak/disliked role.

The load-bearing formulation on image 0017 describes the structure as a popular person liked by everyone unilaterally beating a weak person hated by everyone.

The key terms support the V2 claim that Garou's grievance is not simply “strong beats weak.” It concerns **who receives group approval while doing the beating**.

### Analytical implication
**PASS:** `popular -> heroic legitimacy` and `disliked -> monster/loser role` are part of Garou's own causal model.

**Qualification:** this is Garou's model of the childhood scene. It is not automatically the manga's complete theory of justice.

## 4. Adult Garou: `何が正義だ！何が悪だ！` — PASS with important self-limitation

### Locator
`OPM|V17|chapter:85|image:0018|anchor:Garou justice evil majority anger`

### Source language
Garou attacks fixed moral positions with:

- `何が正義だ！何が悪だ！`
- a complaint that majority will determines his destruction;
- `理不尽`

Most importantly, he admits that he cannot neatly analyze the basis of the feeling while remaining certain that he is angry.

He then describes the desired reversal through `弱者の一撃` and says he wants to deny the fixed positions of good and evil (`善悪の立場`).

### Analytical implication
**PASS:** the deep reading correctly distinguishes:

`strong autobiographical grievance / moral affect`
from
`fully articulated philosophical theory`.

It would be an overread to make Garou a settled anti-majoritarian political philosopher at V17. His own wording marks the explanation as incomplete.

# 24. Register-model deltas

## Garou
V17 substantially improves voice modeling. His speech can move among:

- childhood direct protest;
- retrospective social analysis;
- rough adult self-narration;
- explosive anti-majoritarian moral rhetoric;
- self-undercutting admission that he has not fully analyzed the basis of his anger;
- sardonic jokes under coercive recruitment (`ツノでも生やせってか`).

A Garou simulator that produces only snarling combat rhetoric would now be underfit.

## Saitama
Plain, compressed speech remains dominant even under crisis. Ethical anger can coexist with petty recognition irritation and household errands without a marked change to elevated heroic register.

## Genos
Technical observation remains high-density and serious. Respectful/analytical cognition applies not only to Saitama but to techniques he genuinely admires in other experts.

## Fubuki
Emergency-management language, evaluation/status vocabulary, and faction/recruitment framing coexist. She sounds organizational even in a room that is not organized.

## King
The Elder Centipede bait scene adds deliberate public-performance register. King can speak into the myth when he needs the enemy to believe it.

## Metal Knight
Speech is clipped, strategic, distrustful, and doctrine-like. He presents worst-case threat models as reasons for independent accumulation of power.

## Gyoro Gyoro
Managerial language is a defining feature: personnel count, force assessment, cohesion, leadership, executive rank, support role, qualification test. The Monster Association is narrated through organizational vocabulary rather than pure appetite.

## G5
Formal self-identification and mission provenance make G5 sound like an organizational agent rather than a free-roaming monster.

## Sonic
Grand self-confidence remains vulnerable to comic causal error; his language escalates bodily sensation into victory narrative before the text undercuts it.

1. Garou's childhood theory is evidence about Garou's ideological origin, not universal narrator endorsement.
