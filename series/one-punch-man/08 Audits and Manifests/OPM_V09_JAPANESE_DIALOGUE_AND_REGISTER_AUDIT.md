# One Punch Man — V09 Japanese Dialogue and Register Audit

## Disposition

**PASS.** The Japanese text materially supports the V09 deep-reading claims about Garou's narrative grievance and self-authored monster role, Fubuki's command/subordination language, Saitama's blunt hero-role ethics and status insensitivity, Genos's analytical combat register, and the distinction between externally assigned hero names and self-chosen identity.

This audit is selective. It records language that changes characterization or thematic interpretation; it does not attempt to transcribe the volume.

## 1. Garou

### 1.1 `人気者` / `嫌われ者` / `悲劇`

At `OPM|V09|chapter:41|image:0014-0016`, Garou's childhood grievance is framed through popularity and dislike rather than biological species ontology. The key line is:

`人気者が勝って 嫌われ者が負けるなんて悲劇だ`

The contrast is social/narrative: the popular figure wins; the disliked figure loses. Translating this simply as “heroes win and monsters lose” would erase the mechanism Garou himself emphasizes.

### 1.2 `シナリオを変えてやる`

At `image:0017-0018`, Garou describes becoming the strongest monster and changing the `シナリオ`. `シナリオ` makes his self-conception explicitly meta-narrative: he wants to overturn an expected role/outcome structure, not merely acquire a monster body.

### 1.3 `怪人` as chosen role/project

Across V09 Garou uses `怪人` aspirationally while remaining visually and socially human. In the hero-hunting section he treats defeating heroes as a path toward growth `怪人として`. The grammar supports “as a monster / in the capacity of a monster,” not proof that bodily monsterization has already occurred.

### 1.4 rough challenge register

Garou's normal battle speech is aggressively colloquial, with direct second-person challenge, insult, and rhetorical testing. He often converts evaluation into provocation: if a technique, punch, hero, or reputation is real, it should survive contact. This register supports a model of strength as demonstrated actuality rather than formal title.

## 2. Fubuki

### 2.1 `私の傘下に入りなさい`

At `OPM|V09|chapter:42|image:0052-0054`, Fubuki's recruitment language is hierarchical. `傘下` means being under an umbrella/control structure; `入りなさい` is a directive. “Join me” is too peer-like if used without qualification. The scene is closer to “enter my organization / come under my command.”

### 2.2 B-Class `1位` as identity anchor

Fubuki repeatedly frames herself and others through explicit class/rank language. Her refusal to relinquish B1 is not just implied behavior; `1位` carries strong self-definitional weight. By Chapter 45, her language makes clear that being first in a lower tier is preferable to entering a ladder whose summit she considers inaccessible.

### 2.3 register shift under destabilization

Fubuki's initial language is poised, directive, and socially superior. After Saitama withstands her psychic attack and after she witnesses the Genos/Sonic/Saitama power ecology, her speech contains more questions, internal comparison, and uncertainty. The shift matters more than any single pronoun choice: command certainty gives way to model revision.

## 3. Saitama

### 3.1 `誰?` and status non-recognition

Saitama's first response to Fubuki is effectively `誰?`. The force is not ceremonially disrespectful so much as genuinely uninvested in the prestige map. He lacks the expected recognition before he can choose whether to honor it.

### 3.2 hero-role ethics in ordinary language

At `OPM|V09|chapter:43|image:0068-0069`, Saitama's language contrasts Fubuki's rank/faction strategy with what a hero is for. The semantic core is that there are absurdly strong bad actors in the world and **standing against them is what heroes do**.

The important language finding is stylistic: he does not shift into elevated justice rhetoric. His diction remains blunt and colloquial. Moral seriousness is carried by ordinary speech.

### 3.3 `たとえたった独りでも`

The sequence around `image:0069` begins from the possibility of standing alone. It should not be generalized into “Saitama believes heroes must work alone.” The following criticism targets becoming dependent on weaker subordinates in order to *feel* strong. The contrast is courage/self-sufficiency versus status insulation, not solitude versus teamwork.

### 3.4 refusing subordination without refusing contact

In Chapter 45 Saitama's formulation toward Fubuki is essentially: her presence is not inherently a problem, **but he has no intention of becoming her subordinate**. This distinction is linguistically clear and should govern relationship modeling.

### 3.5 `ハゲマント`

`ハゲマント` is an externally assigned descriptive hero name: literally “Bald Cape/Cloak,” conventionally rendered “Caped Baldy.” It compresses Saitama into two visible traits and carries comic indignity. His negative reaction is evidence that public naming matters to him somewhat; it does not overturn the stronger evidence that rank does not command his behavior.

## 4. Genos

### 4.1 `先生` remains stable

Genos continues to address Saitama as `先生` across chaotic combat and domestic contexts. V09 does not supply a clean disagreement-over-claim test for frozen Checkpoint G-A-03, but it does preserve the mentor register without strain.

### 4.2 analytical compression

Against Sonic, Genos's language consists of compact observation and immediate tactical update: changed speed/movement, target capture, afterimage reassessment, then escalation. The syntax is operational rather than performatively grandiose.

The language supports the behavioral claim that he reasons while acting. It does **not** prove tactical proportionality; his decision to erase the area when precision fails is a separate failure mode.

### 4.3 `鬼サイボーグ`

`鬼サイボーグ` is imposed by the Hero Association. `鬼` adds ferocious/demonic force to a literal cyborg descriptor. Unlike Saitama's name, the public brand broadly matches Genos's visible combat presentation. Neither hero name should be treated as self-authored identity evidence.

## 5. Sonic

Sonic's speech remains self-dramatizing and rivalry-centered. Speed language is not merely technical measurement; it is a claim to selfhood and superiority. His return is framed as a correction of prior humiliation, making competitive comparison part of his motivational register.

## 6. Mumen Rider and Tanktop Master

Mumen Rider's language in the Garou confrontation retains direct moral intervention despite power mismatch. He speaks as someone policing what heroes should do, not as someone claiming he can overpower everyone present.

Tanktop Master's language is comparatively direct and physical. His concern over Garou's human status helps distinguish lethal monster-response expectations from restraint toward a dangerous human opponent.

## 7. Bang / Garou terminology

Garou's identification as Bang's former `一番弟子`/top disciple materially changes the technique evidence. `流水岩砕拳` is not merely a move Garou happens to know; V09 places it inside a teacher-disciple lineage. The martial name should therefore be indexed both under power/technique and relationship history.

## 8. Terminological cautions

- `怪人` in Garou's V09 mouth is **not equivalent to proven nonhuman biology**.
- `ヒーロー狩り` is an operational/social label (“Hero Hunter/hunting”) and does not itself settle personhood or monster ontology.
- `フブキ組` is genuinely group/faction language; “Fubuki's friends” is too weak for its initial institutional function.
- `傘下` encodes hierarchical incorporation.
- `先生` in Genos's speech remains a relational title with behavioral force, not a generic honorific.
- `ヒーローネーム` is bureaucratically assigned public identity in this volume, not necessarily chosen persona.

## 9. Claims validated by the Japanese audit

**PASS — high confidence**

1. Garou's origin grievance is explicitly about popular winners and hated losers.
2. Garou describes his monster project in scenario-rewriting terms.
3. Fubuki's recruitment language is hierarchical, not peer-equivalent.
4. Saitama states a hero-role ethic in blunt ordinary speech.
5. Saitama distinguishes refusing Fubuki's subordination from refusing all contact.
6. Genos preserves `先生` and uses concise analytical combat speech.
7. Hero names are externally assigned and should not be treated as self-selected identity claims.

**Do not overclaim**

1. Garou's V09 `怪人` language does not establish physical monsterization.
2. Saitama's `たった独り` formulation does not establish an anti-team ideology.
3. Fubuki's command register does not prove her followers lack genuine attachment or benefit.
4. Genos's analytical register does not establish good proportionality judgment.

## 10. Audit disposition

**PASS / CANONICAL.** No Japanese-language finding requires downgrading a central V09 deep-reading claim. Several require precision: Garou's monsterhood remains aspirational/social at this boundary; Saitama's solitude language targets dependence on weaker subordinates rather than teamwork itself; and Fubuki's organization combines real hierarchy with evidence of real group functioning.
