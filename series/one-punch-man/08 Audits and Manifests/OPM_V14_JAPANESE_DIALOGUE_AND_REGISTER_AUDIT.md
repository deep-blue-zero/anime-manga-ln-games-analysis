# One Punch Man — V14 Japanese Dialogue and Register Audit

## Audit result

**PASS.** The load-bearing claims in `OPM_V14_DEEP_READING.md` are supportable from the Japanese V14 tankobon at the V13 observational ceiling. No V15+ wording is required to stabilize them.

The highest-risk interpretive traps are:

- describing every tournament fighter who eats a Monster Cell as a freely consenting convert when Gouketsu explicitly threatens death for refusal;
- treating Suiryu's initial rejection of monsterization as a mature ethical rejection when his stated reason is substantially aesthetic/self-image based;
- treating Gouketsu's claim about the Monster Association boss as omniscient narrator power ranking;
- treating Bakuzan's statement that monsterization revealed his “real self” as a universal transformation law;
- inferring “more Monster Cells = more power” from Bakuzan's multiple-cell ingestion;
- treating Suiryu's hero aspiration as fully stabilized character reform rather than an explicit but newly formed post-rescue goal;
- treating Genos's comparison between Gouketsu and Saitama as belief that their strength is equal;
- treating Saitama's `知らん` as rejection of Suiryu becoming a hero; the categorical rejection `絶対に断る` applies to discipleship;
- treating the hero-call montage as proof that the depicted heroes literally hear Suiryu across the city; the sequence is a structural/thematic montage.

## 1. Gouketsu — human limit and monster rebirth

### Locators
`OPM|V14|chapter:72|images:0017-0020`

### Source wording
Gouketsu describes defeat by a monster, transport to the monsters' lair, and the attraction of exceeding:

`人間のままでは超えられねぇ限界`

He later says:

`そして俺は怪人として生まれ変わったのだ`

and frames his present existence as qualitatively beyond the former martial artist Gouketsu.

### Audit
`人間のままでは` means “while remaining human / as a human.” `超えられねぇ` is rough contracted speech for inability to cross. `生まれ変わった` is reborn / born anew, stronger than a mere temporary power-up description.

### Supported claim
Gouketsu narrates his Monster Cell conversion as a voluntary exchange of continued humanity for power after catastrophic defeat and recruitment.

### Do not promote
- “Gouketsu was never coerced in any sense.” He was defeated, abducted, and then offered a path appealing to his ambition.
- “Every Monster Cell user experiences identity discontinuity identically.”

## 2. Gouketsu — explicit lethal coercion

### Locators
`OPM|V14|chapter:72|images:0020-0022`

### Source wording
`その怪人細胞を生のまま食え!`

`食わなきゃ殺すだけだ`

The proposition is effectively `死ぬか 怪人になるか`.

### Audit
`食わなきゃ` is colloquial conditional “if you don't eat [it].” `殺すだけだ` removes a neutral opt-out. The scene offers a formal choice under immediate lethal threat.

### Supported claim
Tournament Monster Cell recruitment is coercive even though individual fighters have different private motives for accepting.

### Do not promote
- “No one wanted the cells.” Several fighters independently desire power or a new life.
- “Everyone consented freely.” The death threat is explicit.

## 5. Gouketsu — monster freedom pitch

### Locators
`OPM|V14|chapter:72|images:0068-0069`

### Source wording
Gouketsu describes a monster-dominated future in which ordinary systems no longer control strong monsters and says they can:

`好きに暴れ殺す 自由を満喫できる世界だ`

He asks Suiryu to imagine living happily there as a monster.

### Audit
`好きに` means as one likes / freely; `暴れ殺す` combines rampaging and killing; `自由を満喫` is enjoying/savoring freedom to the full. This is not neutral political freedom. It is freedom defined partly as lack of restraint on violence.

### Supported claim
The Monster Association recruits Suiryu through a violent version of freedom-through-strength.

## 6. Suiryu — genuine consideration, then aesthetic refusal

### Locators
`OPM|V14|chapter:72|images:0070-0071`

### Source wording
`なるほどねぇ…どんな社会でも自分が楽しく過ごせるなら問題はない…か`

Then, after considering the cell:

`だけど生憎 アンタみたいなブサイクになるのは絶対に嫌なんでね`

### Audit
The first line is genuine conditional consideration: if he can live enjoyably, perhaps the social order is not a problem. `ブサイク` is blunt “ugly/unattractive.” `絶対に嫌` is categorical refusal on that stated ground.

### Supported claim
Suiryu's existing telos makes Gouketsu's offer conceptually legible; his explicit first refusal is substantially self-image/aesthetic rather than a developed civic objection.

### Do not promote
- “Suiryu endorses monster mass killing.” He considers the world proposition but does not join.
- “Suiryu rejects monsterization because it is immoral.” That is not his stated reason here.

# Audit disposition

**PASS / CANONICAL.**

The Japanese supports the V14 deep reading's principal distinctions:

- Monster Cell conversion versus consent state;
- freedom language versus violent license;
- Suiryu's aesthetic first refusal versus later ethical revaluation;
- defeat/fear as bodily experience;
- weaker heroes' rear-guard action as protective duty;
- the Monster Association's explicit three-objective campaign architecture;
- Bakuzan's self-interpreted sadistic telos;
- Suiryu's reclassification of hero behavior as `優しさ`;
- Saitama's plain rescue register;
- Genos's conservative post-defeat threat response;
- Suiryu's new hero goal versus the separately refused disciple request.

Checkpoint-A may now be reopened downstream. This audit itself does not score predictions.
