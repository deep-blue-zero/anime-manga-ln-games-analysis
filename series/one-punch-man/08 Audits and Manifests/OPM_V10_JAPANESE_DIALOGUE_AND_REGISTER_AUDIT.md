# One Punch Man — V10 Japanese Dialogue and Register Audit

## Disposition

**PASS.** The Japanese-language claims promoted in `OPM_V10_DEEP_READING.md` are supported by the V10 tankobon. This audit is selective: it records wording whose register, semantic range, or pragmatic function materially affects characterization or thematic inference. It is not a line-by-line translation.

Source: `One Punch Man - Volume 10 [Japanese].cbz`, Drive ID `15nYfH2GVfM8Api0o7yQCuTT9FbPL9vhH`, 214 manga images, SHA-256 `cae2eb9695232d31f1464062039cb6d8dbb9594067914ac9756ee3063523a347`.

## 1. Mumen Rider: Garou is a human who calls himself a monster

In `48撃目 バナナ`, Mumen Rider's wording is unusually precise:

- `彼は人間だったよ`
- `人間だった…怪人を名乗る人間だ…`
- `あるいはまだ人間と表現するべきか`

The important distinction is not merely that Garou *looks* human. Mumen explicitly categorizes him as a human who **calls himself** a monster, while leaving open whether future evidence could complicate that description. V10 therefore supports separation among body/ontology, self-label, social category, and behavior.

Do not translate this into an already-completed biological monster transformation.

## 2. `強さの種類が違う` / `技`

Mumen and Tanktop Master's hospital discussion isolates a distinct kind of strength:

- `強さの種類が違う…`
- `「技」だ`
- `奴は高度な武術を使う`

`技` here is not decorative praise for a named move. The scene explicitly contrasts technique with the kind of strength Tanktop Master is accustomed to using. V10 may therefore promote **technique as a first-class explanatory category** without claiming that technique is always superior to raw power.

## 3. Saitama's martial curiosity is understated but real

Saitama tells Charanko:

- `ちょっと興味が湧いたんだよ 何かツテとかない?`

`ちょっと` keeps the surface register characteristically casual, but `興味が湧いた` plus the immediate request for a connection and later tournament entry support genuine activation. The line should not be flattened into either total enthusiasm or mere politeness.

Later, in the Super Fight waiting room:

- `本当の戦いか……覚えてねぇな その感覚`

This is best read as loss of experiential access to the feeling of a “real fight.” It is not, by itself, a diagnostic statement about depression, trauma, or any other clinical state.

## 4. Saitama fails to recognize Garou's intended identity

When Garou ambushes him, Saitama's register is ordinary street-conflict language:

- `何すんだ 誰だテメー`
- `カツアゲする気か?`
- `買い物の邪魔すんな`

The pragmatic force matters more than literal gloss. Saitama does not address an infamous `ヒーロー狩り`, martial rival, or unusual combatant. He treats Garou as an unidentified nuisance interfering with shopping. This is direct evidence that Garou's intended social identity fails to transmit in their first contact.

## 5. Garou and Tareo: rough register, different pragmatic functions

Garou's ordinary speech with Tareo preserves his rough, casual voice:

- `怖い方がいい それがかっこいいと思わないか?`

The line helps connect his monster preference to aesthetics and self-mythology rather than to a technical definition of monsterhood.

During a real monster emergency he tells Tareo:

- `今の聞いただろ お前もダッシュで家に帰れよ`

The register remains rough and non-tender, but its pragmatic function is plainly protective: get home immediately. This supports selective practical concern without converting Garou into a generally benevolent actor.

## 6. Metal Bat: `気合い` is a performance concept, not empty bravado

Metal Bat repeatedly uses:

- `気合いがありゃ大抵なんとかなるんだよ`
- `久々に気合いが入ったぜ`

`気合い` can be translated in several ways—fighting spirit, drive, resolve, getting fired up—but V10 couples the term to visible rising performance under damage and activation. Treating it as mere “motivation” understates the combat-state function the manga is presenting. The source does not yet establish a complete mechanism or limitless scaling rule.

## 7. Tatsumaki: performative reluctance versus the need to be useful

In `タツマキの休日`, Tatsumaki says:

- `まぁどうしても私の力が必要っていうなら仕方ないけど 仕方なくよ`
- `は? 別に休みたいなんて言ってないでしょ`
- `暇だわ…`
- `私が戦わなくても平和だなんて…何なのよ`

The repetition of `仕方ない` / `仕方なく` performs reluctance while preserving the possibility of being indispensable. Her later irritation when no request arrives and her self-initiated search for monsters make the contrast explicit. The safest formulation is not “Tatsumaki secretly loves work” but that **being needed and uniquely useful is materially tied to her private self-structure at V10**.

## 8. `センス`: clothing, help, and Saitama's anti-prescriptive register

To the bullied youth, Saitama says of his hero suit:

- `格好良いだろ 貸してやる`
- `今日はこれ着て帰れ`

The action is direct and low-ceremony. He gives up the clothing he intended to use in the contest, then improvises a much worse substitute.

His broader line is:

- `ヒーローの服装に決まりなんてないしな`

This rejects a single mandatory visual code for heroism. It does **not** establish that costume, communication, recognizability, or design never matter. The chapter itself allows a narrow costume-judging rubric to be legitimate while exposing how little that rubric captures about heroic worth.

## 9. Fubuki learns contrary evidence

In `数字`, Fubuki's assessment of Saitama includes:

- `無理よ`
- `強いのよ 彼は`
- `その戦力は おそらくB級で一番`
- `仲間に引き込んでおいて損はない…`

This is important because she is not linguistically denying inconvenient evidence. She updates upward on Saitama's strength while continuing to route the fact through recruitment and rank strategy.

When she labels the opposing side `サイタマ組`, Saitama answers in effect that no such group exists. The contrast supports the distinction between Fubuki's deliberate faction model and Saitama's unselfconscious callable network.

## 10. Saitama's critique: `順位`, `数値`, and `上っ面`

Saitama tells Fubuki:

- `お前は順位とか数値とか上っ面の部分を気にしすぎなんだよ`
- `それじゃ素の本気を出せないだろ`
- `本当に強くなりたいんなら…`
- `周りを気にすんな!!`

`上っ面` is the key qualification. The scene criticizes her overinvestment in surface/external indicators and other people's comparative position. It does **not** say every number is false or every rank is meaningless. V10 itself contains measurements and classifications that are locally useful.

Fubuki's response later includes:

- `あなたや姉が私より強くたって関係ない`
- `私は私のやり方でいく!`
- `今回の負けは認めるわ`

This is a real shift toward self-authorship and explicit admission of loss. It is not a completed cure for comparative fixation.

The final `カロリー計算中` coda deliberately restores measurement behavior in a low-stakes domain and prevents overreading the preceding declaration as total rejection of numbers.

## 11. Child Emperor's metric vocabulary

`オカメちゃん` is presented as measuring **physical strength / bodily strength**, not universal combat power. The chapter's language and calibration should therefore remain domain-bounded.

Important outputs include:

- ordinary hero/civilian comparison numbers;
- `測定不能` for Saitama;
- an explicit upper range ending at `9999`;
- Fubuki's very low physical score despite obvious psychic capability;
- a monster whose score changes sharply after a bodily state change.

`測定不能` means the device cannot return a valid measurement in its configured range/model. Child Emperor's first interpretation—that Saitama is extraordinarily weak—is an inference, not something the machine itself semantically proves. The same failure surface can hide opposite causes.

## 12. Register-level model consequences

V10 supports the following promoted claims:

1. **Garou:** `怪人` remains self-label/social aspiration while the manga explicitly retains human categorization.
2. **Saitama:** recognized novelty can activate curiosity even when his speech stays casual; unrecognized novelty can pass as mundane nuisance.
3. **Metal Bat:** `気合い` is a recurring self-description tied to observable combat activation.
4. **Tatsumaki:** defensive/reluctant phrasing coexists with boredom and a desire to be needed.
5. **Fubuki:** rank/number fixation is real, but so is her ability to update, admit a loss, and articulate `私のやり方`.
6. **Child Emperor:** a valid narrow measurement can support a false conclusion when the inference model omits relevant dimensions or saturation states.
