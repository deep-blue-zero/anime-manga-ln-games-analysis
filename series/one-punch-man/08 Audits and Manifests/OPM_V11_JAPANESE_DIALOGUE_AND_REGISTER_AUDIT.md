# One Punch Man — V11 Japanese Dialogue and Register Audit

## 1. Audit result

**PASS.** The load-bearing V11 character and institutional claims in `OPM_V11_DEEP_READING.md` are supportable from the Japanese tankobon. No later-volume wording is required to stabilize them.

This audit is not a full transcription. It isolates lines whose lexical/register distinctions materially control character modeling, ontology, institutional interpretation, or claim revision.

## 2. Metal Bat / Zenko — violence can be relationship-conditioned

### `お兄ちゃんは私の前で暴力見せないって約束したの!`
### `だからもう終わり!`
### `終わり!! ケンカ終わり!!!`

**Locator:** `58撃目 大怪蟲`, images `0083-0087`.

Zenko does not appeal to Hero Association rank, public duty, or fear. She invokes a **promise** (`約束`) and the specific condition `私の前で` — “in front of me.” Metal Bat’s response is immediate and emphatic.

**Model effect:** the prohibition is relationship- and context-conditioned, not evidence that Metal Bat rejects violence generally. It is a prior sibling norm that overrides his active combat state.

**Do not overread:** Zenko does not physically defeat either combatant, and the scene does not prove that Metal Bat will obey every request she makes in every domain.

## 3. Garou — surveillance aversion and self-authored identity

### `俺は観察されんのが大嫌いなんだよ`

**Locator:** `59撃目 お前だけ`, image `0094`.

`観察されんの` is concrete: Garou objects to **being observed/studied**. `大嫌い` is stronger than mild dislike.

**Model effect:** Garou can seek notoriety and still resist becoming another actor’s object of analysis/control. Public visibility and instrumental surveillance are not equivalent for him.

### `怪人協会?`
### `興味ねぇ 失せろ`

**Locator:** `59撃目`, images `0095-0097`.

`興味ねぇ` is a blunt colloquial rejection (“not interested”), and `失せろ` is an aggressive dismissal (“get lost / disappear”). There is no deferential hedging or curiosity about belonging.

**Ontology effect:** Garou’s repeated self-description through `怪人` cannot be equated with `怪人協会` membership. At V11 the text directly supplies a negative organizational test: he is offered affiliation and refuses it.

**Required boundary:** this does not prove he will reject every later monster approach under all future conditions. It establishes V11 state only.

## 4. Phoenix Man — strategic rather than purely reactive register

Phoenix Man’s recruitment sequence uses controlled explanatory language rather than Garou’s blunt street register. After the invitation is destroyed, he does not immediately treat insult as requiring revenge; he preserves the possibility of future contact if Garou continues hero hunting.

**Locator:** `59撃目`, images `0095-0098`.

**Organization effect:** field agents can subordinate immediate ego response to a longer organizational plan. This supports a Monster Association “strategic patience” claim without importing later hierarchy information.

## 5. Monster Association operational discipline

Rhino Wrestler’s scoring language is boastful and self-evaluative, but Phoenix Man’s corrective register is operational: the current action is not the true main event, arbitrary fighting is not permitted, and resources should not be wasted on Metal Knight’s remotely operated machine.

**Locator:** `59撃目`, images `0099-0102`.

The key semantic distinction is between individual monster appetite and organization-level operation. V11 directly presents them as separable.

## 6. Hostage selection — deliberate provocation

Phoenix Man orders that the child, rather than both parent and child, be taken because the human side’s reaction will be more useful/interesting.

**Locator:** `59撃目`, image `0102`.

**Claim control:** this supports **deliberate hostage strategy**. It should not yet be expanded into a complete theory of the Association’s negotiation demands, which V11 does not disclose.

## 7. Hero Association overload

### `各市で同時多発的に`
### `災害レベル鬼以上の怪人が一斉に出現するなんて…!`
### `対応が追いつかない!`

**Locator:** `59撃目`, images `0107-0114`.

The register is emergency-administrative rather than moralizing. `同時多発的` and `一斉に` foreground simultaneity; `対応が追いつかない` explicitly states response capacity cannot keep pace.

**Institutional effect:** the V11 failure is not evidence that the Association has no procedures. It is evidence that simultaneous demand exceeds those procedures’ available capacity.

## 8. Saitama — weak tournament opposition and status register

Saitama enters Super Fight under Charanko’s name while other participants invoke school legitimacy, seniority, reputation, and threats. His responses remain compressed and often track mundane/socially lateral details rather than martial prestige.

**Locator:** `60撃目 入場`, images `0130-0134`; `61撃目 ダークホース`, images `0172-0175`.

The first-round result is almost immediate. The language and panel economy do not support interpreting Saitama as ceremonially impressed by the tournament field merely because he became curious about `武術` in V10.

**Model effect:** interest in a domain is not deference to its status hierarchy.

## 9. Suiryu — casual confidence rather than duty language

Suiryu is introduced through prize-money interest and desire for an entertaining/strong opponent. He recognizes Bakuzan’s hostile intent without visible linguistic panic and later treats Saitama’s fast first-round result as potentially fixing the identity of a meaningful finalist.

**Locator:** `60撃目`, images `0126-0129`; `61撃目`, image `0175`.

**Readiness effect:** sufficient for an `emerging` model only. V11 does not establish Suiryu’s behavior under civic duty, defeat, intimate relationship, or sustained moral pressure.

## 10. Bang / Bomb — shared Garou problem

### `ガロウを捜そう`
### `奴がこうなっちまう前にな`

**Locator:** `60撃目`, images `0134-0143`.

The wording is prospective and preventive. It does **not** establish a later transformed state as fact. Bang/Bomb are acting because they fear where Garou is going.

The exchange also establishes Bomb as someone Bang can coordinate with directly outside Hero Association command.

## 11. `戦隊` — Saitama’s team-language experiment

### `…チームっぽくなってきたな`

**Locator:** `番外編 戦隊`, image `0184`.

This line is important because it blocks an overly rigid “Saitama hates teams” model. At the beginning of the real mission, he is amused/engaged by the team structure after watching `ZOOMEN`.

He then proposes color/codename logic, only to be reminded that his real hero name is `ハゲマント`. His enthusiasm collapses partly through embarrassment/comic mismatch, not through an abstract ideological rejection of cooperation.

## 12. `戦隊` — Wild Horn’s operational authority

Wild Horn acts as team leader and distributes practical functions. Saitama accepts map duty. Later Wild Horn criticizes him for breaking route discipline and explains that in emergencies individual action creates a collective problem.

The load-bearing term is:

### `連帯責任`

**Locator:** `番外編 戦隊`, images `0189-0190`.

Here the phrase functions as **collective responsibility for team failure**, initially in a disciplinary sense.

Saitama does not challenge Wild Horn simply because Wild Horn is another B-Class hero. He follows the mission structure until the role mismatch becomes evident. This is consistent with a distinction between **status deference** and **practical authority**.

## 13. `戦隊` — Saitama repurposes `連帯責任`

### `連帯責任ってことで!`

**Locator:** `番外編 戦隊`, image `0202`.

This occurs as Saitama bursts out of Urokodon with the swallowed teammates.

**Character effect:** Saitama retains the team’s language but transforms its meaning. The phrase moves from “if one member causes trouble, all share responsibility” to a rescue action in which he takes responsibility for getting everyone out.

**Do not overread:** the line is comedic and does not make Saitama a convert to permanent team organization. The extra itself has him conclude that formal team operations are not a good personal fit.

## 14. `戦隊` — self-fit versus universal doctrine

Inside/after the failed operation, Saitama indicates that the team plan failed and that he has learned this kind of operation does not suit him. The closing King/game sequence immediately destabilizes any universalized anti-team interpretation.

**Audit conclusion:** translate the claim as **“formal team operation does not suit Saitama well”**, not “Saitama believes cooperation is bad” or “Saitama refuses to work with others.”

The distinction is supported by his actual actions in the same extra: he accepts a role, notices useful evidence, rescues everyone, and continues ordinary cooperative activity afterward.

## 15. Hero Association incentive distortion in `戦隊`

The Z-branch mission is designed as a live capture because Urokodon’s blood is expected to have medical/regenerative value. A later report reveals the reinforced enclosure should have required at least Demon-level force to break, while the B-Class team was operating from an outdated lower-threat premise.

**Locator:** `番外編 戦隊`, images `0191-0192`.

The language supports three separate claims:

1. live capture was intentional;
2. the target’s condition/power had changed materially;
3. the team did not receive a risk model matching current reality.

It does **not** justify claiming every Hero Association science/medical project is corrupt.

## 16. Metal Bat — weapon identity

### `折れないバットをくれ`
### `とびきり頑丈な金属バットをくれ`

**Locator:** `巻末おまけ 武器`, images `0208-0209`.

The Association offers bespoke weapons/equipment as an S-Class recognition benefit. Metal Bat does not ask for a new weapon system; he asks for an unbreakable / exceptionally sturdy metal bat.

**Model effect:** equipment preference preserves his existing embodied fighting style. The desired upgrade removes breakage rather than replacing the style with technological complexity.

## 18. Translation-risk controls

- `怪人` for Garou remains identity/role language at V11; do not silently convert it to biological species state.
- `怪人協会` is an actual organization and must remain distinct from Garou’s self-label.
- `気合い` should not be translated analytically as a quantified infinite-power mechanic. “Fighting spirit / activation” is safer at this boundary.
- `連帯責任` is context-sensitive: first disciplinary collective liability, later Saitama’s comic rescue reframing.
- `対応が追いつかない` is capacity failure, not proof of total institutional collapse.
- Bang/Bomb’s `奴がこうなっちまう前にな` is prospective concern, not evidence of later Garou ontology.
- Metal Bat’s request for a durable bat is equipment preference, not proof of technological hostility.
