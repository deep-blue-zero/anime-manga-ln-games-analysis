# One Punch Man — V04 Japanese Dialogue and Register Audit
## Saitama, Genos, Bang, Sonic, Puri-Puri Prisoner, and public-scrutiny voice

## 1. Audit disposition

**PASS WITH MODEL REFINEMENT.**

No checked Japanese line requires reversal of the V04 deep reading. The audit produces four important reconstruction refinements:

1. Saitama's desire for recognition must remain distinct from reliance on public evaluation as a motive authority.
2. Saitama's rough register intensifies dramatically under false public blame; “deadpan” is not a sufficient voice model.
3. Bang's relaxed elder register operates as crisis regulation rather than a literal doctrine of carelessness.
4. Sonic's prison voice is materially less humiliation-activated than his Saitama-facing rivalry voice, strengthening the case for relationship-conditioned language.

The audit uses the canonical tankobon images only. No anime/translation/webcomic wording is used as authority.

## 2. Saitama — crisis identity

### `じいさん こいつ任せるぞ`
Locator: `OPM|V04|chapter:21|image:0065`

Saitama addresses Bang as `じいさん` despite Bang's age and S-class standing. The phrase is colloquial and directive, not deferential. More importantly, the object is Genos: Saitama notices his condition, leaves him with another capable person, and moves to the meteor.

**Model consequence:** concern is expressed operationally rather than through affective reassurance. Do not require sentimental language before modeling protective responsibility.

### `俺はヒーローをやっている者だ`
Locator: `image:0066`

This is a post-registration variation on earlier self-identification. `俺` remains the default masculine first-person pronoun. `ヒーローをやっている` continues to frame hero as an activity/practice rather than a grand title.

`趣味で` is absent here. The omission should not be overinterpreted as rejection of the prior identity; context has changed because Saitama is now registered and is answering a stranger during a mass emergency.

**Model consequence:** preserve plain self-definition; do not generate ceremonious rank-based introductions unless later evidence supports them.

### `避難してな じいさん`
Locator: `image:0067`

Casual imperative/advisory. Again no honorific rank accommodation. The line is socially brusque but functionally protective.

## 3. Saitama — public hostility register

### `文句がありゃ言ってみろ 聞いてやる`
Locator: `OPM|V04|chapter:22|image:0125`

Key forms:
- contracted `ありゃ`;
- challenge-form `言ってみろ`;
- rough but still interaction-opening `聞いてやる`.

Saitama does not initially flee the confrontation. He invites the complaint in a combative register.

### `うるせぇ だまれ`
### `そんなもん隕石に言えボケ`
### `俺はテメェらの評価が欲しくてヒーローやってんじゃねぇからな`
### `俺がやりたくてやってんだ`
Locator: `image:0126`

These lines provide the strongest V01-V04 evidence for Saitama's high-activation rough speech.

Markers include:
- `うるせぇ` rather than neutral `うるさい`;
- `テメェら` hostile second-person plural;
- contracted `～じゃねぇ`;
- imperative `だまれ`;
- insult `ボケ`;
- `～やってんだ` colloquial explanatory contraction.

This is not merely volume-level comedy. It establishes a conditional voice regime:

`false collective blame / reputational coercion -> lexical roughening + direct causal rejection + motive statement`.

**Do not flatten into:** “Saitama always speaks rudely.”

His baseline remains plain and colloquial. V04 shows how far roughness can escalate when he feels the premise itself is illegitimate.

## 4. Recognition wording audit

The crucial clause is:

> `俺はテメェらの評価が欲しくてヒーローやってんじゃねぇからな`

Grammatically and contextually, the target is **motive**: he is not doing hero work *because he wants their evaluation*. It does not assert that he never wants recognition or evaluation in any sense.

That distinction preserves V02 evidence such as `知名度が低い`, the desire for fans, and frustration over misclassification.

### Adjudication

- “Saitama does not care about recognition” — **REJECT**.
- “Saitama's hero activity is motivated by public approval” — **REJECT**.
- “Saitama can desire social legibility while retaining internally authorized hero motivation” — **STRENGTHEN**.

This is the canonical V04 reconstruction formulation.

## 5. Genos — crisis cognition

### `まあ 落ち着け`
Locator: `OPM|V04|chapter:21|image:0053`

The line occurs inside a dense sequence of causal modeling:
- target proximity;
- whether his power can destroy the meteor;
- what happens if the attack hits;
- whether fragmentation itself produces catastrophe.

`まあ 落ち着け` therefore functions as explicit self-regulation after analytical recursion. It is not evidence that Genos normally approaches danger casually.

**Model consequence:** under catastrophic uncertainty Genos can attempt deliberate cognitive stabilization before committing power.

## 6. Bang — elderly-masculine crisis register

### `心に乱れが見える`
### `おぬしは失敗を考えるにはまだ若すぎるのう`
Locator: `image:0054`

Voice markers:
- `おぬし`;
- `～のう`;
- `ワシ` elsewhere in the chapter;
- low-speed advisory phrasing against Genos's high-density internal analysis.

Bang is not speaking bureaucratically as S-class rank 3. His authority is age/experience-coded rather than institutional in register.

Genos's subsequent compression of Bang's advice toward `適当がベスト?` should not be treated as an exact restatement of Bang's philosophy. The chapter supports a narrower interpretation: Genos is over-focusing on failure when the action window is closing.

**Audit ruling:** retain “crisis regulation / anti-paralysis advice,” not “Bang believes careless action is always best.”

## 7. Genos / Metal Knight interaction

Genos's request for cooperation is functional rather than status-challenging. He treats another S-class actor as a potential collaborator because the threat is civic and shared.

Metal Knight's refusal is likewise important linguistically/behaviorally because it leaves no evidence that he misunderstands the situation; his stated priority is testing weapons.

**Model consequence:** Genos's competitive/strength-oriented identity does not prevent cooperative action toward an external threat.

## 8. Sonic — prison register

In the prison material and Deep Sea King transition, Sonic is notably more controlled than in his Saitama rematch scenes.

### `礼をいう`
Locator: `OPM|V04|chapter:24|image:0178`

The phrase is terse and formal-neutral enough to sound controlled rather than affectionate. He is acknowledging an instrumental benefit created by Puri-Puri Prisoner's breakout, not expressing relational warmth.

The bonus chapter reinforces:
- contempt toward prison hierarchy;
- concise superiority claims;
- violence without the extended humiliation rhetoric seen around Saitama.

**Model consequence:** Saitama-specific defeat activates a more obsessive/rhetorically intense Sonic. Baseline Sonic is still proud and violent but can be much cooler and more economical.

## 9. Puri-Puri Prisoner — theatrical intimacy and coercive content

### `S級ヒーロー ぷりぷりプリズナー`
### `あなたに会いに脱獄成功!`
Locator: `image:0176`

He introduces himself theatrically and frames jailbreak through personal/romanticized address.

### Prisoner 418 / predatory statement
Locator: `image:0178`

Puri tells Sonic he has checked all the men he likes and states that he is perpetually imprisoned because he cannot restrain himself and ends up attacking men (`男子を襲っちゃうせいで万年服役中`).

The diminutive/intimate style (`ソニックちゃん`) should not cause the content to be sanitized. Voice reconstruction has to preserve both:
- flamboyant, intimate, affectionate-seeming surface;
- coercive/predatory behavioral meaning.

Sonic's reaction is negative and nonreciprocal.

## 10. Stinger / performative register

Stinger's hero presentation is deliberately public and theatrical. His speech/pose structure advertises the hero persona rather than minimizing it.

The behavioral context prevents a false inference:

`performative register -> fake courage` is unsupported.

He enters real danger and continues fighting multiple monsters. For future modeling, public-facing bravado should be preserved as style, not automatically interpreted as compensation or cowardice.

---

## 11. Rank-addressing rule through V04

A useful cross-character contrast now exists:

- Saitama does not substantially adjust politeness upward for Bang's S-class/age status;
- Genos uses relationship-conditioned deference toward `サイタマ先生` but remains functional with other elite heroes;
- Bang's seniority is encoded through elder pronouns/endings rather than formal bureaucratic language;
- public hero personae such as Stinger/Puri can be highly theatrical without sharing a common politeness system.

**Do not generate hero rank as a universal Japanese register hierarchy.** Relationship, personality, age coding, and situation dominate.
