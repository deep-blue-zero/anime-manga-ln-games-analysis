## Executive synthesis

The uncollected layer extends V37's question—how is value known?—into a larger question: **who governs the conditions under which heroic capacity is produced and used?** Systems can rescue, train and improve people while also making their bodies and performances governable. Accurate recognition and useful output therefore cannot, by themselves, settle institutional legitimacy.

Neo Heroes promises to fix Hero Association weakness through capital, recruitment, equipment, telemetry, publicity and coordinated deployment. It produces real capacity. It also makes bodies, reputations and combat patterns administratively legible. Webigaza's modification, Darkshine's reassignment, Blue's inherited brand, Suiryu's livestreamed work and the New Blood operations show that legibility can support, market or control a person.

The same structure appears outside Neo Heroes:

- King's false reputation prevents accurate training.
- Flash's hierarchy fails to measure Saitama.
- The ninja village turns children into reproducible technique.
- Blast resists treating Empty Void as disposable output.
- Amai Mask's public image cannot contain the relation between body and conduct.
- Machine God Mirror turns readable hero techniques into a copied weapon.
- Garou and Suiryu learn one another through reciprocal adjustment rather than data extraction.
- Saitama repeatedly resolves a local event while remaining unavailable to the system that wants the result to be measurable, ownable and narratable.

The provisional arc is a conflict between forms of recognition and between claims of self-authorship and control over the conditions of action. A wearer reports an involuntary lunge after a suit update; Webigaza says Neo maintenance is necessary to live and she cannot leave; she reports satellite pursuit of Amai; A invokes Neo affiliation while describing killing as a game. These distinct observations and attributed statements do not establish one universal controller. Child Emperor, Sekingar and Metal Bat's covert cooperation supplies a countercurrent inside the same institution (PR-CR-015,022,024,025,027; web285/pages 3-5, web286/pages 9-11, web287/pages 2-12; available through web287).

## Major provisional findings

### 1. Neo Heroes is a credible institution and an unresolved threat

The organization recruits dissatisfied talent, mobilizes quickly and coordinates simultaneous response. Its competence is textually real. A critique that treats every Neo success as fake would reproduce the Hero Association's defensive blindness. [web235/pages 2-35; web266/pages 2-17; web268/pages 2-19]

The ethical danger comes from how success is produced: suits, modifications, operators, ratings, publicity, bodily maintenance and opaque handlers. Web285-287 changes the warrant: the wearer reports an involuntary lunge after an update; the superior describes exploiting public anxiety while accumulating data; Webigaza explicitly reports life-dependent maintenance and inability to leave. These are specific mechanisms and attributed statements, not merely a generalized suspicion of technology. [web251/pages 11-16; web253/pages 2-10; web267/pages 2-16; web285/pages 3-8; web286/pages 9-11; PR-CR-015,016,022]

### 2. Reputation is causal even when false or incomplete

King's legend prevents accurate instruction even when he declares weakness and asks for help. His continued entrapment cannot be explained solely by secrecy or passivity: reception can defeat an attempt at correction. Blue's lineage claim supplies authority before verification; Amai's ideal image disciplines the body that bears it; Garou's history shapes how his present can be received. Reputation changes available action, not only the accuracy of a spectator's opinion. These effects remain particular: neither every teacher nor every civilian is shown to misread every hero. [PR-CR-002,008,009,013; web237/pages 2-35; web253/pages 11-17; web255/pages 2-17; web279/pages 2-15; available incrementally through web279]

### 3. The body is neither proof of virtue nor a neutral container

Darkshine's powerful body coexists with fear. Webigaza's engineered body coexists with self-claimed agency and explicitly reported dependency. Amai's monster body coexists with violent history, aspiration and a costly protective act. Genos's upgraded body supplies real technical resistance while exposing readable signatures to copying. Garou's labor and restraint test an ordinary continuation of the rehabilitation already begun in the collected record. None of these bodies supplies a complete biography or moral verdict. The relevant questions concern what the person does, under which constraints, and toward whom. [PR-CR-006-008,012,013,022; web251/pages 2-16; web263/pages 2-15; web275/pages 2-17; web282/pages 2-24; web286/pages 9-11; available through web286]

### 4. Care is the refusal to make a dangerous person disposable

Blast's aim toward Empty Void, Saitama's response to Amai and Bang's continued observation of Garou differ in tone and history. Each nevertheless preserves a person beyond a threat label. Recovery-oriented care is an attempt to keep a future available, not a verdict that harm has been repaired. Bang's observation does not complete restitution; Saitama's recognition does not annul Amai's violence; Blast's aim does not settle Void's responsibility or condition. These limits give care its difficulty rather than discrediting it. [PR-CR-004,008,013; web245/pages 8-20; web262/pages 8-21; web283/pages 9-17; available through web283]

### 5. Saitama remains an epistemic interruption

Saitama does not found the better institution. He repeatedly notices or does something the surrounding model cannot process:

- he refuses Flash's disciple frame;
- he interrupts Empty Void's scale;
- he recognizes Amai's heroic conduct after bodily exposure;
- he defeats a machine assembled from legible hero signatures.

His limitation is equally important: these exact recognitions do not automatically become durable public systems. The Amai judgment remains local while Webigaza's hostility and reported pursuit continue; the V37 failure to give Genos useful feedback remains a genuine limit. Saitama can expose the insufficiency of a model without understanding its mechanism or replacing the institution that uses it. Nor does refusing an imposed frame mean refusing cooperation or all practical authority. [PR-CR-003,004,008,012,024; web238/pages 7-32; web248/pages 4-20; web263/pages 2-15; web277/pages 9-17; web287/pages 2-4; available through web287]

### 6. Learning differs ethically according to the relation it creates

The ninja village, Neo telemetry, Mirror and Garou/Suiryu all develop or acquire capacity, but they do not make the same claim on the people involved. The village instrumentalizes children; Neo coordinates useful action while recording performance; Mirror makes known signatures available to an adversary; Garou and Suiryu mutually adjust without initially possessing each other's history. Learning is neither inherently liberating nor merely extractive. Its conditions, reciprocity and use matter. Garou's restraint gives that distinction a behavioral test rather than completing a redemption plot. [PR-CR-003,010,012,013; web241/pages 2-23; web268/pages 2-19; web275/pages 2-17; web276/pages 2-12; web280-283; available through web283]

## Institutional model

The Hero Association remains burdened by corruption, sponsor logic, reputation error and weak coordination, alongside real competence and particular accurate judgments. Neo Heroes answers actual failures with resources and coordination. Its danger cannot be assessed only as another mistake in measuring people: it can help create the capacity whose use it then mediates. Treating a member as a performance endpoint obscures the person's continued right to contradict the role or refuse a use of the body.

A durable judgment therefore requires four separate questions:

1. Did the system produce useful action?
2. What did the action cost the acting body?
3. Who owns the data, equipment and public story?
4. Can the person refuse, leave or contradict the role?

The captured material answers the first question repeatedly and makes the others increasingly urgent. Webigaza is the crucial test of a simple victim/empowerment binary. She describes choosing strength as the expression she could not achieve through training and refuses regret. She also says she cannot leave Neo because continued life depends on its maintenance facilities. Both claims matter: choice at entry and control over future exit are different ethical questions. Her self-responsibility cannot certify consent to every later institutional act. Alternatives, maintenance intervals and enforceable refusal rights remain unknown. [PR-CR-021,022; web286/pages 4-11; available web286]

Child Emperor listens without endorsing her accusation or supplying a clinical guarantee. Metal Bat recognizes resolve while refusing a strategy that exposes his sister or civilians to danger for data. Sekingar suspects adults have exploited uncertainty around the bargain. Their coordination locates an ethical resource inside affiliation: immediate obligations and particular relations can contest management's aims. It establishes neither a repaired Neo nor successful protection of Amai; contact, satellite access and interference remain plans. [PR-CR-017,023,025,026; web285/pages 8-9; web286/pages 7-12; web287/pages 5-7; available through web287]

The resulting series argument is provisional but stronger than either institutional nostalgia or faith in the exceptional individual. Useful action, truthful recognition and preservation of another person's agency require separate scrutiny. They can coincide, as coordination and care sometimes do, or diverge, as disclosure, medical dependence and pursuit show. The web continuation makes their relation an ongoing practical problem rather than a conclusion supplied by victory. The [owning thematic arguments](../03%20Longitudinal%20Ledgers%20and%20Checkpoints/OPM_HEROISM_RECOGNITION_RANK_AND_INSTITUTION_LEDGER.md#current-release-overlay-through-web-287) preserve the collected antecedents and distinguish these mechanisms.
