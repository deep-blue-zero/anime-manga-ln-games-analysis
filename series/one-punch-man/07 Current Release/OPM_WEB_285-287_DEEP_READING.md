## Central argument and counter-reading

Neo Heroes' resources are becoming a means of governing action: software moves a body, medical access limits exit, satellite systems search for a person, and the hero label licenses an armed operation. Yet the people inside this system are not interchangeable. Darkshine responds to an injured trainee with concern; Webigaza knowingly owns an irreversible choice while distrusting the institution that sustains her; Child Emperor listens without surrendering his investigation; Metal Bat refuses the logic of delaying rescue for strategic advantage.

The strongest counter-reading is that these chapters simply reveal an evil organization and another assassin encounter. The managerial dialogue and murder-as-game speech support that reading. It is incomplete because Webigaza's agency and the investigators' cooperation occupy the same organization. Institutional affiliation does not identify a shared moral purpose. The sharper conflict concerns who may author another person's conduct, and whether claimed consent survives control over the conditions of continued life.

## Web 285: an improving system and the people it moves

### Darkshine and the involuntary attack — pages 3-5

Darkshine invites full-effort sparring in a trainer's familiar encouraging posture. A trainee is dismissive before receiving a software-update notification. The trainee then surges forward with unexpected force, strikes Darkshine, and is thrown back. His statement that his body moved on its own makes authorship of the attack part of the scene. Darkshine catches and checks him rather than celebrating domination; the distressed close-up ends on Darkshine's uncertainty.

This supplies a concrete autonomy warning. It does not establish which person issued the command, whether the software was deliberately weaponized in this particular test, or that every Neo suit can already control every wearer. It also does not prove Darkshine's psychological recovery or a new combat ceiling: trainer confidence, bodily resilience and fear under other conditions remain separate variables.

### The executive account and Metal Bat's refusal — pages 2, 6-9

McCoy reports on S-Class heroes to a shadowed Neo superior. The superior explicitly describes gathering combat data, continually improving suits and benefiting while conventional heroes struggle and public anxiety grows. The promise of progress without rest, training or decline is the superior's technological doctrine. The boast about eventually reaching a divine realm is not evidence connecting Neo technology to the entity God.

Metal Bat's contrary standard is immediate protection. Gathering data and pretending cooperation do not excuse letting monsters threaten his sister or other civilians. His anger is specific: strategic delay transfers risk to people who did not consent. This extends his established family priority into a civilian-facing objection, rather than reducing his motive to protecting only Zenko.

### Investigation, the chip and returned leaders — pages 10-15

Sekingar reports that he still lacks access to Neo's data core but has leads concerning unexplained funds and remaining executives connected to Neo beyond McCoy. These are investigatory leads, not completed proof against every pictured executive. Child Emperor is then shown with a small electronic component at a fallen monster before Webigaza interrupts him. A chip in this context makes technological involvement a stronger hypothesis; it does not alone prove who controlled the monster or that every simultaneous attack was manufactured.

インフェルネイシヴ and Zaedats (ゼイダッツ) return in conspicuously affirmative form. The former's printed manga name is retained rather than substituted with a webcomic name. Their followers interpret survival as miracle or renewed corporate leadership; A, the sunglasses-wearing operative named by Sonic in web 287/page 10, interprets it through surgery and the men's continuing economic and supporter value. Metal Bat notices the discrepancy. Surgical intervention and instrumental valuation are explicit; total mind control, unchanged personhood and the two men's informed consent remain unverified. A is not identified here with Erimin or Destro.

## Web 286: Webigaza's choice and its operating conditions

### Trust, ambition and Amai Mask — pages 2-9

Webigaza approaches Child Emperor for conversation, calling him a senior hero. He remains guarded, but she offers a particular history rather than a generic recruitment pitch. She describes professional betrayal, insists on choosing whom to trust and what to pursue, and identifies Amai Mask as the ideal she had followed for two years. For her, strength became the most compelling form of entertainment; effort at training did not make her a naturally qualified professional hero.

Amai's revealed body produces disgust and the feeling that her pursuit was foolish. Webigaza asks whether the Association and S-Class heroes concealed his identity. Child Emperor does not confirm that allegation. His quiet response reframes the exposure as an instance in which Amai ultimately did not keep up the disguise. The contrast concerns conduct and disclosure; it neither excuses Amai's prior violence nor proves what every institutional actor knew.

Webigaza's declared self-authorship has a real limit: her ambition still selects a public ideal and a market demand for extraordinary strength. The choice is hers, but it is not made outside reputation, audience or dependence.

### Modification and inability to leave — pages 9-12

Webigaza describes an introduction through entertainment-industry contacts to a research organization associated with Neo. Enhancement delivered the Neo-leader role she sought. It also left her unable to live long without regular maintenance at Neo's research facilities. She says she cannot leave, does not regret her choice and will not blame others for what follows.

This materially revises the earlier formulation that maintenance dependence was merely a plausible risk. Dependence and restricted exit are now explicit self-report. The duration without treatment, independent medical alternatives and the precise enforcement mechanism remain unknown. Her claim of responsibility is evidence about her self-conception, not proof that Neo's arrangement is fair or that future coercion would become her fault.

Child Emperor asks whether her wish was fulfilled, recognizes the weight of a decision without regret, and offers reassurance. His attention and courtesy broaden his noncombat relationship evidence. They do not certify Neo's safety, grant an actual medical guarantee, or show that he has disclosed his undercover purpose.

## Web 287: sympathy, counter-surveillance and a hero-branded attack

### The Amai search — pages 2-7

Webigaza reports a Neo pursuit using satellite heat detection and facial recognition. The team has difficulty identifying Amai after transformation; she wanted to defeat him for a dramatic promotion, but a commercial shoot now takes priority. These are her reports of the team's capabilities and results. Satellite imagery alone does not prove an omniscient search or Amai's exact location.

After Webigaza leaves, Child Emperor contacts Sekingar and Metal Bat. Metal Bat distinguishes her serious resolve from the altered returning leaders. Sekingar judges the adults corrupt and suspects that uncertainty was deliberately preserved around the modification choice. The team plans to reach Amai, interfere with the pursuit and access the search satellite. The communication establishes coordination; the proposed rescue and satellite interference are not yet completed outcomes.

Child Emperor's private reflection that resentment may help Webigaza maintain herself, followed by a wish that the misunderstanding may someday clear, matters alongside his investigation. He can regard her hostility as a possible support structure and preserve concern for Amai without imposing reconciliation on either person. It is a tentative interpretation of her coping, not privileged access to her mind.

### Sonic and A — pages 8-12

Sonic enters a damaged building and encounters A (エー), the sunglasses-wearing operative seen beside Metal Bat in web 285. A claims Neo affiliation and says Sonic, an S-Class bounty target, is surrounded. Sonic names A and cites the assassin's reputation for killing beyond the contracted number, then rejects the hero label as evidence of moral improvement. That reputation is Sonic's attribution, not an independently inspected catalogue of past murders.

A denies a pleasure-murder characterization only to describe taking lives as a game against eventual death, then orders a suited team to attack. Sonic replies in the same game vocabulary. The exterior explosion is the final image. Neither Sonic's victory nor death, A's survival, the civilian occupancy of the building, or the precise source of every explosive effect is shown. A's claimed encirclement is not independently measured.

## Language, visual form and uncertainty

The repeated polite address around Child Emperor matters: Webigaza treats him as a senior while he answers respectfully. Courtesy supports exchange without proving mutual trust. Her self-description alternates between agency and inability to leave. That tension should remain audible in any character reconstruction.

Darkshine's open trainer pose gives way to the trainee's terrified face and his own alarm. Web 285 then cuts between bodies and executive screens, making improvement in the abstract collide with consequences in the room. The chip close-up scales a city-level crisis down to a manipulable object. Webigaza and Child Emperor sit on a monstrous corpse amid ruined streets; the quiet conversation makes that destruction the setting of aspiration and care. Web 286's large smiling close-up follows a life-dependent bargain, so the smile cannot alone certify freedom from distress. Web 287's private reflection then cuts to Sonic and a final exterior blast: the scene changes before any stabilizing victory account arrives.

God's involvement in Neo, the chip's provenance, the men's degree of alteration, the full controller network, Webigaza's alternatives, Amai's precise status and Sonic's fight result remain OPEN. Saitama appears on title art but has no present narrative action in these three installments; no new Saitama behavior is inferred from the covers.
