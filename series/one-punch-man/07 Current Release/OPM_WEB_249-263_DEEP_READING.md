## Prospective synthesis

### 1. Neo Heroes sells visibility as capacity

Suiryu's entrance is not merely recruitment. He performs heroism to an audience, treats engagement as reward and discovers that Neo Heroes can convert publicity, equipment and spectacle into immediate institutional value. His actual ability is real; the mediation is also real. The tranche refuses the easy choice between “authentic strength” and “mere branding.” [web249/pages 2-15]

Neo leadership then assembles people who share a platform without sharing an end: reform, prestige, money, discipline, family, rivalry, recognition and control all fit under the same logo. This is an effective launch coalition and an unstable moral one. [web250/pages 2-17; web252/pages 2-16]

### 2. A role can preserve function while concealing injury

Darkshine's trainer position gives him usefulness and status, but Raiden's challenge reveals that role assignment has not repaired his willingness to fight. The institution can route his knowledge while leaving his fear structurally untouched. [web250/pages 7-17; web251/pages 2-10]

This is a precise continuation of the collected corpus's distinction between being valued and being understood. Neo Heroes sees something Darkshine can do. That does not mean it sees the condition under which he cannot do it.

### 3. Modified bodies are labor infrastructure

Webigaza is presented as both agent and engineered asset. Her extensive modification gives extraordinary output, but overheating, monitoring and the handlers' own near-total modification make bodily autonomy an institutional question. [web251/pages 11-16; web252/pages 12-16; web253/pages 2-10]

The critical point is not “technology bad.” The pages show capability, chosen ambition, assistance and real strain together. What remains uncertain is the quality of consent: what was chosen, what became necessary after prior modification, and what Neo management can compel once the body is operational infrastructure.

### 4. Amai Mask turns the image/body split into an ethical test

Amai approaches Saitama because he treats the ideal hero as something that must be publicly embodied. The amusement park then externalizes that logic. Pesky Clown becomes more dangerous through attention; Amai fights inside the gaze he helped make authoritative. [web255/pages 2-17; web257/pages 2-17; web258/pages 2-15]

When Amai's monster body becomes visible, the story does not erase his violence or certify innocence. It instead separates three things that public hero culture tends to fuse:

1. bodily classification;
2. performed image;
3. conduct under danger.

Saitama's response privileges the third. His recognition of Amai as impressive/cool is not ignorance of the transformation; it follows from seeing the struggle and choice that the crowd's image cannot hold. [web262/pages 8-21; web263/pages 2-15]

### 5. Recognition can be exact without being exculpatory

The strongest reading is not that Saitama “proves Amai good.” Amai's earlier coercion and lethal reasoning remain part of the record. Saitama recognizes a heroic act without converting that recognition into a total biography. This is the same epistemic virtue V37 associated with conduct-based recognition: observe what is actually present, neither inflate nor erase it.

## Visual and register observations

- The amusement park fills panels with spectators, devices and unstable rides. Attention is not background: it is plot mechanism and ethical pressure.
- Amai's altered body receives horror framing, while Saitama's plain response interrupts the expected genre verdict.

## Strongest counter-reading

A sympathetic reading could treat Amai's exposure as a full redemption. The pages do not support that scale. They provide powerful counterevidence to a body-equals-morality judgment and new evidence of aspiration, endurance and choice. They do not nullify his accumulated coercion or guarantee what he will do next.

Likewise, Neo Heroes' visible coercive risks do not make every member fraudulent. The system is effective precisely because it combines real capacity with a platform able to measure, market and potentially own that capacity.
