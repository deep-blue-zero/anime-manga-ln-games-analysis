# SHUUKURA V03 Deep Reading — Transcript Extraction

# 『週に一度クラスメイトを買う話』

## Volume 3 + BOOK☆WALKER SS「私たちの文化祭は始まらない」+ Kakuyomu SS「宇都宮舞香は下校する」

### Leakage, persistence, and the future that refuses to remain outside the contract

### Scope

This pass covers the **Japanese Volume 3 text in full**, including its newly written interlude **「宮城がいる部屋」** and concluding bonus **「仙台さんには似合わない」**, then the Volume 3 BOOK☆WALKER bonus **「私たちの文化祭は始まらない」**, and finally the Kakuyomu release SS **「宇都宮舞香は下校する」**.

Volume 3 is the point at which I would revise our previous governing description of *Shuukura*.

Volumes 1–2 asked:

> **What is this relationship, and how much can the ¥5,000 contract contain?**

Volume 3 increasingly asks:

> **What happens when the relationship begins appearing everywhere the contract was supposed to exclude?**

The answer is: it leaks.

Into school.

Into friendship.

Into clothing.

Into jewelry.

Into university choices.

Into family homes.

Into illness.

Into phone calls.

Into ordinary meals.

Into imagined futures.

And, crucially, into the behavior visible to everyone else.

The relationship has not acquired a name.

It has acquired an **exhaust trail**.

---

# I. Central Thesis: “Until Graduation” Stops Being a Rule and Becomes a Problem

At the end of Volume 2, graduation had already begun changing meaning.

Originally it was Miyagi's safety mechanism:

> This arrangement has a predetermined end.
> Therefore I do not need to fear being abandoned unpredictably.

If the relationship expires automatically, Miyagi can pretend she controls the loss.

Volume 3 destroys that comfort by giving graduation **geography**.

Sendai is not simply going to “graduate.”

She is planning to attend a university outside the prefecture.

Suddenly the endpoint means:

-   Sendai will physically leave;

-   Miyagi will no longer encounter her at school;

-   there will be no accidental sight of her;

-   her voice will no longer be nearby;

-   their after-school world cannot simply continue by inertia.


An abstract deadline can be emotionally managed.

A train ride, another prefecture, another university and another home cannot.

That is why Miyagi's reaction is so physical: poor sleep, lack of appetite, fixation on Sendai's voice, inability to stop imagining absence.

The contradiction becomes impossible to avoid:

> **Miyagi has spent two volumes insisting that the relationship must end at graduation, and now discovers that she cannot tolerate what that statement actually means.**

Volume 3's real antagonist is therefore not another girl, parental opposition, or even social stigma.

It is **the future**.

---

# II. Sendai's University Choice Is Not a Romantic Rejection

This deserves emphasis because the novel deliberately avoids the easy romance solution.

Miyagi wants Sendai to remain nearby.

Sendai refuses.

But Sendai is not saying:

> I don't care enough about you to stay.

Her university choice is bound to her family situation.

She wants to leave home.

Volume 3 substantially sharpens what we had already inferred about the contrast between their households.

### Miyagi's home

Physically empty.

Emotionally abandoned.

Too quiet.

She needs someone to **return**.

### Sendai's home

Physically inhabited.

Emotionally conditional and suffocating.

Structured around comparison, especially with her older sister.

She needs somewhere she can **leave**.

So when Miyagi asks Sendai to stay locally, the conflict is real.

For Miyagi:

> Staying means not abandoning me.

For Sendai:

> Staying may mean not escaping the environment from which I need independence.

Neither need is trivial.

This prevents *Shuukura* from collapsing into a romantic fantasy where love is proven through maximal self-sacrifice.

A healthier future would have to satisfy both:

> **Miyagi needs continuity without captivity.
> Sendai needs autonomy without disappearance.**

They are nowhere near capable of articulating that yet.

But Volume 3 makes it the problem they will eventually have to solve.

---

# III. Miyagi's Preemptive-Abandonment Logic Is Now Explicit

One of Volume 3's most useful contributions is that it removes some interpretive ambiguity around Miyagi's psychology.

She effectively tells herself:

> if something is going to end, it is better that I end it first.

That confirms the pattern we've been identifying since Volume 1.

Miyagi does not merely dislike attachment.

She fears being the **passive recipient of loss**.

Her defenses therefore repeatedly convert:

-   uncertainty into rules;

-   affection into commands;

-   gifts into ownership;

-   voluntary domesticity into paid labor;

-   future possibility into a fixed deadline.


The logic is:

> If I define the terms of the relationship, perhaps I can define the terms of losing it.

The tragedy is that Volume 3 increasingly demonstrates that this doesn't work.

She can control the vocabulary.

She cannot control what Sendai has become to her.

---

# IV. The Piercing Request Reveals Miyagi's New Problem: Temporary Marks Are No Longer Enough

Volume 2 developed a language of bodily marking.

Those marks were useful to Miyagi because they combined two qualities:

> visible evidence of privileged access
> +
> eventual disappearance.

Volume 3 introduces the next logical step when Miyagi asks to pierce Sendai's ear.

Sendai refuses.

Her reasoning matters.

Previous marks fade.

A piercing may remain.

That would cross from temporary evidence into persistent alteration.

This creates what I would call Volume 3's **permanence ladder**:

> temporary bodily mark
> → exchanged necktie
> → retained clothing
> → rejected piercing
> → necklace

And the necklace becomes the perfect compromise.

It is:

-   durable;

-   bodily;

-   wearable;

-   privately meaningful;

-   visible if revealed;

-   but removable.


In other words:

> **Miyagi wants permanence that can still be denied.**

That is almost the entire relationship in miniature.

---

# V. The Necklace Is Not Simply a Romantic Gift

Volume 3's concluding LN-original bonus **「仙台さんには似合わない」** makes this exceptionally clear.

Miyagi first notices the necklace while shopping for the cultural festival with Maika and Ami.

Her immediate reaction is ordinary and intimate:

> it would suit Sendai.

That thought is dangerous.

So Miyagi begins rebuilding it into something psychologically safer.

First:

> I'll just check whether it's still there.

Then:

> perhaps I can buy it for myself.

Then:

> perhaps it can serve as a chain.

Finally:

> it can reinforce the ¥5,000 relationship and remind Sendai who gives the commands.

This is one of the cleanest examples yet of Miyagi's defensive reasoning pattern:

> **desire → anxiety → control theory → action**

The emotional event is:

> I saw something beautiful and thought of you.

Miyagi cannot tolerate that formulation.

So she converts it into:

> I am purchasing an instrument that clarifies our power relationship.

Except then she has it **gift-wrapped**.

The prose is nearly cruel to her rationalization.

She can rename the action.

She cannot stop performing the action of someone buying jewelry for a person who matters to her.

---

# VI. The Necklace as a Secret Collar

When Miyagi finally gives it to Sendai, she still resists calling it a birthday present.

The structure becomes even stranger:

-   Miyagi asks Sendai's birthday;

-   gives her carefully wrapped jewelry;

-   refuses reciprocal gift exchange;

-   commands her to wear it until graduation;

-   wants it worn even outside the apartment;

-   but does not want other people to see or understand it.


That last point is crucial.

This is not straightforward public possessiveness.

Miyagi does **not** primarily want the necklace to tell everyone:

> Sendai belongs to someone.

She wants it to tell **Miyagi**:

> there is something on Sendai's body that only I understand.

That makes the necklace a private sign inside public space.

The best formulation is:

> **a secret collar rather than a public claim.**

Sendai recognizes that implication herself.

And importantly, she ultimately chooses to participate.

Miyagi's original command does not quite apply at school. When challenged, Miyagi refuses to ask nicely and withdraws.

Sendai agrees anyway.

That means the necklace no longer functions purely through contractual authority.

It becomes another case where:

> the command provides an excuse for something Sendai has independently decided to do.

The ¥5,000 structure is becoming ceremonial.

---

# VII. “Sendai Wants to Touch; Miyagi Wants Traces”

Volume 3 clarifies a useful distinction between their forms of possessiveness.

Sendai increasingly wants:

-   contact;

-   proximity;

-   physical initiation;

-   handholding;

-   conversation;

-   ordinary future meals.


Miyagi increasingly wants:

-   evidence;

-   retained objects;

-   marks;

-   exclusivity;

-   proof that Sendai's time or body contains something attributable to her.


So their attachment anxieties take different material forms:

> **Sendai wants access to Miyagi's present.
> Miyagi wants Sendai's present to leave evidence that survives into the future.**

This helps explain why something like the necklace appeals to Miyagi so strongly.

It turns temporary time into durable trace.

---

# VIII. The School Boundary Collapses

Volumes 1–2 maintained a fairly powerful spatial grammar.

### School

Public identity.

Separate friend groups.

Little acknowledgment.

### Miyagi's apartment

Private identity.

Commands.

Money.

Physical intimacy.

Volume 3 systematically destroys this division.

At school we now get:

-   Miyagi and Sendai colliding in front of their friends;

-   Sendai grabbing Miyagi's wrist;

-   Maika noticing Sendai's reaction;

-   Sendai writing Miyagi's name privately during class;

-   the old-building rendezvous;

-   physical intimacy inside school;

-   a hug;

-   university confrontation;

-   covert bargaining;

-   Sendai and Maika directly discussing Miyagi.


The distinction between public and private hasn't disappeared.

But the relationship is now too large to remain inside Miyagi's bedroom.

That is an important developmental threshold.

The room created the relationship.

Now the relationship is beginning to **reorganize the world outside the room**.

---

# IX. The Wrist Grab Is More Important Than It Looks

After Miyagi's sleepless night, she encounters Sendai with Hamina and Mariko while she herself is with Maika and Ami.

Sendai grabs her wrist.

Miyagi's internal response includes one of her clearest possessive thoughts yet:

> **「仙台さんの隣は私の場所だ。」**
> The place beside Sendai-san is mine.

What makes the scene dangerous is not the physical act.

It is the setting.

Until now, Miyagi's privileged access to Sendai could exist because nobody else had to acknowledge it.

The wrist grab introduces the possibility that their private hierarchy could become **socially legible**.

Maika immediately notices that something is strange.

Hamina later notices other things.

The secret is beginning to produce evidence.

---

# X. BOOK☆WALKER — 「私たちの文化祭は始まらない」

The BOOK☆WALKER bonus is short but extremely valuable because it fills in the emotional negative space immediately preceding the main novel's post-festival meeting.

Its title is excellent:

> **「私たちの文化祭は始まらない」**
> *Our Cultural Festival Doesn't Begin.*

The ordinary cultural festival happens perfectly well.

Miyagi works her class café.

Maika and Ami enjoy themselves.

Sendai spends time with Hamina and Mariko.

Everyone participates in the public ritual.

What never begins is:

> **Miyagi and Sendai's cultural festival.**

They spend the event separately while repeatedly, privately orienting toward the other.

---

# XI. Miyagi's Festival: “I'm Not Waiting” While Watching the Door

Miyagi is assigned a server role in the class café.

Her discomfort with the job is revealing.

She doesn't particularly object to serving customers.

She objects to being required to **perform the correct emotional expression**.

She can bring food accurately.

Why should she have to smile on command?

This is a nice miniature of Miyagi's wider personality.

She resists externally authored performance.

A social role saying:

> behave this way because this is what the situation requires

feels intrusive.

That makes the contrast with Sendai particularly sharp.

Sendai is very good at exactly this kind of social performance.

Yet while Miyagi complains internally about the artificiality of her role, she repeatedly notices the entrance.

Sendai never appears.

Miyagi insists that she isn't waiting for her.

But the narration keeps demonstrating that she is.

The structure is classic Miyagi:

> denial at the level of proposition;
> confession at the level of attention.

---

# XII. Sendai's Festival: She Finds Miyagi Without “Looking”

The Sendai half mirrors this beautifully.

She is with Hamina and Mariko.

They go to fortune-telling.

Sendai avoids asking directly about romance.

When she needs a topic, Miyagi enters her mind.

She doesn't say the name.

When they need somewhere else to go, Miyagi's classroom enters her mind.

She doesn't suggest it.

She looks into the courtyard and immediately identifies Miyagi with Maika.

Again:

> I wasn't searching.

The attention says otherwise.

Miyagi repeatedly checks the door.

Sendai repeatedly finds Miyagi in the crowd.

Both insist that the other is not organizing the festival for them.

Both behave as though she is.

---

# XIII. Public Friendship Is Not the Antagonist

Something I particularly like about this SS is that Maika, Ami, Hamina and Mariko are not obstacles in the conventional romantic-comedy sense.

Nobody maliciously keeps the protagonists apart.

They are simply doing what friends do:

> spending the school festival together.

That creates a subtler obstacle:

> **social gravity.**

Miyagi belongs with Maika and Ami in public.

Sendai belongs with Hamina and Mariko.

Those friendships create legitimate expectations of presence.

The protagonists' private relationship is not yet socially acknowledged enough to compete with those expectations.

This is why “let's spend the festival together” would itself function as a declaration.

Neither can say it.

---

# XIV. The Title Is Even Better in Retrospect

The main novel later gives Miyagi and Sendai their private meeting after the festival has effectively ended.

So their cultural festival finally “begins” only when:

-   their friends have left;

-   public activity has quieted;

-   the classrooms are emptying;

-   the school ceases functioning as a social stage.


Their festival can exist only after **the cultural festival is over**.

That is almost absurdly representative of their relationship.

Ordinary social scripts must stop before Miyagi and Sendai can begin.

---

# XV. Miyagi's Lie to Maika and Ami Is a Major Turning Point

In the main volume, Sendai contacts Miyagi after the festival.

Miyagi is supposed to eat with Maika and Ami.

Instead she abruptly claims:

> she forgot something,

and:

> her father will be home early.

Then she returns into the school to meet Sendai.

This matters because Miyagi has now started making **active social sacrifices** for the relationship.

Volume 2's Maika SS showed that Miyagi had become less available.

Volume 3 shows the mechanism.

She is not merely accidentally spending more time with Sendai.

She is choosing Sendai over an existing commitment and fabricating an explanation.

The relationship is no longer occupying spare time.

It is **displacing other claims on her time**.

---

# XVI. The Post-Festival Meeting: They Both Wanted the Same Thing

Sendai eventually admits that she wanted to experience the festival with Miyagi.

That statement retrospectively unlocks the BOOK☆WALKER story.

Both spent the festival not visiting the other.

Both wanted to.

Both noticed the absence.

Both maintained the public fiction that nothing was missing.

This is one of Volume 3's strongest examples of dramatic irony generated by alternating perspective:

> each girl possesses exactly the reassurance the other needs,
> and neither knows how to deliver it plainly.

Miyagi wants proof that Sendai wanted to see her.

Sendai wants proof that Miyagi was waiting.

The reader already has both proofs.

---

# XVII. The Festival Kiss Is Romantic—but the Power Dynamics Need Qualification

The ensuing physical encounter marks another escalation, but Volume 3 also complicates any simple idea that Sendai is the “healthier” member merely because she initiates rather than commands.

Sendai pushes.

Miyagi resists deeper escalation.

Sendai does give her a clear exit: if Miyagi genuinely doesn't want this, she can leave, and Sendai will not pursue her.

Miyagi stays.

That matters.

But Miyagi also explicitly calls Sendai **unfair** for making her carry the decisive choice.

And Sendai later reflects that she herself drove the situation and cannot simply transfer equal responsibility to Miyagi because Miyagi eventually permitted it.

That self-correction is important.

The scene therefore isn't best summarized as either:

> Sendai coerces Miyagi

or:

> Miyagi freely chooses everything, so there is no problem.

The text presents something messier:

> **real desire under interpersonal pressure.**

Miyagi wants Sendai.

She also has difficulty asserting desire positively.

Sendai knows this and increasingly exploits situations where Miyagi must either escape or permit.

That produces mutuality.

It can also produce ethically bad pressure.

The novel seems aware of both.

---

# XVIII. Sendai's Later Boundary-Pushing Confirms the Problem

This becomes clearer during the later school encounter where Miyagi sets an intentionally humiliating condition before allowing Sendai to touch her.

Miyagi expects Sendai to refuse.

Sendai doesn't.

Then Sendai pushes beyond the agreed scope before finally stopping.

This is one of the volume's sharper reminders that:

> **symmetrical power is not automatically healthy power.**

Early *Shuukura* risked appearing structurally simple:

> Miyagi commands; Sendai suffers or complies.

By Volume 3, Sendai has substantially more agency.

But that agency can itself become intrusive.

The protagonists now possess enough confidence in the other's attachment that each can test boundaries.

That is progress in intimacy.

It is also a new source of danger.

---

# XIX. The Hug Is More Destabilizing Than the More Sexual Acts

One of the most revealing choices in Volume 3 is that an ordinary hug can affect Miyagi more deeply than acts that are superficially more transgressive.

This makes psychological sense.

The earlier physical behavior can be classified as:

-   command;

-   experiment;

-   provocation;

-   rule-breaking;

-   payment ritual.


A hug is harder.

A hug says:

> I want to be close to you.

There is less machinery available to disguise it.

The same will become true of handholding.

*Shuukura* has reached an unusual situation where:

> **sexual transgression is psychologically easier than ordinary romance.**

Because transgression can be exceptionalized.

Handholding, hugging, calling someone by their given name, sharing dinner after graduation—those things threaten to make the relationship **normal**.

And normality implies continuity.

---

# XX. “Go Somewhere We Can Eat Together”

During the hug, Sendai introduces one of the most consequential ideas in the volume.

She effectively tells Miyagi:

> don't stay here; go somewhere where we can still eat together.

This is much more important than a conventional confession would be at this stage.

It transforms the post-graduation future from:

> nothing

into:

> perhaps sometimes we eat together.

The modesty is what makes it powerful.

Sendai is not promising eternal love.

She is imagining **ordinary recurrence**.

A shared meal next year.

Then another.

And another.

That is precisely the kind of future Miyagi finds hardest to trust.

Grand passion would be easier to dismiss as temporary.

Habit implies durability.

---

# XXI. Miyagi's Answer: “You Stay Here”

Earlier, during the festival encounter, Miyagi says something equally important:

> **「仙台さんがここに残ってよ」**
> You stay here, Sendai-san.

This is perhaps Miyagi's first real attempt to alter the future itself.

Until now:

> graduation ends things.

Now:

> maybe Sendai could change what graduation means.

The problem is that Miyagi expresses the desire through the logic most natural to her:

> don't leave.

Sendai's emerging logic is:

> come with me.

Those are not yet the same proposition.

But put them together and the future architecture becomes visible:

> Miyagi: **stay**.
> Sendai: **come**.

Eventually they will have to invent:

> **go together.**

---

# XXII. The Sickbed Material Rewrites Sendai's Home

The illness episode, especially the newly written Sendai interlude **「宮城がいる部屋」**, is one of Volume 3's strongest additions.

Miyagi learns Sendai is sick and goes to her home with practical supplies.

She rationalizes this, naturally.

But the action is straightforward care.

She enters Sendai's private domestic world for the first time.

And what she finds confirms something important:

> Sendai's family wound is not spectacular abuse.

It is emotional thinness.

Her mother provides the necessary minimum.

The comparison with her sister remains in the background.

Small acts of care that once occurred have diminished.

Even the simple memory of cooling sheets matters because it represents the difference between:

> someone doing something for you

and:

> the necessary item merely being available.

Miyagi arrives carrying exactly that kind of active care.

---

# XXIII. “A Room with Miyagi” Becomes a Refuge

The interlude's title matters:

> **「宮城がいる部屋」**
> *A Room Where Miyagi Is.*

Sendai's bedroom normally belongs to the family system she wants to escape.

When Miyagi enters it, the room changes function.

Miyagi's presence cuts Sendai off from the suffocating emotional environment around her.

That creates a remarkable inversion.

### In Miyagi's apartment

Sendai makes an empty home feel inhabited.

### In Sendai's bedroom

Miyagi makes an inhabited home feel private.

They solve opposite problems.

Miyagi needs:

> presence.

Sendai needs:

> protected separation.

Each gives the other the missing property of home.

This substantially strengthens our earlier conclusion:

> **Their romance is increasingly about creating a habitable domestic space for one another.**

---

# XXIV. The Most Important Sickbed Request Is Handholding

When Sendai is vulnerable, the physical act she ultimately asks for is remarkably simple:

> stay until I fall asleep, and hold my hand.

Again, handholding arrives very late relative to more transgressive intimacy.

That matters.

This is not primarily erotic.

It is attachment regulation.

Sendai wants bodily proof that another person remains present while consciousness disappears.

For someone whose family environment has trained her not to expect much emotional care, this is a significant regression into vulnerability.

And Miyagi stays.

For all Miyagi's possessive language, one of her most meaningful acts in Volume 3 involves **no command and no ownership**.

She simply remains.

---

# XXV. Money Has Become a Notary Stamp on a Fiction

The curry scene later makes the evolution of ¥5,000 almost comically explicit.

Miyagi stops at the supermarket and buys actual ingredients.

Sendai waits outside Miyagi's home.

Miyagi tells her to cook.

And Miyagi pays her.

Why?

At this point the money is barely functioning as wages.

Its psychological function is:

> If I pay Sendai to cook, then this is not two people choosing to make and eat dinner together.

The note becomes a defense against domestic meaning.

The best formulation I can give it is:

> **The ¥5,000 note is becoming a notary stamp on a fiction.**

It certifies:

> This is still the old arrangement.

Even though virtually everything occurring around it says otherwise.

The contract no longer creates intimacy.

It **launders intimacy into a form Miyagi can survive naming**.

---

# XXVI. Cooking Becomes Deliberate Domesticity

The contrast with earlier food scenes is significant.

Previously, Sendai gradually improved what Miyagi ate.

Now Miyagi herself buys raw ingredients and creates the conditions in which Sendai can cook a proper meal.

She watches Sendai move around the kitchen.

And she likes having someone there.

More specifically:

> she wants that someone to be Sendai.

That's a serious development.

Her apartment is no longer simply:

> the place where her parents are absent.

It has accumulated enough Sendai-time that it is becoming:

> the place where Sendai should be.

And once Miyagi begins imagining university, she even starts thinking about leaving this apartment.

That is symbolically large.

The home defined by abandonment is losing its status as the fixed center of her life.

---

# XXVII. Maika Accidentally Reveals Miyagi's Future to Sendai

The Maika–Sendai encounter is one of the volume's best uses of supporting cast.

Maika mentions her university.

Then she reveals that Shiori intends to apply there too.

Sendai reacts too strongly.

This does two things at once.

First, Maika acquires more evidence that Sendai cares unusually about Shiori.

Second, Sendai suddenly learns that Miyagi may be attending a university geographically close to her own.

But Sendai interprets the information pessimistically.

She assumes:

> Miyagi is following Maika.

Not:

> Miyagi might also be choosing a future that keeps Sendai within reach.

That reaction exposes another wound in Sendai.

She is accustomed to the possibility that someone else is preferred.

Her older-sister comparisons matter here.

Her jealousy isn't only romantic competition.

It is:

> **What if I am once again the secondary choice?**

That is the Sendai-side analogue to Miyagi's abandonment fear.

Miyagi fears:

> you will leave.

Sendai fears:

> even if I am present, you will choose someone else first.

---

# XXVIII. Miyagi's University Choice Has Productive Ambiguity

We should resist reducing Miyagi's university planning to a single motive.

The nearby university is genuinely Maika's university.

Miyagi's improved grades genuinely make it viable.

It also happens to place her geographically near Sendai.

All of these can be true simultaneously.

The story seems deliberately interested in **mixed motive**.

Miyagi can tell herself:

> I'm applying because Maika is going there.

While another part of her knows:

> Sendai will be nearby.

That plausible deniability is psychologically ideal for Miyagi.

She can alter her future around Sendai without having to confess that she has altered her future around Sendai.

---

# XXIX. The Phone Call May Be One of the Volume's Biggest Developments

Late in Volume 3, Sendai calls Miyagi during bad weather.

There is no meaningful practical reason.

Eventually she admits that.

Miyagi talks about being alone.

She also reveals more about how rarely her parents come home.

Then Sendai is about to end the call.

Miyagi asks her to keep talking a little longer.

She immediately tries to disguise or retract the request.

But the request has already occurred.

This matters because the phone call strips away almost every traditional mechanism.

No:

-   ¥5,000;

-   apartment visit;

-   command;

-   homework;

-   food;

-   physical excuse.


Miyagi simply wants:

> **Sendai's voice.**

Volume 3 began with Miyagi unable to sleep because she imagined no longer hearing that voice after graduation.

Now technology allows the voice to cross physical distance.

This is almost a prototype of the relationship they would need after high school.

Presence without possession.

---

# XXX. The Necklace Becomes Remote Touch

During the same phone call, Miyagi asks whether Sendai is still wearing the necklace.

She has Sendai touch it.

The scene quietly turns the necklace into a proxy body.

Miyagi cannot physically touch Sendai.

But:

-   Miyagi's voice;

-   Sendai's own hand;

-   Miyagi's necklace


combine to simulate presence.

That is another step away from the original spatial rules.

Their relationship no longer requires the same room.

This is thematically important because Volume 3's entire problem is impending geographic separation.

The story is beginning to discover forms of intimacy that survive distance **before either protagonist realizes that's what they are doing**.

---

# XXXI. Names Become the Next Boundary

The given-name question grows steadily more important.

Miyagi establishes:

> Shiori is what friends call her.

Sendai probes the implication.

If they were friends, could she call her Shiori?

Miyagi resists.

Later Miyagi asks about who calls Sendai **Hazuki**.

Sendai invites her to.

Miyagi refuses because she is neither family nor friend.

Then comes the wonderfully Miyagi-like:

> **「葉月、なんて呼ばない」**
> I'm not going to call you Hazuki.

Grammatically, the refusal contains the thing being refused.

She has said the name.

Again:

> **denial becomes a vehicle for confession.**

That pattern may be one of Miyagi's defining speech behaviors.

She can express intimacy most safely while negating it.

---

# XXXII. The Final Hand Squeeze Is the Correct Ending for Volume 3

Near the end, Sendai takes Miyagi's hand.

This time Miyagi responds by gently squeezing back.

I would rank this as more important to their developmental arc than another kiss would have been.

A kiss in this series can still be classified as:

-   command;

-   provocation;

-   experiment;

-   transgression;

-   bargaining.


A reciprocal hand squeeze is much harder to fictionalize.

It is:

> I recognize your touch.
> I answer it.
> I choose to remain connected.

Volume 3 therefore ends not on another escalation of extremity but on **reciprocity**.

That's exactly right.

---

# XXXIII. 「宇都宮舞香は下校する」 — The Secret Becomes Legible as Romance

The Kakuyomu release SS is especially valuable when read immediately after both the main cultural-festival material and the BOOK☆WALKER bonus.

It takes place in the exact negative space Miyagi leaves behind when she turns away from Maika and Ami to meet Sendai.

Maika and Ami stand in the quiet hallway after Shiori abruptly claims:

-   she forgot something;

-   her father will be home early;

-   their planned meal is cancelled.


Ami reaches a surprisingly good structural conclusion:

> this looks like a secret boyfriend.

Maika concedes that Shiori's behavior is suspicious but deliberately chooses the more innocent interpretation. She knows Shiori is forgetful, knows her father appears with unusual frequency in Shiori's explanations, and decides to remain curious rather than convict her of lying. Shuukura - LN03 Release SS - 宇都…

This is the third Maika SS, and it records another major change in the relationship's external visibility.

---

# XXXIV. The Maika SS Sequence Now Forms a Clean Progression

The three supplements can be read as an escalating surveillance record from outside the dyad.

### Volume 1 — 「宇都宮舞香は思考する」

Miyagi is an ordinary friend inside an ordinary social environment.

### Volume 2 — 「宇都宮舞香の放課後」

Something is changing.

Shiori is:

-   distracted;

-   less available;

-   spending less time with them.


The cause is unknown.

### Volume 3 — 「宇都宮舞香は下校する」

The behavior now looks like:

> **romance.**

They still identify the wrong person.

But the relational category is becoming visible.

That is an important threshold.

---

# XXXV. Heteronormative Assumption Becomes Camouflage

Ami's boyfriend theory is especially interesting because it is simultaneously:

> wrong in detail

and

> almost exactly right in structure.

Shiori:

-   looks at her phone;

-   abruptly changes plans;

-   fabricates a mundane explanation;

-   secretly returns into the school;

-   meets someone she doesn't want her friends to know about;

-   engages in an emotionally/romantically charged encounter.


Ami has correctly inferred:

> secret romantic attachment.

She gets:

> **boyfriend**

wrong.

That exposes a second layer of camouflage beyond school hierarchy.

Earlier, Maika and Ami struggled to imagine Sendai and Shiori being close because they inhabit different social positions.

Now they can imagine **romance**, but their available model is heterosexual.

So even when the evidence becomes strong enough to suggest a lover, Sendai remains cognitively invisible.

The secret is partially protected by other people's assumptions about **what kind of relationship is plausible**.

---

# XXXVI. The Father Alibi Has a Particularly Bitter Irony

Miyagi uses her father as cover.

That is psychologically interesting because the central relationship developed partly inside the vacuum left by her parents' absence.

Now that same absent family becomes an excuse used to protect the attachment that filled the absence.

Put simply:

> **The father who is rarely present becomes the alibi for the girl who actually is.**

That is a small detail, but a very *Shuukura* detail.

---

# XXXVII. Maika Again Demonstrates Care Without Epistemic Entitlement

Maika knows the explanation is shaky.

She even recognizes that the probability of Shiori genuinely forgetting something may be lower than she wants to admit.

But she essentially chooses:

> I'll notice it.
> I won't force the conclusion.

She and Ami joke about interrogating Shiori the next day.

But the SS is clear that this is ordinary teasing, not a serious attempt to force disclosure.

Maika would like to know if Shiori has a partner.

She is curious about what kind of person Shiori would choose.

She even has the lightly protective thought that she would like to determine whether that person is someone Shiori can be entrusted to.

But she still does not convert friendship into investigative entitlement. Shuukura - LN03 Release SS - 宇都…

That keeps her functioning as the series' strongest control case for:

> **care without possession.**

---

# XXXVIII. The Title 「宇都宮舞香は下校する」 Is Quietly Excellent

The literal action is simple:

> Maika goes home.

But structurally, the title creates an elegant contrast.

Maika and Ami:

> leave school and return to ordinary life.

Miyagi:

> turns back into the deserted school.

And Sendai is waiting there.

So while Maika **下校する**—goes home from school—

Miyagi returns into the hidden after-school world she has built with Sendai.

It divides the two kinds of intimacy spatially:

> public friendship exits through the front door;
> secret attachment begins after everyone else has gone.

---

# XXXIX. The Two Cultural-Festival Supplements Work Best Together

The BOOK☆WALKER bonus and Kakuyomu SS form a very effective pair.

## 「私たちの文化祭は始まらない」

Internal negative space.

We learn:

> Miyagi and Sendai spend the entire festival wanting the other without acting.

## 「宇都宮舞香は下校する」

External negative space.

We learn:

> the moment they finally act leaves their friends behind wondering what just happened.

Together they turn a fairly simple main-novel rendezvous into something much richer.

From inside:

> longing.

From outside:

> suspicious absence.

That is Volume 3's central structure in miniature.

---

# XL. The Private Relationship Now Has a Public Exhaust Trail

By the end of Volume 3, consider how many people are registering something.

### Hamina

Saw Sendai and Miyagi together during summer.

Notices Sendai's unusual availability patterns.

Later notices the necklace and reads it as likely romantic.

### Mariko

Also recognizes that the necklace doesn't fit Sendai's ordinary style.

### Maika

Has now seen:

-   Sendai reacting strangely to Shiori;

-   the wrist grab;

-   Sendai showing unusual interest in Shiori;

-   Shiori's university planning;

-   the suspicious cultural-festival disappearance.


She eventually directly asks whether Sendai and Shiori are friends.

### Ami

Correctly guesses that Shiori's festival behavior resembles being summoned by a romantic partner.

No one has solved the puzzle.

But they no longer need to.

The important literary point is:

> **the secret is generating observable consequences faster than the protagonists can explain them.**

---

# XLI. Volume 3 Also Changes the Meaning of Secrecy

Earlier, secrecy mostly protected the arrangement from embarrassment and social incongruity.

Now secrecy also protects the protagonists from having to define what the arrangement has become.

If Maika asked Miyagi:

> Are you dating Sendai?

What could Miyagi answer?

“No” is formally defensible.

But the reader has now seen:

-   jealousy;

-   jewelry;

-   private exclusivity;

-   handholding;

-   gifts;

-   sick-care;

-   future university calculations;

-   late-night calls;

-   cooking;

-   post-graduation meal fantasies;

-   physical affection;

-   fear of separation.


The problem is no longer simply:

> other people don't know.

It is:

> **Miyagi and Sendai do not possess a category capable of describing what other people might correctly perceive.**

---

# XLII. Updated Analysis of the ¥5,000

After Volume 3, I would divide the money motif into three stages.

### Volume 1 — Transaction

The money establishes:

-   permission;

-   command authority;

-   bounded obligation.


### Volume 2 — Excuse

The payment increasingly allows them to continue seeing each other without admitting they want to.

### Volume 3 — Defensive ritual

The money is sometimes invoked precisely when an act would otherwise look too obviously voluntary or domestic.

The curry scene is the clearest example.

This leads to a stronger formulation:

> **¥5,000 is no longer purchasing Sendai. It is preserving the legal fiction that Miyagi is not choosing her.**

That's a major shift.

---

# XLIII. Updated Analysis of Physical Intimacy

Volume 3 confirms something we suspected after Volume 2:

Sendai is considerably more adventurous about escalating physical closeness.

But the volume adds an important qualification.

She is not merely the “free” counterpart to controlling Miyagi.

Sendai also:

-   pressures;

-   bargains;

-   tests;

-   deliberately creates situations in which Miyagi must choose;

-   occasionally crosses a stated boundary before backing off.


So the old shorthand:

> Miyagi controls permission; Sendai controls momentum

still works surprisingly well.

But it now requires a warning:

> **momentum can become coercive too.**

The relationship is becoming more equal in power.

It is not automatically becoming more ethically mature at the same rate.

---

# XLIV. Updated Miyagi Profile

Volume 3 gives us a significantly sharper Miyagi.

### Her attachment strategy

Preemptively end what might abandon her.

### Her preferred intimacy technology

Objects, rules, traces, secret signs.

### Her central contradiction

She wants Sendai's continued presence but fears any action that would admit continuity is desired.

### Her growing capacity

Care.

The sick visit is important because Miyagi does something she cannot easily reduce to ownership.

She sees someone vulnerable and goes to her.

### Her emerging future orientation

Miyagi is now willing to let Sendai influence:

-   university research;

-   geography;

-   domestic imagination.


She simply refuses to call this influence what it is.

### Her greatest developmental obstacle

Trust.

Miyagi can secure:

-   a necklace;

-   a command;

-   an afternoon;

-   a payment.


She cannot secure:

> someone freely choosing her again tomorrow.

And that is increasingly the only reassurance capable of satisfying her.

---

# XLV. Updated Sendai Profile

Volume 3 also revises Sendai substantially.

### Her attachment strategy

Pursue, provoke, create opportunities, make Miyagi choose.

### Her central vulnerability

Conditional belonging.

This appears both in the family environment and in her fear that Miyagi may prioritize Maika.

### Her growing possessiveness

She doesn't want to “return Miyagi to her friends.”

That's a striking thought because it brings her much closer to Miyagi's emotional territory.

### Her emerging ordinary-romantic desire

This may be Volume 3's biggest change in Sendai.

She begins wanting:

-   handholding;

-   hugs;

-   continued meals;

-   shared geography;

-   phone conversation;

-   given-name intimacy.


These are not simply sexual experiments.

They are relationship behavior.

### Her greatest developmental obstacle

She frequently gives Miyagi the final decision while aggressively shaping the conditions under which the decision occurs.

That lets Sendai tell herself:

> Miyagi chose.

Sometimes that's true.

Sometimes it's an incomplete account of Sendai's own pressure.

Her self-reflection after the festival suggests she is beginning to recognize this.

---

# XLVI. The Most Important Shift: Desire Is Moving from Exceptional to Ordinary

Volume 1's relationship thrives on exceptionalism.

Everything is weird.

Everything needs an excuse.

Volume 2 deepens the weird system.

Volume 3 begins introducing surprisingly mundane desires:

> eat together later.
> stay until I sleep.
> hold my hand.
> call me.
> keep talking.
> go to university nearby.
> wear the thing I gave you.
> come home.
> stay here.

This is where *Shuukura* becomes particularly interesting as a romance.

The major threat to their defenses is not ever-greater erotic escalation.

It is:

> **domestic ordinariness.**

Because ordinary life suggests that what they have could stop being an episode and become a life.

---

# XLVII. Chapter Architecture

Volume 3 has a strong underlying movement.

The volume therefore moves roughly from:

> **fear of future absence**

to:

> **unconscious construction of future continuity.**

---

# XLVIII. Revised Series Thesis Through Volume 3

After these texts, I would now describe the first three volumes like this.

### Volume 1

> **The contract creates a space in which intimacy can happen without being named.**

### Volume 2

> **The intimacy becomes valuable enough that both girls begin manipulating the contract to preserve it.**

### Volume 3

> **The relationship becomes too large for the contract, the apartment, school hierarchy, or graduation deadline to contain.**

And that produces the first major movement of the series:

> transaction
> → ritual
> → attachment
> → leakage
> → imagined continuity.

The girls still insist that graduation is the endpoint.

Their behavior has begun constructing a world in which that endpoint makes less and less sense.

---

# XLIX. Final Assessment of the Three-Text Sequence

Taken together, Volume 3 and its two cultural-festival supplements may be the strongest unit of the series so far.

The main novel makes the future unavoidable.

The BOOK☆WALKER SS shows two girls spending an entire public festival covertly looking for one another while obeying their ordinary social roles.

The Kakuyomu SS shows what that longing looks like from outside: Shiori abruptly abandons her friends with an unconvincing excuse, and her friends correctly infer that this resembles secret romance—even while imagining the wrong kind of partner. Shuukura - LN03 Release SS - 宇都…

The sequence gives us three simultaneous realities:

> **Miyagi:**
> I am not waiting for Sendai. I am not changing my future for Sendai. The necklace is not really a gift. This ends at graduation.

> **Sendai:**
> I am not looking for Miyagi. I do not know what I want this to become. I just want to touch her, hear her, eat with her later, and perhaps have her nearby.

> **Everyone else:**
> Shiori is acting like someone who has a secret romantic life.

The outsiders are increasingly closer to the truth than either protagonist's stated self-description.

And Volume 3 ends with the right image:

not a dramatic declaration,

not another rule,

not another ¥5,000 note,

but a hand being held—

and the other hand finally squeezing back.

That is a very small act.

For these two girls, it is almost a confession.
