# SHUUKURA V01 SS — 「宇都宮舞香は思考する」

# 『週に一度クラスメイトを買う話』

## Volume 1 Supplement — 「宇都宮舞香は思考する」

### An external control case for Miyagi, school hierarchy, and ordinary friendship

**Scope:** Kakuyomu Volume 1 release SS, 「宇都宮舞香は思考する」前編・後編, read immediately after LN Volume 1 according to the agreed analytical order. ShuukuraReadingOrder

---

# I. Central contribution

This short story does something Volume 1 itself cannot do:

> **It lets us observe Miyagi from the perspective of someone who loves her platonically, understands her reasonably well, and has no investment in the ¥5,000 arrangement.**

That makes Utsunomiya Maika an unusually valuable **control case**.

Volume 1 traps us inside Miyagi and Sendai's alternating consciousnesses. Both girls are unreliable in different ways:

-   Miyagi interprets attachment as control, irritation, curiosity, or possession.

-   Sendai translates attachment into comfort, temporariness, and “interesting” behavior.


Maika has no access to the secret relationship at all. She simply knows **Shiori**, her close friend of two years.

And what she sees immediately corrects several impressions a reader could easily overstate after Volume 1.

Most importantly:

> **Miyagi is not a socially isolated girl who does not know how to have ordinary friendship.**

She already has it.

She simply cannot treat **Sendai** ordinarily.

That distinction substantially sharpens the Volume 1 analysis.

---

# II. Maika's Miyagi is much more socially ordinary than Miyagi's Miyagi

The opening establishes a strikingly mundane friendship.

Maika is stuck at cram school during spring break and thinks enviously about Shiori, who refused to attend cram school and is probably enjoying herself. Then her phone shows a message from Shiori consisting of one word:

> 「暇」

“Bored.”

Maika's reaction is essentially: *that's so Shiori.*

She notes that although Shiori's messages are curt, **she is reasonably talkative face-to-face**, and interprets the terse texting positively: they are close enough that neither has to perform politeness for the other. Shuukura - LN01 Release SS - 宇都…

That is already important.

From inside Miyagi's narration, it would be possible to construct a picture of her as:

-   withdrawn;

-   poor at social interaction;

-   unable to initiate;

-   fundamentally lonely at school.


Maika's account says otherwise.

Shiori:

-   has maintained a close friendship with Maika for two years;

-   is also close with Ami;

-   messages Maika spontaneously;

-   invites her places;

-   talks comfortably when they meet;

-   and occupies an established place within a small but genuine friendship group. Shuukura - LN01 Release SS - 宇都…


Maika explicitly calls Shiori a **親友**—a close/best friend—and hopes that she, Shiori, and Ami can spend their final high-school year in the same class. Shuukura - LN01 Release SS - 宇都…

This forces an important revision:

> **Miyagi's inability to call Sendai a friend is not caused by an inability to understand or participate in friendship.**

Miyagi knows perfectly well what friendship feels like.

She has Maika and Ami.

That actually makes her resistance to categorizing Sendai as a friend **more significant**, not less.

---

# III. The ¥5,000 is therefore not a generic social prosthesis

This is perhaps the single most important thing the SS adds to Volume 1.

Our earlier analysis characterized the ¥5,000 as a structure that allows Miyagi to ask for presence without admitting she wants presence.

That remains correct.

But 「宇都宮舞香は思考する」 narrows the diagnosis.

Miyagi does **not** require contracts to ask people she likes to spend time with her.

At the end of this story, Shiori simply messages Maika:

> 「終わったら映画行かない？」

“Want to go see a movie when you're done?”

There is:

-   no money;

-   no command;

-   no elaborate excuse;

-   no coercive framework;

-   no rule system.


Shiori wants to see Maika, so she asks Maika to see a movie. Shuukura - LN01 Release SS - 宇都…

That means the bizarre architecture around Sendai must arise from something more specific.

The strongest formulation after this SS is:

> **Miyagi does not need the ¥5,000 because she cannot ask someone to spend time with her. She needs the ¥5,000 because asking Sendai to spend time with her voluntarily would expose what Sendai specifically means to her.**

That is a substantial refinement.

With Maika, wanting company is safe.

With Sendai, wanting company threatens Miyagi's entire defensive structure.

---

# IV. Why “friend” is so dangerous becomes clearer

Volume 1 repeatedly showed Miyagi becoming defensive whenever Sendai approached the category of friendship.

That seemed paradoxical because they could tolerate far more physically intimate acts than saying:

> we're friends.

This SS makes the reason clearer.

Miyagi already possesses an internal model of ordinary friendship.

It looks something like her relationship with Maika:

-   reciprocal;

-   voluntary;

-   publicly legible;

-   socially recognized;

-   emotionally warm without being possessive;

-   capable of surviving postponed plans;

-   requiring no payment.


If Miyagi admits Sendai is simply another friend, then Sendai should be able to occupy this same social universe.

But she cannot.

Sendai has already become:

-   more exclusive;

-   more physically charged;

-   more possessive;

-   more destabilizing;

-   and more frightening to lose.


So “friend” is not frightening because Miyagi cannot tolerate friendship.

It is frightening because **friendship is an obviously inadequate description of what she wants from Sendai**.

She cannot move upward into a romantic category yet.

She cannot comfortably move downward into ordinary friendship.

The ¥5,000 arrangement becomes a third category invented because the available ones are intolerable.

---

# V. Maika's ordinary care is the moral counterpoint to the contract

The story ends with a small moment that deserves more attention than its scale suggests.

Maika would like to accept Shiori's movie invitation. But cram school finishes too late. Going afterward would mean watching the film and rushing home.

Then Maika remembers:

> Shiori seemed a little down when they met the day before yesterday.

So she decides she would rather have enough time to talk to her properly and replies:

> 「明日でもいい？」

“Would tomorrow be okay?” Shuukura - LN01 Release SS - 宇都…

This is **ordinary relational care**.

No one has to:

-   issue a command;

-   provoke a reaction;

-   manufacture a bodily mark;

-   pretend not to care;

-   create artificial scarcity;

-   threaten withdrawal.


Maika notices something seems wrong and quietly restructures a social plan so that she can spend more attentive time with her friend.

That provides an understated contrast to Miyagi and Sendai.

Their relationship is not intense because intensity is the only form of care available in this fictional world.

The story shows us an alternative right beside them:

> **care can simply mean noticing and making time.**

Miyagi already receives this kind of care from Maika.

She just does not yet know how to build it with Sendai.

---

# VI. Maika is perceptive without being invasive

Maika's observational style is worth establishing now because the supplementary stories appear positioned to use her repeatedly.

She notices Shiori's mood.

She remembers small habits.

She sees when Shiori seems absent-minded.

She thinks about what might help her.

But she does **not** immediately turn uncertainty into entitlement.

The ending is revealing:

> Shiori seemed a little low.
> I'd like enough time to talk.

Not:

> Shiori owes me an explanation.

That distinguishes Maika's curiosity from Miyagi and Sendai's more possessive forms of attention.

Her care preserves Shiori's autonomy.

This may make Maika particularly useful as an external observer throughout the series: she is close enough to notice change, but not so emotionally entangled that every change becomes a threat to her.

---

# VII. The story gives Miyagi a surprisingly affectionate everyday portrait

Maika's tiny observations fill in mundane details about Shiori that Volume 1 tends to subsume beneath the Sendai relationship.

She thinks Shiori can be:

-   somewhat dreamy or absent-minded;

-   inattentive during class;

-   nevertheless conscientious about homework;

-   conversational in person;

-   terse over messaging;

-   resistant to studying when she doesn't want to;

-   comfortable enough with Maika to send bare messages like 「暇」. Shuukura - LN01 Release SS - 宇都…


Maika even doodles Shiori in her notebook and adds:

> 「集中！」

“Focus!”

This is affectionate criticism rather than frustration. Shuukura - LN01 Release SS - 宇都…

It's a useful reminder that the Miyagi we encounter with Sendai is a **situational version of Miyagi**.

Sendai elicits:

-   possessiveness;

-   experimentation;

-   aggression;

-   jealousy;

-   erotic curiosity;

-   fear of abandonment.


Those are real parts of Miyagi.

They are not the totality of her personality.

Maika sees a girl who can simply be a slightly lazy, somewhat spacey close friend.

---

# VIII. Honda quietly introduces one of the series' most important relational ideas

Maika's relationship with Honda is a small detail with disproportionately high analytical value.

She explains that:

-   they attend the same high school;

-   they are in different classes;

-   their friend groups differ;

-   they therefore barely speak at school;

-   **but at cram school they are close and talk frequently**.


Maika describes this as a somewhat strange relationship. Shuukura - LN01 Release SS - 宇都…

That is almost a miniature benign analogue to Miyagi and Sendai.

Consider the structure:

### Maika / Honda

At school:

> barely interact.

At cram school:

> comfortably friendly.

### Miyagi / Sendai

At school:

> deliberately maintain distance.

At Miyagi's apartment:

> extraordinarily intimate.

The story therefore demonstrates an important principle:

> **Relationships do not have to possess the same visible form in every social environment to be real.**

Context-specific intimacy is not inherently pathological.

This prevents us from condemning Miyagi and Sendai's separation between school and apartment simply because it is separation.

The problem lies elsewhere.

Maika and Honda comfortably understand what their relationship is.

Miyagi and Sendai use contextual separation to avoid understanding theirs.

That's the difference.

---

# IX. Social hierarchy looks less absolute from Maika's viewpoint

Volume 1's protagonists perceive the school through fairly strong hierarchical metaphors.

Miyagi sees social groups and distance.

Sendai's interlude imagines the classroom almost as an aquarium with recognizable strata.

Maika recognizes exactly the same phenomenon, but she does not seem psychologically dominated by it.

Her discussion with Honda moves toward **Ibaraki Hamina** and then Sendai.

Maika thinks Hamina is:

-   cute;

-   short-tempered;

-   selfish;

-   socially distant from her.


Honda has stereotyped Hamina as the sort of girl who probably plays around and gets boys to pay for her.

Then Honda reveals that she encountered Hamina working in a family restaurant.

And Hamina was apparently:

> **extremely friendly with customers.** Shuukura - LN01 Release SS - 宇都…

Both girls are surprised.

Their mental image does not survive direct evidence.

That's important.

---

# X. Hamina provides a small epistemological lesson

The Hamina anecdote is not random filler.

Maika openly acknowledges that her image of Hamina is weakly grounded.

They were classmates, but socially distant. Consequently, something as flimsy as **cram-school gossip can alter Maika's mental picture of her**. Shuukura - LN01 Release SS - 宇都…

That gives the story a quiet epistemological theme:

> **The more socially distant someone is, the more easily reputation substitutes for knowledge.**

Hamina-the-rumor is:

-   spoiled;

-   flashy;

-   irritable;

-   probably irresponsible.


Hamina-at-work is:

-   professionally pleasant;

-   apparently conscientious;

-   capable of performing a role nobody expected from her.


The SS never tells us which is the “real” Hamina.

That's the point.

Both may be.

Behavior is contextual.

That immediately connects to Sendai.

---

# XI. Sendai appears here almost entirely as reputation

Maika knows remarkably little about Sendai directly.

She knows that Sendai is said to be:

-   more personable than Hamina;

-   **八方美人**—pleasant/adaptive toward everybody;

-   somewhat rumored to play around;

-   likely good at work;

-   attractive enough to be popular.


But Maika explicitly admits that although they have been classmates, **they never really spoke**, so she does not know whether the rumors are correct. Shuukura - LN01 Release SS - 宇都…

This is enormously useful after Sendai's Volume 1 interlude.

We already know from Sendai herself that her public social ease requires substantial calibration.

Now we see what the performance produces from outside:

> **a coherent social object called “Sendai-san.”**

Maika has an image.

Honda has an image.

The school has an image.

But none of them knows the Sendai who:

-   gets irritated in Miyagi's room;

-   lies around reading;

-   cooks karaage;

-   retaliates when bitten;

-   becomes jealous;

-   pursues Miyagi;

-   kisses her.


This supports our earlier conclusion that what Miyagi increasingly wants is **exclusive access to the Sendai behind the socially circulated version**.

---

# XII. But Maika is more epistemically cautious than the protagonists

A recurring phrase in the second half is essentially:

> I don't know.

Maika says, in effect:

-   I don't really know Hamina.

-   I don't really know Sendai.

-   I know what people say.

-   I can't tell whether it's true.

-   They are socially distant from me.


This is quietly one of Maika's strongest character traits.

She forms impressions.

She participates in gossip.

She has stereotypes.

But she keeps an internal distinction between:

> **what I have heard**

and

> **what I actually know.**

That sounds elementary.

Miyagi and Sendai are much worse at applying the same standard to themselves.

They repeatedly confuse:

-   rationalization with motive;

-   rules with emotional reality;

-   anticipated endings with inevitable endings.


Maika isn't necessarily smarter than them.

She is simply less invested in making the answer come out a particular way.

---

# XIII. 「キラキラした存在」 and Maika's relationship to status

Maika thinks of girls like Hamina and Sendai as **キラキラした存在**—glittering/shining figures.

She admits she is not wholly immune to their appeal.

But then she makes an important distinction:

> It isn't as though anyone can become like them simply by wanting to.

And, more importantly:

> **she doesn't particularly want to become close to them.**

Her friends are already decided. Shuukura - LN01 Release SS - 宇都…

This is a sharp contrast to the social anxiety elsewhere in Volume 1.

Maika recognizes hierarchy without fetishizing it.

She doesn't conclude:

> Sendai is above me, therefore I am deficient.

Nor:

> I should acquire higher-status friends.

Nor:

> Their attention validates me.

She sees the glitter.

Then she returns to Shiori and Ami.

That gives Maika a comparatively secure social identity.

---

# XIV. This revises Miyagi's perception of her own social position

One subtle consequence follows.

Miyagi repeatedly conceptualizes Sendai as belonging to another social world.

That distinction is not imaginary. Maika independently perceives Sendai and Hamina as socially distant, “glittering” girls.

So we should **not** reduce Miyagi's perception to pure insecurity.

There really is a school-status distinction.

But Maika shows that the distinction does not have to carry the psychological weight Miyagi gives it.

For Maika:

> They're socially distant girls. Interesting enough. Anyway, my actual friends are Shiori and Ami.

For Miyagi:

> Sendai belongs to another world, and her presence in mine therefore requires explanation.

The social structure is real.

Miyagi's **interpretation of its importance** is the variable.

That's a useful distinction between fact and psychological magnification.

---

# XV. Maika provides a healthier model of relational sufficiency

The line:

> 「仲良くしたい友だちは決まっている」

roughly, **“I've already decided who the friends I want to be close with are,”**

is simple but revealing. Shuukura - LN01 Release SS - 宇都…

Maika does not pursue relationships for:

-   status;

-   novelty;

-   erotic intensity;

-   social insurance.


She values particular people.

This creates an interesting contrast with both protagonists.

Sendai maintains a broad social network partly because social integration is something she actively manages.

Miyagi keeps her circle narrower but is deeply destabilized when Sendai enters it.

Maika's stance is simpler:

> I know who matters to me.

That may explain why she can care for Shiori without either controlling or idealizing her.

---

# XVI. The ending changes how we should understand Miyagi's loneliness

After Volume 1 alone, we could easily say:

> Miyagi is lonely.

That remains true, but it is too blunt.

「宇都宮舞香は思考する」 demonstrates that she is **not friendless**.

She has at least two genuine close peer relationships.

So her loneliness needs to be separated into different domains.

### Social loneliness

Not absolute.

Maika and Ami genuinely care about her.

### Domestic loneliness

Much stronger.

Her home remains defined by parental absence and solitary routine.

### Romantic/attachment loneliness

Emerging.

Sendai awakens a kind of need that Maika and Ami do not satisfy because it is not the same kind of relationship.

This is psychologically much more convincing than “lonely girl clings to first person who gives her attention.”

Miyagi already receives friendship.

**Sendai produces a new category of wanting.**

---

# XVII. Maika notices the consequences before she knows the cause

The final pages contain the SS's most important dramatic irony.

Maika remembers:

> 「一昨日会ったとき、なんだか少し元気がなかったっけ。」

Shiori seemed a little low when they met two days ago. Shuukura - LN01 Release SS - 宇都…

Maika does not know why.

We do.

Within Volume 1's relational movement, Miyagi's mood has been increasingly affected by Sendai.

This creates our first clear outside evidence that the secret relationship is **leaking into Miyagi's ordinary social self**.

The apartment is not containing it anymore.

Maika cannot name the cause, but she can see the symptom.

That connects directly to one of our longitudinal motifs from Volume 1:

> **intimacy leaves evidence.**

Sometimes the evidence is:

-   a bite;

-   a mark;

-   an eraser.


Here it is:

> a friend noticing that Shiori does not seem quite herself.

---

# XVIII. Secrecy and privacy are not the same thing

This SS also encourages an important distinction.

Maika and Honda have a context-specific relationship: close at cram school, barely conversational at school.

That is **private/contextual**, but not apparently secret in an anxious sense.

Miyagi and Sendai's relationship is different because active concealment is part of its structure.

The difference is:

### Privacy

> This relationship happens mostly here.

### Secrecy

> This relationship must not become legible elsewhere because its visibility would force us to acknowledge what it is.

Volume 1 begins with something that could plausibly have remained merely private.

By the end, it has become increasingly secretive precisely because its emotional meaning has grown.

Maika gives us the comparison needed to see that distinction.

---

# XIX. Why Maika is already analytically important

After only this short story, Maika appears positioned to perform several important functions in the larger work.

### 1\. External observer

She can show us behavioral changes Miyagi does not notice in herself.

### 2\. Reality check

She prevents Miyagi's self-narration from becoming our sole model of who Miyagi is.

### 3\. Social-world interpreter

She gives us information about school reputation without being deeply invested in status.

### 4\. Model of ordinary friendship

Her relationship with Miyagi demonstrates what voluntary, reciprocal, low-drama intimacy looks like.

### 5\. Epistemic contrast

She distinguishes rumors from knowledge better than the protagonists distinguish defenses from feelings.

None of this means Maika is an omniscient “truth teller.”

The Hamina section explicitly shows her carrying stereotypes.

But she is **correctable**.

That may be more important.

---

# XX. The title is better than it first appears

**宇都宮舞香は思考する — Utsunomiya Maika Thinks**

Very little “happens.”

Maika:

-   waits through cram school;

-   messages Shiori;

-   doodles;

-   talks to Honda;

-   thinks about Hamina and Sendai;

-   receives another message;

-   changes tomorrow's plans slightly.


But the story is about how a relatively ordinary adolescent constructs knowledge about other people.

She moves between:

-   direct experience;

-   friendship;

-   gossip;

-   reputation;

-   inference;

-   uncertainty.


And importantly, she usually knows which is which.

The protagonists' great problem is that they do not.

So this innocuous release SS actually introduces a useful epistemological baseline for the entire work:

> **What do I know about another person, and what have I merely decided must be true?**

That question applies to school gossip.

It also applies to love.

---

# XXI. Revisions to the Volume 1 synthesis

After incorporating 「宇都宮舞香は思考する」, I would make five explicit amendments to our Volume 1 conclusions.

### 1\. Miyagi is not generally incapable of ordinary intimacy

She already possesses easy, voluntary friendship with Maika and Ami.

Her elaborate contract with Sendai is therefore **Sendai-specific**.

### 2\. The ¥5,000 is even more clearly an emotional alibi

Miyagi can simply ask Maika to see a movie.

She cannot simply ask Sendai to come over without maintaining the contractual fiction.

The problem is not asking.

The problem is **what asking Sendai would admit**.

### 3\. School hierarchy is real but psychologically negotiable

Maika independently sees Sendai and Hamina as socially distant “glittering” girls.

But she doesn't organize her self-worth around that distance.

Miyagi's anxiety is therefore not invented, but it is magnified.

### 4\. Context-bounded relationships can be perfectly healthy

Maika/Honda demonstrates this.

The unusual part of Miyagi/Sendai is not simply that they behave differently in two locations.

It is that they use those locations to avoid naming their relationship.

### 5\. Miyagi's Sendai-related distress is beginning to become externally visible

Maika notices Shiori has seemed down.

The private relationship is already affecting the public/ordinary Miyagi.

That will be worth tracking.

---

# XXII. Final assessment

This is exactly the kind of supplementary short story that justifies its inclusion in our reading order.

It adds almost no plot.

It adds **substantial calibration**.

Its central contribution is to reveal a Miyagi who already knows how to:

-   have friends;

-   send casual messages;

-   initiate plans;

-   accept ordinary concern;

-   inhabit a secure place in someone else's emotional life.


That means the strange behavior we see with Sendai cannot be explained away as Miyagi simply “not knowing how relationships work.”

She knows how friendship works.

**Sendai is the problem because Sendai is becoming something for which Miyagi has no safe category.**

And Maika's final act—quietly postponing a movie so she can have enough time to talk with a friend who seems unhappy—provides perhaps the cleanest counterimage to Volume 1's entire ¥5,000 machinery:

> **Maika wants time with Shiori, so she makes time for Shiori.
> No contract is necessary.**

That ordinary simplicity is precisely what Miyagi and Sendai are not yet capable of with each other.
