# SHUUKURA V07 Voice-Drama Supplement Analysis

# XLII. The voice dramas fit this stage of characterization unusually well

There is a useful archival clarification here.

For Volume 7, Haneda confirms **three pieces of audio material** in practice: one publicly released newly written drama, **「朝、ベッドの上の私たち」**, and two purchase-bonus limited dramas. Haneda says she wrote the public script herself; Lynn continues as Miyagi and Kana Ichinose as Sendai. # XLIII. But the public audio already gives us a meaningful characterization update

This is one place where Haneda's own response to listeners is unusually useful.

A listener specifically commented that Miyagi's delivery seemed to have **less of the old thorniness** than in earlier voice material. Haneda responded that Miyagi had indeed become **a little softer**. She separately confirmed the strong just-awakened quality of Sendai's performance. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://kakuyomu.jp&sz=128)カクヨム - 「書ける、読める、伝えられる」新しいWeb小説サイト](https://kakuyomu.jp/users/hanedausa/news/16818792438883312910)

That fits the prose exceptionally well.

Miyagi's development is **not**:

> prickly Miyagi disappears and warm Miyagi replaces her.

Her vocabulary remains Miyagi's.

She still:

-   complains;

-   denies;

-   calls Sendai stupid;

-   resists flattering interpretations;

-   disguises requests as irritation.


What changes is the emotional load carried by the same language.

The thorns are becoming less defensive.

That is exactly the kind of character development that voice acting can reveal particularly well.

# XLIV. Lynn's Miyagi should not become “dere”; she should become less armored

Haneda's comment that Miyagi is somewhat softer is almost the ideal vocal direction for this stage.

If Lynn suddenly played Miyagi as openly affectionate, the performance would outrun the prose.

Volume 7 Miyagi still cannot say:

> Sendai is special to me.

She still cannot call her Hazuki.

She still hides presents.

She still insists she wasn't waiting.

But compare the emotional function of her resistance.

Early Miyagi's harshness often creates distance.

Volume 7 Miyagi's harshness often occurs **while maintaining proximity**.

> Don't do that. Stay here.

> I'm not waiting. Hurry up.

> We're still roommates. Don't go away.

The same rough surface increasingly encloses attachment rather than exclusion.

A slightly softened delivery is therefore not merely “cute acting.”

It is a vocal representation of a defense mechanism becoming less necessary.

# XLV. Ichinose's sleepy Sendai represents an equally important development

Sendai was originally introduced through an image of social competence:

adaptable, pleasant, attractive, good at navigating people.

A highly groggy Sendai lying around in Miyagi's bed is almost the inverse of that public persona.

And that is intimacy too.

Not:

> Sendai shows Miyagi an emotionally dramatic secret.

But:

> **Sendai is allowed to be uncomposed.**

Cohabitation gives Miyagi access to a version of Sendai who is:

-   sleepy;

-   lazy;

-   not socially optimized;

-   occupying Miyagi's bed;

-   embedded in ordinary morning routine.


That is perfectly consistent with Miyagi's long-standing desire for the Sendai other people do not receive.

Except now the exclusivity isn't manufactured through humiliating commands.

It emerges naturally from living together.

# XLVI. The audio also confirms that their “couple voice” is becoming domestic

The public drama is titled **「朝、ベッドの上の私たち」**—*Us, in Bed in the Morning*. Haneda explicitly describes it as newly written Volume 7 material. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://kakuyomu.jp&sz=128)カクヨム - 「書ける、読める、伝えられる」新しいWeb小説サイト](https://kakuyomu.jp/users/hanedausa/news/16818792438883312910)

The important word is arguably not “bed.”

It's:

> **morning.**

Earlier audio drama naturally emphasized the strange, private, game-like quality of their after-school relationship.

Now the performative interest lies in:

> how do these two sound after sleeping, when breakfast and the rest of the day are waiting?

Again, ordinary time has become the major romantic frontier.

Their voices can now communicate relationship progression without either character acquiring a new vocabulary.

Miyagi sounds a little less armored.

Sendai is comfortable enough to sound thoroughly unpresentable.

That's a believable acoustic version of cohabitation.

# XLVII. Maika receiving a voice is important even before we hear the limited pieces

Haneda specifically says Sayumi Suzushiro fits her image of Maika. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://kakuyomu.jp&sz=128)カクヨム - 「書ける、読める、伝えられる」新しいWeb小説サイト](https://kakuyomu.jp/users/hanedausa/news/16818792438779290047)

That matters formally because until this point the audio world has been dominated by the closed Miyagi–Sendai dyad.

Adding Maika means another person can **hear and respond to them in real time**.

That is the sonic counterpart of what Volume 7 is doing socially.

The relationship is leaving the sealed room.

Maika's increasing presence in their household means the two-character private language now has to operate in a three-person acoustic space.

Once you obtain **「宇都宮舞香がやってきた日」**, that is what I would listen for most carefully:

not merely “what does Maika sound like?” but:

> **How do Miyagi and Sendai's voices change when someone who knows Miyagi well is physically present?**

Does Miyagi revert toward an old social register?

Does Sendai become more performatively pleasant?

Does their private cadence leak through anyway?

That could be one of the most analytically useful audio pieces in the series.
