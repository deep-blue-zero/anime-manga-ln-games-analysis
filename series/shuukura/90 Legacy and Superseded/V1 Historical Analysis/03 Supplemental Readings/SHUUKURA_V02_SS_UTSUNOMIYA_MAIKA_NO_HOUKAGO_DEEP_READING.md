# SHUUKURA V02 SS — 「宇都宮舞香の放課後」

# 『週に一度クラスメイトを買う話』

## Volume 2 Supplement — 「宇都宮舞香の放課後」

### The negative space of the secret relationship: friendship, suspicion, and where Shiori’s after-school time has gone

### Scope

This is the two-part Volume 2 release SS, **「宇都宮舞香の放課後（前編／後編）」**, read after Volume 2 according to our established order. The merged archival copy contains both halves in sequence. Shuukura - LN02 Release SS - 宇都…

Like 「宇都宮舞香は思考する」, it is narrated by **Utsunomiya Maika**. But its function is substantially different.

The Volume 1 supplement showed us:

> **what Miyagi looks like inside an ordinary, healthy friendship.**

This one shows us:

> **what Miyagi and Sendai's secret relationship looks like from the social world they are withdrawing from.**

And that makes it more important than its light, comic framing initially suggests.

---

# I. Central Thesis: Sendai Is Now Visible Primarily as Miyagi's Absence

The story takes place, pointedly, during:

> **志緒理のいない放課後**
> an after-school period without Shiori.

That phrase is more important than it looks.

The main series has spent two volumes turning **放課後 — after school** into the core unit of Miyagi and Sendai's relationship.

Sendai's after-school time becomes:

-   summoned;

-   purchased;

-   ritualized;

-   accumulated as ¥5,000 notes;

-   eventually claimed by Miyagi through graduation.


Volume 2's LN-original bonus was even titled:

> **「仙台さんの放課後が五千円札になるまで」**
> *Until Sendai-san's After-School Became a ¥5,000 Note.*

Then this supplementary story gives us:

> **「宇都宮舞香の放課後」**
> *Utsunomiya Maika's After School.*

The pairing is almost perfect.

From Miyagi and Sendai's perspective, their strange private arrangement is producing intimacy.

From Maika's perspective, that same intimacy manifests as:

> **Shiori isn't here anymore.**

This is the first strong reminder that time is not infinitely expandable.

Every afternoon Miyagi gives Sendai is also an afternoon she does **not** give Maika and Ami.

Every afternoon Sendai spends in Miyagi's room is likewise time taken from her public social world.

The ¥5,000 arrangement therefore has an unspoken third cost:

> **opportunity cost.**

Miyagi and Sendai aren't merely building a private world.

They are reallocating pieces of their ordinary lives into it.

---

# II. The Opening Joke Is Almost Absurdly Close to the Truth

Ami declares the beginning of the:

> 「第三百二十七回、志緒理と仙台さんの接点を探せ会議」

roughly, the “327th Meeting to Find the Connection Between Shiori and Sendai-san.”

Maika immediately points out that they've held nothing like 327 meetings. Ami simply thinks a large number sounds better. Shuukura - LN02 Release SS - 宇都…

This establishes Ami's mode:

> exaggeration → wild speculation → accidental proximity to reality.

They are trying to explain why Sendai appeared at Miyagi's classroom and why Miyagi behaved strangely.

Ami offers absurd possibilities:

-   perhaps Sendai is extorting money from Shiori;

-   perhaps Shiori knows some weakness of Sendai's and is blackmailing her.


Both are dismissed as ridiculous.

And of course the reader knows:

> **There actually is money.
> There actually is an asymmetrical power arrangement.
> Miyagi actually does use leverage to make Sendai do things.**

Ami has the *wrong mechanism* and often the wrong direction of causation, but the structure of her joke brushes astonishingly close to the truth.

That functions as more than comedy.

The real relationship is so socially implausible that **an approximation of the truth can be spoken aloud and still sound like nonsense**.

That's a remarkably effective form of camouflage.

---

# III. School Hierarchy Is Helping Them Keep the Secret

Ami ultimately concludes something like:

> 「仙台さんと志緒理に接点があるわけないよねえ」

There's no way Sendai-san and Shiori could really have a connection.

This confirms something we have been tracking since Volume 1.

Miyagi's belief that Sendai belongs to a different social world is **not purely insecurity**.

Maika and Ami independently share the assumption that these two girls should not have much to do with one another. Shuukura - LN02 Release SS - 宇都…

That gives the secret relationship an interesting form of social protection:

> **people see evidence, but their model of the school's social structure tells them the evidence cannot mean what it appears to mean.**

Sendai arrives at Miyagi's classroom.

Miyagi behaves strangely.

Maika notices.

Ami notices.

But the explanation:

> Miyagi and Sendai possess an intensely private, physically intimate, financially ritualized relationship

is so far outside their prior expectations that a banal explanation is far easier to accept.

Social hierarchy becomes epistemological camouflage.

The school has taught everyone which relationships are plausible.

Miyagi and Sendai exploit that unintentionally simply by being implausible together.

---

# IV. Maika's Most Important Trait Reappears: She Distinguishes Evidence from Speculation

The first Volume 1 SS established Maika as someone who carries impressions and stereotypes but generally understands the difference between:

> what she believes

and

> what she actually knows.

That becomes explicit here.

Ami has fun inventing explanations.

Maika eventually redirects the conversation:

> instead of speculating about the connection with Sendai, they should worry about something they **can actually observe**.

Shiori has seemed:

> **心ここにあらず** — mentally elsewhere / absent-minded.

She was already somewhat down during spring break.

Maika notes that Shiori is not normally someone whose emotions fluctuate dramatically. Therefore a sustained visible change probably means something has happened. Shuukura - LN02 Release SS - 宇都…

This gives Maika a very different interpretive ethic from the two protagonists.

Miyagi and Sendai often do:

> feeling → theory → treat theory as explanation.

Maika does:

> observation → possible inference → retain uncertainty.

Her implicit principle is:

> **Worry first about what you can actually see.**

It is a modest epistemology, but in this series modest epistemology is unusually valuable.

---

# V. Maika Is Wrong—but Wrong in a Healthy Way

This deserves qualification.

Maika's caution does prevent her from discovering the truth.

She notices inconsistencies but repeatedly tells herself:

> Shiori says nothing is happening with Sendai.
> I have no concrete reason to accuse her of lying.
> Therefore I should trust her.

And she is objectively mistaken.

Shiori **is** lying by omission and sometimes more directly.

So the story does not simply tell us:

> Maika's method discovers truth.

It doesn't.

The tradeoff is more interesting.

Maika refuses to treat intimacy with Shiori as authorization to interrogate her.

She accepts the risk of not knowing everything because she considers Shiori entitled to decide what she discloses.

That may make Maika less effective as a detective.

It makes her a better friend.

---

# VI. The Second Half Reveals a More Precise Miyagi Trait: She Talks, but Rarely Discloses Herself

One of Maika's sharpest observations is:

> Shiori is simultaneously talkative and not talkative.

She will talk about:

-   manga;

-   anime;

-   school;

-   things she dislikes;

-   ordinary preferences.


But Maika cannot remember Shiori coming to her for serious advice about:

-   someone she likes;

-   her future;

-   deeply personal worries.


Ami jokingly wonders:

> Maybe Shiori doesn't trust us.

Maika finds the possibility genuinely depressing. Shuukura - LN02 Release SS - 宇都…

This substantially refines Miyagi's characterization.

After 「宇都宮舞香は思考する」 we established:

> Miyagi is not generally socially incapable.

Now we can refine further:

> **Miyagi is socially communicative but emotionally compartmentalized.**

She can maintain close friendship while preserving an inner territory that even those friends do not enter.

That matters enormously for understanding Sendai.

Sendai is not merely becoming another close friend.

She is gradually occupying precisely the interior territory Miyagi normally withholds from friendship.

---

# VII. That Explains Why Sendai Is So Destabilizing

Maika and Ami have known Shiori for years.

They possess:

-   public legitimacy;

-   ordinary affection;

-   routine invitations;

-   first-name access;

-   comfortable physical familiarity.


Yet they apparently do **not** possess much access to:

-   Shiori's fear of abandonment;

-   her romantic desire;

-   her sexual curiosity;

-   her deepest dependency;

-   the emotionally empty character of her home life in the way Sendai experiences it;

-   her terror of becoming attached to someone who might leave.


Sendai does.

And Sendai obtains that access through what began as an absurd financial contract.

So the hierarchy of intimacy is now strange:

### Maika and Ami

**socially recognized, emotionally safe, publicly close**

but partially excluded from Miyagi's deepest private self.

### Sendai

**socially unrecognized, structurally secret, categorically undefined**

but increasingly given access to Miyagi's body, home, fear, dependency, and desire.

That helps explain why “friend” remains such an inadequate label.

Calling Sendai simply a friend would misleadingly place her beside Maika and Ami.

She occupies a different psychological depth.

---

# VIII. “Our Shiori” Produces an Interesting Mirror of Possessiveness

Ami jokes:

> 「もう私たちの志緒理じゃなくなっちゃったんだよ」

roughly:

> Shiori isn't *our Shiori* anymore.

The tone is theatrical. Ami even mock-cries.

But Maika admits there is something underneath the joke that bothers her too. Shuukura - LN02 Release SS - 宇都…

This is worth comparing to Miyagi and Sendai's possessiveness.

All close relationships involve some expectation of continued access.

Maika and Ami are accustomed to:

> Want to stop somewhere after school?

being an ordinary part of their friendship.

When Shiori repeatedly refuses, they experience a mild sense of loss.

So possessiveness itself is not unique to the central relationship.

The difference is degree and defensive structure.

### Maika/Ami

> We miss our friend.
> We wish she still spent more time with us.

### Miyagi/Sendai

> I need privileged access to you.
> I need proof that your relationship with me cannot be replaced.
> I need rules, marks, names, commands, or repeated return to stabilize that belief.

The SS thus helps distinguish **ordinary relational claim** from the much more intense attachment anxiety driving Miyagi and Sendai.

---

# IX. The Story Gives Us a Date for the Social Consequences

Maika says something especially useful:

> **around the summer of their second year, Shiori became harder to get to spend time with.**

She initially didn't consider it particularly noteworthy.

Now, in third year, repeated refusals combined with Shiori's changed demeanor make her suspect there is some underlying reason. Shuukura - LN02 Release SS - 宇都…

This is extremely valuable because it provides an **external behavioral timeline** for the Miyagi–Sendai relationship.

Inside their alternating narration, change happens incrementally:

-   one meeting;

-   another ¥5,000;

-   another command;

-   food;

-   homework;

-   touch;

-   routine.


From outside, the same process registers much more simply:

> **Shiori stopped being available.**

That is the public footprint of private attachment.

---

# X. Volume 2's “After-School Economy” Becomes Visible

This lets us sharpen the money motif further.

The story's actual scarce resource has never really been ¥5,000.

Miyagi has enough money.

Sendai doesn't even spend it.

The scarce resource is:

> **time.**

Specifically:

> **after-school time.**

Miyagi's money formally converts Sendai's time into something she can claim.

But as the arrangement deepens, Miyagi also devotes her own time to Sendai.

So despite the apparent one-way transaction:

> Miyagi buys Sendai's afternoon,

the actual exchange becomes:

> **Miyagi and Sendai mutually reserve afternoons for one another.**

Maika and Ami experience the result as lost access.

Thus the ¥5,000 is really participating in a broader **economy of attention**.

There are only so many afternoons before graduation.

By the end of Volume 2, both protagonists have realized that.

Maika and Ami unknowingly feel the other side of the same scarcity.

---

# XI. The French Fry Scenes Are a Quietly Excellent Control Case for Physical Intimacy

Maika and Ami repeatedly share food casually.

Maika offers Ami fries.

Later she simply carries one toward Ami's mouth and Ami eats it.

Maika also pulls Ami's cheek while joking about her weight.

No one interprets any of this as extraordinary. Shuukura - LN02 Release SS - 宇都…

This is analytically useful because *Shuukura* contains so much bodily contact that it would be easy to treat touch itself as proof of romantic meaning.

The SS reminds us:

> **adolescent female friendship can comfortably include casual feeding, touching, teasing, and bodily familiarity without that contact becoming erotically charged.**

What makes Miyagi and Sendai different is not simply:

> they touch.

It is the subjective meaning surrounding the touch:

-   obsessive attention;

-   anticipation;

-   jealousy;

-   bodily arousal;

-   exclusive access;

-   fear of escalation;

-   fear of what the act reclassifies.


Maika can feed Ami a fry and immediately continue the conversation.

Miyagi and Sendai can turn a piece of food into a crisis over what their relationship means.

That contrast makes the central dyad's eroticization much more precise.

---

# XII. Maika and Ami Also Model a Completely Different Kind of Bodily Ease

Their friendship possesses something Miyagi and Sendai badly lack:

> **unselfconsciousness.**

A fry goes into Ami's mouth.

A cheek gets pulled.

Nothing needs to be:

-   priced;

-   authorized;

-   reinterpreted;

-   memorialized;

-   denied.


The act means exactly what it appears to mean.

This is why ordinary friendship keeps functioning as such a useful comparison.

Miyagi and Sendai aren't remarkable because they engage in behaviors nobody else would.

They are remarkable because **almost every behavior between them becomes overdetermined**.

A name is not just a name.

A drink is not just a drink.

A shirt is not just a shirt.

A kiss certainly isn't just a kiss.

Even homework and French toast accumulate symbolic weight.

Maika and Ami show us what social life looks like when objects and gestures don't have to carry that burden.

---

# XIII. Ami Is More Perceptive Than Her Comic Persona Suggests

The first half initially positions Ami as the comic theorist.

She exaggerates.

She invents blackmail plots.

She prefers the possibility that “something interesting” is happening.

But in the second half her concern becomes genuine.

She notices:

-   Shiori is unusually absent-minded;

-   Shiori has become difficult to make plans with;

-   something may be bothering her;

-   Shiori does not tell them much about herself.


Her joke—

> maybe she doesn't trust us—

contains a real fear. Shuukura - LN02 Release SS - 宇都…

So Ami should not be reduced to the loud comic friend.

Her method differs from Maika's.

Maika responds to uncertainty by narrowing claims.

Ami responds by generating possibilities.

Both are trying to understand Shiori.

One is epistemically cautious.

The other is imaginative.

And ironically, Ami's ridiculous hypotheses occasionally get closer to the bizarre truth precisely because she is willing to imagine improbable explanations.

---

# XIV. There Is a Genuine Epistemological Tradeoff Between Maika and Ami

This is a particularly interesting pair.

### Maika

Strength:

> does not mistake imagination for knowledge.

Weakness:

> social plausibility strongly constrains what she will consider possible.

### Ami

Strength:

> willing to consider explanations far outside ordinary expectations.

Weakness:

> does not discriminate strongly between plausible and ridiculous possibilities.

If you combined both modes, you would have a much better investigator:

> Ami's hypothesis generation + Maika's evidentiary discipline.

Neither alone discovers the truth.

And that is appropriate.

Their job in the story isn't to expose Miyagi and Sendai.

Their job is to show us how difficult the secret relationship is to infer from ordinary social evidence.

---

# XV. The Real Concern Is Not Sendai—It Is Shiori's Change

The SS ultimately turns away from its nominal mystery.

They begin with:

> What is the connection between Shiori and Sendai?

They end with something closer to:

> **Why does Shiori seem different?**

That movement is important.

Maika explicitly decides that speculative Sendai theories matter less than something she can directly observe:

> her friend seems mentally absent and perhaps unhappy.

This is a morally better question.

It changes Shiori from:

> object of a mystery

back into:

> person they care about.

The difference resembles one of the larger ethical challenges in the central relationship.

Miyagi and Sendai often treat one another as:

-   puzzles;

-   experiments;

-   things to provoke;

-   sources of reactions.


Their healthiest moments occur when they stop asking:

> What can I make this person do?

and start asking:

> How is this person actually doing?

Maika reaches that stance much more naturally.

---

# XVI. But Shiori's Secrecy Is Beginning to Hurt Other People

Up to this point, Miyagi's refusal to define the relationship has mostly harmed:

-   Miyagi;

-   Sendai.


This SS introduces a small but meaningful third-party cost.

Maika and Ami are beginning to wonder:

> Does Shiori trust us?

They feel:

-   excluded;

-   slightly rejected;

-   lonely without her;

-   confused by her repeated refusal of plans.


Miyagi is entitled to privacy.

She does not owe her friends disclosure of her intimate relationship.

But privacy does not mean choices have no relational consequences.

She has been:

-   disappearing after school;

-   giving vague explanations;

-   visibly carrying emotional distress;

-   refusing plans.


Her friends reasonably perceive the behavioral change.

This matters because the story refuses a simplistic principle like:

> Secrecy only affects the people keeping the secret.

It doesn't.

Other relationships adjust around the missing information.

---

# XVII. That Does Not Mean Miyagi Owes Maika and Ami a Confession

The counterargument matters.

It would be easy to say:

> Miyagi should simply tell her friends.

But at this stage, **what exactly would she tell them?**

She and Sendai themselves cannot agree on what they are.

The literal explanation sounds absurd:

> I pay Sendai ¥5,000 to come home with me and obey commands, except sometimes I don't pay her, and we kiss, but we're not friends, and we're not dating, and it ends at graduation.

Disclosure does not automatically create clarity when the people involved lack clarity themselves.

Miyagi's secrecy is therefore not simply dishonesty.

It also reflects the absence of a usable narrative.

She cannot explain the relationship to Maika because she cannot explain it to herself.

That doesn't erase the impact on Maika.

It makes the problem harder.

---

# XVIII. Maika and Ami's Response Is Crucially Non-Coercive

By the end, Maika admits that Shiori's absence feels lonely.

But she concludes:

> they can't forcibly drag her along.

Then:

> 「志緒理のことは待つしかないかな。そのうち話、してくれるでしょ」

They may simply have to wait; perhaps Shiori will eventually talk. Shuukura - LN02 Release SS - 宇都…

This is probably the story's most important ethical counterpoint.

Consider how the protagonists respond when the other withdraws:

### Miyagi

provokes, orders, withdraws preemptively, tests.

### Sendai

pursues, confronts, appears unexpectedly, forces opportunities for conversation.

### Maika

notices, worries, remains available, waits.

That doesn't make Maika automatically “correct” in every situation. Sometimes active intervention is necessary.

But here her restraint provides a model of **care without entitlement**.

She wants disclosure.

She doesn't believe wanting it gives her the right to extract it.

---

# XIX. This Is Exactly What Miyagi Has Trouble Believing Is Possible

Miyagi's abandonment psychology tends to assume that relationships require control to remain stable.

Some version of:

> If I don't create rules, why would this person stay?

But Maika and Ami are literally sitting in a fast-food restaurant feeling hurt by Shiori's absence and still deciding:

> we'll wait for her.

This is enormously important even though Miyagi never witnesses the conversation.

The reader now knows something Miyagi does not fully trust:

> **People can care about Shiori while giving her space.
> They can be disappointed without leaving.
> They can lack answers without demanding control.**

The existence of Maika and Ami quietly falsifies parts of Miyagi's emotional worldview.

---

# XX. “Wait” Is the Exact Opposite of the ¥5,000

The contrast can be stated almost mathematically.

The ¥5,000 arrangement answers uncertainty with:

> **guaranteed access.**

Maika answers uncertainty with:

> **trust without access.**

Miyagi's structure says:

> Come because I paid you.

Maika's friendship says:

> Come when you're ready.

The former feels safer to Miyagi precisely because it seems enforceable.

The latter requires faith.

And faith is much harder for someone who has learned that people can leave.

This may be the deepest thematic value of the SS.

---

# XXI. The Story Makes Volume 2's Ending More Poignant

At the end of Volume 2, Miyagi claims part of Sendai's after-school time until graduation.

Sendai says six months is short.

From inside the relationship, that is romantic and frightening:

> their remaining time feels scarce.

This supplementary story forces us to widen the camera.

While Miyagi is trying to guarantee Sendai's remaining afternoons:

> Maika and Ami are noticing that they receive fewer of Miyagi's.

Nobody is doing anything monstrous.

This is simply how attachment works.

Choosing one relationship more intensely reorganizes other relationships.

That is a maturation theme the series may increasingly have to confront:

> intimacy is not merely something added to a life.
> It changes the distribution of the life already there.

---

# XXII. This Also Complicates Sendai's Jealousy of Maika

Volume 2 showed Sendai envying Maika's effortless legitimacy.

Maika can:

-   call Miyagi Shiori;

-   make ordinary plans;

-   spend time with her publicly;

-   touch her casually.


But this SS reveals something Sendai does not know:

> **Maika is losing access to Shiori because of Sendai.**

The jealousy is therefore asymmetrical in an almost tragicomic way.

Sendai sees Maika and thinks:

> She has a place in Miyagi's life that I don't.

Maika sees Miyagi's absence and thinks:

> Something has taken Shiori away from us.

Both are partly right.

Neither knows the other's position.

This is a good example of *Shuukura*'s favorite structure:

> two incomplete perspectives producing mutually incompatible but individually reasonable interpretations.

---

# XXIII. Maika's “Ordinary Shiori” Remains Crucial

In the second half, Maika again describes Shiori as:

> **ごく普通の彼女** — an entirely ordinary girl like them.

This contrasts sharply with how Sendai increasingly experiences Miyagi.

To Sendai, Miyagi has become:

-   uniquely interesting;

-   uniquely irritating;

-   uniquely desirable;

-   impossible to categorize;

-   difficult to replace.


To Maika:

> she's Shiori.

This is not because Maika understands her less.

It's because familiarity normalizes a person.

That produces an important warning for our analysis:

> **romantic perspective exaggerates salience.**

Sendai's Miyagi is not necessarily more “true” than Maika's Miyagi.

Maika sees dimensions Sendai does not.

Sendai sees dimensions Maika does not.

The fuller character exists in the overlap.

---

# XXIV. The SS Strengthens the Series' Commitment to Multiperspectival Characterization

At this point, we have several Miyagis:

### Miyagi's Miyagi

A girl trying desperately to rationalize her own feelings.

### Sendai's Miyagi

Difficult, fascinating, possessive, increasingly desired.

### Maika's Miyagi

A familiar, slightly spacey, private friend whose recent behavior has become worrying.

None is false.

The character exists through the tension among them.

This is one reason the supplementary material is analytically valuable despite its small word count.

A dual-POV central novel risks allowing the couple to become a closed interpretive system.

Maika breaks the seal.

---

# XXV. The Title Now Looks Deliberately Thematic

After this reading, **「宇都宮舞香の放課後」** feels almost pointed.

Whose after-school is the series about?

At first glance:

> Sendai's, because Miyagi buys it.

But after-school belongs to an entire social ecosystem.

Maika has after-school expectations.

Ami has them.

Hamina and Sendai's other acquaintances presumably do too.

Miyagi's strange purchase does not literally buy only three hours of Sendai.

As the relationship expands, it reorganizes:

> **everyone's access to everyone else.**

So the title shifts attention away from the purchased girl and asks:

> What happens to the afternoon of the friend who is left behind?

The answer isn't melodramatic.

She eats fries with Ami.

She laughs.

She worries.

She misses Shiori a little.

That mildness is precisely why it feels real.

---

# XXVI. Revisions to the Volume 2 Analysis

After incorporating 「宇都宮舞香の放課後」, I would add several explicit refinements to the Volume 2 synthesis.

### 1\. After-school time is the true scarce resource

The ¥5,000 symbolizes ownership, but the actual thing being redistributed is **time and attention**.

Maika and Ami give us direct evidence of the opportunity cost.

### 2\. Miyagi's emotional privacy predates Sendai

She has long been reluctant to discuss crushes, future plans, and deeper worries even with established close friends.

Sendai does not create Miyagi's secrecy.

She enters a preexisting private territory.

### 3\. Miyagi's secret relationship is now externally observable

Her friends cannot identify the cause, but they notice:

-   distracted behavior;

-   mood changes;

-   reduced availability.


The private world is leaking.

### 4\. Social hierarchy actively protects the secret

Maika and Ami see evidence but discount the Sendai hypothesis because the relationship seems socially implausible.

### 5\. Ordinary physical affection provides an important control

Maika and Ami casually feed and touch each other.

Thus Miyagi/Sendai physical contact is not meaningful merely because it is physical; its erotic and possessive *interpretation* is what distinguishes it.

### 6\. Secrecy now has network effects

Maika and Ami begin questioning whether Shiori trusts them.

Miyagi's defense mechanisms therefore affect relationships beyond the dyad.

### 7\. Maika models care without possession

Her final decision to wait for Shiori sharply contrasts with the protagonists' attempts to secure one another through money, commands, pursuit, and ritual.

---

# XXVII. A Small but Important Update to Maika's Character Profile

After two commemorative SS, Maika is becoming more important than a generic “normal friend.”

### Utsunomiya Maika

**Relational style:** stable, low-drama, attentive.

**Epistemic style:** separates direct observation from speculation; willing to admit uncertainty.

**Strength:** notices subtle behavioral changes in people she knows.

**Weakness:** social plausibility constrains what she considers possible; her trust can make deception easier to sustain.

**Care style:** time, availability, ordinary companionship, patience.

**Boundary style:** curious but not entitled to disclosure.

**Function in the narrative:** external calibration of Miyagi and a model of intimacy not organized around possession.

She may eventually become one of the most analytically useful secondary characters precisely because she is not as psychologically extreme as either protagonist.

---

# XXVIII. Ami Also Deserves a Provisional Profile

### Shirakawa Ami

**Relational style:** exuberant, teasing, physically easygoing.

**Epistemic style:** generates possibilities freely, including ridiculous ones.

**Strength:** notices anomalies and is less constrained by assumptions about what “should” be possible.

**Weakness:** poor discrimination between serious inference and entertaining speculation.

**Care style:** humor, presence, open concern.

**Emotional defense:** turns uncomfortable possibilities into jokes.

Her joke about not being trusted is particularly revealing.

She says it lightly because saying it seriously would expose that the possibility hurts.

In a much milder form, that's something she shares with the protagonists:

> use an indirect register to say something emotionally dangerous.

The difference is that Ami's indirection is playful rather than structurally consuming.

---

# XXIX. Final Assessment

「宇都宮舞香の放課後」 is more analytically useful than 「宇都宮舞香は思考する」 in one particular respect:

the first SS established a baseline.

This one records **deviation from that baseline**.

Before Sendai's growing presence:

> Shiori is an ordinary close friend who sends terse messages, goes to movies, talks about anime, and fits comfortably into Maika and Ami's lives.

Now:

> Shiori is still their friend, but increasingly unavailable, distracted, private, and difficult to explain.

The central relationship is becoming visible through **negative space**.

Maika does not see the kisses.

She sees cancelled after-school plans.

Ami does not see the ¥5,000.

She sees Shiori acting strangely when Sendai appears.

Neither sees the summer tutoring.

They see a friend becoming harder to reach.

That is exactly what an intimate secret often looks like from outside:

> not revelation, but subtraction.

And the story's most important ethical contrast is beautifully simple:

> Miyagi fears losing someone, so she tries to secure their time.
> Sendai fears losing someone, so she pursues them.
> Maika fears losing someone, and decides to **wait**.

For two protagonists obsessed with guarantees, that may eventually prove the most difficult form of intimacy to learn.
