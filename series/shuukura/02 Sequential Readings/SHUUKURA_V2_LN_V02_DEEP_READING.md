# V02: keeping a reason to meet while the reasons change

Miyagi Shiori and Sendai Hazuki preserve their paid arrangement by changing its uses. A summer tutoring proposal becomes genuine study, an agreed holiday exception and a weekly fee. Extra payments develop beside it, while some contact is first permitted without the additional money. Both characters try to recover an earlier ordinary relationship; the attempts themselves expose altered desire, expectations and mistrust. A public friendship performance succeeds at choosing a movie but fails as repair. The final small hair-care task permits a chosen proximity, and the first clearly spoken graduation deadline is acknowledged as short. None of this makes every refusal respected, every motive shared or the eventual relationship category settled.

The argument below uses the completed V02 boundary retrospectively. ## Retrospective argument through V02

### Useful study and an increasingly inadequate price

The summer arrangement changes what the money buys before it changes how the participants can describe wanting one another. Sendai proposes instruction three times for¥5,000, with no commands. Miyagi chooses Monday, Wednesday and Friday; the first actual week contains only Wednesday and Friday. Payment is then negotiated again: Miyagi insists on a weekly advance rather than Sendai's preferred completion of three sessions. The last week supplies a corresponding one-session case, again paid in full over Sendai's objection (O026/O028–030/O079). These are actual, distinguishable stages. Calling the entire holiday either a continuation of the old per-visit purchase or a single¥5,000 summer contract would obscure the meaningful renegotiation.

The new service is substantially performed. Miyagi works, makes errors, corrects them and finishes most assignments; Sendai teaches rather than simply completing everything. A limited bargain for some answers coexists with the expectation of genuine effort. By the last chapter, Miyagi completes work herself and correctly solves a problem after explanation (O031/O040/O086). Her June midterm decline and slightly improved July finals precede most summer tutoring, so the later teaching cannot retrospectively cause those earlier results. Nor is Sendai's forecast of better new-term grades an already observed outcome. The narrative allows a defensible modest conclusion—shared study has become practically effective—without certifying an academic transformation or numerical improvement.

An additional payment develops beside this service. On the friend-play Thursday, Sendai first declines the extra note and permits bounded touch without taking it. That is the source endpoint of chapter7. Chapter8 reveals that Miyagi subsequently forced the note on her after the contact, then describes a new fee first refused and eventually accepted. Later those break payments become familiar, though not every visit includes a break (O062/O068/O070/O073). The reader must retain both disclosures. The initial unpaid permission is real; the later money cannot be inserted into the earlier encounter's evidence packet. Sendai's experience of the fee as compensation for kissing is evidence about how it feels to her, not a fully negotiated price schedule for sexual services. The same denomination does not erase the actors' competing definitions of its purchase.

Miyagi insists on separating tutoring and contact rights, even while Sendai offers to combine them as less instruction is needed. Then, on August31, Sendai denies Miyagi's command authority because the *additional* fee has not been paid, although the tutor fee has (O070/O079–080). This reveals how a ritual can acquire several incompatible jurisdictions. It does not make an ordinary refusal invalid when no command fee exists. The distinction has explanatory force in the characters' thinking and can be used to avoid, permit or excuse an act; its normative force still requires examination of the actual request and response.

The June bonus gives this changing abstraction a material history. The original denomination happened to be in Miyagi's wallet. Later she actively learns how to replenish those notes and gives up an outing and a bookstore visit to do it. Chance origin becomes maintained infrastructure (O090–093). Her hypothetical¥1,000/¥10,000 outcomes are not tested histories, and Ami's joking “anything” immediately contracts to “depends on the content.” Miyagi recognizes the existence of limits in this ordinary conversation. She does not simply lack the concept of limited permission. Her selective treatment of Sendai therefore needs a context-sensitive explanation rather than a universal cognitive incapacity.

By September Sendai says privately that she no longer needs the money, but refuses to say so because it supplies a reason to come (O087). The fee is neither irrelevant nor an adequate explanation of the desire. A strong reading treats it as an actively maintained means of continuation that can also obstruct a more direct conversation. The competing ordinary explanation—habitual companionship under an eccentric arrangement—remains useful, especially for meals and study. The source does not require every note to be a disguised confession or every objection to taking one to be theatrical.

### Attempts to restore normality can produce the change they resist

Both characters repeatedly imagine that distance, ordinary activity or the calendar will undo what recent contact has made salient. Miyagi wants summer separation to dispose of feelings; Sendai independently hopes it would restore reason, yet offers tutoring. Neither is shown agreeing on that reset purpose. Both then choose the exception that prevents it (O025–028). They are conflicted decision-makers, not people without options, and not fully transparent to themselves.

Sendai's account after the forced reciprocal-changing episode is particularly discriminating. She tried to make an emotionally loaded act ordinary so that her earlier less affected paid-hours self could return. Her narration revises the passive “it ended up happening” account into an admission of wanting and failing to stop herself. The attempt did not work (O044–046). This explanation appears only in the following chapter; it was unavailable to Miyagi during the action. It is strong evidence for Sendai's later self-understanding, qualified evidence for a complete account of her earlier causal state. It does not make coercion a necessary or justified route to insight.

An ordinary weekend with Umina does help her change mental focus, sleep and return to exam school and tutoring. Shopping alone during the next lesson also provides a reported interruption of the intrusive thought cycle (O047–050). Those successes matter. The text is not saying that all ordinary regulation fails or that the pair have no life beyond each other. But Sendai conceals the reason for the errand, disregards Miyagi's request to continue study, then converts the proposed snack into another complicated interaction. A measure that prevents one possible bodily intrusion can still fail another person's current practical expectation.

Miyagi also anticipates a quiet last lesson and wants the vacation to end before a more consequential crossing. She has actually omitted a break after the restraint episode, a limited change in conduct. Nevertheless, wanting to see Sendai's face turns into another approach; the chapter proceeds beyond her plan (O078–081). The story sets thought, intention, self-instruction and actual behavior beside one another. It does not allow an intention alone to prove either successful restraint or a secretly predetermined plan to escalate.

School resumes, but the final chapter denies the proposed automatic reset. The room remains available, homework continues and the weather stays hot. Seating and conversational ease have changed. Sendai can acknowledge desire yet find accepting this particular desire difficult; Miyagi can summon her while fearing she might not come. Neither anticipated ending actually occurs (O084–087). A familiar environment is not a restored earlier state. That distinction should guide later developmental reconstruction: the same room and vocabulary may now invoke a different history.

There is a credible objection to making failed resets the master explanation. Some scenes show direct appetite, anger, rivalry, privacy protection or ordinary practical concern, rather than a deliberate effort to recover an earlier self. Sendai explicitly cannot always explain herself; Miyagi sometimes knows what she wants but withholds it. The reset mechanism is a supported local pattern, not a psychological law covering every command. Future evidence could show a reset succeeding, or the actors choosing a different way to accommodate change. Nothing beyond this volume is assumed.

### The failed friendship performance asks whose desire is visible

The movie outing grows from Miyagi's request for ordinary behavior rather than habitual kissing. Sendai supplies the friendship category and proposes role-play; Miyagi accepts for mixed reasons, including her hope that shared performance can untwist the relationship. Neither alone authors the entire experiment (O056). Its failure is not proof that ordinary friendship activities are intrinsically impossible for them.

Several parts work. Miyagi prepares clothes, arrives early, apologizes according to a self-conscious script and already knows from Maika that friendship requires compromise. Sendai actually accommodates her qualified horror refusal, remembers a detail about endings, watches through the credits and discusses the movie. Miyagi enjoys it. Her clarification that a sad ending can still have a satisfying romantic outcome corrects an overly simple happy-ending rule (O057–059). Shared viewing succeeds even while the attempt to use it as relational repair fails.

The problem emerges when Sendai's public manner makes Miyagi feel that her companion's own preference has disappeared. Delegating the food choice was meant to let Sendai choose for herself; Sendai instead returns the choice to Miyagi's sweetness preference. What could be considerate in one context becomes alienating in this one. Miyagi wants evidence of an independent, particular other, not simply correct provision of her own favored item. Her rejection of the accommodating smile is therefore more precise than a preference for cruelty or an inability to receive kindness (O059–061).

That interpretation has limits. Miyagi does not tell Sendai the full private history that makes the manner troubling. Sendai later remains hurt and confused despite Miyagi's belief that she probably understands. The more critical review at home can coexist with genuine earlier enjoyment; it does not prove the entire public response was a lie (O061/O068). Public care and tactical adaptation are mixed even in Sendai's own account, and the final hair compliment is both sincere and intended to ease the atmosphere (O087). An “authentic private self versus wholly false public mask” binary would reproduce the very other-mind certainty the narration challenges.

Nor does Miyagi always want the old displeased expression. She explicitly says the wish has partly changed, while still pressing an injury as retaliation. After the outing, familiar irritation brings relief followed by a new emotional pain (O054/O062). This is uneven development, not the end of harmful behavior. The strongest counterreading—that she mainly wants a privileged response she can control—retains evidence in the commands and role tests. It becomes insufficient if it erases her new discomfort at causing distress, her request to know Sendai's preference, or her genuine enjoyment of shared ordinary activity.

The episode also resists equating public visibility with commitment. The distant destination reduces discovery risk; joint travel is contested; no acquaintance actually discovers the arrangement. Sendai's claim that everyone is away for Obon is ungrounded reassurance, not evidence of safe invisibility. Miyagi's school/secrecy concerns persist. The outing expands where the pair interact without establishing that either wants every familiar public friendship gesture or has accepted a new shared social identity.

### Rules, personal refusals and enacted permissions remain different

The volume sharpens distinctions already needed in V01. A proposed rule need not be ratified; an existing rule can be breached; a practical exception can be performed without settling every historical ambiguity; a clear personal refusal need not wait for contractual language. The rain accounts explicitly remember that the no-undressing proposal never obtained clear assent. They nonetheless perform a wet-clothes exception. Sendai later regrets that permission and calls it a rule failure (O019–021/O045/O065–066). All of these states can be preserved. None requires inventing an original shared clause or erasing the local assent because she later reevaluates it.

Commands can both constrain and enable choice. In September Sendai actually remains seated when Miyagi names the distance as a command. She admits privately that the command gives her a reason to retreat after repeatedly burdening Miyagi with decisions. Miyagi also defeats the false inability-to-read-the-maths pretext by turning the book (O086). These are real situational successes, not proof that every boundary is futile. The ability to negotiate one task cannot excuse disregard of another refusal.

The record contains explicit failures in both directions. Miyagi ignores stops, uses movement restriction and extends an act after discomfort. Sendai presses unwanted proximity, knowingly invents an extra-work entitlement, deflects private questions through unwanted contact and forces a choice between two unwanted means (O005–006/O011–014/O032/O036–037/O041–043). “No mark,” “you can flee,” “not phrased as a command,” “you did it too” and “payment already accepted” are their explanations or defenses. They are not analytical substitutes for action-specific permission.

The restraint episode adds a material contextual difference. Sendai distinguishes superficially similar contact from contact under combined movement and visual constraints; the experience becomes intolerable even though she has allowed other brief breaks. A requested voice or endpoint is refused, a plain stop does not suffice, and invocation of the broader no-sex/no-violence boundary finally halts the episode. She then clearly prohibits binding and blindfolding, while Miyagi disputes the status as a new rule (O074–076). The wording “not a rule, but forbidden” is especially useful: lack of formal ratification does not erase a person's stated limit. Future enforcement is still unread. One subsequent omitted break is evidence of consequence, not proof of complete repair.

The August31 sequence must also be described in phases. Initial personal limits are overridden; Miyagi later reciprocates and privately wants further closeness; the persistent bell supplies a pause and she chooses to respond. Sendai's invitation to choose can feel to Miyagi like evasion of responsibility. Miyagi recognizes her own similar avoidance (O080–083). Later willingness cannot retroactively authorize the initial continuation, and earlier refusal cannot erase the later represented choice. The interruption leaves a more intimate progression incomplete. September's statement that no rule was broken is specifically interpreted as no completed sex; it is not an authorial finding that no boundary failure occurred anywhere.

These distinctions support an action-specific agency evaluation, separate from descriptive source facts. They do not establish legal or clinical categories, equal pain, fixed dominance, total helplessness or a single moral identity for the relationship. There is evidence of affection, pressure, practical care, private desire, self-serving rationalization and successful limits. Serious interpretation must explain their coexistence and different conditions rather than cancel one set with the other.

### Partial learning changes the asymmetry without removing it

Miyagi learns concrete things: the magazine is disliked, teacher approval constrains Sendai's piercing choice, Sendai has a university-student sister, and the mother's reception differs from Miyagi's loving-home projection. She explicitly revises that projection after the visit. Her later temperature accommodation has a practical cost and a stated guilt-reducing motive; she refrains from further family inquiry (O003/O005/O035/O038/O040). This is actual other-person learning, not just the reader correcting an eternally uninformed Miyagi.

Yet the mother has no focalized interior account, and the visit does not reveal the full comparison/nonacceptance history to Miyagi. Sendai hides the money jar and buys bottled drinks partly to avoid leaving two used glasses. Care and concealment occur together. Miyagi's expectation that apology would anger Sendai is not tested; Sendai's dislike of perceived pity does not prove she knows Miyagi's exact inward reason. The analysis must preserve those gaps.

Sendai's Friday self-account gives the reader a current decision to leave home for an out-of-prefecture university. It is not shown disclosed to Miyagi, and it cannot establish that her tentative April answer was a lie. A later decision may be firmer than an earlier one (O046). Likewise, the reader's knowledge of Miyagi's maternal memory cannot resolve Sendai's question about a parent returning. Miyagi's “まだ帰って来ない” leaves an omitted referent, and Sendai notices the reply does not quite answer the question but does not pursue it (O072). Without Miyagi's own clarification at this boundary, assigning that reply definitively to father or mother would manufacture a shared fact.

Sendai's particular liking does become spoken, contrary to the V01 endpoint's deliberate withholding. Miyagi hears it but discounts the manner; a statement can be received without being believed at face value. Sendai subsequently confirms the remark was prompted and unplanned. Neither this utterance nor the later acknowledgment of desire establishes an agreed romantic category or a stable orientation identity (O043/O045/O084–085). The epistemic change is real but incomplete.

Miyagi's deliberate Umina-mark story is a different phenomenon from an uncertain interpretation. She knowingly fabricates a sighting to cover her own question, combining an actual remembered remedy with Umina's reputation; Maika accepts the explanation, but no later spread is established (O023). Attribution matters: the story is not evidence that Umina had a mark. The source differentiates intentional deception, partial disclosure, mistaken inference and uncertain memory. “Unreliable narrator” alone cannot do that work.

### A second rain account changes reasons and preserves a small discrepancy

The interlude is placed after the movie chapter, although its event time is earlier July. Shared dialogue, soaked uniform, umbrella and practical actions identify the paired scene. New preceding context explains Sendai's delay and inadequate umbrella without implying a planned provocation. She considers canceling but does not send the message, preferring the house with Miyagi and tea to her own home (O063). Narrative placement withholds that reason during Miyagi's original encounter.

The second perspective also reframes the uncompleted approach. Miyagi had treated open eyes as a risk of refusal; Sendai's interiority instead concerns seeing a less exposed Miyagi and equalizing what is visible. Her outward composure conceals disturbance. The reader can compare those accounts only now, and neither character thereby learns the other's whole explanation (O064–067). Later August behavior already read can qualify the remembered July scene retrospectively, but cannot count as a blind predictive success for a July statement revealed afterward.

A small weather detail remains unresolved: the Miyagi passage names a sunny forecast, the Sendai account a cloudy one. Different forecast sources or times, recollection, or narrative inconsistency are all possible. None is established. The discrepancy is not automatically an edition split, a separate fictional continuity or a reason to reject the otherwise aligned witness. It deserves exact locators and limited weight (O018/O063; DISCREPANCY-WEATHER-LN02-001). Restraint about a small inconsistency is part of the same method that refuses to harmonize larger knowledge differences by assertion.

The June bonus adds a second backward disclosure rather than moving the mainline clock. Miyagi now gives a private reason for bank visits that Sendai partly observes in July without possessing its whole aesthetic explanation. The reader already knows the jar that receives the money; Miyagi still wonders where it goes (O022/O090–093). Repetition lets different participants occupy different positions in the same material circuit. The formal pattern is not merely alternating emotion reports: it makes the timing of access an independent cause of interpretation.

### Objects, dates and ordinary details store what temporary contact cannot erase

The eraser, shirt, note, book, drink, food and hairstyle perform different work. Miyagi keeps the returned eraser unused while using a replacement; Sendai interprets that choice as meanness without Miyagi's memory account. The washed shirt has lost physical traces but keeps its association. Notes require labor and circulation. A folded page carries an unresolved repair dispute into a tutoring proposal. French toast becomes a genuine new preference that also carries an injury memory into a café with Maika (O007/O009/O022/O026/O044/O077/O090–093). Ordinary utility remains a counterexplanation in each case; none requires a universal symbolic key.

Calendar labels are more explicit. Miyagi fears that a remembered anniversary can turn future happiness into loss, and she qualifies her maternal memory as incomplete: she has not asked the reason, may have been told and forgotten, and remembers the event as sudden (O017). This is not proof the mother had no explanation or that childhood memory mechanically determines every act. Marks can be chosen instead of a date, yet fade physically while retaining association; August31 becomes salient despite the wish to avoid a label. Repetition does not guarantee forgetting.

Hair supplies the final modest alternative to escalation. The offer that Miyagi refused in June is requested in September. The permission has a task and a physical distance appropriate to it. Sendai chooses braiding partly to prolong contact and keeps that motive private, reworking the braid while Miyagi remains seated (O004/O087–088). The scene does not prove fully repaired trust—tension only partly loosens—but ordinary care can create a chosen proximity that earlier pretexts failed to obtain. This is an actual difference in means, not proof of a permanent new developmental stage already secure.

During that task Miyagi first clearly commands visits until graduation. Sendai explicitly calls the deadline newly fixed in speech. Earlier private claims that it had always been decided cannot be rewritten as an original shared contract. Both now estimate about six months and say it is short (O006/O088). They articulate a limit while registering the value of what it limits. The strongest restrained conclusion is a present, mutually heard horizon for continued calls, not a guarantee of either permanent closeness or actual final separation. Future behavior remains evidence to be read.

### Supporting people remain subjects with their own purposes

Maika has cram school, shopping wants, plans for the last holiday and an ordinary expectation of hearing about her friend's summer. Ami wants reference books, objects to a partial school holiday and has a reported boyfriend at another school. Umina has parent-imposed study, job plans, boyfriend talk and a desire to see Sendai more. Mariko's study and pickup arrangements affect actual logistics (O008/O023/O028/O047/O063/O077/O090–091). These are not complete private psychologies, but they are independent activities and constraints.

Their relationships also complicate the central narrators' idealizations. Maika's repeated forehead touch is unwelcome before an apology; she and Miyagi can readily negotiate a dessert exchange. Sendai enjoys some friend time and benefits from it despite social effort and concealment. Her belief that Maika's friendship is uncomplicated exceeds her access. Umina's willingness to trade an outing for homework differs from the tutor role in which Sendai insists Miyagi learn (O023/O047–048/O077). Neither a uniformly harmful public world nor an entirely frictionless alternative friendship is established.

The proper next step is to accumulate those bounded perspectives, not manufacture symmetrical supporting monographs. Maika and Umina have recurring, independently motivated roles worth continued attention; Ami becomes more individually evidenced here. Family actors remain substantially mediated by central focalizers. No source establishes the paid secret as shared with these outsiders, no unnamed parent is given an invented name, and no supporting model can be validated from peripheral mentions.
