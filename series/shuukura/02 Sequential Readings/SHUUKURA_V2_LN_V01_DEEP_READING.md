# V01: a price that cannot settle the meaning of an encounter

Miyagi Shiori and Sendai Hazuki begin V01 with a repeated transaction and incompatible explanations of it. Miyagi treats payment as permission to command within her room; Sendai eventually describes herself as permitting those commands. Neither explanation exhausts their actual choices. The volume makes ordinary continuation increasingly particular: eating, silence, a disliked drink finally disclosed, a familiar voice and an object that supplies a reason to return. These changes coexist with pain, ignored refusals and disputed limits. The last scene supplies concrete desire and a locally requested kiss, while leaving its scope contested and the relationship unnamed. It would misread this development to treat growing attachment as either proof that every preceding act was welcome or proof that the participants have become incapable of choosing otherwise.

This is an independent Japanese close reading through **one complete V01 witness**, not a final interpretation of the later series. The source-order record below preserves what could be understood before later disclosures. The opening argument uses completed-unit knowledge and is explicitly retrospective. Current revisions belong in the [claims ledger](../04%20Longitudinal%20Ledgers/SHUUKURA_CLAIMS_PREDICTIONS_AND_REVISIONS_LEDGER.md); this accepted reading and its prospective states remain frozen.

## Retrospective argument through V01

### The initial arrangement emerges from incompatible obligations

The origin is neither a straightforward market exchange nor a fully planned intimacy strategy. Sendai does not need ¥5,000 and repeatedly tries to repay it. Miyagi refuses repayment and change. In Sendai's account, her promise, aversion to unearned gifts, school reputation and rain make following the invitation intelligible; curiosity later makes repetition attractive (O011–014). The included Miyagi account adds that the money derives from a father whose work also empties the house. Yet Miyagi explicitly expects the labor invitation to be rejected and does not know what she wants Sendai to do when she follows (O066–068). Loneliness is evidenced; a conscious original plan to buy precisely this companion is not.

This disagreement persists in the shirt and eraser. Miyagi can call something given while Sendai insists on returning it. Giving ends an obligation for the giver only if the recipient accepts that frame; Sendai repeatedly does not (O030/O033/O061). The dispute is therefore about who can define what one owes, as well as about property. Payment provides a repeatable way to meet without requiring either person to articulate the value of meeting. That is an interpretation, supported by later visits without a useful planned command; its rival is a more ordinary desire for companionship and continuity under an eccentric habit. Both can explain some scenes. The text does not require that money possess one exclusive hidden meaning.

### The room's authority is real in use and incomplete in reach

Miyagi's paid-command model licenses behavior in her own reasoning, including rejecting another's request to stop as an illegitimate counter-command. Sendai initially presents a contrary account: she permits the commands and could leave. Later she withholds a plausible refusal because the room matters, says taking the fee removes her refusal right, and finally solicits the command for an act she independently wants (O005/O016/O019/O042/O054/O062). These are changes in self-understanding and context, not a single objective rule spoken through different mouths.

The strongest objection to an overbroad power account is practical initiative. Sendai chooses reading positions, cooks unasked, negotiates scheduling, refuses, returns first after class separation and breaches the school partition. Miyagi has domain advantages in games, rejects name use and stops further contact. These countercases matter. They do not negate the instances where either participant ignores an immediate refusal or inflicts pain. A useful model must specify the domain, cue and available response, rather than assigning permanent master/subordinate identities (CLM001).

The last school encounter makes the distinction particularly legible. Sendai exploits the omitted subject in the initial no-approach wording, then admits the mutual rule and her sophistry. The rule exists even though she violates it. At home she later calls rules absolute. The contradiction is between an asserted rule ethic and her enacted priority under stress, not evidence that no rules ever bind her (O060/O062).

### Ordinary accommodation changes the relation without repairing every boundary

The first accepted dinner matters because Miyagi expected refusal; it introduces a value beyond obeying. That value becomes easier shared silence, portions prepared and reallocated, memory of a voice, automatic two-meal preparation and eventually a summons with no bad incident or task (O008/O015/O020/O047/O055). These are specific changes in routine and desire. They need not be translated into a diagnosis or an already understood romantic identity.

Sendai's disliked soda supplies a miniature information history. The reader knows the preference from July; Miyagi learns it only in April. Miyagi threatens to continue soda, then actually offers barley tea and later again supplies it. Scheduling and game explanations also show information making an accommodation possible (O013/O035/O040–041/O061). Miyagi's choices therefore challenge the claim that she is uniformly indifferent to the other's experience. The same chapter contains ignored bodily objections, however. Practical accommodation and bodily agency are separate dimensions; combining them into an overall score of improvement would lose what the novel places together.

### Similar actions can have changed functions

Sendai herself distinguishes December resistance from January curiosity in a repeated bodily act. Miyagi later explains games as an attempt to neutralize troubling physical feeling, where Sendai had seen a stray cat becoming trusting. The action does not adjudicate the motive without its focalized account (O016/O040/O047). In each case, the second explanation changes the reader's model and remains unavailable to the other participant.

The two expulsions provide a larger example. Miyagi's retrospective account of March describes an attempt to make permanent departure predictable, tied to the memory of her mother leaving. It is a specific account of this rupture, with regret and self-persuasion preserved; it neither justifies the harm nor explains every earlier demand (O031). In May she stops an approach because losing future visits feels frightening, promises contact and seeks a pause. Sendai sees another flight resembling March. The reader can compare two represented purposes without giving Sendai access to the difference (O058–060). 'Miyagi pushes away because she fears abandonment' is too timeless to serve as either close reading or a behavioral policy. A responsible account must include what she presently hopes the pushing away will accomplish.

### Desire and permission become more explicit but remain distinct

The sleeping contact, aborted approach and final two kisses are three differently structured events. During the first, Miyagi wants a denying voice that sleep cannot supply. Sendai later reports a half-awakened sensation, initially uncertain, and inference from Miyagi's current reaction. A partially welcomed remembered sensation does not establish prior permission, full wakefulness or complete knowledge at the time (O048/O051).

The next approach includes an invitation to specify touch and Sendai's prompting of a command, but Miyagi stops before a completed lip-to-lip kiss (O057–058). Sendai's following account reveals her own wish to discover the reaction. Her frustration does not make completion owed. In the final scene she explicitly wants a kiss before Miyagi requests it, so duty alone cannot account for her action. A second kiss is preceded by a new request after the first refusal of repetition. Further escalation is then specifically refused; Sendai contests scope, bites in protest and is pushed away (O062–063). Desire, request, renewed permission, attempted extension and stopping must remain separate records. Neither payment nor the word 'kiss' resolves the scope in advance (CLM010).

This precision also prevents a falsely conclusive romantic ending. Sendai's closing private phrase acknowledges pronounced particular liking and explicitly withholds it from Miyagi. Miyagi's most recent interior account admits wanting specific contact and fearing lost visits, but the final scene is not her focalization. Both still lack a shared positive category. The contact changes what has happened and what Sendai knows about herself; its future relational meaning is left open (O064).

### Social life supplies alternatives and information the room does not contain

Miyagi enjoys Maika's company and has Maika and Ami as classmates. Maika has lessons, availability limits, invitations and an eye for Miyagi's unconscious arm-touching. She is a source of outside information, with rumor kept distinct from direct fact. Umina takes up Sendai's time and shares her contact without permission, while also being remembered as encouraging and admired. Mariko changes her stance on skipping school as grades grow consequential. Umina's ordinary borrowing of Miyagi's textbook crosses the supposedly rigid school divisions without exposing the private relation (O024/O031/O036–038/O045–046/O052).

These scenes qualify a simple hierarchy of sincere private pair versus artificial public friends. Public adaptation can be labor and still provide pleasure; a private refuge can be restful and still contain harm. Sendai's fish-tank metaphor explicitly avoids simple predation, and Miyagi's colored zones permit crossing while making it uncomfortable (O038/O066). Their different metaphors are focalized social models. Supporting characters are not granted unseen interiority, but their choices are consequential independent actions. The parents' motives, Umina's complete psychology and the absent Ruka's character are not reconstructed from the central narrators' convenience.

### Objects and repeated form make incomplete recognition durable

Sendai explicitly sees saved money as accumulated time. A shirt disputed as gift/loan becomes an accepted cash substitute and then sits unused. A fold she caused identifies Miyagi's book in Umina's hands. A bodily mark fades while Miyagi's unnoticed touching persists. An eraser stays behind, becomes a school pretext and recurs while intimate questions remain unsettled (O023/O030/O033/O040/O045/O052/O055/O060–061). The recurrence supports a bounded argument about traces outlasting the encounters' declared limits (CLM009). It does not make every object an ownership symbol: usefulness, accidental damage and repayment norms explain much of the same evidence.

Repetition also gives the reader opportunities to revise. Initial silence becomes comfort; later excessive talking reveals discomfort. Teacher-directed fluent reading first supports envy, then supplies a private soothing voice. Different classes preserve the paid routine while increasing homework labor and removing ordinary school access to that voice (O015/O021/O047/O051/O061). The book's formal work lies partly in such small reassignments of value. The price stays fixed while what the participants want from the encounter becomes harder to express within it.

## Japanese wording, address and qualified self-knowledge

The analysis distinguishes quoted speech from private narration and uses short Japanese spans only where wording matters. `仙台さん` is Miyagi's ordinary surname-plus-suffix address; Sendai commonly uses `宮城`. Sendai later tries `志緒理`, is expressly refused and knowingly repeats it as a provocation. This is an observed boundary crossing, not an accepted permanent naming transition (O053–054). Maika's ordinary given-name use has a different established friendship context (O032); identical form does not carry identical interpersonal permission.

`命令` is continually used to define, solicit or dispute an act rather than merely identify a fixed hierarchy. At `part0021` p155–160 Sendai limits the paid authority to the house while issuing a school demand herself. At p305–310 Miyagi asks Sendai to initiate, and Sendai narrates a lack of refusal right despite her prior explicitly represented wish. The grammatical framing therefore cannot substitute for identifying whose desire produced the proposal. At p338 Miyagi says two were not ordered; at p355 she says that extent was not permitted; Sendai's p356 response treats all kisses as one kind. The source itself stages the scope dispute (O060/O062–063).

The school-rule sophistry at `part0021` p133–136 depends on who said they would not approach; Sendai then recognizes mutual assent. Preserve both the possible literal reading and its explicit contextual rejection. Likewise, a no-undressing addition is requested at `part0019` p403–408, but Miyagi's joke/denial is not clear ratification. Formal status stays unconfirmed (O054).

Hedges retain substantive force. The absent father's supposed remorse is inferred with `からか`, not accessed directly (O008). The bonus's account of the gift offers several `かもしれない` possibilities and ends in `たぶん、きっと` rather than a settled motive (O066). Sendai's initial insistence in chapter10 that she did nothing is explicitly self-corrected before the reader learns her desired experiment (O059). These passages show different kinds of limited self-knowledge: uncertainty, defensive phrasing, revised account and deliberate withholding. They do not justify declaring everything either narrator says unreliable.

The closing `気に入っている` at `part0021` p377–379 supports strong particular liking in context and is explicitly not spoken to Miyagi. Rendering it as a mutually confessed love or a settled relationship label would add propositions. The strongest reading follows both its force in the intrusion/initiative context and its narrower lexical commitment (O064).
