# V10: wanting the same place, disputing what a promise means

Miyagi and Sendai end this volume with an ordinary promise that matters intensely to both of them. Miyagi will go to see penguins, but rejects Sendai's name for the outing. The distinction is the volume's endpoint rather than a verbal obstacle the analyst can remove. A desired shared future has become discussable; indefinite living together has not become agreed. Earlier, Miyagi proposes saving their accumulated money so Sendai can stay here indefinitely. Sendai asks whether Miyagi means staying together. Miyagi immediately answers that she did not say that. Keeping the suggestive proposal while dropping the answer would manufacture the very agreement the narration withholds. At the end, by contrast, the future outing is explicitly promised, although its date, destination and performance remain open. The form of attachment advances through a commitment whose relationship label remains contested. [O077–078; p-014 p0244–0325.]

The route to that ending contains more than a romance between desire and hesitation. Both characters give care, initiate, assent, refuse, misread and sometimes knowingly override a refusal. Their desire for an exclusive place does not dissolve university, employment, friendship or the practical need for money. The internal extra then discloses earlier family experiences after the adult endpoint has been reached. It gives promises, waiting, cleanliness and performed sociability a new history without making either adult's present behavior inevitable or wholly understood. The main story and the extra therefore require both a relationship argument and a careful account of who knows which part of that argument.

## Jealousy becomes speech without becoming shared certainty

The shopping day advances the LN09 undertaking and the BW10 departure into actual performance. Utsunomiya Maika chooses and buys clothes according to her own taste and budget, discusses work, has event interests and enjoys Miyagi's company. Sendai's discomfort is partly the sight of warmth that is not directed at her. Yet she also enjoys Miyagi's smile, genuinely likes clothing selection and feels grateful that Maika came. Miyagi accepts one try-on after encouragement, buys the blue skirt, and quietly asks Sendai to choose a top. This actual initiative defeats Sendai's assumption that present distance means Miyagi rejects everything she offers. It remains a particular request, not permanent authority over Miyagi's appearance. [O012–016; p-004 p0009–0257.]

Maika notices distance but misreads its cause as a quarrel. Sendai knowingly leaves that interpretation in place, then supplies a selectively true account of a conversation to Miyagi. Sendai's narration tells the reader what she has omitted. The relevant unreliability lies in the character's social answer and its implication, rather than in a global license to disbelieve the narrator. Miyagi's interest in the pupil gift is likewise more specific than simple opposition to work: Sendai has made a choice and has likes not previously visible to her. An unforeseen preference threatens Miyagi's feeling of privileged access. The actual gift is selected for the pupil's use and budget; Miyagi's dolphin choice is not the purchased present. [O014–018.]

Miyagi's eventual '嫉妬した' is an event in the recipient's knowledge. Her following chapter affirms its truth while regretting the pressured timing and the implications she did not intend to invite. Sendai has heard a word Miyagi would prefer not to have released, but cannot immediately assimilate it. When Sendai discloses jealousy of Maika, Miyagi calls it a lie. Later Miyagi again rejects the proposition that Sendai becomes jealous through not knowing. Two authentic utterances therefore do not yield symmetrical belief. The words enter the relationship more quickly than a mutually usable account of their meaning. [O018–025; p-004 p0471–0526; p-005.]

Nor does ownership assurance solve that problem. Miyagi rejects Sendai's proposed vow not to choose anyone else's clothes because she expects it to be broken, and refuses the replacement of always choosing Miyagi's things. Her distrust concerns reliable future performance, not simply lack of an emphatic declaration. Sendai's readiness to affirm belonging coexists with her private fear that saying love would destroy the roommate arrangement. The reader has access to both stakes while each woman has partial, sometimes disbelieved speech from the other.

## Friends make the household social, with limits neither woman fully controls

Komatsu Mio's visit completes the second friend undertaking, but the source does not present a frictionless joining of social circles. Sendai herself is possessive of access to Miyagi and the house. She blocks entry to Miyagi's room and declines given-name pressure in public, even though she privately wants a change of address. Miyagi dislikes some probing, yet wants to answer well, enjoys the custard dorayaki and appreciates Mio's interventions. Mio praises her conversation; Miyagi's self-assessment as disruptive is therefore not the only actual social verdict. [O026–038.]

Mio's own behavior changes in response to answers: an invitation involving men becomes a trio meal, then a later visit becomes food that day; pizza at home replaces a more difficult outing; she stops some probing, accepts the permitted room, offers to treat the pizza and eventually leaves. The meal and ice cream are eaten, but the actual payment is not separately inspected. The postvisit photo discussion is important precisely because its promised action never happens: Miyagi asks about images, Sendai negotiates deletion terms, and later Miyagi partly uses the request as a reason to stay. No actual display or deletion is shown. Neither Mio's earlier access nor Miyagi's abandoned pursuit can be converted into retrospective blanket permission for sharing images. [O029–038.]

Friendship is also more than an external threat. Maika and Asakura have employment, outings and interests away from the pair. Noto coordinates tutoring and voices travel ideas; the phone scene does not reveal a hidden lover or the full location of its speakers. Kikyo's exam acceptance moves from pending to reported success, continued tuition is decided, and she appears as a high-school pupil after parting from friends and voices her reading interests. Her thanks supports gift receipt, while leaving delivery time unshown. Her admiration and request for more lessons do not establish a romantic rival. Miyagi explicitly regards her as a good, innocent pupil; the unsettling object is Sendai's familiar professional voice and an ordinary relation whose details Miyagi does not know. Sendai's response to that distress often misses its object. [O013–014/O053–056/O057/O065–068.]

The extra later supplies 茨木羽美奈, Ibaraki Umina, as an initiating first-day friend with a conspicuous style, parental complaints, invitations and warmth that Sendai partly appreciates. Umina is distinct from Utsunomiya Maika. The source does not disclose why Umina approaches Sendai. Sendai's calculation is real, but it cannot retroactively turn Umina into a wholly manufactured attachment or every later friendship into an empty performance. [O080–081.]

## Affirmative initiative and ignored refusals occupy the same history

Miyagi's initiative in chapter5 changes the distribution of action. She cuts Sendai's nails, states that she wants to touch Sendai herself, offers not to proceed if unwanted, and refuses to accept a vague ownership formula as an answer. Sendai explicitly says '私は宮城にしてほしいって思ってる'. This is a recipient-specific request for Miyagi's action, not the analyst reading consent out of silence. Miyagi says Sendai is always beautiful and sometimes wants this intimacy; Sendai hears these things and responds. The source preserves desire, embarrassment and agency on both sides. [O041–043; p-008 especially p0241–0320.]

The same encounter then requires narrower distinctions. A demonstration occurs before an answer; Sendai's broad grant allowing Miyagi to touch her is later used against current requests to wait or stop, and a grip is needed to halt contact. Sendai subsequently renews a request for a different continuation, directs the action and later reports pleasure. That renewal is consequential, but it has a later time and a particular scope. It does not erase the preceding refusals. Sendai also privately feels unable to refuse some of Miyagi's terms for fear she will leave; this is an experienced constraint, not a literal rule removing all choice. The correct account retains the explicit earlier desire, felt vulnerability, actual stops, renewed choices and limits together. The later 'おわりにして' belongs to the immediate finishing/release sequence and should not be translated by dictionary alone as a new blanket revocation. [O043–048; p-008 p0321–0663.]

Afterward Miyagi briefly uses Hazuki in an ownership question and returns to Sendai-san. The specific utterance is new received naming, not a permanent agreement to use first names. Miyagi asks that this exposed intimate Sendai not be shown to others, while explicitly allowing whoever/wherever in the accompanying reminder of belonging. This has symbolic and bodily force but does not prohibit every friendship or social setting. Sendai knows the assurance has not closed the gap. Her wish for public recognition remains private. [O048.]

Sendai's bath and return to Miyagi's room keep privacy separate from sexual knowledge. Miyagi wants to see Sendai, but refuses the condition of sharing the bath and actually leaves the inner door unopened. Later Sendai knocks, waits and is invited in. There are also an unasked embrace, resistance, continued contact and an eventual release; neither the entry invitation nor the earlier encounter is a standing grant for all these actions. Sendai later chooses not to keep pressing for an overnight stay. Such local responsiveness is real counterevidence to total domination, without canceling the failures. [O049–053.]

That restraint does not govern every action even on the same night. Sendai marks Miyagi without waiting for assent, continues amid pushing and pain, and calls a future reciprocal intimate act a reservation or promise. Miyagi has not accepted that future term. The asserted reservation has to remain separate from the final penguin undertaking, for which Miyagi actually supplies a promise. The contrast makes the difference between declaring a commitment and establishing one visible inside this volume. [O053; p-010 p0092–0110.]

Chapter10 makes a sustained failure unmistakable. Miyagi says that generally not disliking foot attention does not mean wanting it now. Sendai forces the seated interaction, holds Miyagi's leg, removes the sock and continues despite repeated ordinary stop/release/stand requests. She hears the requests and demands deferential 'やめてください' instead. The episode cannot be explained away as missed information: the wording is received and deliberately made insufficient. Miyagi's mixed sensation is not assent. Sendai eventually stops; Miyagi asks for the hairstyle undone and earring hidden, which Sendai performs. A later finite instruction to stop offering and stand normally is carried out. This neither restores the old paid contract nor creates a stored unlimited command. [O068–072; p-013 p0161–0355.]

Miyagi's own foot-pinning and repeated stamping at the final dinner/label discussion also remain bodily actions with another person's discomfort. At the ear-promise ending, Sendai reaffirms the gifted-ear symbol, feels pain and swallows the pain word before pulling Miyagi closer. The scene's intimacy does not establish an unlimited right over her body. The analytical rule is temporal and specific: a present answer governs the present action; later affectionate conduct cannot silently rewrite an earlier answer. [O075/O078.]

## Knowing a body does not make ordinary preference transparent

Miyagi seeks Sendai's own preferences because she resists being the answer to every question about Sendai. Sendai's preference for making Miyagi happy is sincere, but can still leave Miyagi unable to identify a desire she considers independently Sendai's. Conversely, the novel gives repeated counterexamples to literal total ignorance: Sendai names teaching and choosing clothes, Miyagi remembers French toast, chooses pale pink because Sendai likes cute things, recalls a money-saving goal and the older hot-spring proposal, and learns that Sendai walks to maintain her body rather than looking effortless without action. [O016–018/O060–064/O077.]

The April cafe outing has reciprocal specificity. Sendai enjoys the savory French toast, including Miyagi's memory of her cooking; the nail-polish purchase is genuinely wanted, while also being a way to prolong shared time. Miyagi's decision to go home after the purchase is a voiced condition. Further browsing after Sendai presses is actual behavior, but quiet compliance is not a new explicit negotiation. The remembered nail-cutting scene also tempts Sendai into a self-flattering causal reading; she corrects herself because Miyagi's nails were already normally short. The narration models correction as well as wishful inference. [O060–065.]

The final hamburg-steak recurrence makes the communication problem domestic. Miyagi calls it important, Sendai actually cooks it that Sunday and repeatedly afterward, and Sendai privately wants to enter or understand Miyagi's value. Asked why she makes it so often, she supplies an improvised practical explanation about preventing sudden demands. The reader knows the concealed motive; Miyagi receives the substitute. Likewise 'this place' is Sendai's actual answer about what is important, heard by Miyagi, but its full meaning as life with Miyagi is not established as understood. Miyagi's reverse food answer may carry relational implication, yet the analyst cannot certify a secret translated confession. The mismatch persists amid real acts of care. [O071–075.]

## Waiting is painful without making either person helpless

Work distributes time in a way neither bodily closeness nor ownership assurance controls. Sendai wants to teach and earn money, delays telling Miyagi about settled continuation and a planned additional pupil, and later asks a permission-like question when she intends to work anyway. Miyagi's complaint concerns the posthoc character of that consultation as well as jealousy. Miyagi explicitly says work is Sendai's to decide. Her own tentative job comment is later identified as displaced frustration rather than a serious employment plan. The rigged rock-paper-scissors proposal is not an actually played game or a new job. [O053–058.]

Miyagi cooks, cleans, can navigate, considers the food available, prepares and eats a solo meal and declines waiting for Sendai's return and does not want to impose late cooking on her. Her time feels slow and company matters, but the source does not make her unable to function alone. Sendai later supplies an illuminating counterpart: waiting in Miyagi's chair and looking at photos, she loses appetite and motivation to cook for one, searches for cup noodles and finds none. She recognizes the double standard that makes her own lateness tolerable and Miyagi's visit to Maika unacceptable. Her assumption that a fast-food-workplace visit implies a hamburger dinner is then corrected by Miyagi's phone call and homecoming. Attachment disrupts routine on both sides; competence does not prevent loneliness. [O057–060/O073–075.]

The soft toys similarly mark presence without replacing it. A finite penguin/crocodile loan separates them after the visit. Both the retained penguin's meaning and proposed shared ownership are contested; Miyagi's custody is not assent to joint title. The actual return and reinstallation of the crocodile's tissue box happen later. Sendai's private use of the toys as proxies and her hopes about their significance are not a message Miyagi necessarily receives. At the volume's end, seeing living penguins can become a promise precisely because the object has not already solved the problem of presence. [O038–040/O049–056/O078.]

Economic and social autonomy remain consequential. Sendai's account links university enrollment to parental support and eventual work to maintaining a place outside her family home. She cannot abandon every other relation merely because Miyagi is special. Mio is sincerely valued, even while Sendai describes a deeper reluctance to invest in the expanding social world. These are competing priorities within an actor, rather than proof that her attachment makes all other agency false. No settled career, parental future decision or mutual postgraduation household is supplied. [O073–078.]

## The extra changes reader history while preserving incomplete understanding

Sendai's first high-school morning is a double loss and release. The different uniform makes the failed sister-school path visible; cutting her hair would feel like negating the whole self, so her resistance takes smaller styling choices. Her sister's brief compliment is not an objective statement of contempt: Sendai explicitly knows her sister would not mean the cruel comparison she is tempted to imagine. She abandons the aim of becoming her sister and resolves moderate sociability, some enjoyment and continued study because doing nothing makes her anxious. Umina's actual approach gives that plan a concrete social opening. The invitation is accepted; the celebration itself is not shown. This is a source-grounded origin for an adaptable smile, not proof that the later Sendai has no self or feels nothing for her friends. [O080–081; p-016 p0005–0117.]

Miyagi's childhood passage shifts the significance of ordinary words about time. Mother says she will be out briefly and return soon, but gives no destination. A week later Miyagi still waits. Father comes home and withholds the reason for nonreturn. His reassurance that he will return early and wait conflicts with the work absence he next announces. Gentle address is present, but cannot supply the reliable future the child needs. Miyagi imagines that finding a scratch with the same shape as one already noticed would bring mother back, then abandons the thought before searching or testing it because she fears finding none. The text shows the attraction and immediate fear of an arbitrary test; it does not show new damage, effective magic or an already established ritual habit. [O081–082; p-016 p0121–0205.]

At the grandmother's house, care is material, rule-bound and emotionally difficult. Grandmother cooks good food, has prepared bedding and later wipes Miyagi's tears. She also eats separately, speaks formally and expects self-waking under a general self-care promise whose scope the child has not anticipated. Miyagi counts four requirements within the stated three promises. When grandmother wakes her, Miyagi understands herself to have broken the self-care promise and thinks this permits mother's nonreturn. No clock time, waking deadline or lateness is established. That guilt is the child's interpretation, not the analyst's causal account. Grandmother says waiting will not bring mother back, yet never explains why. Miyagi calls her a liar because the statement conflicts with paternal reassurance. An assertion about nonreturn and a child's accusation do not establish either death or deliberate deceit. [O082–083.]

The exact grandmother wording, 'お父さんのお婆ちゃん', distinguishes the father-side relation from the mother-side alternative Miyagi requests. This account does not force a formal genealogical generation from that family idiom. Grandmother's instruction to keep things clean so important things can be found reaches a child who understands less than half of its meaning. Miyagi nevertheless folds the futon, considers this half keeping her promise, and intends to vacuum and continue cleaning at home so mother can return whenever she does. The final action is practical agency amid unresolved hope, not a cured loss or an observed maternal return. [O082–085; p-016 p0178–0297.]

Placed after the adult ending, the extra makes the unbreakable ordinary promise, the refusal of unreliable vows, the long wait and the importance of a place newly resonant. That is an interpretive connection supported by sequencing, wording and the afterword's causal frame. It does not mean adult Miyagi consciously identifies each echo, or that childhood experience licenses her to control another adult. Equally, Sendai's family history explains a stake in having somewhere to be without making each later misleading answer inevitable. The extra increases the reader's knowledge while preserving missing causes and actor access.

## Competing readings and endpoint limits

A strong growth reading can point to actual jealousy speech, fulfilled friend plans, voiced attraction, Miyagi's intimate initiative, explicit requests, domestic care and the penguin promise. Those are changes, and a total-stagnation account would omit them. But they do not produce dependable response to all refusals, a shared love declaration, public relationship recognition or an indefinite household agreement. Sendai's own language of stagnation is therefore a meaningful self-assessment beside developments she has actually experienced.

A strong control reading can point to imposed contact, delayed releases, demands for information, restrictive wishes and pain. These are real and sometimes knowingly enacted. It nevertheless fails if it removes either woman's documented wishes, initiated acts, later particular requests, accepted refusals, independent friends or practical choices. The source supplies unevenly exercised agency, including compromised moments; it does not require a global helplessness or universal-consent verdict.

A strong developmental-causation reading can connect the extra's family experiences to the adult need for place and reliable promises. It has the afterword's explicit support as an origin frame. It exceeds the evidence if it supplies the mother's actual fate, every parent's motive, a diagnosis, an exhaustive cause of personality, or conscious adult recollection at each symbolic action. The present synthesis retains the connection at the level of literary construction and calibrated inference.

At this endpoint the adult source has moved beyond LN09 and the beginning of BW10, while the web-era own and observed states remain separate historical instruments. Miyagi's late-April interior is not newly supplied by Sendai's final chapter; her latest adult own perspective here is chapter10, with chapter11 observations attributed to Sendai. Historical child Miyagi and first-day high-school Sendai are later-disclosed event states, not replacements for current adults. No reconstruction model is validated. M0.13 and S0.14 remain `draft_noncurrent` / `UNTESTED`; all 18 forecast records and 13 historical attempts retain their actual histories. The frontmatter previews, known BW10 context, post-exposure Q1s and future advertisements receive no held-out success credit.

The four-ledger update records individual states/readiness, directional relations and bounded rules, proposition/access histories, and versioned claims/counterevidence. It preserves earlier formulations, finite projections and errata, including the earlier attributed Maika/Umina correction. It does not close G3–G7, the original I1 lane, Narō correspondence, mandatory source gaps or the narrative series. The wider pause remains in effect after this bounded work.
