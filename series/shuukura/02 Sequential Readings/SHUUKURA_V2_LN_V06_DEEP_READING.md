## Recognizing love changes the question, not the other person's mind

Sendai's first substantial disclosure is a change in self-understanding. She recognizes that she likes Miyagi and retrospectively describes a feeling growing beneath the paid arrangement and then beneath the roommate category (O002; p-004p44–56). The text distinguishes the history she now proposes from the moment she can acknowledge it. Her inability to date its beginning prevents the analyst from assigning an earlier volume a conscious confession that its narrator did not make. The monetary covering metaphor explains how a practical form could shelter a feeling from scrutiny; it does not prove that every earlier paid visit expressed one already understood motive.

Recognition also makes an apparently straightforward reunion harder. Sendai wants Miyagi back, is relieved not to face her immediately, and dislikes herself for using that difficulty as a reason to wait. These are simultaneous priorities. Her message at p-004p66 precedes the actual campus search, but the noon deadline she gives herself passes before she leaves. Being able to name the feeling does not produce a consistent strategy. It makes ordinary contact more consequential because Sendai now fears what her own behavior might disclose (O002–004).

The strongest qualification to a simple awakening narrative is that Sendai still prefers preserving their accumulated life to acquiring the lover label. She fears that saying she likes Miyagi would function as an excuse for the previous encounter rather than communicate an independent feeling (p-009p45–49). That fear recognizes a real distinction between affection and justification. Yet withholding the word also protects Sendai from a feared rejection whose outcome she has never tested. An ethically intelligible concern can coexist with avoidance. The novel does not require choosing one of those explanations for all her silence.

Miyagi's corresponding change is less lexically settled. She corrects her explanation of jealousy: she has not generally minded Maika's other friends, so the troubling possibility is Maika taking Sendai's attention, rather than Sendai taking Maika away (p-014p144–151). She knows the two are not currently exchanging the kind of physical closeness she shares with Sendai. Her insecurity therefore cannot be reduced to believing an affair has occurred. Ordinary interest and the exchange of information already matter. Miyagi names that problem without naming her feeling love (O037).

The difference matters at the endpoint. Sendai knows her own love and entertains, then doubts, the possibility Miyagi feels similarly. Miyagi explicitly wants Sendai in ways another companion would not satisfy, but tries to make only the roommate relationship special, rather than Sendai herself (p-014p169–176). Her distinction conflicts with her person-specific desire without becoming a completed self-recognition. A romantic reading has strong evidence; a claim that both characters have privately reached the same named conclusion exceeds this volume's disclosures.

## Returning home is an action with a social cost

Miyagi's absence actually lasts three nights. The opening Thursday is not a permanent separation or the completion of the weeks she considered in V05. Initial embarrassment becomes a different obstacle: each day away makes returning harder (p-005p13). She understands that Sendai worries and that Maika has her own life, yet cannot turn that understanding into the short message she imagines typing. This is failure despite awareness, rather than evidence she cannot recognize anyone else's needs.

Sendai's search is correspondingly limited by what she knows. She can reach Miyagi's university because she researched its location for housing, but does not know the internal geography or Miyagi's schedule. Nearly an hour of searching fails. Finding Maika creates a practical route that Sendai could not generate by simply willing greater intimacy. Her supposed knowledge that Miyagi stays with Maika becomes confirmed only when Maika tells her; an earlier non-denial was an inference (O003–004).

The eventual return is chosen under social pressure. Maika offers a continued stay accompanied by questions, while Sendai asks Miyagi to come home. Miyagi experiences those alternatives as sharply unequal. She actually chooses return and leaves, but the text does not present an unassisted spontaneous decision. Sendai's withheld arrival also matters: Miyagi says she would have left had she known beforehand (p-005p77–80). The device makes a conversation possible by limiting her ability to avoid it; that consequence remains part of the scene even when the resulting conversation helps.

Repair on the walk is more modest than a resolution of the relationship. Both apologize. Miyagi gives a limited true account of difficulty facing Sendai. Sendai says she could not wait another day to see her, and Miyagi treats that desire as implausible because her own behavior has been difficult. The reader knows Sendai's private account, while Miyagi accepts concern but doubts wanting. Her skepticism is selective, not simple distrust of every sentence (O007–008).

The bag handle is an exact material compromise. Sendai threatens to lead Miyagi by the hand, Miyagi calls attention to the promise, and Sendai carries the bag instead (p-005p255–263). Connection occurs through an object while the desired direct gesture remains unavailable. The narrative lets something practically useful carry an unresolved emotional meaning without converting it into a permanent symbolic code.

## Maika has reasons of her own

The interlude changes the moral and epistemic weight of the mediation. From the pair's perspectives, Maika can look like an unusually accommodating friend whose help is readily available. Her own narration reveals hurt at being excluded from a central part of Miyagi's life, curiosity about the unexplained relationship, genuine enjoyment of Miyagi's company, and a wish to respect reasons a friend cannot yet disclose (O016–017). None of those motives cancels the others.

Maika's warmth is therefore neither proof she is unaffected nor proof her hospitality is false. She welcomes the stay while resenting the concealment. She also lies about dropping her phone to keep the planned visit secret. The third focalizer does not stand outside the work's patterns of tact, partial knowledge and cover stories. Her important difference is how she sometimes stops questioning: when Miyagi moves to coursework, Maika elects not to expose everything. She recognizes that assembling fragments can produce an answer she has invented (p-008p81–108).

Her eventual candor belongs to a particular recipient. At the July visit she tells Sendai about feeling left out. Miyagi is absent. Sendai understands that feeling because it resembles her own jealousy, and her invitation for Maika to use Hazuki combines genuine friendship with a strategy to make jealousy easier to bear (p-013p118–130). The text explicitly supplies both motives. Calling the friendship wholly instrumental would discard its strongest counterevidence.

Maika declines the proposed first-name change until Miyagi herself uses Hazuki. Sendai reciprocally defers using Maika. This is Maika's choice about closeness, rather than a rule Miyagi has imposed on the household. Its later consequences are nevertheless uncomfortable: Miyagi learns that taking the step herself would release it to someone else. A considerate attempt to preserve her priority can give her a new reason to postpone the very intimacy Sendai wants (O034–035).

Other supporting lives remain visible at different scales. Hanamaki Kikyō wants her current enjoyable class not to end, talks during study breaks and then returns to work. Sendai becomes less nervous as a tutor, without any demonstrated causal improvement in this already capable pupil's grades (O018). Ami's breakup is a secondhand update conveyed by Maika, not a fully observed new plot (O032). These contributions warrant different forms of later treatment. Maika's independent interiority supports sustained monograph work; neither a pupil's short conversation nor a piece of offstage news should be inflated into equivalent psychological coverage.

## The same home can contain unequal access

Living together has not made Miyagi's room Sendai's. Sendai repeatedly knocks, receives books through Miyagi, and stays outside. Maika can enter the new room, but Sendai still has not done so by the volume's end. The contrast produces jealousy without an enacted invasion. It is important counterevidence to any account in which Sendai always overrides a limit whenever she desires access (O026, O032–034).

Miyagi's explanation is also more specific than permanent refusal. She does not want Sendai's first visit to happen in Maika's presence (p-012p222–223), and later thinks they could have eaten cake in her room but misses the moment to invite her (p-014p84). Privacy here protects authorship of an occasion as well as possession of a space. Failure to voice the invitation prevents Sendai from acquiring knowledge the reader has. The final bonus's earlier fear that Sendai might enter or unlock the room remains Miyagi's anticipation; no such entry is narrated (O047–048).

The common table offers a working alternative. Miyagi consciously spends more time there because avoiding Sendai cannot sustain the life she wants. The particular drink is not a rule: tea, juice, barley tea or ice cream can occupy the occasion (p-007p5–36). Cooking and washing change as well. Miyagi prepares stew and helps with ordinary meals; Sendai researches, cooks, improvises and sometimes uses ready-made food. Their demonstrated capacities expand without either becoming an ideal domestic provider.

The storm-night arrangement makes trust similarly specific. Miyagi asks how long Sendai's promise lasts; Sendai defines it as until Miyagi leaves the room. Miyagi then borrows the bed and offers half of it so its owner need not stay awake (O020–022). This is an affirmative local choice under a negotiated assurance, not a general permission attached to shared sleeping. When both leave for washing and food, the temporal clause really expires. Miyagi knows that, then asks for a new promise. Her trust depends on an instrument she can request again, rather than a discovery that Sendai will always infer the appropriate limit.

## An earring can remind its giver as well as mark its wearer

The new silver plumeria earrings begin as Sendai's penalty proposal. The alleged breach needs care: V05p-004p201 requires contact when staying away, without an explicit address, explanation, response schedule or precise advance deadline. Miyagi did send the minimum first-night message. Sendai now treats prompted, sparse communication and prolonged absence as insufficient (V06p-005p276–280). Her concern is understandable, but her stricter interpretation is not automatically the original rule's full wording. Miyagi's concession does not remove the distinction between explicit scope and retrospective enforcement.

Shopping also gives Miyagi a real but restricted choice. She rejects the first flower design as too cute, rejects an extravagant alternative, then chooses the former rather than making a public dispute (O014). Sendai eventually asks for permission to fit them, and Miyagi separately removes the old studs herself. Those negotiated details matter more than treating the whole gift as either unconstrained generosity or pure coercion.

The meaning is divided. Sendai wants to use her own earnings and privately connects the jewelry with wishing a treasured person happiness. Miyagi receives the promise-reminder or protective-charm account, not the whole explanation. The intended association is with the jewelry tradition Sendai says she researched, rather than a simple equivalence to the flower-language definitions Miyagi finds (p-009p338–365).

Even the hidden wish is internally contested. Sendai says she could tolerate Miyagi not loving her, but not loving someone else; if she could not wish that happiness herself, perhaps the earrings could do it for her (p-009p369–374). The object carries an aspiration beyond what its giver presently feels capable of, alongside a wish that Miyagi remain too shy to confess elsewhere. This is stronger evidence than either an uncomplicated blessing or an ownership-only reading.

Once Sendai vows on the earrings, the gift becomes a reminder to the giver of conduct she has promised. Miyagi explicitly calls compliance imperfect but reassuring (p-010p92–100). Cat existence later supplies another small instance in which Sendai's disputed statement proves true; Miyagi tells her that this has increased trust and asks her not to lose it (p-013p253–255). Such gains are bounded. They do not prove every compliment is sincere, every future promise reliable, or every unknown feeling safe.

## Changed initiative does not cancel a local refusal

The intimate scenes require both positive agency and material failures of restraint. Sendai sometimes stops before contact or withdraws when Miyagi pulls away. She also transfers an unwanted arm hold into a hand hold after being asked to release, and invokes old commanded acts as a reason Miyagi should not refuse now (p-006p176–208). Remembered permission is being used to claim a present entitlement, rather than merely recalling a shared pleasurable history.

Miyagi also violates limits. She bothers a sleeping Sendai, continues after a hand is swatted, and recalls that Sendai once ignored the same ticklish complaint (O022). During the later reciprocal encounter she proceeds without a new affirmative answer to a conditional clothing request and contests an attempt to end a particular touch. Acknowledged pleasure does not render those local negotiations unnecessary (O040–042).

There are meaningful differences between episodes. Miyagi recognizes and honors a specific outer-clothing limit. At the final stopping point she first asks why, then understands the stated limit, apologizes and withdraws. Those actions should not be erased to manufacture uninterrupted violation. Equally, the apology does not rewrite earlier persistence into prompt respect. The analysis is action-specific because the source itself differentiates grants, objection, clarification, delayed release and voluntary continuation.

Miyagi's proposal mirrors Sendai's earlier future-never-again pressure. Sendai clarifies its implication; Miyagi consciously treats her future self as bargaining material. Sendai's subsequent private narration supplies decisive counterevidence to an account in which she participated only because of that condition: she says she would have accepted Miyagi's wish without it, because she wanted her (p-015p16). This supports genuine recipient desire without making the pressure benign or proving the counterfactual through an actual alternate-world test.

Miyagi's purpose changes within the encounter. She first wants exclusive knowledge as reassurance, then regrets hiding Sendai's face and wants to see how Sendai experiences her. She explicitly wants Sendai to feel good, beyond what she herself previously felt (p-014p494–508). Her wish begins to involve another's responsive experience rather than only her own relieved uncertainty. Yet she still wants all of Sendai's past and future disclosed to her. Growing recognition and a totalizing wish remain in tension.

## Sensory knowledge and speech do different work

The novel does not deny the reality of what the characters learn through touch. Miyagi hears Sendai admit embarrassment and present pleasure; Sendai privately confirms the latter was true. Both discover unfamiliar reactions in themselves and revise assumptions about the other. The error would be treating that knowledge as equivalent to knowing the other's motives, future choices or relationship category.

The clearest example concerns an apparently reasonless kiss. Sendai reads Miyagi's kiss as payment to release a hand; Miyagi privately says it was not intended as exchange and lets the misreading stand (p-014p193–196). Elsewhere Sendai hopes a declared information bargain expresses a wish to kiss her. The reader can distinguish these occasions; neither character has all the other narrator's explanations. The old transaction form has not simply suppressed desire. It can also let desire occur while concealing its personal meaning.

Names compound the problem. Miyagi refuses Hazuki because using it would allow Maika the same address, not simply because Miyagi feels no intimacy (p-014p465–467). She mentally uses it without saying it. Sendai actually calls her Shiori in the encounter; Miyagi does not object in that focalized account, but Sendai later says her memory of the response is uncertain (p-015p42–43). Neither fact establishes a standing public naming agreement. Ordinary morning surname address returns.

Even seemingly trivial preference questions remain difficult. Miyagi interprets 'both' kinds of cheesecake as concealment, but the source does not establish that equal preference is false. A wish for one ranked answer can itself misread another person. Conversely, Sendai's invented dream the final morning is explicitly admitted as a lie (p-015p126–130). Some suspicions are right, others remain unproved. Alternation creates local epistemic corrections rather than a global reliable/unreliable classification.

The mainline ends with a successful ordinary statement. Miyagi says she prefers eating together to eating alone, then accepts a summer outing while asking Sendai to choose its destination (p-015p183–195). The trip has not happened. No lover category or love confession is agreed. Yet the ending is more than unaltered stamping: both remain for breakfast after a destabilizing encounter, embarrassment is discussed, and Miyagi gives Sendai an intelligible positive reason for her presence.

## The retrospective bonus preserves the cost of that ordinary ending

The bonus returns to the original late-May Monday. Miyagi leaves after four in the morning, speaks a return-oriented farewell into an empty common space, and moves the black cat from pillow to shelf. Sendai prepares breakfast, deliberately waits rather than forcing an answer, finally verifies the absent shoes, sends messages and expects a return that evening (O047–048). These accounts cannot become knowledge either character possessed during the other's private scene.

Placed after the July breakfast, the two earlier failed attempts at ordinary morning contact give the ending a history. Miyagi's eventual presence is not guaranteed by cohabitation, and Sendai's restraint is not an automatic consequence of recognizing love. They can share a home and an encounter while still having different clocks, different ideas about endings and different accounts of what a gesture means. Through this boundary, the novel's achievement is not to abolish those differences. It makes selected forms of company and communication possible inside them, while leaving the most consequential terms unsettled.
