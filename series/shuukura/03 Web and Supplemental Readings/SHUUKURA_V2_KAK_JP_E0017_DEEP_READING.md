## Admission is an action; not asking is another choice

The prior episode's entrance-only uncertainty is resolved by actual conduct here. Miyagi does not merely imagine opening. She announces it, later opens the apartment door at the chime, and leads Sendai toward the room (O001–002). Sendai's thank-you is an audible response. Her entry and ordinary seat on the bed are performed, whereas a clothes transfer is not. The small bag is presented before shoes are removed; Miyagi's refusal to take it preserves a distinction between admitting its bearer and accepting its contents.

The initial exchange gives Miyagi the immediate choice, but the choice is constrained in her own account by what sending Sendai away would mean. At p12 she calls dismissal easy; at p13 its consequence prevents the ease from becoming action. This is not proof that Sendai has no agency: she has come of her own initiative, poses the alternatives, follows Miyagi, and later articulates conditional willingness to stop coming. It does show that the explicit options are emotionally uneven for the narrator. The narration's insistence that admission is not retention works as self-protection against naming the desire that makes dismissal difficult.

The paper bag gives that tension a material form. Sendai asks what should happen to the clothes, and Miyagi recasts the visit as an occasion for commands rather than taking the bag (p26–36). Sendai accepts that description with a deliberately non-weighty answer. Later, Miyagi asks whether the usual command scope still applies despite today not being five thousand yen (O006). This establishes an explicit difference from a current cash-paid visit and an explicit shared exchange about commands. It does not show a new cash transfer, a completed clothes return, or a complete renegotiated contract spelling out every limit. The clothes remain present in the scene's problem without receiving an enacted disposal by the ending.

At p173–184 the same pattern returns with a longer horizon. Sendai supplies a possible duration and leaves cessation conditional on Miyagi's preference. Miyagi privately translates the speech into what a request might obtain, but refuses to request it. Asking about university keeps conversation going without answering the question posed. The distinction is not between speaking and silence alone: she actively speaks about a neighboring topic. That displacement lets her participate in imagining a future while avoiding the exposed position of asking for it.

## The room recovers a routine without recovering innocence

The room's apparent lack of change and the first-visit manga create a deliberately doubled time scale. Less than a month has passed, but Sendai speaks as though returning after a year (p40–42). The manuscript does not date the interval more precisely. Familiar furniture and habitual seating establish continuity; Miyagi's regret that she left the particular manga visible establishes the discomfort of being seen to have retained the past. The book is not merely a nostalgic object described by a detached narrator. Sendai touches it, identifies it, asks about it, and Miyagi later retrieves and shelves it. Each of those actions makes private preoccupation vulnerable to another person's observation without proving that person knows its full explanation.

Miyagi observes a slightly higher voice and infers probable amusement (p59–61). The qualifying probability matters. Sendai does not narrate amusement or announce that she understands Miyagi's attachment to the book. The petal simile at p47 likewise characterizes Miyagi's experience of light speech rather than establishing that Sendai is unaffected. Her later serious tone about the visitation horizon is a contrast inside this one witness: apparent lightness does not exhaust the encounter's range.

The other recovered routine is unequal instruction. The episode makes it concrete without requiring graphic retelling. Sendai anticipates the intimate instruction after complying with the seating direction, and Miyagi confirms it while claiming she has no other suitable command (O007). Familiarity permits anticipation, but her explanation is still her explanation. Sendai's questioning response and earlier initiative prevent the scene from being reduced to a passive figure obeying without qualification. Conversely, anticipation and current compliance are not grounds to treat later pain or objections as already authorized.

Physical pressure and verbal denial run together. Miyagi kicks to hurry Sendai; Sendai objects to violence, and Miyagi rejects the label (p135–138). The denial does not undo the narrated action. Later Miyagi voices dislike of one action, that action ends, and Sendai changes to a painful act; only the subsequent explicit request to stop ends that act (O009). These are source-local sequences of boundary, response, substitution and cessation. They support analysis of unstable control and local limits. They do not support either a blanket claim that no objection is honored or a blanket claim that every continuation is consensually settled. The source provides neither Sendai's private reason for the switch nor a medical assessment of injury.

The final renewed instruction is followed by actual compliance (O013–014). Miyagi's remaining discomfort is part of the endpoint, rather than an obstacle which the analysis may silently resolve. No private sensation is needed here as an attractive spectacle; its critical function is that renewed routine and unresolved misgivings coexist. The chapter does not turn repetition into a guarantee of safety, mutual interpretation, or secure commitment.

## Two claims of disappointment and two incomplete university positions

Miyagi denies being disappointed about separate classes. Sendai says she herself was disappointed; Miyagi calls that a lie, and Sendai denies lying (p97–107). The text grants each speaker a statement and supplies a skeptical narrator, not a neutral decision about sincerity. Miyagi's preceding roster speculation and attention to the class difference make her denial unstable as a complete account of her own preoccupation. They do not prove that Sendai's statement is sincere or false. Her smile and light voice are observations, not a lie detector.

Education expands the discrepancy between spoken shorthand and available interior. Miyagi's 'third year' explanation for not calling is, by her own account, not accurate but not clearly wrong (p72–80). The cram-school question receives a denial; only the narrator supplies the lack of a plan and lack of strong determination about university. Sendai does not acquire those private formulations simply by hearing that Miyagi is not attending cram school. The later 'I don't know' about university is shared speech, but it is not a complete transcript of the earlier private position.

Sendai, meanwhile, speaks as though their universities will differ, then says she has not selected her own destination (p177–193). A different-university expectation and an undecided institution can coexist. Neither establishes an admission result or a shared educational plan. Miyagi names withholding and genuine indecision as alternatives and leaves them unresolved. The uncertain institution also limits the graduation horizon: it is a tentative spoken answer situated in assumptions about school transition, rather than a binding date for the arrangement's expiry.

## A late ordinary disclosure changes what continuation contains

The carbonated-drink disclosure is small in material scale but exact about an earlier informational gap. Sendai directly says she is not good with carbonated drinks and explains that she initially did not expect to keep coming so long, then missed the timing to say it (p198–203). Before this disclosure, Miyagi is not shown knowing that preference. After it, the information is shared. It does not retroactively make every earlier serving a knowingly hostile choice by Miyagi, and it does not prove that every prior drink was actually consumed.

Miyagi's surprise gives the timing a social consequence. She calls the admission late and then states that she will serve cider next time anyway. Sendai calls her mean. The exchange allows ordinary familiarity, obstinacy and an announced next occasion to occupy the same few lines. A next-time intention suggests Miyagi is speaking within a continuing routine even while she avoids asking Sendai to continue. It is not the explicit answer to the earlier visitation question, a new drink service, or proof that a future visit has occurred.

The closing command interrupts this ordinary disclosure rather than resolving its sting. That interruption returns control to a familiar register after Sendai has introduced information Miyagi did not possess. It supports a reading of continuity built through partial, belated disclosures rather than full access to the other person. It also leaves room for a less strategic account: Miyagi may simply deflect an awkward ordinary exchange using their habitual manner. The source provides conduct and wording; a sole exhaustive motive is unavailable.
