## A friend who does not want to get used to refusal

Mio first places Sendai beside her in a university lecture room, then describes someone who seems understandable yet remains difficult to understand. The juxtaposition gives the mystery a social scale. Sendai is physically available for ordinary acquaintance, but repeatedly unavailable for the further company Mio wants. The rejected mixer is only the current example: Mio also reports refusals of other invitations and remembers greater availability when they first met. Her account therefore gives the feeling of a change without establishing an exact invitation count or Sendai's complete reasons. 「ほとんど」「毎回のように」and「いないも同然」remain Mio's frequency and rhetorical formulations, not a literal record of total disappearance. (O002;01p1–15.)

The small correction from having become accustomed to refusal to not having wanted that familiarity matters. Mio can predict the pattern and still dislike it. Her persistence is grounded partly in an ordinary independent wish to spend time with Hazuki; it need not exist solely to expose Sendai's relationship with Miyagi to the reader. The present supplement never names Miyagi. Wider reader knowledge gives the social distance additional possible significance without making it Mio's knowledge or this story's disclosed explanation. (O002;01p9–15/35.)

Mio changes the invitation from a mixer to Sunday shopping. The substitution makes company itself consequential: the desired interaction is not exhausted by one social format or one opportunity to find a partner. It also shows how refusal becomes negotiation. Sendai apologizes and says she is a little busy; Mio treats the softener「ちょっと」as room to maneuver, and Sendai answers that she is actually quite busy. This is an explicit strengthening of the answer under pressure. The first wording is not a hidden yes, and the second does not prove a fabricated schedule. (O002;01p17–25.)

Mio's displeased face and complaint about being disliked dramatize the cost she assigns to the repeated refusal. She immediately explains that she believes they are close enough to say such things. That interior qualification supports a familiar joking register while retaining the complaint's emotional work: she asks Sendai to account for a gap between friendliness and availability. Familiarity can make the line possible without settling whether Sendai welcomes every form of persistence. The narration records Mio's judgment of their closeness, not a jointly ratified description of the whole friendship. (O002;01p27–35.)

## Reassurance that does not open the whole life

Sendai answers the dislike question directly: if she disliked Mio, she would not be together with her. The reply recognizes their present companionship and supplies the reassurance Mio's words solicit. Sendai smiles and takes her bag, however, so recognition of friendship accompanies an effort to finish the conversation. Mio says she is glad while explicitly catching Sendai's arm. Relief and renewed insistence occupy the same sequence; reassuring someone has not automatically satisfied the desire to know more. (O003;01p37–44.)

The source distinguishes this grip from the earlier interception. At 19 Mio has caught or stopped Sendai for three minutes after the lecture, but no body part or sustained restraint is specified. At 40 the arm is explicit, as is Mio's description of Sendai trying to end the conversation and leave. Reading the latter backward would invent a three-minute grip. Reading the former as proof that the later contact is harmless would also exceed the evidence. Permission, release, duration and injury are unshown. The scene supports naming a specific bodily interruption of attempted departure while leaving its unprinted details and any broader severity judgment open. (O002/O003;01p19/39–40.)

Mio's ensuing portrait explains why reassurance has not ended curiosity. She sees someone kind and smiling with everyone, beautiful and 清楚, yet interprets that good-person aura as concealing distance. The phrase about not letting people too close is forceful, but belongs to the observer who wants closer access. It is not a confession of Sendai's strategy. Mio also reports that Sendai goes out little with other people, so her concern is not simply an allegation that she alone is excluded. Even so, this private appraisal cannot audit every friendship or reveal which priorities Sendai is managing. (O003;01p44–51.)

The distinction preserves Sendai's agency without making Mio's desire trivial. A cheerful surface and a maintained refusal can coexist. The smile may help sustain sociability at the very moment Mio encounters a limit to it; the text does not require the smile to make either the friendship false or the boundary disappear. Sendai's own current interior remains unavailable. That absence is a formal condition of the episode, which gives Mio an interpretive problem instead of providing Sendai as its solved object.

## A mystery that cooperation would not cure

Mio's boyfriend explanation translates uncertainty into a familiar social story. She has asked; Sendai denies having a boyfriend, and Mio sees no likely person at university. Mio nevertheless treats the repeated refusals as evidence that someone must exist. The premise—if there were nobody, Sendai would not decline so often—belongs to Mio's inference. It narrows possible explanations before Sendai supplies one, and it leaves work, study, private leisure, attachment outside that label and other priorities insufficiently considered. The story presents that inference without requiring the reader to adopt it. (O003;01p53–55.)

「彼氏はいない」has a narrower literal scope than no attachment, no intimate relationship or no woman. The larger corpus can make that distinction especially meaningful to the reader, but it does not turn Mio into a person who already knows the central dyad. Nor does it license calling every literal boyfriend denial a lie. Mio's conjecture is not established by her confidence; Sendai's statement is not a complete autobiography. (O003/O005;01p53/62–65.)

Mio then turns her analysis into advice: if Sendai wants fewer invitations, she ought to spend more time with Mio. Her language places the solution in Sendai's compliance. Yet the next paragraph immediately undoes the neat transaction. If Sendai did accept, Mio would wonder why she accepted. This admission gives the curiosity a source inside Mio herself. She notices that the answer she demands might become another question, so the attraction of mystery cannot be reduced to a problem Sendai has created and could simply fix. (O003;01p57–59.)

This is the episode's sharpest self-complication. Mio is interested enough to keep asking and reflective enough to glimpse why satisfaction might recede. The retrospective LN05 appraisal of her as sometimes insufficiently reflective cannot become a permanent rule excluding this thought. Her「惹き付けて」describes fascination here; the episode does not by itself establish romantic desire. Companionship, attraction to an unresolved person and pleasure in the exchange remain possible overlapping motives without being collapsed into one diagnosis.

## An ending offered in the future tense

Sendai renews her intention to leave. Mio says she understands, then converts the urgency into another boyfriend allegation and presses for a confession about summer break. Sendai rejects that premise and states that she merely wants to finish a report soon, continuing to smile. The report is a reason she actually gives, not independently witnessed completed work; the source neither verifies its performance nor disproves it. The later original novel's explicit use of a busy cover in another event cannot supply that missing proof. (O003/O005;01p61–67.)

Mio's final interior language makes her desired access explicit: she wants the key to the mystery beyond the smile. She also decides that now is not the time. That judgment is a local deferral, not a renunciation of curiosity or a completed mutual resolution. Her spoken conclusion asks Sendai to make it up to her at the next mixer. It keeps future company in play and jokingly frames the current refusal as compensation owed. Sendai's acceptance is absent. No future attendance, promise, incurred debt or successful outing enters the exit state. Actual departure and arm release likewise remain outside the narrated endpoint. (O003;01p69–73.)

The returned smile lets the encounter close without revealing what Mio has been trying to extract. There is warmth in the capacity to continue talking and pressure in the repeated effort to convert a refusal into something else. The strongest generous reading sees familiar friends sustaining playful interest despite conflicting plans; the strongest critical reading sees Mio making Sendai's privacy answerable to her own appetite for company and explanation. The specific acts support keeping both in view. Neither a wholly harmless friendship nor a globally coercive personality follows from this short scene.
