# シャンプーと私たち: a shared object and two kinds of change

Using Sendai Hazuki's shampoo gives Miyagi Shiori a familiar person’s scent on her own body. Nothing about her hair's length or colour changes, yet the experience unsettles her. Sendai later notices a different discrepancy: her shampoo appears no less full after Miyagi's first use, although it has become something two people use. Miyagi feels a small change immediately; Sendai imagines changes that will become perceptible only with time. Sharing one product therefore joins their routines without giving either narrator an identical experience or complete access to the other's meaning. (O002–003/O007/O009–011.)

## A familiar scent makes the self feel unfamiliar

The bath should be comfortable. Miyagi describes a small space that does not restrict her, clean hair and body, plentiful warm water and the possibility of falling asleep. She then qualifies that expectation: today she cannot settle. The contrast locates the disturbance in this occasion rather than treating the room as objectively inadequate or bathing as uniformly distressing. She sighs, pulls her bangs and notices a changed smell in otherwise unchanged hair. (O002.)

Her earlier shampoo was satisfactory without being especially cherished. She had not been planning to change it. She recalls the day she learned that Sendai had dreamed of eating a salmon-roe bowl with Penguin, when Sendai told her to use her shampoo. Some time has passed; Miyagi's shampoo and treatment have now run out, and she has begun using Sendai's. The dream belongs to Sendai's reported nighttime content, and the offer belongs to Miyagi's remembered conversation. This sentence neither verifies the dream independently nor performs a new meal. Penguin supplies remembered context, not a new human participant or a speaking object narrator here. (O003.)

Miyagi feels as though part of herself is no longer hers. That is an attributed simile about the sensory change, not an objective loss of identity or a clinical diagnosis. Sendai and the shampoo both smell good to her; the problem is that using the product makes her familiar self seem absent and Sendai seem too near, even while Sendai is physically elsewhere. A pleasant smell and an unwelcome implication can coexist. (O003.)

She slaps the water, splashes her face, wipes it and calls the situation irritating. Her attempted answer invokes ownership: because the shampoo is now hers too, its smell should belong to her as well, so feeling Sendai near seems unreasonable. The reasoning does not make the feeling disappear. A product's shared use and a person's experienced presence are different relations. Calling the bottle hers does not decide what wearing the scent will mean. (O003.)

The room presents the inverse discrepancy. Removing her old shampoo makes the bathroom feel slightly larger although its physical dimensions are unchanged. She feels threatened by a small alteration she might otherwise overlook. One bottle's absence enlarges apparent space while narrowing the distance she feels from Sendai. The source supports that formal contrast without making the room or scent a universal symbolic code. (O003.)

## Practical actions continue through the disturbance

Miyagi judges the direction of her thoughts bad, stretches upward and leans against the tub. As she relaxes, her back slips, her hair dips into the water and she hurriedly stands. The brief physical mishap interrupts her reflection. It does not show Sendai touching her in the bath or establish an injury. She leaves, dries her body and towel-dries her hair, puts on sweats used as sleepwear and enters the common space. Towel drying here is distinct from the later refused offer to use a hairdryer. (O004.)

She pours and drinks barley tea. Seeing little left in the bottle makes her think about buying more tomorrow; a thought about replenishment is not a performed purchase or a newly agreed shopping duty. The ordinary action also keeps the episode from becoming only a metaphor about possession. Miyagi can attend to a household supply while unsettled by a different supply's changed use. (O004.)

Sendai emerges from her room after Miyagi has drunk about half the glass. She approaches close enough that she could hug her and sniffs like a dog. **The approach is performed; a hug is not.** Sendai asks whether Miyagi used her shampoo. Miyagi says she should know without asking, while Sendai distinguishes smelling the same from knowing whether the product was used. Her later private narration confirms that she understands this as Miyagi's first use. The question still gives Miyagi an occasion to state it aloud; the source does not disclose one exhaustive motive for asking twice. (O005–007.)

The exchange has actual limits. Miyagi pushes Sendai's abdomen because she is too close. Sendai steps back, immediately approaches again and pulls her bangs. She calls Miyagi stingy; Miyagi denies it and taps her arm. These local acts are not transformed into an unperformed hug or an agreed new grooming routine. No injury severity is supplied. Calling a refusal stingy is Sendai's characterization, not proof that Miyagi owes proximity. (O005–006.)

## Sharing shampoo does not decide who may dry the hair

After another shampoo question, Miyagi confirms use in a low voice. Sendai answers brightly and offers to dry her hair, saying it should be dried properly. Miyagi says she will do it herself later. Sendai replies that she could let her do it now and kisses the newly washed hair. That first kiss is Miyagi's section endpoint. The refusal of drying is explicit; neither a future intention to dry it herself nor a first shampoo use is an acceptance of Sendai's offered help. (O006.)

Sendai's opening then identifies the same first kiss as the cause of Miyagi's furrowed brow. This is an overlapping account of one act, not an additional third kiss. Miyagi complains in a low voice that she has only just left the bath. Sendai kisses her hair **again**. Her narration recognizes the complaint while continuing the contact. The source does not contain a formal spoken ban on all hair kissing, but it also does not supply a fresh affirmative grant. Shared products and Sendai's pleasure do not settle that question. (O007.)

To Sendai the scent is familiar, yet Miyagi seems to smell much better than she herself does with the same shampoo. That comparison belongs to her attraction and perception; it is not an objective measurement of two bodies or a property proved by the product. She privately wants to keep smelling Miyagi. Miyagi does not hear that full account, just as Sendai has not heard Miyagi's earlier loss-of-self reflection in the bath. The two narrators can recognize the same smell while assigning it different consequences. (O003/O007.)

Miyagi asks whether Sendai will bathe, tells her to go and pushes her abdomen again. Sendai privately describes her as denying both company and hair drying, then asks once more to dry the hair before her own bath. Miyagi repeats that she will do it herself later. After Sendai again calls her stingy, Miyagi lightly kicks her leg and tells her to stop dawdling and bathe. Sendai gives an exaggerated sigh, returns to her room for clothes and a towel, and finds Miyagi already absent when she returns to the common space. The narrated outcome is Sendai's separate bath preparation, without accepted drying, a shared bath, or a shown later fulfillment of Miyagi's own drying plan. The push, tap and kick remain actual contacts without an invented injury outcome or blanket justification. (O008.)

## An unchanged-looking bottle becomes a way to think about time

Sendai enters the bathroom alone, sees what she calls their shared shampoo and picks it up. Its quantity appears unchanged. She cannot tell how much one use has removed and speculates that even ten might not make the difference obvious. The text supplies neither a measured volume nor ten performed uses. Her expectation is that one day she will notice it decreasing faster than when she used it alone. She puts it down and rinses her body under the shower; this ending does not explicitly depict her shampooing her own hair. (O009.)

This apparently unchanged bottle supplies a different scale of change from Miyagi's immediate reaction. Miyagi can already feel altered by the first use. Sendai cannot see an amount missing but imagines the cumulative effect of future uses. She thinks that yesterday and today have not changed them, and that today and tomorrow may likewise be little different. That temporal assessment is her private frame, not a cancellation of the change Miyagi has just felt. The reader can hold both accounts without deciding that one narrator has falsely reported every event. (O002–003/O009–010.)

Sendai extends the comparison to ordinary days. People who formerly used different shampoos can begin sharing one; small events can make successive days differ without the difference being immediately recognized. She expects a day of recognizing change, while admitting that she does not know what it will be or when it will arrive. She **hopes that the change will be good and that the day comes soon**. This is a wish under acknowledged uncertainty, not knowledge that the relationship must improve or a model prediction validated by later corpus familiarity. Even the possibility of using a different shampoo together then remains conditional; no product, date or renewed agreement is chosen. (O010–011.)

The source's arrangement gives the small object several uses without reducing it to one meaning. It is a practical shared supply, an absent old habit, an unwanted felt closeness, a favored smell on another person and an image of gradual time. Miyagi's unease and Sendai's hope are both available to the reader. Neither is fully communicated to the other, and their shared bottle does not itself resolve the local refusals or the relationship's future definition.
