## Maika chooses a response before she has an explanation

Maika doubts Miyagi's departure story and still gives Ami reasons to accept it. The gap is deliberate: she describes putting aside an objection that has not disappeared. Her response preserves distance for the moment, while leaving curiosity and a wish for acknowledgment intact. By the ending, those wishes help support a plan to tease Miyagi the next day. The story makes restraint and pressure parts of one friendship whose members have lost something they wanted.

The cancelled meal matters. Maika recalls an agreed fast-food gathering disappearing when Shiori leaves the hallway. This differs from KSS02's declined invitations and KSS01's unconfirmed alternative timing. Here a prior shared plan has already created an expectation. The quiet corridor, shoe change, sunset and slow walk give the two remaining friends ordinary time in which to experience its loss. Maika's image of Shiori being stolen by a forgotten item and father expresses that loss; it does not certify either excuse as the actual cause. (O002–003;13p12–46.)

Ami turns the uncertainty into a performance. She raises her hand and addresses 宇都宮先生; Maika makes her another teacher. Ami asks for teacher/student roles, Maika counterproposes two teachers, and the exchange continues. Both contribute to its shape. The small negotiation supports an active friendship outside Miyagi's presence, without establishing a grand principle behind Maika's joking preference. It also supplies a form in which Ami can present an opinion that the friends cannot verify. (O002;13p6–22.)

## A plausible defense need not be a settled belief

Ami interprets the phone, forgotten item and father explanation as a boyfriend summons. Maika had not considered that particular hypothesis, but she finds the combination suspicious. She then actually says that Shiori could have forgotten something and that her father often calls her away. Her private account retains the doubt: she thinks no forgotten item is more likely and bends herself away from pursuing that thought. The source therefore gives more than a simple opposition between a credulous friend and an accurate one. (O002–003;13p22–44.)

The father's frequent appearance in Shiori's conversation helps Maika construct plausibility. It does not independently verify every past summons. Nor does the present improvised explanation establish that all earlier father accounts were false. Maika's response may combine loyalty, reassurance, practical conversation management and a choice to leave a private question unanswered today. The narration establishes her continuing doubt and chosen response more firmly than it establishes a single motive for them.

Ami's confidence also needs its later qualification. She initially insists on the boyfriend explanation, then says she thinks Shiori has no boyfriend. She nevertheless wants tomorrow's questioning because it would be fun. Her assertion can function as comic provocation or an entertaining possibility without remaining a confident factual judgment. The later concession does not make her irrelevant: she continues to steer what the friends intend to do. (O002–003;13p33–35,48–70.)

## The romantic questions have different scopes

Maika asks whether Shiori would tell them if she got a boyfriend. Privately, she says she would not demand everything, but would want to know about a lover and assess whether that person could be entrusted with Shiori. The protective wish has a gatekeeping possibility, yet the source shows no actual partner assessment, veto or right accepted by Miyagi. It is Maika's conditional desire, not a relationship rule everyone shares. (O003;13p52–60.)

The wording helps keep the questions distinct. The repeated「彼氏」frames the joke as boyfriend talk, while Maika's private「恋人」has a broader partner scope. Her further observation that Shiori has not discussed a liked person belongs to her conversational experience. Her inference that no such person seems present is less secure. A reader's knowledge of a private connection involving Sendai does not automatically make the literal no-boyfriend belief false, establish a settled couple label, or prove what Miyagi consciously understands at this stage. (O003/O005;13p58–66.)

This distinction also preserves Maika's limits without inventing a rigid theory of her attitudes. She has not received disclosure about a partner, but she can still be curious about one. Miyagi's poor engagement in romance talk could support several explanations, including reticence, discomfort with that conversation or unarticulated priorities. The supplement does not decide among them by giving the reader another person's complete interior.

## The same cancellation includes an attempt at repair

The original novel identifies the contact as Sendai's messages and the destination as the music preparation room. Miyagi explicitly improvises the forgotten-item/father explanation before leaving. These details explain the reader's advantage over the friends, without making Ami's boyfriend hypothesis an accurate discovery. Exact speech and hallway markers align this supplement with the existing LN03 event after the final cultural-festival day, rather than adding another cancellation. (O005;LN03p005281–330.)

That original event also contains important counterevidence. Miyagi apologizes and asks the friends to eat without her; Maika prefers postponement, Ami offers to accompany her, and Miyagi eventually delegates choosing a later day. No new date is fixed or replacement meal performed in these passages. There is nevertheless an actual attempt at coordination and repair. The friends heard it. Its omission from this supplement does not make them forget it or authorize an interpretation of complete abandonment. Their loss and Miyagi's repair remain parts of the same encounter. (O005;LN03p005315–328.)

## The ending settles an agenda, not its consequences

Maika proposes questioning, then offers to drop it when Ami shares her no-boyfriend opinion. Ami insists on the amusement. Maika agrees to limited teasing because Shiori left them and today's incongruity has been allowed to pass. She explicitly denies a serious intention to make Shiori a toy, while still wanting Shiori to accept some teasing. Ami's final declaration of tomorrow's fate gives their agreement a mock punitive flourish. (O003;13p54–78.)

The private qualification limits the force of「おもちゃ」without proving that any resulting teasing would be welcome. Miyagi is absent and has accepted no disclosure obligation. The source does not perform tomorrow's questioning or show its effect. What it freezes is the friends' negotiated response: curiosity, amusement, care and a wish to recognize a broken expectation coexist. The comic ending gives their own ordinary time consequences, while leaving the central person's response outside this story's boundary.
