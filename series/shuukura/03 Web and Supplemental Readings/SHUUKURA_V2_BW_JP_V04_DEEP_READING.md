# 私たちだけの夜: a night extended in thought, a departure performed

Miyagi Shiori has arranged for Sendai Hazuki to stay, yet finds that the guest's temporary absence changes how her own room feels. Sendai wants to stay again, yet departs the next morning. The bonus locates attachment in those interruptions of ordinary action: a manga that will not register, another person's welcome sounds, an arm that delays departure and a remembered meal that almost becomes an excuse to return. Their private reasons remain unevenly disclosed. The reader receives both accounts without either character receiving the whole other account. (O002–012.)

## A host cannot settle in her own room

Miyagi has already made a practical accommodation. She gives Sendai a sweatshirt/change of clothes and a white towel, explains that the hot water was prepared before supper and directs her to the dryer and other supplies. She privately adds an instruction to hurry into the bathroom. Sendai's surprise and not-quite-convinced expression are observed through Miyagi; her full bathroom thoughts are unavailable. The scene supplies an individual bath for the guest, not a joint bath or a grant of unrestricted physical access. (O002.)

The short release from being alone together does not settle Miyagi. Sendai's use of the bathroom Miyagi normally uses promptly makes her uneasy. The room's ordinary objects become unsuccessful ways to regulate that attention. She rises and sits, repeatedly touches the black cat, fetches three manga, turns pages and takes in none of the content. She wants Sendai to return but anticipates not knowing what to say then. Her familiar room no longer feels simply her room; another location would not resolve the conflict either. Calling this ordinary hospitality alone misses the represented disruption. Calling it a confidently named feeling goes beyond her words. (O003–004.)

The university information has a particular route. Miyagi says she heard **from Maika yesterday that Maika had told Sendai Miyagi's preferred university**. The grammatical subject of the reported telling is not Miyagi; this is not her own direct disclosure to Sendai. Today Miyagi questioned Sendai, heard what she wanted to hear, then stopped her from leaving. That retrospective account places the invitation after a consequential conversation, while leaving its full dialogue to the distinct earlier witness. The bonus does not reproduce an exact answer to every university question or show a newly negotiated university plan. Maika is an offstage information intermediary and Miyagi's educational companion, with a future that matters independently of this pair's intimacy. (O003/O005.)

Miyagi's spoken minimization, “only studying,” has a concrete counterweight: they have eaten stew but have not yet done the winter-break work, and she has not bathed. Those unfinished tasks make waiting reasonable while failing to explain the intensity of her distraction. The story does not complete the study simply because studying is the stated reason for staying. Her attempt to think of an empty house is likewise an attempted suppression, contradicted by her immediate awareness of Sendai bathing. It is not evidence that Sendai has actually vanished or that Miyagi is literally alone in the apartment. (O003–004.)

## The possibility she tries to suppress

Miyagi proposes privately to erase Sendai's presence from thought until the bath is finished and to seal away the fact that the stew tasted good. She denies enjoying cooking together. The following conditional imagining complicates that denial: if she treated the graduation deadline as nonexistent and attended Maika's university, she might cook with Sendai again and eat something still better. She is entertaining continuation while trying to suppress its present appeal. The deadline has not actually been revoked, and Maika's university has not become an agreed joint destination in this scene. Academic concerns and loyalty to Maika remain live parts of the imagined arrangement. (O005.)

She recalls Sendai asking whether Miyagi did not want to see her and saying that she herself wanted to see Miyagi. Miyagi then turns that question toward herself but leaves the answer incomplete. The reader can infer that it is difficult to finish, especially beside the conditional future and the failed reading. The text does not supply the missing words as an actual declaration. Sendai's recalled statement is available to Miyagi; Miyagi's full private response and her attempted erasure are available to the reader only. A disclosure ledger must preserve that asymmetry. (O005.)

The section ends with sound and preparation rather than a reunion conversation. Miyagi stops taking new manga, listens to sounds in the corridor, finds the unfamiliar nighttime sound welcome and simultaneously unsettling, straightens and briefly catches her breath. She takes up a manga again and sits. The object can be read as a familiar activity resumed under attention to the returning guest; a deliberate deception or a proved attempt to conceal desire would be a stronger claim. The breakfast and the passage of morning time make prep school imminent. Sendai privately wants not to go and imagines asking to stay again, but doubts Miyagi would invite her twice. ## A milk complaint almost becomes a return

The farewell does not produce confidence in Sendai. Winter break is short, unlike summer; with no date fixed now, she predicts that Miyagi will be slow to contact her. That prediction is neither fulfilled nor tested within the bonus. She reinterprets the familiar farewell tone as proof that the earlier loneliness was imagined. Meanwhile she increases speed toward prep school, wants to turn round, looks for a reason to go back and slows while remembering the milk-free stew. Her conduct and private reasons change by small stages rather than a single resolved decision. (O010–011.)

The missing milk is concrete: there was no milk in the fridge despite the roux, and Sendai attributes that to Miyagi not buying it. She imagines a complaint as an excuse to return, then rejects it aloud to herself because she would be late and the meal was actually good. This is not a performed return, an objective failure of the recipe or Miyagi's deliberate scheme. Sendai thinks it may have tasted good because they ate together. She further infers that Miyagi may have enjoyed their meal because Miyagi did not call it bad. The latter is a limited inference from silence; Miyagi's earlier denial and conditional cooking future give the reader a different basis for comparison, not evidence that Sendai has read her mind. (O011–012.)

The hand no longer contains Miyagi's warmth. Cold makes Sendai curl her back, and that posture summons Miyagi's curled back in the shared futon. She straightens, casts Miyagi as the unfamiliar element disrupting eighteen years of familiar year-end routines, and fears her feet may stop if she lets attention lapse. Her paired statements about yesterday and today serve as a private instruction to change her feeling with the date. The endpoint is **hurrying toward prep school**. Arrival, actual lateness, a return home, another night and the timing of Miyagi's next message are unshown. A successful inner command in this local action is not a permanent capacity proved across future situations. (O012.)
