## Packing order as a measure of affection

The crocodile tissue cover interprets packing as a change in its relationship with Shiori. Its complaint depends on a particular measure of affection: how long it can remain beside her and watch her. Being packed early reduces that time, so the newer black cat appears to have become more important. The narrator itself supplies another explanation—early packing might ensure that something precious is not forgotten—without giving up its resentment. The story makes a material action support competing interpretations of care.

The opening establishes what the object believes it contributes. It protects and supplies tissues, calls itself Shiori's first honorary fan-club member, and describes itself with extravagant praise. Losing the tissue box from its belly interrupts this useful role. Darkness and confinement also prevent the companionship it values. Its irritation therefore combines a disrupted task, loss of access and wounded status; the source does not reduce these to one motive. (O002;09p1–13.)

Its correction to the opening complaint matters. It does not object to Shiori moving as such, because its own place is wherever she is. It will follow her and tolerate confinement for the sake of remaining together. That declaration gives loyalty priority over place, yet the later complaint shows how difficult the narrator finds the temporary cost. Its willingness to accompany Shiori does not make the lost time unimportant. No actual indefinite wait or human acceptance of this promise is shown. (O002;09p14–16.)

## Packing order and the first place claimed

The black cat makes the cost comparative. The crocodile recalls being cherished before Sendai brought the cat, but now feels that the newer object matters more. The crucial wording is「気がする」: the perceived ranking is an interpretation. Packing order is the observable basis the personified account supplies, while Shiori's reason for that order remains unavailable. The crocodile accepts that Shiori chose it and still feels unsettled. (O003;09p29–39.)

Its alternative explanation changes the meaning of being packed before the cat. Packing early could secure inclusion in the move, rather than mark reduced affection. The same act that deprives the crocodile of immediate company might protect its future place beside Shiori. The narrator recognizes that possibility through「かもしれない」but continues to resent the cat's longer access. Its uncertainty is explicit, and a comforting explanation does not automatically settle its feelings. This is a precise account of an interpretive conflict, rather than proof that either imagined motive is Shiori's actual one. (O003;09p41.)

The inflated descriptions sharpen the comedy of that conflict.「エクセレント」「ワンダフル」「ブリリアント」and the proud closing identity turn a familiar household object into the advocate of its own exceptional importance. First membership and earlier companionship become claims of priority. That rhetoric need not make every observation false: the original novel corroborates a phase in which the crocodile is boxed while the cat remains outside. The difficulty lies in moving from placement to an affection ranking. (O002–003/O005;09p7,31–41,47–50;LN04p01442,127–129.)

## A partisan cause of change

Sendai receives the blame for the move, distant university and room sharing. Her full name introduces the supposed rival who is taking Shiori away. The narrator's account gathers several changes into a personal grievance, even while admitting that it does not understand the distant university or room sharing well. Its confident valuation exceeds the scope of its stated understanding. (O002–003;09p18–27.)

The original packing scene qualifies that blame without making Sendai's causal importance disappear. Miyagi says she chose this future and does not regret the decision, while acknowledging considerable unease. She also refuses help with packing because she wants to handle the room's things and memories herself. These are reasons and choices the reader can attribute to Miyagi; the object cannot be used to turn them into a new instance of Sendai forcing her departure. Conversely, Miyagi's agency does not prove that the relationship with Sendai played no causal role. Influence, choice and the allocation of blame remain different questions. (O005;LN04p01414,25,108–123.)

There is a supported structural parallel with Miyagi's own account. Selecting books to take or leave makes her imagine Sendai sorting her out and leaving her behind. The crocodile interprets another kind of sorting through its own place in Shiori's affection. The meanings differ: being packed already indicates inclusion, yet the object experiences its timing as lost intimacy. The comparison joins material decisions and relational interpretation without treating the crocodile as Miyagi's hidden voice. Her private memories and fears do not automatically become its knowledge. (O003/O005;09p31–41;LN04p01422–34.)

## The comfort a cotton body might offer

The ending turns attention toward Shiori's apparent unease. The crocodile remembers her seeming slightly anxious when she put it in the box and wants to comfort her with its soft hands and body. That is an outward impression within the personified account, followed by an intention. The source neither gives the object her complete thoughts at that instant nor performs the desired comforting action. (O003;09p43–47.)

The explicit inability to speak separates the reader's access from human hearing. The first-person voice is available as narration; Shiori and Sendai do not thereby hear its jealousy, blame or loyalty. The closing「心――綿」reworks the idiom of feeling something from the bottom of one's heart through the object's material. The joke preserves both affection and the fact that this is a tissue cover. It invites attention to what familiar things can mean without certifying the narrator as an all-knowing witness to their owners. (O003;09p47–50.)

This wish to comfort also limits a wholly possessive reading. The crocodile wants its own privileged access, but can notice Shiori's difficulty and imagine giving something through its softness. That care does not erase its resentment, establish a purely selfless motive or produce an actual response from Shiori. The story ends with the wish, leaving unpacking and its later role outside the boundary.

The original novel places the corresponding packing phase after graduation and before university entrance. Its command-based routine has ended, while Miyagi's connection with Sendai continues. The crocodile's portable sense of belonging gives that transition an additional perspective: the old room can be left, yet familiar things and the meanings attached to them can travel. The supplement supplies a new comic narrator for this phase; it does not establish that the numbered novel contains this voice, that the entire episode occurs at one precisely identifiable instant, or that a future relationship has already been secured. (O005;LN04p0147–14,127–137.)
