## The familiar Shiori becomes difficult to recognize

The crocodile begins with an accusation:「私の志緒理ちゃん」has celebrated Sendai Hazuki’s birthday. The possessive precedes the explanation. Calling itself an excellent, wonderful tissue cover and the distinguished first member of Shiori’s fan club gives it a comic claim to priority; calling the room its territory extends that claim into domestic space. The celebration threatens a familiar arrangement whose authority the object largely supplies for itself. Its shock therefore reveals how it organizes belonging, rather than establishing what Miyagi owes it. (O002;03p1–13.)

Its address for Sendai is revealingly dependent on Miyagi. It begins with「仙台葉月」, then corrects itself to「仙台さん」because Shiori uses that form. The more respectful address carries an appraisal of excessive familiarity. The cover imitates the person it cherishes while resisting her choice of company. Later it says that Shiori seems to have become someone it does not know and admits a little loneliness. Rivalry is consequently also a response to unfamiliar development: the beloved person continues to act beyond the observer’s established picture of her. (O002–003;03p15,29.)

The remembered celebration contains pizza, cake and congratulations without a song. Its imagined thirty-tissue eruption gives surprise an absurd bodily scale; the text does not report a counted spill. The birthday’s importance comes from a film or television program Shiori watched, an uncertain media memory that the cover converts into a reason its permission should have been sought. Familiar cultural importance becomes an invented personal jurisdiction. The black cat plush reportedly rejoices that Shiori looks happy, furnishing an alternative appraisal inside the crocodile’s account. The cat’s complete interior and Miyagi’s actual happiness remain separately unprovided. (O002–003;03p7–27.)

## A grievance that condenses the human scene

The original novel makes Miyagi the initiator of Sendai’s first admission to her room after the move. She opens the door and pushes Sendai inside; Sendai’s narration and Miyagi’s later bonus account both identify the first admission. This correspondence matters because the object’s grievance might otherwise encourage a reader to treat its territorial objection as the room’s human occupant refusing entry. Miyagi’s initiative survives the cover’s disapproval. Its report of subsequent visits also remains narrower than an unlimited entry right. (O005;03p15–17,31;LN07p00938–48;p015113–114.)

The kiss requires a separate assessment. The cover recalls the question「ここにキスしていい？」, lip contact and the completed kiss. The original sequence specifies Sendai stroking Miyagi’s lips, asking, hearing「聞かれたらやだって答える」and then proceeding through her interpretation that the usual Miyagi has returned. The conditional form leaves room to debate how Miyagi positions the question, but supplies no clear affirmative answer. Her later proposal to count the kiss as a substitute present occurs after the act. Treating that later framing as prior permission would flatten the sequence just as surely as substituting the crocodile’s veto for Miyagi’s words. (O002–003/O005;03p21,27,48;LN07p009225–235.)

The novel also gives reader access to motives unavailable through this object account. Sendai experiences genuine birthday joy and finds a cake newly preferable because Miyagi selected it for her; Miyagi’s next-morning account supplies fatigue and gift uncertainty. Those accounts enrich the occasion without making its every action an uncomplicated expression of shared feeling. The cover’s moral exaggeration and the celebration’s warmth each illuminate part of the scene. Neither removes the need to reconstruct who initiated, asked, answered, interpreted and acted. (O005;LN07p009158–159;p015113–123.)

## The comforter receives care

「先週」separates the remembered birthday from the current room. Miyagi leans against the bed and sighs; Sendai is absent. The cover welcomes that absence for itself, yet thinks Miyagi looks unhappy. Its wish to approach and encourage her encounters an explicit material limit: it cannot move independently. The inward command「動け」is followed by Miyagi pulling its hand, a response-like sequence whose timing does not establish that she heard its thought. The intended comforter becomes the recipient of contact. (O002–003;03p13,31–40.)

The response happens through the tissue cover’s commodity body. Its box compresses with a「きゅんっ」sound it says only it can hear, and its tissues stand up to express joy. Miyagi then says「バイト、むかつく」, and the tissues wilt as the cover takes the remark to concern Sendai. The bodily reversal makes an interpretation visible. Miyagi’s short line is actual quoted speech;「らしき」marks the cover’s attribution of its topic. The scene does not independently place Sendai at work or disclose Miyagi’s entire reason for irritation. (O003;03p40–44.)

This limit is consequential rather than incidental. The cover acts on how Miyagi appears to it, just as it resented the cat’s reported appraisal of happiness. Its concern can be sincere while its access remains incomplete. Original Sendai also pulls and strokes the crocodile during the birthday, which complicates an exclusively antagonistic account of their relation without erasing the cover’s dislike. The different witnesses let ordinary handling carry more than one meaning. (O003/O005;03p33–46;LN07p009150–151.)

## A concession that preserves the conflict

The ending retains the unwanted condition: the cover does not want Sendai in this room. It nevertheless finds Miyagi’s apparent unhappiness painful, decides to forgive Sendai, and prays for her early return. The inflated「三千歩くらい」turns concession into another comic scale. It records no actual movement or human debt. The prayer ends the source before any return can answer it. (O003;03p46–48.)

The change is substantial because the cover allows Shiori’s apparent welfare to qualify its own preference about company. It remains partial because territorial dislike survives. A generous reading sees sympathy outrunning rivalry; a more skeptical reading sees another effort to preserve the familiar, happier Shiori it wants nearby. The source supports both pressures without requiring a single pure motive. Its private forgiveness supplies neither human consent nor a reciprocal reconciliation.

Read alongside the earlier packing stories and the platypus’s dispute with the cat rests, this source gives the objects distinct ways of negotiating place. Their wishes, physical limits and claims to priority create formal perspectives on domestic life. They can help the reader notice contested belonging and partial knowledge while retaining their independent comic particulars. The crocodile’s final wish is credible precisely because it changes what it wants to happen without making every resentment disappear.
