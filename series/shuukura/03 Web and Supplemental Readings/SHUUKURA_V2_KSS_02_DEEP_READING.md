## Curiosity is part of the friendship that absence interrupts

Maika and Ami want to know about Miyagi and Sendai, but they also want their friend to spend an ordinary afternoon with them. The mock investigation opens a conversation whose stakes become personal: what does frequent companionship entitle someone to know, and what can a friend do when familiarity has stopped producing an explanation? The story gives the supporting friendship its own desires, irritations and limits. Its closing decision to wait matters because curiosity and hurt remain present.

The opening makes Ami's theatrical method immediately visible. She announces the 327th meeting; Maika suggests there have been about two. The discrepancy is a joke enacted between friends, not an unreliable count to import into chronology. Ami's self-applause and cola, Maika's fries and the easy correction establish an afternoon with a social rhythm of its own. Shiori's absence is felt within an existing life. It does not render that life empty. (O002;14p1–13.)

Ami says a connection would be more interesting, then proposes extortion or blackmail and concedes their implausibility. A reader who already knows the private arrangement can notice an irony around money and control. The guesses still fail as evidence: Sendai extorting Miyagi reverses the known payment direction, while a conjectured hidden weakness does not establish actual blackmail. The scene lets the outside conversation brush against important structures while displaying how little these speakers know. It gives neither girl secret insight by way of a joke. (O002;14p37–48.)

## Spoken trust can preserve a question

Maika is not simply the sensible friend who has no interest in gossip. She admits curiosity and recalls asking Miyagi about Sendai during that day's sports practice. She recalls Miyagi's teacher-summons explanation and tells Ami the situation is probably as Shiori describes. Privately, however, she retains things that do not fit. The thought and her spoken response may reflect chosen loyalty, a practical stopping point, reassurance to Ami, or some mixture of these. It is not complete conviction, and it does not prove she has discovered the private relationship. (O002;14p13–29.)

The original novels sharpen the access limit. The quoted practice question corresponds directly to LN02p003247. LN01's actual school call leads to the music preparation room and an argument about their private terms; it supplies the reader with a route different from the teacher account Maika has heard. The word「嘘」in LN01p015249 retracts Miyagi's preceding money-loan joke, so that word alone should not be made grammatical proof about the teacher explanation. The depicted private events provide the stronger comparison. Maika's uncertainty therefore matters without becoming full knowledge. (O005;C2.)

The novel also gives Miyagi's local judgment that Maika has lost interest after changing the subject. This supplement shows continued conversation later. The comparison limits any omniscient reading of Miyagi's judgment, while permitting a temporary shift or renewed curiosity rather than requiring contradiction. Her friends' interests extend beyond what she can see at one moment. Their account is likewise partial: Maika does not receive Miyagi's private preoccupation with Sendai, which the already-read novel represents. Neither focalizer becomes the measure of everyone's whole interior. (O002–003/O005;LN02p003199,247–265.)

## Talking often does not settle what a friend can ask

Maika's formulation「お喋りなようでお喋りではない」specifies the domain of familiarity. Miyagi talks about manga and anime and about things she dislikes. Maika recalls no consultation about liking someone or future plans. This refines the earlier KSS01 account of face-to-face talkativeness; it does not require erasing it. A person can speak freely about shared interests while withholding personal problems. The observed distinction belongs to Maika's experience, rather than proving that Miyagi never confides in anyone or possesses no wishes of her own. (O003;15p7–13.)

Ami's joking question about trust hurts because it offers a possible explanation for a real gap. Maika says it depresses her; the theatrical claim that Shiori is no longer theirs voices a fear of losing company. The wording need not become literal ownership, a right to every confidence, or a clinical diagnosis. Conversely, calling it merely a joke would discard Maika's admitted concern. Affection includes wants that may not be met, and these friends are affected subjects rather than transparent instruments of Miyagi's welfare. (O003;15p15–26.)

Their account of reduced availability has its own limits. Maika reports today's refusal and an earlier one, and dates a broader change to around the summer of their second year. That period broadly fits the reader's knowledge of the private arrangement's beginning, but the source does not count every refusal or assign all absences one cause. Maika explicitly adds their own exams and cram school, which reduce opportunities for detours and questions. The social world has constraints independent of the central relationship. A persuasive reconstruction must retain both the reported refusals and these other costs. (O003;15p28–36.)

## Ordinary play carries information and limits

Maika continues to offer Ami fries, and they exchange jokes. Ami supplies her own example of a difficult secret: an earlier two-kilogram weight gain was actually three. Maika points out that Ami is volunteering it, undercutting its supposed unspeakability. This is Ami's autobiographical disclosure, not evidence about Miyagi's body or a guarantee that someone else's secret will be similarly easy to tell. The comic detour keeps Ami active in the scene and tests, without resolving, the larger discussion about what people say. (O003;15p40–50.)

Maika then lightly pinches Ami's cheek. Ami cries to stop, Maika hurriedly releases it, and the exchange turns to the unexpectedly loud voice. The stopping response is concrete; advance permission is unprovided. Public noise may also explain the quick release, and Ami's apology concerns volume. No whole moral identity follows from this small incident. Its useful contribution is specificity: familiar bodily play, refusal, response and apology have different scopes even within an easy friendship. (O003;15p52–61.)

## Waiting is a choice whose result remains open

Maika misses Shiori a little and recognizes that she cannot bring her by force. Her spoken proposal is to wait and hope Shiori will eventually speak. The final narration immediately qualifies that hope: Maika does not know whether her words will become reality. Miyagi is absent and has promised nothing. The endpoint establishes Maika's chosen response, rather than a completed intervention or guaranteed confession. (O003;15p62–67.)

This restraint does not cancel her earlier questions, desire for company or hurt. It is a decision made with those wants still present and with limited access to their cause. The story expands the work's relationship structure by making the friends' ordinary time consequential. Privacy has costs to people who remain outside it; their care can include curiosity and disappointment without proving entitlement to the private world. The closing drink leaves both the friendship and its unanswered question in motion.
