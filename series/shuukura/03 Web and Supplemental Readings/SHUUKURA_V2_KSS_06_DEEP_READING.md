## The expectation the box does not fulfill

The black-cat plush learns what moving means through another object's explanation and its own memory of a bag. Those sources make confinement seem predictably lonely. The story then retains the darkness and narrowness while changing the loneliness. Company alters the meaning of the same material conditions. This small correction gives the move an emotional direction without making either object an authority on Shiori's reasons.

At first, the cat's present is pleasurable. Shiori has stopped going to「こうこう」and spends time at home talking about preparations. The cat hears complaints about Sendai and the bother of moving, but values the company those circumstances provide. Shiori's dissatisfaction and the listener's happiness coexist. A spoken complaint does not make the cat adopt the speaker's entire evaluation. Its temporarily suspended book-watching「お仕事」also gives way to being with her, showing that usefulness is only one source of an object's place in the room. (O002;10p1–10.)

The remembered bag explains the forecast. Sendai selected the cat and brought it to the room; meeting Shiori was welcome, while the passage itself was dark, cramped and lonely. The crocodile's explanation now supplies a category—moving involves another enclosure—into which the cat puts that earlier experience. Its knowledge is limited but intelligibly assembled. The repeated quotation marks around「ひっこし」「箱」「袋」make familiar household terms appear newly learned. (O002;10p12–20.)

Shiori strokes and holds the cat, says packing is nearly finished, and asks it to remain in a narrow place temporarily. The gentle「しばらくここにいてね」gives a duration and addresses the object directly. The cat nevertheless wants more company on the bed. Human handling and the object's narrated wish occupy different positions: the text gives the wish a reply, then closes the lid, without establishing that Shiori hears that reply. The sequence matters as a loss of present contact even though it does not establish a conversation between human and object. (O002;10p22–35.)

The correction arrives inside the expected description:「さびし――くない」. Darkness and narrowness survive the sentence; loneliness does not. The pause makes the reader encounter the forecast's failure before learning its cause. The bag held the cat alone, whereas the box contains something soft and a recognizable voice. Packing can preserve company through a form that had seemed to threaten it. (O002–003;10p35–45.)

## Company with unequal comfort

The crocodile is the reason the cat no longer dislikes the box, but it protests the proximity. Its opening complaint combines cramped bodily contact with a claim over「私の志緒理ちゃん」and a remark that the cat resembles her slightly. The resemblance is the crocodile's attribution. Its assertion that this does not entitle the cat to sit beside it applies a language of permission to a placement Shiori selected, rather than one the cat chose. (O003;10p37–39;O002;10p26–30.)

Their greetings therefore do not announce matching feelings. The cat recognizes softness and a loud voice, offers an eager greeting, and receives a greeting joined to another complaint. One object finds relief in the same proximity the other finds oppressive. The story can make company valuable without making its value perfectly reciprocal. The cat's continued wish to talk might reflect a generous disposition or limited uptake of the crocodile's irritation; the source does not establish deliberate disregard or one complete explanation. (O003;10p39–49,59–66.)

The question about the box sharpens their difference in knowledge. The cat can repeat Shiori's instruction but cannot explain why she chose this box. The crocodile interrupts that report to ask why here; the answer returns to who gave the instruction. A source of authority is available where a reason is missing. This conversational mismatch prevents the reader from treating the shared placement as proof that Shiori consciously designed a comforting arrangement. It also leaves the cat's account of what she said intact. (O003;10p51–55.)

The crocodile then asks whether Shiori was well. Its own lost access makes the new arrival an information source. The cat reports an appearance of loneliness alongside her expressed determination to manage the move. Those are different kinds of evidence, and the report does not convey all of her private uncertainty. Asking for news can express care and a wish to recover privileged access at once; the scene need not choose one exhaustive motive. (O003/O005;10p57–59;LN04p014120–123.)

## A rank that must explain itself

The crocodile's request for silence meets the cat's wish to talk. Its conditional concession demands respect for an「エクセレント」senior, then has to explain what「敬う」means. Earlier presence can support a claim to seniority, but it does not make the requested response self-evident. The supposed superior must turn status into an intelligible instruction. (O003;10p59–63;KSS05 native09p33.)

Its explanation remains vague:「なんかこうすごそうにして」. The cat's response explicitly makes「ワニさんが」the one who will look or act impressive. The request for deferential recognition has become a statement about the claimant's own display. The joke can undercut the crocodile's authority without proving that the cat intends mockery. Nor does a cheerful answer demonstrate that the hierarchy has been understood and enacted. The contrast between inflated praise and simple questions makes power depend on successful interpretation. (O003;10p61–64.)

The closing anticipation gives the cat a wider attachment than the crocodile's rivalrous account in KSS05. It wants Shiori again and looks forward to seeing Sendai, following Shiori's report that moving will make this possible. Sendai's remembered selection and future presence belong to its welcome prospects. The same change blamed by one object becomes a hoped-for reunion for another. The final「ね、ワニさん」invites company in that expectation, but receives no answering assent before the boundary. (O003;10p66–69;O002;10p16;KSS05 native09p18–27.)

## An additional voice in the packing phase

The original novel has the crocodile already boxed while the cat remains at the bedside, then has Miyagi lift the cat and invite it along. It also ends the old paid routine while anticipating a room where someone will be present. KSS06 adds the cat's actual boxing and an object conversation; it does not simply repeat a conversation already in the novel. The shared packing phase supports a comparison between anticipated human company and unexpected company inside the box, without supplying an exact common instant or Shiori's reason for that arrangement. (O005;LN04p0147–14,42,127–137.)

The new dialogue also qualifies the earlier crocodile account. KSS05's inability to speak while wishing to comfort Shiori cannot become a universal claim that excludes the object speech enacted here. The two supplements distinguish what their readers hear from what humans are shown hearing. Their literary personification lets familiar possessions articulate loyalty, irritation and hope while leaving human knowledge incomplete. At this ending, the cat's experience has changed and the move remains unfinished: companionship is present, future release is desired, and the larger transition is still an expectation.
