# E0021 — Restraint, a vanished trace and unequal information

Miyagi tries to limit how often she calls Sendai, but the episode ends with her sending the usual message. Between the decision and the act, friends notice behavior she has not noticed herself, a public encounter leaves her uneasy, and a rumor exposes a gap in what she knows about Sendai. The source makes this investment visible without giving it a settled relationship label, a single cause or a demonstrated violation of a weekly quota.

This is Miyagi's own narration after the intervening Sendai-focalized episodes. The complete frozen Japanese original was read before the current novel comparison and reviewer findings. Its entering freeze, three source-order first notes and whole-source Q1 remain exact. Familiarity includes accepted LN01–10,23supplements and WEB1–20; the ordered current encounter earns no blind or held-out prediction credit.

## Concern for study becomes a private restraint

The opening distinguishes Maika's juku from Sendai's prep school. Miyagi reports that her father would pay if she attended either, yet she understands neither institution much beyond being a place to study and has little interest in the difference (native1–7). A conditional offer establishes neither enrollment nor an actual payment or a fresh household scene.

Her lack of educational interest does not eliminate concern for Sendai's time. Because Sendai studies at prep school, Miyagi feels bad about frequently summoning her. After Sendai left last week, she decided to call her only once weekly and to tolerate minor unpleasant experiences (9–12). The restraint recognizes an activity belonging to the other person. It complicates a description in which Miyagi's authority simply removes all regard for Sendai's circumstances, while supplying no repair of the earlier painful conduct or new shared contract.

The decision belongs to Miyagi's private account. No conversation imposes the ceiling on Sendai, and no number of earlier summons this week is given. Her renewed desire to call is immediate, but remains a wish here. The later message will put restraint and initiative in tension; it cannot by itself prove that she exceeded one call within the same week.

The present bad experience is initially concrete. After world history, Maika and Ami discuss Miyagi being the target of Takahashi's ill temper. Miyagi recalls a difficult question, prolonged criticism and a named sarcastic remark, and describes her resulting low mood (14–29). The teacher's supposed reputation remains her report and the friends' conversation. No complete teacher interior or independent finding about all his lessons follows. Although Miyagi voices wanting to go home, Ami reminds her of PE; she takes gym clothes and leaves with both friends toward the gym (31–40). The scene performs actual companionship and ordinary assistance before returning to Sendai.

## The mark disappears before the behavior does

Maika asks whether Miyagi has injured her arm because she has touched it repeatedly and is doing so now (42–46). Miyagi denies injury, then notices her own hand on the region where Sendai's trace had been. She acknowledges the observation and removes it (48–53). The outside observer supplies a cue to conduct that has escaped the narrator's current attention.

Miyagi reports that Sendai's mark from last week faded before even two days, returning to her normal skin color (54). This later testimony concerns Miyagi's arm. It differs from E20's concealment of Sendai's separate mark under clothing, and supplies no inspected outcome for that other mark. Nor does natural fading demonstrate the earlier proposed removal experiment or a generally reliable duration. Miyagi's lack of a memory of touching during the interval and her admission that she would not have noticed now without Maika are limits on recollection and awareness, not proof that the conduct had never occurred (56–57).

The physical absence does not make the act narratively finished. Miyagi dislikes how the touching makes it seem that she wanted the trace to remain (59–60). The qualified appearance and aversion both matter. Calling this an uncomplicated conscious wish would remove her resistance to that interpretation; calling it meaningless would discard the implication she herself finds troubling. The trace has faded, while its possible significance returns through a habit and another person's attention.

Her absorption stops her walking. Ami calls and pulls her along; Maika pats her back and asks whether the teacher's treatment shocked her that much (62–69). Miyagi privately says that is not the reason and leaves the suggestion uncorrected (70–71). Maika has voiced a guess, not demonstrated settled belief by both friends. Neither friend is shown receiving the trace's maker or the private reason for the pause. The gap is between observed conduct, an offered explanation and unshared interior access.

## Company has more than one possible setting

Miyagi asks Maika about juku. Maika describes both difficulty and endurance until entrance exams, then invites Miyagi to the same place and praises its teaching (73–78). Miyagi's通わないけど answers a prospective attendance question: she is not going or planning to attend. Her later考えとく is a provisional answer to the invitation, not an enrollment decision or an audited history of every past class.

Privately, she wonders whether going with Maika would be better than being alone in her room, despite not wishing to study (79). The possibility gives Maika's friendship independent value. It does not turn the paid relation into Miyagi's only possible source of company, or make the friends interchangeable with Sendai. The source then introduces that different comparison: attending Sendai's prep school. Miyagi immediately pushes away a thought she neither intends nor expects to realize, thinks any hypothetical attendance would probably be juku, and explicitly has no present plan (81–84).

The imagined alternative still matters as a disclosed movement of thought. It is neither a shared dream nor a completed choice. Maika and Sendai are not shown hearing the comparison or its solitude rationale. The real invitation, possible friend company and rejected Sendai alternative coexist with the message that will eventually be sent. They resist a sole-motive account while leaving the dyadic investment guarded.

## A public convention can be accepted and still feel uncomfortable

The approaching group includes Ibaraki and Sendai. Ami remarks on its conspicuousness, and Maika's agreement is explicitly attributed at99 before she moves aside. Miyagi's image of the group walking as though claiming the school is her comparison, not an objective rank verdict or Ibaraki's disclosed intention (90–100).

Miyagi and Sendai briefly meet eyes and immediately pass. They do not talk or wave. Miyagi says this follows their agreement and avows no dissatisfaction with it (102–104). This is actual enactment and narrated recognition of an existing school convention. No new mutual silence term is negotiated, and the fleeting glance does not reveal Sendai's whole school interior.

The next sentences preserve an affective discrepancy rather than cancelling the avowal. Miyagi feels an unexplained, bodily-like unease and becomes unsettled. The teacher's treatment also contributes to wanting to summon Sendai again. For now, she says, it is only wanting; she recalls her decision to tolerate small unpleasantness (106–110). Acceptance of the arrangement and discomfort with the experience can both be true in the account. The source does not voice a demand to abolish the convention or certify complete contentment.

## Private proximity does not supply complete information

Maika reports that a second-year boy from boys' basketball confessed to Sendai, apparently named Yamada. Ami responds with interest (112–121). The speech is an actual event; the confession is its reported content. At first Miyagi does not know where Maika obtained it. She has heard neither the story nor Yamada as a topic from Sendai and does not even know who he is (123–125). No earlier unnamed boy is automatically identified as this reported person.

Miyagi then acknowledges the relevant limit directly: she and Sendai do not talk about everything or about love, and there are many things about Sendai she does not know. Nevertheless, learning something unknown about her from Maika feels unpleasant (127). The account exposes discomfort at how information is distributed. After-school access has not made Miyagi the person through whom every other fact passes.

That response can support an interpretation of guarded attachment or jealousy-like discomfort, but the strongest limit stays visible. Miyagi does not consciously name a settled romance, assert a right to all disclosures or accuse Sendai of intentionally hiding a verified event. The same source acknowledges why information might be absent from their conversation. The reader cannot use the unpleasant feeling alone to settle its whole cause.

The friends debate the boy's attractiveness and ask Miyagi unexpectedly. She stops, answers that she cannot identify him and asks how they know (129–138). The assessments belong to the speakers, not to a newly positive Miyagi judgment. Maika says she heard from someone at the same juku and moves to another rumor (139–141). The source category is now stated, but the person remains unnamed and uninspected. The extra link does not independently confirm a confession, acceptance, dating or an event date; the next rumor's content is absent.

## The ending performs contact and leaves its result outside the source

Miyagi knows today is a prep-school day and expects that, even if called, Sendai would come tomorrow. She repeats her concern about frequent summons. After PE, however, she sends the usual message (143–147). The temporal change from hallway wish to performed send is explicit. Her initiative does not require a current public greeting or an expectation of immediate arrival that day.

The exact message wording, receipt, answer, next-day visit and financial transaction are unshown. Teacher distress, trace-related preoccupation, uncomfortable passing and the new rumor all precede the act. The sequence makes them available as contributing contexts without isolating one experimentally established cause. It also provides no earlier same-week count, so the ending supports conflicted restraint followed by contact rather than a proved breach of a weekly quota.

Miyagi's own WEB state advances beyond E17. Sendai's own state remains at E20; the new observed packet ends at the instant corridor encounter. Sending a message to her does not silently deliver Miyagi's private reflections or certify that she has read it. The two participants therefore leave this episode with asymmetric access even as the usual channel is used again.
