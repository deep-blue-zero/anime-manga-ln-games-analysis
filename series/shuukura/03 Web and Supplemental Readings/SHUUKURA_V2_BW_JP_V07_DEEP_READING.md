## Gifts hold attention without giving complete access

Miyagi begins by denying a deep meaning to the lip product in her bag, then describes a deliberate decision to carry it: leaving a received gift unused at home seems heartless. Courtesy is a real explanation the text supplies, not something the analyst can dismiss simply because attachment is plausible. Yet it also acknowledges a claim made by the giver's act upon her attention. The useful contrast is between deliberate care and the extent of meaning Miyagi will acknowledge. Her claim of coincidence does not make the packing random; concern for a gift does not by itself prove consciously named romance. The novel already records birthday use (LN07p013p54), so this morning is not the product's first application. (O002; p-001p8–13.)

The piercing makes the asymmetry more visible. Miyagi repeatedly notices it and explicitly enjoys seeing a long-held wish attached to Sendai's ears. Sendai, meanwhile, wants Miyagi to look at her while they walk. The final glance is specifically toward the piercing. The desired person, the visible trace and the giver's pleasure overlap without becoming identical. Sendai may welcome the look, but the text does not certify that it satisfies every part of wanting to be seen. Nor does Miyagi's pleasure in a mark establish ownership of Sendai's agency. (O002–003; p21,43,61–62,107–108.)

Sendai's opening evaluation—「いい宮城」 and continuing generosity—also has a viewpoint. The desirable acts are allowing application, carrying the gift and remaining outside for her. Her happiness is represented, but the approval measures behavior against her own wishes. Miyagi has just thought that granting the application should already satisfy Sendai. Each can value the other while prescribing how much satisfaction the other ought to obtain from a particular act. The reader sees those incompatible expectations; the characters do not exchange complete explanations. (O002–003; p37,57–62.)

## Waiting is an action, an intention and an admission

The door sequence makes a simple rule about Miyagi's denials inadequate. She rejects waiting, pulls free of an actual arm grip and leaves. Her ability to move matters: the text does not keep her physically detained. Outside she stops after five steps, returns two steps and looks toward the door while insisting there is no need to wait. When Sendai emerges, she quietly denies waiting. Intent, bodily hesitation and the acknowledgment she gives Sendai do not line up neatly. Attachment is a strong possible explanation, but uncertainty about Sendai's seriousness and the limited station route remain part of Miyagi's own account. (O002; p23–44.)

The scene should not teach the reader that every no means yes. The grip ends because Miyagi breaks away, and the subsequent pause occurs after she has exited. Her agreement to cosmetic application does not require her to remain indefinitely, and today's behavior makes no agreement about tomorrow. Likewise, her slow descent is explicitly careful fall avoidance. It may allow Sendai to catch up, but that consequence does not prove its sole private cause. Her desire to arrive early is stated before the door scene; the text gives no timetable that permits the analyst to dismiss it as wholly invented. (O002–003; p29–53,98–108.)

Miyagi is also perceptive in ways a universal self-deception account would obscure. She correctly identifies Sendai's casual pointing toward possible cat locations and recognizes that the proposal to skip university is unserious. Sendai's narration expressly confirms both judgments. A minimized account of her own waiting therefore coexists with accurate readings of another's behavior. Alternating first person supplies local corrections, rather than ranking one narrator as globally truthful and the other as globally mistaken. (O003; p79–90.)

## A cat search creates a look and a slower route

Mike-chan is a recurring calico on this route, with a previous search reported. The current invitation draws on an actual shared history, but no cat appears. Sendai knows that sightings are mostly on the way home and regards a morning search as unlikely to help. Her disclosed purposes are to get Miyagi's gaze and slow their walk. The first purpose briefly succeeds when Miyagi turns to look at her; that does not make the animal's absence irrelevant or the proposed search its complete stated purpose. An ordinary external object becomes a way of negotiating attention. (O003; p63–83.)

This resembles BW06's farther-checkout wish in a bounded respect: a practical route offers time Sendai would like to extend. The tactics and outcomes differ. Here Miyagi detects the casual pretext, rejects slowing, and continues to press punctuality. Sendai later slows herself; Miyagi looks back instead of leaving her, then Sendai increases her pace slightly. A small mutual adjustment is represented without a spoken settlement of all preferences. Sendai's deliberate self-slowing can also be read as a nudge that makes Miyagi sustain the company, rather than pure accommodation. The narration gives the pace change and response without explicitly naming a test or leverage strategy; her final speed increase and stated nonlateness aim remain counterevidence to an unlimited-delay reading. Later source encounter or publication order alone cannot establish development between the two bonus scenes, since BW06's exact relative fictional date remains unassigned.

Graduation gives Sendai's wish an explicit limit. She says skipping for Miyagi would be easy, then imagines that repeatedly accepting that reason could make absences outnumber attendance and prevent graduation. This is not a report of actual truancy or an attendance record. It states a consequence she refuses to accept. She wants to use the greatest time compatible with Miyagi not being late, rather than declaring that their schedules have no value. Miyagi hears Sendai agree that she will not skip, but not this whole private calculation. A meaningful independent constraint can coexist with intense interest in time together. (O003; p85–95.)

The limit is not perfect coordination. Miyagi says a pace harmless to Sendai may still trouble her, since their universities differ. Tomorrow's joint route is refused, including the revised condition of a nonlate day. Today's companionship cannot be converted into a recurring commitment by treating a later look or smile as retrospective assent. The ending preserves proximity and disagreement together.
