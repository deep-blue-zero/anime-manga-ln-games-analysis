## A holiday desired from another room

Maika begins by describing Miyagi repeatedly staying away from her parental home, then imagines the holiday she would choose if she could stay in her own apartment: books, films and freely timed sleep. She is actually at her parents’ home. The contrast gives her envy concrete, ordinary objects without making parental company a punishment. Her earlier summer enjoyment of coming home can remain true alongside this winter wish for autonomy. A preference emerges in a circumstance rather than becoming a permanent verdict on family life. (O002;02p1–11;earlier KSS08.)

That wish also provides the first measure she applies to Miyagi. The freedom Maika imagines enjoying does not seem to make Miyagi happy: messages about her activities produce unsatisfying answers. The narration initially gives Maika’s appraisal, not a private account from Miyagi. Her concern exposes a limit in comparison. Another person’s apparent opportunity need not feel as desirable to its recipient as it looks to an observer. (O002;02p9–19.)

The choice of a call follows explicit deliberation. Maika considers leaving the matter alone, asking by message and speaking by phone. She expects Miyagi probably will not explain, but thinks conversation can reveal how she is. The purpose therefore need not depend on obtaining a confession. It rests partly on recognizing familiar responses, a form of interpersonal knowledge whose usefulness and limits the remainder tests. (O002;02p17–28.)

## What the call makes available

Miyagi’s「大丈夫」answers the question about whether she has time. The narrow exchange permits this conversation without granting indefinite availability or unrestricted personal inquiry. After five rings and a voice described as neither bright nor dark, Maika asks about Sendai. The report of her absence becomes a quarrel tease, which Miyagi counters with cafe work and「知ってるくせに」. Maika’s subsequent narration explicitly frames her words as teasing. The sequence preserves both her real earlier concern and her pleasure in provoking a recognizable response. It does not establish an actual quarrel or explain the route by which she previously learned about the job. (O002–003;02p28–46.)

The proposed return home then changes the terms of discussion. Miyagi says Sendai is not especially busy and returns at night. Maika translates that expectation into an account of solitude without loneliness; Miyagi answers that she is enjoying being alone. These are different speakers’ formulations. The exchange strongly invites a reader to notice Sendai’s importance, while withholding Miyagi’s full account of that importance. Her claim and Maika’s inference should remain distinct even when the latter feels persuasive. Sendai’s actual return lies beyond this source. (O003;02p47–50.)

Maika presses the activity question through a vague answer to the example of reading. The text supplies Miyagi’s words, Maika’s laugh and an imagined furrowed brow. The「いそう」matters: this is an anticipated expression during a phone call, not a seen face. Voice and hesitation can support recognition of a familiar Miyagi without becoming transparent access to everything she feels. (O003;02p51–59.)
