## Maika's promise gives the supporting character a future of her own

Maika begins with a little envy and supplies its immediate cause: she is at cram school while Shiori seems free to enjoy spring break. Her own position has two commitments. She promised her parents to begin preparation and thinks the exam purpose sensible; she also wants to play, correcting the strength of that wish upward to 「かなり」. Neither side makes the other insincere. The story gives Maika an ordinary future to manage before the central pair becomes a topic. She recalls Miyagi explicitly declining cram school. Her envy concerns a friend's apparent freedom, not a fully informed comparison of their private lives. (O002;16p1–11.)

The opening qualification also matters. Maika's little envy is not proof she has no substantial wishes outside friendship; her wish for play is explicit. Conversely, the admitted wish does not erase her endorsed study purpose. No full family history establishes whether the promise was wholly free of pressure. What is represented is her own recognition of its purpose, current attendance and competing preference. The parents remain unspecified parties, not developed psychological explanations for everything she does.

## A terse message can belong to an active friendship

Miyagi's 「暇」 arrives while Maika is thinking about her. Maika contrasts its brevity with a person who is fairly talkative when they meet, then interprets unceremonious messaging as comfort between friends. That interpretation has a basis in two years of shared classrooms and play. It is still Maika's explanation, not direct access to Miyagi's whole motive. The later movie invitation supplies a separate, concrete fact: Miyagi initiates an ordinary activity with this friend. Both the reported familiarity and actual initiative qualify any all-context picture of Miyagi as socially empty. (O002–003;16p15–29/17p41.)

Maika's academic impression is similarly divided. Miyagi sometimes seems absent-minded and attracts a teacher's warning, yet does homework fairly diligently. The sketch's 「集中！」 is a close friend's admonitory label, not a diagnosis or proof of a cause involving Sendai. ## The outside view admits where its evidence is weak

Honda's report that she saw Ibaraki working with great affability surprises Maika. It differs in kind from the suggestion that Ibaraki would usually be playing and having boys pay.
