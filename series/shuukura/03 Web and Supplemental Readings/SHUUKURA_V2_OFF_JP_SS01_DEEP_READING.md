## A container with a history before Miyagi

Sendai's glance at the container extends the story beyond its present relationship. The piggybank stands on her chest of drawers and predates visits to Miyagi. It was intended for ¥500 coins and described as holding a million yen when filled with that denomination. Miyagi's ¥5,000 notes have entered an arrangement that existed before her. This chronology matters: interpreting the object only as a receptacle for attachment to Miyagi would erase Sendai's earlier practical project and family experience. (O002;L033–039/053–066.)

The million-yen capacity and the arithmetic of two hundred ¥5,000 notes tempt a count, but the narration expressly refuses the necessary premises. Sendai does not remember how many notes she has put inside and says she had no firm goal of saving a million yen. Questions about past and future visits make accumulated time significant without supplying an exact number of visits, notes or yen. Her image of the notes taking a larger face than the coins is marked 「はず」: she imagines the closed contents rather than opening the container and measuring their occupation. The shared graphic 五 at L080 likewise links unlike denominations, not equal monetary value. (O002/O003;L044–055/080.)

The origin story gives saving a material direction. Sendai recalls being outside the family circle and experiencing home as a place of alienation. An out-of-prefecture university followed by staying away seems to her a feasible route out, and money could help make it possible. The account presents her experience and reasoning. It does not supply her parents' inner motives, stage a new family confrontation or establish that admission and departure have already occurred. The object belongs to an anticipated future that she is trying to equip herself to reach. (O002;L057–066.)

Retrospection adds another purpose without simply replacing that practical one. Sendai thinks that what she really wanted to put away may have been feelings that had nowhere to go. Each coin seemed to take dissatisfaction down with it and lighten her heart; 「思う」「たぶん」and「気のせいでしかないかもしれない」retain the uncertainty of this retrospective explanation. ## What becomes troublesome about not seeing

Sendai explains the new banknotes through a search for a suitable place. The piggybank keeps coins and notes visually distinguishable even when they share a receptacle. A bank is an imagined alternative, but it would erase the difference she wants to retain and might invite parental questions about unexplained money. A desk is unsuitable for the amount involved. These ordinary storage considerations give the object practical plausibility alongside its emotional force. (O002;L076–086.)

「出所がわからない」leaves its knower implicit. The surrounding narration identifies Miyagi's notes, so the strongest contextual reading concerns disclosure and accountability if parents question the deposits; it does not establish that Sendai has forgotten who paid her. The source narrates an anticipated problem, not an actual interrogation or a finding about illegal origin. Similarly, calling one portion hers and another Miyagi's preserves a meaningful distinction of source and association, without establishing a legal ownership ruling about earnings already paid. (O003/O005;L080–084.)

The container's opacity originally helped Sendai avoid looking at what she had put away. Now that advantage also generates inconvenience. The discontent associated with coins could not itself be seen; the banknotes have a shape and occupy finite space, so they keep drawing attention despite being hidden. The object ceases to be a simple device for removing a subject from consciousness. Its closedness becomes part of what makes the new contents matter. This change follows Sendai's own comparison between the older feelings and the physically accumulating notes, rather than an analyst's assumption that every hidden object must symbolize repression. (O002;L086–092.)

Her spoken remark that the money is not something to spend and her decision to keep the container closed coexist with an unresolved question about what to do with the ¥5,000. A local inclination is present; a permanent settlement is not. The strongest pragmatic reading emphasizes safe storage, preserving distinguishable sources and avoiding explanations to parents.
