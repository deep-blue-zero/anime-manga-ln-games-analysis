# E0020 — Reciprocity, rejected labels and a finite horizon

The reciprocal act happens, but Sendai does not obtain the equal position she meant to create. Miyagi's ready cooperation first frustrates her, and the act is eventually performed under Miyagi's explicit order. Their dispute about friendship produces no shared alternative definition. Later, a particular approach stops, yet a separate painful act continues until Sendai forcibly ends it. The ordinary dinner arrangement that follows does not resolve those failures. Sendai closes by affirming her need for the place while fearing what a lasting trace would mean for a connection she expects to end.

## An answer that frustrates the intended reciprocity

E19 ended with Sendai's polite arm request and a private wish to put Miyagi in the same position. E20 answers the request with more than a conditional possibility: Miyagi speaks permission, removes her blazer, rolls the sleeve and presents her arm (native1–4). This later outcome belongs to E20. It does not alter the earlier source's unfinished cutoff.

Sendai's disappointment makes the desired process visible. She privately explains that she did not want resistance as such, yet unhesitating permission also fails to supply what she wanted. Pulling Miyagi down and Miyagi voluntarily coming down are different to her. Her sense of having become the one following Miyagi, and her demand that Miyagi feel comparable confusion or anger, belong to her private account (6–12). The mere symmetry of two possible marks is insufficient: Sendai wants a particular relation between initiative, reluctance and response. That is evidence about her present wish, not independent access to Miyagi's complete intention or proof that Sendai has no agency.

She actually withdraws and lowers the sleeve (14–21). Her private judgment that this act is unnecessary between them is not a permanent rule both negotiate. Miyagi then renews the offer after their category dispute (51–55). When Sendai still does not move, Miyagi explicitly names an order and directs her to do what Miyagi did (87–91). Sendai acknowledges understanding, privately distinguishes it from being convinced, and performs the act (100–119). Specific permission, actual withdrawal and eventual commanded performance must all remain in the account. Reducing the sequence to either free mutual reciprocity or total passive obedience would discard represented choices and pressures.

The reciprocal trace is visible. Miyagi's greater-than-expected redness comment is followed by Sendai looking at Miyagi's arm and seeing it (116–119); Sendai then presses the mark she made (123). This is formation, not a removal experiment, proof of equal authority or a clinical duration finding. The earlier shoulder grasp and Sendai raising her face are a particular cessation sequence (114), without a disclosed whole Miyagi interior.

## The category dispute enlarges the information gap

Sendai's claim that this is not something friends do is actually voiced. Her broader private classification rests on visiting after school, spending time together and talking, despite their irregular limits on meetings and contact (24–26,41–43). Miyagi's denial of friendship is equally actual (29). Sendai is hurt by being denied by someone she considered a friend, then qualifies how much this particular connection warrants insisting on that word (56–58). The qualification does not erase either the original classification or the hurt.

Her “now I understand” turn rereads earlier Valentine's, dinner and command behavior through Miyagi's denial (31–37). It is a newly disclosed Sendai explanation of old events, not a fresh Miyagi confession about their causes. The earlier event-as-of evidence stays intact. Asked what they are instead, Miyagi says she does not know (45–49); a negative label and an unknown alternative do not supply a positively agreed romance, a complete absence of attachment or a full reason for the paid frame.

Sendai deliberately chooses a negative description she thinks unambiguous: she is not Miyagi's lover (64–66). The ensuing reputational exchange stays attributed. Miyagi questions the supposed lover-only scope of the act and makes a disparaging personal suggestion, which Sendai corrects (67–77). Her private certainty that the wording is deliberate does not certify Miyagi's whole purpose or Sendai's complete personal history. Likewise, Miyagi's suggestion that having already received a mark defeats Sendai's category objection does not make that inference true. Sendai briefly finds force in it, then rejects the passage from a received act to a generalized kind of person (79–85).

The order subsequently sounds to Sendai as if Miyagi wants proof that they are not friends. Her 踏み絵 comparison belongs to that qualified interpretation (93–98). Miyagi does not speak the proposed proof-purpose herself. The distinction matters: a command is represented directly, while the symbolic reason assigned to it remains another person's reading. Continued offers, dialogue and dinner complicate a simple account of complete distancing, but cannot fill the missing own-focalized motive.

## A recipient's permission has a scope

Miyagi's permission concerns her own arm in the reciprocal act. It does not settle the later proposal involving Sendai at another site. When Miyagi asks about another button and touches it, Sendai remembers the earlier cider-day intervention, refuses and asks what she intends (143–155). Her conditional warning is renewed without a strike being performed.

Miyagi cites limited aversion to making the mark and concealability under Sendai's school clothing. Sendai privately acknowledges the accuracy of the clothing observation while expressly rejecting it as sufficient permission (156–165). The source's active wording about applying the mark must not be converted into consent to receiving a further act. Nor can an accurate observation about what others might see answer the recipient's objection.

The subsequent intervention is expressly unpermitted and is not named an order (168–169). Sendai pushes Miyagi's shoulder and says stop, and Miyagi withdraws readily (175–180). That cessation is real. It is also incomplete as a description of the whole exchange: Miyagi then pinches through the blouse, continues after Sendai's pain cry and arm grasp, invokes trace-making and increases force. Sendai forcibly removes the hand (181–190). A statement that the stop was simply respected would erase the later act; a statement that nothing stopped would erase the earlier withdrawal.

Miyagi calls it a joke and assumes it will not leave a trace. Sendai explicitly rejects the joke defense and privately insists on the pain (192–199). No lasting pinch mark or injury extent is inspected, but the absence of such an outcome does not erase the represented pain or turn Miyagi's prediction into a harmlessness finding. Sendai's angry common-sense metaphors and expectation that further explanation would not reach Miyagi remain attributed judgments (201–204), not a diagnosis or a tested account of every future response. The analysis remains clinical and does not extend the source's sensory description.

## Ordinary company leaves the conflict open

The dinner exchange changes the immediate activity without a represented apology. Miyagi invites; Sendai accepts and privately prefers company to the expected alternative of eating alone after returning home (207–211). That expectation is not a fresh inspection of her household or proof of a permanent family condition. She buttons her blouse, agrees to any food, and Miyagi rises and leaves the room (212–216). The transition is described as if the preceding acts and conversation did not exist. That is Sendai's comparison, not literal forgetting or mutually achieved repair.

Sendai puts on her blazer and cannot see the arm mark (218–219). Clothing hides it; the source does not show it fading. Alone, she murmurs that she should have refused (221), without naming the object of that refusal. The immediate trace context matters, but the grammar does not authorize a categorical choice of dinner, the mark or the whole connection as the utterance's exclusive object. No Miyagi receipt of that private regret is shown.

The ending differentiates two kinds of access. Sendai thinks Miyagi probably needs her (224); she directly affirms her own need for this place (225). She then expresses confidence that theirs is a relation of reciprocal need (226). The increased confidence remains part of Sendai's account. It supplies neither Miyagi's own confession nor a shared mutual-need agreement. Person-directed inferred need and place-directed avowed need are asymmetrical in this WEB wording.

She expects the connection to end around the completion of high-school life and feels a lingering bodily trace could make it perpetual (228). The anticipated end is approximate and unperformed. The perpetual meaning is her present anxiety, not literal permanence, a shared forever promise or proved Miyagi intent. The last paragraphs leave the duration unknown while she heads toward the living room holding her arm (230–232). Dinner has been agreed, not served or eaten; no public discovery, home departure, refund, fading result or settled relationship definition follows within E20.
