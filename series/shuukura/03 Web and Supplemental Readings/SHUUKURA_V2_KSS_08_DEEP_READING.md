## A return that has more than one cost

Maika's summer starts with a practical conversation. Her mother repeats instructions about the refrigerator, rubbish and gas, and Maika understands the risks those instructions address. She gives an assurance before the call ends; locking up is added as a final reminder. The distinction between understanding a task and performing it remains open. Nothing in the later return proves that every check occurred. Repetition may feel constraining, but the source also gives Maika her own reasons for regarding food, rubbish and a possible leak seriously. A hostile or helpless family portrait would discard that counterevidence. (O002;05p1–11.)

Her reluctance has a different object: she wants to see her parents without losing the freedom of living alone. Wanting both makes the choice costly rather than incoherent. Shiori's plan to stay away attracts her envy, while Ami's wish for a reunion makes Shiori's absence a problem she tries to address. Maika therefore occupies several relationships with different priorities. Helping Ami is an actual motive, but it does not exhaust her reasons for thinking about home. (O002;05p13–22,52.)

The message establishes a completed action. Maika types and sends the question, receives Shiori's reply in under five minutes, and then calls. Three rings lead to the familiar voice she expected. These stages matter because an intention to communicate, a drafted message, a sent question and a received answer offer different evidence. The source supplies all four here. Maika's expectation of the reply belongs to her narrated familiarity; it is not a new analyst's predictive test. (O002;05p24–35.)

## Firm refusal beside ordinary care

Maika asks about a short Obon visit despite expecting another refusal. Shiori cites travel and crowds, says she has already explained this to Ami, and adds that she dislikes crowded places. Maika shares that dislike and proposes visiting outside the busy period. The alternative is concrete, but it does not change the answer. Shiori prefers the ease of staying where she is and maintains her plan. Ami's reported wish reaches her without becoming a duty to return. (O002;05p35–44.)

The study exchange complicates the friends' pictures of change. Shiori says she has various things to do, including study; Maika replies that this seems unlikely. Shiori's serious claim that university differs from high school can be a sincere self-description, a convenient deflection, or a mixture. The source does not show the study session that would settle performance. Maika's old image is partial evidence too. Neither her joke nor Shiori's claim should become a complete developmental verdict. (O002;05p45–53.)

Shiori then redirects attention toward Maika's preparations and reminds her about locks and rubbish. Maika recognizes the echo of her mother's call, exaggerates a sigh and laughs. The placement turns ordinary domestic caution into a formal connection between two relationships. It does not make Shiori Maika's parent or give her the mother's whole checklist: the refrigerator and gas belong to the earlier call. Shiori learns about that earlier reminder only when Maika tells her. A firm refusal to join a reunion and practical concern for a departing friend can coexist. (O002;05p55–62.)

## Familiar play changes the value of going home

The second part changes scenes. Maika is now at the familiar mall, where Ami arrives on time and calls her a traitor in an exaggerated performance about a ninja village and the evil organization「ト・カイ」. Both explicitly deny ever having been ninjas, then continue the game. Ami's actual welcome and Maika's「ただいま」sit inside the mock accusation. The joke uses departure to restore familiar rapport rather than establishing that moving to a city was a literal betrayal. Its harmlessness in every possible context is a larger claim the scene cannot prove. (O003;06p1–12.)

The friends feel that a long time has passed despite the short interval since graduation. Ami notices Maika's fashion, and the city joke becomes a way to discuss changes in Shiori too. Maika mentions pierced ears while resisting an image of wholesale transformation; Ami had already heard about the piercing and found it surprising. This is the circulation and interpretation of existing information, not another piercing event or proof that Sendai caused every change in appearance. The friends' older expectations remain part of what makes the changes noticeable. (O003;06p15–28.)

Maika's inward evaluation shifts through the encounter. University is enjoyable, but this particular local conversation feels better, and coming home now seems worthwhile even without the freedom of living alone. The shift is a revaluation of a cost she still recognizes. It does not establish that university friendships are false or that independence has failed. It also gives the earlier return assurance a limited outcome: Maika has returned. The duration of her stay, the parental meeting and the household checks remain unshown. (O003;06p30–32;R156.v2/R157.)

## Information received does not settle belief

Ami explicitly names Sendai as Shiori's roommate, and Maika explains that Shiori has told her. April's kinship-cover uncertainty therefore cannot be carried unchanged into this scene. Yet Ami still says she cannot believe the arrangement and repeatedly asks what the two are like. Receipt of the identification is established; her degree of assent is less secure. The incredulity may express difficulty reconciling the pair with an older school image, or some residual factual doubt. The source does not force a choice between those readings. (O003;06p24,36–42.)

Maika supplies an assessment: they are very close, and seeing them would surprise Ami. The earlier home visit gives her a route to such an assessment, but not access to every private motive. The novel's account of that visit includes Shiori's jealousy, her valuation of Maika's lasting friendship and her interpretation of clothing choices. Those are distinct reader privileges, not thoughts Maika automatically knows while describing the household. An informed outsider remains an outsider to some parts of the relationship. (O003/O005;06p34–54;LN06p01281–205.)

The absent third friend limits the present reunion. Maika imagines how much more fun the old trio would have been, but Shiori has not returned. This is a difference between the desired gathering and the actual one, rather than a breach of a promise Shiori never made. Maika becomes the person who must answer the household question in her absence. The narrative ends as she recalls the visit and continues speaking, without giving the detailed answer. That threshold makes an account possible while withholding its contents from this source's reader. (O003;06p44–54.)

## The comparison that must keep its own boundary

The independently read novel corroborates Shiori's earlier disclosure to Ami and later places Ami with the returning Maika, discussing Sendai before a video call. These circumstances strongly connect the sources, but they do not identify an exact mall minute or prove that the novel contains this supplement's full conversations. The actual video and its aftermath have their own textual witness. They cannot supply an unwritten Maika answer or become the supplement's frozen endpoint. (O005;LN07p008144–183,209.)

Maika's family wishes, independence, advocacy and renewed pleasure in local friendship make this more than a bridge toward the central pair. Ami likewise contributes welcome, performance, perceptions of change and uncertain acceptance of new information. Their perspectives reveal what the household looks like from outside without defining it completely. The story's domestic repetitions and comic reunion make those partial positions intelligible while preserving the costs and limits of returning.
