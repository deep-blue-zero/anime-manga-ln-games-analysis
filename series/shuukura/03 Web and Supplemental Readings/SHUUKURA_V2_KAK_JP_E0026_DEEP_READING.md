## Developed interpretation and competing accounts

The episode makes Sendai's private closeness visible through an ordinary object's circulation, then follows a possibility of initiative that she recognizes and declines. Neither step resolves her relationship category. Her school friendships have their own agency and educational stakes; they are not only a route to Miyagi. The episode's pressure comes from these simultaneous attachments and from the difference between what Sendai knows, what she lets Umina see and what she is willing to examine in herself.

Native1–16 qualify the familiar-refusal comfort at E25's endpoint. Miyagi's limited talk is described as a return to an older normal. Sendai accepts that she cannot simply make Miyagi's mood conform to her own, but does not find the quiet especially enjoyable. Golden Week intervenes, and two days after the break she has not seen Miyagi, even in the corridor; different classes are her explanation. She explicitly denies loneliness and gives concrete reasons: people to talk to, new friends and a reasonably enjoyable school life. The discomfort of silence, her denial of loneliness and later desire for contact are different, contextual claims. None licenses dismissing her genuine social life or diagnosing a lie from apparent tension alone.

The classroom distinguishes three positions rather than a uniform friendship group. Umina considers skipping after forgetting a textbook, is willing to accept a reflection essay and chooses borrowing this time. Mariko, who formerly joined her, now tries to stop her and is described as concerned with school assessment as educational choices approach; the wish for a recommendation remains qualified by らしい. Sendai thinks it may already be late to repair that record but concedes effort is better than nothing. She does not intend to skip and explicitly works to preserve a good image as different from her companions. Her practical preparation and assent to Mariko's later notebook-copying request are real actions, not proof that every helpful act has only an image-management motive. These independent aims complicate both a caricature of bad friends and an idealized account of effortless social harmony.

Umina's returned book changes the emotional register through a visible fold. Sendai privately recognizes Miyagi's textbook because she made the fold herself. During the lesson Sendai returns to the board and actually writes notes, but finds her arm heavy when thinking of Umina using the book. She expressly has no intention of calling it her own.
