## 2. Prior-volume developments

Asumi and Motoya enter V02 with the Ashina case behind them. Morio Ashina's death was ruled suicide. Marina Matsumoto's testimony and the recovered manuscript and will explained why the documents vanished. Kazue Mimura's admission and her implication of Minoru Tomita supplied the narrative account of Shōichi Uehara's killing, with the phone and legal outcome still unresolved. Asumi and Motoya have learned to rely on each other's work despite friction over rank and disclosure. At the bar after the case, the reader heard Motoya's private reaction to Asumi's embrace. Asumi's recollection and intent remain open. [V01 close](KSOA_V01_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v01-h-0013) · [V02 entry](KSOA_V02_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v02-h-0000)

## 3. Current-volume story synopsis

On the Yuigahama shore an unnamed speaker weighs an act that already seems irreversible. The narrative moves to a concert, where off-duty station chief Hideo Takanashi is listening for a cymbal entry in Dvořák when the performance breaks and concertmaster Michito Miura collapses. Takanashi takes charge of the scene and stage manager Aiki calls for help. Investigators determine that Miura was killed deliberately. The timing first directs suspicion toward cymbalist Sadato Yura. The seating and technical checks put a much wider group of people within reach of the stage. [Prologue and concert](KSOA_V02_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v02-h-0001) · [Opening inquiry](KSOA_V02_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v02-h-0003)

Asumi and Motoya walk the hall and test who knew the stage layout and the program. Aiki explains that Keiichi Shiina's piano concerto, the second piece, required the stage extension that displaced Miura's seat during the first. Musicians and staff knew the arrangement from rehearsal. Hall manager Endō's account covers staff roles rather than what everyone did on Friday. The access record supplies an unexplained interval between two better-understood security events. Asumi apologizes to Yura for the earlier questioning. Yura explains that Miura mentored him and translated conductor Yoshihisa Satomi's abstract directions into cues the orchestra could follow. Sōsuke Ishikawa, who handles finances, describes the orchestra's revival under Satomi and the possibility that Miura would succeed him, with Satomi's daughter Ayane taking the concertmaster's chair. [The hall and security record](KSOA_V02_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v02-h-0006) · [Yura and Ishikawa](KSOA_V02_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v02-h-0007) · [Succession](KSOA_V02_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v02-h-0008)

Satomi confirms that he had wanted Miura to succeed him and says the death has ended his own conducting. Ayane handles much of the interview while protecting her father. Shiina, Satomi's former pupil, is distressed to hear that Satomi will never conduct his concerto. Retired conductor Takao Mibu reports the elder Miura's loss of his position abroad and Satomi's subsequent support for young Michito's training. Asumi sees devotion. Motoya wonders why it lasted so long. The two make another appointment with Satomi to find out what he and Ayane have been keeping private. [First Satomi interview](KSOA_V02_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v02-h-0009) · [Shiina and Mibu](KSOA_V02_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v02-h-0010) · [Older history](KSOA_V02_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v02-h-0011)

At the second interview Asumi plays music without warning and draws attention to Satomi's hearing loss. Satomi shows his hearing aid and explains how he used Miura's bow as a signal while conducting. Ayane describes her transcription support and objects to Asumi's test. Motoya corroborates what he saw and joins a qualified undertaking about confidentiality. Ayane says Ishikawa admitted diverting orchestra funds after Satomi confronted him. She later files a complaint about Asumi, and the police separately take up the financial allegation. [Second Satomi interview](KSOA_V02_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v02-h-0012)

When officers arrive with the financial warrant, Ishikawa runs and attacks Asumi. After the arrest, Motoya confirms that Ishikawa used to work at the hall. Ishikawa admits setting up and triggering the fatal device but says Satomi was his target. He recalls going to the shore afterward, giving the anonymous prologue a plausible speaker. The access and purchase evidence support his presence and preparation, while the middle security interval remains unexplained. Nikaidō proceeds on a single-actor theory and the special team dissolves. Asumi keeps following the discrepancy. She calls Motoya back to the hall, reminding him that she once stood up for him against a bully. [Ishikawa's account](KSOA_V02_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v02-h-0013)

At the rescheduled rehearsal Asumi uses a harmless replica to prompt a reaction from Shiina. Shiina admits he saw Ishikawa's preparations the night before and redirected them toward Miura, leaving Ishikawa to trigger the attack. His account explains the middle security interval and why he omitted his usual tuning check. He says Mibu's earlier disclosure of Satomi's part in Miura's father's career disaster had strained the younger Miura's relation with Satomi. Shiina claims he wanted to protect his mentor's reputation. Ayane names a prize Shiina stood to gain. The sequence of the two men's acts is better established than the balance of Shiina's motives. [Rehearsal and second account](KSOA_V02_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v02-h-0014) · [Two-actor resolution](../KSOA_CASE_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md#ksoa-v02-res-0002)

Back at the shore Asumi tells Motoya what it felt like to take Shiina in after hearing the music. When Motoya starts to leave, two patrol volunteers take them for a couple and scold him. He returns to wake her. The novel closes on their awkward care for each other, with the criminal proceedings and the orchestra's future still ahead.
