## 1. Identity, authorization, and source contract

*極楽寺逍遙* is the V03 Japanese novel by Kyōichi Narukami, published by Bungeishunjū under the Bunshun Bunko imprint. The completed KSOA-G1 run read witness `KSOA-JP-V03-W01` from the prologue through epilogue p[0128], ending at [KSOA-V03-H-0013](KSOA_V03_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v03-h-0013). ## 2. Prior-volume developments

The missing Ashina manuscript in V01 and the concert death in V02 both took further inquiry after the police had reached an apparent stopping point. In V02 Ishikawa's admission accounted for preparation and initiation. Shiina's later account explained the change of victim. Asumi and Motoya work effectively together while disagreeing about when the evidence is sufficient and what may be disclosed. V02 also gives Motoya new knowledge of Asumi's eye for color through her family trade and makes the depth of their childhood connection explicit. The shore scene leaves their private intentions unresolved. [V02 closing developments](KSOA_V02_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v02-h-0015) · [V03 entry](KSOA_V03_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v03-h-0000)

## 3. Current-volume story synopsis

A woman remembers leaving a man who mistreated her and finding a short period of refuge with a painter in a secluded valley. As she climbs above the fishing town, her pleasure in that haven is overtaken by guilt. The prologue ends with what she feels and wishes, before it establishes how her story ended. In the present, a dog walker finds art curator Takaharu Wanibuchi dead near a reservoir. Asumi and Motoya look at the paths and sightlines before visiting his family. His wallet and phone are still with him, and calls from a public phone suggest that someone arranged to see him. [Prologue](KSOA_V03_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v03-h-0001) · [Reservoir](KSOA_V03_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v03-h-0002)

Takaharu's son Haruto says he and his father lived in the same house without knowing much of each other's lives. He names people who can place him at Manazuru and says his grandfather Ichiyō pays for his expensive habits. Ichiyō, a painter with a terminal prognosis, plans to donate his own paintings and leave Haruto other substantial property. He has kept one picture by his old friend Sōjirō Yuhara, *A Stroll Through Gokurakuji*. Takaharu wanted to study it and bring attention back to Yuhara's work. Ichiyō identifies the woman in the picture as Mariko Wakao, whom both painters loved, and remembers her death as an accident. [Haruto's account](KSOA_V03_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v03-h-0003) · [Painting and estate plans](KSOA_V03_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v03-h-0004)

At the museum, Takaharu's colleague Maya Hino tells the detectives he suspected something unusual about the brushwork and had Yuhara's daughter Keiko's consent for examination. She says he wanted the work donated. Dealer Kenji Nonaka describes growing interest after a French critic's article and expects a fee from a sale. Keiko confirms her assent and says that her father's reputation matters to her own work. Each encounter adds a different reason to care about the ownership and examination of the same object. The evening briefing still leaves the caller unidentified and the possible sale and inheritance routes open. [The museum](KSOA_V03_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v03-h-0005) · [The dealer](KSOA_V03_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v03-h-0008) · [Keiko](KSOA_V03_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v03-h-0009)

Ichiyō tells them how Mariko fled violence at Yuhara's house and took refuge in Ichiyō's studio. She left a note before the fall at Jōju-in, which was then recorded as an accident. Asumi notices a slight difference in the foliage colors of Yuhara's picture. Ichiyō confirms it and first blames a remixed batch of paint. Asumi then tells him about the proposed examination, despite Maya's request for silence, and suggests the color difference was Takaharu's reason. Ichiyō concludes that Yuhara painted something beneath the hedge. He permits the test and offers to pay. [Ichiyō's account and consent](KSOA_V03_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v03-h-0010)

Police corroborate Haruto's alibi and find footage that links Keiko to the public-phone calls. Haruto and Nonaka acknowledge an arrangement concerning the picture. Maya admits introducing a buyer for a fee. The examination reveals concealed writing that the police read as Yuhara's confession. Keiko reports finding another private message in which Yuhara admitted causing Mariko's death. The two routes lead back to Yuhara, and the precise fatal encounter remains unseen. Keiko withdraws her earlier account and says she phoned Takaharu twice that afternoon from a station call box, asked him to name a time and place after work, and met him at the reservoir plaza to stop a disclosure she feared would damage her mother's name and her own standing. She says she struck him once with a stone and left. The autopsy records three blows, and the stone was found away from the plaza. A restaurant witness contradicts Maya's account of her movements. Asumi claims to have material for DNA comparison, a bluff Motoya recognizes. Maya says she followed Takaharu after overhearing the calls, found him still alive after Keiko left, and inflicted the remaining blows. Her account explains the injuries and where the stone ended up. [The concealed writing](../KSOA_CASE_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md#ksoa-v03-res-0001) · [First admission](KSOA_V03_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v03-h-0011) · [Second admission](KSOA_V03_DEEP_READING.md#ksoa-v03-h-0012)

Maya says she is Yuhara's elder daughter and Keiko's half-sister. She describes an acknowledgment in writing, envy of Keiko, and her hope of making a career under Yuhara's name. The projected sale promised money and a way to keep the painting from examination. Ichiyō apologizes to both women and decides to donate it. Haruto says he will renounce his inheritance. The novel ends without confirmation of Maya's kinship, formal settlement of the estate or a verdict. In the epilogue Asumi tells Motoya how being seen as self-sufficient has left her without care she wanted. When a resident calls an officer about them, Motoya stays after identifying himself and considers getting a taxi.
