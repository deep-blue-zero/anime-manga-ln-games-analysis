# Mystery form and causal attribution

The seven novels repeatedly finish one inquiry while leaving another open. The first official classification can survive a change in explanation, a verified alibi can redirect suspicion toward a different kind of contribution, and an arrest can answer a different question from the one the reader came to solve. The mystery form works through the distance between the events, the team’s acquisition of evidence and the book’s distribution of evidence to the reader. The [terminal reconciliation](../audits/KSOA_SOURCE_COMPLETION_AND_LONGITUDINAL_RECONCILIATION.md#causal-and-institutional-reconciliation) supplies all seven dispositions.

## Three kinds of closure

The corpus distinguishes an account that explains the action, an official action taken on a specified basis and the judgment a witness or a detective makes about a person. V01’s suicide finding and the later attribution of the second death give those layers room to diverge. V02 continues after management has dissolved the special team. V03 and V06 use a move to formal questioning followed by different evidentiary outcomes. V05 names the specific arrest suspicion before a predicted change of charge. V07 is the clearest test. The police act on a concrete extortion exhibit while the narrative invites the reader to recognize the unnamed attacker in Eguchi. The arrest is a closure in one process, the search opens another, and the epilogue resolves why a civilian could not give timely evidence. Each ending can provide literary closure without narrating the court’s judgment. [V01 RES-0003](../KSOA_CASE_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md#ksoa-v01-res-0003) · [V02 RES-0001](../KSOA_CASE_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md#ksoa-v02-res-0001) · [V06 RES-0003](../KSOA_CASE_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md#ksoa-v06-res-0003) · [V07 CL-0004](../KSOA_CLAIM_REVISION_LEDGER.md#ksoa-v07-cl-0004).

A counterreading would describe the series as relying on climactic confessions with incomplete prior evidence. It fits the identity of the V06 witness at the final meeting and the late extortion exhibit in V07. It explains less of the textual work done by V02’s earlier security anomaly, V03’s mismatch between injury count and first admission, and V04’s visible but unexplained wrist detail. The better-supported difference is between the reader’s ability to find a question the first account cannot answer and the ability to assign all roles. The novels repeatedly make the first task shareable. The latter has uneven access and needs volume-specific judgment.
