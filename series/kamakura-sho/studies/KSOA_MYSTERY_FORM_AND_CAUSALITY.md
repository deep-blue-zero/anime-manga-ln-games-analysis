# Mystery form and causal attribution

The seven novels repeatedly finish one inquiry while leaving another open. The first official classification can survive a change in explanation, a verified alibi can redirect suspicion toward a different kind of contribution, and an arrest can answer a different question from the one the reader came to solve. The mystery form works through the distance between the events, the team’s acquisition of evidence and the book’s distribution of evidence to the reader. The [terminal reconciliation](../audits/KSOA_SOURCE_COMPLETION_AND_LONGITUDINAL_RECONCILIATION.md#causal-and-institutional-reconciliation) supplies all seven dispositions.

## Direct action, enabling action and knowledge

V02 and V03 each have two people in a causal chain whose intentions do not amount to a common plan. The order changes what a second role means. Shiina redirects a prior arrangement before the event in V02, while Maya arrives after Keiko’s act in V03 and makes a new decision. V04 adds affirmative assistance. Hachiya’s absence excludes a direct act at the scene within the novel’s account, and the separate transfer of access is admitted. Asumi’s account of the full inducement has more dependencies than the transfer itself. Miyazaki dates his own decision to kill to Akao’s April demand [V04 C03 p0920], while his withheld account of the accomplice who gave him access opens in late March [p0964–p0972], so the instigation’s causal weight stays open. The attractive formulation of a single mastermind would obscure the different timing, evidence and intent of each contribution. [V02 RES-0002](../KSOA_CASE_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md#ksoa-v02-res-0002) · [V03 RES-0002](../KSOA_CASE_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md#ksoa-v03-res-0002) · [V04 RES-0002](../KSOA_CASE_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md#ksoa-v04-res-0002).

V05 supplies the counterexample. Several people have plans for how to expose the professional wrong, while one person admits the central assault. By Asumi’s reconstruction, Hani used the same secret to demand money from Wakao rather than to expose him; the prologue corroborates a demand about Wakao’s past, and Hani admits only the blow [V05 PRO p0022–p0026; C03 p0922, p0947–p0954]. The jealousy and professional disrespect voiced in the prologue cannot be distributed to the other pupils simply because their later stories disclose a history of competition. V06 then gives the clearest example of knowing joint participation. The two workshop colleagues are implicated in both historical and present acts, and the direct actor changes across those events. Yakushiji learns of their act afterward, initially provides cover and finally discloses their account. His knowledge is consequential at both stages. It has a different causal position from arranging the death. V07’s Shiobara was cleared for the critical interval. Her prior work and evidence custody are crucial and come with their own responsibility. Her alibi is fully compatible with the role she later discloses. [V05 CL-0003](../KSOA_CLAIM_REVISION_LEDGER.md#ksoa-v05-cl-0003) · [V06 ACT-0001–ACT-0004](../KSOA_CASE_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md#ksoa-v06-act-0001) · [V07 ACT-0001](../KSOA_CASE_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md#ksoa-v07-act-0001).

These differences are legible in the specific force of negative evidence. The restaurant confirms Yura’s visit while supplying no useful time for it. Haruto’s corroborated location in V03 has real exclusionary force for direct action. A visit at the start and end of an interval in V05 or V06 permits a different conclusion. In V06 multiple apparent corroborators depend on a common unsupported middle: the housekeeper upstairs is out of earshot, as Asumi’s shouting test shows, and the host slept from about eight, after a champagne that Asumi charges Kume with drugging [V06 C03 p0684–p0687, p0711, p0717–p0721]. In V07, staff photographs put an alibied person within a historical relationship, and the epilogue supplies an account that an interested witness could have provided earlier. That late account contradicts Izumida’s own statement that he went home alone to Tokiwa (常盤) [V07 C02 p0403]; if it is true, he concealed the relationship by giving a false location, not merely by withholding an alibi. The report itself is uncorroborated [EPI p0114–p0121]. ## Three kinds of closure

The corpus distinguishes an account that explains the action, an official action taken on a specified basis and the judgment a witness or a detective makes about a person. V01’s suicide finding and the later attribution of the second death give those layers room to diverge. V02 continues after management has dissolved the special team. V03 and V06 use a move to formal questioning followed by different evidentiary outcomes. In V01 and V03 the decisive admission is drawn by a claim the narration calls Asumi’s bluff (`ハッタリ`): a promised police dive for a submerged phone makes Kazue deny the killing and name Tomita as its proposer [V01 C03 p0965–p0977], and an invented skin trace on the weapon makes Maya give up [V03 C03 p0480–p0483]. What closes those scenes is the admission, not the evidence the bluff claimed. V05 names the specific arrest suspicion before a predicted change of charge. V07 is the clearest test. The police act on a concrete extortion exhibit while the narrative invites the reader to recognize the unnamed attacker in Eguchi. The arrest is a closure in one process, the search opens another, and the epilogue resolves why a civilian could not give timely evidence. Each ending can provide literary closure without narrating the court’s judgment. [V01 RES-0003](../KSOA_CASE_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md#ksoa-v01-res-0003) · [V02 RES-0001](../KSOA_CASE_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md#ksoa-v02-res-0001) · [V06 RES-0003](../KSOA_CASE_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md#ksoa-v06-res-0003) · [V07 CL-0004](../KSOA_CLAIM_REVISION_LEDGER.md#ksoa-v07-cl-0004).

A counterreading would describe the series as relying on climactic confessions with incomplete prior evidence. It fits the identity of the V06 witness at the final meeting and the late extortion exhibit in V07. It explains less of the textual work done by V02’s earlier security anomaly, V03’s mismatch between injury count and first admission, and V04’s visible but unexplained wrist detail. The better-supported difference is between the reader’s ability to find a question the first account cannot answer and the ability to assign all roles. The novels repeatedly make the first task shareable. The latter has uneven access and needs volume-specific judgment. Part of that unevenness is structural. Outside the prologues the narration stays close to Motoya, so Asumi’s reasoning reaches the reader only as he infers it, sometimes wrongly [V05 C02 p0843–p0845], or when she states it, usually at the reveal. V04 even cuts away at a scene break as Miyazaki begins his account of the accomplice [V04 C03 p0968–p0974].
