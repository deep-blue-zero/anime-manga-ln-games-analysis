Yes — for **Mei and Shiki**, I would put their relationship very close to romance in emotional structure, while still stopping short of explicit textual romance.

They do not have a formal confession, dating language, or the kind of narrative confirmation that would make them an unambiguous couple. But their bond has many of the ingredients that, in another genre, would read as plainly romantic: mutual exclusivity, embarrassment around affection, “I can only try if you’re with me” emotional dependency, Shiki’s fear of being abandoned, Mei’s intense desire to remain beside her, and the Season 3 “if the light is too bright, I’ll be your shade” framing.

That last idea is especially important. Mei does not merely encourage Shiki to become a center. She offers herself as the condition that makes Shiki’s center possible. She becomes shade, shield, and amplifier. That is a very intimate relational image.

So my answer would be:

> **Mei/Shiki is not canon romance, but it is very strongly romance-compatible. It is one of the closest relationships in** ***Superstar!!*** **to functioning like a romantic pair while remaining inside the franchise’s official language of friendship, partnership, and school-idol mutual support.**

# Mei/Shiki: near-romance through co-dependence and co-permission

Mei and Shiki are not merely friends who happen to join together. Their character problems are written as interlocking halves.

Mei secretly wants school idols but is ashamed of wanting them.
Shiki secretly wants connection but has convinced herself she is fine alone.
Mei hides desire behind hostility.
Shiki hides need behind detachment.

Their relationship solves both problems at once:

> Mei gives Shiki permission to be wanted.
> Shiki gives Mei permission to want.

That is why the pair feels more romance-adjacent than many ordinary “best friend” pairs. The emotional question is not merely “will they both join the club?” It is “can either of them admit what they want unless the other is there?”

That is a highly shippable structure.

It also has a very classic yuri grammar: one girl is visibly flustered and abrasive, the other is deadpan and emotionally indirect; one burns hot, the other runs cool; both are socially awkward; neither can express affection normally; their bond is legible through embarrassment, proximity, and mutual permission rather than through direct romantic declaration.

So, no, the show does not cross the line into confirmed romance. But it walks right up to the line of “this could be read as a love story without changing very much.”

# How *Love Live! Superstar!!* handles yuri overall

*Superstar!!* uses what I would call **romance-adjacent relational architecture**.

The series is not officially a romance. It does not canonize pairings as girlfriends. But it repeatedly builds its most important emotional arcs around intense girl-girl bonds that have the shape, exclusivity, and affective charge of romance while being kept within the safer language of friendship, admiration, rivalry, devotion, and “standing together.”

That is very typical for *Love Live* as a broader franchise, but *Superstar!!* has a slightly sharper flavor because Kanon is such a strong emotional gravitational center and because several pair dynamics are unusually specific.

The series’ own structure makes this easy to ship because Liella! is built from blocked forms of desire: Kanon’s blocked voice, Keke’s distant dream, Sumire’s denied visibility, Chisato’s usefulness-bound attachment, Ren’s inherited grief, Mei’s hidden idol love, Shiki’s loneliness, Natsumi’s failed dreams, Wien’s proof-based singing, and Tomari’s fear of emotional risk. The full-series analysis described Liella! as a group that turns blocked dreams into shared song and eventually into inheritance. That means almost every major relationship is already charged with emotional rescue, recognition, and transformation — exactly the material shippers gravitate toward.

# The strongest yuri-readable relationships

## Mei/Shiki: closest to “mutual private world”

Mei/Shiki is probably one of the most textually yuri-compatible pairs in *Superstar!!* because their bond is so reciprocal and enclosed. They have a private emotional world before they fully enter Liella!. Mei is not simply inspired by Shiki; she chooses Shiki’s world even when school idols are what she actually loves. Shiki is not simply helping Mei; she is terrified Mei is sacrificing herself by staying.

Their romantic readability comes from mutual indispensability.

They feel like two girls who would rather pretend their feelings are practical than admit how much they need each other.

## Keke/Sumire: strongest romantic-comedy energy

Keke/Sumire is the most overtly “anime ship dynamic” pair: bickering, insults, denial, competitive friction, open vulnerability hidden behind hostility, and eventually a very strong emotional dependence.

Their Season 2 material is probably the peak: Sumire is willing to make herself the villain if it might keep Keke in Japan, and Keke’s “I hate you, but I love you” sentiment is basically their entire relationship in one line. It is not canon romance, but it uses a lot of romantic-comedy grammar.

They are also the pair whose future feels most practically entwined. By the finale, Keke and Sumire both continue toward the stage, and Keke’s desire to support or produce Sumire’s future has a very satisfying “we’re not done with each other” quality.

## Kanon/Chisato: deepest life-bond, least flirtatious

Kanon/Chisato is emotionally the deepest early relationship, but it is not the most overtly flirtatious. It is more like a life-bond.

Chisato’s whole early arc is built around Kanon: Kanon protected her as a child, Chisato turned to dance partly because she wanted to become strong enough to stand beside Kanon, and she later becomes mature enough to release Kanon rather than possess her. The Chisato analysis framed her arc as moving from “wanting to become strong enough to support Kanon” to supporting Liella! without needing Kanon at the center.

That is profoundly intimate. But the tone is mature attachment more than romantic tension. Chisato’s love for Kanon is intense, but the story’s emphasis is non-possessive support: she can want Kanon beside her while still protecting Kanon’s need to move elsewhere.

So as a ship, Kanon/Chisato is powerful because it is foundational. But it feels less like “will they confess?” and more like “these two have shaped each other’s lives.”

## Kanon/Keke: founding devotion

Kanon/Keke is the original catalytic pair. Keke hears Kanon’s voice and effectively says: this must not disappear. Kanon then turns Keke’s idol faith into executable song.

This has very strong “first love of the dream” energy. Keke’s adoration of Kanon is not subtle early on. But because Keke is so idol-brained, her attachment can be read both romantically and as devotional fan-belief. She falls in love with Kanon’s voice, with Kanon as the necessary singer for her dream, and later with Liella! as the realized form of that dream.

Romantically, it works best if read as Keke’s original emotional awakening toward Kanon becoming sublimated into Liella! itself.

## Kanon/Kinako: kouhai adoration

Kanon/Kinako is adorable but less romantically loaded than Mei/Shiki or Keke/Sumire. Kinako’s affection is senpai admiration, gratitude, and awe. She sees Kanon as the person who made Liella! reachable and who gives ordinary girls like her permission to try.

That can absolutely become ship fuel, especially because Kinako’s “Kanon-senpai” energy is strong. But textually, it is more about inheritance than romance. Kanon is the senior whose courage Kinako internalizes.

## Kanon/Wien: rival fixation with tsun flavor

Kanon/Wien is one of the funniest and most compelling late-series ships because Wien brings a much sharper emotional register. Her “ふん！” / “hmph!” attitude, contempt, embarrassment, and refusal to admit admiration give her interactions a very strong tsun texture.

But the core of Kanon/Wien is not romantic softness. It is ideological intimacy. Kanon is the person who disproves Wien’s worldview. Wien thinks singing is proof, hierarchy, and elite legitimacy; Kanon shows her singing as warmth, relation, and reaching others. The full-series analysis identifies Wien as the challenge Liella! must answer: whether school idolhood is inferior amateurism or whether it can produce a form of singing elite institutions cannot measure.

That makes Kanon/Wien very shippable in a rival/mentor way. Wien fixates on Kanon because Kanon is both the person who beat her and the person who understands musical failure. It is not as emotionally mutual as Kanon/Chisato or Mei/Shiki, but it has a lot of sharp fan appeal.

# “Keke, Chisato, Kinako, and Margarete all adore Kanon”

This is genuinely entertaining because it is true in four very different registers.

Keke adores **Kanon’s voice**. She hears the dream before Kanon can believe in it.

Chisato adores **Kanon the person**. She remembers Kanon before the wound and loves her across childhood, recovery, and departure.

Kinako adores **Kanon the senpai/founder**. Kanon represents the proof that Liella! can make weak beginnings meaningful.

Wien adores — while loudly denying it — **Kanon the rival/teacher**. Kanon is the singer she cannot dismiss, the school idol who beat her, and the person who teaches her that singing is more than proof.

So Kanon really does end up with a little “idol protagonist yuri gravity well” around her. But it is not harem logic in the usual male-protagonist sense, because Kanon is not collecting affection as validation. Her function is interpretive: she helps each girl name what her blocked dream really means. The Season 3 analysis describes Kanon as someone who becomes a teacher of singing beyond victory, talent, or measurable utility, and creates distance so the group can find centers other than her.

That means the “everyone adores Kanon” joke works because it is emotionally grounded. Kanon really is the girl who changes people’s relationship to their own dreams.

# My overall evaluation

Yuri shipping in *Superstar!!* is highly supported at the level of **subtext, pair architecture, emotional exclusivity, and genre convention**, but not at the level of explicit canon romance.

I would rank the major ship-readability roughly like this:

**Most romance-coded:** Mei/Shiki, Keke/Sumire.
**Deepest emotional life-bond:** Kanon/Chisato.
**Most foundational dream-pair:** Kanon/Keke.
**Best senpai-kouhai adoration:** Kanon/Kinako.
**Sharpest rival/tsun dynamic:** Kanon/Wien.
**Most fandom-comedic Kanon gravity:** the idea that Keke, Chisato, Kinako, and Wien all adore Kanon in mutually incompatible ways.

The important thing is that *Superstar!!* does not need explicit romance to be yuri-rich. Its whole dramatic language is about girls seeing each other’s hidden selves, refusing to let each other disappear, singing because someone else believes in them, and learning that love sometimes means standing together and sometimes means letting someone move forward.

That is already extremely close to the emotional vocabulary of romance.
