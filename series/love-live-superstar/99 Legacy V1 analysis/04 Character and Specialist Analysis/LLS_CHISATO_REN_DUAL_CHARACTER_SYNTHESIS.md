Spoilers for all three seasons of *Love Live! Superstar!!*.

Chisato Arashi and Ren Hazuki are two of the most important non-Kanon characters in *Superstar!!* because they anchor two different kinds of continuity.

**Chisato** is continuity through the body: dance, training, rhythm, practical support, and the ability to stand beside someone without consuming them.

**Ren** is continuity through institution: school, memory, inherited duty, student council, Yuigaoka, and the question of how a dead parent’s dream becomes a living community.

If Kanon is Liella!’s first voice, Keke its first spark, and Sumire its hunger for the spotlight, then Chisato and Ren are the two characters who make Liella! durable. Chisato teaches the group how to move together. Ren gives the group a school, history, and institutional meaning. The broader synthesis we built identified the original five as a formation miracle built from personal wounds: Kanon as blocked voice, Keke as distant dream, Sumire as denied visibility, Chisato as self-worth through usefulness, and Ren as misread inheritance.

# Chisato Arashi

Chisato is easy to underrate because she is quiet in a series full of more immediately expressive personalities. Keke is louder, Sumire is sharper, Ren is more formal, Kanon is more central, Wien is more abrasive, and Natsumi is more theatrical. Chisato’s strength is subtler. She is the character who most consistently understands that emotional support has to become structure.

She is not merely “Kanon’s childhood friend.” That is her starting relationship, but not her full function. Chisato is the one who turns affection into practice schedules, choreography, club presidency, group arrangement, and eventually a philosophy of leadership.

Her central arc is:

> **from wanting to become strong enough to support Kanon**
> **to becoming someone who can support Liella! without needing Kanon at the center.**

That is a major transformation.

## Chisato as a person

Chisato’s surface personality is warm, gentle, playful, and slightly odd in her love of circles. She often seems more emotionally composed than the others. She is soft-spoken but not passive, affectionate but not sentimental, and funny without being noisy.

Her real interior, though, is disciplined and severe. She is one of the most hard-working members of the original cast. Dance is not merely her hobby; it is her answer to childhood weakness.

As a child, Chisato was timid, isolated, and bullied. Kanon defended her, invited her into play, and became the person who made the world feel less frightening. Chisato then turned toward dance partly because she wanted something she could love as deeply as Kanon loved singing, and partly because she wanted to become strong enough to do what Kanon could not.

That means Chisato’s devotion to dance is not just artistic. It is ethical and relational.

Dance says:

> I do not want to remain the protected child.
> I want to become someone who can stand beside the person who protected me.
> I want to support Kanon not as someone rescued, but as someone strong in my own right.

This is the emotional root of her Season 1 arc. She is not simply choosing between dance and school idols. She is deciding whether her strength has to be proven separately before she is allowed to belong.

## The danger inside Chisato’s support

Chisato’s great strength is support, but her early weakness is that she misunderstands support as usefulness.

She thinks she has to win alone, become strong alone, and prove herself alone before she can stand beside Kanon. That is why her dance competition matters. It is not merely a talent checkpoint. It is a self-worth ritual.

Her hidden logic is:

> If I cannot achieve results by myself, I cannot truly help Kanon.
> If I cannot help Kanon, then standing beside her is selfish.

That is very similar to several other *Superstar!!* emotional distortions. Kanon thinks she will drag Keke down if she cannot sing. Sumire thinks being offered the center might be pity. The Season 2 first-years think stepping aside might help Liella! win. The series repeatedly rejects love as disappearance.

Chisato’s correction comes when Kanon tells her that she has already been supporting her. She does not have to become useful before she is allowed to belong. Their relationship was never one-way. Kanon saved Chisato as a child, but Chisato’s effort, presence, and belief also sustained Kanon.

That is the first major Chisato thesis:

> **Support is not a debt that must be repaid through usefulness.**
> **It is a mutual relation that already exists before it can be measured.**

## Chisato as performer

Chisato is Liella!’s bodily center.

Kanon gives voice.
Keke gives dream-language.
Sumire gives showbiz pressure and visibility.
Ren gives musical/institutional memory.
Chisato gives motion.

Her dancing is not ornamental. It solves one of Liella!’s earliest performance problems. Sunny Passion’s critique in Season 1 is that Kanon, Keke, and Sumire are moving through Chisato’s choreography without Chisato herself being part of the group. Their movement has a borrowed center. Chisato joining resolves that contradiction: the group’s body finally matches its membership.

That makes her performer function quite different from Kanon’s. Kanon’s singing is exposed, vulnerable, and expressive. Chisato’s dance is structural, kinetic, and communal. She is not just “good at dancing.” She understands how bodies in space produce group identity.

This becomes more important as Liella! expands.

A five-member group can rely heavily on Kanon’s voice and Chisato’s choreography. A nine-member group requires developmental training. An eleven-member group requires arrangement, spacing, center logic, and the ability to let different members shine without collapsing into chaos. Chisato becomes increasingly important precisely because Liella! becomes harder to move.

Her artistry is therefore choreographic in the broadest sense:

> She arranges possibility.

## Chisato as leader

Chisato is not the same kind of leader as Kanon.

Kanon interprets.
Chisato structures.

Kanon often identifies the emotional blockage: what someone is hiding, misnaming, or afraid to want. Chisato turns emotional direction into training, pacing, and form. That is why it makes sense for Chisato to become school idol club president in Season 2. Kanon remains the emotional meaning-maker, but Chisato becomes the operational center.

Her leadership has three major qualities.

First, she is developmental. She knows when to reduce pressure and when to raise it. In Season 2, she initially adjusts training so the first-years can enter without being crushed. But once they demonstrate seriousness, she raises the standard and asks them to learn the same choreography as the second-years. That is good leadership because it does not confuse kindness with ease.

Second, she is structural. She understands that emotional problems often need formal solutions. Season 3’s same-song contest between Liella! and TomaKanoTe is a good example of the kind of thinking she represents: rivalry needs a structure that lets both sides compete without becoming enemies. The larger analysis of Season 3 emphasizes that Kanon creates distance so Liella! can find centers other than her, while Chisato becomes one of the people who gives that meaning a workable shape.

Third, she is non-possessive. Chisato loves Kanon intensely, but she repeatedly refuses to trap her. In Season 2, she is the one who says Kanon should study abroad. In Season 3, after Kanon physically returns but cannot emotionally return to the old Liella!, Chisato welcomes her home without demanding that she reoccupy the old position.

That is one of Chisato’s most mature qualities:

> She can want Kanon beside her while still protecting Kanon’s need to move elsewhere.

This is a very different emotional texture from “childhood friend who wants to be chosen.” Chisato is not trying to win Kanon back from the world. She is learning to let Kanon’s world expand.

## Chisato and Kanon

Chisato and Kanon are the oldest emotional pair in the series.

Keke is the person who reopens Kanon’s school-idol future. But Chisato is the person who remembers Kanon before the wound. That matters. She knows the girl who once sang freely, believed people could do anything if they tried, and made others happy with her smile. She also knows the precise nature of Kanon’s collapse.

This gives Chisato a special authority. She is not merely encouraging the current Kanon. She is witnessing continuity between the child Kanon, the wounded Kanon, and the recovered Kanon.

Their relationship begins with Kanon protecting Chisato. Then Chisato tries to become strong enough to support Kanon. Then Kanon tells Chisato she has already been supporting her. Then Chisato becomes strong enough to let Kanon go.

That arc is beautifully symmetrical:

> Kanon once reached out to Chisato.
> Chisato learned to stand beside Kanon.
> Then Chisato learned to release Kanon without losing the bond.

By the finale, Chisato no longer needs physical proximity to prove the relationship. She can pass into her own dance future while Kanon goes to Vienna. That is a mature attachment model: closeness that survives distance.

## Chisato and Liella!

Chisato’s role inside Liella! changes over time.

In Season 1, she is the missing bodily center.
In Season 2, she becomes club president and developmental leader.
In Season 3, she becomes one of the guardians of succession.

She is especially important because she prevents Kanon’s centrality from becoming too total. The earlier Kanon analysis noted that Kanon’s mature leadership includes giving structural space to Chisato and making room for other centers. Chisato is the person who can occupy that space without trying to become “the new Kanon.”

That distinction matters. Chisato does not lead by copying Kanon’s interpretive speeches. She leads through embodied attention: who is moving well, who is overstrained, who needs the standard raised, who needs a role redefined, how the formation should function, whether a center has to be obvious or can be quiet like Shiki.

In Season 3, her role in Shiki’s center episode is especially revealing. Shiki cannot become a Kanon-like center or a Sumire-like center. Chisato’s leadership breakthrough is to redefine the center itself: a center can be someone who does not stand out in the conventional way. That is Chisato at her best. She does not force the person to fit the role; she changes the role so the person’s truth can become expressive.

## Chisato’s performance authorship

Chisato’s authorship is choreographic rather than verbal.

Kanon writes and names.
Keke believes and ignites.
Ren preserves and institutionalizes.
Chisato arranges movement.

This makes her easy to under-discuss in text analysis, because her contribution is often spatial and kinetic. But in an idol story, that is not secondary. Idol performance is not just voice. It is line, formation, timing, gesture, presence, and the distribution of attention. Chisato is the one most consistently responsible for making Liella! visible as a collective body.

Her authorial signature is:

> **turning relationships into movement.**

When Liella! is incomplete, the dance shows it.
When Chisato joins, the body aligns.
When the first-years struggle, training reveals the generational gap.
When Shiki becomes center, choreography must make quietness luminous.
When eleven-member Liella! forms, movement must prove that eleven is not just a number but a working body.

That is Chisato’s art.

## Chisato’s completed arc

Chisato begins as the girl who wants to become strong enough to support Kanon.

She becomes the dancer whose presence completes Liella!’s body.

She becomes the club president who supports the entire group.

She becomes the friend mature enough to release Kanon.

She ends as someone with her own dance future, no longer defined only by standing beside Kanon.

Her final meaning is:

> **Support is not self-erasure.**
> **Support becomes love when it lets both people move forward.**

That is Chisato’s deepest contribution to *Superstar!!*.

# Ren Hazuki

Ren is the character who makes Yuigaoka matter.

Without Ren, Yuigaoka would be a school setting. With Ren, it becomes a living inheritance: a dead mother’s dream, a new institution struggling for identity, a school whose meaning has to be recovered, shared, and eventually handed down.

Ren’s arc is less immediately emotional than Kanon’s or Keke’s, but structurally it is one of the most important in the series. The full-series synthesis describes Yuigaoka as one of the central “characters” of *Superstar!!*, and Ren’s arc as the clearest school arc: she begins as a solitary guardian, learns that her mother’s dream was connection rather than hierarchy, shares responsibility with Kanon and the student council, passes the school to Kinako, and graduates knowing Yuigaoka is safe.

That is Ren in one sentence:

> **the girl who mistakes inheritance for solitary duty, then learns to turn it into shared stewardship.**

## Ren as a person

Ren’s surface personality is formal, elegant, disciplined, polite, and severe. She speaks and carries herself like someone older than she is. She seems aristocratic, almost old-fashioned. Early on, this can make her look like a standard strict student-council antagonist: the refined girl who opposes the messy idol club.

But that surface is armor.

Ren is lonely. Her mother is dead. Her father is often absent overseas. Her family home is large but emotionally empty. She is surrounded by the architecture of status without the warmth of ordinary family life. She carries the school not as a student casually proud of her campus, but as the last living custodian of her mother’s unfinished wish.

This is why she initially feels so severe. She is not simply snobbish. She is afraid that if Yuigaoka fails, her mother’s sacrifice becomes meaningless.

Her early hostility toward school idols comes from misread grief. She believes her mother’s school-idol memories may have been erased because they were painful or shameful. So Ren tries to honor her mother by suppressing the very thing her mother actually treasured.

That is a strong tragic structure:

> She betrays the dream by trying to protect it.

## Ren’s core wound: misread inheritance

Ren’s central wound is not performance anxiety, denied visibility, or lack of self-confidence. It is inheritance.

She inherits a school before she understands what the school means.

This is why her Season 1 arc is so important to the series’ philosophy. The hidden diary reveals that her mother’s school-idol memories were not worthless because the old school closed. They were precious precisely because the students connected through them. The old school-idol club failed to save the school, but the experience mattered enough to inspire Yuigaoka.

The full synthesis identifies this as one of the series’ key claims:

> **Failure does not make a memory worthless.**

Ren needs that lesson more than anyone. She has been treating failure as evidence that a path should not be repeated. But her mother’s life says the opposite: a failed form can still carry a living feeling.

That places Ren inside the broader *Superstar!!* pattern: Kanon’s failed audition does not invalidate singing; Natsumi’s failed childhood dreams do not invalidate dreaming; Keke’s graduation does not invalidate performing; and Ren’s mother’s failed school-idol club does not invalidate Yuigaoka.

## Ren as performer

Ren brings classical refinement to Liella!.

Her background in piano, figure skating, formal music, and disciplined movement gives her a different performance aura from the others. Kanon has natural vocal vulnerability and warmth. Chisato has dance-body fluency. Sumire has showbiz flair. Keke has idol faith. Ren has poise, precision, and tradition.

This matters because Liella! is not simply a rebel club against Yuigaoka’s formal musical world. Once Ren joins, the school idol club becomes capable of integrating that formal world. Ren’s presence says:

> School idols are not the enemy of Yuigaoka’s musical inheritance.
> They are one of its truest expressions.

She gives Liella! legitimacy, but not merely in the bureaucratic sense. She brings the aesthetic seriousness of Yuigaoka’s music-course ideal into school-idol form. Her singing and dancing are not the loudest or most emotionally raw, but they are stabilizing. She makes Liella! feel more complete because she connects the group to the school’s classical/institutional side.

In the original five, her performance role is therefore:

> tradition entering the new form.

That is why her joining is so symbolically important. The founder’s daughter, student council president, and original opponent of school idols becomes one of the school idols. The school’s institutional memory joins the dream it had misread.

## Ren as leader

Ren begins as a bad leader for understandable reasons.

She is serious, responsible, competent, and principled, but she tries to carry too much alone. She treats leadership as guarding. Protect the school. Protect prestige. Protect the music course. Protect mother’s dream. Protect Yuigaoka from embarrassment.

That kind of leadership is rigid because it is fear-based. She cannot risk letting others define the school, because the school feels like the last piece of her mother she can still hold.

Her development is learning that leadership is not solitary guardianship. It is shared stewardship.

This happens in stages.

First, she learns the truth of her mother’s school-idol past and joins Liella!.

Second, she becomes student council president in a more open sense, eventually sharing responsibility rather than trying to govern from emotional isolation.

Third, she lets Kanon become vice president, which matters because Kanon is not simply helping with paperwork. Kanon is helping Ren carry Yuigaoka as a living school rather than a memorial.

Fourth, she eventually passes the presidency to Kinako. This is Ren’s true final leadership act.

Choosing Kinako is meaningful because Kinako is not an obvious successor. She lacks Ren’s elegance, social status, and formal authority. But she has the quality Yuigaoka actually needs: gratitude turned outward into care. Kinako knows what it means to be weak and supported, and she wants to build a school where others can feel supported too.

Ren’s leadership ends when she can trust that.

## Ren and Kanon

Ren and Kanon have one of the best ideological relationships in the original group.

Early Ren represents institutional judgment: school prestige, music-course hierarchy, and the suspicion that school idols are frivolous. Kanon represents reopened possibility: the idea that music can begin again in a form the institution does not yet respect.

Kanon does not defeat Ren by arguing “school idols are fun.” She investigates the emotional truth Ren has misread. That is crucial. Ren does not need a debate win. She needs the recovered memory of her mother’s actual feelings.

Kanon translates Ren’s inherited grief into a recovered school memory. The Kanon analysis identifies this as part of Kanon’s authorial signature: she “translates Ren’s inherited grief into a recovered school memory.”

After that, their relationship matures. Kanon gives Ren permission to treat memory as a beginning rather than a burden. Ren gives Kanon a connection to Yuigaoka as an institution, not merely as a place where Liella! practices.

This becomes especially important in Season 2, when Kanon becomes student council vice president. Their bond shifts from rescue to shared stewardship.

Ren carries memory.
Kanon creates beginnings.
Together, they help Yuigaoka become a living school.

## Ren and Yuigaoka

Ren’s relationship to Yuigaoka is the emotional center of her character.

At first, Yuigaoka is almost a shrine. It is her mother’s dream made physical. Because of that, Ren treats it with reverence but also fear. She is afraid of its fragility. The school has a prestige problem, a music/general-course divide, financial concerns, applicant concerns, and no established history. Ren wants to make it worthy of her mother by protecting its dignity.

But the series slowly teaches her that a school does not become meaningful through protection alone.

Yuigaoka becomes meaningful because students build things inside it:

- Liella! forms.
- The school supports their Christmas stage.
- Students gather behind their prelims.
- The road stage turns the school’s location into a symbol of paths crossing.
- The school festival chooses eleven-member Liella!.
- Kinako becomes student council president.
- The new Liella! begins after the third-years graduate.

The synthesis describes Yuigaoka’s arc as moving from fragile new school to inherited dream to shared institution to living future. That is also Ren’s arc.

Ren’s final graduation is powerful because she is the first student to graduate from Yuigaoka. That makes her not only a daughter of the founder, but the first completed proof that the school can carry students into the future.

Her mother’s dream has produced graduates.

That is Ren’s emotional closure.

## Ren as comic character

Ren’s seriousness also makes her comedy work surprisingly well.

The gaming episode is the best example. On paper, “Ren gets addicted to games” sounds like filler. In practice, it exposes a real flaw: Ren believes she must maintain an image of dignity because she is student council president, founder’s daughter, and guardian of Yuigaoka. Something as ordinary as staying up too late playing games becomes shameful because it punctures the identity she thinks she must uphold.

The joke works because the psychology is real.

Ren can confess grief more easily than she can confess ordinary weakness. That is revealing. She is less afraid of being tragic than of being silly.

The multiplayer metaphor resolves it neatly. Ren has been living as though everything is a solo-player challenge. Her friends join the game, help her beat the boss, and turn the embarrassing hobby into a shared space. That is Ren’s whole arc in miniature:

> Stop playing alone.

## Ren as institutional author

Ren’s authorship is institutional.

Kanon writes songs and names meanings.
Chisato choreographs movement.
Keke gives dream-language.
Sumire gives stage hunger.
Ren gives continuity.

She is the character through whom the story asks:

> What does it mean for a school to inherit a dream?
> What does it mean for memory to become institution?
> What does it mean to protect something without freezing it?

Ren’s final handoff to Kinako is therefore as important as Kanon’s lyrical handoff to Kinako. Kanon gives Kinako part of Liella!’s expressive voice. Ren gives Kinako Yuigaoka’s institutional future. Together, those handoffs make Kinako the key inheritor.

That is why Ren’s arc cannot be separated from the series’ final thesis. *Superstar!!* is about beginnings becoming inheritances. Ren is the character who most directly lives the burden and release of inheritance.

## Ren’s completed arc

Ren begins as the solitary guardian of a school she does not fully understand.

She opposes school idols because she thinks they may dishonor her mother’s memory.

She learns that her mother’s school-idol memories were precious, not shameful.

She joins Liella!, turning the school’s hidden inheritance into living performance.

She shares responsibility with Kanon and others.

She becomes ordinary enough to be teased, supported, and helped.

She passes Yuigaoka to Kinako.

She graduates knowing the school is safe.

Her final meaning is:

> **Inheritance is not preservation through control.**
> **It is preservation through trust.**

# Chisato and Ren together

Chisato and Ren are both support characters in the richest sense, but they support different things.

Chisato supports bodies, rhythms, emotional movement, and group form.

Ren supports memory, institution, school identity, and continuity.

Chisato’s danger is defining herself through usefulness to Kanon.

Ren’s danger is defining herself through duty to her mother’s school.

Chisato has to learn:

> I do not need to prove usefulness before I can belong.

Ren has to learn:

> I do not need to guard inheritance alone for it to remain meaningful.

Both arcs are about moving from solitary proof to shared belonging. Chisato thinks she must become strong alone. Ren thinks she must protect Yuigaoka alone. Both are wrong for similar reasons: the dream becomes livable only when it is shared.

Their performance roles mirror this.

Chisato makes Liella! move.
Ren makes Liella! belong to Yuigaoka.

Chisato’s dance turns the group into a body.
Ren’s memory turns the group into a school tradition.

Together, they make Liella! more than Kanon and Keke’s beautiful beginning. They make it sustainable.

# Relative to other idol-character types

Chisato and Ren both look like familiar archetypes at first, then deepen.

Chisato initially resembles the childhood-friend supporter: gentle, loyal, emotionally close to the protagonist, quietly skilled. But *Superstar!!* complicates that by making her support both powerful and dangerous. Her love for Kanon could have trapped her in satellite status, but instead it becomes a path to her own dance future and her own leadership. She is not “the girl who supports the protagonist.” She is the girl who learns what support means when it stops being self-erasure.

Ren initially resembles the strict student-council ojou-sama: elegant, rule-bound, prestige-conscious, antagonist to the idol club. But the story reframes that archetype through grief and institutional inheritance. She is not blocking the club because she hates joy. She is misreading a dead parent’s dream and trying to protect the wrong part of it. Once corrected, she becomes one of the series’ most important custodians of continuity.

In idol terms, Chisato is **craft as care**.

Ren is **memory as responsibility**.

Neither is the central singer. Neither is the original spark. But both are necessary for Liella! to become something more than a temporary emotional miracle.

# Final synthesis

Chisato and Ren are the two characters who most strongly prevent *Love Live! Superstar!!* from being only a story about Kanon’s voice.

Chisato says:

> A song needs a body.
> A group needs movement.
> Support must become structure.
> Love sometimes means standing beside someone, and sometimes means letting her go.

Ren says:

> A song needs memory.
> A school needs continuity.
> Inheritance must become connection, not control.
> A dream is safe only when others can carry it differently.

Chisato turns Liella! into a moving body.

Ren turns Liella! into Yuigaoka’s living inheritance.

And by the end, both have learned the same deeper truth in different forms:

> **You do not preserve what you love by holding it alone.**
> **You preserve it by trusting others to carry it forward.**
