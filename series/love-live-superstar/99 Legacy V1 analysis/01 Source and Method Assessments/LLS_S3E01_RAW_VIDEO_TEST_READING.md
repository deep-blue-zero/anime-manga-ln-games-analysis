## Impression of Season 3 Episode 1

This is a pretty strong season opener, though it relies on a very *Love Live*\-style contrivance: Kanon’s Vienna study-abroad plan is abruptly postponed, and Wien Margarete is ordered to learn about singing from Kanon as a school idol at Yuigaoka. On paper, that is a fairly artificial way to keep Kanon in the story. The episode’s strength is that it does not simply use the cancellation to restore the old status quo.

The real dramatic question becomes:

> Can Kanon return without undoing the meaning of her farewell?

That is the best part of the episode. Everyone already treated the Season 2 ending as a major emotional separation: Liella would continue in Japan, Kanon would grow in Vienna, and both sides would motivate each other from afar. So when Kanon comes back, she feels almost embarrassed by the anticlimax. That is a very human reaction. It would be emotionally cheap for her to just say “I’m back!” and slide into the old center position.

Wien’s role is also stronger than I expected. She is still proud, abrasive, and theatrical, but her refusal to join Liella is not meaningless brattiness. Her point is emotionally coherent: Liella is the group that defeated her. Joining them would get her closer to the practical goal of returning to Vienna, but it would bypass the wound that matters to her. She does not merely need to “win Love Live”; she needs to beat Liella, or at least confront the defeat on her own terms.

That gives the new school idol club setup more legitimacy than it might otherwise have. It is not just “we need a new gimmick for Season 3.” It creates a situation where Kanon’s kindness, Wien’s pride, and Liella’s post-victory identity all come into conflict.

## Kanon’s choice is the key

Kanon choosing **not** to return to Liella immediately is the most interesting decision in the episode.

Her reasoning is not that she no longer loves Liella. It is almost the opposite. She recognizes that Liella has become motivated by her absence. They are trying to grow because she is gone. If she simply returns, everyone risks collapsing back into the old emotional shape: Kanon at the center, everyone orbiting her, and Wien awkwardly appended.

Instead, Kanon frames separation as a way to create growth:

> two groups competing inside Yuigaoka, pushing each other forward, eventually becoming one stronger whole.

That is very Love Live, but with a slightly sharper edge than usual. The franchise normally treats unity as the obvious emotional good. This episode suggests that immediate unity can sometimes be premature. Rivalry can be a form of care if it preserves the other person’s agency.

## Chisato is quietly excellent here

Chisato’s scene with Kanon at night is probably the emotional center of the episode. Keke reacts from attachment: “we get to stay together another year.” Chisato, by contrast, wants Kanon back but does not pressure her into a quick answer. Her “welcome home” lands because it gives Kanon emotional permission without trying to claim her.

That makes Chisato feel more mature than the episode’s surface comedy might suggest. She understands that Kanon’s return is not just happy news; it creates an identity problem for everyone.

## The episode’s main weakness

The biggest weakness is that the premise is undeniably mechanical.

The Vienna school/family condition — “learn from Kanon for a year, win or produce equivalent results, then transfer” — feels like a writerly device. It is functional, but not entirely naturalistic. It exists to create the Season 3 structure.

The episode mostly gets away with it because the character reactions are good. Kanon’s embarrassment, Wien’s wounded pride, Keke’s joy, Chisato’s restraint, Sumire’s irritation, and Natsumi immediately trying to monetize the chaos all feel right.

## Visual/staging impression

The episode uses physical separation well. Kanon hiding from Liella, Wien standing apart with the new club flyer, Kanon and Chisato speaking at night by the water, and the final image of Kanon handing Wien the application form all reinforce the idea that closeness and distance are now being renegotiated.

The school/campus imagery also matters. Yuigaoka finally having all three years of students gives the setting a sense of institutional completion. That fits the story: Liella is no longer a fragile new group at a new school. It has become something established enough that a rival club can form inside its own home.

## Overall

I’d call it a **good, thematically purposeful opener**. It turns what could have been a cheap reversal — “Kanon isn’t leaving after all!” — into a real question about growth after victory.

The episode’s thesis is basically:

> Returning home is not the same thing as going back to how things were.

That is a strong place for Season 3 to begin.
