# Targeted Audio Reacquisition Plan

I would prioritize **dialogue-rich character episodes over the most visually spectacular performances**. The existing bundles already support the series’ broad thematic architecture—voice becoming connection, success remaining reachable, and Liella! becoming an inheritance—and give us strong provisional models of each character’s performance philosophy. What remains least secure is the embodied layer: register, timing, hesitation, code-switching, vocal persona, and how recurring expressions change meaning. Love Live Superstar Understandi…

## Core five

### 1\. Season 3, Episode 2

This is probably the **single highest-value acquisition**.

It places Kanon, Wien, and Tomari together before their interpersonal rhythms have settled, and culminates in TomaKanoTe performing even when victory or measurable benefit is impossible. That makes it ideal for examining three sharply different voices:

-   **Wien:** contempt, embarrassment, pride, reluctant participation, and the exact shades of her recurring 「ふん！」.

-   **Tomari:** analytical and instrumental language—how emotionally cool she actually sounds, and where concern for Natsumi leaks through.

-   **Kanon:** her mature mentoring voice. She is no longer the hesitant beginner, but she also does not teach by lecturing or commanding.


It would also reveal how Kanon changes her conversational approach for two skeptics. Wien distrusts school idols artistically; Tomari distrusts them practically. Kanon has to meet each objection in a different register.

Most importantly, this episode could establish whether Wien’s “tsun” delivery has already softened relative to Season 2, even while the literal vocabulary remains severe.

### 2\. Season 2, Episode 4

This would fill perhaps the largest unresolved **voice-characterization gap** in the original bundle set.

Mei and Shiki’s characterization depends enormously on delivery:

-   Mei’s intimidating roughness versus her helplessly excited school-idol-fan voice.

-   Whether she becomes louder, faster, or more aggressive when embarrassed.

-   Shiki’s apparent flatness and the tiny inflections within it.

-   Whether Shiki sounds measurably warmer, quicker, or less detached when speaking specifically to Mei.

-   Their forms of address and sentence endings.

-   The difference between Shiki’s observational statements and emotionally protective actions.


Their relationship is built around each girl giving the other permission to acknowledge something she denies: Mei’s desire to become an idol and Shiki’s desire not to be alone. Japanese subtitles would help identify how explicitly or indirectly those wants are formulated, while the audio would show how much emotion is hidden beneath their contrasting defenses.

### 3\. Season 3, Episode 8

This is the decisive Natsumi–Tomari episode and therefore essential for understanding two characters whose surfaces are deliberately misleading.

Audio would allow us to distinguish:

-   **OniNatsu**, the monetized performer persona;

-   Natsumi’s ordinary group voice;

-   Natsumi speaking vulnerably as Tomari’s older sister;

-   Tomari’s rational critique as genuine philosophical conviction;

-   Tomari’s rational critique as protective fear;

-   the moment her emotional composure actually breaks.


The subtitles would be particularly useful for the vocabulary of **dreams, failure, value, usefulness, pain, and happiness**. Tomari does not simply learn that dreams are “good.” She learns that suffering and failure do not automatically make a dream worthless. Exact Japanese wording would clarify how completely her philosophy changes and what skepticism she retains.

The episode also contains the movement toward eleven-member Liella!, so it connects intimate sisterhood with the season’s larger thesis: separation has accomplished its work, and reunion can now become a choice.

### 4\. Season 1, Episode 1

This is indispensable as a **baseline recording**.

Later audio becomes much more valuable if we know exactly how Kanon sounded before Liella!:

-   her dry, self-minimizing conversational rhythm;

-   her response to praise;

-   the bodily and vocal symptoms of performance anxiety;

-   how her singing voice differs when she believes nobody is evaluating her;

-   how quickly she retreats when singing becomes socially meaningful;

-   how much firmness already exists when she defends someone else.


It also establishes Keke at maximum initial intensity: her speech speed, breathlessness, school-idol enthusiasm, linguistic quirks, and the way she treats Kanon’s voice as precious before Kanon can do so herself.

Because *Superstar!!* is fundamentally about a failed voice becoming communal and then inheritable, having the protagonist’s actual starting voice is unusually important.

### 5\. Season 1, Episode 11

This should be acquired as a matched counterpart to Episode 1.

Kanon returns to the setting associated with her original failure and sings physically alone while emotionally carrying the others. Audio could show precisely how the series marks that change:

-   breathing and hesitation before singing;

-   whether her speaking voice remains tense even after she decides to perform;

-   how Chisato’s encouragement sounds—gentle reassurance, firm instruction, or something between them;

-   whether Kanon’s solo vocal performance is directed outward toward an audience or inward toward the people she has internalized;

-   how the line-level Japanese connects “alone,” “together,” “voice,” and “singing.”


The Episode 1/Episode 11 pairing would permit a genuine **longitudinal voice analysis** rather than isolated impressions:

> singing when unseen → failing under evaluation → singing while supported → standing alone without becoming emotionally solitary.

## Second-wave acquisitions

### Season 1, Episode 10 — Sumire as center

This is the best episode for Sumire’s public/private vocal split and for Keke–Sumire hostile intimacy.

The key material is not merely that Sumire becomes center. It is that she distrusts the offer because recognition has repeatedly been false, temporary, or humiliating. Audio would reveal the difference between:

-   theatrical ambition;

-   professional show-business analysis;

-   defensive arrogance;

-   genuine fear that she is being selected out of pity;

-   the moment Keke’s trust becomes emotionally credible.


It would also clarify how much Keke’s attacks on Sumire are comic performance, genuine irritation, moral scrutiny, or affection expressed through refusal to flatter her.

### Season 3, Episode 6 — the resolution of the Shanghai arc

Ideally, the whole **Season 3 Episodes 5–6** Shanghai pair should be reacquired. With only one slot, the resolution episode would provide the larger payoff.

This is Keke’s most important mature character material. It shows the difference between:

-   the exuberant school-idol evangelist;

-   the dutiful daughter;

-   the young woman who feels indebted to her family;

-   the performer who has mistakenly concluded that school idols must remain a bounded high-school experience.


Audio would also let us examine Mandarin/Japanese code-switching, family-facing politeness, and whether Keke becomes quieter or less theatrical at home. Kanon returning Keke’s own philosophy to her is one of the series’ strongest relational reversals, while Sumire’s concern reveals how far their antagonistic relationship has matured.

### Season 3, Episode 12 — graduation and inheritance

This episode adds less missing **plot information** than the others, but it would be extremely valuable as a final-state voice archive.

It includes:

-   the third-years speaking about their futures;

-   Ren entrusting Yuigaoka to others;

-   Kinako’s succession voice;

-   Tomari asking for Kanon’s ribbon;

-   Kanon’s graduation-era public voice;

-   the original five returning to the roof but refusing to reclaim it;

-   the final eleven-member performance.


It would let us compare how each character sounds after completing her arc. Does Sumire still project stardom defensively? Does Wien’s severity now sound companionable? Does Tomari retain analytical restraint while speaking sentimentally? Does Kinako sound like a leader without imitating Ren? That is precisely what “inheritance without imitation” should sound like.

## Character-specific specialist additions

Several episodes become particularly useful when targeting one character whose coverage remains thinner.

**For Chisato:** Season 1, Episode 6.
This gives the strongest contrast between her soft social voice, competitive dance discipline, childhood vulnerability, and belief that she must become useful before she can belong.

**For Ren:** Season 1, Episode 8.
Her formal institutional register collides with grief and recovered memory. It would show how much of her elegance is habitual upbringing, how much is student-council performance, and what happens vocally when that armor fails.

**For Natsumi before Tomari:** Season 2, Episode 6.
This is the best origin point for contrasting the polished OniNatsu content voice with the failed dreamer underneath it. It would make Season 3 Episode 8 much more analytically productive.

**For Wien’s development:** Season 2, Episode 11.
Paired with Season 3 Episode 2, this would provide the clearest before-and-after comparison: Wien as defeated solitary proof-seeker versus Wien learning to sing relationally while still resisting emotional exposure.

**For Kanon’s first breakthrough:** Season 1, Episode 3.
Adding this between Episodes 1 and 11 creates a complete vocal triptych: collapse, relational recovery in “Tiny Stars,” and mature internalized support.

## Best acquisition sets by size

### Five episodes

1.  **S1E1**

2.  **S1E11**

3.  **S2E4**

4.  **S3E2**

5.  **S3E8**


This gives the maximum marginal gain: Kanon’s vocal evolution, Mei/Shiki’s speech characterization, Wien and Tomari’s ideological voices, and Natsumi/Tomari’s emotional resolution.

### Eight episodes

Add:

6.  **S1E10**

7.  **S3E6**

8.  **S3E12**


This adds Sumire/Keke, mature Keke, and the complete end-state of the ensemble.

### Most analytically complete ten-episode set

Add:

9.  **S2E6**

10.  **S2E11**


That would provide Natsumi’s persona before her mature sister arc and Wien’s original proof-oriented voice before TomaKanoTe.

The most important principle is to preserve **comparative pairs**, not merely collect isolated climaxes:

-   **S1E1 ↔ S1E11:** Kanon before and after internalizing support.

-   **S2E11 ↔ S3E2:** Wien before and after entering Kanon’s relational model of song.

-   **S2E6 ↔ S3E8:** Natsumi as monetized persona versus Natsumi speaking directly as a sister.

-   **S1E10 ↔ S3E6:** Keke and Sumire’s early hostile trust versus their mature post-graduation concern.


Those pairings would produce more characterization value than reacquiring ten unrelated “best episodes.”
