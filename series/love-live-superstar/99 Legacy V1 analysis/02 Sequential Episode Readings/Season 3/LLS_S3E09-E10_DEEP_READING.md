# S3E09-E10 V1 Deep Reading

Episodes 9–10 are about what happens **after** the eleven-member Liella! finally exists.

Episodes 7–8 resolved the split between Liella! and TomaKanoTe. But reunion by itself is not enough. Episode 9 asks whether Wien can actually become part of Liella! rather than merely stand inside it. Episode 10 asks whether the next generation can inherit not just the group name, but the creative and institutional responsibilities behind it.

So the pair’s core movement is:

> **Episode 9: Wien learns that “joining” means more than sharing the same stage.
> Episode 10: Kinako learns that “inheriting” means more than admiring the people before you.**

This is exactly where Season 3 needed to go after the eleven-member reunion. It cannot simply celebrate the number eleven. It has to show what eleven people require: new formations, new centers, new writers, new successors, and new emotional responsibilities.

# Episode 9: Wien learns Liella!’s grammar

Episode 9 begins with the comedy of eleven members finally practicing together. The visual joke is that Liella! has become huge. The roof, the stretching lines, the pair work — everything now feels crowded. That is important because “eleven-member Liella!” is not only an emotional ideal. It is a practical problem.

A five-member or nine-member group has a certain internal grammar. Eleven members require different spacing, different line distribution, different choreography, different emotional balance. The episode uses Wien’s integration to make that concrete.

Wien does not know how to join casually. She has spent most of her life understanding performance through individual excellence, elite family expectation, and humiliation over failure. So when everyone tries to welcome her, she interprets the warmth as forced friendliness or pity.

Her line about hating people trying to start conversations when there is nothing to say is very revealing. She is not simply antisocial. She is allergic to a certain kind of well-meaning softness because it reminds her of being treated gently after failure.

The others eventually understand this. People in Vienna were probably kind to her after she failed to enter the school she wanted, and that kindness itself became painful. It marked her as someone who had failed and needed consolation. So when Liella! tries too hard to “get along,” Wien hears: “We are managing you.”

That is why Kanon’s first approach does not work.

## Forced closeness versus real belonging

Kanon initially tries to make things easier for Wien by proposing group practice, smaller units, and a schedule that resembles the TomaKanoTe days. That is very Kanon: she wants to create a softer landing. But Wien rejects it because she does not want to be accommodated as a fragile outsider.

This is the key distinction:

> Wien does not need Liella! to become easier for her.
> She needs Liella! to give her a role that respects her seriousness.

The episode’s breakthrough comes when Kanon realizes that Wien should handle the formation and line distribution for the preliminaries. That is a very smart move because it stops treating Wien as a social problem and starts treating her as a contributor.

Wien is not integrated through tea, games, or being called “Marmar.”
She is integrated through responsibility.

That is why the assignment works. It says:

> We trust your eye.
> We need what only you can see.
> You are not a guest. You are part of how this performance will be made.

## Wien’s brutal objectivity

Wien’s first assessment is harsh: Kinako should be placed somewhere she will not stand out, and Natsumi is not good enough to stand next to Tomari. This is exactly the kind of thing Season 2 Liella! would have struggled to hear. But Season 3 Liella! is more mature.

Kinako accepts it. Natsumi accepts it. They ask where they should stand.

That is a huge sign of growth.

In Season 2, the first-years feared that their weakness would disqualify them. In Episode 9, Kinako and Natsumi can hear a blunt assessment without collapsing into self-erasure. They do not interpret Wien’s critique as “you do not belong.” They interpret it as “this is where the group will work best right now, and if we want something else, we need to improve.”

That is a much healthier relation to skill.

Wien’s harshness is also not cruelty. It is her honest performance judgment. She is still socially clumsy, but her analysis is useful. The group needs someone who can see formation objectively, especially now that the seniors’ affection may cloud their judgment.

This gives Wien a real place inside Liella!: she can bring the edge, objectivity, and elite seriousness that the group did not naturally have.

## The second breakthrough: heart matters too

The episode then complicates Wien’s own objectivity. In Kanon’s room, Wien admits that although she can give an outsider’s view, she is not sure she should be the one deciding everything. She says that when it was just the three of them, she came to understand that skill is important, but for school idols, the heart is just as important.

This is perhaps Wien’s clearest growth statement so far.

Season 2 Wien would never have said that. She believed songs were power, and she treated school idols as amateurs. Now she understands that a technically optimal arrangement may not be emotionally optimal. If one person is ahead of the others but their heart is out of sync, the performance fails.

That is Liella!’s philosophy finally entering Wien’s own language.

She has not become soft. She has not stopped caring about skill. But she now understands that school-idol performance is not merely the sum of isolated abilities. It is alignment.

That is why Kanon cries and hugs her. Kanon is not just happy that Wien is being nicer. She is happy because Wien finally understands the thing Kanon has been trying to teach her since the beginning:

> Singing is not only proof of individual excellence.
> It is the expression of hearts moving together.

## Kinako and Natsumi ask to be pushed

The late-night practice scene with Kinako and Natsumi is another crucial inheritance moment. They come to Wien and ask her to tell them what they can do to improve. Kinako says that not being able to contribute to the prelims performance is so frustrating that it keeps her up at night. She wants to stand with everyone as an equal and know she is the best she can be.

This is the Season 2 first-year anxiety transformed into mature effort.

Earlier, the first-years feared they would drag Liella! down.
Now, they ask for specific help so they can contribute more fully.

The difference is agency. They are not offering to disappear. They are asking to be trained.

Wien’s role becomes meaningful precisely because she can meet that desire. She is younger, but her seriousness lets the older girls ask for help without worrying about hierarchy. Kinako and Natsumi humble themselves before a first-year because the performance matters more than pride.

That is one of the best signs that Liella! has become eleven in more than name.

## Wien’s pre-stage speech

Wien’s speech before the prelims is the emotional payoff of Episode 9. She reframes the Love Live not as the third-years’ last alone, but as the first and last Love Live where all eleven can sing together. That is a major shift.

Earlier, Wien was indifferent to Love Live as a competition. She saw it as a tool or a lesser stage. Now she understands this specific Love Live as irreplaceable because it is the only one where this exact eleven-member configuration can exist.

That is the school-idol time limit finally reaching her.

She also thanks Kinako and Natsumi for teaching her how important it is to face the future hand-in-hand. That is not decorative. It shows that Wien has learned from the very members she initially evaluated as technically weaker.

Her final desire is beautifully specific:

> She wants everyone to feel that Liella! is better now that she and Tomari joined.

That is the true integration point. Wien no longer only wants to prove herself. She wants her presence to make Liella! better.

# Episode 10: Kinako becomes inheritance

Episode 10 turns the focus to Kinako, and this is exactly right. Kinako was Season 2’s first new member, the ordinary rural girl who entered Liella! feeling unqualified. By Season 3 Episode 10, she becomes the person asked to inherit two enormous responsibilities:

1.  writing the lyrics for the Tokyo regionals

2.  becoming the next student council president


That pairing is very deliberate.

Kanon passes her part of Liella!’s creative voice.
Ren passes her part of Yuigaoka’s institutional future.

Kinako becomes the point where Liella!’s song and Yuigaoka’s school identity meet.

## Kinako as lyricist

Kanon asking Kinako to write the lyrics is a major handoff. Kanon has always been Liella!’s primary interpreter — the girl who turns feelings into song. Asking Kinako to write is not just a practical delegation. It is Kanon saying:

> Your generation has to start giving Liella! its words.

Kinako panics because she understands the stakes. This is the Tokyo regionals. This is not a casual practice song. She worries that because everyone is trusting her, she cannot write anything negative. She wants lyrics that make people smile, so she thinks her confusion and insecurity are unfit material.

That is a very Kinako problem. She is sincere, grateful, and self-effacing. She does not want to burden the song with her weakness.

But Ren gives her the key: if those confused feelings are what she has, then those feelings can become lyrics.

This is one of the episode’s strongest ideas:

> Lyrics do not have to begin from confidence.
> They can begin from the honest shape of uncertainty.

That is also why Kinako is such a good successor to Kanon. Kanon’s songs have always come from vulnerable truth. Kinako’s lyrics must do the same, but in her own voice.

## Ren and Kinako as mirrors

Ren saying that Kinako is similar to her is funny at first, but it becomes increasingly convincing. Both are sincere, awkward, stubborn, and more self-doubting than they appear. Ren hides behind elegance and duty. Kinako hides behind humility and panic.

Their shared problem is inheritance.

Ren has to hand over the school her mother founded.
Kinako has to accept that someone like her can carry part of that school.

Ren is afraid no one can succeed her because Yuigaoka still feels fragile and personal. Kinako is afraid she cannot accept because she feels weak, ordinary, and not leader-like. These fears fit together perfectly.

Ren’s breakthrough comes when she watches Kinako’s words and feelings pour out while Kinako draws and sings. Kinako is not performing leadership in a formal way. She is simply expressing how much everyone has given her. But that is exactly what Ren recognizes as the heart of Yuigaoka.

Yuigaoka was not founded to create flawless elites. It was founded to connect people through music, courage, and shared feeling. Kinako embodies that.

So Ren chooses her not because Kinako is impressive in the conventional sense, but because Kinako is the kind of person who can receive the school’s kindness and return it outward.

## “I’m not alone” returns

The most important line in Episode 10 is Kinako’s realization:

> “I’m not alone.”

That phrase has enormous weight in *Superstar!!*.

For Kanon in Season 1, “I’m not alone” was the truth that let her sing. It transformed the stage from a place of judgment into a place of support.

For Kinako in Season 3, “I’m not alone” becomes the truth that lets her write, lead, and stand as center. She does not become capable because she suddenly stops being clumsy or anxious. She becomes capable because she realizes that capability is not solitary.

This is the deepest inheritance in the episode.

Kinako inherits Kanon’s original lesson, but applies it to different responsibilities:

-   Kanon: I can sing because I am not alone.

-   Kinako: I can write, lead, and support others because I am not alone.


That is a beautiful generational progression.

## Wien’s encouragement

Wien’s conversation with Kinako is also very important. Kinako suggests that Wien should become student council president because Wien is strong. Wien rejects this, saying she will return to Vienna next year. But then she tells Kinako that Kinako is the kind of person she would want to listen to.

Her reasoning is excellent: Kinako, despite being older, asked a younger person directly for help. She did not let pride stop her from wanting to improve. That made Wien want to help her.

This is another major step for Wien. Earlier, she valued superiority. Now she recognizes dignity in humility. She admires Kinako not because Kinako is strong in the obvious sense, but because she can ask for support without disguising her weakness.

That is very different from Wien’s old worldview.

Wien also tells her:

> If you cannot do something, work hard every day until you can.

That line sounds simple, but coming from Wien it has changed meaning. In the past, it might have sounded like elite severity. Here it becomes encouragement. She is telling Kinako that inability is not final.

Wien has absorbed Liella!’s ethic without losing her own bluntness.

## Kinako chooses the presidency

Kinako’s decision to run for student council president is the episode’s emotional culmination. She does not suddenly become confident. In fact, her speech begins by listing all the ways she is weak: clumsy, bad at sports, not senior-like, panicky, unreliable.

But this time, she does not let those flaws become disqualifications.

She says she could write her lyrics because everyone supported her. She remembered how important Liella! and the people cheering them on at school are. Because everyone helped her learn to smile and have courage, she now wants to become someone who supports others.

That is the exact transformation Yuigaoka needs.

Kinako is not trying to become Ren. She is not trying to become Kanon. She is becoming the kind of president only Kinako can be: someone who knows what it means to be weak and supported, and therefore wants to build a school where others can feel supported too.

Her phrase — that she will make sure these binding feelings live on — is the whole succession theme of Season 3 in one line.

Inheritance is not copying the person before you.
Inheritance is preserving the feeling they gave you in your own form.

# “Smile Promise” and Kinako as center

The Tokyo regional performance makes Kinako center, and that is the correct final step.

The song’s lyrics are almost transparently Kinako’s interior:

> all the things I could not do alone
> knowing everyone is counting on me, I will do my best
> even little me can give birth to enormous power
> we will connect everyone with your sparkling smile
> I will tell you how much I love you and how happy I feel without hesitation
> step onto our next stage with a smile

This is Kinako’s whole journey condensed into song.

She began as the girl who thought Liella! was too amazing for someone like her. Now she stands at the center of an eleven-member Liella!, carrying lyrics about weakness, gratitude, support, and courage. She does not become center because she is the flashiest. She becomes center because she can express what the new Liella! is becoming:

> a group whose future is built by people who once thought they were too small to carry anything.

The title **“Smile Promise”** is also very fitting. Kinako’s promise is not to be perfect. It is to carry forward the smiles and courage she received.

That is why the song leads naturally into both passing the Tokyo regionals and naming Kinako as the future student council president. The performance and the presidency are two expressions of the same promise.

# Kanon Shibuya: updated through Season 3 Episode 10

Kanon’s role in these episodes is increasingly that of someone who enables succession.

In Episode 9, she brings Wien into Liella! not by forcing friendship, but by giving her meaningful responsibility. In Episode 10, she asks Kinako to write the lyrics, effectively handing over part of Liella!’s expressive center.

This is one of Kanon’s most mature developments.

Season 1 Kanon needed Keke to help her sing.
Season 2 Kanon helped others enter and protect Liella!’s wholeness.
Season 3 Kanon is teaching Liella! to keep generating meaning without relying on her as the sole interpreter.

She is still central, but she is no longer hoarding centrality. She is distributing it.

That matters because graduation is now unavoidable. Kanon’s future plans are still not fully announced to everyone, but the episode reminds us that she will leave. If Liella! depends on Kanon to write, interpret, center, and lead everything, it cannot survive her graduation. By entrusting Kinako, Kanon actively prevents that.

# Ren Hazuki: updated through Season 3 Episode 10

Ren’s arc reaches a quiet but beautiful payoff.

Season 1 Ren tried to guard Yuigaoka alone because she misunderstood her mother’s school-idol past. Season 2 Ren learned to share responsibility with Kanon, the student council, and the school. Season 3 Ren finally has to let go of formal control.

This is harder than it sounds. Yuigaoka is not just a school to her. It is her mother’s dream, her family’s grief, and the place where she learned to stop being alone. Handing it to someone else means trusting that the dream can live beyond her direct stewardship.

Choosing Kinako is perfect because Kinako is not an obvious successor. She does not have Ren’s elegance, status, grades, or institutional aura. But she has the emotional qualities Yuigaoka needs: gratitude, humility, sincerity, stubbornness, and the desire to return courage to others.

Ren recognizes that.

Her handoff is therefore not administrative. It is spiritual. She is entrusting Yuigaoka’s heart.

# Kinako Sakurakoji: completed current profile

Kinako’s growth from Season 2 to now is one of the clearest examples of *Superstar!!*’s generational writing.

She began as the ordinary outsider: rural, overwhelmed, physically weak, and intimidated by Liella!’s brilliance. Her initial function was to prove that Liella! could remain reachable after becoming admired.

Now she becomes the person who carries that reachability into leadership.

Her core strength is not talent in the conventional sense. It is **responsive sincerity**. She receives support deeply, remembers it, and wants to return it. That makes her exactly the kind of person who can lead a school built around connection.

Kinako’s central belief after Episode 10 is:

> Because everyone helped me become someone who can smile, I want to become someone who helps others smile.

That is simple, but it is the right foundation for both her lyrics and her presidency.

She is not Kanon’s replacement. She is Kanon’s lesson living in another person.

# Wien Margarete: updated through Season 3 Episode 10

Wien’s development in Episodes 9–10 is excellent because it does not soften her into generic friendliness. She remains blunt, proud, awkward, and impatient with forced intimacy. But she is now capable of understanding school idols from inside.

The key transformations:

-   She recognizes that heart matters as much as skill.

-   She wants Liella! to be better because she and Tomari joined.

-   She can encourage Kinako without condescension.

-   She sees humility as a strength.

-   She accepts that Love Live matters because this exact eleven-member group is temporary and irreplaceable.


That is a complete reversal of her Season 2 stance, but it feels earned because her surface personality remains intact.

Wien has not stopped being Wien. She has simply learned that her seriousness can serve a shared heart rather than isolate her above others.

# Natsumi and Tomari

Natsumi’s role in Episode 9 is small but meaningful. Being called out by Wien as not good enough to stand next to Tomari could have triggered insecurity, but instead she accepts the challenge. That shows how much she has matured since the “money and content” days. She now wants to improve as part of Liella!’s future.

Tomari, meanwhile, is less emotionally central here than in Episodes 7–8, but her presence continues to matter because Wien’s speech explicitly includes her: “now that Tomari and I have joined.” Tomari is no longer merely Natsumi’s observer or critic. She is part of the condition by which Liella! becomes better.

# Chisato and leadership structure

Chisato’s leadership continues to work in the background. She recognizes the need for different practice groups and formation planning, but she is willing to let others take charge of specific pieces. That is important because eleven-member Liella! cannot be run through one center.

The new Liella! requires distributed authority:

-   Kanon entrusts lyrics to Kinako.

-   Chisato manages the broad training structure.

-   Wien analyzes formation.

-   Ren passes institutional leadership to Kinako.

-   Kinako becomes lyrical and student-council center.

-   Natsumi and Tomari embody the future second-year core.

-   Mei and Shiki continue supporting the younger-generation creative/emotional work.


This is what an eleven-member group needs: not one charismatic center, but a network of centers.

# Season 3 thesis after Episodes 9–10

Episodes 7–8 proved that separation was necessary so reunion could become a choice. Episodes 9–10 prove that reunion is only the beginning.

The refined Season 3 thesis is now:

> **A group survives graduation not by preserving its original center, but by teaching new people how to carry the group’s feelings in their own form.**

Episode 9 does this with Wien: she stops being an outsider/rival and becomes someone whose objectivity and seriousness help eleven-member Liella! perform better.

Episode 10 does this with Kinako: she stops being merely the ordinary kouhai and becomes the person entrusted with both Liella!’s words and Yuigaoka’s future.

That is a major evolution. Season 3 is no longer asking whether Kanon can return to Liella!. She has returned. The real question is now:

> When Kanon, Keke, Chisato, Sumire, and Ren graduate, what will remain?

Episode 10 gives the first real answer:

> What remains is not a copy of the founders.
> What remains is the courage they gave to people like Kinako, now turned outward as song, leadership, and promise.
