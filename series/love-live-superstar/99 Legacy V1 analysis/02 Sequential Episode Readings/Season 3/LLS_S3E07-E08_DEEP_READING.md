# S3E07-E08 V1 Deep Reading

Episodes 7–8 are the payoff to the Season 3 split-club structure. They show that Kanon’s separation from Liella! was not meant to become permanent antagonism. It was a **developmental detour**: a way to let Wien and Tomari settle their unresolved feelings, let Liella! grow without simply absorbing everyone, and let the younger generation begin taking responsibility.

The key movement is:

> Episode 7 turns “enemy” into “true rival.”
> Episode 8 turns “true rival” into “we can now reunite as eleven.”

So these episodes function almost like the end of Season 3’s first major arc. The point was never that Kanon should leave Liella! forever. The point was that Liella! could not become eleven until the three outside members had reasons of their own to join.

# Episode 7: the purpose of rivalry

Episode 7 begins right after the Shanghai performance. The most important visual and behavioral detail is Wien secretly practicing the Shanghai choreography. She denies it, of course, but the meaning is obvious: the performance moved her. She has not intellectually accepted Liella!’s school-idol ethic yet, but her body is already repeating it.

That is a very good way to write Wien. She is too proud to say “I enjoyed that” or “I want to be part of that,” but she cannot stop responding to the experience. Her body betrays what her pride refuses.

Then Love Live season begins again, and the structural problem becomes unavoidable: Yuigaoka has two school idol groups, but only one group can enter. The headmistress therefore proposes a school festival contest to decide the representative.

At first, Liella!’s instinct is simple and emotionally correct: they want all eleven to perform together. The Shanghai performance proved that eleven-member Liella! is possible and powerful. But Tomari and Wien cannot accept that yet. Their reasons matter.

Tomari’s goal is still to test whether Natsumi’s dream is real and sustainable. She does not consider Natsumi her enemy, but she cannot simply merge into Liella! because the unresolved question is precisely whether Natsumi can keep chasing this dream without being hurt again.

Wien’s goal is still to defeat Liella! and win recognition from her family. For her, joining Liella! too quickly would feel like surrendering the very wound that brought her here. She needs to face Liella! before she can stand with them.

That is why Episode 7’s answer is not reunion yet. It is honest rivalry.

## Natsumi cannot reach Tomari alone

Natsumi trying to invite Tomari and Wien is both funny and revealing. She has become more sincere, but she still struggles to speak plainly to Tomari. The sister relationship is close, but precisely because it is close, it is tangled. Natsumi’s old failures, Tomari’s protectiveness, and their mutual awkwardness make direct communication difficult.

This is why Natsumi’s grand speech about chasing the brightest light mostly falls flat. She is trying to speak in school-idol rhetoric, but Tomari needs something more basic: emotional honesty from her sister, and proof that Natsumi’s dream is not half-hearted.

The scene also shows that Tomari is not unreachable. She is listening. She just refuses sentimental shortcuts.

## Kanon’s sleepover strategy

Kanon’s solution is very Kanon: not a debate, not a formal negotiation, but a sleepover.

This could look frivolous, but it fits her philosophy perfectly. She says the reason their Shanghai performance was so strong was that they came together as one, and that simply talking outside school is therefore part of making a better performance.

That is one of the most explicit statements of *Superstar!!*’s performance theory:

> A good song is not created only through practice.
> It is created through knowing the people you are singing with.

The sleepover reveals ordinary details: Wien’s manga panic, Tomari’s love of sweets, her jellyfish interest, her self-discipline, Wien’s first takoyaki party, the awkward domesticity of Kanon’s house. These details matter because Wien and Tomari have mostly presented themselves through hardened positions: elite ambition, rational analysis, rivalry, refusal. Kanon pulls them into ordinary life.

She is doing for them what Yuigaoka did for Ren and what Liella! did for Natsumi: making room for the person underneath the stance.

## Kanon’s diagnosis: their feelings are wavering

The nighttime walk to the site of the previous regional performance is the episode’s emotional center. Kanon points out that Shanghai moved her deeply, but that Wien and Tomari’s feelings are still wavering.

That is exactly right.

Tomari does not hate Liella! and does not think of Natsumi as an enemy. She is afraid Natsumi will be hurt again. Her opposition comes from love, not contempt.

Wien does want to defeat Liella!, but that desire is no longer simply scorn. It has become serious rivalry. She has seen what Yuigaoka’s school idols can do together. Now she wants to face them fully.

Kanon’s solution is to let those feelings become action:

> If the feelings are real, compete with them honestly.

That is the difference between “enemy” and “rival.” An enemy blocks or destroys. A rival helps you discover how serious you really are.

## TomaKanoTe becomes real

By the end of the episode, Kanon, Wien, and Tomari decide to compete directly against Liella!. This is not regression. It is actually the first time TomaKanoTe feels legitimate as its own unit.

Before this, the group existed partly because Kanon needed distance from Liella!, Wien needed a path back to Vienna, and Tomari wanted to inspect school idols. After Episode 7, all three can say they have a shared immediate purpose:

> We will face Liella! head-on.

Kanon’s role here is important. Wien and Tomari initially assumed she created the group mostly for Liella!’s sake — to push Liella! to grow. But they begin to realize she also cares about them. That realization is what lets the group become emotionally real.

Kanon is not using Wien and Tomari as tools for Liella!’s development. She is trying to give them a stage where their own unsettled feelings can become song.

# Episode 8: Yuigaoka versus Yuigaoka

Episode 8 takes the rivalry and immediately complicates it. The student body does not want a crude popularity vote. They want the two groups to compete on equal footing, as Yuigaoka’s school idols. That is a crucial phrase.

The students recognize what the characters have been slowly learning:

> Liella! and TomaKanoTe are different groups, but they are not different worlds.

So the solution is elegant: both groups will perform the same new song, divided between them, and the school festival audience will decide. This preserves rivalry while preventing the rivalry from becoming alienation.

It also forces cooperation. They cannot just compete by doing unrelated performances. They must create a shared musical object.

That is the perfect test.

## Kanon and Chisato hand responsibility to the juniors

The strongest structural decision in Episode 8 is that the song will be written by four younger members, two from each group. Kanon wants juniors in charge because the third-years will graduate. That is not incidental. It is the season’s inheritance theme made concrete.

The task is not given to Kanon, even though she is the obvious songwriter. It is not given to the seniors. It is given to the people who must carry Yuigaoka’s school-idol future.

This is exactly where Season 3 has been going:

-   Shiki learned she could be a center who does not stand out.

-   Natsumi declared she wants to become a future center of Liella!.

-   Tomari is still testing whether that dream is real.

-   Mei, Shiki, Kinako, and Natsumi need to become more than kouhai.

-   Wien and Tomari need to contribute rather than remain oppositional satellites.


So the songwriting assignment is a succession ritual. The adults do not announce it that way, but that is what it is.

## The failed first meeting

The first meeting fails because each person still carries a different definition of what the song should be.

Wien has a song she wrote already, presumably intense, cool, and victory-driven. Mei objects that it does not feel like a school-idol song. That is a very Mei response: beneath her roughness, she has deep school-idol faith. She knows the song must overflow with love for school idols.

Wien rejects that as narrow. She wants something new, something different, something that does not simply fit Liella!’s inherited style.

Tomari proposes a rational split: each side writes separately, then the leaders decide. This is efficient, but it defeats the purpose. It would preserve quality control while avoiding emotional negotiation.

Mei is the one who states the real problem:

> The point is to make a song both groups can be satisfied with.

That line is important because it shows Mei’s growth. She is no longer merely the secret school-idol fan or Shiki’s protector. She understands the ethical meaning of the task. The song must not only be good. It must be shared.

## Natsumi and Tomari must reconcile before the song can exist

Shiki diagnoses the next problem: unless Natsumi and Tomari make up, no good song can be written.

This is very perceptive. The songwriting problem is not primarily musical. It is relational. Tomari’s resistance to Natsumi’s dream is still unresolved. Natsumi’s attempts to push through it with positivity are not enough. The song cannot become “No Rain, No Rainbow” unless the sisters can honestly face the rain between them.

So the second-years go to the Onitsuka home. This is a major growth moment for them. They do not call Kanon to solve it. In fact, they explicitly recognize that relying on Kanon would be the old pattern.

This matters enormously.

Kanon’s separation is working because the younger members are starting to do Kanon-like work themselves: reading emotional blockages, gathering people, and creating the conditions for a song to exist.

## Natsumi’s confession to Tomari

Natsumi’s confession is one of her best scenes.

She first tries to smile because Mei and Shiki remind her that Tomari loves her smile. But the real breakthrough comes when Natsumi stops trying to simply cheer Tomari up and instead says what Tomari needs to hear:

> I will accept everything about you.
> Let me into your heart.
> You can share anything with me.
> I want to keep talking to you forever.
> Because I love you.

That is a huge step for Natsumi. Her old “OniNatsu” persona converted everything into performance, money, and audience response. Here, she strips that away and speaks with direct sisterly love.

Tomari finally breaks because her coldness has always been protective loneliness. She was worried. It was hard for her too. She was not simply judging Natsumi from above; she was carrying fear without knowing how to share it.

This scene also deepens Tomari. Her problem was never that she lacked feeling. It was that she mistrusted the forms feeling took: dreams, smiles, school idols, risk. When Natsumi tells her that every piece of who she is can be accepted, Tomari receives what she had been unable to ask for.

## “No Rain, No Rainbow”

The phrase “No Rain, No Rainbow” becomes the conceptual bridge between the sisters and the song.

The important part is that the episode does not reject Tomari’s fear of pain. It validates the pain and then gives it a different meaning. Rain is not merely suffering to be avoided. It nourishes the earth and gives strength. The rainbow does not erase the rain; it exists because of it.

That is the proper answer to Tomari’s anti-dream position.

Tomari believed dreams are dangerous because they create tears. The episode’s answer is not “dreams never hurt.” It is:

> The hurt is part of the growth that makes the smile real.

This is also a direct continuation of Natsumi’s arc. Money gave her a safer smile. School idols gave her a vulnerable one. “No Rain, No Rainbow” names why the vulnerable one matters more.

# The school festival: rivalry becomes reunion

The school festival sequence makes the broader Yuigaoka setting feel alive again. Ren turning Yuigaoka into “the birthplace of music,” Sumire’s rainbow takoyaki, Natsumi and Tomari’s horrifying “healthy” matcha smoothie, Mei and Shiki’s support, the students gathering signatures — all of this matters because the school itself is not passively watching the idol drama.

Yuigaoka participates.

That has been the school’s role since Season 1, but now it becomes decisive. The student body does not simply choose between two idol groups. It declares what kind of school it wants:

> A Yuigaoka where these eleven girls sing together.

## The two performances

The performance contrast is very clear.

TomaKanoTe’s side has sharper, more combative lyrics and staging: forging the path to the top with bare hands, desire to win, youth not being just for show, burning brightly. It carries Wien’s intensity, Tomari’s seriousness, and Kanon’s forward drive. It is still school idol music, but it has edge.

Liella!’s side is more collective and connective: linking sparkling pieces, future brighter than before, the visual sense of many students and colors gathering. It feels closer to Liella!’s established ethos.

The important thing is that neither side cancels the other. They feel different, but compatible. That means the song succeeds. It does not flatten the two groups into one bland compromise; it lets their differences form a single arc.

This is the musical version of the season’s argument:

> Eleven-member Liella! should not erase TomaKanoTe’s intensity or Liella!’s warmth. It should connect them.

## No winner, because both groups choose the same wish

The result scene is excellent because it refuses the simple outcome. The students say: enter Love Live as eleven.

Then the characters reveal that both groups had the same request if they won:

> “Let’s enter Love Live! as eleven.”

This is the true resolution. They did not reunite because one side was defeated. They reunited because both sides independently reached the same desire.

That is crucial. If Liella! had simply beaten TomaKanoTe and absorbed them, Wien and Tomari’s feelings would have been overridden. If TomaKanoTe had beaten Liella! and demanded union, Liella!’s existing identity would have been subordinated. Instead, both groups arrive at the same answer from different paths.

This proves the rivalry has done its work.

Wien can now join without feeling merely absorbed.
Tomari can now join without feeling her test was incomplete.
Kanon can now return without undoing the developmental distance she created.
Liella! can become eleven without pretending the split never mattered.

# Kanon Shibuya: updated through Season 3 Episode 8

Kanon’s arc in these episodes is subtle but very mature.

In Episode 7, she still performs her familiar role: she identifies the unresolved feelings in Wien and Tomari and creates a situation where those feelings can move. But in Episode 8, she steps back. She lets the younger members write the song. She does not rescue the meeting. She does not solve the Onitsuka sisters’ conflict. She trusts the juniors to become the kind of people who can create Liella!’s future.

That is important because Kanon’s Season 3 separation was not only about helping Wien and Tomari. It was also about letting Liella! grow beyond her.

By Episode 8, she has succeeded. The juniors can now do Kanon-like work without Kanon:

-   Shiki identifies the emotional blockage.

-   Mei tells Natsumi what Tomari needs.

-   Natsumi speaks honestly.

-   Tomari finally shares vulnerability.

-   The younger members collectively write the song.


Kanon can rejoin because her absence has produced real growth.

# Chisato: updated through Season 3 Episode 8

Chisato’s role is also mature. She does not try to force Kanon back, and she does not cling to the split. She designs the fair contest, distributes the song, and helps create the conditions for both rivalry and reunion.

She has become very good at understanding when structure matters. The same song, split between two groups, is exactly the right format because it prevents the contest from becoming a mere popularity battle. Chisato gives the emotional problem a formal shape.

That has been her Season 3 strength:

> Kanon creates emotional meaning.
> Chisato creates structures where that meaning can be tested.

# Wien Margarete: updated through Season 3 Episode 8

Wien’s development is visible in her contradictions.

She still says Liella! is the enemy. She still wants to defeat them. She still hates emotional softness. But she attends the sleepover, eats takoyaki, participates in ordinary conversation, accepts the same-song contest, works on the performance, and finally accepts the eleven-member answer.

Her language lags behind her heart.

The key development is that her desire to defeat Liella! becomes less contemptuous and more legitimate. In Season 2, she wanted to defeat Liella! to prove the inferiority of school idols. In Season 3, she wants to face Liella! because she is serious about singing, recognition, and growth.

That is a healthier rivalry. It still contains pride, but it no longer depends on despising the other side.

# Tomari Onitsuka: updated through Season 3 Episode 8

Tomari’s arc advances decisively.

Her earlier worldview was based on protecting Natsumi from the pain of dreams. Episode 8 shows that she also needed Natsumi to accept her fear, her awkwardness, and her stubbornness. Her cold rationality was not only an argument. It was a wall.

Natsumi’s confession breaks through because it does not demand that Tomari instantly love school idols. It says:

> I love you first.
> You can tell me anything.
> You do not have to hide behind distance.

That lets Tomari participate in the song honestly.

Her naming sense — “Sister dearest,” “Margarete” — is funny, but it also shows that she is beginning to process people through affectionate categories, not just utility. She still thinks oddly, but she is emotionally involved now.

# Natsumi Onitsuka: updated through Season 3 Episode 8

Natsumi has come a long way.

Season 2 Natsumi used performance to generate money and attention.
Season 3 Natsumi wants to become a future center of Liella!.
Episode 8 Natsumi becomes someone who can directly tell Tomari: I love you, I accept you, I want to keep talking with you.

That is a major transformation. The old Natsumi converted feeling into persona. The new Natsumi can use persona, performance, and sincerity together.

She is still comic, still promotional, still OniNatsu. But now that identity is rooted in real emotional courage.

# The second-years and inheritance

Episodes 7–8 strongly confirm that the second-years are becoming the future of Liella!.

The task of writing the shared song is specifically given to them because the seniors will graduate. The conflict between Natsumi and Tomari is resolved by the younger members, not by Kanon. The final reunion into eleven is made possible because the younger members can now connect across the split.

This is the clearest evidence yet that Season 3’s “inheritance without imitation” thesis is working.

The second-years do not become Kanon.
They become people who can do what Kanon once did: turn emotional conflict into a song.

# Liella! as eleven

The final hand-stack and “Liella!” call matter because the group name now means something different.

Originally, Liella! named five different-colored lights connecting.
Season 2 expanded it to nine, proving the dream could be inherited.
Season 3 split it apart so that Kanon, Wien, and Tomari could find reasons to stand with the others.
Now “Liella!” names eleven people who have chosen connection after testing separation.

That makes the eleven-member formation feel earned.

It is not simply “add the new girls.” It is:

> Kanon returns without regression.
> Wien joins without surrendering her seriousness.
> Tomari joins without abandoning her concern for Natsumi.
> Liella! expands without erasing the growth produced by rivalry.

# Updated Season 3 thesis

After Episodes 7–8, I would refine the Season 3 thesis this way:

> **Separation was necessary so that reunion could become a choice rather than a default.**

Kanon could not simply return after the study-abroad cancellation.
Wien could not simply be absorbed after losing.
Tomari could not simply accept school idols without testing Natsumi’s dream.
Liella! could not simply become eleven because the audience wanted it.

They had to compete, talk, write, hurt, reconcile, and discover that both sides wanted the same future.

That is why the school festival result works. The students ask for eleven-member Liella!, but the group itself had already arrived there. The school’s wish and the idols’ wish finally align.

Season 3’s first major arc is therefore complete:

> Kanon stayed, but did not regress.
> Liella! lost Kanon temporarily, but did not collapse.
> Wien and Tomari stood apart, but found a way in.
> The rival groups became one because rivalry had finally done its job.
