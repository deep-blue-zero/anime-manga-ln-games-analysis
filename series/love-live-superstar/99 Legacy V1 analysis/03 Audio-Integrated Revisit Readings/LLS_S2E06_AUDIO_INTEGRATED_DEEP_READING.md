# S2E06 Audio-Integrated V1 Revisit

# *Love Live! Superstar!!* — Season 2, Episode 6

## 「DEKKAIDOW!」— Audio-integrated analysis and cumulative character profiles

I reviewed the complete Season 2, Episode 6 analysis bundle: the 23:42 stereo audio track, original Japanese subtitles, 369 ordinary dialogue cues, 1,176 retained frames, and 59 contact sheets.

This is the episode in which **Onitsuka Natsumi stops being merely the girl filming Liella! and becomes someone willing to stand inside the image herself**.

Its central argument is:

> **Natsumi did not abandon dreams because she lacked imagination. She abandoned them because she had imagined too many futures, publicly failed at all of them, and concluded that measurable income was safer than hope.**

Money is not her original dream. It is the substitute she adopts after dreaming becomes humiliating.

Her camera, branded speech, clickbait instincts, and obsession with “マニー” all create controlled relationships with the future:

-   views can be counted;

-   money can be accumulated;

-   footage can be edited;

-   personas can be rehearsed;

-   other people can be converted into content;

-   failure can be reframed before anyone else frames it for her.


School-idol performance threatens this system because it asks her to pursue something whose value cannot be guaranteed in advance. Kanon does not overcome that fear by promising success. She offers something more compatible with the series’ philosophy:

> **Natsumi does not need to possess a private dream before joining. She can borrow a shared dream long enough to discover what she herself wants.**

The audio makes her transformation unusually clear. Her bright, high, sing-song **OniNatsu** delivery contrasts sharply with the lower, tighter register she uses when denying she has a dream. Her panic around the “bear” sends her voice into its highest and least controlled range. After the performance, her voice becomes quieter and less packaged; even her signature 「ですの」 briefly sounds less like branding than an emotional fragment she is using to steady herself.

---

# I. Natsumi begins by trying to brand a group that does not yet exist

The opening scene shows Natsumi proposing a new name for the four first-years.

She frames them as Liella!’s “younger-sister unit”:

> 「せっかくLiella!の妹分として４人で始動するのですの」
> “Since the four of us are beginning as Liella!’s junior group…”

Mei immediately rejects the premise:

> 「そもそも私たちはLiella!の妹分じゃない」
> “We aren’t Liella!’s junior group in the first place.”

Natsumi rapidly retreats:

> 「そ…それは分かってますの」
> 「ユニット、そう、ユニット名ですの」
> “I-I know that. A unit—yes, a unit name.”

This is already characteristic. Natsumi speaks as though she is presenting a polished proposal, but the polish is fragile. Once challenged, she changes the category without acknowledging that the underlying plan has changed.

Her suggested names also reveal how she thinks. She treats identity as something that can be manufactured through branding before the group has decided what it means. The others are beginning from aspiration:

-   Kinako wants to reach the seniors.

-   Mei wants to contribute meaningfully to Liella!.

-   Shiki wants a practical goal and remains closely aligned with Mei.


Natsumi begins from market legibility:

> What should these four girls be called?
> What image can be sold?
> How can the temporary separation from the seniors become a distinct product?

Mei then states the first-years’ actual principle:

> 「Liella!の力になれないなら、スクールアイドルやるつもりはない。少なくとも私はな」
> “If I can’t become a strength to Liella!, I have no intention of being a school idol. At least, I don’t.”

Shiki adds only:

> 「Me too」

That tiny English response is perfectly consistent with her established voice: compressed, affectively restrained, and aligned with Mei without unnecessary elaboration.

Natsumi’s private follow-up exposes her motive:

> 「思ったより強情ですの」
> 「引き離せば思いのままにできると思いましたのに」
> “They’re more stubborn than expected. I thought separating them would let me control them as I pleased.”

This is the real starting point of her episode. She has not joined the first-years’ developmental project. She wants to convert their insecurity and separation into manageable content.

---

# II. The senior group and the ethics of waiting

Kanon is troubled by the first-years’ decision to train separately. Her voice is subdued and self-questioning:

> 「何が悪かったのかなって」
> “I was wondering what we did wrong.”

She had imagined a lively summer in which all eight members practiced together. The first-years’ departure therefore feels like a relational failure, even though they explicitly said it was not.

Chisato responds with the perspective of a newly emerging club president:

> 「自分たちだけでやってみたいって言ったんだよ」
> 「私ね、それはすごいステキなことだと思う」
> “They said they wanted to try by themselves. I think that’s a really wonderful thing.”

Her tone remains gentle, but the judgment is firm. This continues the leadership style established in Season 2, Episode 4 and foreshadowed by her intervention with Kanon in Season 1, Episode 11.

Chisato understands that support sometimes means not immediately closing distance.

Keke reinforces the point:

> 「何も言わずに待つのも、上級生として必要なことです」
> “Waiting without saying anything is also something seniors need to do.”

Sumire translates waiting into competitive aspiration:

> 「私たちも更にレベルを上げて、ギャラクシーな目標になるのよ」
> “We’ll raise our own level and become a Galaxy-class goal for them.”

The senior group therefore does not simply abandon the first-years. It tries to become a better destination for their effort.

That contrasts with Natsumi’s original plan. Natsumi separates people in order to control them. The seniors permit separation in order to let the first-years develop agency.

---

# III. Hokkaido changes Kinako’s voice before it changes anyone else

When the first-years arrive in Hokkaido, Kinako sounds different from the anxious newcomer seen in Tokyo.

She says:

> 「ようこそ。きな子の故郷へ、っす」
> “Welcome to Kinako’s hometown.”

Her characteristic **〜っす** remains, but her speech is more assured. She knows the geography, explains the family pension, communicates with the animals, sets the route, and casually proposes running to the house.

In Tokyo, Kinako often asks whether she is keeping up.

In Hokkaido, other people ask Kinako where to go.

This is an important environmental refinement of her personality. Her rural speech and wonder are not signs of generalized incompetence. She becomes confident when she occupies a world whose rhythms she understands.

Even the joke in which she interprets the animal’s bleating—

> 「『元気出せ』って言ってるっす」
> “‘Cheer up,’ it says.”

—sounds entirely natural coming from her. Mei doubts that she can understand it, but Kinako answers without hesitation:

> 「もちろんっす」
> “Of course.”

Her sincerity is so complete that it becomes its own authority.

That same quality later makes her uniquely capable of disturbing Natsumi’s cynicism. Kinako does not know when she is supposed to treat another person’s declared dream as a joke, defensive branding, or lie. She takes people seriously.

---

# IV. Natsumi’s first collapse: “Manii” as comic security language

Natsumi is forced to accompany the others partly because they remind her of the travel expenses she has already been fronted.

She laments:

> 「マニーが…」
> 「命の次にたいせちゅなマニーが…」
> “My manii… My manii, the most precious thing after life…”

Her pronunciation slips on 「大切」, producing a more childish sound. The voice that normally presents a savvy entrepreneurial personality briefly regresses into exaggerated despair.

The joke reveals a deeper truth. Money is not merely one interest among others. It is the object Natsumi has assigned the role of existential safety.

Her later explanation confirms this:

> she wants to earn enough money so that she will not suffer when she is older.

That is not the fantasy of luxury. It is anticipatory fear.

The deliberately childish loanword **マニー** helps her package the fear as cute content. “Money” becomes “manii,” a branded object she can chase comically rather than a sign of how little confidence she has in the future.

---

# V. OniNatsu: the voice Natsumi can control

At Kinako’s home, Natsumi records herself:

> 「オーニナッツー！」
> 「今日はオニナッツ風夏のスタミナスムージーをご紹介…」
> “OniNatsu! Today I’m introducing an OniNatsu-style summer stamina smoothie…”

Her creator voice has a distinct acoustic profile:

-   higher placement;

-   stronger projection;

-   exaggerated melodic rises;

-   brisk, rehearsed pacing;

-   carefully timed catchphrases;

-   brighter affect than her ordinary conversation.


It is the voice of someone who knows exactly where the camera is.

The important point is that this is not simply a false voice. It contains genuine skills:

-   rapid presentation;

-   self-branding;

-   visual awareness;

-   tonal consistency;

-   audience-address;

-   content structure;

-   improvisational resilience.


But the episode immediately reveals its limitation. The smoothie material is not producing enough views:

> 「やっぱりスムージーネタでは、全然稼げませんの」
> “As expected, smoothie material earns nothing.”

She then turns to the training footage and decides it needs something more provocative:

> 「もう少し刺激的な方がいいかもしれませんの」
> “It might need to be a little more stimulating.”

She proposes a framing that begins:

> 「Liella!解散…」
> “Liella! disbands…”

The group is not disbanding. Natsumi is constructing a narrative that will produce attention.

This is the first ethical problem in her authorship:

> **She knows how to give events a marketable form, but she is willing to let that form distort the people inside it.**

Her private editing voice is less elevated and less sing-song than the OniNatsu introduction. It becomes quicker, more calculating, and more muttered. Yet she still uses 「ですの」 even while alone.

That matters. The ending is not merely something she puts on for fans. Her branded language has become part of how she thinks privately.

Natsumi has built a persona so thoroughly that it now functions as psychological armor even without an audience.

---

# VI. The first real interruption: Kinako watches the old Liella! performance

Kinako enters while Natsumi is editing. Natsumi hurriedly hides the sensational version and substitutes footage of the seniors’ performance.

Kinako watches and says:

> 「このステージ、すごいっすよねえ」
> “This stage is amazing.”

She then explains the first-years’ dream:

> 「きな子たち、これを超えるのが夢なんす」
> “Our dream is to surpass this.”

More precisely, they want their addition to prove that Liella! has become stronger:

> the first-years joined;
> the group expanded;
> therefore the new Liella! should be able to exceed what the five-member group achieved.

Natsumi repeats:

> 「夢？」
> “A dream?”

The word sounds foreign to her despite the childhood history later revealed.

Kinako then asks the most dangerous possible question:

> 「夏美ちゃんの夢は何なんすか」
> “What is your dream, Natsumi?”

Natsumi’s answer is evasive:

> 「別に、特にないんですの」
> “Nothing in particular.”

Pressed about her CEO identity, she says:

> 「強いて言えば、お金をたくさん稼ぐことですの」
> “If I had to say, it’s earning a lot of money.”

Kinako asks why.

Natsumi responds:

> 「年を取った時に困らないようにですの」
> “So that I won’t have trouble when I’m older.”

This is security planning, not dream language.

Her answer points away from aspiration and toward prevention:

-   avoid poverty;

-   avoid helplessness;

-   avoid an uncertain future;

-   accumulate something measurable before adulthood arrives.


When Kinako continues looking at her, Natsumi ends the conversation and asks her to leave.

Kinako has done nothing accusatory. That is why the question hurts.

---

# VII. The childhood recordings: Natsumi did not lack dreams—she was trained out of them

The episode presents Natsumi’s childhood ambitions through recorded self-presentations. The frames retain camera overlays such as **REC** and menu graphics. Her younger self is already performing her future for a recording device.

She announces a series of enormous dreams:

> 「私の夢は、将来オリンピックで金メダルをとること」
> “My dream is to win an Olympic gold medal someday.”

Then she is told:

> 「夏美ちゃん、またビリだね」
> “Natsumi, you came last again.”

She says:

> 「私の夢は、ノーベル賞を取れるような科学者になること」
> “My dream is to become a scientist who can win a Nobel Prize.”

A disastrous test score answers her.

She says:

> 「私の夢は、モデルさんになって世界を駆け回ること」
> “My dream is to become a model who travels around the world.”

Then:

> 「全然、伸びてない」
> “I haven’t grown at all.”

The sequence is funny in presentation, but structurally brutal.

Natsumi repeatedly chose dreams that promised exceptional visibility:

-   Olympic champion;

-   Nobel-winning scientist;

-   international model.


She did not merely want a tolerable future. She imagined becoming extraordinary.

Each dream then encountered an external metric:

-   placement;

-   grades;

-   bodily growth.


The world answered her imagination with numbers.

That history explains why money becomes her replacement objective. Money is also numerical, but unlike a dream, it does not require her to claim that she herself is exceptional. It can be accumulated incrementally and defended as practicality.

After the montage, she says:

> 「嫌な夢」
> “A horrible dream.”

The Japanese allows “dream” to carry both aspiration and nightmare. Her ambitions have become memories that wake her in distress.

The most accurate formulation is:

> **Natsumi does not believe dreaming is childish. She believes dreaming is a process by which a child volunteers to be measured and humiliated.**

---

# VIII. Chisato raises the hurdle: care as developmental demand

The seniors watch the popular training video. Keke is delighted that online promotion has worked. Sumire immediately warns that popularity may give the first-years too much confidence. She also identifies Natsumi as suspicious:

> 「あの子がわざわざ手伝うなんて、何かたくらんでるとしか思えない」
> “For that girl to go out of her way to help, I can only think she’s plotting something.”

This is Sumire’s show-business realism. She recognizes that someone filming and packaging idols may have interests different from theirs.

Chisato is distracted by another implication:

> 「これだけできるなら、自信つけるために、ハードル上げていいかもって」
> “If they can do this much, maybe it’s all right to raise the hurdle to build their confidence.”

She assigns the first-years the same step level as the seniors for the school festival.

The audio again reveals the defining contradiction in Chisato’s leadership. She speaks gently, without theatrical authority, but what she says is demanding:

> 「もちろん、今のままじゃ難しいと思うよ」
> “Of course, I think it would be difficult at your current level.”

Then:

> 「夏休みの間頑張れば、学園祭にはきっと間に合うよ。私はそう思う」
> “If you work during summer break, you can surely make it by the school festival. That’s what I think.”

She does not falsely reassure them that they are already capable.

She identifies a gap and gives it a deadline.

This is the same “soft severity” seen when she arranged Kanon’s solo confrontation in Season 1, Episode 11. Chisato’s care repeatedly takes the form:

> I believe you can reach this, so I will not protect you from knowing how far away it still is.

---

# IX. The first-years falter—and Natsumi becomes sincere before she becomes an idol

The higher target overwhelms the first-years.

Kinako notes that the popular video has also raised expectations. Mei says Chisato’s demand is too sudden. They have only recently begun.

Natsumi interrupts in her creator persona:

> 「オーニナッツー！」
> 「あなたの心のオニサプリ、鬼塚夏美ですの」
> “OniNatsu! The Oni-supplement for your heart, Onitsuka Natsumi!”

The formula initially sounds like another attempt to make content from their exhaustion. They point out that practice has not started. She bluntly says:

> 「実際、サボってますの」
> “You are, in fact, slacking.”

The tone then changes.

She says:

> 「超えるのが夢なんでしょ」
> “Surpassing them was your dream, wasn’t it?”

And:

> 「だったら、責任は持つべきですの」
> “Then you should take responsibility for it.”

Finally:

> 「諦めるくらいなら、夢なんて語ってほしくない」
> “If you’re going to give up, I don’t want you talking about dreams.”

This is not OniNatsu promotional brightness. Her voice becomes firmer, less decorative, and morally heated.

The severity is projection. Natsumi is speaking to the children she used to be:

-   the athlete who came last;

-   the aspiring scientist with the poor score;

-   the would-be model whose body did not cooperate.


She has concluded that dreams demand proof. Therefore anyone fortunate enough to possess a reachable dream must not treat it casually.

But the speech does not remain purely punitive.

She says her footage has shown her something:

> 「皆さんの夢は、決して実現不可能な夢ではない」
> “Your dream is by no means impossible.”

Then:

> 「頑張れば、手が届くかもしれない」
> “If you work, it may be within reach.”

And:

> 「そういう夢があるというのは…」
> “To have a dream like that is…”

She pauses before finishing:

> 「とてもすばらしいことですの」
> “A truly wonderful thing.”

The pause matters. She is not simply praising them. She is recognizing a condition she believes has been denied to her.

This is Natsumi’s first authentic act as a producer.

Her filming has given her real evidence:

-   their performance is rough;

-   the goal is difficult;

-   the goal is not impossible;

-   their dream deserves to be protected from their own panic.


She began by trying to exploit their separation. She ends up using observation to identify their actual potential.

---

# X. Natsumi’s philosophy of dreams

Her line about “responsibility” makes her philosophy clearer.

For Natsumi, speaking a dream is almost contractual. Once declared, a person owes effort to it.

This explains both her harshness and her withdrawal.

She once declared huge dreams. Repeated failure made those declarations feel embarrassing. So she now refuses to declare anything whose fulfillment she cannot control.

Her worldview is:

> A dream creates a standard.
> The standard creates judgment.
> Failure reveals that the dreamer was foolish.
> Therefore do not dream unless success is plausibly defensible.

That is why she can support the first-years more easily than herself. She sees evidence that their dream may be attainable. She sees no equivalent evidence for her own worth.

---

# XI. Kinako refuses to accept cynicism as maturity

During the bath, Kinako thanks Natsumi for the earlier speech.

She says:

> 「夏美ちゃん、すごいなあって」
> 「目標持ってるってすごいっす」
> “I thought you were amazing. Having a goal is amazing.”

Natsumi corrects her:

> 「だから、ないって言いましたの」
> “I told you I don’t have one.”

Kinako interprets this as modesty:

> 「それは謙遜っす」
> “That’s humility.”

She reasons that someone without a goal would not be acting as a CEO.

Natsumi insists:

> 「だから、目標なんてないんですの」
> 「本当にないから、こうしているんですの」
> “I don’t have any goal. It’s because I truly don’t that I’m doing this.”

Then:

> 「マニーを稼ぐくらいしかないんですの」
> “All I have is earning manii.”

Her voice becomes less bright and more compressed as Kinako continues. The repetition indicates that she is not clarifying a neutral fact. She is protecting a painful self-conclusion.

Kinako’s sincerity is difficult for Natsumi because it grants meaning to actions Natsumi wants treated as purely instrumental.

Kinako sees:

-   initiative;

-   work ethic;

-   media skill;

-   entrepreneurial persistence;

-   ability to motivate people.


Natsumi sees:

-   failed dreams;

-   monetizable activity;

-   something to do because no meaningful future remains.


Kinako does not defeat Natsumi in argument. She makes Natsumi’s self-description increasingly hard to sustain.

---

# XII. The bear panic: the creator loses control of the frame

When Kanon approaches in the bear costume, Natsumi assumes she is facing a real bear.

Her voice shoots upward and fragments:

> 「クマ、クマ！」
> 「どどど…どうするんですの」
> “A bear! A bear! W-w-w-what do I do?”

She rapidly cycles through survival strategies:

-   run;

-   no, she will not make it;

-   play dead;

-   no, she has heard that does not work.


Then she screams.

This is broad comedy, but vocally useful. Natsumi usually constructs the circumstances under which people see her. Here she has no time to edit, brand, narrate, or calculate.

The result is:

-   stammering;

-   high pitch;

-   rapid self-interruption;

-   panic stripped of presentation.


The bear scene is the episode’s most extreme collapse of OniNatsu control.

Kanon then removes the costume, and Natsumi immediately tries to restore composure through irritation.

---

# XIII. Kanon invites Natsumi without demanding that she first become sincere

Kanon reveals that she overheard some of the bath conversation.

Natsumi responds by trying to turn the situation back into content:

> 「では、ここでかのん先輩にも動画に出演して頂きますの」
> “Then I’ll have Kanon-senpai appear in a video here…”

Kanon interrupts:

> 「それより、スクールアイドルやってみない？」
> “More importantly, why not try being a school idol?”

Then:

> 「夏美ちゃんに、９人目のLiella!になってほしいんだ」
> “I want you to become Liella!’s ninth member.”

Natsumi answers:

> 「話の脈絡が見えませんの」
> “I don’t see the connection.”

Kanon’s invitation appears abrupt only if one accepts Natsumi’s own division between creator and performer.

Kanon has already seen:

-   Natsumi’s fascination with Liella!;

-   her understanding of the first-years’ dream;

-   her ability to motivate them;

-   her persistent work;

-   her buried response to performance;

-   her claim that she lacks a dream.


Kanon then offers the episode’s decisive proposition:

> 「夢がないなら、みんなと一緒に同じ夢、追いかけてみない？」
> “If you don’t have a dream, why not pursue the same dream together with everyone?”

This is one of *Superstar!!*’s strongest statements about collective aspiration.

Kanon does not say:

> Find your true individual passion, then return.

She says:

> Join us first.
> Let the shared practice reveal what the dream means to you.

In this series, desire does not always precede relationship. Sometimes relationship creates the conditions under which desire becomes legible.

---

# XIV. 「夢をみていい人とは違う」— Natsumi’s self-exclusion

Natsumi refuses:

> 「私はこれまでたくさんの夢をみてきて」
> 「何も叶わないって分かったんですの」
> “I have had many dreams, and I learned that none of them come true.”

Then:

> 「かのん先輩のような、夢をみていい人とは違うんですの」
> “I’m different from someone like Kanon-senpai, who is allowed to dream.”

This is the deepest statement of her wound.

Natsumi does not merely think Kanon is more talented. She divides people into moral-existential categories:

-   people permitted to dream;

-   people whose previous failures have revoked that permission.


Kanon belongs to the first category in Natsumi’s imagination because she is visibly talented, admired, and central to Liella!.

Kanon immediately complicates that image:

> 「私もいろいろ挫折してきたよ」
> “I’ve also suffered many setbacks.”

She explains that she dreamed of entering Yuigaoka’s music course and failed.

Her delivery is important. She does not present the failure as heroic mythology. She speaks plainly and without competition. She does not say, “My suffering was greater.” She says, in effect:

> The person you believe is dream-worthy also had a dream rejected.

Then she names what Keke and the others taught her:

> 「みんなとなら、頑張れるよって」
> “That with everyone, I could keep trying.”

And:

> 「お互い欠けてるところや、届かないところを補い合って」
> 「一緒に夢を追いかけることはできるよって」
> “We can compensate for what each of us lacks and pursue a dream together.”

This is Kanon’s mature performance philosophy stated as social ontology:

> nobody needs to be complete before belonging;
> the group is partly a structure for making incomplete people capable of action.

---

# XV. Kanon does not leave the idea abstract

Kanon says:

> 「来て」
> “Come.”

Then:

> 「私をまねして」
> “Copy me.”

She teaches Natsumi a movement.

After they complete it, she asks:

> 「どう？ 気持ちいいでしょ」
> “How is it? It feels good, doesn’t it?”

The wording is sensory, not ideological.

Kanon explains:

> 「これをみんなで息をそろえて決める」
> “Everyone synchronizes their breathing and hits this together.”

Then:

> 「応援してくれる人の前で」
> “In front of the people supporting us.”

The imagined space opens into multiple bright paths. Kanon continues:

> 「客席の人たちも、心の中で一緒に踊ってくれるの」
> “The people in the audience dance with us in their hearts.”

And:

> 「ステージ全てが一つになる」
> “The entire stage becomes one.”

Finally:

> 「それが最高の瞬間」
> “That is the greatest moment.”

This is the direct opposite of Natsumi’s original content model.

Natsumi’s camera creates a division:

-   creator;

-   subject;

-   audience;

-   monetized attention.


Kanon describes performance as unity:

-   performers synchronize with one another;

-   the audience participates internally;

-   the whole stage becomes one event.


Natsumi initially asks what performance can yield.

Kanon asks what performance can connect.

The episode visualizes the difference by moving Natsumi out from behind her phone and placing her bodily beside Kanon. She must imitate, breathe, move, and feel before deciding what the act is worth.

---

# XVI. The rainbow road and the restoration of possibility

The dance demonstration expands into a field of parallel colored paths.

Earlier, Natsumi’s childhood dreams appeared as discrete recorded declarations, each closed down by a specific metric. Here the visual field remains open. No single lane is identified as the correct predetermined future.

Kanon stands apart from Natsumi but extends a hand across the space.

The image does not promise that Natsumi will become a great singer, a center, a producer, or a wealthy CEO. It offers movement before certainty.

That is exactly what Natsumi’s defensive system has prohibited.

She has wanted a reliable outcome before permitting herself to begin.

Kanon offers:

> begin with others;
> let the meaning emerge through the act.

---

# XVII. Natsumi’s creator identity is preserved rather than erased

At the school festival, Natsumi still begins:

> 「オーニナッツー！」
> 「あなたの心のオニサプリ、鬼塚夏美ですの」
> “OniNatsu! The Oni-supplement for your heart, Onitsuka Natsumi!”

Joining Liella! does not cure her by deleting the persona.

This is important. The episode does not claim that:

-   branding is inherently false;

-   social media is incompatible with sincerity;

-   entrepreneurship is morally inferior to art;

-   the cute 「ですの」 register must disappear before she can be authentic.


Instead, her existing skills are relocated inside a less extractive relationship.

The difference is no longer whether she performs a persona.

The difference is what the persona is serving.

Before joining:

> OniNatsu uses Liella! to generate Natsumi’s views and income.

After joining:

> Natsumi can use OniNatsu’s media intelligence, energy, and audience awareness to help Liella! reach people.

The self-authored brand becomes a potential group resource.

---

# XVIII. “ビタミンSUMMER!” and Natsumi’s first embodied dream

The nine-member debut performance is not aesthetically neutral. Its saturated colors, neon graphics, energetic framing, exaggerated poses, and playful camera awareness are unusually compatible with Natsumi’s established visual language.

It feels closer to OniNatsu’s extroverted media world than the restrained or emotionally lyrical stages associated with Kanon.

That makes it the correct performance for her initiation.

The opening declaration is:

> 「今日は思いっきり、楽しんじゃおう」
> “Today, let’s enjoy ourselves completely.”

The song repeatedly emphasizes:

-   play;

-   collective excitement;

-   physical release;

-   high-fives;

-   audience participation;

-   vivid seasonal intensity;

-   refusal to hold back.


This directly counters Natsumi’s instrumental mindset.

She has evaluated activities by:

-   views;

-   money;

-   usefulness;

-   future security;

-   probability of success.


“ビタミンSUMMER!” asks her to experience immediate joy without first justifying it as investment.

The song’s staging also does not isolate her as a lone new center. It introduces her within the nine-member formation. The dream she discovers is already relational.

---

# XIX. The final 「ですの」

After the performance, Kanon asks:

> 「どうだった？」
> “How was it?”

Natsumi initially answers only:

> 「ですの」

Her signature sentence ending appears as a standalone fragment.

That is a remarkable vocal moment. Throughout the episode, 「ですの」 gives her speech continuity and recognizable brand identity. Here emotion disrupts the sentence, leaving only the habitual ending.

Then she manages:

> 「最高だった、ですの」
> “It was the best… desuno.”

The pause before the final phrase matters. She sounds as though she has experienced the feeling before she can package it.

Finally:

> 「見つけたかも。私の、夢」
> “I may have found it. My dream.”

The **かも / maybe** prevents the transformation from becoming artificially complete.

She does not say:

> I have found my permanent destiny.

She says:

> This might be the feeling I had concluded was no longer available to me.

Her last phrase also includes a slight separation around:

> 「私の、夢」

“My dream” is still difficult to say as one fluent unit.

That uncertainty makes the ending more convincing.

# XX. Initial comprehensive voice profile: Onitsuka Natsumi

## 1\. OniNatsu creator voice

This is her most overtly performed mode.

Its characteristics include:

-   high, bright placement;

-   exaggerated melodic movement;

-   fast, polished introductions;

-   catchphrase rhythm;

-   self-naming as brand;

-   conspicuous 「ですの」 endings;

-   playful lexical branding such as **オニサプリ** and **マニー**.


It is effective because Natsumi has real presenter instincts. She knows how to seize attention.

## 2\. Private entrepreneurial voice

When alone editing, she becomes less sing-song and more rapid, low, and calculative.

The vocabulary shifts toward:

-   views;

-   stimulation;

-   editing;

-   monetization;

-   sensational framing.


Yet 「ですの」 remains. This shows that OniNatsu is not a costume she completely removes. It has become her private cognitive voice.

## 3\. Dream-denial voice

When Kinako or Kanon asks about her dream, her range narrows. She repeats conclusions:

> 「目標なんてないんですの」
> 「本当にないから…」
> 「無理ですの」

The polished energy drops. Her speech becomes defensive through closure rather than through exuberance.

## 4\. Moral-projection voice

Her speech to the first-years is sharper, more forceful, and less commercially playful:

> 「諦めるくらいなら、夢なんて語ってほしくない」

This voice carries genuine conviction. It is the failed dreamer speaking through the producer.

## 5\. Panic voice

The bear sequence strips away every stabilizing system:

-   strong pitch rise;

-   stammering;

-   rapid self-correction;

-   repeated nouns;

-   uncontrolled scream.


It reveals how much of her normal composure is constructed.

## 6\. Post-performance voice

After “ビタミンSUMMER!”, her delivery becomes softer and less packaged. The characteristic 「ですの」 survives, but no longer functions only as showmanship.

The persona has not disappeared. It has become capable of carrying sincerity.

## Personality refinement

Natsumi is not primarily greedy.

She is:

-   future-anxious;

-   serially disappointed;

-   afraid of non-measurable effort;

-   highly media-literate;

-   capable of manipulative framing;

-   observant enough to recognize real potential;

-   harsher toward abandoned dreams than toward ordinary failure;

-   embarrassed by the possibility that she still wants something;

-   already equipped with genuine production and promotional talent.


## Performance philosophy at introduction

Before joining:

> performance is attention converted into measurable return.

After her first stage:

> performance may be a shared bodily event whose value exists before measurement.

## Core vocal thesis

> **Natsumi turns herself into a brand because brands can be controlled. Her arc begins when a shared performance produces a feeling too immediate to edit before admitting it.**

# XXI. Cumulative voice profile: Sakurakōji Kinako

This episode substantially strengthens Kinako’s profile.

## Her rural voice is not merely a cute speech marker

The **〜っす** register becomes most natural and authoritative in Hokkaido. She is more confident, knowledgeable, and directive at home.

Her speech identity therefore expresses both humility and belonging.

## She takes aspiration literally

Kinako assumes:

-   CEO means a meaningful goal;

-   hard work implies hope;

-   Natsumi’s motivational ability reflects aspiration;

-   denial is probably modesty rather than despair.


This can make her naïve, but it is also morally productive. She refuses to cooperate with cynical self-description.

## She asks unusually direct questions

> 「夏美ちゃんの夢は何なんすか」
> “What is your dream?”

Because Kinako lacks Natsumi’s media self-consciousness, the question is not strategic. That makes it harder to deflect.

## She is the relational bridge of the first-year group

Kinako welcomes, hosts, thanks, admires, and keeps conversation open. Kanon eventually completes Natsumi’s recruitment, but Kinako creates the emotional opening through which Natsumi’s wound becomes visible.

## Updated core thesis

> **Kinako’s ordinary sincerity is socially disarming: she treats people’s stated hopes as worthy of belief before they have proved themselves exceptional.**

---

# XXII. Cumulative voice profile: Shibuya Kanon

Episode 6 shows Kanon as a fully developed recruiter and mentor.

## She no longer offers generic confidence

Kanon does not tell Natsumi she is secretly destined for success.

Instead, she says:

-   Kanon herself has failed;

-   group members compensate for one another;

-   a shared dream can be entered before it becomes personal;

-   the value of performance can be felt through practice.


This is psychologically precise encouragement.

## Her authority is autobiographical

When Kanon says failure does not revoke permission to dream, she is speaking from the music-course rejection and the recovery of her voice.

She does not stand above Natsumi as the naturally successful idol Natsumi imagines. She deliberately dismantles that image.

## She teaches through body and image

> 「来て」
> 「私をまねして」

Rather than continuing an abstract speech, she gives Natsumi a movement to perform. This recalls the series’ insistence that artistic understanding is embodied and relational, not merely verbal.

## Mature performance philosophy

Kanon describes the ideal live as:

-   shared breath among performers;

-   audience participation;

-   unified stage;

-   highest moment.


Her definition of art is neither pure self-expression nor proof of technical superiority. It is **co-created presence**.

## Updated core thesis

> **Kanon now invites wounded people not by promising that their private dream will come true, but by giving them a place inside a shared dream where desire can reappear.**

---

# XXIII. Cumulative voice profile: Arashi Chisato

## Her presidency is becoming audible

Chisato now sets developmental standards for members beyond Kanon.

Her tone remains warm, but she gives the first-years:

-   a clear technical goal;

-   an honest assessment of their present insufficiency;

-   a plausible timeline;

-   explicit confidence that disciplined practice can close the gap.


## She trusts evidence

She raises the hurdle after seeing what the first-years can already do. Her demands are not arbitrary toughness. They are based on observed capacity.

## Her leadership risks creating pressure

The first-years experience the new target as abrupt. Chisato recognizes the possibility of pressure but decides the video demonstrates readiness.

As in Season 1, Episode 11, her belief can become demanding because she acts on the potential she sees before the person fully believes in it.

## Updated core thesis

> **Chisato’s leadership is developmental rather than comforting: she treats latent ability as something the group has a responsibility to cultivate.**

# XXIV. Cumulative voice profile: Yoneme Mei

Mei receives less intimate material than in Episode 4, but several traits are confirmed.

Her statement—

> 「Liella!の力になれないなら、スクールアイドルやるつもりはない」

—is delivered with rough conviction rather than embarrassment. Once Mei has admitted her school-idol desire, the same force previously used for denial can become principle.

Her rough register now communicates:

-   seriousness;

-   loyalty;

-   refusal to treat idolhood casually;

-   frustration with her own limitations.


She also objects openly when Chisato’s demand feels too sudden. Mei does not perform cheerful compliance for senior approval.

## Updated core thesis

> **Mei’s rough voice is beginning to move from concealment toward guardianship: she uses the force once spent denying love to defend what school idolhood should mean.**

# XXV. Cumulative voice profile: Wakana Shiki

Shiki remains the episode’s most compressed speaker.

Notable lines include:

> 「Me too」
> 「戻らない。あのバスのせい」
> 「ランニング・マシーン」
> 「Reverse」
> 「目標があった方が、計画は立てやすい」

She converts situations into:

-   factual summaries;

-   commands;

-   causal models;

-   planning principles.


The English fragments are not random decoration. They fit her preference for minimal, functionally precise units.

Her explanation that goals make planning easier also reveals how she relates to aspiration. Shiki does not romanticize dreams as Natsumi fears them. She treats a goal as a variable that makes a system organizable.

## Updated core thesis

> **Shiki translates emotional aspiration into workable structure. For her, a dream becomes less frightening when it can be decomposed into a plan.**

# XXVI. Smaller ensemble refinements

## Sumire

Sumire is the first senior to treat Natsumi’s offer of help as suspicious. Her entertainment-industry experience makes her attentive to hidden incentives, image management, and the possibility of exploitation.

This is not generic cynicism. It is protective realism.

## Keke

Keke celebrates the success of the uploaded video and argues that Liella! should post more actively rather than hide from attention. Her school-idol belief has always included the need to reach people. Natsumi will later provide a more technically sophisticated version of that impulse.

## Ren

Ren agrees that the seniors should speak directly to Natsumi. Her formal voice frames the issue as something requiring responsible inquiry rather than rumor or suspicion.

She remains the group’s institutional conscience.

# XXVII. Visual language

## The camera as shield

Natsumi is repeatedly positioned:

-   behind a phone;

-   behind a laptop;

-   watching other people through footage;

-   adding titles to their behavior;

-   directing attention while keeping herself outside the event.


The camera allows her to participate without risking the same form of exposure she once experienced as a child declaring dreams.

## Recorded childhood

Her childhood aspirations are themselves presented through recordings. The **REC** overlays imply that Natsumi has long understood the self as something presented to a lens.

The tragedy is not only that she failed. It is that the hopeful self was documented before failure answered it.

## Open Hokkaido versus enclosed screens

The episode surrounds Natsumi with enormous landscapes, but she repeatedly retreats into devices and constructed frames.

The title’s “DEKKAIDOW!” joke emphasizes scale: Hokkaido is physically vast, while Natsumi has reduced her future to the narrow security of views and income.

## The rainbow pathways

When Kanon explains performance, the visual world opens into parallel colored routes. No single fixed destination is imposed. Natsumi is asked to move before knowing which future will prove correct.

## From observer to formation

The episode’s formal movement is:

> filming the first-years
> → evaluating their dream
> → being seen by Kinako
> → being addressed by Kanon
> → copying a movement
> → joining the nine-member formation.

Natsumi crosses the boundary between image-maker and embodied subject.

# Final synthesis

Season 2, Episode 6 is often remembered as Natsumi’s recruitment episode, but its deeper subject is **what repeated failure does to a person’s right to imagine herself differently**.

Natsumi’s childhood was full of dreams. That is precisely the problem.

Each dream produced a public or measurable contradiction:

> champion — but last place;
> scientist — but poor grades;
> model — but no physical growth.

She then builds a new identity around objectives that do not require faith in herself:

> views, money, branding, production, future security.

OniNatsu is therefore both talent and armor.

The episode does not condemn that identity. Natsumi’s eye for presentation, her persistence, and her ability to recognize the first-years’ potential are all genuine. What must change is the direction of the skill.

At first she asks:

> How can other people’s dream become my content?

By the end she asks, tentatively:

> Could their dream become mine too?

Kinako makes her self-description unstable by admiring her.

Chisato creates the difficult goal that allows Natsumi’s buried seriousness to emerge.

The first-years show her a dream that appears reachable.

Kanon gives her permission to borrow that dream without first proving that she deserves one.

The live supplies the evidence no argument could provide.

That is why her final line uses **かも**:

> 「見つけたかも。私の、夢」
> “I may have found it. My dream.”

It is not the declaration of someone suddenly certain of her destiny.

It is the much more fragile statement of someone who had stopped believing that the category “my dream” still applied to her.
