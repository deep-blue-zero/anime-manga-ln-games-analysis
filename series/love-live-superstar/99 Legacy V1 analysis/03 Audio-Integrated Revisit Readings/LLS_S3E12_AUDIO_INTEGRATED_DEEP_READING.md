# S3E12 Audio-Integrated V1 Revisit

# *Love Live! Superstar!!* — Season 3, Episode 12

## Final audio-integrated analysis and completed running voice profiles

I reviewed the complete Season 3, Episode 12 analysis bundle: approximately 23:42 of stereo audio, 393 Japanese subtitle cues, 743 retained episode frames, and 38 contact sheets.

The broader series analysis described *Superstar!!* as a story about **beginnings learning how to become inheritances**. Love Live Superstar Understandi… This finale realizes that thesis with unusual precision:

> **Liella! succeeds not merely when Kanon recovers her voice, nor when the group wins Love Live, but when the founding members can leave without the voice, group, school, or dream disappearing with them.**

The episode is about graduation, but it repeatedly rejects the idea that graduation is equivalent to erasure.

The founders leave the school.
Their daily proximity ends.
The rooftop changes owners.
The group’s configuration changes.
Kanon goes to Vienna.
Keke returns to Shanghai.
Ren, Chisato, and Sumire move into their own futures.

Yet their voices remain in:

-   the younger members’ speech;

-   the principles they repeat;

-   the school’s institutional language;

-   the memories attached to physical places;

-   the new Liella!’s distribution of roles;

-   and a song that begins with five voices and expands into eleven.


The most accurate formulation of the finale is:

> **The original Liella! does not survive by remaining unchanged.
> It survives by becoming audible in people who are no longer the original Liella!.**

---

# I. Margarete’s 「ふん」 audit

I did **not** find a confident new Margarete 「ふん」 in this episode.

Two moments could easily be misidentified:

-   At approximately **03:37**, the clearly subtitled

    > 「フン！ 私についてこられるかしら？」
    > is **Sumire**, speaking to Keke.

-   At approximately **11:26**, the small final

    > 「ふん！」
    > belongs to **Kinako**, as she declares that she will ask the seniors to repeat a year.


Margarete does use closely related defensive vocal behavior:

> 「泣いてない！」
> “I’m not crying!”

and later:

> 「言われなくたって」
> “You don’t have to tell me.”

Both serve the same broad function as her recurring huffs—concealing that she has been emotionally affected—but she does not punctuate them with a distinct, confidently attributable 「ふん」.

That absence is itself mildly revealing. In earlier episodes, the huff often repaired a leak in her emotional defenses. Here, her feelings repeatedly escape too completely for that small repair mechanism:

-   she openly reassures Kanon;

-   she cries at graduation;

-   she accepts responsibility for Liella!;

-   and she responds forcefully to the seniors’ farewell principle.


Her defenses remain, but they have become ordinary embarrassment rather than an entire philosophy of superiority.

---

# II. Kanon packing: the protagonist becomes frightened in an ordinary way

The episode begins not with a triumphant winner preparing for international greatness, but with Kanon failing to fit her possessions into a suitcase.

Her speech is full of stretched endings and unsettled fragments:

> 「どうしよう…」
> 「こんなに持っていけないよぉ…」
> 「こんなの入るわけないよ～！」

Her voice rises and elongates around the impossible packing problem. This is a noticeably younger, more domestic Kanon than the measured leader who confronted Wien or guided Natsumi.

The scene deliberately makes her Vienna anxiety mundane:

-   bedding;

-   food;

-   unfamiliar rooms;

-   strangers;

-   what happens if something goes wrong;

-   whether familiar café au lait will be available.


Kanon is not afraid that she lacks the artistic ability to study abroad. That question has largely been resolved.

She is afraid of living somewhere unfamiliar.

This is healthy development. Her fear is no longer:

> My body will betray me, and therefore I am not permitted to sing.

It is:

> I am leaving home, and leaving home is frightening.

The dream is not being invalidated by the fear. The fear is now allowed to accompany the dream.

---

# III. Margarete reassures Kanon using her own migration story

Margarete initially corrects Kanon in her familiar manner:

> 「昨日言ったでしょ？」
> 「そんなに必要ないって」

The delivery is clipped and practical. She has already explained that her sister will arrange Kanon’s room and meals.

But when Kanon says that she will know nobody there, Margarete changes register:

> 「私が日本に来た時も、知り合いなんて一人もいなかったけど？」
> “When I came to Japan, I didn’t know anyone either.”

Then:

> 「それでも、私、今とても楽しいわ」
> 「だから大丈夫よ。かのんなら」
> “Even so, I’m very happy now. So you’ll be all right—because you’re Kanon.”

The correction voice is firmer and more energetic; the reassurance is softer, less percussive, and composed of longer phrases. Margarete stops prescribing and starts testifying.

This is one of the strongest reversals of her entire arc.

Season 2 Margarete understood international movement through:

-   elite institutions;

-   family judgment;

-   examination;

-   proof;

-   and hierarchy.


Season 3 Margarete can now describe migration through:

-   loneliness;

-   new relationships;

-   ordinary happiness;

-   and confidence that another person can become connected too.


She has become capable of saying:

> I arrived alone, and relation made the unfamiliar place livable.

That is Kanon’s artistic philosophy translated into Margarete’s biography.

## Margarete and Aria as Kanon’s temporary family structure

Aria agrees immediately:

> 「うん！」

Kanon then embraces both of them:

> 「マルガレーテちゃん…ありあ…だ～い好き！」

Margarete is no longer merely the rival who will inherit Liella!. She occupies Kanon’s home, helps at the café, advises her on Vienna, and gets pulled into a family hug beside Aria.

The episode begins by showing that Kanon’s departure is possible partly because Margarete has crossed from rival to domestic continuity.

Margarete will not accompany Kanon to Vienna as originally expected. Instead, she helps make leaving emotionally possible—and remains behind to protect what Kanon leaves.

---

# IV. Kanon’s mother and Aria: home does not romanticize departure

Kanon’s mother responds to her anxiety with dry practicality:

> 「向こうにもあるでしょ？」
> 「なかったら自分で作ってよね」

She does not treat Kanon’s departure as tragic destiny. She assumes Kanon will learn to solve ordinary problems.

Later she says:

> 「ひとりになるのはしょうがないわね」

And, when Kanon wishes Margarete could divide herself into two people:

> 「何ばかなこと言ってるの」

This familial bluntness is important. Liella! members often treat Kanon as:

-   founder;

-   emotional interpreter;

-   great singer;

-   center;

-   or future international artist.


At home, she remains a daughter saying foolish things because she is nervous.

Aria similarly tells her:

> 「じゃあ、お姉ちゃんも前向いていかなきゃだね」

Aria’s speech has none of the ceremonial weight with which Liella! discusses Kanon’s future. She offers a sister’s simple correction:

> The new six are moving forward. You have to do the same.

Home keeps Kanon human at the exact moment the narrative could have turned her into a completed heroic symbol.

---

# V. Keke and Sumire: school-idol love becomes an adult vocation

Kanon calls Keke for advice and discovers her at the same agency as Sumire.

Keke initially attempts an extremely poor deception:

> 「き…奇遇ですね～！」

Her pitch rises and the phrase is stretched with obvious nervousness. The woman capable of declaring enormous dreams before crowds becomes comically transparent when caught hiding something from Kanon.

She then explains her future with increasing steadiness:

> 「『ラブライブ！』は高校生の間だけ輝ける世界」
> “Love Live is a world in which one can shine only during high school.”

But:

> 「この先も歌い続けたい」
> 「ステージに立ちたい」
> “I want to keep singing. I want to keep standing onstage.”

Therefore she has joined an entertainment agency.

She will return to Shanghai, attend university, and work toward returning to singing and dancing onstage. The plan is neither the complete abandonment of her family’s educational expectations nor an impulsive refusal of adulthood.

It is a transnational compromise authored by the mature Keke:

-   education in Shanghai;

-   professional affiliation;

-   continued preparation;

-   and eventual return to the stage.


Season 3, Episode 6 prevented her from sealing school idols inside the category of “youth.” Episode 12 shows her constructing an adult life in which that love persists.

## Keke’s vocal integration

Keke’s characteristic brightness and 「デス」 remain, but they now carry a more concrete future.

Earlier Keke’s exuberance often announced faith:

> School idols are wonderful.
> Kanon’s voice must reach people.
> Our dream cannot end.

Here the same voice presents:

-   university;

-   agency work;

-   practice;

-   auditions;

-   photography schedules;

-   and long-term professional preparation.


Faith has acquired administration.

That becomes comically obvious when she produces Sumire’s packed schedule:

> 「これからの練習メニューデス！」
> 「更に！ これがオーディションの予定」
> 「更に、撮影の予定デス」

Her speech accelerates and regains its missionary rhythm, except the object is now Sumire’s career management.

Keke has transformed from school-idol recruiter into aspiring producer-manager without losing the emotional excess that made her Keke.

---

# VI. Sumire’s 「フン！」: pride as affectionate professional rivalry

Sumire says:

> 「何か負けた気分…！」

Keke answers that they now stand on the same starting line and that she is happy they can work hard together again.

Sumire responds:

> 「フン！ 私についてこられるかしら？」

This huff belongs to Sumire, and its function resembles one of Margarete’s friendlier variants:

> I am pleased by this development.
> I refuse to state that directly.
> Let me convert pleasure into a challenge.

The delivery is theatrical rather than genuinely contemptuous. Keke immediately laughs.

Their relationship has matured to the point that abrasive performance is a stable language of affection. Sumire no longer fears that Keke’s criticism means she is unworthy of center, and Keke no longer fears that Sumire’s pride means she lacks commitment.

They can carry their Season 1 rhythm into professional adulthood.

## The deeper significance

Keke and Sumire once represented competing relationships to idolhood:

-   Keke: faith, devotion, and dream;

-   Sumire: industry, visibility, and professional ambition.


Their shared agency future indicates that those philosophies have become complementary.

Keke supplies relentless belief and planning.

Sumire supplies performance experience, worldly instinct, and the hunger to be chosen.

The pair that spent the first season fighting over whether idolhood was sacred dream or show business will pursue adult entertainment work together.

---

# VII. Kanon refuses to look in on the new six

Aria suggests that Kanon visit the school and watch Margarete practice with the new Liella!.

Kanon says:

> 「そういうの、したくない」
> 「これからは、６人でスタートするんだから」

The delivery is quiet and firm. There is no dramatic renunciation.

Kanon understands that her presence would change the meaning of the rehearsal. The six would become:

> the remaining members being observed by the founder.

Her absence gives them ownership.

This is one of the clearest signs that Kanon has learned to distinguish support from surveillance. She remains interested in them and wants to cheer them on, but she will not make their beginning answer continually to her gaze.

The act is emotionally costly. Immediately afterward she admits:

> 「４月から…また、ひとりか…」

The withdrawal is not effortless detachment. Kanon is choosing the successors’ autonomy while feeling the solitude that choice creates.

---

# VIII. Kanon’s private guitar song: solitude transformed into accompaniment

Kanon begins playing guitar alone in her room.

The lyrics include:

> 「ほんのちょっぴり かなしいときなら」
> “When you’re just a little sad…”

> 「せすじのばして こえをとばせば」
> “Straighten your back and send your voice outward…”

> 「いつでもそばで ひかりをくれた。うた」
> “Song was always beside me and gave me light.”

> 「てをつなごう」
> “Let’s hold hands.”

This is one of the finale’s most important uses of voice.

The arrangement is spare: guitar, Kanon, and the domestic room. Her singing is not functioning as:

-   tournament proof;

-   audition;

-   center performance;

-   institutional evaluation;

-   or formal gift to an audience.


It is how she processes being alone.

That brings the series back to the earliest Kanon, who could sing privately but lost her voice when judged. Yet the meaning of private song has changed.

Early private singing represented a split:

> the true singer exists only when nobody is looking.

This private singing represents integration:

> the public singer, leader, friend, graduate, and frightened daughter can still sing when nobody is looking.

She no longer needs an audience to validate the voice.

She also no longer experiences solitude as proof of abandonment. The song itself contains everyone who taught her what voice could do.

Her private voice is now relational even when physically alone.

---

# IX. The new six: Liella! already has a functioning successor culture

The new Liella! practice scene distributes the future with remarkable efficiency.

## Wien and Tomari: two forms of severity

Tomari says calmly:

> 「私とマルガレーテでメニューを作ったのですが…」

Wien follows:

> 「今までがたるんでいたのよ」
> 「来年はこの６人で優勝しなきゃいけないんだから」

Tomari’s authority is procedural. She reports that they constructed a program and is genuinely puzzled that it seems unusually hard.

Wien’s authority is evaluative and competitive. She declares the old standard inadequate and invokes the necessity of victory.

Their voices remain distinct:

**Tomari:** measured, neutral, explanatory.
**Wien:** projected, categorical, impatient.

Together they form a powerful training apparatus:

> Tomari can systematize the route.
> Wien ensures the destination remains difficult.

## Kinako and Natsumi: comic bodily protest

Kinako collapses into:

> 「疲れたっす～！」
> 「はひぃ～！」

Natsumi cries:

> 「いきなり練習がハードすぎですの～！」

Their speech markers survive physical exhaustion:

-   Kinako’s 「〜っす」;

-   Natsumi’s melodic 「ですの」.


The contrast with Tomari and Wien makes the six sound like an actual group rather than an abstract succession chart. The future Liella! will remain full of incompatible bodily and vocal rhythms.

## Mei: the new club president

Mei declares:

> 「なめんな！ 私はまだまだやれるぞ！」

Shiki immediately asks:

> 「どうする？ 部長」

Mei’s response:

> 「…あ。部長…誰が？」
> 「はい～⁉」

Her voice moves from rough confidence into high, flustered disbelief. As in her original recruitment episode, the rough register proves less stable when she is directly entrusted with something she wants.

Everyone has already chosen her.

Mei protests until she makes one condition:

> 「お前が副部長になるなら、私が部長、引き受けてやる！」

This is a mature version of her earlier confession:

> 「四季が近くにいてくれたら、頑張れそうな気がするんだ」

Mei does not have to disguise Shiki as the reason she cannot pursue a role. She openly makes Shiki’s partnership the condition under which she can accept it.

Connection no longer obstructs agency. It supports it.

## Shiki: rare warmth in minimal language

Shiki answers:

> 「部長の頼みなら…喜んで」

The pause before 「喜んで」 allows a little more feeling through than her usual compressed statements.

She remains characteristically restrained, but the content is openly affectionate:

> If Mei asks, she will gladly stand beside her.

The science-room dyad has become an institutional leadership pair.

## Natsumi: successor producer

Natsumi immediately declares:

> 「プロデュースは夏美にお任せですの～！」

She has found the place where OniNatsu’s media instincts belong.

She will not merely film the next Liella! from outside. She will help shape how it reaches the world from within.

---

# X. The leadership succession is now complete

The six-person Liella! has a surprisingly coherent division of labor:

No one simply becomes “the new Kanon.”

That is one of the finale’s wisest choices.

Liella! survives Kanon not by replacing her with a single equivalent center, but by distributing the functions the founding generation had concentrated around several members.

This is what institutional maturity looks like:

> leadership becomes plural.

---

# XI. The original five reunite because they hear Kanon’s condition

Keke asks Sumire, Ren, and Chisato to help cheer Kanon up.

Chisato enters with a playful slow approach and sudden:

> 「そぉ～…わぁっ！」

Sumire assesses Kanon bluntly:

> 「案外普通ね」

Ren explains:

> 「声を聞いたら、何だって伝わります」
> 「３年間、一緒に過ごしてきたのですから」

This is almost a direct statement of the method behind the audio-integrated analysis.

Ren does not say they understood Kanon from the content of her words. Keke heard her voice on the telephone.

Voice carries:

-   concealed fear;

-   hesitation;

-   forced brightness;

-   loneliness;

-   and what a speaker is trying not to request.


After three years, the original five possess a shared acoustic intimacy. They know one another’s ordinary baselines well enough to hear deviation.

That is what makes a relationship audible.

---

# XII. The rooftop: memory without reenactment

The five return to the rooftop and remember:

-   how bad they initially were;

-   Keke’s lack of stamina;

-   how much they practiced;

-   how frightened they were;

-   and how often they said 「きっと大丈夫」 without evidence.


They recognize something important about that phrase.

It had no factual guarantee behind it.

Yet it gave them:

> 「不思議な力」

Sumire’s practical skepticism and Keke’s belief no longer conflict here. They understand that encouragement need not be a prediction to be effective.

> “It will be all right” was not reliable information.
> It was a relational act that made continued effort possible.

They then describe the path honestly:

> 「迷ったり、考えたり、立ち止まったり…」
> 「嫌なことがあったり、逃げ出したくなったり…」

Their success is not narrated as evidence that every earlier choice was secretly correct.

It is evidence that mistakes, detours, and fear can become part of a path without disqualifying it.

## Kanon refuses one final rehearsal

Someone suggests practicing a little.

Kanon answers:

> 「ううん。ここはもう、新しい『Liella!』のための場所」

This is perhaps the episode’s cleanest act of succession.

They are standing on the rooftop where the original group was formed and trained. Nostalgia makes reenactment tempting.

Kanon declines.

The rooftop is no longer theirs simply because their memories are attached to it.

They can carry the memories away. The physical space must remain usable by people whose future is not an imitation of theirs.

> **Inheritance requires the predecessor to stop occupying every symbolic place.**

---

# XIII. The overnight gathering: leave a song, not graffiti

The original five spend the night at school eating Chisato’s takoyaki hotpot.

The scene allows them to sound less like legendary founders and more like longtime friends:

-   blowing on hot food together;

-   praising Chisato;

-   worrying about whether they have permission;

-   teasing Ren about becoming “RenRen director”;

-   and imagining leaving a mark on the blackboard.


Ren explains that she will attend university in preparation for working in Yuigaoka’s administration. Her institutional arc will continue beyond graduation.

The girl who once treated school as the fixed remnant of her mother’s will now wants to participate in its ongoing governance.

## The song begins as an ethical alternative to possession

Sumire proposes writing:

> 「一期生参上！」

Kanon objects that they should not cause trouble.

The founders want to leave something behind, but the episode refuses a territorial mark. Instead, Kanon suggests:

> 「この５人で、ここで曲を作るっていうのはどう？」

A song is the correct trace because it can be:

-   received;

-   reinterpreted;

-   shared;

-   performed by others;

-   and expanded.


Graffiti says:

> We were here, and this surface belongs to our memory.

Song says:

> We were here, and this can become yours too.

Kanon says there are people to whom she wants to show the live.

The final song is thus born not as self-memorialization, but as a gift to successors.

---

# XIV. Graduation morning: six different ways to cry

The younger members’ emotional performance is almost a compressed vocal anthology.

## Kinako: complete emotional transparency

Kinako openly wails:

> 「先輩たち、いなくなっちゃうっす～！」

She proposes asking them to repeat a year.

Her crying is expansive and unregulated. The same sincerity that made her an accessible recruit now makes separation impossible to disguise.

## Natsumi: persona-assisted control

Natsumi says:

> 「今から泣いてどうするんですの～！」

But she is also audibly affected.

The 「ですの」 persona helps her remain functional. As in the opening of the episode, OniNatsu has become a structure for acting through emotion rather than denying its existence.

## Mei: rough grief

Mei says:

> 「分かってたことだろ…！」

When Shiki points out that Mei is crying too, Mei snaps:

> 「うるせ～！」

Her roughness cannot conceal the tears, but concealment is no longer really the purpose. Everyone knows she is crying. The harsh phrase is simply the form grief naturally takes in Mei’s mouth.

## Shiki: dry observational intimacy

> 「メイ、泣きすぎ」

Shiki does not console Mei through elaborate reassurance. She names what is happening and remains beside her.

The line recalls her first photographs of Mei sobbing over school idols, but the context has transformed completely. Mei’s tears are now shared group knowledge rather than an incriminating secret.

## Wien: practical excuse

Asked why she has attended despite first-years being free to skip the ceremony, Wien says:

> 「暇だったしね」

The excuse is transparently inadequate.

It belongs to her familiar grammar:

> meaningful action
> → minimal practical explanation
> → refusal to name attachment.

## Tomari: the “large task”

Tomari says she has one large task to complete.

Only later do we learn that it is asking Kanon for her ribbon.

She converts a deeply personal request into task language so she can continue approaching it without becoming overwhelmed.

---

# XV. Sumire’s graduation styling: the performer refuses understatement

Sumire arrives with an elaborate hairstyle.

Keke observes:

> 「盛り盛りデス…」

Sumire answers:

> 「卒業式だもん。このくらい当然でしょ？」

This is perfectly consistent with her mature identity.

The girl who once feared being reduced to background refuses to leave Yuigaoka looking visually ordinary. Graduation is a stage, and she will honor it through presentation.

The impulse is no longer desperate.

She does not need the hairstyle to prove that she deserved to be seen. She wants the day to look as important as it feels.

---

# XVI. Ren receives Yuigaoka’s first diploma

Ren is called first:

> 「第１期生 音楽科１組 葉月恋」

The director initially reads the formal text:

> 「あなたは、本校が定めた…」

Then changes it:

> 「いや…あなたの母、葉月花が定めた課程を修了したことを証します」

Ren gives a small:

> 「あっ…」

This tiny voice is more revealing than a speech would have been.

Ren’s cultivated register disappears for a moment. She is not:

-   student council president;

-   school representative;

-   formal respondent;

-   or institutional heir.


She is Hana’s daughter.

Her mother’s dream has become concrete enough to award its first diploma to her own child.

The director then says:

> 「おめでとう」

This completes Ren’s foundational conflict.

She once mistook preservation of her mother’s dream for controlling what the school could become.

Now she graduates from a school whose success came from allowing students to create meanings Hana could never have predetermined.

Inheritance has replaced obedience.

---

# XVII. Kinako’s farewell address: the beginner acquires a public voice

Kinako’s ordinary speech is marked by:

> 「〜っす」

The suffix gives her voice humility, regional texture, and beginner warmth.

At the podium, it disappears.

She adopts a formal ceremonial register:

> 「在校生一同、心よりお祝い申し上げます」

The change is not erasure of personality. It shows expanded capacity. Kinako can now speak as the representative of the continuing school.

She recounts:

-   the founders’ first Love Live;

-   their Tokyo second-place finish;

-   their failure to reach nationals;

-   the frustration she witnessed;

-   and the way they overcame it.


Her address does not reduce the seniors to champions. She identifies their formative defeat as the moment through which their character became instructive.

Then:

> 「そんな先輩方の気持ちを背負って、私たちはこれからも頑張ります」

Kinako accepts inheritance without claiming that the successors will replicate the same path.

They will carry the feeling forward.

## Vocal significance

Kinako spent much of Season 2 sounding as though she was apologizing for occupying space. Here she sustains formal public speech while visibly emotional.

The voice does not cease being soft. It becomes institutionally audible.

The girl who made Liella! reachable to new members becomes the person who speaks for those who remain.

---

# XVIII. Ren’s response: formality becomes communal philosophy

Ren’s answer is delivered in the highly structured register that has defined her from the beginning:

> 「わたくしたちは…」

Complete syntax, deliberate pacing, careful formal vocabulary, and a public cadence remain intact.

But the philosophy inside that voice has completely changed.

She recalls that the first students entered:

-   with no upperclassmen;

-   into a slightly lonely school;

-   with anxiety and hope;

-   and with no road before them.


Then:

> 「その道が、常に正しかったとは思いません」
> “I do not believe that path was always correct.”

> 「時に間違い、時に回り道をして」
> “At times we were mistaken; at times we took detours.”

This is a profound statement coming from Ren.

The early Ren sought correctness through inherited authority. The graduating Ren publicly acknowledges that institutional life is made through fallible movement.

She credits the younger students:

> 「後から入ってきた在校生の皆さんに背中を押され」
> “We were pushed forward by the students who came after us.”

The founders did not simply create Yuigaoka for their juniors.

The juniors also completed the founders’ experience of Yuigaoka.

Finally:

> 「ここにいる全員の一歩一歩が、結ヶ丘をつくっていきます」
> “Every step taken by everyone here will create Yuigaoka.”

And:

> 「それが、羽ばたくわたくしたちの力となっていく」
> “That will become the strength of those of us taking flight.”

Ren’s formal voice no longer defines the institution from above.

It recognizes the institution as an accumulation of everyone’s movement.

This completes her transformation from gatekeeper to steward.

---

# XIX. The group photograph: history made ordinary

After the ceremony, the girls gather for a photograph.

Natsumi calls:

> 「いきますの～！」

Then everyone performs the familiar:

> 「ラブライブ！」

The photograph contains:

-   graduates;

-   continuing members;

-   friends;

-   rivals;

-   and the full historical accumulation of Yuigaoka’s school-idol world.


The image is important because the finale does not preserve the founders primarily through a heroic monument.

It preserves them through a photograph full of:

-   imperfect poses;

-   tears;

-   laughter;

-   and crowded bodies.


The legend becomes personal memory.

---

# XX. The cherry-blossom farewell: emotional styles remain distinct

Everyone begins crying.

Wien insists:

> 「泣いてない！」

Mei immediately points out the obvious contradiction.

Wien’s delivery is forceful and elevated, but it contains none of her earlier contempt. She is not rejecting the value of emotion. She is embarrassed that the emotion is publicly legible.

That is an enormous difference.

Season 2 Wien used strength claims to prevent others from treating her as wounded.

Graduating-scene Wien merely refuses to admit that she is crying while standing among other crying friends.

The defense has become socially ordinary.

---

# XXI. Tomari asks Kanon for her ribbon

Tomari approaches:

> 「かのん先輩！」

Then, after a pause:

> 「リボン…リボン下さい！」

The repetition matters. Her carefully managed syntax briefly fails.

She explains:

> 「ずっと憧れてました…」
> 「スクールアイドルを始めた時から…」

This revises how we understand her Season 3 entrance.

Tomari did not investigate school idols only because she feared for Natsumi. She also admired Kanon from the beginning.

She could not initially integrate that admiration with:

-   her distrust of unprofitable dreams;

-   her fear that school idols would hurt Natsumi;

-   and her own self-presentation as a neutral evaluator.


The ribbon request is the first time she asks Kanon for something that has:

-   no measurable utility;

-   no professional return;

-   no procedural necessity;

-   and enormous personal meaning.


It is a symbolic object.

Tomari is learning that value may reside in a relationship precisely where calculation cannot prove it.

## 「アグリーです！」

Kanon gives her the ribbon and asks:

> 「これからもよろしくね。Liella!のこと」

Tomari answers:

> 「アグリーです！」

Her use of English maintains the compact, slightly technical flavor of her established voice. She does not transform into an emotionally expansive speaker merely because the scene is moving.

She accepts the inheritance in her own linguistic form.

That is better characterization than having her suddenly deliver a conventionally sentimental promise.

---

# XXII. Kanon entrusts Liella! to Wien

Kanon says:

> 「マルガレーテちゃんも、よろしくね」

Wien replies:

> 「言われなくたって」

There is no long declaration and no huff.

The line means:

> You do not need to ask.
> I have already accepted this responsibility.
> I refuse to make the acceptance sound ceremonially tender.

This is the final form of Wien’s tsun grammar.

Season 2 Wien stood outside Liella! demanding proof of superiority.

Season 3 Wien stands inside its future treating protection of the group as self-evident.

She no longer needs to say that Liella! is the enemy.

She no longer needs Kanon’s invitation to define her place.

---

# XXIII. Kinako’s final fear: “You will disappear”

Kinako cannot accept the abstraction of graduation.

She says:

> 「行っちゃったら終わりなんすよね…？」
> 「いなくなっちゃうんすよね⁉」

She imagines entering:

-   the clubroom;

-   the classroom;

-   the familiar school spaces;


and not finding the seniors there.

This is more concrete than saying she will miss them. Kinako fears that places will become evidence of absence.

Kanon answers:

> 「大丈夫。いなくならないよ」
> 「私たちは、ずっと一緒」

Then qualifies what “together” means:

> 「顔が見れなくても、声が聞けなくても」
> “Even when we cannot see one another’s faces or hear one another’s voices…”

> 「一緒に過ごした時間は、その思い出は、なくならない」
> “The time we spent together—the memories—will not disappear.”

This is not a denial of physical separation.

Kanon explicitly acknowledges:

-   faces may not be visible;

-   voices may not be audible;

-   ordinary contact will change.


Continuity is not identical to uninterrupted access.

The relationship has become part of the person carrying it.

This is the same psychological development Episode 11 gave Kanon when she learned that “not alone” did not require everyone to stand physically beside her.

She now teaches that principle to Kinako.

---

# XXIV. The seniors’ final principles and the successors’ answers

The farewell becomes a compressed exchange of inherited philosophies.

## Kanon → Kinako

> 「あなたの歌を響かせて」
> “Let your song resound.”

Kinako answers:

> 「全力で響かせるっす！」
> “I’ll make it resound with everything I have!”

This is the central succession.

Kanon does not ask Kinako to sing Kanon’s song.

She says:

> **your song.**

Kinako, once overwhelmed by the mere possibility of becoming an idol, now answers with total commitment.

## Keke → Shiki

> 「自分の大好きを大切にするのデス」
> “Treasure the thing you love.”

The response:

> 「大好きを心の中に！」
> “Keep what you love inside your heart!”

Keke’s school-idol faith passes toward one of the cast’s quietest speakers.

Shiki’s love has always been less loudly expressed than Keke’s:

-   love for Mei;

-   interest in school idols;

-   pleasure in participation;

-   attachment to the group.


The inheritance is not “become as loud as Keke.”

It is:

> Do not deny the love merely because your mode of expression is quieter.

## Sumire → Wien

> 「諦めなければ、何も終わらないから」
> “As long as you don’t give up, nothing ends.”

Wien answers:

> 「諦めるわけないでしょ！」
> “There’s no way I’d give up!”

This is the most obvious temperament match.

Sumire and Wien are both girls whose ambition survived repeated refusal:

-   background roles and failed recognition for Sumire;

-   academy rejection and family hierarchy for Wien.


Sumire’s mature principle is not that everyone will eventually be chosen.

It is that refusal does not have to author the ending.

Wien answers in exactly the forceful register with which she should inherit that idea.

## Chisato → Mei

> 「足を止めちゃダメだよ」
> “Don’t stop moving.”

Mei answers:

> 「最後まで走り続けてみせる！」
> “I’ll keep running until the very end!”

Chisato’s entire life is built around bodily persistence:

-   dance;

-   training;

-   becoming strong;

-   structuring support;

-   and turning attachment into movement.


Mei, the new club president, receives a leadership principle stated through the body.

Her response retains her rough force, but it is no longer defensive. It is a vow.

## Ren → Natsumi and Tomari

> 「夢をみることを忘れないで」
> “Do not forget how to dream.”

They answer:

> 「絶対に忘れないですの！」
> 「絶対に忘れません！」

This is the most thematically loaded pairing.

Natsumi once believed repeated failure had revoked her right to dream.

Tomari treated dreams as dangerous, irrational exposures likely to hurt Natsumi again.

Ren—whose own life was once trapped inside a misread inherited dream—tells them to keep dreaming without allowing the dream to become rigid doctrine.

The sisters answer in their distinct registers:

-   Natsumi’s emotional, branded 「ですの」;

-   Tomari’s formal 「ません」.


They remain different while making the same promise.

---

# XXV. The final song: the first Liella! song becomes the last anime song

The five graduates announce:

> 「これが、私たち３年生の、スクールアイドルとして歌う最後の歌」
> “This is the last song we third-years will sing as school idols.”

Then:

> 「私たちの、そして、みんなにおくる…始まりの歌！」
> “A song of beginning—from us, and for everyone!”

The song is:

> **「始まりは君の空 ～11 Ver.～」**

At the franchise level, this is Liella!’s original debut song.

Within the anime, it functions as the song the founding five create at school before graduation.

That double position is perfect:

> Liella!’s first real-world song becomes its final television-anime song.
> The song of beginning becomes a graduation song.
> The five-member song becomes an eleven-member song.

The series does not close with a newly written song about endings.

It returns to beginning.

---

# XXVI. Why 「始まりは君の空」 is the correct final song

The lyrics begin:

> 「なにか目覚めそうだよ」
> “Something feels as though it may awaken.”

> 「まだ名もないキモチが」
> “A feeling that does not yet have a name…”

This was the language of girls before their future became legible.

At graduation, the lines acquire a different meaning. The original five now know what awakened:

-   Kanon’s recovered voice;

-   Keke’s vocation;

-   Chisato’s leadership;

-   Sumire’s right to center;

-   Ren’s living inheritance;

-   Liella!;

-   Yuigaoka’s school-idol tradition.


But the younger members remain inside the condition of unnamed future feeling.

The song therefore speaks simultaneously from:

-   remembered beginning;

-   present farewell;

-   and future possibility.


## 「あとは勇気ときっかけ」

> “All that remains is courage and an opportunity.”

Keke was Kanon’s opportunity.

Kanon became an opportunity for the others.

The continuing Liella! will become an opportunity for people the founders have never met.

The mechanism outlives the origin.

## 「一歩目を一緒に飛ぼうよ」

> “Let’s take that first leap together.”

This is the series in one line.

*Superstar!!* repeatedly insists that first steps are relational:

-   Kanon and Keke;

-   Kanon and Chisato;

-   Mei and Shiki;

-   Natsumi borrowing Liella!’s dream;

-   Wien singing with Kanon after the goal became impossible;

-   Tomari entering to understand Natsumi;

-   the new six accepting responsibility together.


## 「手をつないでおけば…怖くない！」

> “As long as we hold hands…we aren’t afraid!”

Episode 11 complicated this principle by showing Kanon that visible physical support cannot be the only condition under which she can sing.

The finale gives the mature interpretation:

> Holding hands does not always mean physical presence.
> It can mean carrying words, memories, practices, and relationships inside oneself.

---

# XXVII. The vocal structure: five voices open into eleven

The arrangement initially foregrounds the original five.

The familiar pairings are preserved:

-   Chisato and Ren;

-   Sumire and Keke;

-   Kanon as the organizing central voice.


The first section allows us to hear the founding group after three years of development. The song’s youthful optimism is no longer naïve. The same words now pass through voices that have experienced:

-   defeat;

-   expansion;

-   family conflict;

-   rivalry;

-   separation;

-   championship;

-   and graduation.


The bright melodic language remains, but the emotional knowledge underneath it has deepened.

Then the arrangement expands.

## Kinako receives the sky

Kinako sings:

> 「空に描くのは」

Kanon and Kinako then sing together:

> 「どんな どんな夢か教えて」

This is the central vocal handoff.

Kanon does not disappear before Kinako sings.

They overlap.

Succession is represented not as replacement but as temporary unison:

> the old center and emerging successor share a line until the new voice is securely inside the song.

## Keke and Sumire remain paired

They sing:

> 「星に願うまえに」

Their partnership survives graduation and points toward their shared professional future.

## Mei and Shiki sing together

> 「語りあってみようよ」

The assignment is almost an explicit summary of their relationship.

Their original problem was that each tried to decide what was best for the other without speaking honestly.

Their mature inheritance is:

> talk it through.

## Chisato and Ren remain together

> 「トキメキを」

The two characters responsible for Liella!’s bodily and institutional durability carry the emotional spark forward in unison.

## Natsumi, Wien, and Tomari sing together

> 「分かちあいたいよ」
> “I want to share it.”

This line belongs to the Season 3 trio whose original problems all involved resisting shared value:

-   Natsumi once converted experience into monetizable content.

-   Wien treated song as proof of solitary excellence.

-   Tomari doubted that unprofitable emotional activity had value.


They now sing:

> We want to share this excitement.

That is perhaps the most concentrated musical statement of their development.

---

# XXVIII. Singing identities in the final ensemble

The eleven-member arrangement does not erase vocal individuality.

## Kanon

Her voice remains the melodic and emotional center, but it no longer monopolizes the song’s interpretive authority. She introduces and then shares.

## Keke

Her bright forward energy still communicates public belief. Even within the larger blend, her vocal presence gives upward propulsion to phrases about shared excitement.

## Chisato

Her smoother, steadier delivery supports ensemble continuity. She sounds like the member who helps phrases land inside the collective rather than pushing herself ahead of it.

## Sumire

Her stage-trained projection gives declarative clarity. She sounds comfortable being heard without needing to exaggerate “Galaxy” theatricality.

## Ren

Her cultivated, controlled timbre gives ceremonial weight to the shared phrases. The formality that once separated her now enriches the ensemble’s texture.

## Kinako

Her softer earnestness makes the inherited lines feel newly vulnerable. She does not sound like a substitute Kanon; she sounds like someone still discovering the scale of her own voice.

## Mei

Her speaking voice is rough, but her singing channels that force into direct emotional attack. She contributes heat without the defensive aggression of her earliest scenes.

## Shiki

Her more restrained vocal presence works especially well in paired material. She does not compete for sonic dominance; she gives contrast and clarity.

## Natsumi

Her performative brightness becomes musically productive. The energy once used for OniNatsu branding now contributes genuine ensemble color.

## Wien

Her polished, technically confident voice remains distinct. Crucially, that distinction no longer sounds as though it is attempting to stand above the others. It sharpens the ensemble.

## Tomari

Her controlled speaking identity carries into a clean, composed singing presence. In the trio line, she no longer occupies the role of skeptical observer. She is sonically inside the shared claim.

# XXIX. Completed cumulative voice profile: Shibuya Kanon

## Initial state

Kanon’s early voice was divided:

-   free and warm when singing privately;

-   blocked under judgment;

-   dry and self-minimizing in ordinary speech;

-   defensive around praise;

-   often braver for others than for herself.


## Mature state

By the finale she can move among:

-   anxious daughter;

-   affectionate sister;

-   domestic friend;

-   private songwriter;

-   institutional founder;

-   public mentor;

-   international student;

-   and ensemble singer


without one mode invalidating the others.

She still becomes frightened. She still complains, elongates endings, and seeks reassurance. Growth has not replaced her personality with perfect leader composure.

Her mature strength is integration.

## Final vocal thesis

> **Kanon’s voice began as something she could access only in safety. It ends as a portable home: a voice that carries other people within it and can create safety for those who will sing after she leaves.**

---

# XXX. Completed cumulative voice profile: Tang Keke

Keke remains the cast’s most uninhibited believer.

Her voice is:

-   bright;

-   quick;

-   emotionally immediate;

-   marked by her stylized 「デス」;

-   capable of moving instantly from tears to plans to outrage.


What changes is the time horizon.

She begins by wanting school idols now.

She ends by creating a transnational adult structure through which she can keep singing later.

Her scheduling frenzy toward Sumire shows that belief has become professional labor.

## Final vocal thesis

> **Keke’s voice gives dreams permission to exist, then refuses to let adulthood become the excuse that ends them.**

---

# XXXI. Completed cumulative voice profile: Arashi Chisato

Chisato’s gentle baseline remains stable across the series:

-   warm;

-   relaxed;

-   familiar;

-   lightly playful;

-   rarely loud without reason.


Underneath it lies exceptional will.

She becomes:

-   Kanon’s developmental challenger;

-   club president;

-   choreographer;

-   ensemble architect;

-   and the person capable of letting physical spaces pass to successors.


Her finale voice is neither possessive nor mournfully withdrawn. She creates food, jokes, remembers, writes a song, and tells Mei to keep moving.

## Final vocal thesis

> **Chisato’s soft voice is structural strength. She turns love into movement, then makes that movement durable enough to continue without her.**

---

# XXXII. Completed cumulative voice profile: Heanna Sumire

Sumire’s theatricality survives intact:

-   grand self-presentation;

-   sharp indignation;

-   competitive challenge;

-   visible pride;

-   and comic huffs.


But the wound beneath it has healed enough that theatricality can become enjoyment rather than emergency self-validation.

Her final advice to Wien proves what her story became:

> Do not allow refusal to create your ending.

## Final vocal thesis

> **Sumire’s voice began by loudly asserting that she was special because nobody else would. It ends as the voice that teaches another fiercely ambitious girl how to continue without needing every setback to become humiliation.**

---

# XXXIII. Completed cumulative voice profile: Hazuki Ren

Ren’s formal register changes less acoustically than almost anyone else’s.

That is why its ethical transformation is so powerful.

The same:

-   complete syntax;

-   careful pacing;

-   refined diction;

-   「わたくし」;

-   ceremonial projection


moves from exclusion to gratitude, from inherited prohibition to shared stewardship.

Her tiny 「あっ」 during the diploma scene reveals the daughter inside the institution.

Her answer restores the institutional voice, now capable of admitting mistakes and crediting juniors.

## Final vocal thesis

> **Ren does not abandon formal speech to become emotionally authentic. She makes formal speech capable of carrying humility, plurality, memory, and love.**

---

# XXXIV. Completed cumulative voice profile: Sakurakōji Kinako

Kinako’s voice retains:

-   warmth;

-   softness;

-   open fear;

-   uncomplicated admiration;

-   and her characteristic 「〜っす」.


The finale demonstrates that she can also enter a formal public register when the institution needs her.

She remains emotionally transparent afterward, sobbing that the seniors will disappear.

That is not regression. Public competence and private vulnerability coexist.

## Final vocal thesis

> **Kinako makes inheritance approachable. She speaks with enough humility to welcome beginners and enough acquired authority to represent those who remain.**

---

# XXXV. Completed cumulative voice profile: Yoneme Mei

Mei’s rough register undergoes one of the series’ clearest functional transformations.

It begins as:

-   concealment;

-   rejection prevention;

-   embarrassment armor;

-   denial of school-idol love.


It becomes:

-   artistic conscience;

-   group protection;

-   leadership;

-   public devotion;

-   and a vow to keep running.


She still rises into flustered disbelief when entrusted with authority, but she can now openly say she needs Shiki beside her.

## Final vocal thesis

> **Mei does not become softer by losing roughness. Roughness becomes honest once it no longer has to hide what she loves.**

---

# XXXVI. Completed cumulative voice profile: Wakana Shiki

Shiki remains the most economical speaker.

Her language is often:

-   one-word observations;

-   brief declaratives;

-   minimal social padding;

-   emotionally precise facts.


Her finale role confirms that this economy is valuable leadership.

She identifies Mei as president, accepts the vice presidency, and gives a rare:

> 「喜んで」

Her love remains quiet, but no longer ambiguous to the people around her.

## Final vocal thesis

> **Shiki’s quiet voice is not emotional absence. It is concentrated recognition—and the group has learned how to hear it.**

---

# XXXVII. Completed cumulative voice profile: Onitsuka Natsumi

Natsumi’s OniNatsu voice survives every stage:

-   high;

-   melodic;

-   branded;

-   quick;

-   visually and commercially aware;

-   built around 「ですの」.


The series does not reveal a “real Natsumi” by discarding it.

Instead, the persona becomes increasingly integrated with:

-   responsibility;

-   artistic seriousness;

-   sisterly love;

-   group production;

-   and dream.


In the finale she can complain, cry, promise, produce, and inherit without changing into another person.

## Final vocal thesis

> **Natsumi’s performed voice began as a way to control how others saw her. It ends as a tool through which she helps many different people become visible together.**

---

# XXXVIII. Completed cumulative voice profile: Wien Margarete

Wien’s severe speaking voice remains:

-   sharply articulated;

-   feminine without softness;

-   categorical;

-   impatient with weak standards;

-   highly sensitive to pity.


But its social function transforms.

Her final episode contains:

-   practical correction;

-   autobiographical reassurance;

-   demanding training leadership;

-   transparent attendance excuses;

-   tear denial;

-   and tacit acceptance of Liella!’s future.


She does not become generically sweet.

She becomes relational without surrendering distinction.

## Final vocal thesis

> **Wien’s voice began as a hierarchy spoken aloud so failure could not define her. It ends as artistic severity embedded within friendship, responsibility, and a group she no longer needs to call an enemy.**

---

# XXXIX. Completed cumulative voice profile: Onitsuka Tomari

Tomari’s controlled register remains:

-   procedural;

-   complete;

-   low in ornament;

-   causal;

-   formally defensible;

-   reluctant to state unmeasurable desire.


Her finale gives her the most non-utilitarian request of her story:

> Kanon’s ribbon.

She stammers, repeats herself, and admits longstanding admiration.

Then she accepts responsibility in her own register:

> 「アグリーです！」

She does not lose analytical identity. She expands what analysis is permitted to coexist with.

## Final vocal thesis

> **Tomari’s voice began by asking whether dreams justify their cost. It ends by accepting that some forms of value must be requested, inherited, and protected before they can be calculated.**

# XL. The complete longitudinal map

The ten selected audio episodes now form several clear vocal arcs.

## Kanon: S1E1 → S1E11 → S3E12

**S1E1:** Voice exists privately but collapses under judgment.
**S1E11:** She internalizes support and sings alone.
**S3E12:** She can leave home, sing privately, sing publicly, and pass the organizing voice to others.

## Keke and Sumire: S1E10 → S3E6 → S3E12

**S1E10:** Keke’s trust allows Sumire to remain center.
**S3E6:** Keke’s dream is protected from being sealed as youth.
**S3E12:** They carry dream and show business into adult professional partnership.

## Mei and Shiki: S2E4 → S3E12

**S2E4:** Each uses the other as a reason not to state desire.
**S3E12:** Mei openly accepts leadership on the condition that Shiki stand beside her; Shiki gladly accepts.

## Natsumi and Tomari: S2E6 → S3E2 → S3E8 → S3E12

**S2E6:** Natsumi rediscovers permission to dream.
**S3E2:** Tomari investigates whether that dream has value.
**S3E8:** The sisters learn that protection requires communication, not unilateral prevention.
**S3E12:** Together they inherit Ren’s injunction never to stop dreaming.

## Wien: S2E11 → S3E2 → S3E8 → S3E12

**S2E11:** Song is proof; audience disagreement is invalid.
**S3E2:** Song becomes enjoyable again after measurable success is impossible.
**S3E8:** Distinct talent voluntarily joins ensemble strength.
**S3E12:** Wien reassures Kanon through lived connection and assumes responsibility for Liella! without requiring formal persuasion.

# Final synthesis

The finale’s greatest achievement is that it does not treat continuity as the prevention of loss.

Something really does end.

The founders will not:

-   train on the rooftop every day;

-   sit in the same classrooms;

-   share the clubroom in the same way;

-   compete in another Love Live;

-   or remain physically accessible to the younger members.


Kanon does not invalidate Kinako’s fear by pretending otherwise.

Instead, the episode distinguishes:

> **ending from disappearance.**

A school-idol career ends.
A relationship changes form.
A physical space changes owners.
A group gains new leadership.
A song gains new singers.

None of those changes means the past has ceased to exist.

The final performance embodies that distinction perfectly.

The founding five sing a song of beginning.

Then the six successors enter the song.

Kanon and Kinako share a line.

Mei and Shiki speak through duet.

Natsumi, Wien, and Tomari sing about sharing excitement.

The title itself changes into:

> **「始まりは君の空 ～11 Ver.～」**

The first song is not preserved in glass.

It is revised to contain everyone whom the beginning eventually reached.

That produces the final answer to the entire series:

> Kanon recovers her voice because Keke hears it.
> That voice helps form Liella!.
> Liella! helps other girls find their voices.
> Those girls become capable of singing when Kanon is gone.
> Kanon then joins them one final time—not to prove that they still need her, but to hear that they no longer need her in the same way.

And that is why the finale is not fundamentally sad.

The original five leave.

The song does not.

> **A beginning becomes an inheritance when the people who received it can make it begin again.**
