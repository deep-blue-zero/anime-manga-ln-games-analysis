# Love Live! Superstar!! — Season 2 Frozen Checkpoint
## Prospective nine-member Liella! state at the S2E12 boundary

# 0. Freeze declaration

This document freezes the **prospective Season-2 understanding of *Love Live! Superstar!!*** after the canonical V2.2 sequential reading of `S2E01` through `S2E12` and **before any semantic evidence from S3E01 or later is admitted**.

Season 2 was not read retrospectively from Season 3. Each episode was admitted only after the prior semantic boundary was sealed, and each major claim transition was expressed against recoverable earlier authority rather than silently replacing it. The immutable Season-1 checkpoint remains the authoritative record of what was reasonable at S1E12; this document records how Season-2 evidence pressured that baseline and what is reasonable at S2E12.

The governing freeze rule is:

> **Season-2 endpoint claims may be revised by later evidence, but the fact that they were reasonable prospective conclusions at the S2E12 boundary must never be erased.**

A second rule is especially important because of the finale:

> **A self-authored decision and the institutional realization of that decision are different facts. Kanon genuinely chose Vienna at S2E12. The route was then externally canceled before enactment. Later explanation of the cancellation must never be used to rewrite Season 2 as though she never made that choice.**

This checkpoint therefore distinguishes:

1. **historical state** — a belief, defense, relationship condition, or behavioral rule that was genuinely active earlier;
2. **endpoint state** — the best-supported model at the S2E12 boundary;
3. **revision path** — how prior claims were preserved, strengthened, narrowed, revised, downgraded, rejected, or left open;
4. **prospective choice state** — what a character genuinely decided before later institutions or circumstances could alter the route;
5. **realized outcome state** — what actually occurred by the S2E12 ending;
6. **OPEN uncertainty** — anything Season 2 itself does not establish and that must not be filled using later knowledge.

No Season-3 plot, relationship consequence, cancellation explanation, membership state, competition result, or retrospective franchise knowledge is used here.

# 1. Executive Season-2 thesis

Season 2 begins by asking whether an already-successful group can remain **enterable** and ends by asking whether that group can remain **continuous** when one of its founders is preparing to leave.

The founding five begin the season as a healthy internal system whose very competence has become a social obstacle. To new students, Liella!'s excellence can look less like a developmental outcome and more like a prerequisite for admission. Kinako makes that problem visible first. The initial answer is not to lower standards or pretend everyone has equal skill. It is to reveal developmental history: the seniors were once incomplete too, competence was produced through practice and relationships, and a novice must be allowed to choose meaningful difficulty rather than having either harsh standards or paternalistic accommodation imposed on her.

That model is then stressed repeatedly.

- Kinako chooses a hard route and discovers that self-authored difficulty can still become self-blame after failure.
- Mei and Shiki show that access can fail before technique even begins when a person converts self-image into categorical non-eligibility: “I am not the kind of person suited for this.”
- Natsumi shows that aspiration itself can become unsafe after repeated disappointment, making money and measurable attention attractive as substitutes for vulnerable dreams.
- The first-year cohort shows that belonging can survive formal admission while hierarchy is recreated internally through senior comparison.
- The regional competition shows that inclusion must survive moments when exclusion looks strategically rational.
- The winter camp converts belonging into **co-authorship**: juniors do not merely stay in the group; they alter the competitive artifact itself.
- The Tokyo regional then gives that nine-member, cross-year-authored system a favorable ranked verdict.
- The national final gives it the highest available competitive verdict: Liella! becomes Love Live! champion as nine.

Season 2 therefore does not solve succession by flattening difference. Skill hierarchy, specialist authority, senior experience, and Kanon's unusual relational centrality all remain real. The season's stronger accomplishment is to make hierarchy **permeable** rather than sovereign. A junior can be less experienced and still possess decisive epistemic authority. A senior expert can retain mastery while creating a lane in which a junior changes the work. Formal office can separate from informal influence. Competitive optimization can be rejected when it turns less experienced members into optional bodies. Group identity can be constitutive in the present without requiring physical co-presence forever.

Kanon's own finale mirrors this architecture. S2E11 gives her a prestigious Vienna route and she genuinely refuses it because Yuigaoka and school idols have restored the meaning of singing. S2E12 does not expose that refusal as fake; it exposes it as mixed. Kanon eventually names a real fear of losing the place and people that restored her. Chisato challenges the weighting without claiming decision rights. Kanon's family refuses coercion. Wien offers candid counterevidence. Kanon then independently re-chooses Vienna as a route for growth, for representing Yuigaoka, and for making her singing reach farther.

Liella! in turn must imagine itself without her. The group initially states the emotionally true synchronic claim that the present nine are Liella! and one missing person would change that identity. Kanon does not deny it. She asks them to continue anyway. The result is a more mature distinction between **formation identity** and **organizational continuity**.

The season then denies everyone the comfort of treating authored choice as control. Kanon packs to leave. Her friends are already rehearsing continuity. Wien arrives and states that the study abroad has been canceled. A German letter exists; the reason is not established.

The strongest frozen Season-2 thesis is therefore:

> **Season 2 begins by asking whether an already-successful group can remain enterable and ends by asking whether that group can remain continuous when one of its founders is preparing to leave. Liella! becomes a nine-member national champion not by flattening expertise or eliminating hierarchy, but by making developmental history visible, pacing entry, protecting belonging through failure, permitting juniors to author real work, and preserving differentiated authority. Kanon's own endpoint mirrors that succession architecture: she can revise a fear-conditioned route choice after relational challenge without surrendering authorship, and she can ask Liella! to continue beyond her physical presence. The final cancellation matters because it separates a real self-authored decision from the institution's power to keep the chosen route open.**

Compactly:

> **Season 2 makes belonging, authorship, identity, choice, and outcome separable: people can belong before they are equal in skill; juniors can author before hierarchy disappears; a group can remain itself while preparing for change; and a person can truly choose a route that an institution later makes impossible.**

# 3. Season-2 developmental architecture

Season 2 can be divided into four analytical movements. These are not merely plot blocks; they describe changes in the level at which succession and self-authorship are being tested.

## 3.1 S2E01-S2E03 — From external prestige barrier to failure-safe belonging

S2E01 opens with the paradox created by successful seniors. Liella!'s competence is internally healthy but externally intimidating. Kinako encounters the group not as five people with a visible developmental history but as already-accomplished performers. The first succession task is therefore **developmental transparency**: show how competence was produced rather than presenting the finished state as the entrance requirement.

Kinako contributes an anti-fatalistic principle before she can fully apply it to herself: `やってもないのに 向いているかどうかなんて 分からないでしょ？`. That line becomes one of Season 2's governing ethical grammars. Eligibility cannot be known entirely in advance of trying.

S2E02 then shows that inclusion cannot be reduced to either “catch up to us” or “we will make everything easier for you.” The episode's strongest route is:

> **developmental history + individualized pacing + honest standards + junior agency in choosing the stakes.**

Kinako's willingness to choose the hard route matters because accessibility includes informed access to difficulty, not only protection from difficulty. But S2E03 supplies the necessary correction: self-authored difficulty does not make a novice's later causal diagnosis infallible. After competitive shortfall, Kinako becomes vulnerable to burden attribution. The group refuses to turn her into the explanation for failure.

This creates a stronger inclusion rule:

> **belonging is only real if it survives failure.**

S2E03 simultaneously introduces Wien as an external talent/ranking pressure and reactivates a narrower Kanon vulnerability: competitive scarcity can still produce discounting of positive non-result evidence. Importantly, the later season will narrow this vulnerability rather than pretending it never existed.

## 3.2 S2E04-S2E06 — Type-fit exclusion, cohort formation, manipulated separation, and reclaimed desire

S2E04 shifts access from “can I keep up?” to “am I the kind of person allowed to try?”

Mei is a devoted Liella! fan whose barrier is not lack of desire. She hides that desire and treats face/personality as categorical disqualification. Shiki enters through a strongly relational route and also describes school-idol-style expression as incompatible with herself. Chisato's newly formal presidency becomes behaviorally real when she first applies the anti-fatalistic rule to herself and then uses it with Mei: `やったこともないのに 「向いてない」は禁止だよ`.

Kanon's formal centrality and Chisato's office now separate cleanly. Kanon remains a major integrative node while Chisato owns club administration. This is not decorative delegation; S2E05-S2E08 repeatedly show Chisato making actual governance decisions.

Mei and Shiki join at S2E04, turning first-year affiliation into formal membership. Kinako ceases to be the sole junior and becomes a peer-facing access bridge.

S2E05 then relocates the access problem inside Liella!. The three first-years are members, but they compare themselves collectively with the second-years. Natsumi discovers that insecurity and deliberately amplifies it because separation gives her leverage. The causal chain is mixed rather than pure:

> **real comparison anxiety + Natsumi manipulation + genuine cohort desire for self-assessment + legitimate Chisato authorization.**

S2E06 refuses both easy interpretations. The juniors do not simply remain manipulated, and the manipulation does not become irrelevant. They reject Natsumi's attempt to redefine them as a separate sister-group identity; their object remains Liella!. The separation becomes partly **reclaimed self-authorship**.

Natsumi herself becomes the second major anti-fatalistic case. Repeated failed dreams have taught her to treat herself as someone for whom dreaming is unsafe. Money, views, and measurable production become a defensive fallback. S2E06 explains that architecture without erasing her exploitative methods. Her movement into Liella! occurs through a sequence of refusal, failed-dream disclosure, embodied trial, sustained effort, uncertainty about belonging, and finally the tentative discovery `見つけたかも… 私の…夢！`.

By the endpoint she is the ninth member.

## 3.3 S2E07-S2E09 — Co-bearing, task-contingent visibility, and the ethics of protective sacrifice

S2E07 returns to Ren's solitary-duty architecture in a lower-stakes but revealing form. She treats an ordinary private hobby as potentially incompatible with the role-pure student-council president who holds the school together. Kanon becomes vice president, but does not replace Ren. Liella! cooperatively beats a game boss explicitly framed as punishing for solo play while Ren still calls tactics.

The systems lesson is unusually literal:

> **co-bearing can reduce solitary burden without de-authoring expertise.**

S2E07 also demonstrates upward junior-to-senior mentorship. Mei's relationship history with Shiki lets her tell Ren that honest disclosure and even occasional conflict need not destroy friendship. Authority is therefore becoming increasingly **relevance-based**, not simply seniority-based.

S2E08 tests visibility under a preliminary format explicitly decided by viewer popularity. The episode differentiates spectacular scale, transactional attention capture, and relational invitation. Liella!'s chosen `Chance Way` strategy makes the ecology that produced the group visible rather than treating attention as a commodity to be extracted.

A durable rule emerges:

> **high visibility is a tool, not a permanent entitlement.**

Liella! should stand out when the competition structure requires visibility, but can recede at an open campus when its own performance would crowd out the other clubs the event is supposed to represent.

Kinako's contribution is especially important for succession. She does not become the most technically experienced creator. Her outsider experience of Tokyo becomes the most relevant knowledge in the room and helps Kanon identify the relational “roads/intersections” concept. This establishes that junior authority can be **load-bearing without pretending technical equality**.

S2E09 then supplies the hardest inclusion test. Keke conceals the Shanghai/result burden to protect the others' self-authored joy. Sumire privately carries the burden and attempts to optimize victory by excluding the first-years. The first-years sincerely offer to exclude themselves.

The episode rejects all three unilateral substitutions. The key rule is:

> **self-authored sacrifice can still be a bad governance outcome when the sacrifice substitutes one subgroup's judgment for collective choice.**

The group discloses the relevant stake, preserves nine-member Liella!, and all nine accept the increased cost together.

## 3.4 S2E10-S2E12 — Co-authorship, competitive validation, route self-authorship, continuity, and championship

S2E10 converts membership into production architecture.

Chisato and Shiki work on choreography; Ren and Mei on music; Kanon and Kinako on lyrics. Senior expertise remains visible and real, but the senior's role is not to own the task permanently. Each pairing creates a structured lane in which junior input changes the actual competitive artifact. By the end, the episode explicitly states that lyrics, choreography, and music are complete through those cross-year processes.

This is the season's strongest succession transition:

> **juniors do not merely belong to the future; they help author it.**

Wien simultaneously makes her ideology explicit: `歌は力`, and she will build her future herself through that power. Liella!'s reflective answer is situated rather than universal: for **them**, “true song” overflows from effort, difficulty, support, play, growth, and shared joy. Wien's `Edelstein` itself prevents a simplistic binary, because her song contains vulnerability and aspiration even when her dominant organizing logic is power/victory.

S2E11 resolves the Tokyo verdict. Liella! wins; Wien is second. Kanon does not call the result luck and does not treat Wien's individual superiority as more ontologically “real” than Liella!'s victory. This materially downgrades the S2E03 result-discounting vulnerability at the current boundary.

Wien's reaction finally makes `歌は力` biographically legible. She failed admission to the Vienna school associated with her older sisters; winning Love Live! was an institutional condition for a reconsideration route. Victory was therefore not merely ego gratification. It was a credential strategy for reversing exclusion.

The same institution then offers Kanon a fully supported place. Kanon refuses. The refusal is genuinely self-authored and grounded in Yuigaoka/school idols restoring singing as a loved practice. Chisato challenges the refusal, but S2E11 ends before the result of that challenge.

S2E12 protects the sequence rather than flattening it. Kanon insists `決めたのは私`, later admits `そんな大切な場所と 仲間を失ってしまうのが 正直 怖いんだ`, and then independently arrives at the opposite decision before returning to the group. She chooses Vienna as a route for challenge, growth, representing Yuigaoka, and making her singing reach farther.

Liella! then performs the national final as nine and wins Love Live!.

The final scene separates authored choice from institutional realization: Kanon has packed and prepared to depart, but Wien arrives with `留学は中止`. The cause is outside the admissible Season-2 evidence.

# 4. Nine-member Liella! endpoint at S2E12

Liella! at the Season-2 boundary is best modeled as a **differentiated, nine-member national-champion system with permeable authority and prospectively portable identity**.

## 4.1 Membership is equal; competence is not flat

Season 2 repeatedly rejects the equation:

> equal membership = equal skill = equal authority in every domain.

The second-years remain more experienced in many school-idol tasks. Chisato remains the strongest established dance/training specialist. Ren remains a highly developed composition/piano and institutional-administration resource. Kanon remains an unusually strong vocalist and integrative meaning-builder. Keke retains school-idol ecosystem/competition expertise. Sumire retains show-business/visibility expertise.

The first-years, however, are no longer merely trainees:

- Kinako becomes an access bridge, student-council participant, situated-knowledge contributor, and lyric co-author.
- Mei becomes a relationship mentor in a senior problem and a composition co-author.
- Shiki becomes a boundary enforcer, relational/environmental strategist, and choreography co-author.
- Natsumi becomes a media/production specialist whose competence is real even when its norms require governance.

The strongest frozen rule is:

> **authority is justified by relevance and problem-fit, not by a single seniority or protagonist hierarchy.**

## 4.2 Formal office and informal centrality remain distinct

Chisato's presidency is behaviorally real. Across the season she:

- calibrates practice to new members;
- authorizes junior autonomy experiments;
- observes progress remotely and raises hurdles when evidence changes;
- regulates recovery/rest;
- protects the distinction between trying and pre-emptive type-fit judgment;
- creates collaborative creative lanes.

Kanon remains the strongest informal integrative node, but she does not reclaim club presidency. Her later student-council vice presidency likewise demonstrates that S2E04's decentering was anti-concentration rather than anti-office.

By Season end:

> **formal authority, specialist authority, relational authority, epistemic authority, and symbolic/integrative centrality are multiple axes.**

## 4.3 Current nine are constitutive synchronically

When Kanon's departure becomes credible, the group initially says that without her Liella! would not be Liella!. Kanon herself agrees with the emotional premise that `一人でも欠けたら｢Liella！｣じゃない` and that these nine are Liella!.

This must be preserved as a real identity fact, not dismissed as melodrama.

## 4.4 Liella! is prospectively continuous diachronically

Kanon nevertheless asks the others to continue. They accept before learning that her departure will be canceled.

The frozen distinction is therefore:

> **the current nine-member formation is constitutive of the present Liella! identity, while the organization is prospectively capable of continuing through future formation change.**

This is only a **prospective continuity commitment**. Season 2 contains no actual extended eight-member operation after Kanon's departure because the departure does not occur within the seal.

Later evidence may test whether the group can enact what it was willing to imagine. It may not erase the fact that the willingness existed.

# 5. Character endpoint models

# 5.1 Shibuya Kanon — integrative centrality without self-erasure, route self-authorship without route control

## Endpoint state

Kanon ends Season 2 as:

- a founding member and unusually powerful relational/integrative node;
- a strong vocalist and creative synthesizer;
- student-council vice president but not school-idol club president;
- someone who can receive favorable competitive evidence without defaulting to `たまたま` or global self-negation;
- someone who can recognize a rival's superior individual ability without treating Liella!'s collective success as fraudulent;
- someone capable of revising a genuinely authored decision after identifying a previously underweighted fear;
- someone who genuinely chooses Vienna and prepares to leave;
- someone whose chosen route is externally canceled before enactment.

## Result-discounting transition

S2E03 established a narrow competitive legitimacy vulnerability. S2E10 provides strong counterevidence: Wien's overwhelming individual performance does not trigger self-discounting before the verdict. S2E11 provides stronger counterevidence: after Liella! wins Tokyo, Kanon accepts the favorable result and refuses Wien's invitation to invalidate it.

Frozen transition:

> **DOWNGRADE as a current default vulnerability; PRESERVE historically as a possible trigger-conditioned pattern.**

Do not claim cure under every future adverse result. Do not model Kanon as currently organized around chronic positive-evidence discounting.

## Vienna decision sequence — hard temporal lock

The exact Season-2 sequence is:

1. Kanon receives the fully supported Vienna offer.
2. She deliberately refuses it because Yuigaoka/school idols restored the meaning of singing and she wants the present school years.
3. Chisato challenges the refusal and argues the opportunity may be unique.
4. Kanon later admits separation fear: losing the place and people who restored her is `正直 怖い`.
5. Family response remains non-coercive.
6. Wien supplies additional information and candidly argues that Kanon can repay Yuigaoka by expanding its reach.
7. Kanon decides **before** the second school-night conversation that she will study abroad.
8. She publicly owns growth, challenge, representing Yuigaoka, and global reach as her reasons.
9. She formally confirms the choice to the headmistress.
10. She packs and prepares for departure.
11. Wien states the study abroad is canceled; a German letter exists.
12. Season 2 ends without establishing why.

Later analysis must never collapse 1-12 into “Kanon chose to stay.”

## Self-authorship rule

The strongest endpoint rule is:

> **an authored choice can be challenged and later revised without ceasing to be self-authored, provided the person is allowed to name the fear/value conflict and generate the revised commitment as her own.**

Chisato's influence is strong. The evidence does not support “Chisato decided for Kanon.”

# 5.2 Arashi Chisato — presidency as governance, care as challenge, autonomy without solitary worthiness

Chisato's Season-1 specialist authority and revised solitary-worthiness model both continue into Season 2.

Her presidency is not ceremonial. She develops a distinctive style organized around:

- individualized calibration rather than flat standards;
- explicit rest/recovery governance;
- permission for junior experiments without constant senior surveillance;
- evidence-responsive escalation of difficulty;
- self-application before anti-fatalistic challenge of others;
- distinction between expertise and competition (`別に ダンスで 私と競争しようっていうんじゃないよ？`);
- creating co-authorship rather than simply delegating finished tasks.

The S2E11-S2E12 Kanon intervention adds a new personal-relational mode. Chisato advocates physical separation from someone she deeply values because she believes the opportunity matters for Kanon's future. She explicitly frames the position as her honest feeling rather than presidential authority.

At S2E12 she says she wants to verify `かのんちゃんが考えて出した答え`, and Kanon arrives with her revised decision already made.

Frozen transition:

> **STRENGTHEN shared-agency/answer-verification; DOWNGRADE paternalism as the best explanation for this specific instance; PRESERVE paternalism risk as a general future possibility rather than globally erasing it.**

# 5.3 Sakurakoji Kinako — access recipient becomes access bridge, contributor, and successor

Kinako begins as the person through whom Liella!'s external prestige barrier becomes visible.

Her Season-2 movement is:

> **access recipient → self-authoring novice → failure-vulnerable member → peer recruiter/access bridge → first-year cohort member → student-council participant → creative/epistemic contributor → prospective successor.**

Important endpoint points:

- she does not need technical equality with seniors to possess real authority;
- her outsider perspective on Tokyo becomes load-bearing evidence in S2E08;
- she contributes to lyrics in S2E10;
- she remains part of the national-champion nine;
- possible Kanon departure produces uncertainty but not categorical incapacity;
- she says `できるかな…` and then `きな子 やるっす！ やってみるっす！`.

Historical burden vulnerability remains trigger-dependent. Ordinary recruitment and later success do not prove it can never recur under a new adverse outcome.

# 5.4 Yoneme Mei — type-fit exclusion revised; belonging becomes active but partly utility-conditioned

Mei begins Season 2 as an overdetermined Liella! fan who denies desire and converts face/personality into categorical school-idol unsuitability.

S2E04 materially revises that model:

- desire becomes actionable with Shiki's co-presence;
- difficult training does not reactivate `向いてない` as an exit rule;
- rough language does not equal relational rejection;
- she becomes capable of direct relational advice to a senior (Ren);
- she contributes to composition in S2E10;
- she remains part of the national-champion nine.

A real endpoint vulnerability remains:

> `｢Liella！｣の力になれないなら スクールアイドル やるつもりはない｡`

This is not the old “I am inherently unsuited” rule. It is **utility-conditioned belonging**. Season 2 never supplies the clean adverse trigger in which Mei is genuinely convinced she cannot contribute. Keep that future test OPEN.

# 5.5 Wakana Shiki — relational entry becomes sustained membership and permeable authorship

Shiki enters largely because of Mei, and Season 2 never requires the false claim that her independent school-idol desire was equally strong from the start.

Her endpoint includes:

- sustained school-idol participation beyond the original recruitment moment;
- indirect/environmental care strategies;
- willingness to create pressure conditions for Mei that can verge on control;
- concise boundary enforcement, including toward Natsumi;
- ability to distinguish pessimistic probability judgment from withdrawal;
- choreography co-authorship with Chisato at S2E10;
- membership in the national-champion nine;
- laconic emotional defense after victory (`これは汗`).

The strongest safe endpoint is:

> **Shiki's entry is securely relationship-supported; her own school-idol commitment becomes behaviorally durable, but Season 2 does not require retroactively equating her initial independent idol passion with Mei's.**

# 5.6 Onitsuka Natsumi — defensive measurability, manipulative methods, dream re-entry, and internal governance

Natsumi's Season-2 trajectory is deliberately non-linear.

S2E05 establishes real manipulation:

- opaque monetization/revenue framing;
- attention optimization;
- deliberate amplification of first-year insecurity;
- private statement `分断成功`.

S2E06 explains but does not excuse the behavior. Repeated failed dreams made aspiration feel unsafe. Money becomes a measurable future-security fallback. She explicitly says she has had many dreams and learned none would come true.

Her school-idol entry then produces a genuine shift:

- she can defend another person's dream before fully permitting herself one;
- she trains and becomes the ninth member;
- `見つけたかも… 私の…夢！` later develops into more stable school-idol commitment;
- she can state non-monetized personal wants;
- she can use media competence diagnostically without always extracting value;
- nevertheless, attention/clickbait/monetization methods recur and require explicit internal boundaries.

The best endpoint is not “Natsumi stops caring about money.” It is:

> **goal alignment changes faster than learned method; Liella! must govern a valuable internal media competence whose default attention logic is not yet fully norm-internalized.**

National victory acquires unexpectedly private meaning. Natsumi publicly boasts, then quietly calls it her `初めての１等賞` and conceals the admission when noticed. Numerical success is no longer only monetizable measurement; it can carry relational/autobiographical value.

# 7. Succession and access endpoint

Season 2 builds a layered succession model rather than one recruitment rule.

The strongest frozen formulation is:

> **developmental transparency + individualized pacing + honest standards + failure-safe belonging + junior agency + permission to try before identity-based fit is known + room for relationship-supported entry + permeable authorship + protection from optimization-based self-removal.**

Each term was added because an earlier version proved insufficient.

## 7.1 Developmental transparency

Senior excellence becomes an access barrier when novices cannot see how it was produced.

## 7.2 Pacing without deception

Pacing is not the same as lowering the actual stakes or pretending difficulty does not exist.

## 7.3 Junior choice

A novice must be allowed to choose meaningful difficulty. Senior care cannot decide in advance which stakes the junior is permitted to value.

## 7.4 Failure-safe belonging

Inclusion is fake if the new member becomes removable at the first disappointing result.

## 7.5 Permission to try before fit is known

Mei and Shiki show that self-description can become premature categorical exclusion. Trying may reveal real mismatch later; fear, awkwardness, or inherited self-image are not enough to declare non-eligibility before experience.

## 7.6 Relationship-supported agency

Needing someone nearby can be part of agency rather than proof of invalid dependence.

## 7.7 Permeable authorship

Juniors must eventually change the actual work, not remain permanent recipients of senior competence.

## 7.8 Protection from self-removal under optimization pressure

S2E09 demonstrates that junior consent to step aside is not automatically the healthiest group outcome. Agency must be respected without treating self-sacrifice as infallible.

## 7.9 Founder independence is only partially tested

By S2E12 the group can **conceptually** authorize continuity beyond Kanon's presence. That is substantial progress.

However:

- Kanon remains a concentrated trust/integration node;
- no actual long-duration post-Kanon operation occurs;
- the cancellation prevents behavioral observation of the planned continuity.

Frozen transition:

> **STRENGTHEN founder-independent concept; PARTIALLY REVISE any claim that the group is already empirically founder-independent.**
