# Love Live! Superstar!! — Season 1 Frozen Checkpoint
## Prospective five-member Liella! state at the S1E12 boundary

# 0. Freeze declaration

This document freezes the **prospective Season-1 understanding of *Love Live! Superstar!!*** after the canonical V2.2 sequential reading of `S1E01` through `S1E12` and **before any semantic evidence from S2E01 or later is admitted**.

Its purpose is not to produce a retrospective “what Season 1 was really setting up” account. It preserves what the first season itself has established, what it has revised internally, what remains uncertain, and what later evidence is allowed to pressure.

The governing rule is:

> **Season-1 endpoint claims may be revised by later evidence, but the fact that they were reasonable prospective conclusions at the S1E12 boundary must never be erased.**

Therefore this checkpoint distinguishes four things that later synthesis can too easily collapse:

1. **historical state** — a belief, defense, relationship condition, or behavioral rule that was genuinely active earlier in the season;
2. **endpoint state** — the best supported model at the end of S1E12;
3. **revision path** — how the earlier state was preserved, strengthened, narrowed, revised, downgraded, or rejected by later Season-1 evidence;
4. **OPEN uncertainty** — a question that Season 1 itself has not resolved and that must remain open rather than being answered with later knowledge.

No Season-2 plot, character state, recruitment information, competition result, relationship development, song, or retrospective franchise knowledge is used here.

# 1. Executive Season-1 thesis

Season 1 begins with a problem of **whether desire can survive judgment** and ends with a problem of **what a shared desire owes to the people who have invested in it**.

At the beginning, Kanon loves singing but treats one failed evaluative route as evidence that singing may no longer be a legitimate future for her. Keke arrives with the opposite bias: desire is something to expose, pursue, recruit around, and defend before certainty exists. Their first partnership therefore forms through a productive asymmetry. Kanon gives Keke feasibility, musical form, social judgment, and eventually the voice Keke recognized; Keke refuses to let Kanon reduce herself to someone who merely supports other people's dreams.

The season then repeatedly expands the same problem across new people and institutions.

- Sumire asks whether **recognition counts** when one is always useful but never decisively chosen.
- Chisato asks whether **care and intimacy count** if one has not independently earned the right to stand beside the person one admires.
- Ren asks whether **inheritance and responsibility count** if a school survives only by imposing the standards she believes her mother required.
- Liella! eventually asks whether **victory counts** as the measure of a group whose artistic, relational, and institutional value is already demonstrable without winning.

The five-person group that emerges does not solve these problems by adopting one universal answer. Its defining development is instead the replacement of **single-axis worth tests** with a more distributed ecology of value and authority.

By S1E12:

- Kanon can fail consequentially without making failure a proof of personal worthlessness.
- Keke can treat competition as sacred without making direct ranking immune to new evidence.
- Sumire can receive central recognition without needing every later role to remain central.
- Chisato can retain specialist authority without using independence as the price of relational worth.
- Ren can preserve responsibility and school pride without monopolizing institutional burden.
- Liella! can make winning a serious future goal without treating winning as the sovereign measure of artistic, relational, institutional, or human value.

The strongest frozen Season-1 thesis is therefore:

> **Liella! becomes viable when the five stop requiring one person, one role, one skill, one institution, or one competitive result to carry the total meaning of worth. Their connectedness matters because their differences remain operational: support can move, center can move, expertise can remain asymmetric, responsibility can be distributed, and defeat can hurt without invalidating the project.**

S1E12 adds a crucial final step. The group does not merely learn that winning is optional. After the school and surrounding community invest materially in their performance, defeat creates a new desire to win **as reciprocity**. Kanon's movement from `勝ちたい` to `優勝しよう` freezes the first-season endpoint: personal desire is converted into collective volition without erasing the non-ranked values that made the group meaningful before the result.

# 3. Season-1 developmental architecture

Season 1 can be divided into four analytical movements. These are not merely plot arcs; they describe changes in the kind of problem the ensemble is capable of carrying.

## 3.1 S1E01-S1E03 — Desire, failure, and relational permission

The opening movement establishes Kanon's performance problem and Keke's disruptive function.

Kanon begins with a classic evidence-weighting asymmetry: successful or ordinary singing is treated as weak evidence, while failure under consequential evaluation is treated as identity-defining. Her defensive system includes minimization, substitute aspirations, withdrawal from self-relevant discussion, and a safer helper position in which another person's dream can be supported without Kanon exposing her own equivalent desire.

Keke makes that arrangement unstable. She not only praises Kanon's ability but insists on Kanon as a specific collaborator. By S1E03, when Kanon attempts to remove herself because she believes she is `足手まとい` and would damage Keke's dream, Keke corrects the underlying preference model: Kanon herself is part of the dream. The resulting `Tiny Stars` configuration establishes that relational scaffolding can enable action without first eliminating uncertainty.

The important prospective conclusion at S1E03 was **not** “Kanon is cured.” It was:

> Kanon can sing under a high-stakes configuration when trusted co-presence, preparation, contingency, and supportive audience interpretation alter the meaning of the performance situation.

S1E11 later proves why preserving that narrow formulation was essential.

## 3.2 S1E04-S1E06 — Recognition, expertise, autonomy, and the danger of worth tests

Sumire and Chisato expand the season from “can Kanon pursue desire?” to “what kinds of recognition and independence make a person feel entitled to belong?”

Sumire arrives with genuine professional/show-business competence and a history of being useful at the edge while decisive recognition goes elsewhere. The center vote and her response reveal that what she seeks is not generic praise. Her wound is organized around **consequential selection**. Kanon's first response—offering up center—is insufficient because it would turn recognition into pity. Her stronger response is to make center contestable: claim it herself and invite Sumire to take it through demonstrated value.

Chisato's arc initially appears to be a clean model of self-authored independence. She has real school-idol interest but chooses dance as her current priority, pursues a recognized competition route, and provides structured support without abandoning her own goal. S1E06 revises that model. Her independence is real, but it has become entangled with a relational worthiness test: because Kanon protected her when they were younger, Chisato believes she must become independently strong enough to stand beside and help Kanon without relying on Kanon in return.

Kanon's correction is crucial because it does not devalue independence. She supplies evidence of reciprocity: Chisato's existence and childhood promise also helped Kanon continue singing. The debt is not one-way. Chisato's later transfer and membership therefore become possible without requiring the conclusion that her dance goal was false.

This movement establishes a recurring Season-1 principle:

> **A self-authored goal can be real even when the meaning attached to succeeding or failing at it is distorted by a worth test.**

## 3.3 S1E07-S1E09 — Institution, inherited responsibility, source correction, and group identity

Ren scales the same structure to Yuigaoka.

Her early gatekeeping is genuinely exclusionary. It should not be rewritten as merely misunderstood kindness. She argues that music-related activity must protect institutional value and tries to prevent school-idol activity she judges inadequate. S1E07 reveals why that policy carries such force: the school is her deceased mother's legacy, the household resources are exhausted, Ren is materially isolated, and she concludes `わたくしが頑張るしかない`.

That disclosure revises motive without retroactively legitimizing method.

S1E08 then supplies direct primary evidence that changes Ren's interpretation of her mother. The predecessor school's idol activity did not prevent closure, but the surviving notebook explicitly records no regret because music and idol activity made the school one and connected people. Ren responds to source evidence by changing policy and affiliation. This is a major modeling transition: **direct evidence can override inherited-policy inference without erasing the original material risk that made the inference attractive**.

Ren's incorporation completes the founding five and changes the ensemble's operating structure. By S1E09, Liella! is explicitly named. The group's own explanation emphasizes differently colored light becoming connected while remaining open-ended/unfinished. This identity is not merely thematic. S1E09 also makes distributed competence visible:

- Kanon: singing, lyrical/semantic integration, motive-sensitive mediation;
- Keke: school-idol ecosystem knowledge, competition urgency, mobilization;
- Sumire: show-business/stage knowledge, public presentation, branding;
- Chisato: dance, training, movement/capability diagnosis;
- Ren: composition/piano, formal procedure, quality discipline, institutional administration.

Kanon remains narratively and relationally central, but S1E09 explicitly demonstrates the danger of treating that centrality as infinite capacity: she objects that everyone is relying on her too much. The mature five-member architecture is therefore not “Kanon leads and everyone contributes around her.” It is **distributed competence with an integrative but bounded Kanon node**.

## 3.4 S1E10-S1E12 — Task-contingent authority, internalized support, and reciprocal competition

The final movement stress-tests whether the five-person identity can survive actual Love Live! pressure.

S1E10's rap assignment is decisive because Kanon is not the best person for the task. Sumire is. Kanon's response—`今までは今まで / 大切なのはこれから`—turns S1E09's connected heterogeneity into an operational rule: prior centrality does not determine future role ownership. Sumire receives center because the problem being solved fits her skill profile.

S1E11 prevents that principle from hardening into the opposite slogan that “good leadership means Kanon always steps back.” The new task is `独唱`, a pure-vocal problem that fits Kanon's strongest domain. Responsibility moves back to her. The frozen rule is therefore **bidirectional task-contingent authority**, not decentralization for its own sake.

S1E11 also reopens Kanon's oldest problem in its most specific form. Returning to the elementary-school auditorium associated with her original failure reactivates the inability to begin. This demonstrates that successful later performances did not erase autobiographical conditioning. Chisato's difficult intervention tests whether support must remain visibly co-present at the moment of action. The episode itself rejects a crude independence doctrine: when Kanon says she must accomplish it alone, Chisato answers that she is not alone. The decisive realization is instead `そう　怖かったんだ　あの時も`—the younger Kanon was afraid too.

The result is a model of **integration rather than restoration**. Kanon does not recover a fearless original self; she discovers continuity between fear and love of singing.

S1E12 then gives that model its first major failure test. Liella! loses the Tokyo stage. Kanon feels regret and disappointment but does not return to global identity-negation or self-removal. At the same time, the school and community have already demonstrated that Liella! matters independent of victory. Only within that already-secured field of value does Kanon develop a new explicit desire to win.

The Season-1 movement therefore ends with:

> **performance value already secured + communal investment received + competitive defeat experienced → reciprocal ambition rather than worth panic.**

# 4. Five-member endpoint at S1E12

The frozen five-person Liella! is best understood as a **differentiated cooperative system** rather than a flat friendship group or a fixed hierarchy.

## 4.1 Current authority topology

Authority is domain-sensitive.

| Domain/problem | Strongest established authority at S1 boundary | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Singing / vocal integration | Kanon | autobiographically loaded performance contexts remain a vulnerability; authority is not total leadership ownership |
| School-idol ecosystem / Love Live! urgency | Keke | urgency can become coercive or harsh before evidence is gathered |
| Show-business / visibility / improvisational stagecraft | Sumire | recognition history can still become sensitive under genuine replacement threat |
| Dance / movement / physical training | Chisato | specialist authority can carry paternalistic risk if autonomy is tested without transparent consent |
| Formal procedure / composition / institutional administration | Ren | inherited responsibility can become overcontrol when co-bearers appear absent |
| Integrative motive/relationship interpretation | often Kanon | Kanon can over-assume responsibility, estimate others' costs for them, or become over-relied upon |

This table is not a ranking of character worth. It freezes demonstrated **problem-fit**.

## 4.2 Center is not property

By the end of Season 1, “center” cannot be modeled as a permanent entitlement held by Kanon or as a symbolic prize that must always go to the person with the highest total worth.

S1E10 and S1E11 jointly establish:

> **visible centrality should move when the performance problem changes.**

Rap supports Sumire. `独唱` supports Kanon. Ren can carry center/vocal responsibility in S1E08 when the dramatic/institutional task makes that appropriate. Sumire can defer center without erasing ambition. Kanon can accept or redistribute center without treating either choice as self-negation.

This does not prove every future role decision will be healthy or conflict-free. It establishes the Season-1 baseline against which a later fixed hierarchy or status conflict must be measured.

## 4.3 Connected difference is operational, not decorative

S1E09's color/light explanation of `Liella!` is confirmed by later behavior and staging.

The group does not become coherent because the members converge toward one personality or one competence profile. Coherence emerges because differentiated capacities become available to the group without requiring the specialist to disappear.

The S1E12 public stage makes this visible: differentiated member colors and lanes repeatedly reaggregate into shared formations. The group can be visually and behaviorally plural while still acting as one representative unit.

## 4.4 Group cohesion does not imply total information symmetry

A major unresolved counterexample is Keke's concealed results pressure.

By the S1E12 boundary:

- Keke has not made that burden whole-group knowledge;
- Sumire is the one established private carrier;
- Sumire supports continuity without forcing public disclosure;
- the group nevertheless functions effectively and recommits after defeat.

Therefore Season 1 does **not** establish that healthy cohesion requires all burdens to be immediately disclosed. It also does not establish that concealment is harmless. The information asymmetry remains OPEN.

# 5. Shibuya Kanon — frozen Season-1 model

## 5.1 Core endpoint formulation

Kanon's Season-1 development should not be frozen as “a shy singer becomes confident.” That formulation is too broad and loses the causal structure that the sequential reread established.

The stronger endpoint is:

> **Kanon moves from treating consequential failure as identity proof toward acting from desire, relationship, and process even when fear or disappointment remains. Her leadership develops not by becoming universally decisive, but by learning when to claim responsibility, when to redistribute it, when to correct another person's model, and when to let other people author support for themselves.**

Her centrality is therefore **integrative and relational**, not equivalent to permanent performance center or unilateral group authority.

## 5.2 Singing and fear: what is resolved

The following are now strongly established by the S1 boundary:

- Kanon genuinely loves singing. This is no longer a desire she can plausibly model as extinct simply because one route failed.
- She can sing in consequential public settings.
- Relational support can enable action without first eliminating fear.
- Support does not have to remain visibly co-present at the exact execution point once it has become internally portable.
- Fear can be acknowledged without becoming evidence that the “real” Kanon is broken or incapable.
- Major competitive defeat can hurt without reactivating global talent-negation or self-removal.
- Incremental practice can itself become positively valued: effort is capable of producing `楽しい`, not merely judgment.

## 5.3 Singing and fear: what is not resolved

S1E11 prevents any claim of a total cure.

The elementary-school auditorium reactivates the initiation block after many successful performances. Therefore the best frozen model is:

> **autobiographically/meaning-loaded performance context can reactivate a learned singing block even after substantial functional recovery.**

OPEN questions include:

- how broad the trigger class is beyond the specific remembered location;
- whether recurrence frequency decreases with new experience;
- whether future high-stakes evaluation can reactivate the same response without the original place cue;
- whether internally portable support remains sufficient under qualitatively different stressors;
- whether new kinds of loss produce different self-evaluative pressure.

The endpoint is integration, not immunity.

## 5.4 Failure and self-worth transition

Historical Kanon repeatedly overweights negative evidence.

Early states include:

- `たまたま` treatment of positive counterevidence;
- `今の姿が本当の私なんだよ　きっと`;
- `私のせいで`;
- `足手まといにしかならない`;
- attempts to remove herself when she believes her presence harms another person's valued future.

These states remain canonical historical evidence. They should **not** be deleted from a character simulator as “out of character” merely because the endpoint is healthier. Under an early-Season-1 temporal boundary they are highly relevant.

At S1E12, however, the same broad category of event—meaningful competitive failure—produces a different response:

- apology;
- `悔しい`;
- concern about reciprocity;
- future commitment.

It does **not** produce global self-condemnation.

Frozen transition:

> **REVISE/STRENGTHEN:** failure remains emotionally consequential, but “failure → identity proof → self-removal” is no longer the dominant current rule.

## 5.5 Burden sensitivity and the residual over-restraint problem

Kanon's concern for third-party costs is not simply a flaw. Across Season 1 it often supports ethical judgment:

- refusing impractical solutions;
- respecting other students and clubs;
- noticing when a plan imposes on another person's chosen path;
- rejecting privileged treatment when it violates peer fairness;
- setting workload boundaries when too much is placed on her.

But the same tendency can overshoot.

Two historically different failure modes should be distinguished:

1. **S1E03 self-erasure:** Kanon believes she is actively harmful and should remove herself.
2. **S1E12 over-restraint:** Kanon assumes that asking for a more ambitious stage would burden others, even when others actively want to help.

Sumire's `気を遣いすぎ` supplies the endpoint correction.

Current rule:

> **Kanon should not be modeled as naturally entitled or demanding, but neither should her cost sensitivity be treated as perfect moral perception. She can underestimate other people's desire to contribute by deciding their willingness for them.**

## 5.6 Leadership and centrality

Kanon's strongest Season-1 leadership behaviors are not orders. They are acts of **interpretive correction and role authorization**.

Examples include:

- defending Keke when Kanon can speak more clearly for another person's agency than for her own;
- correcting her model of Keke's dream once Keke reveals that Kanon herself is part of it;
- discovering Sumire's recognition logic and replacing pity-concession with contestable opportunity;
- challenging Chisato's one-way protector debt with symmetric causal evidence;
- refusing to essentialize Ren when contradictory evidence suggests hidden motive;
- seeking primary evidence about Ren's mother rather than confidently mind-reading the legacy;
- redistributing center to Sumire when rap makes Sumire the better fit;
- accepting solo responsibility when `独唱` makes Kanon the better fit;
- converting a private desire to win into `優勝しよう` once she recognizes that the desire is actually collective.

Kanon's leadership is therefore best frozen as:

> **motive-sensitive integration + fairness/cost checking + willingness to revise role allocation + capacity to turn personal insight into group-usable form.**

Counterweights:

- she can over-assume others' constraints;
- she can become overloaded when the group routes too much work through her;
- she can initially use another person instrumentally for group optimization before self-correcting;
- close familiarity does not make her perfectly accurate about Chisato or others.

## 5.7 Competition at the endpoint

Pre-S1E12 Kanon is not secretly a conventional competitive maximizer. Her statement that singing freely can already be enough happiness is direct and historically coherent.

The Tokyo loss changes her through experience.

Frozen rule `LLS-BHV-KANON-031`:

> **received collective investment + meaningful competitive loss + intact relational/artistic value → increased likelihood that Kanon converts disappointment into a collective future goal rather than self-erasure.**

The lexical sequence matters:

`勝ちたい` → `私　勝ちたい` → `結ヶ丘の歌で優勝したい` → `いや` → `優勝しよう`

The final `いや` is a self-correction from individual desiderative language to collective volition. This is not a claim that Kanon will always subordinate individual desire to group language. It is evidence that when she recognizes the object of a desire as genuinely shared, she actively reformulates it that way.

## 5.8 Voice endpoint

Frozen high-value Kanon voice modes include:

- defensive/minimizing language under threatened self-exposure;
- direct principle-confrontation when another person's agency/fairness is at stake;
- qualified ownership (`本気で` alongside mitigation such as `ちょっと`);
- practical boundary language (`待って`, `それは無理`, etc.);
- identity-negative language in historically earlier failure states;
- action-under-uncertainty language that commits to trying without claiming certainty;
- reflective, structurally simple declarations after conceptual clarity;
- process-positive language about effort and improvement;
- reciprocity vocabulary (`お返し`, `返す`) after S1E12 defeat;
- individual desire followed by collective reformulation.

Do not simulate “hesitant Kanon” as a universal register. Her directness is highly context-sensitive.

## 5.9 Kanon claim-transition register

| Earlier claim/state | Season-1 pressure | Frozen transition | S1 endpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| High-stakes failure may mean she cannot sing / lacks the right future | repeated successful performances; S1E11 contextual relapse; S1E12 defeat without self-negation | **REVISE** | fear is meaning/context-sensitive; failure no longer automatically defines identity |
| Helping others is safer than exposing own desire | Keke demands reciprocal participation; Kanon later claims center/competition goals | **STRENGTHEN then REVISE** | other-directed care remains strong, but personal desire can be owned directly |
| If she harms another's goal she should remove herself | Keke corrects preference model; later losses do not trigger exit | **DOWNGRADE as current rule / PRESERVE historically** | self-removal remains a known stress response, not current default |
| Support is required in visible co-presence | S1E11 solo/internalization | **REVISE** | support can be internally portable; not equivalent to “needs nobody” |
| Kanon is the natural center | Sumire rap center; Kanon `独唱` return | **REJECT as fixed-property claim** | authority/center is task-contingent |

| Singing freely is enough; ranking is secondary | S1E12 direct loss after communal investment | **STRENGTHEN then EXPAND** | non-ranked value remains; winning becomes an additional reciprocity goal |

## 5.10 OPEN Kanon tests carried into later delta work

- Does the autobiographical performance block recur outside the original location?
- Does `悔しい` continue to avoid identity-negation under repeated defeat?
- Does new competitive ambition remain reciprocity-centered, or acquire additional motives?
- Can Kanon retain workload boundaries as group expectations rise?
- Does task-contingent authority remain genuinely bidirectional when stakes increase?
- Does she continue allowing other people to author support rather than pre-emptively minimizing what she asks of them?

# 6. Tang Keke — frozen Season-1 model

## 6.1 Core endpoint formulation

Keke is the founding force who treats school-idol desire as something that should be made actionable before certainty exists. Her Season-1 development does **not** move from naive enthusiasm to disillusionment. It moves toward a more complex coexistence of:

- desire-first authorization;
- disciplined effort;
- deep domain reverence;
- competition urgency;
- evidence-based revision;
- person-specific attachment;
- increasingly intimate but still abrasive relationships;
- and a private burden she chooses not to distribute to the whole group.

Frozen formulation:

> **Keke is highly willing to externalize goals but selectively unwilling to externalize burdens when she believes disclosure could contaminate other people's motive.**

That asymmetry is one of the most important OPEN Season-1 states.

## 6.2 Desire and effort

Keke's early claim that `気持ち` is the most important thing about school idols is never shown to mean “technique does not matter.”

Across Season 1 she:

- accepts harsh physical training despite severe stamina weakness;
- contributes lyrics and school-idol knowledge;
- studies competition requirements;
- participates in costume/production labor;
- revises performance judgments based on observed evidence;
- repeatedly recommits after failure.

Frozen transition:

> **PRESERVE/STRENGTHEN:** sincerity authorizes participation and effort; it does not exempt a person from technique, practice, or adaptation.

## 6.3 Recruitment and coercive overreach

Keke's strength and risk share the same mechanism.

When she recognizes affinity or ability, she moves quickly toward recruitment and mobilization. Under normal conditions this gives the group momentum. Under uncertainty it can outrun consent, pacing, or strategic processing.

Examples include:

- immediate pursuit/recruitment of Kanon;
- accelerated assumption that tentative interest means membership;
- theatrical public mobilization;
- intense defense of school-idol seriousness;
- Love Live! urgency that can harden a prior judgment before she has enough evidence.

Kanon often functions as a pacing/boundary counterweight.

Current rule:

> **Keke's enthusiasm should be modeled as action-generating rather than socially neutral. It can become coercive or overfast, especially when a sacred domain or urgent opportunity is involved.**

## 6.4 Evidence-based revision

S1E10 is the strongest endpoint evidence against modeling Keke as simply stubborn.

She initially judges Sumire as a poor center choice under high Love Live! stakes. After watching practice and hearing the actual performance evidence, she reverses decisively and becomes one of the strongest defenders of Sumire's center legitimacy.

Frozen rule:

> **high stakes can harden Keke's initial ranking, but direct domain-relevant evidence can trigger stronger revision rather than entrenchment.**

This is essential for later simulation: value intensity does not imply evidence immunity.

## 6.5 Hidden results pressure

Season 1 establishes a concealed personal stake in producing results. The corrected Japanese independently supports:

- that Keke feels she must win/produce results;
- that Kanon and the others do not know;
- that Keke does not want them doing school idols because they are worried about her.

The more specific auxiliary-English wording about returning home is **not** promoted to governing Japanese fact in this checkpoint.

The frozen state is:

> **Keke protects the group's self-authored motive by withholding a burden that would likely change how the others understand the stakes.**

This is neither automatically noble nor automatically deceptive in the moralizing sense. It is a protective nondisclosure strategy with real relational cost potential.

S1E12 does not resolve it. The group still does not know. Sumire remains the established private carrier.

**Status: OPEN.**

## 6.6 Keke and Sumire

This relationship undergoes one of the season's largest register changes without becoming conventionally soft.

Early structure:

- Keke treats Sumire as insufficiently serious or improperly status-oriented;
- Sumire sees Keke as an amateur whose lack of professional history complicates her judgments;
- insults, competition, and domain defense dominate.

Later structure:

- Keke revises her center judgment from evidence;
- creates center-specific costume/tiara labor for Sumire;
- first uses bare `すみれ` at a high-intensity recognition moment;
- repeats bare `すみれ` in ordinary disagreement and private request contexts;
- entrusts a treasured Sunny Passion fan item to Sumire;
- Sumire holds Keke's private results-pressure knowledge and gives an omamori for continued togetherness;
- `グソクムシ` banter persists.

Frozen relational rule:

> **increased person-recognition and confidential trust do not require removal of abrasive rivalry. Intimacy and antagonistic banter coexist.**

Do not simulate bare-name use as mandatory in every utterance, but it is now a directly established available/default-close form rather than a one-scene anomaly.

## 6.7 Admiration and rivalry

Sunny Passion demonstrates that Keke can maintain hierarchy-sensitive admiration while competing seriously.

S1E12:

`今日だけはライバルです / 今日だけはファンをやめます`

The repeated temporal boundary is analytically important. Keke does not need to devalue an admired superior to oppose them competitively.

Frozen rule:

> **role conflict can be managed through explicit temporal/contextual switching rather than value reversal.**

# 8. Arashi Chisato — frozen Season-1 model

## 8.1 Core endpoint formulation

Chisato's Season-1 arc should be frozen as a problem of **autonomy, reciprocity, and specialist care**, not simply “dance girl joins the idol club.”

Her self-authored commitment to dance is genuine. The reread does not revise that away. What changes is the meaning she attaches to independence.

The decisive historical distortion is:

> because Kanon once protected and inspired her, Chisato comes to believe that she must become independently strong enough to stand beside and support Kanon without needing equivalent support back.

That belief turns autonomy into a **relational worthiness test**.

Frozen endpoint:

> **Chisato retains strong self-authored expertise and can challenge others toward capability, but relational worth no longer requires solitary proof. Shared support is compatible with dignity. Her care nonetheless carries a paternalistic risk when she decides another person's needed autonomy test for them.**

## 8.2 Dance identity remains real

Several potential misreadings are rejected by the Season-1 evidence.

Chisato did not merely use dance as an excuse to avoid school idols. She:

- explicitly states genuine school-idol interest;
- explicitly identifies dance results as her current highest priority;
- refuses casual dual commitment because she treats both paths seriously;
- pursues the dance competition;
- wins;
- later makes an institutional transfer and membership decision after her own threshold is met.

Therefore:

> **PRESERVE:** dance is a genuine self-authored identity and achievement route.

The revision concerns the **worth meaning** attached to success/failure, not the authenticity of the goal.

## 8.3 Solitary worthiness and its repair

S1E06 reveals the strongest form of the problem.

Chisato prepares a withdrawal route if she fails to win and interprets relief at Kanon's arrival as evidence of weakness. This is a high-value example of how a seemingly admirable independence ethic can become punitive.

Kanon's response matters because she does not say independence is bad. Instead she breaks the one-way debt model by revealing that Chisato also helped sustain Kanon.

Frozen revision:

> **REVISE:** “I must prove I can stand alone before I deserve to stand beside you” is not the endpoint governing rule. Shared/facilitated agency can preserve self-respect when the goal remains self-authored.

S1E07 onward strengthens this through Chisato's acceptance of institutional help in transferring while retaining ownership of the choice.

## 8.4 Specialist authority

Membership does not flatten Chisato into just another equal generalist.

At S1E12 she remains the group's clearest authority for:

- dance diagnosis;
- training design;
- movement correction;
- physical load management;
- differentiation between what members can currently do and what a performance requires.

The frozen ensemble model therefore includes **expertise asymmetry without personhood hierarchy**.

This distinction is analytically important. “Everyone is equal” should not be translated into “everyone has equal authority over every technical problem.”

## 8.5 S1E11 paternalism risk

S1E11 creates a necessary complication.

Chisato correctly identifies a vulnerability: if Kanon can sing only while visible companions are physically present, the support configuration may be too externally dependent. Her concern is not trivial.

But her method—coordinating deception so Kanon believes she must face the elementary-school stage alone—is not ethically neutral.

Season 1 itself supplies counterevidence:

- Keke calls it `ひどい` and `かわいそう`;
- Sumire describes Chisato's position as an ideal;
- Kanon resists the inference that one person alone is Liella!;
- Chisato explicitly corrects Kanon's conclusion that she must accomplish everything alone.

Frozen formulation:

> **Chisato's care can become paternalistic when she believes another person's durable capability requires a difficult test and assumes authority to create the test without transparent consent.**

This is a risk state, not her universal support style.

## 8.6 Anti-isolation boundary

The most important constraint on that risk is Chisato's own `それに一人じゃない`.

She is not trying to teach Kanon that mature people need nobody. The intended distinction is between:

- support as visible external scaffold that must be physically present at execution;
- support/history/relationship that can be internally carried while acting.

S1E12 strengthens the anti-isolation boundary. After defeat, Chisato does not impose another solitary lesson. Kanon first names `私　悔しい` to her, and Chisato allows the meaning to emerge in dialogue rather than supplying a doctrine.

Frozen transition:

> **STRENGTHEN:** demanding autonomy tests remain available in Chisato's repertoire, but they are bounded by a continuing belief that independent action is not equivalent to relational isolation.

## 8.7 Training ethic at the endpoint

S1E12 is important because competitive urgency does not reactivate the S1E06 worthiness logic as training policy.

Chisato gives exact corrections and manages physical limits. Bodies are not used as proof-of-worth instruments.

Frozen rule `LLS-BHV-CHISATO-016`:

> **high competitive stakes do not suspend physical-limit governance.**

This should be treated as a meaningful endpoint correction to any model of Chisato as someone who always demands solitary maximal effort.

## 8.8 Voice endpoint

Chisato's voice remains strongly state- and role-conditioned.

Established modes include:

- familiar teasing with Kanon;
- concise diagnostic coaching;
- positive reframing that finds usable strengths without denying deficits;
- clear positive refusal when another path conflicts with her chosen priority;
- seriousness language that respects activity rather than using seriousness to gatekeep personhood;
- dyad-aware support language that knows when her presence should recede;
- demanding autonomy-test rhetoric in a specific S1E11 state;
- quiet/listening support after S1E12 defeat.

Do not flatten Chisato into either “gentle supportive friend” or “tough-love coach.” Both are available modes under different perceived needs.

| Earlier claim/state | Season-1 pressure | Frozen transition | S1 endpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chisato simply prefers dance to school idols | explicit interest + later membership | **REVISE** | she genuinely values both; priority changes after self-authored threshold |
| Independent dance pursuit is uncomplicated autonomy | S1E06 worthiness/debt disclosure | **REVISE** | goal authentic; worth meaning partly distorted |

# 9. Hazuki Ren — frozen Season-1 model

## 9.1 Core endpoint formulation

Ren undergoes the season's largest transformation in the relationship between **institutional responsibility and control**.

The endpoint must preserve the fact that her early gatekeeping was real. Later motive does not erase earlier harm.

Frozen formulation:

> **Ren's exclusionary quality control emerges from a context in which inherited school survival, maternal legacy, financial exhaustion, and perceived absence of co-bearers collapse into solitary responsibility. Direct evidence and the material arrival of co-bearers allow her to retain seriousness, formal competence, and school pride while relinquishing monopoly over legitimate action.**

## 9.2 Early prestige logic

S1E01-S1E03 Ren treats school-idol activity through institutional legitimacy and quality.

Her objection develops from procedural/formal restriction into explicit prestige protection: inadequate musical activity could lower Yuigaoka's value.

This is not merely a communication problem. It produces real exclusionary behavior.

Frozen historical state:

> **PRESERVE:** early Ren can equate protecting an institution with restricting participation whose quality she judges inadequate.

## 9.3 Solitary-burden context

S1E07 changes the explanatory model.

Ren is not simply status-conscious. The school is a maternal legacy; household resources are exhausted; she is effectively alone in the household; and she says `わたくしが頑張るしかない`.

The best causal model is therefore:

> **inherited survival threat + perceived absence of co-bearers → heightened probability of solitary control and overextension of formal authority.**

This explanation does not justify the gatekeeping. It specifies the state in which it becomes likely.

## 9.4 Source correction

S1E08 is foundational for Ren's later modeling.

Her understanding of her mother's regret is not corrected through generic reassurance. It is corrected through direct primary evidence in the surviving notebook.

Crucially, the evidence does not deny failure:

- the predecessor school still closed;
- idol activity did not accomplish the instrumental survival goal;
- nevertheless, her mother states that she regrets nothing because music/idol activity united people and mattered experientially/relationally.

Ren's resulting transition is epistemically specific:

> **direct primary evidence can override an inherited-policy interpretation without requiring denial of the material event that generated the policy.**

This is a strong general character-modeling rule.

## 9.5 From sole bearer to co-bearer

After the source correction, Ren accepts:

- shared festival construction;
- Liella! membership;
- center/vocal responsibility within the group;
- peer correction;
- composition and procedural labor as contributions rather than monopolized authority.

S1E12 materially strengthens the transition.

The context that supported `わたくしが頑張るしかない` is altered by:

- sufficient applicant numbers for Yuigaoka to continue;
- financial support from Ren's father;
- Saya's return and explicit desire to help Ren protect the school;
- Liella! as a functioning co-bearing group;
- students and local community self-organizing around the school and performance.

Actual competitive defeat does not reactivate unilateral gatekeeping.

Frozen transition:

> **STRENGTHEN/STRUCTURALIZE:** co-bearing is no longer merely an interpersonal lesson. It is materially instantiated in family, school, group, and community structures.

## 9.6 Pride without exclusion

Ren's `結ヶ丘は一番の学校です` at the S1E12 boundary should not be read with the same pragmatic function as her early prestige logic.

The grammar can remain formal and emphatic while the meaning changes.

Early “value” rhetoric is used to restrict activity judged unworthy.

Late “best school” rhetoric follows:

- shared support;
- successful school continuation;
- community participation;
- competitive defeat.

It therefore functions as belonging and valuation rather than proof that only the highest-ranked output deserves legitimacy.

Frozen principle:

> **formal diction and pride remain stable resources while the pragmatic function of institutional language changes substantially.**

## 9.7 Remaining vulnerabilities

Ren is not frozen as permanently cured of over-responsibility.

Season 1 still shows:

- strong role conscience;
- catastrophic private self-disqualification language when she perceives incongruence with the student-president ideal (`失格です / 停学です / 退学です` in comic form);
- sensitivity to quality and institutional representation;
- a history of turning inferred legacy requirements into restrictive policy when evidence and co-bearers were absent.

These remain relevant vulnerability conditions.

## 9.8 Voice endpoint

Ren's voice is one of the clearest examples of stable register with changing function.

Established features include:

- formal/polite grammar in both conflict and affiliation;
- institutional/procedural vocabulary;
- capacity for severe judgment without casual insult;
- concise deference to recognized higher authority;
- literal inquiry in unfamiliar informal/digital domains;
- collaborative quality analysis after group incorporation;
- reciprocal fairness challenges toward Kanon;
- formal pride language that can move from exclusionary protection to shared belonging;
- catastrophic private role-conscience language in a distinct comic shame state.

Do not equate politeness with gentleness, nor formality with rigidity.

# 10. Liella! as a Season-1 ensemble system

## 10.1 Founding-five identity

At the S1E12 boundary, five-member Liella! is not merely “the five friends who became school idols.” It is a system with a specific architecture:

> **connected heterogeneity + distributed expertise + task-contingent visible authority + reciprocal representation + incomplete information + shared future commitment.**

Each term matters.

### Connected heterogeneity

Members are not interchangeable and are not required to converge toward one temperament.

### Distributed expertise

Different problems legitimately route to different members.

### Task-contingent visible authority

Center and public responsibility can move by performance need rather than permanent status.

### Reciprocal representation

Liella! represents Yuigaoka because the school/community actively invests in and co-authors that representation, not merely because Liella! claims the school's prestige.

### Incomplete information

Keke's concealed burden demonstrates that group cohesion is not total transparency.

### Shared future commitment

`優勝しよう` gives the first season a new explicit common competitive horizon.

## 10.2 The group is not hierarchy-free

The Season-1 endpoint should not be romanticized as flat equality.

Asymmetries remain:

- Kanon has unusual narrative/relational integrative centrality;
- Chisato has superior dance/training authority;
- Keke has superior school-idol ecosystem literacy and urgency;
- Sumire has distinctive show-business and visibility experience;
- Ren has distinctive formal/institutional and compositional competence;
- some relationships are more intimate or historically dense than others;
- Keke's private burden is asymmetrically known.

The important claim is not absence of hierarchy. It is that **hierarchy is plural and domain-bounded rather than reducible to one total rank order**.

## 10.3 Decision ecology

The five increasingly operate through corrective biases.

Examples:

- Keke generates momentum; Kanon checks pace/feasibility.
- Kanon can over-restrain requests; Sumire legitimizes ambition/visibility.
- Sumire can become status-sensitive; Keke can supply unusually credible evidence-based recognition.
- Chisato can diagnose capability; Kanon can challenge the relational meaning Chisato attaches to independence.
- Ren can impose institutional structure; Kanon can force motive/source verification; later Ren supplies formal competence without monopolizing legitimacy.

This creates a system in which one member's recurrent bias can become another member's useful corrective **without the correcting member being globally wiser**.

Frozen ensemble principle:

> **Liella! works best when differences are allowed to correct each other rather than when one member becomes the universal regulator.**

## 10.4 Workload and the bounded integrator problem

S1E09 explicitly reveals a group-risk state: Kanon becomes the default recipient of too many tasks.

Her response is not self-removal but boundary-setting.

This is a major development from S1E03.

Frozen group risk:

> **integrative centrality can become workload centralization unless the group deliberately routes tasks to domain owners.**

S1E10-S1E11 provide positive counterexamples through center/task redistribution.

## 10.5 Representation

The group's relation to Yuigaoka changes across the season.

Early:

- school prestige is used by Ren as a reason to restrict activity.

Middle:

- school history and community-rooted purpose are reconstructed through evidence and shared festival work.

Late:

- students themselves call Liella! `この学校の希望`;
- classmates take over logistics so Liella! can practice;
- neighborhood participants help build the public stage;
- Kanon calls Yuigaoka the best school after receiving this support;
- future victory is framed as `結ヶ丘の歌` rather than a detached Liella!-only achievement.

Frozen institutional relation:

> **Liella! becomes a representative of the school through reciprocal co-authorship, not through prestige extraction.**

## 10.6 What five-person Liella! has already accomplished before victory

At the S1 boundary, the group has already:

- helped generate enough applicant interest for Yuigaoka to continue into the next year;
- created a public identity recognized by peers and community;
- converted a divided school ecology toward more shared participation;
- established multiple non-ranked performances as meaningful in their own right;
- developed a five-person capability architecture;
- produced evidence that performance can matter independently of placement;
- created a community willing to materially invest in its future performances.

Therefore future competitive success cannot be treated as the first proof that Liella! matters.

## 10.7 The loss and the new shared goal

Sunny Passion wins the Tokyo stage and Liella! places second.

The result matters. The checkpoint should not minimize it by saying “winning doesn't matter.”

What Season 1 establishes is more precise:

- ranking is real;
- defeat hurts;
- competition can sharpen aspiration;
- winning can become a meaningful shared goal;
- but ranking does not retroactively erase the performance's artistic, relational, or institutional value.

Frozen ensemble competition state:

> **Liella! now wants to win. It does not yet follow that the group believes winning is the sole or highest source of worth.**

# 12. Yuigaoka, institution, and school ecology

## 12.2 But causal attribution must remain disciplined

Season 1 strongly links Liella! to school visibility and students call the group the school's hope.

However, the checkpoint should avoid an overly simple causal claim such as:

> “Liella! alone saved Yuigaoka.”

The episode also establishes:

- applicant numbers as the continuation criterion;
- paternal financial contribution;

# 13. Love Live! competition — frozen Season-1 model

## 13.1 What competition has taught the group

By the S1E12 boundary, competition has demonstrably taught or generated:

- pressure that exposes character-specific vulnerabilities;
- need for task-fit role allocation;
- value of domain-specific excellence;
- capacity to learn from admired rivals;
- direct comparison that can sharpen performance goals;
- experience of meaningful defeat;
- a new shared desire to win;
- a framework in which rivalry and admiration can coexist;
- stronger connection between group performance and represented community.

## 13.2 What competition has not established

Season 1 does **not** establish:

- that the winner is morally superior;
- that the winner's song is the only artistically valuable song;
- that second place makes Liella!'s stage a failure in every dimension;
- that technical ranking equals total human worth;
- that school value depends on beating every rival;
- that every member must personally be maximally competitive at all times;
- that non-ranked community performances are lesser or merely preparatory;
- that support is legitimate only if repaid through victory;
- that defeat proves a character's self-conception wrong in a totalizing way.

These negative findings are part of the frozen checkpoint because later success could otherwise retroactively make Season 1 appear more meritocratic than it actually is.

## 13.3 Sunny Passion

At the Season-1 boundary, Sunny Passion functions as:

- admired model;
- source of useful external critique;
- direct competitive superior in the Tokyo result;
- evidence that rivalry need not require contempt;
- voice for the developmental argument that competition can elevate participants.

The checkpoint does **not** infer their complete philosophy or later role beyond what S1 establishes.

## 13.4 Competitive ambition is acquired and layered

Kanon's pre-result ambivalence is important because it proves that the final `優勝しよう` is a transition, not revelation of a timeless hidden trait.

The endpoint model allows both of the following to remain true:

1. singing freely with others is already deeply valuable;
2. winning has become a genuine additional goal.

Frozen competition thesis:

> **Season 1 adds competitive ambition to Liella!'s value system; it does not replace the pre-existing value system with competition.**

# 23. Final freeze statement

At the end of S1E12, the most important fact about Liella! is not that the group has achieved an ideal equilibrium. It has not.

Fear remains.

Recognition wounds remain historically active and potentially triggerable.

Private burdens remain undisclosed.

Care can still become paternalistic.

Responsibility can still become overcentralized.

Institutional hierarchy is softened more clearly than it is structurally abolished.

Competition has become a stronger motive without having resolved its ethical relationship to art, belonging, and personhood.

What Season 1 has achieved is a more important prerequisite for future development: **the five have generated internal counterevidence to their most restrictive worth models.**

Kanon has evidence that fear and action can coexist.

Keke has evidence that revising a judgment does not weaken commitment.

Sumire has evidence that consequential recognition can actually remain hers through the decisive moment.

Chisato has evidence that receiving support does not invalidate self-authorship.

Ren has evidence that sharing responsibility does not betray institutional seriousness or maternal legacy.

Liella! has evidence that a group can lose and still have mattered—while also discovering that this very field of received meaning can make winning newly worth pursuing.

That is the frozen prospective state.
