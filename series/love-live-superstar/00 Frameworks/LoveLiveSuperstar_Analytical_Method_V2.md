# Love Live! Superstar!! — Analytical Method V2

## 0. Purpose

This protocol governs a second-pass deep reading of the complete three-season animated *Love Live! Superstar!!* corpus.

It borrows two complementary principles from the existing project models:

- from the *THE IDOLM@STER CINDERELLA GIRLS (2015)* workflow: sealed sequential reading, frozen checkpoints, explicit later-delta revision, native audiovisual analysis, and claim-to-source locator discipline;
- from the *Revue Starlight* workflow: explicit evidence states, primary analytical homes, separation of local close reading from retrospective synthesis, and mandatory stress-testing of mature theses.

The V2 pass is not a plot recap and not a franchise encyclopedia. Its central task is to explain how *Superstar!!* constructs personhood, group identity, aspiration, school belonging, succession, performance, competition, and adulthood across three years of changing Liella! membership.

The governing principle is:

> **Read every episode simultaneously as character drama, school/institution narrative, musical-performance text, visual work, and a chapter in an evolving ensemble ecology—without allowing later knowledge to erase what was genuinely uncertain at the time.**

A second principle is:

> **Membership growth is not background logistics. The transition from five to nine to eleven members changes the ethical and dramatic meaning of Liella! itself.**

A third principle is:

> **Performance should be analyzed as action. A song can resolve, defer, contradict, or transform a dramatic problem; it is not automatically a detachable emotional illustration.**

A fourth principle is:

> **The school-idol institution must be distinguished from the girls' personal desires. Competition, school reputation, recruitment, graduation, professional opportunity, and audience recognition may align with individual flourishing, but they are not identical to it.**

A fifth principle is:

> **Character analysis should remain reconstructable as behavior, not merely describable as theme. Episode readings must distinguish stable disposition, current developmental state, relationship-conditioned mode, situational trigger, perceived stakes, decision process, speech behavior, and counterexample so that later character modeling can remain evidence-grounded and time-indexed.**

# I. Corpus boundary and source hierarchy

## 1. Governing corpus

The primary corpus is the complete television anime:

- Season 1: Episodes 1–12
- Season 2: Episodes 1–12
- Season 3: Episodes 1–12

Use episode codes:

- `S1E01` … `S1E12`
- `S2E01` … `S2E12`
- `S3E01` … `S3E12`

## 2. Tier 1A — Direct audiovisual evidence

Authoritative for:

- shot composition;
- movement and blocking;
- facial performance;
- editing rhythm;
- choreography;
- costume staging;
- music entry/exit;
- silence;
- sound effects;
- vocal delivery;
- crowd and audience sound;
- spatial relation;
- performance transitions.

A retained frame can establish composition. It cannot by itself establish timing, movement, or vocal interaction.

### Mandatory local audiovisual extraction

Before an episode is canonicalized, its analysis bundle must be unpacked into the local workspace when the bundle contains a complete audio derivative. The analyst must:

- locate the complete episode audio, corrected Japanese subtitles, dialogue/scene indexes, manifests, and retained frames;
- verify audio codec, sample rate, channels, duration, and byte-level accessibility;
- compare audio duration against bundle/source-video duration within a documented tolerance;
- align high-value dialogue/performance moments against exact subtitle timestamps;
- perform targeted acoustic inspection of pauses, silence, mixed-track dynamics, entrances/exits, overlaps, and recurrence where those features bear on interpretation.

This is a **native episode-reading requirement**, not a retrospective optional extra.

When the environment does not provide human-like auditory audition, computational acoustic evidence may establish measurable properties of the waveform/spectrum, but it must not be inflated into unsupported claims about subjective timbre, instrument identity, accent quality, or emotional color.

## 3. Tier 1B — Japanese textual evidence

Use Japanese dialogue/subtitle layers and onscreen text for:

- exact wording;
- pronouns and address;
- register;
- repeated lexical fields;
- school terminology;
- competition terminology;
- naming and self-naming;
- expressions of desire, obligation, fear, and belonging.

When translation smooths or generalizes a phrase, the Japanese governs linguistic claims.

## 4. Tier 1C — Song/lyric evidence

Where Japanese lyrics are available, distinguish:

- song text as performed by the characters;
- song allocation among singers;
- staged narrative function;
- extra-diegetic production convention.

Do not assume every lyric is a literal first-person confession by every singer.

## 5. Tier 2 — Retrieval/navigation material

Use screenshot manifests, contact sheets, subtitle indices, scene lists, and metadata for navigation. Load-bearing claims must be rechecked against direct evidence.

## 6. Prior analyses and franchise knowledge

Prior analyses are retrieval aids and hypothesis records, not substitutes for source verification.

External franchise material, interviews, games, manga, live-event material, seiyuu commentary, guidebooks, and fandom consensus are excluded from the governing V2 interpretation unless explicitly introduced as labeled supplementary evidence.

# II. Sequential evidence boundary

The sequential pass must preserve prospective uncertainty.

For an analysis of `SxEyy`:

- semantic evidence may use only episodes through that episode;
- later-season information may not be used to explain away ambiguity;
- obvious production metadata such as total season length may be known, but future plot/character information is sealed;
- a future episode preview may be described only as paratext unless the user explicitly authorizes preview interpretation.

Each episode artifact must state:

- `semantic_evidence_boundary`;
- whether future semantic evidence was used;
- source-bundle identity;
- available Japanese-text/audio/image evidence;
- any source limitations.

After later evidence arrives, revise earlier claims by **delta**, not silent rewrite.

Use:

> Earlier reading → later pressure → revised full-series understanding.

# III. Evidence-state labels

Use labels when a claim's status could otherwise blur.

## AF — Audiovisual fact
Directly visible/audible event, composition, blocking, edit, or action.

## JT — Japanese textual fact
Direct wording, register, address, written text, or formal terminology.

## PF — Performance fact
Audible/visible acting feature: pause, interruption, crying coloration, breathlessness, unusual pitch/register, vocal overlap, hesitation, laughter, or emphasis.

## MF — Musical fact
Direct musical event: entrance, dropout, reprise, unison, solo allocation, instrumentation change, rhythmic shift, silence, tonal contrast.

## AM — Acoustic measurement
A measured property derived directly from the local audio file: pause duration, RMS/dBFS level, spectral activity, onset timing, pitch estimate, stereo/mix relation, or quantified similarity. AM claims must name the measurement boundary when it matters and must not be treated as equivalent to human auditory description.

## FR — Formal recurrence
Demonstrable repeated or transformed line, image, place, costume form, movement, staging geometry, musical phrase, object, or dramatic situation.

## SI — Strong inference
Interpretation supported by multiple independent evidence streams.

## IT — Interpretive thesis
Higher-order explanation organizing multiple scenes or episodes.

## SP — Speculation
Plausible but underdetermined.

Mandatory distinctions:

- character belief ≠ series endorsement;
- competition result ≠ moral truth;
- talent judgment ≠ total human worth;
- public success ≠ private flourishing;
- group cohesion ≠ absence of hierarchy;
- support ≠ self-erasure;
- admiration ≠ equality;
- leadership ≠ unilateral authority;
- seniority ≠ maturity;
- performance lyric ≠ literal biography;
- visual recurrence ≠ fixed symbolic meaning.

# IV. Episode-level V2 workflow

Every episode receives the following passes.

## Pass 0 — Source lock and local extraction

Record:

- source filename/bundle;
- checksum if available;
- retained-frame count;
- subtitle/dialogue source;
- audio availability;
- contact-sheet coverage;
- missing/corrupt components;
- exact episode title if visible.

Then, before semantic analysis is finalized:

1. unpack the episode bundle into the local workspace;
2. verify the complete-audio derivative with `ffprobe` or equivalent metadata inspection;
3. record audio codec, sample rate, channel count, duration, byte size, and SHA-256 where practical;
4. verify that the corrected Japanese subtitle layer and dialogue index can be aligned to the audio timeline;
5. identify the episode's high-value speech, silence, score, and performance moments for targeted acoustic review;
6. retain the local source only as long as needed for the episode audit, unless storage policy requires otherwise.

An episode is not ready for canonicalization until this audio preflight is complete when complete audio exists in the bundle.

## Pass 1 — Naive structural read

Before building a thesis, reconstruct the episode as experienced in sequence:

- opening dramatic problem;
- scene progression;
- reversals;
- performance placement;
- ending state;
- unresolved residue.

Do not force a thematic thesis before the dramatic mechanism is clear.

## Pass 2 — Character-state analysis

For every materially affected character, track:

- stated desire;
- enacted desire;
- fear;
- self-conception;
- public role;
- private role;
- defensive strategy;
- capacity gained/lost;
- who can contradict her;
- who she listens to;
- what she cannot yet articulate.

### Pass 2A — Behavioral-rule extraction

For modeling-relevant behavior, normalize important observations into a conditional rule rather than leaving them only as prose description. Record, when evidence permits:

- character and active episode-boundary state;
- trigger/context;
- what the character appears to perceive as being at stake;
- active goal or avoidance goal;
- internal conflict or competing motive;
- default behavioral response;
- escalation/de-escalation conditions;
- relationship-conditioned variation;
- exception or counterexample;
- exact evidence locator;
- confidence: high / medium / low.

Do not convert a single memorable scene into a stable trait. A rule becomes stronger only through recurrence, contrast, or a scene that explicitly establishes the character's governing logic.

The preferred form is:

> **trigger + perceived stakes + current state → likely response, with named exceptions.**

## Pass 3 — Relationship analysis

Identify the episode's active relationship systems.

Track:

- asymmetry;
- care;
- rivalry;
- admiration;
- dependence;
- mentorship;
- conflict style;
- repair;
- mutual recognition;
- changes in who gets to define the relationship.

Avoid reducing the ensemble to fixed pairs when the episode is functioning through triads, cohorts, or whole-group ecology.

For character modeling, also record **directional relationship conditioning**: how Character A's behavior/speech changes specifically with Character B, rather than assuming a trait transfers unchanged across interlocutors.

## Pass 4 — Institution and school ecology

Track Yuigaoka and the school-idol environment as institutions.

Questions:

- What does the school demand?
- What does the club demand?
- What does Love Live! reward?
- What does public recognition change?
- Who performs administrative/emotional labor?
- How are recruitment and succession handled?
- When does institutional survival help or distort personal aspiration?

## Pass 5 — Cohort and succession analysis

This is a *Superstar!!*-specific mandatory layer.

Track three levels:

1. original five-member Liella!;
2. second-generation expansion;
3. third-generation expansion and graduation horizon.

For each episode ask:

- Who is treated as an insider?
- Who feels like a guest, junior, successor, rival, burden, or inheritor?
- Is tradition enabling or constraining?
- What knowledge can be transmitted?
- What cannot be inherited and must be discovered personally?
- Does seniority produce responsibility, authority, anxiety, or distance?

## Pass 6 — Performance and musical dramaturgy

For every musically or dramaturgically significant event, first assign a significance class:

- **M0 — incidental:** music is present but yields no material analytical evidence; no full ledger entry;
- **M1 — supportive:** music reinforces an already-established state; compact notation only;
- **M2 — diagnostic:** musical/performance form supplies important character, relationship, institutional, visual, vocal, or thematic evidence; full ledger entry;
- **M3 — state-changing:** the musical event materially produces, demonstrates, legitimizes, or transforms a character, relationship, group, institutional, or thematic state; full entry plus applicable longitudinal updates.

For M2/M3 events, analyze proportionally:

- event class and diegetic/quasi-diegetic/presentation-space status;
- immediate pre-performance dramatic question and unresolved state;
- authorship, song choice, creative authority, assignment, and veto structure;
- performer configuration, center/periphery, absences, and changes within the event;
- audience configuration: who witnesses, judges, recognizes, legitimizes, or is conspicuously absent;
- lyric allocation, not merely aggregate lyric content, and whether lines are character-aligned, aspirational, contradictory, or collective;
- internal sectional architecture: changes in performer allocation, lyric perspective, visual grammar, musical density, staging, or dramatic function inside one song;
- vocal allocation and solo/duet/unison/layering only where direct evidence supports the claim;
- choreography, blocking, formation, camera emphasis, costume/color grammar, and stage-space transformation;
- audible musical construction and arrangement only to the level justified by the available source;
- performance interpretation and performance ideology: what conception of school-idol performance the event enacts;
- formal competitive result separately from dramatic, character, relationship, and institutional result;
- backward recurrence/callback links available at the current semantic boundary;
- evidence state and confidence per claim domain.

Mandatory causal distinction:

> **Does the performance merely accompany a state change, represent it, demonstrate that it is possible, legitimize it, or actually enact it through musical/performance form?**

Required question:

> **What can the performance do dramatically that ordinary dialogue could not?**

If the answer is “nothing,” say so rather than overinterpreting.

### Pass 6A — Canonical musical-dramaturgy write-through

`LLS_MUSICAL_DRAMATURGY_LEDGER.md` is the canonical longitudinal home for music-as-action evidence. For every M2/M3 event, write or update an entry after direct source verification. M1 events may receive compact entries when they contribute to a longitudinal pattern. M0 events are normally omitted.

Existing episode prose may seed dramaturgical observations, but it may not substitute for direct audiovisual evidence when the new claim concerns vocal realization, arrangement, instrumentation, internal musical segmentation, staging synchronization, or recurrence.

Historical backfill uses `observation_status: retrospective_backfill`; later targeted rechecks use `retrospective_reaudit`; newly analyzed episodes use `prospective`. Frozen checkpoints remain immutable. If backfill pressures an earlier claim, route it through **PRESERVE / STRENGTHEN / REVISE / DOWNGRADE / REJECT / OPEN** rather than silently changing the checkpoint or canonical episode reading.

### Pass 6B — Internal performance segmentation

Do not treat an insert song as one indivisible block when its internal construction changes dramatic function. Segment at source-grounded transitions such as:

- performer-group handoff;
- solo/ensemble change;
- lyric-perspective change;
- stage-space or formation change;
- costume/color-grammar transformation;
- audience-position change;
- acoustic entrance/dropout or marked density change;
- rivalry-to-convergence or private-to-public transition.

The segment boundary is an analytical locator, not a claim that the music is formally a new composition section unless that is independently established.

### Pass 6C — Evidence limits

When unrestricted human-like audition is unavailable, do not infer singer identity solely from camera focus, or claim subjective timbre, exact harmony, instrument identity, or arrangement detail from mixed-track measurements alone. Use **AM** for measurements and keep the interpretation narrower than the measurement warrants.

## Pass 7 — Japanese voice and performed vocal state

Track recurring voice behaviors:

- honorifics/nicknames;
- register shifts;
- sentence endings;
- multilingual code-switching;
- formal versus intimate address;
- interruptions and hesitations;
- public-stage voice versus private speech;
- how senior/junior relations alter speech.

Character “voice” means both linguistic style and performed delivery.

For model-ready voice reconstruction, distinguish at minimum:

- baseline casual/private register;
- public/formal register;
- family mode;
- peer/intimate-friend mode;
- senior/junior mode when applicable;
- conflict/confrontation mode;
- embarrassment/avoidance mode;
- mentoring/support mode;
- self-talk or monologue mode;
- recurrent self-reference, address terms, sentence endings, discourse markers, code-switching, lexical preferences, ellipsis, interruption, and nonverbal vocal events.

The voice ledger must separate **what is directly attested** from what would merely sound plausible in imitation.

### Pass 7A — Mandatory local acoustic audit

For interpretively important spoken or sung moments, inspect the local audio against exact subtitle timestamps. Where useful, measure:

- duration of pauses and response latency;
- disappearance or return of soundtrack energy;
- RMS/dBFS changes in the mixed track;
- onset/offset timing;
- overlap between dialogue and song;
- spectral/harmonic continuity across reprises;
- pitch contour only when the signal is sufficiently clean for a defensible estimate.

Use **AM** for computationally measured claims. Use **PF/MF** only for performed/musical properties that the available evidence actually supports.

If direct human-like auditory perception is unavailable, do not manufacture claims such as “warm timbre,” “breathy delivery,” “accented vowel,” or exact instrumentation from numbers alone. Instead state the measurable acoustic fact and the narrower interpretation it supports.

## Pass 8 — Visual and spatial grammar

Track recurring spaces and their changing function, especially:

- Yuigaoka campus;
- practice spaces;
- streets and urban public space;
- home/family spaces;
- rooftops/stairs/thresholds;
- transit and travel;
- competition stages;
- backstage/holding spaces;
- locations tied to individual members.

Ask how composition distributes:

- center/periphery;
- isolation/grouping;
- height;
- distance;
- barriers;
- reflected or framed bodies;
- forward movement versus stasis.

## Pass 9 — Music and sound outside songs

Track from the **actual local audio file**, not only subtitle placement:

- recurring score cues;
- silence and near-silence;
- ambient school/city sound;
- audience noise;
- rehearsal sound;
- transitions into performance;
- dialogue/song overlap;
- relative mixed-track dynamics;
- acoustically demonstrable reprises or dropouts;
- when music refuses to sentimentalize a scene.

The canonical episode reading must include the strongest episode-local audio findings before finalization. Retrospective audio work remains appropriate for **cross-episode recurrence, later comparative questions, or improved tooling**, but it must not substitute for basic native audio review that could have been performed during the original episode pass.

## Pass 10 — Counterevidence and alternative readings

Every episode must include at least one meaningful stress test:

- What evidence resists the governing thesis?
- Could the same scene support a narrower explanation?
- Is the episode resolving a problem or merely changing its form?
- Are we mistaking aesthetic approval for ethical approval?
- Are later franchise expectations being projected backward?

## Pass 11 — Cumulative deltas and model-ready normalization

Update ongoing ledgers. Four model-facing ledgers are mandatory canonical homes:

- `LLS_CHARACTER_STATE_LEDGER.md`;
- `LLS_CHARACTER_BEHAVIOR_AND_DECISION_LEDGER.md`;
- `LLS_CHARACTER_VOICE_MODEL_LEDGER.md`;
- `LLS_RELATIONSHIP_CONDITIONING_MATRIX.md`.

Also update the established longitudinal systems as applicable:

- cohort/succession ledger;
- institution/competition ledger;
- performance/song ledger;
- Japanese dialogue/address index;
- visual/motif ledger;
- callback/recurrence ledger;
- open-questions ledger.

### Model-ready normalization rules

1. **Time-index every state.** `Kanon@S1E01` and a later Kanon are not interchangeable models.
2. **Separate disposition from state.** Stable tendencies, temporary stress responses, learned capacities, and relationship-specific modes require separate fields.
3. **Preserve alternatives.** Novel-situation prediction should report the most likely response, plausible alternatives, and the conditions that would change the prediction rather than claim a single canon answer.
4. **Track negative evidence.** A character failing to exhibit an expected tendency can be as informative as recurrence.
5. **Record counterexamples.** Every strengthened behavioral rule should carry known exceptions.
6. **Do not overfit catchphrases.** Surface verbal mimicry without pragmatic/contextual fit is not a successful voice model.
7. **Route evidence, not invented interiority.** Unspoken motives may be modeled only as labeled inference with confidence.
8. **Use the episode boundary as the active model boundary.** Later development may revise a model by delta but must not silently overwrite earlier states.

The goal is not deterministic roleplay. The goal is an auditable probabilistic reconstruction of what a character is likely to perceive, value, say, avoid, or do under specified conditions.

# VI. Season checkpoints

Freeze a checkpoint after each season before beginning retrospective reinterpretation from the next season.

Each checkpoint records:

- strongest current series thesis;
- character states;
- relationship states;
- ensemble hierarchy;
- cohort structure;
- unresolved questions;
- competition/institutional interpretation;
- song/performance findings;
- major visual/musical recurrences;
- claims considered fragile;
- explicit predictions or expectations.

Later seasons may pressure the checkpoint, but the checkpoint itself remains unchanged.

Write later correction files as deltas.

This preserves the difference between:

> what Season 1 reasonably meant at its ending

and

> what the complete three-season corpus later reveals about that ending.

# VII. Full-series retrospective operations

After all 36 episode analyses are complete, the retrospective pass performs:

## 1. Consolidation
Bring distributed evidence into primary analytical homes.

## 2. Stress-testing
Search deliberately for episodes/scenes that weaken mature theses.

## 3. Differentiation
Keep superficially similar arcs distinct: fear, ambition, envy, admiration, responsibility, dependence, belonging, professional aspiration, and romantic/affective intensity should not be collapsed.

## 4. Retrospective revision
Use:

> Initial formation → later pressure → persistence / complication / regression / extension / transformation.

## 5. Cohort comparison
Compare what “Liella!” means at 5, 9, and 11 members.

## 6. Ending analysis
Treat graduation, succession, future career, group continuity, and remaining school life as separate variables.

# VIII. Core hypotheses to test, not assume

## H1 — Liella! changes from chosen small-group intimacy into an institution capable of succession.
Test whether expansion actually redistributes ownership, authority, and emotional centrality.

## H2 — Kanon functions as a center but the series increasingly tests whether a healthy ensemble can survive decentering her.
Test scenes where her leadership enables others versus scenes where narrative gravity recenters her.

## H3 — The series treats talent as real but rejects talent as a complete theory of worth.
Test competition outcomes, training arcs, recruitment judgments, and responses to failure.

## H4 — School-idol performance converts private aspiration into public relationship.
Test whether songs genuinely create mutual recognition or merely aestheticize already-resolved feelings.

## H5 — Growth requires both inheritance and non-repetition.
Test how younger members learn from seniors without becoming copies, and how seniors respond when juniors exceed, reinterpret, or refuse inherited models.

## H6 — Competition is narratively useful but ethically incomplete.
Test where Love Live! results clarify capability and where they fail to measure friendship, artistic identity, or future possibility.

## H7 — Graduation is not simply separation; it is a test of whether Liella! is a relationship, a name, an institution, a practice, or all four.

# IX. Required analytical safeguards

- Do not diagnose characters clinically without textual warrant.
- Do not convert shipping preference into evidentiary conclusion.
- Distinguish romantic coding, intimate friendship, admiration, dependence, and explicit romance.
- Do not treat every song costume/color as a fixed symbolic lexicon.
- Do not use competition victory as proof that a worldview is correct.
- Do not use later success to erase earlier failure or uncertainty.
- Do not assume “more members” automatically means stronger ensemble writing.
- Do not confuse narrative centrality with moral superiority.
- Do not reduce foreignness, multilingualism, family background, regional identity, or class position to decorative character flavor.
- Prefer exact Japanese wording when linguistic nuance carries the argument.

# X. Source lifecycle and storage discipline

The canonical primary-source archive remains the episode ZIP corpus in Google Drive. The local workspace is a temporary analytical execution environment, not a second source archive and not a reason to duplicate the full episode corpus into File Library.

For every future episode use this lifecycle:

1. **Fetch** the exact episode ZIP from its canonical Drive source folder.
2. **Verify** filename, Drive/source-lock identity, byte size, checksum when available, and ZIP integrity.
3. **Unpack locally** into a temporary workspace. Full frames, contact sheets, subtitles, manifests, indexes, and complete audio may be used during the active episode pass.
4. **Analyze under the full V2 workflow**, including local acoustic audit and model-ledger extraction.
5. **Distill durable knowledge** into the canonical episode Markdown, evidence locators, cumulative ledgers/indexes, and checkpoint infrastructure.
6. **Write and read back durable artifacts in Drive** before deleting the local source payload.
7. **Clean the temporary workspace** before fetching the next episode.

### Durable analytical material

Preserve in the analytical corpus:

- canonical episode deep reading;
- character-state, behavior/decision, voice-model, and relationship-conditioning ledger deltas;
- claim/evidence locators and revision routing;
- exact timestamps/cue IDs/frame IDs needed to reacquire evidence;
- source checksum and bundle identity;
- compact metadata required for provenance;
- derived analytical objects that are genuinely expensive or impossible to reconstruct.

### Optional small derivative cache

Small text derivatives may be retained when they materially improve longitudinal retrieval, especially:

- corrected Japanese subtitle track;
- dialogue index;
- scene index;
- bundle metadata;
- compact timing/manifests.

They are **optional**, not mandatory duplicates. If the canonical ledgers and locators already preserve the needed retrieval path, the Drive source bundle remains sufficient.

### Delete after canonicalization/readback unless specifically justified

Normally remove from the local workspace:

- downloaded episode ZIP;
- complete extracted audio;
- ordinary retained-frame JPEGs;
- contact sheets;
- temporary waveform/spectrogram images;
- temporary audio clips;
- intermediate extraction directories;
- any redundant copy already recoverable from the canonical Drive bundle.

Do **not** keep complete audio merely because later synthesis may need it. Record the exact locator and reacquire the episode on demand. Likewise, ordinary screenshots should be represented by timestamps/frame IDs unless an annotated or comparative derived image has independent analytical value.

### Selective reacquisition

Later checkpoints and specialist syntheses should begin from ledgers and locators, then reacquire only the episode bundles needed to verify the load-bearing cross-episode claim. This turns storage into a cold-source/hot-workspace model:

> **Drive source once → temporary full extraction → compact analytical memory → selective reacquisition.**

The episode YAML should normally record:

- `source_lifecycle: drive_cold_source_temp_local_extract`;
- `local_cleanup_status`;
- `retained_local_derivatives` or `none`;
- `model_ledgers_updated: true/false`.

# XI. Revision history

## V2.1 audio-workflow revision

Effective 2026-08-14, complete episode audio is treated as a required local analytical source whenever present in the bundle. The change was prompted by the S1E01 source audit, which demonstrated that unpacked audio supports reliable timestamped analysis of silence, response latency, mixed-track dynamics, song/dialogue overlap, and recurrence even when the environment does not provide human-like headphone audition.

S1E01 is the migration case: its initial text/visual reading was backfilled with same-episode acoustic evidence **before the Season 1 checkpoint freeze**. Future episodes should complete this step before canonicalization rather than requiring later repair.

## V2.2 character-modeling and storage revision

Effective 2026-08-24, the episode workflow explicitly produces time-indexed model-ready character evidence and uses a cold-source/hot-workspace storage lifecycle. The change adds behavioral-rule extraction, relationship-conditioned behavior, contextual Japanese voice modeling, four canonical cumulative modeling ledgers, probabilistic simulation discipline, and mandatory post-readback cleanup of redundant local source payloads.

S1E01 is backfilled into the four model-facing ledgers without changing its sealed semantic boundary. S1E02 is the first episode to use the complete V2.2 workflow from initial fetch through cleanup.

# XII. Final output standard

The finished V2 corpus should make it possible to answer, with traceable evidence:

- how each principal character changes;
- how each major relationship changes;
- how Liella! changes as an ensemble and institution;
- how seniority and succession are negotiated;
- how performance functions dramatically;
- how school identity and competition shape desire;
- how Japanese voice and address encode relationship;
- how visual/music systems recur and transform;
- what the three-season ending resolves and leaves open;
- which interpretations were strengthened, weakened, or overturned by hindsight.
- what stable and state-dependent behavioral rules are supported for each principal character;
- how each character's speech changes by context and interlocutor;
- what novel-situation responses are high-confidence, conditional, or underdetermined;
- which counterexamples limit any proposed character model.

The target is not maximal interpretation.

The target is **maximal explanatory power under disciplined evidence constraints**.
