# Love Live! Superstar!! — S3E04 Deep Reading V2
## `No Rain, No Rainbow`

# 1. Governing thesis

S3E02 introduced Onitsuka Tomari as a participant who could act seriously without conceding that school-idol activity had value. S3E03 preserved that asymmetry while Liella! demonstrated a separate form of succession through Shiki's reconstructed center role. S3E04 connects those two threads by asking a harder question than whether dreams are “good”:

> **What is a rational response to aspiration when there is genuine evidence that aspiration can hurt, waste time, and fail?**

The episode refuses the easy answer that Tomari is simply too cold to understand dreams. Her anti-dream position is historically learned and relationally protective.

As a child, Natsumi repeatedly generated enormous aspirations: Olympic gold medalist, Nobel Prize-winning scientist, supermodel, and many others recorded in a `夢ノート`. The episode does not present those projects as secretly successful. It presents repeated failure. Natsumi eventually says:

> `あれもダメ これもダメ 全部ダメ。`
>
> `私には 才能がないんですの。`
>
> `夢を追う資格も きっとない。`
>
> `夢を追うなんて 無駄な行為ですの。`

Her familiar money language then appears not as an unrelated personality quirk but as an explicit safety substitution:

> `お金でもためて 困らないように生きていこう。`
>
> `お金なら裏切らない。`
>
> `大切なのは夢なんかより現実。マニーですの！`

This **STRENGTHENS** the Season-2 model without sanitizing it. Natsumi's commercial competence, metric orientation, and exploitative media reflexes were real. S3E04 now gives stronger causal evidence for why money became psychologically attractive: unlike dreams, it appeared countable, accumulable, and less likely to produce another identity-level failure.

Tomari watched this transformation from beside her sister. She then internalized Natsumi's conclusion:

> `夢なんかより… 現実。`
>
> `夢なんて… 持つものじゃない。`

S3E04 therefore **REVISES** the provisional S3E02 Tomari model from generic utility skepticism into something more precise:

> **Tomari's instrumentalism is partly protective pessimism learned from witnessing Natsumi's repeated injury.**

Her goal is not merely to win an argument. She states why she is willing to oppose Natsumi's current school-idol dream:

> `あなたたちと姉者との関係は スクールアイドル活動をしている間だけになるかもしれない。`
>
> `けれど私は これまでも これからも ずっと見ていくのです。姉者のことを。`
>
> `もう… 傷ついてほしくないのです。姉者に…。`

Tomari assigns herself a longer temporal horizon than Natsumi's current teammates. School-idol relationships may end; sisterhood will not. From that perspective, pessimism becomes a form of attempted harm prevention.

The episode does not defeat this position by proving Natsumi will achieve her dream. It cannot: Natsumi herself concedes that future failure may hurt, and the second-years openly admit that winning by their own strength feels like `夢のまた…夢`. The solution is **accepted risk**, not predictive certainty.

Natsumi's mature answer is therefore fundamentally different from her childhood optimism. She does not say “this dream will definitely work.” She says:

> `落ち込む時や 傷つく時があったとしても`
>
> `スクールアイドルに出会えて 初めて分かったの。`
>
> `本当に 楽しいって思える笑顔。`
>
> `マニーよりも もっと もっとステキな 最高の笑顔になれる日が来ると 信じているの。`

Then she asks Tomari not to stop watching, but to **keep watching while Natsumi accepts the risk herself**:

> `だから これからも 私を 見ていてほしい！`

That request preserves Tomari's care while rejecting care as veto.

The title's rain/rainbow structure works because the episode does not redefine rain as secretly pleasant. Hurt remains hurt. Failure remains possible. The second-years instead re-read Natsumi's dream notebook as evidence of repeated agency: she tried many things, failed many times, and is still capable of choosing another aspiration. Shiki supplies the phrase explicitly:

> `No Rain, No Rainbow.`

The strongest reading is not “suffering guarantees reward.” It is:

> **a life designed to eliminate all possibility of disappointment can also eliminate some forms of meaningful joy, relation, and self-authored striving; therefore accepting an uncertain dream can be rational even when success cannot be promised.**

S3E04 simultaneously changes the Season-3 organizational experiment. Yuigaoka now faces an explicit Love Live! rule:

> `出場は１校につき １グループ。`

Only one group from the school may participate. The two formations are therefore **institutionally zero-sum** at the entry gate. Yet the episode refuses to make their relationships zero-sum. Students say they like both groups; Kinako cannot think of the other formation as an enemy; Kanon praises Liella!'s performance; Wien insists competition is real; Kanon answers that she wants the groups to raise one another rather than drag one another down. Public observers repeatedly misread shared space as reunification, while the characters explicitly preserve organizational distinction.

This produces a useful dual model:

> **the institution can impose a scarce slot without requiring the people competing for it to become socially antagonistic.**

Finally, S3E04 materially advances succession. Natsumi states a concrete second-year dream:

> `今度は 自分が中心になって`
>
> `いつか かのん先輩たちが卒業したあとも`
>
> `頼られるような存在になりたいって！`

and then says Kinako, Mei, Shiki, and Natsumi all share that orientation. This is stronger than seniors delegating creative work to juniors. The second-years now **self-author a future in which they carry Liella! after the seniors graduate**.

Kanon's role correspondingly becomes less epistemically dominant. She tries to answer Tomari with the general proposition that dreams produce unexpected strength. Tomari challenges the argument as talent-biased, using Kanon's singing ability and championship victory as evidence. When Kanon reaches for Natsumi as counterexample, Natsumi stops her and speaks for herself. The decisive interpretation of Natsumi's history then comes from Natsumi and the second-year cohort, not from Kanon.

The strongest sealed S3E04 thesis is therefore:

> **S3E04 reveals Natsumi's money-first worldview and Tomari's utility-first worldview as two generations of the same injury-avoidance system. Natsumi once converted repeated failed dreams into `お金なら裏切らない`; Tomari learned from that collapse and turned it into a protective doctrine meant to keep Natsumi from being hurt again. The episode does not answer Tomari by promising success. Natsumi accepts that future failure can hurt, identifies school idols as the source of a form of genuine joy she values more than pure safety, and asks Tomari to remain a witness without becoming a veto. In parallel, Yuigaoka's two formations become institutionally zero-sum but relationally nonzero-sum, while the second-years explicitly author a post-senior Liella! future.**

Compactly:

> **S3E04 does not say that rain guarantees a rainbow. It says that refusing all rain is not a neutral choice when some of the life one values exists on the other side of taking that risk.**

# 4. Episode architecture — two versions of injury avoidance

S3E04 is structured around two temporally separated reactions to the same problem.

## 4.1 Natsumi's first reaction: replace uncertain aspiration with countable safety

The cold open already places failure before the title. Young Natsumi returns from another unsuccessful attempt; Tomari angrily defends her effort; Natsumi says the desired possibilities have mostly disappeared. Rain follows.

The later flashback expands the pattern into repeated ambition/failure. The `夢ノート` visually records accumulation rather than one isolated disappointment. Natsumi eventually turns repeated outcomes into a category judgment about herself:

- no talent;
- no qualification to dream;
- dreaming is useless.

Money appears precisely at that transition. It is not merely “Natsumi likes cash.” It becomes a risk-management system:

- accumulation can be observed;
- utility is legible;
- money can prevent material difficulty;
- `お金なら裏切らない` imagines money as less relationally/psychologically treacherous than aspiration.

This directly strengthens the S2 interpretation that monetization was partly defensive without converting every commercial behavior into trauma symptom. Natsumi still learned real media skills, cared about money materially, and sometimes violated group boundaries for attention/metrics. The causal explanation adds dimensionality; it does not erase responsibility.

## 4.2 Tomari's second reaction: protect Natsumi from re-entering the injury system

Tomari observes Natsumi's anti-dream turn and adopts it as doctrine. That doctrine becomes especially forceful when Natsumi later returns to aspiration through school idols.

From Tomari's point of view, Natsumi has already produced the evidence:

1. dream;
2. work;
3. failure;
4. hurt;
5. abandonment;
6. stabilization through money/realism.

School idols appear to reopen the loop.

This explains why S3E02's “school idols will probably only hurt `姉者`” was so load-bearing. S3E04 converts it from a plausible protective hypothesis into directly grounded family history.

Tomari's action style follows naturally:

- join the activity to inspect it directly;
- measure feasibility;
- distinguish necessary from unnecessary effort;
- challenge unsupported optimism;
- oppose a future she predicts will recreate earlier injury.

Her pessimism is therefore relationally costly but internally coherent.

# 5. The Onitsuka sibling model

## 5.1 Tomari's care is not softness

Tomari's sibling attachment is expressed through defense, analysis, future surveillance, and prevention rather than sentimental reassurance.

As a child she tells Natsumi that evaluators who fail to see her greatness are incompetent. As a teenager she has moved toward almost the opposite surface position—Natsumi's dream is impossible—but the relational constant is recognizable: **Tomari is still organizing herself around Natsumi's outcome**.

The strategy changes because the evidence changed.

Earlier:

> protect Natsumi's worth by blaming the evaluator.

Later:

> protect Natsumi's wellbeing by attacking the risky aspiration itself.

This is a valuable character-model distinction. Tomari is not simply “supportive” or “unsupportive.” She is highly supportive of Natsumi as a person while being actively oppositional toward a choice she predicts will harm her.

## 5.2 Different time horizons produce different authority claims

Tomari explicitly contrasts teammate temporality with sibling temporality:

> school-idol relationships may last only while school-idol activity lasts;
>
> Tomari has watched Natsumi before and will continue watching after.

This is not proof that teammates matter less. It is Tomari's reason for claiming a special protective standing.

The conflict is therefore partly about **who gets to decide what care permits**.

Tomari's position:

> because I will bear witness to the long-term aftermath, I am justified in trying to prevent predictable injury.

Natsumi's final response:

> continue bearing witness, but let me own the risk.

That is a much more precise repair than “Tomari learns to support dreams.”

## 5.3 Natsumi does not ask Tomari to stop caring

The final `見ていてほしい` matters because Natsumi does not solve autonomy by demanding relational distance.

She thanks Tomari for loving her smile. She recognizes the motive. She then changes the requested form of care:

- not preemptive prohibition;
- not certainty that nothing bad will happen;
- continued witness while Natsumi chooses uncertain aspiration.

This is **attachment-preserving autonomy**, not separation from the protector.

## 5.4 Tomari's endpoint remains ideologically unresolved

Tomari listens to Natsumi's final declaration, and the visual sequence gives her sustained reaction shots inside the rain-to-light transition. But she receives no explicit line equivalent to:

- school idols have value;
- dreams are now worthwhile;
- I agree with your choice.

Therefore:

> **emotional reception is established; philosophical conversion is not.**

This preserves the S3E02 methodological distinction between behavior and belief.

# 6. Natsumi — from dream-ineligibility to accepted-risk aspiration

S3E04 gives Natsumi one of her most diagnostically important episodes because it makes her past and current value systems directly comparable.

## 6.1 Childhood aspiration was expansive but fragile under repeated categorical failure

Young Natsumi does not lack aspiration. She has too many aspirations to fit a stereotype of naturally transactional thinking.

What changes is her interpretation of accumulated failure.

She moves from:

> this attempt failed

through:

> many attempts failed

to:

> **I am the kind of person who has no talent / no right to dream.**

This matches and strengthens the categorical-self-exclusion mechanisms seen earlier in the corpus.

## 6.2 Money is a defensive certainty claim

`お金なら裏切らない` is stronger than simple preference. It personifies money as a safer partner than aspiration.

The S2 model should therefore be preserved in dual form:

- **genuine commercial/producer orientation**;
- **defensive substitution for aspirations that became identity-threatening.**

Neither invalidates the other.

## 6.3 School idols do not make Natsumi forget the evidence against dreams

Natsumi's S3E04 account explicitly concedes the negative record. She stops Kanon from turning her into an easy inspirational counterexample and says she had not realized any dream before joining Liella!.

That self-interruption is crucial for simulation and future synthesis. Natsumi does not want support that requires falsifying her history.

## 6.4 Her current dream is concrete and successor-oriented

Natsumi's new desire is not the abstract statement “dreams are good.” It includes:

- singing again on the final stage;
- becoming central rather than peripheral;
- becoming a person others can rely on;
- carrying responsibility after the current seniors graduate.

This makes S2's future-center imagination substantially more durable.

## 6.5 The mature difference is risk ownership

Child Natsumi treated failed aspirations as evidence to stop aspiring.

Current Natsumi says that even if she becomes depressed or hurt, school idols have shown her a kind of genuinely enjoyable smile worth pursuing.

Thus the developmental transition is not:

> pessimist -> optimist.

It is:

> **avoid uncertain desire because failure invalidates me -> choose uncertain desire while knowing failure remains possible because the activity/relationship can carry values not exhausted by successful outcome.**

This connects directly with S3E02's performance ontology without making Natsumi's arc derivative of Kanon's argument.

# 7. Tomari — utility reasoning as evidence discipline and protective pessimism

## 7.1 Necessity-filtered practice remains real

The episode does not caricature this objection. Kanon cannot simply answer with a universal success formula.
