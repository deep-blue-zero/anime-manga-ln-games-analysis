# Love Live! Superstar!! — S3E05 Deep Reading V2
## `你好！上海！小籠包～！` / `Ni Hao! Shanghai! Xiaolongbao!`

# 1. Governing thesis

S3E04 turned Season 3's rival-group split into a genuine institutional scarcity problem: Yuigaoka may sustain two school-idol formations, but Love Live! permits only one school representative. At the same time, S3E04 made the second-years think prospectively about what Liella! becomes after the seniors graduate. S3E05 moves the same temporal pressure directly onto the third-years. Its opening object is not a microphone, club application, or tournament notice, but the school's `進路希望調査票`: a demand that students give administrative form to the person they expect to become after graduation.

The episode's central problem is therefore **not yet “what should Keke choose?”** It is more basic:

> **What happens when a person has fully authored a meaningful present but has not yet authored the self who comes after that present ends?**

Keke can describe her dream with unusual precision:

> `可可の夢は 高校３年間を`
>
> `精いっぱい スクールアイドルとして過ごすことです。`

This is not a vague aspiration. The dream has a domain, an intensity, and an explicit duration: **three years of high school, lived fully as a school idol**. Kanon immediately recognizes it as something she shares. But when Kanon asks what lies beyond it, Keke cannot convert that completed present-tense authorship into an equally owned future route.

She does not claim that the future is irrelevant. She says:

> `可可は とにかく 今を一番大事にしたい。`
>
> `今のために 生きてるんデス。`

and then carefully blocks the easiest negative interpretation: she knows the future matters and has not abandoned her studies. Her difficulty is not generalized irresponsibility. It is **post-boundary self-definition**.

Her family provides two plausible directions rather than one cartoonishly coercive order. Her parents have suggested returning home and attending her father's alma mater in Beijing. Her older sister—the person Keke describes as the strongest supporter of her school-idol activity—has instead encouraged her to continue the singing she loves and continue doing what she wants. Kanon asks whether Keke dislikes the first route and then whether she dislikes the second. Keke's answer is load-bearing:

> `正直 分かりません。`
>
> `ほんとに 今は スクールアイドル活動以外 考えられない。`
>
> `考えたくないって思ってしまうんデス。`

So S3E05 refuses to let “family expectations” explain the whole problem. Keke has **more than one legitimate possible future and still cannot yet choose**. Her present school-idol identity is so saturated with meaning that imagining what follows can feel like prematurely converting the current life into something already ending.

That is why the episode's Shanghai movement matters formally. It is not merely a travel interlude or a return-to-roots episode. Keke's peers literally cross the boundary between the Japanese school life she chose and the family/cultural space from which the next decision may have to be made. They do so without knowing exactly what they are going to fix. They travel because the future problem has become relationally consequential before it is conceptually solved.

The same episode also stress-tests the Season-3 rivalry model. Wien correctly reminds Kanon that Kanon is **not currently a Liella! member** and argues that a rival group preparing for Love Live! should leave a Liella! problem to Liella!. Kanon does not erase the separation. She says:

> `確かに 私たちは 今 別のグループ。`

Then she adds another identity layer:

> `でも 結ヶ丘の スクールアイドルであることは同じ。`

Her resulting competitive norm is sharper than the earlier generic language of mutual improvement. She wants to compete when both sides can produce their best live, and she explicitly rejects winning because the opponent happened to be damaged by a non-performance crisis:

> `相手が困っている時に 差を広げて`
>
> `それで勝っても 全然うれしくないよ。`

The groups remain **institutionally zero-sum** but refuse to become **morally opportunistic**. This is not de facto reunification. Separate membership is repeatedly named, Wien polices it, and the one-school entry problem remains unresolved. What changes is the ethical model of competition:

> **a legitimate rival is someone one wants to defeat at full strength, not someone whose unrelated vulnerability should be converted into advantage.**

Tomari advances through a parallel distinction. She again states that she has no reason to commit to the Love Live! objective. But her behavior has changed: she is attending summer practice seriously. When Kanon asks why, Tomari gives the clearest statement yet of the relationship between skepticism and participation:

> `姉者の気持ちを確かめるには`
>
> `私も 本気で挑むしかないというだけです。`

S3E04 established that Tomari joined partly to obtain firsthand evidence. S3E05 **STRENGTHENS** this into an embodied epistemology. If the question is what Natsumi experiences in school-idol activity, Tomari now judges that observation from outside is insufficient. She must seriously perform the practice herself. Crucially, the episode still does **not** give her a line stating that school idols have value. Participation has become more serious than belief.

Wien also gains a useful voice-model expansion. S3E05 contains two textually explicit `フン！` instances. The first follows a competence/status comparison when Tomari's academic result threatens Wien's self-presentation; the English track explicitly renders `Hmph!`. The second follows Kanon's refusal to exploit a rival's trouble; Wien calls her too much of a softhearted do-gooder, while the English dialogue omits the nonlexical marker. These two contexts broaden the marker beyond one single function while confirming that it is still **state-conditioned rather than obligatory punctuation**.

The Shanghai half then makes organizational distinction spatially permeable. Members of both formations sightsee, search, eat, ride together, and form cross-group conversational pairs. Wien and Tomari, for example, move briefly from shared competitive work into ordinary anticipatory sociality when Tomari asks to hear about the countries Wien has visited and Wien agrees. None of this requires organizational reunion. Season 3 is increasingly separating **membership boundary** from **social boundary**.

Keke's solo performance at the end intensifies that structure. The Japanese governing subtitle track marks the performance simply as `(中国語)`, while the English full ASS supplies a comparison-layer lyric translation under the `HoshikuzuCruisingEnglish` style. Those lyrics repeatedly invoke proximity, distance, dreams, and continuing connection, which strongly resonates with the episode's concern about graduation and separation. But the lyric layer is not treated as a literal confession or as governing Japanese dialogue. Formally, what is undeniable is that Keke appears alone at the center of a Shanghai performance space while the people from her chosen Japanese school life watch from across the water: **she is simultaneously socially connected and spatially separated**.

The older-sister reveal deliberately complicates any simple autonomy narrative. Tang Mengmeng is the sister Keke described earlier as her strongest school-idol supporter and as someone who tells her to keep singing and doing what she loves. Yet Mengmeng's method is highly unilateral: she secretly distributes international flight tickets, stages a mystery tour, withholds her identity, arranges Keke's surprise performance encounter, and then recruits the group into a new school-idol festival intervention. Support for Keke's self-authorship therefore coexists with **high-control orchestration**.

And S3E05 refuses to let the audience know whether that orchestration is justified. Mengmeng ends with:

> `お願い。 可可ちゃんを 助けて！`

The exact nature of what Keke needs help with remains **OPEN** at the sealed endpoint. It would violate the prospective method to import S3E06's explanation here.

The strongest sealed S3E05 thesis is therefore:

> **S3E05 makes “after” the problem. Keke has authored the present—three high-school years lived fully as a school idol—but has not yet authored the post-present self, even though both her parents and her supportive sister can offer plausible routes. Her friends cannot solve that identity problem for her, but they refuse to let organizational rivalry become an excuse for abandonment or opportunistic advantage. Tomari similarly deepens serious participation without yet conceding value, while Mengmeng enters as a family-level agent whose autonomy-supportive ideals coexist with unilateral intervention. Across all of these threads, the episode distinguishes support from solution, rivalry from abandonment, participation from agreement, and future uncertainty from lack of seriousness.**

Compactly:

> **Keke knows what she chose to be now. S3E05 begins asking who she can choose to be after “now” ends.**

# 3. Episode chronology and diagnostic evidence

## 3.1 Graduation becomes an institutional object

The opening survey makes the approaching boundary administratively explicit. A retained close-up shows `結ヶ丘女子高等学校 進路希望調査票`, with ranked preference fields and route categories such as `進学 / 就職 / その他`.

This matters because the episode begins by turning an abstract future into a document that demands selection. The problem is not merely “the seniors feel nostalgic.” The school is now asking them to state what comes next.

Kanon initially responds to the idea of graduation with lag rather than a new route choice. Wien says:

> `ウィーン国立音楽学校への留学。`
>
> `決まってることでしょ。`

Kanon answers:

> `何だか 頭が 全然 追いつかなくって…。`
>
> `気持ち 切り替えていかなきゃ…なんだけどぉ～！`

At the sealed S3E05 boundary, this **STRENGTHENS** the current expectation that the postponed Vienna route remains active. It does not constitute a new institutional acceptance event, and actual enactment remains OPEN. More importantly, Kanon does not say she changed her mind. The pressure is temporal-emotional: the route may be decided while her felt transition into “graduating senior who leaves” lags behind.

## 3.2 Keke's dream is complete in the present tense

Keke's formulation of her dream is unusually bounded:

> `可可の夢は 高校３年間を`
>
> `精いっぱい スクールアイドルとして過ごすことです。`

That line should not be paraphrased into “Keke wants to be an idol forever.” The primary text says something narrower and more analytically useful: her dream is the **high-school school-idol life** itself.

When Kanon asks about afterward, Keke answers with presentist language:

> `可可は とにかく 今を一番大事にしたい。`
>
> `今のために 生きてるんデス。`

The later admission `考えたくないって思ってしまうんデス` shows that this is not only philosophical mindfulness. Present-focus also functions partly as avoidance of a future identity problem whose arrival would mark the current dream's finitude.

Yet Keke explicitly says she knows the future matters and has kept her studies in order. Therefore:

> **presentism is a value priority plus an avoidance strategy, not evidence that Keke is globally incapable of planning or responsibility.**

## 3.3 Two family routes, neither yet authored by Keke

Keke says her parents suggested that she return home and attend her father's alma mater in Beijing. The episode does not show direct parental dialogue, threats, or punishment. Keke's strongest explicit concern is anticipated disappointment:

> `親に 将来のこと考えてないなんて 絶対 言えないんデス。`
>
> `きっと すごく がっかりする。`

Her sister, by contrast, is described as the person who supports Keke's school-idol activity more than anyone and who advises her to continue singing and continue what she wants to do.

Kanon tests both options symmetrically: does Keke dislike Beijing? Does she dislike continuing song? Keke cannot yet answer either with ownership:

> `正直 分かりません。`

This prevents a false binary of “oppressive family route versus authentic singing route.” The episode establishes **option plurality without decision authorship**.

## 3.4 Kanon as listener rather than resolver

Kanon's response to Keke is notably bounded:

> `聞いてるよ。`
>
> `言える範囲でかまわないから。`

She asks questions that separate parental proposal, sisterly advice, and Keke's own preference. She does not tell Keke which future is correct.

This **STRENGTHENS** the S3E04 decentering result. Kanon remains a high-value confidant and value articulator, but another person's future is not converted into a Kanon-authored synthesis.

## 3.5 Rival boundary policing

The episode deliberately preserves formal group separation. Wien challenges Kanon's closeness to Liella! and later says directly:

> `あなた 今は「Liella!」のメンバーじゃないでしょ！`

Her point is structurally correct: if the groups intend to compete for a single Love Live! school slot, one group cannot simply behave as though organizational separation has no consequence.

Kanon does not deny the separation. Her earlier formulation is:

> `だ～から 敵じゃないもん。`
>
> `お互いを高め合えるライバル。 でしょ？`

The key conceptual pair is **rival / not enemy**. S3E05 then subjects that distinction to an actual practical cost.

## 3.6 Tomari: serious participation as evidence production

Tomari explicitly preserves noncommitment to Love Live!:

> `以前も言いましたが`
>
> `「ラブライブ！」出場という目的に コミットする理由は 私にはありません。`

But Kanon notices that Tomari is now attending practice even during summer break. Tomari first answers through her usual necessity logic (`休む理由もありませんので…`) and then supplies the deeper reason:

> `姉者の気持ちを確かめるには`
>
> `私も 本気で挑むしかないというだけです。`

This is one of the episode's most important S3E04 pressure-test results.

**Transition: STRENGTHEN.** Tomari's skepticism does not diminish her demand for adequate evidence. Instead, adequate evidence now requires embodied participation. The activity can be worth taking seriously **as a method of knowing** before Tomari decides whether it is valuable as an end.

What remains absent is equally important: there is no explicit `価値がある`, no reversal of her prior school-idol valuation, and no Love Live! commitment. Philosophical conversion remains OPEN.

## 3.7 The Shanghai tickets turn uncertainty into collective action

International tickets are distributed through the network while Keke becomes unusually unreachable. Liella! infers that the trip may relate to Keke's future situation.

Wien argues for respecting organizational division: Keke's issue belongs to Liella!, while Kanon's group should use the time to improve. That provides a real alternative, not a straw man.

Kanon's answer then specifies a supra-group identity without erasing subgroup membership:

> `確かに 私たちは 今 別のグループ。`
>
> `でも 結ヶ丘の スクールアイドルであることは同じ。`

and a fairness condition:

> `お互い 最高のライブができる状況で 競い合いたい。`
>
> `相手が困っている時に 差を広げて`
>
> `それで勝っても 全然うれしくないよ。`

This is **STRENGTHEN** for the S3E04 zero-sum/nonzero-sum model. The scarce entry remains scarce. But not every route to competitive advantage is treated as normatively legitimate.

## 3.8 Wien's second S3E05 `フン！`: disagreement without exit

Wien answers Kanon's competitive ethic:

> `フン！ あんたは`
>
> `お人よしがすぎるのよ！`

This is a disagreement, not verbal endorsement. Yet Wien goes to Shanghai. Behavior therefore establishes at minimum that she does not make her disagreement a veto on the support action.

Do not overstate this as “Wien now believes Kanon is right.” The stronger model is:

> **Wien can preserve competitive skepticism and still participate in an action that protects a rival's capacity to compete.**

## 3.9 Shanghai as a mixed social field rather than organizational reunion

The 42-contact-sheet visual pass shows that Shanghai repeatedly mixes members of the two formations in shared sightseeing, navigation, meals, search work, and paired conversations. The group boundary is far less predictive of spatial pairing than it was during the immediate split.

But no one declares organizational reunion. The episode has just emphasized separate membership. Therefore the correct formal result is:

> **social permeability increases while organizational differentiation remains intact.**

The travel setting matters because it removes the familiar Yuigaoka rooms/rooftops that normally encode group routine. In an external city, the characters can recombine socially without that recombination automatically constituting a club merger.

## 3.10 Natsumi's media/commercial reflex persists without a new violation

Natsumi quickly recognizes Shanghai as fresh video material:

> `上海に来たからこそ「Liella!」の新しい一面を`

and later responds to food-content opportunity with `バズりますの～！`.

This **PRESERVES** the mixed-value model. Her media instincts remain active even during a trip motivated by concern for Keke.

But S3E05 does not establish a new rule violation comparable to the S3E01 unauthorized posting problem. No one tells her that filming is prohibited here. So the evidence does **not** justify either “Natsumi has regressed to pure profit” or “Natsumi has now spontaneously internalized all media norms.” Both stronger claims remain unsupported.

## 3.11 Wien and Tomari gain ordinary teammate sociality

On the night boat, Wien notes that she has traveled to many countries since childhood. Tomari asks her to tell her about other countries sometime. Wien agrees; Tomari says she looks forward to it.

This is small but model-useful. Their relationship now includes an anticipatory conversational future unrelated to immediate performance logistics.

**Transition: STRENGTHEN teammate social relation.** Do not inflate it into deep intimacy from one scene.

## 3.12 Keke's Chinese-language solo: relation across distance

The Japanese analytical track marks the song as `(中国語)`. It therefore does not provide a governing Japanese lyric text.

The English full ASS supplies a comparison-layer translation in a style labeled `HoshikuzuCruisingEnglish`, including lines such as:

- `Always stay by me…`
- `Always by my side…`
- `In our dreams, hand in hand, sailing down the starry seas`
- `Think of me now and then though you're far`

These lyrics should not be treated as literal autobiography or as Japanese-dialogue evidence. However, the episode has already established graduation, Shanghai distance, and Keke's inability to define the post-high-school future. The song's distance/connection grammar therefore has strong **formal resonance**.

Visually, Keke performs alone in a highly centralized illuminated Shanghai space while her friends watch from a different physical position. Her solo is both a display of autonomous performance capability and a staging of connection under separation.

## 3.13 Tang Mengmeng: autonomy-supportive ideal, high-control method

Keke identifies the guide as her older sister Tang Mengmeng and describes her as kind but somewhat fond of pranks.

The earlier Keke/Kanon conversation establishes the sister's ideological position: Mengmeng tells Keke to continue singing and continue doing what she wants.

Her enacted method in S3E05 is much more controlling:

- secret international tickets;
- no advance explanation to most travelers;
- mystery-tour navigation;
- withheld identity;
- arranged surprise encounter with Keke;
- unilateral recruitment of the group into a Shanghai school-idol festival.

This is not a contradiction that needs to be “fixed.” It is analytically productive:

> **Mengmeng may value Keke's self-authored desire while still believing she herself is entitled to control the environment, timing, and social resources around Keke in order to create an opportunity.**

At this boundary that is a one-episode model and must remain provisional.

## 3.14 The cliffhanger remains semantically sealed

Mengmeng shows the group a large Shanghai school-idol festival venue and says she wants them to participate. Then:

> `お願い。 可可ちゃんを 助けて！`

The group responds by asking what “help” means.

That question is the exact S3E05 endpoint.

**OPEN:**

- what Mengmeng believes is wrong;
- what the festival is meant to change;
- whether Keke requested this intervention;
- what Keke ultimately wants after graduation;
- whether the Beijing route, singing route, or another route becomes Keke's choice.

No S3E06 answer is imported.

# 4. Character-model revisions

## 4.1 Tang Keke — from dream pursuit to temporal identity problem

### PRESERVE

- school idols remain a deeply self-chosen domain rather than an imposed extracurricular activity;
- Keke remains academically capable enough that commitment to school idols should not be equated with neglect of studies;
- family remains important to her self-evaluation;
- Kanon remains a uniquely high-trust person for vulnerable disclosure.

### STRENGTHEN

Keke's present-oriented intensity is now explicitly self-described: `今を一番大事にしたい` / `今のために生きてる`.

## 4.2 Shibuya Kanon — listening competence and fair-rivalry ethics

Kanon has two distinct S3E05 functions.

First, she is a **listener**. She asks Keke questions that differentiate outside options from Keke's own desire and explicitly permits partial disclosure.

Second, she is a **network-value articulator**. When Wien wants clean organizational separation, Kanon articulates the fair-rivalry rule that an unrelated crisis should not be converted into competitive advantage.

These do not restore interpretive monopoly. Kanon cannot answer Keke's future for her and is not the source of Mengmeng's intervention.

The Vienna line also matters: Kanon's future route is spoken of as decided, while Kanon's difficulty is emotional catch-up. Preserve the route as active but not enacted.

## 4.3 Wien Margarete — competitive boundary, face defense, and reluctant solidarity

S3E05 strengthens Wien's commitment to real organizational rivalry. She notices behavior that looks too friendly, reminds Kanon she is not a Liella! member, and argues that time helping the opponent could instead be used to improve their own chances.

That is not simple hostility; it is a coherent competition model.

Her eventual participation in Shanghai therefore matters. She does not verbally surrender the model. Instead, the episode shows that competitive skepticism can coexist behaviorally with participation in rival-support action.

Her international upbringing also gains a small new autobiographical fact: she says she traveled to many countries from childhood.

## 4.4 Onitsuka Tomari — participation as epistemic method

S3E05 is a **STRENGTHEN** episode, not a conversion episode, for Tomari.

She still rejects commitment to the Love Live! objective. Yet her attendance at practice has become serious enough that Kanon remarks upon it, and Tomari explicitly explains that understanding Natsumi requires her to challenge the activity seriously herself.

This yields a useful model rule:

> **For Tomari, firsthand participation can be instrumentally necessary to form a trustworthy judgment even when she has not accepted the activity's terminal value.**

This is valuable for later simulation because skepticism need not predict refusal to participate. Under sufficiently high epistemic stakes, skepticism can predict *more rigorous participation*.

## 4.5 Onitsuka Natsumi — mixed value state persists

The accepted-risk dream established in S3E04 is not contradicted. Natsumi travels to help Keke and participates in the group search.

At the same time, the content-creator reflex remains immediate. Shanghai is novel material; food can become a viral video.

# 5. Relationship conditioning

## 5.1 Keke → Kanon

Kanon becomes a chosen confidant for a problem Keke cannot solve by enthusiasm alone. Keke tells her not only the options but the socially risky admission that she may simply not want to think beyond school-idol life.

Later, Keke's suspected difficulty activates solidarity even though the two are organizationally separated.

Mengmeng's relation is simultaneously supportive and interventionist. She appears willing to mobilize Keke's entire peer network without Keke first controlling the process.

This creates an OPEN autonomy question for later episodes rather than a settled “good sister” or “controlling sister” verdict.

## 5.5 Wien → Kanon

Wien repeatedly activates the *cost* side of Kanon's rivalry philosophy. Where Kanon emphasizes mutual elevation, Wien emphasizes that the groups actually must beat one another and should allocate time accordingly.

The `お人よし` criticism shows that Wien interprets Kanon's fairness ethic partly as over-kindness.

- separate groups;
- common Yuigaoka school-idol identity;
- formal Love Live! rivalry;

This is **not reunion**.

## 5.7 Tomari → Natsumi

No new sibling conflict in S3E05 establishes whether she has fully learned to preserve care without veto. That remains OPEN.

## 5.8 Wien ↔ Tomari

The night-boat exchange adds low-stakes future-oriented sociality. Tomari asks to hear more about Wien's international experiences sometime; Wien agrees.

This strengthens the plausibility of ordinary teammate conversation beyond performance mechanics.

## 5.9 Group → Keke

The whole traveling network acts before the exact problem is known. Their action is therefore based on relationship and uncertainty management rather than a solved causal diagnosis.

This is important: **support can precede explanation** without the story treating support as equivalent to deciding for Keke.

# 6. Voice-model audit

## 6.1 Keke: presentist conviction versus future uncertainty

### Presentist declaration

`今を一番大事にしたい` and `今のために 生きてるんデス` are direct, generalized statements. They should be modeled as a coherent value position, not merely procrastination language.

### Unshielded uncertainty

`正直 分かりません` is unusually plain. Keke does not substitute theatrical rhetoric or exaggerated optimism for an answer she does not have.

### Meta-avoidance admission

`考えたくないって思ってしまうんデス` demonstrates that Keke can identify avoidance as avoidance. This raises model sophistication: she need not believe every reason she gives herself is fully justified in order to enact it.

### Family-disappointment anticipation

Keke imagines parental disappointment before any on-screen parental confrontation occurs. Simulation should therefore distinguish **anticipated family judgment** from directly observed coercion.

## 6.2 Kanon: permission-based listening

`聞いてるよ。 言える範囲でかまわないから。`

This is a strong intimate-support template: presence + permission + bounded disclosure, rather than immediate advice.

Her Vienna-transition speech (`頭が全然追いつかなくって`) also gives a useful mode for accepted decision / lagging affect: the choice can remain active while emotional adaptation is incomplete.

## 6.3 Tomari: formal epistemic participation language

Tomari retains precise formal constructions around commitment and necessity:

- `コミットする理由は 私にはありません`
- `休む理由もありませんので`
- `本気で挑むしかないというだけです`

The last phrase minimizes romanticization while nevertheless describing serious commitment of effort. This is exactly the sort of speech a simulator should preserve rather than replacing with sudden enthusiastic school-idol language.

# 7. Visual/formal analysis

## 7.1 The blank future form

The `進路希望調査票` provides the episode's core visual object. Structured boxes exist before the characters have structured answers.

The form externalizes the mismatch between **institutional time** (“declare next route now”) and **subjective time** (“I am still inside the present life”).

## 7.2 Keke/Kanon disclosure space

The future conversation is visually narrow and dyadic rather than a full-group intervention. That protects the scene from becoming a committee decision about Keke.

The formal choice aligns with the content: Kanon is hearing an unresolved self-report, not convening a solution process.

## 7.3 Group boundary versus spatial boundary

Before Shanghai, the script repeatedly names separate membership. In Shanghai, the retained-frame/contact-sheet corpus repeatedly mixes the memberships spatially.

The formal result is not contradiction but layered identity:

> **organization can remain separate while everyday affiliation crosses the organization.**

## 7.4 Shanghai as external relational laboratory

Removing the characters from Yuigaoka removes the school's familiar clubrooms, roofs, and training zones. Navigation, tourism, food, and the boat ride generate new pairings that are not determined by club structure.

This supports the claim that the rival formations are socially porous even while competitively real.

## 7.5 Keke solo: center and distance

Keke is visually isolated as the focal performer. Her peers' role is spectatorship from elsewhere in the scene.

Because the episode is about an uncertain future that may separate current lives, the staging makes **distance compatible with connection** without resolving the future itself.

## 7.6 Mengmeng reveal and festival scale

The reveal collapses the mystery-guide distance into family relation, but immediately expands the problem outward again through a large external school-idol festival venue.

The ending therefore moves from:

`private future uncertainty` → `family intervention` → `international collective performance problem`.

S3E05 ends before proving whether this scale-up is the right solution.

# 11. Character-reconstruction snapshot at S3E05

If placed in a novel situation asking her to choose between a meaningful present commitment and a poorly specified post-boundary future, the best current model is **not** impulsive irresponsibility. Expect:

3. reluctance to let future planning psychologically consume the current dream;
