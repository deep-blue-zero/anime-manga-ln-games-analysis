# Love Live! Superstar!! — S3E06 Deep Reading V2
## `タカラモノ` / `Treasure`

# 1. Governing thesis

S3E05 ended with Keke in a structurally difficult state: she knew exactly what she wanted the present three high-school years to be, but could not yet author the person who would exist after them. Her parents' Beijing-university route and Mengmeng's continue-singing route were both intelligible futures, yet neither had become Keke's own answer. S3E06 resolves part of that problem by making a distinction that the prior episode could only leave implicit:

> **owning a decision is not always the same thing as owning the desire that generated it.**

Keke does make a decision in S3E06. She tells Kanon that she intends to go to university in Beijing and later tells her parents the same thing. The episode does not dismiss this as fake speech forced into her mouth by tyrannical parents. On the contrary, Keke describes her parents as people who worried about her, nevertheless sent her to Japan, recognized her effort, and allowed her to spend three years doing what she loves. Her university decision arises from a serious moral logic:

> `大学に行けば お父さんもお母さんも安心する。`
>
> `両親への恩返しにもなる。`

Her problem is therefore not simple obedience. It is **gratitude converted into temporal foreclosure**. Keke reasons that because her parents gave her three years of freedom, those three years should now function as a bounded exception. She must repay them by returning to a reassuring path that predates the self she became through school idols.

That logic reaches its clearest form in:

> `可可の青春は　ここまで。 もうすぐ終わるんです。`
>
> `今年が 最後デスから！`

S3E05's presentism is thus **REVISED**. Keke is no longer merely unable to imagine after. She temporarily creates an “after” by declaring that the current self has an expiration date. University becomes not simply an academic option but a means of drawing a moral line beneath youth, school idols, and the freedom she feels she owes her parents for granting.

S3E06 does not respond with the shallow proposition “ignore your parents and follow your dream.” The parents are not established as villains, and Keke's gratitude is not treated as irrational. Instead the episode asks whether gratitude should require **self-cancellation**.

Sumire supplies the first important answer. When Keke says Beijing is `自分で決めたこと`, Sumire does not seize decision ownership from her. She answers, in effect, that if Keke truly wants it, then it is Keke's choice. Only afterward does Sumire state her own preference:

> `私は見たいわ！`
>
> `可可が　ステージに立ち続ける姿を！`

This is unusually mature support for their relationship. Sumire does not say that Keke is forbidden to choose university. She makes her relational stake legible: **I want to keep seeing this version of you exist.**

Kanon then goes further. S3E05 had shown a permission-based listener who allowed Keke to disclose uncertainty without converting that uncertainty into Kanon's project. S3E06 deliberately moves beyond that mode. Kanon directly says:

> `可可ちゃん 自分に　うそついてるよ！`

That would be an overreach if it were merely Kanon imposing her own unrelated ideals. The episode instead grounds the confrontation in Keke's own prior language. Kanon returns to Keke the principle Keke once gave her:

> `好きなことを　頑張ることに おしまいなんてあるの？`

and identifies it as `私の宝物にしている言葉`.

The intervention is therefore not “Kanon knows Keke better than Keke knows herself” as a general rule. It is more constrained:

> **when a close relationship supplies extensive longitudinal evidence that a claimed decision requires the person to contradict a value they themselves once treated as foundational, direct challenge can be agency-supportive rather than automatically agency-denying.**

Kanon's next move reinforces the distinction. She does not announce what university or career Keke must choose. She says what **she** has chosen:

> `私は　卒業したら ウィーンに　歌を勉強しに行く。`

This is a major longitudinal update. The Season-2 checkpoint must remain exact: Kanon genuinely chose Vienna, prepared to go, and then had the study-abroad route externally canceled before enactment. S3E06 does not rewrite that history. It establishes something new after it:

> **Kanon has renewed Vienna as a self-authored post-graduation plan to study singing.**

The path has therefore moved from “previously chosen but externally blocked / later realization OPEN” to **renewed explicit future intention**. Actual enactment remains future evidence.

Kanon then says she wants Keke to move forward just as directly. When Keke breaks down, Kanon again avoids choosing the career route for her. Her intervention changes the conditions under which Keke can address the problem:

> `可可ちゃんが 一番　祝福される状況を　私が作る。`

She recruits Wien and Tomari to use TomaKanoTe's own stage identity to call Keke into a public space where Keke can be heard by her hometown audience and parents. The contrast with Mengmeng is revealing. Both Mengmeng and Kanon are high-agency interventionists. Both manipulate opportunity structures. But Mengmeng's plan begins through secrecy and assumption—`可可ちゃんは これからも　歌の道を突き進むのです` before Keke has explicitly selected that future—whereas Kanon's later intervention follows direct conflict with Keke and tries to create a context in which **Keke herself can speak**.

The distinction is not “Mengmeng controlling, Kanon perfectly non-directive.” It is:

> **intervention can either substitute for another person's answer or construct a better condition for that person to articulate one.**

The episode still does not give Keke a complete career plan. That restraint is essential. Through the English comparison lane for her Chinese public address, Keke tells her parents that she wants to remain on stage, says school idols changed her world, and asks them to keep watching her as she moves forward. The Japanese track then returns to an exact present-state declaration:

> `可可は…　今　一番幸せデスよ。`

That is enough to revise the endpoint dramatically without inventing an implementation:

> **Keke now authors a post-graduation desire for continuity of singing/stage life; whether that means a professional idol path, university plus performance, another educational route, or something else remains OPEN.**

The future is no longer only “what replaces the present.” It can become a transformation or continuation of what the present proved valuable.

S3E06 simultaneously advances Season 3's organizational experiment. TomaKanoTe publicly takes the stage under its own name and uses that rival identity to help create Keke's entrance condition. Wien even advertises their own future stage while doing so. Chisato then—not Kanon—proposes a one-day exception:

> `今日だけ　特別！`
>
> `一緒に歌おう！`
>
> `結ヶ丘のスクールアイドル全員で！`

The resulting eleven-person performance does **not** dissolve the two groups. The script marks the integration as time-bounded, Mei explicitly remembers the rivalry, and the one-school/one-Love-Live-entry problem remains unresolved. What S3E06 proves is instead:

> **shared artistic form can be layered above organizational plurality without requiring organizational merger.**

This is also another decentering result. Kanon designs the crowd-call intervention; Chisato independently sees a further possibility and turns it into a full Yuigaoka performance. Leadership is becoming a network of local authorship rather than a sequence of Kanon instructions.

Finally, the title `タカラモノ / Treasure` becomes unusually well grounded. In Keke's translated Chinese address, the people and relationships surrounding her are described as her beloved treasure. In Japanese, Kanon independently calls Keke's old words `私の宝物`. The episode therefore does not locate treasure in a single trophy, career outcome, or person. Treasure is **portable relational and value-forming experience**: something learned in one stage of life that can survive the end of that stage and shape the next.

The strongest sealed S3E06 thesis is therefore:

> **S3E06 distinguishes owning a decision from owning a desire. Keke can sincerely announce Beijing university because she has converted parental support into a debt she wants to repay, yet the “youth ends here” logic requires her to deny continuity to the school-idol self that transformed her life. Sumire respects the claimed decision while stating her own wish to keep seeing Keke onstage; Kanon goes further, returning Keke's own value language to her and changing the social conditions so Keke can speak directly to her parents. Keke ultimately authors a post-graduation desire for continued stage life without yet selecting its concrete educational or professional implementation. In parallel, TomaKanoTe's rival identity becomes a public mechanism of support, and Chisato independently turns that support into a one-day eleven-person Yuigaoka performance, proving that temporary shared form can coexist with persistent organizational plurality.**

Compactly:

> **Keke does not leave S3E06 with a finished career plan. She leaves it having stopped treating graduation as proof that the self she discovered through school idols must also end.**

# 4. Mengmeng: “save Keke” resolves into parent-facing performance persuasion

S3E05 deliberately froze Mengmeng's final `お願い。可可ちゃんを助けて！` without allowing later explanation to leak backward. S3E06 immediately supplies the missing object.

At approximately **00:01:19–00:01:48**, Mengmeng says she wants their parents to watch Keke and asserts:

> `可可ちゃんは これからも　歌の道を突き進むのです。`

Her theory is causal and performative: if the parents see Keke shining onstage, they will support that route.

This converts Mengmeng from an ambiguous rescuer into a much more specific model:

- she believes continued singing is the future that fits Keke;
- she treats parental recognition as a major obstacle/condition;
- she believes direct performance evidence can change parental judgment;
- she is willing to mobilize peers, international travel, and a festival stage to create that evidence.

But an important asymmetry survives. Mengmeng speaks of Keke's future as if it is already decided **before Keke has said so**. Her value—Keke should continue doing what she loves—sounds autonomy-supportive. Her method is not low-control. It remains orchestration on Keke's behalf.

The later episode partly vindicates Mengmeng's diagnosis of Keke's desire: Keke ultimately does say she wants continued stage life. It does **not** retrospectively prove that every method Mengmeng used was consented to or that Mengmeng is entitled to define Keke's route in advance.

Transition: **REVISE / STRENGTHEN motive; PRESERVE method tension; Keke's approval of method remains OPEN.**

# 5. Childhood Keke: school idols as an interruption of a study-defined future

The childhood segment is especially important because S3E05 had already established a family/education frame but not the experiential origin of Keke's alternative self.

The governing Japanese SRT frequently labels the Chinese dialogue only as `(中国語)`, so semantic details here are taken only from the English comparison lane and are not represented as Japanese quotations.

Visually, young Keke is repeatedly situated amid:

- desks and books;
- intensive study;
- ordinary route/home movement;
- an academic/family environment in which extracurricular freedom is constrained by expectation and worry.

The decisive visual interruption is her encounter with school idols in public space. The framing turns Keke from routine movement toward an external luminous/performance object; her attention visibly reorganizes around it.

The point is not that her pre-idol life was worthless or that study itself was oppression. The episode later makes clear that Keke respects education and her parents. The stronger longitudinal claim is:

> **school idols expanded the ontology of possible selves available to Keke.**

Before that encounter, a study-defined future was normal enough to be almost invisible as a choice. After it, “what do I want to do?” became a question with more than one answer.

This is why returning to the pre-idol route in S3E06 is psychologically loaded. Beijing university is not simply another option; it can function as an attempt to restore the path that existed before Keke discovered a self-authored competing value system.

# 6. Keke and her parents: gratitude becomes a repayment model

The tea conversation with Kanon at approximately **00:10:32–00:11:53** prevents a simplistic family-conflict reading.

Keke says her parents:

- gently saw her off to Japan;
- worried at first;
- recognized her effort;
- allowed her three years to do what she loves.

She then derives an obligation:

> `大学に行けば お父さんもお母さんも安心する。`
>
> `両親への恩返しにもなる。`
>
> `だから可可は 進学しようと思っています。`

This is a self-generated moral equation:

`parental support -> debt/gratitude -> reassuring future -> Beijing university`

Keke is not shown receiving a current harsh command in this episode. Instead she anticipates what would reassure them and makes that anticipated relief part of her own decision criterion.

The critical line is not merely `進学`. It is the temporal closure surrounding it:

> `可可の青春は　ここまで。 もうすぐ終わるんです。`

The college choice becomes fused to the proposition that **youth itself must end and therefore the school-idol self must end with it**.

This is why Kanon's later challenge targets self-deception rather than university as such. The episode never establishes “university is incompatible with singing” as an objective rule. Keke herself has fused the two into mutually exclusive symbolic futures.

Transition from S3E05:

- **PRESERVE** Keke's seriousness and future-awareness;
- **REVISE** post-boundary avoidance into temporary, obligation-based foreclosure;
- **STRENGTHEN** family gratitude as an active decision variable.

# 7. The Beijing decision is real—but not stable

Keke later tells her parents, through the Chinese-language interaction rendered by the English comparison lane, that she will attend college in Beijing. The decision therefore cannot be dismissed as a private hypothetical or something Kanon merely fears.

However, “real decision” and “stable endpoint” are different analytical categories.

The episode proceeds to place the decision under three distinct pressures:

1. **Sumire:** respects the claimed ownership but states a contrary relational wish.
2. **Kanon:** challenges the internal consistency of the choice with Keke's own values.
3. **Keke's own public performance/address:** produces a positive future-oriented desire that the hard “last year / youth ends here” formulation cannot easily contain.

By the end, the Beijing route is therefore not **REJECTED** as impossible or definitively canceled. It is **DOWNGRADED from settled future to unresolved implementation option**.

This distinction should remain explicit in later retrieval. S3E06 does not license the shorthand “Keke decides not to go to university.”

# 8. Sumire and Keke: ownership first, desire second

The backstage exchange at approximately **00:14:15–00:14:46** is one of the episode's best relationship-modeling scenes.

Sumire asks whether Keke is going to Beijing university. Keke responds defensively:

> `自分で決めたことデス。 ほっといてクダサイ。`

Sumire's first answer is structurally important:

> `ふ〜ん。　ま　可可がいいならいいけど。`

She does not immediately invalidate the claimed choice. Only then does she say:

> `私は見たいわ！`
>
> `可可が　ステージに立ち続ける姿を！`

The sequence is:

`acknowledge your choice -> make my own desire legible`

rather than:

`your choice is invalid -> I decide your future`.

This is a meaningful maturation of the Keke/Sumire relationship. Their longstanding rivalry/comic antagonism has always carried unusual attention to one another's competence and vulnerabilities. S3E06 shows that this bond can support serious future-oriented care without abandoning Sumire's directness.

The subsequent close paired staging and physical closeness reinforce repair/support without requiring the dialogue to become sentimentalized.

Behavioral model:

> **when Sumire believes a close person's choice is genuinely theirs, she can refrain from overriding it while still stating her own emotionally consequential preference directly.**

# 9. Kanon and Keke: when permission-based listening becomes direct challenge

S3E05 had strengthened a valuable Kanon mode: allow Keke to disclose only what she can, ask discriminating questions, and avoid prescribing a future.

S3E06 does not reject that mode. It defines its limit.

At approximately **00:15:18–00:16:12**, Keke insists:

> `もう　決めたんデス！`

Kanon answers:

> `可可ちゃん 自分に　うそついてるよ！`

Keke reacts strongly:

> `うるさいデス！`
>
> `かのんなら 分かってくれると思ってたのに…。`

This is not a costless confrontation. Keke explicitly experiences it as a violation of the understanding she expected from Kanon.

Kanon's justification therefore matters. She does not appeal first to her own generic “follow your dream” philosophy. She returns Keke's own historical principle:

> `好きなことを　頑張ることに おしまいなんてあるの？`

and says those words became one of Kanon's own treasures.

This creates a narrower intervention rule than “challenge friends whenever you disagree”:

> **high-confidence longitudinal contradiction + high relational trust + evidence from the person's own prior value statements can justify direct challenge of a claimed self-owned decision.**

Even then, the result is not that Kanon becomes decision maker. She can expose a contradiction; Keke still has to articulate what follows.

Transition: **REVISE / EXPAND** S3E05 listener mode into a two-mode system: permission-based listening under uncertainty, direct value challenge under strongly evidenced self-foreclosure.

# 10. Kanon's renewed Vienna plan

Kanon's statement:

> `私は　卒業したら ウィーンに　歌を勉強しに行く。`

is one of the most consequential longitudinal facts in the episode.

It must be routed through the frozen S2 checkpoint correctly.

### Frozen S2 fact

Kanon genuinely chose Vienna and prepared to depart. Before enactment, the external institution canceled the study-abroad route. Season 2 ended there; it did **not** establish why.

### S3 prior state through S3E05

The route had not yet received an equally direct post-cancellation future commitment. Its eventual realization remained OPEN.

### S3E06 transition

Kanon herself now states an explicit **post-graduation** plan to go to Vienna and study singing.

Therefore:

- do **not** rewrite S2E12 as a fake or abandoned choice;
- do **not** say the original cancellation never mattered;
