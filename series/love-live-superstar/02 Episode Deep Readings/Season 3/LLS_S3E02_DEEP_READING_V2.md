# Love Live! Superstar!! — S3E02 Deep Reading V2
## `トマカノーテ` / `TomaKanoTe`

# 1. Governing thesis

S3E01 created a deliberately unstable experiment. Kanon refused to treat the cancellation of Vienna as permission to reset the old nine-member Liella!, joined Wien Margarete's rival club, and proposed that two Yuigaoka formations could make one another better before perhaps becoming one team later. The episode left two immediate questions open:

1. whether Wien would actually accept Kanon without becoming a satellite of Kanon's authorship; and
2. whether the new club could develop a value system different from both Liella!'s inherited norms and Wien's pre-existing proof/rank logic.

S3E02 answers neither by simple convergence. Instead, it creates a three-person laboratory in which three different theories of activity collide:

- **Wien** enters from a hierarchy/proof model: song has been a way to establish worth, defeat rivals, and reclaim institutional legitimacy.
- **Tomari** enters from a utility model: time should produce measurable return, and activity without profit or useful future qualification is prima facie suspect.
- **Kanon** enters from a relational/non-instrumental model: performance can be valuable because it creates enjoyment, connection, effort, expression, and changed feeling even when a ranking or material return is unavailable.

The episode does not settle this argument by having everyone verbally agree with Kanon. It stages a more diagnostic test.

The trio prepares for a remote live whose admission criterion is explicitly quantitative: **10,000 positive evaluations**. Tomari models the audience, publicity, and expected conversion rate. Wien takes center and contributes lyrics. Kanon protects shared authorship rather than simply writing the entire project herself. The plan then collapses because Wien's own previous public conduct has created reputational resistance. The stream is visibly around **8,032 viewers** immediately before the performance, and Sumire/Tomari judge the 10,000-evaluation target unattainable at that point.

Tomari applies her governing rule consistently:

> `この時点で 不可能と判断されます。`
>
> `したがって 歌う必要はありません。`

If the objective cannot be achieved, continued expenditure is irrational.

Wien does something more important than merely becoming sad about failure. She recognizes the causal relation between her own earlier conduct and the audience's reluctance:

> `笑顔になりたいと思っていた場所で`
>
> `不快な思いをさせたんだものね。`

and then:

> `この顔を見ているだけでも`
>
> `腹が立つ人がいるってことよね。`

This is a significant revision of her Season-2 relation to public judgment. She is no longer only saying that an inferior public or institution failed to recognize her. She can now acknowledge that an audience has a legitimate experiential grievance caused by **her own behavior**.

Kanon then proposes performing anyway:

> `歌おう。`

Her argument is not that the metric secretly remains attainable. She directly changes the governing question. The trio has practiced every day; song can move hearts; challenge must precede result; if Wien genuinely loves singing, she should trust that capacity and begin **from here**.

The ensuing performance therefore functions as a controlled philosophical test:

> **What remains worth doing when the measurable goal attached to the act has already ceased to justify the act?**

The episode's answer is deliberately asymmetric.

Wien emerges saying:

> `歌… 久しぶりに`
>
> `楽しいって思えた。`

She has recovered a form of singing value that precedes victory, Vienna, proof, and even successful qualification. This does **not** erase her ambition or rivalry. It demonstrates that the proof system had crowded out an older non-instrumental relation to song that the childhood flashback already establishes.

Tomari, however, says after the same experience:

> `価値のあるものとは`
>
> `思えませんが。`

She participated. She contributed. She stayed when the measurable route failed. But she has **not** yet revised her stated value model.

This distinction is central:

> **behavioral participation can precede philosophical assimilation.**

S3E02 also supplies the first real evidence that Kanon's S3E01 organizational intervention need not automatically erase Wien's authorship. Kanon does not simply become leader-composer-center. She asks all three to make the song together; gives Wien lyric responsibility because Wien is center; uses Tomari's analysis rather than dismissing her competence; and rejects Tomari's monetization proposal only at the value boundary where it conflicts with the event's non-commercial school-idol form.

That does not eliminate the protagonist-gravity problem. Kanon still supplies the episode's dominant interpretive language and the decisive answer when the live's metric fails. But the new group's artifact is visibly co-authored rather than merely Kanon exported into another club.

The strongest sealed S3E02 thesis is therefore:

> **S3E02 tests the new club by attaching school-idol labor to a measurable objective and then making that objective cease to justify the labor. Wien, Tomari, and Kanon expose three competing value systems—proof, utility, and relational/non-instrumental experience. The failed metric produces different developmental results: Wien accepts reputational accountability and recovers enjoyment in singing outside proof; Tomari contributes behaviorally while retaining explicit skepticism about value; Kanon demonstrates that her new-club role can distribute authorship while still setting strong ethical boundaries. The episode therefore shifts the question from “can this rival club succeed?” to “what makes an act worth continuing when success, profit, and institutional qualification no longer explain why one should do it?”**

Compactly:

> **When the reason that can be counted disappears, S3E02 asks what reasons remain—and reveals that the three members do not yet share the same answer.**

# 3. S3E01 pressure tests and explicit S3E02 claim transitions

## 3.1 Will Wien accept Kanon as a teammate?

**S3E01 OPEN:** Kanon formally applied, but reciprocal team acceptance was not yet established.

**S3E02:** Wien initially says `私 認めないから` and tries to exclude Kanon despite the posted open-recruitment language. Tomari points out that a contractual term cannot normally be retroactively rewritten and judges Kanon's claim valid. Wien therefore begins from resistance, not eager partnership.

By the episode's working phase, however, she trains beside Kanon, accepts the center role, contributes lyrics, performs with the trio, addresses Kanon directly, and after the live tells her that singing felt fun again.

**Transition: REVISE / STRENGTHEN reciprocity.**

The relationship becomes functionally teammate-like even though Wien's initial acceptance is coerced by her own stated recruitment terms rather than enthusiastic invitation.

## 3.2 Can Kanon join without overwhelming Wien's authorship?

**S3E01 OPEN:** Kanon is still system architect and risks turning the rival club into a Kanon satellite.

**S3E02:** Kanon proposes collaborative composition rather than sole authorship, explicitly asks Tomari to contribute, gives Wien first lyric responsibility because Wien is center, and validates making something new rather than reproducing inherited school-idol trends.

Tomari contributes audience modeling, publicity, and planning. Wien contributes center and lyrics. Kanon still supplies major value judgments and the decisive late-live intervention.

**Transition: STRENGTHEN distributed authorship; PRESERVE protagonist-gravity caution.**

The club is not merely Kanon with two satellites, but Kanon remains the strongest interpretive integrator.

## 3.3 Can Liella! tolerate the organizational split?

Keke remains openly unhappy and initially imagines manipulation. Chisato again interprets Kanon's choice through the reciprocal-growth rationale rather than betrayal. Shiki describes the Kanon/Wien relation as something the group should recognize rather than control.

At the live, Liella! watches the new formation with investment rather than boycott; Keke's ending praise `かのん 最高デス～！` is direct support despite her earlier objection.

**Transition: STRENGTHEN behavioral accommodation / PRESERVE emotional resistance.**

Keke has not become indifferent to the split. She can nevertheless support Kanon inside it.

## 3.4 Who was the unidentified first-year?

S3E01 left the observer unnamed.

S3E02 identifies her as:

> `鬼塚冬毬`

Onitsuka Tomari, Natsumi's younger sister.

**Transition: RESOLVE identity; OPEN deeper model becomes active.**

She joins not from established school-idol attraction but to investigate what Natsumi found valuable enough to choose despite an apparent lack of profit or useful qualification.

## 3.5 Does Wien's proof grammar change?

S3E01 preserved Wien's need to defeat Liella! and self-legitimation.

S3E02 does not erase that. But it recovers an older reason she loved song. As a lonely child she heard singing and became warm/encouraged despite still being alone. After performing when qualification has ceased to justify performance, she says she had fun singing for the first time in a long while.

**Transition: REVISE / EXPAND.**

Song-as-proof remains part of Wien. It is no longer the only actively evidenced value relation. A pre-proof, affective, non-instrumental relation to singing is recovered.

## 3.6 Does Wien accept responsibility for audience alienation?

Season 2 often showed Wien defending hierarchy against public/institutional verdicts.

S3E02 gives unusually clear accountability. When criticism suppresses stream participation, Wien connects the outcome to her own prior conduct at the Tokyo event and to the audience's legitimate desire not to be made uncomfortable at a school-idol venue.

**Transition: STRENGTHEN social-perspective capacity / REVISE public-judgment relation.**

She can now understand some negative public response as consequence rather than mere failure of recognition.

## 3.7 What does Tomari's utility model actually protect?

Natsumi initially describes Tomari as even more profit-oriented than herself and says Tomari has looked down on her school-idol activity because it is non-profitable.

The episode complicates this. Tomari quotes Natsumi's own claim that school idols gave her a dream and later, alone, says:

> `スクールアイドルは… きっと姉者を傷つけるだけです`

The utility/profit frame may therefore be partly protective risk control. Tomari is not merely maximizing money. She expects vulnerable dream investment to injure Natsumi.

**Transition: OPEN → emerging protective-instrumental model.**

This remains early and should not be totalized into a complete personality theory yet.

## 3.8 Natsumi's value change

S2 established a move from failed-dream defensiveness toward self-authored school-idol desire while preserving commercial instincts.

S3E02 supplies unusually valuable external testimony: Tomari remembers Natsumi saying school idols mattered more than money/future utility because they gave her a dream. Natsumi is embarrassed by the quotation rather than denying the activity altogether. She later tells Tomari that school idols do not make money but insists that if Tomari is going to participate, she must do it seriously enough that Kanon and Wien are glad she joined.

**Transition: STRENGTHEN non-commercial value alignment; PRESERVE commercial competence.**

Tomari's memory makes Natsumi's S2 value transition independently visible from outside Natsumi herself.

## 3.9 Does the 10,000-evaluation failure invalidate Kanon's philosophy?

No final qualification result is stated at the episode boundary. Before performance, the visible stream count is approximately **8,032 viewers**, and both Sumire and Tomari judge the 10,000-evaluation objective unattainable at that point.

The trio then performs anyway.

**Transition: OPEN on institutional qualification; STRENGTHEN non-instrumental performance thesis.**

The episode does not need to secretly rescue the metric for its dramatic argument. The key action occurs after the metric ceases to justify action.

No new admissible evidence materially changes the S3E01 postponed-Vienna state.

# 4. Naive structural read

## Movement 1 — Kanon's application exposes Wien's contradiction

S3E02 begins exactly where S3E01 stopped. Wien refuses Kanon despite having posted an open recruitment notice. Kanon points to the notice; Wien produces a new exception; Tomari intervenes with contract-like reasoning and rules that Kanon's claim is valid.

The scene establishes all three voices quickly:

- Kanon uses Wien's own stated rule;
- Wien protects rivalry through ad hoc exclusion;
- Tomari converts social conflict into procedural adjudication.

## Movement 2 — Liella! reacts to Kanon's separate affiliation

Keke experiences the move as relationally threatening and comic betrayal. Sumire refuses the premise that Kanon is easily manipulated. Natsumi sees publicity potential. Ren accepts the headmistress's non-objection. Chisato returns to Kanon's reciprocal-growth rationale.

The group is not emotionally uniform, but it does not organize to block the second club.

## Movement 3 — The festival gives the new club a quantified task

Kanon proposes the Yoyogi school-idol festival. Liella! already has the school slot; the new club must enter a remote rookie live and obtain more than 10,000 positive evaluations to gain a guest position.

Wien initially interprets the event through status: she won it last year and defeated Liella!, so being forced through a qualifying lane feels like institutional amnesia.

Kanon reframes the festival's main purpose around local audience enjoyment.

## Movement 4 — Tomari asks the question Wien does not

Tomari asks what material return the performers receive. Kanon explains that there is no direct profit or sale. Tomari confirms Natsumi's earlier description of school-idol activity as something people do despite lacking profit or useful future qualification.

Wien, who may devalue school idols, is nevertheless irritated by Tomari's reduction of artistic labor to utility. The new club is already internally nonhomogeneous.

## Movement 5 — Tomari enters formally but conditionally

Tomari says she will practice if she judges it necessary and refuses wasted time. Yet she demonstrates strong physical fundamentals and submits an application. Her skepticism therefore does not take the form of incompetence or unseriousness.

This is the first episode-long example of a third-generation entrant whose barrier is not “can I do this?” but “why is this worth doing?”

## Movement 6 — Natsumi's sister becomes an external witness to Natsumi's development

Tomari visits Liella! and quotes Natsumi's own account of school idols as something special because they gave her a dream. Natsumi becomes embarrassed. The episode then reveals the sisters' disagreement: Tomari supported Natsumi's solo streaming but sees school-idol activity as a nonprofitable detour.

The later private line about school idols hurting Natsumi makes the conflict more relational than a simple money-versus-art disagreement.

## Movement 7 — Kanon asks Wien for the pre-proof origin of song

Wien questions why school idols need song, dance, costumes, and audience-centered design. Kanon asks when singing itself first made Wien's heart light up.

Wien recalls childhood loneliness, crying, hearing a song, and feeling warmth/energy return.

This supplies a chronological value layer beneath her later Vienna/proof system.

## Movement 8 — Domestic proximity exposes Wien's reciprocity and anti-pity rules

Kanon discovers Wien is staying at the Shibuya home while Kanon's father is away. Wien helps in the cafe because repaying help is her motto. She explicitly denies that cohabitation means friendship and rejects Kanon's momentary silence when her family/sister history creates the risk of pity.

The episode therefore makes pride behavioral, not merely rhetorical: Wien wants reciprocal footing and would rather receive blunt engagement than pity-managed delicacy.

## Movement 9 — Kanon distributes creative authorship

Kanon proposes writing the song together, including Tomari. She does not claim school-idol trend knowledge as a reason to dominate; she says making something new is precisely the point.

Tomari supplies analytics and publicity logic. Wien is asked for lyrics because she is center. The three-person title becomes a working-production architecture rather than mere naming joke.

## Movement 10 — Tomari's competence crosses into monetization overreach

Tomari's analytical skills are real and useful. She models audience numbers and constructs a promotional frame around Kanon/Wien visibility. But she also proposes spectacle and a unique monetization system. Kanon's answer is immediate:

> `ダ～メ！`

The episode distinguishes accepting a person's expertise from accepting her governing values.

## Movement 11 — Reputation makes the numerical goal collapse

The live begins with fewer viewers than expected. Critical comments are tied to Wien's previous Tokyo conduct. Tomari judges 10,000 evaluations impossible; Sumire independently makes the same practical observation.

Wien recognizes the result as a consequence of having made people uncomfortable at a place where they came to enjoy themselves.

## Movement 12 — Kanon changes the question

A low-energy reflective interval follows Wien's acknowledgment. Kanon then says `歌おう`.

The criterion changes from:

> Can this performance earn the required number?

to:

> Is the performance still worth doing, and can it create a different relation from the one Wien previously created?

## Movement 13 — Performance becomes action after instrumentality fails

The trio addresses the people who are actually present/reachable rather than an imagined optimal audience and performs. The song's lyric field repeatedly links rising hope, failure, persistence, love, and continuing even when arrival is uncertain.

## Movement 14 — The same performance produces unequal internal outcomes

Wien says singing felt fun again.

Tomari says she still does not think the activity has value.

Kanon does not demand immediate conversion. She laughs and says Tomari may understand someday.

The group therefore closes not in ideological unity but in sufficient shared action to continue an unresolved experiment.

# 5. The opening `フン！`: a rule contradiction, not generic contempt

The corrected Japanese ASS explicitly contains:

> `フン！`

at **00:45.39–00:47.33**.

The English full and spoken tracks do not contain an equivalent `Hmph`/`Huff` entry.

The placement matters. Kanon has just pointed to Wien's own recruitment notice—`学年 経験 問いません`—and Wien has tried to write in an exclusion after the fact. Tomari has not yet taken over the argument.

This is different from both S2E11 baseline functions:

- S2E11 ~08:18: grudging engagement / pride-preserving concession;
- S2E11 ~11:22: defensive dismissal after Kanon's relational theory pressures Wien's hierarchy model.

The S3E02 opening instance is best read as:

> **rule-trapped resistance / pride-preserving objection.**

Wien is affected not because she has been emotionally softened but because Kanon has caught her using a rule she authored against the exclusion she now wants.

This strengthens the longitudinal model:

> **`フン！` appears when Wien has been forced into an interactional position she does not want to grant cleanly; its pragmatic function depends on what has penetrated the defensive surface.**

It is not a universal “Wien is annoyed” sound.

# 6. Tomari's entrance: proceduralization as emotional distance

Tomari's first substantial act is adjudication:

> `契約において 後から書き足した事項は通常無効とされます`

She treats a school-club quarrel like a contract dispute. This is comic, but analytically precise.

Tomari repeatedly translates ambiguous social space into structures she can reason about:

- contractual validity;
- required conditions;
- practice necessity;
- time expenditure;
- audience modeling;
- expected view count;
- conversion rate;
- monetization;
- impossibility judgment.

Her recurrent syntax includes forms such as:

- `必要と判断すれば`
- `無駄な時間は過ごしたくない`
- `不可能と判断されます`
- `したがって`
- `お構いなく`

The common function is not mere intelligence. It is **proceduralization**:

> convert uncertain value into conditions, then act only on conclusions that can be defended impersonally.

This matters because Natsumi later reveals an emotional stake Tomari herself does not volunteer. Tomari's impersonal frame may be a defense against the type of dream investment she expects to hurt her sister.

# 7. Tomari is not an amateur skeptic

The episode avoids an easy structure in which Tomari doubts school idols because she does not understand the labor.

She possesses enough physical competence to surprise the others and explicitly says:

> `体幹には自信があります`

She also contributes materially to the group's planning. Her audience forecast is not mocked as useless simply because the later prediction fails. The model was based on site traffic; new reputational evidence intervenes.

This distinction matters:

> **Tomari's skepticism is not caused by incapacity to participate.**

She can participate effectively while withholding belief in the activity's value.

That makes her a more diagnostic challenge to Kanon's philosophy than a lazy outsider would be.

# 8. Natsumi through Tomari's eyes: a dream became evidence

One of S3E02's most valuable longitudinal moves is that it lets another person report Natsumi's post-S2 value state.

Tomari remembers Natsumi explaining that school-idol activity did not matter because of money or future qualification. It was special because it gave her a **dream**.

Natsumi's embarrassment is important. She does not respond as though Tomari has invented a false statement. The exchange functions as external corroboration that the S2 transition was not merely a temporary line produced inside a recruitment climax.

Later Natsumi tells Tomari:

> school idols do not make money;
>
> but if Tomari is going to do it, she should do it properly.

Her criterion has become seriousness toward the practice and the people one joins.

This does not turn Natsumi anti-commercial. Her monetization instincts remain part of her competence and risk profile. The stronger model is:

> **money ceased to be the only safe metric through which Natsumi could validate activity.**

Tomari enters as someone who has not yet made that transition.

---

# 9. Tomari's protective hypothesis: profit language as risk management

Tomari's private line after Natsumi leaves is one of the episode's most important recontextualizations:

> `スクールアイドルは… きっと姉者を傷つけるだけです`

This cannot support a complete backstory by itself. But it changes the strongest current hypothesis.

A purely acquisitive reading would say:

> Tomari values money and despises activities that do not produce it.

S3E02 supports something narrower and richer:

> **Tomari privileges measurable return partly because unmeasurable aspiration appears dangerous.**

Natsumi has a history of failed dreams. S3E02 does not explicitly narrate how much of that history Tomari knows or how she interpreted each failure. But her own language connects school idols with expected injury to Natsumi.

Profit/utility can therefore operate as an anti-vulnerability technology:

> if value is measurable, disappointment can be modeled;
> if value is a dream, a loved person may be hurt.

This remains an **emerging** model, not a settled trait theorem.

# 10. Wien's question: why does a singer need school-idol excess?

Wien asks why the activity needs all of it:

> song;
> dance;

# 37. Institution, competition, and post-championship ambition

S3E02 complicates the meaning of competition after Liella!'s championship.

The Yoyogi festival still imposes a hard gate. The new club's institutional problem is real. Kanon does not dismiss it. Tomari does not invent the 10,000 criterion; the event does.

But the episode distinguishes three layers of success:

1. **qualification success** — meeting the required positive-evaluation threshold;
2. **reputational/relational success** — changing how an audience experiences Wien/the new group;
3. **experiential/artistic success** — recovering enjoyment and making a work the members can perform together.

The first remains unresolved at the endpoint. The second is only partially evidenced. The third is directly evidenced by Wien.

This is consistent with the series' broader anti-collapse rule:

> **one metric should not be allowed to stand in for all forms of value.**

That does not make ranking irrelevant. Vienna still has institutional conditions. The festival still has a gate. The episode simply refuses to let either consume the entire ontology of performance.

S3E02's answer remains intentionally incomplete because Tomari is not convinced.
