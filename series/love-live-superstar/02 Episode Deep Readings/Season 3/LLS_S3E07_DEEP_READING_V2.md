# Love Live! Superstar!! -- S3E07 Deep Reading V2
## `Liella!に勝つため` / `To Defeat Liella!`

# 1. Governing thesis

S3E06 supplied the strongest evidence yet that the Yuigaoka split need not equal emotional or artistic separation. TomaKanoTe publicly helped Keke, and Chisato turned that support into an explicitly one-day eleven-person live. S3E07 begins with the obvious temptation created by that success: if all eleven can sing together, and if students themselves want the eleven-person form, why not simply reunify now?

The episode's answer is **not** that the Shanghai unity was false. It is that **shared possibility and correct timing are different questions**.

Liella!'s members sincerely want the eleven-person possibility. Shiki describes Shanghai as having a breadth beyond the earlier nine-person formation and argues that eleven could create something even larger. The school audience reinforces that desire, including a late visible wish plaque asking for Liella! to become eleven. But neither desire nor successful precedent automatically owns Wien's and Tomari's decisions.

Tomari gives the clearest argument against immediate merger. Her purpose remains narrow and self-authored: she joined to test whether Natsumi's renewed dream is real. She explicitly says that Natsumi is **not her enemy** and that she does not want to see her sister hurt again. Yet she also says she does not think becoming one through easy familiarity right now is best. If she merges before the claim is genuinely tested, the evidence she wants becomes harder to obtain.

Wien's reason differs. She still wants to defeat Liella! and win Love Live! under a recognition logic that remains tied to being acknowledged by the important people she has been trying to convince. The Shanghai live made her more behaviorally invested in the people she calls rivals, but it did not erase the goal that gives her current separation meaning.

S3E07 therefore moves the Season-3 problem from **whether rivalry can coexist with care** to **whether rivalry can itself be an honest form of care, evidence, and self-definition**.

Kanon's most important leadership move follows from that distinction. She does not answer the refusals by escalating the merger pitch. At the sleepover she explicitly says the gathering is not another attempt to make them join Liella!. Her explanation is much more basic: the three have become one group and even traveled to Shanghai together, yet they have barely talked about themselves. She wants to know them.

That move changes the order of operations:

> **person-knowledge -> motive legibility -> shared action**

rather than:

> **desired structure -> persuasion -> forced convergence**.

The domestic conversation initially looks trivial beside the Love Live! conflict. It is not. Wien clarifies a minor food preference; Tomari reveals that she loves sweets despite regulating intake, that she is strongly attached to jellyfish as a nightly source of comfort, and that the absence of a numbered manga/book volume can produce immediate urgency. Kanon explicitly refuses Tomari's attempt to classify such information as irrelevant. Knowing Tomari herself is `すっごく大事`.

The result is not instant ideological agreement. It is enough relational knowledge for the trio to carry incompatible reasons honestly into the night-walk discussion.

At the site of Kanon and Wien's earlier competition, Kanon identifies the unresolved state created by Shanghai: the eleven-person live was moving, but Wien's and Tomari's underlying reasons are still suspended. Tomari names hers. She does not want Natsumi to chase a dream halfway and be hurt again. Wien names hers. She wants to defeat Liella!. Kanon then supplies the synthesis without erasing either reason:

> `今こそ 私たち３人で`
>
> `全力で Liella!にぶつかろう！`

Wien answers that this is exactly what she wants; Tomari answers `アグリーです。`

This is stronger evidence of TomaKanoTe as an authentic team than merely performing together. The members do **not** share one motive. They now understand enough of one another's motives to choose the same consequential action for different reasons.

The headmistress then gives this chosen rivalry an institutional form. The one-school/one-group Love Live! rule can no longer remain an abstract Season-3 problem. Yuigaoka will hold a representative-deciding contest at the school festival the following month. One group will represent the school; the other will not enter Love Live!. Both groups accept the procedure.

So S3E07's institutional transition is precise:

> **the representation problem is procedurally resolved while the competitive result remains unresolved.**

That distinction matters because the episode refuses two shortcuts. It does not dissolve scarcity by calling everyone Liella!, and it does not let an administrator privately select the winner. The groups choose to expose their different projects to a public, symmetrical school procedure.

The late Tomari-Wien conversation then revises a deeper assumption about Kanon. Tomari says she had believed Kanon created their group for Liella!'s sake: TomaKanoTe would become a rival that made Liella! grow. Now she sees that Kanon was genuinely considering **their** feelings. That is a crucial revision of Season 3's decentering model. Kanon can still influence the system, but TomaKanoTe is no longer correctly understood as an instrument of Kanon's former group. Kanon has helped create conditions in which the rival unit's purposes become its own.

Wien and Tomari then bring Kanon an important proposal. The episode deliberately withholds its complete content. The audible formulation reaches:

> `Liella!に勝って`
>
> `そして…`

Kanon likes the proposal, and the three run forward under the TomaKanoTe name. But the semantic content after `そして…` is **not available at the S3E07 boundary**. Filling it from S3E08 would violate the sealed method. It remains OPEN.

The strongest sealed S3E07 thesis is therefore:

> **S3E07 turns separation from an awkward residue into a chosen competitive form. Shanghai proves that all eleven can create something powerful together, but neither Wien nor Tomari accepts the conclusion that immediate merger is therefore best. Tomari needs an uncompromised confrontation to test whether Natsumi's restored dream is serious; Wien still needs to defeat Liella! under her own recognition logic. Kanon's leadership therefore shifts from trying to produce unity to building enough mutual knowledge that three different reasons can become one shared action: face Liella! at full strength. The headmistress gives that choice an institutional form through a school-festival representative contest. By the end, TomaKanoTe has ceased to be merely Kanon's instrument for improving Liella!: Tomari explicitly recognizes that Kanon has been protecting their own feelings and goals, and the three choose competition together. The exact `そして…` after victory remains sealed and OPEN.**

Compactly:

> **The episode's answer to "why not become eleven now?" is that honest rivalry can itself be a form of care, evidence, and self-definition.**

# 4. Sequential reading

## 4.1 Opening: Shanghai leaves an afterimage, not a merger

The episode opens in the social afterglow of Shanghai. Students discuss the joint live. Wien's quiet `あの時… / 私が感じたのは…` marks unfinished reflection. Tomari is then observed mentally rehearsing Shanghai choreography before dismissing it as mere image training.

These are important precisely because the next movement restores separation. When the second-years greet Wien familiarly, she responds with an explicit speaker-labeled `フン！`, tells them not to get chummy, and immediately says she will defeat Liella!.

The sequence should not be flattened into either "Shanghai changed nothing" or "Shanghai secretly reunified them." The more accurate state is:

> **shared performance created relational/artistic aftereffects while the current competitive boundary remains actively meaningful.**

## 4.2 Final Love Live! and the return of institutional scarcity

Keke announces the coming Love Live! season with characteristic energy. The third-years explicitly recognize it as their last high-school Love Live!. Younger members frame the objective as another championship.

Then the Shanghai eleven-person experience becomes a governance problem. Liella!'s members can now imagine `The Liella!` as eleven, but Sumire immediately points out that desire is not enough. The headmistress names the actual rule: Yuigaoka may enter only one group.

Her solution is neither forced merger nor private selection. At the school festival next month, the two groups will hold a representative-deciding contest. The other group will not enter Love Live!.

This transforms the season architecture:

- before S3E07: zero-sum scarcity known;
- at S3E07: **selection procedure known**;
- after S3E07: winner still unknown.

## 4.3 The eleven-person invitation is sincere

The second-years' attempt to recruit Wien and Tomari should not be treated as a straw-man merger argument. Shiki states that the Shanghai performance gave her a real sense of expanded possibility beyond the nine-person form. Liella!'s desire for eleven is grounded in experience.

This matters because S3E07 does not settle the question by revealing that one side never really wanted unity. **Both positions are genuine**:

- Liella! genuinely wants the enlarged creative form;
- Wien/Tomari genuinely need separation to finish current goals.

The conflict is therefore about timing, purpose, and decision authority.

## 4.4 Tomari refuses merger as part of an evidentiary ethic

Tomari's `アグリーしかねます。` is followed by unusually exact purpose language. She joined to test whether Natsumi's words are true. She wants to determine with her own eyes whether Natsumi is serious rather than halfhearted.

The strongest line is not merely the refusal; it is her claim that becoming one through familiarity **right now** is not best.

That temporal qualification is important. She is not declaring permanent anti-Liella! identity. She is saying that merger would interfere with a current diagnostic task.

S3E04 established that Tomari's anti-dream pessimism was partly protective. S3E07 now shows a significant behavioral revision: protective care can accept risk and even generate challenge when challenge is the best available evidence.

## 4.5 Wien refuses merger as part of her recognition project

Wien's refusal is simpler in form but not shallow. She wants to defeat Liella! and be recognized by the people whose judgment still matters to her. Her `Liella!は敵` language remains a pride-protective organizing category.

Yet the episode continues to accumulate counterevidence to a literal enemy model. She accepts Kanon's sleepover, permits increasing personal knowledge, agrees to full-force team action, and later collaborates with Tomari on a shared post-victory proposal.

Enemy rhetoric therefore remains useful as **boundary language**, not a complete predictor of relational conduct.

## 4.6 Chisato refuses to let desire for eleven become coercion

When Liella!'s members look toward Kanon as the person who might solve the split, Chisato instead says to proceed without rushing and to value Wien's and Tomari's feelings.

This is a meaningful continuation of her S3E03-S3E06 governance development. Chisato can want a collective outcome while recognizing that the role/structure must remain plastic around the actual people involved.

## 4.7 Kanon does not hold another merger meeting

At home, Kanon accurately knows Wien's stated goal before Wien finishes saying it. The corresponding `フン！` is useful relational evidence: Wien is not denying the formulation; she is accepting being known without surrendering pride.

Kanon then proposes a three-person sleepover/talk. The most important clarification comes when Tomari suspects another Liella! recruitment pitch:

> `違うよ。`

Kanon says they have shared a group and Shanghai but barely talked about one another. She wants to know them better.

This scene is the best direct evidence so far for a Kanon leadership rule that the corpus had only partially implied:

> **communication can be an end before it becomes a tool.**

Kanon eventually connects communication to performance, but she refuses Tomari's attempt to make private person-knowledge disposable once its immediate instrumental purpose is satisfied.

## 4.8 Tomari's private-interest breakthrough

The domestic sequence is especially valuable for character simulation because it opens Tomari beyond the high-stakes Natsumi/utility frame.

She likes sweets strongly but normally regulates intake for physique maintenance. She is strongly attached to jellyfish, rejects a dismissive description emphatically, describes them as uniquely cute/soothing, and says she watches them before sleep. A missing seventh volume in an ordered series produces an immediate plan to go buy another copy.

These details matter less as trivia than as register evidence. Tomari's formal/analytic baseline is real; it is not her entire emotional range. Safe, person-specific preferences can make her speech markedly more emphatic and affect-forward.

Kanon's response is equally important. Tomari says her own material is unnecessary. Kanon answers that it is very important because she wants to know Tomari.

The relational point is therefore mutual:

- Tomari is more affectively available than her formal register suggests;
- Kanon treats that availability as intrinsically valuable rather than merely useful to the next live.

## 4.9 Shanghai unity is reinterpreted as shared goal, not permanent structure

Kanon asks why the eleven-person Shanghai performance worked. Her answer is not "because eleven is the true final membership." She says that, at that moment, everyone reached toward the same goal and could take one another's hands.

This is a subtle but important structural claim:

> **unity is something enacted under aligned purpose, not necessarily a permanent organizational state.**

It lets the episode preserve the reality of the Shanghai performance while denying that it dictates today's institution.

## 4.10 The old competition site converts unresolved feeling into objective

The night walk returns Kanon and Wien to a space associated with prior competition. Tomari knows the history through records; Kanon names the fact that Liella! advanced.

The scene then turns from historical rivalry to current motive.

Tomari says:

- Natsumi is not her enemy;
- she does not want Natsumi to chase a dream halfway;
- she does not want to see her hurt again.

Wien says simply that she wants to beat Liella!.

Kanon's synthesis is not psychological reduction. She does not tell Tomari that she "really" wants the same thing as Wien, or tell Wien that she "really" just loves Liella!. She says practice has shown that both are serious, so now the three should meet Liella! at full strength.

This is the episode's central behavioral transformation:

> **different motives become one team objective without ceasing to be different motives.**

## 4.11 Representative contest: rivalry becomes legitimate institutional action

The headmistress confirms that the confrontation will proceed as planned. Both sides answer yes.

The contest therefore becomes more than interpersonal rivalry. It is now a recognized institutional process for determining representation under real scarcity.

This is also why the S3E05-S3E06 fair-rivalry model survives an unusually hard test. There is now something material to lose. The groups nevertheless choose full-force contest rather than opportunistic sabotage, merger by pressure, or withdrawal.

## 4.12 Tomari revises her model of Kanon's motive

After Kanon leaves, Tomari asks Wien what she thought when Kanon originally formed the group. Tomari says she had assumed it was for Liella!: create rivals so Liella! would improve.

She now says that was incomplete. Kanon really was thinking about **them**, and she properly valued their feelings.

This is one of S3E07's most consequential relationship transitions because it corrects a plausible interpretation of Season 3 itself. Kanon's original mutual-growth theory was real. But the rival group has developed beyond being a training stimulus for the old group.

Tomari now recognizes Kanon as someone who has made their own motives matter.

## 4.13 `そして…` remains sealed

Wien and Tomari present an important proposal to Kanon. Kanon approves. Wien later says they will defeat Liella! and then trails into `そして…`.

The episode does not give the audience the completion. This is precisely the type of prospective ambiguity the V2.2 method must preserve.

Therefore:

- a shared post-victory thought exists;
- Kanon has heard and approved it;
- Wien and Tomari share it;
- its exact content is **OPEN** at S3E07.

## 4.14 TomaKanoTe becomes a socially owned name

Kanon closes with `レッツゴー！ トマカノーテ！`

Wien complains about the name. Tomari says she does not dislike it. Kanon thanks her.

The exchange is comic, but longitudinally useful. The name is no longer merely an analytic convenience or external label: all three can negotiate it as part of their shared social object.

# 5. Character-model transitions

## 5.1 Kanon -- from structural synthesis to relationally informed synthesis

**Transition: STRENGTHEN / DIFFERENTIATE.**

Kanon still synthesizes. S3E07 does not erase that central competence. The change is what she treats as legitimate input.

Earlier Kanon could be tempted to solve a system through a value principle. Here she accepts that two people can refuse the structure she and Liella! desire for reasons that remain valid. Her first intervention is not a better argument; it is more relationship data.

The final synthesis therefore has a stronger basis:

- Wien's purpose remains Wien's;
- Tomari's purpose remains Tomari's;
- Kanon identifies an action that honors both.

This is a more distributed form of leadership than simply persuading everyone into Kanon's preferred ontology.

## 5.2 Tomari -- protective skepticism becomes testing rather than veto

**Transition: STRENGTHEN.**

S3E04 established why Tomari distrusts dreams. S3E05-S3E06 showed that skepticism could motivate serious embodied inquiry. S3E07 now supplies the missing escalation condition: when the loved person insists on a dream, Tomari can choose consequential evidence over preventative control.

That is not yet philosophical conversion. Her school-idol value judgment remains unresolved.

But it is a major autonomy revision. She can love Natsumi, fear Natsumi's pain, and still permit the dream to encounter a real test.

## 5.3 Tomari -- formal reserve is not affective emptiness

**Transition: STRENGTHEN character-model bandwidth.**

Jellyfish, sweets, and ordered reading provide a new low-stakes window. In simulation, Tomari should not be reduced to polite analytics, utility calculation, and `姉者`. The formal mode remains real, but strongly preferred safe topics can produce emphatic language, rapid correction, and visible personal enthusiasm.

## 5.4 Wien -- boundary defense increasingly coexists with intimacy

**Transition: STRENGTHEN.**

Wien gains four new explicit huffs in contexts that all involve face/boundary regulation. Especially diagnostic are the two around being accurately known and reluctantly accepting the sleepover.

The marker increasingly tracks the problem:

> **how to remain proud/competitive while permitting relational exposure.**

Her actual behavior continues to exceed the coldness implied by enemy rhetoric.

## 5.5 Chisato -- patience as governance

**Transition: STRENGTHEN.**

S3E06 showed Chisato capable of inventing a temporary higher-order eleven-person performance. S3E07 demonstrates the complementary capacity: she can refrain from treating a successful exceptional form as a mandate. Her `急がず焦らず` position is governance through timing and respect for member-specific state.

## 5.6 Keke -- no false route closure

**Transition: PRESERVE / OPEN.**

S3E07 contains final-Love-Live temporal language but no new implementation answer. The correct model remains:

> continued stage/singing desire authored; educational/professional route OPEN.

This negative result is important enough to preserve because S3E06 could easily tempt retrospective overclaiming.

# 7. Institutional and thematic architecture

## 7.1 One-school scarcity becomes procedure

The headmistress's school-festival contest is the episode's institutional hinge. It separates three questions that earlier episodes could conflate:

1. Can all eleven make a good live? -- **yes, demonstrated in Shanghai**.
2. Do all eleven currently want one formal group? -- **no**.
3. Which group represents Yuigaoka at Love Live!? -- **to be decided by explicit contest**.

The procedure prevents the first answer from silently deciding the other two.

## 7.2 Public desire is not member authority

The visible `Liella!が11人になりますように` wish is a powerful formal image because it externalizes the intuitive audience answer: eleven together looked good, so make eleven permanent.

The episode places that desire beside member refusals and institutional procedure. The visual should therefore be read as **social pressure / collective hope**, not proof that merger is canonically correct.

## 7.3 Rivalry can be relationally pro-social

S3E07 further dismantles the simple competition-versus-care binary. Tomari selects competition because she cares about Natsumi and needs real evidence. Wien selects competition because victory is central to her self-definition. Kanon selects competition because it gives both teammates an honest action. Liella! accepts competition while still wanting the rivals near them.

Competition here is not automatically virtuous. Its value comes from being the form that preserves honest stakes better than premature unity would.
