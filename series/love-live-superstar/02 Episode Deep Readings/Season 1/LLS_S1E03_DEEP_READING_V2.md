# Love Live! Superstar!! — S1E03 Deep Reading V2

## 1. Governing thesis

S1E03 is the first episode to force Kanon's newly recovered desire into the exact domain that can still defeat her: **public performance whose outcome matters to somebody else**.

S1E01 established that Kanon can sing when the act is not structured as an evaluative test of her worth. S1E02 showed that she can sustain a school-idol project through advocacy, songwriting, and training while the actual singing problem remains open. S1E03 now demonstrates something harsher: once the festival's first-place condition becomes salient, even the relatively permissive conditions under which she previously sang cease to be enough. She says:

> 「今まで　大事な時以外は歌えてたのに」
>
> 「フェスで１位取らなきゃいけないのに」

The new competitive condition does not simply reveal a pre-existing technical deficit. It changes the meaning of singing. The act is no longer only *Can I sing?* It becomes *Can I sing well enough that Keke's dream is not destroyed because of me?*

That distinction governs the episode.

The episode then spends much of its middle trying approaches that implicitly treat Kanon's problem as an **individual deficit to be corrected**. Chisato proposes exposure to being watched through takoyaki work; Keke proposes costume-mediated confidence; both reassure her that she has sung before. None of these procedures resolves the block. Kanon instead converts the repeated failure into a moral indictment of herself:

> 「私じゃなかったら　可可ちゃんもっと楽だったろうな」
>
> 「やっぱり私は足手まといにしかならない」

The decisive intervention is relational rather than technical. Keke refuses the premise that Kanon's present utility determines whether she belongs. She first offers to carry the vocal burden herself while still insisting that Kanon stand onstage with her. Later, when Kanon argues that her weakness may force Keke to abandon the dream she crossed countries to pursue, Keke answers by revealing that **Kanon herself has become one of those dreams**:

> 「だからもう　かのんさんは私にとってのスターなんデス　夢なんデス」
>
> 「かのんさんと同じステージに立つことは / 可可にとって夢の一つなんですから」

This does not magically make Kanon competent. It changes the moral geometry of the problem. If Kanon is part of what Keke wants, Kanon cannot protect Keke by erasing herself from Keke's project.

Kanon reciprocates by asking Keke to drop the distancing `さん` and call her simply:

> 「かのんでいい」
>
> 「かのんって呼んでよ」

At this boundary, the request matters more than whether Keke immediately verbalizes the new address form. It is Kanon's attempt to reduce the asymmetry implied by Keke's `スター` / `夢` language: she accepts being valued, but asks to be related to as a person at closer range rather than preserved at honorific distance.

The stage crisis then makes the same structure visible. Kanon repeats 「大丈夫」 to herself, but self-reassurance is insufficient. The breakthrough occurs only after the environment changes from a faceless evaluative audience into a field of recognizable support. The audience's distributed lights visually turn the crowd into something like the `Tiny Stars` metaphor before the song begins; Keke remains physically beside Kanon; and Kanon finally says:

> 「歌える」
>
> 「一人じゃないから」

The key proposition is not “I am finally fearless.” It is **I can act because the stage is no longer experienced as solitary judgment**.

`Tiny Stars` then refuses a simplistic inspirational conclusion. Its lyrics include:

> 「信じてる　それだけじゃ / 叶うわけないよ」
>
> 「叶うまで　走るしかない」

This is an unusually explicit refinement of the “feelings matter most” language surrounding Keke in S1E02. Feeling and belief remain necessary motives, but the song denies that belief alone produces achievement. Desire must become sustained action.

Finally, the episode deliberately separates **performance success from competitive success**. Kanon and Keke receive a newcomer special award, but Sunny Passion takes first place. The condition attached to their club therefore remains unresolved, and Kanon explicitly says they may no longer be able to continue as school idols. Yet she also says she has no regret because she and Keke fulfilled their promise to make the best live they could.

At the S1E03 boundary, the series has therefore created four distinct measures of success:

1. **technical success** — Kanon manages to sing onstage;
2. **relational success** — Kanon and Keke perform together as mutually chosen partners;
3. **experiential/artistic success** — they judge the live itself as worth having done;
4. **competitive/institutional success** — they fail to take first place.

The episode refuses to collapse these into one score.

The strongest prospective thesis is consequently:

> **Kanon's problem is not solved by becoming invulnerable to judgment. S1E03 shows that she becomes capable when the meaning of the gaze changes—from solitary evaluation to shared participation—and it establishes that competition can determine institutional consequences without exhausting the value of what a performance accomplishes.**

# 3. Sequential dramatic architecture

## Movement I — The breakthrough fails to generalize (`00:00–03:29`)

The recap carries forward S1E02's hopeful formulation, 「次の私が　始まった」, and immediately places it under pressure.

Keke asks Kanon to sing. Kanon's first response is not a refusal:

> 「ご…　ごめん」
>
> 「ちょっとびっくりしただけ　歌うよ」

She intends to try. But the attempt does not become sustained singing. Keke begins to notice something—「かのんさん　もしかして…」—and Kanon cuts the inference off with:

> 「何でもない　だいじょぶだいじょぶ」

The repetition is already a miniature version of the later backstage 「大丈夫　大丈夫　大丈夫…」. Kanon can use reassurance language as **self-management**, not only as a report of actual safety. [JT]

After the OP, she states the problem directly:

> 「今まで　大事な時以外は歌えてたのに」
>
> 「朝だし　人も少なかったから大丈夫だと思ったんだけど」
>
> 「余計悪くなっちゃったのかなあ」

The episode therefore prevents a comforting retrospective rewrite of S1E01. The earlier distinction between important/evaluative contexts and ordinary contexts was real, but it is not static. S1E02 added a new evaluative condition: first place at a festival. That condition can infect an otherwise low-stakes morning attempt because the act now points forward to a consequential performance.

This is a significant character-model revision. Kanon's block is **meaning-sensitive**, not location-sensitive. “Few people present” is not sufficient protection if she understands the singing as preparation for a consequential future test.

Ren appears and immediately translates Kanon's difficulty into institutional risk:

> 「フェスで醜態をさらせば　この学校の評判にも関わります」

Keke answers that it has not yet been decided that Kanon cannot sing. Kanon herself then chooses a practical stance:

> 「とにかく　やれることをやってみようと思う」
>
> 「まだ時間はあるし」

That response matters. S1E01 Kanon often treated the possibility of failure as evidence she should withdraw. S1E03 Kanon can now hold a frightening uncertainty open long enough to work on it.

Ren's final remark in the scene targets Chisato's involvement—she hopes they do not interfere with Chisato's practice. Kanon immediately apologizes to Chisato, and Chisato refuses the apology. This repeats a broader Kanon pattern: when her desire may impose cost on another person, she is quick to treat herself as a possible burden.

The episode's problem is therefore established before any proposed cure:

> **Kanon is not merely afraid she will fail; she is afraid her failure will consume other people's opportunities and expose the school to shame.**

## Movement II — Individual desensitization fails (`00:03:29–04:35`)

Chisato proposes a practical diagnosis:

> 「その原因は絶対プレッシャー」
>
> 「フェスで1位を取らなきゃって / 新たなプレッシャーがかのんちゃんの中に生まれているはず」

This is a character's theory, not automatically the series' final clinical explanation. But it fits the evidence available through S1E03 better than a generalized inability to sing.

Her experiment is inventive: takoyaki work forces Kanon to perform a task under continuous customer observation. If she can become functional while watched in an unfamiliar setting, perhaps the relationship between gaze and paralysis can change.

The experiment is useful precisely because it **fails**.

Kanon becomes able to handle the task, but when the question turns back to singing the answer remains no. Keke observes:

> 「やはり歌は別みたいですね」

The episode has therefore performed a small causal stress test. Generic social exposure is not sufficient. Kanon's singing problem cannot be reduced to “being watched makes her nervous.” Singing is tied to a specific history of desire, evaluation, and self-worth.

This failed intervention is analytically valuable because it narrows the model:

> audience gaze alone ≠ singing paralysis.

The gaze must be understood together with **what singing means in that context**.

## Movement III — Costume confidence fails, but intimacy grows (`00:04:35–08:12`)

Keke proposes another route: if Kanon wears a cute costume, the positive affect may lift her enough to sing.

Again, the episode lets the idea fail without treating the attempt as meaningless.

Kanon genuinely likes the clothing:

> 「いや　衣装かわいいなあって」

but draws a line at herself wearing it:

> 「かわいい服すぎて…」
>
> 「私には無理だよ」

Keke and Chisato physically/comedically push her through the threshold. Once dressed, Kanon can endure a photo but becomes acutely uncomfortable when Keke proposes uploading it for likes:

> 「消して」
>
> 「それは全然大丈夫じゃない」

This distinction is important for later behavior modeling. Kanon is not uniformly averse to appearance, cute clothing, or play. What intensifies the discomfort is **public circulation and evaluation**. The same structural variable that matters for singing appears in another domain, though less severely.

Kanon then returns to the singing problem:

> 「そう簡単に歌えるなら / 今までだって苦労してないよぉ」

Keke's answer is evidence-based rather than purely motivational:

> 「かのんさんは絶対歌えマス」
>
> 「その瞬間を可可は見てマシタ」

Kanon rejects the evidentiary value of the earlier success:

> 「たまたまだよ」
>
> 「今の姿が本当の私なんだよ　きっと」

This is a major self-modeling line. Faced with conflicting evidence—one successful public song versus repeated blocks—Kanon privileges the negative state as more ontologically “true.” Success is recoded as accident; failure is recoded as identity.

Keke does not argue abstractly that Kanon should “believe in herself.” Instead she discloses dependence:

> 「かのんさんがいてくれたから / 可可は今　頑張れているんデス」

Kanon answers with a fact Keke cannot erase: she sang once and then “went back.” Keke's solution is therefore unusually pragmatic:

> 「今は無理に歌おうとするのはやめましょう」
>
> 「今回のライブは　可可が一人で歌いマス」
>
> 「だからかのんさんはステージに立つだけでいいんデス」
>
> 「一緒に全力のライブをしましょう」

This is one of the episode's most important relationship moves. Keke **decouples belonging from immediate output**. Kanon does not have to earn the right to stand beside her by providing the superior voice Keke originally recruited. The offer is not “stay home until you are fixed.” It is “stand with me now; healing can remain unfinished.”

That is not simply indulgence. Keke simultaneously accepts a heavier burden—she will sing the full live herself despite having already admitted inferior vocal ability relative to Kanon. She is redistributing task responsibility to preserve shared participation.

Keke then reaffirms the S1E01 promise:

> 「かのんさんが歌えるようになるまで諦めないって」

Chisato supports the relational offer: 「可可ちゃんがここまで言ってくれてるんだよ」. Kanon finally accepts a narrower immediate goal:

> 「まずは2人で1位取らなきゃだもん」
>
> 「いいライブができるように頑張る」

The episode has therefore moved from **fix Kanon before the stage** to **allow unfinished Kanon onto the stage**.

### The temporary “クーカー” name

Keke reveals a sign and penlights bearing a group name she created by combining `可可` and `かのん`: 「クーカー」.

The name is comic and Kanon is not fully convinced about the sign, but the construction matters prospectively. Keke's first naming instinct is not an abstract brand. It is a literal joining of two personal names. At this boundary, the project remains intensely dyadic and person-specific.

This should not yet be treated as a permanent group ontology. It is evidence that Keke currently imagines the unit through **Keke + Kanon**, not through an institution larger than either of them.

## Movement IV — Sunny Passion converts difficulty into apparent impossibility (`00:08:12–11:26`)

At the festival venue, Kanon and Keke learn that Sunny Passion will participate.

Keke's reaction first takes the form of fandom. She explains that seeing Sunny Passion was a major reason she came to Japan at all. Her descriptions are reverential and individuated; Sunny Passion is not just a competitive obstacle but an origin point in her own desire.

This matters because their arrival creates a conflict between **admiration and competition**. Keke can sincerely revere them while also needing to defeat them to satisfy Yuigaoka's imposed condition.

The contradiction initially escapes her because fandom overwhelms calculation. Kanon and Chisato are the ones who point out the obvious: if Sunny Passion are overwhelmingly likely to win, Keke and Kanon may lose the right to continue.

Keke's delayed realization is comic:

> 「今？」
>
> 「やっと気付いたんだ」
>
> 「勝てる気がしないデス」

But the scene is also a useful counterweight to modeling Keke as a consistently strategic actor. She is operationally energetic but can become **attentionaly captured by admiration**, temporarily failing to process consequences that are obvious to the others.

With only five days remaining, Chisato correctly notes that simply making training harsher is not necessarily useful. Kanon's inability to sing also means Keke must prepare to cover the entire vocal load.

Kanon apologizes. Keke immediately responds with evidence of progress: she can now perform the training sequence much better than before and insists she can cover Kanon.

Chisato then leaves for her own dance training and says something structurally important:

> 「それに　2人で過ごす時間も大事だよ」
>
> 「ステージに立つのは君たちなんだから」

Chisato understands support partly as **knowing when to withdraw**. She has supplied expertise, but refuses to make herself the center of a performance relationship whose final responsibility belongs to Kanon and Keke.

## Movement V — Chisato defines commitment; Keke tests recruitment without violating it (`00:11:26–12:27`)

Keke follows Chisato and asks directly whether she has any interest in school idols.

Chisato says:

> 「あるよ」

This is the first clean evidence that her non-membership should not be interpreted as lack of attraction to school idols.

She then sets a boundary:

> 「私にはダンスがあるの」
>
> 「ダンスで結果を出すことが今の私の一番の目標」
>
> 「掛け持ちできるほど余裕はないんだ」

Most importantly:

> 「かのんちゃんも真剣だし　生半可な気持ちではやれない」

This line retrospectively pressures Kanon's S1E02 assumption that Chisato's music-course path makes recruitment simply impossible. Chisato's own reason is more specific and more agentic: **her present priority is dance, and she respects Kanon's seriousness enough not to join casually**.

This is a valuable autonomy distinction. Kanon was not malicious in answering for Chisato, but S1E03 shows why a model should not let Kanon's protective assumptions substitute for Chisato's own articulated priorities.

Keke accepts the answer without coercion. She explains that she asked partly because Kanon would be happy if Chisato joined. Chisato thanks her.

This adds a more socially perceptive dimension to Keke. Her recruitment is not only resource acquisition; she notices Kanon's existing attachment and imagines what another membership configuration would mean emotionally to Kanon.

## Movement VI — Kanon's burden logic reaches its harshest form (`00:12:27–13:50`)

Kanon and Keke are alone after family contact. Keke admits she invited Chisato again, then quickly clarifies:

> 「私はかのんさんと同じステージに立ちたい」
>
> 「その気持ちは変わりません」

Kanon answers:

> 「すごくうれしい」

But she cannot retain the reassurance. Sunny Passion's entry and her own inability to sing become an ethical equation:

> if they cannot win → Keke may lose the dream → Kanon's weakness contributed → Kanon has wronged Keke.

Her wording intensifies through repetition:

> 「私のせいで」
>
> 「私のせいで…」
>
> 「私のせいで夢を諦めなきゃいけないなんてなったら / 申し訳なさすぎるよ」

She then converts situational weakness into total role judgment:

> 「やっぱり私は足手まといにしかならない」
>
> 「それが分かってるのに / ステージに上がるなんてできないよ」

This is more than low confidence. Kanon constructs withdrawal as **moral responsibility**. If she is a burden, then stepping aside becomes the ethical act.

That mechanism is important for simulation because it explains why ordinary reassurance may fail. Telling Kanon “you are good enough” does not fully answer a fear formulated as “my presence may damage somebody else's future.” The intervention has to change her understanding of what the other person actually values.

## Movement VII — Keke makes Kanon part of the dream (`00:13:50–15:22`)

Keke's answer begins with a prohibition against Kanon's self-denigration:

> 「自分のことを悪く言わないでクダサイ」

She follows with a characteristic comic inversion:

> 「かのんさんに心奪われた　私もかわいそうになっちゃいマス」

The line refuses Kanon's attempt to make her own worth a unilateral judgment. If Kanon declares herself worthless, she is implicitly declaring Keke's attraction/admiration mistaken too.

Keke then supplies the deepest autobiographical evidence available for her through S1E03. Her family has been highly education-focused; she studied without a strong personally chosen desire and assumed that path was simply correct:

> 「これでいいんだろうな　これで正しいことなんだって思ってマシタ」

School idols introduced another mode of life:

> 「こんなふうに自分の気持ちや感じたことを自由に歌ってみたい」

And Kanon's singing reproduced the same excitement:

> 「かのんさんの歌を初めて聴いた時 / それと同じぐらいわくわくしたんデス」

The culmination is explicit:

> 「だからもう　かのんさんは私にとってのスターなんデス　夢なんデス」

followed by:

> 「かのんさんと同じステージに立つことは / 可可にとって夢の一つなんですから」

This is the episode's decisive logical reversal.

Kanon's burden argument assumed:

> Keke's dream = successful school-idol continuation; Kanon's weakness threatens that dream; therefore remove Kanon.

Keke reveals:

> Keke's dream includes **standing with Kanon**; therefore removing Kanon in order to “protect Keke's dream” would itself destroy part of the dream.

The exchange does not establish that Keke sees Kanon with perfect realism. `スター` and `夢` remain elevated language, and future episodes may pressure whether admiration becomes idealization. At this boundary, however, Keke's behavior is not merely worshipful. She has already accommodated Kanon's weakness, planned to cover her vocals, and remained beside her after repeated failure. Her admiration currently coexists with practical knowledge of limitation.

### Acoustic hinge before Kanon chooses to try again

Keke's 「最高のライブにしましょう」 ends at approximately `14:48.40`. Kanon's 「私　歌ってみる」 begins at approximately `14:52.11`, creating a **3.71-second** gap. The mixed-track 100 ms median during that interval is about **−41.3 dBFS**, compared with about **−26.0 dBFS** during Keke's immediately preceding line. [AM]

This does not by itself prove an emotion. It does establish that the response is given temporal/acoustic space rather than delivered as instantaneous motivational compliance.

Kanon's reason remains relational:

> 「可可ちゃんがこんなに頑張ってるんだもん」
>
> 「こんな私でも　一緒にステージに立ちたいって言ってくれてるんだもん」

Then she formulates a bounded agency statement:

> 「ギリギリまで自分を信じて　やれることを精いっぱい頑張る」

This is notably weaker and more credible than “I know I can sing.” She commits to effort under uncertainty.

### 「かのんでいい」 — reducing relational distance

Keke says 「かのんさん」. Kanon answers:

> 「かのんでいい」
>
> 「かのんって呼んでよ」

Between the two Kanon lines is a **1.96-second** interval whose 100 ms median mixed-track level drops to approximately **−65.2 dBFS**. [AM]

The request should not be over-romanticized at this boundary, but it is clearly an intimacy/address-state transition. Keke has just elevated Kanon into `スター` and `夢`; Kanon responds by asking for **less formal distance**.

The episode does not provide a later Keke utterance using bare 「かのん」 after the request, so uptake should remain **open**, not silently assumed.

## Movement VIII — Preparation reaches the stage, not certainty (`00:15:22–16:54`)

The remaining training montage shows real competence growth. Chisato praises how far they have come in a short time; both girls are physically exhausted.

This matters because the episode does not oppose emotional support to work. The breakthrough has a material substrate:

- Keke has improved stamina and dance execution;
- Kanon has continued practicing despite uncertain vocals;
- costumes and staging exist;
- the song is complete enough to perform;
- they have distributed penlights and created audience-facing identity materials.

At the venue, Kanon sees the crowd and says she is getting nervous. Keke establishes an explicit contingency plan:

> 「もし歌えそうだったら　始まりの時合図をクダサイ」
>
> 「歌えなかったとしても　堂々としていてクダサイ」
>
> 「可可が歌いマス」

This is extremely useful character-model evidence. Keke does not make Kanon's participation conditional on last-minute proof of ability. She pre-authorizes both outcomes:

- if Kanon can sing, they sing together;
- if Kanon cannot, she still stands there and Keke carries the vocal part.

That contingency reduces the performance from a binary identity test into a shared task with fallback states.

Kanon's answer is simple: 「ありがとう」.

## Movement IX — The gaze changes meaning (`00:16:54–18:53`)

Immediately before the live, Kanon repeats:

> 「大丈夫　大丈夫　大丈夫…」

This is not sufficient. The visual evidence keeps her body tense while she attempts to regulate herself.

A chain of events involving Sumire around the event's technical area changes the lighting environment. At the present boundary, the exact causal/mechanical details should not be inflated beyond the retained visual evidence: Sumire is shown near equipment and reacts with 「やっちゃった」 / 「やっちゃったったらやっちゃったのよ」 while the stage environment changes. The important direct visual fact is that Kanon and Keke are left in a darkened/blue-toned stage state before the audience's distributed colored lights become visually dominant. [AF]

Keke physically remains close behind/beside Kanon. A retained frame at approximately `18:07.93` places the two bodies in direct contact, Keke leaning into Kanon while Kanon faces outward. [AF]

Then Keke directs Kanon's attention outward:

> 「かのん」
>
> 「見て」

The address `かのん` here is spoken by **Chisato**, not evidence of Keke's uptake of the bare-name request. That distinction should be preserved.

The visual sequence spends roughly eighteen seconds between 「見て」 and Keke's 「きれいですぅ」 without explanatory dialogue. The audience lights fill the dark field. The sequence therefore changes the crowd from a mass of judging faces into a field of individually held points of light. [AF]

This is precisely the transformation the episode's earlier interventions could not produce. Kanon does not become unwatched. She becomes able to experience being watched as **supported presence** rather than solitary exposure.

Cheers follow. Kanon says:

> 「歌える」
>
> 「一人じゃないから」

The causal grammar is explicit in her own language. She does not say “I'm not scared anymore,” “I became confident,” or “I remembered my talent.” She says she can sing **because she is not alone**.

This is the strongest available statement of the mechanism through S1E03.

The phrase should also be interpreted at more than one level:

- Keke is physically with her onstage;
- Chisato and Aria are present as supporters;
- the audience has visibly become supportive rather than abstractly evaluative;
- the song itself is a co-created product rather than Kanon's solo audition object.

The important contrast with the entrance examination is therefore not only “duet versus solo.” It is **different social ontology of the performance**.

## Movement X — `Tiny Stars`: belief becomes work, and two voices become a unit (`00:18:53–21:10`)

The performance begins with:

> 「駆け抜けるシューティングスター / 追いかけて星になる / 煌めけ！」

The star image is now unusually dense with episode-local meanings.

Keke has just called Kanon her `スター`; the audience has visually become a field of small lights; the performers sing about pursuing a shooting star and becoming stars themselves. The imagery therefore works less as individual celebrity fantasy than as **transmitted aspiration**: one visible light produces movement in another, which may itself become light.

The verse explicitly narrates that structure:

> 「何も見えない夜空　ひとすじの流れ星」
>
> 「キラキラまぶしい姿に　勇気をもらったよ」
>
> 「いつかあんな風になれる日がくるかもしれない」

At the S1E03 boundary, this maps cleanly onto Keke's account of seeing school idols, and onto her account of hearing Kanon. It should still not be treated as literal one-to-one autobiography for every sung line, but the dramatic alignment is unusually strong. [JT/MF/SI]

Then:

> 「My Dream ハジメテを始めよう」
>
> 「不安でも / 行ける　平気」
>
> 「絆がここにある」

The performance does not deny anxiety. `不安でも` makes anxiety the condition inside which action occurs. This matches Kanon's pre-performance state far better than a “fear has vanished” reading.

The chorus includes:

> 「ちいさくても / ひとりじゃないから」
>
> 「諦めないで進めるんだ」

The direct recurrence of 「ひとりじゃないから」 immediately after Kanon's spoken statement makes the song function as an expansion of her newly available performance logic. [FR/JT]

Most important for the episode's theory of aspiration is the later section:

> 「信じてる　それだけじゃ / 叶うわけないよ」
>
> 「叶うまで　走るしかない」

This is not cynical. Belief remains present, but the lyric explicitly refuses magical causality. The line retrospectively refines Keke's earlier `気持ち` language:

> feeling/belief creates direction;
>
> repetition, training, and continued action create possibility.

The concluding formulation:

> 「いつまでも一緒に / 同じ夢見続けたいから」
>
> 「かたく手と手つないで行こうTiny Stars」

makes relation itself part of the performance's content. This is not merely a song performed by two girls who happen to share a stage; the song narratively articulates **why two-person co-presence changes what either can do**.

### Visual grammar of the performance

The retained frames repeatedly alternate:

- two-person wide formations;
- one performer foregrounded while the other remains visibly present;
- paired directional gestures;
- moments of back-to-back or closely offset alignment;
- shared frame occupancy rather than isolating Kanon into a triumphant solo center for the entire sequence.

This supports the episode's relational solution. Kanon succeeds, but the staging does not rewrite the event as a solitary star finally proving herself.

The audience's lights remain visually integrated into the stage image. The performance is therefore produced by a **performer–partner–audience system**, not only the two bodies on the stage.

## Movement XI — External defeat, internal completion (`00:21:10–23:36`)

After the performance, the episode does not give Kanon and Keke first place.

Kanon's narration states:

> 「その日のフェスは / 新人特別賞をもらえた」
>
> 「でも　1位はやっぱりサニーパッション」

The imposed condition was first place. Therefore, at this boundary:

> 「私たちはスクールアイドルを続けられないかもしれない」

The institutional problem is real and unresolved. The episode does not pretend that emotional success automatically cancels an external rule.

Yet Kanon adds:

> 「でも　全然後悔してないんだ」
>
> 「だって可可ちゃんと約束した　最高のライブができたから」

This is the first clean separation between **winning** and **the value of performance**.

The claim is not that competition is meaningless. Competition has concrete consequences: continuation may be denied. Nor is it that artistic self-satisfaction proves objective superiority. Sunny Passion won.

Rather, the episode insists that a competitive ranking cannot measure every thing the performance accomplished:

- Kanon sang under conditions that had repeatedly defeated her;
- Keke reached a stage she had dreamed of;
- the two created a genuinely shared live;
- their relationship crossed a new level of mutual recognition;
- they received external acknowledgment as promising newcomers;
- they still failed the explicit institutional criterion.

This is the first strong evidence for a series-level distinction that should be tracked longitudinally:

> **competition can distribute opportunity and recognition without becoming a complete theory of human or artistic worth.**

The episode ends before resolving what the institution will do with that distinction.

# 4. Character-state analysis

## 4.1 Shibuya Kanon

### Desire

Kanon's desire is now behaviorally difficult to deny. She wants to perform with Keke and remains in the project despite repeated evidence that the task may end badly.

Her most important change is not increased certainty but increased ability to **act without certainty**:

> 「ギリギリまで自分を信じて　やれることを精いっぱい頑張る」

This is a stronger model of agency than the earlier binary “I can / I cannot.”

### Fear

The performance block remains active and initially worsens. The new evidence suggests that the fear is sensitive to anticipated consequences. A small audience is no longer enough to protect Kanon once the act is connected to a first-place requirement.

### Defensive/self-protective logic

The episode reveals a dangerous moralized form of withdrawal:

> if my weakness harms another person's dream, I should remove myself.

This can present as altruism while also preserving Kanon from the terrifying test. The episode does not reduce it to selfish avoidance; Keke really could be harmed by losing. But Kanon's model gives herself unilateral authority to decide that Keke would be better off without her.

### Self-conception

Kanon privileges negative evidence as identity-defining:

> successful singing = “chance”;
>
> current blocked state = “the real me.”

Keke's intervention matters because it supplies external counterevidence about Kanon's value that is not reducible to performance output.

### New capacity

Kanon can now reinterpret support as a legitimate condition of agency rather than proof that her achievement is invalid. Her final 「歌える / 一人じゃないから」 explicitly names dependence/co-presence as enabling rather than shameful.

### Boundary limit

S1E03 does **not** establish that she can now sing reliably under any future evaluative condition. The success is highly context-dependent and relationally scaffolded.

---

## 4.2 Tang Keke

### Desire and personal history

Keke's school-idol desire is now grounded in a conflict between externally organized life and self-authored feeling. Her family is described as education-focused; before encountering school idols she lacked a strong personally chosen aim and assumed the prescribed path was simply correct.

School idols offered a mode of expression in which inner feeling could be sung freely.

This significantly deepens her apparent exuberance. Her intensity is not merely “fangirl energy.” It is attached to a discovery of **self-authored desire**.

### Kanon as star and dream

Keke's valuation of Kanon is extreme but not yet purely idealizing. She has seen Kanon fail repeatedly and adjusts plans around that failure. The current model should therefore hold both:

- **elevated admiration** — `スター`, `夢`;
- **practical acceptance of limitation** — Keke is willing to sing alone and keep Kanon onstage anyway.

### Adaptive support

Keke shows a notable capacity to stop pushing the same intervention when it is not working:

> 「今は無理に歌おうとするのはやめましょう」

This revises any model of her as simply relentless pressure. She is relentless about the **relationship/project**, but can become flexible about the immediate task demand.

### Blind spot

Sunny Passion briefly overwhelms her strategic attention. Her admiration is so strong that she fails to process the competitive consequence until others say it explicitly.

### Competence

Her stamina/dance ability has measurably improved through practice. The episode therefore strengthens S1E02's rule that `気持ち` does not function as an excuse to avoid labor.

---

## 4.3 Arashi Chisato

Chisato becomes more clearly individuated from both Kanon and Keke.

She is interested in school idols, but her current highest goal is dance achievement. She refuses to join not because school idols lack value, but because she believes Kanon's commitment deserves reciprocal seriousness and she lacks capacity for divided attention.

This gives her a strong **priority discipline**:

> attraction to an option does not automatically outrank an already chosen goal.

Her support style also includes withdrawal. She can coach intensively and then deliberately leave Kanon and Keke alone because “the ones who stand on the stage are you two.”

Chisato therefore models support without annexation.

---

## 4.4 Hazuki Ren

Ren's institutional logic remains consistent with S1E02. Kanon's inability to sing is immediately framed as possible damage to Yuigaoka's reputation.

This strengthens the model that Ren evaluates individual activity through **representational consequences for the institution**.

The episode provides little new access to her private motive. Her position should not be psychologized beyond the stated prestige concern at this boundary.

---

## 4.5 Heanna Sumire

Sumire remains peripheral to the central relationship but gains a behavioral contour:

- she is attentive to public visibility/scouting;
- she inserts herself physically around the festival environment;
- her interaction with event equipment contributes to a comic/technical disruption in the pre-performance sequence;
- she reacts through theatrical self-commentary rather than quiet withdrawal.

The exact technical causality should remain narrowly described from the available retained frames. Her deeper motives remain underdetermined.

---

## 4.6 Sunny Passion

Sunny Passion functions as both aspiration and competitive ceiling.

For Keke they are an origin object: evidence that another life could be desired. For the festival they are the obvious superior benchmark. Their presence makes visible a central tension:

> the people who inspire you may also be the people you are institutionally required to defeat.

The episode does not resolve that tension by devaluing them. Keke's admiration remains intact even when their participation makes first place appear impossible.

# 5. Relationship systems

## 5.1 Kanon ↔ Keke — from recruitment to mutual necessity

This is the episode's governing relationship.

S1E01: Keke recruits Kanon; Kanon can support Keke more easily than herself.  
S1E02: they become co-authors/trainees and Keke explicitly chooses Kanon as collaborator.  
S1E03: the relationship is tested by a possibility that Kanon's participation may objectively worsen Keke's odds.

The resolution is not “Kanon is actually the superior strategic choice.” The episode does not prove that. It is that Keke's goal is not reducible to maximum competitive efficiency.

Keke wants **this person** on the stage.

That is why Kanon's burden calculation fails: it solves a different optimization problem from Keke's actual values.

The bare-name request marks an intimacy transition, but it should remain precisely described. Kanon requests a reduced address distance; immediate Keke uptake is not heard later in this episode.

## 5.2 Kanon ↔ Chisato — support plus autonomy tension

Chisato remains a trusted regulator/coach for Kanon. But S1E03 also pressures Kanon's tendency to decide what is best for Chisato. Chisato's own explanation for not joining is more nuanced than Kanon's earlier “music course means she can't.”

The relationship therefore contains both deep familiarity and a possible asymmetry in which Kanon may overprotectively pre-interpret Chisato's obligations.

## 5.3 Keke ↔ Chisato — recruitment becomes respectful inquiry

Keke asks again, receives a clear no-for-now grounded in another goal, and accepts it. This is useful counterevidence against reading Keke's recruitment energy as inability to respect boundaries.

## 5.4 Kanon/Keke ↔ audience

The audience changes function during the episode.

Earlier, being watched is part of the problem. At the live, the crowd initially remains a source of pressure. Through the light/cheer sequence, however, the audience becomes part of the support system that enables performance.

The series therefore does not present audience attention as inherently alienating or affirming. Its meaning depends on how the performers construe the relation.

# 6. Institution, school, and competition

S1E03 creates the first direct test of the headmistress's performance condition.

The result is analytically important because it is **mixed**:

- newcomer special award = external recognition of merit;
- not first place = formal failure under the stated condition;
- successful performance = personal/relational achievement;
- possible inability to continue = institutional consequence remains live.

This prevents several simplifications.

### Competition result ≠ moral truth

Sunny Passion wins. Nothing in the episode suggests the result is illegitimate. But their victory cannot tell us whether Kanon should have gone onstage, whether Keke was wrong to choose her, or whether the live was worthwhile.

### Recognition ≠ authorization

The special newcomer award recognizes performance quality without necessarily satisfying the club's institutional gate.

### Failure ≠ regret

Kanon can accept a failed external objective while regarding the action as worth taking.

This distinction should become a standing longitudinal question:

> When does *Superstar!!* allow competitive institutions to distribute real consequences without granting them final authority over artistic/personhood value?

# 7. Cohort and succession state

The cohort state remains **pre-Liella / two-person unit plus external supporters**.

There is still no basis for treating later membership architecture as present.

Current roles:

- Keke — initiator, recruiter, co-performer, motivational and relational driver;
- Kanon — co-performer, composer, increasingly explicit co-owner of the project;
- Chisato — outside supporter/coach with a separate primary goal;
- Sumire — peripheral peer whose festival behavior intersects the performance environment;
- Ren — institutional opponent/gatekeeper;
- headmistress — authority who established the condition but is not present as a member of the performing system.

The temporary `クーカー` label is evidence of dyadic identity formation. It should not be treated as proof of stable future group naming.

# 8. Performance dramaturgy — `Tiny Stars`

## 8.1 Immediate dramatic question

Before the song, the question is not “can they win?” The immediate question is narrower and more psychologically urgent:

> **Can Kanon enter the song at all?**

Keke has already prepared a fallback in which she sings alone.

## 8.2 What changes that dialogue could not

Ordinary dialogue can tell Kanon that she is valued. It cannot make her experience a public audience as supportive while she is actually standing before one.

The performance sequence changes the social meaning of the stage **inside the feared condition itself**.

## 8.3 Song allocation and relational function

The song is structured as a two-person performance rather than Kanon's recovery showcase. The visible choreography repeatedly preserves the partner relation.

That matters because Kanon's success could otherwise be narratively appropriated into “the talented protagonist finally returns.” S1E03 instead makes recovery inseparable from partnership.

## 8.4 Lyric/drama convergence

Especially load-bearing lines:

- 「不安でも」 — action does not require absence of fear;
- 「絆がここにある」 — relation becomes an enabling condition;
- 「ひとりじゃないから」 — direct recurrence of Kanon's spoken causal statement;
- 「信じてる　それだけじゃ / 叶うわけないよ」 — belief is necessary but insufficient;
- 「叶うまで　走るしかない」 — aspiration becomes repeated labor;
- 「いつまでも一緒に / 同じ夢見続けたいから」 — shared future desire becomes song content.

## 8.5 Star ontology at this boundary

The star motif operates through transmission:

Sunny Passion → inspires Keke.  
Kanon's singing → inspires Keke.  
Keke calls Kanon a star.  
Audience lights → become visual “tiny stars.”  
Kanon/Keke → sing about pursuing and becoming stars.

A star is therefore not simply the person at the top of a ranking. It is a figure whose visible expression makes another person imagine movement.

This is an **interpretive thesis**, not a fixed symbol yet. Later episodes must test it.

# 9. Japanese dialogue and voice modeling

## 9.1 Kanon — reassurance as self-regulation

Repeated `大丈夫` is not always an assertion of actual confidence. In both the opening and backstage sequence it functions as attempted regulation under visible uncertainty.

Simulation implication:

> Kanon may verbally insist on “fine/okay” while still needing contextual evidence before behavior stabilizes.

## 9.2 Kanon — moralized self-blame

The repeated 「私のせいで」 and final 「申し訳なさすぎるよ」 reveal a speech mode in which performance weakness is translated into interpersonal guilt.

This is more load-bearing than generic “I can't do it” language.

## 9.3 Keke — direct countervaluation

Keke does not respond to self-blame through vague reassurance. She names her own valuation:

> 「かのんさんは私にとってのスターなんデス　夢なんデス」

Her characteristic directness therefore extends from recruitment into intimate counterargument.

## 9.4 Keke — autobiographical explanatory mode

S1E03 supplies a comparatively sustained reflective register about family expectations, education, absence of desire, and discovery of school idols. This is important because Keke's voice should not be reduced to hyperactive `デス` comedy. She can narrate personal history coherently when the relational stakes demand it.

## 9.5 Chisato — boundary-setting without devaluation

`あるよ` followed by a clear explanation that dance is her present first goal allows Chisato to decline membership while affirming school-idol interest. She does not need to demean the rejected option to protect her chosen priority.

## 9.6 Address shift

Kanon's:

> 「かのんでいい / かのんって呼んでよ」

is an explicit request for relational register change. Preserve it as a **requested transition** until Keke's actual later usage can be observed.

# 10. Acoustic audit

## 10.1 Opening failed attempt

The `00:47.1–00:51.5` interval after Kanon's 「歌うよ」 has mixed-track RMS about **−34.6 dBFS** with a 100 ms median around **−41.0 dBFS**. The soundtrack is not literally silent, but the episode supplies no sung Japanese cue before Keke notices something is wrong. [AM/JT]

The evidence supports “the attempt does not become the intended singing,” not a claim about exact throat/vocal physiology.

## 10.2 Keke's relational disclosure → Kanon's decision

Keke's 「最高のライブにしましょう」 interval measures roughly **−24.0 dBFS RMS**. The **3.71 seconds** before Kanon's 「私　歌ってみる」 drop to approximately **−33.0 dBFS whole-window RMS**, with a 100 ms median near **−41.3 dBFS**. [AM]

The scene gives Kanon's choice measurable temporal and acoustic separation.

## 10.3 Bare-name request

After 「かのんでいい」 there is a **1.96-second** interval before 「かのんって呼んでよ」. The 100 ms median mixed-track level is approximately **−65.2 dBFS**, with minima below **−70 dBFS**. [AM]

This is one of the episode's clearest local acoustic withdrawals around relationship language.

## 10.4 Pre-performance support field

After 「見て」, the episode spends roughly **18.23 seconds** before the next spoken reaction 「きれいですぅ」. This interval is not acoustically empty—the RMS remains around **−25.2 dBFS**—so the effect is not silence. It is a **dialogue withdrawal filled by non-dialogue soundtrack/visual spectacle**. [AM/AF]

That distinction matters: the sequence gives the audience-light transformation time to operate without explanatory speech.

## 10.5 「歌える / 一人じゃないから」 → song

Kanon's two lines occur at approximately `18:48.22–18:50.64`. The first indexed `Tiny Stars` lyric begins at `18:53.35`, leaving about **2.71 seconds** after 「一人じゃないから」 before the lyric onset. The mixed track remains active rather than dropping into silence. [AM]

The song therefore emerges from an already energized support environment rather than from an isolated silent declaration.

# 11. Visual and spatial grammar

## 11.1 Being looked at

The episode repeatedly varies the geometry of observation:

- morning singing under sparse public presence;
- takoyaki preparation under customer gaze;
- costume photography and potential internet circulation;
- festival crowd;
- audience transformed into distributed light.

This supports a recurring formal question:

> **What kind of gaze is Kanon inside?**

The episode's answer is not “less gaze is always better.” Sparse public space fails; a full audience eventually becomes enabling.

## 11.2 Costume scene

The costume sequence visually confirms that Kanon can enjoy aesthetic play while feeling exposed by becoming its object. Keke and Chisato cluster around her as enthusiastic evaluators; Kanon's resistance increases when the image is proposed for online circulation.

## 11.3 Keke's disclosure

The scene visually keeps the two girls at close interpersonal scale rather than turning Keke's backstory into detached exposition. Kanon's tearful/self-condemning close-ups are answered by Keke's proximity, reinforcing that the argument is about how one person is permitted to define herself in the eyes of another.

## 11.4 Performance threshold

Before `Tiny Stars`, Kanon and Keke are repeatedly framed touching or extremely close. A retained frame near `18:07.93` places Keke physically leaning against Kanon from behind/side while Kanon faces the stage/audience direction.

The body relationship therefore precedes the verbal 「一人じゃないから」.

## 11.5 Crowd lights

The darkened venue allows the distributed audience lights to dominate depth and color. Kanon and Keke are not made less public; the public is visually transformed into points of support.

## 11.6 Performance framing

`Tiny Stars` repeatedly returns to shared frames and paired geometry. The duet does not erase individual close-ups, but neither does it visually detach Kanon's success from Keke's co-presence.

# 12. Music and sound outside the performance

The strongest episode-local sound finding is not a particular instrument but the **allocation of acoustic space**.

# 13. Counterevidence and alternative readings

## Alternative 1 — “Kanon simply has stage fright and exposure therapy eventually works”

Too broad.

Counterevidence:

- takoyaki exposure under customer gaze succeeds behaviorally but does not restore singing;
- low-population morning conditions no longer protect her once the festival stakes are salient;
- the successful stage event occurs under **maximum public exposure**, not minimum exposure.

A narrower interpretation is better: the block is tied to the meaning of evaluative musical performance, and relational support changes that meaning.

## Alternative 2 — “Keke's praise cures Kanon”

Too simple.

Keke has praised Kanon's singing since S1E01. Praise alone did not prevent relapse. What changes in S1E03 is more specific: Keke refuses Kanon's burden logic, accepts a contingency where Kanon may not sing, and reveals that sharing the stage with Kanon is itself a valued end.

## Alternative 3 — “Kanon overcomes dependence and becomes self-confident”

The episode says almost the opposite.

Kanon's breakthrough line is 「一人じゃないから」. Dependence/co-presence is not depicted as contamination of autonomous achievement. The healthier development is accepting relation as a legitimate condition of action.

## Alternative 4 — “Not winning proves the headmistress/Ren were right”

The first-place condition is indeed not met, so the institutional consequence cannot be ignored. But the newcomer award and successful live make it impossible to infer that the girls had no value or should never have performed.

Competition result is real evidence about ranking, not total evidence about personhood or artistic worth.

## Alternative 5 — “Keke idealizes Kanon so completely that she cannot see her actual weakness”

Possible future pressure, but presently incomplete.

Keke explicitly sees the weakness, changes the plan around it, and offers to sing all vocals herself. Her `スター` language is elevated, but current behavior shows practical contact with Kanon's limitations.

## Alternative 6 — “Chisato secretly wants to join but is simply suppressing it”

Not supported at this boundary.

She says she is interested, but also clearly states that producing results in dance is currently her highest goal and that she lacks capacity to do both seriously. Treat that explanation as authoritative unless later evidence pressures it.

# 14. Cumulative-series deltas through S1E03

## Kanon

**S1E01:** Can name love of singing under relationally supportive, non-evaluative conditions.

> Kanon's singing block is context- and meaning-sensitive. High-stakes evaluation can convert desire into self-judgment, especially when she believes another person's future depends on her performance.
