# Love Live! Superstar!! — S1E07 Deep Reading V2
## 第7話「決戦！生徒会長選」

# 1. Governing thesis

S1E07 moves the series' problem of **belonging and shared agency** from the school-idol group into the governance of Yuigaoka itself.

The episode begins by answering one of S1E06's most concrete open questions. Chisato's `転科届` destination was visually underdetermined in the previous episode. Here it is no longer open. She appears in the ordinary-course uniform and asks Kanon:

> 「どう 普通科の服」

She then explains:

> 「本当は退学して 普通科を受け直そうと思ったんだけど」  
> 「理事長先生が転科を許可してくれるって」

and states the reason for accepting that change:

> 「これからは かのんちゃんたちと同じ目標に向かって頑張りたいと思って」

This is an important behavioral test of S1E06's Chisato revision. The earlier Chisato was prepared to make institutional belonging depend on solitary proof, even to the point of leaving school. S1E07 presents a less self-punishing choice. Rather than insist that the only legitimate route is withdrawal and a fresh entrance examination, Chisato accepts the headmistress's offered course transfer. She remains serious about dance—indeed her first substantive group-role statement is that practice will become **more** intensive—but she no longer treats shared pursuit with Kanon as something she must defer until she has earned it through isolation.

This does **not** establish that the protector/debt rule is permanently cured. The episode does not place Chisato in another direct situation of receiving emotional support under failure. But it supplies the first post-S1E06 behavioral evidence that she can accept a facilitated route into a shared future without immediately invalidating it as dependence.

The transfer also produces an institutional result larger than Chisato herself. Through S1E06 the quartet already existed as a performing formation. S1E07 makes that formation ordinary rather than exceptional. Chisato is in the same course as Kanon, Keke, and Sumire, classmates know that she is becoming a school idol, and she immediately retains specialist authority:

> 「これからは前よりもみっちりダンスの練習するんだから」

Membership therefore does not flatten competence. Chisato becomes an **internal specialist**, not simply one more interchangeable member. Her dance authority survives the loss of music-course status, which further weakens any equation between the institution's prestigious course hierarchy and actual artistic competence.

The episode then creates a second institutional problem: Yuigaoka's first student council. Keke attempts to solve the school-idol conflict by placing Kanon in formal power. She fills out Kanon's candidacy form, creates an account, prepares a sash, and schedules a campaign video before Kanon consents. Kanon's answer is unambiguous:

> 「嫌だ 絶対に出ない」

This extends a recurring distinction in Keke's model. Her commitment to school idols makes her highly effective at initiating action, but it can also cause her to treat a person-specific role as obviously justified by the collective goal before that person has agreed to occupy it. Chisato mediates with `とりあえず話そ`; Sumire eventually becomes the actual challenger to Ren.

The election becomes a test of whether **visibility, popularity, technical performance skill, and political authority are the same thing**. They are not.

Sumire has professional show-business experience, a strong public persona, and a long-standing desire for central recognition. Yet she is not initially enthusiastic about becoming student council president. Once committed, she campaigns through the tools she knows—performance, slogan, self-branding, free takoyaki, `ギャラクシー`—and loses badly. A visual result graph shows an overwhelming Ren advantage and a penalty that pushes Sumire's count below zero; the dialogue explicitly asks `なんでマイナス` and identifies the takoyaki as the reason for the penalty.

The episode refuses to translate this electoral defeat into a total judgment of Sumire. More importantly, **Sumire herself does not do so either**. She protests, collapses comically, and becomes angry, but does not repeat S1E04's center-loss logic that if she cannot occupy the role there is no reason to remain. The stakes are different: president was not her deepest aspiration. This contrast narrows the character model. Sumire's catastrophic recognition wound is not activated by every public defeat; it is especially dangerous when the denied role is one she has made central to her identity.

Kanon and Chisato then make an institutionally mature judgment that would be easy to miss because of the comedy:

> 「でも やっぱり葉月さんが生徒会長でよかったような気がする」  
> 「私も」

They can oppose Ren on school-idol policy while recognizing that she may be better suited to governing the school. **Factional interest does not become a complete theory of competence.** Keke immediately raises the unresolved school-idol problem, but the group does not claim that its own interests automatically entitle it to political control.

That distinction becomes more difficult when Ren's conduct appears to violate her own electoral promise. During the campaign Ren says:

> 「普通科と音楽科が手を取り合う学校を目指し」  
> 「この秋の学園祭を共に盛り上げていくことを約束します」

After winning, she gives her inaugural address. Her declared goal is especially important in Japanese:

> 「この結ヶ丘女子を地域に根ざし」  
> 「途切れることなく続いていく学校にするために」  
> 「誠心誠意努力する所存です」

She wants Yuigaoka to be **rooted in the local community/region** and to continue without interruption. The paired English track materially changes this phrase by rendering it as being rooted in “traditions,” which is not the same claim. The Japanese wording creates a direct thematic bridge to S1E05–06's community-oriented performance model—but S1E07 makes that bridge adversarial. `地域に根ざす` does not guarantee inclusive policy.

At the exact moment Ren converts that general continuity mission into a concrete school-festival decision, her public speech breaks down. She says:

> 「そのために…」

then stops. After an audience prompt, she repeats:

> 「そのために…」

and finally announces:

> 「最初の学園祭は」  
> 「音楽科をメインに行うことと決定しました」

The complete audio gives these interruptions unusual weight. The first gap, from the end of Ren's initial `そのために…` to the audience's `どうしたのでしょう`, lasts about **4.26 s**. Its mixed-track RMS is about **−44.5 dBFS**, the 100 ms median is roughly **−54.6 dBFS**, and approximately **90.5%** of 100 ms blocks fall below −45 dBFS. After the audience prompt ends, a second roughly **4.42 s** gap precedes Ren's repeated `そのために…`; mixed RMS is about **−50.4 dBFS**, with roughly **97.7%** of blocks below −45 dBFS. [AM]

The result is not proof of a specific emotion, but it is strong evidence that the festival decision is acoustically/formally presented as difficult to say. Ren's polished inaugural register is interrupted precisely where continuity rhetoric becomes course-prioritizing policy.

Ordinary-course students immediately interpret the decision as a broken campaign promise. They discuss protest petitions and Sumire proposes recall. Keke rapidly extends the breach into a predicted future school-idol ban. Kanon is worried too, but her response is characteristically different:

> 「それにしても 何か理由がある気がするんだよねえ」

The Japanese is important. `きっと` and `気がする` combine conviction with epistemic limitation. Kanon does not say that she **knows** Ren's motive. Earlier, when she directly confronts Ren, she frames the problem this way:

> 「私たちのことが嫌いとか 意地悪したいってことじゃないんでしょ」  
> 「理由が知りたいんだ」  
> 「それが分かれば 私たちも何か方法がないか考えられるから」

This is a major extension of Kanon's emerging leadership style. She can maintain a substantive normative commitment—`スクールアイドルはやっぱり悪いものじゃない`—while still modeling an opponent as constrained by reasons she does not yet understand. Her goal is not merely to defeat Ren but to discover enough of the hidden structure to negotiate around it.

Ren refuses the invitation to explain:

> 「別に何もありません」  
> 「ただ学校のためには スクールアイドルはない方がいい」  
> 「それがわたくしの考えです」

At this point the S1E02–04 prestige reading remains possible: Ren may believe school idols damage Yuigaoka's musical status. But S1E07 now introduces much heavier pressure against reducing her to elitism.

Immediately before the inaugural speech, Ren asks the headmistress for the number of next year's applicants. She does so not only as student council president but explicitly corrects herself:

> 「生徒会長として…」  
> 「いえ 創立者の娘として」  
> 「知る権利があります」

The episode does not disclose the number in the evidence boundary used here, and it does not explicitly state a causal equation between applications, music-course priority, and money. That link must remain inference.

But the final act reveals facts that radically change the stakes around `学校のため`.

The group follows Ren home. The mansion initially appears to confirm assumptions of extreme wealth. Sumire says she expected Ren to be rich but that the scale exceeds imagination. The comedy of Chibi—the enormous dog initially mistaken for a stuffed animal or taxidermy—then drives the group deeper into the apparently grand but strangely empty house. They encounter an old graduation-style photograph of a school visually resembling Yuigaoka. The photograph strengthens the institutional-inheritance question already opened by the weathered `学校アイドル部` sign, but the exact institution, date, and identities in the photograph remain OPEN at S1E07.

The girls then overhear Ren dismissing the remaining household staff member because she can no longer afford to employ her:

> 「来月からは あなたを雇っておけるお金もないのです」

Ren refuses unpaid labor because it would violate what she believes her deceased mother would accept:

> 「あなたのような人を ただ働きさせたら」  
> 「それこそ亡くなったお母様に怒られてしまいます」

The staff member warns that this will leave Ren truly alone. Ren answers:

> 「平気ですよ」  
> 「わたくしにはチビがいますから」

and remembers a past in which her father was present and her mother was healthy:

> 「あのころはお父様がいて お母様も元気で」  
> 「皆仲が良くて」  
> 「家の中はいつもにぎやかで」

She then insists:

> 「悲観しているのではありませんよ」  
> 「わたくしには まだお母様が残してくれた結ヶ丘がありますから」

When the group is discovered and asks directly, Ren removes the ambiguity:

> 「この家に残っているのはわたくし一人」  
> 「お金もありません」  
> 「このままでは学校を運営していくことも」  
> 「母が残した学校を続けるためには」  
> 「わたくしが頑張るしかないのです」

This is the episode's governing reversal. Ren's enormous inherited house is **not evidence of present financial security**. Visible inherited capital and current usable resources are separated. Likewise, her institutional conservatism can no longer safely be modeled as prestige protection detached from survival. Yuigaoka is the remaining institution left by her mother, and Ren understands herself as responsible for keeping it alive.

The episode does **not** yet tell us whether her chosen methods are correct. It does not prove that prioritizing the music course will increase applications, preserve funding, or save the school. It does not excuse campaign-promise violation. It does not establish that school idols actually threaten continuity. What it does establish is that Ren's own perceived stakes are existential rather than merely aesthetic:

> **for Ren, Yuigaoka's continuation is bound to bereavement, family inheritance, financial precarity, and the belief that she is the person who must carry the institution forward.**

This produces a striking parallel with Chisato.

S1E06 exposed Chisato's rule:

> I must become strong enough **by myself** before I can deserve to stand beside Kanon.

S1E07 ends with Ren's rule:

> 「わたくしが頑張るしかないのです」

> I am the one who has no choice but to keep trying.

The two are not identical, but the structural rhyme is important. Both girls convert attachment into solitary responsibility. Chisato has begun revising that rule through reciprocity and accepted institutional assistance. Ren has not yet reached an equivalent revision at this boundary.

The community-performance question therefore does survive S1E07, but in a more difficult form. S1E05–06 showed a non-ranked island performance organized around supporting a community. S1E07 gives Ren the explicitly community-rooted phrase `地域に根ざし`, yet her attempt to preserve the community's school produces internal exclusion pressure. The episode thus adds a necessary ethical distinction:

> **acting “for the school” or “for the community” does not itself establish that a policy is inclusive, proportionate, or correct. Purpose and method must be evaluated separately.**

At the S1E07 boundary, the strongest prospective thesis is:

> **S1E07 institutionalizes the series' reciprocity problem. Chisato enters the quartet by accepting both a shared goal and an institutionally assisted course transfer while retaining internal specialist authority; Sumire's electoral defeat separates public performance skill from governance legitimacy; Kanon increasingly leads by holding conviction together with epistemic charity; and Ren's apparent course elitism is pressured into a much more serious survival model grounded in bereavement, financial precarity, and inherited responsibility. The episode does not vindicate Ren's policies. It reveals that the conflict cannot be solved by treating her as a simple antagonist, because both sides now claim to act for Yuigaoka while possessing different models of what the school's continuation requires.**

# 3. Sequential dramatic architecture

## Movement I — S1E06 is recapped as completed but not simplified (`00:00–00:31`)

The recap states three things as accomplished facts inside S1E07:

- Chisato won the dance competition;
- the island live succeeded;
- Chisato became a school-idol member.

The recap retains the earlier `退` / Chisato-dance material, reminding the viewer that the membership result emerged through the self-worth conflict rather than replacing it.

**Sequential effect:** S1E07 does not reopen whether Chisato is functionally part of the group. It tests what membership looks like institutionally and behaviorally.

## Movement II — Chisato enters ordinary-course life without surrendering specialist identity (`02:55–04:03`)

Kanon and Keke return from the island exhausted. Chisato's first response is not indulgence:

> 「これからは前よりもみっちりダンスの練習するんだから」

Then she reveals the ordinary-course uniform and explains the transfer.

The sequence answers two questions at once:

1. Chisato's destination course is now **ordinary course**, no longer OPEN.
2. her dance-specialist role does not depend on music-course membership.

Her explanation that she had intended to withdraw and retake the ordinary-course entrance exam, but accepted the headmistress's transfer permission, is the first direct post-S1E06 evidence that she can receive institutional facilitation rather than insist on a maximally solitary route.

Keke's `愛デス` interprets the change with characteristic maximalism. Chisato answers:

> 「そんな大したことじゃないけど」

That mismatch is useful. Chisato can make a structurally major commitment while linguistically minimizing its magnitude.

## Movement III — Yuigaoka creates a governance institution, and Keke assigns Kanon a role without consent (`04:09–06:08`)

The school announces formation of its delayed first-year student council and a presidential election if multiple candidates apply.

Keke immediately writes Kanon's name on a candidacy form, creates an account, plans a campaign video, and tries to equip her with a sash.

Kanon's resistance escalates to locking herself away:

> 「嫌だ 絶対に出ない」

Keke justifies the intervention through group interest:

> 「スクールアイドルのためデス」

The argument is not frivolous. Ren is the sole declared candidate and has repeatedly opposed school idols. But the episode makes the consent problem visible through Kanon's categorical refusal.

Chisato's response is mediation rather than automatic alignment with Keke:

> 「とりあえず話そ」

The same Chisato who has just been incorporated into the group therefore does not immediately become a bloc member who treats all school-idol strategy as overriding individual agency.

## Movement IV — Sumire accepts a role she does not deeply want (`06:08–07:09`)

Sumire initially rises to the prospect of using her show-business skill, while the others teasingly destabilize her persona and even pretend to forget her name. She says explicitly:

> 「生徒会長選挙と聞いて 正直それほど気は進まないけれど」

Keke immediately dismisses her. Kanon instead thanks her and promises full support.

This distinction matters for Sumire. She enters the election less as fulfillment of a core personal dream than as group service plus a possible recognition opportunity. That lowers the identity stakes relative to S1E04 center selection.

## Movement V — Ren treats electoral blocs less cynically than the opposition does (`07:09–09:31`)

Ren learns that an ordinary-course challenger exists. A peer worries that ordinary-course numerical superiority makes the election dangerous. Ren answers:

> 「必ずしも普通科の生徒が普通科の候補者を応援するとは限りません」  
> 「頑張るしかないです」

This is significant counterevidence to a crude “Ren sees everything through course caste” model. She does not assume automatic music-course loyalty or ordinary-course hostility.

The group, by contrast, immediately starts treating the ordinary-course numerical advantage as an electoral bloc opportunity. They also hear the rumor that Ren looks down on ordinary-course students.

Ren counters that rumor publicly by promising course cooperation and a shared school festival.

## Movement VI — Keke converts policy threat into theatrical authoritarian fantasy (`09:42–10:34`)

Keke imagines Ren as an authoritarian president invoking absurdly specific rules and ordering the school idols arrested. The fantasy includes:

> 「校則第10条325項」  
> 「結ヶ丘におけるスクールアイドルの活動は この一切を禁止する」  
> 「ひっ捕らえなさい」

She later says Ren's eyes are those of someone who hates school idols.

This is not reliable evidence about Ren. It is high-value evidence about **Keke under institutional threat**: uncertainty can be filled by vivid worst-case narrative, especially when school-idol continuation is at stake.

## Movement VII — Sumire's show-business campaign collides with election rules (`10:34–11:30`)

Sumire distributes free takoyaki as part of campaign greetings. Kanon and Chisato immediately question whether this is acceptable:

> 「でも これはよろしくないのでは」  
> 「どうだろう ギリギリセーフ？」

The answer is institutional rather than interpretive: it is not safe. Sumire receives a penalty.

Her result is a crushing defeat. The graph visually shows Ren overwhelmingly ahead and Sumire's penalized result below zero. Sumire responds with anger and disbelief rather than existential withdrawal.

The scene therefore distinguishes:

- showmanship from governance credibility;
- attention from votes;
- public defeat from identity collapse.

## Movement VIII — The group respects governance competence even when it threatens its interests (`11:30–12:03`)

Kanon says she feels Ren was probably the better president. Chisato agrees.

This is one of S1E07's strongest institutional-ethics moments. The quartet does not argue that school idols deserve to capture the presidency because they are protagonists or because Ren opposes them.

Keke correctly identifies that policy conflict remains. Kanon chooses direct conversation rather than immediate factional escalation.

## Movement IX — Ren's founder-daughter identity precedes her policy break (`12:03–12:31`)

Before Kanon reaches Ren, Ren asks the headmistress for next year's applicant count.

She begins:

> 「生徒会長として…」

then corrects herself:

> 「いえ 創立者の娘として」  
> 「知る権利があります」

The self-correction matters. Ren's deepest perceived authority/responsibility does not come solely from the office she has just won. It comes from inherited relation to the school's creation.

The episode withholds the number and the headmistress's substantive response. Do not fabricate them.

## Movement X — Kanon seeks reasons without surrendering her own judgment (`12:31–13:30`)

Kanon reminds Ren that she previously said she could support their efforts if they were not school idols. Kanon therefore rejects the simplest motive model—personal hatred or desire to bully them—and asks for the reason.

Her proposed logic is explicitly compromise-oriented:

> 「それが分かれば 私たちも何か方法がないか考えられるから」

She then states her own normative position:

> 「スクールアイドルはやっぱり悪いものじゃないって思うから」

Kanon is neither neutral nor submissive. She holds a judgment while leaving the opponent's internal model open to revision.

Ren gives only the school-centered conclusion:

> 「学校のためには スクールアイドルはない方がいい」

The causal mechanism remains withheld.

## Movement XI — Ren's inaugural speech fractures at the moment of exclusionary prioritization (`13:38–14:34`)

Ren speaks in highly formal institutional language about honor, responsibility, regional rootedness, and continuity.

Then:

> 「そのために…」

The first long low-energy pause follows. An audience member asks what is wrong. Ren repeats:

> 「そのために…」

A second long low-energy pause follows. She then announces that the first school festival will be conducted with the music course as its main focus.

The formal structure is precise:

`community-rooted continuity mission` → **speech interruption** → `music-course-centered policy`.

This does not tell us the exact thought inside the interruption. It does show the episode marking the transition as difficult.

## Movement XII — Democratic legitimacy becomes part of the school-idol conflict (`14:34–16:01`)

Ordinary-course students call Ren's festival decision a total campaign-promise violation. A petition begins. Sumire proposes recall.

The group briefly revives the fantasy of Kanon as president. Kanon again resists, including:

> 「私なんて全然目立たないし」

Given Kanon's established center role, public performance, and prior center vote, the sentence cannot be treated as an objective social fact. Sumire immediately mocks it as the kind of statement that makes people dislike someone, and Kanon insists she did not mean it that way.

The exchange suggests that Kanon's self-model still does not fully track her actual visibility, but the scene is comic and insufficient to establish a new pathology.

More important is what follows:

> 「何か理由がある気がするんだよねえ」

### Kanon refuses formal authority imposed without consent

Her school-idol center role does not make her hungry for generalized leadership titles. Keke's attempt to make her president produces categorical refusal.

This protects an important distinction:

This is systems-oriented reasoning: understand constraints before designing a solution.

Kanon's later suspicion that Ren must have a reason is strong but qualified. In a novel situation where another person's action conflicts with Kanon's established evidence of their values, a high-confidence Kanon response should often include:

1. discomfort or disagreement;
2. search for omitted context;
3. direct request for reasons if possible;
4. attempt to preserve the other person's agency while still defending her own norm.

`私なんて全然目立たないし` is plainly inconsistent with the public evidence available to the audience and characters. Sumire calls out how irritating such a claim can sound.

The Kanon candidacy sequence strongly extends the recruitment-overdrive rule. Keke does not merely suggest president; she pre-fills administrative steps and plans publicity.
