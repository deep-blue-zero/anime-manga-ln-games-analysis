# Love Live! Superstar!! — S1E09 Deep Reading V2
## 第9話「What's a name?」

# 1. Governing thesis

S1E09 converts the five-person formation from a **functional coalition** into a **self-describing collective**, but it does so by discovering that the group has no single pre-existing essence to uncover.

The episode begins with Love Live! forcing an external identity problem. Entry requires more than five capable individuals standing together. The group needs a new song, a public presence, a name, and some answer to the question of what distinguishes them from the rapidly expanding field of school idols.

The episode initially distributes value very clearly across the five members. Chisato names Kanon's singing, her own competition-winning dance, Ren's figure-skating and piano experience, Sumire's long stage history, and Keke's unusually intense school-idol commitment. This is a direct counterexample to any model in which Kanon is the only meaningful source of competence. Yet the members cannot immediately convert those differentiated strengths into a shared identity.

That failure appears repeatedly:

- they have operated as `結ヶ丘スクールアイドル` without an actual group name;
- their first naming attempts simply aggregate quirks or initials (`チクレカス`, `まるまるサークル`, `ギャラクシー`);
- an external observer says they still feel `真っ白` and lack a clear image;
- Kanon cannot write a song intended to represent both **the group and the school** because she cannot yet say what the group is;
- Ren cannot begin composing because she expects the lyrics first, while Kanon expects the melody first;
- the group apologizes for relying too heavily on Kanon even as it continues to expect her to solve the representational problem.

The breakthrough therefore is not discovery of a hidden fixed trait such as “we are the strongest,” “we are ordinary girls,” or “we are defined by Kanon.” Kanon's answer is almost the opposite:

> 「それはまだ何色でもない」  
> 「私たちだからできること」  
> 「始まったばかりのこの学校だからできること」

The group and Yuigaoka are valuable partly because they are **not yet fixed into one color**.

This lets S1E09 preserve difference instead of solving difference through sameness. Kanon explains the new name `Liella!` as something she made from a French word meaning `結ぶ`, linking it to Yuigaoka's inherited `結` architecture. But the object being connected has changed. S1E08 recovered the founding wish that a school be joined through music; S1E09 applies the same logic to the five members as **different colors of light** whose connection can produce colors none of them could imagine alone.

The visual form supports the argument. After outsiders call the group `真っ白`, Kanon leaves the collective practice space and moves alone through an intensely colored city. The retained frames shift through pink, amber, purple, neon, and evening light before Kanon returns home and consults a visibly labeled French dictionary. When the name is accepted, a large hand-painted `Liella!` banner renders the letters in different colors. The group does not become visually monochrome when it acquires a name; the name becomes a container for plurality.

S1E09 therefore produces an important full-series hypothesis at the current boundary:

> **Liella! is not yet best understood as a group whose members possess one shared defining trait. Its first explicit identity is a relation among heterogeneous people who have only recently connected, are still changing, and can generate new forms precisely because neither the group nor its school is finished.**

This does not eliminate hierarchy or specialization. Sumire's desire for a massive spotlight immediately reappears when she sees the Love Live! final venue. Chisato remains the dance authority. Ren becomes the registrar, quality-control voice, and composer. Keke remains the competition evangelist and external-mobilization engine. Kanon remains the principal semantic integrator and lyricist. The episode's solution is therefore **distributed function + relational identity**, not interchangeability.

It also directly tests S1E08's Ren revision. Ren does not revert to `わたくしが頑張るしかない`. She registers the group for Love Live!, accepts composition work, participates in naming and promotion, submits to Chisato's training, calls Kanon out when Kanon attempts to remain safely behind the camera, and finally performs the application action as one member among others. Her responsibility remains strong, but it is now exercised **inside shared work** rather than through solitary institutional control.

At the same time, ordinary interaction shows that social intimacy does not flatten Ren's voice. Other members comfortably call her `恋ちゃん`; S1E08's Keke-specific `レンレン` is **not re-attested** in S1E09 and therefore should not yet be promoted to Keke's universal default. Ren herself continues to use polite forms such as `かのんさん`, formal self-introduction, and `わたくし`. Membership changes pragmatic function more rapidly than register.

Finally, S1E09 itself authorizes the collective name. The group proposes, explains, accepts, paints, publicly announces, and enters under **`Liella!`**. This is therefore the first V2 boundary at which `Liella!` may be treated as the diegetic group identity rather than paratext or future knowledge.

## 2.2 Dual-subtitle status

Corrected Japanese remains the governing textual source. English remains a comparison/retrieval lane.

S1E09 again demonstrates why this matters:

1. Keke's `そんな簡単にここに立てると思うなですよ / このすっとこどっこい` is flattened into an ordinary insult in English. The Japanese preserves the comic collision between rough lexical content and Keke's stylized `ですよ` register.
2. `まずぃあまどぇす（マジ甘です）` is represented orthographically in Japanese as an exaggerated/stylized utterance. English normalizes it to “too naive.” This is usable as textual voice evidence but **not** license to invent phonetic characteristics beyond the attested written representation.

5. `それはまだ何色でもない` is translated with a “blank canvas” metaphor. The translation is thematically compatible with the earlier `真っ白`, but the Japanese at the naming climax specifically returns to **color** (`何色でもない`), which matters because the surrounding explanation explicitly concerns differently colored light.

# 3. Sequential dramatic architecture

## Movement I — Love Live! converts aspiration into a competition system (`02:47–04:14`)

Keke announces that Love Live! entry has opened and immediately frames it at maximal scale: `夢`, `魂`, and `命の源`. Chisato initially translates it into the familiar analogy of a national tournament, but Keke rejects the reduction and explains that Love Live! can produce fame, applicant growth, and even rescue schools from closure danger. [JT]

This matters immediately after S1E08. The series has just insisted that school-idol value cannot be reduced to institutional rescue, yet S1E09 does not abandon instrumental effects. Love Live! **can** affect enrollment and institutional visibility. The correction is not “instrumental outcomes are fake”; it is “instrumental outcomes are not the whole value structure.” [IT]

The enormous final venue then reactivates Sumire's core ambition almost instantly:

> 「ここで私が何万人もの注目を浴びて」

followed by `アリーナ / ギャラクシー！`. [JT]

This is strong recurrence for S1E08's stage-conditioned center model. Sumire can defer center on a local festival stage while still becoming intensely activated by an arena-scale fantasy of mass attention. [SI]

## Movement II — course hierarchy loosens visually without disappearing institutionally (`04:17–04:52`)

The others notice Ren in the ordinary-course uniform. Sumire initially thinks Ren might be transferring as Chisato did, but Ren explains that the headmistress has proposed allowing students to select either uniform rather than using clothing to distinguish course membership. [JT]

Chisato immediately jokes about obtaining the music-course uniform, and Kanon proposes they all try it together. The significance is modest but real: **course identity no longer has to be permanently inscribed on the body through uniform difference**. [AF/SI]

This does not erase the ordinary/music-course structure. It alters its visible enforcement.

## Movement III — the five-person competence map is explicit (`04:55–06:08`)

Keke warns that Love Live! has become increasingly difficult as participation grows. Chisato responds with the episode's clearest compact competence inventory:

- Kanon: outstanding singing;
- Chisato: dance at competition-winning level;
- Ren: figure skating and piano;
- Sumire: extensive childhood stage experience;
- Keke: unusually intense school-idol commitment as a weapon.

Chisato still refuses certainty about victory: `優勝は分からないけど`. [JT]

The formation is therefore neither “five ordinary interchangeable girls” nor “Kanon plus support.” Different capabilities are explicit before the group receives a name.

Ren then behaves as a co-bearer:

> 「とりあえず エントリーはしますが」

and, when assigned composition:

> 「わたくしが作曲ですか」  
> 「まあ できないことはないと思いますが」

She accepts operational responsibility while still setting a high performance threshold. [JT]

## Movement IV — the absence of a name exposes the absence of a shared self-description (`06:13–08:16`)

A literal `結ヶ丘スクールアイドル部` label triggers the obvious question:

> 「グループ名？」

The members realize they never established one. Early functioning had been sufficient under generic references such as `結ヶ丘スクールアイドル`; Keke had once tried `クーカー`, but the five-person structure now makes that inadequate. [JT]

The brainstorm is revealing precisely because every proposal overfits a member quirk:

- Keke mechanically combines initials into `チクレカス`;
- Chisato offers `まるまるサークル` and launches into her circular ontology;
- Kanon suggests the descriptive `結ヶ丘スクールアイドル`;
- Sumire reaches for `ギャラクシー`.

None explains the **relation among the five**.

Ren contributes little because she says she does not understand the purpose well enough. That is useful modeling evidence: when a social-symbolic task lacks clear rules, Ren's confidence drops even though she is highly competent in formal institutional tasks. [JT/SI]

## Movement V — public indifference is first misread as a legacy problem (`08:26–09:26`)

Following school-idol precedent, the group requests outside name suggestions. The box remains empty.

Ren immediately produces a guilt-oriented explanation:

> 「もしかしたら わたくしがずっと反対していたからかもしれません」

Kanon/Chisato reject that account, noting that students saw Ren's position at the festival. [JT]

This is a small but important S1E08 aftereffect. Ren's guilt remains available as an explanatory default even after joining. The group does not allow her to make herself the cause of every collective problem.

Sunny Passion then explains that their own fans supplied their name and that they first built recognition through videos. This redirects the problem from **Ren's past wrongdoing** to **the group's weak public legibility**.

## Movement VI — Ren's formal competence meets digital unfamiliarity (`09:26–11:08`)

The group realizes that school-idol work includes more than singing and dancing. Ren then asks:

> 「動画配信とは何なのですか」  
> 「動画を配るのですか どこかに」

She explains that she had been told not to approach such things. [JT]

During the trial stream, Ren defaults to her most institutionally legible identity:

> 「わたくし結ヶ丘女子高等学校の生徒会長をしております」  
> 「葉月恋と申します」

She begins explaining her school-idol/Love Live! status, then stops amid incoming audience comments. When she discovers the stream was started without adequate explanation, her response is procedural and direct:

> 「こんなの断りもなく始めないでください」  
> 「すぐ切ってください」

This is not a regression to anti-group authority. It is Ren applying a consent/preparation standard inside the group. [JT]

## Movement VII — Ren reverses the accountability direction and pulls Kanon into exposure (`11:08–12:25`)

For the planned stream, the group prepares what to say. Kanon tries to remain behind the camera.

Ren notices immediately:

> 「かのんさん あなたは」  
> 「映らないのですか」

When Kanon says she is filming, Ren replies:

> 「ずるいですよ」  
> 「わたくしたちだけに押しつけて」

This is one of S1E09's most useful relationship-state reversals. In S1E07–08 Kanon repeatedly pushed Ren toward disclosure/shared action. Now Ren is comfortable enough to tell Kanon that **she** is offloading an unpleasant collective responsibility. [JT/SI]

Kanon participates, but soon says the stream is embarrassing and wants to stop. Her public-performance history therefore should not be flattened into generic comfort with visibility. [JT]

The stream receives engagement largely through comic member-specific behavior—Chisato's `まる`, Sumire's `グソクムシ`—rather than a clear collective brand. Public attention increases without yet solving identity.

## Movement VIII — naming/promotion consume practice time and expose coordination failure (`12:26–13:42`)

Ren complains that the day's naming and video work prevented practice. This is not mere rigidity: Love Live! imposes multiple simultaneous demands, and the group lacks process maturity.

The most direct example comes in songwriting. Ren expects Kanon's lyrics before composing; Kanon expects Ren's melody before writing:

> Ren: 「わたくしは詞が出来たらと思ってましたけど」  
> Kanon: 「うそ 私は曲が出来たらそれに合わせて書こうと」

The five-person formation has gained more expertise but not automatic workflow coordination. Shared membership does not imply shared assumptions. [JT]

Chisato responds to the absence of song/choreography by returning everyone to fundamentals. Her specialist authority remains intact.

## Movement IX — the representational task blocks Kanon (`13:49–15:16`)

Kanon admits the problem:

> 「このグループと学校を代表するような曲って言われると」  
> 「なかなか…」

This directly generalizes S1E05's creative block around Chisato. When Kanon cannot honestly conceptualize the thing she is supposed to translate into form, writing stalls. [FR/SI]

Ren offers a deceptively simple instruction:

> 「この5人を見て感じたことを そのまま歌にすればよいのですよ」

But none of the five can answer the resulting question cleanly. Keke rejects `最強`, `最高`, and `エクセレント`; Sumire says `ギャラクシー`; Chisato says `まる`; Kanon says they are `バラバラ` and did not originally assemble around one shared goal. [JT]

Keke's solution is to confine Kanon until the task is finished. Kanon explicitly protests that the work is being pushed onto her. The comedy therefore contains a real organizational asymmetry.

## Movement X — the group acknowledges overreliance but has not yet solved it (`15:36–18:20`)

Kanon eventually escapes the confinement condition by claiming she has an idea, then later admits she lied because otherwise she could not go home.

At home she tells family:

> 「みんな私に頼りすぎなんだよ」

This is a valuable contrast with S1E03. Perceived burden no longer automatically becomes “I should remove myself because I harm everyone.” Kanon can instead identify **others' dependence on her as excessive**. [FR]

The next day outside observers provide the formulation the group lacked:

> 「あなたたちってこれっていうイメージないもの」  
> 「まだ真っ白っていうのかなあ」

They further say the five are neither a collection of exceptional superstar talents nor an obvious “ordinary girls overcoming a single goal” type. Sumire calls this fatal in show-business terms and Keke says such weakness will not survive the current school-idol environment. [JT]

Kanon's response is only:

> 「そうかな」

The audio then gives her a pronounced low-energy processing interval before Chisato calls her name. [AM]

Kanon skips practice because something about the criticism has generated an idea.

## Movement XI — Kanon turns absence of a fixed image into generative possibility (`18:24–20:26`)

Kanon cannot initially say whether the idea concerns lyrics or the name:

> 「分からないけど」

After a measured low-energy interval she continues:

> 「でも」  
> 「ぜーんぶ」

and finally:

> 「何かこの5人が何なのか分かった気がした」

The episode then overlays the insert-song lane while the sentence itself is still in progress. Formal resolution arrives as movement/music, not as an abstract definition delivered first and illustrated later. [MF/AM]

The song's governing motion is exploratory: walking alone produces things one wants to try; possibility provokes `できるかな？ 大丈夫だよ！`; the city feels like something may happen; the speaker wants to begin today even before language is fully adequate; connection among everyone makes genuinely interesting things possible. [JT/MF]

Visually, Kanon moves from the relatively muted school problem into saturated urban color, then returns home and visibly consults a French dictionary. [AF]

When she comes back, Ren says the resulting lyrics represent `今のわたくしたち` very well. The group apologizes for relying on Kanon, while Kanon apologizes for taking time. This is repair, not proof that the workload is now fully distributed.

## Movement XII — `Liella!` names connection without fixing the group's color (`20:27–21:28`)

Kanon announces the name:

> 「リエラですか」  
> 「フランス語で『結ぶ』って意味の言葉から作ってみたの」

The episode itself therefore supplies the name and its in-story etymological explanation. No future evidence is required. [JT]

Kanon connects the name to Ren's mother's founding idea behind Yuigaoka and then develops the group-specific extension:

> 「スクールアイドルを通して」  
> 「いろんな色の光で結ばれていくといいなあって思ったんだ」

Connected colors can become colors the members themselves could not imagine. Crucially:

> 「それはまだ何色でもない」  
> 「私たちだからできること」  
> 「始まったばかりのこの学校だからできること」

Identity is thus **relational and prospective**. `Liella!` says how difference can connect, not what every member must already be. [IT]

Sumire's eventual `悪くないんじゃない` accepts a collective symbol that does not foreground `ギャラクシー` or her individual brand.

## Movement XIII — a name immediately becomes a public/material identity (`21:39–22:08`)

The episode does not leave `Liella!` as a private concept. A large multicolored banner appears on the school exterior and the group announces:

> 「結ヶ丘女子スクールアイドル Liella!でーす」

The letterforms are visibly painted in different colors. [AF/JT]

Kanon complains the signage is too large; the response is that they need to become more famous. Ren adds:

> 「勝たないといけませんね」

Her quality/competition orientation therefore survives incorporation. The difference is structural: she now applies it from **inside** the group rather than as an external gatekeeper. [FR]

Ren then executes the Love Live! entry action:

> 「それでは皆さん エントリーしますよ」

and the five look together toward the device as `私たちの名は` begins the transition into the ending. [AF/JT]

# 4. Character development

## 4.1 Shibuya Kanon — semantic integrator under pressure, not all-purpose leader

S1E09 strengthens Kanon's role as the person most likely to synthesize heterogeneous emotional/social material into language and song. Keke explicitly says `このグループを一番理解しているのはかのん`, and the final name/lyrics do in fact come from Kanon.

But the episode makes that role uncomfortable rather than simply celebratory.

Kanon is assigned lyrics because she has prior creative competence. She then becomes the bottleneck around which everyone waits. Ren and Kanon fail to coordinate whether melody or lyrics come first. Keke confines her until the task is done. At home Kanon says everyone relies on her too much.

This is important for later modeling: **Kanon's leadership should not be simulated as effortless desire to own every central task**. She can resent excessive delegation, seek escape, lie to terminate an unreasonable confinement condition, and still return to the task when she finds a formulation she believes in.

### Her creative process remains truth-sensitive

S1E05 established that Kanon could not easily write Chisato into a simple role category because the relationship exceeded `member` / `coach`. S1E09 generalizes the same mechanism to the entire formation.

The command “write a song representing this group and school” blocks her because she does not yet know what the group is. Once the answer becomes **we are still undefined and connected difference is itself the possibility**, writing unlocks.

The best current rule is:

> **representational task + category that feels falsely simplified → creative block; discovery of a formulation that preserves the relevant complexity → intense synthesis and renewed production.**

### Public visibility remains context-conditioned

Kanon can speak publicly and perform under some conditions, but she attempts to stay behind the camera during the livestream and soon says the exposure is embarrassing.

This is not a return to S1E01. She participates after being challenged. But it remains strong negative evidence against “Kanon has become generally comfortable being watched.”

### External criticism can trigger exploration instead of defensive rebuttal

When outsiders say the group is `真っ白` and lacks a strong image, Kanon does not argue. Her `そうかな` is followed by a low-energy interval and then a decision to leave practice because the criticism is producing an idea.

The episode formalizes thinking as locomotion: she walks alone through the city, notices color/light, consults language resources at home, and returns with a relational concept. This is a strong candidate Kanon cognitive pattern: **movement through ordinary space can support creative integration when abstract group discussion has stalled**. Recurrence is still needed before treating it as stable habit.

## 4.2 Hazuki Ren — co-bearer confirmed through mundane work

S1E08's most important open question was whether Ren's shift from sole guardian to co-bearer would survive outside the reconciliation climax.

S1E09 answers **yes, behaviorally**.

Ren:

- handles the Love Live! entry process;
- accepts composition responsibility;
- maintains quality expectations;
- joins brainstorming even when unsure how;
- participates in external promotion;
- accepts Chisato's physical training regime;
- contributes to group accountability;
- performs the final entry action.

There is no return to unilateral school-level control.

### Quality orientation is preserved rather than erased

At the beginning Ren says progression requires an excellent song and overwhelming performance. At the end, after the name is publicly displayed, she says `勝たないといけませんね`.

The earlier Ren was not wrong to care about quality simply because her maternal-regret inference was wrong. V2.2 should preserve the distinction:

> **prestige/quality orientation = stable candidate disposition/institutional value; exclusionary policy built from inferred maternal regret = revised.**

### Formal speech survives intimacy

Present-time direct address to Kanon is `かのんさん`. Ren introduces herself online with the ceremonial structure one would expect from a student-council president, even though the task is casual school-idol promotion. Membership changes whom she works with before it changes the syntax of how she presents herself.

### Digital unfamiliarity reveals a domain-specific novice state

Ren does not know what livestreaming is and had been discouraged from approaching such media. Her confusion is not generalized stupidity. Once the mechanism is explained, she participates; her strongest objection concerns insufficient consent/preparation.

This creates a useful simulation rule: **new informal/digital social technology can expose uncertainty beneath Ren's broad institutional competence; when uncertain, she tends to ask literal definitional questions and restore procedure.**

### Ren can now hold Kanon accountable

`ずるいですよ / わたくしたちだけに押しつけて` is relationally important. Ren is no longer only the person being rescued from overresponsibility. She can identify when Kanon is trying to escape a shared unpleasant task and insist on equitable participation.

That is healthier evidence of co-bearing than mere gratitude would have been.

## 4.3 Arashi Chisato — internal specialist plus concrete ensemble evaluator

Chisato remains the dance authority. When song/choreography are incomplete, she defaults the group to fundamentals and directs the physical regimen.

But S1E09 adds another form of expertise: she can inventory the five members without collapsing them into a hierarchy. Her assessment of their chances is grounded in specific competencies and still qualified by `優勝は分からないけど`.

This supports a Chisato mode of **evidence-based encouragement**: identify what each person can actually do, avoid false guarantees, and translate uncertainty into trainable work.
