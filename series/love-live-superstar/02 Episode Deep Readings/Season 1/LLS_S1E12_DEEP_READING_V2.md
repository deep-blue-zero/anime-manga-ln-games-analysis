# Love Live! Superstar!! — S1E12 Deep Reading V2
## Season 1 finale — `Song for All`

# 1. Governing thesis

S1E12 asks the question that Season 1 has repeatedly postponed: **what does winning add to a project whose value has already been established without it?**

The episode is unusually careful not to answer by making competition retroactively sovereign. Before the Tokyo event, Liella! has already produced effects that no ranking can take away. Yuigaoka's applicant numbers have risen enough that the headmistress can state that the school will continue into the following year. Ren's father additionally offers the money the school needs. Students describe Liella! as `この学校の希望`, and the final competition stage exists only because students and neighborhood participants jointly build a route and performance space for them. [JT/AF]

The first major result of the episode is therefore institutional and relational **before** the Love Live! result is known:

> Liella! has already mattered.

That fact is essential because Liella! then loses.

Sunny Passion is selected as Tokyo's representative for the national competition; the retained results display places Liella! second. The performance is not treated as a technical or emotional failure. Kanon's first response to the people who supported them is `ごめんね　勝たせてあげられなくて`, while the immediate reply is `ありがとう　最高のステージだったよ / 私　すごく誇らしくて　感動した`. [JT/AF]

The episode thus repeats and sharpens a distinction present since S1E03:

- artistic/performance success;
- relational success;
- institutional/community consequence;
- competitive ranking

are real but non-identical dimensions.

What changes is not that Kanon finally learns ranking is the only true measure. What changes is that she finally **wants the ranking as one form of reciprocal action**.

Before the event, Kanon tells Sunny Passion:

> `私は歌で勝ったり負けたりって　あんまり`
>
> `いい歌を歌いたいって気持ちはあるんです`
>
> `みんなとたくさん練習して　最高のライブを目指したい`
>
> `でも私　ずっと歌えないかもって不安があったから`
>
> `自由に表現できるだけでもう…`
>
> `本当は　それだけで幸せで`

This is not apathy. It is a historically intelligible value structure. For someone whose Season-1 starting problem was whether she could sing at all, **being able to sing freely with other people is already an achieved good**. [JT/FR]

Sunny Passion does not tell her she is morally wrong. They concede that song may not fundamentally be something one competes over and that one can enjoy it alone. Their narrower claim is developmental:

> `競い合うことで　より高め合うことができる`

and experiential:

> `ラブライブ！で歌えば　すぐ気付くはずよ`
>
> `なぜみんな勝ちたいか`
>
> `いや　勝たなきゃって思うのか`

Kanon does not immediately accept the conclusion. Her answer is `まだそこまで気持ちが…`. [JT]

The episode then gives her the experience instead of the lecture.

Yuigaoka's students take over stage construction so Liella! can practice. They explain that because they are all first-years and the other clubs cannot yet make deep tournament runs, Liella!'s progress has become a shared school hope. The neighborhood then cooperates in giving them a larger illuminated public space. Immediately before performing, Kanon says:

> `私は胸を張って言えます`
>
> `結ヶ丘の生徒になれてよかったって`
>
> `この学校が一番だって`

This `一番` is crucially different from the prestige logic that drove early Ren. Kanon does not establish Yuigaoka as “best” by excluding lower-performing activity or by proving objective superiority. She calls it best after experiencing the school as a community that chooses to make space for them. **Belonging precedes ranking.** [JT/SI]

The stage itself makes the same argument visually. It is not an isolated professional venue secured by Liella!'s status. The five are led through a path of classmates holding lights; the route opens into a neighborhood-built star stage; the performance surface uses five differently colored lanes; the girls appear in differentiated member colors while repeatedly returning to whole-group formations. The visual grammar operationalizes S1E09's explanation of Liella!: differently colored light becomes connected without losing difference. [AF/FR]

The insert performance intensifies the reciprocity model rather than erasing it. Its Japanese text includes:

> `出会いがくれた　大きな勇気`
>
> `始まりは君から`
>
> `でも次は僕から`
>
> `いま動き始めた僕らの物語`
>
> `想いを歌にして伝えよう`

The song's dramatic action is therefore not “we deserve first place.” It stages a chain of initiative: something begins from another person, then responsibility moves back toward the speaker/collective; encounter creates courage; feeling becomes transmissible through song. [JT/MF/IT]

This is why defeat can produce a new desire without invalidating the old one.

After the result, Kanon first processes the loss through failed reciprocity:

> `私　悔しい`
>
> `せっかくみんなが協力してくれたのに`
>
> `何もお返しできなかった`
>
> `みんなが協力してくれたのに`
>
> `何も返せず　おしまいになっちゃった`

The wording is not “second place proves our song was bad.” It is `お返し` / `返す`: giving back. [JT]

Keke and the others answer future-ward:

> `また全力で挑みましょう`
>
> `そうです`

Kanon then reaches the episode's central transition:

> `勝ちたい`
>
> `私　勝ちたい`
>
> `勝って　ここにいるみんなを笑顔にしたい`
>
> `やったって　みんなで喜びたい`
>
> `私たちの歌で　Liella! の歌で`
>
> `結ヶ丘の歌で優勝したい`

Then she corrects herself:

> `いや`
>
> `優勝しよう`

The grammatical movement matters. The first form articulates an internal desire. The final form converts that desire into collective commitment. S1E12 therefore does not merely add “competitive ambition” to Kanon's traits. It establishes the following mechanism:

> **received communal investment + meaningful shared loss + intact belief in the performance's value → desire to turn future competitive success into reciprocal collective joy.**

This is a narrower and more faithful model than “Kanon finally becomes competitive.” [SI]

The episode's strongest Season-1 conclusion is therefore:

> **Liella! does not need victory in order to become valuable, legitimate, or capable of saving something real. Precisely because the group has already received and generated value outside ranking, defeat can make victory newly meaningful as a way of answering other people's investment rather than proving their own worth.**

That distinction is also what lets the finale stress-test S1E11 successfully. Kanon loses after a major public performance and does **not** return to S1E03's identity-negative logic. She never says the loss proves she cannot sing, lacks talent, is a burden, or should withdraw. She can feel `悔しい` without turning disappointment into ontological self-condemnation. The fear-integration model therefore survives its first major competitive consequence. [FR/SI]

# 3. Naive structural read

S1E12 proceeds through seven linked problems.

## 3.1 Practice has become intrinsically rewarding

The episode opens with Chisato coaching the five. She corrects Keke's breathing and Sumire's stopping position, finishes practice, and is then surprised when Kanon wants more running.

Kanon explains:

> `何か　頑張った分だけできるようになっていくのって`
>
> `楽しいなって思って`

Ren comments that Kanon seems more positive than before. Chisato answers:

> `これがかのんちゃん`
>
> `私が知ってる`

The Season-1 endpoint begins not with a victory fantasy but with **effort itself becoming non-punitive**. [JT]

## 3.2 Yuigaoka's survival problem is materially resolved

Ren is called to the headmistress. Two forms of support appear:

- applicant numbers have increased enough that the school will not lack students;
- Ren's father offers to donate needed funds.

The headmistress states:

> `来年以降も結ヶ丘は続いていきます`

The long-running school-survival uncertainty from S1E07-S1E08 is therefore no longer merely aspirational at this boundary. [JT]

The group immediately links the increased interest to the district performance. Sumire theatrically credits her rap; Keke tells her not to get carried away; another member explicitly praises Sumire's rap; the group notes the follower increase and that people became interested in the school after the live. [JT]

## 3.3 The Tokyo competition makes venue choice a problem of ambition and entitlement

The Tokyo round is remote and requires each participating school to prepare its own stage.

Keke wants a venue capable of competing with other districts' best locations. Kanon gravitates toward the gym because it is realistic and because winter break/external logistics make larger demands difficult.

A middle-school acquaintance challenges Kanon directly:

> `東京大会でしょ`
>
> `決勝進出がかかってるんでしょ`
>
> `なのに体育館？`
>
> `欲がなさすぎる`
>
> `本気で勝つ気あるの`

Sumire later sharpens the same diagnosis:

> `あんたは気を遣いすぎなのよ`
>
> `もはやLiella! はこの学校の代表よ`
>
> `わがまま言うくらいでいいんじゃないの`

Kanon's care for third-party burden is again visible as a virtue with an overextension risk. [JT]

## 3.4 Sunny Passion gives Kanon a question, not an answer

Sunny Passion visits while preparing for the same competition. After mutual competitive declarations, Kanon asks what winning means.

Her explanation is historically grounded: after living with the possibility that she might not be able to sing at all, free expression and the pursuit of a good live already feel sufficient.

Sunny Passion agrees that song may not intrinsically be a competitive object, then argues that competition can raise participants' level and that Love Live! experience will reveal why people want—or feel they must—win.

A 1.61 s sub-−35 dB mixed-track interval begins almost immediately after `勝たなきゃって思うのか`, creating a measurable hinge before the next thematic beat. [AM]

The question remains open.

## 3.5 The school/community takes stage-building agency away from Liella!

Kanon proposes a star-filled gym concept. The scale of work is large. Other Yuigaoka students tell the five to leave construction to them and concentrate on practice.

Their stated rationale is important:

> `かのんちゃんたちは　この学校の希望なんだよ`
>
> `だから応援させてほしいの`

The students do not merely “help the idols.” They claim agency in the project. Support becomes **self-authored contribution by the wider school**, not passive fandom. [JT/SI]

The final plan exceeds Kanon's original gym proposal. Students and neighborhood participants secure a public illuminated street space. Liella! is led through classmates holding lights toward a stage built from that collective effort.

The explanation is explicit:

> `私たちだからできる　私たちにしかできないステージ`

The phrase refers to a “we” larger than the five performers. [JT/AF]

## 3.6 The performance succeeds; the competition does not

Kanon introduces the group and publicly declares pride in belonging to Yuigaoka. The insert song follows on the multicolored star stage.

The performance is then separated from the result announcement. The mixed-track performance region (998-1135 s) has mean level approximately **−19.8 dBFS**, while the 1168-1190 s result region falls to approximately **−27.6 dBFS**. [AM]

Sunny Passion is announced as Tokyo representative. A textual gap of approximately **3.58 s** follows the result line before Sumire's first spoken reaction; within that interval the mixed track contains a **~1.997 s** sub-−35 dB interval. [JT/AM]

The edit therefore gives defeat an acoustic processing space rather than immediately covering it with reassurance.

## 3.7 Kanon discovers why she wants to win

Peers insist the performance was something to be proud of. Kanon initially returns attention to cleanup and thanking the neighborhood.

Then:

> `そっか　こういうことなんだ`

Two low-energy intervals of roughly **0.86 s** and **1.00 s** occur around this reflective region before she articulates the new state. [AM]

Her explanation is not status-first. It begins with disappointment that the group's supporters could not receive a winning result back from them. Only then does she say `勝ちたい`.

The episode ends not with despair but with a new collective future orientation and resumed practice.

# 5. Shibuya Kanon — from recovered singer to reciprocal competitor

## 5.1 Effort no longer functions primarily as proof of deficiency

At the beginning of Season 1, difficulty repeatedly became identity evidence. Failure to enter the music course licensed conclusions about talent and whether Kanon should sing at all.

S1E12 gives the opposite learning relation:

> effort -> measurable improvement -> enjoyment -> desire for more practice.

Her `頑張った分だけできるようになっていく` is unusually important because it is process language. She is not waiting for the next performance to tell her whether she is allowed to value herself. [JT/SI]

This strengthens S1E11's integration model. The restored continuity with younger Kanon is not merely a one-scene memory event; it changes how current practice feels.

## 5.2 Competitive ambivalence is not low ambition

Kanon works hard, wants an excellent live, and willingly performs under major stakes while still saying she does not yet strongly understand `歌で勝ったり負けたり`.

Therefore simulation should not infer:

> weak desire to win -> weak effort.

At the S1E12 pre-result boundary, Kanon can be extremely committed to preparation while ranking remains secondary in her conscious value hierarchy.

## 5.3 Overconsideration remains a live leadership risk

Kanon's gym preference is partly realistic. Winter break, permissions, weather and burden on others are real constraints. The episode does not portray caution as foolish.

But two people independently pressure the same tendency:

- the middle-school acquaintance calls her insufficiently desirous;
- Sumire says she is too considerate and should allow herself more selfishness as Yuigaoka's representative.

This is a familiar Kanon pattern at a new scale: sensitivity to third-party costs can become premature restriction of what others are actually willing to contribute. [FR/SI]

The correction does not come from Kanon learning to commandeer resources. It comes from **others voluntarily choosing contribution**.

## 5.4 “Best school” changes the meaning of institutional pride

Kanon's `この学校が一番だって` could be misread as simple competitive chauvinism.

Context resists that reading. She says it after:

- Yuigaoka's continuation has been secured;
- classmates volunteer construction labor;
- the neighborhood permits use of public space;
- the group is led through visible support toward the stage.

Her “number one” is therefore an affective/belonging claim before it is a ranking claim.

This revises the series' earlier prestige language. Institutional pride can be grounded in experienced reciprocal belonging rather than exclusionary quality gatekeeping.

## 5.5 Defeat does not reactivate the old identity collapse

This is the strongest S1E11 carry-forward result.

After losing, Kanon says:

- `ダメだった`;
- `ごめん`;
- `私　悔しい`.

She does **not** say:

- “I cannot sing”;
- “I ruined everything because I am useless”;
- “I should leave”;
- “the successful performance was a fluke.”

The difference from S1E03 is structural. Negative outcome remains painful but no longer monopolizes self-definition. [FR/SI]

## 5.6 The new desire to win is relationally generated

Kanon's sequence is specific:

1. receive support;
2. perform well;
3. lose;
4. be thanked anyway;
5. feel the asymmetry of having received more than she believes she returned;
6. discover `勝ちたい`;
7. specify the desired effect: smiles and shared celebration;
8. identify the vehicle: `私たちの歌 / Liella! の歌 / 結ヶ丘の歌`;
9. revise desire into collective commitment: `優勝しよう`.

This should be the canonical Season-1 model of her competitive turn.

# 6. Arashi Chisato — expertise without re-imposing solitary worthiness

S1E12 is quieter for Chisato than S1E11, but it matters as a durability test.

## 6.1 Training authority remains domain-specific and legitimate

The opening establishes Chisato correcting breathing, stopping position, and training load. No one treats her authority as global command. Her expertise remains dance/body/practice centered. [JT]

She is also the one who notices when her own enthusiasm may be excessive (`ありゃ　気合い入りすぎちゃった`). This is a useful self-correction counterweight to S1E11's willingness to engineer a difficult autonomy test.

## 6.2 Her recognition of Kanon becomes less idealizing

Ren says Kanon seems more forward-looking. Chisato replies:

> `これがかのんちゃん`
>
> `私が知ってる`

After S1E11, this line should not be modeled as “the fearless Kanon has returned.” S1E11 explicitly established that younger Kanon was afraid too. Chisato is now recognizing a pattern of energetic engagement, not absence of fear. [FR/SI]

## 6.3 Physical care remains compatible with ambition

On the snowy competition day Chisato supports shortening practice for health and helping the construction team rather than maximizing rehearsal at all costs.

This is useful negative evidence against a model in which competitive stakes turn her coaching into unconditional optimization.

## 6.4 Kanon uses Chisato as an intimate first recipient of disappointment

After `そっか　こういうことなんだ`, the next interpersonal address is:

> `ちぃちゃん`

followed by:

> `私　悔しい`

Chisato does not immediately reinterpret the feeling for her. The group then lets Kanon articulate it into `勝ちたい` herself.

The anti-isolation constraint therefore survives S1E11: independent agency does not mean private emotional self-containment.

# 7. Tang Keke — admiration becomes rivalry without ceasing to be admiration

## 7.1 Competition urgency remains high

Keke pushes for a venue that will not let Liella! be visually buried by other districts' special locations:

> `地味なステージではLiella! は埋もれてしまいます`

This is consistent with S1E10: Love Live! remains a sacred/high-stakes domain where she can become more demanding than the others.

But she is not the sole decision-maker. Kanon, Sumire, the school, and practical constraints all reshape the venue outcome.

## 7.2 S1E03 Sunny Passion reverence is behaviorally revised

Earlier, Sunny Passion admiration could briefly overwhelm strategic processing.

On competition day Keke takes an object she describes as a treasure connected to her fandom and asks Sumire to hold it for one day:

> `しかし今日だけはライバルです`
>
> `今日だけはファンをやめます`

The important point is not literal rejection of fandom. The time limitation `今日だけ` explicitly preserves admiration outside the competitive frame.

New rule:

> **admired superior becomes direct competitor -> Keke can temporarily suspend fan-role behavior in order to inhabit rival-role commitment without needing to devalue the admired person.**

This is a significant maturation of her competition ecology.

## 7.3 Hidden results pressure remains compartmentalized

S1E12 contains no group disclosure of Keke's S1E10 results pressure.

The loss therefore occurs while the information asymmetry remains intact.

This is analytically important: do not rewrite the finale as though all five understand the same personal stakes.

## 7.4 Sumire becomes the private carrier of the hidden stake

The shrine exchange makes the compartment visible without disclosing it.

Keke uses bare:

> `すみれ`

then entrusts the fan object.

Sumire later gives Keke a shrine charm and says:

> `これからも一緒に続けられるように`

Because Sumire is the person who already knows Keke's concealed results pressure, the line is much more specific than generic good luck. It directly frames the desired outcome as **continued togetherness**. [JT/FR/SI]

The episode does not specify the exact family mechanism in Japanese here. Therefore preserve the narrow inference:

> Sumire understands Keke's competitive situation as threatening continued participation and privately responds to that threat.

Do not upgrade the auxiliary English-only S1E10 return-home wording into Japanese textual fact.

## 7.5 Defeat produces recommitment rather than disclosure

After Kanon explains her disappointment, Keke responds:

> `また全力で挑みましょう`

and later:

> `Liella! はこんなところで終わりません`

Her burden remains private; her public mode is forward collective action.

# 10. Keke–Sumire relationship — from contested recognition to confidential continuity

S1E10 changed the pair through evidence-based recognition and bare-name address. S1E11 proved the address shift could survive ordinary disagreement. S1E12 adds a different layer: **confidential future-protection**.

The shrine sequence has three linked acts:

1. Keke calls `すみれ` without honorific/formality.
2. Keke entrusts Sumire with a personally important fan object because Sunny Passion must be treated as a rival for this day.
3. Sumire returns a shrine charm whose explicit purpose is `これからも一緒に続けられるように`.

The pair is now capable of carrying a problem the whole group does not know about.

That should not be romanticized into perfect transparency. In fact, the asymmetry is the point: **Sumire knows something the others do not, and the relationship now bears the ethical/emotional burden of that knowledge.**

The final `グソクムシ` / `じゃかましい` exchange then protects continuity of their recognizable social register. Neither care nor confidentiality requires a total stylistic conversion.

# 11. Kanon–Sumire relationship — mutual correction of opposite under/over-claiming

# 12. Liella! and Yuigaoka — representation becomes reciprocal rather than extractive

The phrase `もはやLiella! はこの学校の代表よ` raises an institutional danger: if Liella! represents the school, the group might begin consuming school labor/resources while calling it representation.

The episode avoids that by giving the wider school agency.

Students themselves say:

> `ステージのことは私たちに任せて`
>
> `かのんちゃんたちは練習に集中`

and explain why they want to do it.

This is not a top-down demand from Liella!. The school claims the right to support its representatives.

The relationship becomes reciprocal:

- Liella!'s district performance brings attention/applicants;
- school peers interpret Liella! as hope;
- peers and neighborhood create the Tokyo stage;
- Liella! publicly identifies with Yuigaoka;
- the loss creates a new desire to return that investment through future victory.

This circular model is one of the episode's most important institutional achievements.

# 13. School survival — competition matters without becoming a magical causal explanation

S1E12 finally gives direct outcome evidence for Yuigaoka.

What can be said safely:

- applicant interest has increased;
- the headmistress says current numbers mean there will not be a student shortage;
- the school will continue into the following year;
- Ren's father offers needed financial support;
- group dialogue attributes at least some increased school interest to the district live.

What should **not** be overstated:

- the episode does not provide a full admissions/finance accounting model;
- Liella! should not be treated as the sole cause of school survival merely because characters credit the live;
- the father's donation and applicant growth are both material inputs;
- winning Tokyo is demonstrably **not required** for the school to continue, because continuation is established before the Tokyo result.

This last point is especially important for the competition synthesis.

# 14. Sunny Passion — competition as developmental relation

Sunny Passion's Season-1 role becomes clearer here.

They are simultaneously:

- admired model;

- direct rival;
- source of a competition thesis;

The episode later validates the experiential half of the claim but does not prove a broad meritocratic ontology.

# 15. Performance dramaturgy — the song as reciprocal initiative

The final Tokyo-stage insert is strongly integrated into the episode's dramatic question.

## 15.1 Immediate dramatic question before the song

Can Liella! convert a stage made by other people's investment into a performance that expresses what the five and Yuigaoka have become?

The answer is yes at the performance level, even though the competition answer is no.

## 15.2 Song text

Key Japanese lines include:

- `出会いがくれた　大きな勇気`;
- `始まりは君から`;
- `でも次は僕から`;

- `信じる気持ちが揺らぐときは / 想いを歌にして伝えよう`.

The lyrical subject is neither solitary Kanon nor an abstract victory machine. It is a `僕ら` whose initiative circulates.

## 15.3 Visual formation

The retained contact-sheet sequence shows:

- five distinct costume colors;
- five colored lanes/fields in the stage design;
- rotating individual closeups;
- frequent reaggregation into five-person lines/arcs;
- a star/illumination architecture that visually joins differentiated color fields.

This strongly recalls S1E09's diegetic explanation of Liella! as differently colored light becoming connected. [AF/FR]

## 15.4 What the performance can do that dialogue cannot

Dialogue can state that school and neighborhood support Liella!.

The performance **places the five inside a stage literally made from that support**, turns the path of supporters into the threshold to the song, and makes the five-color collective visible as one performance system.

The performance therefore converts institutional/social reciprocity into audiovisual form.

## 15.5 Result separation

The song ends before the ranking is announced. The episode protects the distinction:

> the performance happened and mattered; then the competition judged it.

The latter does not rewrite the former.

The retained result/aftermath frames repeatedly show the five in a compact line or semicircle while the supporting school remains physically nearby.

Defeat does not visually scatter the group into isolated personal failures. Kanon may receive closeups, but the frame repeatedly returns her to an ensemble/support network.

# 20. Counterevidence and alternative readings

## 20.1 Alternative: the episode simply teaches Kanon that winning is good

Too broad.

Counterevidence:

- Sunny Passion concedes song may not inherently be competitive;
- Yuigaoka survival is achieved before Tokyo victory;
- Liella!'s performance is praised after loss;
- Kanon's new desire is explained through `お返し` / collective smiles, not abstract status.

Narrower conclusion: competition becomes one meaningful medium for reciprocal ambition.

## 20.2 Alternative: community support proves Liella! owes victory

The episode flirts with this danger because Kanon initially interprets loss as failure to return support.

But peers directly reject that equation by thanking her and calling the stage something to be proud of.

Therefore the text contains both:

- Kanon's felt obligation;
- social counterevidence that support was not conditional on first place.

This should remain a tension at the Season-1 checkpoint.

## 20.3 Alternative: Kanon should simply have demanded a bigger stage earlier

Too simplistic.

Her caution is grounded in real burden/weather/permission constraints. The ethical solution is not unilateral entitlement. It is that other people state their own desire to contribute.

The episode favors **consensual widening of ambition**, not “leaders should ignore costs.”

## 20.4 Alternative: Keke's hidden problem is resolved because the group chooses to continue

Not supported.

The group chooses to continue, but the external condition that created Keke's private pressure is not shown as resolved in Japanese dialogue. Sumire's charm line actually preserves the problem as meaningful.

Keep it OPEN.

## 20.5 Alternative: Ren's school burden is permanently solved as a personality issue

Overreach.

The episode materially removes the immediate structural crisis. It does not prove Ren can never relapse into overresponsibility in another future context. The correct claim is temporal:

> at the Season-1 boundary, the specific school-survival sole-bearer architecture is dismantled.

## 20.6 Alternative: second place means the stage/performance was inadequate

The episode deliberately separates ranking from local performance evaluation. The supporters say the stage was excellent and they are proud. Visual/audio evidence presents a fully realized performance. Ranking is a competition outcome, not a complete artistic diagnostic.
