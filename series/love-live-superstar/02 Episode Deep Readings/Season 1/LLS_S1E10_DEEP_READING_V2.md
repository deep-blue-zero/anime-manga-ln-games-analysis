# Love Live! Superstar!! — S1E10 Deep Reading V2
## 第10話「チェケラッ!!」

# 1. Governing thesis

S1E10 is the first episode to place the newly named **Liella!** under a formal Love Live! requirement, and the result is not a collapse from S1E09's plural identity into a single permanent hierarchy. Instead, competition forces the group to prove whether its claim to heterogeneous connection can survive a task whose best solution temporarily relocates the visible center away from Kanon.

The preliminary assignment is rap. Kanon can sing but cannot readily generate the needed words in rap form. Chisato has some exposure through dance training but becomes absorbed in dancing once she starts moving. Ren tries to solve the problem through a carefully constructed tanka and wordplay rather than rap. Keke's attempt crosses into Chinese. Sumire, whom Keke initially judges as lower-potential than the others, immediately produces an effective improvised self-introduction and explains that years in show business have made improvisation one of her strongest domains. [JT]

The task therefore does something analytically valuable: it creates a problem that **Kanon is not best positioned to solve**. The group responds by putting Sumire in the most visible position because the rap passage and her ad-lib/show-business competence fit each other. Kanon explicitly defends that redistribution against precedent-based objections:

> 「今までは今まで」  
> 「大切なのはこれからだよ」  
> 「そうそう Liella!と同じで」

The S1E09 identity of unfinished possibility is thus immediately operationalized. `Liella!` does not mean the first allocation of prestige and authority is sacred. Because the group is still beginning, new people can occupy new functions. [JT/IT]

That does not mean hierarchy disappears. S1E10 is intensely about center. But it separates at least four things that could otherwise be conflated:

1. **task fitness** — who is best suited to the assigned rap requirement;
2. **technical specialization** — who is strongest in singing, dancing, presentation, composition/quality judgment, or competition knowledge;
3. **symbolic center** — who occupies the most visually salient position in this specific performance;
4. **human worth / legitimacy as a member** — which cannot be reduced to being singularly best at one skill.

Sumire's problem is precisely that she has learned to collapse the last two. Her childhood history repeatedly put her close to the center without allowing her to remain there. When school feedback now says the group would clear the preliminary more easily with Kanon or Ren in front, Sumire does not hear merely a tactical suggestion. It activates the old rule: **however hard I work, the important position ultimately goes to someone else**. She therefore tries to withdraw before that replacement can be imposed and rhetorically demotes Love Live! to an amateur competition whose final matters more than this preliminary. [JT/FR/SI]

The episode refuses to heal that wound through pity. This is the reason Keke becomes the decisive counterparty rather than Kanon. Keke begins the episode as Sumire's harshest internal skeptic. She says the first Love Live! task is too important to entrust to someone she judges below Kanon and Chisato in potential. That makes her later endorsement unusually strong: it cannot plausibly be explained as pre-existing favoritism. After watching Sumire practice and hearing her sing, Keke explicitly says that Sumire is suited to be **Liella!'s center**, has the necessary power, and receives the position for that reason. [JT]

Keke's support is not soft. She is insulting, demanding, secretive, competitive, and willing to press Sumire even after Sumire threatens to quit. She has also concealed a private results pressure from the other members because she does not want them to pursue school idols out of concern for her. S1E10 therefore complicates Keke at the same time it deepens her relationship with Sumire. Her strongest care here is expressed through **standards, labor, and refusal to patronize**: she alters Sumire's costume for the center role, watches her practice, updates her judgment, defends that judgment against public opinion, creates a tiara specifically for the center, and physically chases it when Sumire rejects it. [JT/AF/SI]

The conflict culminates in a voice event rather than a formal apology. After Keke ends up in the bushes still holding the tiara, she calls her simply:

> 「すみれ」

Sumire immediately notices that this is the first time Keke has called her by her name. Keke dismisses the observation verbally, but the form recurs after the performance when Sumire says `ありがとう 可可` and Keke again answers `すみれ`. [JT]

That address shift is not equivalent to the disappearance of rivalry. It is more specific and more useful: Keke's antagonistic distance has become compatible with direct person-recognition. Their conflict remains rough, but the person on the other side is no longer reducible to `この人`, `グソクムシ`, a rival, or an irritant.

The episode also preserves S1E09's plural group identity under competition. Immediately before the performance, the group does not say that Love Live! exists only to defeat the other school idols. Kanon frames the coming stage as:

> 「私たちLiella!が」  
> 「たくさんのスクールアイドルとつながって」  
> 「歌を響かせるんだ」

Competition remains real—Keke repeatedly insists on aiming for the top—but the group's own articulated meaning is relational rather than purely eliminative. [JT]

The resulting `ノンフィクション!!` performance resolves the local dramatic question by **letting Sumire actually remain center**. The episode does not merely tell her that she is worthy and then return the visible hierarchy to Kanon. The performance opens its lyric lane around Sumire, gives her the distinctive center styling/tiara, repeatedly frames her at the visual axis, and lets the five-member choreography move around that temporary centrality without erasing the other members' presence. [AF/MF]

The central S1E10 thesis is therefore:

> **Under formal competition, Liella!'s plural identity survives by making center task-contingent rather than permanently proprietary. Sumire's growth does not require giving up the desire to be seen; it requires learning that recognition can be grounded in specific competence rather than pity, and Keke becomes the crucial witness precisely because she changes her mind after evidence.**

A second thesis follows for Keke:

> **Competition urgency is both one of Keke's greatest assets and one of her pressure risks. S1E10 shows that she can begin from rigid comparative judgment, observe new evidence, revise strongly, and then defend the revised judgment even when public opinion points the other way.**

And a third for Kanon:

> **Kanon's S1E09 semantic centrality does not become universal command. Her most important leadership act in S1E10 is to authorize decentralization: she recognizes that another member should represent Liella! at the most visible point because the task fits that member better.**

## 2.2 Dual-subtitle status

Corrected Japanese remains the governing textual source. The paired English derivative is useful but demonstrably non-equivalent in several S1E10 passages.

### Ren's rap/tanka explanation

Ren gives:

> 「秋あかね 歌にいざよう 葉月恋」  
> 「想いはいまだ十六夜なり」

She corrects `俳句` to `短歌` and explains a Japanese wordplay between `いざよう` (hesitate) and `十六夜`, tied to being sixteen. The English rendering reconstructs this as an English “rhyme” relationship. That is localization, not evidence that Japanese Ren was making the English rhyme described by the translation. [JT]

### Keke's Chinese rap

The corrected Japanese track does not transcribe the Chinese utterance itself; the next Japanese line says the attempt has become Chinese (`中国語になってしまうし`). The paired English derivative supplies translated performance text. It can establish navigation and that English localizers chose to render the content, but it must not be mined for Japanese lexical/voice claims. [JT]

### Keke's phone call

This is the largest textual asymmetry. From roughly `15:00–15:27`, the corrected Japanese track contains no transcription of the call itself. The paired English derivative renders a conversation with Keke's sister and includes a condition that Keke will return if she does not produce results.

The **existence of concealed results pressure is nevertheless independently established by later corrected Japanese**:

- Sumire: `勝たなきゃいけないんでしょ`;
- `あんた 絶対勝たなきゃいけないんでしょ`;
- Keke confirms the others do not know;
- Keke: `可可のことを気にして スクールアイドルをやってほしくありません`;
- Sumire: `結果を出さなきゃ`;
- Keke answers that this is why she wants Sumire as center.

Therefore:

- **JT:** Keke faces concealed pressure to win/produce results and has intentionally not told Kanon and the others.
- **Auxiliary English-supported detail:** the exact “go back if I do not get results” formulation and family-call wording.

The latter must not be silently upgraded to Japanese textual fact.

### `今までは今まで`

English's “the past is in the past” is broadly adequate, but the Japanese paired with `大切なのはこれから` is especially important because Kanon uses temporal separation to reject precedent as destiny. The analytical force comes from the explicit **past / from-now-on** opposition, not an abstract motivational cliché. [JT]

# 3. Sequential dramatic architecture

## Movement I — formal competition introduces a task rather than a generic demand to “be better” (`~02:47–04:23`)

The preliminary system is explicitly changed from a more directly eliminative design. Rather than simply having groups confront each other immediately, each district receives a song requirement. Southwest Tokyo, including Shibuya and framed as a trend-leading area, receives **rap**. A song that does not incorporate the task is disqualified. [JT]

This is institutionally significant because competition now tests adaptability, not only previously accumulated skill. The group cannot simply repeat the competence map of S1E09 and assume that “best singer + best dancer + other strengths” automatically wins. The institution inserts a form none of them has yet established as a group specialty. [SI]

## Movement II — the rap problem reveals limits inside every existing specialty (`04:24–06:23`)

Kanon begins confidently enough with a self-introduction but stops when words no longer come:

> 「こ… 言葉が…」  
> 「出てこない」

and says rap is difficult in a way ordinary singing is not. [JT]

This is **not** her old evaluative singing block. She is making sound, experimenting, and staying in the task. The difficulty is form-specific lexical generation. Treating it as “Kanon's stage fright returned” would collapse distinct mechanisms. [SI]

Chisato says her dance school has taught her rap several times, which initially makes her a plausible domain bridge. Yet once she begins dancing, she becomes absorbed in the movement and loses the rap task. Her strongest expertise competes with the new assignment rather than solving it. [JT]

Ren, after explicitly saying Chisato should preserve dance as the group's differentiating advantage, attempts a highly composed tanka. The scene makes her literate/structured cleverness visible, but also demonstrates mismatch: the joke works because she has solved the wrong formal problem. [JT/SI]

Keke's attempt shifts into Chinese. Multilingual capacity is real, but it does not by itself produce the required Japanese rap solution. [JT]

The problem is therefore not “everyone is bad except Sumire.” It is that previously useful skills have to be matched to a new form.

## Movement III — Sumire's show-business experience becomes task-specific authority (`06:13–07:19`)

Keke immediately rejects the idea of using Sumire:

> 「大切なラブライブ！の最初の課題ですよ」  
> 「2人よりポテンシャルが低いのこの人に任せるわけには」

The first statement reveals why Keke is rigid: the task's placement at the start of Love Live! magnifies perceived risk. The second is a comparative talent judgment, not an objective series verdict. [JT]

Sumire answers not with an argument but a demonstration. She produces a rap-like improvised self-introduction; the others identify the performance as impressive, and she explains:

> 「小さい頃からショウビジネスの世界で場数は踏んでいるの」  
> 「アドリブだったら負けないわ」

This is the first clean S1E10 example of **competence forcing authority redistribution despite prior social judgment**. [JT/SI]

The choreography planning then puts Sumire at the most visible position. Kanon explains this is because the assigned task is there and Sumire should sing it in the most eye-catching place. The center allocation begins from **function**, not pity. [JT]

## Movement IV — center assignment itself activates Sumire's recognition wound (`07:03–09:30`)

Sumire's response is revealingly hesitant despite years of insisting on visibility:

> 「私がセンターでいいったらいいの」

She wants the position, but actual entrustment activates disbelief rather than simple triumph. [JT]

Keke objects using historical precedent: Sumire has reached this point without ever standing in the middle, which may mean she is unsuited. Kanon answers with the strongest anti-determinist statement in the episode:

> 「それ言ったら私だって歌えなかったよ」  
> 「今までは今まで」  
> 「大切なのはこれからだよ」  
> 「そうそう Liella!と同じで」

Ren adds that she herself has little stage-singing experience. The group thus refuses to let past role allocation become proof of future incapacity. [JT]

This is a direct application of S1E09's `まだ何色でもない` logic. Unfinishedness is not decorative group branding; it becomes a rule for evaluating members. [FR/IT]

Keke accepts but immediately imposes a seriousness condition. She says that anyone standing center must take school idols seriously, warns that this Love Live! will be especially difficult, and demands a genuine intention to aim for the top. [JT]

Kanon then explains to Ren that Sumire normally talks as though she wants the front but often hands roles back to Kanon/Chisato/Keke when actually entrusted. Kanon's inference is that Sumire probably lacks confidence because of her history. She also identifies unresolved interpersonal residue: Sumire likely still cares about having angered Keke over school idols. [JT/SI]

The episode therefore makes the center problem both intrapsychic and relational before any external audience feedback arrives.

## Movement V — private labor precedes public rejection (`09:30–12:58`)

Sumire practices alone at home late enough that her family asks when she will stop; she tells them to eat first. The next sequence revisits fragments of her earlier professional disappointments and near-center roles while she focuses on `センター…`. [AF/JT/FR]

Keke then supplies a custom alteration to the center costume. Her delivery is characteristically abrasive: she says even someone without aura needs to be able to fight as center, so she changed the shape from the others. The action and insult coexist. [JT/AF]

This matters because Keke's care does not arrive after Sumire breaks down. She has already invested labor in Sumire's success before public opinion forces the confrontation.

The group rehearses/records. Kanon proposes giving Sumire another singing part, further treating center as a practical performance design rather than ceremonial compensation. [JT]

Then audience/school feedback arrives. Respondents praise the song but say Kanon or Ren would be better center; some explicitly suggest changing center to clear the preliminaries. [JT/AF]

That feedback is strategically intelligible. The episode does not make everyone who prefers another center malicious. The danger lies in what Sumire does with the information.

## Movement VI — Sumire converts external doubt into anticipated replacement (`12:59–13:53`)

When asked for her view, Sumire says:

> 「この学校のスクールアイドルなんだから」  
> 「みんなの意見に従うのが当然でしょ」

This sounds procedural and collectivist, but its surrounding behavior shows that it also enables self-removal. She quickly reframes Love Live! as a novice/amateur tournament preliminary beneath her show-business identity and says her real turn can wait for the final. [JT/SI]

Keke immediately identifies the move as escape:

> 「何逃げようとしてるデスカ」

and says that once they have decided, Sumire should remain center regardless of what others say. [JT]

Sumire then states the rule under the defense:

> 「無理よ」  
> 「どうせ最後はいつも私じゃなくなるんだから」

The `どうせ` is crucial. She is not merely accepting a poll. She is using past pattern as a forecast of inevitable future replacement. [JT]

## Movement VII — the four-member diagnostic separates specialization from adequacy (`14:08–14:55`)

With Sumire absent, Ren gives a careful evaluation. Sumire's singing and dancing are both high-level, but Ren does not judge her the group's number one in either. Kanon is strongest in singing, Chisato in dance, Ren is named most elegant, and Keke is named most visually/stage striking. Sumire has all of these qualities, but not one isolated category in which she is uniquely first. [JT]

Ren suggests that this structure may help explain why Sumire's earlier hopes did not come true. This is a **character judgment**, not an omniscient ranking system. [JT]

Kanon reaches the opposite practical conclusion from the same premises:

> 「だからこそ すみれちゃんがセンターやるべきだと思う」  
> 「だって 実力では全く引けを取ってないんだから」

The group's most versatile member need not be disqualified because specialists can beat her on isolated axes. S1E10 therefore turns “generalist” from a story of always being second into a possible center logic. [SI]

## Movement VIII — Keke's hidden result pressure makes her support more, not less, diagnostic (`~15:00–17:52`)

The corrected Japanese track leaves Keke's phone dialogue untranscribed, while the paired English derivative supplies a family/results conversation. What matters for canonical Japanese analysis is that Sumire has heard enough to know Keke must win and produce results. [JT + auxiliary EN]

Keke has not told Kanon and the others. Her reason is explicit:

> 「可可のことを気にして スクールアイドルをやってほしくありません」

She does not want the group pursuing school idols out of concern for her. [JT]

This is ethically mixed. It protects the others' self-authorship from a hidden personal obligation, but it also means that Keke is privately carrying a constraint that materially shapes her competitive urgency. The group cannot calibrate pressure it does not know exists. [SI]

The most important consequence is evidentiary. Sumire tries to explain Keke's center insistence as pity, Kanon's influence, sunk-cost practice, or private desperation to win. Keke rejects all of them and says the hidden results pressure is **exactly why** she wants the strongest fit in center. [JT]

Because Keke needs results, her support of Sumire is not charitable indulgence. She believes Sumire gives them the best chance under this task.

## Movement IX — Keke's judgment becomes explicit recognition (`16:14–19:04`)

Sumire asks whether she is center because everyone pities her for working hard and repeatedly failing to get the role. She says pity would make center meaningless and threatens to leave school idols if they continue forcing the position. [JT]

Keke answers through a sequence of increasingly concrete evidence:

- she does not make costumes out of pity;
- Sumire's understanding of Keke's motives is wrong;
- Keke watched Sumire train;
- Keke heard her sing;
- Keke judged her suited to be Liella!'s center;
- Keke believes Sumire has the necessary power.

The key formulation is:

> 「可可があなたに任せたのは」  
> 「あなたがふさわしいと思ったからデス」

followed by:

> 「Liella!のセンターにふさわしいと思ったからデス」

This is not generalized self-esteem reassurance. It is domain-specific judgment from the member who began the episode most skeptical. [JT]

Keke then says she put all her feeling into something made for Sumire and produces a purple jeweled tiara for “the center you.” [JT/AF]

## Movement X — the tiara turns recognition into costly action and changes address (`19:04–20:20`)

Sumire rejects the tiara physically; retained frames show it airborne. Keke immediately chases it. A high/wide composition reduces both girls against the school grounds as Keke runs after the object; subsequent frames show her lunging/reaching and then in the bushes still holding it. [AF]

Keke repeats `届いて… / 届いて` while reaching. [JT]

The action matters because the tiara is not just a center token. Keke has just defined it as the material concentration of her judgment and labor. Chasing it makes the claim expensive: she does not let Sumire's defensive refusal annul the recognition. [SI]

Afterward, Keke says:

> 「すみれ」

Sumire's response establishes the novelty inside the episode itself:

> 「初めて名前呼んだわね」

Keke verbally brushes that aside and returns to standards:

> 「Liella!のセンターとして 恥ずかしくないステージにしてクダサイ」

Sumire's answer—`当然でしょ / 誰だと思ってるの`—returns to confident self-presentation, but now after recognition rather than as a defense against its absence. [JT]

## Movement XI — competition is re-described as connection immediately before performance (`20:02–20:20`)

The group notes that Yuigaoka's school idols are about to stand on a Love Live! stage. Kanon then frames what they will do:

> 「私たちLiella!が」  
> 「たくさんのスクールアイドルとつながって」  
> 「歌を響かせるんだ」

Sumire answers with `ギャラクシー！`. [JT]

This is an important balance. Keke's survival/competitive logic is real and increasingly consequential; Kanon's relational performance logic is also real. The episode does not force one to erase the other.

## Movement XII — `ノンフィクション!!` gives the contested center to Sumire in fact (`20:20–21:46`)

The performance follows through on the argument rather than returning center to the safest previous choice. Sumire appears with the distinctive tiara and a more visibly differentiated center costume; early performance framing places her on the visual axis with other members arranged behind/around her. [AF]

The song text includes lines about wanting to shine, proving impossibility wrong, breaking a poker face, drawing the listener into a new world, and opening a new door. These lyrics strongly rhyme with the episode's concerns, but they remain performance text rather than literal autobiography. [JT/MF]

The most reliable dramaturgical claim is structural:

> **dialogue argues that Sumire is suited to center → visual/action sequence tests whether she can accept that judgment → performance lets her inhabit the role.**

The song can do what conversation cannot: make recognition publicly executable in the exact medium that has historically denied her the center. [IT]

## Movement XIII — reciprocal naming closes the local relationship arc (`21:46+`)

After the performance, Sumire says:

> 「ありがとう 可可」

Keke answers:

> 「すみれ」

The bare name therefore recurs after the crisis, not only in the extraordinary bush/tiara moment. [JT]

That recurrence is enough to update the current voice/relationship model: from the S1E10 boundary onward, bare `すみれ` is an attested Keke→Sumire form. It is **not** yet proof that every future Keke utterance must use it, but the earlier distance has unquestionably changed.

# 4. Sumire — recognition without pity

## 4.1 S1E10 confirms that Sumire's center desire is not generic narcissism

S1E09 showed that a gigantic final venue immediately activated Sumire's fantasy of mass attention. S1E10 gives her actual competitive center and reveals how frightening that can be.

If Sumire merely wanted attention, being assigned center should solve the problem. It does not. Actual entrustment produces disbelief (`私がセンターでいいったらいいの`) and later withdrawal once public opinion prefers someone else. [JT]

The governing mechanism is better stated as:

> **high-value visible role + history of peripheral replacement → desire for recognition coexists with expectation that recognition will be revoked.**

## 4.2 `どうせ最後はいつも私じゃなくなる` is the clearest current formulation of the wound

The phrase turns episodic events into an invariant rule. `いつも` globalizes; `最後は` places the expected injury at the endpoint; `どうせ` communicates anticipatory futility. [JT]

This is more precise than “Sumire lacks confidence.” She can be highly confident in specific abilities, public persona and show-business knowledge. The vulnerability concerns whether competence will be recognized **at the decisive point**.

## 4.3 Public opinion triggers compliance language that masks agency withdrawal

`みんなの意見に従うのが当然` is superficially civic. But the immediate sequel is defensive devaluation and retreat. S1E10 therefore warns against reading Sumire's willingness to defer as automatically healthy humility. [SI]

A future model should ask:

- Is she deferring because the task truly fits someone else better?
- Or is she using others' judgment to pre-empt the pain of being displaced?

S1E08 and S1E10 show that both are possible.

## 4.4 Defensive status reversal remains available

After being threatened with replacement, Sumire calls Love Live! a novice/amateur competition and says she can save her appearance for the final. [JT]

# 9. Institution, competition and merit

# 10. Performance dramaturgy — `ノンフィクション!!`

## 10.1 Immediate dramatic question

Before the song, the unresolved question is not “can Liella! perform?” in general. It is:

> **Can Sumire accept and publicly inhabit center after outside opinion reactivates her expectation that center will be taken away?**

## 10.2 The performance answers through enacted position

The song keeps Sumire visually central in the opening and gives her the tiara/costume differentiation established in the preceding drama. Other members remain active around her; the group does not vanish into a solo showcase. [AF]

This matters because a dialogue-only solution could still have been revoked at the stage. Performance makes the group's judgment operational.

## 10.3 Visual center is temporary role, not total narrative sovereignty

Sumire receives the center without taking over every skill or every relationship. Choreography continues to redistribute close-ups, pairings and group formations. [AF]

The visual answer is therefore compatible with S1E09's plurality:

> **one person can be center now without becoming the essence of Liella!.**

## 10.4 Lyric/drama relation

The lyric lane includes:

- wanting to shine now;
- proving impossibility false;
- breaking a poker face;
- taking someone toward a new world;
- opening a new door;
- `But this is ノンフィクション!!`.

These are strong thematic resonances with Sumire's self-presentation/recognition problem. [JT/MF]

But the song is a group performance text. Do not convert every first-person line into literal Sumire autobiography.

## 10.5 What the performance does that dialogue cannot

It converts recognition from proposition into **public fact of staging**.

Keke can say Sumire is suited. Kanon can argue history is not destiny. Ren can assess her capability. None of that alone gives Sumire the experience she has repeatedly lacked: remaining in the center when the performance actually happens.

That is the song's dramatic necessity.

# 14. Counterevidence and alternative readings

No.

The center assignment is explicitly tied to the rap task and Sumire's suitability. The episode's strongest contribution is that center **can move**.

## 14.2 Does Keke's initial low-potential judgment establish that Sumire is objectively weaker?

No.

It is Keke's character judgment, and the episode itself exists partly to revise it.

## 14.3 Does Ren's capability inventory establish an objective total ranking?

No.

Ren identifies specialized strengths. Kanon then derives a different center conclusion from the same evidence. Talent judgment is not narrator truth.

## 14.4 Is Sumire's withdrawal simply mature deference to public opinion?

No.

Her language immediately reveals an old fatalistic model—`どうせ最後はいつも私じゃなくなる`—and she defensively devalues the competition.

## 14.5 Is Sumire simply insecure in every domain?

No.

She confidently claims show-business experience and ad-lib ability and demonstrates both. Her insecurity is relationship/recognition conditioned, especially around whether important visibility will be revoked.

## 14.6 Does Keke support Sumire only because Kanon told her to?

No.

Sumire proposes that explanation; Keke rejects it and gives observation-based reasons. Her hidden need for results makes a pity-only explanation even less plausible within the episode.

## 14.7 Does Keke's concealed pressure mean her support is manipulative?

Not enough evidence.

She does hide a material personal constraint, which creates asymmetry. But she explicitly says she does not want concern for her to motivate the others, and her judgment of Sumire is tied to observed performance. Keep the ethical tension open.

## 14.8 Can the exact “return home if I do not get results” condition be treated as Japanese textual fact?

No.

That wording is present only in the paired English speech derivative for a span absent from the corrected Japanese text. The later Japanese independently authorizes **win/results pressure**, but not every detail of the English rendering.

## 14.9 Does Keke saying `すみれ` mean their rivalry is over?

No.

The address shift is real and repeated, but their rough conflict style remains part of the relation. Intimacy does not imply register homogenization or conflict disappearance.

## 14.10 Does Kanon giving center to Sumire prove Kanon has fully decentered as protagonist?

No.

Kanon remains the main semantic integrator and motive-reader. S1E10 proves only that this centrality does not require monopolizing visible center or every representational task.

## 14.11 Does Chisato's stable teamwork prove the S1E06 worthiness/dependence issue is cured?

No.

There is no fresh personal failure/dependence stress test here.

## 14.12 Does Ren's absence of solitary-control behavior under competition prove it can never recur?

No.

It is now a stronger longitudinal revision across S1E08–10, but future major failure/institutional threat remains a valid stress test.

## 14.13 Does `ノンフィクション!!` lyric equal Sumire's literal internal monologue?

No.

Lyric/drama resonance is strong, but performance text is not automatically literal biography.

## 14.14 Does pre-performance “connect with many school idols” erase competitive stakes?

No.

Keke's language makes winning/top ambition explicit. The episode deliberately allows relational and competitive performance logics to coexist.
