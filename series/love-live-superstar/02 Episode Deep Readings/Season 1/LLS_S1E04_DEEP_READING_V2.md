# Love Live! Superstar!! — S1E04 Deep Reading V2
## 第4話「街角ギャラクシー☆彡」

# 1. Governing thesis

S1E04 is an episode about **recognition, centrality, and the difference between being valued and being placed at the center**.

Heanna Sumire enters the school-idol project with a history that makes those categories difficult for her to separate. The episode opens by presenting the problem before explaining it. Sumire works around performance and entertainment spaces, is visibly alert to scouting, and interprets a talent-agency approach as the possibility that she has finally been noticed. The recruiter does notice her—but only as someone ideally suited to an unnamed passerby role:

> 「その容姿 放たれるオーラ」  
> 「あなたこそまさに通行人役にぴったり」

The structure of the joke matters. Sumire is not invisible in the literal sense. She has enough appearance, presence, and experience to be selected repeatedly. What she cannot obtain is the **kind of recognition she wants**: being identified as the person around whom attention is organized.

That distinction governs nearly everything that follows.

When she discovers school idols, Sumire initially interprets them through the same recognition economy that has shaped her professional childhood. They are, in her first formulation, effectively amateurs; Love Live offers a huge stage; Sunny Passion's public following is measurable; therefore school idols appear to offer a more accessible route to fame and center position than the professional entertainment system that has repeatedly relegated her to peripheral roles. She joins quickly once that opportunity becomes legible.

But S1E04 does not reduce this to fraudulent vanity. Sumire possesses **real performance capital**. Chisato verifies that her fundamentals are strong. She can reproduce dance material that took Keke substantial effort to learn. She has actual television/entertainment experience. Her center claim is therefore not simply fantasy. The episode makes the harder point that genuine skill can coexist with an unhealthy theory of what recognition must look like.

The center conflict then renders this theory numerical. Sumire argues that the center should be the strongest singer/dancer. Keke argues that center presence also contains less measurable qualities such as charisma. A school vote is used to settle the dispute. The visual result board is brutal:

- Kanon: **34**
- Keke: **2**
- Sumire: **0**

For Sumire, the zero does not function merely as losing a game. It reproduces the organizing trauma of her entertainment history: effort and competence fail to convert into central recognition. Her reaction—「センターになれないんだったら / こんなところいる意味ないもの」—therefore reveals the actual condition she has placed on belonging. If the group cannot certify that she is the center, remaining in the group becomes meaningless.

The episode subsequently explains why. Sumire has pursued auditions since childhood because she wanted the main role. No matter how hard she worked, she says, she ended as an expendable supporting player. She converts repetition into destiny:

> 「私はさ そういう星のもとに生まれているの」  
> 「どんなに頑張っても 真ん中で輝くことはできない」

This is the S1E04 analogue to Kanon's earlier talent/fate logic. It is **character belief, not audiovisual fact**. Indeed, the episode supplies counterevidence inside Sumire's behavior. She still walks the scouting route. She still accepts entertainment work. She still notices visibility metrics. She still carries the same shrine charm that later becomes part of Kanon's recruitment joke. The desire has not disappeared. Fatalism is a protective explanation for why repeated disappointment should no longer be allowed to surprise her.

Kanon recognizes the structure because she has recently inhabited it herself:

> 「私も歌えなかった時思ってたもん」  
> 「そういう運命なんだって 続けても無駄だって」

The decisive intervention is therefore not reassurance that Sumire is secretly already the best center. Nor is it giving her the center position to stop her leaving. Kanon's first instinct actually **is** concession—if giving Sumire the center keeps her in the group, why not do it? But after understanding what Sumire's demand means, Kanon changes strategy.

She approaches Sumire using the exact social form Sumire has been waiting for: a scout.

> 「平安名すみれさん」  
> 「私 こういう者です」  
> 「すみれさん あなたをスカウトに来ました」

Unlike the opening recruiter, Kanon names Sumire and names the specific things she wants from her: her knowledge and skill from the show-business world. The opening false recognition—*you are perfect as an unnamed passerby*—is transformed into specific relational recognition—*we need what you know and can do*.

Kanon then refuses to turn recognition into pity:

> 「センターが欲しかったら 奪いに来てよ」

This is the episode's most important leadership act so far. Kanon does not tell Sumire that center does not matter, because it matters to Sumire. She does not guarantee that Sumire will receive it, because that would reproduce unearned validation. She creates a **contestable relationship** in which Sumire's desire is legitimate, Kanon's own center role becomes something she chooses rather than passively receives, and rivalry can become a mechanism for collective improvement:

> 「競い合えば グループもきっと良くなると思うから」

Sumire's entry is therefore not completed by learning to stop wanting attention. The episode preserves her ambition, theatricality, `ギャラクシー`, show-business pride, and center desire. What changes is the social form in which those traits can operate. She can become a rival and skill-provider inside a group instead of treating membership as worthless whenever she is not immediately top-billed.

S1E04 also advances the institution story. Kanon and Keke did **not** win the festival, yet their newcomer award and public response are enough for the headmistress to authorize a school-idol association, provide a clubroom, and open the rooftop. The supposedly absolute first-place gate from S1E02 has therefore already become negotiable. Achievement still matters materially, but institutional legitimacy is not mechanically identical to winning. The headmistress later states an even broader principle:

> 「努力しようとする者からその場を奪うのが良いことだとは思いません」

The school should not take a place away from people who are trying to work hard.

This principle links institution and character. Sumire's fear is that effort which does not result in central visibility is meaningless. Yuigaoka's emerging counter-principle is that **effort can justify space before it guarantees victory**.

At the S1E04 boundary, the strongest prospective thesis is:

> **The episode separates recognition from centrality. Sumire has been repeatedly seen without receiving the role she wants, and she has responded by making center status a test of whether participation has value. Kanon recruits her by replacing anonymous/external recognition with specific relational recognition while refusing to make the center a consolation prize. The resulting group model permits ambition, rivalry, unequal roles, and belonging to coexist without declaring any one of them identical to human worth.**

# 3. Sequential dramatic architecture

## Movement I — Sumire is noticed, but not as the person she wants to be (`00:00–02:51`)

The recap of S1E03 is followed by a new pre-OP sequence centered on Sumire's entertainment work. The contrast is immediate. Kanon and Keke have just received a newcomer award for a performance in which being seen became relationally meaningful. Sumire enters an environment where being seen has long been industrial and hierarchical.

She participates in production work and is then approached by someone carrying a talent-agency business card. Her bodily/visual reaction makes the possibility of scouting salient before the dialogue confirms it. The recruiter says she wants to scout Sumire, praises her looks and aura, and then delivers the humiliating specification:

> 「あなたこそまさに通行人役にぴったり」

The visual joke and Sumire's later complaint establish that this is not an isolated category error. Her problem is recurrence.

Afterward:

> 「もう」  
> 「私ってばいつもこう」

The line is modest in wording but structurally important. She does not say *this recruiter is stupid*. She says *I am always like this*. A disappointing external event is immediately translated into a stable self-pattern.

Acoustically, the line `私ってばいつもこう` occupies approximately `00:52.20–00:54.00` at about **−25.2 dBFS RMS** in the mixed track. The following 1.17 s interval drops to about **−30.6 dBFS** before the next person addresses her. [AM] The drop is not near-silence, but it gives the self-generalization a short isolated beat before attention returns from outside.

The charm later used in the recruitment scene is already visible on Sumire's bag during the opening material (`contact_sheet_003`). This matters retrospectively within the episode: her later claim that such things do not work coexists with the fact that she already carries one.

The opening therefore establishes four Sumire variables before the school-idol plot reaches her:

1. she actively inhabits entertainment spaces;
2. she wants recognition more central than anonymous/background work;
3. she converts repeated peripheral assignment into self-explanation;
4. she continues seeking opportunity despite that explanation.

The fourth point will become essential to distinguishing pessimistic speech from actual desire.

## Movement II — Losing first place does not extinguish Kanon's school-idol desire (`02:51–04:25`)

After the OP, Sumire complains that if the agency wanted an extra it should simply have said so and reveals that she has been walking the relevant street repeatedly despite having no other reason to do so. [JT]

The story then shifts to Kanon and Keke. Their festival result has produced concrete institutional consequences even though they did not satisfy the original first-place demand:

- they receive recognition as a **同好会**;
- their follower count has increased to about 2,000;
- the headmistress gives them rooftop practice access;
- they receive a dedicated room/key.

This materially revises the S1E02 institutional model. The first-place condition functioned as a gate, but the headmistress is willing to respond to a different form of credible result. Competitive ranking matters, yet the institution is not mechanically bound to one number.

Kanon approaches Ren with a conciliatory ambition:

> 「私たち 頑張るね」  
> 「頑張って この学校の力になるような成績を収められるようにする」

She wants results that become a strength for the school and begins to imagine that this might eventually change Ren's attitude.

Ren's reply narrows her opposition further:

> 「だったら スクールアイドル以外の活動にしてください」  
> 「スクールアイドルじゃなければ / いくらでも応援してあげられますから」

Ren is not anti-Kanon, anti-effort, or anti-extracurricular activity in general. The objection is specifically **school idols**.

Kanon's answer is one of her strongest self-authored statements so far:

> 「それはできない」  
> 「私 ステージで歌って」  
> 「スクールアイドルって本当にステキだなって思った」  
> 「もっと練習して もっといいライブがしたい」  
> 「いい歌を歌いたいって思ったの」

This is a major delta from S1E02's 「本気で ちょっと…興味がある」. S1E03's actual stage experience has converted provisional interest into a chosen practice. Crucially, this occurs **after** failing to take first place. Competitive failure therefore does not function for Kanon as proof that the activity itself should end.

Ren responds with a predictive quality judgment—she does not believe they can win the current Love Live. This may be a reasonable assessment of current competitive readiness, but it remains **Ren's judgment**, not the series' declaration of total worth. [JT]

## Movement III — The inherited room and the first bare-name confirmation (`04:25–06:40`)

Kanon and Keke locate the room they have been assigned in an older part of the school complex. The visual evidence is significant: the corridor and room are visibly aged compared with the new-school spaces, and a weathered sign reads:

> 「学校アイドル部」

Kanon/Keke explicitly notice that the sign seems too old to have been newly installed by the headmistress. [AF/JT]

At the S1E04 boundary, the safest conclusion is only that **some school-idol-related room/signage predates the current association**. We do not yet know whose club it was, how it relates to Yuigaoka's institutional predecessor/history, or whether it bears on Ren's opposition. This should be carried as an institutional open question rather than prematurely solved.

The scene also verifies the address transition requested in S1E03. Keke asks:

> 「かのん 怖いのデスカ」

There is no `さん`. Across S1E04's story dialogue Keke repeatedly uses bare `かのん` (`かのんがいいデス`, `かのんのほうが…`) and does not return to `かのんさん`. [JT]

This is the first canonical post-request evidence that Kanon's S1E03 request—「かのんって呼んでよ」—has been accepted. The relationship ledger can therefore move this from OPEN to **confirmed uptake**.

Sumire arrives saying she has a little interest in school idols. Her phrasing is cautious:

> 「その 実はちょっと興味があって スクールアイドルに」

Keke immediately interprets it as membership desire and tries to move her straight onto the form:

> 「今日からあなたもスクールアイドルデス」

Kanon intervenes:

> 「可可ちゃん…」  
> 「ちょっと待とうか」

This is useful relationship-conditioned evidence. Keke's recruitment enthusiasm continues to outrun the other person's stated commitment; Kanon increasingly functions as a boundary/pace regulator rather than merely a fellow recruit.

## Movement IV — Sumire chooses school idols as a route to visibility (`06:40–08:31`)

Sumire studies school-idol material and initially classifies the institution in professional-status terms:

> 「スクールアイドルとは 学校でアイドル活動を行っている」  
> 「要するにアマチュアみたいなものね」

Then she sees the scale of the Love Live final stage and asks the question that matters to her:

> 「ここに立てば もちろん有名になれるわよね」

Keke confirms the visibility potential and cites Sunny Passion's current follower count. Sumire reacts:

> 「9万8千…」  
> 「ギャラクシー」  
> 「やるわ」  
> 「やるわったらやってやるわ スクールアイドル」

At entry, then, her motive is not yet the same as Kanon's or Keke's. She sees a channel through which she might finally obtain a large central audience.

This should not be simplified to “Sumire is unserious.” Her seriousness is directed toward **show-business success and center recognition**, and she initially underestimates school idols as a professional hierarchy. Those are different propositions.

Training quickly establishes that Sumire's confidence is not empty. Chisato finds that her fundamentals are already strong. Sumire explains that she spent time in show business and appeared on television. She can execute increasingly demanding movements without the beginner struggle that characterized Keke.

Keke immediately interprets this as destiny for Love Live victory. Sumire, having watched prior winners, says she thinks they may be able to win.

The episode therefore gives Sumire both an instrumental motive and real domain-transferable competence. Any model that treats her as merely boastful would already fail the evidence.

## Movement V — What is a center: skill, charisma, history, or public recognition? (`08:31–11:06`)

The addition of a third performer creates a structural question that did not exist when Kanon/Keke were a pair: **who stands at the center?**

Keke quickly chooses Kanon:

> 「かのんがいいデス」

Sumire objects that the strongest singer/dancer should naturally stand at the center if the aim is to win:

> 「先とか後とか関係ないでしょ」  
> 「勝つためには実力がある人が中心に立つ」  
> 「それが当然なんじゃない」

`先とか後とか` is important. Sumire rejects **order of entry / incumbency** as a legitimate basis for hierarchy. The fact that Kanon was there first should not settle centrality.

Keke replies that center quality includes something beyond visible technique:

> 「センターというのはそれだけではありません」  
> 「カリスマ性のような見えない力も必要デス」

Sumire's challenge is epistemically fair:

> 「そんなものどうやって計るのです？」

The episode answers by turning charisma into a schoolwide popularity vote. This is narratively efficient but analytically imperfect. Public preference is not a neutral measure of charisma, skill, or future center performance. Kanon and Keke have just become publicly visible through the festival and their follower increase, while Sumire has only just joined. The vote therefore carries strong familiarity/incumbency confounds.

The result board (`000570_shot-change_00-10-08.566.jpg`) reads:

| Candidate | Votes |
|---|---:|
| かのん | **34** |
| 可可 | **2** |
| すみれ | **0** |

[AF]

The zero is the central wound. Sumire had reason to believe she could compete on technique. Instead, a test framed as measuring suitability produces the same result as her childhood entertainment history: no one chooses her for the central role.

She protests that her singing and dancing were not worse. Kanon points out that everyone voted after seeing their appeal performances. Keke attributes the result to less tangible `オーラ` / `華` and says Kanon looks more center-like.

Again, these are **character interpretations**. The vote proves only how this electorate voted under these circumstances. It does not objectively establish that Kanon is metaphysically the correct center.

Sumire then says simply:

> 「やめる」

and explains:

> 「センターになれないんだったら」  
> 「こんなところいる意味ないもの」

Her membership condition is now explicit: participation without centrality does not satisfy the reason she entered.

## Movement VI — Kanon's first response is self-sacrificial concession; Keke resists (`11:06–12:37`)

After Sumire leaves, Kanon's first solution is straightforward:

> 「だって センター任せるって言えば」  
> 「すみれちゃんやめないんだよ」  
> 「だったら任せようよ」

This is consistent with a recurring Kanon tendency: when her own status appears to impose a cost on another person, she is willing to remove or demote herself to solve the conflict.

Keke rejects the idea:

> 「センターはスクールアイドルの憧れ」  
> 「誇りを持つべきデスよ」

Keke's stance has two effects. First, it protects the center from becoming mere appeasement currency. Second, it forces Kanon to ask the better question:

> 「でも すみれちゃんどうしてそこまでこだわるのかな」

This is a major improvement in Kanon's relational reasoning. Rather than solve the conflict by surrendering the contested object, she begins to investigate **what the object means to the other person**.

That question drives the second half of the episode.

## Movement VII — Sumire's theatrical surface and shrine/private history (`12:37–16:13`)

Kanon notices Sumire still moving through the entertainment/scouting environment. A stranger asks Sumire for directions; Sumire responds:

> 「スカウトじゃないなら 声かけないで」

The line is comic and abrasive, but it reveals how continuously the possibility of being selected occupies her attention.

Kanon then sees a deliberately unglamorous part of Sumire's entertainment work: the `グソクムシ` performance. Sumire's mortification at being seen by Kanon triggers an extended shrine comedy in which she attempts to erase Kanon's memory through improvised ritual.

The comedy has a serious structural function. It moves the relationship from **Sumire as self-branded aspiring star** to **Sumire as someone whose embarrassing labor Kanon has witnessed**.

Kanon learns that Sumire is associated with the shrine. The same red shrine charm visible on Sumire's bag earlier is part of this local/family environment.

When the comedy exhausts itself, Sumire gives the first sustained account of her history:

> 「私ね 小さい頃からずっと いろんなオーディション受けてたの」  
> 「主役に憧れて」  
> 「子役の頃から 一生懸命頑張って」  
> 「でも どんなに頑張っても」  
> 「いつも最後はどうでもいい脇役」

The retained visual sequence (`contact_sheet_028`) literalizes **center/periphery**. Child Sumire appears in tree/background/animal or other supporting roles while the imagined/desired central performer occupies the privileged visual position. [AF]

Kanon asks whether that history is why she wanted school-idol center. Sumire admits that she thought an amateur domain might be easier, then says the center vote taught her otherwise.

Her conclusion:

> 「私はさ そういう星のもとに生まれているの」

is followed by a **2.13-second** interval whose mixed-track RMS falls to approximately **−39.1 dBFS**, compared with about **−23.8 dBFS** during the preceding line—a drop of roughly **15.4 dB**. [AM]

Only after that withdrawal does she complete the fatalistic proposition:

> 「どんなに頑張っても 真ん中で輝くことはできない」

The soundtrack therefore gives the destiny claim temporal/acoustic isolation rather than treating it as ordinary banter.

But the episode has already given counterevidence to the literal truth of her fatalism. A person who truly has no expectation left would not need to walk a scouting route every day, react intensely to a talent-agency card, seek a new center route, or keep working performance jobs. Sumire's speech is better modeled as **protective fatalism after repeated rejection** than as absence of aspiration.

## Movement VIII — Keke defends school-idol seriousness; Sumire proves her professional skill (`16:13–18:10`)

When Kanon reports Sumire's remark that she thought school idols might be an easier/amateur route, Keke reacts strongly:

> 「それはスクールアイドルに対する侮辱デス」  
> 「冒涜デス」

Her anger escalates into exaggerated punishment fantasies and the rough-edged summons:

> 「お昼休みに屋上に来やがれデス」  
> 「いいから 来やがれデス」

This is a new Keke voice state: the stylized `デス` remains, but it is combined with `来やがれ`, a deliberately rough/aggressive imperative. It should be modeled as context-specific comic anger/defense of a sacredly valued domain, not as her baseline politeness level.

On the roof, Keke tells Sumire that school idols take the stage seriously and objects to treating them as an easier substitute. Sumire answers not with theory but with performance competence. She executes the dance Keke worked hard to learn.

Keke recognizes the fact:

> 「可可があれだけ練習したダンスを…」

Sumire then reframes the comparison:

> 「ショービジネスの世界を甘く見ないで」  
> 「これくらいはできるの」  
> 「ただ それでも私にスポットは当たらない」  
> 「こんなアマチュアの世界でもね」

Keke responds:

> 「アマチュアではないデス」

Taken literally as employment classification, that claim is questionable: school idols are students rather than professional entertainers in the conventional sense. But Keke is not making a labor-law classification. She is rejecting `アマチュア` as a **pejorative for low seriousness or low effort**. The scene therefore reveals a semantic conflict:

- Sumire uses professional/amateur to describe status hierarchy and expected difficulty;
- Keke hears amateur as devaluation of commitment and labor.

Neither girl's wording should be silently converted into the other's meaning.

## Movement IX — The headmistress makes effort a claim to institutional space (`18:10–19:10`)

Ren asks the headmistress why the rooftop has been opened to the school-idol association. The headmistress replies simply that they had nowhere else to practice.

Ren objects, but the headmistress states:

> 「努力しようとする者からその場を奪うのが良いことだとは思いません」

This is perhaps the clearest institutional principle the series has articulated through S1E04. It does not promise success or endorsement. It says that **people trying to work should not be deprived of the place in which effort can occur**.

The line revises the meritocratic pressure established in S1E02. Results can matter to recognition and resource allocation, but effort also produces a claim to basic institutional space.

The headmistress then asks:

> 「そう言うと思いませんか あなたのお母さんも」

This is notable beside S1E02, where she told Ren that her mother was irrelevant to present authority. The two statements are not necessarily contradictory. S1E02 denies that maternal status gives Ren a veto in current governance; S1E04 invokes the mother's presumed values as a moral standard. **Inherited authority and inherited ethical memory are being separated.**

Ren's deeper relation to her mother remains sealed/open.

## Movement X — Kanon recognizes Sumire's fate logic because it resembles her own (`19:10–20:04`)

This is the first explicit cross-character use of Kanon's own failure as an interpretive resource. Her earlier pain is no longer only something others help her survive; it gives her a way to recognize another person's defensive theory.

Someone in the group answers, in effect, that Kanon learned it was not true and therefore has to tell Sumire. The scene identifies Kanon as the person currently best positioned to understand her.

The episode then cuts to Sumire in the rain beside a public screen. `Tiny Stars`—the song/performance that marked Kanon and Keke's S1E03 relational breakthrough—is being publicly replayed/broadcast as the newcomer-award performance. [AF/JT/MF]

This changes the song's dramatic status. In S1E03 it was the internal culmination of Kanon/Keke's shared stage relation. In S1E04 it has become **publicly circulating evidence**: other people can encounter it, and Sumire watches it precisely while telling herself that she is not someone who can occupy the center.

Her line:

> 「やっぱり私じゃ…」

remains incomplete, but its direction is clear from the established context: the performance has not automatically cured her fatalistic self-classification.

The rain/umbrella imagery keeps her visually isolated while the screen displays a successful two-person performance. This sets up the episode's final intervention.

## Movement XI — The real scout names Sumire and recruits her skill (`20:04–21:44`)

Kanon appears and deliberately adopts a formal recruitment performance:

> 「平安名すみれさん」  
> 「私 こういう者です」  
> 「すみれさん あなたをスカウトに来ました」

The recurrence with the opening scout scene is exact enough to treat as **FR** rather than loose thematic similarity.

Opening recruiter:

> notices Sumire → praises aura → assigns anonymous passerby.

Kanon:

> addresses Sumire by full name → explicitly scouts her → identifies her show-business knowledge and technical skill as things the group needs.

The episode therefore repairs the recognition problem without pretending the external entertainment history never happened.

Kanon explains that they need results in order to continue as school idols and asks for Sumire's cooperation. Sumire begins to repeat her withdrawal, but Kanon interrupts with a different proposition:

> 「センターが欲しかったら 奪いに来てよ」

The line is followed by a **1.75-second** lower-energy interval at approximately **−33.8 dBFS RMS**, compared with about **−20.9 dBFS** during the line itself. [AM] The challenge is given space before Kanon explains why she is making it.

Her explanation is crucial:

> 「すみれちゃんを見て 私思った」  
> 「センターやってみようって」  
> 「だから 奪いに来てよ」  
> 「競い合えば グループもきっと良くなると思うから」

This is not merely recruitment of Sumire. Sumire's desire causes a change in Kanon. Kanon had been willing to give the center away; after understanding Sumire, she **chooses to inhabit the center strongly enough to make it worth contesting**.

The roles are co-created:

- Sumire gives Kanon a reason to claim center;
- Kanon gives Sumire a rival rather than a consolation prize;
- the group gains an internal competitive mechanism that is not identical to external Love Live ranking.

This is the strongest evidence so far that Kanon's other-oriented perceptiveness may develop into actual leadership rather than merely protective intervention. The claim should still remain provisional, but “leadership” is now better supported than it was in S1E01.

## Movement XII — The charm exposes the gap between Sumire's fatalism and her continued hope (`21:44–22:14`)

Sumire protects her pride by converting Kanon's `scout` language into a contractual joke:

> 「いくら出すのよ」  
> 「スカウトって言うなら当然契約金は必要よ」

Kanon/Keke produce a small red charm from Sumire's family's shrine as the mock signing consideration. Sumire says it does not work.

The visual then reveals that **the same charm is already attached to Sumire's own bag**. Keke observes:

> 「同じデスね」

This is one of the episode's best small pieces of character evidence. Sumire can verbally dismiss the charm's efficacy while already carrying the identical object. As with walking the scout route, the behavior does not fit a person who has truly ceased hoping.

After `同じデスね`, the mixed track enters a roughly **2.92-second** lower-energy interval at about **−31.4 dBFS**, compared with about **−20.4 dBFS** during Keke's line. [AM]

Kanon then says:

> 「でも まだわからないよ」  
> 「諦めないかぎり 夢が待っているのは」  
> 「まだずっと先かもしれないんだから」

This is carefully different from “your dream will definitely come true.” Kanon does not promise outcome. She attacks Sumire's certainty of impossibility.

The epistemic structure is:

> **failure so far does not establish permanent destiny.**

That is precisely the lesson Kanon is qualified to offer because her own S1E01–S1E03 arc has not been a guarantee of effortless future success; it has been evidence that a self-classification formed under failure can be wrong.

The rain then clears and the girls explicitly notice it:

> 「晴れマシタ！」  
> 「夏だ！」

The weather change is an obvious formal accompaniment to the reopening of possibility. It should not be treated as proof that Sumire's conflict is permanently solved.

## Movement XIII — Integration preserves Sumire's pride rather than sanding it down (`22:14–end`)

The final practice scene refuses an assimilation story in which Sumire becomes acceptable only by abandoning the traits that caused conflict.

> 「今日から私が教えてあげる」  
> 「本物のショービジネスの世界を」  
> 「ギャラクシー！」

She remains theatrical. She remains proud of professional knowledge. She remains ambitious. What has changed is that those traits can now become **contribution**.

This is a better ensemble foundation than “Sumire learns to be humble.” Her professional background becomes a resource the group explicitly needs, while the center remains contestable.

The ED lyrics' recurring language of choosing, not giving up, and continuing toward a distant dream is compatible with the episode's ending, but the ED remains production-level thematic framing rather than literal autobiography of Sumire alone.

# 4. Character development

## 4.1 Heanna Sumire — from centrality as admission price to rivalry as belonging

S1E04 is the first episode with enough evidence to build a substantial Sumire model.

### Stated/enacted desire

Sumire wants central visibility. She has wanted main roles since childhood, repeatedly auditioned, and continued working around entertainment despite being assigned peripheral roles. School idols attract her because they appear to provide a route to a large stage and fame.

### Core wound at this boundary

Repeated effort has not produced the kind of recognition she values. The result is not lack of ambition but a theory that she is **fated for periphery**.

### Defensive strategies

- theatrical self-branding (`ギャラクシー`);
- aggressive dismissal before another person can disappoint her (`スカウトじゃないなら 声かけないで`);
- status comparison (professional vs amateur);
- quitting when center is denied;
- fatalistic generalization (`そういう星のもと`);
- humor/violence parody around embarrassing exposure at the shrine;
- contractual joking when emotionally recruited.

### Demonstrated capacities

- real dance fundamentals;
- rapid choreography uptake;
- entertainment-industry familiarity;
- comfort with public self-display when she controls the frame;
- persistence in pursuing visibility despite repeated disappointment;
- ability to teach/contribute technical knowledge.

### Critical modeling distinction

Sumire is **not simply attention-seeking because she lacks skill**. Her skill and her recognition hunger coexist. The latter is partly a response to a history in which the former failed to obtain the role she wanted.

### Current unresolved question

Can Sumire remain invested when she is neither guaranteed center nor protected from losing? S1E04 creates the possibility by converting center into an ongoing rivalry rather than a one-time certification.

---

## 4.2 Shibuya Kanon — chosen school-idol identity and adaptive relational leadership

S1E04 strengthens two Kanon trajectories.

First, she now explicitly wants school-idol practice itself. Ren offers a conceptual exit—do something else and she will support it—and Kanon refuses because performing changed what she wants. This is much stronger than merely supporting Keke.

Second, Kanon demonstrates a more sophisticated intervention pattern with Sumire than in earlier episodes.

Her first instinct is still self-sacrificial: give Sumire center if that prevents her leaving. But she does not stop there. She asks why Sumire needs the role, learns the history, recognizes the fatalistic mechanism from her own experience, and changes the intervention.

The final recruitment is tailored to Sumire:

- **recognition:** scouts her by name;
- **competence:** asks for specific professional knowledge/skill;
- **dignity:** does not pity her;
- **ambition:** validates center desire as something worth contesting;
- **agency:** requires Sumire to choose to return and compete;
- **group benefit:** explicitly frames rivalry as a way to improve the whole group.

This is now credible evidence for emerging leadership. It is still relationship-specific and should not yet be generalized into a complete leader archetype.

---

## 4.3 Tang Keke — intimacy uptake, sacred-domain defense, and boundary pressure

Keke confirms Kanon's requested bare-name shift: she now uses `かのん` repeatedly and no longer uses `かのんさん` in S1E04 story dialogue.

Her school-idol identity also deepens. Sumire's amateur/easy-route framing does not merely annoy her; Keke treats it as insult/blasphemy. This shows how morally/affectively invested she has become in the seriousness of school-idol labor.

At the same time, Keke continues to have a **pace/consent blind spot** during recruitment. Sumire says she is only a little interested and wants information; Keke nearly turns that directly into an application. Kanon has to slow her down.

Keke's center theory is relationally biased but conceptually important: technical skill alone is not sufficient; charisma/`華` matters. Her belief that Kanon is the center is inseparable from the fact that Kanon is already her `スター` / `夢` from S1E03, so it should not be treated as neutral measurement.

---

## 4.4 Arashi Chisato — specialist support and non-membership continue

Chisato remains a non-member support specialist. She helps assess Sumire's dance fundamentals and supports Kanon's center suitability while continuing to spend significant time assisting the group.

Kanon again apologizes for consuming Chisato's time. Chisato replies:

> 「2人のために力になりたいの」  
> 「気にしないで」

This preserves the S1E03 distinction: Chisato can choose dance as her own primary path while still giving substantial support to friends. Support is not evidence that she has secretly abandoned her own priority.

## 4.5 Hazuki Ren — opposition remains domain-specific; maternal inheritance grows more complicated

Ren tells Kanon she could support them fully if their activity were anything except school idols. This is stronger evidence that the objection is specific rather than a generalized control drive.

Her conversation with the headmistress also adds a new maternal layer. The headmistress denies Ren the right to remove practice space and invokes what Ren's mother would presumably think about depriving people who are trying to work.

This does not tell us why Ren opposes school idols. It does tell us that her mother's memory can be mobilized **against** Ren's current exclusionary position, not only by Ren in support of it.

# 5. Relationship systems

## 5.1 Kanon ↔ Sumire — recognition through challenge

This relationship begins with curiosity and comic intrusion, then becomes the episode's central dyad.

Kanon understands Sumire because she recognizes the **form** of her fatalism, not because their circumstances are identical. Kanon's own “I have no talent / this is my fate” history becomes interpretive empathy.

The final intervention avoids two failures:

- pity: “you deserve center because you've suffered”;
- dismissal: “center shouldn't matter to you.”

Instead it offers contest:

> **I see what you can do; I see what you want; come take it.**

Sumire's pride is therefore made compatible with membership rather than treated as something the relationship must eliminate.

## 5.2 Keke ↔ Sumire — sacred practice versus professional hierarchy

Keke initially sees a recruit; Sumire initially sees an accessible fame vehicle. Their conflict is partly semantic and partly ethical.

Keke: school idols are serious because people invest feeling and labor in them.  
Sumire: school idols are amateur relative to professional entertainment structures.

Sumire's instant dance competence forces Keke to confront real professional skill. Keke's refusal to accept devaluation forces Sumire to confront the seriousness of the practice she entered instrumentally.

Neither simply disproves the other in S1E04.

## 5.3 Kanon ↔ Keke — intimacy confirmed; functions differentiate

Keke's bare-name `かのん` confirms the S1E03 intimacy request.

Functionally, the two also differentiate:

- Keke accelerates recruitment, protects the symbolic dignity of school idols, and articulates charisma;
- Kanon regulates pace, investigates motive, and designs relationship-specific compromise/challenge.

This division of labor is becoming more analytically useful than calling either girl simply “the leader.”

## 5.4 Headmistress ↔ Ren — authority versus inherited moral memory

The headmistress again has superior formal authority. But unlike S1E02's “your mother is irrelevant here,” S1E04 uses the mother's presumed ethic to challenge Ren.

The emerging relation is not anti-tradition versus tradition. It is a dispute over **what the inherited tradition requires**.

# 6. Institution, school, and competition

## 6.1 The first-place gate is already negotiable

Kanon/Keke do not win first place. Nevertheless, special-award recognition and public response produce institutional access.

This means:

> competition result affects institutional legitimacy, but no single ranking fully determines it.

The headmistress is willing to recognize meaningful performance short of the original explicit target.

## 6.2 Effort produces a claim to space

The rooftop line establishes a distinct institutional ethic:

> people trying to work should not be deprived of a place to do so.

This does not abolish standards. It supplies the minimum condition under which standards can even be pursued.

## 6.3 The old 「学校アイドル部」 room is an unresolved institutional anomaly

The room appears to predate the current association. Because Yuigaoka is itself presented as new but historically inherited, this old club marker could become important. At S1E04, however, any explanation beyond “pre-existing school-idol-related infrastructure/signage exists” is speculative.

## 6.4 Center selection exposes competing merit theories

Sumire: technical ability.  
Keke: charisma/visible and invisible star quality.  
Group/electorate: popularity vote.  
Kanon at ending: ongoing rivalry and improvement.

The episode does not present a single objective center metric.

The 34/2/0 vote should particularly **not** be treated as proof that Sumire lacks center capacity. She is a newly arrived candidate competing against two girls whose previous live is already circulating publicly.

# 7. Cohort and succession state

The correct state remains **pre-cohort / founding-formation**.

S1E04 is the first episode to convert the two-person project into a three-member group possibility. That produces the first internal hierarchy question—center—and immediately demonstrates that membership order, skill, public recognition, and symbolic authority may conflict.

Important prospective lesson:

> expansion creates governance questions that do not exist in a dyad.

Sumire explicitly rejects `先とか後とか` as a basis for center selection. This is early evidence that joining later need not imply accepting permanently subordinate artistic status.

No broader generation/succession claim is justified yet.

# 8. Performance dramaturgy

S1E04 contains no new full dramatic insert performance comparable to S1E03's `Tiny Stars`; performance is instead distributed across **work, rehearsal, mediated replay, and selection tests**.

## 8.1 Entertainment labor as performance evidence

Sumire's background roles and `グソクムシ` work establish a crucial difference between performance and recognition. She performs frequently; what she lacks is central billing.

## 8.2 Appeal/election performance

The center election turns everyday school space into a mini-audition. The result is explicitly public and numerical. For Sumire, this recreates audition logic inside the supposedly easier school-idol domain.

## 8.3 Roof dance confrontation

Sumire's execution of Keke's hard-learned dance is performance-as-argument. She does not persuade Keke verbally that professional training matters; she demonstrates embodied competence.

## 8.4 `Tiny Stars` as mediated object

The prior episode's song is replayed on a public screen while Sumire watches in the rain. Performance now does something new: it has become evidence that circulates beyond the performers and can exert pressure on an outsider's self-understanding.

Required V2 question—what can performance do that dialogue could not?

> It makes Kanon/Keke's prior effort publicly observable to Sumire. Kanon's later argument against fatalism is therefore not only verbal reassurance; Sumire has just encountered a concrete artifact of two beginners acting despite uncertainty.

# 9. Japanese dialogue and voice

## 9.1 Keke's bare-name uptake

S1E03 left open whether Keke would actually adopt Kanon's requested `かのん`.

S1E04 confirms it:

- `かのん 怖いのデスカ`
- `かのんがいいデス`
- `かのんのほうが可可やあなたより…`

No `かのんさん` appears in the S1E04 story dialogue. This is a genuine address-system transition and should be preserved in simulation.

## 9.2 Sumire's emphatic `ったら` pattern

S1E04 repeatedly gives Sumire self-dramatizing emphatic constructions:

- `やるわったらやってやるわ`
- `納得できないったらできないの`
- `いくら出すったら出すのよ`

This is now a candidate voice signature, but it should be modeled by pragmatic state—assertion, frustration, theatrical insistence—not inserted mechanically into every line.

## 9.3 `ギャラクシー`

The exclamation appears around moments of intensified self-branding/excitement: the opening, the visibility/follower realization, and the final training scene. It is a genuine signature expression, but S1E04 also provides long stretches of serious speech without it. A faithful model must preserve both modes.

## 9.4 Sumire's public/private contrast

Public/self-presentational language is bold, dramatic, status-aware. Shrine disclosure becomes comparatively sustained and explanatory:

> `私ね 小さい頃からずっと…`

Fatalism then becomes concise and absolute:

> `そういう星のもとに生まれているの`

This contrast matters more than catchphrase imitation.

## 9.5 Keke's anger register

`来やがれデス` combines a rough imperative with her stylized `デス`. It is evidence that strong anger can produce deliberately coarse grammar without erasing her existing speech stylization.

## 9.6 Kanon's strategic formal mimicry

`平安名すみれさん / 私 こういう者です / スカウトに来ました` is unusually formal/theatrical relative to Kanon's ordinary peer speech. The register is chosen because it mirrors the social script Sumire longs to receive. This is evidence of **pragmatic register adaptation**, not a new baseline.

## 10.1 Center/periphery is literal, not only metaphorical

Sumire's childhood sequence repeatedly places her in visual/background roles while the desired star occupies center. The episode's central psychological distinction is therefore built into blocking and costume history.

# 12. Counterevidence and alternative readings

## 12.1 Is Sumire simply vain?

There is real evidence for vanity/status hunger: fame metrics excite her, she wants center, and she quits when denied it.

But “simply vain” fails because:

- she has a long history of serious audition effort;
- she has actual performance skill;
- she continues doing humiliating/peripheral entertainment work;
- she carries the shrine charm while claiming hope is futile;
- her pain is specifically tied to repeated failure of effort to produce central recognition.

The narrower reading is **status hunger built around a genuine competence/recognition wound**.

## 12.2 Does the 34/2/0 vote prove Kanon should be center?

No. It proves a specific electorate's preference after Kanon/Keke already had public exposure and Sumire had only just joined. It is evidence about social recognition, not an objective total-skill ranking.

## 12.3 Is Keke correct that school idols are “not amateurs”?

As a statement about professional status, not necessarily. As a rejection of `amateur` meaning unserious/easy, her position is clearly supported by the labor shown across S1E02–04.

## 12.4 Is Kanon's final recruitment pure altruism?

No. Kanon explicitly says competition with Sumire can make the group better, and Sumire's presence motivates Kanon herself to try being center. The intervention is reciprocal and strategically useful as well as caring.

## 12.5 Has Sumire learned that center does not matter?

No. The episode deliberately preserves the center desire. The change is from **center-or-no-membership** toward **center-as-contestable ambition inside membership**.

## 12.6 Has Ren softened?

Not clearly. She remains specifically opposed to school idols. The headmistress, not Ren, articulates the effort/space principle. Ren's response and deeper motive remain open.
