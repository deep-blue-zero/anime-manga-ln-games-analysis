# Love Live! Superstar!! — S2E05 Deep Reading V2
## `マニーは天下の回りもの` / `Money Makes the World Go 'Round`

# 1. Governing thesis

S2E05 demonstrates that **formal entry does not end the senior/junior access problem; it relocates the problem inside Liella!**.

Kinako, Mei, and Shiki are now genuine members. They train with the second-years, reveal real individual strengths, are praised for those strengths, and are explicitly told that beginners are not supposed to arrive already equal to their seniors. Yet almost immediately the three first-years begin comparing themselves collectively against the second-years and ask whether they can possibly become like them within one year.

That shift matters. S2E01's barrier was external:

> **Liella!'s visible excellence makes outsiders feel unqualified to enter.**

S2E05 reveals the internal continuation:

> **after entry, the same excellence can become a benchmark against which juniors interpret normal developmental difference as evidence that they are holding the group back.**

The episode therefore does not permit the easy conclusion that recruitment has solved succession. Membership gives the juniors access to the practice system; it does not automatically give them a healthy model of developmental asymmetry.

S2E05 simultaneously tests the leadership architecture created in S2E04. Kanon remains capable of making the episode's strongest integrative value judgment. When someone proposes protecting the first-years by having only the second-years perform at the school festival, Kanon immediately objects:

> `それは ダメだと思う。`
>
> `だって この８人で`
>
> `学校のみんなの前で歌うんでしょ？`

Chisato then endorses the principle while translating it into practical governance:

> `できる範囲でいいから`
>
> `完璧じゃなくても いいと思うの。`
>
> `大切なのは ８人で 学校のみんなに`

This is the clearest evidence yet that **Kanon's informal centrality and Chisato's formal presidency can coexist without either becoming redundant**. Kanon often names relational or representational meaning; Chisato owns training design, load regulation, and the formal decision rights of the club president.

The ending makes that separation explicit. The first-years ask to train separately over summer because practicing beside the second-years makes them feel as though they will remain a burden. Chisato, not Kanon, makes the institutional decision:

> `分かった。部長として許可します。`

She explains the decision autobiographically:

> `…私も そうだったから。`

The sentence connects S2E05 leadership to Chisato's Season-1 history without erasing the ethical complication of that history. Chisato once turned independent achievement into a worthiness test. Here she does **not** demand that the juniors prove themselves alone. She grants a self-requested experiment and asks to see what they discover by summer's end. Whether that experiment is healthy remains OPEN because the request has been manipulated by Natsumi and is partly grounded in senior-comparison anxiety.

That manipulation is the episode's second governing system.

Natsumi enters Liella!'s orbit not as a school-idol aspirant but as a self-branded producer/CEO seeking monetizable attention. She offers production help under a contract that nominally charges no fee while assigning the video's income to her company as production costs. She repeatedly frames Liella!'s popularity as a revenue opportunity, conceals the scale of that revenue, flees when confronted, privately calls the group easier to handle than expected, and finally exploits the first-years' fear of visible skill inequality in order to separate them from the seniors:

> `分断成功。あとは 夏美の思うがまま。`

At the S2E05 seal, this is not merely comic greed. It is a directly attested pattern of **instrumental relationship management**: Natsumi studies other people's incentives and insecurities, then reorganizes social conditions to preserve her own access to monetizable content.

Yet the episode also refuses to make the surrounding group naive in one undifferentiated way. Sumire identifies the commercial problem quickly and insists that the arrangement is unacceptable by show-business standards. Ren investigates projected revenue. Kanon does not deny their evidence; she says instead that she suspects there is some reason behind Natsumi's behavior and asks the group to observe a little longer. That is a continuation of Kanon's motive-sensitive leadership, but here it acquires a sharper risk:

> **charitable motive-seeking can preserve relational openness after wrongdoing, but it can also delay a necessary boundary when the other person is actively exploiting that openness.**

The strongest sealed S2E05 thesis is therefore:

> **Succession is not solved by adding juniors; it requires a way to live with unequal experience without converting inequality into either exclusion or self-separation. S2E05 gives Liella! a genuinely distributed leadership structure—Kanon supplies integrative meaning while Chisato exercises formal and training authority—but then shows how the juniors' internalized comparison pressure can be exploited. Natsumi enters as an external producer whose monetization logic converts attention, trust, and developmental insecurity into resources. The episode ends with a formally legitimate junior autonomy experiment whose motive is already contaminated by manipulation, making the next test not simply whether the first-years can improve, but whether self-directed separation can remain self-authored when another person engineered the conditions under which it was requested.**

# 3. Naive structural read

## 3.1 Recap and immediate eight-member normalization

The recap closes the S2E04 problem: Mei and Shiki are now members, and the narration states that Liella! has become eight. The school immediately begins treating Kinako, Mei, and Shiki as a recognizable first-year subset. A classmate even imagines that one of the three might someday be center.

Kinako's answer is revealing: the three still have far to go before reaching the seniors. The episode therefore opens by coupling membership success to renewed comparison.

## 3.2 Natsumi's attention economy

Natsumi sees Liella!'s follower growth while her own videos are not gaining views. The episode then repeatedly returns to her equation of visibility with `マニー`.

Nothing at this boundary establishes why money matters to her beyond the directly visible facts that she is working, creating/editing content, and aggressively seeking revenue. Do not import a later motive.

## 3.3 Eight-member practice exposes heterogeneous junior strengths

Chisato has revised the practice menu for the expanded club and says she tried to create starting points appropriate to each first-year.

The practice sequence refuses to make the juniors one undifferentiated weak bloc:

- Shiki handles the physical work unexpectedly well enough to prompt questions about prior experience;
- Mei reveals enough keyboard familiarity to play `Tiny Stars` and enough idol familiarity that the others recognize prior private practice;
- Kinako is revealed to be accumulating lyric fragments in a notebook.

The first-years repeatedly minimize these capacities. The seniors answer by normalizing development rather than pretending the gap is absent.

## 3.4 Internalized comparison follows immediately

After the reassuring exchange, the three juniors privately return to the same conclusion:

> `やっぱり ２年生は すごい人ばかり…`

and Mei asks whether they can become like them by this time next year. Shiki answers:

> `到底 無理。`

The scene is central because no senior is currently excluding them. The hierarchy has become **self-measured**.

## 3.5 Natsumi approaches Liella! as a producer

Liella! initially assumes another first-year has come to join, which itself shows how S2E04 changed expectations. Natsumi instead introduces herself formally as CEO of `株式会社オニナッツ` and proposes producing Liella!'s online content.

Ren and Kinako can see practical upside: delegating promotion could free time for practice. Sumire immediately asks about cost.

The contract says no compensation fee will be charged, but video income is assigned to Natsumi's company as actual production costs. Kanon accepts because the group is not pursuing the activity for revenue. The episode later makes clear that Natsumi has not transparently foregrounded the likely scale of her financial upside.

## 3.6 Filming ordinary life humanizes the seniors to the juniors

Natsumi's first concept is `「Liella！」の日常`. The group plays cards and games rather than staging a polished idol performance.

For the first-years, this matters independently of Natsumi's profit motive. Kinako and Mei observe that the seniors are surprisingly ordinary outside practice. The everyday footage therefore temporarily reduces prestige distance.

But the reduction is fragile. The next hard practice immediately restores the juniors' perception of a large ability gap.

## 3.7 Competition and the school festival create a role-allocation dilemma

With district preliminaries approaching and Sunny Passion still a major opponent, the group anticipates a demanding summer. The school festival also creates a second performance obligation.

Someone proposes reducing load by having only the second-years perform at the festival. Kanon rejects that solution because the relevant dramatic/institutional task is not only competitive optimization; the school is expecting the eight-member Liella!.

Chisato agrees while explicitly lowering the requirement from perfection to feasible eight-member participation.

## 3.8 Sumire and Ren identify Natsumi's concealed upside

Sumire confronts Natsumi about exploiting the group for money. Ren has independently checked the projected revenue. Natsumi cannot supply a clean answer and flees.

When asked, Kanon does not deny the evidence. She asks the group to wait and see because she suspects there may be a reason behind Natsumi's behavior.

## 3.9 Natsumi discovers the first-year comparison vulnerability

Kinako, Mei, and Shiki tell Natsumi that if fans see their singing/dancing beside the second-years, the skill difference will be obvious. Kinako fears they will be laughed at.

Natsumi recognizes this as an opportunity. Her `思いつきましたの` is the explicit pivot from observing insecurity to planning around it.

## 3.10 First-years request summer separation

The three club first-years ask to work separately over summer. Their stated reason is that beside the seniors they feel they will keep getting in the way; they want to look at themselves as first-years.

The seniors initially fear they may have caused offense. Chisato listens and grants permission as president, explicitly identifying with the desire for a period of self-directed growth.

The juniors accept the challenge.

## 3.11 Ending recontextualization

Immediately afterward Natsumi privately states:

> `分断成功。あとは 夏美の思うがまま。`

The scene does not retroactively make the juniors' concern fake. Their comparison anxiety existed before Natsumi exploited it. What changes is the authority state of the decision: **a real junior concern has been strategically amplified and routed toward a separation useful to an outsider.**

That contaminated-agency problem remains OPEN at the episode boundary.

# 5. Character-state analysis

## 5.1 Arashi Chisato — presidency becomes operational

S2E04 established the office. S2E05 establishes a **presidency style**.

### Training administration

Her first visible response to expansion is not symbolic leadership but infrastructure:

> `練習メニューも ちょっと変えてみたんだけど`
>
> `１年生も増えたし`
>
> `それぞれに合ったところから 始められたらいいかなって。`

This is consistent with Chisato's long-standing specialist identity but expands it. She is no longer merely the strongest dance diagnostician; she is converting specialist knowledge into a group system that must work across unequal experience levels.

### Load governance

Later she says the district preliminaries are close, acknowledges that the session became hard, notes summer heat, and tells the group to know their limits. She ends one session early rather than converting competitive urgency into indiscriminate workload.

This **STRENGTHENS** the S2E03 evidence that serious practice and individualized safety regulation can coexist.

### Formal authority

At the climax, Chisato does something Kanon does not: she invokes office explicitly.

> `分かった。部長として許可します。`

This matters because the request is not a trivial preference. It restructures the club's summer practice topology by cohort.

### Autobiographical empathy as governance

Her reason is:

> `…私も そうだったから。`

The relevant Season-1 history is not flattened into “Chisato likes independence.” Her earlier solitary-worthiness experiment became ethically distorted. The new scene can therefore support two simultaneous readings:

1. **positive:** Chisato recognizes a self-authored desire for temporary separation and does not paternalistically deny it;
2. **risk:** because the request resembles her own past, autobiographical identification may make her less likely to investigate whether the juniors' stated autonomy is being distorted by comparison pressure or external manipulation.

The second is a stress test, not yet a finding that she made the wrong decision.

### S2E05 state

> **Chisato's presidency is consultative, training-literate, safety-aware, autonomy-permitting, and willing to make formal decisions. Its first major vulnerability is that respect for self-directed challenge may be exploitable when the request itself is socially engineered.**

Confidence: high for enacted style; medium for vulnerability generalization.

## 5.2 Shibuya Kanon — informal centrality survives decoupling from office

S2E05 strongly rejects the idea that declining presidency meant narrative or relational withdrawal.

### The eight-member school-performance decision

When the group considers reducing first-year load by using only the second-years at the school festival, Kanon is the one who immediately says:

> `それは ダメだと思う。`

Her reasoning is not “the juniors are already technically equal.” It is representational:

> `だって この８人で`
>
> `学校のみんなの前で歌うんでしょ？`
>
> `みんな それを楽しみにしてると思うんだ。`

This is Kanon's established integrative mode: she reframes a technical optimization problem by asking what the performance is *for* and whose relationship it expresses.

Chisato then translates that meaning into an implementable rule: do what is possible; perfection is unnecessary; eight-person delivery matters.

This interaction is modelically valuable because it is not a hidden Kanon presidency. Chisato openly endorses and operationalizes rather than merely rubber-stamping.

### Motive-sensitive charity toward Natsumi

When Natsumi's hidden financial benefit is exposed, Kanon says:

> `少し 様子を見てみない？`
>
> `すみれちゃんの言うことが間違ってるって 言ってるわけじゃない。`
>
> `でも… 何かある気がするんだよね。`

This preserves a long-standing Kanon tendency: before defining someone by harmful behavior, she looks for motive/context.

But unlike Ren S1E07-S1E08, where investigation uncovered materially exculpatory/corrective evidence, the current episode immediately lets Natsumi exploit the group's openness further. The correct S2E05 rule is therefore **not** “Kanon is right that Natsumi has a good reason.” No such reason has been admitted.

Instead:

> **Kanon's motive-seeking is a strength in preventing premature total judgment, but becomes a risk if it delays proportionate boundaries after concrete exploitation evidence.**

### Competition pressure

S2E03's `たまたま`/result-discounting state does not recur. S2E05 does contain real competitive pressure—Sunny Passion, district preliminaries, demanding summer preparation—but Kanon responds by preserving all eight members' school-stage participation rather than by treating weaker juniors as obstacles to winning.

This is positive counterevidence against competition becoming her total value metric, but it is not an equivalent retest of post-defeat self-evaluation.

## 5.3 Sakurakoji Kinako — access bridge becomes comparison-vulnerable cohort member

Kinako's S2E05 state has two sides.

### Social incorporation

She is delighted to have two same-year members, speaks naturally as part of the trio, and participates in ordinary group recreation. The loneliness risk of being the sole first-year has materially changed.

### Continuing self-development

Her lyric notebook becomes visible. When she minimizes it, the seniors identify it as a real developing competence rather than pretending it already equals senior work.

This is exactly the succession language S2E02 sought: **nascent capacity can be named without falsely declaring mastery.**

### Comparison vulnerability

Yet Kinako quickly joins the three-person question of whether they can become like the seniors within a year. Later, after Natsumi asks about filming singing/dance, Kinako says:

> `きっと 笑われるっす…。`

This expands the burden model. The trigger is not an actual competitive loss. It is anticipated public side-by-side comparison.

The current rule becomes:

> **visible skill asymmetry + expectation that outsiders will compare cohorts → Kinako may anticipate ridicule and interpret junior presence as reputational cost even without evidence that seniors want her removed.**

At the ending she helps articulate the separate-practice request. This is not straightforwardly self-removal: she is not leaving Liella! or asking the seniors to perform without them permanently. She asks for a bounded development experiment. But the choice is partly built from the same fear that proximity makes the gap visible.

## 5.4 Yoneme Mei — entry survives practice, shame shifts from eligibility to competence exposure

S2E04's most important Mei question was whether `向いてない` would collapse participation once actual practice began.

S2E05 answers locally: **no**.

Mei trains, displays hidden school-idol practice, demonstrates enough keyboard familiarity to reproduce `Tiny Stars`, and remains present through demanding physical work.

Her embarrassment does not disappear. She repeatedly minimizes her private practice:

> `たしなむ… 程度に…。`
>
> `だから ちょっとだけだって！`

The defensive system has therefore shifted rather than vanished.

Earlier:

> **“I am not the kind of person who can be a school idol.”**

Now:

> **“Do not expose how much preparation/fandom I already had.”**

That is developmental progress because action is no longer blocked, but it remains important for voice and relationship modeling.

Mei also participates in cohort comparison and is the one who asks whether they can reach the seniors' level within a year. She later states directly that public singing/dancing videos would make the first-/second-year gap obvious.

Her realism is not intrinsically pathological—the skill gap is real. The problem is the conclusion that visibility of that gap is humiliating or harmful enough to justify separation.

## 5.5 Wakana Shiki — relational entry survives, comparative pessimism remains concise

Shiki's S2E04 independent school-idol desire remained less explicit than Mei's. S2E05 shows that this did not make her participation fake or passive.

She handles practice seriously and appears physically capable enough to surprise the others. When asked whether she had practiced idol dance at home, she becomes guarded; the episode does not fully resolve the history behind that competence.

# 6. Cohort and succession analysis

## 6.1 The three first-years become a genuine social unit

S2E04 created a three-member junior cohort numerically. S2E05 gives it shared cognition.

They now:

- practice together;
- compare strengths;
- privately discuss the senior gap;
- anticipate public comparison together;
- request a separate summer development block together.

This is more than same-year friendship. It is an emergent **cohort position inside Liella!**.

## 6.2 Cohort solidarity can carry a distorted premise

The dangerous part is that solidarity does not guarantee epistemic correctness.

The trio mutually reinforces:

> senior = already highly accomplished  
> junior = visibly behind  
> public comparison = likely ridicule/burden

Some premises are factual—the seniors are currently more experienced. The transition to ridicule/burden is inferential.

The group now needs a succession method capable of validating the real inequality without validating every conclusion drawn from it.

## 6.3 Senior reassurance has limited reach

The seniors do several things correctly:

- identify junior strengths;
- refuse the expectation of immediate equality;
- say improvement takes practice/time;
- individualize training;
- regulate overwork.

Yet the juniors still return to comparison anxiety.

This is important counterevidence to any simplistic model in which “tell juniors everyone starts somewhere” solves hierarchy. **Developmental transparency helps, but social comparison can survive correct explanation.**

## 6.4 Everyday access temporarily reduces prestige distance

Natsumi's ordinary-life video accidentally does something analytically useful: it shows the seniors playing games, arguing, cheating badly, becoming absorbed, and behaving as ordinary students.

Kinako/Mei explicitly note that the seniors seem less distant in this context.

But the next practice restores the skill gap. The episode therefore distinguishes:

- **social distance** — reducible through ordinary shared life;
- **performance-skill distance** — real and persistent;
- **meaning assigned to that distance** — still negotiable.

This three-part distinction should become part of the succession model.

## 6.5 The first-year separation is both agency and contamination

The ending must not be collapsed into either of two extremes.

### Too positive

“Juniors autonomously choose their own development path.”

Incomplete because Natsumi consciously shaped the request.

### Too negative

“Natsumi brainwashes the juniors into separation.”

Also incomplete because the skill-gap anxiety predates her intervention and is voiced independently.

Best current formulation:

> **existing junior comparison anxiety + Natsumi's strategic amplification + a legitimate desire for cohort-specific self-assessment → formally self-requested separation whose authorship is mixed/contaminated rather than absent.**

That distinction is a high-value future test.

# 14. Counterevidence and alternative readings

## 14.1 Is separate practice simply good developmental differentiation?

Possibly in part. The juniors have a real skill gap and explicitly want time to focus on themselves. Chisato's permission is consistent with individualized development.

Counterpressure: Natsumi has deliberately amplified the concern and wants separation for her own control. Therefore S2E05 cannot canonize the plan as uncomplicated healthy autonomy.

## 14.2 Are the seniors actually over-demanding?

There is some evidence of hard practice: Kinako is exhausted, competition is near, and the schedule is daily over summer.

Counterevidence is substantial:

- Chisato apologizes for intensity;
- heat/overwork are discussed explicitly;
- pace is individualized;
- sessions can end early;
- festival perfection is explicitly rejected.

The best current formulation is **high standards under active load management**, not coercive senior overtraining.

## 14.3 Does Kanon's eight-member insistence pressure the juniors?

Yes, potentially. Saying that all eight should perform creates additional work for less-experienced members.

But the scene directly pairs Kanon's inclusion claim with Chisato's `できる範囲でいい` / `完璧じゃなくてもいい`. This prevents the current evidence from supporting “belonging requires immediate senior-level performance.”

## 14.4 Is Kanon naive about Natsumi?

The episode gives that reading real support. Concrete evidence of concealed monetization already exists, and Natsumi immediately continues manipulating the group after Kanon asks for patience.

However, Kanon never says Natsumi did nothing wrong. She explicitly preserves Sumire's evidence while asking for more motive/context. The correct model is therefore **risk-prone charitable deferral**, not simple credulity.

## 14.5 Is Natsumi merely comic rather than ethically serious?

Comic framing is undeniable: `マニー`, exaggerated CEO language, physical gags, and self-branding make her entertaining.

But the episode also gives her explicit private strategic language (`ちょろかった`, `分断成功`) and a concealed-profit conflict. Comic form does not erase the behavior being represented.

## 14.6 Does the first-year bloc prove a permanent senior/junior faction?

No. The episode establishes a temporary cohort-specific development request, not permanent schism. The seniors consent and challenge them to return with growth. Long-term topology is sealed.
