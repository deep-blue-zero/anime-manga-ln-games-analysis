# Love Live! Superstar!! — S2E03 Deep Reading V2
## `優勝候補` / `Favorites to Win`

# 1. Governing thesis

S2E03 subjects the new six-person Liella! to the first consequential test of the succession model built across S2E01-S2E02. The answer is neither that the model works cleanly nor that the founders were wrong to include a novice.

Three things happen at once.

First, **Kinako improves under serious practice without simply reproducing S2E02's overtraining failure**. She keeps Keke's inherited strengthening program, trains early, receives direct technical feedback from Chisato, and is told repeatedly that injury avoidance and rest are part of practice. Chisato can say both `基礎はバッチリ！` and `今夜はちゃんと休むこと`; the episode therefore gives concrete evidence that high standards and individualized pace regulation can coexist. [JT/AF]

Second, the access theory begins to work beyond Kinako, but only partially. A classmate tells Kinako that having a large high-school goal looks good; Mei and Shiki secretly leave sports drinks and a written message for Liella!; later, first-years fill the school live audience and Mei fights for a front-row seat. The episode therefore supplies **external affiliation and interest**, not a solved recruitment pipeline. No additional first-year formal membership is established within the S2E03 seal. [JT/AF/SI]

Third, the first meaningful competitive shortfall reactivates vulnerabilities that S2E02 had not resolved. Liella! fails to win the Yoyogi School Idol Festival and receives another special award. Kinako immediately concludes:

> `きな子が うまくなかったせいですよね…`
>
> `先輩たちだけで歌っていれば きっと…`

Her S2E02 self-authored choice therefore survives **difficulty**, but not yet **failure attribution**. When a group result disappoints her seniors, Kinako can still convert her lower current skill into a counterfactual removal argument: perhaps the group would have done better without her. [JT]

The founders reject that model explicitly. Sumire says that the stage is made by everyone and that the result is not one person's fault or one person's credit. Keke agrees despite teasing Sumire's historical credibility and reframes failure as preparation for future success. Kinako does not leave or ask to stop aiming for the championship. Her ambition survives, but it survives because the group corrects her causal model. [JT/FR]

S2E03 simultaneously introduces a second competition pressure through the girl who ended S2E02 by saying `澁谷… かのん…`. The current episode now establishes, within the seal, that she is **Wien Margarete** (`ウィーン･マルガレーテ`), a third-year middle-school student allowed into the festival because her ability was recognized, and the Yoyogi festival winner. The episode does **not** yet establish her deeper motive, history, or reason for specifically seeking Kanon. Those remain OPEN. [JT]

What it does establish is her evaluative grammar. Before the festival she calls Kanon by full name and says:

> `優勝候補なんでしょう？ 歌ってみてよ`
>
> `フフッ できないの？`

After winning, she dismisses Love Live! on the premise that if Liella! counts as a favorite, the competition cannot be very formidable, says she can win without difficulty, and then singles Kanon out:

> `少なくとも 今日 聴いた中では`
>
> `あなたには才能があった`
>
> `歌のね`

At this boundary, Wien therefore approaches value through **selection, competitive hierarchy, and individualized talent attribution**. Her `Butterfly Wing` performance intensifies that formal contrast: a solitary performer occupies a dark, gear-patterned stage while the lyric culminates in `選ばれるのは / 強く願う者だけ`. The lyric is not automatically a literal manifesto, but its staged relation to Wien's explicit later talent/ranking language makes the alignment analytically relevant. [AF/JT/MF/SI]

Wien's pressure reveals the episode's most important Kanon revision. Frozen S1 established that defeat no longer automatically produces global self-negation. S2E03 **preserves that gain**: Kanon does not call herself worthless, leave the group, stop singing, or attempt self-removal. But she does begin discounting non-result evidence in a familiar way.

Sunny Passion tells her directly:

> `そんなことは関係ない`
>
> `単純に すばらしかったから`

when Kanon asks why Liella! was called the most exciting group despite having no results. Later, after the Yoyogi loss and Wien's challenge, Kanon says:

> `私たちは たまたま`
>
> `優勝した すごい人の目に留まって`
>
> `褒められただけ`
>
> `何も結果は残してない`

The recurrence of `たまたま` is formally important. S1E03 Kanon had used `たまたま` to discount positive singing evidence while treating failure as more identity-revealing. S2E03 does **not** recreate that earlier totalized state, but the evidence-weighting habit survives in a narrower competition-status domain. [JT/FR]

That narrowing matters. Kanon's problem is no longer “I failed, therefore I am not really a singer.” It becomes:

> **we have not won, therefore perhaps praise does not justify calling us legitimate favorites or even justify our ambition to win.**

The next morning she says:

> `私たち また 結果を残すことができなかった`
>
> `そんな私たちが 「優勝」を目指す`
>
> `本当に それでいいの…`

This is a **REVISE / NARROW**, not a rejection, of the frozen S1 competition model. Defeat no longer annihilates Kanon's personhood or belonging, but competitive failure can still make her question the legitimacy of aspiration and discount qualitative recognition. [JT/SI]

Yuigaoka's students provide the episode's answer. They do not tell Liella! to stop caring about results. They show that a school made almost entirely of first-years has few formal club achievements and then tell the six:

> `誇りなんだよ｡ 自慢なんだよ`
>
> `「Liella！」は この学校の… スーパースターなんだよ`

They then explicitly ask Liella! to **keep aiming for victory**. The structure is crucial:

> **value and representation are affirmed before victory → therefore competitive ambition is authorized rather than required to manufacture value.**

This strongly PRESERVES the frozen Season-1 distinction between ranking and worth while also repairing Kanon's narrower S2E03 result-discounting state. [JT/SI]

The closing school live makes that repair performative. Kanon asks to perform with no singular center:

> `今日は センターは なしでいきたい`
>
> `センターは… ここにいる全員`
>
> `そして… 結ヶ丘の生徒全員`
>
> `この最高に ステキな学校全部！`

This does not reject Season 1's task-contingent center principle. It applies it. The dramatic task of this live is not to prove one member's superior fit; it is to answer a community that has just said Liella! is its shared pride. For this task, **symbolic centrality is distributed to performers, audience, and school**. The retained performance frames rotate individual foregrounds and group formations while repeatedly preserving the crowd/school as part of the performance field. [JT/AF]

`Go!! リスタート` then gives the episode its final grammar: difficulty, crying, stumbling, growth, another step, pain carried forward, restarting, and continuing to aim at a star. Again, lyric should not be flattened into literal biography, but here the performance's immediate dramatic function is unusually direct: the group has just failed, questioned its right to aim high, been reauthorized by its community, and now performs a song structured around resumption rather than proof of prior invulnerability. [JT/MF/SI]

The strongest sealed S2E03 thesis is therefore:

> **Succession becomes real when the junior is allowed to fail without becoming removable, and ambition becomes sustainable when neither failure nor praise is forced to bear the total meaning of worth. Kinako's first competitive shortfall reactivates burden logic, while Wien's talent/ranking challenge reactivates Kanon's tendency to discount non-result evidence. Liella!'s answer is collective authorship plus community recognition: the group can still pursue victory precisely because its value is already socially real before victory arrives.**

# 3. Naive structural read

S2E03 progresses through nine linked tests.

## 3.1 Kinako's chosen difficulty becomes routine rather than crisis

The recap carries S2E02's decision forward: Kinako chose the harder championship-oriented route. The first new scenes show her actually living that choice. She remains physically behind the seniors, complains about Tokyo heat, and works through training rather than instantly becoming equivalent.

The group visits the stage associated with Kanon and Keke's first major public performance. Kinako is therefore not only taught present routines; she is placed spatially inside the founders' developmental history.

## 3.2 External status rises before internal certainty catches up

Liella! is invited to close the Yoyogi School Idol Festival. Sumire immediately translates `トリ` into star/main-attraction language. Kanon is less certain: `本当にいいの？ 私たちで`.

The episode then explicitly reconstructs the gap between reputation and results. Last year's festival gave them the newcomer special award, not first place. The group recognizes that they have not yet won a major contest.

This establishes the episode's title problem: other people may classify Liella! as favorites before Kanon can treat that status as earned.

## 3.3 Sunny Passion separates aesthetic recognition from result while preserving rivalry

Kanon calls Sunny Passion and asks why they previously called Liella! the group that made their hearts dance most when Liella! had `結果も何も出ていない`.

Sunny Passion answers that results are irrelevant to that judgment; Liella! was simply excellent. Yet Sunny Passion also wants an unprecedented repeat Love Live victory to place the group, school, and island into history.

The scene therefore rejects an easy opposition between “authentic art” and “competition.” Sunny Passion can believe Liella! was artistically exciting without a win **and** pursue their own highly consequential historical win.

## 3.4 The S2E02 access strategy gains partial external evidence

Kinako's dance improves. Chisato says the basics are secure, notices extra night practice, and orders rest because injury avoidance is part of training.

Kinako also reports that a classmate praised her for aiming at something large in high school. Kanon interprets this as evidence that earnest effort can communicate.

Mei and Shiki covertly leave drinks with an encouraging note. Mei celebrates from hiding when Liella! receives them, while Shiki remarks that she could have handed them over directly.

These are not membership events. They are affiliation events.

## 3.5 Competition pressure becomes personally attractive to Kanon

In conversation with Chisato, Kanon says she wants results and wants to answer the school's expectations. She also describes competition itself positively: many groups aiming at one thing and raising one another is `楽しい` and `すごく わくわくする`.

This matters for the frozen model. Competition is not only debt or pressure. At S2E03 Kanon has an intrinsic process-level attraction to competitive striving.

## 3.6 Wien targets Kanon before the festival

Immediately afterward, the unidentified girl calls her `澁谷かのん`, asks the presumed favorite to sing, and taunts her when she does not comply.

Kanon asks who she is. No answer is supplied in this first encounter.

When Chisato returns, Kanon briefly uses `何でもない`, though later she privately wonders what the encounter meant. The moment is insufficient to establish a stable concealment pattern; it does establish that Wien can abruptly move Kanon's attention from ordinary group conversation into individualized evaluation.

## 3.7 Yoyogi supplies an external performance benchmark

Liella! arrives at a crowded festival. Yuigaoka students support them in person and online. Kanon is acknowledged as the most nervous member; Chisato tells her to trust the work they have already done.

Before Liella!'s outcome is known, Wien performs `Butterfly Wing` as a solo. The retained frames construct a radically different performance geometry from Liella!'s later school live: one performer, dark costume, dark/cool stage field, large gear imagery, and repeated isolation of Wien's body against the stage architecture.

The Japanese lyric repeatedly frames struggle, an opponent, self-polishing through conflict, flight without wings, and selection of those who strongly wish. Treat this as performance dramaturgy, not automatic biography.

## 3.8 Failure first produces junior self-removal logic, then Kanon result-discounting

Liella! does not win. It receives another special award.

Kinako immediately says her own lack of skill caused the outcome and imagines the seniors performing without her. Sumire/Keke reject this causal model and preserve collective authorship.

Then the result announcement identifies the winner as Wien Margarete, a middle-school third-year admitted because of recognized ability. Wien confronts the group and dismisses Love Live! if Liella! is really considered a favorite.

Kanon defends Love Live!, but does so partly by de-authorizing Liella!'s status: they only happened to catch the champion's attention and have produced no results.

Wien then attributes singing talent to Kanon individually.

## 3.9 Community recognition rebuilds the basis for ambition

The next practice begins with Kanon asking whether people like them—who again failed to leave a result—really should aim for victory.

Classmates interrupt with congratulations. They show how little formal achievement the new school has accumulated and tell Liella! that, regardless, the group is the students' pride and `スーパースター`.

Their conclusion is not “therefore winning does not matter.” It is “therefore keep aiming for victory.”

Ren proposes a live. The first-years all attend. Kanon asks for no singular center because everyone present and the whole school is the symbolic center. `Go!! リスタート` turns the episode's argument into performance: difficulty and disappointment become material to carry into another attempt rather than proof that the attempt was illegitimate.

# 5. Sakurakoji Kinako — chosen difficulty survives, burden logic does not disappear

## 5.1 Training adaptation is now evidenced

Kinako does not remain frozen at “novice who cannot keep up.”

At `00:06:35–00:07:03`:

- she continues Keke's training schedule;
- Chisato says her dance has improved;
- Chisato says the basics are secure;
- extra night practice is inferred/acknowledged;
- Chisato immediately imposes rest/injury-prevention guidance.

The current state is therefore:

> **real skill gap + measurable adaptation + continued need for load regulation.**

Do not model Kinako as either magically caught up or permanently incapable.

## 5.2 The S2E02 access theory begins to generate social proof

Kinako tells Kanon that a classmate praised her for entering high school and aiming at something significant. The direction of public interpretation has changed from S2E02, where exhaustion made the club look frightening.

This is not proof that every observer has changed. It is one positive counterexample with downstream reinforcement from the later first-year audience.

## 5.3 Failure activates burden attribution

The Yoyogi result is the first strong test of Kinako's S2E02 self-authorship.

Her response is not “the difficult route was a mistake.” It is:

> `きな子が うまくなかったせいですよね…`
>
> `先輩たちだけで歌っていれば きっと…`

This matters because the counterfactual is **removal**, not merely more practice.

A useful new rule is:

> **disappointing group result + Kinako knows she is currently the least experienced performer → elevated risk that she treats skill asymmetry as causal guilt and imagines the group without herself as the better-performing counterfactual.**

This is not identical to S1 Kanon's `足手まとい` state, but it is a meaningful formal parallel. Both convert a real limitation into a relational conclusion that the group may be better off without the limited person.

## 5.4 The group supplies causal correction

Sumire answers first:

> `みんなで ステージに立っているんでしょ`
>
> `誰のせいとか 誰のおかげとかじゃない`
>
> `みんなで つくり上げるものでしょ スクールアイドルって`

This is stronger than reassurance. It challenges the causal decomposition Kinako is performing.

Keke then agrees and supplies future orientation:

> `失敗は 成功の準備運動！`
>
> `次は きっと うまくいきます！`

Kinako answers `はい` rather than escalating the self-removal counterfactual.

## 5.5 Current S2E03 Kinako state

Kinako is now best modeled as:

- self-authored in the **choice** to pursue demanding practice;
- improving in physical/dance adaptation;
- receptive to senior safety regulation;
- increasingly capable of inspiring/attracting peer attention;
- still highly vulnerable to **burden-based causal overattribution** after group failure;
- able to accept collective correction without giving up the goal.

Her S2E02 autonomy should therefore not be rewritten as completed self-confidence.

# 10. Competition, talent, meritocracy, and recognition

S2E03 creates three distinct evaluation systems.

## 10.1 Festival ranking

Yoyogi produces an explicit winner and special award. This is the narrowest ranked system.

Wien wins. Liella! does not.

## 10.2 Expert-peer aesthetic recognition

This is competitive-peer recognition without ranking identity.

## 10.3 Community/institutional recognition

Yuigaoka students call Liella! their pride and `スーパースター` despite few formal wins.

This is representational/social recognition.

The episode's analytical danger is to collapse these into one scalar.

Wien's dialogue pressures toward scalarization: if the favorites are weak, perhaps the whole contest is weak; Kanon has singing talent. Yuigaoka's students resist that scalarization by asserting that Liella!'s value to the school already exists, while still asking the group to pursue the highest result.

The current model is therefore:

> **rank, talent, peer admiration, and community representation are partially correlated but non-identical evaluation systems.**

S2E03 does not decide that one is fake. It dramatizes what happens when characters treat one system as if it exhausted the others.
