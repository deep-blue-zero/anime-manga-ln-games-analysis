# Love Live! Superstar!! — S2E07 Deep Reading V2
## `UR 葉月恋` / `UR Hazuki Ren`

# 1. Governing thesis

S2E07 returns to one of Season 1's most important Hazuki Ren problems, but it does so in a deliberately ordinary domain. The issue is no longer whether Ren will exclude school idols to protect Yuigaoka, whether she will carry the school alone because of her mother's legacy, or whether she can accept help during a public institutional crisis. Instead, the episode asks a quieter question:

> **What happens when Ren believes the person who holds an important role is not allowed to possess an ordinary, badly regulated private desire?**

Ren is simultaneously overloaded by student-council work, composing for the newly announced Love Live! preliminary round, and staying up late playing games. The first two obligations are legible extensions of the responsible Ren already known to the group. The third is treated by Ren herself as potentially disqualifying. When Mei discovers the game room and Ren's escalating hobby, Ren does not merely say that she is embarrassed. She links secrecy directly to office:

> `仮にも わたくしは生徒会長。`
>
> `この学校をまとめる 私が`
>
> `こんなことがバレた日には…！`

The imagined condemnations that follow are Ren's own catastrophic projection, not the group's actual judgment. That distinction is essential. S2E07 does not show Liella! demanding role purity. It shows Ren **anticipating** that a student-council president who loses sleep over games would invalidate the school, the club, or others' respect.

Her solution is equally revealing. She asks Mei to hold the room key until the composition is finished and says:

> `ゲームさえ視界に入らなければ`
>
> `今までのわたくしに`
>
> `きっと戻れるはずですから！`

The desired state is not merely “better time management.” It is restoration of an older, more role-pure self. The game becomes a contaminant to be removed from sight; self-control is externalized into a literal locked threshold.

The episode answers this solitary and purifying strategy twice.

First, Kanon sees Ren's administrative overload and offers to become student-council vice president. This is a major clarification of Kanon's S2E04 refusal of Liella!'s club presidency. Kanon is **not anti-office** and does not believe formal authority is intrinsically corrupting. She selects formal roles by task and relational fit. Here she obtains the headmistress's permission in advance, then asks Ren for final consent:

> `私に 副会長 やらせてほしい。`
>
> `力になりたいの。`
>
> `恋ちゃんが お母さんから受け継いだ`
>
> `この学校を`
>
> `私も 一緒に盛り上げていきたい！`

and:

> `頼りない私だけど`
>
> `恋ちゃんを助けることができたらなって。`

The grammar matters. Kanon does not say that Ren is unfit to remain president. She does not seize the school problem or reinterpret Ren's mother's legacy as her own. She creates a **co-bearing role** under Ren's continuing presidency.

Second, after Ren eventually discloses the gaming secret, Liella! does not solve the problem by confiscating the games or declaring the overuse harmless. The group converts private shame into shared activity. Ren reveals that she is stuck on a game boss, and the others respond that the boss is notorious as `苦行` for solo play. Their solution is explicit:

> `協力プレイで打ち倒しましょう！`

Once the co-op begins, Ren does not become passive. She immediately gives tactical instructions:

> `皆さん まずは 体力を半分まで削ります！`
>
> `かのんさん！ 後ろに回って！`

The episode's game system therefore becomes a compact analogue for its institutional system. **A task that becomes punishing when solo can be distributed without stripping the original expert or office-holder of authorship.** Kanon can become vice president while Ren remains president; the group can help defeat the boss while Ren remains the tactical caller.

This yields the episode's strongest formulation:

> **co-bearing without de-authoring + ordinary selfhood without role disqualification.**

S2E07 also expands the project's transparency model. Healthy cohesion has never meant total prior disclosure. Keke's concealed Shanghai burden already proved that close relationships can contain asymmetrical information. Here, however, the concealed fact is not an externally imposed life-changing condition but a shame-laden ordinary hobby. Mei, initially entrusted with the secret, tells Ren that she should disclose it herself. When Ren worries that Kanon may become angry, Mei does not promise a conflict-free response. She says, in effect, that conflict can be survived:

> `たまには ケンカもして`
>
> `仲良くなるもんだろ。`

Mei begins to ground this in her own history with Shiki (`私も昔 四季と…`) before stopping. S2E07 therefore makes a stronger distinction than “friends tell each other everything”:

> **Healthy cohesion does not require perfect prior transparency. It requires a relationship system in which shame can be disclosed, disappointment or anger can be survived, and concealed personhood can become shareable without redefining belonging.**

When Ren finally tells everyone, her imagined catastrophe is falsified by direct social evidence. The group laughs with relief, says she should have said so sooner, and Kinako explains that the revelation makes the previously distant Ren feel more relatable. Importantly, this is **not** an endorsement of sleep deprivation. The concrete overwork remains real. What is rejected is Ren's belief that possessing the hobby makes her morally or institutionally illegitimate.

The ending then revises the maternal-school model one more time. Ren says:

> `お母様のつくってくれた学校は`
>
> `わたくしに とてもステキな出会いを`
>
> `与えてくれました。`

and commits to making the school still more wonderful. The school is no longer framed only as something inherited that Ren must preserve through solitary adequacy. It is also the ecology that **gave relationships back to her**. Stewardship remains; its emotional basis has become more reciprocal.

The strongest sealed S2E07 thesis is therefore:

> **A role becomes sustainable when the person holding it is allowed to remain a person. S2E07 revives Ren's solitary-duty architecture as role-purity shame rather than exclusionary prestige: she tries to hide ordinary pleasure, externalize self-control, and return to an imagined older self worthy of office. The episode corrects that logic without de-authoring her. Kanon formalizes co-bearing by becoming vice president under Ren, Mei supplies upward relational counsel about honest disclosure and survivable conflict, and Liella! converts Ren's “solo-punishment” game problem into cooperative play in which Ren still leads tactically. The result is not that responsibility disappears, but that authority, imperfection, leisure, and help can coexist inside the same person and group.**

# 3. Naive structural read

## 3.1 The cold open withholds the player before revealing Ren

The episode begins inside game logic rather than inside school-idol or student-council logic. First-person/action imagery and game mechanics occupy the foreground before the sequence reveals Ren as the player. Formally, the episode introduces **a domain in which Ren is currently a learner, enthusiast, and imperfect self-regulator** before returning to the public role by which other people know her.

This order matters. The episode does not first say “Ren is the president, but secretly…” It first lets the private activity exist as activity, then exposes the contradiction Ren herself experiences between that activity and office.

## 3.2 Love Live! returns under a nine-member formation

The new competition details arrive after S2E06's nine-member incorporation. The preliminary round is fully remote, song choice is unrestricted, and advancement to the Tokyo qualifier is compressed into a demanding selection step.

Natsumi immediately confirms that her S2E06 dream has not vanished overnight. She says that now that she has designated school idols as her dream, she will mobilize her money and influencer knowledge to support Liella! fully.

The statement is important because **membership changes the object of her competence without instantly changing all of her methods**. S2E07 will later show that sensationalist attention-economy reflexes remain available.

## 3.3 High-stakes creative authority remains senior-weighted

As club president, Chisato proposes the established pairing: Kanon for lyrics and Ren for composition. Keke immediately asks whether that is appropriate now that first-years have joined. Kinako notes that this is the first-years' first Love Live!, and Natsumi jokes that their presence itself is already fresh.

No first-year is excluded from membership or performance. But high-stakes creative authorship is not flattened into formal equality. S2E07 therefore preserves the distinction between:

> **equal belonging**

and

> **equal authority in every technical domain**.

Whether this distribution remains appropriately evidence-based or becomes too sticky as a senior default is OPEN.

## 3.4 Ren's exhaustion is initially legible as responsibility

Ren dozes during the Love Live! discussion and mutters game mechanics. Kanon and the first-years already know she has been busy with student-council work, and Ren repeatedly uses `心配ご無用です` to close concern.

The headmistress supplies independent evidence that the student-council workload is genuinely over-centralized: she has repeatedly told Ren to appoint a secretary and treasurer, while Ren insists that the school is still small enough that she should do the work herself.

This is the first sign that the old solitary-duty model has **recurred in ordinary institutional administration**, even after Season 1's crisis-level correction.

## 3.5 Mei discovers that overwork has a second source

Mei finds Ren playing games and learns that the hobby expanded after Ren was exposed to games at Keke's home and encouraged to broaden her horizons because she had been working too hard. Ren's father has also sent games as a gesture framed as compensation for prior hardship.

The episode establishes current father contact and reparative gifting. It does not establish a complete father-daughter relationship model, so that remains outside the thesis.

The actual practical problem is simple: Ren enjoys games enough to play late into the night, producing sleep deprivation and interfering with composition.

## 3.6 Ren converts the problem into a locked-room problem

Ren asks Mei to keep the room key until composition is done. When Mei suggests Kanon as the more obvious custodian, Ren refuses because Kanon knowing would expose the contradiction between her public office and private behavior.

The line `仮にも わたくしは生徒会長` marks the hinge from ordinary embarrassment into **role-purity shame**.

The following apparent condemnations by Keke, Sumire, Chisato, and others are represented as Ren's imagined consequences. They must not be recorded as actual group attitudes.

## 3.7 “Return to the old me” reveals the self-model

Ren's statement that removing games from sight will restore `今までのわたくし` makes the strategy more than pragmatic stimulus control. The desired solution is purification through environmental exclusion: remove the object and restore the older role-consistent self.

That is structurally continuous with an older Ren tendency to solve complexity by increasing personal control.

## 3.8 Kanon wants to help but initially excludes herself from office

Separately, Kanon and Chisato discuss Ren's student-council burden. Kanon says she wants to help, but when Chisato suggests trying, Kanon's first response is:

> `私なんて…`

Chisato then returns Kanon's own anti-fatalistic language to her:

> `私も 部長にチャレンジしたよ？`
>
> `自分ができないって思い込んでるだけ。`
>
> `かのんちゃんの言葉だよ？`

This makes S2E04's lesson explicitly reciprocal. Kanon did not become the permanent owner of the insight by teaching it to Chisato and others; Chisato can now use it to correct Kanon.

## 3.9 Natsumi's commercial reflex survives dream-aligned membership

The group spies on what they misunderstand as a potentially romantic Mei/Ren meeting. Sumire interprets the possibility through show-business risk. Natsumi immediately interprets it through attention capture and begins framing a “Liella! romance rumor” content idea until Shiki vetoes it with `禁止`.

The scene is comic but model-relevant. Natsumi's S2E06 school-idol dream is real enough to persist, yet **goal alignment has changed faster than method alignment**. The group is now in a position to govern those methods from inside rather than merely decide whether to admit or expel her.

## 3.10 Kanon chooses formal office after rejecting a different office

Kanon approaches Ren privately and asks to become student-council vice president. She has already obtained the headmistress's permission and leaves the final decision to Ren.

This clarifies S2E04 materially. Kanon's refusal of the school-idol club presidency was not a generalized aversion to institutional responsibility. Her decision logic is task-specific:

- club presidency would have formalized an already excessive concentration of Liella! centrality in Kanon;
- student-council vice presidency distributes an excessive concentration of school administration in Ren.

The two decisions are therefore consistent under the same anti-concentration principle.

## 3.11 Mei becomes an upward relational mentor

After hearing Kanon's offer, Mei tells Ren to disclose the game problem honestly. Ren fears anger. Mei says Kanon might indeed become angry—because she was genuinely worried—but argues that friendship can survive learning uncomfortable things and sometimes fighting.

This is not a senior teaching a junior how relationships work. It is a first-year using her own lived relationship with Shiki to advise a second-year. The succession architecture has become **bidirectional**.

## 3.12 Disclosure falsifies Ren's imagined social catastrophe

Ren tells the group. Their first response is relief and laughter rather than condemnation. Kinako says she had thought Ren belonged to a distant world, and the revelation makes Ren more relatable.

The social meaning of the hobby is therefore the inverse of what Ren predicted: rather than destroying respect, it reduces status distance.

Again, the practical problem remains. They do not say sleep deprivation and delayed work are ideal. They reject only the inference that ordinary private weakness is incompatible with Ren's personhood or office.

## 3.13 The boss fight converts private weakness into shared competence

Ren explains that she is stuck on a boss. The others identify solo play as `苦行` and propose co-op. Once play begins, Ren immediately becomes the tactical coordinator.

The sequence refuses a simple rescue hierarchy. Ren needs other players, but the need does not prove that she lacks expertise. She directs the shared task.

## 3.14 Mei and Shiki quietly continue S2E04's relation

During co-op, Mei thanks Shiki for genuinely worrying about her. Shiki becomes flustered enough to accuse Mei of being `思わせぶり`, while Mei rejects responsibility for Shiki's reaction.

The exchange provides further direct evidence of mutual concern and embarrassment around its expression. It should remain distinct from any stronger claim about romantic motive, which S2E07 does not establish.

## 3.15 Ren no longer hands away the key

At the end, Ren is asked whether the room key should again be held for her. She answers:

> `いえ 大丈夫です。`

This is a meaningful episode-local reversal of the earlier external-control strategy. It does **not** prove durable self-regulation or cure. It establishes that after disclosure, co-bearing, and shared play, Ren no longer treats external lockout as immediately necessary.

## 3.16 The school becomes something that gave Ren relationships

Ren's final reflection recodes maternal inheritance. Yuigaoka is still the school her mother made, and Ren still wants to make it better. But the crucial new predicate is that the school has **given Ren wonderful encounters**.

The institution is therefore not only a burden inherited from a dead parent or an object requiring preservation. It has become a relational good returned to Ren.

## 3.17 Piano returns as creative motion, not proven completion

The episode closes with Ren at the piano saying `いい歌` and continuing to play. This is enough to establish resumed creative engagement and positive musical appraisal after the blockage. It is **not** enough, inside the S2E07 seal, to assert that the Love Live! competition song is complete.

# 5. Hazuki Ren: solitary duty becomes role-purity shame

## 5.1 The institutional overwork is real before the secret is known

The headmistress's account is crucial because it prevents the episode from collapsing all exhaustion into “Ren played too many games.” Ren has been repeatedly told to create a functioning student council with secretary/treasurer support. She declines because Yuigaoka remains small and she believes she can or should handle the work herself.

This is a genuine recurrence of S1's `わたくしが頑張るしかない` architecture, now in lower-stakes administration.

## 5.2 The game problem is also real

At the same time, Ren is not merely an innocent workaholic falsely accused of leisure. She genuinely plays late enough to lose sleep and genuinely has trouble stopping. The episode permits both facts:

- institutional over-centralization;
- private overuse.

The interpretive mistake would be to make either cancel the other.

## 5.3 Role purity changes ordinary self-control into identity shame

The key phrase is not “I should play less.” It is `仮にも わたくしは生徒会長`.

Ren's fear is that a president who struggles with gaming becomes evidence that:

- the school cannot be properly represented;
- Love Live! success becomes implausible;
- peers will be disappointed;
- the club itself becomes contaminated by her flaw.

Because those reactions are visually dramatized as her imagination, they tell us about **Ren's model of others**, not the others' actual standards.

## 5.4 Externalizing self-control through the key

Handing the room key to Mei is practical stimulus control, but the stated purpose gives it a larger meaning. Ren believes that if the game disappears from vision, she can return to her earlier self.

The key therefore literalizes a defensive strategy:

> **remove the troublesome desire from access → recover the role-consistent self.**

This is the private-personhood analogue of solving institutional ambiguity through tighter control.

## 5.5 Disclosure changes the social meaning, not the practical facts

When the secret becomes shared, Liella! does not tell Ren that losing sleep is good. Instead, the group's response proves that the private hobby was never the social disqualifier Ren imagined.

Kinako's line that Ren had seemed like someone from a distant world is especially important. Ren feared ordinary imperfection would lower her; for a junior, the same imperfection actually makes her more accessible.

This creates a direct succession effect: **ordinary vulnerability can reduce senior/status distance without requiring the senior to become less competent.**

## 5.6 Co-op is the episode's systems model

The boss is explicitly described as punishing solo. The proposed solution is not “Ren should become better until she can solo it.” It is co-op.

Yet co-op preserves specialization:

- Ren calls phases and movement;
- other members perform support, damage, and positional tasks;
- the shared system succeeds because contributions differ.

This is almost a miniature enactment of the frozen connected-heterogeneity thesis.

## 5.7 Ending state: not cured, but no longer purified by lockout

Ren's `いえ 大丈夫です` when offered key custody matters because it reverses the earlier strategy. But one evening of changed affect cannot establish durable self-regulation. Current state:

> **reduced shame + accepted co-bearing + lower immediate need for external lockout; long-run balance OPEN.**

# 6. Kanon: formal service after formal decentering

## 6.1 S2E04 did not establish office aversion

Kanon's refusal of Liella! club presidency could have supported a weaker reading: perhaps Kanon simply dislikes formal titles. S2E07 rejects that simplification.

She voluntarily seeks the vice presidency.

## 6.2 The two office decisions share one deeper rule

In S2E04, giving Kanon club presidency would have concentrated formal office on top of already strong integrative centrality.

In S2E07, giving Kanon vice presidency **reduces** an excessive concentration of labor already sitting on Ren.

So the stable principle is not “avoid office.” It is closer to:

> **accept or refuse formal authority according to whether the role improves distribution of responsibility for the actual task.**

Kanon still produces the old self-downranking form `私なんて…` when Chisato first suggests action. The recurrence is real.

- checks permission with the headmistress;
- formulates a specific office;
- asks Ren privately;
- states motive;
- leaves consent to Ren.

This strongly **STRENGTHENS** the post-S1 distinction between residual self-doubt and behavioral self-removal.

## 6.4 `頼りない私だけど` is qualified agency, not withdrawal

The phrase still marks humility/uncertainty. But because it occurs after Kanon has already done the administrative prerequisite and while she is actively volunteering, it belongs with the S1E02 pattern where hedging coexists with commitment.

For simulation, Kanon should not be modeled as needing to feel fully qualified before acting.

## 6.5 Co-bearing is consent-seeking

Kanon does not tell Ren that she has decided to “fix” the student council. She gets institutional permission, then explicitly says the remaining condition is Ren's agreement.

That preserves the mentoring/governance rule developed around Kinako:

# 17. Counterreadings and limits

## 17.1 “Ren's only problem is gaming addiction”

Too narrow. The hobby is behaviorally overused enough to matter, but independent evidence also establishes student-council over-centralization. The episode explicitly joins workload, shame, and self-control.

## 17.2 “Ren's gaming is harmless because the group laughs”

Rejected. Sleep deprivation and composition interference remain real. The group's laughter falsifies **role disqualification**, not the practical cost of overuse.

## 17.3 “Liella! secretly believes exactly what Ren imagines”

Rejected. The condemning lines are Ren's imagined catastrophe. Actual disclosure produces a substantially different response.

## 17.4 “Kanon becoming vice president means she was wrong to refuse club presidency”

Rejected. The two choices solve opposite distribution problems. One avoids over-concentrating Liella! authority in Kanon; the other reduces over-concentrated student-council labor in Ren.

## 17.5 “Kanon has finally become confident because she accepts office”

Too strong. `私なんて` and `頼りない私だけど` preserve uncertainty. The change is that uncertainty no longer blocks action.

## 17.6 “Mei's advice proves all secrets must be immediately disclosed”

Too strong. The episode supports disclosure when concealment is distorting a close relationship and relevant shared work. It does not establish a universal rule that intimacy erases privacy.

## 17.7 “Friends should never get angry”

Explicitly contradicted by Mei's model. She allows anger and occasional conflict as survivable parts of knowing one another.

## 17.8 “Natsumi's problematic media behavior was cured by joining Liella!”

Rejected. The romance-rumor clickbait reflex recurs directly. Membership/dream alignment changes the stakes but leaves method-level governance necessary.

## 17.9 “First-years are still only passengers because Kanon/Ren get the song roles”

Too broad. High-stakes creative authority remains senior-weighted, but first-years already exercise meaningful authority in other domains. The narrower OPEN question is whether technical/creative opportunity broadens with development.

## 17.10 “The co-op boss is merely a joke with no analytical value”

The scene is comic, but the structural parallel is unusually explicit: the game itself is called punishing solo, cooperation is proposed as the solution, and Ren remains tactical leader. That supports a bounded systems analogy without requiring the episode to be a one-to-one allegory.

## 17.11 “Ren is cured because she keeps the key”

Rejected. `いえ 大丈夫です` is current-state evidence, not a longitudinal guarantee. Future recurrence remains OPEN.

## 17.12 “Ren has finished the Love Live! song by the ending”

Not established. The source supports resumed piano work and positive musical appraisal only.

# 20. Episode-bound final model

At the end of S2E07, Liella! is a nine-member group whose inclusion problem has become increasingly sophisticated. The decisive question is no longer simply who may enter. It is whether people can remain legitimate members and leaders while carrying unequal skill, imperfect self-control, private interests, embarrassment, and the need for help.

Ren supplies the strongest test because her older solitary-duty architecture is already historically established. S2E07 shows that architecture can recur after major growth without resetting the character to S1. She still over-centralizes work and still imagines that responsibility requires a purified self. But she now exists inside a relationship system capable of correcting that recurrence without humiliating or replacing her.

The final state can be summarized as:

> **role-purity shame → concealment + externalized control → relational challenge → consent-based institutional co-bearing → self-disclosure → non-disqualifying group response → cooperative action with preserved expertise → relational stewardship.**

That sequence **STRENGTHENS** rather than overturns the frozen Season-1 model. The mature form of Liella!'s connectedness is not that no one ever becomes solitary, ashamed, or over-responsible again. It is that the group has increasingly developed ways to redistribute burdens **without making the person who needed help disappear from authorship**.

The S2E07 boundary therefore closes on:

> **co-bearing without de-authoring; ordinary selfhood without role disqualification; disclosure without a promise of conflictlessness; and authority that remains differentiated without becoming solitary.**
