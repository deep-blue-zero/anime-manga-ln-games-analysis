# Love Live! Superstar!! — S2E04 Deep Reading V2
## `科学室のふたり` / `The Two in the Science Room`

# 1. Governing thesis

S2E04 moves Season 2 from the problem of **making Liella! accessible to newcomers** to a more difficult problem: **what kinds of self-description are allowed to function as disqualification before a person has acted?**

The episode centers that problem on three people at once.

Mei already has abundant evidence of school-idol desire. She cries while watching Liella!, follows the group closely, owns rare school-idol material, chose Yuigaoka because Liella! was there, and repeatedly watches practice. Yet when entry becomes personal she says:

> `この顔だし この性格だぞ。`
>
> `どう考えても向いてないだろ…！`

Her obstacle is therefore not absence of interest. It is a categorical model of **type-fit**: a person with her face, manner, and personality is not the sort of person who may become a school idol. [JT/AF]

Shiki has a different but structurally related defense. She describes herself as someone who was always alone and says that was fine. Yet Mei's presence has become a stable part of her life, and Shiki responds to Mei's stalled school-idol desire by trying to engineer conditions under which Mei can move. She experiments with Liella! herself, reveals Mei's hidden fandom to the seniors, and later tells Mei to go to the school-idol club even if that leaves Shiki alone in the science room. Her support becomes **self-removing**: she tries to solve Mei's blocked desire partly by subtracting herself from Mei's decision. [JT/SI]

Chisato begins the episode with the same dangerous grammar in a third domain. Kanon proposes Chisato as school-idol-club president because the group is trying to become something new. Chisato immediately answers that she is not suited for that kind of role. Later, when the group is stuck on Mei and the word `向いてない` returns, Chisato recalls childhood Kanon telling her that she only *thought* she could not do something. The episode then reveals Chisato as the new club president, explaining:

> `迷惑かけるかもしれないんだけど`
>
> `自分にもできるんじゃないかって…`
>
> `チャレンジしてみたいんだ。`

Only after this self-revision does she confront Mei with the explicit rule:

> `やったこともないのに`
>
> `「向いてない」は禁止だよ。`

The episode's ethical center is therefore not the slogan “everyone can do anything.” It is narrower and stronger:

> **A person may discover a real mismatch after trying, but neither embarrassment nor an inherited self-image is sufficient evidence to convert “I am afraid / inexperienced / awkward” into “I am categorically not the kind of person who can choose this.”** [IT]

That rule also changes the Season-2 succession model. S2E01 established that senior excellence can look like an entry prerequisite. S2E02 added developmental history, individualized pacing, honest standards, and junior choice. S2E03 showed that entry must survive failure. S2E04 now demonstrates that **access sometimes fails before technique even becomes relevant**. Mei's barrier is not that Liella! is too skilled; she is already a committed fan. Her barrier is a self-authored exclusion category. Shiki's barrier is different again: her own school-idol desire is less clearly articulated, but relationship commitment brings her into the activity.

The conversion from affiliation to membership is therefore not one uniform recruitment pipeline. By the endpoint, both Mei and Shiki are formally announced as new members, but they arrive through different motives:

- Mei moves from hidden fandom + self-type exclusion toward conditional participation enabled by Shiki's co-presence;
- Shiki moves from indirect facilitation + self-described solitary independence toward entering with Mei, while her own independent school-idol passion remains less fully articulated.

This **resolves the S2E03 membership-conversion question locally**—affiliation does become formal entry for these two first-years—while making the larger model more plural rather than simpler. [JT/AF/SI]

S2E04 also develops leadership inside the founding cohort. Everyone initially treats Kanon as the obvious president; Kinako says she assumed Kanon already held the role. Kanon refuses the logic that de facto centrality should automatically become formal office. She corrects the founding narrative (`始めたのは可可ちゃん`) and argues that precisely because she has been the person bringing people together, a Liella! trying to become new may benefit from someone else as president. This **STRENGTHENS the frozen task-contingent-authority model** and S2E03's distributed-center result: protagonist gravity is not converted into permanent institutional ownership. [JT/SI]

Chisato's acceptance of presidency is equally important. It is not proof that every member is equally suited to every role. The episode gives a specific reason she can take this one: she recognizes her own premature self-exclusion, chooses to try despite possible burden, and immediately uses that learning to help a junior distinguish fear from evidence. Leadership is therefore framed as **chosen responsibility under uncertainty**, not certification that one was always the natural leader. [JT/FR/SI]

The strongest sealed S2E04 thesis is:

> **Liella!'s succession becomes more inclusive when the group stops treating self-description as destiny. S2E04 does not erase skill differences, temperament, or difficulty; it rejects the shortcut by which fear and self-image become categorical non-eligibility before experience. Kanon decouples informal centrality from formal office, Chisato applies a received childhood lesson to herself before using it with Mei, and Mei/Shiki enter not by becoming copies of the seniors but by admitting that desire and relationship can justify trying before “fit” is known.**

# 3. Naive structural read

## 3.1 Cold open — desire is already present before recruitment

The cold open immediately distinguishes *interest* from *entry*. A classmate says Liella!'s live moved her and is told that members are still being recruited. Kinako enthusiastically offers to bring an application. Mei reacts to school-idol talk with guarded denial.

The episode therefore does not begin with a population of indifferent first-years. It begins with visible interest plus heterogeneous barriers to action.

## 3.2 Liella! discovers it never formalized a club president

Ren's plan for a meeting of club presidents exposes that the school-idol club has never formally chosen one. The group collectively looks toward Kanon. Kinako says she had assumed Kanon was already president; Keke also supports her.

The comedy works because de facto centrality is socially legible. Yet Kanon resists formalization.

## 3.3 Shiki exposes Mei's hidden investment

From the science room, Shiki has placed a microphone where Liella! practices. Mei is interested enough to ask who becomes president despite denying that she cares. Shiki challenges the contradiction directly: if Mei does not care, why does she watch every day?

Shiki also possesses photographs of Mei crying during the earlier school live. Mei explains her behavior through repeated coincidence language: `たまたま`.

The scene establishes Mei as an intensely invested fan whose public self-model forbids that investment from becoming identity.

## 3.4 Kanon refuses founder-to-president inevitability

In a quieter dyadic conversation, Chisato asks why Kanon is reluctant. Kanon first corrects the founding story: school-idol activity was started by Keke, not Kanon. Chisato answers that Kanon nevertheless brought everyone together.

Kanon accepts that observation but draws the opposite institutional conclusion from what the others expect:

> `だからこそ 新しくなろうとしている`
>
> `「Liella！」の部長は 自分じゃない人の方がいいと思う。`

She proposes Chisato.

## 3.5 Kinako recruits naturally; Mei and Shiki remain stuck

Kinako now speaks about club activity openly and enthusiastically with classmates. She invites interest rather than treating the club's difficulty as shameful. Mei's social reaction remains prickly but not purely hostile.

Mei also challenges Shiki's isolated habits, warning that high school may reproduce middle school if she never gets closer to classmates. This matters later: each of the “two in the science room” wants the other to move beyond a defensive isolation that she herself continues to claim.

## 3.6 Shiki enters practice as an experiment / intervention

Shiki appears at Liella! practice and is introduced as a first-year interested after the prior live. Kinako reacts intensely to finally having a same-year participant. Shiki performs basic flexibility/steps with relative ease and enters formation practice.

Mei watches from concealment and becomes visibly agitated/jealous at seeing Shiki inside the formation.

When Mei is exposed, the seniors directly invite her too. She nearly entertains the possibility, then challenges Shiki: is Shiki actually going to become a school idol? Shiki says she has not decided. Mei leaves.

## 3.7 The seniors discover Mei's room is already an archive of school-idol desire

Shiki shows the others Mei's room/window. They identify Liella! posters and rare school-idol material; Keke reacts with collector/fan intensity. The comic spying scene is ethically imperfect, but narratively it removes the possibility that Mei's obstacle is lack of interest.

Shiki then supplies the deeper relationship history.

## 3.8 Mei and Shiki's mutual one-way sacrifices produce stalemate

Shiki says she had always been alone and was fine with it. Mei first approached her in middle school, eventually stayed with her every day, and even joined the science club despite lacking interest in science.

Shiki knows Mei chose Yuigaoka because Liella! was there and wanted to try school idols, yet Mei never starts. Asked whether Shiki is testing the school-idol club only for Mei, Shiki does not supply a strong independent school-idol desire.

The pair later confront each other. Shiki tells Mei to be honest and go to the school-idol club. Mei says the issue is Shiki, not Mei. Shiki replies that she likes being alone, the science club can survive with one member, and Mei should not worry. When Mei again says she is not suited for school idols, Shiki tells her not to come to the science room if she has no real interest in science.

Both are trying to free the other by removing themselves.

## 3.9 `向いてない` becomes the episode's recursive hinge

The group is unsure what to do. Chisato repeats `向いてない…` and the episode immediately enters a childhood memory in which young Chisato says `できないよ` and Kanon tells her she only thinks she cannot do it.

Later, while the group again struggles with Mei, `向いてない` returns. Chisato recognizes that Mei has decided the result before trying.

## 3.10 Chisato becomes president before she asks Mei to try

The first club-presidents' meeting identifies Wakana Shiki as president of the Science Lovers' Association and then Arashi Chisato as president of the school-idol club.

A flashback gives Chisato's reason: she may cause trouble, but she thinks perhaps she can do it and wants to challenge herself.

The order is essential. She does not merely quote Kanon's childhood advice at Mei. She first applies it to her own current fear.

## 3.11 Chisato reframes Mei's self-exclusion

Chisato approaches Mei directly. Mei cites face and personality as proof she is not suited. Chisato answers:

> `やったこともないのに`
>
> `「向いてない」は禁止だよ。`

She then interprets Mei's hesitation as partly relational: Mei does not want to leave Shiki. When Mei invokes Shiki's claim to prefer being alone, Chisato says that is not the whole truth. She sees Mei and Shiki as similar in embarrassment, loneliness, and their tendency to tell themselves that their defensive arrangement is what they really want.

This remains partly **Chisato's interpretation**, not omniscient narration. The following reconciliation supplies substantial supporting evidence without proving every adjective universally.

## 3.12 Mei and Shiki replace unilateral release with explicit co-presence

Mei and Shiki finally speak directly. Shiki denies liking school idols and says she only investigated because Mei seemed interested; her embarrassment undercuts the simplicity of the denial but does not prove an independent idol vocation of equal intensity to Mei's.

Shiki then says she cannot even make the kind of smile she associates with school idols. Mei answers that if this disqualifies Shiki, what would that make Mei? Each calls the other cute; both reject the compliment about herself.

Mei finally states the actionable relation:

> `四季が近くにいてくれたら…`
>
> `頑張れそうな気がするんだ。`

The solution is not “you were actually independent all along.” It is that **co-presence is allowed to be part of agency**.

## 3.13 Formal membership and ritual incorporation

Ren announces that two new members have joined. Mei and Shiki stand with the expanded group in practice clothes. The seniors immediately begin adapting them to group conventions, including the pre-live `Song for Me / Song for You / Song for All` ritual.

Mei is embarrassed by the ritual but participates. The episode ends not on proof that the new pair have already caught up, but on **formal incorporation into a shared practice**.

# 5. Arashi Chisato — from specialist authority to chosen formal leadership

## 5.1 Initial refusal is not simple laziness

When Kanon suggests Chisato as president, Chisato immediately says she cannot do it because she is not suited to that sort of thing.

That response resembles an older Chisato structure without simply repeating the S1 dance arc. Season 1 established that Chisato can turn independence into a worth test; S2E04 instead shows a **role-fit presumption**. She knows herself primarily through dance expertise and practical support, not formal club presidency.

The important word is `向いてない`.

## 5.2 The episode makes Chisato hear her own logic in Mei

When Mei's impasse is summarized as `向いてない`, the mixed track around Chisato's corresponding line is unusually low-energy. For **00:16:04.660–00:16:08.060**, 100 ms median mixed-track RMS is approximately **−61.81 dBFS**. The transition into the childhood recall rises to about **−45.59 dBFS** median over the next 2.74 s, and the recalled interaction becomes substantially fuller thereafter. [AM]

The numerical claim is narrow: `向いてない` functions as an acoustically marked hinge into autobiographical memory.

## 5.3 Childhood Kanon becomes internalized evidence

The recalled Kanon tells crying child Chisato:

> `ちぃちゃんは じぶんができないって`
>
> `おもいこんでるだけ！`
>
> `だから だいじょうぶ！`

S1 had already established the importance of Kanon/Chisato's childhood relation. S2E04 does something new with it: the memory is no longer only explanation for why Chisato values Kanon. It becomes **portable self-correction**.

This parallels, but is not identical to, S1E11 Kanon's internally portable support. In each case, a once-external relational intervention becomes usable without the original speaker having to repeat the full lesson in the present.

## 5.4 Presidency is chosen under uncertainty

Chisato does not suddenly claim natural leadership talent. Her explanation preserves uncertainty and cost:

> `迷惑かけるかもしれないんだけど`
>
> `自分にもできるんじゃないかって…`
>
> `チャレンジしてみたいんだ。`

This wording matters for modeling. The decision is not:

> I discovered I am definitely suited.

It is:

> I do not yet know that I am unsuited, and the role is worth trying.

That is exactly the distinction she later offers Mei.

## 5.5 Chisato earns the right to challenge without becoming omniscient

After accepting presidency, Chisato says `「向いてない」は禁止だよ` to Mei. This is strong language, but its function is not to dictate that Mei **must** become an idol. It blocks a particular premature argument: “my face/personality proves I cannot choose this before trying.”

Chisato then goes further and interprets Mei/Shiki's relationship. That interpretation is more contestable. She calls them alike: shy, lonely, dissatisfied with those traits, and prone to convincing themselves that their defensive state is what they genuinely want.

The later scene substantially validates the relational core—both deny their own attractiveness, both have organized choices around the other, and Mei explicitly says Shiki's proximity would help her try—but the precise psychological labels should remain **strong inference**, not timeless diagnosis.

## 5.6 Current Chisato state

At S2E04:

- dance/physical-training expertise remains intact;
- she gains **formal club presidency**;
- formal authority does not replace Kanon's integrative importance;
- she can apply received support to herself before using it with a junior;
- her leadership style currently favors challenging premature self-exclusion and reading relational blockage;
- whether presidency increases her paternalistic risk remains OPEN.

This is a meaningful expansion of the frozen Chisato model.

# 6. Shibuya Kanon — decentering formal office without withdrawing responsibility

## 6.1 The group already perceives her as leader

Kinako's `かのん先輩が部長だと思ってたっす` is revealing because it comes from a newcomer. Kanon's de facto group position is legible from outside.

The founding members likewise converge on her quickly.

## 6.2 Kanon corrects the myth of singular origin

When Chisato says Kanon started school idols at Yuigaoka, Kanon answers:

> `違うよ 始めたのは可可ちゃん。`

This preserves Keke's initiating agency. It is also consistent with the frozen Season-1 model in which Kanon is integrative but not the sole source of Liella!.

## 6.3 “Because I gathered everyone” becomes a reason to move office elsewhere

Chisato counters that Kanon is the one who brought everyone together. Kanon does not deny that entirely. Instead:

> `そうかもしれないけど`
>
> `だからこそ 新しくなろうとしている`
>
> `「Liella！」の部長は 自分じゃない人の方がいいと思う。`

The logic is generative rather than self-denigrating. There is no `足手まとい`, no claim that she lacks worth, and no attempt to disappear from the group. She is still choosing how authority should be distributed.

This matters because “Kanon refuses leadership” would be too broad. S1E10-S1E11 already showed that she can accept visible responsibility when the task fits. Here the task is **institutional office in a group entering a new cohort phase**. Kanon thinks another person should carry it.

## 6.4 S2E03 result-discounting does not recur under this episode's conditions

# 17. Counterevidence and stress tests

## 17.1 Does `「向いてない」は禁止` mean the episode denies real aptitude differences?

No.

The series has repeatedly acknowledged real differences in stamina, dance skill, vocal ability, and domain authority. Chisato's statement is specifically conditioned by **not having tried**. S2E04 attacks premature categorical exclusion, not evidence-based assessment after experience.

## 17.2 Does Chisato become president because Kanon correctly knows her “true” role?

Not exactly.

Kanon proposes Chisato, but Chisato initially refuses. The decisive change comes from Chisato's own recognition of her `向いてない` logic and her choice to try. Kanon's suggestion creates an opportunity; it does not automatically determine the result.

## 17.3 Is Kanon refusing presidency another form of self-removal?

Current evidence says no.

She does not describe herself as a burden, lesser member, or incompetent leader. She offers a positive structural reason: Liella! is trying to become new, so formal leadership should not automatically reproduce her existing centrality. She remains active afterward.

## 17.4 Does Mei join only because Shiki is present?

The episode establishes Shiki's proximity as an enabling condition, not necessarily the sole cause. Mei's independent school-idol desire is already strong before the reconciliation.

A better model is:

> existing school-idol desire + self-type exclusion + fear of relational separation → co-presence makes trying actionable.

## 17.5 Does Shiki join because she secretly loved school idols all along?

Not securely established.

Her behavior shows interest/knowledge acquisition and her denial is embarrassed, but the strongest explicit motive remains Mei. Preserve independent school-idol desire as plausible/OPEN rather than flattening her into a hidden superfan equivalent to Mei.

## 17.6 Does S2E04 prove the recruitment problem is solved?

No.

It proves that **two specific affiliated first-years** convert to formal membership. It does not establish a general recruitment rate, future retention, or whether other first-years find the expanded group more accessible.

## 17.7 Does S2E04 resolve Kanon's S2E03 result-discounting?

No.

The relevant trigger is absent. The episode shows the state is not globally continuous, which narrows it, but recurrence under competitive/talent pressure remains OPEN.

## 17.8 Does S2E04 add new Wien evidence?

No substantive new evidence. Wien appears only in recap material already semantically admitted from S2E03. Her targeted Kanon/talent framework and unknown motive remain unchanged.
