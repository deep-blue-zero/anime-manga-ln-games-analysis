# Love Live! Superstar!! — S2E10 Deep Reading V2
## `渋谷に響く歌` / `A Song That Rings Through Shibuya`

# 1. Governing thesis

S2E10 converts Season 2's succession problem from **shared membership** into **shared authorship**, while converting Wien Margarete's competitive challenge from a vague superiority threat into an explicit disagreement over what song is for.

The episode never solves the first-/second-year difference by pretending it has vanished. Shiki still believes Chisato's dance competence is in a different category from her own. Mei minimizes her piano history and warns Ren not to expect much. Kinako treats her old lyric notebook as embarrassing and initially withdraws it. Those are real asymmetries and vulnerabilities. The episode's answer is not equalized skill. It is **structured co-authorship**.

Chisato says the Tokyo-regional song and dance will be made jointly by first- and second-years. When Shiki interprets that as an impossible skill contest, Chisato explicitly rejects the premise: `別に ダンスで 私と競争しようっていうんじゃないよ？`. She will propose movement first; Shiki will watch, evaluate, and help shape it. Ren uses her own compositional competence and an ordinary shared gaming rapport to pull Mei's piano knowledge into the arrangement. Kanon responds to Kinako's creative shame not by saying the shame is childish, but by disclosing that she herself still finds showing lyrics deeply embarrassing and had once wanted to rip up her notebook. The junior is therefore not asked to imitate a fictional senior confidence. She is invited into a creative process in which vulnerability is already legitimate.

By the end of the camp, the junior contributions are not symbolic. The episode names the outputs: Kinako reports the lyrics complete; Shiki says the choreography is decided; Mei says the music is complete. The first-years have become people who help author the form Liella! will take into its highest-stakes stage so far.

At the same time, Wien's `本当の歌` claim becomes substantially more legible. Publicly she states that she entered Love Live! to show school idols how low-level the field is and to teach them true song. Backstage she gives the governing proposition:

> `歌は力｡`
>
> `そして 私は 未来を 私自身でビルドする｡`
>
> `歌の力で｡`

Song, for Wien in this moment, is power through which she will build her own future herself.

Liella!'s reflective counter-formulation is intentionally less universal. After effort, support, play, renewed desire, and distributed creation, the group describes school idols as simultaneously joyful and difficult, as a practice through which people are supported and grow together, and says:

> `その気持ちが 歌になってあふれる｡`
>
> `それが 私たちにとっての 本当の歌なんじゃないかな｡`

The `私たちにとって` matters. Liella! does not establish a metaphysical monopoly over music. Its answer is situated: **for us**, true song is what overflows from shared lived experience—effort, difficulty, support, play, growth, joy, and the relationships that made the song possible.

The confrontation complicates that pluralism. When Wien repeats `歌は力`, Kanon answers `違う` and `そんなの… 本当の歌じゃない`. Under direct antagonistic pressure her language becomes more categorical than the earlier group reflection. That is a real state difference, not something to smooth away. But the episode supplies important counterevidence against a simplistic moral binary: `Edelstein` itself contains language of weakness, self-belief, a wish to fulfill, tears, and passion. Kanon's claim that what reaches her from Wien is merely `勝つ` is therefore **Kanon's reception of the dominant organizing motive**, not an exhaustive transcript of everything Wien expresses.

This matters for the incoming S2E03 test. Wien is now an objectively formidable rival who has defeated Sunny Passion and whose stage visibly intimidates Liella!. Kanon says her song and dance were overwhelming and that Wien completely draws her own world. Yet Kanon does not respond with `たまたま`, does not discount `Chance Way`, and does not transform Wien's superiority into an identity claim that Liella! or Kanon herself is fake. The pressure produces inquiry—`本当の歌って 何なんだろう？`—and then value clarification. Talent comparison alone is therefore insufficient to reproduce S2E03's positive-evidence discounting. A direct adverse ranked result remains untested because S2E10 ends before first place is revealed.

The strongest sealed S2E10 thesis is therefore:

> **Succession becomes durable when juniors help author what the group will become rather than merely being permitted to remain inside it. S2E10 preserves real skill asymmetry but structures first-/second-year collaboration so Kinako, Shiki, and Mei make load-bearing contributions to lyrics, choreography, and composition. That shared authorship becomes part of Liella!'s answer to Wien: where Wien publicly defines song as power for building her own future, Liella! defines its own song as the overflow of effort, difficulty, support, play, growth, and shared joy. Competitive superiority is acknowledged rather than denied, but it does not force Liella! to imitate the rival's value system or reactivate Kanon's pre-result self-erasure. The Tokyo result remains OPEN at the S2E10 seal.**

Compactly:

> **The group becomes harder to exclude from itself when juniors help author its future, and competition sharpens rather than replaces its own account of why it sings.**

# 3. Naive structural read

## 3.1 Winter camp turns nine-member inclusion into production design

Liella! holds a winter training camp in Hokkaido at Kinako's family location. The premise is not only increased practice. Kanon explains that a place unlike their normal environment should help concentrated work and adds that she wants to reduce the distance with the first-years: `１年生とも もっと距離を縮めたいなって`.

Shiki compresses the desired state into `一体感`.

The first-years immediately expose the limitation of treating closeness as sufficient. Emotional familiarity does not erase the technical gap that S2E09 made competitively salient. S2E10 therefore turns unity into an **authorship design problem**: if nine-member integration is real, how can juniors contribute to the actual construction of the Tokyo-regional stage without pretending they already possess senior craft mastery?

## 3.2 Chisato gives Shiki a real choreography lane

At **00:04:17–05:08**, Chisato asks Shiki to create choreography with her. The governing policy is explicit:

> `東京大会は 曲もダンスも全部`
>
> `１年生と２年生が 共同で作る！`

Shiki understands the unity argument but says it is impossible: `それは理解｡ でも無理｡` She then uses a beetle/roly-poly comparison to emphasize how far apart their dancing levels feel.

Chisato does not answer by denying the gap. Instead she rejects Shiki's interpretation of the task as competition:

> `別に ダンスで 私と競争しようっていうんじゃないよ？`
>
> `一緒に 振り付けを考える｡`
>
> `力を貸してほしいんだ｡`

The process is then scaffolded: Chisato will propose movement first, Shiki will watch it and give opinions, and they will build iteratively.

This is more analytically important than simply “Chisato encourages Shiki.” She changes the **unit of qualification**. Shiki does not need to equal Chisato's dance ability to possess judgment relevant to choreography. Expert initiation and junior authorship can coexist.

## 3.3 Ren and Mei move from cooperative play into composition

At **00:05:10–06:06**, Ren and Mei are initially absorbed in cooperative gaming. Ren admits she has gotten too excited about co-op; Mei reminds her they are supposed to compose.

When the task becomes personal, Mei minimizes her competence:

> `私に期待すんなよ？`
>
> `ピアノっつっても 小さい頃から`
> `親にやらされてただけだからな｡`

Ren points to direct evidence: she has heard Mei play. She then infers that continuing this long likely means Mei did not hate it:

> `続けてこられたということは`
> `嫌いじゃなかったはずです｡`

That inference should not be canonized as omniscient knowledge of Mei's childhood motive. What is directly established is that Mei has a concrete piano history and that Ren recognizes usable capacity rather than accepting Mei's self-minimization as a complete qualification judgment.

The scene ends with Ren turning the task back to Mei: `次は メイさんですよ`. Mei participates.

S2E06's utility-conditioned belonging therefore receives constructive pressure rather than a failure trigger. The same desire to be useful that can produce self-removal when Mei believes she is a liability can support active contribution when a legitimate, scaffolded contribution lane exists.

## 3.4 Kanon and Kinako make creative shame reciprocal

At **00:06:06–07:18**, Kinako asks whether lyrics have arrived yet and then reveals she has an old notebook in her room where she accumulated lyrics. She immediately withdraws the offer because it is embarrassing.

Kanon does not pressure her:

> `無理しなくていいよ｡`
>
> `きな子ちゃんの大切な気持ちが詰まった ノートだもんね｡`

Kinako then devalues her words. Kanon answers not with authority but with exposure of her own continuing vulnerability:

> `私もね すっごい恥ずかしいんだ｡`
>
> `最初 みんなに見せる時`
> `ノート全部 ビリビリにしたいくらい｡`

Most importantly, Kanon says:

> `私なんて 一人じゃ てんでダメ｡`

This must be read contextually. It is not S2E03's global identity-negative `足手まとい` grammar. Kanon is describing collaborative creative dependence: the work becomes possible through other people, and Kinako's embarrassment reassures her because it makes the vulnerability shared.

Kinako jokes that she is being made collateral damage. Kanon extends the metaphor:

> `でも そのあと待っている うれしさとか`
> `感動とかも 巻き添えにできるから`

and asks:

> `一緒に 頑張ってみよ？`

This is one of the strongest mentoring scenes in the V2 pass because Kanon does not give the junior a story in which the senior has already transcended the relevant weakness. She says, in effect: **I still feel this too; we can carry the embarrassment together and also share what comes after it.**

## 3.5 Keke and Sumire demonstrate post-repair conflict durability

At **00:07:18–08:09**, Keke and Sumire prepare a handmade backdrop for the remote press conference while Natsumi watches. Natsumi explicitly notices the discontinuity:

> `抱き合って 泣いてみたり けんかしてみたり`
> `それで いいんですの？`

Sumire says Keke is stubborn. Keke says Sumire is annoying. They continue arguing and damage the backdrop, to Natsumi's budget horror.

S2E09's high-stakes disclosure and attachment repair therefore does not produce sanitized agreement. More usefully, their relationship can return to ordinary irritation without the conflict reacquiring the prior meaning of abandonment or membership rupture. This strongly supports a distinction that Mei had articulated earlier about friendship surviving anger and disagreement.

## 3.6 The press conference integrates failure, ambition, and audience value

At **00:08:36–09:10**, Liella! formally introduces itself. Keke visibly stalls at `えっと…`; Kanon continues the statement. The group says that last year it could not advance to the final, that for a year everyone has worked to reach the final together, and:

> `叶うことなら 優勝を目指して`
> `みんなを笑顔にできる ライブをしたいと思っています｡`

This is a precise public formulation of the frozen competition model. Past failure is admitted without identity collapse. Winning is named directly. Audience joy remains part of the purpose rather than an ornamental justification added after competition.

Afterward Kanon says she still cannot get used to press exposure. Keke thanks her for helping; Kanon says the words simply came out. Formal public representation has therefore become behaviorally available without becoming emotionally effortless.

Keke's momentary stall is also valuable negative evidence against a flattened “Keke is always fearless/extroverted” simulation model. Her usual directness does not guarantee fluent formal representation under every public condition.

## 3.7 Wien turns personal challenge into a public institutional judgment

At **00:09:29–09:48**, Wien is introduced as the major competitor and states:

> `私が 『ラブライブ！』に出場するのは`
> `ここが いかに低レベルであるかを`
> `スクールアイドルたちに 知ってもらうため｡`
> `私が 本当の歌を教えてあげる｡`

S2E09 had already established Wien's habit of addressing rivalry as aesthetic legitimacy. S2E10 expands the target. This is not only a private insult aimed at Kanon. Wien publicly generalizes the claim to Love Live! and school idols as a domain.

The other members become angry. Shiki immediately supplies the difficult counterweight: Wien defeated Sunny Passion. Her claim may be offensive, but the group's evidence model cannot simply dismiss her demonstrated competitive potency.

## 3.8 Kanon responds to superiority with inquiry rather than self-negation

At **00:10:33–11:39**, Kanon says being alone makes her think too much about Wien. She nevertheless says the first-years are working hard and are `すごく頼もしい`.

Her question is not “Are we frauds?” but:

> `本当の歌って 何なんだろう？`

Kanon evaluates Wien without denial:

> `歌 ダンス 共に圧倒的だった｡`
> `すごいと思うよ｡`
> `自分の世界を完全に描けてる｡`

She then makes an inference:

> `マルガレーテちゃんも きっと歌が大好き｡`

Because of that assumed love, Kanon expects Wien to understand song's ability to move people. Instead, what Kanon says reaches her is `勝つ` and only that, `まるで氷`.

Three evidentiary layers must remain separate:

1. **fact:** Wien's demonstrated performance is strong enough that Kanon calls it overwhelming and fully controlled;
2. **Kanon inference:** Wien probably loves singing;
3. **Kanon reception:** the dominant feeling she receives from Wien is winning, experienced as ice-like.

The later `Edelstein` lyrics provide counterevidence to treating layer 3 as a complete semantic description of Wien.

## 3.9 Voluntary junior overwork triggers Chisato's rest authority

After the press event, the first-years continue practicing voluntarily. The renewed rival threat has not made them withdraw; it has made them intensify effort.

Chisato then claims the next day's training plan and announces:

> `今日は～ 練習なし～！`

When the juniors protest, she states:

> `頑張るためには 休みも大事！`

Ren diagnoses that the group may have become too fixated on improvement and winning. Chisato says she wants them to forget that for a day:

> `歌も練習も 全部忘れて`
> `みんなで楽しく遊ぼう！`

This adds an important presidency/coaching function. Chisato does not only calibrate **how much** effort the group can bear. She can recognize when the activity is becoming over-instrumentalized and authorize genuinely non-instrumental time.

The later creative breakthrough does not retroactively make the rest day a covert productivity trick. The stated purpose is precisely to stop using every hour as a means to get better or win. That non-instrumentality is part of why the scene matters.

## 3.10 Natsumi identifies a desire that cannot be monetized

During play, Natsumi still asks for more `映える` material and evaluates a beautiful snow object through whether it can make money. Another member explicitly says this is enough and points out that now that they are nine they should make memories together.

Then Natsumi is asked:

> `今度は 夏美ちゃんのやりたいこと しよ？`

She pauses:

> `私の… やりたいこと…｡`

and finally answers:

> `思い切り 雪合戦したかったんですの～！`

The desire is ordinary, social, and non-monetized. She needs the group precisely because a real snowball fight requires more people.

Afterward, however, the metric reflex returns immediately. She says a pre-regional “we played around” video cannot be uploaded and that the time has been wasted. Yet in the next breath she admits she already wants to practice again.

S2E10 therefore strengthens a two-system model rather than replacing one with another:

- Natsumi **can** identify wants not reducible to money/content when explicitly invited to ask what she herself wants;
- the productivity/virality frame remains her default explanatory reflex afterward.

## 3.11 Non-instrumental play precedes distributed creation

After the rest day, a new musical idea appears. The first-years realize the day was not “just” play in the narrow experiential sense; more importantly, the group now chooses to complete the emerging song together:

> `かのんの生み出そうとしている曲を`
> `みんなで完成させましょう｡`

The later status report is unusually diagnostic:

> `出来たっす 歌詞｡`
>
> `振り付け 決まった｡`
>
> `曲も完成したぞ｡`
>
> `衣装も考えてみたわよ｡`

Kinako, Shiki, and Mei now hold named creative outputs. Sumire contributes costume expertise. The episode does not flatten role asymmetry: Chisato, Ren, and Kanon still structure their domains. It demonstrates that structure and authorship are compatible.

Natsumi's response—`これは バズる話題ですの！`—also preserves her media reflex inside the now pro-group system. Membership has aligned her goal more rapidly than it has rewritten every method of valuation.

## 3.12 Liella! formulates a situated `true song`

The group reflects on the process:

> `一生懸命 頑張って`
> `みんなに応援してもらって`
> `みんなと一緒に成長できる｡`

School idols are `すっごく楽しくて` and, as Mei adds, `すっごく大変`. Yet there is a joy available here that is not available elsewhere, and:

> `その気持ちが 歌になってあふれる｡`

The conclusion is explicitly situated:

> `それが 私たちにとっての 本当の歌なんじゃないかな｡`

This is a stronger model than “Liella! believes friendship is the real music.” It includes effort, pain, public support, reciprocal growth, play, joy, and accumulated shared history. Song is the form through which that lived ecology overflows.

## 3.13 Wien defines song as self-authored power

Immediately before the Tokyo stage, Wien addresses Kanon again:

> `私が 本当の歌を教えてあげる｡`
> `歌は力｡`
> `そして 私は 未来を 私自身でビルドする｡`
> `歌の力で｡`

This is the clearest Wien worldview statement yet.

It establishes:

- song as **power**;
- future as something to be **built**;
- the builder as **Wien herself**;
- song as the instrument through which that self-authored future will be constructed.

It does **not** yet establish why her future requires this construction, what external constraint she is fighting, or why Kanon is the privileged witness/rival. Those remain OPEN.

# 5. Succession after S2E10: from access to authorship

## 5.1 The unresolved problem entering S2E10

Season 2 had already established that the first-years legitimately belong. S2E09 even rejected removing them when exclusion appeared competitively rational. But a weaker system could still preserve them as **protected participants** whose meaningful decisions remain mostly senior-authored.

S2E10 directly attacks that residual hierarchy.

## 5.2 Chisato does not fake skill equality

Shiki's refusal is analytically reasonable. If “co-create choreography” means “perform at Chisato's level as a choreographer,” the task appears absurd from Shiki's current self-model.

Chisato's key intervention is to change the task definition:

- Chisato may generate an initial movement;
- Shiki may evaluate it;
- opinion is itself contribution;
- iteration can create shared authorship.

This is a durable model of inclusive expertise because it does not require either lowering the expert to novice status or pretending the novice already possesses the expert's full competence.

## 5.3 Mei's utility rule gets a positive branch

Mei has repeatedly tied school-idol legitimacy to helping Liella!. S2E09 showed the dangerous branch: if she thinks juniors lower the chance of victory, she can offer to remove herself from the stage.

S2E10 shows the constructive branch:

> **salient usable competence + trusted domain partner + concrete shared task → contribution rather than withdrawal.**

Her piano history becomes part of Liella!'s composition. That does not cure the failure-triggered utility rule, but it expands the evidence she has available when judging whether she can help.

## 5.4 Kinako becomes a co-author through reciprocal vulnerability

Kanon's technique differs from simple reassurance. She does not say Kinako's lyrics are definitely excellent before reading them. She protects the option not to disclose, validates the notebook as carrying Kinako's important feelings, and then supplies autobiographical evidence that creative shame persists even in someone Kinako treats as a senior model.

The resulting invitation is not “be like me.” It is “I am still like you in this relevant way; let's do the difficult part together.”

That is a particularly strong anti-prestige mechanism because it makes senior ordinariness directly productive rather than merely comforting.

## 5.5 Authorship is distributed but not symmetrical

The episode should not be summarized as “everyone does everything equally.” It shows differentiated production:

- Chisato ↔ Shiki: choreography;
- Ren ↔ Mei: composition/piano;
- Kanon ↔ Kinako: lyrics;
- Sumire: costume proposal;
- Natsumi: media/production framing remains available but is not made sovereign;
- Keke participates in group preparation/representation but S2E10 does not establish an equivalent named specialist output that should be invented for symmetry.

This is exactly the architecture the frozen S1 model predicted: differences remain operational.

# 6. Chisato's presidency: effort includes the authority to stop effort

## 6.1 S2E09 had authorized harder shared training

The prior episode ended with informed consent to special training: the nine would try to win together rather than optimize through exclusion.

A simplistic continuation would reward that decision by intensifying every hour.

S2E10 instead introduces a second-order leadership requirement: Chisato must govern the **meaning of effort**, not only its volume.

## 6.2 Voluntary overwork is still governable

The first-years are not being coerced when they practice extra. Their effort is real and self-authored. But the S2E09 lesson already established that self-authorship does not automatically make every sacrifice a healthy group-governance result.

Chisato therefore does not treat voluntary extra practice as outside her responsibility.

## 6.3 Rest is explicitly non-instrumental

`頑張るためには 休みも大事` could by itself be read as conventional performance optimization. The following lines prevent that reduction:

> `それを １回 忘れたいんだ！`
>
> `歌も練習も 全部忘れて`
> `みんなで楽しく遊ぼう！`

The point is not merely recovery so that tomorrow's metrics improve. The group is permitted to exist together without converting the moment into a resource for victory.

The creative breakthrough that follows is therefore an **emergent payoff**, not the stated justification for leisure.

## 6.4 Current Chisato governance model

The presidency now includes at least four distinct modes:

1. individualized technical pacing;
2. honest competitive calibration;
3. consent-based escalation when stakes are shared;
4. deliberate de-instrumentalization when skill/winning becomes too totalizing.

That is a substantially richer operating style than merely “Chisato is the dance coach who became president.”

# 8. `True song`: competing organizing principles

## 8.1 Wien's public definition is hierarchical

Wien says Love Live! is low-level and that she is there to teach school idols true song. This places her in an evaluator/teacher position above the institution.

## 8.2 Wien's backstage definition is self-authored

`歌は力` is followed by `未来を 私自身でビルドする`. The important sequence is:

**song → power → self-authored future construction**.

The target of the power is not yet known. The source boundary does not establish a family demand, institutional gate, migration condition, or other external mechanism. Do not fill that gap retrospectively.

## 8.3 Liella!'s definition is ecological

Liella!'s campfire formulation is built from:

- hard work;
- receiving support;
- growing with other people;
- fun;
- difficulty;
- a joy unique to the practice;
- those feelings overflowing as song.

Song is not only an instrument for reaching the future. It is an expressive consequence of relations already being lived.

## 8.4 The contrast is not individualism versus collectivism in the abstract

Wien's stage itself contains desire, vulnerability, and self-belief. Liella! also has personal ambition and explicitly wants to win. The episode therefore does not support a clean binary in which Wien is selfish/power-hungry and Liella! is pure communal selflessness.

A more precise contrast is:

- **Wien:** song is organized around self-authored power capable of constructing the future;
- **Liella!:** song is organized around shared experience whose accumulated feeling overflows into performance.

Both models contain agency and ambition. They differ in what makes song meaningful enough to authorize the ambition.

## 8.5 Kanon's `氷` metaphor is relational reception

Kanon says only `勝つ` reaches her and describes it as ice-like. Because `Edelstein` contains more than that, the metaphor should not be promoted into narrator truth.

It reveals what Kanon is currently unable to receive from Wien: relational warmth/purpose beyond the competitive vector.

That may say something about Wien; it certainly says something about Kanon's evaluative grammar.

# 19. Episode-bound final model

At the S2E10 boundary, Liella! is a nine-member group that has now crossed an important threshold: its junior members are not merely protected participants, recruited successors, or sources of perspective. They are **co-authors**.

That fact does not erase hierarchy. Chisato remains the stronger dancer and the person capable of structuring choreography work. Ren remains a more established composer. Kanon remains the experienced lyric/meaning integrator. The episode does not apologize for those asymmetries. It makes them permeable.

Shiki can matter to choreography before she can match Chisato's dance. Mei can matter to composition while still minimizing a piano history she did not originally choose. Kinako can write lyrics while still finding her notebook mortifying. The seniors' job is not to pronounce the juniors equal. It is to design conditions in which the junior's relevant judgment and material can change the product.

This makes S2E10 one of the strongest character-modeling episodes of Season 2 because the method by which inclusion works is observable. Chisato changes the task definition. Ren moves through shared ordinary activity into turn-taking craft. Kanon shares the exact kind of shame that is blocking Kinako and protects the right not to disclose before inviting collaboration.

The resulting song then becomes evidence for the group's philosophy. Liella! does not answer Wien by claiming she lacks talent. Kanon does the opposite: she calls Wien overwhelming and recognizes the completeness of her artistic world. The disagreement shifts from **who is better** to **what singing is doing**.

Wien's current doctrine is now clear enough to model without overreaching: song is power, and through that power she will build her future herself. The reasons for that doctrine remain sealed. Her performance also resists caricature, carrying weakness, wish, tears, and self-belief alongside victory/future construction.

Liella!'s own account is explicitly relational and situated. They work hard, are supported, grow together, have fun, suffer difficulty, experience a unique joy, and let those feelings overflow into song. `私たちにとって` prevents the reflective claim from requiring every singer in the world to share their ontology.

Kanon's backstage rejection is more absolute because the conflict is direct. That tension is useful character evidence: her reflective meaning-making is plural/situated, while her protective confrontation can compress into categorical language.

Most importantly for the prospective experiment, Wien's visible superiority does **not** restore S2E03's `たまたま` logic before the result. Kanon is intimidated, thinks deeply, and disagrees, but she does not invalidate Liella!'s prior achievement. After `Edelstein`, she sees the group freeze and redirects them toward the people who came to hear them and the city in which their song will sound.

S2E10 therefore ends with two things unresolved at once:

1. the **competitive institutional verdict**—first place is not shown;
2. the deeper **philosophical verdict**—the episode gives both performances rather than granting an omniscient declaration that one conception of song is universally true.

The sealed endpoint is:

> **Liella! enters the unresolved Tokyo-regional result as a more genuinely nine-authored group than it was when the episode began. Wien has forced the group to define its own musical purpose but has not forced it into self-denial or imitation. Competition is now severe enough to threaten the future, yet the nine preserve differentiated authorship, non-instrumental relationship, and an audience-facing reason to sing.**
