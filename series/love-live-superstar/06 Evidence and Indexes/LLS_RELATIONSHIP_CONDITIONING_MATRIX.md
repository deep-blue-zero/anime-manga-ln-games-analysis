# Love Live! Superstar!! — Relationship Conditioning Matrix

## Purpose

Directional model of how a character's behavior and speech change by interlocutor and relationship state. `A → B` is not assumed to equal `B → A`.

This is a modeling ledger, not a shipping chart. Intimacy, admiration, dependence, conflict, mentorship, romance coding, and institutional authority remain distinct variables.

## Matrix fields

- A → B
- temporal boundary
- relation state
- authority/asymmetry
- what B activates in A
- characteristic A behavior/speech with B
- conflict/repair pattern
- counterexample/uncertainty
- evidence
- confidence

## S1E01 directional states

### Kanon → Keke

- **State:** newly met; Keke identifies Kanon through singing and attempts recruitment.
- **What Keke activates:** exposure of Kanon's disowned musical desire; protective impulse when Keke is dismissed; possibility of participating through helping someone else.
- **Behavior:** initial avoidance/resistance; later sustained defense of Keke against Ren; eventually accepts reciprocal support rather than remaining only helper.
- **Speech effect:** can speak assertively *for Keke* while remaining hesitant about herself.
- **Uncertainty:** no stable friendship hierarchy or dependency should yet be inferred.
- **Confidence:** high locally.

### Keke → Kanon

- **State:** recruiter → unusually valued prospective collaborator.
- **What Kanon activates:** enthusiastic recognition of singing ability and persistent recruitment.
- **Behavior:** overt praise, pursuit, repeated invitation; challenges Kanon's attempt to declare singing “over.”
- **Relational function:** refuses Kanon's one-way arrangement in which Kanon supports Keke but excludes herself from equivalent desire/risk.
- **Confidence:** high locally.

### Kanon → Chisato

- **State:** pre-existing familiarity/friendship.
- **Conditioning:** Kanon can discuss the entrance-exam failure and promise to quit singing with someone who already knows the context.
- **Current evidence:** interaction does not produce the same defensive confrontation as institutional evaluation, but the sample is small.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### Chisato → Kanon

- **State:** familiar supportive friend.
- **Behavior:** states desire to hear Kanon's singing without making that desire an instruction.
- **Relational implication:** currently supports agency rather than trying to decide for Kanon.
- **Confidence:** medium-high.

### Kanon → Ren

- **State:** peer encounter mediated by institutional conflict.
- **What Ren activates:** assertive defense when Kanon perceives unfair treatment of Keke; self-protective hesitation when Ren redirects the question to Kanon's own desire.
- **Speech contrast:** fluent challenge → incomplete `私は…`.
- **Confidence:** high for the contrast; motive beyond it remains open.

### Ren → Kanon

- **State:** institutional challenger; recognizes Kanon as an intervening party and then potential participant.
- **Behavior:** responds to Kanon's defense by asking whether Kanon herself wants to become a school idol.
- **Relational effect:** forces Kanon to move from third-person advocacy to first-person self-definition.
- **Confidence:** high locally.

### Keke → Ren

- **State:** aspiring organizer/recruiter versus student enforcing/protecting institutional procedure.
- **Conflict axis:** Keke treats Yuigaoka's musical identity as reason school idols should belong; Ren treats the same identity as reason unauthorized activity is especially inappropriate.
- **Behavior:** direct clash over legitimacy rather than established personal rivalry.
- **Confidence:** high.

### Ren → Keke

- **State:** procedural opposition to an unauthorized new activity.
- **Behavior:** formal restriction/demand for permission; currently evaluates the activity before knowing Keke deeply.
- **Uncertainty:** do not convert institutional opposition into a complete personal judgment of Keke.
- **Confidence:** high for observable stance; low for deeper relational motive.
