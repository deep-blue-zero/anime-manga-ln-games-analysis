## From anticipated danger to an ordinary recipe

Sensei is struggling to finish work when Satsuki arrives voluntarily rather than as the scheduled duty student. She presents handmade chocolate as a way to become more intimate and says that an exhausted worker needs sugar (scene:001:u:0001–0014). The intimacy claim and practical attention to fatigue coexist with a declared political aim: Sensei is a potential Pandemonium Society collaborator. The scene therefore permits both personal care and instrumental self-presentation without proving which is her only motive.

The delay before tasting and the announcement of a special recipe provoke anticipation that something is wrong. Satsuki amplifies that anticipation by saying that it is too late and asking whether an effect has been felt (u:0016–0026). The adult’s suspicion is part of the comic structure; it is not independent evidence that the chocolate contains a harmful substance.

The secret turns out to be repeated wishes for deliciousness, presented as privileged intelligence from another organization (u:0027–0034). She describes circling the chocolate while reciting `おいしくな～れ`. Her institutional language magnifies an ordinary labor of care into a clandestine method. The story does not disclose a chemical recipe or demonstrate that the recitation caused the taste.

She forecasts Sensei’s submission to Pandemonium Society, interprets a reasonably good reaction as partial success, and stays until all the chocolate is eaten (u:0035–0039). These are her forecast and appraisal plus an observable persistence, not accomplished recruitment. The ending lets the gift succeed as food and an occasion for attention even when her official victory claim remains comic and unverified.
