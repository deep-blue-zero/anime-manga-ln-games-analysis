## What changes during the encounter

Kirara summons Sensei with a possible failing-grade problem. The narration establishes the message as the adult’s reason for visiting; it does not independently prove the alleged academic danger. Her unusually excited account and Sensei’s comment on that excitement put the pretext under comic pressure from the start (scene:001:u:0001–0006; choice:001).

Her desired crepe is chocolate-rich and materially difficult: she identifies the thin batter as the problem. Sensei offers to work together. The narration then shows repeated adjustment of batter thickness and heat, followed by a round, attractive crepe (u:0008–0018; choice:002). This joint practice is real even though the emergency claim will be withdrawn. It establishes an adult participating in a student’s ordinary project and lets a small skill problem receive attention without requiring a crisis to justify it.

The request for a score initially preserves the apparent educational framing. Kirara then explicitly admits that the alarming assessment was false and explains that she wanted Sensei to eat a handmade crepe for Valentine’s Day (u:0019–0022). The disclosure revises the audience’s interpretation of the invitation. It does not erase the shared labor already shown or prove that all of her difficulties were fabricated: thin crepe production did require trial and error.

Sensei’s delighted evaluation and the final shared eating complete the aim as an enjoyable encounter (choice:004–005; u:0023–0025). The story’s result is situated pleasure and a successful invitation. It supplies neither an institutional grade nor a promise of exclusive affection.
