## Social world argument

These stories make gift-making a field of agency. Umika chooses a festival form, Reijo a martial form, Chiaki a photographic form and Rio an engineering/institutional form. Their practices are not interchangeable symptoms of generic affection. Each student tries to make a recognizably personal object and encounters a recipient whose response may differ from the intended meaning. Pleasure, embarrassment and minor mismatch deserve preservation even when no public status changes.

Hospitality also carries authority and boundaries. Kisaki’s tea invitation includes an undisclosed modification; Seia secures prolonged attention by deception; Satsuki recasts handmade care as recruitment. A mature account should retain the comic or intimate frame while identifying information and role asymmetry. The adult’s gratitude or participation is not blanket endorsement of every claim made about the encounter.

The counterpart-like Shiroko visitor offers a particularly important directional reversal: she visits to relieve Sensei’s worry and thanks the adult for remaining familiar. Care here includes a student watching over an adult. It is a situated interaction that broadens crisis-heavy evidence, not proof of permanent recovery or the complete resolution of two-Shiroko coexistence.
