Sensory pleasure is therefore an articulated aim, not decorative scenery. She anticipates that Sensei has tired of sweet chocolate and designs an alternative; that anticipation is her appraisal rather than an independently verified survey of the adult’s tastes.

Her health-benefit assertions and the narrator’s feeling of relaxation must stay separate (u:0015–0019). The source shows a pleasant subjective experience and Kisaki’s claims about cacao. Neither establishes real therapeutic effects. The proposed future exchange of tea, invitations and Schale visits extends hospitality as possible continuing contact (u:0020–0023), without proving that those visits subsequently occur.

When Sensei’s preference for her tea is echoed back, Kisaki stages a rebuke and threatens to keep appearing where Sensei is. She then explicitly calls the punishment a joke and admits her own mischief (u:0024–0038). She says she asked the alchemy research club to add a further effect to the tea, then approaches Sensei and frames the aroma as her own way of conveying feelings to an important person on Valentine’s Day (u:0039–0056). The intense fragrance and physical approach supply intimacy coding. The exact added substance, mechanism and full bodily consequences are not disclosed.
