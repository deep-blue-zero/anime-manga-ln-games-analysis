## 3. Preferences and refusals are substantive outcomes

In scene 2, Hina finds no favorite accessory design, expresses dislike and is unfamiliar with its name while recognizing that others find it cute. In scene 3, little arcade experience, limited interest, noise, glitter and crowding lead her to want the next place. Her acknowledgment of other people's tastes matters alongside her own refusal.

These are local preferences. They do not establish a universal dislike of all cute objects or settle what separate written-data evidence might show. An unfulfilled purchase is not a reason to discard the preferences that prevented it.

Scene 4 retains her awareness of street fashion, cost, lack of need for private clothes and explanation of wearing a uniform. `s4/C001` offers two alternatives under selections 7 and 8, followed by equal questions at `u8–u9`. No clothing selection or purchase is completed.

Together these stops establish a repertoire of taste and restraint. They show what Hina does not want on this outing without turning that repertoire into a fixed global personality rule.
