## 2. Safety is Hina's account, not a demonstrated guarantee

The `#place` record at `u1/raw128106` is a system record with an empty person field. Its Gehenna-outskirts/slum location supports the canonical prose setting without turning the system record into a character's speech.

Hina's early account of a disciplinary operation and access seal supplies her reason for expecting safety. The operation is not actually depicted. Japanese describes safety for a while; Korean describes a few days. Neither is a certified safety interval or evidence of universal observer absence.

At `u5` she expects nobody who recognizes her; at `u6` she says she can meet Sensei with peace of mind. No enemy is actually present. Her protective explanation at `u8`, followed by the many enemies inside and outside, continual targeting and possible danger to a special contact at `u9–u11`, remains attributed to her. The episode does not establish a completed attack or identify a culprit.

These statements give institutional work a private consequence: meeting somebody requires attention to visibility and the risks she perceives. The worry matters without making every asserted threat an independently verified condition.

## 3. Alternative arrangements expose hesitation rather than impossibility

`C003` offers two alternatives about remote work and email. The associated command-only records at `raw128120–raw128121` have equal empty Japanese text while retaining selections 1 and 2. Empty command text is not grounds for collapsing the alternative identities or declaring their content absent.

Hina's hesitation and agreement at `u12–u15` do not prove remote work impossible. It neither independently establishes that face-to-face work is necessary nor converts the encounter into a confessed date.

The episode thus preserves the awkward process of finding an acceptable reason to meet. Institutional language, embarrassment and desired company coexist; one need not cancel the others.

## 5. A revised risk assessment leaves the practical question open

At `u33–u34`, Hina revises the problem: night may be dangerous while a daytime meeting may be safe. Japanese keeps a modal possibility; Korean is more definite in retrospect. Her daytime busyness at `u35` then obstructs that possible solution. The question at `u37` about when to meet remains unresolved.

`u38/raw128152` actually narrates deep thinking. It does not narrate a departure, a daytime meeting, a new work product, rest, an attack or a fulfilled schedule. The printed outcome is continued deliberation.

That unfinished outcome preserves the importance of privacy, hesitation, ordinary work reasons, company and difficulty arranging future meetings. Low dramatic stakes do not make these disposable. Reducing the scene to dangerous enemies or comic embarrassment would erase the practical scheduling constraint that survives the conversation.
