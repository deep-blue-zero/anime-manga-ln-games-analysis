3. Base uniform, joking and the uncomfortable proximity of blood

BA:bond:20003:002, 「黒い制服」, scene1 units2–20, begins with a material inconvenience. Mashiro has borrowed Hasumi's sewing box for a provisional repair; vigorous movement could tear the skirt again, and a new one is pending. Her statements about Hasumi's size and Tsurugi's blood-marked skirt are reports in this conversation, not a new examination of either peer's body or a complete inventory of uniforms. The whole linked thread200030010, message sequence010–050/2136–2144, reports successful work and no injury, expresses gratitude for the teacher's command and mentions the frayed seam. Those retrospective statements remain Mashiro's messages rather than newly enacted battle scenes.

Units8–10 offer a frightening explanation of black uniforms: hiding an enemy's blood. The teacher's printed fearful response is followed by Mashiro's explicit declaration that the explanation was a joke. Her admission that people become afraid and that she lacks a talent for jokes makes this a local failure of intended lightness, not evidence that she cannot interact or has no humor. Units14–17 explicitly deny blood as the origin of the uniform's color while affirming that washing blood after operations is real. The teacher's attempt to classify the latter as another joke receives denial. The ending's crossing of joke and truth does not authorize treating both as harmless fiction or both as proven institutional history.

The actual endpoint is time spent hearing her worries, not replacement of the skirt, a successful comedy lesson, identified casualties or the removal of fear. Close attention to those endpoints preserves two things at once: practical uniform care and wanted adult conversation have ordinary value, while the joke/truth distinction retains a materially violent institutional environment. The profile's quietness therefore should not become a diagnosis of interpersonal incapacity. Her unsuccessful humor is a particular event.

5. Wanted practice, touching limits and pressure on the participant
