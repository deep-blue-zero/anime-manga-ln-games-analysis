## 2. Atsuko offers a specific but untested causal model

Atsuko states that the angel-like figures are **not** the literal seven trumpet angels of scripture. She says Subaru first **unconsciously** caused them and that Arius's accumulated anger, grief, resentment, despair and resignation became a `現象`. Earlier C002 E008 material about that accumulation and Ui's deprivation analogy is replayed (`u:0018-0044`). This is an explicit in-story explanation with Atsuko's Royal Blood authority and the observed phenomena behind it, but the exact physical/ritual mechanism, temporal baseline and alternate sources have not been independently tested. The replay is not a second source or new confirmation. Her suggestion that the forms *could have become* genuine, sufficient to return a “recompense” to Trinity if Subaru were a perfect judge, is a conditional supernatural counterfactual, not a realized attack on Trinity (`u:0022-0024,u:0045-0048`).

She identifies Maia as what Subaru's judgment lacked: when Subaru “discarded” her, the attempt to judge all Arius lost legitimacy. Atsuko distinguishes Subaru as a **medium** for the phenomenon from Subaru trying to **become** the trumpet angel/judge herself, invokes a scripture line about falling into one's own trap, and says Maia's reliance on Sensei might have prevented the perfect-judge path (`u:0049-0064`). These are Atsuko's causal and moral interpretations. Maia's E005 removal order was place-limited to the basilica, though E006 shows real isolation; “discarded” is Atsuko's judgment of the relationship, not a new all-Arius decree. The claim about what would have happened if Maia had not sought Sensei is untestable and does **not** assign blame to Maia for the dangerous event. Subaru gives broken, faint responses, suggesting this appeal reaches her but not proving control of the figures or reversal of the effect (`u:0053,u:0065-0069`).
