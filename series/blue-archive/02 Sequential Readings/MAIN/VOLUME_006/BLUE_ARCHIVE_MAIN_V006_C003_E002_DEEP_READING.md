## 1. Saori contrasts learned repetition with the possibility of chosen change

Saori asks where Subaru's Arius way leads. Subaru says clinging to memories signals weakness. Saori admits past harshness and casts their old roles as “strict instructor” (Saori) and “kind centurion” (Subaru). She inwardly recalls especially hard training imposed on her as Squad leader and how she reproduced it with others (`scene:001:u:0001-0020`). Subaru says people can only live as they have lived. Saori agrees that history shapes thought and thought often reproduces life, then says people also have a **possibility** of living by what they desire, something she learned after leaving Arius (`u:0021-0027`). This is a situated self-revision, not proof that anyone can simply escape constraints or that Subaru's material burden has vanished.

Saori recognizes Subaru gave lost children direction and, in the process, made that purpose her own. She asks what the method leads to and answers that hatred/anger alone remain, comparing it with the strict role she herself would have kept without outside experience and Sensei (`u:0028-0036`). This is Saori's diagnosis and counterfactual, not a demonstrated end state for every Arius resident. She does not declare Subaru's path judicially right or wrong; instead she asks her to learn even a little of another world and reconsider. She emphasizes that this appeal comes from Squad, who shared Arius's deprivation, rather than from outsiders who might sound condescending (`u:0037-0049`). Her insider status makes the invitation relationally different, but does not erase the residents' E005 charge that Squad left them.

Saori inwardly distinguishes shallow optimism from someone who knew the world's ugliness and still sought another chance. Logged lines recall Sensei's desire to speak on equal terms and to create a next opportunity, and she says she was persuaded by an `それでも` she once disliked (`u:0050-0059`). These are present self-understanding and callbacks, not a quotation audit of a new Sensei statement in this scene. Her change is a commitment to possibility amid hardship, not amnesia about injury or assured future success.
