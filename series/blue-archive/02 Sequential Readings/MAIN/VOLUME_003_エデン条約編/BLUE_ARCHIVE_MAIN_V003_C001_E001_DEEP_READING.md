Her stated *one interpretation* (`一つの解釈`) is a proof paradox: a person reaching true paradise would never leave it; someone who leaves thereby did not reach true paradise; therefore no true returnee can be observed outside to certify it (`u:0010-0015`). This is Seia's philosophical construction, not an audited cosmological property or proof that paradise exists or does not. She then asks whether unprovable truth is worthless and whether the apparently cynical rule asks something deeper (`u:0016`). The question is left open. She likens the Eden Treaty to an unattainable dream of paradise (`u:0017-0019`); the simile registers skepticism, not observed diplomatic failure. The fifth-rule wording and treaty name are a strong local motif, but neither resolves treaty efficacy.
