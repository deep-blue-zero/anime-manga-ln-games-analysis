## 2. Mika's recollection complicates motive without absolving harm

When Sensei asks why Mika refused the militants, she says she still dislikes Gehenna, recognizes an “opportunity,” but cannot explain why she does not want to act now (`choice:004; `u:0027-0032`). Seia's question about Mika's hatred leads into an apparent earlier Tea Party discussion (`u:0033-0052`): Nagisa asks Mika's intent in reconciling with Arius, Mika initially proposes straightforward contact/tea because they were once Trinity, and Nagisa notes they lack a district location or guarantee of acceptance. Seia presses political/war consequences, and Mika says those could be useful against Gehenna too. This does not certify the feasibility of reconciliation, a completed visit, or a single underlying motive. The dialogue's unmarked transition should not be treated as a live reunion of currently sleeping Seia and missing Nagisa with Mika.

An apparent continuation of the recollection has Mika saying Seia can be sent to hospital, an Arius student deferring to Squad, and Mika inwardly reacting to believed news that Seia died (`u:0053-0064`). She asks herself why she ordered an attack, then reconstructs wanting the host position to prevent a Gehenna treaty; she rationalized Seia as a necessary sacrifice, but ends `そうだった、はずなのに`—“that was how it was supposed to be”—in doubt (`u:0065-0074`). This deepens the prior self-confession and C003 E003's motive dispute, but neither proves Hanako's specific initial-abduction theory nor rewrites Seia's known survival. Mika directly apologizes to Seia and wants to see her and Nagisa again, doubting she is worthy (`u:0075-0083`). Those are genuine present wishes/remorse, **not delivered apologies, reconciliation, acquittal or restored Tea Party authority**.

Seia's commentary infers Mika sought to rationalize a bare dislike, then acknowledges she herself failed to know Mika's feelings and context; she says she forgives Mika and perhaps must ask forgiveness in turn (`u:0084-0100`). This is an important revision of Seia's own prior harsh judgment, but the frame does not establish that Mika hears her, that Nagisa agrees, or that institutional accountability is waived. Seia also says she still knows too little, preventing a total psychoanalytic closure. The harm and earlier command remain real even if personal forgiveness becomes possible.
