# 1. Story placement and local chronology

E011 established the Black Market as a city-scale zone outside effective Federal Student Council reach, but stopped before Abydos found a weapon seller, broker, or transaction record. It also introduced Hifumi as a local-information advantage: a student who initially needed rescue but knew enough about the zone's security environment to stop Abydos from escalating recklessly.

E012 develops both threads, then deliberately splits **character knowledge** from **audience knowledge**.

Its movement is:

> **Hifumi expands the Black Market's institutional profile → local expertise becomes a consensually recruited resource → narrative cross-cut to Problem Solver 68 → Aru receives a call from the Kaiser PMC director → failed combat is rhetorically reclassified as “practice” → Kaiser-side analysis treats Abydos as a data deviation → Black Suit proposes investigating the change factor → Problem Solver 68 confronts financial exhaustion → Aru's corporate self-image collides with solvency constraints → formal banking access appears blocked → Aru insists another financing method exists**

The crucial epistemic point is that **Abydos does not discover the Kaiser PMC director in this unit**.

The reader sees the client directly because the narrative leaves the investigators and cuts to the contractor. Therefore E012 advances `BA-C012` at the level of **narrative evidence** without granting the Countermeasures Committee equivalent in-world knowledge.

That distinction must be preserved in every later synthesis.

# 2. Narrative reconstruction

Hifumi and the Abydos students reach the entertainment district and believe they have created enough distance from the earlier fighting.

Shiroko observes that Hifumi clearly regards the Black Market as highly dangerous. Hifumi agrees and explains that it is one of the places beyond Federal Student Council reach. She then adds a scale estimate stronger than E011's visual impression: the Black Market alone is comparable to **several schools** in size and cannot simply be ignored.

Her institutional description becomes more concrete. She says she has heard that various `企業` compete over vested interests surrounding illegal activity. She also reports that the Black Market has dedicated financial institutions and security/public-order institutions of its own.

Serika is startled enough to translate these categories into familiar language: banks and police. She asks whether these are, of course, unauthorized illegal organizations. Hifumi confirms that they are.

Nonomi remarks that the scale is on another level. Hifumi repeats her practical warning from E011: of all these institutions, the security organization is the one they should especially avoid. If they cause trouble, hiding is the safest immediate response.

Hoshino notices how much Hifumi knows. Hifumi explains that she researched the area carefully beforehand precisely because it is dangerous.

Hoshino then jokingly converts the earlier rescue into a reciprocal obligation. As repayment for helping Hifumi, she says Hifumi will accompany them until they find what they are looking for.

Shiroko deadpans:

> `なるほど、誘拐だね。`

Hifumi panics. Serika immediately corrects the framing:

> `誘拐じゃなくて、案内をお願いしたいだけでしょ？`
> `もちろん、ヒフミさんが良ければ、だけど。`

Hifumi then agrees explicitly. She doubts whether she will be useful, but says Abydos has helped her and she is happy to assist.

The episode then cross-cuts away from Abydos.

A phone rings repeatedly near Problem Solver 68. Mutsuki asks why Aru is not answering. Kayoko reads Aru's dark expression and guesses that it is the client. Mutsuki immediately understands the problem: they have to report failure.

Aru finally answers as `便利屋68`.

The caller is labeled `カイザーPMC理事`.

The director calls the report interesting and says:

> `ここまでの練習は拝見したよ。`
> `で、実戦はいつだ？`

Aru starts to say that the previous battle **was** the real battle, then stops herself. She accepts the client's framing and promises that the real operation will occur soon—within a week.

Kayoko and Mutsuki react with shock. Aru recovers her executive performance and closes with `お任せください`.

After the call, the Kaiser PMC director privately returns to the problem of Abydos's performance. He says the data on them should have been accurate, wonders whether there was a calculation error, and notes that their power was clearly different from expectation.

Another role-labeled actor, `黒服`, appears and asks whether he is having difficulty.

The director denies being troubled and instead calls the problem a small error in calculation: Abydos was much stronger than the data.

Black Suit replies:

> `……データに不備はありません。`

and proposes a different interpretation:

> `これは単に、アビドスの生徒がさらに強くなった、と解釈すべきかと。`

When the director asks what that means, Black Suit says:

> `アビドスにどのような変化要因があったのか、確認してみましょう。`

He leaves.

The narrative returns to Problem Solver 68. Aru looks exhausted. Kayoko asks whether they are really going to fight again. Aru says she does not know much about the client personally, but the client is an enormous `超大物`, so failure is unacceptable.

Mutsuki supplies the contractor's operational assessment. Abydos was stronger than expected, and with the Schale teacher alongside them, Problem Solver 68 cannot win alone.

She also identifies the fiscal constraint: they already spent all their money.

Haruka volunteers to take a part-time job. Kayoko estimates that, at that earning rate, everyone would have to work for another year to afford mercenaries again.

Kayoko then criticizes the expensive office as unnecessary overhead. Aru protests that a proper company needs an office because a real office attracts more work. Mutsuki says she would have been fine returning to their former park tent.

Aru becomes defensive and then announces a new plan:

> `……融資を受けるわ。`

Mutsuki responds that Aru is blacklisted. Aru corrects her with comic precision: she is not blacklisted; she is wanted and her bank account has been frozen. Mutsuki recalls that the Gehenna Prefect Team was responsible. She adds that the central bank would probably refuse them at the door anyway.

Aru insists there are other methods.

The episode ends with her promising Abydos that the matter is not over and that the Problem Solver mission is only beginning. Mutsuki wonders what Aru intends to do.

The next-title marker is:

# 3. Central thesis

The strongest E012 thesis is:

> **E012 transforms the Abydos conflict from a partly anonymous proxy structure into a more legible but still stratified adversarial system. The reader now sees that Problem Solver 68's Abydos client is a Kaiser PMC director, while a separate Black Suit possesses enough confidence in Abydos performance data to reject “bad data” and begin investigating a new change factor. Yet Aru herself admits that she barely knows the client beyond his status as a `超大物`, and Abydos learns none of this in-world. The episode therefore resolves one layer of the sponsor architecture while preserving both information asymmetry and the distinction between narrative revelation and investigative knowledge.**

A second thesis concerns the Black Market:

> **E012 confirms that the Black Market is not merely a place where ordinary law fails. It reproduces major institutional functions—companies, finance, security—through formally unrecognized organizations operating outside effective Federal Student Council reach. Institutional capacity and legal recognition are therefore analytically distinct.**

This confirmation is strong enough to promote the E011 open hypothesis into a new longitudinal claim, `BA-C014`.

A third thesis concerns obligation and consent.

Hoshino jokingly invokes repayment for Hifumi's rescue and frames continued accompaniment as something she will now `してもらう`. Shiroko instantly calls the arrangement kidnapping. Serika then repairs the moral ambiguity by making Hifumi's willingness explicit:

> `もちろん、ヒフミさんが良ければ、だけど。`

Hifumi agrees because she wants to reciprocate for the help she received.

The episode therefore places **reciprocity under consent discipline**. A favor can generate a desire to repay without becoming a license to commandeer another person.

A fourth thesis concerns Problem Solver 68's corporate identity.

Aru's role-performance has material costs. The office that symbolizes a `ちゃんとした会社` consumes scarce money; the previous assault exhausts operating capital; the contractor cannot replace hired mercenaries without a year of collective labor; and formal credit appears unavailable because Aru is wanted and financially sanctioned. The gap between **performing corporate legitimacy** and **possessing sustainable business capacity** becomes concrete rather than merely comic.

# 4. Scene-by-scene close reading

Because E012 is encoded as one canonical scene but contains several internal cross-cuts, the following subsections track narrative functions rather than pretending the source object represents one physical space.

## 4.1 `学園数個分`: E011's city-scale impression becomes an explicit comparative claim

Stable evidence: `scene:001:u:0002-0005`, especially `DataList[1892]–[1893]`.

E011 had Shiroko estimate the Black Market as roughly city-sized. E012 gives Hifumi a second scale description:

> `ブラックマーケットだけでも、学園数個分の規模に匹敵しますし、決して無視はできないかと……。`

This does not provide square kilometers or population. It does something narratively more important: it says that the zone is large enough to be compared not with a street, market hall, or neighborhood, but with **multiple school jurisdictions**.

The phrase `決して無視はできない` also frames scale as political relevance. A space outside ordinary federal reach cannot simply be ignored when it has grown large enough to reproduce social systems internally.

## 4.2 `様々な「企業」`: illegality does not abolish organizational specialization

Stable evidence: `u:0006`, `DataList[1894]`.

Hifumi reports:

> `様々な「企業」が、この場所で違法な事柄を巡って利権争いをしていると聞きました。`

The quotation marks around `企業` matter. They preserve distance from ordinary recognized corporate status while still describing organized economic actors.

The point is not that these firms are lawful companies in the same sense as ordinary registered institutions. The point is that even in a shadow economy, actors form durable organizations, compete over interests, and adopt economic structures capable of pursuing control over profitable activity.

`利権争い` is stronger than simple shop competition. It implies contest over entrenched benefits, spheres of interest, or profitable rights/positions.

## 4.3 Finance and security are reproduced locally

Stable evidence: `u:0007-0011`, `DataList[1895]–[1899]`.

Hifumi says the zone has:

> `ここ専用の金融機関や治安機関`

Serika immediately translates the abstraction into ordinary institutional categories:

> `銀行や警察があるってこと……！？`

She then asks whether these are unauthorized illegal groups. Hifumi answers yes.

This is the decisive E012 confirmation of E011's institutional hypothesis.

A lack of Federal Student Council recognition does not mean that residents or power holders simply abandon finance and public-order functions. Those functions are reproduced under a different authority structure.

Current evidence therefore supports:

- formal nonrecognition;
- alternative institutions performing recognizable social functions;
- some coercive/public-order capacity;
- local economic organization.

It does **not** yet establish:

- who charters these institutions;
- whether they have territorial monopoly;
- whether the security body protects residents, firms, rents, or all three;
- whether the financial system is predatory, functional, or both;
- whether “illegal” describes them only relative to federal recognition or also their everyday conduct.

## 4.4 Hifumi's expertise is researched, not mysteriously innate

Stable evidence: `u:0012-0013`, `DataList[1900]–[1901]`.

Hoshino asks why Hifumi knows the area so well. Hifumi's answer is modest and practical:

> `危険な場所なので、事前調査をしっかりしたせいでしょうか……。`

This is a useful refinement to the E011 Hifumi baseline.

Her local competence does not require a secret background or unexplained worldly expertise. She researched because she knew she was entering a dangerous place for a private goal.

That combines:

- anxious risk perception;
- preparation;
- hobby-driven determination;
- enough self-discipline to compensate for fear through information.

Her anxiety is therefore not opposed to competence. It partly motivates competence.

## 4.5 Rescue reciprocity almost becomes coercive language

Stable evidence: `u:0014-0020`, `DataList[1902]–[1908]`.

Hoshino says:

> `助けてあげたお礼に、私たちの探し物が手に入るまで一緒に行動してもらうねー♪`

The tone is playful, but the syntax is unilateral enough that Shiroko's immediate gloss is:

> `なるほど、誘拐だね。`

This joke is especially pointed one episode after Hifumi was literally targeted for abduction.

The scene briefly exposes how the language of `お礼` and repayment can become coercive if the helper treats gratitude as ownership over the beneficiary's future conduct.

## 4.6 Serika makes consent explicit

Stable evidence: `u:0021-0023`, `DataList[1909]–[1911]`.

Serika corrects the joke:

> `誘拐じゃなくて、案内をお願いしたいだけでしょ？`
> `もちろん、ヒフミさんが良ければ、だけど。`

The final clause is the moral center of the exchange.

Abydos can ask for reciprocal help. It cannot simply convert rescue into a debt enforceable against Hifumi's will.

Hifumi then says she is happy to help because Abydos has taken care of her.

This preserves reciprocity while protecting voluntariness:

> **help received → gratitude → request → explicit permission boundary → voluntary reciprocation**.

That sequence contrasts sharply with the coercive extraction forms already seen elsewhere in the arc: formal debt burden, ransom kidnapping, paid assault, and proxy violence.

## 4.7 The narrative itself changes information domains

Stable evidence: transition from `DataList[1911]` to telephone narration at `[1913]–[1917]`.

At this point, the Abydos investigation has **not** found the strategic weapon seller.

Instead of rewarding the investigators with an immediate clue, the narrative leaves them and reveals information elsewhere.

This matters for archival reasoning. From E012 onward, we must track at least three different epistemic layers:

1. **what Abydos knows**;
2. **what Problem Solver 68 knows**;
3. **what the audience knows because the narrative cross-cuts**.

Collapsing these would create false hindsight inside the character analysis.

## 4.10 `練習` as rhetorical domination or literal reclassification remains ambiguous

The director says:

> `ここまでの練習は拝見したよ。`
> `で、実戦はいつだ？`

Aru begins:

> `あれが実戦だったんです……が……。`

then retracts and promises another operation within a week.

Two readings remain possible:

2. the director knowingly humiliates or pressures Aru by refusing to dignify failure as a real operation.

The later data discussion makes the second reading plausible, but the source does not explicitly settle the director's intent.

## 4.11 `お任せください`: Aru restores the corporate mask under pressure

Stable evidence: `u:0037-0041`.

> `……お任せください。`

The sequence captures her now-familiar strategy. When reality threatens the executive persona, she does not abandon the persona; she intensifies it.

E009 showed `一企業の長として` overriding moral hesitation. E012 shows the same role identity overriding fear of client failure.

The important refinement is that corporate performance is not only vanity. It is a coping and decision mechanism.

## 4.15 The client becomes more visible while remaining strategically opaque to Aru

Stable evidence: `u:0057-0060`, `DataList[1957]–[1960]`.

After the call, Aru tells the others:

This sentence is a compact statement of asymmetric information.

Aru knows enough to rank the client socially or institutionally as a major figure. She does not know enough to explain who he really is, what larger objective he serves, or why Abydos matters.

Therefore E012's named-client reveal does **not** overturn the earlier distributed-knowledge thesis. It sharpens it:

> **the audience can identify a contractor-facing principal more precisely than the contractor can explain that principal's strategic purpose.**

## 4.16 Failure remains unacceptable because status substitutes for understanding

Aru continues:

> `……この依頼、失敗するわけにはいかないわ。`

The reason she supplies is not moral conviction about the mission or knowledge of its necessity. It is the client's magnitude.

This is important for her ethical state.

E009 showed that she could recognize Abydos as kind and still proceed because she understood herself as a company president with a job to complete. E012 adds another layer: perceived prestige/power above her intensifies the pressure to perform.

Aru's commitment is therefore currently grounded in:

- role identity;
- contract completion;
- fear of failure before a major client;
- aspirational professionalism;

not in demonstrated agreement with the client's hidden strategic goal.

## 4.17 Problem Solver 68 has exhausted its attack budget

Stable evidence: `u:0062-0065`, `DataList[1962]–[1965]`.

Mutsuki says:

> `お金も全部使い果たしちゃったしね。どう戦うのさ？`

Haruka offers to work. Kayoko estimates that at that rate all of them would need another year before they could afford mercenaries again.

E009's wage-bounded subcontracting therefore has a longer fiscal consequence. Purchased coercion is not merely contractually limited at the worker level; it can bankrupt the contractor layer.

The proxy architecture now has visible resource friction:

> **client demand does not automatically generate contractor capacity.**

Aru must solve financing before she can reproduce the previous scale of violence.

## 4.18 Haruka again converts loyalty into labor availability

Haruka's response to fiscal crisis is:

> `わっ、私がバイトでもしてきましょうか？`

This is less violent than her E009 offer to `始末` the target, but structurally similar.

Her first instinct is to make herself useful to Aru's objective through personal labor.

That strengthens the provisional Haruka baseline:

> **low self-assertion combines with high willingness to convert herself into whatever form of useful input the group presently lacks.**

The episode still does not explain the psychological origin of that deference.

## 4.19 Kayoko converts the problem into a solvency calculation

Kayoko replies that all four would need to work another year to fund new mercenaries.

This preserves her practical role from E008–E009. She repeatedly turns Problem Solver 68's theatrical ambitions into resource arithmetic:

- hired personnel cost money;
- the group depleted its funds;
- replacement capacity has a labor-time price;
- the office itself creates overhead.

Kayoko is not shown resolving the moral question of whether another assault should happen. Her function here is to expose whether the organization can actually execute what Aru wants.

## 4.20 The office is symbolic capital with real cash cost

Stable evidence: `u:0066-0069`, `DataList[1966]–[1969]`.

Kayoko complains about their expensive office. Aru answers:

> `ちゃんとした会社なら、事務所は基本でしょ！`
> `そのほうが仕事の依頼も増えるんだから！`

Mutsuki says she would be fine returning to the old park tent.

The argument exposes two competing theories of institutional legitimacy.

Aru believes form helps create reality:

> proper office → proper company → more clients → greater legitimacy/success.

Kayoko and Mutsuki treat the same form as dispensable overhead when it threatens operational survival.

Aru's office is therefore not merely decoration. It is **symbolic capital purchased with scarce financial capital**.

Whether it actually increases business is not demonstrated here.

## 4.21 Corporate aspiration creates a self-reinforcing expenditure loop

Aru's model potentially contains a circular problem:

1. to be treated as a real company, she wants a real office;
2. the office costs money;
3. to pay for the office and fulfill jobs, the group needs more jobs;
4. a prestigious client creates pressure for costly performance;
5. costly performance exhausts operating cash;
6. the resulting shortage pushes the organization toward borrowing.

The episode does not present this as a formal business-school case. But the comedy is built on recognizable institutional economics: identity performance has overhead.

## 4.22 `融資`: violent capacity becomes a financing problem

Stable evidence: `u:0070-0077`, `DataList[1970]–[1977]`.

Aru's response to depleted funds is:

> `……融資を受けるわ。`

This is a significant continuation of the arc's economic grammar.

The organization is not asking how to become stronger in an abstract shōnen sense. It is asking how to finance another operation.

The path from money to violence now includes:

> credit → operating capital → hired force → coercive capacity.

E012 therefore brings debt/credit and proxy violence into closer conceptual proximity without yet proving that the same institutions control both systems.

# 7. Institutional-state analysis

## 7.1 Black Market governance moves from hypothesis to repeated evidence

E011 supplied:

- city-scale spatiality;
- weak Federal Student Council reach;

E012 adds:

- scale comparable to several schools;
- competing illegal `企業`;
- dedicated financial institutions;
- dedicated security institutions;
- explicit lack of formal authorization.

This repetition is now sufficient to treat the Black Market as a recurring institutional dimension rather than a one-line curiosity.

## 7.2 Nonrecognition and nonexistence are different categories

Serika's shock is analytically productive:

> “There are banks and police?”

Hifumi confirms that these are unauthorized/illegal organizations.

The episode thereby separates two questions:

1. Does an institution perform a recognizable function?
2. Is that institution recognized by the federal order?

The Black Market can answer “yes” to the first and “no” to the second.

This distinction warrants `BA-C014`.

## 7.3 Competing companies imply organized shadow political economy

`利権争い` suggests more than atomized crime.

Multiple organizations pursue durable stakes in illegal activity. This implies recurring economic interests, organization, and likely territorial/network power.

Exact market structure remains unknown.

## 7.4 Local finance implies shadow credit/payment infrastructure

Hifumi reports dedicated financial institutions. E012 does not show a transaction with them, but their existence is institutionally important because shadow economies require ways to store, transfer, lend, settle, or otherwise mediate value.

Do not infer specific services from the word `金融機関` alone.

## 7.5 Local security implies coercive governance capacity

The Black Market's `治安機関` is not merely rumored in abstract. Hifumi organizes her behavior around avoiding it.

That gives the institution practical deterrent effect even without showing its officers.

## 7.6 Kaiser PMC becomes the first named principal in the Abydos proxy chain

E012's direct client label materially revises the coercion architecture.

Current safest structure:

> **Kaiser PMC director/client → Problem Solver 68 → hired day-labor mercenaries**

Alongside this is a distinct but interacting data layer:

> **Black Suit ↔ Kaiser PMC director: shared assessment of Abydos data / unexplained strengthening**

The exact relation between the Black Suit layer and the contracting hierarchy remains unresolved.

## 7.7 Data becomes an institutional technology of coercion

The adversarial side is not operating blindly.

It has performance expectations derived from data. When reality diverges, it performs diagnosis:

- data error?
- calculation error?
- target changed?
- what factor produced the change?

This is a more sophisticated threat model than random gang violence.

It indicates that coercion against Abydos is increasingly **informational as well as financial and military**.

## 7.8 Formal banking can enforce nonfinancial sanctions

Aru's frozen account demonstrates that finance can act as an enforcement surface for security/legal status.

The episode does not explain the institutional mechanics, but it clearly shows that being wanted affects access to money.

## 7.9 Problem Solver 68's internal economy remains fragile

Their business model presently combines:

- expensive office overhead;
- irregular contract income;
- costly subcontracting;
- prestige-sensitive spending;
- weak formal credit access;
- members willing to supply labor personally.

This is enough to call the organization financially fragile at E012, but not enough to calculate revenue or solvency precisely.

## 7.10 Kaiser PMC and Kaiser Loan remain separate evidence objects

E010 established `カイザーローン` as Abydos's creditor.

E012 establishes `カイザーPMC理事` as Problem Solver 68's client.

The repeated `カイザー` name makes a possible connection analytically salient, but **shared naming is not yet sufficient evidence of corporate identity, ownership, coordination, or common strategy**.

Until primary text supplies a relationship, `BA-C012` and `BA-C013` remain separate claims.

# 9. Japanese-language analysis

## 9.1 `手が及ばない`

Repeated from E011, this idiom denotes reach/influence failing to extend into a place.

It supports a territorial/institutional reading of federal weakness rather than a metaphysical claim that no law or organization exists there.

## 9.2 `学園数個分の規模に匹敵`

`匹敵する` means to rival/be comparable to.

Hifumi therefore gives a relative scale claim, not an exact measurement. The comparison makes the Black Market legible against the franchise's school-centered political geography.

## 9.3 `企業` in quotation marks

The quotation marks signal that these are called or function as companies without straightforwardly granting ordinary legal-corporate status.

This is precisely the kind of wording that should be retained in institutional synthesis rather than normalized into “companies” without qualification.

## 9.4 `利権争い`

`利権` refers to vested interests, profitable rights/privileges, or entrenched economic stakes; `争い` is struggle/conflict.

The phrase suggests organized competition over durable benefits rather than isolated petty crime.

## 9.5 `金融機関`

Broader than “bank.” It means financial institution.

Serika's `銀行` is her explanatory paraphrase, not proof that every Black Market financial institution is a conventional bank.

## 9.6 `治安機関`

A public-order/security institution or apparatus.

Again, Serika's `警察` is a natural analogy, but current evidence should retain the broader original term because legal status and exact function are unresolved.

## 9.7 `事前調査をしっかり`

Hifumi explicitly attributes her local knowledge to careful advance research.

`しっかり` emphasizes doing it thoroughly/solidly rather than casually.

## 9.8 `お礼に`

Ordinarily “as thanks / in return.”

Hoshino's use becomes comic because she is the helper specifying what repayment will be. The phrase enters the arc's expanding vocabulary of debt, favor, and reciprocal obligation.

## 9.9 `もちろん、ヒフミさんが良ければ`

`～が良ければ` functions here as an explicit voluntariness condition: if Hifumi is okay with it.

This is stronger evidence than merely assuming consent from later compliance.

## 9.10 `クライアント`

The English-derived business term reinforces Problem Solver 68's self-understanding as a service organization and clearly identifies the caller as the customer/principal layer in the job relationship.

## 9.11 `練習` / `実戦`

The director creates a hierarchy between “practice” and “real battle.”

Because the previous operation was intended by Aru as real combat, calling it `練習` either misclassifies or deliberately demeans the failed performance. The pragmatic intent remains open.

## 9.12 `拝見した`

`拝見する` is the humble/polite form of “to see/look at,” but here the director's status makes the politeness rhetorically interesting rather than submissive.

At minimum it establishes that he claims to have observed/reviewed the prior performance. It does not specify the surveillance medium.

## 9.13 `お任せください`

A service-provider formula: leave it to us/me.

Aru uses it precisely when she is internally least certain, making professional language a mask of regained control.

## 9.14 `データに不備はありません`

`不備` means defect, deficiency, flaw, incompleteness.

Black Suit's categorical wording is stronger than “the data seem fine.” He rejects the flawed-data hypothesis.

## 9.15 `変化要因`

Literally a factor/cause of change.

The term reduces the new Abydos condition to something discoverable and isolatable. It is analytically important for the adversarial datafication motif.

## 9.16 `超大物`

A “very big figure / heavyweight / major player.”

Aru's phrasing communicates perceived status, not a precise office or hierarchy beyond the already visible `カイザーPMC理事` label.

## 9.17 `融資`

Financing/lending, usually institutional credit rather than simply borrowing cash from a friend.

Its appearance turns the next coercive operation into a capital-formation problem.

## 9.18 `指名手配` / `口座が凍結`

`指名手配` is wanted status; `口座凍結` is account freezing.

The juxtaposition concretely links enforcement status to financial restriction.

## 9.19 `門前払い`

Literally being turned away at the gate/door; figuratively dismissed without consideration.

Mutsuki expects recognized finance to reject Aru before a substantive lending decision is even reached.

## 9.20 `他にも方法はある`

“There are other methods.”

This is intentionally underdetermined at E012. Do not fill in the method from the next episode title or later memory.# 10. Motifs, symbols, and longitudinal callbacks

# Conclusion

E012 is a major structural episode despite containing no Sensei choice and no direct completion of Abydos's weapons-provenance search.

Its first half confirms that the Black Market is a **parallel institutional ecology**: large enough to rival multiple school territories and organized enough to reproduce companies, finance, and security outside formal federal recognition. That confirmation warrants opening `BA-C014` and establishes a durable distinction between **being unrecognized** and **being institutionally empty**.

Its second half resolves one layer of the adversarial system for the audience. Problem Solver 68's client is now a **Kaiser PMC director**, while Black Suit appears as a separate data-informed actor who rejects a bad-data explanation for the failed attack and begins investigating the factor that made Abydos stronger. The immediate client layer is therefore named, but the larger strategic hierarchy remains incomplete. Aru herself still knows little about the client beyond his magnitude.

The episode also gives the strongest opponent-side recognition yet of Sensei's material significance: Mutsuki says that with the Schale teacher alongside Abydos, Problem Solver 68 cannot win alone. That strengthens the model of Sensei as an additive force multiplier while preserving student agency; the hostile data conversation still describes **the Abydos students** as the entity that has become stronger.

Finally, E012 places Problem Solver 68's corporate fantasy under financial stress. The office that symbolizes legitimacy drains scarce resources, mercenary subcontracting has exhausted cash, legal status constrains bank access, and Aru turns toward `融資` without revealing her alternative method. Violence is again shown to depend on institutions of money, credit, information, and organizational form—not simply on willingness to fight.

The correct closing boundary is therefore:

> **Abydos still lacks the procurement answer it entered the Black Market to find; the audience now knows the immediate contractor-facing client is Kaiser PMC; Black Suit is investigating an unexplained strengthening factor; Problem Solver 68 needs new financing; Kaiser Loan/Kaiser PMC identity remains unproven; and the Black Market's parallel institutions are now a recurring series-level analytical problem rather than a one-scene curiosity.**
