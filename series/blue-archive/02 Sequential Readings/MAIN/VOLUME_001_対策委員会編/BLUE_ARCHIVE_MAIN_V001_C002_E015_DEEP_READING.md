## 0. Boundary and topology

## 1. A former contractor chooses its partner

The injured director calls Problem Solver 68 a `飼い犬`. Aru rejects client ownership in practical terms: Sensei was easier to work with than the director, and that preference is enough. Mutsuki converts employer betrayal into villain convention, while Shiroko accepts it as the correct outlaw move.

This is not a claim that PS68's methods are benign. It is a direct refusal of the idea that payment or prior hiring creates durable possession. Contractor agency survives the contract, and relationship quality changes alignment.

PS68's intervention restores the Countermeasures Committee's capacity to act. Nonomi says there is no time for paralysis; Serika and Shiroko prioritize recovering Hoshino. Serika declares unofficial or even illegal status irrelevant to that immediate duty. The line refuses status-based erasure under emergency pressure; it should not be generalized into a claim that legality never matters.

## 2. Sensei asserts protective standing

Sensei's two initial singleton lines call Hoshino `my precious student` and demand her return. The director replies that Sensei has no such right.

The possessive grammar matters. It is not identical to Black Suit's contract claim, because Sensei demands restoration of Hoshino rather than transfer of her rights to himself. Within Sensei's known information, Hoshino's exit arose under manufactured debt pressure and immediately enabled invasion. The intervention asserts a teacher's protective obligation against capture.

Yet benevolent relational possession cannot be exempted from scrutiny. Hoshino intentionally signed, even under deception/coercion. A mature agency-preserving account must explain why opposing that transaction restores rather than overrides her authorship. E015 supplies strong grounds—engineered alternatives, hidden purpose, community attack—but not Hoshino's current voice or consent.

## 3. Tactical victory remains strategically incomplete

Neutralizing/injuring the commander breaks the immediate command structure. The director orders retreat, headquarters repeats it through units, and Ayane confirms enemy withdrawal. Kayoko judges the tactic broadly successful.

The outcome validates distributed coalition capacity: PS68's explosives and planning, Abydos resistance, and Sensei collaboration achieve what none had achieved alone. It does not restore territory, validate the committee, resolve debt, or retrieve Hoshino.

Sensei proposes returning. Ayane agrees, anticipates the largest battle so far, and prioritizes finding a rescue method. Rest and planning prevent a tactical success from becoming reckless pursuit.

## 4. First direct Sensei–Black Suit encounter

At an unspecified Kivotos location, Sensei internally says `Enter`. Black Suit is waiting and says he has wanted a face-to-face conversation. This establishes the first direct encounter at the current boundary.

The scene does not disclose how Sensei found the location, whether entry was invited, what Black Suit will propose, or Hoshino's physical proximity. Those questions belong to E016.

## 5. Central theses

### Relationship can defeat client capture

The director thinks PS68 remains a bought dog. Aru evaluates partners and chooses collaboration. Voluntary realignment, not moral purity, makes former proxies capable of resisting the coercive architecture.

### Protective claims require a restoration test

Sensei's `my student` differs from Black Suit's `all rights` only if the purpose and effect are to return Hoshino's capacity for self-directed relation. The wording strengthens care while keeping nonpossessive authority under examination.

### Winning a battle does not solve the institution

PMC retreat proves capacity but leaves the debt, land, recognition, and Hoshino crises intact. The coalition creates time and options; it does not manufacture closure.
