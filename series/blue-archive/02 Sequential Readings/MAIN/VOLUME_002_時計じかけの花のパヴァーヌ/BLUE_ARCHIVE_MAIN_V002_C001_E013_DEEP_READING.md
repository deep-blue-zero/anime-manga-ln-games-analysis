## 1. The plan makes Alice a captured decoy and weaponizes repair

The operation opens after Alice appears to have been hit and falls, asking for a revival spell. Yuuka is stunned that the intruders smashed through the fingerprint-controlled elevator door. She refuses Akane's joking proposal to take Alice as a sixth maid and orders her held in the council's reflection room as an alleged attacker. This establishes actual custody and Alice's distress/fall as represented, not a medical diagnosis, proof that her injury is feigned, evidence of her consent to this exact decoy role, or a completed rescue. In the hallway Momoi mourns Alice's removal; Yuzu says it is according to plan and promises to rescue her. Momoi admits failure of the follow-on step would have left Alice confined for nothing. Their plan therefore knowingly exposes a companion to custody; its intended rescue has not yet happened.

Yuuka initially reasons that Alice's oversized weapon was probably Engineering Club-made, and that the club could set a trap if commissioned for repair. She directs purchase of a powerful non-Engineering replacement security lock. The hallway receives a message quoted as Engineering Club successfully inserting a `トロイの木馬`. The later reveal is that the apparently non-Engineering newest system was, in Momoi's account, still engineer-made under a concealed name. The represented approval of Momoi, Midori and Sensei, plus rejection of Akane's and Noah's fingerprints after installation, supports compromise of the replacement. Yuuka's inference that Alice's door-breaking was meant to induce this repair is borne out by the plan's reported design and observed result, but exact procurement records, exploit code and every system consequence are not shown. Alice's body/weapon is used as a means to induce a foreseeable security response; the ethics cannot be answered solely by whether the trick works.

Scene 4's flashback describes formidable defenses: Maki estimates roughly 400 cameras, 50 security robots and additional seized combat robots; Utaha specifies 442 cameras and 52 robots in three classes and says engineers helped build the original system. Those are informed participant reports, not a live inventory audit. Hare describes the elevator fingerprint gate and compartment shutters: first-stage fingerprint release, stronger second shutter after unregistered print/impact, then council fingerprint and iris requirements. Hibiki proposes a mini-EMP/external-power interruption exposing a network opening and perhaps disabling systems for about six seconds; Hare calls that enough.
