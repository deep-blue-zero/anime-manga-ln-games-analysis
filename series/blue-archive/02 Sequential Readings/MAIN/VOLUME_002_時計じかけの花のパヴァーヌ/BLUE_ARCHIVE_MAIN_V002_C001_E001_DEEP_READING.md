The club's artistic medium is also its way of making distress legible.

The narration then revises the addressee's role. The arriving person will not be called hero but `先生`. This does not magically grant a game-protagonist's power. It positions Sensei's real institutional relationship against the game script's chosen-one trope, a distinction the later injury and request preserve. ## 2. Comic injury removes heroic omnipotence

Sensei wakes after Momoi threw the club's `プライステーション` out a window and it struck Sensei's head by accident. ## 3. Schale is contacted by students, not self-deployed

## 4. Creative labor and threatened institutional existence

At the club, Momoi names herself its scenario writer; Midori identifies herself as the illustrator responsible for visual work; their absent president Yuzu handles planning. These divisions matter: the club is presented through makers and roles before the alleged dissolution can reduce it to an administrative problem. Momoi says they have been making 16-bit games peacefully and depicts the Student Council's intervention as a sudden `襲撃`. She reports a final ultimatum from Yuuka two days earlier and playfully calls Yuuka one of the council's `四天王`.

All of this is the club's account at this boundary. Neither the written order, Yuuka's grounds, the council's institutional structure, nor Yuzu's own position has appeared. `襲撃` and `四天王` characterize Momoi's presentation; they are not neutral proof of armed assault or a formal four-person rank. A new voice offers to explain the ultimatum directly, but the episode ends before that explanation. Even identifying the voice as Yuuka from the next-episode card would be a forward inference, not a current speaker identification.
