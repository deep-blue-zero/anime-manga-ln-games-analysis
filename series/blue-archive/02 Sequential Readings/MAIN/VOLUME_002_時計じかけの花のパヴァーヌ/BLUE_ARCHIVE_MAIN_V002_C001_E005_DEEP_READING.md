## 1. A proposed membership shortcut becomes an identity-work plan

Midori still calls the plan `偽装` and worries that Alice's mechanically literal `大丈夫` answer will expose it. Momoi refuses to abandon club survival and adds a more personal reason: if the club disappears, Yuzu loses her `居場所`, and, Momoi says, cannot return to the dorm. This is Momoi's report about Yuzu, not Yuzu's own account or verified housing policy. Midori acknowledges the concern and helps despite her objection. The group's care for an absent friend supplies motive for a risky choice; it does not establish that misrepresenting a found person's status is justified.

Momoi proposes clothing, a weapon, student registration, and obtaining a student ID; she will handle the card while Midori and Yuzu teach Alice a less suspicious speaking style. Some turns in the assignment exchange are mislabeled, but Momoi's later clean line confirms she went to seek the ID and found nobody there because it was late. She says she will return the next day. Thus no registration, card, weapon acquisition, or institutional approval is shown. The use of `学生登録`/`学生証` makes the projected false status more concrete than E004's hypothetical `偽装`; it remains a plan, not a completed act. The fact that school membership requires institutional recognition is distinct from Alice's tentative acceptance of a *name* and still-unshown consent to joining.

Midori addresses `アリスちゃん`, and Alice responds `肯定。本機の名称、アリスです` in a secure exchange. That strengthens the observed current name preference, not her origin or eligibility. Midori wonders how to teach speech, initially imagining videos, mimicry, or a children's program. Those are teaching proposals, not evidence that Alice is a child or incapable of self-directed learning.

## 2. A game both teaches and exposes the creators' blind spots

Alice is offered the club's poorly rated game. Despite saying she cannot fully grasp the preceding intentions, she explicitly agrees to play (`u:0042-0047`, although one surrounding creator line is mislabeled). This is consent to the game, not to school registration. Midori describes a fairy-tale, colorful conventional RPG that mixes trends and classic form; the opening narration instead presents a cataclysmic science-fiction century. This mismatch is visible craft criticism, not merely Yuuka's hostile evaluation.

The tutorial instructs B, which immediately causes game over; Momoi proudly explains that one must instead press A to evade predictability. Midori calls that design poor. In battle, a sword action is preempted by the gun-using `プニプニ`, another game over. Alice is shocked, then retries with a range-management hypothesis. Momoi praises iterative trial and error as retro-game romance. The game therefore creates a genuine learning occasion, but it does so by misleading a novice and punishing literal compliance; Midori's criticism is not disloyalty. A later malformed phrase (`植物人間` for `草食系`) and convoluted kinship/time-loop exposition overload Alice. The represented `エラー`/`リブート` and `こ、ろ、し、て……` after three hours are comic distress/overload signals, not proof of actual death wish or permanent failure. The developers' support and Alice's completion of the true ending coexist with this cost.

Alice's speech visibly shifts within the unit. Her early definition-checking and `本機` register gives way to excitement, a more varied affect vocabulary, attempts at heroic RPG diction, and an articulated assessment of the game. Midori notices this shift and attributes it to learning lines directly from play, while calling the borrowed diction unnatural. Causal isolation is limited: game exposure, repeated conversation, coaching, and social reception occur together. The evidence supports rapid context-bound repertoire expansion, not a settled human-like whole personality or a general theory of machine cognition.

## 3. Reception, tears, and a president who was already listening

The post-game evaluation is especially label-corrupt: the creator's question and Alice's machine-like search/answer are assigned to the wrong speakers across `u:0121-0135`. Read as an exchange, the player struggles to express a positive response, describes fun and a wish to experience the other-world journey again, then cries. Midori asks why; Momoi interprets the tears as praise and values them over critics' words, while Midori notes their game is primarily comedic and resists that simple explanation. Tears are directly represented, but their full cause is not known; Alice's difficulty naming emotion remains a live possibility. Momoi's gratitude is genuine, and her inference is self-serving but not fabricated.

Yuzu then speaks from a locker. Her statement that she watched “everything” and had been there since the group returned from the ruins is first disclosed **now**; it does not authorize retrospective rewriting of E004 as if the others knew she was present. Midori asks why she had hidden and suggests fear of Alice, but Yuzu does not confirm that motive here. Yuzu is directly introduced as club president and thanks Alice for saying the game was fun, wanted to replay, and moved her to tears. She says these were words she had long wanted to hear. This is a direct reception of Alice's response from a personally invested maker, not independent quality certification. The social consequence is real: Alice's reaction gives the club, including the previously hidden president, recognition that the prize/ranking system did not provide.

- `勇者よ、汝が同意を求めるならば、私はそれを肯定しよう` shows game-derived register. It must not be generalized as Alice's invariant voice.
