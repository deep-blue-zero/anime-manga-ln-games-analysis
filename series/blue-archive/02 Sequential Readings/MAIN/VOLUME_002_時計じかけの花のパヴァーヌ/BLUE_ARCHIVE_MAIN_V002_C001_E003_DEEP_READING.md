## 1. The ruins are physically dangerous, but not yet explained

The group hides from numerous patrolling robots whose rendered speech is opaque squares. Momoi directs Sensei to lower their head; Midori questions the wait and then demands an account of the place. The patrol's presence and the later movement toward the group are directly observed. Robot origin, language, commands, purpose, and who controls them are not. No robot is identified as `G.Bible` or as proof of a particular hidden institution.

Momoi says the missing General Student Council President had tightly restricted entry and tried to keep the region's existence obscure. She says federal forces withdrew after the disappearance, leaving it unattended, and that Veritas assistance made the current entry possible. These are Momoi's reports; the story does show the group inside, but not the earlier policy, withdrawal order, or technical means of access. The site is no longer a hypothetical destination from E002; it is an inhabited/guarded spatial risk. Sensei's presence is shown, but the unit does not depict the prior consent exchange.

## 2. A carefully hedged metaphor becomes an overconfident search hypothesis

Momoi cites Himari of Veritas, who suggested the ruins *might* be like an `時代の下水道` where things lost and forgotten from Kivotos collect. Midori explicitly notices Himari's unusual `かもしれない`, reading it as a sign of genuine uncertainty. The metaphor is not a verified physical rule that everything lost moves here.

Momoi's stronger evidence is a Veritas-provided coordinate for the *last confirmed operation* of `G.Bible`; it points to a place absent from ordinary maps. Combining that with the site's secrecy and Himari's metaphor, she concludes the object must be in the ruins. This is a plausible working hypothesis, not direct detection of present location. Last operation does not imply current possession, and an unmapped coordinate does not identify its contents. Midori initially fears her sister came merely because of the metaphor, then hears the coordinate reason; her skepticism is responsive rather than categorical refusal.

Momoi describes `G.Bible` as a legendary game creator's work from student days at Millennium, allegedly containing a secret method for making the best game. She admits she does not know its contents. Midori likens the pitch to an advertisement for a game-creator school; Momoi insists it exists and will enable `TSC2`. This pits creative aspiration and need for a rapid contest result against the seductive possibility of a shortcut. The creator's identity, the object's form, actual content, and causal efficacy remain OPEN. The name `ゲームの聖書` is Momoi's reverent gloss, not evidence of a sacred object or guaranteed design algorithm.

## 3. Risk materializes and authority becomes requested command

Before the object is found, robots converge. Midori recognizes the encirclement risk; Momoi panics. Sensei spots a factory-like structure as a possible refuge. Momoi accepts the cue and urges a break through the robots into it; Midori explicitly requests Sensei's combat command. The order is consequential: student initiative brought the group into the site, student uncertainty and danger are spoken, Sensei supplies a tactical observation, and a student asks for command. No battle outcome, factory safety, or escape is shown in E003.

This is the first Millennium activation of Sensei's coordination role. It strengthens the bounded-command pattern without turning earlier student choice into adult ownership. It also sharpens the ethical accounting of E002: the danger Momoi could not specify is now visible, so future analysis must distinguish her justified search evidence from the risk she drew others into. Her private recklessness is not proven by one surprised reaction; she had anticipated some danger, but underestimated or could not identify its form.
