## 1. Welcome through games is more than a speech-disguise exercise

Yuzu introduces herself as club president, thanks Alice for coming, and offers other RPG recommendations because Alice enjoyed their game. Alice tests the meaning of `よろしく`, reframes Yuzu's greeting as an RPG party-join announcement, and asks whether the usage is right. Yuzu tentatively approves. This is an interaction with correction and mutual pleasure, not merely the club programming a disguise. Yet the recommendation argument also reveals instrumental intent: a role-flipped speaker says better games should make Alice's speech more natural and raise the plan's success rate. Several game-title preferences cannot safely be allocated to Yuzu/Momoi/Midori because `u:0014-0017` labels contradict their dialogue. The general contrast is secure: one or more girls recommend games for pleasure, and at least one explicitly links it to concealment.

Alice herself says she anticipates playing again. Two hours later Momoi observes that she appears to read dialogue almost as soon as it displays. Midori suggests another game. The others eventually sleep, while Alice continues pressing through games, announces clears, and is still awake at morning. This shows an unusually rapid and sustained *observed session*, not that Alice never needs rest or has unlimited processing capacity. The chapter's educational means are mixed: exposure to varied texts provides repertoire, but selection by peers and a hidden-registration goal shape what she is learning.

At morning Alice greets Midori in lofty RPG speech (`ようやく気が付いたか`, `必滅者よ`). Midori worries it is still skewed rather than natural. The earlier lesson has not produced an ordinary unmarked school register; it produced an extensive, highly game-contaminated one. Momoi later calls it `洗練`, while Midori calls it retro-game conversation itself. Their disagreement is a useful check against treating more speech as automatically more social competence.

## 3. Japanese wording and choice topology

- `ユズが仲間になりました、パンパカパーン！` is Alice's early transfer of RPG party language to Yuzu's greeting; the directional reversal itself shows she is still testing social meaning.
- `期待。再び、ゲームを始めます` is Alice's own expressed anticipation before continued play; it cannot be reduced to external coercion, nor projected into a lifelong preference.
- `必滅者よ` is borrowed fantasy diction addressed to Midori. Midori calls the result biased; Momoi's later `洗練` is a competing judgment, not settled linguistic progress.

- `仲間` operates both as RPG party membership and the club's desired social category. Alice's celebratory uptake evidences current participation, not knowledge of credential provenance.
