These are the system's strings. Neither “disposal” nor “human understanding” is an independently explained purpose, provenance, quality judgment, or reason to accept/reject the object. Momoi calls it a treasure for game makers, a value claim carried forward from the legend, not validated content.

The system says `G.Bible` is inside it and requests external storage for transfer because it is near disappearance. Momoi offers her `ゲームガールズアドバンスSP` memory card, and Yuzu securely reports connecting a data cable. The system says storage is insufficient and that it will delete existing data. Momoi pleads to preserve her save; the terminal says `残念、削除`, and she laments the loss. This is a real resource/consent conflict within the scene: Momoi offers the card for `G.Bible`, not explicitly for sacrifice of existing saves, yet the transfer proceeds under countdown. The source reports deletion; it does not show an independent filesystem audit or recovery attempt.

After a moment that Midori reads as power loss, the display reports transfer complete and shows `<G.Bible.exe>`. Momoi attempts to run it, but a password prompt blocks access. This establishes a **file with the expected name and a claimed successful transfer**, not inspected contents, authentic legendary authorship, usable method for making a better game, malware safety, or proof of completed knowledge acquisition. Midori thinks Veritas should be able to handle an ordinary password; that is a future hypothesis, not a performed unlock. The system's “I will disappear”/“in me” phrasing does not specify whether the transferred file contains just `G.Bible`, any part of the system, or an agent.
