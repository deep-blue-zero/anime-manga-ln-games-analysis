## 1. Chapter thesis

The strongest supported chapter-local proposition is:

> **A serious hazard can justify investigation and protective action without making a classified person's assigned origin or programmed function the total truth of who she may be. Rio's and Key's danger evidence is not imaginary; their leap from risk to unilateral destruction/exclusion or a fixed “Princess” destiny is not established. Alice's first-person choice becomes legible through concrete friendship and shared work, then carries into ordinary club interaction. That return is meaningful, while technical safety, institutional standing and accountability remain unfinished.**

This is not a universal theorem that desire disables weapons, that friendship nullifies machine code, that every fearful actor is malicious, or that any one adult saved the school. E025's narrator says a possible threat to Millennium and Kivotos was averted by everyone; Himari immediately says the facilities/underlying matter remain unresolved. The immediate ark-resource episode ends, but the source does not disclose Key's durable condition, a full Eridu shutdown audit, the exact residual risk, or a formal inquiry into Rio's actions.

## 2. Prospective narrative architecture

### E001–E005: hidden classification beside ordinary play

E001 gives the audience Rio and Himari's conflict over Alice before the club hears the full story. Rio's retrospective Mirror/C&C test testimony and Himari's dissent are not club knowledge. E002–E005 return to familiar club life: Momoi's unsettled genre plans, Yuzu's fairness/creative-social rule, Alice's Sensei “level-one” play and idea-seeking, Sumire's exercise encounter and a public day/Nel arcade acquaintance. These scenes establish ordinary relation and preference but do not disprove future danger; Alice's happy experience also fails to produce the requested new-game idea automatically. The C001 prize and provisional room remain, not an indefinite charter.

### E006–E011: a device event becomes a personhood and governance crisis

Veritas's inquiries and Alice's encounter with the device strengthen an `AL-1S`/ATRAHASIS connection without establishing all origin, control and future outcomes. The incident damages the room and seriously injures Momoi; she is unconscious for two days before waking. Her self-reported “fully restored” game language is not medical clearance. Alice's fear/amnesia and desire not to harm others are consequential. Rio states a high-stakes threat and proposes halo destruction; her evidence and forecast are not independent proof of inevitability or that no safer alternative exists. Toki/AMAS carry out Rio's seizure, and Alice's farewell is under coercive pressure. Momoi's return, Yuzu's insistence that Alice be heard and Nel/C&C's participation reject a forced “ending” without showing that the dangerous hardware has vanished. E011 explicitly calls the removal abduction. Rio's adult-duty claim has to be tested against the excluded child's agency and the actual costs of her secrecy.

### E012–E021: many agents, uncertain plans and bodily costs

Yuuka/Noa uncover concealed-looking records and Eridu coordinates, while Engineering seeks the route, C&C creates a diversion and Veritas supports hacking. These are distributed authorships, not one master plan. Himari remains an explicit Rio dissenter and later escapee from isolation; Rio's percentage claims and foreknowledge have uncalibrated scope. Toki's local deductions are capable but fallible, and Eimi's intervention, Chihiro/Mirror, Sumire's aid and Engineering's hardware further widen capacity. The coalition makes real tactical gains but loses communications and exhausts resources. Nel suffers grave symptoms in the Abi-Eshuh fight; Yuzu's observed contact and Chihiro's compute-limit hypothesis are not a guaranteed weak-point theorem. The E021 elevator trap—earlier Momoi idea as credited by Yuzu, Chihiro's hypothetical mechanics/hack and C&C's enactment—locally paralyzes evasion and lets Nel win. Rio wrongly credits Sensei alone; Akane directly corrects that attribution. Local victory does not itself heal Nel or reach Alice.

### E022–E024: Key's assigned “Princess” versus Alice's stated wish

The party reaches a physically silent Alice. Key, a provisional distinct speaking subject, denies her friend-given name, claims her surface personality isolated and starts an ATRAHASIS ark-resource process. Broken communication, city changes and follower deployment establish danger, while Key's quantities, protocol architecture and world-end certainty remain its reports. Rio sees possible backfire from Eridu's concentrated resources and offers fatal solo shutdown. Sensei's secure overlooked-helper questions resist the forced trolley frame; Yuuka/Noa cut power, and Key reports immediate resource failure/shutdown. Key is not thereby erased; Engineering returns with modified Avant-Garde-kun, Eimi/C&C reportedly cover the entrance and Himari offers a hazardous mind-dive. Yuzu explicitly volunteers.

Inside the dive, Key shows true Eridu injuries as evidence for Alice's guilt and “Princess” purpose. Alice fears she may destroy Kivotos, says she is a demon king rather than hero and contemplates disappearing. These are serious self-erasure words under curated pressure, not a free settled consent or proof of destiny. Momoi supplies concrete counterhistory: `TSC2`'s special prize, club survival, play and even a changed Nel relation. The facts are collectively made, not Alice's sole achievement. Midori recalls Alice's no-abandonment maxim; Yuzu calls her `仲間（友達）`. Momoi treats `魔王` as a changeable `ジョブ`. Alice directly says she wants to continue with them, become a hero and be Alice; she draws an in-space sword/light and rejects Key's assigned destructive function. Key fragments into incomprehension/silence. This is strong *in-space* self-definition, not an observed physical wake-up, Key deletion or global weapon-safety test.

### E025: a return, not an all-clear

Alice is externally speaking, asks Sensei to sit beside her and joins the club's new-game study. That closes E024's immediate external-response question. Her question about attacking a monster that did nothing first is a situated ethics of play, not proof of permanent pacifism. The new game remains study, not finished work or renewed official club term. E025 also reports Nel hospitalized then discharged; she objects to Toki's party appearance before grudgingly contextualizing Toki's obedience. Toki is socially present, not formally reassigned or fully forgiven. Rio declares resignation, disappears and leaves `ごめんなさい`; Yuuka's protest and Himari's continuing facility task prevent this from counting as completed accountability. The episode's warm coda is earned as a relational continuation, not a technical/legal cure.

## 3. Evidence, causality and ethical failure modes

The chapter repeatedly shows **a real observation plus an overlarge inference**. The terminal/device responds to Alice and Momoi is injured; Rio infers a threat and unilateral halo removal. Key's system acts and surveillance captures coalition injuries; Key infers Alice's moral essence is destruction. Rio computes high percentages; unanticipated student action defeats guard plans. Key counts zero remaining defenders; Engineering returns. These failures do not make all danger false. They show why actor reports, quantities, confidence and exclusive-remedy claims must be separated from directly observed effect.

Rio's protective intent cannot erase the coercive process: she knows Himari dissents, keeps the club/Alice outside the decision, uses Toki to seize Alice, plans destruction and builds Eridu with disputed resources. Conversely, her E022 fear and possible willingness to die argue against a flat cruelty caricature. The safer alternative is not magic. It is specific labor by Yuuka/Noa, Engineering, C&C, Veritas, Eimi, Himari, Sensei and the club, with wounds and risk. E025 shows no completed budget audit, hearing, medical aftercare plan or durable system control.

Alice's agency is also not a blank permission slip. Her E010 pressured farewell and E024 wish to disappear cannot stand as unpressured consent; the friends' memories answer “I only harm” but do not dismiss Momoi's/Nel's injuries. Her E024 first-person naming and E025 ordinary return are positive, distinct observations. A responsible interpretation preserves all three: the hazard, the choosing person, and obligations to contain/repair.

## 6. Institutional and thematic synthesis

C001's `BA-C019` becomes more demanding. Creative worth and prize recognition were never a safe substitute for Alice's welfare or a durable school place. E007–E011 show why; E024–E025 show her lived friendship is not erased by risk classification and survives into ordinary shared making. Thus *making, reception, recognition, belonging and safety* are related but non-substitutable tests. The next game is merely in study, while the official reprieve still expires by C001's terms.

Rio's fortress is the chapter's most visible governance irony: concentrated power and secret protection are meant to preempt a future catastrophe, then provide Key's resource opportunity. Rio notices possible backfire herself. The text does not prove a complete causal chain, but it certainly defeats a simple “secrecy plus superior forecast equals safety” rule. The rescue works through plural institutions and local corrections; each has costs, competence limits and unfinished repair. Institutional legitimacy cannot be inferred from the good ending alone.

The game motif is not just decorative. Alice learns through RPG language, fears `魔王`, is designated `王女`, and elects `勇者`. Momoi's `転職` argues that a class is revisable, while the club's development practice makes another story possible. But the crisis is not solved merely by changing words: power cutoff, combat, hacking, risky dive and post-event closure work all matter. E025's monster question keeps “hero” ethically interrogative rather than a permanent self-congratulatory label.
