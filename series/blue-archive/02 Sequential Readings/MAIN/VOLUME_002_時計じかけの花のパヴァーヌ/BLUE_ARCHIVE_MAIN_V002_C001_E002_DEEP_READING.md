## 1. Game-villain framing meets a real accountant

Momoi's `生徒会四天王` and `冷酷な算術使い` translate an administrator into a game adversary. Yuuka rejects being renamed a monster. She recognizes Sensei and indicates unfinished personal conversation, but sets that aside for club business. The prior relationship is established only as recognition and something to discuss later; its history and valence are unknown.

Yuuka says the club deliberately involved Schale to stop dissolution, yet school club operations are largely delegated to each school's student council—even the federal president's return would not itself override this school-level matter. That is her institutional-jurisdiction assertion, not an independently audited legal rule. It does test the E001 appeal: a cross-school adult's arrival cannot by itself cancel a locally administered decision.

Yuuka's grievance is not merely dislike of games. Her clean later speech requires demonstrated achievement and points to budget/room allocation; a membership route is suggested by the corrupted early exchange and the sisters' later recruiting discussion, not safely attributable word-for-word to her here. She cites a casino-like gambling event in an odd school building and an attack on the Ancient History Research Club under cover of seeking retro games. These are Yuuka's accusations, not scenes independently shown. She says the club has consumed resources while producing no persuasive outcome. Her language moves from accountable stewardship into humiliation: a poor game, `今年のクソゲーランキング1位`, `ガラクタ`, and a judgment that continued activity would damage the school's reputation. The first game's existence and notorious ranking are corroborated by Momoi's concession; the exact review quotations remain Yuuka's reported citations.

Her `ミレニアムでは「結果」が全て` and claim that aggregate user evaluation approaches truth should be read as her expressed evaluative ideology, not a settled theorem or the voice of every Millennium student. The episode distinguishes an intelligible allocation problem from Yuuka's needlessly derisive handling of it.

## 2. A contest opens a conditional, time-limited route

Yuuka explicitly says demonstrable achievement could reverse dissolution and gives examples from sports and engineering. Momoi names `TSC2`—a second `テイルズ・サガ・クロニクル`—for the Millennium Prize, the school-wide competition for club products. The prize's exact descriptive line at `u:0074` carries a suspect Yuuka label, so it is treated as conversation-level exposition, not Yuuka's secure voice. Yuuka accepts the contest as a possible test but rates success as extremely unlikely, comparing it to a high-school player suddenly entering the major leagues. She then chooses to wait the two weeks until the contest, partly because she herself has become curious.

This revises her earlier `誰にも覆せない` rhetoric. The current dissolution trajectory is serious, but she retains discretion to defer and to honor a result. The two-week extension is a concrete institutional alternative, not victory. Yuuka then expresses embarrassment at showing Sensei an unflattering side and says this too is student-council work; public office and desired private presentation diverge briefly, without establishing a private personality.

Sensei's sole intervention in the public argument is a comic response to `1位`, not a demonstrated federal override, budget decision, or negotiated extension. The outcome follows Momoi's challenge and Yuuka's conditional agreement.

## 3. After Yuuka leaves: recruitment, creative value, and instrumental risk

Midori proposes recruitment as perhaps more likely than making a winning game in two weeks. Momoi says a month of recruiting brought nobody and ridicule of their retro-game preference; Midori attributes at least some failure to their own conduct rather than accepting Momoi's charge that Yuuka exploited their lack of friends. This disagreement gives Midori an independent appraisal: she shares the survival goal but checks Momoi's blame story and odds.

Momoi reveals that the `切り札` she promised was Sensei. The source shows Sensei's surprised question, not prior informed consent to any particular hazard. Momoi describes a mysterious region near Millennium formerly subject to General Student Council entry restriction because it was said to be dangerous. She herself does not know what the danger is, whether entry is impossible, or whether prior visitors returned. Those alternatives are speculation inside her speech, not established disappearances. The restriction's current status is likewise not independently confirmed.

Her reason for wanting the ruins is not simply an administrative loophole. She wants to make a good game and prove that games that made her happy are treasures, not junk, even if her present skill and first game's reputation are poor. Yuuka's `ガラクタ` insult is answered as a value claim about creative experience. To do this, Momoi says the group must enter and find `あれ`, then names `G.Bible` without explaining it. The object, provenance, and necessity of the ruins remain OPEN. Sensei's usefulness may involve access or authority, but the mechanism is not yet stated.
