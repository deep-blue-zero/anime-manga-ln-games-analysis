# 1. Why the Prologue is a genuine analytical boundary

The Prologue is only two canonical story objects, but it is structurally complete enough to justify a checkpoint.

E001 begins at an apparent catastrophic endpoint and then places Sensei inside a federal authorization crisis. E002 resolves the immediate crisis, activates the institution around which Sensei will operate, introduces the continuing system partner Arona, disperses the temporary opening ensemble, and ends by declaring the beginning of Schale's first official mission.

The transition is therefore not merely chronological. The Prologue moves through a complete constitutional sequence:

> **catastrophic warning → arrival without knowledge → institutional vacuum → delegated exceptional office → demonstrated competence → privileged system access → opportunity to retain control → transfer/restraint → Schale becomes operational → ordinary student problems become the mission field**

The next canonical story changes both numerical volume grouping and narrative focus to `VOLUME_001 / 対策委員会編`. The Prologue has therefore completed the work of establishing the narrative's governing role, political topology, ethical vocabulary, and principal unresolved mysteries.

---

# 2. Checkpoint thesis

The strongest Prologue-level formulation is:

> **Blue Archive begins by defining legitimate adulthood as the responsible exercise of discretionary power inside a world where students already possess agency, institutions, weapons, and political authority of their own. Sensei is not legitimized simply by being an adult, by being selected by the missing President, or by possessing exceptional access. The Prologue instead stages legitimacy as a sequence of choices: accept dependence on students for local knowledge, intervene where useful, coordinate rather than replace student action, acquire extraordinary administrative capacity, return ordinary authority to the existing government, and then direct an underconstrained exceptional institution toward service.**

This thesis is stronger after E002 than it was after E001 because the Prologue does not merely *say* that responsible choice matters. It creates a concrete circumstance in which power can be retained and then makes transfer/restraint the structurally stable action.

The resulting political-ethical pattern is:

> **responsibility and obligation → entrusted authority → discretion → opportunity for possession → restraint / transfer → service**

The checkpoint does **not** elevate this immediately to a full-series thesis. What has been established is narrower and more defensible:

- this pattern governs the **Prologue's construction of Sensei**;
- it establishes the baseline against which later uses of adult and Schale authority should be measured;
- later arcs may preserve, complicate, expose limits in, or contradict the baseline.

# 3. The Prologue as a theory of choice

## 3.1 Choice precedes ordinary exposition

The first significant conceptual vocabulary in the work is not academy lore, weapon mechanics, or character biography. The unknown speaker foregrounds:

- `選択`
- `責任`
- `義務`
- `大人`
- the possibility of reaching a different result from a catastrophic endpoint.

The key distinction is explicit:

> `大事なのは経験ではなく、選択。`

At the first-pass boundary, the exact mechanism behind this statement remains unknown. What the Prologue does make clear is that knowledge and experience are not treated as sufficient conditions for right action.

E002 then gives the proposition practical form. Sensei partially recognizes the Shittim Chest and can reproduce the required password without receiving a full explanatory memory. The narrative therefore preserves an epistemic gap while still demanding action.

This creates an important Prologue distinction:

> **knowing everything is not a prerequisite for being responsible for what one chooses.**

That is not the same as celebrating ignorance. Sensei repeatedly depends on Rin, students, and Arona for information. Rather, Blue Archive's opening refuses to collapse ethical judgment into perfect information.

## 3.2 Player choice is bounded, but not therefore meaningless

The Prologue's choice groups rarely create conventional branching narrative routes. They more often vary tone while converging on a stable structural commitment.

Examples include:

- concern for Rin expressed plainly or lightly;
- alternative phrasings of taking tactical command;
- nonaggressive first-contact responses toward Wakamo;
- playful versus ordinary interaction with Arona;
- required repair when teasing genuinely hurts Arona;
- `大丈夫。` / `承認する。` converging on transfer of Sanctum Tower control;
- mutual partnership language with Arona at the end of the Prologue.

This means “choice” should not be evaluated only by asking whether a menu option produces a different plot branch.

The Prologue instead appears to separate two layers:

1. **structural Sensei** — certain ethical/role commitments are authored as stable;
2. **choice-space Sensei** — the player can modulate tone, warmth, humor, phrasing, and sometimes immediate stance within that authored range.

The strongest current example is the Tower transfer. The options vary in wording, not moral destination. The Prologue therefore defines a Sensei who can be personalized but not emptied of all authored ethical content.

### Checkpoint judgment

The claim that Blue Archive's theme of choice concerns **ethical/persona agency in addition to, and perhaps more fundamentally than, route branching** is now supported at the Prologue level. It remains longitudinally testable because later choice structures may behave differently.

# 4. Sensei: from blank outsider to bounded discretionary authority

## 4.1 What the Prologue deliberately withholds

Sensei enters without the biographical apparatus normally used to legitimate a protagonist.

The Prologue does not establish:

- a detailed prior career;
- ideological doctrine;
- family background;
- personal ambition;
- comprehensive knowledge of Kivotos;
- physical superiority over the students.

Instead, Sensei begins in dependence.

Rin knows the city better. Students understand local institutions and violence norms better. Arona understands parts of the Shittim system better. Sensei's authority therefore cannot initially rest on epistemic omniscience.

## 4.2 Four sources of opening legitimacy

Across E001–E002, Sensei accumulates four distinguishable forms of legitimacy.

### A. Ethical expectation

The unknown opening speaker treats Sensei as a `信じられる大人` whose choices extend from adult `責任と義務`.

This is expectation, not proof.

### B. Institutional delegation

The missing General Student Council President selected Sensei and designed Schale around that selection.

This provides formal access and an initial presumption of trust, but neither Rin nor the assembled students simply treats the selection as sufficient evidence of competence.

### C. Demonstrated competence

The first student group experiences an improvement in combat under Sensei's tactical direction. The role becomes experientially credible.

### D. Demonstrated restraint

E002 adds the strongest legitimacy test. Sensei obtains unique access to a system capable of recovering federal administrative control and then approves transfer back to the General Student Council rather than retaining it.

The Prologue therefore moves from delegated authority to enacted authority.

> **selection opens the office; conduct begins to justify it.**

## 4.3 Sensei's power is asymmetrical, not absent

E001 made a “coordination rather than personal power” reading attractive because Sensei's first useful action is command and the students are physically more resilient.

E002 requires revision.

Sensei is uniquely able to operate the Shittim Chest and, through Arona, access administrative power unavailable to the General Student Council. The correct Prologue model is therefore not “Sensei has no special power.”

Nor is “conventional omnipotent player-avatar” adequate.

Sensei is simultaneously:

- physically vulnerable;
- locally uninformed;
- dependent on student expertise;
- dependent on Rin for institutional orientation;
- dependent on Arona for system execution;
- unusually effective as a coordinator;
- uniquely authorized for the Shittim Chest;
- entrusted with a highly discretionary institution.

The power profile is **distributed and asymmetric**.

That matters thematically because responsibility is meaningful only if Sensei actually has capacities whose use can matter.

## 4.4 The adult is not simply “above” the students

The Prologue repeatedly prevents a simple age hierarchy.

Students protect Sensei physically. Rin instructs Sensei institutionally. Sensei coordinates students tactically. Rin then steps aside when Sensei alone can access the Shittim Chest. Arona executes technical actions Sensei cannot directly perform. Sensei decides whether authority is retained or transferred.

This produces a network of reciprocal dependencies rather than a one-directional adult-command structure.

The adult's distinctive ethical burden therefore does not erase student competence. It exists **within a field of student agency**.

# 5. Student governance: crisis without infantilization

A central counter-reading after E001 was that the story might be introducing an adult because student government is inherently incompetent.

The completed Prologue does not support that formulation.

## 5.1 The failure is specific

The General Student Council remains populated by officials who:

- identify the crisis;
- communicate across institutions;
- organize emergency response;
- understand the missing authorization problem;
- preserve enough bureaucracy to resume administration immediately once control is restored.

The failure is tied to the disappearance of the final administrator and loss of Sanctum Tower's administrative-control authorization.

This is a **specific institutional bottleneck**, not a demonstration that students cannot govern.

## 5.2 Sensei restores rather than replaces

E002 is decisive.

When federal control becomes technically available through the Shittim Chest and Arona, Sensei does not become the new General Student Council President or permanent controller of Sanctum Tower. Control is transferred back to the existing government.

That is incompatible with the strongest version of an “adult sovereign replaces failed children” reading.

## 5.3 The leadership vacuum remains

The restoration is not total.

The President remains missing. The General Student Council must devote substantial capacity to finding her. Kivotos still contains many distributed problems that ordinary institutions cannot immediately address.

The Prologue therefore distinguishes:

- **technical administrative capacity** — restored;
- **political leadership continuity** — unresolved;
- **institutional workload/capacity** — still strained;
- **local academy autonomy** — still present;
- **cross-jurisdictional residual problems** — now Schale's likely field.

This is a more complex political world than a binary of competent adult versus incompetent students.

# 6. Schale: exceptional institution, corrective function, unresolved legitimacy risk

## 6.1 What is established

Schale is introduced as federal and `超法規的`, capable of crossing academy boundaries and recruiting or working with students irrespective of ordinary affiliation limits.

E002 sharpens the design with Rin's unusually direct description:

> `権限だけはありますが目標のない組織`

and the corresponding statement that, in practical terms, what Sensei does is largely `先生の自由`.

The institution therefore has two defining features:

1. **exceptional jurisdiction**;
2. **weakly specified ends**.

## 6.2 Why Schale is not yet a replacement sovereign

Its first major institutional action after Sensei gains access is to restore another institution's control.

Its prospective mission is then described not as ruling Kivotos but handling problems that cut across or exceed ordinary institutional capacity:

- relief supplies;
- environmental problems;
- remedial teaching;
- club support;
- other student requests.

At the Prologue boundary, Schale is best understood as a **bridging / corrective / overflow institution** rather than a replacement government.

## 6.3 Why this does not solve the legitimacy problem

The fact that Sensei behaves responsibly once does not make underconstrained authority safe by definition.

E002 actually increases the political stakes of the project by clarifying that:

- Schale's discretion is extremely broad;
- ordinary procedural goals do not fully determine its actions;
- its legitimacy therefore depends heavily on operator judgment.

The opening speaker's language about adult responsibility becomes institutionally relevant precisely here.

The Prologue is not saying “exceptional power is fine because Sensei has it.” A more defensible reading is:

> **exceptional power creates the circumstances in which Sensei's ethics become consequential.**

Later arcs must test whether restraint, service, proportionality, respect for student agency, and institutional nonreplacement remain stable under pressure.

# 7. Authority as custody rather than possession

E002 supplies the checkpoint's strongest political image through the lexical and structural movement from `支配` to `移管`.

Arona is capable of describing the recovered state in terms of effective control over Kivotos. The text then immediately turns to whether that control should be transferred.

This creates a distinction between:

- **capacity to control**;
- **authorization to act**;
- **political legitimacy**;
- **choice to retain or relinquish**.

The Prologue refuses to treat those categories as interchangeable.

Sensei can possess access without treating access as ownership.

That is why “custodial authority” is the most useful current formulation. Sensei receives capabilities in order to repair, enable, coordinate, or support; the first use does not convert temporary access into a personal sovereignty claim.

### Forward test

This motif is strongly supported in the Prologue but must remain a longitudinal test rather than a completed series thesis.

The important question for later arcs is:

> When Sensei has the ability to decide for students or institutions, does Sensei continue to create conditions in which they can act, or begin substituting adult will for their agency?

# 8. Relationships at the first checkpoint

No relationship is mature enough for a specialist synthesis. The Prologue does, however, establish several distinct relational starting states.

## 8.1 Sensei ↔ Rin — reciprocal competence

The relationship begins asymmetrically in Rin's favor with respect to local knowledge. She wakes Sensei, explains Kivotos, and translates the institutional crisis.

The dependency later reverses in a specific domain. Rin cannot operate the Shittim Chest. She entrusts it to Sensei and deliberately steps away. Sensei and Arona repair the authorization failure, then return control to Rin's institution.

The resulting relationship is not well described as adult command over a student subordinate.

Its Prologue state is:

> **reciprocal administrative dependence + provisional institutional trust.**

Rin provides local/governmental competence; Sensei provides unique access and judgment.

## 8.2 Sensei ↔ Arona — technical partnership humanized immediately

Arona could have been introduced as a neutral operating-system interface. The text instead gives the system administrator:

- a body/persona in a classroom-like space;
- sleep and confusion;
- technical competence;
- explicit knowledge limits;
- embarrassment and tears;
- playful interaction;
- a repair sequence after teasing;
- a promise-like biometric interaction;
- a secretary/support role;
- a mutual closing commitment to future work.

The relationship therefore begins as both functional and affective.

It should not yet be forced into parental, romantic, metaphysical, or other mature categories. The stable checkpoint formulation is:

> **playful, emotionally responsive operational partnership.**

## 8.3 Sensei ↔ Wakamo — strong asymmetry, explanation withheld

Wakamo is first presented through correctional status and destructive appetite. Her elaborate politeness coexists with enjoyment of destruction.

Upon seeing Sensei, her linguistic and behavioral state collapses:

- hesitation;
- stammering;
- apology;
- immediate retreat;
- continued private preoccupation later.

Sensei shows no equivalent intensity.

The relationship state is therefore strongly asymmetrical, but its cause is intentionally unknown at this boundary.

The checkpoint explicitly rejects importing later material to name the motive.

## 8.4 Sensei ↔ the initial academy representatives — trust becomes access

The temporary ensemble begins with institutional urgency rather than friendship.

Sensei's tactical effectiveness creates practical credibility. By the end of E002, students from multiple academy contexts invite Sensei to visit or contact them later.

The transition is:

> **role recognition → competence trust → cross-school future-access network.**

This is important to Schale's narrative design. Cross-jurisdictional authority is not introduced solely as a legal abstraction; it begins acquiring social routes through student recognition.

# 9. Character-state checkpoint

## Sensei

**Current Prologue state:**

- adult outsider;
- biographically underdefined;
- incomplete local knowledge;
- physically vulnerable relative to students;
- selected by missing President;
- Schale advisor/operator;
- tactically effective coordinator;
- uniquely authorized Shittim user;
- holder of extraordinary but mediated system access;
- chooses transfer rather than retention of federal control;
- receives broad discretion without fixed mission;
- becomes a multi-academy contact.

**Principal open tension:** how much of Sensei's legitimacy will continue to arise from restraint and enabling others versus direct exercise of exceptional authority?

## Rin

**Current Prologue state:** competent federal administrator functioning under a leadership/authorization crisis.

Rin is important because she prevents the student-government-incompetence thesis from becoming textually easy. She understands institutional boundaries, recognizes what she cannot solve, uses Sensei instrumentally but not blindly, and resumes administration once authority is restored.

**Principal open tension:** how Rin and the General Student Council will treat Schale once immediate emergency cooperation gives way to routine institutional overlap.

## Arona

**Current Prologue state:** highly capable resident system administrator / main OS / secretary with incomplete knowledge and pronounced affective personhood.

**Principal open tension:** whether the humanized interface is merely presentational or points to a deeper ontology of personhood, system authority, and relationship.

## Wakamo

**Current Prologue state:** escaped/suspended student associated with destructive pleasure, formal speech, and a dramatic unexplained reaction to Sensei.

**Principal open tension:** motive and meaning of the Sensei reaction. No later answer is admitted here.

## Yuuka, Hasumi, Chinatsu, Suzumi

**Current Prologue state:** academy-linked representatives/combatants who experience Sensei first as an institutional stranger, then as a useful commander, and finally as a person worth maintaining contact with.

The Prologue is insufficient for full personality reconstruction. Their primary checkpoint function is to demonstrate cross-academy plurality and the transition from formal role recognition to practical trust.

# 10. Japanese-language checkpoint

The Prologue's thematic architecture is unusually visible in its Japanese lexical choices.

## 10.1 `選択` — choice as moral act

The opening's repetition of `選択` creates the central term before the player has conventional context for what is being chosen.

The later choice design then repeatedly makes ethical orientation stable across tonal variants. This supports treating `選択` as more than UI vocabulary.

## 10.2 `責任` / `義務` / `大人`

The phrase `大人としての、責任と義務` makes adulthood normative rather than merely demographic.

At the checkpoint boundary, “adult” is best understood as a role carrying obligations toward people with less structural power or different kinds of vulnerability—not as a blanket claim that adults are wiser than students.

## 10.3 `超法規的`

Schale's exceptional status is not hidden behind soft language. `超法規的` foregrounds that normal legal/jurisdictional rules do not fully bind the institution in ordinary ways.

That term should remain a high-priority longitudinal marker because later stories must reveal whether the narrative treats this exception critically, pragmatically, or aspirationally.

## 10.4 `権限` / `目標` / `自由`

Rin's E002 description links broad authority with lack of predetermined purpose.

The rhetorical effect is striking because discretion is described plainly rather than as a mystical destiny.

The relevant combination is:

> **power exists; purpose must be chosen.**

This is the institutional version of the opening's moral problem.

## 10.5 `支配` / `移管`

The shift from effective control to transfer provides the checkpoint's clearest lexical distinction between capability and possession.

`支配` describes what could be exercised. `移管` describes what Sensei authorizes instead.

This pair should remain in the longitudinal terminology index.

## 10.6 `先生`

Across only two units, `先生` expands rapidly:

- uncertain institutional identification;
- title used by students;
- tactical authority role;
- authenticated identity in the Shittim system;
- stable term under which multiple academy representatives recognize the adult;
- partner identity used by Arona.

The title is therefore already doing more than naming an occupation. It functions as the stable social identity through which a biographically blank adult becomes legible to disparate institutions and systems.

## 10.7 Sacred / technical fusion

`七つの嘆き`, `ジェリコの古則`, password authentication, OS/admin vocabulary, and the Shittim Chest all coexist without the text yet separating “religious” from “technological” explanations.

The safest checkpoint conclusion is formal rather than ontological:

> **Blue Archive deliberately makes sacred/apocalyptic diction and computational language occupy the same narrative register.**

The reason remains open.

# 11. Violence, bodily difference, and ethical power

The Prologue's violence is tonally light in places but analytically important.

## 11.1 Students are resilient, not unharmed

E001 establishes that bullets hurt students and can leave scars. It simultaneously establishes that Sensei may face lethal danger from a single round in a way the students do not.

The correct world model is therefore not “guns are harmless.” It is **differential bodily vulnerability**.

This inversion complicates adult protection narratives. The students are often the ones who must physically shield the adult.

## 11.2 Sensei's authority is not grounded in superior violence

The first proof of Sensei's battlefield value is tactical coordination. E002 adds technical/administrative privilege, not frontline invulnerability.

Thus the Prologue distributes forms of power:

- students: bodily/combat resilience;
- Rin/GSC: institutional knowledge and bureaucracy;
- Arona: system operation;
- Sensei: coordination, judgment, unique authorization, discretionary office.

No single actor possesses every form.

## 11.3 Wakamo as a contrast in discretionary freedom

Wakamo exploits disorder for destructive enjoyment; Sensei uses unusual access to restore and transfer authority.

This creates an opening contrast between freedom as transgressive appetite and freedom as responsibility.

The checkpoint does not yet claim Wakamo is designed as Sensei's mature philosophical foil. It records only that the juxtaposition makes the Prologue's ethical treatment of discretion more visible.

# 12. Institutional topology at the end of the Prologue

The first checkpoint establishes a useful political map.

## Kivotos

A vast academy city whose basic organization is plural rather than unitary. Thousands of academies exist inside a broader federal structure.

## Academy governments / institutions

The Prologue implies meaningful local jurisdiction and affiliation, though the exact constitutional relationships remain underdefined.

## General Student Council

Federal administration. Operational competence is restored after technical authorization returns, but the President remains missing.

## Sanctum Tower

Administrative-control infrastructure. The Prologue demonstrates that technical access to the Tower is not identical to political legitimacy to keep it.

## Schale / `連邦捜査部`

Exceptional cross-jurisdictional institution built around Sensei. It is powerful, mobile, and underconstrained in purpose.

## Shittim Chest / Arona

A privileged technical-administrative layer accessible to Sensei. Its relation to ordinary institutional authority remains only partially understood.

### Checkpoint political model

The Prologue does not establish a simple hierarchy with Sensei on top.

A better model is **overlapping authority domains**:

> local academy agency ↔ federal student administration ↔ exceptional Schale discretion ↔ privileged Shittim technical access

Sensei occupies a bridging position across these domains rather than replacing all of them.

# 13. Motif and theme checkpoint

## 13.1 Choice versus experience — STRENGTHEN

The opening explicitly privileges choice over experience. E002 preserves incomplete memory while requiring action and makes consequential commitments stable through choice-space.

## 13.2 Adult responsibility — STRENGTHEN

E001 states the proposition. E002 enacts it under meaningful power.

## 13.3 Counterfactual / alternative outcome — OPEN

The catastrophic endpoint remains unexplained. The Prologue gives no basis for identifying its cause or mechanics.

## 13.4 Institutional vacuum — REVISE / STRENGTHEN

The crisis is more specifically an authorization and leadership problem than general governmental collapse. Technical administration is repaired; leadership absence remains.

## 13.5 Exceptional jurisdiction — STRENGTHEN, risk increased

E002 clarifies that Schale has authority without a compulsory objective. Exceptional jurisdiction is therefore both useful and politically risky.

## 13.6 Enabling authority — STRENGTHEN

Sensei first makes student action more effective, then restores student-government administration.

## 13.7 Sacred / technical hybridity — STRENGTHEN sharply

Ritual/apocalyptic vocabulary becomes literally functional in system authentication.

## 13.8 Authority as transferable custody — STRENGTHEN at Prologue level

The Tower transfer is strong direct evidence. Recurrence remains to be tested.

## 13.9 Freedom as ethical burden — STRENGTHEN at Prologue level

Schale's underdetermined mission converts freedom into a problem of judgment rather than mere empowerment.

## 13.10 Service after crisis — STRENGTHEN at Prologue level

The narrative deliberately descends from federal crisis to requests concerning ordinary student life.

## 13.11 Capability does not equal knowledge — STRENGTHEN

Rin, Sensei, and Arona each possess indispensable capabilities while lacking other key information. The Prologue systematically resists omniscient authority.

Two simplifications should no longer be used as live current-authority interpretations of the Prologue:

# 15. Competing readings and checkpoint counterevidence

## Reading A — The Prologue is an adult-savior fantasy about fixing incompetent children

### Supporting surface evidence

- Sensei is the only adult foregrounded in the role.
- Sensei was selected by the missing President.
- Schale has extraordinary jurisdiction.
- the federal crisis cannot be fully repaired without Sensei's unique access.

### Counterevidence

- Rin is competent and indispensable.
- students are the physically resilient combatants.
- Sensei depends on local student knowledge.
- the GSC resumes administration once authorization is repaired.
- Sensei actively transfers control back rather than assuming government.
- Schale's likely work includes support for student-defined problems rather than substituting adult rule.

### Checkpoint judgment

**REJECT in strong form.** The Prologue is better read as asking what responsible adult intervention looks like **without erasing student agency**.

---

## Reading B — Schale is automatically legitimate because the missing President created it

### Supporting surface evidence

- the President selected Sensei;
- she created Schale;
- she left the Shittim Chest for Sensei.

# 22. Checkpoint conclusion

The two-unit Prologue functions less like a conventional lore introduction than like a **normative constitution for the role of Sensei**.

It begins with catastrophe narrated through failed choice. It then withholds biographical certainty and places the adult inside a society already inhabited by competent, armed, politically organized students. The adult is physically fragile, informationally incomplete, institutionally delegated, tactically useful, uniquely authorized, and eventually given extraordinary discretion.

The decisive move is that Blue Archive does not make legitimacy synonymous with the possession of that power.

Sensei's first major encounter with system-scale authority ends in transfer. The student government resumes administration. Schale remains exceptional but does not become the government. Its broad freedom is redirected toward the unresolved problems of students.

The Prologue's strongest current proposition is therefore:

> **A trustworthy adult is not defined by having no power over students, nor by possessing superior power, nor by occupying a formally authorized office. Trustworthiness emerges from what the adult chooses to do when power is available but procedure does not fully determine the answer: enable rather than erase student agency, repair rather than seize institutions, relinquish control that need not be possessed, and turn exceptional freedom toward service.**

That proposition is now canonical **for the Prologue checkpoint**, not yet for Blue Archive as a whole.

The next arc must be read as a test.
