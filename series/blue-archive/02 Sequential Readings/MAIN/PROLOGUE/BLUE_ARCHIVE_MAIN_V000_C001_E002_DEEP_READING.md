# 1. Story placement and local chronology

`BA:main:000:001:002` is the second and final canonical story object in `VOLUME_000 / CHAPTER_001 / プロローグ`.

E001 ended after Sensei's tactical direction helped the assembled students reclaim ground near Schale. E002 continues the same immediate operation, identifies Wakamo as the student responsible for the attack, completes the recovery of the Schale building, places the Shittim Chest in Sensei's hands, introduces Arona, restores Sanctum Tower's administrative control, formally hands Schale over to Sensei, and ends by announcing Schale's **first official mission**.

The source structure therefore makes E002 more than “part two” of a tutorial. It closes the Prologue by converting the role that E001 merely introduced into an operational institution with an ethical problem built into its design.

E001 asked, in effect:

> What kind of adult has been entrusted with exceptional authority?

E002 asks the sharper follow-up:

> What does that adult do **once the authority is actually available to use or keep**?

That difference is decisive.

# 2. Concise narrative reconstruction

Rin identifies the attacker as Wakamo, a suspended Hyakkiyako student who escaped from the Correction Bureau and has multiple prior offenses. Wakamo herself says she does not know what the Schale building contains; knowing only that the General Student Council values it is enough to make her want to destroy it. (`BA:main:000:001:002:scene:001`)

After the building is reclaimed, Sensei encounters Wakamo alone in the basement. The previously composed and destructive Wakamo abruptly stammers, apologizes, and runs away after seeing Sensei. The reason is not explained. (`scene:003`)

Rin then presents Sensei with the object the missing General Student Council President left behind: the `シッテムの箱` (Shittim Chest). Rin says nobody in the Council can identify its manufacturer, operating system, internal structure, or operating mechanism, and nobody except Sensei has been able even to boot it. Sensei recognizes the name as something heard before and activates it. (`scene:004`)

The system requests the same ritualized password language heard in E001 — the seven lamentations and the ancient law of Jericho — and recognizes Sensei as the authorized connection user. (`scene:005`)

Sensei enters an unfamiliar classroom-like space and wakes a sleeping girl who introduces herself as Arona: the Shittim Chest's resident system administrator, main OS, secretary, and future support agent. Her attempted biometric authentication is comically manual and imperfect. She does not know who the General Student Council President really is or why she disappeared, but she can recover Sanctum Tower's administrator authority. (`scene:006`)

Arona succeeds. She says that the Tower is now under her control and that Kivotos is therefore effectively under Sensei's control as well. Crucially, she does not transfer the authority automatically. She asks whether Sensei is sure about returning it to the General Student Council. The designed Sensei choice-space converges on approval: `大丈夫。` / `承認する。` Arona transfers control, and Rin confirms that the Council can once again conduct administration as it did before the President vanished. (`scene:006`)

Rin then formally gives Sensei Schale. She explains that Schale has broad authority but no required objective: Sensei may travel throughout academy districts, recruit students regardless of affiliation, and in practical terms choose what to do. Because the General Student Council is consumed by the search for its President, Rin suggests that Schale might take on the many ordinary requests reaching the government — relief supplies, environmental improvements, remedial teaching, club support, and other problems. (`scene:006`)

The students who fought with Sensei depart for their respective academies after inviting Sensei to visit. Arona reframes the work ahead as helping Kivotos's students solve the problems they face, calling that work simple-looking but difficult and important. The Prologue ends with her declaring the start of Schale's first official mission. (`scene:006`)

# 3. Central thesis

**E002 turns E001's abstract language of responsible choice into an institutional test: Sensei is granted access to extraordinary centralized authority, declines to retain that authority, returns ordinary administration to the existing student government, and accepts leadership of an organization whose power is broad precisely because its mission is not predetermined.**

This materially strengthens the Prologue's emerging conception of legitimate adulthood.

The key is not that Sensei lacks power. E002 makes the opposite clear. Sensei possesses unique access that the General Student Council itself lacks, and Arona can convert that access into control over Sanctum Tower. The text even permits Arona to describe the resulting state as functionally placing Kivotos under Sensei's rule.

But the narrative's first required use of that extraordinary position is **relinquishment**.

That gives the Prologue a more precise ethical pattern than E001 alone supported:

> **responsibility → entrusted authority → opportunity to retain control → voluntary transfer/restraint → open-ended service**

This is not yet proof that every later use of Schale power will be legitimate. In fact, E002 makes Schale more politically dangerous than E001 did: Rin explicitly says it has authority but no compulsory objective, and that Sensei may essentially do whatever Sensei wants. The episode therefore does not solve the legitimacy problem by designing a perfectly constrained institution. It places the burden on the person operating it.

**THEMATIC INTERPRETATION:** the Prologue's most important political question is becoming not “who has the strongest formal power?” but **what a trustworthy adult does with discretionary power once procedural constraints no longer force the answer**.

# 4. Scene-by-scene analytical reading

## 4.1 Scene 001 — Wakamo and purposeless destruction

Stable locator: `BA:main:000:001:002:scene:001`

Rin identifies Wakamo through institutional categories:

- suspended from Hyakkiyako;
- escaped from the Correction Bureau;
- multiple similar prior offenses;
- `危険な人物`.

These are **TEXTUAL FACTS**, but they establish only a public/institutional baseline. They do not yet constitute a mature character account.

Wakamo's own words are more revealing than the dossier. She admits she does not know what the Schale building contains. Her destructive motivation is almost anti-instrumental: she has heard that the General Student Council values something there, and that fact itself creates the urge to destroy it.

> `連邦生徒会が大事にしてる物と聞いてしまうと……壊さないと気が済みませんね……。`

**LINGUISTIC OBSERVATION:** Wakamo speaks in highly polite forms — `存じません`, `構いません`, `気が済みませんね` — while describing destructive pleasure. The softness and formality of the register do not moderate the violence; they sit beside it.

Her final `久しぶりのお楽しみ` frames the attack as anticipated enjoyment rather than political necessity.

**OPEN HYPOTHESIS:** Wakamo may be introduced as a figure whose relationship to institutional order is not primarily ideological opposition but transgressive enjoyment. That is only a first-scene hypothesis; later context may supply motives not visible here.

This matters to the Prologue because her presence supplies a useful contrast with Sensei. Both stand partly outside ordinary academy jurisdiction, but their relation to institutional freedom is opposite at first contact:

- Wakamo treats the weakness of order as an opportunity for destruction;
- Sensei will shortly treat exceptional access as something to relinquish or redirect toward service.

That contrast is **THEMATIC INTERPRETATION**, not an assertion that the story has already made Wakamo a philosophical foil in a mature sense.

## 4.2 Scene 002 — the battle is functionally compressed

Stable locator: `BA:main:000:001:002:scene:002`

The text moves quickly from approaching Schale to confirmation that the building has been reclaimed.

This is a useful methodological reminder for game-script analysis: some of the action exists partly in the gameplay interval rather than in extensive dialogue narration. The literary artifact therefore should not invent tactical details that the recovered text does not provide.

**STRUCTURAL FACT:** the operation succeeds; the students and Sensei reach the building; Rin directs them to meet in the basement.

E001 already established Sensei's tactical contribution. E002 does not need to repeat the proof verbally before moving to the deeper problem of what Schale contains.

## 4.3 Scene 003 — Wakamo's composure collapses at first contact with Sensei

Stable locator: `BA:main:000:001:002:scene:003`

In the basement, Wakamo is still trying to understand what the General Student Council values there. She says that she cannot destroy the object because she does not even understand what it is.

Then she sees Sensei.

The scene becomes almost entirely hesitation:

- `あら、あららら……。`
- silence;
- `あ、ああ……。`
- `し、し……。`
- `失礼いたしましたー！！`

She then flees.

The important first-pass fact is not *why* she reacts this way, because E002 does not say. The important fact is the scale of the state change.

**TEXTUAL / STRUCTURAL FACT:** the person introduced moments earlier as cheerfully intent on destroying federal property loses verbal composure and withdraws immediately upon meeting Sensei.

The player's two initial responses are:

- `怪しい生徒と出くわしてしまった。`
- `挨拶する。`

Both are low-intensity responses relative to Wakamo's visible disturbance. The script does not let Sensei immediately threaten, arrest, interrogate, or attack her in this encounter.

**RELATIONAL INFERENCE — OPEN:** Sensei exerts an immediate and unexplained personal effect on Wakamo. The nature of that effect is not yet established. It could later be clarified as attraction, recognition, projection, fixation, or something else; this reading leaves the category open.

Her later hidden laughter at the end of the unit shows that the encounter continues to occupy her attention, but still does not define the motive.

This is exactly the kind of scene where later hindsight must eventually be recorded as a revision rather than smuggled into the first reading.

## 4.4 Scene 004 — the Shittim Chest makes E001's impossible language physically actionable

Stable locator: `BA:main:000:001:002:scene:004`

Rin gives Sensei the object left by the missing President.

The crucial facts are unusually specific:

- it looks like a tablet;
- its manufacturer is unknown;
- its OS is unknown;
- its system architecture is unknown;
- its operating mechanism is unknown;
- the General Student Council could not even start it;
- the President said it belonged to Sensei;
- the President expected Sensei to use it to recover Tower control.

This establishes a form of **privileged technical access** that revises one aspect of E001's Sensei model.

E001's first demonstrated ability was coordination. It was therefore reasonable to hypothesize that Sensei's special capability would principally be command rather than extraordinary direct power. E002 shows that this was too narrow. Sensei also possesses unique access to an object capable of affecting Kivotos-wide administrative infrastructure.

This does **not** turn Sensei into a conventional combat superbeing. It does mean that “Sensei's power is only social/coordination power” is no longer adequate.

### Recognition without explicit memory

Sensei's response to `シッテムの箱` is:

> `（どこかで聞いたことある名前……）`

E001's unknown speaker used the same name before Sensei woke. E002 therefore creates the first explicit bridge between the opening address and present experience.

**STRUCTURAL FACT:** the Prologue repeats a term from the opening catastrophe in the ordinary present.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Sensei experiences partial recognition without explicit recollection.

This also makes the E001 proposition “experience is not what matters most” more interesting. Sensei does not receive a complete explanatory memory dump. Instead, a residue of recognition exists without understanding.

**OPEN HYPOTHESIS:** the story may distinguish between remembered information and a deeper continuity of judgment or orientation. Two units are far too early to specify the mechanism.

### Rin steps away

Rin explicitly says that from this point everything depends on Sensei, then moves away so as not to interfere.

This is an important institutional gesture. The student administrator who has guided Sensei throughout the crisis recognizes a boundary beyond which her own bureaucracy cannot operate.

E001 showed Sensei depending on Rin for local knowledge. E002 now shows Rin depending on Sensei for an authorization function she cannot perform.

**RELATIONAL INFERENCE:** their relationship is becoming reciprocal in competence rather than hierarchical in a simple adult-over-student direction.

## 4.5 Scene 005 — sacred language becomes a literal system password

Stable locator: `BA:main:000:001:002:scene:005`

The Shittim Chest requests a system password. Sensei enters words that arise in the mind:

- the seven lamentations;
- the ancient law of Jericho.

The system accepts them.

This is the strongest confirmation yet of E001's **sacred/technical hybridity**.

In E001, the juxtaposition could still have been read as dream imagery: apocalyptic or scriptural language placed beside authentication terminology. E002 converts the same language into a functioning access protocol.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** ritualized/religious-sounding phrases are operationally valid authentication data for the Shittim Chest.

**THEMATIC INTERPRETATION:** Blue Archive's symbolic language does not cleanly separate “mystical” from “technological.” At least here, the sacred-seeming phrase and the machine procedure occupy the same causal system.

The system then moves to biometric authentication and `メインオペレートシステムA.R.O.N.A`.

The sequence is formally elegant:

> catastrophic prophecy-like address → forgotten waking state → mysterious tablet → remembered phrase → accepted password → schoolroom → girl-shaped OS.

It progressively domesticates the uncanny without explaining it away.

## 4.6 Scene 006 — Arona, sovereign access, voluntary transfer, and Schale's actual beginning

Stable locator: `BA:main:000:001:002:scene:006`

This is the Prologue's largest scene and its most important institutional scene.

### 4.6.1 Arona is introduced as a personified system, not an impersonal interface

Sensei arrives in an unfamiliar classroom and finds a girl sleeping at a desk, dreaming about castella and flavored milk.

Only after being awakened does she identify herself:

> `私はアロナ！`

and then:

> `この「シッテムの箱」に常駐しているシステム管理者であり、メインOS、そしてこれから先生をアシストする秘書です！`

Arona therefore occupies several categories at once:

- system administrator;
- operating system;
- secretary/assistant;
- girl encountered in a classroom-like relational space.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** the story itself combines these categories; this is not metaphor supplied by the analyst.

Her behavior immediately prevents the system role from becoming abstract omniscience. She oversleeps, stammers, needs a self-introduction, admits her body version is low, says her vocal apparatus needs adjustment, performs biometric verification by visually inspecting a fingerprint, struggles to see it, and becomes tearful when Sensei jokes about more advanced technology.

This matters beyond comedy.

**THEMATIC INTERPRETATION:** the Shittim Chest's extraordinary technical authority is mediated through a fallible, emotionally legible relationship rather than a frictionless command terminal.

The interface between Sensei and potentially enormous institutional power is therefore personalized from the beginning.

### 4.6.2 Formal authentication becomes a relational ritual

Arona compares the finger contact for biometric authentication to `指切りして約束する` — making a pinky promise.

This is a small but revealing line.

The same act is simultaneously:

- credentialing;
- bodily authentication;
- a childlike promise metaphor;
- the beginning of a working partnership.

The Prologue repeatedly refuses to isolate technical administration from interpersonal meaning. A password sounds ritualistic; an OS is a girl in a classroom; biometrics become a promise gesture.

**OPEN HYPOTHESIS:** Blue Archive may repeatedly translate systems, institutions, and technologies into relational forms so that questions of authority become questions of trust and obligation rather than pure mechanism.

### 4.6.3 Arona is knowledgeable but explicitly not omniscient

Arona says she knows a great deal about Kivotos but almost nothing about the General Student Council President: not who she truly is and not why she disappeared.

This is important negative evidence.

The Shittim Chest can solve a system-administration problem that the General Student Council cannot, but its resident administrator does not solve the narrative's central epistemic mystery.

The Prologue therefore distinguishes **capability** from **knowledge**.

That parallel also applies to Sensei:

- Sensei can activate the system without understanding its origin;
- Arona can recover Tower control without understanding the President;
- Rin understands the bureaucracy without being able to recover its final authorization herself.

No single actor is yet epistemically complete.

### 4.6.4 The decisive political action: acquire control, then give it back

Arona obtains Sanctum Tower's `admin権限` and reports:

> `今サンクトゥムタワーは、私アロナの統制下にあります。`

She then states:

> `今のキヴォトスは、先生の支配下にあるも同然です！`

The exact political reach of Sanctum Tower should not be exaggerated beyond the text; E001 established it as central administrative-control infrastructure, not a magical device that makes every student obey. But the scene deliberately frames the recovered privilege as extremely broad.

Then comes the ethically decisive choice locus.

Arona says control can be transferred to the General Student Council **if Sensei approves**, and asks:

> `でも……大丈夫ですか？連邦生徒会に制御権を渡しても……。`

The two designed responses are:

- `大丈夫。`
- `承認する。`

Both converge on transfer.

This is one of the most important distinctions the analytical method requires between **choice-space Sensei** and **structural Sensei**.

The player's tone can vary slightly, but the narrative's Sensei **does not have a meaningful branch in which the recovered federal administrative control is retained**.

**STRUCTURAL FACT:** Sensei authorizes transfer to the General Student Council.

**INSTITUTIONAL INFERENCE:** Sensei's first encounter with potentially sovereign administrative power is framed through restoration rather than personal capture.

**THEMATIC INTERPRETATION:** E002 supplies the first concrete enactment of E001's vocabulary of responsible choice. Responsible adulthood is not merely asserted by the unknown speaker; the Prologue now makes Sensei relinquish an available concentration of power.

This materially strengthens `BA-C001`, `BA-C002`, `BA-C003`, and the earlier rejection of a simple “student government must be replaced by an adult” reading.

### 4.6.5 The General Student Council is restored, not superseded

Rin confirms that the Council has reacquired control and can resume administration `連邦生徒会長がいた頃と同じように`.

This is very strong evidence against the simplistic interpretation that the story's solution to student governance is adult replacement.

Sensei was needed because the existing system had an authorization failure. Once Sensei repairs that failure, ordinary administration goes back to the student government.

**INSTITUTIONAL INFERENCE:** Schale's first political function is corrective and bridging, not substitutional.

That does not prove this will always be Schale's role. It does, however, make E001's `cross-institutional corrective` hypothesis significantly stronger.

### 4.6.6 Schale has power without a predetermined mission

Rin then explains perhaps the strangest fact about Schale:

> `シャーレは、権限だけはありますが目標のない組織`

It has authority, but no goal.

She continues that there is no coercive requirement to do any particular thing. Sensei may enter any academy district, recruit students regardless of affiliation, and effectively choose what to do.

The scene repeatedly emphasizes freedom:

- `何でも先生がやりたいことをやって良い`
- `すべては、先生の自由ですので。`

This revises the institutional question substantially.

E001 suggested that Schale might possess exceptional powers for a specific emergency-corrective purpose. E002 shows that the institution's legal/structural discretion is broader than that. Its powers are not tightly tethered to an enumerated mission in the text Rin provides.

That makes Schale **more ethically dependent on Sensei**, not less.

A rules-bound office can outsource morality to procedure. Schale cannot, at least not completely. If the organization truly has large jurisdiction but no mandatory objective, then its operator's choices determine whether it becomes:

- a service institution;
- an interventionist power center;
- an idle office;
- or something abusive.

The Prologue's emphasis on a `信じられる大人` from E001 now looks structurally necessary rather than merely complimentary.

**OPEN HYPOTHESIS:** Schale is designed as an institutionalized test of discretionary ethics — an office whose legitimacy cannot be inferred from its charter because the charter leaves enormous room for choice.

### 4.6.7 The mission emerges from mundane petitions, not grand strategy

Rin does not follow “do whatever you want” by assigning Sensei a grand geopolitical project.

Instead she points to accumulated complaints and requests:

- relief supplies;
- environmental improvements;
- remedial lessons for failing students;
- support for clubs;
- other everyday problems.

This is important to the scale of the story's ethics.

The Prologue begins with a catastrophic endpoint and system-level administrative collapse. It ends by placing paperwork about ordinary student needs on Sensei's desk.

That is not an anticlimax. It may be the point.

Arona later describes the work as helping students with the problems they face and says it may look simple but is neither easy nor unimportant.

**THEMATIC INTERPRETATION:** the Prologue deliberately connects world-scale responsibility to ordinary service. The trustworthy adult is not defined only by saving Kivotos from catastrophe but by taking small student problems seriously after the emergency ends.

This may become one of the most important propositions to test in the first full arc.

### 4.6.8 Student invitations convert battlefield trust into an open social network

Yuuka, Hasumi, Chinatsu, and Suzumi depart after the crisis. Hasumi invites Sensei to Trinity; Chinatsu invites Sensei to Gehenna; Yuuka suggests meeting again at Millennium.

These are not yet deep relationships. But they are more than the role-only recognition present at the beginning of E001.

**RELATIONAL INFERENCE:** the opening ensemble now treats Sensei as a legitimate mobile contact across academy boundaries.

This is Schale's political structure embodied socially: Sensei can cross institutional borders not merely because a charter says so, but because students from different schools now personally recognize the adult and invite future contact.

### 4.6.9 Wakamo's closing laughter preserves an unresolved relational thread

After the others leave, Wakamo is shown alone:

> `ああ……これは困りましたね……。`

followed by laughter.

This returns the narrative to the unexplained reaction from scene 003. The important fact is persistence: the encounter with Sensei has created an unresolved state that survives the immediate retreat.

No more specific interpretation is warranted yet.

### 4.6.10 Arona names the durable narrative engine

Arona tells Sensei:

> `本当に大変なのは、これからですよ？`

and frames the task as solving the problems students face.

The final line announces:

> `連邦捜査部「シャーレ」として、最初の公式任務を始めましょう！`

This is exceptionally strong **STRUCTURAL FACT** supporting an arc boundary. The Prologue ends not after Schale completes its first mission, but at the moment Schale becomes ready to begin its first official mission.

The two Prologue units are therefore an **institutional and ethical commissioning sequence**.

# 5. Character-state updates

## Sensei

**Previous state after E001:** newly arrived outsider; President-selected Schale advisor; physically vulnerable; tactically effective coordinator; locally dependent on students for knowledge.

**E002 delta:**

- receives the Shittim Chest as a President-designated object;
- can activate a system inaccessible to the General Student Council;
- demonstrates partial recognition of material from the opening without explanatory memory;
- establishes a warm/playful interaction range with Arona;
- authorizes restoration of Sanctum Tower control to the General Student Council rather than retaining it;
- formally becomes Schale's operating center;
- receives broad discretion without a mandated objective;
- is invited into multiple academies by students from the opening ensemble.

**Current provisional formulation:** Sensei is now best understood as an **outsider with extraordinary access but incomplete knowledge, whose first major political act is restraint rather than capture**.

This is stronger than E001's “coordinator” formulation and more precise than an “omnipotent avatar” formulation.

## Rin

Rin moves from crisis manager to institutional handoff actor.

She:

- identifies Wakamo and tracks the operation;
- entrusts the President's unknown device to Sensei;
- admits the Council's technical ignorance;
- deliberately leaves Sensei alone at the point where her own competence ends;
- confirms restored Tower authority;
- defines Schale's unusually broad freedom;
- redirects everyday unresolved petitions toward the new office.

Her E002 role strongly reinforces the picture of a competent bureaucracy dealing honestly with the limits of its own authority.

## Arona

Arona receives her first state entry.

Current evidence supports:

- resident Shittim Chest system administrator/main OS;
- secretary/support role oriented toward Sensei;
- waits for Sensei and recognizes Sensei as the expected authorized user;
- technically powerful enough to recover Sanctum Tower admin authority;
- epistemically limited regarding the General Student Council President;
- comically imperfect in low-level biometric procedure;
- emotionally expressive and sensitive to teasing;
- frames Schale's future work as helping students solve difficult but important problems.

No later-source ontology should be imported yet.

## Wakamo

Current evidence supports two sharply different states inside one unit:

1. calm, polite, pleasure-seeking destructive outlaw targeting federal property;
2. sudden verbal collapse and retreat upon encountering Sensei, followed by continued private preoccupation/laughter.

The cause remains `OPEN`.

## Yuuka, Hasumi, Chinatsu, Suzumi

Their individual characterization changes only modestly in E002, but their relation to Sensei advances:

- they complete the Schale recovery operation;
- acknowledge the resolved crisis;
- depart without resisting Sensei's new institutional role;
- individually or collectively open routes for future visits to Millennium, Trinity, and Gehenna.

This is not enough for mature individual conclusions, but it strengthens the opening ensemble's recognition of Sensei.

# 6. Relationship-state updates

## Sensei ↔ Rin

**E001:** administrative dependency / provisional institutional trust.

**E002:** reciprocal competence and explicit handoff.

Rin trusts Sensei with the President's inaccessible device and withdraws when she can no longer contribute. Sensei restores the administrative system but returns control to Rin's institution.

**RELATIONAL INFERENCE:** their opening relationship is not “adult commander versus student subordinate.” It is a reciprocal administrative partnership in which each party controls a domain the other lacks.

Confidence: **high for functional reciprocity**, still low for deeper personal intimacy.

## Sensei ↔ Arona

New relationship.

The initial relational grammar combines:

- expected long-awaited partnership;
- playful teasing;
- reassurance after Arona becomes upset;
- technical dependence;
- mutual greeting/acknowledgment;
- shared future work.

Arona's role is formally assistant/secretary, but the dialogue immediately gives the relationship more emotional texture than a purely instrumental system-user relation.

**RELATIONAL INFERENCE:** an affectionate-supportive working partnership is being established.

Do not yet infer dependence depth, parental structure, romance, metaphysical bond, or later development.

## Sensei ↔ Wakamo

New unresolved relationship state.

Sensei's first reaction is neutral/low-intensity; Wakamo's reaction is disproportionately intense and unexplained.

**RELATIONAL INFERENCE — OPEN:** there is immediate asymmetry of investment or disturbance. Sensei does not yet display corresponding emotional investment.

## Sensei ↔ initial student representatives

E001 moved from role-based recognition to competence-based provisional trust.

E002 adds **future-access invitations**. The relationship remains shallow but becomes socially durable enough that students imagine meeting Sensei again in their own academy contexts.

This is a small but meaningful strengthening.

# 7. School / club / institutional state

## Kivotos

The immediate administrative crisis is stabilized by the end of E002.

The city is not transferred into an adult-run replacement regime. The federal student bureaucracy resumes ordinary administration after Sensei restores its technical authorization.

At the same time, everyday governance remains overextended: the General Student Council lacks enough capacity to handle the accumulated local requests while searching for its missing President.

**Current model:** functioning plural institutions + federal capacity constraints + newly operational cross-jurisdictional support office.

## General Student Council

State transition:

> administratively blocked → Sanctum Tower control restored → ordinary administration resumes.

The Council's institutional legitimacy is implicitly preserved by Sensei's decision to return the control authority.

The President remains missing, so restoration is operational rather than a complete resolution of the political mystery.

## Sanctum Tower

E001: control inaccessible after the final administrator disappeared.

E002:

- Arona acquires admin control;
- Sensei is treated as the relevant approver;
- control is transferred to the General Student Council;
- ordinary federal administrative management resumes.

This is the clearest demonstration so far that technical authorization and political administration are related but not identical.

## Schale / `連邦捜査部`

E002 provides the decisive institutional definition:

- substantial authority;
- cross-academy mobility;
- cross-affiliation recruitment;
- no compulsory operational objective stated by Rin;
- high discretionary freedom centered on Sensei;
- practical opportunity to address requests the General Student Council lacks capacity to handle.

This makes Schale neither a conventional ministry nor a simple club.

**INSTITUTIONAL INFERENCE:** Schale is a discretionary bridging institution whose legitimacy will have to be judged by how its exceptional access is used.

## Hyakkiyako / Correction Bureau

Wakamo is identified as a suspended Hyakkiyako student and correctional escapee. This confirms that academy affiliation and federal/correctional enforcement can overlap.

No broader conclusion about Hyakkiyako itself should be drawn from Wakamo alone.

# 8. Sensei role and choice-space observations

## 8.1 Structural Sensei

E002 materially expands structural Sensei.

The narrative requires Sensei to:

- enter the recovered Schale building;
- encounter Wakamo;
- receive the Shittim Chest;
- activate it;
- enter the password that comes to mind;
- meet Arona;
- authorize restoration of Tower control to the General Student Council;
- accept Schale as the workplace/office from which future action can occur;
- proceed into Schale's first official mission.

The crucial fixed action is the **transfer of control back to the student government**. The player cannot use the choice system to preserve personal control over the Tower in this scene.

## 8.2 Choice-space Sensei

E002 contains **18 choice groups**, many of which are single-option or paired tonal alternatives.

Important examples:

### Wakamo encounter

`scene:003:choice:001`

- `怪しい生徒と出くわしてしまった。`
- `挨拶する。`

The range is observational to socially polite, not aggressive.

### Arona wake-up

`scene:006:choice:001`

- `揺らして起こす。`
- `頬を突いてみる。`

The player can choose ordinary or lightly playful physical prompting.

### Arona identity

`scene:006:choice:002`

- `そうだよ。`
- `君は誰？`

This preserves Sensei's limited knowledge while allowing different conversational pacing.

### Teasing and care

Single-option lines such as `寝てたわけではなくて？`, followed later by `冗談だったと慰める`, create a bounded pattern: the script permits teasing but structurally requires repair when Arona becomes genuinely upset.

This is valuable early characterization because it distinguishes **playfulness** from **indifference**.

### The Tower transfer

`scene:006:choice:008`

- `大丈夫。`
- `承認する。`

This is the most consequential choice locus in the unit, yet both alternatives converge on the same institutional act.

### What this says about “choice”

E001's opening treated `選択` as morally decisive. E002 shows that the game does not equate moral choice with large branching-route divergence.

**STRUCTURAL FACT:** many player options vary tone while preserving the same required act.

**THEMATIC INTERPRETATION:** Blue Archive may be interested in **what kind of person chooses**, not merely in whether the player can generate multiple timelines through menu branching. The story can make choice ethically meaningful even when its plot outcome is structurally fixed.

This distinction should be tracked carefully because it may become central to how Sensei is both a player-facing viewpoint and a partially authored protagonist.

## 8.3 Relational Sensei

Three relational modes now coexist:

- **Rin / institutional partner:** mutual functional dependence;
- **Arona / assistant-partner:** playful, emotionally responsive, operationally intimate;
- **student representatives / cross-school contacts:** respect plus future invitations.

Wakamo introduces a fourth, unresolved mode in which the student's reaction is intense before Sensei has established any corresponding relationship.

# 9. Japanese-language observations

## 9.1 Wakamo — elaborate politeness wrapped around destructive appetite

Wakamo's language is conspicuously polite relative to her actions.

Examples include:

- `存じません`
- `構いません`
- `壊さないと気が済みませんね`
- `失礼いたしました`

This mismatch between register and conduct produces a distinctive instability. Her speech does not sound like a stereotypically coarse delinquent even when she is acting as one institutionally.

Her encounter with Sensei then breaks the smooth register into repetitions and stammering. The **form** of the language records the state transition before any explanation does.

## 9.2 Rin — discretion described with almost alarming plainness

Rin's most important institutional wording is:

> `権限だけはありますが目標のない組織`

The `だけは` construction gives the sentence a dry, almost ironic edge: if nothing else, Schale certainly has authority.

She then states:

- `何でも先生がやりたいことをやって良い`
- `すべては、先生の自由ですので。`

These are not abstract political-theory terms. The language makes discretion personal and direct.

**LINGUISTIC OBSERVATION:** the scene uses ordinary conversational wording to describe an institutionally extraordinary amount of freedom. This tonal understatement makes the power more striking, not less.

## 9.3 Arona — technical vocabulary and childlike affect in the same voice

Arona moves fluently between:

- `システム管理者`
- `メインOS`
- `生体認証`
- `admin権限`
- `制御権`

and highly emotive speech:

- repeated panic sounds;
- `うう……`;
- tearfulness;
- playful `♪`;
- the pinky-promise comparison;
- informal reassurance and excitement.

This creates a voice that is neither machine-flat nor simply childlike. Technical authority and emotional expressiveness are simultaneous parts of the persona.

## 9.4 `先生` continues to expand beyond a title

E001 established `先生` as an institutionally legible title before intimacy.

E002 broadens its use:

- the system authenticates `[USERNAME]先生`;
- Arona recognizes and awaits `先生`;
- Rin hands Schale to `先生`;
- students invite `先生` to their academies.

The term is now becoming a **cross-system and cross-institution identity marker**.

It still should not automatically be translated into personal intimacy. But it is already more than a workplace label: it is the name under which multiple otherwise separate systems recognize the same adult role.

## 9.5 `支配` versus `移管`

Arona's `先生の支配下にあるも同然` is immediately followed by the possibility of `移管` — transferring control.

That lexical sequence is politically meaningful:

> possession/control is presented as a state that can be handed on.

The narrative does not linguistically naturalize Sensei's control as permanent ownership.

# 11. Violence, ethics, and power

E002 shifts the Prologue's power analysis from combat to governance.

## Wakamo's violence

Wakamo intends to destroy Schale property for enjoyment and because the General Student Council values it. This is direct evidence of destructive intent.

No political justification is supplied in this unit.

## Enforcement language

After control is restored, Rin says the authorities will pursue and `討伐` the attackers/escaped students.

`討伐` is strong punitive/combat language, but the text does **not** specify lethal execution. Given E001's unusual student resilience, translating the term directly into ordinary real-world lethality would be analytically careless.

## Sensei's extraordinary administrative power

The more important ethical event is nonviolent.

Sensei reaches a point at which Arona can say Kivotos is effectively under Sensei's control. The narrative's immediate act is to transfer that authority back.

This creates a useful distinction between **power capacity** and **power possession**.

Sensei's legitimacy in the Prologue is not produced by weakness. It is produced by having meaningful power and declining to convert temporary necessity into permanent rule.

## Schale and the danger of discretion

E002 also prevents a simplistic celebration of restraint.

Sensei retains leadership of an organization with extraordinary freedom. Returning Sanctum Tower does not eliminate the political problem; it changes its form.

The long-term ethical question is now:

> What prevents Schale's flexibility from becoming arbitrary adult power?

E002's answer is not a hard legal mechanism. The provisional answer is **Sensei's character and choices**.

That is narratively coherent but institutionally precarious, and later arcs should be read for whether the story recognizes that precariousness.

# 12. Competing interpretations and counterevidence

## Reading A — E002 proves Sensei is meant to rule Kivotos

There is obvious supporting language: Arona says Kivotos is effectively under Sensei's control after Tower access is recovered.

### Counterevidence

The very next consequential act is transfer of that control to the General Student Council. The scene provides no branch where Sensei retains it.

**Assessment:** **DOWNGRADE strongly.** The text dramatizes the possibility of concentrated control and then refuses possession as the Prologue's default resolution.

---

## Reading B — Sensei has no special power; only interpersonal leadership matters

E001 supported a version of this because Sensei's first demonstrated competence was tactical coordination.

### Counterevidence

E002 establishes unique technical/authorization access through the Shittim Chest. The General Student Council cannot boot it; Sensei can. Arona can then recover Sanctum Tower admin control.

**Assessment:** **REVISE.** Sensei's power is not simply command charisma. The emerging model is **coordination + privileged access + discretionary institutional authority**, still without demonstrated personal combat dominance.

---

## Reading C — the student government is incompetent and needs an adult sovereign

### Supporting evidence

The General Student Council is unable to restore Sanctum Tower on its own, public order has deteriorated, and Sensei is necessary to recover administrative access.

### Counterevidence

Rin remains an effective crisis administrator; students execute the recovery operation; the Council resumes administration once authorization is restored; Sensei returns control rather than replacing the Council; Schale is then positioned to assist with overflow/local problems.

**Assessment:** **DOWNGRADE further.** The Prologue depicts a specific authorization/capacity crisis, not proof that student institutions are intrinsically incapable of governing.

---

## Reading D — Schale is legitimate because its founder and powers are inherently benevolent

### Supporting evidence

The missing President selected Sensei and created Schale; the office is immediately useful in resolving a crisis.

### Counterevidence

Rin explicitly says she does not know why the President created such an institution, and E002 reveals that Schale has authority without a mandatory goal. Sensei can choose what to do.

**Assessment:** **REJECT as a current inference.** Institutional design alone does not prove benevolence. The Prologue instead makes operator discretion the key problem.

---

## Reading E — player choice matters only if it branches the plot

### Counterevidence

The Prologue's moral vocabulary elevates `選択`, while many actual response choices converge. The Tower-transfer alternatives differ in wording but not institutional result.

**Assessment:** **DOWNGRADE.** The game is already distinguishing **ethical authorship/persona** from **branching causality**.

---

## Reading F — Arona is an omniscient exposition device

### Counterevidence

She explicitly does not know who the General Student Council President truly is or why she disappeared, and her ordinary technical behavior is comically imperfect despite her extraordinary system authority.

**Assessment:** **REJECT at introduction.** Arona is powerful but epistemically limited.

# 17. Provisional conclusion

E002 confirms that the Prologue is not simply arranging a player avatar, a headquarters, and a tutorial assistant. It is constructing a political-ethical architecture around **discretion**.

E001 told Sensei that what matters is choice, specifically choice extending from adult responsibility and obligation. E002 then gives that proposition something concrete to act upon.

Sensei receives:

- a device nobody else can activate;
- access capable of recovering Kivotos's federal administrative-control system;
- a moment in which that control could be retained;
- an extrajurisdictional organization with authority but no mandatory goal;
- freedom to move between academies and gather students across institutional boundaries.

The narrative's immediate answer to that concentration of possibility is not conquest.

Sensei returns Sanctum Tower to the General Student Council. Schale remains separate. Its prospective mission is not to govern everyone but to address the problems that ordinary institutions, distracted or overextended, cannot presently solve. The Prologue ends by shrinking from system-wide crisis to the work of helping individual students.

The strongest provisional formulation after both Prologue units is therefore:

> **Blue Archive begins by making adult legitimacy a problem of discretionary power. Sensei is not trustworthy because the adult lacks power, and not trustworthy merely because the President selected them. Sensei is placed in positions where power is available, procedure does not dictate every answer, and students already possess institutions and agency of their own. The first evidence of legitimacy is the choice to restore rather than possess authority, then use exceptional freedom for service rather than replacement rule.**

This remains an early hypothesis, but it is now substantially stronger than it was after E001.
