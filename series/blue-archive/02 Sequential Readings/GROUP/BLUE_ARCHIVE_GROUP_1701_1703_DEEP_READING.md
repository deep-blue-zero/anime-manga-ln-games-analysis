## 4. Patient pleasure and differences within care

Hanae arrives loudly asking the sick to reply; Serina asks for quiet in a ward of unwell people. Hanae invokes cheerful speech/song and smiling as ways to improve health. Patients A/B say she makes admission less boring and gives them energy; Serina welcomes their response (`1701 scene:001:u:0002-0009`). Those are subjective comfort reports and a caregiver's optimistic theory, not clinical proof that smiles cure every illness. Serina's quiet norm and Hanae's sociability are different accommodations to recipients, and both receive a voice.

Serina notices Shiroyagi's uneaten bread and offers assisted feeding, arguing for nutrition. Other patients ask for the same affection; she refuses indiscriminate indulgence and threatens to tell the head nurse. Hanae offers to feed people with injured hands (`u:0010-0017`). Actual feeding completion for every claimant is not shown. The scene nevertheless depicts attention to appetite, need and a boundary between help and being indulged. The refusal is meaningful ordinary care evidence rather than an obstacle to a total sweetness profile.

Patients call the students angels for weekly voluntary visits and predict any evil person would become docile before them (`u:0018-0020`). Praise is patient rhetoric. In the lobby, Serina reports steady recovery, Hanae says the kindly treated visit was fun, and the head nurse reports improved mood and fewer complaints (`scene:002:u:0002-0007`). Their enjoyment of volunteering is affirmative pleasure. None supplies audited outcome causality or a license to assume every patient is satisfied.

Serina notices unused fourth-floor rooms on the lobby guide. The nurse explains patients feared a bandaged ghost at midnight and were relocated, while saying she does not believe in a real ghost (`u:0008-0017`). Fear's institutional cost is a reported unavailable floor, not a proved metaphysical danger. Serina wants to avoid it and argues they treat the living rather than exorcise spirits. Care and inquiry motivate the plan; Serina's reluctant agreement does not make her unafraid or prove an exhaustive investigation method.

## 6. Compassion betrayal and injury

In 1703, the students accept that the apparition is not a real ghost. The intruder admits attempted theft but then alleges the motive was recovering a father's lost necklace from last year's surgery, with access denied by the hospital. A seam-marked compassionate response precedes Hanae's clean search offer; Serina says anyone suffering deserves help but forbids touching other valuables (`scene:001:u:0002-0018`). This is conditional assistance with a real boundary. It is also acceptance of an uncorroborated explanation, distinct from knowing deception.

They lower a heavy metal box; the intruder seizes it, says the father's relic never existed and admits stealing for leisure money. The actor explicitly rejects an obligation to act honestly and blames the deceived (`u:0019-0030`). Earlier reported bereavement is thus reclassified as fabrication at this local reveal. It should not become a true grief explanation in a longitudinal subject model. Theft and exploitation of good will are shown without proving all other patients' praise insincere.

Hanae says the box was a high-pressure sterilizer, not a safe, and warns its forced opening would explode. A written explosion and cry follow; Serina tells her to share such information sooner because an injured person has been added (`u:0031-0041`). Device workings are Hanae's fictional explanation, not real engineering guidance or an inspected incident report. The timing means knowledge was not shared before the theft. It does not prove Hanae intentionally set a trap, caused the opening herself or planned injury from the beginning.

Next morning patients complain that night gunfire and an explosion prevented sleep; one asks whether the volunteers saw anything during their reported night duty. Serina denies approaching the fourth floor because of fear and denies knowing about a bomb (`scene:002:u:0002-0010`). This answer conflicts with the now-shown exploration/explosion. Audience knowledge is stronger than these patients' information. Patient B calls her fear cute, retaining affection without learning the cause of disturbance. Their warmth is real as an expressed response, but not informed approval of everything the students did.

The intruder is now an inpatient. Patients envy the apparent quick friendship and ask about the ghost nickname. Hanae starts to reveal the performance; the raw-supported interruption by the intruder gives another explanation (`u:0011-0019`, `u:0018` seam retained). Hanae/Serina say they come wherever patients are and mean to treat, not stalk. Serina explicitly says their mistake caused injury and they want to help recovery; she adds that the injection will sting a little and asks the patient to prepare. The actor begs to avoid pain and apologizes (`u:0020-0026`). An injection is threatened/prepared in wording, not shown administered, medically necessary, abusive or completed. Ominous framing and the patient's fear make retaliation a live counterreading, while the explicit acknowledgment and offer of care prevent a definitive revenge-treatment verdict.

The early boast that every evil person becomes a puppy returns in a frightened, apologetic new patient, through a route unlike innocent angelic comfort. This is literary irony, not proof that coercion cures moral character. The floor's reopening, stolen objects' security, formal disciplinary outcome, complete medical recovery and patient-informed repair are not shown.
