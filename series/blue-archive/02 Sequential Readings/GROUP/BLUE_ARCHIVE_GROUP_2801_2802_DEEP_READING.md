## 3. A fictional opening inside a real petition problem — GROUP2801

A stop interrupts it. Scene002 then explicitly identifies the passage as Mel's embellished writing, produced after Nodoka asked for a record of the old building's conditions (`s2:u0002–u0010`).

The initial affectionate lines therefore **cannot enter relationship or character ledgers as observed Shigure/Nodoka conduct**. They are an in-story creative representation. The outer scene supplies Nodoka's report of cold, hunger and unsuitable living conditions, not independent confirmation of each dramatized gathering, cup of cocoa or intimate exchange. Reading the full object changes the authority of its own opening; isolated-scene extraction would produce a false relationship finding.

This reclassification does not discard the passage's analytical value. It shows what Mel imagines will interest a reader, what Nodoka refuses to have attributed to herself, and what Shigure can enjoy as writing. Those are different evidential functions.

### 3.2 Taste and commissioned purpose are different criteria

It is not consent to Mel's invented intimacy or evidence that exile is comfortable.

Nodoka fears officials could read the draft as proof they are enjoying themselves and extend suspension (`u0015`). This is her prediction, not an actual decision yet. She asks for facts and stronger expression of complaint. Mel produces a deprivation opening that Nodoka first welcomes, then diverts into another unwanted romantic invention (`u0018–u0029`). The second story is explicitly unfamiliar to Nodoka; it cannot establish an actual candy-sharing incident or intimate act.

Nodoka terminates the commission, Mel defends her anticipated literary success and Shigure regrets losing an enjoyable work (`u0030–u0036`). Aesthetic enjoyment is meaningful while unsuited to the requested advocacy. The distinction does not require declaring Mel's tastes worthless or requiring every story to serve administrative goals.
