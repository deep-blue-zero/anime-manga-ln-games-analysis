## 5. Ordinary value, relationships and contrary evidence

The sequence's mechanism is not “professionals always solve mundane life.” Professionally learned skills coexist with selective food enthusiasm, tiredness, bodily discomfort and distracting banter. Rations move from spoils to a shared promise, from a disliked remainder to a public-risk object, and finally to an embarrassing collective consequence. The object changes function; it is never merely a badge of crisis competency.

Saki's pre-admission tasting, post-watch breakfast and dislike of a menu deserve distinct entries. Miyu is neither only a helpless fear figure nor only a sniper: she lands a shot, distinguishes escaped enemies, notices food, expresses pleasure, keeps watch despite fear, accepts relief and voices solidarity. Miyako coordinates both combat and breakfast, considers outsiders and promises support, while her authority does not guarantee a successful household operation. Moe's equipment appetite and playful affinity coexist with preparation for the task and a fascination with destructive outcomes; no actual malicious intent against peers follows.

Directed relations are specific. Saki takes Miyu's duty; Miyu thanks Saki. Miyako lets Saki choose first after that labor. Moe seeks similarity with Saki, who resists her characterization. Miyako entrusts disposal to Saki while doubting Moe; Miyu offers shared fate. These are not interchangeable statements of universal group harmony. Neither the care nor the failure should overwrite the other.

**Counterreadings:** the first raid really displays operational capacity and a cooperative aftermath plan, despite no legal audit. The duty transfer really helps Miyu despite dismissive explanation. Miyako's concern for outsiders is explicit despite the unsuccessful result. Moe's tease need not be a accurate biography of Saki. The last comic disaster need not prove SRT training worthless or RABBIT broadly dangerous; a particular attempt fails in the narrated circumstances.
