## 3. Waiting, feeding and the limits of affectionate teasing — GROUP2001

### 3.1 The desired pastry and the valued company are different goods

`BA:group:2001:s1:u0001–u0024` opens with Yoshimi restless, Kazusa irritated by her movement and Airi trying to hold the meeting together. The scarce dacquoise matters: the shop reportedly opens at two, makes only two hundred daily and limits purchases to one per person. The queue makes disappointment plausible. It does not establish its actual headcount or prove the club cannot buy anything. At this opening point, Kazusa explicitly says that the agreed meeting time is one thirty and that thirty minutes remain. Yoshimi's early impatience therefore cannot be treated as an already established breach by Natsu.

Kazusa's “earthquake” response and Yoshimi's complaint that Kazusa's supposedly surprised face does not change turn waiting into a small comic dispute. These written reactions give texture to the relationship: Yoshimi wants her dissatisfaction received expressively; Kazusa wants relief from her repetitive complaint and bodily disturbance. Neither position requires a high stakes justification. The facial complaint is a character's assessment in text, not an observation of performed voice or exact animated affect.

Airi offers a snack and argues that Natsu may have a reason for not arriving (`u0025–u0035`). She then gives the clearest independent statement of value: although she wanted the pastry, “こうしてみんなで集まって、お話してるだけでもすごく楽しい” (`u0032`). The gathering does not need to achieve its purchasing aim to be worthwhile to her. Yoshimi's delighted description of the offered sweet and willingness to accept a disappointing day preserve another ordinary good: a particular pleasure can change how waiting feels without solving the original scarcity. Do not convert Airi's willingness into a universal requirement that the others enjoy missing out.

### 3.2 A feeding offer occurs inside an already contested exchange

Kazusa calls Yoshimi simple; Yoshimi answers with a childish imitation suggesting Kazusa wants attention (`u0036–u0044`). Kazusa twice asks her to stop, and Yoshimi imitates the objection itself. Airi mediates and offers to feed Kazusa a cream puff (`u0045`). Affection and boundary violation coexist in this sequence. The scene supports reciprocal familiarity and Airi's practical soothing, while Kazusa's repeated refusals prevent treating all imitation as mutually welcomed intimacy.

The two responses after the feeding offer (`u0046–u0047`) are canonically labelled Airi, but their raw text-bearing commands name Kazusa; `u0049` has the same kind of display/text difference. Section 5 retains the exact receipts. The local sequence suggests Kazusa's hesitant response, but the source-facing record must not silently relabel the canonical witness or claim a securely attributed consent statement. No completed swallowing is established.

Gunfire interrupts the offer. Airi says the bullet hit the cream puff neatly (`u0048–u0052`), and two source-local students accuse each other of cutting into the queue (`u0054–u0055`). The object of hospitality is actually damaged. The source does not allocate the cream-puff shot to a securely identified shooter or establish a planned attack on Kazusa. “生徒A” and “生徒B” are local roles with empty person joins; they are not automatically students using the same letters in other stories.

### 3.3 Familiarity does not erase humiliation or its costs

Yoshimi calls the quarrel common and proposes ignoring it; Airi also treats continuing the outing as possible (`u0056–u0059`). This normalization is the actors' local stance. It cannot become a finding that every Trinity outing is violent or that such danger never matters. Kazusa has cream on her face; Yoshimi's laughter, comparisons to art and later disguise jokes make the accidental embarrassment a new shared entertainment (`u0060–u0079`).

Airi initially says it is not funny, then laughs and apologizes, explaining that the laughter came from her throat. This is evidence of a lapse in mediation, not an absence of affection. Kazusa's request for a mirror and anger have an immediate context: prior requests to stop imitation were ignored, food was ruined and her appearance became a joke. Reducing the sequence to an uncaused “short temper” would discard precisely the interaction needed to interpret it.

Yoshimi reports Kazusa charging into the queue with a gun (`u0075–u0079`), followed by gunfire and breaking sounds (`u0082`). The narrated ending says that, after the usual scene, they all went home as if nothing happened (`u0083`). It does not settle each shot, injury, compensation, disciplinary outcome or successful purchase. The closing account says that Natsu apparently overslept again (`u0084`, “らしい”): retain that reported qualification. Natsu speaks no line in this object. Her absence and the narrator's explanation do not warrant a complete laziness model.

## 4. Holiday plans, a real rumor and a costly rescue — GROUP2002

### 4.1 Doing nothing is a substantive chosen activity

The narrator identifies the club walking home beside the river (`BA:group:2002:s1:u0001`). Discussion of an approaching four-day holiday distinguishes several desired lives. Kazusa wants to do nothing; Airi has lessons and study; Yoshimi has no fixed plan and considers touring sweets shops (`u0002–u0009`). Kazusa later insists that doing nothing is itself her plan (`u0054–u0055`). Her leisure is therefore not an analytical blank or an obligation to be filled by somebody else's adventure.

Natsu wants something sweet, adopts a dramatic pause, and calls for stimulation, romance and adventure (`u0010–u0039`). Yoshimi is open to another sweet stop; Airi critiques the expression “もう一軒” and then uses it herself (`u0033–u0034`). That small verbal uptake matters as peer play, without requiring a major consequence. Natsu's claim that teenagers cannot eat too much sugar (`u0018`) is a fictional speaker's assertion; it supplies neither nutritional authority nor a source-independent conclusion.

Her proposed legendary sweet also has distinct value. She wants the experience of seeking it, not merely a cheaper or nearer purchase. The others can decline that form of leisure without disproving that it matters to her. Kazusa's immediate dismissal and the subsequent objections preserve differences among peers rather than an undifferentiated club preference.

### 4.2 Refusal survives both a true rumor and theatrical persuasion

Natsu reports a cave outside Trinity that remains below freezing and produces maple-syrup icicles, citing the mystery corner of “月刊スイーツと機関銃” (`u0040–u0049`). Yoshimi treats it as almost certainly false. At `u0046`, the canonical Natsu label and raw Yoshimi text-bearing command differ; do not make a stable self-questioning trait from the derived attribution.

The friends refuse to spend their holiday on it (`u0050–u0066`). Natsu's rhetoric about wasting youth and Kazusa's suspicion that she enjoys being corrected do not establish a general theory of her inner motive. Her desire for adventure is explicit; delight in being opposed is Kazusa's interpretation.

Natsu then performs sadness, apology and sniffles. Airi opens the possibility of reconsidering; Yoshimi stops the appeal by citing repeated previous versions; Natsu drops the act with “だめかぁ、残念” (`u0067–u0078`). This episode supports identifying this attempted persuasion as theatrical. It does not prove that every future tear is false. The laughter at `u0077` has a Yoshimi display label but Airi text-bearing command, so a voice-specific pattern cannot depend on the canonical label alone.

The club disperses (`u0079–u0083`). The refused outing has not become a collectively accepted plan. The ending later confirms that the maple icicles actually exist (`u0108`). Retain this contrary evidence: Yoshimi's skepticism was factually wrong about existence. That error does not make every mystery-column claim reliable, prove the excursion safe or invalidate her right to choose another holiday.

### 4.3 Rescue does not retroactively authorize the excursion

The same structured scene moves to “その日の夜” (`u0084`); narrative time changes without a second source scene. Airi alerts Yoshimi and Kazusa. Communication-mode Natsu reports that she went alone, hoped to get photographs, became lost in the dark and needs help (`u0085–u0102`). The call directly presents her plea and report; it does not visually show every part of her solitary trip. The later narration confirms the cave and the rescue.

Airi says they will of course go, Yoshimi agrees with resignation, and the narrator states that the three friends depart to rescue Natsu (`u0105–u0107`). Refusing an unwanted recreational expedition and helping a stranded friend are different decisions. The latter does not prove that the first refusal was insincere, nor that the club owes participation in any future adventure. Natsu's nighttime tears must remain distinct from her earlier admitted performance.

The narrated result is deliberately mixed: the icicles are real, Natsu is rescued, all eat them and get upset stomachs, and the holiday is spoiled (`u0108–u0109`). Rescue succeeded; the experience still imposed a collective cost. No pathogen, nutritional mechanism, clinical diagnosis, treatment or completed recovery is supplied. The story supports friendship expressed through inconvenient help, with no need to redefine the original danger or illness as harmless.
