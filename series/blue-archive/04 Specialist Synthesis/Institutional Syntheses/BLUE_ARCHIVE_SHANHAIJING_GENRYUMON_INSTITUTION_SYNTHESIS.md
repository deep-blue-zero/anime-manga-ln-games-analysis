## 1. Core thesis: tradition becomes governance through disputed allocation

**GI001.** Shanhaijing's central institutional problem is who may define what a valuable tradition requires of someone else. The work supplies several answers: protect the weak, preserve procedure, retain artistic purposes, maintain a material livelihood, defend students and uphold an office. These answers can cooperate, but none automatically speaks for every resident. Genryumon's head can stop ideological accusation while maintaining surveillance; a procedural critic can refuse personal power while practicing coercion; commercial hospitality can provide genuine pleasure and press a full recipient to continue. The institution is therefore neither one benevolent person's essence nor a uniform apparatus of oppression. It is a contested distribution of decisions whose different recipients and unfinished consequences remain legible. [A02:L087–089; A03:L084/L128; A04:L111/L119–124]

**GI002.** Tradition has positive content before it becomes an argument about crisis. Theatre can move a visiting audience; lanterns, fireworks, foods, clothes and pandas are things people genuinely want to encounter. Rumi values friends talking over a single dish, rather than simply maximizing table income. The danger lies in turning those goods into obligations whose alleged beneficiary cannot answer: guests interrogated in the name of welcome, unlimited culinary foresight demanded in the name of loyalty, or people assigned office and work through someone else's imagined improvement. Ordinary pleasure supplies the objects governance ought to make available, and also the particular limits that test its means. [A02:L079–081; A03:L090–106; A04:L084/L093; A05:L078–080]

**GI003.** Authority can make useful decisions without resolving its own legitimacy. Kisaki's rejection of premature accusation, actual apology, refusal of a coercive cure and opening of the festival are consequential positive acts. Her acknowledged shortcut expulsion and record erasure, failed staff communication, continuing secrecy and unchosen office remain consequential contrary evidence. Treating the good acts as proof of complete just government would erase the procedure and recipients that make the argument difficult. Treating every good as counterfeit because the system is imperfect would erase achieved meals, refuge, apology, festivities and defense. This synthesis keeps both sides and asks what an action actually changes. [A02:L087/L141–149; A03:L090–094; A04:L104/L116/L126–130]
