## 2. Whole literary institutional thesis

School Lunch's recurring problem is a mismatch between the social confidence placed in feeding and the arrangements necessary to make feeding answerable. The sources show skill, effort, appetite, affection and even completed good food. They also show missing oil and equipment, unconsulted intervention, pressured cooking, blocked warning, personal hospitality beyond an inspection's criteria, harmful results and continuing work with unprinted repair. The institution works when particular people coordinate material preparation and acknowledge one another's limits. It becomes coercive or unreliable when a desire for food, a claim of rescue, a good intention or an encouraging evaluation is allowed to stand in for that coordination. This is an interpretive mechanism supported by the scenes, not a formal behavioral model or a claim that every School Lunch interaction follows one rule.

Five questions therefore remain distinct throughout the study: was food actually made; did someone want to undertake that work; what could the recipient know and refuse; what was the observed result for this recipient; and what work, damage or obligation was subsequently repaired? GROUP1104 brings oil and willing helpers without printing a finished meal. GROUP1602 passes hygiene criteria before a harmful offered drink and a later F rating. EVENT844 supplies actually eaten and appreciated pancakes after instruction and explicit permission to decline, while time pressure and institutional undervaluation remain. None is a substitute answer to all five questions. Their differences make a richer account than either “School Lunch always fails” or “sincere cooking makes everything care.” [G11 §6; G16 §5; E844 §5.]

The positive ends are intrinsic. Fuuka's soba conversation, amusement, ingredients negotiation, chosen clothes and wishes for peace do not need to improve a military operation to matter. Juri's café pleasure, gardening, trophy, unexpectedly edible parfait, desired service and renewed attempts matter without proving permanent competence. The completed cookie tasting in MINI06020 is valuable as taste and cute making even though captivity closes the encounter badly. A synthesis that retained only coercion or safety failure would erase precisely the ordinary life whose conditions the institution is supposed to support. Pleasant food is an achieved good at its printed extent; it is not evidence that an associated burden has been consented to or repaired. [F-CHK P01/P04; J-CHK R02–R04; MINI §8.]

The institution is also larger than either worker's temperament. Fuuka's competence cannot make four thousand eggs, one pan, absent oil and a two-hour deadline a practicable arrangement merely by moral expectation. Juri's desire to feed people cannot make Fuuka permanently requisitionable, or remove the need to test and inform before serving. Consumers are neither a unanimous audience nor passive measurements: one person accepts boiled eggs, another requests the unwanted serving, another defends the promised menu; some New Year customers welcome a dish others dislike. Thus distributive responsibility includes multiple ordinary tastes and budgets, rather than a single ideal customer whose delight certifies the institution. [G11 1104:u0001–0024; E820 007/014; E844 010:scene003.]

The strongest synthesis is consequently neither a story of inevitable progress nor a permanent pathology. There are actual local revisions: a renewed request after apology, a bounded agreement, a learned cooking accomplishment, a refusal option, a decision to preserve another person's day off. There are repeated failures and contrary acts as well. Those changes can be interpreted within their sequences. Unanchored group, private and miniature stories cannot be placed after a particular apology to manufacture either reform or relapse. The repertoire is coherent through its continuing questions of work and recipient agency even where a global chronology is unavailable.

## 3. Instituted roles and unequal responsibilities

Meal provision makes Fuuka responsible to a timetable and many eaters, without giving every eater authority over her person or every kitchen action. GROUP1601 opens three hours before service. Izumi wants cookies now and offers soup as an alternative; Fuuka insists that lunch must wait for its time. The institutional issue is distribution, not proof that appetite is shameful or that soup is intrinsically unacceptable. Someone who prepares communal food must preserve its availability for other recipients. This locally asserted boundary is practical authority grounded in a task. It is not a complete legal entitlement, and the study does not need one to recognize her refusal as meaningful. [G16 1601:u0001–0028.]

Juri is a working participant rather than an interchangeable deficit in Fuuka's capacity. She helps with capture and calming, knows ingredients and order, wants to teach visitors, values serving, fetches vegetables and asks about potato preparation. Some of those acts are successful and some belong to interrupted or failed cooking. EVENT844's later Fuuka statement that School Lunch cannot operate without Juri recognizes operational dependence; Juri hears both appreciation and a painful implication about her cooking role. Their differentiated labor is therefore visible without pretending that every division is agreed, measured or settled. The institution must preserve the contribution of practical assistance alongside the person's wish to make food herself. [G16 §4; M020 P03; J-CHK R04; E844 010:scene002–003.]

Consumers have their own responsibilities and rights. Izumi's delight in juice does not make her a qualified assessor of its effect on Iori. Haruna's menu complaint can be a legitimate stated expectation while her ensuing kitchen interference disregards the provider's warning. Junko's willingness to help does not guarantee coordinated help. Haruna's useful oil and Akari's appeal to friendship do not resolve who may move what at the stove. Describing these differences avoids giving either cooks or customers a monopoly on the meaning of food. Good taste, promised menus, hunger, preparation conditions and actual recipient safety must be negotiated in the same social space, although they remain different tests. [G11 §6; G16 §5.]

Iori is a substituted evaluator in GROUP1601–1602. Her threatening entrance pressures the workers, while her subsequent checklist response genuinely recognizes the clean hands, equipment, furniture and ingredients presented. The task she states is checking hygiene, not inventing faults. That local fairness matters, and Juri explicitly praises it. It does not establish the full mandate of substituted inspectors or a scientific food-safety audit. The later incident supplies new evidence and a new rating. Institutional authority here is situated and revisable; neither her first menace nor her first praise exhausts it. [G16 1601:u0041–0073;1602:u0001–0059/u0092.]

Helpers and outside employers create another asymmetry. The café manager can praise Juri's service and propose permanent work, which she declines, yet later pressure her to cook after the chef's separate accident despite her stated inability. Offering to bring Fuuka for higher pay does not solve that pressure or authorize it. Juri elects to try partly to protect Fuuka's rest, making the act meaningful without making its conditions uncomplicated freedom. Likewise, a Gourmet rescue project can listen to a proprietor and later ask Fuuka properly while beginning with her capture. The institution's question is who can turn a request into another person's obligation, not whether the requesting actor ever has a generous motive. [E844 001:scene002–004; E820 002/003/012.]

Sensei's role varies. No teacher participates in G16's inspection, so the adults' absence cannot be filled with an imagined regulating lesson. Gift039 shows an invited shared table and gratitude; gift040 shows emotional validation alongside dismissed risk and a harmful local result. EVENT844 includes encouragement of rest, objection to patient treatment and temporary operational command, followed by accepted food after an explicit refusal permission. MAIN shows both confinement and later coalition work. These are different relations to institutional authority; one warm adult encounter cannot make the cooks' labor generally available or resolve another consumer's uninformed position. [G16 §6; gifts039/040; E844 §4/§5; MAIN C002.]

## 4. Resources, labor and actual constraints

GROUP1104 makes resource limits a voiced account, not mere scenery. Fuuka says she cannot fry four thousand eggs alone in two hours; Juri is absent, oil is unavailable and there is one pan. Those quantities and shortages are her reports, not independently audited inventories. Their narrative force is nevertheless clear: a menu expectation meets an arrangement the responsible worker describes as impossible. Haruna anticipates the problem and brings actual oil; the friends volunteer actual help. The ingredients and intentions change, but the institutional relation fails at Fuuka's interrupted warning to ask before touching. This is why resource supplementation cannot be equated with a completed remedy. [G11 1104:u0009–0024.]

The ending is a work outcome in its own right. Oil, eggs, flour, falls and crashes leave the kitchen in disorder and Fuuka silent. Exact intermediate choreography is uncertain; no particular injury, intention to attack or completed restitution should be invented. What is absent is equally specific: no prepared replacement meal, cleanup, apology, cost assessment or repaired work arrangement is printed. Silence supports the interrupted conclusion, not a diagnosis or a permanent attitude to the Society. The act of volunteering may be sincere. Its failure demonstrates how the provider's practical knowledge can be overridden by help that never gives her effective control over its execution. [G11 1104:u0025–0049; §8/§9.]

The club-trial MINI02020 gives a parallel constraint at a different scope. Fuuka discusses dinner preparation and the missing food truck. Juri offers a tour and instruction; curiosity and hospitality are real, but the unusual omelette and retreat interrupt the lesson. Japanese thousands of portions and Korean a few thousand each day remain distinct reported quantities. No repaired truck, completed edible meal, successful training program or general relief follows. This episode matters because teaching is a wanted institutional function, rather than merely spare capacity: visitors and workers have reasons to value learning even when the setting prevents it. [M020 P03; current MINI §3.]

EVENT820 widens resource responsibility from the cafeteria to a threatened restaurant and suppliers. The proprietor describes franchise prices, rent and inherited place; merchants refuse or explain stock; company personnel speak of job dependence and handling food. These are situated testimonies. They support a conflict over material access and livelihoods without an audited contract, stock census or payroll record. Fuuka's demonstrated preparation knowledge and Juri's ingredient interest operate within those constraints. Affordable menu proposals revise Haruna's initial neglect of price, while customers' budgets remain actual local preferences, not a statistical population. [E820 003/004/007–009.]

The contest provides real work and a finite win without a full resource settlement. Fuuka initially objects to forced participation, then Haruna apologizes and asks again; Fuuka agrees. Reported damaged vegetables, meat, bottles and deliveries qualify supply success. Japanese less-than-half usable vegetables differs from Korean about one third. Particular preparations are done—Japanese pickling is complete where Korean drying is current, and shrimp and carrots have their own endpoints. The announced hundred boxes and four hundred servings, as well as Juri's daily-volume figure, remain attributed statements rather than certified output capacity. Announced victory and thanks do not print paid overtime, continued employment, final ownership or every supplier remedy. [E820 012–014.]

EVENT844 likewise refuses a simple equation between accomplishment and relief. A rare day off is interrupted; after several days School Lunch is again preparing food and receives varied household-style complaints. Fuuka states that time and recognition remain insufficient. Juri's actual shared pancakes supply a local success, not a staffing increase or a recovered holiday. Recognition can be necessary without being adequate: appreciation does not itself redistribute work, provide equipment, pay a worker, or complete the time someone lost. The study does not infer that compensation is impossible; it records that these sources do not show it. [E844 001/002/010.]

The military and emergency comparators sharpen this limit. In MAIN C002, Fuuka refuses an impossible three-day whole-school ration demand and plans to slow supply; reported power does not turn infeasibility into competence. In Final C004 E003–004, her pressured driving contributes to an actual arrival. Successful public action demonstrates a particular role, while the argument explicitly preserves the original boarding's lack of retrospective consent. No kitchen capacity, general future vehicle availability or labor settlement follows from the terminal operation's success. These episodes show institutional usefulness under pressure, not the exhaustion of the worker's independent purposes. [MAIN C002 E011–015 checkpoint; Final004:003:u0053–0064/004:u0011–0013.]

## 5. Ordinary wants and private ends

Fuuka's private repertoire constrains an institutional interpretation from within. The produce negotiation ends in buying and returning ingredients; an arrangement for later remains conditional. Soba eating and quiet conversation, clothing nerves, amusement, home accompaniment and wishes for peace are positively represented parts of her life. Her practical care does not require every pleasure to be a recovery technique or an efficiency investment. A provider can want food, time and company as a recipient. The institution's feeding purpose would be impoverished if the cook's own enjoyment were treated as irrelevant whenever it does not change public throughput. [F-CHK P01/P04; F-CMP Q01.]

Her limitations are equally ordinary and meaningful. An invitation can be qualified by fatigue; a not-teasing request and a confidentiality request retain their own authority. A replacement joke is refused; later compliance with secrecy is unprinted. Care improves locally when it asks the recipient's preferences, without becoming a promise of daily service. Written holiday or shop lines remain contextual possibilities, not a calendar of fulfilled outings. Clothes, hair, embarrassment and desired company do not warrant an involuntary public image or a settled romance. They show that chosen presentation and reception belong in the account of a worker who is often treated chiefly as a needed cook. [F-CMP Q03/Q04; F-CHK P03–P06.]

Juri's pleasure is not cancelled by repeated failure. A café order and conversation give enjoyment and recipe curiosity. Her actual contest win, trophy and shared return oppose a blanket incapacity claim; actual eating and appraisal of a parfait oppose the idea that no food she makes ever works. The award is a represented organizer result, not a tested general algorithm or professional ranking. An unexplained successful taste is real without explaining why every other attempt failed. Desire to learn, embarrassment about publicity, customer-violence refusal, a part-time uniform, garden pleasure, particular hand warmth and invitations have value whether or not they produce institutional reform. [J-CHK R02/R03; J-CMP Q01/Q02/Q04.]

Gardening is a modest practical and wanted activity, not a departure from cooking. Juri's supply and budget accounts, sun-care preparation, Fuuka's encouragement and locally useful cultivation broaden what a participant can contribute. The later wait and tentative or withdrawn explanation do not certify illness, a cure, a permanent disappearance or abandoned cooking. Her future weaker-sun invitation remains future. This is a serious contrary case to the assertion that only culinary success can give her a place in School Lunch. It is not sufficient evidence that cultivation solves the club's procurement problem or gives her independent control of its budget. [J-CHK R04/R05; J-CMP Q05.]

EVENT844 turns ordinary choices into the event's first substantive asymmetry: Fuuka values a day free from service, while Juri chooses service because it resembles the happiness of people enjoying her cooking. Fuuka first suggests rest, then supports Juri's different preference; Juri wants Fuuka's free day protected. They do not have to want the same activity to care for one another. A coerced-work-only account would lose Juri's actual choice and skill as a waitress; a work-as-love account would make Fuuka's day off disposable. The sources make both goods intelligible before the accident, and the interruption does not retrospectively make either preference a mistake. [E844 001:scene001–002.]

Gifts039 and040 keep private making and reception distinct from payment for service. Fuuka chooses to bring a meal, accepts shared eating, offers handmade cocoa and receives a narrated warm response. She denies special intention in matched cups; storage, container return and hoped-for sleep are not printed outcomes. Juri's first tasty chocolate is bought, while a handmade batch is withheld because she reports earlier tasters collapsing. The fallback preserves agency and a wish to give without harming. Adult validation and eventual tasting lead to fading consciousness, so emotional appreciation is neither meaningless nor a safety certificate. A gift cannot purchase the giver's subsequent labor or make every recipient objection void. [gift039:u0031–0052; gift040:scene002:u0005–0034; current E810 §6.]

Finally, wanted food is plural. Junko's successful New Year meal, Akari's boiled-egg preference, a visitor's price limit, ordinary menu complaints, Mine's cute-cookie pleasure and Fuuka's desire to help hungry people across schools all matter. They cannot be collapsed into a single superior taste. In MINI06020, food and cute craft achieve pleasure before the final restraint. Keeping that accomplishment and the adverse ending together protects ordinary evidence from both utility reduction and sentimental acquittal. The cook's own stated wish to make cookies is also a reason to postpone another lunch request, not evidence that all cooking can be demanded on the same schedule. [G11 §6; E820 001/007; E844 010; MINI §8.]

## 6. Evaluation, care and safety

G16's ordered inspection is the clearest test of institutional evaluation. Fuuka reports that a previous apparently pleasant inspection led to poor marks and a halved budget. Juri tries to reassure her; a remembered smile does not constitute a favorable report. Iori reports a former inspector's poisoning diagnosis and possible pathogens; those remain reports and possibilities. Fuuka's distinction between poor taste and negligent hygiene is therefore an argument about criteria, not an already established universal safety claim. A frightening entrance can coexist with later evidence-based concession. [G16 1601:u0029–0073.]

The present checks genuinely favor the club. Hands, tools, furniture and ingredients receive favorable marks. Iori's explicit distinction between hygiene and bad taste prevents interpreting the later F as proof that every earlier compliment was dishonest. A moving dish is represented, but Juri's autonomous-selfhood explanation is untested; Iori asks whether that phenomenon belongs under hygiene. The source does not settle ingredient causes, a completed fight or a new person ontology. It instead exposes how a local evaluation domain can omit a risk which the participants do not know how to classify. [G16 1602:u0001–0057.]

Personal hospitality then changes the evidence. Fuuka offers water when Iori is thirsty. Juri provides fresh fruit juice, identified as the same welcome offered to the earlier inspector rather than ordered stock already inspected. Fuuka recalls the earlier diagnosis and tries to stop consumption; Iori has drunk and reacts adversely. Juri calls the unresponsiveness sleep. The sequence strongly supports a source-local explanation of the present incident, while the repeated diagnosis line in Fuuka's realization is not a newly verified medical report. Clinical chemistry, enduring injury and a general pathology remain unestablished. [G16 1602:u0061–0083, attribution table.]

Two contrary reactions must survive: Izumi drinks and enjoys the juice, and Iori later safely awakens and gives an F. Enjoyment is her own positive experience; it does not answer what happened to Iori or prove immunity. Waking is narrated; clinical clearance is absent. The grade is actual; another budget halving is Fuuka's fear rather than printed implementation. These distinctions make evaluation responsive to new evidence without treating every judgment as infallible. They also show why a worker may genuinely protect hygiene and nonetheless contemplate a profoundly inadequate response to a harmed guest. [G16 1602:u0084–0092.]

Fuuka's proposed concealment belongs in the institutional argument precisely because she is ordinarily competent and concerned. Fear of another F and lost funds makes the temptation intelligible; it does not justify withholding a victim or make the proposed removal prudent. The source prints an alarming suggestion and then says no solution worked. It does not print disappearance, killing, successful concealment or a coordinated cover-up. The serious inference is that evaluation pressure can make protecting the institution's apparent standing compete with answering to the affected person. That explanatory relation remains an interpretation of the local sequence, not a diagnosis or proof of an academy-wide incentive mechanism. [G16 §5/§7.]

Private care provides additional limits on success language. Fuuka's eighteen-dish effort has Japanese eating versus Korean tasting all eighteen, an adverse endpoint and an actual prescription; provision, administration, ingestion and cure are not printed. Eel purchase and return differ from later whole-thread testimony about today's dish, preparation and improved stamina. Juri's medicine is contemplated rather than prescribed, and repeated soup attempts leave the final dish unresolved. These cases qualify a competence-only account without equating all Fuuka care with Juri failure. The institutional synthesis uses them to distinguish a made dish, a reported effect, a proposed remedy and a completed recipient result. [F-CHK P02/P03; F-CMP Q05; J-CHK R01; J-CMP Q03.]

EVENT844 supplies an especially important positive countercase. Delicious pancakes and Juri's happiness occur before the dangerous movement, so later harm cannot rewrite the earlier tasting as imaginary. The ending supplies new instruction and practice as reports, followed by explicitly permitted refusal, acceptance and narrated shared eating. This is stronger than another unfinished offer. It supports a bounded accomplishment and affection, while no controlled test of long-term reproducibility or universal harmlessness follows. To preserve both failures and actual good food is a requirement of fair institutional judgment, not a compromise made to protect a likable character. [E844 002:scene001;010:scene003:u0027–0047.]
