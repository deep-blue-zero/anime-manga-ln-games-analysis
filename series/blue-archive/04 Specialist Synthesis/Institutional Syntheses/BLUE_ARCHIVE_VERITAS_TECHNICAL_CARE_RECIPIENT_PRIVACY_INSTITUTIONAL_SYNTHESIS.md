# Veritas: technical care, answerable access and different wanted lives

## 1. Institutional question and thesis

**VCLAIM001.** Veritas becomes intelligible as an institution through the decisions that make its expertise available, the interests those decisions serve, and the people who can contest them. Its members can protect records, build useful things, understand a game, enjoy a sound or want a shared view. They can also alter another person's information, preserve accessed data against advice, retaliate, record without permission, and impose a programme of care. Those acts have different agents and recipients. The institution's positive capacities and its failures of answerability belong in one argument: a stated commitment to truth and freedom gives members a reason to scrutinize their own use of access, while the ability to obtain or change information does not itself establish authority over the person concerned. [S01:L154–205; S02:L243–253; S03:L195–219; S04:L193–215; S05:L22–24,54–92.]

**VCLAIM002.** The governing question is: how can Veritas make information access and technical care answerable to the people affected while preserving members' different games, art, company and rest, without treating successful correction as permission, complete recipient restoration or durable safeguards? This existing institutional home also owns the substantial P3-XMI-E001 plural game/art/rest argument below. That section examines the club's actual shared practice, unequal demands and different pleasures; it supplies no additional package or named-duty denominator. The seven maintained carriers have been received completely at their written analytical/report scopes. Their earlier canonical, native and source-owner reading credits remain attributed, and their current contextual qualifications govern retained creation-stage statements. Comparisons across private, group, event and MAIN accounts establish an available repertoire, with only their supported local temporal relations. [S01:L82–95,174–176; S02:L142–155,259; S03:L132–145,205–219; S04:L142–155,211–215; S05:L38–48; S06:L31–38,63–65,94; S07:L66–77,216–229.]
