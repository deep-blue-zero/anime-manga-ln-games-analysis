# Sumire and baseball Rei — chosen help and revisable limits

Sumire and Rei build an association around help that each has reason to want, while continuing to disagree about what that help should do. Rei seeks strength for a particular beautiful hit; Sumire finds purpose in developing another person's abilities. Their exchanges become most revealing when those ends cease to coincide automatically. A granted break, an enjoyable outing and shared baseball knowledge give their cooperation positive substance. Repeated attempts to move an endpoint or override a request expose its continuing difficulty. The relation asks how an expert can remain answerable to the particular person who chose her, including that person's changing answer about the means. [G330:71–113; E845:96–110]

The received coaching sequence begins with a referral that acknowledges limits. Rei's baseball seniors can discuss the aspiration, but they recognize that training expertise must be sought elsewhere. Rei asks Sumire for temporary instruction and allows her time to consider. That request establishes an active participant with an objective, rather than a vacant place awaiting a senior's project. Sumire's acceptance is a real contribution: she makes expertise available to someone who has chosen to ask. This is the locally established beginning of their coaching arrangement; it does not establish their first-ever encounter or an unlimited agreement about future methods. [G330:71–85]

Their initial asymmetry concerns knowledge and pace. Sumire can begin a programme and prescribe work with an immediacy that exceeds Rei's readiness. Rei needs the senior's knowledge, yet still has to judge whether an exercise serves batting and whether she can continue it. Sumire's broader interest in whole-body development does not simply reproduce Rei's home-run aspiration. Each can therefore participate sincerely while giving a different answer to what counts as a useful next step. The disagreement belongs inside their chosen association from the start; accepting a pupil and accepting instruction do not settle its terms once and for all. [G330:83–89]

The repeated last set makes that problem concrete. Sumire praises an achieved count but repeatedly extends the apparent endpoint. Rei's objection has a particular object: work presented as nearly finished continues to require more. When Rei says she cannot go on and asks for a break, Sumire grants it. Both sides of this exchange matter. The changing endpoint creates pressure, and the accepted request is an actual local response to a limit. Calling the break a completed reform would outrun the scene, but reducing it to empty kindness would erase something Sumire really does for Rei. [G330:87; SQ02/O023]

Sumire also ends a session and proposes a change of venue. This supplies another stopping act, with a different result: their shared time opens onto something besides the next exercise. Rei can bring Sumire into the world that gives the training request its particular destination. The outing does not need to deliver a home run to make that change meaningful. An expert who calls time and accompanies a pupil becomes available to learn what the pupil wanted the work for, rather than encountering that want only as a justification for more work. [G330:93–103]

At the reserve ground, quietness and a sparse crowd belong to Rei's pleasure in baseball. Sumire explicitly enjoys watching with someone. Their company has value in its actual, modest setting, without requiring an athletic result to validate it. The two are sharing an activity that each can enjoy for her own reasons. That difference gives the outing its relational force: Rei does not have to become a general fitness enthusiast, and Sumire does not have to arrive as an accomplished baseball spectator, for spending the time together to matter. [G330:97–107]

Expertise also changes direction. Rei explains baseball rules and corrects Sumire within the domain that made the original request meaningful. The junior who needs training can supply knowledge the senior lacks. Their hierarchy remains real, but it is neither total nor self-sufficient. Sumire's capacity to prescribe an exercise does not settle a rule of baseball, just as Rei's rule knowledge does not make her a training expert. Their cooperation works through these particular differences. Rei's informed corrections are part of her contribution to the association, rather than interruptions to a one-way transfer of knowledge. [G330:99–107,144–150]

Food talk gives this reciprocity another ordinary form. Rei values stadium food and offers to treat Sumire; Sumire permits one hotdog and makes her own dietary and alternative-food claims. The offer and allowance are already ways of responding to one another's preferences. They do not supply an acted purchase or a meal consumed by Sumire. Sumire's explicitly enjoyed good here is shared watching. Keeping those endpoints distinct preserves the food interest that is actually Rei's and the company Sumire actually enjoys, without making either person's pleasure borrow an unprinted action from the other. [G330:99,103; SQ04/O023]

After the outing, Sumire asks to continue together and Rei agrees. This affirmation gives the relation more than its recurring objections: Rei can still want the association, and Sumire can value the company she has found through it. Their trust is expressed through a particular renewed answer, not an exhaustive disclosure of either person's inner life. Its local character is also what makes the later objections intelligible. Someone can affirm this person and this shared activity while still judging a later device or demand unsuitable. Continuation leaves an answering participant in place. [G330:103–113,144–150]

The restraint device brings that distinction to a sharper point. Sumire explains a means of making Rei finish, while Rei identifies the loss of an ability to stop. Completion and stopping are different goods in this exchange. Rei's objection challenges the terms of the proposed help, rather than showing that her original aspiration was insincere. The represented endpoint is a displayed and explained device, an objection and a stated intention not to return; activation, release, safety and an enacted departure remain unprinted. A major rupture and completed repair cannot be built out of that intention alone. The unresolved objection nevertheless counts in full. [G330:111–123]

E832 places the same coaching difficulty inside an outing where the Training Club's help has a positive public use. The bear diversion helps the party, while Rei disputes the relevance of cardio and shouting to the long-hit strength she wants, reports soreness and objects to danger. Other participants can admire dedication, and Sensei can register a mismatch, without answering for her. When Rei directly wants to quit and Sumire refuses before the menu is finished, useful help to the surrounding group does not resolve the pupil's separate question about stopping. Her objection remains addressed to her coach. [E832:81–83,93–108]

The later continuation choices in that outing make the difference between recipients especially clear. Chihiro gives her own trio an off-ramp, and they choose to continue. Sumire separately imposes a summit sprint on Rei over her protest. These are two relations with different questions and answers, even within shared surroundings. The trio's chosen continuation supplies a positive example of an offered option; it cannot be transferred into Rei's permission. Sumire and Rei's difficulty is consequently more precise than a general complaint that exertion is always unwanted. It concerns the answer given by this participant to this demand. [E832:85–89,93–108]

Arrival and the view remain positive endpoints. Their value does not depend on making the preceding protest disappear. Breakfast, bread, coffee and protein are still proposed future choices, while the later apparent bodily gain belongs to a simulator's game character. The relation can retain an actual shared destination and the prospect of ordinary food without inventing a consumed breakfast or an improved Rei body. It can likewise retain a wanted rest or a refusal even when the party reaches the summit. Pleasure and objection do not have to cancel one another to belong to the same received occasion. [E832:85–91,110–115]

In E845, Sumire articulates a limit that reaches the heart of her association with Rei: she wants to help people pursue chosen selves and says she cannot decide for another person. Her contributions also acquire visible boundaries. She admits limits in negotiation; her Morse knowledge is useful but constrained by noise. This mixture gives her more particularity than an all-purpose expert or a merely intrusive trainer. She can possess relevant knowledge, want to contribute and still need another person's decision. The stated limit gives a standard against which her conduct can be read, rather than proof that she has already learned to observe it everywhere. [E845:78–98]

Rei's public contest also distributes support among several people. Hanae's wanted cheering, Sensei's tactics and forceful contact, Sumire's particular contributions and Rei's decision at the plate belong to distinct actors. Rei remains the person whose aesthetic and team-directed desire give the attempt its destination. Recognizing that ownership preserves what Sumire's help is for while limiting what can be credited to it. The relation does not become more meaningful by absorbing all encouragement, all costs or another person's bodily intervention into the coach's care. Rei's chosen attempt occurs within that plurality of assistance. [E845:78–106]

The grand slam and its joyful reception are real local achievements. They give Rei an actual success of the kind she wanted, without making Sumire the sole cause or establishing a reliably acquired ability. The following machine defeat remains part of the same account's local aftermath. It does not retrospectively make the earlier joy false. Together, the two outcomes keep achievement particular: a fulfilled aspiration matters, and its value need not depend on a guarantee that every later attempt will reproduce it. A relation organized around helping a goal can acknowledge both outcomes without turning a win into ethical absolution. [E845:104–110]

The next morning also keeps ordinary choices in view. Rei refuses a professional contract in favour of what she wants to do with her own club and thanks others for equipment. These choices give her continuing aims beyond being the object of a successful intervention. Sumire's wish to keep the vendor outfit as a memory belongs to a different, modest good: preserving something of a shared occasion. That wanted memory need not improve batting or prove a new permanent intimacy to matter. Their shared world includes the club Rei chooses and the occasion Sumire wants to remember, with neither end reducible to a training result. [E845:108–114]

Yet moderation talk is followed by another disputed endpoint. Rei asks for today off, and Sumire renews endurance demands. The proximity of the stated principle and the contrary response makes the tension difficult to explain away as a completed lesson already learned. The account supplies both a capacity to express another person's decisional ownership and a failure to honour a particular request for rest. Good intentions, a successful contest and wanted memories do not settle that contradiction. It remains possible to value Sumire's contribution while asking whether her preferred process is again displacing the recipient's answer. [E845:96–114,130]

The settings change how this association is mediated, while leaving that question recognizable. G330 gives the peer exchange without Sensei mediation; E832 places it beside an adult's observation of mismatch; E845 gives the adult an active part in a public attempt. None of those roles makes Sensei the author of Rei's request or her later assent and protest. The written pattern is carried by asking, prescribing, granting a pause, affirming company and objecting again. Seniority shapes who proposes the work, but Rei's corrections and refusals keep the junior present as a judging participant. The received accounts establish these local redefinitions, rather than a single dated progression toward final reform. [G330:83–123,144–150; E832:81–87; E845:78–114]

What holds Sumire and Rei together is therefore richer than either harmonious coaching or coercion as an explanation of everything. Rei finds someone worth asking, offers food and baseball knowledge, and can want to continue in her company. Sumire grants an actual pause, opens an outing and enjoys sharing it, while repeatedly making her own process too difficult to contest. Quiet spectatorship, an allowed hotdog, a wanted memory and a day-off request give that tension its particular human scale. The relation keeps two goods in view: being able to ask someone for help, and remaining a person whose answer can change the help. [G330:87–113; E832:81–91; E845:108–114]
