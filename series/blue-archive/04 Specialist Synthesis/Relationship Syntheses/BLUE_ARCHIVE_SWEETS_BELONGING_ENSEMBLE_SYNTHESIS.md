## 1. Core thesis

The Sweets Club's belonging is strongest when understood as **a wanted place for different people, with differences that can become both resources and costs**. Airi's pleasure in simply gathering and talking is not identical to Yoshimi's desire for a scarce pastry, Kazusa's wanted quiet or Natsu's wish for a romantic search. These people can share an afternoon without sharing every objective. Their care becomes visible in ways of offering food, trying conversation, making room, returning and helping; their limits become visible in imitation after a stop request, an unwanted excursion, mistaken explanations and unequal experience of a guest. The narrative asks who actually receives the desired benefit, rather than allowing the label “friends” to answer in advance. [G:L57–77,L83–105; X:L112–116]

Airi supplies a positive center without becoming the club's sole moral representative. She explicitly values the conversation even when a purchasing aim may fail, offers a snack, mediates and initiates the later rescue. She also laughs after saying the cream-covered face is not funny, apologizes for the lapse and can act on a recipient preference she has not yet asked. A synthesis that calls her merely passive misses her wanting and initiative; one that makes her perfectly responsive misses her local failures of reception. Her stated worry that she blurs her opinions is her own concern, not proof the others universally experience her as empty. [G:L61,L65,L75,L101–105; P:L397–403]

Kazusa's objection does not place her outside the group. She wants quality sweets, quiet company, a longer shared bus interval and future contact; in another setting she explicitly wants to do nothing during a holiday. Those preferences coexist with irritation, boundaries on appearance and jokes, and unwillingness to follow every club adventure. Yoshimi likewise wants appreciation and reciprocity while imposing a joke after a refusal. Natsu can offer a thoughtful taste combination and protect a fragile site while pulling friends into costly help elsewhere. The thesis therefore distinguishes **valued presence from universal permission**, without treating refusal as a failure to belong. [P:L416–425,L449–470,L480–501; G:L83–105]

Unequal knowledge is a second constitutive feature. Readers learn Airi's reason for wanting change before the friends do; their semla explanation is demonstrably mistaken, yet their wish to keep her rather than find a technically better replacement is real. Tsurugi can sincerely report an enjoyable welcome while the Sweets peers are anxious, partly through thoughts she is not shown hearing. These are not contradictions to be repaired into one true group mood. They show why care must be specified as an actor's intention, a recipient's experience and an achieved local outcome, with no automatic equivalence among the three. [A:L168–180; X:L86,L112–116]

## 5. Ordinary life

Dessert and company are related goods, with neither reducible to the other. Airi can enjoy talking while waiting and also have a particular dessert she strongly wants. Her cupcake selection distinguishes tried raspberry, banana's appealing color and an old chocolate-mint favorite. Kazusa identifies her own strawberry shortcake and macaron tastes and avoids some cheap mass products. Yoshimi's scarce-sweet rivalry and willingness to recruit another purchaser are not proof she lacks affection; Natsu can seek the history of expired sweets rather than edible food. The club name gives a common activity, while the sources supply different sensory and imaginative uses of it. [G:L61; P:L397,L419,L458,L486]

Waiting offers a precise example of nonidentical benefit. Yoshimi's restless dissatisfaction asks for expressive reception; Kazusa asks for relief from disturbance; Airi tries to sustain the gathering and supplies another sweet. Missing the original pastry would not erase Airi's conversational pleasure. It would also be wrong to use her contentment as a demand that Yoshimi and Kazusa experience the same waiting as equally pleasant. Food, successful acquisition, expression and company can be distributed differently across the same minutes. These ordinary stakes explain the interaction even before the cream puff is damaged. [G:L57–61]

Doing nothing is a wanted life, not an empty state awaiting useful service. Kazusa names inactivity as her holiday plan; Airi has lessons and study; Yoshimi has no fixed plan and considers sweet-shop touring; Natsu wants a more elaborate search. The refusal of the cave expedition is therefore a choice among real activities and degrees of effort. Natsu's desired romance belongs in the record, but so do the others' preference for a holiday that does not contain it. The later rescue must not absorb those earlier wants into a general duty of enthusiasm. [G:L83–103]

Ordinary company also need not originate in a problem. Natsu's pie, milk, pleasant sun and wind, and wish for shared time explicitly reject the assumption that a visit must mean distress. Kazusa's continued bus time is chosen, while Yoshimi's study request is serious without needing an academic emergency. These ends preserve the ordinary as an intrinsic explanatory domain. [P:L406,L419,L464,L489]

Making and attention broaden the common room. Natsu's handmade cookies and changing taste combination make exchange possible without guaranteeing perfect interpretation. Kazusa wants imperfect ensemble memory on a tangible LP rather than a commercially polished album; a worker supplies the turntable help. Yoshimi wants personally selected clothing and a serious private musical audience, while Airi's keyboard practice and new-skill wish may unsettle the identity friends already value. Costs remain ordinary too. Yoshimi becomes exhausted after agreeing to repeated encores. Natsu must rebuild the drum setup after a feared crawling thing proves to be moving dust. ## 10. Competing readings
