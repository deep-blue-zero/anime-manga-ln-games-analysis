## 1. Core thesis: company survives through differentiated answers

Pandemonium's company with Ibuki is strongest where a member stops treating her affection as a guaranteed return. Makoto can love her company while wanting her admiration, treating her comfort selectively, or borrowing her innocence to make a leadership scene appealing. Iroha can recognize her preferences and offer protection while wanting quiet, leaving the organization, hiding information, or using her hurt to increase a punishment. Ibuki can enjoy both members, be frightened, make mistakes, want to help and refuse a proposed return. These directions do not add up to one benevolent family essence or one covertly manipulative machine. Their repeated encounters put the recipients' answers between a giver's account of care and the action that follows. ([Private account][PS01], Pandemonium lines 502–512; [pudding trilogy][PS35], §14; [MAIN return visit][PS14].)

A promise about pudding makes that distinction unusually clear. In MINI15030, Makoto's apology becomes acceptable when Ibuki asks her not to take **anyone's** pudding, not simply to remember that Ibuki's dessert is special. The finger promise and forgiveness are real represented ends. The ten replacement puddings remain Makoto's account, and no general account of future compliance follows. What changes in the encounter is the terms under which Ibuki agrees to resume company: her grievance is heard, her own answer is sought, and the wrong's scope widens beyond a favored child. That is a relational achievement with an unfinished afterlife, not an institutional acquittal. ([MINI32][PS35], §14, MINI15030:u0088–0114.)

Iroha's offered exit establishes a different achievement. In MAIN S2V002C2E011, she offers Ibuki hair combing, reading and company away from the changed Makoto. Ibuki likes the offer and nevertheless chooses to remain because Makoto is lonely. Iroha checks that this is what she wants and accepts the answer. Respect is visible in the option and in accepting refusal of that option. It would be an error to count only removal from Makoto as protective care, just as it would be an error to turn Ibuki's choice into proof that the environment is safe or that she understands every political danger. The scene gives an answer to an offered course of action; its worth is not cancelled by the missing safety certificate. ([MAIN return visit][PS14], u0041–0110.)

The ensemble's ordinary ends make those answers intelligible. Ibuki wants to play, taste things, make a collage, see a parade, help with work and spend time with people she likes. Iroha wants rest and chosen reading, not only the opportunity to serve. Makoto wants admired company and sometimes obtains it through genuine practical adjustment. Satsuki wants relief, food and reassuring contact; Chiaki wants scenes worth seeing and recording. These are ends whose partial achievement and frustration matter before they become examples of political virtue or crisis resilience. A child wanting more time with PS68 is not valuable only because the request chastens a leader. A quiet outing is not discarded because no government changes. ([Private account][PS01], lines 502–510; [MINI32][PS35], §§12,14; [summer account][PS04], 001–015.)

The synthesis is consequently about the distribution of choice within shared participation. Makoto and Iroha differ in what they notice, offer, withhold and ask back. Ibuki's agency is neither adult sovereignty nor passive moral symbolism. The social structure permits real kindness and real asymmetry together. It changes locally when members act on an answer that complicates their own preferred story: a refused return, a refusal to issue a stay order, a child who already knows about a quarrel, or a member who does not want another photograph. No uniform affection score can replace these encounters. ([MINI32][PS35], §14; [summer account][PS04], 004–005,012–014.)

## 4. School and club role: participation exceeds office, while labor still has costs

The ensemble lives within an office that distributes work unequally. Iroha's departure makes Makoto perform practical tasks herself; Chiaki sees a potential scoop in this change. The old newspaper's hiatus and the members' absence make the room less lively. These are social and administrative effects of a person's absence, not evidence that the organization has legally dissolved. The member is wanted partly for what she does and partly for the company she makes. A reading that names only labor extraction misses the latter; a reading that names only family affection conceals the former. ([MAIN Chiaki account][PS11], u0001–0014.)

Iroha's ordinary rest is therefore an interpretive check on office entitlement. In MINI120, she is assigned an unattended broadcast and wants escape. Kirara and Erika voluntarily enjoy doing it, solve a local cable problem and later decline to replace the absent host permanently. Makoto's regard for difference is not constant. MAIN's screw metaphor gives way, after local recovery, to a speech about freedom requiring continuing choice. Ibuki's useful-work enthusiasm is not reducible to a place in that hierarchy. ## 6. Named duty P3-REL-MAKOTO-IBUKI: selective affection meets a wider promise

### 6.1 The direction and its unequal stakes

Makoto→Ibuki is a direction of attention whose giver often wants an answer back: admiration, reassurance, a place beside the child, or restored favor. Ibuki→Makoto includes wanted play, worry for her loneliness, forgiveness and requests for other people's benefit. The two are not identical motivations exchanged symmetrically. Makoto holds office and can mobilize resources; Ibuki's choices are local and situated among older members. The meaningful reciprocity is therefore not equal power but a visible capacity to answer, refuse and alter the terms of closeness. Reading only Makoto's declared affection would miss the very feedback through which this relationship becomes intelligible. ([MINI32][PS35],§§2,14; [MAIN return visit][PS14]; [Final rescue][PS33].)

The first MINI pudding episode already differentiates regret. Makoto boasts about theft and lacks remorse while discussing an abstract victim; when she recognizes Ibuki's pudding, she acknowledges fault and apologizes. The source gives no shown replacement or accepted apology at that point. That pivot is real selective responsiveness, with a serious contrary implication: another person's equal grievance did not initially command the same concern. Ibuki's question about whether wanting her pudding was bad makes the recipient's uncertainty visible, rather than simply ratifying the leader's affection. The scene warrants neither “Makoto cannot apologize” nor “Makoto respects everyone's property because she loves Ibuki.” ([MINI32][PS35],§2, MINI70000020:u0064–0074 and surrounding account.)

The later trilogy has more demanding consequences. It begins with a repeated offense and a prior promise, so Ibuki's departure cannot be explained as a random tantrum or failure to understand kindness. Makoto's initial appeal to absent name labels evades the wider problem; Iroha insists no one's pudding should be taken. Ibuki is lost and guilty about harsh words, but these feelings do not retract her objection to the theft. Aru's escort offer is refused. The audience must preserve a child who is both vulnerable and capable of withholding the return that older members want. Concern for her whereabouts is not an entitlement to make her answer disappear. ([MINI32][PS35], MINI15010:u0002–0097.)

### 6.2 Departure, chosen company and conditional return

Discovery does not complete reconciliation. Ibuki says she left of her own accord and rejects the kidnapping story. She can say she is no longer angry and still want to stay with PS68 because play with the older girls is pleasant. This distinguishes forgiving a feeling from accepting a proposed itinerary. Haruka's wish for her company and Aru's office invitation create an actual alternative social occasion, not permanent adoption or a right to keep her. Makoto's care is tested precisely because Ibuki can find wanted company elsewhere. If her value were only to mirror the chair's benevolence, this interval would be narratively unnecessary. ([MINI32][PS35], MINI15020:u0025–0077.)

At the office, Ibuki interprets Aru's rhetoric through observed kindness rather than accepting a self-description automatically. She wins a balance game and wants a different rule. These are small acts of judgment and initiative before Makoto arrives. They matter to the named duty because the later request is authored by a recipient already doing more than waiting to be rescued. Her independence does not have to become mature political knowledge to be substantial. The office is a place of pleasure and negotiated company, while its registration, address disclosure and legal status remain separately reported or contested. ([MINI32][PS35], MINI15030, play and office-boundary account.)

The apology then produces the strongest local revision. Ibuki's condition is that Makoto take nobody's pudding. Makoto's finger promise and Ibuki's forgiveness are represented, so “nothing changes” is too weak a reading. But the change is to the terms of this encounter, not a verified permanent policy. Makoto's ten replacement puddings are reported, and actual handing-over, eating or later universal compliance is not supplied. The relation therefore achieves a widened acknowledgment and consent to renewed company, without acquiring evidence about every future action. The wider promise is both a criticism of selective care and an actual end within it. ([MINI32][PS35], MINI15030:u0088–0114.)

### 6.3 Care beyond the apology and its adverse cases

Other contexts show attention that cannot be reduced to an attempt to buy forgiveness. On the festival trip, Makoto is willing to stand at the photograph's edge if Ibuki looks good at the center. During the summer drift, she immediately denies Ibuki's self-blame for the bad rental decision and takes the first entry into a dangerous ship rather than delegating it to the child. She draws pursuit away in the pirate encounter. A bad organizer can still make actual protective acts, and these acts have specific costs and recipients. Their acknowledgment is not an instruction to excuse her earlier choices or ignore Iroha's burden. ([Festival trip][PS03],003; [summer account][PS04],002,005,007.)

The same summer sequence refuses an ideal-parent conclusion. Makoto plans around her own triumph, wants an apology from Iroha after that imagined success, and contributes to the unrecognized labor that makes Iroha refuse work. After recovery, Ibuki urges reconciliation, while Iroha asks her to wait; the narrative does not supply the mutual apology a benevolent-family reading might expect. Ibuki and Chiaki say the quarrel shows love, but Iroha rejects that simple conclusion.
