## 1. Core thesis

Kazusa and Reisa can become important to one another without agreeing on the terms by which they are important. Reisa's rediscovery of the legendary rival supplies an available way to keep a valued person within reach. Kazusa wants an ordinary life in which that legend does not dictate every encounter. Her irritation is therefore more than a disguise for attachment, while her choice to seek and help Reisa is more than reluctant compliance with a teacher's moral script. The most sustained account is a relationship capable of particular company and care, repeatedly endangered by treating one person's desired meaning as the other's obligation. The directed difference, rather than an agreed friendship or romance label, is the analytical object. [K:L109–115,L124,L138–139]

There are strong positives. Reisa listens to Kazusa's correction, withdraws from pursuit and acts protectively; Kazusa becomes concerned when the familiar nuisance disappears, seeks her and returns the challenge letter. A separate public context gives this relation a small, concrete form: Kazusa anticipates Reisa's presence and actually buys an extra ice cream, while Airi welcomes her even without Kazusa. These are achieved choices and hospitality. They do not license suppressing the final renewed challenge in EVENT816, Kazusa's anger at misinformation or the difference between wanting someone present and accepting every version of that presence. [K:L111,L123–124,L138–139; E010–015; A:L201/E020:s001:u0003–0024; L17:L26–28]

The quiet evidence makes the distinction intelligible. A café's warm laughter and shared food were Kazusa's positive reasons for changing her life before the present teacher consultation. Reisa's private repertoire, separately, values friends' play as much as school and patrol; she does not want rest to require deleting pleasure from life. Neither person's ordinary ends can be reduced to the other, but those ends make company worth choosing. The relation is not valuable only because it produces a rescue. The ice cream, the wanted café, the possibility of talking and the wish to be heard are themselves its stakes. [L16:L28–32/E016:s001:u0036–0049; R:L420–425]

This thesis has a limiting contrary: the represented ending does not show durable reciprocal agreement. Reisa can respond to correction and later revive the old challenge under a distorted account of the rescue. Kazusa can help her and still refuse sentimental labels, just as the club can affirm belonging and mishandle privacy or consent. The synthesis therefore treats capacity for revision and accomplished company as local achievements, with unfinished transmission, repetition and relationship repair kept visible. It adds no prediction, model grade or executable rule. [K:L124,L139–142; L17:L26–36]

## 10. Competing readings
