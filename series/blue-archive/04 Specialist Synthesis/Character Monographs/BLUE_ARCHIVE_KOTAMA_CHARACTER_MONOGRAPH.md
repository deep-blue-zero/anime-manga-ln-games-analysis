## 1. Core thesis

Kotama's central tension is between listening as a wanted way of living and access as a claim upon other people. She wants the texture of café conversation, winter sound, the process of making fire, food and goods, a comforting voice and company in which she can speak less fearfully. Those wants are real literary ends. Their sincerity does not decide how she may obtain them. The complete repertoire repeatedly distinguishes invited listening from interception, voluntary disclosure from concealed devices, appreciation of a gift from agreement to its hidden contents, and shared pleasure from a recipient's surrendered right to refuse. Kotama is neither exhausted by the surveillance joke nor excused by the tenderness that often accompanies it. Her listening becomes most revealing when she is also a participant: a tired body accepting a hand, a maker whose fire does not catch, a hungry recorder unable to remove herself from the environment, or someone asking that her own disclosure be forgotten.
