## 1. Core thesis: attention has different objects and different obligations

Nodoka's sustained question is how an attentive life can be both sincerely pleasurable and troublesome to other people. The telescope, stars, clouds, food, warm water and a familiar building are things she values, not merely symbols of deprivation or substitutes for the adult. Her wish to see Sensei belongs to that repertoire, but the means matter: covert observation, accepted shared watching, a declined invitation and a request to be carried produce different relations. The evidence supports a person who can savor, object, work, improvise, want care and sometimes overreach. It does not support one appetite explaining every act. The strongest literary unity is a persistent wish for worthwhile sights and company within conditions that do not reliably let her choose how to obtain them. [BA:bond:26006:003/006/008; BA:bond:20010:002/003/005/006; BA:group:1302/1303/2801/2802.]

Attention becomes an ethical question because neither beauty nor affection settles the recipient's terms. In the covert-watching bond, the opportunity for direct contact does not meet her stated preference for secret observation; she refuses it and asks to be ignored. Elsewhere she accepts the adult's work limit, takes her own mountain trip and later wishes he had come. These are adverse and contrary cases within the same character responsibility. They prevent both an innocent-observer interpretation and a person-wide inability to respect refusal. A shared night of meteor watching supplies a third arrangement: her wanted experience really occurs, while an apparently sleeping adult leaves her question unanswered before the two actually continue watching until the display stops. Pleasure and imperfect responsiveness coexist. [BA:bond:26006:003:scene:001:u:0012–0018; 26006:008:u:0012–0018 and whole thread260060280; 26006:006:scene:002:u:0017–0023.]

Material conditions give these wants weight without swallowing their content. Nodoka can defend the cold old building as important to its inhabitants and simultaneously seek heating, bedding, food and a return to the main school. She can value togetherness while disagreeing with Shigure about whether company makes the former life sufficiently enjoyable. She can become a host whose work brings scenery, pride and small luxury while still being overworked and unable to save the resort. Her aspirations therefore ask who gets a livable setting, who must labor to maintain it, and whose account of comfort is allowed to stand. Their ordinary objects remain valuable before a political or crisis consequence appears. [BA:group:1303:u:0033–0036; 2801:scene:002:u:0006–0044; BA:event:808:006/010/011/012.]

This monograph argues for situated attention and differentiated home, with substantial contrary evidence, rather than an operational rule about watching or hospitality.
