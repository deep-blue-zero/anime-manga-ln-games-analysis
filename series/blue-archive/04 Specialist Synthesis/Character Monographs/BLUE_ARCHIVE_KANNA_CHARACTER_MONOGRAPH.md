## 1. Core thesis

**K001.** Kanna's governing conflict is between the public legibility of an intimidating officer and the ordinary person that this legibility both protects and confines. The reputation makes her recognizable, permits intervention and can deter an opponent. It also enters other people's expectations before she speaks: the adult initially anticipates arrest, a child cries, and Kanna imagines that appearing at a celebratory meal would spoil it. She sometimes treats this accumulated image as a necessity of work; she sometimes wants company beyond it. The resulting character is neither a benevolent essence hidden by an unfortunate face nor merely an instrument of corrupt authority. Her central literary movement is learning, in specific encounters, which uses of authority serve another person, which cover her own fear or institutional compromise, and when a limit or a refused demand should be allowed to stand. [Private account][R15], [rooftop confrontation][M08], [water park][E837].

**K002.** Her practical intelligence is real and unevenly distributed. Sorting receipts, mapping sightings, noticing a thief's container clue and finding an accepted exception at a corporate checkpoint accomplish things that an intimidating persona alone cannot. So do her wounded rescue of Sensei and her organization of a route for Miyako. Those successes coexist with a mistaken accusation of Love, a frightening response to a child and an excessive visitor-restraint procedure. A rescued person can be grateful while another has a valid complaint. Kanna's competence therefore needs a recipient and an actual endpoint, rather than a universal rating of goodness or effectiveness. [Private account][R15], [hospital-bound resident][M11], [rescue][M16], [water park][E837].

**K003.** Ordinary enjoyment gives this conflict another axis. The familiar stall offers oden, yakitori and tea without a solemn institutional transformation. Coffee is actually drunk in the gift encounter. She initiates use of the cleaned spa and says she enjoyed it. Her novel and film interests, hopes for peace and preferred meal are not rewards that matter only after a dramatic moral victory. They are wanted forms of living. Conversely, fear on the slide and refusal of another ride remain legitimate ends rather than obstacles that the adult must overcome. The monograph gives these distinctions as much interpretive force as crisis courage. [Private account][R15], [gift 834110][E834].
