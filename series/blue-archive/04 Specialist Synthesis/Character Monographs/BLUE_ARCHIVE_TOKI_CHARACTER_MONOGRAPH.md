## 1. Core thesis

Toki's central contradiction is that she wants to be useful and wants to be wanted, while repeatedly making usefulness the language through which she asks for company. Her maid and agent roles are substantive sources of skill, pride and pleasure. They also give her a way to avoid asking directly whether people will still welcome her when she has no assignment. The result is neither an empty obedient instrument nor a fully emancipated person whose earlier dependence has vanished. She increasingly chooses tasks, friends and amusements, but still negotiates belonging through work, praise, protection and exaggerated availability.

The strongest evidence for this contradiction is the contrast between the opening and ending of EVENT825. Initially, Toki reports that Seminar refused a transfer of command rights and that only Schale can direct her; without orders, she must remain on standby. Her insistence that she is perfectly calm sits beside denials of loneliness, anxiety and boredom that conspicuously name the feelings at issue. Sensei's represented request is explicitly distinguished from an instruction or order, and she responds to it as a meaningful first commission in her callsign capacity. By the ending, she says she chose to fight as the game club's companion and friend. Neru answers with praise and an apology for leaving her behind on an urgent assignment. The change is local and substantial: requested service becomes chosen shared responsibility, and anticipated punishment encounters actual welcome. It is not a charter establishing universal freedom. (`BA:event:825:001:u:0013–0032`; `:016:u:0030–0080`, all scene001.)

Her interests establish another side of the person that cannot be reduced to this relational problem. Toki enjoys tea and small luck, is curious about manga and games, watches commentary videos, finds animals adorable, wants equipment because it looks interesting, values a place where she can host someone, and wants to share a beautiful dawn. Some desires involve Sensei; others are positively independent of Sensei's wishes. When proposing a rocket-like arm mechanism, she says she wants to see it even if the adult does not. When describing games, she says playing with Alice is enjoyable. These are wanted ends, not merely instruments for receiving affection. (`BA:bond:16019:003:u:0062–0083`; `:005:u:0013–0031`; `BA:event:854:020:u:0030–0038`.)

Belonging also remains an ethical difficulty because Toki can intrude while trying to care. She asserts an employer relationship despite protest, gatekeeps student access, enters an office without a printed permission account, treats Sensei's bed differently from other people's beds, and presses unwanted equipment proposals. A portrait that celebrates her warmth while removing those refusals would discard the recipient's experience. Conversely, a portrait defined by intrusion would erase her capacity to apologize, accept some limits, express uncertainty and choose friends. The defensible thesis concerns a growing repertoire of choice and connection under continuing relational and institutional constraints; it does not promise a final cure, a universal obedience rule or an invariably welcome form of care.

## 2. Longitudinal arc: from assigned isolation to differentiated choice

### The public beginning is coercive, and its competence is fallible

In Pavane, Toki enters as Rio's dedicated C&C agent, callsign zero four. Her first direct public appearance follows the refusal by Neru to abduct an uninformed student. Toki supplies the force that overrides that refusal: she follows authorization, restrains Neru, threatens her arm and assists Alice's seizure. Later she fights the rescue coalition. Following orders is an explanatory circumstance here; it does not make the acts harmless or place every threatened person in agreement. Nor does Rio's claim that C&C has no private feelings establish Toki's interior emptiness. (`BA:main:002:002:009:scene:001:u:0097–0140`; `:010:scene:001:u:0001–0030`.)

The sustained confrontation prevents her equipment from becoming an omnipotence myth. She identifies a diversion but wrongly predicts Neru's absence from the front. She recognizes the strength of coordinated C&C and separates the team through Rio-granted city authority, yet cannot settle the duel simply by claiming superior speed. Rio orders a withdrawal; that withdrawal precedes the later defeat and must not be compressed into it. Abi-Eshuh adapts to a roof-and-fall tactic, while successive statements concern different referents: light impact, operator injury and machine damage. Finally, Chihiro's intervention and the coalition's pressure produce a local evasion failure and close combat defeat. These scenes show real capability, situational judgment and limits. They do not independently measure a computation budget, gravitational load or total technical specification. (`BA:main:002:002:014`; `:015:scene:001:u:0001–0026`; `:018`; `:019`; `:020`; `:021:scene:001:u:0100–0130`.)

The chapter's party opens a social possibility after Rio disappears. Toki joins the gathering because she heard C&C was there. Neru's objection about injury survives alongside Asuna's welcome and the recognition that Toki was following orders. Attendance is an achieved opening; complete forgiveness, formal reassignment and erased harm are stronger claims than the scene supplies. The contemporary public portrait ends with access to company, not a finished account of how she will live. (`BA:main:002:002:025:scene:002:u:0039–0067`.)

### Freedom creates a question rather than supplying an answer

Final makes this opening explicit. Toki reports that Rio told her to live freely. She says the seniors have been good to her, admits uncertainty about freedom and chooses a familiar place in which to offer help. This is already a decision: freedom need not be expressed by rejecting skilled work. It also leaves open whether she can imagine belonging without being assigned a useful function. Himari's acceptance provides an available working relation, not proof of a completed administrative transfer. (`BA:main:100:001:003:scene:004:u:0027–0052`.)

Later, Himari and Eimi interpret her solitary work through possible loneliness and guilt. Toki says she is used to fighting alone; she does not confess their proposed motives. Keeping that distinction protects the monograph from turning benevolent concern into omniscient diagnosis. In the same way, Himari's indictment of Rio's treatment of Toki supplies a morally important external account of isolation and dangerous assignments, but remains Himari's account. The text permits criticism of the arrangement without certifying every detail of Toki's subjective experience. (`BA:main:100:002:019:scene:002:u:0007–0033`; `:003:007:scene:001:u:0060–0084`.)

The late crisis brings the conflict to its sharpest point. Toki independently holds a new site despite withdrawal advice; when she cannot move, she admits that she wanted to fight beside Schale and C&C. She then asks for a self-destruct code, assuming Rio prepared one against betrayal, and invokes the trolley problem to make herself expendable. Rio denies that such a code exists and says the premise was wrong because she had not sought help. C&C's arrival and later escort report make the counteranswer concrete. Toki's survival is supported; the imagined betrayal code, her proposed destructive capacity, complete recovery and permanent abandonment of self-sacrifice are not. (`BA:main:100:004:003:scene:001:u:0025–0052`; `:005:scene:001:u:0006–0066`; `:006:scene:001:u:0049–0057`.)

## 3. Public and private self

Public Toki initially presents through formal capability: an agent name, callsign, authority, weapon and expectation of compliance. She is not thereby a simple extension of Rio's intellect. Her mistaken prediction, recognition of C&C's coordination and adaptation under pressure give her situated thought, while the accepted text does not resolve every private motive for obeying. The contrast with private scenes is therefore one of access and setting, not discovery that an apparently non-person suddenly becomes human.

Her private formality works as both stable courtesy and a comic resource. She can meticulously execute paperwork while treating window-shopping and films as inefficient, then become anxious about whether a hobby needs a reason. She can offer an absurd justification for a gift, correct “shackle” to token, partially concede strained arithmetic, admit the practical failure of a sympathy plan, and finally say plainly that she wants to give something. In these passages, the mask does not simply conceal a single authentic feeling underneath. It organizes a conversation in which the feeling becomes easier to state. (`BA:bond:10062:005`, scenes001–004; `:015:scene:001:u:0013–0076`.)

The most direct contrast is EVENT825's reiterated composure against the later admission that she has not yet sorted out her feelings. The opening's denials are humorous but not a clinical reading test. The later confession is specific and affirmative; it does not prove every earlier denial was false in precisely the same way. Likewise, she can characterize herself as someone who never jokes in one private exchange while other scenes explicitly show joking. EVENT854 distinguishes dangerous-situation joking as endurance from her ordinary habit of saying whatever occurs to her. That distinction should replace a total explanatory claim that all humor is a trauma response. (`BA:event:825:001:u:0009–0025`; `:011:u:0001–0008`; `BA:bond:10067:005:u:0060–0068`; `BA:event:854:020:u:0063–0069`.)

Written profiles add their own perspective: the original Toki envelope says she can be lonely, while the bunny envelope places inner excitement beside outward composure. These are authorized contextual characterizations, not independent observation of an unseen event or retrospective certainty about every gesture. They fit the scene evidence without eliminating its tensions.

Privacy also includes things she chooses not to disclose. The true hobby remains withheld; the end-of-world plan in written recon dialogue remains undisclosed; the dawn encounter seeks a memory that cannot simply be held still. Those absences need not be solved to make her legible. They define the limit of the reader's access and preserve a person who is not exhausted by her reports to Sensei. A monograph should analyze the work of secrecy without inventing the secret.

## 5. Ordinary life: what Toki wants when survival is not the only question

### Pleasure need not pass a test of usefulness

The first hobby outing exposes a practical problem: Toki can evaluate a tactical film or comic for operational efficiency yet does not initially understand why an outing would be worthwhile without a result. The adult's alternatives challenge the requirement that liking have an instrumental reason and can acknowledge having rushed her. Her anxious question matters more than a tidy answer. The subsequent smile and withheld hobby show a local positive turn while leaving the object of that pleasure private. The scene does not establish that she has no desires; it shows that selecting and explaining leisure is unfamiliar. (`BA:bond:10062:005:scene:004:u:0007–0025`.)

Recon private evidence and EVENT854020 make the repertoire more concrete. Watching streams involves reactions and donations; she is a viewer, not thereby a broadcaster. Her evaluation of Michiru's channel can be comic without certifying its production process. Games with Alice are enjoyable; the account of a rivalry and Yuzu's skill remains Toki's report rather than a scene in which those peers are all present. Manga and games are explicitly enjoyable, even when the hobby label stays provisional. EVENT854 adds chips and commentary videos and Toki's observation that pleasure can include irrationality and inefficiency. The absence of a final hobby choice is an open-ended ordinary life, not a failed personality milestone. (`BA:bond:16019:003:u:0036–0083`; `BA:event:854:020:u:0030–0038`.)

The arm-gear proposal is valuable for the same reason. Toki first says the modification would be cool and interesting, then attempts to substitute a combat-strength rationale and calls the adult's hearing mistaken. Her own wish remains printed. She wants to see it even if Sensei does not. Rio and Himari's reported refusal and the adult's maintained refusal prevent the wished mechanism from becoming a completed or safe device. Her desire is still real when its execution is disallowed. (`BA:bond:16019:005:u:0013–0051`.)

### Small comforts and work matter on their own terms

Tea, chips, aroma and a tea-stalk sign of luck are not dispensable bridges to a crisis argument. They show what a visit can contain when no decisive plot turn occurs. Written café lines add sweetness, interiors, quiet space and wanting a room within time and budget limits. The bunny written envelope even permits wanting to slack off and becoming bored. The recon envelope explicitly provides days off, caffeine, sugar and rest. These conditioned lines do not certify how often she enacted each wish, but they resist the universal claim that only labor is tolerable to her.

School life remains less accomplished. She welcomes an earlier repeated year because of the prospect of more time with Sensei and C&C, proposes moving the adult to Millennium, struggles with homework and treats rule enforcement as a possible form of combat. The teacher and peers do not simply endorse all these methods. A café invitation is positive ordinary reception; wanting Sensei included also reveals how quickly the adult relation becomes her route into new experience. Apology and willingness to retry are real, while completed educational adjustment is unprinted. (`BA:bond:10062:003`, both scenes.)

EVENT834108 makes hospitality a wanted creative activity. She escorts Sensei to a valued office, explains a selected course, reports visiting shops and tasting candidates, and supplies a handmade-cake claim. She wants a special place and special person to receive an exceptional welcome. Her competitive proof language and concern about causing trouble coexist with sincerity, excitement, relief and gratitude. The adult's sufficient-satisfaction response is counterpressure, not unlimited permission for everything that follows. The ending positively narrates tasting her cake and a calming flavor; it does not establish the proposed feeding mechanism, complete consumption or clinical relief. Quiet achieved pleasure deserves to be retained as carefully as unfulfilled proposals. (`BA:event:834:108:u:0001–0054`.)

### A beautiful moment is not a wish for catastrophe

The dawn encounter makes Toki's interest in recon work more than equipment and utility. She wants to show Sensei a sight that moved her, corrects an initially daily description to the few days she has actually experienced, and holds beauty against the threat represented by the enemy's world. Her conditional wish to be the last person with the adult is not a prophecy or a wish to destroy the world. The adult's protective response remains a promise. ## 6. Crisis behavior: choice, competence, fear and expendability

Toki is capable under pressure, but that competence does not entail invulnerability, perfect interpretation or pleasure in danger. Pavane shows obedience in a coercive institutional operation. Final shows a solo stand she elects despite advice, followed by a proposed sacrifice that others reject. EVENT854 shows coordination chosen by the team while she openly says she is afraid. These are different crisis relations, and the monograph should preserve their difference rather than average them into an invariant response.

The self-destruct exchange is particularly revealing because Toki gives it a personal counterfactual before a technical one. She wanted to fight together. Her next step treats her own life as a disposable means and assumes a betrayal contingency exists. The narrative gives no such code; Rio's denial is explicit. It also rejects a supposed forced binary through seeking others' help, and the seniors arrive. The secure reading is that the text contests her conclusion of isolation and expendability. It is not secure to diagnose a continuing condition, declare her attachment healed, or certify an armor's capacity to erase a site. The later escort report and her alive afterstate supply bounded positive outcomes, not every bodily or institutional repair. (`BA:main:100:004:005:u:0019–0066`; `:006:u:0049–0057`; `:009:u:0080–0098`.)

EVENT825 makes a related problem smaller and more negotiable. Toki asks the adult and club to withdraw because she regards the recovery as her assignment and fears further injuries. Alice refuses on friendship grounds and says she wants to help. Toki admits she is inexperienced with companionship. By the ending, she owns fighting beside the club as a chosen path and shares any alleged responsibility. This is not simply an order replaced by another superior's order. It is a peer response that changes what counts as a reason to act together. Her expectation of confinement or discipline nevertheless persists, so the explicit praise and apology are an answer to a live fear, not ceremonial confirmation of an already settled relation. (`BA:event:825:011:u:0019–0047`; `:016:u:0030–0080`.)

EVENT854020 gives fear its own language. Toki says that the possibility of not reaching tomorrow with Sensei makes her chest feel painful. That is a subjective expression, not a diagnosis. Her wish that no one be hurt and that suffering disappear is an ethical aspiration; Eimi immediately recognizes its difficulty and narrows practical concern toward people within reach. The apparent ranking of Eimi's life is retracted as a joke, as are shelter confinement and war proposals. Treating them as actual plans would invert the scene's explicit correction.

Their conversation does not stop with distress. Toki explains joking under dangerous conditions as a way to endure, distinguishes it from ordinary joking, and participates in a coordinated operation in which Rio leaves entry timing to the two field agents. She selects the forward role, acknowledges Eimi's support role and exchanges wishes for safety. Competence, agency, fear and desired leisure coexist. The event's later endpoint remains open: communications and a bodily transformation report do not supply the final defeat, return home or universal restoration. Toki's reassurance that someone can communicate is also not clinical clearance. (`BA:event:854:020:u:0021–0088`; `:026:u:0112–0125`; `:029` actual EOF.)

## 7. Sensei: employer language, wanted company and reciprocal limits

Toki repeatedly translates affection and access into service. In the original private introduction she asserts that Sensei is her new employer after the former employer's disappearance, then ignores a protest. Her access screening of other students makes protection interfere with the adult's ordinary work. The initial problem is not that service can never express care; it is that she makes a relation effective by assertion while another party's refusal is printed. Subsequent moderation and accepted presentation are local negotiations. They cannot retroactively authorize the initial behavior. (`BA:bond:10062:002:scene:001:u:0023–0034`; scene002:u:0012–0051.)

Sensei's role includes listening to wishes without merely ratifying them. The hobby encounter permits liking without an instrumental reason. The school encounter encourages peer company and asks for gentleness, while resisting combat solutions to ordinary rules. Maintenance provides attention beside actual work. The gift encounter accepts an item but declines continuous wearing, explaining that a precious gift can be used when needed. Toki takes that explanation and then deliberately teases the couple-like headset framing. This is a particularly clear model of a warm local exchange with a maintained boundary; it establishes neither constant connection nor a romantic contract. (`BA:bond:10062:003`; `:005`; `:006`; `:015:u:0062–0076`.)

The sleepover supplies a necessary countercase. Her report says she entered the office, carried the sleeping adult to bed and stayed. The mechanism and permission are not independently supplied by the report. Sensei objects to the bed intrusion; she recognizes other people's beds as different but cannot explain the exception she makes for Sensei. Boredom and pressure for attention continue until the actual ending describes an enjoyable day. Enjoyment does not cancel the earlier objection. The scene reveals special attachment and asymmetric reasoning, without establishing mutual romance or blanket domestic access. (`BA:bond:10067:005:u:0037–0070`.)

Recon scenes also combine care with overreach. She positively recognizes that people need rest and that emergency work is chosen for the sake of tomorrow. Yet her expansive protective service offers—back washing, a warmed bed and contact—meet a stop and continuing pressure. The equipment encounter provides a different endpoint: the adult maintains a safety refusal, she sulks, and the narrated head-patting finally ends her stubborn insistence. A boundary can be accepted without a universal transformation in her habits. (`BA:bond:16019:002:u:0014–0047`; `:005:u:0039–0051`.)

Her relationship with Sensei therefore contains trust, mentorship, dependency, care, affection and flirtatious or romantic coding at specific scenes. It remains an adult/student relation with institutional asymmetry. Neither the gift's couple teasing, the hosted special day nor the dawn wish turns all intimacy into an established reciprocal romance. Nor should that coding be removed because the relationship also performs mentorship. The permitted alternatives remain a choice space: paired resource answers in the recon MomoTalk diverge and reconverge; alternative hobby responses express different adult positions. They are not cumulative acts by a single historical player.

The monograph's positive conclusion is modest but meaningful. Sensei can welcome Toki's company and craft, encourage a wider ordinary life and refuse some demands. Toki can ask, tease, accept, sulk, apologize and keep secrets. A good account preserves the particular exchanges and the unprinted durable outcomes, rather than turning the adult into the sole author of her personhood or the student into an automatic obedient partner.

### Rio, Himari and Eimi

Rio is the original source of assigned authority and equipment, then a reported giver of freedom and a direct rejecter of expendability. These relationships collectively broaden Toki's available reasons for action. They do not establish that every peer interaction is through Sensei, that each coworker is equally close, or that one rescue permanently removes the possibility of solitary work. The actual persons, recipients and modes are part of the explanation.

## 9. Japanese language and written voice

Toki's written register repeatedly combines `私`, `先生`, polite endings, formal acknowledgment and role vocabulary with exaggerated propositions, ellipsis, correction and teasing. Courtesy is sufficiently stable to be recognizable across contexts, but its use is not evidence of unconditional submission. `リオ様`, `ヒマリ部長` and `ネル先輩` distinguish relations; the politeness surrounding a request can coexist with a refusal to accept the other's first answer.
