## 1. Core thesis

Koyuki wants activities to offer something she can care about: surprise, a pleasurable challenge, useful work in a comfortable place, a companion, or an object whose meaning she chooses to keep. Her ability to calculate does not make repetitive calculation a wanted life. Her interest in luck does not make every arbitrary outcome enjoyable. The most adequate account therefore begins with how she values an activity and who participates in it, then asks what she actually does, what costs she transfers and how the encounter ends. This explains more than either a lazy troublemaker or an endlessly delighted genius. [P23 L205–214/245–253][private]

There is a serious contradiction within that repertoire. She wants freedom from other people's rules and scrutiny while sometimes treating their information, money, time or supplies as available for her use. A local privacy concession can be followed by a loophole that renews intrusion. A desired VIP life can coexist with admitted spending of others' money. An invitation to research a drink can become shared-resource waste. These are concrete acts and recipient problems, not mere comic decorations. At the same time, her refusal of pursuit, fear in a dark passage, resistance to bodily restraint and worry about losing a valued adult's patience remain her own morally and psychologically consequential responses. Being responsible for one harm does not supply assent to everything subsequently done to her. [P23 L207/251–253][private] [E806 E008/E010–011][e806] [E842 E006–008/E013][party]

The ordinary material establishes positive capacities as well as limits. She can finish office work quickly, prepare a game attempt, reserve a seat, concede a cost, enjoy failure with someone, keep a clover and share a later home-game victory. She can also forget an errand, spend all her allowance without clearing a game, fail an all-night plan, press an exception after apologizing or continue to reproach a nursing adult. No single successful ending cancels the failed or contested endings. The recurring literary problem is how an interesting, wanted activity becomes a shared one without making another person's participation or resources automatic. [P23 L208–214/245–253][private]

Her public cipher contribution gives that problem a consequential institutional form. Yuuka and Noa urgently recall her because of a capacity useful to the operation.
