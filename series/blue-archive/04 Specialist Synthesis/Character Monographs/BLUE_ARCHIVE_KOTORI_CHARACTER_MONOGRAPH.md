## 1. Core thesis

Kotori wants understanding to be something people share: she enjoys the face that lights up when a listener grasps a subject, the excitement of discussing it together, and the possibility of being useful through what she knows. The difficulty is that a subject's explainability does not establish a recipient's willingness, and mastery of information does not make either her body or her personal answer immediately ready. Her most revealing scenes retain these distinctions instead of resolving them into one joke. Students ask her to stop; a willing listener enjoys an hour of digression. A promised account of somebody else's feelings becomes a guarded secret. Asked about her own likes, she requests time. At sunset she positively refuses explanation. These are several kinds of relation to another person, including the right to withhold what cannot or need not be explained. [16002:002/003/005/006/015;10076:006;L201–245](../../02%20Sequential%20Readings/BOND/BLUE_ARCHIVE_KOTORI_PHASE2_PRIVATE_CONTEXTUAL_ACCOUNT_20261008.md)

The public engineer is connected to this private repertoire without being its simple enlarged version. Kotori knows the cost of a prototype and resists its loss, shares her club's delight in repurposing an object, helps conceal an altered function, reports exhaustion, and returns with her colleagues when they detect something suspicious. These acts make recipients and costs necessary parts of her characterization. Her knowledge can serve company, protect a project, expose a material limit or rationalize a decision that another person did not authorize. The chocolate story supplies a particularly strong contrary endpoint: she changes the means of her explanation into a carefully made gift, prepares to leave, then actually shares eating and talk when invited. The thesis is therefore an uneven practice of mutual understanding, accompanied by her own ordinary wants and by other people's independent power to welcome, refuse or contest what she offers. ## 3. Public self, private self, and interpretation

Publicly, Kotori often makes a situation intelligible by providing causes, specifications or context. She reports the space-battleship project, the railgun's budget share, weight and recoil. She explains the festival's electronic fuse and admits its hurried manufacture. In Final she asks whether Veritas controls the anomalous AMAS after Himari has denied involvement. These are meaningful forms of attention: she notices resource limits and uncertain control rather than simply admiring a device. But a number in her account remains a speaker report, a question remains a question, and engineering membership does not grant complete knowledge of an opaque system. The public register identifies what she regards as worth clarifying; it does not certify the physical truth of everything she says.
