## 1. Core thesis

**Mari's characteristic difficulty is finding language in which receiving rest, attention and enjoyment can belong to her life without disqualifying her from the vocation she wants. Her strongest changes occur when she names a particular wish, receives a particular answer and retains the ability to decide its form.** This is a literary argument about the selected repertoire, not a diagnosis of compulsive self-denial. She already finds pleasure in prayer, enjoys company and makes effective practical decisions. The material repeatedly gives her reasons to accept something for herself; it also gives her reasons to say no. A monograph that equated growth with accepting every offered gift or every protective intervention would reproduce the pressure it was supposed to understand.

In B23008E006 she wants occasional undemanding time near Sensei. In E015 she admits buying a white corsage because it was cute, keeps its pledge meaning and declines another purchase. AthleticE003 contains genuine festival pleasure despite exhaustion; E005 establishes a continued exercise habit. IdolE003 preserves her objection to excessive protection alongside a freely accepted escort. Event841 ends with her own happy leadership assent, a genuinely enjoyable week, a chosen return to ordinary Sister work and an emphatic limit on circulating photographs. Those are compatible decisions. None needs to be translated into a hidden rejection of faith, a permanent idol identity or an unlimited willingness to receive care. [B23008E006 u31–58/T230080340; E015 u29–58/T230080460; B10054E003 u30–42/E005 u16–58; B10105E003 u17–49; BA:event:841:010–011.]

Her listening practice provides a useful starting distinction. She describes sharing pain as sometimes enabling people to recover for themselves and declines the grander title of adviser. That formulation gives the recipient a process of their own. Yet the sources offer actual limits rather than guaranteed reciprocity. ## 10. Competing readings
