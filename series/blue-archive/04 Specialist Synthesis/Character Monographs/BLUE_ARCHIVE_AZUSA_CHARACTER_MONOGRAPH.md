## 1. Core thesis

Azusa's strongest sustained question is how a person trained to anticipate the destruction of everything can choose particular things worth keeping. Her tactical preparation is real, often effective and sometimes harmful. Her enjoyment is equally real: a toy, a café, a fragile castle, a panoramic view, a quiet interval without a task. These are not simply signs that she has become fit for a new institution. They are parts of the life she chooses, including pleasures that neither improve security nor demonstrate an irreversible recovery. The monograph therefore reads her preparedness alongside her capacity to value, ask, refuse, revise and remain with others. It does not make training the hidden explanation of every delight. [P01; M02–M04; E01]

This is more demanding than a conversion from a nihilistic combatant to an ordinary student. Azusa explicitly opposes the course assigned to her by Arius and protects Nagisa, yet the protective counterplan includes close-range gunfire, incapacitation and traps. She receives a formal Trinity-student declaration, yet Saori still attempts to erase her acquired study and relationships. Hifumi's friendship and the other students' defense help her continue; they do not make force harmless or permanently secure her belonging. Her successful official examination is an achieved educational result, not a controlled measurement of a teacher's cure. [M02:E014–E019; M03:E001/E013–E025]

Nor is her ordinary life a soft epilogue that can be cut without changing the account. The last cute chocolate, consumed too quickly for her liking, reveals that choosing to give does not surrender every wish concerning its reception. Her later laughter and accepted Skullman play achieve company locally; they do not erase the preceding mismatch. A request merely to sit and then a call made because she wanted contact establish wants without a mission. Azusa's ability to cherish vulnerable things matters even when those things do not survive, solve a crisis or produce measurable growth. [L07:u0011–0033; P01:summer E006]

## 3. Public and private self

The public and private materials share a tension between preparation and valued exposure. Publicly Azusa must decide what to risk for the people and life she has acquired. Privately she has to negotiate less spectacular vulnerability: accepting an outing, asking where she is being taken, wishing an edible gift would be looked at longer, calling without a useful reason. Her defensive vocabulary can therefore outlast an actual danger without becoming meaningless. Azusa also regulates the adult relationship. A false warning framed as a joke produces a weapon response; she asks the adult to stop, and an apology follows. ## 10. Competing readings

The strongest alternative is a recovery narrative: friendship and adult reassurance gradually let Azusa leave a destructive doctrine behind. It has genuine support in her self-chosen countercourse, formal admission, fulfilled gift, willingness to enjoy and repeated choice of company. It becomes inadequate when it assigns every private route a later place in one calendar or treats joy as permanent cure. Firing at shop people, disputed threat readings, route uncertainty and Ichika's concern remain part of the present repertoire. The account warrants changed local decisions and new possibilities, not an irreversible finished transformation.

A second reading treats ordinary pleasure as camouflage over a fundamentally unchanged combat identity. Tactical words, continued preparation and serious force support its concern. But it cannot explain away the actual chosen café, taskless sitting, wanted toys, slower looking and rebuilding without discarding the ends the sources explicitly value. Absence of a printed hobby field is not stronger than those actions. An account that preserves vulnerability and continuing caution need not deny pleasure to remain critical.

A third reading makes preparedness and benevolent motive sufficient justification. It can point to effective defense and to past danger as reasons that Azusa's caution is intelligible. Its error is transferring those reasons to every recipient and outcome. Nagisa's gunfire, shop people, beach opponents and a worried Ichika require separate judgments. A fourth, opposite reading reduces Azusa to danger inflicted on others; it neglects independent protection, explicit revision and the scope of the completed academic and social acts. The stronger thesis keeps both the chosen life and the cost of methods available for criticism.
