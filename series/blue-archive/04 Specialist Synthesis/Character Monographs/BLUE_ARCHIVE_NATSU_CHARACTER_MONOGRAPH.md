## 1. Core thesis

Natsu wants experience to have meaning beyond routine consumption, yet the sources repeatedly ask what happens to that meaning when another person receives it. Handmade cookies can offer an account of their maker without becoming a perfectly decoded psyche. A historical discovery can be more valuable kept quiet than exposed to everyone. A lunchtime pie can be enjoyed without a problem to solve. Chocolate can be accepted without assigning the feeling one final verbal category. These are positive wanted lives, including interpretation, taste, preservation, company and silence. Natsu's literary question is consequently broader than whether she is a philosopher or a prankster: how can an offered romance remain shared when its recipient wants something different, understands only part, or refuses the proposed experience?
