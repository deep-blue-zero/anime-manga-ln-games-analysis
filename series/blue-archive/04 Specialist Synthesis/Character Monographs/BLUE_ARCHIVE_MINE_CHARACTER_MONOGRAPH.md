## 1. Core thesis

Mine expands rescue to include a puppy, a child, paperwork, food, a peaceful rest, a colleague's supposedly mistaken justice and an offender's correction. That breadth can recognize needs a narrow combat role would miss. Its central difficulty is the warrant for moving from a worthy end to a particular intervention: whose account has been heard, whose refusal can change the method, and which person's benefit is being used to settle another person's cost? The sources give actual benefits, actual damage, received objections and withheld endings. None warrants making every use of her rescue vocabulary morally equivalent.

The normal patrol makes the distinction unusually clear. A puppy is retrieved, treated and reunited; a child is protected from a truck. The wall and vehicle owners separately object to damage. Mine apologizes but says she would break the wall again if necessary and continue apologizing until forgiven. The child's gratitude does not provide the owner's consent, and promised apology is not completed restitution. Conversely, the actual rescues cannot be dismissed because liability remains unresolved. (`BA:bond:10058:002:scene:001:u:0010`–`:0066`, especially u0041–0052.)

Other sources show that a bodily or personal boundary can have a real effect. An explicit stop interrupts her proposed self-wounding demonstration without destroying her collecting pleasure. The photography project produces photographs, but her opposition keeps them privately held rather than publicized. She withdraws a proposed confinement of Sensei before custody occurs. These are serious contrary cases to a captain who never permits refusal. They coexist with the Christmas assault, a prank-caller correction whose ending is deliberately withheld, and sudden Valentine feeding without shown prior assent. (Normal E003/E005/E006; idol16016 E002; `BA:event:819:005`–`:011`; `BA:event:810:092`.)

Mine also has wanted selves whose value is not exhausted by helping. Collecting fascinates her; tea soothes her; she likes beautiful and cute things, wants clothes and memories to remain, seeks an excuse to meet Sensei, chooses a quiet river and wants to be remembered on her birthday. These particulars make her requests, refusals and feared conflicts intelligible. The strongest account is therefore a person negotiating desirable forms of life and limited care, sometimes receiving correction and sometimes imposing a label that outruns inquiry. It is a situated literary thesis, not a medical certification or a rule for predicting her next act.
