## 1. Core thesis and the limits of a festival identity

Umika makes celebration a way of paying attention. She notices preparations, saves objects, revises a personal ranking, wants other people to enjoy themselves, and gives a familiar person something deliberately made for him. These pursuits have value before they produce business success or avert a crisis. Her private café rest, the anticipation of a parade, and the pleasure of preparing a small festival cannot be treated as preliminary illustrations of a more important heroic function. The same sources also show that wanting a festival is insufficient to know what a visitor wants, to control a timetable, or to guarantee safety. Her characteristic tension is between expansive invitation and the particular people, materials and limits that invitation must accommodate.

The strongest bounded thesis is therefore that Umika tries to make meaningful occasions for others while learning, locally and imperfectly, that the meaning she supplies is not the same as the reception they give.
