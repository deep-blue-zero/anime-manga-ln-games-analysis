## 1. Core thesis: enabling a wanted life can coexist with pressure

Kasumi's central literary problem is how she makes a life of chosen work and companionship possible while repeatedly treating another person's resistance as something to work around. She enables Meg to pursue digging rather than office, values quiet study and reading, and appreciates people who freely return to spend time with her. She also proposes coercive transport, teaches a suspicious authority-encounter script, shifts a commissioned excavation against the client's objection, and uses leverage against Ichika. Her care is particular and real; the available evidence does not make it a universal permission for her methods. The most defensible portrait preserves both her capacity to ask what another person wants and her temptation to decide the conditions under which that person must answer. [G3501–3502; M2.6, M2.20; E830:005–009; E833:003; E848:008–011]

The positive case begins with intrinsic ends. Hours of silent study beside the adult's paperwork, preparatory shopping, seaside walking, a declined redundant swimsuit and chosen reading are valuable activities in their own right. Their importance does not await a major plot consequence, a successful spring, or proof that Kasumi has become harmless. The adult's refusal to grant continuing use of Schale likewise matters independently: enjoying a visit is compatible with a boundary against future access. Ordinary company supplies evidence of a life she wants, rather than an excuse that must redeem every destructive act. [K03:P01–P03; K05:P01; K09:P01; K11; E848:011]

The strongest counterpressure is also intrinsic to her characterization. Ichika's denied similarity and denied friendship are genuine recipient positions; continued cooperation does not erase them. Meg's first-person wanted-work account is stronger evidence of her preference than a member's speculation about surrendering the director's title. Hina's refusal of the scripted bag and thanks for a separate ice pop differentiate contexts, rather than certify every gift or every enforcement act. Kasumi's monograph therefore asks where enabling leaves another person's answer open, where persuasion becomes leverage, and which outcomes are actually completed. It does not require one permanent benevolent or manipulative essence. [G3502:u0032–0065,u0079–0087; E830:007–009; E848:004,008–009]
