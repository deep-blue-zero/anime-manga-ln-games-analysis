## 1. Core thesis

Serika wants her contribution to count, her commitments to be understood and her refusals to retain force. Earning, paying, staying and protecting are ways of asserting that she can answer for herself and for people she values. Yet that ethic also creates pressure: help becomes a debt, rest threatens usefulness, concern can arrive as teasing, and other people sometimes take her affection as permission they have not received. Her strongest longitudinal pattern is the negotiation of contribution and recognition, rather than a simple movement from hostility to obedience.

The first-wage bag makes the pattern tangible. Serika bought it for practical use and has maintained it through school, work, sport and gun care. She minimizes its importance while repeatedly calling it a favorite. The repairer recognizes that care before restoring the strap and torn corner. Serika's pleasure concerns an object carrying her own effort and history. Its value does not depend on helping pay Abydos's debt. The later concealed arrangement between Sensei and the proprietor makes useful care possible while leaving her knowledge and the precise payment unprinted. (`BA:bond:13008:020:scene:001:u:0023–0082`; retain anomalous `u:0083` in its canonical position.)

The cleaned classroom and kept swimsuit offer a related distinction. Serika accepts help, sets expectations of genuine effort and proposes a ramen treat. The work finishes early. She then elects to keep swimwear because it recalls a resort visit with everyone. This personal valuation is actual; wearing it on request is not. Clothing persuasion and diminutive praise lead her to withdraw the meal promise and demand an end to laughter. Shared work, gratitude, memory and refusal coexist within one encounter. (`BA:bond:20036:005:scene:001:u:0001–0060`; whole `BA:momotalk:20036:thread:200360230`.)

MAIN establishes why recognition matters so intensely. Serika privately intends her wages for school interest payments, then fears in captivity that disappearance will make her friends think she deserted them. Rescue restores access to that community without proving her objections to adult familiarity mistaken. Later she thanks Sensei and permits tomorrow's contact while preserving her right to judge the adult's value to Abydos. The person being rescued is already working, assessing danger and defining what staying means. (`BA:main:001:001:005`, especially `u:0105–0110`; `:006:scene:001:u:0059–0062`; `:007:scene:003:u:0035–0043`.)

This ethic is fallible. Serika can presume someone else's availability, conceal the intended destination of wages, trust a profit promise too quickly or pursue an opponent beyond necessary protection. She also serves unfamiliar visitors graciously, limits miraculous claims and refuses a specialist career others propose. A serious account must hold her kindness, credulity, demands and boundaries together. Her wanted holidays, meals, play, clothes and company are intrinsic goods; they are not lesser evidence awaiting a crisis to make them meaningful.

## 4. School and club belonging

For Serika, belonging is practiced through staying, work and concern for how others read that commitment. Her fear in captivity is not simply fear of dying. It includes being unable to correct the belief that she left like others. This gives institutional affiliation an interpersonal form: the committee is where her conduct can be recognized and where permanent misrecognition would hurt. It does not establish that students who actually left were traitors, or that loyalty requires every possible sacrifice.

The first-wage bag and classroom memory show what school belonging contains. A maintained possession spans school and work; the remembered resort includes everyone. Neither has to become a payment instrument to matter. The classroom is also a lived space needing wiping, harboring dust and forgotten belongings. Serika undertakes that ordinary maintenance when peers have other commitments. It is an actual contribution without proving she is the sole person who cares for the room. Shared help speeds the task; she remains entitled to reject a different demand afterward. (`BA:bond:13008:020`; `BA:momotalk:20036:thread:200360230`; `BA:bond:20036:005`.)

## 6. Crisis behavior and its limits

Serika's courage does not guarantee sufficient capacity. C001E005 shows immediate engagement with familiar attackers; an arranged ambush and heavier support overwhelm her. Their targeting identifies committee membership, making the institution's pressure personal. Captivity leaves her reasoning about escape and communication even while fearing separation. Being saved does not invalidate that competence or establish that her earlier privacy boundary caused the attack. The attackers' coercion is their act; no lesson requiring her to surrender autonomy follows from vulnerability.

The emergency rescue introduces an ethical difference of context. Accessing a location for a missing captured student serves restoration of agency; following a present student's rejected ordinary destination does not have the same justification. Yet the rescue choices preserve alternatives, including a safety rationale versus concealment language and stalker versus hero jokes. No single flawless protagonist can be manufactured by choosing one favorable option and pretending it is the entire interface. The tactical system is named and recovery succeeds, but precision, safety margins and engineering remain uninspected. (`BA:main:001:001:006:choice:002,005`; complete V001 working account.)

C003 demonstrates that crisis authorship extends beyond this early rescue. Serika refuses Nonomi's sacrifice, insists against being left out, prepares with Shiroko to dismount and observes the changed sky. The evidence distinguishes direct speech, readiness and observation from a performed jump, independently solved mechanism or guaranteed outcome. Her endpoint digging report also remains narrower than an exhaustive survey. The final Binah approach and both Shirokos' cooperation open a response; the outcome is not supplied by that endpoint. (`BA:main:001:003:011`, `:014`, `:022`, `:032`, `:042`, `:043`.)

Private protective action adds contrary evidence about Serika's methods. In the bag scene she hides Sensei against stray bullets, then pursues retreating attackers until the adult intervenes. Protection is a good purpose, but the need to stop escalation and her initial resistance show that anger can narrow attention. The prior escort thread also interprets reassurance as permission and preempts refusal. Care can be unilateral when she gives it, just as adult care can be intrusive when she receives it. Serika's most useful crisis continuity is therefore concern joined to participation. She acts, receives help, returns to peers and continues to judge methods. Her sacrifice-prone labor and immediate combat are not invincible safeguards; her doubts and requests for care do not make her a dependent exception. Claims of complete medical recovery, trauma resolution, legal rescue authority, inherited contract validity or permanent safety exceed this monograph's source boundary.
