## 1. Core thesis

## 10. Competing readings

A plausible reading treats Hifumi as the ordinary moral center whose modesty conceals extraordinary goodness. It can appeal to her refusal to spy, gift, care for attackers and intervention for Azusa. It fails if moral goodness consumes her preferences or makes every costly action admirable. The cherished pillow remains hers; the rare product is urgently wanted; the free plush is refused during a distressing contest; fatigue persists after shared pleasure. These particulars let a good act be good without turning every other desire into disposable evidence of the same virtue.

A rival reading treats the innocent surface as camouflage for Faust, a hidden criminal mastermind. Real armed participation and later deniable support give it a superficially suggestive route. The consequential public arguments decisively distinguish operation design, imposed leader role, anxious mitigation and later aid. Hifumi knows things and can act; knowledge does not prove that she devised an operation for which others symbolically expose her. Ichika’s tank-thief nickname and Nagisa’s intelligence likewise remain attributed labels rather than adjudicated essence.

Another reading makes her a victim of every stronger personality, with agency visible only when Sensei rescues it. The local withdrawal, documentary caution, pillow refusal, gift choice, peer intervention and later support refute that total passivity. Its valuable warning survives: warmth and usefulness can coexist with pressure, and later thanks do not supply earlier assent. Finally, a wholly cheerful friendship account is corrected by tired driving, missed swimming, cancelled plans and other people's independent objections. The strongest joined thesis therefore preserves chosen company without making it compulsory happiness or unrestricted consent.
