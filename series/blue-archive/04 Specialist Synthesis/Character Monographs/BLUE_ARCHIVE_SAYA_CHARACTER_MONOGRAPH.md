## 1. Core thesis: curiosity is a wanted life, and its recipient remains a person

Saya's curiosity cannot be reduced to a useful school service, a gag about failed medicine or a collection of harms. She wants to remain a high-school girl, continue research indefinitely and enjoy esteem (BA:bond:20002:002:s2u6–8). She also wants the familiar noodle order, particular cake, quiet tea, a rematch, fashion, discovery outside the laboratory and someone with whom to enjoy them. The research wish and those smaller ends belong to the same repertoire. In826005 she explains why an impossible goal can matter: trying to solve it may discover other things, while the researcher should distinguish means from ends (u76–83). That is a meaningful philosophy of inquiry even when no immortality is achieved. It also supplies a standard against which to read her own substitutions of a successful-looking result for the promised benefit.

The tension is not merely between competence and incompetence. A gold-colored body becomes a new success criterion after an immortality claim; unchanged weight becomes success while drug recipients complain of hunger and weakness. A mistaken sauce is praised and eaten, yet the intended fix was not what she made and the Japanese afterstate is nearly a week of stomach burning. Her curiosity has agency and pleasures of its own, but a recipient's experience cannot be settled by her preferred measurement of success (20002E002/E005;20006E005). This is why the person receiving an experiment is central to the monograph. The recipients can refuse, complain, enjoy company, need help or want a benefit without understanding every consequence. Those positions are different from becoming evidence that Saya has already been vindicated.

Care is neither absent nor uniformly accomplished. Saya values Nezusuke as family-like, searches when he disappears and experiences relief; she also doses deceptively, demands endorsement, qualifies an apology with an entitlement to serious experiments and tries to restrain him. She apologizes to the teacher, shares a real meal and thanks sustained help without thereby ending further sample requests or pressure. Public840 explicitly distinguishes her freely supplied requested antidotes from Kai's coercive dependency, while retaining her troublemaking and the teacher's acknowledged difficulties. That comparison matters precisely because it does not make the two researchers ethically identical or make every Saya experiment safe. Her positive acts and recipient costs have to remain specific (20002E003/E005/E009;840010/015).

The resulting thesis is bounded: Saya inhabits inquiry as a desired identity and a social practice, whose warmth, discoveries and practical care coexist with failures of attention to particular recipients. Rest, play, food and recognition are intrinsic goods rather than compensation for being flawed or proofs of reform. Her actual willingness to supply requested help and acknowledge some limitations gives a serious alternative to a wholly unfeeling portrait. The harms and unfinished repairs give an equally serious alternative to harmless genius. No complete person model, chemistry, diagnosis, empirical technique or permanent moral change follows from this literary argument.
