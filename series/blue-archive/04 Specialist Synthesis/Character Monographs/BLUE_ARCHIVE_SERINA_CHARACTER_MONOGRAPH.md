## 1. Core thesis

Serina's distinguishing achievement is to make care particular. A child needs a bandage, an injured hand makes opening a gift difficult, an exhausted adult needs time away from work, and a patient worries that a school's nurse might refuse him. She notices these differences rather than treating everyone as an interchangeable occasion to enact benevolence. Yet her skill at noticing need does not guarantee that she knows what another person wants, may do whatever seems beneficial, or has repaired the harm her group causes. The strongest account holds together three propositions: her care is often useful and sincerely enjoyed; she wants gifts, company, recognition and pleasure of her own; and some encounters leave recipients' refusal, knowledge or injury inadequately answered. None can serve as a convenient explanation that cancels the others.

The park bond makes the first two propositions inseparable. Children's reports establish balloon retrieval, play and help with injury; Serina says other people's happiness pleases her, then realizes that concentrating on children has made her inattentive to Sensei's needs. His ordinary wish for juice becomes a concrete gift rather than an abstract lesson in protecting a powerful adult. Her apology and his alternative replies matter: reassurance that an adult does not need attention produces her objection to that exemption; acknowledging her labor produces gratitude and another apology. These are distinct routes into a shared ending, not one ideal speech by Sensei. Care here widens through a recipient encounter. It does not begin only when the plot assigns a medical emergency. (`BA:bond:26003:002:scene:001:u:0009`–`:0015`, `:0016`–`:0027`, `:choice:001`, `:0028`–`:0037`; complete linked thread `BA:momotalk:26003:thread:260030010`.)

The picnic reverses the direction of provision. Sensei prepares food and a place for Serina, who asks why he has done all this for her and finally enjoys a shared meal. Her stated happiness and the complete post-encounter thread support genuinely received care, not merely a caretaker rewarded with another assignment. The adult's false cry of pain is also an adverse method: he recruits her responsiveness by deception. The pleasant endpoint does not make that method a general permission to manipulate caregivers. Christmas then gives Serina further intrinsic ends: scarf warmth, a wish for company, delight in a holiday role, and an awkwardly withdrawn wish to spend time alone with Sensei in written costume material. These are different forms of wanting, with different actual endpoints. (`BA:bond:26003:006:scene:001:u:0001`–`:0004`, `:0021`–`:0042`; `BA:momotalk:26003:thread:260030250`; `BA:bond:10056:002:scene:001:u:0054`–`:0076`; costume `1900908201`, lobby 004–006.)

The contrary evidence is consequential. Christmas massage proceeds under pressure; a night-care scene declares that Sensei has no right to refuse proposed stroking; the unused hospital floor contains force after an intruder's surrender and a later public denial inconsistent with what readers have seen. These qualify an account of good care at precisely the point where another person's body or knowledge matters. They do not establish that Serina always enjoys injury, that every request is coercive, or that she secretly causes every problem she treats. Her ordinary feeding limit, explicit invitations, self-questioning and cooperation with other nurses supply real counterexamples. The monograph therefore treats her as a fallible participant in particular relationships, not a person-wide diagnosis or a prediction rule. (Christmas `10056:003`, scene 002 u0025–0041; normal `26003:003`, u0042–0048; `BA:group:1702`, u0027–0035, and `BA:group:1703`, scene 002.)

## 3. Public and private selves

Serina's public reputation often receives its strongest praise from people who know only part of an encounter. Patients call the nurses angelic or describe Mine as a docile companion.
