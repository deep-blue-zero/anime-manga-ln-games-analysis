# T7S_B0158 — Poe-Poe Pandemic

## Result and entering boundary

Ayumu's enthusiasm for a proposed stage with Sisara and NI+CORA is interrupted by a high fever. She insists that a maid cannot rest in front of her masters, but Musubi and Sisara make rest itself part of responsible care. While she sleeps, Sisara, Musubi and the Player involuntarily begin using her “poe-kyun” speech; Susu and Coney later do likewise. The group guesses at maid power or a transformed cold virus, but no cause is established. They prioritize Ayumu's actual illness, take turns caring for her, and observe both fever and odd speech subside. The Player then becomes ill. Ayumu cares for him, remembering the others' warmth and naming reciprocal care—not one-way self-exhaustion—as her maid path. Her claim that she infected him is not medically verified; neither is his denial.

## Factual reconstruction by work, illness and care

### EV-B0158-001 — Ayumu's maid language is intentional craft

Ayumu tells Sisara that her `ぽえきゅん語` is an individually composed expression of her maid-service ideal, chosen to warm others. Her claims that she “restrains” maid power and that a maid-only secret art might have consequences are playful self-descriptions, not independently validated metaphysics. Sisara says she does not understand the style. This opening matters when the other speakers later cannot control their borrowed wording. [A:P2–16]

### EV-B0158-002 — planned collaboration, not completed performance

The Player proposes Ayumu/Sisara with NI+CORA (Musubi/Susu), and all four agree in principle. Ayumu proposes a maid-themed play and costumes; Musubi qualifies that ordinary maid clothes might be acceptable, Susu is interested, and the Player says audience flower-stamp distribution may exceed the performance slot. He will consider the idea, not approve a final show plan. Their discussion is interrupted before production. [A:P17–64]

### EV-B0158-003 — fever overrides Ayumu's duty claim

Sisara notices Ayumu acting unusually, checks her forehead and reports a strong fever. Musubi sees flushing, and the group moves her to the women's dorm. Sisara says medicine was given; the Player has called nearby hospitals but found them closed and says he could take her farther if needed. Ayumu coughs and resists resting because a maid ought not stop serving. Musubi answers that caring for her health is also work, and Sisara tells her to sleep; Ayumu complies. This is in-story lay response, not an endorsed medical protocol. [A:P58–95]

### EV-B0158-004 — involuntary speech and uncertain mechanism

After Ayumu sleeps, Sisara, Musubi and the Player unexpectedly use Ayumu's speech patterns and voice their embarrassment. Ayumu briefly wakes, interprets it as the spread of her maid power, and sleeps again. The others review temporal proximity and quote her earlier “maid power” talk, but they admit the cause makes little sense. The speech change is observed within the chapter; biological contagion, actual supernatural power and a deliberate impersonation are not established by those guesses. [A:P98–108; B:P1–26]

### EV-B0158-005 — late-arriving observers join the gag

Susu and Coney return from buying care supplies. Susu initially laughs and assumes the others are playing a joke. When she points out that she and Coney should also be affected if the story were true, both start speaking similarly. Sisara then hypothesizes that Ayumu's maid power changed a cold virus; Musubi imagines wider spread. This is character speculation, not a medically demonstrated pathogen, transmission route or public outbreak. [B:P28–50; C:P1–20]

### EV-B0158-006 — the group serves the sick maid

Ayumu coughs. Even while speaking oddly, the group shifts from self-consciousness to her discomfort and divides care. Ayumu senses warmth during sleep; afterward Musubi says her color improves, Sisara says the fever seems down, and Player/Coney plan watch shifts. Ayumu later reports recovery, and peers say their speech returned to normal at about the same time. Co-occurrence does not prove the mechanism; no actual show, medical clearance or perfect outcome is demonstrated. [C:P21–56]

### EV-B0158-007 — Player becomes ill and care is reassigned

The Player is then down with a cold, according to Musubi. Musubi/Susu, Sisara and Coney have other work; Coney asks the newly recovered Ayumu to care for him until they return. She accepts. The episode thus moves service toward the person who earlier stayed by her, but the Player's illness is not clinically traced to Ayumu. [C:P57–74]

### EV-B0158-008 — Ayumu reframes her maid path

Ayumu changes his ice pillow and says caring for him is what she can do now. She thinks she passed him the cold; he attributes it to his own poor self-care, which she regards as a kind lie. She remembers Musubi, Susu, Sisara and Coney alternating at her bedside and the Player staying through the end. Though she first calls receiving service a “bad maid” feeling, she admits being happy to be cared for and decides that seeking his smile through reciprocal service is part of her maid path. He praises her and expects to improve. No exclusive bond, romantic declaration, certain infection chain or future health outcome follows. [C:P75–98]

## Interpretation and alternatives

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0158-001.** Ayumu's invented language is intentional relationship work, but illness makes her role standard (“maid must not rest”) unsustainable. Musubi/Sisara's correction and Ayumu's later gratitude make receiving care compatible with service rather than a failure of identity. [A:P2–16,58–108; C:P75–98]

Rival: because she speaks whimsically, all her service is empty play, or because she is devoted, she should work through fever. Both ignore observed care needs and her explicit later redefinition.

**BOUNDED_INFERENCE — CL-B0158-002.** Multiple speakers' involuntary wording changes are narratively real, but explanations by maid power or altered cold virus remain hypotheses inside a comic frame. The Player's later cold and the speech reset when Ayumu recovers cannot establish microbial or supernatural causation. [A:P98–108; B:P1–50; C:P1–60,75–83]

Rival: “pandemic” is a clinical outbreak, or every changed utterance is deliberate teasing. Neither accounts for the characters' surprise without adding a mechanism the source withholds.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0158-003.** The shared speech embarrassment becomes less important than the sick girl's actual discomfort; the ensemble puts care before a hypothetical public spread. The chapter does not supply an infection-control standard, physician judgment or evidence the care caused the fever to fall. [C:P14–56]

Rival: focusing on Ayumu proves medical strategy or that possible infection never matters. Their choice is shown; its epidemiological merits are not.

**BOUNDED_INFERENCE — CL-B0158-004.** The maid-stage proposal has assent but unresolved costume, audience and timing details before illness interrupts it. Agreement to explore is not a performed NI+CORA collaboration or authorization of every costume and fan-interaction idea. [A:P17–64]

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0158-005.** Ayumu's return care is a chosen answer to care she remembers receiving, not evidence she owes labor while still sick. The Player's illness explanation and her counterclaim are untested, while their mutual warmth need not be promoted into exclusive romance. [C:P48–98]
