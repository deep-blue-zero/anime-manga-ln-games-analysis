Second, deliciousness and recognition are different results. The first meal is good and enjoyed; the second answers a remembered family taste. This separates effort from successful knowledge of the recipient without treating effort as worthless. Recipes found online, regional assumptions and practiced techniques need her judgment to determine whether the intended connection has been made.

Third, food is received through labor that is both practical and relational. Hime searches and reframes the aim, Sumire teaches, Miwako learns, Musubi cooks and Player cleans and coordinates. Shirayuki advises, encourages, worries and judges. Her declaration of another home arises from this particular care, not simply from an illustration of a pot or an institutional label.

The strongest local inference is that home here can be extended by attention to someone's prior attachments. The rival reading is that Nanasta merely substitutes its own meal and absorbs the child into a new family. The first failed taste match, continuing mother attribution and “also home” recognition favor extension over erasure. This remains a chapter-local account, without a total judgment of Nanasta's arrangements.

The ending complicates a simple gift economy. Shirayuki wants other residents to receive their own home meals, then imagines herself giving daily food to a special person. The present achievement creates possible futures at different scales. Neither future has happened yet. The work must still be chosen, learned and shared; a sentimental promise does not finish those practical requirements.
