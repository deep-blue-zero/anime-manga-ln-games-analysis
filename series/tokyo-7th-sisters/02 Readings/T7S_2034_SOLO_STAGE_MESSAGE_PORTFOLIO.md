## What changes when the result changes

### Who can claim a solo success?

The solo stage repeatedly invites characters to distribute credit. Kyoko seeks to blame the manager's coaching after a bad result, directs the manager to praise her after a good result, and offers the manager qualified praise after the perfect one. Rona gives collective rehearsal a different role. Rona credits practice with the others for the good response and identifies completing the dance without falling as her own mark of success in the perfect response. Shizuka credits backstage support after promising to dance admirably by herself. Xiao connects the strongest result to confidence gained with help from the manager. Each source supports a different attribution by the speaker.

Hitoha and Futaba challenge the assumption that a solo menu route implies one performer. Their joint owner slot alternates direct address and visibly marked unison. In the bad branch the two diagnose a failure of coordination and agree to practice. In the good branch they read progress through each other. In the perfect branch the voices say the result was not theirs alone and thank the manager. Emoko provides a resistant example. After the good score she questions what her addressee did to deserve sharing in the pleasure. At perfect she credits the audience's judgment while ranking the addressee below it. A model that assigns gratitude to every successful response would lose both Emoko's boundary around credit and Kyoko's qualified transition.
