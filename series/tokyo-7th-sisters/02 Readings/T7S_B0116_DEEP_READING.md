## Factual reconstruction by actor knowledge and frame

### EV-B0116-001 — a hypothetical solo brand, not Kajika's request

The Player thinks about Kajika's possible future solo promotion. He finds an `ordinary classmate` image too plain and a sexy angle incongruous, while Coney asks whether a solo-debut project actually exists; the Player says not yet. He thinks Kajika lacks initiative and confidence because she is usually with others. Coney responds with `cosmic idol` and calls Kajika humanity's first, which the Player initially rejects as absurd. This is private managerial categorization plus Coney's pitch, not Kajika's self-defined aspiration or approved plan. [A:P0–20]

### EV-B0116-002 — inside the staged space frame

After a discontinuity the Player and Kajika find themselves in a starry cockpit-like room. Kajika wears a space suit and says Coney told her she would be a cosmic idol, but neither knows how they arrived. A Haru-voiced `HAL777` claims to be a ship computer based on Haru, claims the craft is 300,000 km from Earth and tells them to make Kajika a cosmic idol. It further claims 2034 space travel is routine and a future Hanenari Airport route is coming. The Player questions this but partly accepts the explanation. These are statements by an in-world performance character later revealed as museum fiction; they do not establish historical/technological world states. [A:P21–59]

### EV-B0116-003 — threatened punishment turns failure into another person's risk

`HAL777` relays Coney's improvised-seeming trials. Kajika tries dominoes, then other tasks, and failures bring `biri-biri` shocks to the Player; he calls them light, while Kajika apologizes and grows less confident. The character escalates to a threat to eject the Player if Kajika fails a ten-minute exercise followed by a no-miss `Cocoro Magical` dance. The Player says that would be deadly and tells Kajika not to overdo it. Kajika says she must try for his sake, performs, and the Player calls the result perfect. She says the attempt gave her evidence she can succeed even while uncertain. The actual device/sensation and recorded dance quality are not independently verified; the ejection is a threat within the staged fiction, not an observed real hazard. [B:P1–75]

### EV-B0116-004 — a fictional apocalypse and sister farewell

`HAL777` then claims Nanasta and ordinary memory are false, Earth has been devastated by a Seventh Strike, humanity survives only in a few people, and machine intelligences must eliminate an emergent idol. A Shinju-looking `Shi-3PO` arrives, says the familiar younger-sister history was implanted, and offers to remain behind to release an escape craft. Kajika pleads for her sister to come and grieves the farewell. The later reveal identifies this as a performed story. Its dialogue cannot revise the series' actual 2034 world chronology or erase Harumi family relations. The tears/feelings in the scene can be real for Kajika even though the proposition is false. [C:P1–43]

### EV-B0116-005 — reveal, reported casting and unequal acceptance

Coney says it was a story; Haru says she was supposed to play a guide character at a space museum, with a facility tour and voice test, but Coney made it an elaborate experience. The Player says Coney put him and Kajika inside while they slept and questions the tea she served beforehand; its contents and whether it induced sleep are not shown. Coney says Kajika has been selected as a guide character because of Haru and Shinju's performances and the adventure. No formal contract, payment or Kajika's audition terms are shown. Kajika says she was surprised, felt real sadness at `Shi-3PO`'s farewell, enjoyed much of it and gained some confidence. The Player says he does not forgive Coney. Coney's claim of a deep prior plan is not demonstrated, especially after her stated wish to avoid a boring tour. Kajika wonders how Sawara would react. [C:P44–70]

## Interpretation and alternatives

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0116-001.** The chapter marks three evidentiary levels: actual Nanasta/museum setup, a staged space fiction, and Kajika's genuine feelings/actions within it. The false space claims and `HAL777`/`Shi-3PO` biographies cannot enter the series world ledger as reality. [A:P21–59; C:P1–55]

Rival: detailed distances, dates and costumed robot bodies prove actual space travel. The reveal identifies a themed facility and scripted performances. The opposite rival—that the fiction is irrelevant—would discard the emotions and choices it elicited.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0116-002.** Kajika's demonstrated effort is tied to a manufactured threat against the Player. Her protective decision is hers, but Coney's setup makes confidence depend on fear, unannounced relocation and a false safety premise; a reported successful guide casting does not retrospectively supply informed consent. [B:P27–75; C:P44–65]

Rival: Kajika enjoyed it and therefore Coney's method was harmless. She describes distress as well as pleasure, and the Player explicitly objects. Another rival says her performance is unreal because the danger was fictional; her effort and felt fear still occur in the staged situation.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0116-003.** The Player's initial `Kajika solo` promotion question assumes confidence should be produced by a marketable image, while the actual experiment lets her act under pressure for a person she values. That contrast supports a local shift in what he can observe, not a settled solo identity or validated training technique. [A:P0–20; B:P49–72; C:P58–62]

Rival: Kajika becomes a cosmic/solo idol through the trial. No solo debut exists; the actual role reported is a museum guide character. She says only that she feels somewhat more confident.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0116-004.** Kajika and the Player differ in retrospective evaluation of the same event. Her enjoyment and mild confidence gain matter; his refusal of Coney's deception matters too. Neither actor's response can be substituted for the other's, and Coney's after-the-fact `deep plan` claim is not proof of foresight. [C:P44–70]

Rival: one positive reaction settles everyone else's consent. The Player says he does not forgive the method. The opposite single-harm account erases Kajika's explicit pleasure and reported confidence.
