# Ei's chosen company and the ownership of affection

Ei seeks the manager's company and finds ordinary ways to care for him. She offers something she says she sewed, asks for quiet conversation, imagines his help with the family shrine and initiates an exchange of shoulder massages. These cases give her attachment material forms and specific requests. They also make the ownership of affectionate speech a problem: one possession comparison is explicitly a joke, another occurrence interrupts quoted affection with a denial that Ei herself was acting, and an imagined son-in-law arrangement ends by calling itself premature joking.

The distinction matters to the cumulative character. Ei's spirit perception has already enabled care, secrecy and a collaborative stage, but the earlier stories also preserve body boundaries, unapproved recording, limited work assent and private memories she chooses not to share. Neither a supernatural explanation nor an affectionate invitation makes every act hers or every future use authorized. The seven cases develop wanted closeness while keeping the speaker's qualification, the recipient's unavailable response and the analyst's evidence separate.

## A — Finding the manager and wanting his company

A:P1 begins with finding the manager and compares Ei's recent ability to locate him with finding spirits. Ei's claim is playful and relationally directed: her attention now has a wanted living destination. P2 supplies her own reason. She describes the manager's surroundings as brighter and easier to recognize than other people's, then says she wants to enter that brightness and keeps trying to catch up.

Brightness can carry her supernatural vocabulary and her attraction to the social space around him without becoming an analyst-measured aura. No image independently displays it, and the manager has no supplied reply confirming the description. The relevant fact is Ei's explanation of her attention and desired inclusion. Reducing it to a generic crush would lose the way her established perceptual idiom expresses ordinary belonging; accepting every word as a new supernatural tracking mechanism would exceed the evidence.

P3 sharpens that ambiguity by comparing her persistence to possessing the manager, imagining never letting go, then immediately marking the statement as a joke. The retraction belongs to the occurrence, not to an external excuse supplied by the analyst. It does not erase the wanted company expressed at P2, but it blocks conversion of the simile into an actual possession, a standing right to follow him or a mutual relationship agreement. A distinguishes an affectionate image from a completed act.

## B — Care as practiced making

B:P1 reports making a protective charm the previous day, after an interval without doing so. Ei invokes being a shrine daughter and says she is good at this work. The local yesterday dates the making relative to this conversation alone; it does not place B before or after another menu occurrence.

P2 names kinran fabric, stresses that the material is genuine and offers the charm to the manager. P3-P4 dwell on the difficulty: fraying and handling require sewing practice and care, together with thought for the person who will hold the result. These are Ei's account of material and skill, not an independently inspected textile or universal craft rule. Even so, the specificity changes the care from an abstract blessing into claimed labor. She describes attention passing into an object through repeated practice and recipient-directed making.

P5 qualifies her authority: she is not a trained shrine priest. The hesitant naming of something like her affection as the charm's contents makes personal investment more explicit while retaining its qualification. Her suggestion that the manager may expect benefits is a character claim, not demonstrated supernatural efficacy. The strongest supported relation is an offer of practiced, personally directed care. ## C — Quiet as a condition for listening

## G — Receiving care in return

G:P1 notices the manager's tired-looking face and offers to massage his shoulders. P2 supports the offer by recalling doing this for her father and claiming skill; its present-tense comfort question represents the proposed care moving into a local exchange. The unnamed father is a remembered family role, not a new identity, portrait or independently observed earlier scene.

P3 announces completion, asks whether he feels somewhat better and requests a turn. P4 praises his skill, compares it favorably with her father's and suggests that they might sometimes exchange massages. P5 thanks him and reports relief. Ei thus receives as well as gives care: she can ask for a return, evaluate it and imagine repeating it. The evidence is her sequence of address and response, not an independently animated hand action or the manager's supplied account of comfort.

The final explanation that school spirits had all been weighing on her is immediately qualified as a joke, with an appeal not to recoil. This preserves a social risk in her idiom: what makes her experience familiar to her might unsettle the person listening. It neither proves actual spirit weight nor authorizes a clinical diagnosis. Her reported relief also does not establish treatment efficacy.

The occasion gives a local frame for reciprocal touch, not permanent permission, professional expertise or an unlimited duty on either side. Read against B0176's distributed care, G makes receiving help legible without erasing earlier failed protection. Read against B0167's protests, its wanted exchange cannot retroactively authorize a different episode's contact. Recipient, act and occasion matter.

## Cumulative model and competing accounts

The seven cases support Ei as an initiator of company, not only the keeper of unusual perceptions. She finds, makes, requests, plans, thanks and asks for a turn. Her wanted relation with the manager is expressed through brightness, skilled sewing, protected conversational space, possible future cooperation and bodily ease. Spiritual language is part of that expression, but practical care is not reducible to supernatural technique.

An exclusively romantic reading has real local material to work with: wanted proximity, personally directed affection and a joking son-in-law future. Its limit is ownership and reciprocity. D refuses to make quoted affection straightforwardly hers, E retracts the completed arrangement, and no new manager confession is given. A purely comic reading would make the opposite error by treating the making, listening and stated gratitude as disposable setup. The jokes qualify claims and allow approaches without making every expressed wish insincere.

The cumulative counterevidence remains active. B0129's one-day performance assent and repeated refusal of ongoing work are not replaced by these affectionate requests. B0167's preferred clothing carries memories she may explain selectively; her most important scouting association is withheld inwardly, not communicated to the manager by default. B0172's disclosure is checked, and B0176 distinguishes planners' intentions, Saori's knowledge, attempted protection and actual failure. Intimacy does not collapse those unequal perspectives.

No case is used to disprove the existence of the earlier spirits. Equally, the analyst does not endorse every report of protection, perception or school burden as independently verified. Diegetic events, attributed appraisals, explicit jokes and missing mechanisms have different evidentiary strength. Keeping those strengths separate gives Ei more than one register of agency rather than making her a universal oracle or an unreliable gag.
