# T7S_B0022 — the machine built from other people's wishes

## Result

Public reporting turns KARAKURI and Nanasta's joint practice into a sabotage plot. The resulting crisis does more than threaten a festival appearance. It reveals how a pair of orphaned children was made into a container for adult failure, how the twins seized that container as a survival technology, and why new friendship feels less like rescue than the danger of losing the only self that kept them alive.

## Mayday: quotations without persons

Articles redescribe joint training as a KARAKURI plan to destroy the last idols and revive the twins' old hostile statements. Nanasta's words are distorted as well. Fans on both sides claim to speak for the performers, and management suspends joint training for safety.

Nothing essential has been fabricated from nothing: old quotations exist, competition exists, and the twins did once declare idols unnecessary. The manipulation consists in stripping statements of changed context and treating the resulting symbol as the person. Fans who demand KARAKURI's “lonely charisma” protect an image that requires the actual girls to remain isolated.

The twins respond by accepting blame for departing from their assigned purpose. They decide to close themselves inside KARAKURI again. Withdrawal promises to stop harm by returning to the identity the public already knows. It is a crisis response and a form of self-erasure.

## History: Dr. Serge is a system, not a father

Ume breaks into the studio and takes Hitoha and Futaba to karaoke. Her intervention matters because 4U has already survived a conflict between individual person and group identity. Ume can say that when she is alone she remains 4U and can sing fully to protect her own song. A member need not vanish when the unit is not physically assembled.

The twins then describe an orphan childhood without family, school, or stable adults. Talent scouts and producers selected children for a project. “Dr. Serge” names the shifting collective of people and money behind it, not one continuous guardian. Children were discarded as resources changed; Hitoha and Futaba endured.

The twins did not passively remain manufactured objects. They found the strings attached to them and made KARAKURI their own name. Singing became the condition of permission to exist, then the practice through which they appropriated existence. This double status is crucial. KARAKURI is both an imposed machine and the pair's genuine survival work. Simply telling them to abandon it would repeat the adult power that first defined them.

Tokyo-7th's light is correspondingly ambiguous. The entertainment city may offer real connection and opportunity, but its glow can also be illumination placed on children for consumption. The twins have accumulated victory while other children disappeared from the project.

## The protective creed

Because singing attracted projections rather than durable care, “only each other is enough” became both love and defense. Seven Sisters—especially Nicole—was installed as a virtual enemy who could justify the system. Nanasta's emergence initially feels fated: a new Sisters relation might finally complete the old purpose. But once relation becomes real, it also makes loss possible. The twins plan to withdraw from Grand Fest before anyone else can abandon them.

## Crossing hearts without claiming ownership

Nanasta rejects the withdrawal. Hitoha and Futaba assume the group will lose and be publicly destroyed; Nanasta refuses to let that prediction decide what it may attempt. Haru defines friendship not as compulsory closeness but as supporting what the twins actually want. Musubi identifies that want as singing in their own way for someone else, not merely accumulating wins.

At Nanasta's small stream stage, the Player offers an intentionally weak category theory: a stage does not care whether the person on it is called idol or artist; performers perform and audiences receive. The twins warn that the stage can be cruel. Sawara's answer is procedural—judge after standing there—rather than a guarantee of safety.

Nanasta describes victory as a consequence rather than a governing purpose. Susu says an idol dream enabled relations she did not have. Musubi does not claim to know a final identity; communication and joy are enough to continue. These answers do not refute the twins' history. They propose that uncertainty need not invalidate practice.

Hitoha and Futaba ask for time and say “goodbye” instead of their habitual promise to meet again. The altered farewell registers that relation has reached the point at which loss can be chosen or feared.

## Exit state

- Media/fan discourse has weaponized old quotations and converted both groups into opposed symbols.
- KARAKURI's production origin is established as a distributed, unstable adult project that selected and discarded orphaned children.
- The twins appropriated the imposed machine as a self-authored survival identity; imposed and authentic are not simple opposites.
- “Only each other” is established as a defense against projected desire and anticipated abandonment.
- 4U supplies a model in which individual and unit identity need not cancel one another.
- Nanasta refuses withdrawal without demanding idol conversion, offering present practice under uncertainty rather than safety guarantees.
- The twins have not yet chosen the festival; the relation remains suspended at a potentially final goodbye.
