## The teacher container and the person who teaches

Miwako's account on the street deepens the earlier distinction between teacher and companion. At C:P80–90 she doubts whether she is someone entitled to teach, worries that she is relying on the teacher position, describes herself as a university student rather than a “real teacher,” and imagines having nothing to offer without the teacher's “container” (`器`, P88). She also reports children approaching her with “sensei” calls (P86). Their approach supplies a positive relational observation inside her own account, but she reads it as potentially dependent on a borrowed position. This is not proof that they reject Miwako personally or that her teaching is fraudulent.

The paradox is that her anxiety concerns both insufficiency and dependence. She thinks teaching while carrying such feelings is not good, yet says she cannot leave that position (C:P90). It would be too simple to call this a rejection of the teacher role: she also finds usefulness and attachment there. It would be equally simple to treat the role as a secure identity merely because the children approach her. The chapter presents a person whose useful work and uncertainty coexist, without having the pupils appear to adjudicate the matter.

Player answers by changing the relation between title and person. His P94 distinction places “Miwako-sensei” beside “Miwako-san who is teaching”: the teacher is not an empty shell interchangeable with its occupant. At P93 he values her ability to contain difficult feelings and keep helping with a smile; at P96–100 he cites his impression of happy pupils and expresses his own belief in her particular contribution. These are supportive assessments, not universal evidence of pedagogical quality or direct access to every child's affection.
