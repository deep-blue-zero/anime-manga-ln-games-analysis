# T7S_B0059 — courage without self-presentation

## Result

Kurt freezes when a young fan offers flowers and later cannot complete a simple purchase from a shop clerk. Nicole and the other Seven Sisters treat the problem as shyness to be cured through exposure. The exercise appears to work only after Nicole stages a robbery: Kurt immediately intervenes, kicks the apparent thief, explains that stealing is wrong, apologizes for the violence, and then introduces herself to the clerk.

The result does not show that danger magically creates a confident “true Kurt.” It distinguishes two kinds of demand. Self-presentation under another person's evaluation immobilizes her; a concrete moral need gives action an object outside the self. Her agency was present before fluent introduction. Nicole's method exposes that structure, but does so through deception, surveillance, and escalation the episode does not establish Kurt authorized.

## A secret converted into a group problem

Nicole reveals that Kurt recently froze before a small fan offering flowers. The disclosure turns a vulnerable encounter into ensemble knowledge. The group wants to help and defines an errand as treatment: Kurt must enter a shop and purchase snacks while they observe.

Care and privacy are already in tension. The scene gives no indication that Kurt chose to share the fan incident or designed the exercise. The group's familiarity allows rapid support, but also allows Nicole to control the account of what happened and what remedy counts.

At the counter, Kurt can repeat or hold pieces of the social script but cannot convert them into a request. The difficulty is not lack of language, physical competence, desire, or understanding. It appears when she herself becomes the object being evaluated by an unfamiliar person.

## The false emergency

Nicole enters as a supposed robber without informing even all of the observing members. The escalation therefore exceeds a jointly agreed group plan as well as the clerk's knowledge. Kurt responds instantly and physically. She stops the theft, says that stealing is wrong, apologizes for kicking, and identifies herself.

The intervention works by replacing the question “How am I appearing?” with “What must be stopped?” A morally legible external need organizes perception and action. Once a relation to the clerk has been created through protection and apology, introduction becomes possible inside that relation.

This is why “courage” should not be equated with smooth performance. Kurt is unable to perform ordinary consumer confidence and fully able to accept bodily risk for another person. The former is a narrow social fluency; the latter is agency under ethical orientation.

## The cost of successful treatment

Nicole's staged crisis generates a useful diagnosis, but success does not validate every means that produced it. The clerk is made to experience an apparent robbery; Kurt is made to experience urgent danger; the group loses control of the exercise; physical force occurs. The scene remains comedic, yet its facts do not support assuming informed consent or harmlessness.

Kurt's own conduct after the kick is more accountable. She names the rule she acted on, recognizes that violence still requires apology, and returns to reciprocal introduction. Her agency is not merely instrumental to Nicole's lesson.

The closing purchase preserves the episode's scale. Kurt selects only one snack because buying everything would be unhealthy. Appetite has not disappeared, but neither has judgment. The “cure” does not replace her with an extroverted persona; it produces one successful route through a particular encounter.

The title's `その①` marks this as “part one,” but the bounded family contains no second native episode. This reading does not invent a missing continuation or generalize permanent cure from the label.

## Character and relationship state

- Kurt's shyness is context-specific. She can freeze under self-presentation and act decisively under an intelligible duty to another person.
- Nicole responds to vulnerability through intervention and theatrical escalation. Her creativity reveals capacities but repeatedly outruns permission.
- The other members participate in the exercise but are themselves surprised by the fake robbery; responsibility for that escalation belongs specifically to Nicole.
- Kurt and the clerk establish a relation through protection, apology, and introduction. The clerk's later interpretation or emotional impact is not supplied.

## Closeout

Kurt does not lack courage; she lacks an easy path through situations organized around presenting herself. The false robbery changes the object of attention and reveals an already available moral agency.
