# T7S_B0076 — beyond the rainbow

## Result

The finale resolves Dream★Age's capture through distributed refusal, not through purity defeating commerce. Eleven members refuse to simulate unity without Haru. An ordinary production worker cuts the forced track because nobody is smiling. Musubi tells a worldwide audience that not performing is their chosen act. 4U, KARAKURI, stage workers, fans, Nero, Sadamoto, Sand Mountain, and the managers interrupt different control points. Mana uses Misonoo power to secure the aftermath at Nicole's request. No single heroic subject owns the rescue.

This matters because the antagonistic chairman's thesis is also institutional. Free children produce unstable values, so adults should monitor them and sell fabricated, safe stimulation. Gotoda's debt and ill mother make him both operator and coerced subordinate. Haru does not absolve him. She tells him he has never tested his own desire and invites him to change the world together. His later refusal of the executive begins accountability; it does not cancel prior harm.

The Rainbow live becomes real before the song begins. The group refuses the category others assigned, speaks candidly, and retains the right not to perform. Haru reaches the stage and chooses to perform despite an injured foot after the manager objects and secures a promise of treatment. The scene retains an ethical tension: authorship does not make injury harmless. The epilogue's clinic confirms consequence rather than magical immunity.

Nanasta survives by expanding trusted infrastructure while remaining independent. The ending therefore rejects both corporate capture and solitary handmade heroism. A self-authored institution is not one without money, adults, expertise, or scale. It is one whose support preserves participants' ability to refuse, revise, and remain more than the role being sold.
