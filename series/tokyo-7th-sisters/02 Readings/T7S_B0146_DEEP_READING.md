# T7S_B0146 — Someone's Light

## Result and entering boundary

An unnamed girl who has watched Rona's debut and solo lives asks Rona how to become bright like her. Rona undertakes private park lessons, while Coney proposes an amateur singing event conditional on their agreement. As the event nears, the girl withdraws out of fear, saying she cannot become Rona by magic. Rona locates her at the park, admits she too once wanted to flee, and refuses the model of becoming Nicole or Rona as someone else's exact copy. She invites a spontaneous duet/dance, which attracts passersby; the girl then chooses to try a stage again. A later scene reports a successful amateur appearance and her refusal of a scouting offer because her family is moving overseas. Rona in turn names a new ambition to give courage to more people. This is a chapter about transmitted possibility without reproduced identity, not a proof that fear requires only courage, that a public rehearsal is risk-free, or that the girl has joined Nanastar.

## Factual reconstruction by presentation and actor knowledge

### EV-B0146-001 — Rona is someone else's apparent light

Haru and Kajika introduce Rona's solo part at a Nanastar live. Rona stumbles over an introduction, thanks cheering fans and says she will sing so they can enjoy themselves and feel able to face tomorrow. A girl in the crowd calls to her. Afterward Coney, the Player and peers say Rona has lately shone; Rona deflects praise. She says she has private plans and resists Coney's mock proposal to photograph her. The chapter shows crowd response and poststage praise, not a technical audit of the song. [A:P2–31]

### EV-B0146-002 — a protected request and a possible small stage

Rona explains that a girl who often visits VIVADO gave her a letter after a live. The girl had watched Rona's debut and first solo, loves song/dance, feels she has nothing special, and asks whether singing like Rona could change her. Rona agreed to meet her for lessons and says the girl requested that they remain just the two of them. Coney wants to go scout, but Rona refuses that intrusion. Coney later proposes a shopping-mall amateur singing event, says she will obtain an application if Rona and the girl want it, and promises a place suited to the girl even if she does not become an idol. Rona agrees to help; the girl's specific consent to the event is not shown at this initial proposal. [A:P55–62; B:P1–40]

### EV-B0146-003 — distributed coaching is not a one-week formula

The Player says Rona accompanies the girl in daily lessons; Rona checks on her fatigue. Musubi shares how she learns choreography; Haru offers Nanastar costume access with the Player's permission. Rona and Coney work late on a practice plan; Coney jokingly says it could make any child an idol in a week and volunteers as a test student. The Player quietly absorbs some of Coney's other work. Peers notice Rona is also maintaining her own lessons, lives and part-time work. This is support and workload transfer, not proof that any standardized plan can make a performer in a week, that the child is not tired, or that safeguarding/logistics are settled. [B:P50–94]

### EV-B0146-004 — withdrawal is the girl's decision, not Rona's failure

Before the event, Rona tells the group it is canceled because the girl cannot do it. The girl's own words name chest-tight fear and the belief that she cannot equal Rona or make magic happen. The team worries about Rona; she asks to borrow two costumes and goes to the park instead of asking the managers to confront the girl. The girl says she fled, and Rona says she also once tried to flee before a live. A proposed event can be withdrawn; Rona's initial “bad senior” self-blame does not establish negligent instruction. [B:P91–107; C:P1–55]

### EV-B0146-005 — the idol refuses to be copied exactly

The girl says she admired Rona specifically because Rona looked close to tears yet got onstage, and had wanted to become her. Rona says she herself wanted to become Nicole of Seven Sisters, but now recognizes she is not Nicole and cannot become her. Being Rona may require a different shape of aspiration. She tells the girl she need not move closer to Rona as a copy and that there is more than one answer. This revises an imitation hierarchy from Rona's earlier arc while retaining the real encouragement she received from Nicole; it does not erase Nicole or make mentorship unnecessary. [C:P55–75]

### EV-B0146-006 — an unplanned public park performance

Rona invites the girl to dance/sing together as a “live battle” and counts in; the girl initially sounds surprised. Passersby notice the guerrilla performance, praise the girl's singing and ask who she is. The Player arrives after hearing music. Afterward the girl says she has performed, Rona affirms her accumulated practice and says her own stage should be distinct. The girl says she wants to try the stage and change; Rona produces costumes and invites another attempt. The visible sequence establishes subsequent participation and renewed willingness, but no explicit affirmative answer appears *before* Rona starts the improvised public act. It supplies no venue permit, guardian consent, crowd-management or minor-performer policy. Rona's statement that courage rather than magic is needed is motivational, not a literal account of all prerequisites. [C:P76–107]

### EV-B0146-007 — the amateur event succeeds without recruitment

At a later time, Rona cries over how well the girl performed at the amateur singing contest; the Player says her smile was still somewhat stiff but considers her an idol. Coney says she tried to scout the girl and was refused because the family will relocate overseas. Rona says the girl regarded the move as an opportunity for change. The source reports an actual contest appearance, distinct from the earlier canceled plan and park performance, but does not show its song, date, destination country, legal arrangements, future career or relocation itself. The refused scouting offer stands; Nanastar membership is not acquired. [C:P108–120]

### EV-B0146-008 — mentor and learner both revise their horizons

Rona says Nicole gave her a dream, Haru and peers helped realize it, and she had wondered if that was enough. Meeting the girl gives her a new aim: to become a bigger idol whose lives give courage to many, even inspiring new idol aspirants. The Player and Coney welcome that aspiration. Coney's joke about 100 doughnuts and running to VIVADO is not a nutrition or training prescription, nor proof Rona carries out those actions. The stated goal extends beyond this child's single stage; no future reach is verified. [C:P119–144]

## Interpretation and alternatives

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0146-001.** Rona receives a mirror of her earlier Nicole imitation when the girl asks to become like Rona. Her answer preserves the predecessor's enabling light but rejects identity-copying as the required result. The chain Nicole → Rona → girl transmits courage through distinct people, not sameness. [B:P6–19; C:P55–75,119–127]

Rival: she has renounced Nicole or should train a miniature Rona. She explicitly credits Nicole and says the girl needs her own stage.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0146-002.** The girl has a direct right to withdraw from the planned amateur event; Rona's subsequent intervention elicits renewed willingness, but the park start precedes an explicit yes and deserves a consent/safety limit. A positive result cannot retroactively prove every exposure decision appropriate. [B:P100–107; C:P1–107]

Rival: her fear proves coaching failure or Rona may perform publicly on her behalf regardless of her wish. Neither follows from the girl's account.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0146-003.** Coaching is collective and material: Rona's time, Musubi's technique, Haru's costume access, Coney's event planning and the Player's absorbed office work all matter. Rona's “courage” speech is effective in context, but not a sufficient recipe for a child without those resources or for any performer. [B:P21–94; C:P76–107]

Rival: the one-week plan alone creates an idol or courage alone dissolves training, scheduling and safety requirements. Both overstate the source.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0146-004.** The girl later performs and declines scouting because of an impending overseas move. The chapter permits Rona to remain a mentor without ownership or recruitment; neither the move nor an idol career after it is shown. [C:P108–120]

Rival: successful appearance means she joins Nanastar, or refusal means Rona's mentorship failed. The scout refusal and Rona's pride oppose both.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0146-005.** Helping another person clarifies Rona's own next horizon after achieving an earlier dream. Her ambition expands from imitation/arrival to enabling others, but remains a voiced future project, not a quantified social effect. [C:P119–144]

Rival: she is now permanently fearless or already a mass-inspiring star. The chapter asserts neither.
