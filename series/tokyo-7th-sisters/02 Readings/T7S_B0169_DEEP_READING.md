## Ordinary interaction and the invention of hidden motives

The daytime mini-cases are variations on interpretation that retains a recognizable surface while changing its motive. Ayumu really uses service language and really knows a tea preference. Yumeno's version exaggerates Kyoko's demanding manner, adds an irrevocable command and then declares that the servant secretly controls the mistress. The “actually” in A:P50 belongs to Yumeno's gloss on her own fiction. It does not reveal a hidden power structure in the real service exchange. The repetition of the tea-opening sentence helps the reader recognize the borrowed material before noticing how much has been added. A:P19–24 and P34–45 therefore have different factual responsibilities despite familiar sprites and nearly identical beginnings.

Tasha's concern that Sisara might lose her cake establishes consideration, while Sisara's explanation makes sharing intelligible. The invented tableau replaces that reciprocity with a knowing child who manipulates the older girl through feeding and proximity. Yumeno's “sadistic” and “little-devil” labels are sexualizing assignments, not corroborated private traits. Actual Tasha's recoil and Rona's counterjudgment matter precisely because an attractive fictional reconstruction can sound internally coherent while misrepresenting its subject. Matsuri's A:P103 rule permits imagination while refusing its harmful spillover into conduct. That boundary is real in the daytime frame; it must not be replaced with the later, dream-qualified admonition as the only evidence of restraint.

The cat case adds an explicit associative mechanism. An ordinary photograph prompts Tomoe's self-deprecation and two girls' encouragement. Yumeno retains Tomoe's anxiety but converts Jeda's kindness into pursuit and Rona's kindness into possession. Player's B:P48 explanation connects the cat keyword with 泥棒猫, not with a discovered relationship. Rona's B:P45 denial defeats the specific dating assertion rather than denying every possible romantic disposition. Her represented abusive lines may be useful evidence about Yumeno's repertoire; they cannot become Rona's ordinary linguistic baseline. Jeda's invitation, Tomoe's presumed attractiveness and the alleged earlier warnings likewise remain inside the imposed plot.

The QOP tableau needs even less initiating content: the actual bandmates have only arrived. Murasaki's plain confession and Ferb's jealous interruption reproduce recognizable stylistic possibilities without proving actual feelings. Being used to an incident, waiting it out and valuing a member are different from wanting every representation.
