# Kazumi's words, taste and chosen interpreters

Kazumi uses literature to approach another person's preferences, to describe her own difficulty speaking, to explore an unfamiliar language and to understand how a manager presents an idol. Books are neither a simple wall against people nor an automatic route to intimacy. In one address she wants a topic belonging to herself and the manager; in another she cannot produce the thanks she wanted to give and asks him to remain beside her while she finds words. The invitation is selective, and the wished-for expression remains unfinished.

These cases also make taste practical. Curtain colors become a personal criterion for trusting a library; a manager's tie becomes an object of daily attention and a proposed gift; spectacles become an occasion for shopping, colleague appraisal and an imagined Nanasta project. Reflection can create possibilities as well as inhibit speech. The strongest tension arrives when Kazumi asks to trust her own words in one case but calls herself an untranslated original whose story the manager decides in another. Her expansive offer of noncriticism should remain visible beside her established authorship and privacy defenses. It is an expression of interpretive trust, with an asymmetry the analyst must examine rather than turn into a complete theory of who owns her life.
