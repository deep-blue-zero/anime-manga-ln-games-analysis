# T7S_B0017 — the rival who arrives through the system

## Result and integrity boundary

KARAKURI enters the story not by visiting Nanasta but by taking control of the medium through which Nanasta is normally managed. Hitoha and Futaba interrupt the Player's HoloCom summons, force their holographic image into Egg, and issue a challenge. The entrance makes their formal problem visible before their biography is known: they appear as a perfectly coordinated, technologically mediated unit whose two members speak as if a single will can occupy two bodies.

This reading consumed only `204001001` and `204001002`. The first eight pages of `204001001` had been displayed during packet verification after the live source/routing check but before the written admission decision; that bounded technical exposure is recorded in the prerequisite audit. ## The hack as characterization

The HoloCom intrusion is more than spectacle. Nanasta's institutional chain normally runs from Coney through a device to the Player and performers. KARAKURI reverses that direction. They do not ask to be represented, recruited, or scheduled; they seize the channel and make the Player their audience. Their command of mediation supports their public reputation for impossible synchronization while preserving distance from the ordinary social world.

Their combined hologram is likewise not proof that Hitoha and Futaba are literally indistinguishable. It is an authored presentation of indistinguishability. The static visual reconstruction confirms an intertwined twin body asset with a shared face layer at the native offset. At this boundary, the story wants the viewer to meet a unit before meeting two people.

## “Real Sisters” and the problem of succession

The twins initially call Nanasta's members false sisters and false idols while claiming the name of true Sisters for themselves. This is an authentication claim, not merely an insult. In a world living after Seven Sisters, resemblance, inheritance, and naming are contested resources. KARAKURI treats perfect twin identity as a stronger warrant for “Sisters” than Nanasta's deliberately heterogeneous collective.

Coney escalates rather than defuses the encounter. Yet the twins' judgment changes after the elided performance. They recognize Nanasta's potential, single out 4U as the only earlier group that seriously challenged their synchronized sound, retract the “fake” designation, and name Nanasta rivals and Sisters. Recognition therefore comes through encounter rather than pedigree. Nanasta does not prove itself by becoming more uniform; its performance makes plurality legible as another valid mode of relation.

## Recognition without capture

Coney immediately tries to convert recognition into recruitment. KARAKURI refuses on categorical grounds: they are not idols. The refusal matters because the prior arc established that a successor world needs autonomous contemporaries rather than one institution absorbing every valuable performer. Hitoha and Futaba can recognize Nanasta, promise another encounter, and remain outside it.

That outcome also limits interpretation. The twins' insistence that they are artists rather than idols cannot yet be dismissed as false consciousness, nor does the challenge prove hostility to every idol as a person. What is established is a chosen public boundary. The Player and Coney must relate to them through rivalry rather than ownership.

## Exit state

- KARAKURI has directly encountered Nanasta and revised “fake” to a recognized rival/Sisters designation.
- The twins acknowledge 4U as the only prior group to have challenged their synchronized sound.
- KARAKURI refuses Nanasta recruitment and retains the non-idol category.
- The Player has met KARAKURI only through a mediated challenge and performance aftermath; Hitoha and Futaba remain largely unit-presented.
- “Sisters” is now a contested relational title rather than an exclusive inheritance.

The two episodes form a complete prologue function. They warrant their own bounded reading because they establish the arc's central formal opposition—unit perfection versus plural relation—without borrowing the later explanation for why that opposition exists.
