# T7S_B0159 — You are Today

## Result and entering boundary

The Player offers Kyoko a lead-role audition for a television remake of *Agapanthus for You*. She knows the original film and accepts with unusual urgency; only later does she disclose that its retired lead actress, Midorikawa Juri, is her mother Uesugi Asuna. Whether the production knew that connection when it invited her is unknown. Kyoko studies and rehearses, with Rena and Honoka's help turning from awkward scene-partner work toward food and chores. She fails the audition to another agency's newcomer, whom she herself calls impressive. Rena refuses to leave her alone to hide grief; Honoka names the disappointment and Kyoko cries, then thanks them. She says the experience lets her respect her mother's skill without trying to become her and recommits to her own idol work. This is a present, voiced resolution after a loss, not proof of permanent freedom from comparison, an objectively fair audition, or a future acting career.

## Factual reconstruction by disclosure, support and loss

### EV-B0159-001 — Honoka's small practice victory is interrupted

Honoka's revised rolled omelet is praised by Rena and Kyoko after her earlier over-seasoning. She reserves portions for the Player. He returns but calls Kyoko away before tasting it. This opening gives Honoka a modest cooking competence later used in practical support; no later tasting or recipe assessment by Player occurs. [A:P0–27]

### EV-B0159-002 — an invitation, not an awarded lead

The Player says he met a production worker by chance, who named Kyoko for a lead-role audition for a two-hour remake of *Agapanthus for You*. He does not know why. Kyoko recognizes the original, accepts, asks to emphasize acting lessons, and plans to rewatch the film. The offer is to compete, not a role or contract. Her urgency is intelligible after the later mother disclosure but should not be retrospectively treated as fully known by the Player. [A:P30–77]

### EV-B0159-003 — Kyoko authorizes family disclosure

Rena searches the cast list and initially refuses to explain Kyoko's private past without her consent. Honoka starts reading a name and the Player recognizes former film actress Midorikawa Juri; Kyoko returns and explicitly permits Rena to speak. Kyoko states Juri is her mother, whose personal name Rena supplies as Uesugi Asuna. She says her mother has retired. This resolves the two names as one reported person, but the actress does not appear, the Player had only seen Asuna in documents, and the production's reason for invitation is still unknown. [A:P79–125]

### EV-B0159-004 — Kyoko does not infer nepotism as a fact

Kyoko says even she does not know whether the industry contact recognized her lineage, imagines that a producer could know the personal name or see a resemblance, and says she will audition either way. Later she admits a rebellious fear of being sought as a second-generation performer. These are her possible explanations and feelings, not an authenticated casting rationale. [B:P23–35; C:P134–145]

### EV-B0159-005 — friends rewatch and volunteer as scene partners

Rena checks on Kyoko's sustained practice and whether she is overdoing it; Kyoko reports sleeping well. Rena and Honoka have watched the original and offer to read opposite her, with Honoka visibly moved by the film. Kyoko accepts. Their subjective viewing reaction is not an audited judgment of the film's technical quality or an obligation for Kyoko to replicate her mother. [B:P1–16,40–63]

### EV-B0159-006 — rehearsal reveals limits of peer acting help

Honoka stumbles over “agapanthus” and becomes flustered at a fictional confession delivered to Kyoko's face; Rena also reads flatly. Kyoko describes her acting approach as asking what she would do in a character's position, rather than applying an external personality label. She cites her mother as having played male parts, then says that ability takes effort and that she herself was once weak at acting. This is her situated craft account, not a universal acting method or evidence that Honoka's fictional line is a real romantic confession. [B:P68–117]

### EV-B0159-007 — help changes form without claiming the audition

Rena/Honoka accept that their scene partnering is not giving Kyoko much useful practice. They offer dinner, laundry and chores instead; Honoka says the omelet is now reliable. Kyoko protests the fuss but privately says the support makes her feel stronger and vows to pass. Her confidence/ambition does not guarantee a result, and peer help should not be credited with technical acting gains absent evidence. [B:P118–138]

### EV-B0159-008 — audition result is a loss with limited process visibility

On audition day Rena, Honoka and Player wait, then go to meet Kyoko. She initially defers the result and says another agency's newcomer got the role, praising that performer's skill and saying the inviter apologized. Kyoko attributes failure to her own insufficient ability; the audition itself and criteria are not shown. Her later view that casting was not just nostalgia is a participant judgment, not independent proof of an unbiased selection procedure. [C:P3–92,134–145]

### EV-B0159-009 — grief becomes shareable

Kyoko tries to leave alone under a pretext. Rena states she will not leave her to hide tears this time, referring to a changed decision about how to support her. Honoka tells Kyoko she worked hard and is disappointed; Kyoko cries, later says she feels clearer, and thanks both peers and Player for the handkerchief. Rena's inference about her need is accepted in this moment, not a standing entitlement to disregard future privacy. [C:P93–132]

### EV-B0159-010 — respect without replacement

Kyoko says she is glad she tried despite loss: she respects her mother's skill anew and sees her own distance from it, while feeling less trapped by acting and second-generation comparison. The Player says her forward look is her own growth and that she is “Uesugi Kyoko,” no one else. She agrees and calls on peers and manager to keep watching her idol path. “Today” marks a bounded claim to her own work, not mother–daughter rupture, guaranteed future booking or settled self-esteem. [C:P133–164]

## Interpretation and alternatives

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0159-001.** Legacy here is a relationship rather than an inherited job guarantee. Kyoko can respect Asuna/Juri's work, lose the remake audition, and still claim a distinct idol self. Neither dismissal of her mother nor imitation is required. [A:P44–77,103–125; C:P133–164]

Rival: her mother's fame means Kyoko was favored and deserved to win, or her failure proves she can never act. Both exceed what the shown outcome and Kyoko's own revised stance support.

**BOUNDED_INFERENCE — CL-B0159-002.** The staff member's reason for naming Kyoko is not known. Her second-generation suspicion and eventual assessment of a serious production are informed participant views, while no casting rubric or audition performance is inspectable. [A:P30–55; B:P23–35; C:P82–85,134–145]

Rival: a shared surname proves nepotism, or an apology proves the audition was entirely fair. Neither follows.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0159-003.** Rena's initial withholding and Kyoko's explicit permission matter to the family-name disclosure. The friends' later move from failed line-reading to food/chores is support that can admit limits rather than claim mastery over her craft. [A:P79–125; B:P40–63,68–138]

Rival: care is only valuable if it directly raises Kyoko's score, or friendship licenses sharing private family identity without her say-so.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0159-004.** Rena/Honoka's presence at the loss permits Kyoko to cry and thank them. Her later composure is neither proof she had not hurt nor evidence that public tears have cured a deeper comparison burden. [C:P75–164]

Rival: Rena had universal authority to override solitude, or Kyoko's grief invalidates her final determination. The local scene supports neither generalization.

**BOUNDED_INFERENCE — CL-B0159-005.** Kyoko offers a subjective acting method and cites her mother, but the text supplies no scored lesson, original-film performance, audition tape or technical basis to rank acting quality. Honoka's rehearsal confession is performed dialogue, not a declared personal romance. [B:P68–117; C:P82–85]

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0159-006.** Honoka's opening cooking practice becomes concrete help; unlike a merit-only story, this chapter lets ordinary tasks support another person's chosen risk without owning its outcome. [A:P0–27; B:P118–138; C:P93–132]
