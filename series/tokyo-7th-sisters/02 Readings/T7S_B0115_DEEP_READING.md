# T7S_B0115 — The Disappointing Queen

## Result and entering boundary

Sawara volunteers at an endangered fashion show after multiple models are delayed by a road accident; the designer and Player praise her untrained result. Offers for modeling, news presenting and anime voice acting follow, but Sawara declines: the show was something she wanted to try once, while her continuing wish is idol work with Kajika and Shinju. The Player initially treats her apparent ease as an obligation to expand career scope. Coney then stages a story about a sealed Nanasta room open only to a legendary idol, which piques Sawara's curiosity and gets her to accept the offers. Shinju immediately recognizes the story as implausible, and Coney admits privately she has no resolution ready. Later phone reports describe work that gets a response but misses the production task: Sawara dances so clothes are obscured, wanders from news, and ad-libs beyond the voice cast's ability to respond. Talent, enjoyment, consent to the work and fitness for its collaborative purpose are different questions. Sawara's local choice to accept becomes real, but is elicited by Coney's unsupported premise.

## Factual reconstruction by actor knowledge

### EV-B0115-001 — a one-time show invitation, emergency and volunteering

The Player takes Sawara to a fashion show because a designer for an upcoming Nanasta outfit invited them to inspect the work and meet the person for whom the costume will be made. Sawara had thought the outing was a date and says she likes daring clothes; the Player worries a strange self-presentation could affect the proposed stage costume. The designer reports that several scheduled models are stranded by one traffic accident; according to him nobody is injured but congestion prevents arrival for at least two hours. Sawara, who says she has only seen modeling on television, volunteers because she has wanted to try a show. The show proceeds; audience sound, the designer and the Player indicate a successful appearance. No model's injury or lost work is shown, and the chapter gives no contract, pay, training or design approval. [A:P2–48]

### EV-B0115-002 — unsolicited offers meet a specific refusal

After the show, the Player receives phone offers for another show, a news presenter and an anime voice role. Shinju and Kajika recount Sawara doing playful improvised dubbing and fake newscasts in spare production time; Shinju calls her able to do most things when interested and cites a world-history school rank from what she has heard. These are partly family and manager assessments, not objective across-job performance tests. The Player converts the offers into a plan to expand her career. Sawara refuses: she did the runway once because she wanted to, has no particular desire for news or voice work, and is content doing idol work with Shinju and Kajika. Momoka envies the voice opportunity, but the Player correctly says a Sawara-named offer cannot simply be handed to her. Sawara mentions skydiving as something she might want; that is a wish, not an arranged job. [B:P1–70]

### EV-B0115-003 — a nickname with two incompatible definitions of talent

Shinju says Sawara can perform well when she sets her mind to it, but also reports low marks in school subjects other than world history and a neighborhood nickname, `残念女王`/`Miss Wasted Talent`. The Player and Shinju disagree about what this means. The Player sees general competence as a reason to maximize opportunities; Shinju stresses that capacity without interest does not make the offer desirable. The nickname is attributed social labeling, not a neutral diagnosis of Sawara's value. [B:P23–35,65–70]

### EV-B0115-004 — Coney's sealed-room story recruits Sawara

Coney takes Sawara, the Player and Shinju to a Nanasta room and claims it contains a momentous secret, sealed against everyone but a legendary idol. She evades how the mechanism works. Sawara restates the proposition and decides becoming a legendary idol to see the secret sounds fun, then asks the Player to arrange the model, newscaster and voice offers she had declined. Shinju says she has seen Coney enter the room and calls it an ordinary storeroom. Coney later admits to Shinju that she will need to think of something if Sawara actually reaches the promised status. The text supports a motivational fiction, not an actual supernatural lock, a verified Nanasta secret or achieved legendary status. Shinju's cryptic aside about Coney satisfying the story's condition does not itself disclose a new identity fact to the Player. [B:P71–77; C:P1–47]

### EV-B0115-005 — a public response can fail the commission

Later, the Player says Sawara has been working hard and imagines her approach to legendary status. Calls report that a later fashion show failed for the designer because Sawara danced to revive a quiet audience but left the clothes unnoticed; her news talk was amusing but too digressive to function as news; and her voice ad-libs outpaced other actors. These are second-hand reports in this chapter; neither the three productions nor their full audience data are shown. Shinju restates her warning: being able to do something when motivated does not mean the result is what collaborators requested. The Player reaches for a `migratory bird` image, then fails to understand Sawara while she speaks through a rice cracker. Nothing shows her responding to the reports, losing the jobs, renegotiating terms or learning the storeroom truth. [C:P48–73]

## Interpretation and alternatives

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0115-001.** Sawara's successful first runway rescue is a chosen experiment, not consent to turn all inferred talents into a wider career. Her reason for declining is an affirmative preference for idol work with her sisters, rather than proof she lacks ambition. [A:P28–48; B:P38–47]

Rival: the manager must exploit every opportunity because she is good at it. She explicitly differentiates trying once from continuing. The opposite claim that she cannot work professionally would ignore the praised first show and her later choice to take offers.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0115-002.** The case separates talent from willingness and from collaborative fit. Shinju's social nickname marks a norm that talent must be maximized, but her own caution is that motivated Sawara may redirect an assignment toward her spontaneous performance rather than its agreed purpose. [B:P23–35,65–70; C:P49–63]

Rival: audience enthusiasm means the second fashion show succeeded. The call says the clothes were obscured. The opposite claim that all three jobs objectively failed in all respects exceeds short phone reports and unseen productions.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0115-003.** Coney changes Sawara's stated preference through a fabricated or unsupported secret-room challenge. Sawara chooses the new route out of curiosity, but the reason presented to her lacks a real disclosed basis and Coney has not planned what fulfillment would mean. [C:P1–47]

Rival: the sealed door is confirmed magic because Shinju says Coney can enter. Shinju calls it a storeroom and Coney cannot explain the mechanism. Her cryptic remark is actor-limited, not proof for Sawara or the Player.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0115-004.** The Player's professional knowledge remains partial: he seeks a personalized stage costume, praises a successful emergency show, then tries to convert success into an expansion plan and overreads later effort as evidence of likely `legendary` outcome. Shinju supplies a correction, but the final joke shows no settled understanding. [A:P7–18,35–47; B:P9–47; C:P46–71]

Rival: he merely seeks money or acts in bad faith. The text also gives an outfit purpose and sincere praise. Good intention does not supply Sawara's consent or production fit.
