# T7S_B0035 — a duet beyond zero-sum desire

## Result

Makoto proposes a duo with Ayumu to make the Player look at her, hiding that motive even from her prospective partner. Their first audition fails because they stand beside one another while orienting separately toward NI+CORA and the manager. Repair begins when the two discover that each had independently helped the same abandoned kitten, Citrus: Makoto found and sheltered him, the Player joined the search, and Ayumu located a responsible home. Their relation precedes their knowledge of it. By exchanging histories, the girls turn rival love for the Player from a zero-sum contest into something each can protect in the other. They still lose the rematch to NI+CORA, but pass because the duo now generates a force unavailable to either alone. Citrus names not possession of the same romantic object, but a chain of care that lets two unrequited loves coexist.

## Two desires that cannot yet name themselves

Months after the cold open in which Makoto shelters a wet abandoned kitten, Ayumu begins spilling tea around the Player, blushing when he touches her forehead, and describing an inexplicable floating warmth that began after he cared for her during an illness. She interprets the sensation through sickness and her elastic “poe-kyun” vocabulary. The source strongly frames this as emerging romantic feeling, but Ayumu does not yet understand the word “unrequited love” when Makoto applies it at the end. Her affect is explicit; her own conceptual recognition remains incomplete.

Makoto identifies enough of Ayumu's condition to become jealous. She already wants the Player—whom she calls “big brother”—to look only at her. Watching NI+CORA suggests an instrument for obtaining that attention: two blades can be stronger than one, so an Ayumu–Makoto unit might be more visible than Makoto alone. She asks Ayumu to join without disclosing that private objective.

The proposed duo is consequently instrumental before it is relational. Makoto does care about Ayumu, but she recruits a friend as an amplifier for another attachment. Ayumu accepts because performing together sounds joyful, without knowing the transaction Makoto has imagined. Neither has asked what strength their combination produces or what the other wants from it.

Their inability to name desire is not symmetrical. Makoto knows she wants exclusive attention and later calls it love; what she initially withholds is purpose. Ayumu openly describes bodily and emotional experience but lacks the category that organizes it. A functioning duet will require more than a common crush. It will require enough communication for these differently structured unknowns to become part of a shared project.

## NI+CORA as inheritance and impossible shortcut

NI+CORA appears first in rehearsal, correcting details that the Player would accept: a slightly disturbed final step, the bodily effect of live excitement, safety in a forceful movement, placement for the next chorus, and the relation between two performers' ideas. Musubi and Susu can disagree inside a practice that improves the whole. Their expertise is not perfect mirroring but an ability to convert difference into revision.

Makoto and Ayumu initially mistake the visible result for a model they can reproduce. When they ask how to become like NI+CORA, Musubi answers that becoming another person is a waste; what matters is what each wants to do and become. Later she and the Player explain that the established duo accumulated its form through time, quarrels, laughter, tears, mistakes, and entry into one another's protected interior spaces.

That final point is ethically uneasy. Musubi says a unit can force entry into domains that ordinarily should not be crossed. The story treats such exposure as a practical burden, not an unconditional entitlement. Makoto and Ayumu's later response is to offer reciprocal disclosure—“I will talk, so tell me”—rather than claim total access. Their partnership needs knowledge, but the invitation to speak matters.

The Player also describes the formal difficulty of a two-person act. Each member remains highly visible; audiences read two bodies as a mirror, making small divergences look like failure; a quarrel or communication error has nowhere else in the formation to diffuse. In retrospect, he says he might never have formed NI+CORA had he understood the risk. Its present excellence is therefore neither a natural property of pairing nor evidence that the institution designed it wisely from the start. It is knowledge produced by Musubi and Susu surviving the experiment.

They become senior witnesses rather than a mold. Musubi transmits the distinction between individual skill and “the strength of a unit”—something created only by combination. Susu refuses consolation after the first failure because she had genuinely anticipated discovering the juniors' bond. Their severity is a form of respect insofar as it expects Makoto and Ayumu to produce their own reason to exist, not a lesser copy of NI+CORA.

## A gate designed around fear

The Player initially supports the pair but resists putting them on the same streamed bill as Nanasta's only established formal duo. Comparison will be unavoidable, and he asks whether they are ready. He then sets a private audition for himself and NI+CORA, gives the juniors two weeks, and has the senior unit perform at full force before them. Makoto and Ayumu become intimidated and cannot recover their own performance.

The test is rationally connected to the proposed work: a public debut would expose them to the same comparison under harsher conditions. The Player warns Makoto about that risk, limits the first audience, and does not call their technical work bad. But he does not know her actual motive, and the test lets an incumbent define the emotional threshold for a new act. It is both protection and gatekeeping. NI+CORA is asked to become an obstacle so management can learn whether the pair survives fear.

Susu's anger identifies what the binary result misses. The girls are looking only at themselves and the senior duo. They do not know one another's tastes, dislikes, sleeping habits, breakfast choices, or wishes. Their collapse before NI+CORA is not simply a deficit of courage; it reveals the absence of an inward relation strong enough to redirect attention from the benchmark.

Ayumu concludes that the partnership came too early and apologizes for knowing nothing about Makoto. Makoto cannot answer, then reads the broken attempt as proof that her selfishness has cost her an important friend. She admits internally that she never cared whether she performed solo or in a unit so long as the Player watched her. Her self-condemnation is severe: because she treated happiness as scarce and another girl as a means, she says she hates herself.

The failure is necessary information but not proof that management's initial standard should remain final. In the rematch the pair still does not defeat NI+CORA. What changes is the meaning of losing and the criteria for authorization.

## “Big brother” as a made category

The kitten's history reveals that Makoto did not always know the Player. During the original rainstorm he finds her and the animal, offers help locating an adopter, and respects her disclosure that close proximity to men frightens her. Because Makoto is holding the kitten and he is holding the umbrella, he extends his arm awkwardly to preserve distance even though this leaves him soaked.

Makoto solves the categorical problem by asking to call him “big brother.” In this scene the term does not name biological kin. She invents a fictive relation so that one particular man can become safe enough to share the umbrella. He agrees; she moves closer, asks him to remain covered, and smiles. He notices the smile, calls it cute, and begins to scout her.

The later romance grows inside that protective designation. Makoto initially imagines she has always loved him, then remembers this exact encounter as the moment she fell in love: he accepted her distance, took discomfort onto himself, and saw her smile. The recollection gives desire a contingent history rather than treating it as an eternal essence.

It also leaves a material asymmetry unresolved. The Player is the manager who scouts Makoto, controls opportunities, judges her duo, and distributes attention across Nanasta. She wants exclusivity from the person institutionally obliged to attend to everyone. He does not recognize that her performance theme concerns him, does not reciprocate romantically, and receives no authored choice. Calling him brother makes proximity possible; it does not remove the manager–performer boundary or turn her longing into a mutual contract.

The Player's broader vocation is precisely what Makoto experiences as loss. Ayumu explains that he makes everyone smile. Makoto had remembered the day when he seemed to be only “her” big brother; as Nanasta expands, she feels him moving away. Her arc cannot be solved by granting possession of him without destroying the collective function she admires.

## Citrus as a relation no one owned

After the failed audition, Makoto retreats to the cat she once found. Ayumu independently arrives at the same home and immediately calls Makoto an important friend. This public naming precedes the repair conversation and disproves Makoto's fear that the relationship has vanished.

The girls then assemble a history distributed across people. Makoto first protected the kitten but could not keep him. The Player helped search rather than simply posting an anonymous notice because he wanted a responsible adopter. He later encountered Ayumu at her maid café. Ayumu used her social network to locate the older woman, who accepted the animal and continues to provide a home. The Player supplied the name Citrus, while both girls returned separately to visit.

No single participant can claim the rescue as an individual achievement. The kitten's safety depends on Makoto's notice, the Player's practical persistence, Ayumu's connections, and the adopter's continuing labor. Even the two future unit members had already collaborated without knowing it. Their relation exists as an effect of care before it becomes a conscious partnership.

The older woman calls their meeting not an odd coincidence but a connection through “that person,” meaning the Player. He is indeed the relay through whom their actions touch. Yet the scene exceeds a manager-centered triangle. Ayumu found the home; Makoto sustained concern; the woman and Citrus now host the encounter. The network is not reducible to the man both girls desire.

Citrus also provides a nonverbal witness onto whom Makoto projects loneliness and hope. As with the prior animal chapter, the cat's scripted responses support the human characters' interpretation but do not grant transparent access to animal intention. The stronger claim is structural: a creature neither girl possesses becomes the durable site where their separate histories can meet.

## From scarcity to reciprocal support

Ayumu describes the Player's persistence as strength. She believes real happiness may require facing sadness rather than fleeing it and admits she might have abandoned the cat's problem alone. Makoto answers that she too would have fled alone. Both were enabled by another person's example and, retrospectively, by one another's unrecognized contribution.

This lets Makoto revise her governing belief. She had assumed one person's happiness required someone else's loss. Shared care offers evidence that benefit can propagate: Makoto shelters Citrus; the Player helps; Ayumu finds a home; the adopter receives companionship; Ayumu joins Nanasta; the girls find each other. Happiness is not automatically noncompetitive, but this particular chain grows by being passed on.

Makoto apologizes not for loving the Player but for failing to speak and for instrumentalizing a relation she genuinely values. She tells Ayumu that she is important, asks to know more about her, and promises reciprocal disclosure. Ayumu enthusiastically accepts. They discuss the Player, Nanasta, tea, sleep, preferences, and the unit. Information becomes relationship because it is volunteered and answered, not because a duo license authorizes invasion.

The girls do not solve their romantic triangle by suppressing desire. Makoto still calls her love enduring even if it remains unrequited. Ayumu says she also loves the Player, though she continues not to understand Makoto's diagnostic term. They playfully escalate who loves him more. The difference is that Makoto now resolves to value her own unrequited love and support Ayumu's.

This is not a guarantee that every incompatible romantic outcome can make everyone happy. The Player has promised neither girl anything, and the chapter offers no future distribution of partnership. Their present achievement is refusing to make one another enemies in advance. Each can protect the other's capacity to desire while building a friendship whose value does not depend on winning the man.

The older woman's remark that “two women can be wonderful too” adds deliberate interpretive openness. Makoto and Ayumu explicitly name friendship and parallel desire for the Player, not romantic desire for each other. But the story's emotional repair, shared history, and new duet recenter the female relation. A queer reading is invited by the staging without being fixed as canonical mutual romance.

## The rematch and the message its recipient cannot decode

Makoto and Ayumu request another audition because they have found “our shared feeling.” They train, face NI+CORA again, and lose. This time defeat produces shared laughter rather than collapse. They can recognize superior current performance without treating rank as a verdict against their relation.

The actual performance is elided, so no choreography, vocal blend, musical quality, or staging detail should be invented. The source supplies the evaluators' narrative response. The Player says their brightness is rough but unusually pure and direct. He cannot identify their intended content, yet it reminds him of his own earlier unrequited love. He names the affective form without knowing that he is its object.

This partial communication is the duo's first artistic success. The performers do not need to confess privately to the manager for an audience to recognize the structure of longing. Their secret remains theirs while the feeling travels beyond its exact referent. Conversely, the Player's correct analogy does not give him authority over their interior content; he explicitly says he does not understand the theme.

His decision also changes. The pair passes not because it defeats NI+CORA or becomes symmetrical enough to avoid comparison, but because he wants to keep watching what this still-unfinished combination can become. Makoto and Ayumu simultaneously ask to continue before hearing the result. Authorization and desire meet rather than management inventing the unit unilaterally.

They choose Citrus as the unit's name. The Player named the cat, but the name now belongs to the shared care history the girls uncovered and to their agreement to sustain one another's longing. Musubi mentions that citrus has a flower meaning suited to them but does not state it; importing a specific meaning would exceed this chapter's evidence. The source itself leaves that symbolic content withheld.

## A new unit and a changed institution

The formation is also evidence of institutional memory. NI+CORA's difficult history becomes a resource for younger performers. Musubi can articulate what she learned, Susu can transform remembered defeat into demanding encouragement, and the Player can limit exposure while revising his benchmark when the new act supplies something unanticipated. Experience becomes mentorship rather than monopoly.

There are still risks. Existing success controls the audition; the manager is also the unknowing love object; personal longing will become professional material; and the promised debut compresses song, costume, and formal launch into one month. The chapter does not construct governance for those entanglements. Its advance is narrower and real: a duo that began as one girl's strategy for monopolizing attention becomes a project both girls request, name, and inhabit together.

Citrus therefore does not eliminate unrequited love. It makes longing livable by attaching it to friendship, conversation, artistic work, and nonexclusive care. The “strength of a unit” is not two individuals adding volume like swords. It is the capacity of a relation to change what each person can do with a desire that would otherwise isolate her.
