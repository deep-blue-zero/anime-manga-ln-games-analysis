# T7S_B0135 — Dance on the Moon

## Result and entering boundary

Hime, Manon, Tasha and Shirayuki ask to take a bon-dance job; the Player cannot accompany them and authorizes it with Hime and Jedah helping the younger members. Hime proposes Jedah dance on the raised platform. Jedah is usually seen as a quick learner, but Hime's improvised rhythm syllables and her own effort to parse the beat leave her stuck. ## Factual reconstruction by actor knowledge

### EV-B0135-001 — permission for a job and a proposed lead

Hime and the three younger members request bon-dance work. Tasha's sweets-and-yukata description and Manon's mistaken `くろう` are corrected in conversation; Jedah compares the event to Halloween and asks to experience an unfamiliar dance. The Player says he has another assignment and cannot escort them, but permits the job if Jedah and Hime support the younger members. Hime proposes Jedah on the `やぐら`, the Player likens it to a stage center, and the younger members endorse her. Jedah accepts and Hime volunteers to teach. This is authorization and intended role, not evidence the event has happened or that staffing/safety details are complete. [A:P0–42]

### EV-B0135-002 — rhythm instruction fails on both sides

At practice Jedah cannot parse Hime's rhythm syllables and asks about meter. Hime's later syllables differ and she herself cannot explain the pattern consistently. Manon and Shirayuki are surprised because Jedah often learns quickly. The Player speculates that Japanese taiko rhythm is hard for non-Japanese people, immediately complicated by Tasha saying she learned the pattern readily; he later infers Jedah may be overanalyzing. Those are his hypotheses, not verified nationality, cognitive or musicological laws. Hime assumes Jedah will master it eventually. [B:P1–25]

### EV-B0135-003 — repeated failure and withdrawal

Later Hime reports little improvement; Manon says Jedah practices late and stops partway. Hime worries her teaching is at fault. Jedah says she cannot master the dance in time, apologizes to Hime and withdraws from participating, offering to watch outside. The Player and Hime later acknowledge they relied on her reputation and did not notice her struggle. Her withdrawal is stated at this point; it must not be erased by the opening plan or by later encouragement. [B:P28–40; C:P1–9]

### EV-B0135-004 — a private sister-oriented motive persists

Manon, Tasha and Shirayuki decide to seek Jedah and teach her themselves. Alone, Jedah looks at a photograph of her younger sister and says she hoped to demonstrate bon dance when she returned home because the sister loves Japan. She continues practicing despite saying the public event may be too soon and that she does not want to hold the others back. The sister's direct voice, actual future travel and reception of any recording are absent. [C:P10–33]

### EV-B0135-005 — embodied reciprocal teaching reopens practice

The younger members ask Jedah to imitate them, sing the drum syllables while moving, raise a hand toward the moon, and sway in an amusing bodily analogy. Jedah laughs at their explanations, wonders whether she had been thinking too hard and asks to see more of *their* dance. The Player arrives but does not interrupt. This shows an accessible lesson and renewed engagement, not measured mastery of the choreography or confirmation that intellectual analysis is intrinsically wrong. [C:P34–51]

### EV-B0135-006 — Hime's material repair and a pre-event exit

Before the evening event, Jedah appears in a yukata Hime obtained through a shopping-district contact. Hime says she wanted Jedah to enjoy the occasion and calls them family-like; Jedah thanks her. The Player still cannot attend, and Jedah promises to show him Holocon footage afterward. The group changes and declares its intention to do a cool bon dance. The ending is *departure/anticipation*, not the performance, an actual recording, a verified audience response or a legally established family relation. [C:P52–78]

## Interpretation and alternatives

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0135-001.** Jedah's difficulty cannot be assigned simply to being foreign or too intellectual: Hime's teaching is inconsistent, Tasha contradicts the Player's national generalization, and younger peers later supply different motor/metaphoric cues. The case supports a local pedagogy mismatch and revised practice, not a universal theory of dance learning. [B:P1–35; C:P34–51]

Rival: Jedah's giftedness should make Hime's first explanation sufficient, or her nationality makes failure inevitable. Both the instructor's uncertainty and Tasha's counterexample undermine those accounts.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0135-002.** Hime and the Player initially entrust responsibility to Jedah because she usually excels; when she withdraws, both recognize that admiration had hidden her need for help. The younger members repay care by teaching her, while Hime contributes material welcome. This is reciprocal support, not proof all event-safety or labor obligations were adequately planned. [A:P23–40; B:P11–40; C:P5–16,34–66]

Rival: Jedah's temporary inability disqualifies her as the older helper, or the younger members' success removes the Player's responsibility. The story shows support can change direction without documenting complete supervision.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0135-003.** Jedah's sister-oriented wish explains why she keeps practicing after public withdrawal. She is not shown telling the group that reason, and neither a returned-home demonstration nor the promised Holocon recording occurs within the chapter. [C:P21–33,67–78]

Rival: she comes back only to avoid letting the group down, or a future recording is already evidence of successful performance. Her private speech gives another motive; the ending remains prospective.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0135-004.** The final yukata and group declaration make participation more likely and materially prepared, but the episode stops before any audience-facing bon dance. The static costume change cannot stand in for the actual event or for technical dance competence. [C:P52–78]

Rival: the title and pre-event celebration imply a completed show. The script's last action is preparing and leaving for it.
