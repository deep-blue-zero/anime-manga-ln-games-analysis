# T7S_B0109 — Surely the Same

## Result and entering boundary

Hime tries to continue a Nanasta voice lesson despite a fever and unsteadiness because the others are working; after the Player and peers intervene, she agrees to rest at home. Sui and Rona visit after their lesson with the Player's assent, invoking an earlier promise to help Hime's family. They make the practical tasks a competition. Sui breaks dishes; Rona falls chasing Hime's younger brother and accepts the younger sister's trick to get him dressed; later both delay Hime's supper while vying to feed her. They do supply care, but not with the effortless competence their contest assumes. When Hime falls asleep holding their clothing, her father invites them to stay. The two privately admit rivalrous jealousy: each wanted to be as close to Hime as she imagined the other was. They recast Hime's habitual older-sister work as something they can sometimes relieve rather than a scarce possession to win. Next morning Hime is working at the tofu shop again while they help. The closing claim of a shared precious thing strongly points to Hime, but she is not told their private agreement; renewed work does not certify medical recovery or a durable household labor rule.

## Factual reconstruction by actor knowledge

### EV-B0109-001 — a fever interrupts the lesson, not the group obligation in Hime's mind

At voice practice Momoka jokes and Hime's usual rough rejoinder is weak. Rona sees her sway and her face redden. Sui and Momoka initially joke about lovesickness; Hime tells Sui that the nearest prince is Sui, a fevered quip that makes Sui flustered. The Player touches Hime's forehead and says she has a high fever. Hime resists leaving while her friends rehearse, but he, Sui and Rona urge rest, and she agrees to go home. The episode gives no temperature reading, diagnosis beyond the characters' cold/fever language, infection course or return-to-work instruction. Momoka's comic attempt to claim a sore throat is met by the Player's finding she feels normal and an extra-lesson order; it is not a proper health screen. [A:P2–32]

### EV-B0109-002 — promised household help becomes a contest

At home Hime says she has no energy for housework and asks her younger sister to order dinner. Sui and Rona arrive after finishing their lesson with the Player's permission. Rona explicitly recalls the earlier promise to work for Hime and her family when needed. Hime agrees to rely on them for this day, but they announce a competition over who can nurse and help her best. Hime's apprehension and the sister's image of sparks show that care is already becoming a contest about the helpers. [A:P34–54; B:P1–10]

Sui offers to do kitchen work, immediately breaks a plate and breaks more while her father tries not to make it consequential. Rona attempts to dress Hime's younger brother; he runs, she falls, and the younger sister invents a nonexistent doughnut so he stops long enough for the shirt to go on. The children and father contribute rather than being inert beneficiaries. The father jokes about hoping the loss stops at five plates; the text does not inventory an exact number. The brother's compliance under a trick is local and comic, not a household care norm. [B:P11–36]

### EV-B0109-003 — care succeeds in part while its competitive form burdens Hime

Sui and Rona report the housework and younger-child tasks mostly finished. Sumire has supplied porridge and Kajika jelly, so the meal is not presented as Sui and Rona's own cooking. Hime accepts feeding, but each friend insists she should be the one to feed her, and Hime asks them to stop arguing while she holds her mouth open. The scene tests the difference between wanting a rare moment of Hime's dependence and noticing her immediate need. Later she sleeps; Sui and Rona prepare to leave to avoid imposing on the household. Hime, sleeping, grips the hems of their clothing. Rona wonders whether she unconsciously wants them to stay; her father agrees this looks like unusual dependence and invites them to sleep in the adjacent room with the younger children. The invitation and their acceptance are explicit; the sleeping gesture is not Hime's conscious verbal request, and no permanent residence or family change follows. Sui says Hime's cold seems somewhat calmer, but no medical reassessment is shown. [C:P26–38]

### EV-B0109-004 — reciprocal jealousy becomes a bounded redistribution of care

Hime murmurs both friends' names and worries about their live readiness in sleep. Rona and Sui infer that their rivalry has only caused Hime more worry. Rona admits she was jealous of Sui's recent closeness to Hime and wanted to prove her own closeness; Sui says she envied how Hime treats Rona like a real younger sister. Their imagined exclusivity is mutually mistaken. Rona says Hime is family to everyone at Nanasta. Sui notes that Hime is too practiced at being an older sister and proposes sometimes making her their younger sister; Rona welcomes a chance to be the older one. This is an explicit private promise between peers, not Hime's prior consent to every caretaking gesture or a new adoption relation. [C:P39–56]

### EV-B0109-005 — visible morning help, not a settled burden

The next morning Hime calls out a tofu-shop sale. The Player comments that she looks recovered and asks whether she should already be working; Hime gives no medical clearance. Sui and Rona help with shop display and cleaning and appear closer. Hime cannot get them to explain their agreement. Rona says they cherish the same thing; the Player and Momoka infer its referent, while Hime does not. That they both cherish Hime is well supported by their private talk; no romantic declaration, exclusive tie or guarantee that Hime can now rest whenever needed is made. Her swift return to shop work keeps the B0005 labor question open. [C:P58–80]

## Interpretation and alternatives

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0109-001.** Hime's reluctance to miss a lesson while feverish and her next-morning shop work continue the B0005 pattern of treating care as her own obligation. The immediate group response makes resting possible for a day but does not show who routinely covers the shop, siblings and Nanasta after that. [A:P11–46; C:P58–65]

Rival: because the father and peers help, B0005's promised burden sharing is now durably complete. B0109 supplies a concrete instance, but Hime returns to work quickly and no standing allocation is shown. The opposite rival—that nobody helps—ignores permission to leave, the visit, food from Sumire/Kajika, family participation and morning assistance.

**ETHICAL/THEMATIC INTERPRETATION — CL-B0109-002.** Rona and Sui turn an intimate act of care into a contest for proof of closeness. Their broken dishes, child-chase and feeding standoff make the cost comic but legible: competitive attentiveness can delay the sick person's need. The later apology and reciprocal disclosure shift the measure from winning Hime to relieving her. [A:P47–54; B:P11–36; C:P7–17,39–56]

Rival: the girls are simply incompetent, so their help has no value. They complete some tasks, bring supplied food, secure the father's invitation and help the next morning. The problem is the framing, not a verdict of incapacity.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0109-003.** The sleeping hem grip is a potent visual/textual sign of Hime allowing dependence, but the story itself routes interpretation through Rona and her father. Their inference warrants an offered overnight stay, not certainty about a conscious wish or a medical cure. [C:P19–38]

Rival: her hand proves she expressly invited the sleepover. She is asleep; the actual invitation is her father's. The opposite reading that the grip has no relational significance overlooks the dramatized response and Hime's subsequent sleep talk.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0109-004.** The same valued person is not a scarce prize. Rona and Sui discover that each had misread the other's closeness to Hime as a threat; agreeing to sometimes act as older sisters preserves their distinct bonds while making a reversibility of care imaginable. [C:P39–56,63–78]

Rival: this makes Hime permanently a younger sister or resolves all domestic work. Their words say `たまには`—sometimes—and the next morning she is back at the shop. Nor should Momoka's earlier love joke turn the private agreement into an explicit romance claim.
