# T7S_B0113 — Shizuka Strives

## Result and entering boundary

Shizuka's grandfather criticizes her stream answer to a shopping-district question: mobilizing the Kuonji group is powerful, but it does not meet an audience's ordinary concerns. Shizuka asks to learn how people live. Sawara offers a stay and work at the Harumi fish shop 魚晴, which Shizuka accepts; Sawara also sends Kajika to the Kuonji home against her visible reluctance, while Shinju declines the exchange to protect the shop. Shizuka learns shop vocabulary but copies `service` at the wrong scale, promising a luxury Kuonji knife with a fish purchase. The resulting rush invites guards and a helicopter-borne grandfather's offer to buy all stock; Shinju refuses the knife, guards and buyout. She later shows Shizuka a different model: knowing a regular customer's household, tastes and cat, making a fair recommendation and small surplus-aware gift. Shizuka experiences labor's weight and Sawara's account of its joy, then publicly thanks fans for working to support idols and immediately offers Nanasta merchandise. The shop reports increased custom, but Kajika says the mansion swap was lonely. The episode shows a bounded gain in local attention, not a completed transformation into a universal “ordinary” person or proof that every exchange participant was happy.

## Factual reconstruction by actor knowledge

### EV-B0113-001 — an invitation to learn, and a coercive companion swap

Shizuka reports that her grandfather watched a prior live and found her Kuonji-group shopping-district reform answer too remote from fan feeling. He orders her to learn the lives of ordinary people. She tells the Player that she knows she sometimes misses peers' talk and wants better connection. Sawara, Kajika and Shinju have overheard from outside; Sawara offers Shizuka a home stay including meals, bath and work at 魚晴. The Player asks whether Shizuka wants it; she agrees. This is a specific visit, not proof the grandfather's `ordinary people` category can be mastered like a role. [A:P2–45]

Sawara then proposes Kajika and Shinju stay at the Kuonji house instead. Shinju refuses, saying the shop needs her. Kajika protests being the sole substitute but Sawara calls for attendant Oosugi to take her, and she goes. Her lack of enthusiasm is explicit. Sawara's assertion that everyone benefits already fails at the point of assignment; the eventual loneliness confirms a cost. Shizuka's assent to her own stay does not constitute Kajika's assent to the swap. [A:P46–66]

### EV-B0113-002 — a service lesson at the wrong resource scale

At the shop Shizuka learns `マケる` can mean a discount or small bonus. Sawara explains it as service that pleases customers. Shizuka offers a Kuonji-group `Zantetuken` luxury knife as a free extra with flounder. Customers know a reputation for extreme value and rush to buy fish. The text gives their claims about the knife, not an inspected product price, inventory, authority or completed giveaway. Player sees the crowd exceed ordinary shop flow. Oosugi and guards propose queue/order help, and Shizuka's grandfather calls from a helicopter that he will buy all stock. Shinju tells Shizuka to cancel the knife and send the guards and grandfather home, explicitly forbidding buyout and landing. Nothing shows the knife transferred or all fish purchased. [B:P1–45]

The grandfather's intervention is affectionate by his account, but it again imports family scale into an independent shop. Shinju's refusal protects the shop's capacity and its actual customer relations; it is not a rejection of Shizuka as a person. She apologizes. [B:P32–45]

### EV-B0113-003 — local knowledge, not a generic class costume

Shizuka worries she has harmed the shopping district. Shinju says she is not angry and that some neighbors enjoyed the noise, while admitting she dislikes commotion herself. She introduces Shizuka to a regular as a friend. With an older customer, Shinju changes the recommendation from her earlier generic tuna to flounder because the customer's husband dislikes red fish. She gives an additional fish, explaining the household has a cat and that unsold stock would otherwise go to waste. This is situated knowledge of one household and a small shop decision, not a universal rule that every customer deserves an uncosted gift. Shizuka says she now understands something about why 魚晴 is loved while admitting she still has much to learn about work, money and ordinary lives. [C:P2–45]

### EV-B0113-004 — effort and pleasure coexist in the shop

The Player helps lift heavy wet packing material and Shizuka sees how early shop work, school and idol duties overlap for her peers. She says she had not pictured their daily lives or what fans' earned money costs. Sawara interrupts the idea that commerce is only suffering: selecting good fish, seeing customers smile and doing good work are enjoyable, as idol performance can be. Shizuka decides to continue serving. Neither Sawara's joy nor Shizuka's shock measures pay, exhaustion or every worker's experience. [C:P46–67]

### EV-B0113-005 — a speech sells merch and Kajika pays a different price

At a public address Shizuka thanks working fans for supporting idols, asks them to keep working and announces Nanasta goods for purchase; the crowd responds enthusiastically. The Player compares its strange sales energy to another performer's stage talk. Sawara says Shizuka's work at the shop went online; Shinju reports more customers and higher sales. Those statements establish reported local commercial effects, not net profit, continued affordability or universal audience approval. [C:P68–81]

Kajika then reappears with an affected young-lady manner and says she felt lonely sleeping alone in a large bed at the Kuonji home. Shizuka offers a future shared sleepover with the sisters. Shinju acknowledges Kajika was not equally happy. The final comic beat is an important counterexample to Sawara's early arithmetic of universal benefit; no repeat stay or restoration of Kajika's ordinary comfort is shown. [C:P82–88]

## Interpretation and alternatives

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0113-001.** Shizuka's first `service` mistake is not inability to care; she translates a small fish-shop practice through Kuonji resource power without checking the shop's authority, capacity or customer relationship. Shinju's refusal puts local governance over spectacular abundance. [A:P7–16; B:P7–45]

Rival: a luxury free knife and grandfather buyout are unquestionably generous. They produce a crowd but also override the shop's scale and choice; Shinju cancels both. The opposite rival—that Kuonji resources are always harmful—exceeds the case. Their use is the question.

**THEMATIC INTERPRETATION — CL-B0113-002.** Shinju's regular-customer exchange gives Shizuka an example of `understanding people` as particular knowledge plus modest economic judgment, not passing as a generic commoner. Sawara adds that such work can also be joyful. Shizuka learns a method, not every person's life. [C:P2–67]

Rival: one flounder recommendation proves complete class transformation. Shizuka explicitly says she still lacks much knowledge. Nor does the case romanticize shop labor: its weight and scheduling are stated alongside pleasure.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0113-003.** Sawara's sisterly experiment has asymmetric consent and outcome. Shizuka agrees to stay; Shinju can decline; Kajika is sent despite resistance and later reports loneliness. The pleasures of Shizuka's new friendship and higher shop sales do not erase that cost. [A:P33–66; C:P82–88]

Rival: Kajika's mansion visit is an enviable perk, so she must be happy. She says otherwise. A pure harm account also misses Shizuka's voluntarily chosen visit and Kajika's later chance to voice her experience.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0113-004.** Shizuka's final pitch blends an emerging awareness of working fans with the idol institution's wish to sell merchandise. Her gratitude is explicit, and so is the sales invitation; audience cheers do not decide whether she has resolved the tension between reciprocity and extraction. [C:P68–81]

Rival: the merch offer proves she learned nothing. She names labor's cost and works at the shop. The opposite claim that increased sales prove ethically perfect reciprocity ignores Kajika and the unreviewed resource, pay and fan-consent terms.
