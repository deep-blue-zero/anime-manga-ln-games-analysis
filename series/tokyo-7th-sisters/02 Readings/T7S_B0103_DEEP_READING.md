He asks, hesitates, gives in to the feeding and is alarmed by her unexplained `手遅れ` remark. His internal narration then enumerates a pattern he finds excessive. Makoto sends detailed messages while bathing and, he says, sends more when he does not answer. ## Interpretation and alternatives

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0103-001.** The Player's need for a boundary is intelligible without diagnosing Makoto or endorsing his nightmare's accusation that attachment alone is pathological. He names concrete unwanted intrusions, a flood of messages and professional interference, and explicitly refuses the bathroom encounter. Her claimed sleepless cooking and cost of gifts also make relentless devotion appear costly to her, but the text does not let the reader measure that cost independently. The deferred confrontation matters: recognizing an obligation to speak is not the same as setting or enforcing a boundary. [A:P3–57]

Rival: because the ending destabilizes the frame, none of the opening conduct should matter. The chapter itself makes the Player's represented distress the condition for the nightmare, and B0039 supplies independent earlier evidence of his objections to intrusive pursuit. Yet B0103 cannot by itself make every exact gift, message or apparition an objective waking fact. The threatening figure is expressly his suspected hallucination.

**THEMATIC INTERPRETATION — CL-B0103-002.** The nightmare tests two incompatible desires in the Player: freedom from exclusive surveillance and the comfort of being indispensable. His apparent relief turns into loss when Makoto has other sources of recognition. The symmetry is comic but not an ethical equivalence. Feeling needed does not retroactively authorize intrusion, and the dream's declaration of his own dependence does not erase Makoto's agency or settle his waking feelings. [A:P50–57; B:P31–69]

Rival: the final twist reveals a stable reciprocal romance. The explicit wake-up and unanswered dream boundary prevent that conclusion. The alternative that everything after the wake is wholly real is also underdetermined; the blank background and final scream do not independently locate Makoto in physical space. What survives is the structure of the Player's fear and attraction to attention, not an established couple or cured pursuit.

**THEMATIC INTERPRETATION — CL-B0103-003.** The pre-wake peer scene offers a different imagined distribution of attention: Makoto can be seen by Coney, Haru and Yumeno without demanding that the Player be her only witness. Her own pleasure in their kindness would be a real relational gain if the scene were waking. But the group also converts `つきまとう` into a permissive joke, and Coney claims managerial authority to permit conduct that affects the Player without asking him. The scene can represent his desired redistribution and his unease about losing singularity at once; its ontic uncertainty makes it a formal test, not a new institutional policy. [B:P5–39,40–67]

Retrospective B0035 gives Citrus a nonexclusive way for Makoto and Ayumu to carry attachment, while B0039 retains the boundary cost of uninvited proximity. B0103's nightmare does not refute either bounded case and establishes no chronology between them. Nor does Coney's praise of a “sexual solo” establish actual vocal tone, choreography or adult status; it is a character's response to an unreviewed performance.
