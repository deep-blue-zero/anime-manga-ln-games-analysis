# T7S_B0112 — The Body Is Capital!

Sui is told to review her diet before a swim competition and relays a coach's criticism of a meat-heavy pattern. Hime seizes on tofu as a useful plant-protein food and offers her shop's varieties; Sui likes them but treats the example as an exclusive rule and eats tofu with toppings in place of varied meals. ## Factual reconstruction by actor knowledge

### EV-B0112-001 — a product example becomes a complete rule

Sui says a coach asked her to review eating before a swimming competition. She and the Player describe meat-heavy or convenience-heavy habits; Sui reports the coach said plant protein would help with her meat bias. Sumire identifies tofu, and Hime, present on a delivery, offers multiple Nonohara products. Sui tastes silk tofu, yuba and okara and appreciates distinct textures. Hime says she can bring tofu daily and invites Sui to try more at the shop. This is specific peer/product access, not a registered diet prescription from the coach or proof that the shop's product is uniquely effective. Sumire and Rona watch Sui and Hime rush off and voice concern. [A:P2–49]

### EV-B0112-002 — correction preserves appetite rather than banning it

The next day Sui says the coach scolded her for eating too much tofu. Asked what else she ate, she lists chilled tofu with soy sauce, kimchi, natto or their combination—variations of one base, not a broadly varied meal. Hime and the Player have no ready answer. Sumire calls Sui's single-mindedness a strength even as she corrects the application. She has made a tofu hamburger because Sui likes hamburgers; Sui initially worries about meat, tastes it and says she likes both tofu and meat. Sumire says she thinks the coach meant substitution within a balanced pattern, not tofu alone. Her phrasing is her plausible interpretation of a second-hand instruction, not a reviewed clinical plan. Hime's claim that her shop's tofu is the best in the world is promotional affection, not an adjudicated product rank. [B:P1–46]

### EV-B0112-003 — treat and sharing are not nutritional proof

Rona has made okara doughnuts for a sweet change of pace. Sui and Shizuka enjoy them. Coney appears, eats multiple doughnuts and later takes the remaining supply, leaving none when the Player asks; Sui offers him half of hers and says she has learned not to overeat. The scene shows peer care, Sui's local moderation and Coney's opportunistic consumption; it does not certify calorie balance, performance benefit or a safe portion. The Player says Sui's body is no longer hers alone, a manager's care claim that can also sound possessive; no ownership transfer or formal athlete agreement is shown. [B:P47–52; C:P1–40]

### EV-B0112-004 — a win gives the shop customers and a new cost

Several days later the Player sees the Nonohara tofu shop crowded. A remembered interviewer asks Sui her victory secret, and she credits its delicious tofu. The result is a source-stated win, not a viewed race with times, field, margin or causal isolation. Customers ask for tofu and okara; Sumire, Sui and the Player help serve. One younger girl asks whether she can swim like Sui and another asks whether tofu can make her slim. Sui answers encouragingly, adding balanced meals for the latter. ## Interpretation and alternatives

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0112-001.** Sui converts a coach's second-hand advice into a single-item routine because she treats the named solution as the whole problem. Sumire's tofu hamburger works locally by retaining Sui's taste and reintroducing variety rather than shaming her appetite. [A:P2–44; B:P7–45]

Rival: Sui merely followed the coach and was unfairly scolded. The coach's complete instruction is not present, and Sui herself lists only tofu preparations. The opposite rival—that tofu has no value—ignores Sui's real enjoyment and Sumire's use of it in a more flexible meal.

**ETHICAL/THEMATIC INTERPRETATION — CL-B0112-002.** The title's body-as-capital logic applies unequally to Sui and Hime: peers attend to the swimmer's preparation, but her endorsement transfers demand to a shop whose key worker cannot rest or attend Nanasta. This is a local distribution-of-care problem, not a demonstrated nutrition or marketing policy. [B:P22–45; C:P42–70]

Rival: the rush is wholly good because it means more sales. Hime voices fatigue and lost Nanasta time; revenue and staffing are unreported. A purely harmful reading misses her shop pride, demand and real peer help.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0112-003.** The reported swim victory and Sui's sincere product praise are narratively adjacent but not causal proof. The customer questions expose how a narrow success story can become universal promises about swimming ability or body size, which the source does not warrant. [C:P42–63]

Rival: because an interviewer asks for a secret, tofu must have produced the result. No counterfactual, diet record, coaching regime, race footage or measured customer outcome exists. The win itself is still reported; it need not be denied to restrict causal claims.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0112-004.** Sumire's cooked meal, Rona's treats, Hime's product knowledge and Sui's sharing are distinct forms of care with different limits. Coney's taking all remaining doughnuts humorously defeats equitable sharing, and the Player's `body no longer yours alone` line shows how care can lean toward institutional claim over a performer. [B:P22–52; C:P1–40]

Rival: these scenes establish a comprehensive care or diet system. They are local gifts and jokes; no approved plan, consent framework or repeatable provision is shown.
