# T7S_B0117 — Play Like a Child

## Result and entering boundary

At a children's-program rehearsal, Shinju's strained attempt to act like a conventional young child is poorly received by staff and herself. She asks the Player to correct un-childlike speech. His `big brother` coaching becomes a wooden register; Sumire's house-play and Momoka's children's anime expose how Shinju brings concrete economic and narrative questions to activities others have coded as childlike. Sawara and Coney take over but report not correcting her at all. At the recording, Shinju speaks in her usual manner, draws an unusual frog she knows about, and accepts a child's compliment. The Player recognizes he had confused formal language with not being a child. Shinju later decides she need not suppress reading macroeconomics to satisfy a child image. The local encounter shows children responding to one authentic interest, not a full production/audience verdict or a reason to deny her intellectual agency. The ending holds both facts: she is an elementary-age child, and her serious economics study is also hers.

## Factual reconstruction by actor knowledge

### EV-B0117-001 — a poor rehearsal and self-requested correction

The chapter opens with the show's host inviting `Shi-chan` to introduce herself. Shinju says the diminutive awkwardly and falls silent. After rehearsal she tells the Player staff reaction was poor and she knows she did not meet a children's-program expectation to be more `childlike`. She had tried standard exclamations, but they sounded fake. The Player begins with an inadequate breakfast joke, then calls her adult-like when she apologizes for her sharp answer; that praise lands badly in this context. Shinju asks him to correct her when her speech does not sound childlike. He suggests she call him `big brother` and speak brightly. She attempts it, stiffly. Her request to rehearse is explicit, but the preferred image is already under outside evaluation. [A:P0–48]

### EV-B0117-002 — role-play exposes practical reasoning rather than eliminating it

Sumire finds their `big brother` drills unsettling and says forced wording may not change Shinju's inner manner. She suggests children's play. In family-role play, Shinju asks the Player-father's income and works through living standard, dual-earner childcare timing and division of labor. The Player and Sumire's own flustered pretend-spouse talk adds a separate social tension. Shinju notes this is only a setting. The exercise does not prove her incapacity for play: her economic questions are how she elaborates it. It also does not establish the Player's actual salary, a marriage or an actual childcare plan. [B:P1–35]

### EV-B0117-003 — children's animation prompts analytic play

Momoka recommends `Magical Revolution! Chekera-chan` when Shinju asks for an uncomplicated children's anime. The Player unexpectedly enjoys it. Shinju notices that a supposedly ruthless enemy does not attack during long transformation sequences, asks why costumes and hair changes hide identities, and speculates about an `empire` without an emperor. She even weighs the ethics of supporting a claimant against a military coup. These are questions she raises about a fiction, not political facts about the T7S world. The Player sees the effort to make her conventionally childlike has failed, though the intensity of her questioning can also be read as a child's game of following a story's rules. [B:P36–61]

### EV-B0117-004 — Sawara and Coney stop policing the persona

With recording imminent, Shinju and the Player despair of changing her style. Sawara volunteers to take over with Coney, claiming she can do better. Shinju distrusts the pair; the Player nevertheless hands the coaching over. The chapter does not show every moment of their interval. At recording Shinju still uses formal, direct speech, and Sawara and Coney say they never corrected it. The Player calls this inaction until the on-air encounter illustrates their point. The fact that the outcome turns useful does not prove a formal production approval of their method or justify every undisclosed coaching step. [C:P1–58]

### EV-B0117-005 — the frog drawing and unhidden book

At a drawing prompt Shinju chooses a frog of an unusual color and starts explaining the species. A child calls the drawing cute, and Shinju smiles and thanks her. Sawara says Shinju needed no alteration. Coney asks whether the Player regards serious nitpicking of a children's anime as adult-like; he now calls it immature, then concludes that Shinju's formal register had masked for him that she is a child. This inference is partly corrective but also too simple if used to dismiss her knowledge. Afterward Shinju openly reads macroeconomics, saying she had held back because it seemed un-childlike but this experience taught her not to manage that appearance. The book and the frog are both part of her current self. No full child-audience, staff, ratings, long-term role or economic competence verdict is given. [C:P59–92]
