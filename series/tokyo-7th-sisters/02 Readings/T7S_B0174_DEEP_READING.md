## Interpretation, rivals and continuity

### Expertise is relational before it is a heroic label

Matsuri begins by boasting and posing as the older sister, but the chapter supplies concrete work behind the pose. She considers beginner access, selects roles when asked, gives advice after failed attacks and reminds the party of work they accomplished together. Her expertise provides others a place in play rather than making them an audience to her solitary mastery. The rival reading of empty bragging cannot account for their explicit reliance and the correction to Ferb's praise.

The game does offer a specially favorable competence setting. That is not the same as proving that competent Matsuri is fake or limited to avatars. Murasaki's "always" is precisely a retrospective defense of the familiar person. It remains a character's local judgment, but it matters that the chapter gives the defense to a skeptical beginner rather than to Matsuri's self-description alone. Prior B0098's audience-making game competence can be brought into comparison without claiming that either episode establishes all-purpose wisdom or permanent economic/social success.

### Different reasons for joining remain different ethical stakes

The invitation is not one unified band decision with one motive. Murasaki's uncertainty, Ferb's availability, the pair's protective plan and Yumeno's costume attraction each shape the entry. Matsuri's preconfiguration shows eager initiative, while the later assent does not supply unshown biometric/privacy understanding. The analysis need not invent a hidden refusal or condemn all play to notice that invitation, enthusiasm and consent to specific uses are distinct.

Yumeno's healer pretext repeats a boundary problem: care can be used rhetorically to seek bodily access. The source makes the real reason explicit and lets the others recognize it. Their familiarity is comic context, not permission. No actual touch is depicted here, so a claim of completed abuse would overstate the source just as a claim of legally authorized touching would. ### The guards discover the attraction they intended to manage

Ferb and Murasaki enter partly to protect QOP from Matsuri's absorption. They then enjoy altered appearance, shared danger, successful cooperation and the band's recognition within the game. Their desire to continue matters: the chapter exposes the limitations of observing a peer's habit from the outside. Their initial care does not vanish, but they share the experience that makes restraint difficult.

This supports a local reversal of position, not a diagnosis of addiction or a general moral rule that immersive play destroys judgment. The adventure yields pleasures and relational recognition that the source values. The problem arises when the group cannot keep the desired boundary. Making Matsuri solely responsible would erase the other three's enthusiastic continuation; making the experience wholly corrupt would erase the supported cooperation.

### The band analogy preserves the value of play without proving transfer

The game's front/rear roles are initially chosen through band-function analogies; later the players themselves recognize mutual cover as a QOP session. ### Familiar comedy qualifies the lesson without cancelling the person

The waking sequence can look like a return from a glorious hero to childish Matsuri. Ferb's familiarity and Yumeno's acceptance, alongside Murasaki's heroic question, preserve that coexistence.
