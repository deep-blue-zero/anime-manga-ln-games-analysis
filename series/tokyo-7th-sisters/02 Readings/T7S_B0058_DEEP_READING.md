# T7S_B0058 — the sales-mode camera

## Result

Memoru explicitly calls her fan-facing sweetness `営業モード`: a mode of doing business. She believes fans want dreams and idols supply them for money, a reciprocal exchange she names without shame. This self-aware commercial persona is not exposed as a counterfeit self. It is labor she has designed, priced, and wants to control.

Nicole's demand for something more “raw” or interesting therefore does not simply liberate authenticity. She assumes that backstage access is owed to the camera and tries to redirect Memoru's fan-club video into comedy. Memoru refuses, asserts her own production plan, and then creates chaos through the “Memoru Juice” segment she chose. The episode locates agency not in the absence of performance but in the ability to author, negotiate, and revise the performance being sold.

## Mode declared inside the mode

Memoru opens in a sweet fan-facing register. Nicole immediately asks why she is in sales mode backstage, prompting Memoru to ask camera operator Mito to stop recording before rebuking her (logs 1–5). The comic leak is important: the text itself announces the construction rather than requiring an investigator to unmask it.

Memoru explains that the video is intended to bind fan-club members more closely and calculates its commercial value (logs 6 onward). Fans want a dream; idols provide it; money returns in exchange. Her `ギブ＆テイク` and capitalism vocabulary makes the transaction visible without claiming that every party has equal power or complete information.

The persona remains expressive because Memoru made it for a purpose she endorses. A role can be strategically selected and still belong to the person performing it. The relevant distinction is not natural versus artificial but authored labor versus captured labor.

## “Raw” access as another imposed script

Nicole argues that simply repeating the normal idol character will not make a compelling video. Fans should receive the “raw” Memoru, which Nicole promptly defines through a demand for comic entertainment. The rhetoric of authenticity conceals a new direction imposed by someone else.

Memoru resists. She has planned the content and does not accept Nicole's authority to replace it. Mito's evident enjoyment of the camera further pluralizes the production: camera operation is not an invisible neutral function, and Nicole is not the only participant with ideas about what should be shown.

The dispute mirrors the birthday video from `T7S_B0055` but redistributes control. Mana's recording was redirected toward an image she explicitly rejected. Here Memoru names the competing frame and preserves her own segment. The ensuing disorder is not proof that Nicole found the hidden true Memoru; it is what Memoru's own chosen demonstration produces.

## Memoru Juice and controlled unpredictability

Memoru introduces a secret drink and begins a grotesquely improvised preparation. The ingredients and reactions turn a fan-club sales object into ensemble slapstick. Her commercial instinct does not prevent mess, excess, anger, or peer participation. It gives those elements a container through which they can become content.

The episode's joke depends on the coexistence of calculation and loss of control. Memoru can plan a product and still generate results she did not master. Authorship is therefore practical rather than sovereign: she determines the undertaking's initial purpose and can resist replacement, while coworkers, ingredients, and accidents alter the finished artifact.

This is a more useful account of idol performance than the binary Nicole proposes. “Sales mode” and backstage aggression are not mutually exclusive truth claims. They are relational vocabularies for different audiences, both susceptible to consent and labor questions.

## Character and relationship state

- Memoru understands her idol persona as commercial production and refuses shame about exchange. She is neither an innocent product of branding nor the sole controller of its reception.
- Nicole values surprise and privileged access, but her appeal to authenticity can become another command. Her intervention reproduces the representational pressure she treats as liberation.
- Mito occupies the camera position with visible interest, making recording itself a participant role rather than transparent observation.
- The ensemble knows the difference between Memoru's fan address and backstage register. That knowledge permits comedy; it does not make private access a public entitlement.

## Closeout

Memoru's episode makes commercial performance compatible with agency while refusing to romanticize exposure. The camera does not become more truthful merely by crossing backstage. Truthful analysis must instead track who selected the mode, who can change it, whose audience is being addressed, and whether the performer can refuse the proposed version of herself.
