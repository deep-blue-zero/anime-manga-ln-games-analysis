# Kajika's ordinary self and wanted recognition

Kajika wants to be seen as a particular person. Calling herself ordinary beside Sawara and Shinju makes the manager's attention feel potentially exceptional: perhaps he is the first to have looked properly at her alone. Yet these nine cases also give her concrete things to say and do. She recommends seasonal fish to customers, chooses to study, wants to return care and explicitly wants to continue idol work. Her self-description does not exhaust the contribution the dialogue supplies.

The difficulty is often how to recognize that contribution. In an imagined future fish shop, Kajika can readily assign distinctive roles to her sisters and then wonder what remains for her. In idol work, other people's happiness becomes a reason she herself wants to continue. Receiving attention and giving something back are connected, but they are not a settled account of debt, worth or permanent confidence. The cases leave her questions present as written questions, without manufacturing the manager's answers or arranging the menu into a completed cure.
