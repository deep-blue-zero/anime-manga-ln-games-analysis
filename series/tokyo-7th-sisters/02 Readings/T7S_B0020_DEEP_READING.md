# T7S_B0020 — family is the person who has already made room

## Result

EPISODE 2.0 closes with two household stories. Susu has fled an overseas family because she believes receiving a non-biological mother's affection would displace her younger sisters. Sawara, temporarily incapacitated by a cold, believes adult illness makes her disappear from everyone else's field of care. Both beliefs are answered less by a declaration than by evidence that somebody anticipated the absent person's return.

## Susu: refusing love to preserve a place for others

Susu's distance from home is structured by a zero-sum model. Her biological mother is absent from the current household for reasons the source does not establish; her father's current wife is not her birth mother and has younger daughters. Susu fears that accepting the new mother's affection would take something that belongs to those girls. She therefore ignores letters and gifts and relocates to Japan, the country associated with Seven Sisters, hoping to find a special relation that will not require this theft.

The fear is morally generous and relationally destructive. Susu tries to protect her sisters by removing herself from the family, but this makes her decide for everyone what their bonds can hold. Distance becomes an attempt to govern love by scarcity.

## Musubi's imperfect intervention

Musubi follows Susu and discloses her own history of running away and feeling alone. The identification is valuable because it does not reduce Susu to ingratitude. Musubi understands flight as an effort to manage the fear of disappointing or burdening people. She nevertheless opens Susu's letter without permission. The text acknowledges the breach; empathy does not confer access rights.

The perfume enclosed with the letter becomes the decisive evidence. Susu's current mother selected it from her memory of Susu's scent. This is not blood resemblance or formal status. It is sustained attention to a particular person across absence. The letter's promise that Susu can always return and that her father remains her father refuses the zero-sum premise.

Susu retains the nickname given by her biological mother rather than replacing one mother with another. Her reconciliation adds relation without cancelling prior attachment. She writes back, names her parents, and can describe Musubi as a dependable and cute older sister. “Sister” expands because distinct bonds need not compete for one slot.

## The Harumi sisters: care before request

The Harumi chapter begins with farce: Sawara eats Kajika's cream puffs, Shinju hides hers in the fish-shop refrigerator, and it absorbs the smell of fish. Shinju becomes ill; then Sawara catches the cold. The tonal smallness is the point. After public branding and official commissions, family is tested through refrigeration, food, money, work, and who notices a person in bed.

Sawara experiences adult illness as a collapse of social visibility. Because she normally performs the eldest sister's competence, incapacity produces the fear that the world has abandoned her. Kajika uses scarce spending money to bring her a cream puff. Shinju had already predicted that the Player would visit and preserves her own gift for him. Care appears as action taken before the recipient can ask and sometimes before the carer can know whether the anticipated visitor will come.

The role of “older sister” is therefore reversible. Sawara is not expelled from it when she needs care; Kajika and Shinju are not merely dependents when they provide it. The household survives because responsibility moves.

## Food as a grammar of recognition

Perfume and cream puffs are modest carriers of memory. Neither solves structural distance or household strain. Each says that somebody pictured a specific person while that person was absent. The later squid cream puffs restore comedy, but they also keep care material: Sawara answers being cared for by making something in the language of the family shop, even if the result is dubious.

## Exit state

- Susu's family distance is traced to an unresolved earlier maternal absence and a zero-sum fear of displacing her younger sisters, not simple rejection.
- Her current mother's remembered-scent gift and family letter establish particularized, non-biological care.
- Susu preserves her biological mother's nickname while reopening relation with her father and current mother.
- Musubi becomes an explicitly named older-sister figure while her unauthorized reading remains an ethical boundary failure.
- Sawara's illness reveals the vulnerability concealed by eldest-sister competence; Kajika and Shinju provide anticipatory care.
- EPISODE 2.0 ends with “Sisters” as a practice of making room for distinct people, not proof of blood, brand, or self-sufficiency.
