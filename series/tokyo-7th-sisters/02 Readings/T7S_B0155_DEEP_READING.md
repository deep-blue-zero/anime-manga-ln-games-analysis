# T7S_B0155 — Shinju and the Mysterious Frog

## Result and entering boundary

Shinju gives Sui cautious advice about a teammate's possible withdrawal from sport, then privately discounts her own worth because she is a child repeating book learning. She appears to rescue a frog, which speaks and calls itself Kerosuke. In the ensuing possible dream, it asks her to relocate a stone-like gate to save an unseen frog kingdom from unsuitable urban surroundings. She evaluates several habitats and places the stone in a Kuonji-built park. Kerosuke says her apparently simple act mattered because others who saw him did not help, then says he will become an ordinary frog whom she cannot identify. Shinju wakes in the park with tears. Later she agrees more readily to hear Sumire's problem and understands that modest help, including idol work, can be valuable. Whether a talking Kerosuke, magic kingdom, interdimensional gate or rescue actually existed outside her experience remains deliberately unconfirmed.

## Factual reconstruction by frame and actor knowledge

### EV-B0155-001 — a careful answer, then self-doubt

Sui recounts a schoolmate who says she may quit a long-held sport. Shinju notes the reasons sound unclear, acknowledges possible undisclosed distress, and recommends that Sui state her own wish that the teammate stay rather than decide for her. Sui values the advice; Sumire and the Player agree. Shinju privately thinks she is being overrated: her knowledge is book-derived, a child sounds impressive only beside expectations of children, and she still knows little. Her advice is bounded and compassionate even though she rejects its significance. The other student's intent and circumstances remain unverified secondhand information. [A:P2–43]

### EV-B0155-002 — rescue turns into ontological uncertainty

Shinju sees a frog struggling in a water channel and lifts it to safety. The frog thanks her in Japanese. She considers a lucid dream the likeliest explanation; the frog counters with logical possibilities, then claims an endangered magical kingdom. It says the kingdom is a parliamentary democracy with a nominal king. Its self-description is presented as speech *within* the extraordinary encounter, not externally verified world-state. Shinju's doubt is explicit before the eventual waking. [A:P44–64; B:P1–18]

### EV-B0155-003 — Kerosuke names himself and invites confidence

The frog asks Shinju to take him toward his home, and they ride a train. He says most others cannot see him and calls her a chosen hero; she interprets that as the sort of role a dream gives her. He names himself Kerosuke and asks her to introduce herself. Noticing her discomfort, he invites her to talk as a passing frog or friend. She tells him that adult-like speech makes people overestimate her, while she can only do small, ordinary things. Kerosuke says he too is labeled a hero though he wanted to travel, and that any person can only act within capacity. Shinju experiences being understood; the relationship is vivid inside the scene without proving a persistent external frog identity. [B:P19–74]

### EV-B0155-004 — a stone-gate relocation task

Kerosuke says development made the kingdom's present connection inhospitable. The plain stone Shinju picks up is, by his account, a beacon/gate to an alternate-space country; moving it to a suitable habitat would help. Shinju observes that carrying a stone is a task almost anyone could do and agrees. They reject a shoreward water site over salinity and pedestrian traffic, a green place over possible future development, and a protected scenic site over tourist numbers. She chooses a larger city park with quiet water, noting Kuonji built it and speculating it is unlikely to disappear, perhaps with Shizuka's help if needed. Kerosuke declares the kingdom saved. These environmental, science-fiction and land-stability details are within the uncertain episode; no actual ecosystem survey, land-rights protection or relocation effect is independently shown. [C:P1–62]

### EV-B0155-005 — the cost of being unrecognizable

When Shinju asks to meet again, Kerosuke says the spell ends after the journey: outside frogs revert to ordinary frogs and cannot return, to prevent over-contact between intelligent species. He says she may see him but never know which frog he is. The claimed rule intensifies a farewell, not a confirmed taxonomy or permanent death. He also says others could see him but only Shinju helped, and distinguishes the ease of an action from the willingness to act. Even if another might save the country, he says Shinju alone saved *him* at that moment. This is Kerosuke's account, not an independently checked comparison with every passerby. [C:P63–113]

### EV-B0155-006 — waking preserves emotion, not ontology

Shinju wakes or becomes aware in the park; Kajika says she was napping and notices tears. Shinju says it was a dream and gives no account to Kajika. She later goes to the park, cannot identify Kerosuke or even confirm his presence, and still wonders if it was a dream. Sawara makes a broad, playful comment about unknown encounters, not a corroborating eyewitness statement. The lingering tears are evidence of felt significance, not a proof that the gate existed. [C:P114–135,144–160]

### EV-B0155-007 — small help becomes enough to offer

Back at Nanastar, Sumire asks Shinju for an opinion. Shinju now agrees without the previous visible reluctance and privately reasons that even small help can matter. She explicitly extends the thought to being an idol as a small light. The conclusion does not make her omniscient, cure every counselee's problem or establish Kerosuke's objective reality; it changes her willingness to use her limited knowledge with care. [C:P136–165]

## Interpretation and alternatives

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0155-001.** The chapter separates generic *ability* from an enacted decision to help. Shinju's advice and possible frog rescue are modest acts whose relational value does not depend on adult expertise or unique physical power. [A:P2–43; C:P85–111,136–165]

Rival: because anyone could speak or move a stone, Shinju's action is worthless; or because she helps, she is a uniquely infallible “chosen hero.” Both overstate what the record shows.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0155-002.** Dream, magic and a material talking frog remain open narrative alternatives. Her waking, subsequent uncertainty and lack of external witness prevent promotion of Kaeru, its beacon or environmental relocation into ordinary 2034 world-state; the episode's emotional outcome does not depend on choosing one ontology. [A:P44–64; B:P1–74; C:P1–160]

Rival: tears prove reality, or a waking scene proves that nothing meaningful occurred. Neither claim follows.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0155-003.** Kerosuke's friendship and farewell give Shinju a space to say she is a child rather than the “little adult” others seek. His claim that he too is called a hero relativizes role labels and lets her revalue care without pretending expertise. [A:P38–43; B:P44–74; C:P63–113]

Rival: the story makes every child responsible for saving strangers, or says books and competence are irrelevant. Shinju's opening answer models uncertainty and a limited responsibility.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0155-004.** The habitat search names plausible concerns—salt water, foot traffic, future development and tourism—but is not an ecological or planning finding. Even the Kuonji park's continuity is Shinju's expectation; no protected status, consent to place an object, species assessment or measured impact is documented. [C:P1–62]

Rival: a good-looking park and Kerosuke's “saved” declaration verify a real conservation intervention. Only the framed account supports that conclusion.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0155-005.** After waking, Shinju accepts that incomplete knowledge need not prevent attentive response to Sumire. Her “small light” analogy links peer counsel with idol work without ranking one as the same technical practice as the other. [A:P2–43; C:P136–165]

Rival: the frog has bestowed supernatural expertise or all consultations will succeed. The ending says her acts remain small.
