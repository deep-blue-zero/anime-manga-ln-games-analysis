## Interpretation and alternatives

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0102-001.** The first conflict is neither simply a lazy performer needing discipline nor a group whose need for coordinated rehearsal is imaginary. Murasaki identifies concrete family-shop pressure and sleep loss; Musubi identifies the real cost of absent shared practice. The Player's reluctance to interfere is ethically legible but leaves the schedule unnegotiated. Musubi's comparison with other shop-working members names a common problem but assumes comparable capacity without evidence. The local solution changes Murasaki's motivation and participation; no one is shown changing staffing, rest or lesson hours. [A:P10–34; B:P42–50]

Rival: the episode's final early arrival might prove the practical conflict over. It proves attendance on one day, not that her mother's shop can spare her regularly or that the promised practice is sustainable. Conversely, Murasaki's earlier labor explanation is not an alibi for indifference: she also explicitly chose self-practice and treated exclusion as acceptable. Both constraints matter.

**THEMATIC INTERPRETATION — CL-B0102-002.** Murasaki's Hami ideal first selects the wrong feature of the model: a solo singer's apparent self-sufficiency. Coney restores a relational origin to the music, and Murasaki chooses collaboration without renouncing a distinct voice or her ability to sing at the bar piano. The chapter thereby tests independence as making a contribution within relation rather than completing oneself by severing it. Her return of Musubi's `足並みをそろえて` phrase signals a changed use of group language: from an external demand to her own teasing commitment. [A:P27–32; B:P19–37,42–49]

Rival: Coney merely deploys a celebrity anecdote to pressure a fan into conformity. Her timing and authority do create that risk. But Murasaki identifies the missing part of her own stated ideal, invites the private song, and chooses the repeated rehearsal herself; Musubi corrects her actual step rather than asking for silent obedience. This does not erase the authority imbalance or prove unconstrained consent in every later setting.

**THEMATIC INTERPRETATION — CL-B0102-003.** The two work sites exchange who is visible and who is misunderstood. Daytime Nanasta sees late attendance but not the night work in full; the Player's final surprise sees improved attendance but not Coney's intervention. Coney meets Murasaki where the burden is enacted, and their exchange about several faces resists reducing either woman to a public role. The Player's final complaint that Coney is late in turn repeats the chapter's initial limited view of lateness. Unlike the opening, Murasaki knows the private reason and laughs. This is a deliberately bounded structural echo: the text does not say Coney's one overslept morning and Murasaki's ongoing family labor are materially equivalent. [A:P10–19,38–44; B:P1–5,40–55]
