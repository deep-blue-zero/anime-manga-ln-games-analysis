# Tokyo 7th Sisters — B0006 deep reading

## The missing middle: Momoka joins because Nanasta learns to refresh interest, not because she stops finding idol labor troublesome

## 0. Result, scope, and integrity

B0006 closes the complete nine-episode native Main chapter `300060`, モモカ・ゴー・ホーム！. Its repeated reversals are more coherent than the label “lazy” suggests. 芹沢モモカ attends intensely to anime, is highly skilled at a fighting game, chooses elaborate self-presentation, learns a live routine in a short interval, and performs successfully when a role captures her interest. What she persistently avoids is the unglamorous interval between an attractive finished identity and its repetition: preparation, lessons, instrument practice, and durable obligation. **The chapter does not cure this disposition. It turns anime from a source of false effortlessness into a renewable action script, then makes Nanasta responsible for keeping that script charged. Momoka's effort and pleasure are real while the identification lasts; her desire for future lessons remains unstable at the exact moment she is formally admitted.**

That solution is productive and ethically compromised. Coney accurately recognizes Momoka's responsiveness to media, but reaches the result through pursuit, invented opportunities, blocked exits, a baited audition, a scripted loss, and a deliberately selected sports-training anime hidden with the next schedule. Momoka repeatedly gives real but local assent—to a stage, a band role, one product audition, a later stream, and present membership—while repeatedly refusing or withdrawing from continuing labor. Neither side can be collapsed into the other.

## 1. Descriptive reconstruction

### 1.1 Momoka is selective before she is recruited (`201000501`)

Momoka watches `魔女っ子アイドル ヨシコ・ザ・ギャザリング` closely enough to praise its animation and dance and to remember nearly dropping it after episode two. She imagines idol work as only singing and dancing and therefore easy. In the same monologue she remembers next-day preparation, decides that her full effort could finish it quickly, and assigns it to `明日のモモカ`. [E01] The scene establishes neither incapacity nor generalized inattention. It establishes confidence without process and a comic division between the present self who wants rest and the future self expected to absorb the cost.

Coney and the Player are themselves avoiding or disputing work at the arcade. An unknown challenger defeats Coney so decisively that the Player calls the latter helpless. Momoka then prioritizes home, unwatched recordings, a late-night broadcast, and sleep. She says she has chosen to live honestly according to her own feelings. Coney's talent/cosplay sensor activates, but personal rematch anger displaces the stated scouting mission. [E02] Momoka enters the chapter as both skilled and work-averse; Coney enters this recruitment as both perceptive and competitively compromised.

### 1.2 A desired image produces real participation but no lesson commitment (`201000502`–`201000503`)

Coney returns with Haru, Musubi, Rona, Hime, and the Player under the language of scouting, while plainly seeking an opponent. Momoka no longer wants the same fighting game. When Coney changes the offered “game” to a nearby stage and Haru names idol work, Momoka's affect and answer change immediately. Coney calls Nanasta legendary; the Player notices the exaggeration. Momoka interrupts the introductions with `やる！ モモカもアイドルやりたいっ！` [E03] Her first idol participation is self-initiated after the category becomes clear, but her information is thin and fantasy-shaped.

The next document opens after an elided stage. Haru enjoyed working with her, Coney judges her talented, and the Player reports rising comments/view counts. Momoka says anime was her study and volunteers for Nanasta, citing talent and cute clothing. Then she asks what lessons are. Talent, in her model, should remove their necessity; anime success seemed to require none. When Coney says effort increases later emotion, Momoka declares the process too troublesome and quits immediately. [E04]

This is not a revelation that the original interest was counterfeit. The stage, image, and costume remain things she wanted and apparently handled well. It is the first direct collision between imagined completed form and maintenance labor.

### 1.3 The band repeats the structure and makes self-fashioning visible (`201000504`–`201000505`)

At a music shop Momoka has transferred her interest to a girls band after watching a current anime. She has already chosen a complete pink-and-black “rock” costume but cannot play guitar. Coney invents a Nanasta band opening and offers immediate leadership. Momoka accepts because anime has already taught her how this should go. The Player's only choice group either corrects Momoka's mistaken `ハチスカ` immediately or briefly joins the joke; both branches converge before the offer and change no durable state. [E05]

At Nanasta Momoka presents Haru, Musubi, Rona, and Hime as the other members. The visual tableau works well enough for the Player to say that merely holding instruments looks convincing, while Rona says keyboard is impossible and Hime reports numb hands from drumming. Coney converts the presentation toward a contract and major debut. Momoka instead says it was fun, admits she cannot play, prefers to return to anime, and dissolves `モモカバンド` for “musical differences.” Coney offers mimed instruments, mimed singing, and later audio repair; the Player rejects this as impermissible. [E06]

The borrowed dissolution phrase is another usable media script: it lets Momoka narrate withdrawal as part of the fantasy rather than failure to complete it. Her costume and leader role are genuine pleasures, but neither implies consent to learn an instrument or sustain a group.

### 1.4 Recruitment becomes an explicit capture operation (`201000506`–`201000507`)

Coney next stages a full mission. She calls Momoka the most important target, compares persistence to spending credits until an arcade prize is obtained, and orders the Player and Rona to block exits. Haru and Hime perform a badly copied period-drama appeal. Momoka says she dislikes troublesome work, wants candy and idleness, believes many others are more suited, and explicitly refuses idol work because it only tires her. Rona's assigned line—Momoka has her own life—accidentally states the ethical objection most clearly. [E07]

The group then displays an unreleased Chubax candy and makes cooperation conditional on access. They promise an audition, a year's unlimited candy, and the power to design flavors. Momoka agrees to be an idol `あと一回だけ`, specifically for that product opportunity. [E08] This is an assent to a bounded exchange, not agreement to Nanasta membership.

After the elided audition, the Player reads a scripted result awarding the commercial to contestant 14. Momoka asks whether contestant 14 even existed. She receives candy and a keepsake, says the labor has exhausted her, and again leaves idol work. The Player later says that if she chooses another path, that should be accepted. Coney's villainous laughter and Haru's remaining script expose that the participation gift carries a further plan. [E09]

### 1.5 Media changes from fantasy shortcut to a temporary practice script (`201000508`)

Nanasta has prepared a five-person stream formation even though Momoka has not arrived. Hime says an unwilling person should not be forced, directly carrying forward the boundary she asserted through her own prior costume flight. Rona suggests `ニコさ――じゃなくてコニーさん` as substitute; the group falls silent when the Player asks why a manager should perform. The slip strengthens the existing Coney/Nicole knowledge asymmetry without showing that every silent participant knows the exact answer. [E10]

Momoka then arrives without being physically brought back. She says she came to do idol work, memorized what she needed, and has full power. Coney explains the mechanism: the participation gift included an old sports-training idol anime selected because Momoka would be influenced, and the schedule was placed with it. [E11]

The distinction from the opening anime matters. The magical-girl show made idol work look effortless; `アタックオンリーワン` makes training itself narratively exciting. Media has not ceased to mediate Momoka's desire. It temporarily supplies the missing middle as an attractive role she can enact.

### 1.6 Formal admission occurs before durable lesson consent (`201000509`)

The document opens after the stream. The Player reports favorable response and declares Momoka formally admitted. Peers praise how much she achieved in a short interval. Momoka accepts enthusiastically, calls the result natural for her, and says she loves idols when Coney warns of strict future training. Coney describes the intervention as digging up motivation already asleep inside Momoka. [E12]

The claim is immediately stress-tested. Musubi proposes lessons; Momoka's confident sentence breaks at `がん――` and turns toward `めんどくさい`. Coney asks for a monitor and the anime's second series. The new footage partially restores the effort script, but Momoka says she has seen it. Her speech then literally collides—`レッスンめん――ばる`, `アイドルめん――がん、がんばる`—while Coney and the others cheer the “try” fragment and rush her onward before the charge expires. The Player concludes that they must never run out of anime. [E13]

The exit therefore records a real successful act and current membership alongside a live counterexample to motivational conversion. Momoka has not become an internally stable worker. Nanasta has adopted ongoing stimulus management as its answer.

## 2. Analytical findings

### 2.1 `めんどくさい` is an allocation rule, not proof of emptiness

Momoka uses “troublesome” to rank demands against immediate interest. She will repeatedly watch an anime, master a fighting game, design a band image, memorize a stream routine, and appear before an audience. She will not prepare merely because tomorrow requires it, repeat a game after interest has gone, or accept lessons because institutions define them as necessary.

The chapter's comedy calls this laziness, but its evidence is selective. “Tomorrow's Momoka” externalizes delayed cost; going home protects anime, sleep, and candy time; band dissolution prevents practice from inheriting authority from momentary play. A useful reconstruction must distinguish capability, current interest, willingness, and sustained obligation. None can stand in for the others.

### 2.2 Momoka's reversals have a stable logic

The object of fascination changes—fighting game, magical-girl idol, girls band, candy commercial, sports idol—but the decision rule recurs. She approaches an immediately vivid role, performs or styles it quickly, and withdraws when continuation becomes repetitive labor or when a different interest wins. Her assertion that she lives honestly according to her feelings is therefore not refuted by inconsistency. It describes a self whose present preferences hold unusually strong authority over past commitments and future costs.

This is why the chapter title's repeated “go home” matters. Home is not merely retreat from development. It is the location of her chosen media life and the reset point from which the next identification can begin.

### 2.3 Anime is neither mere deception nor an autonomous true self

Momoka is an active viewer. She notices animation quality, makes drop/continue judgments, schedules broadcasts, accumulates recordings, and rewatches. Media gives her models through which unfamiliar practices become legible. The initial model omits labor; the band model supplies costume and leadership; the sports model makes effort temporarily desirable. In each case she genuinely acts through what she has seen.

Coney's final intervention works because this is a real capacity. It is manipulative because she selects and plants a model to produce a desired behavior without candidly negotiating the long-term obligation. The best account therefore avoids both extremes: anime does not hypnotize an empty subject, and every media-activated preference is not automatically a durable, fully informed commitment.

### 2.4 Talent is imagined as exemption from process

Haru's chapter separated desire from talent because praise had displaced wanting. Momoka's chapter exposes a different relation. She eagerly accepts evidence of talent because it seems to remove the need for lessons. Talent is not alienating praise here; it is a shortcut from attractive identity to finished performance.

The source gives the belief partial fuel. Momoka does perform well at once and later learns unusually quickly. But neither success establishes repeatability without preparation. The finale preserves this gap rather than proving that Coney's “dormant motivation” explanation is complete.

### 2.5 Nanasta turns motivation into an external supply chain

The opening makes the present self transfer work to a future self. The ending enlarges that structure: Coney curates narrative fuel, peers cheer the usable fragment, and the Player resolves to maintain an anime supply. What Momoka does not consistently carry across time, the institution proposes to store and reactivate around her.

This can be read as accommodation rather than simple correction. Nanasta does not demand a new personality before recognizing her talent; it finds a medium that lets her work. But the accommodation remains fragile and managerial. It depends on novelty, can be exhausted by already-seen material, and is introduced through deception. Whether Momoka may help choose the system, whether it remains effective, and what happens when supply fails are open institutional questions.

### 2.6 The chapter makes recruitment ethics unusually visible

Coney's methods escalate from contaminated motive to explicit capture. She treats Momoka like an arcade prize, blocks exits, scripts peers, makes access to candy conditional, rigs or at minimum scripts an audition result around a possibly absent winner, and plants media/schedule to induce return. Her insight and the successful stream do not neutralize those facts.

The Player supplies intermittent safeguards. He notices Coney's initial dereliction, her exaggerated “legendary” claim, and the impermissibility of mimed performance with repaired audio. He later voices the principle that a different path should be accepted. Yet he also blocks an exit, reads the scripted audition result, accepts the planted schedule, formally admits Momoka without a shown renewed terms discussion, and embraces continuous anime charging. Ethical recognition again exceeds institutional control.

Hime's `やりたくないヤツに無理矢理やらせてもな` is the strongest peer counter-speech. It shows that the prior chapter's target of pressure has learned a general boundary. It does not prevent the group from having rehearsed a formation that assumes Momoka's arrival.

### 2.7 Consent is local, current, and information-conditioned

The chapter preserves at least seven distinct states:

1. Momoka refuses a rematch and leaves for chosen leisure.
2. She explicitly volunteers for an idol stage after the category attracts her.
3. She volunteers for Nanasta after success, then refuses lessons and quits.
4. She accepts an invented band role and leadership, then dissolves it rather than practice.
5. After an explicit idol refusal, she agrees to one candy audition under promised rewards and leaves again.
6. After receiving planted sports-anime and schedule material, she returns, prepares, and completes a stream.
7. She accepts present formal membership enthusiastically, then immediately resists the next lesson until more media partially restores `がんばる`.

This is development without retroactive authorization. The stream demonstrates effort; it does not convert every prior refusal into hidden consent. Conversely, the later relapse does not make the stream counterfeit. Long-term membership is textually current at the exit, while informed and durable assent to its recurring labor remains unresolved.

### 2.8 The Coney/Nicole asymmetry advances without disclosure

Rona nearly names Coney `ニコさま` while proposing her as a missing performer and corrects herself to the presented name. When the Player asks why the manager should substitute, the assembled girls answer with silence. This is further evidence that the Player remains outside a socially shared implication and that Rona continues actively managing the cover story.

It does not prove that Hime or every silent person has independently resolved the identity, and Coney offers no confession. `THREAD-0002` therefore strengthens at the knowledge-distribution layer while remaining open at literal disclosure and machine identity.

### 2.9 Textual and visual form stage the motivational collision

The ending does not merely report ambivalence. Momoka's Japanese utterances splice the beginnings of `めんどくさい` and `がんばる`, making two action scripts compete inside single words. Coney then repeats only the productive completion as a public commitment. The technique preserves resistance even as the group operationalizes assent.

### 3.1 Open obligations

- Preserve Momoka's skill, taste, fandom, present-feeling honesty, work aversion, and unstable follow-through as simultaneous states; do not reduce one to proof that another is false.
- Test whether `めんどくさい` changes by task, relation, reward, fatigue, novelty, or control, rather than treating it as a context-free personality essence.
- Preserve each voluntary role entry, refusal, withdrawal, transactional assent, stream preparation, membership acceptance, and lesson resistance as a distinct decision under its actual information.
- Test whether anime support becomes collaborative accommodation or remains a deceptive external control system, especially after new material is exhausted.
- Carry Hime's force objection forward and test whether peer ethical learning acquires institutional effect.
- Strengthen the Coney/Nicole knowledge-distribution evidence without assigning exact knowledge to silent participants or converting inference into explicit disclosure.
- Keep performed voice, singing, music, continuous motion, full runtime placement/effects, and all elided acts non-load-bearing until directly reviewed evidence exists.
- Do not promote Momoka beyond `R2_STATE_BOUNDED` or create a monograph/model from one introduction chapter.
