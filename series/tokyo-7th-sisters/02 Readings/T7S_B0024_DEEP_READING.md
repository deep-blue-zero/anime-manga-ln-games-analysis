# T7S_B0024 — the political force of “see you tomorrow”

## Result

The KARAKURI unit ends not at Grand Fest but in Nanasta's rediscovered bathhouse. Hitoha and Futaba clean, eat, bathe, sing casually, receive separate pajamas, and sleep beside other girls. The coda translates the climax's public proposition—two distinct people may choose relation—into repeated domestic time. A future is no longer only another victory. It is tomorrow.

## Recovering the building's unused promise

Nanasta's building was originally designed to contain a large bathhouse. Restoring it makes institutional history materially useful without asking the new generation to reproduce the old one. A leftover structure becomes a shared facility through present labor.

Musubi teaches Hitoha and Futaba ordinary cleaning and bathing practices. The twins' lack of experience reflects the training system that optimized performance while withholding daily autonomy. Here competence is transferred without testing, ranking, or discarding the learner. Other members join the work; instruction becomes participation.

## Repetition with difference

The group shares another hot pot, but the twins' separate bowls and chopsticks have been saved. The Player buys separate pajamas. These objects repeat B0021's lesson without turning it into a speech. Common food, room, and schedule coexist with personal possession.

The reviewed bath stills use distinct body assets and stances for Hitoha and Futaba, with separate warm expression layers at native offsets. After the solo-stage differentiation, the coda insists that embodiment remains distinct even inside maximal domestic intimacy.

## Singing after the contest

The girls sing in the bath without a scored public competition. Musubi thanks Hitoha and Futaba separately for singing. They answer that people like Musubi enable their song. This revises the isolated-genius story without replacing it with institutional ownership. Singing remains theirs, but its conditions include people who teach, welcome, praise, and return.

Because the performed audio was not reviewed, the analysis makes no claim about vocal timbre, blend, acoustics, or musical execution. The narrative function is established by the dialogue and page events.

## Warmth and the next day

At bedtime, physical warmth becomes the language of emotional safety. The twins can be close to others without declaring permanent fusion or guaranteed non-abandonment. The final “good night” and “see you tomorrow” is stronger than a promise of eternity precisely because it is limited. Tomorrow can arrive through repeated care.

This makes the bathhouse chapter structurally necessary rather than disposable fan-service. Grand Fest proves that KARAKURI can revise its public form and survive. The epilogue tests whether the named children can inhabit an ordinary social duration after the applause stops. Their answer is provisional and therefore credible.
