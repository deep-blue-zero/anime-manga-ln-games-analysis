# T7S_B0111 — My Special Thing

## Result and entering boundary

Sumire says idol work is rewarding and fun, yet wonders whether the fulfillment is `real`. An offhand proposal that she use her nail-design sense in artistic promotion leads her to ask a school art club about supplies and technique. She enjoys the visit and imagines she might have found an absorbing practice there instead. The alternative frightens her not because art is bad but because she reads its possibility as making everything she has done at Nanasta counterfeit. She withdraws from both places. Sui and the Player mistake the absence for possible art-club involvement; the Player says he would be sad but accept a genuine change of preference. Haru finds Sumire at Nanasta's entrance, hears the actual reason, then announces that Sumire will join the imminent stream before obtaining a clear answer. On the stage-like presentation Sumire takes Haru's argument—that her fear of treating idol work as interchangeable already shows it has become special to her—and later says she did not join the art club and wants to give Nanasta her full effort now. Her present choice is explicit at the exit; Haru's earlier insistence is a separate consent issue, and neither choice devalues her real enjoyment of art.

Native `アタシの特別なもの`, `EPISODE.2.5-006`; the English heading is a gloss. Committed B0110 is the portfolio entry horizon, not an adjacent fictional day. ## Factual reconstruction by actor knowledge

### EV-B0111-001 — enjoyed work and an alternative that begins with nails

Sumire's opening self-account says her idol work and companionship are fulfilling but sometimes asks whether that feeling is authentic. While she paints her nails, Sui wonders whether nail art would look feminine on her; she then remembers swimming later and postpones. Sumire accepts the practical constraint and offers to do her nails another day, saying she thinks they would suit Sui. The exchange is not a conversion of Sui's self-description into a compulsory feminine role. [A:P0–21]

Haru and Shizuka return from an idol museum-guiding program. Shizuka says her art learning is still elementary; Haru was less knowledgeable, and the Player values a viewer's direct impression. The Player speculates that an idol making art could be promoted. Haru and Sui praise Sumire's nail/fashion sense; Sumire calls art too grand a category and suggests Shizuka would fit better. Shizuka cautions that lessons are not the same as moving viewers. The Player says it is only an idea and Sumire need not do it. No actual art commission, exhibited work, specialist qualification or consent to promotion occurs. [A:P22–51]

### EV-B0111-002 — one enjoyable club visit becomes an authenticity crisis

Sumire arrives late with paint on her hand. She says she visited her school's art club to ask about materials and technique, not to join. She admits enjoying painting in class and nail design, and the club's technical explanation interested her. When Haru assumes the invitation to join recognizes special talent, Sumire says the club merely has few members; this is Sumire's account, not an independently verified motive. Sui describes how swimming with a club feels fulfilling. The Player notices a changed expression but cannot identify the cause. Sumire's repeated `ordinary` dismissal thus coexists with sustained curiosity and a real pleasant visit. [B:P1–39]

### EV-B0111-003 — absence is known; the reason is not

At an upcoming stream rehearsal Haru, Sui, Shizuka and the Player note Sumire has not been around Nanasta recently. They infer she may have joined the art club; none has confirmed it. Sui fears that a more satisfying club might lead Sumire to leave. The Player says he would feel lonely but would accept the choice if it were hers, while rejecting the inference from absence alone. This is a voiced person-first principle, not evidence that Sumire has actually selected art. Sumire is near the entrance, then leaves; Haru sees enough to go after her. [C:P1–40]

### EV-B0111-004 — Sumire distinguishes possible paths from a supposedly false past

Haru finds Sumire, who says she only visited the club once and did enjoy it. Seeing committed students there and remembering Haru's love of song, she realizes she had entered idol work partly because the others shone and she wanted something to devote herself to. She imagines art might have met that desire as well. That possibility makes her fear all her idol work and shared history were fake; she therefore could go to neither the club nor Nanasta. Her counterfactual is her interpretation of her own motive, not proof her earlier B0007 assent was unreal. Art's attraction is not denied by this self-doubt. [C:P41–65]

### EV-B0111-005 — Haru's forceful route and Sumire's later, bounded choice

Haru says they should go, brings Sumire back, and tells the Player she will appear in the stream before Sumire agrees; Sumire objects. Haru then argues that Sumire would not be distressed by substitutability if idol work and Nanasta had not already become particular to her. This interpretation is emotionally apt but not logically the only explanation for distress; it also does not supply prior consent to perform. Haru says the stage itself will make the answer clear. Native C:P88–92 shows Sumire against a stage/crowd presentation speaking an incomplete realization, without a depicted song or performance result. [C:P66–92]

In the final office scene Sumire explicitly says she decided not to join the art club. She does not dislike it, but *now* wants to devote herself fully to Nanasta; the Player welcomes her back. The definite local choice does not prove exclusive vocation for life, invalidate art, or erase the earlier pressure of Haru announcing an appearance on her behalf. The episode ends with Sumire's private formulation of having found her special thing. [C:P93–103]

## Interpretation and alternatives

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0111-001.** Sumire's art-club encounter reactivates her B0007 adequacy rule in a new form: if she could have found meaning elsewhere, she fears her present work lacks authenticity. Multiple possible origins do not logically make completed care, labor and pleasure fake. Her panic is a genuine mental state, not a proof about the past. [A:P1–6; B:P11–38; C:P46–64]

Rival: art was only a decoy because Sumire chooses Nanasta. She says she liked the visit and could imagine staying. The opposite rival—that possible art interest means she was never serious about idols—mistakes counterfactual possibility for historical falsity.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0111-002.** The Player's acceptance of a genuine departure and Haru's fear-driven retrieval are non-equivalent responses to uncertainty. He does not know why Sumire is absent; Haru does learn it, but turns from listening to announcing a stream appearance without clear assent. Sumire's later chosen focus must not retroactively turn that announcement into permission. [C:P10–84,93–98]

Rival: Haru's encouragement is simply coercion without insight. Her interpretation addresses the precise fear Sumire confesses and precedes an explicit later choice. The contrary claim that outcome validates her entire method fails because Sumire objects before the stage scene.

**THEMATIC INTERPRETATION — CL-B0111-003.** Haru's proposition that the present can become special without a uniquely destined beginning offers Sumire a route out of all-or-nothing authenticity. The text confirms Sumire adopts the conclusion at this horizon, not that idols are necessarily her one true essence or that every artistic avenue closes. [C:P76–103]

Rival: the stage proves an objective hidden vocation. Its performed act is not shown, and Sumire's later phrase is `今は`—now. Her preference is no less real for being revisable.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0111-004.** Sumire's optional nail offer to Sui and Shizuka's humility make creative legitimacy plural and practical: swimming, nail design, school art, museum guidance and idol performance each carry different skills and constraints. Peer praise should not be inflated into certified artistic mastery or an unoffered promotion job. [A:P9–50; B:P6–33]

Rival: without formal art credentials, Sumire's nail practice is trivial. The peers' interest and her own curiosity are direct evidence of value, even though no specialist qualification follows.
