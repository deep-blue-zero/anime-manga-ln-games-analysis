# T7S_B0142 — Emerging into the World

## Result and entering boundary

Kazumi wants a scarce, expensive out-of-print collected edition at Chacha's family bookshop but lacks the cash; Chacha has no authority to discount it, Nanastar cannot advance pay, and Kazumi herself rejects a debt. When Murasaki suggests work in general, Kazumi takes it as an offer at Murasaki's family bar; Murasaki agrees and arranges a start. Kazumi hauls stock and does back-of-house tasks. A customer objectifies her and requests that she pour his drink; Murasaki says no, and Kazumi uses a literary riddle to leave. The Player, Coney and Honoka visit; Murasaki sings a number dedicated to the new worker, after which Kazumi praises its different resonance. Later Kazumi has bought the edition but says the unfamiliar labor was more stimulating than its contents, while Murasaki asks her to recommend a book. The case is not a cure of Kazumi's bookishness by a supposedly superior “real world”: labor and reading become reciprocal sources of unfamiliar experience. The chapter does not specify wage, price, shifts, employment paperwork or the song's audible form.

## Factual reconstruction by actor knowledge

### EV-B0142-001 — acquisition has several distinct constraints

Kazumi says a favorite writer's collected edition appeared once, is out of print, scarce and expensive. Chacha says her family's used-book store acquired it and worries another fan may buy it first. Kazumi has idol wages but recently bought other books and lacks ready money. Chacha refuses the Player's proposed discount because the store is run by her family, not her; the Player says Nanastar is too poor to advance the fee, and Kazumi rejects an advance as debt anyway. These separate authority, liquidity and preference constraints are not evidence that Chacha controls stock or that Kazumi is destitute. [A:P2–37]

### EV-B0142-002 — a general suggestion becomes accepted bar work

Murasaki says Kazumi might earn money through part-time work and asks whether school forbids it. Kazumi assumes Murasaki has invited her to the family shop; Murasaki says she meant work in general but, if Kazumi wants to, the bar always needs hands. She asks Kazumi to meet at the office next day so she can guide her there. The Player and Honoka are surprised. Kazumi's acceptance and Murasaki's eventual invitation are shown; actual school rules, a formal hiring process, pay rate, working hours and the family's long-term staffing outcome are not. [A:P38–63]

### EV-B0142-003 — physical and backstage work tests a different competence

The next day Murasaki starts with shopping at a wholesale supermarket. Kazumi is surprised by its scale and struggles to carry goods; Murasaki, accustomed to such runs, carries more and tells her to speak up if she cannot manage. At the bar Kazumi washes dishes, retrieves towels and bottles and clears a table. Murasaki's mother praises her quick work; Murasaki is surprised too. Kazumi repeatedly says she wants to learn and do her best. Her literary seriousness transfers into effort, not instantly equal physical capacity or full knowledge of shop work. The bar facade is native foreground art, while the mother and customer have generic silhouettes rather than individualized portraits. [B:P2–35]

### EV-B0142-004 — customer demand meets two refusals

A regular calls Kazumi attractive, comments on her `色気` and asks her to pour his drink. Murasaki immediately says that is not her task: she was assigned back-of-house work. Kazumi then offers an unattributed quotation about true freedom and asks the customer to name its author; he is nonplussed and she leaves. The exchange lets Kazumi use a literary move to exit after Murasaki has already established the task boundary. No customer contact, compelled drink service, proven author of the quotation, legal classification or permanent bar policy is shown. [B:P36–53]

### EV-B0142-005 — visitors see work, then hear Murasaki in another setting

Coney jokes about being served by a glasses-wearing newcomer, but Kazumi greets them and prepares seating. ### EV-B0142-006 — purchase and reciprocal curiosity, with elapsed time unmeasured

## Interpretation and alternatives

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0142-001.** The book purchase is achieved through a locally chosen work path after discount and pay advance are unavailable or unwanted. Chacha's family-store link supplies opportunity and market risk, not her authority to favor a friend; Kazumi's rejection of debt remains a choice, not proof every advance is inherently bad. [A:P19–63; C:P37–40]

Rival: Chacha should simply discount the book, or Nanastar can solve every member's private purchase. The dialogue explicitly assigns the store to her family and names the agency's limited cash, while withholding actual budgets/terms.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0142-002.** Kazumi's book knowledge is not discarded when she works; it helps her deflect a demand outside her assigned task. Murasaki's immediate refusal matters too: the literary riddle is not the only boundary, and a worker need not be clever to deserve one. [B:P24–53]

Rival: Kazumi's quotation alone solved the customer's conduct, or Murasaki overreacted to ordinary hospitality. The request follows explicit appraisal of her appearance, and Murasaki had assigned back-of-house work.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0142-003.** The chapter's final exchange is reciprocal discovery rather than a one-way descent from literature into life. Kazumi ranks unfamiliar work as especially stimulating yet still reads and treasures the edition; Murasaki asks for a book matched to her. [C:P26–50]

Rival: the purchased set disappoints and she abandons reading, or Murasaki's curiosity proves a durable reading habit. Neither is shown.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0142-004.** The bar is productive and personal space as well as a stage for Murasaki's song. Her practiced carrying and backstage instruction make family labor visible, while the song and Kazumi's response let each see the other's competence anew. This does not erase possible labor cost: B0102 separately records Murasaki's exhaustion, and this chapter gives no hours/pay/rest account or full song. [B:P2–35; C:P1–35]

Rival: a pleasing performance proves employment was costless or bar work cured Murasaki's earlier strain. No such resolution is supplied.
