# T7S_B0028 — needing someone who does not need to be helpless

## Result

Musubi's collapse reveals that NI+CORA has confused one member's constant management with the natural shape of intimacy. Musubi makes Susu punctual, remembers their schedule, apologizes for her lateness, plans the set list, maintains student-council duties, and calls the remaining intervals rest. Susu answers by carrying their work and live alone. Her competence does not prove that Musubi is unnecessary; it proves that being needed cannot depend on keeping a partner incapable. NI+CORA becomes reciprocal when each can act independently and still names the other's presence as constitutive of her own.

## Competence as invisible infrastructure

The opening quarrel makes Musubi's work audible because she lists it while scolding Susu: morning calls, schedule memory, early departure, contingency time, apologies to staff, and live planning. Much of this labor is anticipatory. When it succeeds, nothing happens—Susu arrives, the job proceeds, and no one sees the avoided failure. Musubi therefore appears merely controlling while the unit depends on her constant production of order.

The Player offers to accompany NI+CORA, but Musubi refuses because he has the whole agency to manage. Her independence is both real competence and a transfer of institutional responsibility downward. She adds student-council work to idol and unit management, then answers a question about rest by reporting sleep and pauses between tasks. The Player's “not that” distinguishes available minutes from relief from responsibility. Musubi can stop moving without ceasing to monitor what must happen next.

The source identifies her condition in dialogue as a severe cold and explicitly connects the collapse with accumulated fatigue. It does not provide a clinical record, and this reading makes no diagnosis beyond that narrative account. The analytical point is structural: a person celebrated for never getting sick has learned to treat endurance as evidence that the load is acceptable, until her body interrupts the argument.

## Public discipline and private feeling

Musubi's objection to Susu calling the Player “darling” combines legitimate professional concern and disavowed personal affect. She invokes scandals that have destroyed careers for reasons unrelated to performers' substantive work. Susu asks whether the jealousy is directed at the Player or at her; Musubi relocates the exchange into role discipline. The chapter never forces a definitive confession, and its later return to the question preserves that ambiguity.

This is not simply coy romantic comedy. Public legibility constrains how affection may be expressed, while Musubi uses professional responsibility as a language through which she can remain close without naming her own desire. Susu's provocations are likewise both sincere and playful. Their unit intimacy is conducted in the unstable space between staged persona, workplace relation, friendship, and possible romantic attachment.

## The dangerous wish to be indispensable

When ill, Musubi repeatedly apologizes for disrupting work and tries to remove herself from care as quickly as possible. She sends Susu back to the job, refuses an escort, returns before recovery, and proposes performing in whatever capacity remains. Beneath this is more than conscientiousness. Musubi says Susu crossed the sea alone, can be lonely and absent-minded, and is like a younger sister whom she does not want left alone. “Susu is no good without me” gives her overwork an affective reward: indispensability certifies intimacy.

The formulation misrecognizes both women. Susu is two years younger but an actual elder sister, and her looseness outside performance coexists with professional resolve. Musubi's care is valuable, yet treating Susu's dependence as its justification prevents her from seeing that the relation may survive Susu's competence. It also makes rest feel like abandonment rather than one possible form of trust.

The Player's managerial refusal is unusually firm. He withholds work updates, directs Musubi to leave, and bars her from the stage because her unplanned presence would endanger both her and Susu's concentration. Care here requires overriding a performer's immediate wish. His answer is not that the unit no longer needs Musubi, but that trust must include permitting a partner to act beyond one's supervision.

## A solo performance in the name of a duo

Susu accepts the displaced work, practices, and performs the NI+CORA live alone. Audience members notice Musubi's absence because the duo remains the expected form. Susu names the illness rather than concealing it, admits that she relied on Musubi for details, and defines the solo set as her turn to support both NI+CORA and Musubi. She performs `Girls Talk!!` under the unit name and asks the audience to love NI+CORA, not to recognize a new solo replacement.

The event therefore performs independence without converting independence into separation. Susu can hold the stage, communicate with the audience, and carry the shared name. At the same time, she experiences the stage as too large alone and says that without Musubi beside her she loses sight of “Susu.” The claim is not logical incapacity—the live has just disproved that—but relational self-knowledge. A person can be operationally capable while still being partly oriented through another person's presence.

## Reversing care without reversing domination

After the live, Susu obtains a blanket, prepares porridge after learning from Hime, orders Musubi to rest, and promises another duo live after recovery. Musubi calls the situation a role reversal; Susu reminds her that she is in fact an older sister. The joke destabilizes the fixed assignment of mature caretaker and carefree dependent.

Susu then refuses Musubi's mistaken conclusion that successful substitution means she can do without her. She worked precisely to show that Musubi need not bear everything alone. Autonomy is offered as care: “I can do this too” means the other may rest, not “I no longer choose you.” NI+CORA's healthier form depends on both propositions remaining true.

The final girls' talk takes place without the Player's participation. Susu asks directly about Musubi's feelings for him; Musubi withholds an answer, and the two move into mutual thanks and private complaint. The Player, delayed with the blanket, cannot find a point of entry. That exclusion matters. Although he enforces the health boundary and enables the live, he does not mediate the partners' reconciliation. Their relation acquires a conversational space that belongs to them.

## Exit state

- Musubi's scheduling, emotional regulation, apology, and planning labor becomes visible as infrastructure rather than personality background.
- Her collapse connects continuous responsibility with a body that cannot be managed as an inexhaustible resource.
- The Player exercises protective authority by withholding work information and refusing an ill member's request to perform.
- Musubi recognizes that her wish to protect Susu has become entangled with a wish to be indispensable.
- Susu demonstrates independent professional capacity while carrying the NI+CORA name explicitly for both partners.
- Competence is separated from dispensability: Susu can act alone and still need and choose Musubi.
- Care becomes potentially reciprocal and role-flexible rather than permanently assigned to a mature manager and a carefree dependent.
- The duo's private “girls' talk” proceeds outside the Player's access, preserving an interpersonal domain management can support but not own.
