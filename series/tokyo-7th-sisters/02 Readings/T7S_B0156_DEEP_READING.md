# T7S_B0156 — My Song for You to Walk On

## Result and entering boundary

Tomoe's first close school friend is also her fan: seeing someone similarly shy perform gave the girl courage to pursue research study in Sendai-6th. Tomoe is proud yet grieves the impending separation and tries to give a farewell song. Her first mini-performance meticulously imitates Haru, whom she admires. The friend walks away without explaining then; Murasaki and Sisara argue the fan needed Tomoe's own expression, and their severe criticism exposes Tomoe's fear that she has no such talent. The Player offers privacy and encouragement; Tomoe asks Murasaki and Sisara to help create her first song. On departure day, after she asks the friend if she will listen again, Tomoe sings with their chorus. The friend explicitly calls the song her best and the two encourage each other's separate ambitions. The friend leaves; Tomoe still panics over having declared she will be the best idol. A self-authored song enables this particular parting, not a cure of performance anxiety, a guarantee of future reunion or proof of a fully solo artistic product.

Native `貴方が歩くための私の歌`, `EPISODE.3.5-013`. The English title is a gloss rather than a quoted lyric. ## Factual reconstruction by presentation and actor knowledge

### EV-B0156-001 — a friend names Tomoe as her model

Murasaki and Sisara meet Tomoe shopping with a classmate. The girl says she first followed Nanastar because she was Tomoe's fan: identifying with Tomoe's difficulty joining groups, she took courage from seeing her perform. She calls Tomoe her best idol; Tomoe is embarrassed, worries she cannot live up to the praise and tries to make herself secondary to the older performers. Sisara and Murasaki are glad someone has noticed Tomoe beyond their own circle. The girl's testimony directly establishes her own reason for fandom, not a universal effect of Tomoe's work. [A:P2–40]

### EV-B0156-002 — a hoped-for future creates a present loss

Tomoe later says the classmate will transfer next month to study toward becoming a researcher in Sendai-6th. She relates the girl's decision to seeing Tomoe face fear onstage, but calls this “losing” her first classmate friend and fan because they will not see each other often. Murasaki reframes “because of me” as “thanks to me”; Tomoe recognizes she should support the dream while still feeling lonely. This is a geographic separation and friendship strain, not death or a shown permanent break. The girl's application/enrollment outcome is not independently checked. [A:P45–60; B:P1–22]

### EV-B0156-003 — a first private stage, then silence

Murasaki, Sisara and the Player arrange an otherwise unscheduled small performance on Tomoe's debut stage for the girl. The girl says she values both Tomoe's singing and her courage. Tomoe instead announces a medley of Haru songs, her admired model, promising exact choreography and lyrics. After the performance, Tomoe says she sang without stumbling or nausea, yet the girl turns and leaves without answering her question. The girl's act is observed; why she leaves at that moment is not stated by her. A performance was staged for one fan, but permission, safety and musical exactness are not independently audited. [B:P23–66]

### EV-B0156-004 — sharp diagnosis exposes a real fear

Murasaki calls the performance a technically high-quality copy; Sisara says the friend admired *Tomoe*, not Haru. The Player says expression needs Tomoe's feeling to reach this listener, but also warns Murasaki and Sisara their language is too harsh. Tomoe says Haru's songs gave her courage and that she cannot imagine singing her own; she finally says she lacks talent beside them. Their reading of the fan's disappointment may be plausible, but the girl has not yet spoken her reason for leaving. The strong correction helps reveal insecurity and also hurts Tomoe; later success does not make every harsh word necessary. [B:P67–110]

### EV-B0156-005 — support without substitution

The Player finds Tomoe crying, honors her request not to look and gives a handkerchief. She says she has long believed she should not aim high and could be dismissed from idol work, yet cannot stop crying. He identifies frustration, citing practice after lessons and notes taken at others' lives as evidence that she keeps trying even while doubting herself. He does not claim to have made Haru shine; he says he and Coney found her and she grew by her own effort. He asks Tomoe to stand on her own feet and promises to remain nearby. His interpretation is grounded in behaviors he reports, not a talent test, medical assessment or requirement to perform while distressed. [C:P1–57]

### EV-B0156-006 — asking peers to help make her own song

At Nanastar, Murasaki and Sisara have waited, each attributing the decision to the other. Tomoe apologizes for fleeing and asks them to collaborate on a song of her own; both agree. The product will be Tomoe's first composition by her later description, yet she openly credits their help and asks them to sing chorus. Authorship and collaboration coexist; this is neither a solitary masterpiece nor someone else replacing her voice. [C:P62–79,105–109,118–120]

### EV-B0156-007 — the second offer is accepted

On the friend's departure day, the girl and mother await a flight; the girl wonders whether Tomoe stayed away because of the earlier scene. Tomoe arrives, says she could not reserve the airport stage on short notice but obtained permission to sing in an event space, and apologizes for not understanding before. The girl apologizes for leaving abruptly. Tomoe asks whether she may sing once more, acknowledging that another attempt might not be wanted; the girl explicitly agrees. This direct request/answer matters more than assuming fans always owe artists an audience. The claimed permission is not a reviewed permit, and boarding/arrival are not shown. [C:P83–109]

### EV-B0156-008 — a local success lets both walk onward

After a cut over the song, the girl recognizes that it differs from Tomoe's classroom self-deprecation, then calls the new song hers and says it was her best. The Player and a couple of passersby respond positively; Murasaki/Sisara note a small crowd and stop before it grows. The friend says it strengthens her determination to pursue research and asks Tomoe not to abandon her own idol ambition. Tomoe promises to become an idol only she can be. They exchange a future-facing farewell; the girl departs. This is direct listener approval, not proof of vocal technique, chart impact, guaranteed research success or a date of reunion. [C:P108–157]

### EV-B0156-009 — ambition remains vulnerable, not false

Murasaki and Sisara privately call Tomoe both friend and stimulating rival; Sisara jokes that Tomoe calls Murasaki and Haru “-chan” but still calls her “-san.” The Player tells Haru the event succeeded, and Haru says she admires Tomoe too. Then Tomoe panics that her “best idol” promise might be mocked online, worries about fans of Sisara and QOP, and clings to the Player for reassurance. He praises her, and she says she will walk on her own but asks for praise again next time and for him to stay. None of this cancels the sincerity of her airport commitment; neither does one strong performance eliminate her anxiety or her need for support. Murasaki consented to the chorus, so Tomoe's “unauthorized use” worry is not a verified permission breach. [C:P163–199]

## Interpretation and alternatives

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0156-001.** The friend's dream both honors Tomoe's example and produces a real grief of separation. Tomoe's song is ultimately support for the friend's independent path, not a device to keep her nearby. [A:P18–40; B:P1–22; C:P132–157]

Rival: a good friend would never leave, or Tomoe should feel only pride. The source lets care and sadness coexist.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0156-002.** In this one-to-one farewell, virtuoso imitation of Haru is insufficient as *Tomoe's answer* to someone who came for her; her later first song is personally accountable even with coauthors/chorus. This does not denigrate covers generally or establish a technical superiority of the second performance. [B:P43–85; C:P76–79,102–125]

Rival: covering Haru is inherently inauthentic, or the friend's silence proves a definitive expert verdict. Admiration for Haru is real, and the girl does not explain her first exit then.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0156-003.** Murasaki/Sisara's diagnosis may fit the later response, but their accusation that Tomoe betrayed the fan is more certain and harsher than the girl's own first words warrant; the Player names that excessive force. The girl later apologizes for leaving, without spelling out all her motives. [B:P59–110; C:P83–104]

Rival: eventual success proves the severity was ethically necessary. The narrative shows Tomoe crying and the Player's warning, not a controlled causal test.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0156-004.** The Player's “walk on your own” and the collaborators' help are not opposites: Tomoe chooses the song/request, others assist, and she can seek support again while taking responsibility for her voice. The final panic makes autonomy an ongoing practice rather than a completed cure. [C:P1–79,105–109,181–199]

Rival: autonomy requires severing support, or continued anxiety invalidates her self-authored step. Neither is required by the source.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0156-005.** The second listener's positive response and small crowd support a local success, but the actual song is not fully available as lyrics/audio in this review, and there is no audited permit, wider audience measurement, school enrollment, future friendship outcome or mental-health resolution. [C:P83–199]

Rival: the reported emotional reach guarantees a commercially successful composition or permanent recovery. This chapter does not show those outcomes.
