## 2. Analytical findings

### 2.1 Alessandra arrives as an agent with a target, not as raw scouting material

The order of knowledge matters. Before the managers notice her, Alessandra has crossed an international boundary, selected Nanasta, and named Nicole. Coney later interprets visual attractiveness and coincidental destination as recruitability. The episode lets the audience see that this gaze is incomplete: Alessandra already has a project whose content she has not granted them.

Her refusal reinforces that agency. She diagnoses the interaction as a scout attempt and specifies a condition of acceptable address—greater delicacy. The text does not show a categorical rejection of idols. It shows control over how strangers may solicit her.

### 2.2 The language comedy exposes projection on both sides

The Player and Coney infer foreignness from appearance and dress, then assume a communication problem. Coney's confidence produces less communicative precision than the Player's simple question. Alessandra's fluent Japanese overturns the premise. Meanwhile, her soy-sauce comment and `最果て` framing show that the visitor also arrives with compressed images of Japan. The comedy is reciprocal misapprehension, though only the hosts turn theirs into an intrusive recruitment act.

### 2.3 Coney's concealed identity creates dramatic irony before recognition

B0009 has just confirmed Coney/Nicole for the audience. B0010 opens with a traveler explicitly seeking Nicole and then places her opposite Coney. Neither the Player nor Coney hears the private line, and Alessandra is not shown recognizing the pink-haired manager. The result is not yet revelation but pressure: the object of the search is present under a cover, while the seeker and uninformed Player talk around that fact.

This preserves four distinct states: the audience knows Coney/Nicole; the Player still does not; Alessandra knows the name Nicole but her knowledge of the cover is unknown; Coney's knowledge of Alessandra and her purpose is absent at the episode's exit.

### 2.4 An unethical act yields a real institutional benefit without becoming ethical

Coney says the secret Kuonji stream led to the airport opportunity. That is an explicit causal payoff. It cannot supply the permission missing when she streamed. The episode thus makes a recurring governance problem sharper: reach and opportunity may reward unilateral conduct. A durable Nanasta requires a way to preserve the benefit of initiative without allowing favorable outcomes to decide whether consent mattered.
