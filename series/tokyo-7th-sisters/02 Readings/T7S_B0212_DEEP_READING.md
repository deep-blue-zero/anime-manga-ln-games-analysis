# Shinju's dependable company and chosen limits

Shinju values having someone reliable to turn to. Meeting the manager gives child-idol work a meaning she can acknowledge even while saying the occupation still does not fit comfortably. Yet wanting dependable company does not suspend her right to qualify an address, criticize his conduct, decline a photograph or ask whether he can actually provide the help she needs. These nine cases make affection and limits speak together.

Her practical register is also only part of her life. She deliberately tries a conspicuously cute greeting, worries that she lacks charm, reports embarrassment about swimming, and takes comfort in collecting frog goods. Those disclosures need not be decoded as the emergence of a hidden, more authentic child beneath an adult facade. Shinju remains the child who reasons, plays, judges and asks for help. The portfolio's strongest connection is between being taken seriously and being allowed these different ways of participating.

## A - Valuing reliability without promising an address

The concession preserves both sides: a positive relational meaning and continuing occupational unease. It would be inaccurate either to erase the unease because she likes him or to treat the unease as proof that idol work has no value to her.

Comparing him with her two sisters, Shinju describes a reliable older person as something fresh and wonders whether having a brother might feel like this (P2). Her brief assessment of Sawara and Kajika belongs to her perspective; it is not a complete audit of their care.
