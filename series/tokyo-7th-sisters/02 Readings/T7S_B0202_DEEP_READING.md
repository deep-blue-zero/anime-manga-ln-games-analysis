# Madoka's chosen bridges and unfinished invitations

Madoka's ordinary addresses make reaching another person an achievement that can remain incomplete. She asks the manager to consider a visit while making refusal possible, receives praise after anticipating a painful reception of her horn playing, and begins another home invitation only to withdraw it. The portfolio does not turn these into a smooth ascent from shyness to confidence. Gratitude and a felt change at Nanasta are substantial, but hesitation, self-comparison and fear still have places in the same field of evidence.

Her effort to learn Tokyo speech is especially important to that distinction. The manager has reportedly said that her dialect is acceptable, yet she chooses to learn because she imagines how pleased she would be if someone from Tokyo tried her home speech. Adapting is an offered act of reciprocity rather than simply surrender to correction. The sauce she seeks gives the complementary case: unfamiliar local flavor is something she need not learn to prefer. Belonging can involve choosing what to learn and what to keep. Neither decision requires the analyst to erase her home voice, assign an unprovided birthplace or promise that insecurity has ended.

## A — A visit is requested with room for refusal

A:P1 asks permission to consult the manager and reports her father's tears after she told him about the manager. P2 explains what she says she told him: this is a special person to her. The reaction and its cause are available through Madoka's account. Her father does not explain whether the tears express pride, worry, loss or something else. A familiar story about a father and a growing daughter should not be substituted for that missing interior.

Madoka wonders whether she did something wrong, then P3 asks the manager to come over another time. The invitation allows that refusal would be all right and asks him to think about it. That is a real attempt to make an uncertain conversation possible, without a visit already occurring or a promise already being given. Her special-person category is meaningful affection; it is not a mutually declared romantic status or biological relationship established by the chosen address anchan. The ending leaves room for the other person's decision. No particular address, meeting date or guaranteed explanation from her father is supplied.

## B — Making sound and fearing its reception

It is a report of shared time and her pleasure in playing, not an auditioned performance or a technical verdict. P2 extends that pleasure toward the city: she likes the number of parks and their use for music, painting and theatrical rehearsal. This is her appraisal of a hospitable creative environment. It is not an independently verified rule governing every Tokyo park, nor does the stock playground background show all the activities she names.

P3 nevertheless discounts her own playing, apologizing for its clumsiness and assuming listening was painful. Pleasure in doing something and doubt about what it gives a listener coexist.
