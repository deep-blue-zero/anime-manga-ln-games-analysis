## 1. Descriptive reconstruction

### 1.1 A public description spoken from inside its impossibility (pages 0–7)

Musubi's private prologue lists the person others say she is: best at study and sport, everyone's model, cool, enviable, a perfect honor student. [E01] The repetition initially resembles self-description, then breaks: `それが私……でも――そんな人、いるわけないじゃない`. [E02] The scene does not yet explain whether she means that the image omits weakness, is impossible to maintain, or is wholly false. It does establish that the public predicate “perfect” and Musubi's private self-knowledge are not equivalent.

### 1.2 Legitimate boundary enforcement becomes recruitment material (pages 8–38)

Connie and the Player come to prestigious St. Corsica Girls' School to scout. At a boundary beyond which non-authorized people may not proceed, Musubi, the student-council president, stops the “suspicious pair” and tells them to leave. [E03] Her act is ordinary governance with a clear object: protect the boundary.

Peers immediately convert it into a ceremony. They cheer their president, list top grades, athletic excellence, beauty, and universal attention, name her the academy's `パーフェクトガール`, then chant her name. Musubi asks them not to disclose her information and tells them to stop because she is embarrassed. [E04] Their praise does not register the correction. The social group speaks *about* her more loudly than she can speak *as* herself.

Connie performs the same conversion from another direction. Rather than accepting the expulsion as a boundary, she reads the students' response as charisma and makes Musubi the next Sisters candidate. [E05] Competent resistance becomes an audition she did not agree to give.

### 1.3 Explicit refusal and observers who continue authoring possibility (pages 39–52)

Musubi says she has no interest in idols. [E06] Her peers call idols outdated, apparently supporting the refusal, but do so by adding that Musubi would of course be top-class at singing and dancing and would wear the clothes perfectly. Their defense therefore reproduces the same perfect-person script: even the activity she declines becomes another arena in which she is imagined to excel.

Musubi refuses again and orders Connie and the Player out. Connie admires the cool departure and becomes more determined; the Player says Musubi remains interesting and beautiful. [E07–E08] The episode ends with observers' desire to recruit, not with evidence that Musubi wants recruitment. Her current state is an explicit refusal under conditions in which praise and solicitation repeatedly ignore what she says.

## 2. Analytical findings

### 2.1 “Perfect” is a relation of public authorship

The perfect girl is not yet a hidden true self or simply a lie Musubi tells others. It is a social object produced by repetition: peers select achievements, bodily appearance, and institutional office, then return that composite to her as identity. Her prologue reveals that she cannot inhabit the result as a complete person. The question mark in `パーフェクトガール？現る！` is therefore substantive at this horizon.

The scene's comedy depends on the admirers' good intentions, but good intent does not make their attention responsive. When Musubi says disclosure is unwanted and cheering is embarrassing, praise continues. “Perfect” works less as knowledge of her than as a rule for what the group is prepared to see.

### 2.2 Boundary, performance, and the scouting gaze

Musubi's strongest on-page agency is refusal: she identifies suspicious outsiders, states the access rule, rejects the offer, and makes them leave. Yet Connie interprets the social effectiveness of that refusal as charisma, while the Player finally emphasizes beauty. The scouting gaze absorbs an act directed against it and recodes the act as evidence for its own project.

This does not prove that recruitment must fail or that Musubi can never choose idol work. It sets the ethical requirement for any later change: her actual desire must become distinguishable from an image authored by peers and recruiters. At the current boundary, `興味はありません` is the only explicit preference and remains controlling evidence.

### 2.3 A bounded echo of Haru's `すごいね`

B0002 made “amazing” lonely when praise displaced the first memory of wanting. Musubi's introduction supplies an independent adjacent variation: competence praise is abundant, but it gives the observers a finished “perfect” person and leaves no room for her private objection. The recurrence strengthens a local motif—admiration can estrange a girl from her own desire when it becomes totalizing identity—without yet establishing a full-series law.

### 2.4 Japanese textual voice

These are textual observations only. Musubi's public language is controlled and role-indexed: `関係者以外、立ち入り禁止です`, `お引き取り願います`, and direct imperatives enact student-council authority. Under peer attention, the register breaks into embarrassed repetition (`ちょ、ちょっと`, `は、恥ずかしい`), exposing the gap between the cool public image and the person affected by it. Her refusal remains polite before becoming firmer. The peers use elevated praise and collective chants, while Connie's familiar diminutive `ムスビちゃん` assumes intimacy before permission.
