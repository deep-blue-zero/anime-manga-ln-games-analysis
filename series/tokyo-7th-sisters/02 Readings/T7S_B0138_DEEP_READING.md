# T7S_B0138 — The wall of smiles

## Result and entering boundary

After a live in which a light fixture falls toward her, Saori treats small injuries as unimportant because the audience enjoyed itself. A dog-walking girl who regularly watches her had encouraged her practice, but then disappears; when Saori meets her again, the girl says her dog has died and she cannot smile. Saori offers to laugh with her and imagines an idol can turn any suffering into happiness. The girl refuses that demand: Saori's song cannot bring the dog back or fill the loss. Coney and Sawara help Saori separate future availability from making the girl smile *now*. Later the girl returns to a live wanting to apologize; Sawara invites her to watch and then come backstage. The source ends with Saori greeting the audience, not with the apology, a completed reunion or a cure for grief. Saori's own injury exposure likewise is not made safe by her resilience.

## Factual reconstruction by actor knowledge

### EV-B0138-001 — joy after a hazardous live is not clearance

At a Saori–Sawara live, a light fixture reportedly falls toward Saori; a spectator says they worried yet enjoyed the show. Coney apologizes for another equipment trouble. Saori calls her small wounds inconsequential because people were pleased. She is especially proud of a girl who brings a dog, attends when she performs and gave feedback while Saori practiced in the park. Saori promises to keep making her smile. No medical evaluation, engineering finding or guarantee against repetition is shown. The spectator's pleasure and Saori's willingness to proceed cannot substitute for safety clearance. [A:P2–20]

### EV-B0138-002 — absence is misread before the girl's account

After that day the girl stops appearing at Nanasta. Saori wonders if she has bored the fan. She then sees the girl in the park without the dog and initially offers to search for a lost pet. The girl says her mother told her the dog had gone beyond the sky and that it can no longer walk there. She says she had seen the dog's weakness coming but could not prepare herself; remembering its bark makes smiling difficult. The story treats the dog's death through the girl's account; cause, date and medical details are unavailable. Her absence was not evidence that Saori's art had lost its appeal. [A:P22–39]

### EV-B0138-003 — the girl rejects an immediate smile obligation

Saori first acknowledges the girl's hard time, then promises to laugh with her and says she aims to transform any misfortune into happiness. The girl says Saori cannot bring the dog back or fill that space. She insists grief does not put her under an obligation to smile, and refers to Saori smiling through daily injuries as something she herself cannot do. Saori continues to say she will try if any chance of a smile remains, then falls silent. Sawara and Coney have overheard. The child's refusal is direct testimony about her own current need, not a rejection of Saori as a person or a command never to perform again. [A:P35–59]

### EV-B0138-004 — Saori names a limit without losing her vocation

The next day Sawara and Coney reveal they saw the encounter. Saori feels ashamed that she could not make the girl laugh, then distinguishes comic accidents she herself can absorb from an already occurred loss and invisible loneliness she cannot take over. Coney says another person cannot simply carry that grief. Saori recognizes forcing a smile from someone who cannot laugh now would be wrong. She also says she is not angry at the girl's words; Sawara's `sandbag` joke and Coney's follow-up test whether Saori is confusing being available with having to absorb every blow. These are situated reflections, not a universal rule that aid is useless or a license for Saori's repeated injuries. [B:P2–39]

### EV-B0138-005 — Coney proposes waiting, not a guaranteed cure

Coney asks whether Saori must make the girl smile immediately and proposes waiting until she herself wants to move forward. Saori says she had not considered that, and reframes her task as remaining an idol nearby until a later meeting. Sawara promises to join her then and jokingly names her Nanasta's smile captain. Waiting is an alternative to imposing a response, not proof the girl will return on a schedule, laugh, or overcome grief because of an idol. [B:P40–42; C:P1–17]

### EV-B0138-006 — Sawara's smile is not identical to happiness

After Saori leaves, Coney thanks Sawara for listening with a gentle smile despite discomfort at seeing Saori sad. Sawara says she is starting to understand idol work as more than smiling relentlessly for others and suggests Coney has encountered such sorrow before; Coney confirms in broad terms. The scene complicates the appearance of a smile without supplying a full account of Coney's past or proof that Sawara has solved the dilemma. [C:P18–33]

### EV-B0138-007 — voluntary return stops short of reconciliation scene

At a later live, the girl appears outside, says she may have spoken harshly and wants to tell Saori something. She worries Saori will not smile at her unless she apologizes. Sawara, drawing on Saori's stated lack of anger, invites her to watch the stage and then visit backstage to find out. The girl agrees and enters. Saori's final line addresses the audience as an idol who rises after trouble. The script does not show the girl and Saori speaking again, an apology accepted, a renewed smile, a healed grief process or another injury event. [C:P34–57]

## Interpretation and alternatives

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0138-001.** Saori's generous `make everyone smile` vocation reaches a boundary when it makes the bereaved girl's present expression a task. The girl's direct refusal revises the timing and ownership of healing: a future meeting can be welcomed, but no idol can require an immediate smile or restore the dog. [A:P22–59; B:P17–42; C:P1–17]

Rival: the child is ungrateful or Saori should cease caring. She names a real loss and Saori learns to care without immediate proof of success.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0138-002.** Saori's acceptance of injury and Sawara's joking list of mishaps cannot certify safe work; delight after a near falling light coexists with an unanswered equipment and medical obligation. Coney apologizes, but no root-cause or clearance is shown. [A:P2–20; B:P17–22]

Rival: audience enjoyment retroactively justifies the near miss and cuts. Neither spectator praise nor Saori's preference documents a safety review.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0138-003.** Coney's waiting proposal preserves the girl's autonomy and Saori's vocation, but the girl's later return is a request to communicate/apologize, not proof that grief is over or that Saori's stage has cured it. Sawara facilitates a voluntary route to a later conversation; that conversation remains outside the chapter. [C:P1–17,34–57]

Rival: the ending proves the child smiles again and reconciles with Saori. It shows entry and an audience greeting, not their exchange.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0138-004.** Sawara's gentle smile while Saori is sad and Saori's own effortful smile distinguish visible affect from resolved feeling. Coney notices that Sawara's smile was care rather than simple happiness; this is a local interpersonal reading, not a diagnostic ability to infer every hidden state. [B:P17–39; C:P18–33]

Rival: anyone smiling is already healed, or a smile is always a mask. The two scenes show a contextual, nonbinary use of expression.
