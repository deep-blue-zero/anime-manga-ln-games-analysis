# T7S_B0106 — Tokyo-7th Cleaning Operation

## Result and entering boundary

Haru mobilizes an off-day office cleaning, then extends it into the shopping street. Growing public recognition turns an ordinary helpful activity into a crowd-management problem, and the group retreats before finishing. At the park, Haru addresses onlookers as potential co-workers: some decline, others join. The invitation temporarily converts spectators into participants, but an unsupported rumor that the cleanup is a live campaign creates a new obligation. Coney reports securing the park-stage permission from Shizuka's grandfather; the performers separately agree to a reward live, and afterward say both cleaning and live were enjoyable. The chapter establishes one locally completed event and Haru's chosen public address. It does not establish a standing civic program, universal participation, safe crowd management, free off-day labor as an institutional norm, or that a rumored campaign existed.

The native title is `Tokyo-7th お掃除大作戦！`; the English heading is a gloss. Committed B0105 is the entry horizon. B0002 establishes Haru's cleaning role and her right to choose work after coercive recruitment. B0079 later highlights how preparation for care can become overwork. These are retrospective comparisons, not cross-chapter chronological placement or proof her motive here is identical. The present title's city-scale aspiration is not evidence that all of Tokyo-7th was cleaned.

## Factual reconstruction and access

### EV-B0106-001 — chosen office work and a boundary at Coney's desk

Haru assembles Kajika, Shizuka, Sumire and the Player for an off-day Nanasta office cleaning. She says busy work has crowded cleaning out and calls room dirt “heart dirt,” a motivational slogan rather than evidence of mental condition. Sumire questions why they are working on a day off, but says she prefers a clean office and dislikes being the only one resting. The others join. They judge the completed room clean in dialogue. They have also cleaned Coney's desk without asking: Kajika says they discarded only unmistakable trash, such as an opened chip bag and a crumpled crossword, while Sumire worries about permission. That restraint reduces but does not eliminate the access question. [A:P2–24]

Haru is still eager to clean. Shizuka's pun and the group's surprised response frame her enthusiasm as comedy. When she proposes cleaning their city, the title's ambition is voiced as a new plan, not evidence of a citywide accomplishment. [A:P25–40]

### EV-B0106-002 — recognition interrupts the shopping-street task

The group cleans in the shopping district. A shopkeeper thanks Haru; she says she intends to cover more. The Player and peers remark on her energy. Haru explicitly asks whether the others want to keep spending their day off on her wish. Kajika points to her hometown and to growing enjoyment, Shizuka to familiar service activity and public encounter, Sumire to wanting to see it through. These are participant reasons, not proof there was no peer pressure or labor cost. [B:P1–13]

Passersby recognize them as idols from online exposure. Some ask whether it is an event or if photography is allowed; others call more people over. No actual photography or planned publicity event is established by these questions. Sumire worries about a disturbance. A second shopkeeper says they have done enough and thanks them, and the Player directs a retreat. Sumire helps the group get away; Kajika reports a side stitch. Haru likes being known but regrets that ordinary cleaning has become harder and unfinished. Shizuka hears more than literal tidiness in that regret, while Haru says she simply likes cleaning. The scene does not adjudicate her private motive. [B:P14–48]

The Player suggests the large central park as a place to continue. His confidence that it will be safe from recognition is a guess, immediately tested in the next episode. [B:P49–52]

### EV-B0106-003 — from audience to possible co-worker, then a rumor

Visitors recognize the group at the park too. The Player considers retreating again, but Haru greets them, describes the cleanup and invites people with time to join. One explicitly declines because cleaning is troublesome; another accepts partly to work beside idols. Others leave or participate. The Player says Haru did not appear to calculate this as a crowd-control strategy. Her invitation is genuine in the presented dialogue, yet attendance is not uniform and idol proximity is also a motive. [C:P1–26]

Participation grows, followed by a false or at least unsupported story: visitors ask whether this is a campaign culminating in a live. Kajika cannot confirm it; the Player notes there is no prepared venue and connects the rumor to the growing numbers. Haru apologizes for having invited people and worries it would be wrong to end without a live. The audience's expectation is a newly created pressure, not a preexisting organizational promise. The text does not trace its origin or establish any coordinated marketing plan. [C:P28–36]

## Interpretation and alternatives

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0106-001.** Cleaning is a durable part of Haru's Nanasta idiom: the former office cleaner now organizes peers and extends care into a shared public place. The transition does not mean she alone should shoulder maintenance. Her renewed desire to clean after the room is finished can be read as exuberant agency, while Sumire's off-day objection and later fatigue reveal labor costs. Haru repeatedly gives peers room to choose; their own reasons matter. [A:P2–40; B:P1–13]

Rival: the group's cheerful participation proves off-day work is automatically free of pressure. Sumire initially joins partly because being the only one to rest feels bad, and Haru's desire is socially contagious. There is explicit assent here, but it is not a pay, rest or scheduling policy. Conversely, a purely exploited-Haru reading would erase her initiative and the peers' stated enjoyment.

**THEMATIC INTERPRETATION — CL-B0106-002.** Visibility makes idol life less simply public-spirited: the same recognition the group welcomes can obstruct ordinary activity and create safety uncertainty. Haru's invitation at the park changes the relation from staring/photographing to optional shared work. The explicit refusal shows that belonging remains selective; the acceptor's wish to be near idols shows participation is not pure disinterested civic virtue. The invitation locally reduces the first crowd tension but does not prove safe universal conversion. [B:P14–52; C:P1–26]

Rival: Haru had a planned publicity tactic. The Player expressly doubts that she calculated one; the earlier shopping-street retreat and her apology at the rumor argue against a covert campaign. The opposite rival, that no one recognized them, is contradicted by multiple passersby and the group's flight.

**ETHICAL/INSTITUTIONAL INTERPRETATION — CL-B0106-003.** The campaign rumor demonstrates how a crowd can manufacture a performance obligation. Coney's reported stage permission supplies one piece of infrastructure, but the group does not simply have a prewritten plan: performers are asked and assent, and Haru retains the public address. The same sequence also keeps Coney's labor asymmetry visible: she avoids cleaning yet has access to a fast permission route and organizes the live. The result is a bounded cooperative improvisation, not a general event-governance solution or proof of complete licensing. [A:P21–24; C:P28–75]

Rival: the live was guaranteed from the beginning. The visitors raise it as rumor, Kajika cannot confirm, the Player says no site has been prepared, and Haru apologizes. Later performance cannot rewrite the initial invitation into a campaign. A rival that no live occurred is also limited by the explicit later reports that the group performed.

B0002's account of Haru's initially coerced cleaning/recruitment makes her current choice meaningful but does not absolve the earlier method. B0079's overwork caution remains separate. Shizuka's intuition that Haru means more than sweeping is plausible but not a certified hidden psychological cause; Haru's own answer stays in the record.
