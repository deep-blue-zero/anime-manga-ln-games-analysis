# T7S_B0070 — the handmade institution

## Result

Episode 6.0 opens after 777☆SISTERS has already achieved the thing formation stories usually postpone: recognized professional success. The tour-ending movie, unit shoots, a radio appearance, and younger idols' admiration establish reach, competence, and a revived field. The new conflict is not whether the twelve can become idols. It is whether the small institution that let them become themselves can remain theirs when success distributes their time, exposes their private lives, and concentrates invisible adult labor in two exhausted managers.

Their answer is neither nostalgic amateurism nor simple corporate expansion. They ask to share sales, scheduling, and management work while preserving Nanasta's handmade character. They understand this as becoming adults and professionals: not escaping dependence, but becoming answerable for the infrastructure that supports their choices. The first manager and the Player had carried burdens the performers could not yet see. Recognition creates a debt of participation, not a transfer of ownership.

The offered Rainbow Dome live then scales the test. It recalls the legendary reach of Seven Sisters but arrives immediately after the group has defined success as collective authorship. The venue is therefore a promise and a threat before any antagonist appears: global visibility can complete their present ambition only if the means do not make their answer somebody else's.

## Success creates distance inside the group

The opening movie makes 777☆SISTERS plural before it makes them monumental. Costume jokes, fatigue, correction, and mutual anticipation occupy the backstage frame before the live declaration. Months later, the members appear through separate units and jobs. Rona can carry a demanding WITCH NUMBER 4 shoot and become an admired senior; SiSH can explain on radio that idol work was not an original destiny; Musubi can coordinate calls that the manager no longer has time to hold alone. Professionalization is real, but its first felt effect is scarcity of shared time.

Sumire names the private cost. The manager is constantly present as organizer and increasingly absent as a person. Her wish to see him cannot be solved by more efficient scheduling because it concerns relation, not throughput. When he runs to Nanasta and says he wanted to see their faces, the scene briefly restores reciprocal desire. A watcher observing them converts that ordinary intimacy into an exploitable surface. Nothing in the episode establishes wrongdoing; the danger is that public visibility allows another institution to manufacture the meaning of a private image.

## “We will become adults” means sharing the hidden work

The group revisits the manager's earlier question—what do you want?—with an answer produced across time rather than under pressure. Sawara proposes that the idols share sales and management responsibilities; Hime names the handmade office as the thing they want to keep. This does not prove that minors should absorb unlimited staff labor. The members explicitly recognize work previously carried by Coney and the Player, and the episode gives no payroll, safeguarding, or workload design. Their ethical insight is narrower and stronger: the people whose lives a system organizes should participate in governing it. “Professional” is revised from specialization alone toward knowledge of consequences.

The request also corrects a weakness in the series' recurring language of desire. Here the desired object includes that supporting labor. Self-authorship becomes institutional rather than merely expressive.

## The Rainbow Dome offer changes the scale, not the question

The first manager announces an invitation to perform at the newly opened 7th Rainbow Dome with worldwide distribution. The name carries Seven Sisters' monumental legacy, while the opening tour establishes that 777☆SISTERS is no longer merely local. Yet the offer follows, rather than precedes, the group's governance statement. The sequence prevents scale from becoming the automatic definition of growth.

The group must decide whether the dome is a place their shared desire can inhabit or a machine that will decide what their desire means. The episode does not answer yet. It closes with opportunity, privacy exposure, managerial overload, and handmade governance placed in the same causal field.

## Visual grammar: public scale and ordinary rooms

The sampled opening movies alternate candid backstage bodies, rehearsed stage light, promotional imagery, and a radio studio. The static selection places Sawara and Hime's governance statements in Nanasta's crowded ordinary office (`bg_545`). Their open, familiar expressions do not make the proposal unserious; they locate institutional thought inside the social space the proposal aims to preserve.

The contrast matters. Monumental screens and global venues can display the unit, but the office is where members see one another, notice absence, and redistribute work. Neither space is inherently authentic. The test is whether movement between them remains revisable by the people whose bodies and relations are being moved.
