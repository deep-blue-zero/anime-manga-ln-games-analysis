# Tokyo 7th Sisters — B0013 deep reading

## An authenticity doctrine made of performances: the band begins to dispute its own mission

## 0. Result, scope, and integrity

B0013 follows three linked movements—“Identity,” “Supremacy,” and “Ultimatum”—in which both institutions spy on one another, public personae are exposed as constructions, and Ume's anti-idol doctrine acquires its first exact form. Ume says idols ended when Seven Sisters dissolved and Nanase Nicole retired. Unlike idols, she argues, 4U writes and performs its own music and can therefore leave something eternal. She wants that superiority proved to Nicole. The claim presents itself as an opposition between authentic making and manufactured image, but the block has already shown Ume manufacturing a deliberately cute public persona and pursuing validation from the very figure she says is irrelevant.

The deeper fracture arrives through Hina and Shinju. Hina explains that Ume and Emoco were childhood friends and that she joined because she heard the music she wanted to make with them. Shinju notices that Ume is not happy when the band plays before Nanasta/Coney. Hina finally tells Ume that 4U does not make music to defeat idols. Ume leaves rather than answer. This is the first direct internal challenge to Ume's ownership of the band's purpose. Hina's promised persistence—she will say it again however many times are needed—turns mediation into dissent without withdrawing love.

## 1. Descriptive reconstruction

### 1.1 Reciprocal spying destabilizes simple authenticity (`203001101`–`203001202`)

Coney wants to study the same-era rival and brings the Player and Rona. Rona nearly calls her `ニコさま` before correcting to Coney. The slip supports her continued active cover management but does not show that the Player understands it. At 7th Coast, Ume's “natural” cute-genius public image is exposed as a knowingly false character; even the official claim that the real Ume is a crybaby is another fabrication. Yet the same scene shows disciplined rehearsal and Ume's insistence that fans receive full value. Artifice and serious labor coexist. [E01]

Ume then states the ideology that organizes her attacks. Seven Sisters' dissolution and Nicole's retirement ended idols. Idols depend on youth, fashion, and dreams; 4U makes its own music and can leave something eternal. She does not merely want Nanasta beaten. She wants Nicole to see the proof. Haru suspects that someone so invested in this judgment may not truly be indifferent to idols. [E02]

Emoco later enters Nanasta's space and humiliates Musubi with a retained image and precise insults. The act mirrors Nanasta's own surveillance and privacy failures rather than placing either group outside the problem. Emoco recognizes Coney beneath the cover and treats the presentation as a charade. Coney answers that the Nanasta girls shine through themselves, not because she makes them shine. This is a meaningful revision of the controlling manager seen in EPISODE 1.0, but it is an aspiration stated inside continued concealment and without an apology to those she has coerced. [E03–E04]

### 1.2 The public ultimatum cannot create a valid decision (`203001301`–`203001302`)

4U interrupts a Sanbon Ribbon event. Sawara proposes a dissolution wager, but there is no cleanly constituted competition; the audience interprets the chaos as staged entertainment. No group's existence can be legitimately wagered by one enthusiastic member, and the scene's comic reception blocks any claim that public acclaim adjudicates the dispute. [E05]

Away from that spectacle, Shinju and Hina speak as people accustomed to carrying responsibility inside larger groups. Hina says Ume and Emoco are childhood friends. She joined after hearing the music she wanted and loves making it with them. She also recognizes that Ume envies idols and has long wanted to meet Nicole. Shinju makes the decisive observation: Ume does not look happy while playing in this confrontation. [E06]

Hina tells Ume that their music is not made to defeat idols. Ume flees. Hina concludes not that the bond has failed, but that she must keep saying the truth until Ume can hear it. This is not passive peacemaking. It is a member asserting that the band's shared practice cannot be reduced to the leader's private war. [E06]

## 2. Analytical findings

### 2.1 Ume's authenticity hierarchy contradicts its own evidence

The story does not expose Ume's stage persona in order to declare all performance fake. Her fans plausibly know it is a bit, while the band still rehearses seriously. Constructed persona can coexist with labor and genuine relation. This directly weakens her claim that idols are false because they are images while self-made rock is real. The relevant difference cannot be whether mediation exists; both practices mediate.

### 2.2 “Eternal music” is addressed to an absent judge

Ume says Nicole and idols are over, yet makes Nicole the desired witness of 4U's supremacy. The contradiction is causal, not merely logical. Her future remains organized by the person whose disappearance supposedly proves that such attachment is worthless. Eternity functions here as a demand that something finally survive abandonment.

### 2.3 Coney articulates distributed agency but does not complete accountability

Her statement that current performers shine themselves is the strongest managerial principle yet voiced: the successor's value is not Nicole's property. But Coney controls the cover, withholds recognition, and does not answer why Ume addresses her as Nicole's accountable remainder. The block distinguishes ethical insight from repaired relation.

### 2.4 Shinju and Hina produce the unit's best diagnosis through attention to happiness

Neither wins an argument about genre. Shinju observes affective incongruence: Ume's performance in service of victory does not look like the practice Hina says she loves. Hina then asks the band to be responsible to its shared reason. Their intervention shifts the question from “Which form is real?” to “What are these people actually doing together?”

### 2.5 Public competition is structurally incapable of deciding shared existence

The audience reads the intrusion as entertainment; Sawara cannot pledge the group; and performance quality remains elided. A dissolution wager would still fail even with a clear winner because it turns membership into another person's stake. The later repair must therefore come from member-local speech and renewed choice, not scoreboard legitimacy.

## 4. Exit proposition

B0013 turns 4U's external challenge into an internal dispute over who owns the band's reason for existing. Ume's doctrine is precise enough to test, but its dependence on Nicole and its conflict with Hina's account show that “eternal rock” is not yet the band's shared truth. The next required evidence is not another contest. It is the history that made impermanence intolerable to Ume.
