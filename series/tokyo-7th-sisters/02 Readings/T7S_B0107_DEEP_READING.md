# T7S_B0107 — One Step Forward

## Result and entering boundary

Nanasta selects Musubi for a televised yakisoba-bread contest before asking her. She refuses because the solo PR, revealing-costume precedent and variety performance exceed what she wants or thinks she can do. Haru and Coney then invoke her love of yakisoba bread and a speculative future commercial; Musubi reverses her decision while disguising the attraction as professionalism. She works seriously, loses, and appears neither angry nor sad to the Player. Later, Rona finds her still training at eleven at night. Musubi says the real problem is not simply losing but not knowing whether she wanted to win, or even what she wants from idol work after having spent childhood performing others' “perfect Musubi.” The Player finally asks for her account and says her answer could include leaving idol work. Musubi chooses the dorm for that night and asks for next-day lessons. The chapter opens a space for self-authorship, not a solved identity or an unconditional management safeguard.

## Factual reconstruction by actor knowledge

### EV-B0107-001 — a refusal precedes the food appeal

The Player, Haru, Sumire, Rona and Coney agree Musubi should enter `Vi☆少女☆DEキッチン` while she is absent on student-council business. She arrives to hear her name. Musubi says no: she is not especially good at cooking, thinks Sumire more likely to win, and objects to doing a three-minute solo PR on a national midday program. She also expects another revealing outfit; Coney's reassurance is a yakisoba-bread-girl costume, which she dislikes even more. Musubi says her serious cooking and stiff talk will not energize a variety show outside an already friendly audience, then declines and starts to leave. These objections are her articulated professional and bodily boundaries, not merely a coy opening. [A:P0–36]

Sumire says perhaps Musubi does not need this event because she is already “fine”; Musubi stops. Haru then mentions the yakisoba-bread theme, and Coney imagines a future bread commercial and authority status if she wins. No offer of such a commercial exists. Musubi says they should have said so earlier, claims she is reconsidering for professional reasons, and launches into plans to source ingredients from water onward. The humor makes her actual fondness evident, but her later assent is produced inside a social appeal that knew the exact preference used against her in B0003. It does not make the earlier refusal illegitimate or authorize any costume she had rejected. [A:P37–66]

### EV-B0107-002 — real craft, not a performance of effortless success

Rona and Sumire praise Musubi's test yakisoba breads. Musubi diagnoses sauce penetration, excess margarine, moisture, dryness and how an egg/cabbage layer might undermine the bread's convenience. When Coney suggests spectacle, including a donut topping, Musubi defends cheap, one-handed, quick food whose ordinary identity should not be lost to novelty. Her craft principle is specific; it is not proof of objectively superior taste beyond the peers' praise. [B:P1–20]

Sumire says Musubi becomes lively when discussing something she likes. Musubi insists she is doing it for Nanasta and says being serious is all she can do. That line narrows the seeming confidence of her recipe expertise. The rehearsal and filming interval is then elided; the group later reports that she cooked silently while other contestants attracted attention through talk. There is no independent footage of cooking technique or a full contestant comparison. [B:P21–41]

### EV-B0107-003 — losing is known; her motive is not

The host announces the judging result. The Player's narration skips the decisive details and says Musubi did not win. He is more shocked that she does not look upset. That is an observation and his interpretation, not direct access to her inner state. The three-minute winner's PR is therefore not hers; no solo singing/dance claim follows. The source provides no ranking, judge rationale, allegation of unfairness, or heard/seen performance by Musubi. [B:P34–50]

### EV-B0107-004 — late training and a disputed safety frame

The Player is still thinking about Musubi's apparently ordinary behavior after the show when Rona tells him she is training. He finds Musubi exercising outside at eleven at night. She says her schedule began at noon and is nearly finished; the chapter does not enumerate breaks or quantify actual exertion. He runs out in office slippers, falls and is helped up. He asks her to stop training in a dark place at that hour. His safety concern is grounded in the situation, but he frames it first as “because you're a girl”; Musubi answers that she is an idol before a girl, and he says that gives even more reason for caution. He then takes her training menu to examine it and calls it too much. The text does not make the gender framing a complete account of her agency, nor does her insistence on being an idol make the site safe. [C:P1–54]

Musubi says she does not know what she should do, whether she really wanted to win, or what action would have won. The Player's earlier shock at her lack of visible regret thus misses the question she is carrying. She begins apologizing for saying it; he asks to hear more and acknowledges he may never have spoken with her in this way. The dialogue does not diagnose an illness or establish that overtraining caused the contest loss. [C:P55–66]

### EV-B0107-005 — permission for an unfinished answer

Musubi recalls studying and doing sports for others' expectations, performing the imagined “Tendoji Musubi.” Meeting Haru and the others showed her another self might exist, but she says she still does not know its content or even whether she wants to be an idol. Sumire's “fine without this” comment has become painful: Musubi hears it against peers finding purposes and says she alone remains at the starting line. This is her current self-assessment, not proof the other peers are finished people or that her previous consent was false. [C:P68–84]

The Player asks if she likes Nanasta and offers his view: the group is trying to ask what an idol is anew, and answers may differ, even including leaving idol work. He says this is Musubi's story rather than an idol story. This does not supply her answer, override her current membership, or establish a formal right-to-exit procedure. Musubi then says she will go, rejects the offer of an escort, chooses the Nanasta dorm for the night, and asks for next-day lessons. Both the withdrawal from this night's unsafe training and the continued lesson request are specific choices, not a final career verdict. [C:P85–119]

Rona has overheard enough to compare the exchange to her own earlier talk with Haru and the Player. She says Musubi was probably happy; that is Rona's inference, not Musubi's stated emotion. ## Interpretation and alternatives

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0107-001.** The contest process repeats a boundary fault from Musubi's formation: others decide on her candidacy in her absence, then leverage her love of yakisoba bread after she refuses. Her eventual participation is a new, explicitly expressed decision, not evidence that the first refusal was feigned. It comes under interest, peer expectations, professional obligation and a future commercial Coney only imagines. Her bodily costume concern is especially concrete given B0003's prior coerced themed outfit and disclosure, though the B0107 stills do not establish that exact old costume reappears. [A:P0–66]

Rival: because she loves the theme, management's tactic is simply thoughtful accommodation. The theme genuinely matters to her, and her food expertise grows visible. Yet Coney and Haru introduce it only after she has refused a national solo PR and revealing costume; delight in one component does not consent to every other component. A purely coerced-participation account would also miss her later serious practice and actual decision to proceed.

**THEMATIC INTERPRETATION — CL-B0107-002.** Musubi's defense of a cheap, handheld bread that remains itself underpins the chapter's identity problem. Coney wants impact through additions; Musubi wants a form that can be improved without losing its ordinary use. The analogy to her own “perfect” and “real” selves is suggestive, not a literal claim that recipe theory reveals her subconscious. What is certain is that serious craft gives her a language of care and limits even while she believes seriousness may be her only contribution. [B:P1–31; C:P68–82]

Rival: the loss proves her craft is worthless. No judge reasoning or technical taste evaluation is given; peers praise the test breads. The opposite rival—that her recipe must have been best—likewise exceeds the source. Her test results and the televised decision are separate evidence.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0107-003.** The Player's disappointment at an untroubled face is an epistemic error. He assumes her lack of visible anger/sadness means the loss does not matter, while Musubi later describes a more destabilizing uncertainty: she cannot tell if winning is what she wanted. Her excessive late practice can be read as an attempt to manufacture a next action when desire is unavailable, not proof of a medical syndrome or completed change. The gendered safety warning notices real risk but initially talks over her occupational self-description; asking for her account is the actual turn toward care. [B:P44–50; C:P1–84]

Rival: she is simply ashamed of losing and will feel better once reassured she is good. She herself questions the object of victory and the larger decision to be an idol, so competence praise alone cannot answer. Nor does her calm on television prove genuine indifference.

**ETHICAL/THEMATIC INTERPRETATION — CL-B0107-004.** The Player's most consequential support is not a declaration of Musubi's fixed authentic self but an answerable invitation: she may tell him what she feels, and her answer need not preserve the idol role. This is bounded by his earlier complicity in coercion and the absence of enacted safeguards. Musubi's dorm/next-day lesson choice keeps both rest and continued practice available without requiring a permanent identity solution that night. Rona's B0004 recollection expands the pattern of person-first recognition while remaining her own analogy, not proof of Musubi's happiness. [C:P55–136]

Rival: the speech cures her uncertainty or makes quitting inevitable. Musubi gives no identity formula, asks for lessons, and Rona's confidence about her feelings is not Musubi's. The alternative that nothing changed misses her disclosure, the Player's acceptance of an open answer and her immediate end to unsafe training.
