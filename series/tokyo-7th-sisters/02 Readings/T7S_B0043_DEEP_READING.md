# T7S_B0043 — the rescue story written by the attacker

## Result

AXiS converts its seizure of Egg Hall into a public story in which it rescued 777☆SISTERS from equipment failure. The lie works because it is not built from wholly invented events: Nanastar's live did fail, AXiS did provide a show, and remote audiences lack access to the causal link between the two. Episode 2 thus moves the conflict from infrastructural occupation to narrative expropriation. The victims lose not only their event but the authority to describe what happened to them. Rona supplies the essential counter-test. However similar AXiS sounds, Seven Sisters did not invade another performer's live; resemblance of sign cannot establish continuity of conduct. Hime supplies the complementary material test: years of repeated labor have been deleted from the public account in a day. When AXiS seizes Nanastar's attempted recovery stream as well, the episode closes the obvious route back. “Starting over” cannot be private or basic when the opponent owns the channels through which a restart becomes visible.

## Reading position and exposure boundary

This reading entered with the completed prior Main sequence and `T7S_B0042`. AXiS episodes 03–13 were withheld during the first pass. Its one inline-movie transcript was read as part of the episode. Later clarification of Nero's plan is retrospective and is not used to make the characters know more here than they do.

## Resemblance tested by ethics

The group initially reacts from shock. Rona, the member whose life was most explicitly changed by Seven Sisters, refuses the audience's identity equation. She acknowledges extreme vocal similarity while saying she can distinguish the voices and, more importantly, that Seven Sisters never did what AXiS did.

Her claim is both affective and analytical. She does not possess an objective acoustic instrument, so her assurance cannot independently prove that the sounds differ. But she does possess a model of the relation Seven Sisters offered her. A performer who invades another live violates that model even if every outward sign can be reproduced.

This makes ethics part of identity without reducing persons to moral purity. Rona is not saying Seven Sisters never caused harm. At this boundary she says the current act cannot be cited as proof of their return. Continuity requires more than the audience recognizing a voice.

## Ten million views and causal erasure

AXiS' uploaded debut reaches ten million views by the next morning and attracts international attention. Coverage describes it as the return of Seven Sisters. More damagingly, reports say equipment trouble interrupted 777☆SISTERS and that AXiS generously supplied a free replacement performance.

The account is difficult to disprove from its wording. Each visible event is rearranged around an omitted cause. An audience sees a failed show and a successful intervention; the infrastructure attack that joins them is unavailable. This is narrative expropriation: AXiS receives moral credit for mitigating the deprivation it produced.

The strategy also isolates eyewitnesses. People physically present experienced intrusion, but remote circulation reaches far more people and provides the vocabulary through which the incident will be discussed. A testimony that says “our live was stolen” can now be answered with a seemingly neutral report saying “your show was saved.”

The episode therefore refuses a naïve faith that facts automatically defeat propaganda. Facts become public through institutions, and those institutions can select which causal edges survive.

## Hime's account of what disappeared

Hime rejects the increasingly abstract discussion and names the loss. The group practiced every day, failed, grieved, and continued for years. One spectacular intervention has made that accumulated work look like incompetence followed by rescue. She leaves in tears to confront AXiS, and Sui follows.

Her action is unsafe and strategically vulnerable, but her diagnosis is precise. Reputation is not merely a cosmetic layer over labor. Once the public account erases the work, the group loses venue access, bargaining power, audience trust, and even the intelligibility of its own effort. A false story can become a material condition.

Hime also prevents “successor legitimacy” from staying an abstract heritage debate. What has been taken is the daily life of current performers. The ethical question is not only who resembles Seven Sisters but whether the people standing here are allowed to retain the history they actually made.

## Investigation under asymmetry

The Manager and Coney choose information discipline. They need to know how the venue was penetrated, how media framing was coordinated, who supports AXiS, and which apparent allies may be compromised. Coney judges the threat larger than the KARAKURI crisis, because the adversary acts across companies, contracts, facilities, and public narration.

The Manager correctly insists that this cannot be resolved as a spontaneous confrontation among young performers. Venues and broadcasters have duties; companies can impose liabilities; adults and institutions authored many of the conditions. The story thereby resists relocating all responsibility into a dramatic idol rivalry.

Their caution has a relational cost. Hime wants someone to affirm that the obvious wrong matters now. Strategic silence can feel like acquiescence when the attacked person is watching her history disappear.

## Nero's economy of proof

At AXiS' office, Nero exploits the absence of conclusive evidence. Even if Hime's accusation were true, she says, AXiS merely chose the most efficient way to sell. She asks why effectiveness should be wrong and answers Sui's concern for injured people by saying AXiS is only doing the same thing others have done.

Efficiency here is not a neutral tool. It becomes a procedure for stripping acts of recipients and consequences. If market ascent is the objective, whatever produces ascent can be redescribed as method rather than injury.

Nero then addresses Coney as `先輩`, knows that she changed her name, and invokes a sin the change cannot erase. She says she learned the method from Coney, intends to repeat what Seven Sisters did, and calls the coming destruction the curse of Seven Sisters.

This transforms imitation into accusation. AXiS is not simply borrowing Seven Sisters because the brand is valuable. Nero claims to reenact a historical violence that its former member refuses to name. At this boundary, the alleged sin is unresolved. What matters is that market instrumentality and personal grievance are already fused.

## When reassurance can no longer be a role

After the confrontation, Coney makes Hime promise not to move alone again. A staged altercation could be circulated as proof that Nanastar is unstable or violent. Hime agrees but asks Coney to become the familiar mentor: joke, smile, and say everything will be all right.

Coney cannot do it. Her inability is not lack of care; it is recognition that the old affective routine would be false under a prepared, institutional attack. Yet the moment exposes how much of Nanastar's stability rested on Coney appearing able to contain danger.

The request also brings Coney's concealed history into present pedagogy. She has been able to guide successors while keeping Seven Sisters at a distance. Nero has made that distance unavailable. Coney can no longer reassure the group without answering what her earlier career caused and why someone calls its effects a curse.

## The channel that captures every restart

Two days later, 777☆SISTERS attempts a modest live through Nanastar's own stream. The choice is deliberately humble: if the grand venue has been taken, perhaps direct performance can begin again from home.

AXiS seizes the stream and power as well. It adopts a solicitous pose, offers to replace the failed content, and turns Nanastar's recovery attempt into another AXiS promotional surface. The attack demonstrates that “return to basics” is not outside infrastructure. Even a small live requires electricity, network access, account control, and discoverability.

The repeated substitution has a cumulative semantic effect. Every new technical failure makes the original rescue narrative more plausible. AXiS can cause the pattern from which audiences infer that 777☆SISTERS simply cannot perform reliably.

The storm in the title is therefore not one coming event. It is the formation of a media environment in which the attacker controls both disruption and explanation.
