# T7S_B0044 — a coalition made from borrowed intimacy

## Result

`苛立つ者たち` opens AXiS from the inside and reveals neither a natural sisterhood nor six interchangeable villains. Its members carry divergent moral vocabularies, resentments, and needs. Nero coordinates them through a written plan, rescue debt, threat, and assigned social labor. Sabina mobilizes devoted followers as deniable hackers; Taki and Oto cultivate influence through gendered access to powerful people; Seneca supplies a justice language she cannot defend when questioned. Their apparent ascent is therefore built from outsourced intimacy: fans, admirers, producers, and rescued recruits act as both instruments and liability buffers. Hime and Rona meanwhile refine the Seven Sisters dispute into intent versus effect. Seven Sisters may not have intended to destroy an ecology, but other groups did disappear in the wake of their supremacy. The chapter permits that historical harm to become visible without accepting AXiS' conclusion that intentional repetition is therefore justified.

## Encirclement by people who do not appear

Nanastar's experts cannot secure a stream against the number and skill of the attackers. Alternate venue access is also closing. Online discussion now treats repeated failures as proof of incompetence or even alleges that AXiS and 777☆SISTERS jointly staged the incidents for publicity.

The reputational story has become self-reinforcing. If Nanastar speaks, it looks defensive; if it attempts another live, AXiS can interrupt it; if it remains silent, the available account hardens. Technical penetration, booking pressure, and interpretive suspicion form one encirclement before the formal name for that operation appears.

Musubi notices that simple ascent does not explain the precision of the destruction. The Manager likewise concludes that six young performers cannot independently coordinate this scale of hacking, venue exclusion, and media influence. The relevant opponent must include the institution behind them.

This is an important allocation of adult responsibility. The girls will make consequential choices, but the means surrounding them belong to companies, producers, experts, and political-economic networks. Calling the event a battle of idol groups would hide the actors who make one side's power possible.

## Intent, consequence, and historical succession

Hime says AXiS appears determined to eliminate idols as Seven Sisters once did. Rona again rejects intentional equivalence. Hime answers with outcome: whatever Seven Sisters intended, idols disappeared after their rise.

Neither account cancels the other. Rona preserves a distinction between deliberate invasion and unintended cultural effect. Hime refuses to let good intention make displaced livelihoods and unrealized futures disappear. Their dispute creates the moral problem the arc must eventually answer: how can successors accept responsibility for a field shaped by their success without treating aspiration itself as intentional violence?

Hime apologizes and returns to formation practice with Rona. Her recommitment is voluntary and relational. Coney has constrained solo action but has not threatened expulsion or cited what Nanastar once gave Hime as a collectible debt. This becomes the immediate ethical contrast with the AXiS interior.

## A written plan in place of common purpose

Taki says events are proceeding exactly according to a script. Seneca feels guilty that AXiS became the top group immediately while others work and fail. Oto calls failed effort evidence of a wrong method and equates losing with stupidity. Nero concludes that the public requires spectacle and method, not substantive content.

The exchange reveals shared execution without shared justification. Seneca wants goodness, Oto wants proof through results, Taki wants personal recognition, and Nero wants the plan followed. Their agreement exists at the level of action because no one needs to agree about why the action is legitimate.

This makes AXiS efficient and brittle. A collective that does not deliberate about purpose can move rapidly along an authored route. It also has no internal practice for revising that route once member motives cease to align.

## Fans as labor and expendable shields

Sabina reports that her `しもべ` have surrounded 777☆SISTERS. They block bookings and attack broadcasts. She avoids explicit orders, signals what devoted followers should do, and plans to present them as independent culprits if the arrangement is exposed.

The relation is counterfeit reciprocity. Followers experience intimacy, usefulness, or loyalty; Sabina receives distributed labor and deniability. Their agency is real—they choose acts—but the information and incentives are controlled so that responsibility flows downward when needed.

Nero objects only to careless execution. The ethical problem is not an aberration inside the plan. Outsourced action is its scalable form.

Taki and Oto receive parallel assignments. Nero sends Taki to powerful older women and Oto to a female television producer. AXiS' reach thus depends not only on anonymous hacking but on embodied, gendered social access. Attraction, admiration, and professional contact become infrastructure.

## Moral language without a revisable judgment

Seneca believes AXiS exists to correct 777☆SISTERS because their way is bad. Oto asks her to define the wrong. Seneca cannot do so and answers circularly: bad people must be corrected by a just method. Oto calls the reasoning contemptible.

Oto's critique is accurate but not an ethical exit. She rejects incoherence while maintaining that winners demonstrate value and losers stupidity. Analytical sharpness can expose another person's rationalization while leaving one's own outcome worship intact.

Seneca's language is dangerous precisely because it feels principled. Once a target is categorized as bad, the chosen punishment becomes evidence of justice rather than an act requiring independent evaluation. Her later history will explain the origin of this model, but this episode already shows that it cannot tolerate a question it has not been supplied an answer to.

## Taki and the hostile reading of attention

Fan letters bring AXiS' public intimacy into the room. Taki delights in praise directed at her. When an older male fan calls Seneca pure, Taki interprets the compliment as sexual projection onto a stranger's body and pressures Seneca to let her destroy the letter.

The critique contains real insight: the fan cannot know Seneca's inner purity, and idealization can sexualize under the cover of reverence. Taki nevertheless makes her own interpretation absolute, humiliates the writer, and overrides Seneca's decision about a letter addressed to Seneca. She opposes projection through another projection backed by interpersonal force.

Her boast about putting gum on a politically opinionated boy's bag as “education” extends the pattern. She casts punishment as correction and aggression as protection. When Oto probes the source of her hostility toward men, the two nearly fight.

## Rescue debt as management

Nero ends the quarrel by reminding Taki and Oto that she removed them from unbearable prior lives. She then threatens to discard anyone who does not work.

The combination converts rescue into ownership. A past act that preserved or transformed a person's life becomes an account permanently payable through obedience. This is the inverse of the Manager's developing model: assistance is valuable insofar as it enlarges the recipient's ability to decide, not insofar as it guarantees the helper's future authority.

AXiS is therefore neither purely coerced nor freely allied. Its members receive real rewards and may sincerely choose aspects of the project, but those choices occur inside a hierarchy able to weaponize gratitude and replaceability.

## The last Dr.Serge

The Manager and Coney arrive at End Production seeking the institution behind AXiS. Nero sends them to a concealed figure who calls himself Dr.END, AXiS' producer, formerly part of the Dr.Serge collective and now its last member. Coney recognizes the category before his explanation.

The reveal joins symbolic imitation to production lineage. AXiS is not a fan-made copy assembled only from public memory. It arises from expertise continuous with the regime that made Seven Sisters. The source withholds what Dr.Serge did, why only one remains, and how the voices were found or produced.

That continuity complicates the language of curse. The past does not return mystically. Its institutions, techniques, grievances, and personnel remain capable of organizing new people.
