# T7S_B0050 — need before enemy

## Result

Episode 9 answers Nero's claim that all life is war before anyone can refute it in argument. Aguri hears a child drowning and enters a flooded river without calculating group, status, vote, or debt. When Aguri begins to drown, Sui rescues the person designated as her enemy for the same reason: someone needs help. The scene does not restore innocence or deny risk. Sui hesitates because she is afraid; Aguri acts instantly partly because she treats her own life as already over. Identical outward care can therefore arise from valuing another and discounting oneself. Around Haru's collapse, Nanastar develops a related answer at collective scale. No single person fixes the crisis. Musubi stays, Hime continues Haru's appeal, Sui and Sumire accompany Hime, Kajika obtains supplies, and Shinju cares for the carer. Relation becomes distributed action rather than one heroic will. Nero's visit to a patient who cannot recognize her places failed recognition beside these reciprocal rescues without yet naming the patient or diagnosis.

## Presence without recognition

Haru remains motionless in the rain after Nero leaves. Elsewhere, Nero enters a hospital to visit a patient recovering from a cold. A nurse recognizes Nero and begins to suggest speaking with the patient, then apologizes.

The patient does not initially notice Nero, asks who she is, and wonders whether the visitor cannot speak. Nero flees in distress and repeats that only a little remains. The relationship, diagnosis, and intended endpoint are withheld.

The scene literalizes the recurring dream's wound. Nero can be physically present and still fail to exist for the person whose recognition matters. This is not ordinary anonymity. The nurse's apology implies an established condition known to staff, while Nero's repeated visits imply enduring attachment.

The episode does not authorize a medical conclusion or prove that the patient's failure is caused by Seven Sisters. It establishes failed recognition as a material relation rather than only a nightmare image.

## Haru's body reaches the limit of solitary appeal

Musubi finds Haru and cannot obtain a response. Hime, Sui, and Sumire arrive. Musubi says Haru has a cold and accumulated physical and emotional exhaustion from remaining in the rain, but should recover with rest.

Haru's collapse is not narrative proof that coexistence was naïve. It is the bodily result of one person attempting to carry a collective and structural problem alone after being denied an answer she can use.

Hime understands that Haru did not go to accuse Nero. She likely bowed and begged for the battle to stop. Hime first blames her own argument for sending Haru, then decides to continue the action: she will bow despite believing Nanastar owes no apology.

This is the first synthesis of their divided strategies. Hime does not revise her judgment that AXiS is attacking them. She accepts Haru's nonviolent appeal as a responsibility the group should not leave with the person now unconscious.

Sumire and Sui follow. Their accompaniment prevents Hime's continuation from becoming another solitary sacrifice. Musubi remains with Haru. The group begins to recombine by assigning different forms of care rather than demanding one consensus act from every member.

## A truthful surrender that cannot reach the real objective

The three find Aguri and learn that Nero is in EZO-5th, the northern medical special zone. Hime bows and says 777☆SISTERS does not object to AXiS existing or working independently. Nanastar will surrender the number-one position; it asks only that the targeted destruction stop.

The offer is sincere and strategically revealing. If Nero merely wanted the top, Hime has removed the reason for conflict. The fact that the appeal cannot complete shows that position is not the final object.

Hime also says Nanastar did nothing wrong because it only worked earnestly. That statement remains under pressure from Susu and Nero's accounts of unseen losers. It is true that the group did not commit AXiS' deliberate attacks; it is incomplete as a claim that success had no external effects.

Before the disagreement can continue, an immediate need interrupts the abstraction.

## Aguri enters the river

Aguri hears a distant cry from a swollen river. A boy is drowning. Before Sui can enter, Aguri jumps, reaches him, and pushes him close enough for Hime, Sui, and Sumire to pull him out.

No one asks which group the boy supports, whether cameras are present, or how rescue will affect the vote. The situation supplies its own reason: a person will die without action.

Aguri then loses her hold. As she begins to drown, her inward response is not fear for herself but apology to Nero for leaving Nero alone. Death feels like return to a state she already occupies.

Sui enters and saves her. Sui later admits hesitation. She was afraid, assessed danger, and then acted because Aguri needed help. Her fear keeps the scene from turning care into magical fearlessness.

Aguri's immediate action is admirable and alarming. Her speed is partly enabled by treating her life as valueless. A system can praise self-sacrifice while leaving the person's self-erasure intact. Hime's insistence that Aguri is factually alive is therefore more than comfort; it contests the premise that made the rescue easy.

## Need as a category prior to allegiance

Aguri asks why an enemy was saved. Sui says enemy and ally are irrelevant when someone needs help, and notes that Aguri followed the same rule with the boy.

The exchange does not prove that political or structural antagonism is unreal. AXiS remains capable of dissolving Nanastar. It establishes a category that war ontology cannot fully absorb. A person can be an institutional opponent and a body whose need makes a claim now.

Nero's model would redescribe both rescues as strategic acts, assertions of power, or means of self-definition. Nothing in the sequence supports that reduction. The rescuers do not know the reputational outcome, and Sui acts after the child is already safe. Aguri's continued life is the object.

Hime offers to bring Aguri to Nanastar to dry off. Aguri instead brings the three to her own room. Hospitality begins after reciprocal rescue without requiring prior ideological reconciliation.

## Care distributed around the hospital bed

Kajika, Shinju, and Sawara join Musubi beside the feverish Haru. Kajika cannot solve the battle, so she obtains a drink. Shinju accompanies her and notices that Musubi also needs food.

The actions are small enough to escape heroic narration. They are nevertheless the collective answer the episode is building. Haru's isolated action failed because one body could not contain every obligation. The group restores capacity by seeing different needs: negotiation, supervision, warmth, hydration, food, and companionship.

Care also becomes recursive. Shinju does not only care for Haru; she cares for Musubi, who is caring for Haru. This is redundancy rather than dependence on one indispensable protector.

Kajika says AXiS frightens her and notices that the rain continues. Seneca then appears. The episode ends before fear is overcome or the encounter is resolved. Distributed care enables continuation; it does not guarantee safety.

## Rain as shared exposure, not equal consequence

Rain joins the episode's locations. Haru collapses under it, Aguri and Sui enter its flood, and the others move through it to find one another. The shared weather makes bodies mutually vulnerable without equalizing their histories or resources.

This qualifies the prior phrase that rain is equal. Exposure can be common while consequences differ. What matters is not sameness of condition but whether people respond to the particular need in front of them.
