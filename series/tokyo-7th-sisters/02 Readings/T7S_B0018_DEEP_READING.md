# T7S_B0018 — making a city by learning to point at it

## Result

The first EPISODE 2.0 chapter gives Nanasta an official commission: make a same-day tourism video for Tokyo-7th's Central district. The assignment looks like a light ensemble tour, but it performs three large operations at once. It locates the cast inside a civic and economic geography; it turns each member's competence and attachment into a way of seeing that geography; and it ends by naming Nanasta's shabby office—not a landmark—as the place from which the city becomes “ours.”

## The seventh city is an administrative project

Tokyo-7th is identified as the seventh new Tokyo district, a special administrative and economic zone built on reclaimed Tokyo Bay. Its modernity is designed: transit, Skydome, technology, leisure districts, and redevelopment form an official image. The now-ended idol subsidy policy makes Nanasta's history part of the same civic machinery. Idols were once useful enough to subsidize; after the boom, the support vanished, leaving the institution to survive without the state promise that helped produce it.

The tourism commission therefore asks former beneficiaries of a discarded cultural policy to sell the city's continued brightness. That does not make their enthusiasm fraudulent. It makes their local knowledge politically meaningful. The video is credible because the cast inhabits what the promotional script would otherwise flatten.

## Twelve partial maps

The chapter distributes Central through member-specific attention. Rona sees the Neo-Seventh Dome through the memory of Seven Sisters and turns grief into renewed aspiration. Sui treats transit as a system that must be learned accurately. Hime understands neighborhood circulation through a bazaar, shopkeepers, and festival labor. Harumi's fish shop embeds the district in intergenerational work. Momoka reads Akihabara-like space through games and anime. Sumire recognizes fashion. The Skydome joins grand technological ambition to the cast's wish for a venue large enough to hold ten thousand people.

No single member is the city's authoritative narrator. The city emerges through accumulated partial maps. EPISODE 1.0 assembled twelve people; this chapter gives that plurality spatial form.

## The camera and uneven consent

Coney leaves equipment, script, costumes, deadline, and responsibility while absent. The ensemble improvises effectively, but the managerial pattern remains ethically unstable. Susu pushes Musubi into a sexualized tourism performance; the Player records while Musubi is visibly uncomfortable. Musubi later reverses the discomfort and both girls apologize, giving the incident reciprocal comic closure without retroactively making the filming consensual.

The scene matters because the chapter celebrates spontaneous personality as promotional value. “Authenticity” can be another extraction technique when the institution controls the camera and surprise. The video succeeds partly by capturing responses that the performers did not design.

## Children outside the idol memory

Young children fail to recognize “idol” as a meaningful category. This is a quiet historical measure of the industry's collapse. Nanasta cannot rely on cultural inheritance alone. Rona's emotional continuity with Seven Sisters is real, but it is not automatically transmitted to the next audience. Revival requires making a present-tense practice visible to people for whom the old word carries little content.

## The office as final attraction

Haru chooses the unglamorous Nanasta office as the video's last destination. The choice reorganizes the preceding tour. Landmarks show what Tokyo-7th officially offers; the office shows why these people remain in it. The members name it a gathering place, a rest, a source of warmth, a link to predecessors, and the point from which they will revive idols and become number one in the seventh district.

This is not a claim that Nanasta owns the city. The office becomes a center because multiple routes return to it. “Home” here is neither biological household nor architectural prestige. It is the place where dispersed lives become mutually expected.

## Exit state

- Tokyo-7th is established as a reclaimed special administrative/economic district whose earlier idol subsidy has ended.
- Nanasta completes an official Central tourism video by combining member-local knowledge rather than one homogeneous brand voice.
- Children’s nonrecognition demonstrates that idol revival cannot depend on inherited familiarity.
- The Skydome becomes a concrete scale of collective ambition.
- The office is explicitly claimed as home, gathering point, and civic center of the group's own map.
- Coney's absent assignment and the camera's use of unplanned discomfort keep managerial consent unresolved.
