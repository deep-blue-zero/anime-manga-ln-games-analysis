# T7S_B0151 — My Name

## Result and entering boundary

An unnamed television sponsor's new chief treats Kuonji Shizuka's appearance as advertising for a rival conglomerate and pressures broadcasters to stop booking 777☆SISTERS while she remains visible. Shizuka hears the managers' private account, takes responsibility for the resulting cancellations, and volunteers to withdraw temporarily from television without leaving Nanastar. The others are told she is ill, not the sponsor's condition. When Haru recognizes the concealment, the Player discloses that the exclusion may not be one night only. Coney shows Shizuka a pre-broadcast stream lobby filled with viewers asking for her by name and brings her to the studio. On camera, Haru refuses to let Shizuka decide the group's wishes for them; Shizuka finally says she wants to sing with them. A live full-group appearance and later report of the sponsor's approval resolve this particular broadcast, but neither audience affection nor a mercurial executive proves a stable right of access to television.

## Factual reconstruction by presentation and actor knowledge

### EV-B0151-001 — growing screen access and an interrupted celebration

Sawara and Shizuka tape a nationwide late-night mini-segment; Shizuka describes mobilizing Kuonji resources to research regional puns, which Sawara treats comically as extravagant use of wealth. At Nanastar, members list the rotating mini-segments and anticipate a televised live with all 777☆SISTERS. Sawara says shopping-street customers have increased; this is her report, not measured causal impact. Shizuka proposes a celebration and repeatedly overestimates the scale of food and venue needed. The director then calls Coney to a producer, and Shizuka overhears Coney tell the Player that the group must leave its programs. The chapter's opening establishes a concrete opportunity and a class contrast before explaining the obstruction. [A:P2–68]

### EV-B0151-002 — sponsor exclusion travels through broadcasters

In a later private explanation, Coney says the programs share a major sponsor whose new chief sees the Kuonji group as a rival. The chief's condition is that Shizuka's television appearance promotes Kuonji, so 777☆S cannot appear in programs led by that sponsor while she is in the group. Coney reports a producer resisting and similar messages at multiple stations; the Player's idea of another show founders on the company's widespread television spending. These are manager-relayed facts and forecasts, not direct testimony from a broadcaster or sponsor. Coney plans to approach the chief cautiously and asks the Player to remain with the performers. The scene depicts concentrated commercial power over access, not a demonstrated law or explicit ban on every possible screen medium. [B:P22–49]

### EV-B0151-003 — selective secrecy converts loss into self-blame

Shows are rescheduled; Susu, Musubi and Sui notice, and Kajika fears that a personal failure caused the disruption. The Player evades explanation, while Shizuka has already heard enough to infer that her surname is implicated. He nevertheless urges rehearsal for the planned live. The earlier A overhearing and B flashback explain the audience's knowledge before the rest of the group learns anything; a reschedule is not proof the underlying sponsor demand has disappeared. ### EV-B0151-004 — apology work and fans' anticipation

Shizuka volunteers for Hime's deliveries, the fish shop and Nanastar cleaning, saying this is the apology she can offer; Hime says she is overworking. She then hears pedestrians anticipate the full televised group and blames herself for jeopardizing it. The work does not address the sponsor's condition or transfer responsibility from its author. ### EV-B0151-005 — a constrained temporary withdrawal

Shizuka asks Coney and the Player to preserve 777☆S television appearances by excluding her from those performances for a time. She explicitly says she is *not* quitting Nanastar and names friendship and supporting others as her motives. The Player objects. Coney first insists that a decision to withdraw for others must not simply be blocked, then asks what Shizuka herself truly feels. Shizuka makes an additional request, withheld at B:P123. Her agency is real—she proposes the plan and sets a boundary around it—but the choice is made under externally imposed sponsor pressure and before the other members can consent to being represented by her sacrifice. ### EV-B0151-006 — the absence is disguised as illness

Before the live, the members believe Shizuka is abruptly absent through sickness and are distressed. Sawara urges them to honor what she thinks Shizuka wants; Haru observes that the Player's face and evasive answer do not fit the illness account and demands the truth. The Player later tells them Shizuka made this choice, admits it might not be only this occasion, and promises a later conversation. The illness cover gave the others neither accurate information nor an opportunity to answer Shizuka's supposition about their wishes. It also risks imposing a false health account on her. ### EV-B0151-008 — peers refuse unilateral representation on camera

## Interpretation and alternatives

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0151-001.** The sponsor's competitive inference converts an idol's surname into a commercial conflict and uses multi-station spending to pressure the entire group. Shizuka's altruism responds to the pressure but does not create it. Coney's report and later phone account are all the case has of the chief; no independent executive motive or binding broadcaster decision is inspected. [B:P22–49,95–111; C:P148–152]

Rival: Shizuka's mere presence is objectively advertising and therefore makes the exclusion her fault. The text attributes that premise to an interested outside actor, while Shizuka states another purpose for performing.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0151-002.** Shizuka's temporary television withdrawal is a constrained act of care, not proof that sacrifice is freely required or that her group membership ends. Coney's defense of a person's ability to step back is important, but Haru later establishes that Shizuka cannot choose *for everyone* without hearing them. [B:P84–123; C:P1–19,98–134]

Rival: either all withdrawal is forbidden or, because she volunteered, the others must accept it silently. Both erase a different participant's agency.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0151-003.** The managers' concealment is materially consequential: Kajika first blames herself; the group later acts on a fabricated illness explanation until Haru challenges the Player. The chapter does not show a protective confidentiality rule, health disclosure consent or a full repair after the reveal. [B:P1–19; C:P23–45,77–84,158–162]

Rival: the eventual on-air reunion retroactively validates the false story. It does not undo the period of misinformed rehearsal and worry.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0151-004.** The name motif moves from Kuonji as involuntary market signal to fans actively naming the missing performer and Shizuka speaking a personal “I” while retaining her full family name. The stage-costume entrance and visible emotional changes support, but do not alone establish, that shift; the decisive evidence is dialogue. [A:P2–18; B:P26–30; C:P18–19,50–73,87–134,153–167]

Rival: “true self” means renouncing Kuonji identity. She explicitly says that identity will not change.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0151-005.** A sponsor's reported phone conversion and a successful live do not prove that the original commercial leverage has been dismantled. The actual performance, contracts, public explanation and durable booking policy remain unreviewed or unstated. [C:P95–152,168–177]

Rival: one enthusiastic phone call guarantees continuing access across all stations. The story shows relief, not such a guarantee.
