# Makoto's offered closeness and the pressure of being known

Makoto wants the manager to understand the strength of her feelings, but understanding repeatedly approaches a demand that he return them. She arranges solitude, offers to disappear if she is a nuisance, conditions access to her diary on taking responsibility, and admits that a story of exclusive company was false. These gestures do not all have the same force. A confession of fear about her own excess is different from a threat, a withdrawn joke is different from an enacted harm, and a wish for reassurance does not establish permission for everything that follows it. Their common problem is how to make another person's attention feel secure without absorbing that person's freedom to respond.

The seven addresses preserve both self-awareness and pressure. Makoto can name difficulty communicating, be frightened of doing too much and acknowledge a lie. Such moments complicate a flat image of an unreflective pursuer; they do not by themselves repair a boundary. The last makeup address adds a smaller alternative. After wanting to capture the manager's gaze and reproaching his laughter, she wonders whether imperfect makeup might be acceptable if it makes him smile. That local possibility matters without becoming a permanent cure, a proof that his laughter was kind or a general renunciation of exclusivity.
