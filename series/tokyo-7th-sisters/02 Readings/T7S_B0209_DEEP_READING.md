# Sumire's recognition and the pace of closeness

Sumire wants the manager to recognize something particular about her: the taste she has learned to prepare, the seriousness of her effort, the suitability of her name, and her wish to spend time together outside work. She also wants to help determine how that recognition becomes closeness. An invitation can carry embarrassment without ceasing to be an invitation; an affectionate possibility can remain conditional without becoming an accomplished relationship. These nine addresses make those distinctions part of her agency. Her initiative includes both proposing shared time and saying that the proposed scale is too early.

The cases also complicate an account of Sumire as simply governed by fashion. She enjoys fashionable food and notices a popular mascot, but neither scene ends with anonymous consumption. A sweet unavailable to buy becomes an experiment in making something for a particular recipient. The manager's ignorance of a school trend becomes an occasion to invite him along. Her changing investment in idol work similarly joins pleasure in effort to a desire for personal recognition. Familiar rhetorical qualifications can shelter what she wants to say; they do not authorize the reader to erase the actual conditions she attaches to it.
