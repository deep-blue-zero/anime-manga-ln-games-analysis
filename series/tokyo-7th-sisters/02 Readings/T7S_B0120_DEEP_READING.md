# T7S_B0120 — Ayumu's Victorian Maid Event

## Result and entering boundary

Ayumu's maid café plans a themed event whose requested "Victorian" style forbids the `ぽえきゅん` idiom she considers essential to her usual service. Miwako's demonstrations help but Ayumu cannot keep that register; Kazumi then teaches her for a week. The resulting formal performance and costume impress the others, while its claimed historical fidelity also brings bitter tea and a rigid hierarchy: Ayumu refuses Coney's cleaning request under an invoked service "contract," calls Chacha a lady and the Player a laborer. The Player and Coney recognize a café-customer mismatch; Coney's thought of marketing an insulting maid remains a joke/proposal, not a policy. The event is later *reported* trouble-free, not directly shown. Ayumu returns to her preferred `ぽえ☆きゅん` voice without disowning the formal mode, although a playful `チャチャお嬢様` service relation persists. Her acquired craft expands her range; it does not replace her identity or verify the historical and contractual claims inside the skit.

## Factual reconstruction by actor knowledge

### EV-B0120-001 — a style demand Ayumu experiences as a threat

Ayumu clings to the Player while upset; Miwako and Chacha tease or accuse him, and he asks Ayumu to let go before she does. Ayumu says her café will hold a Victorian-themed service event and that `ぽえきゅん` is forbidden, which threatens the idiom she uses for her own maid work. Chacha describes a strict British-maid *image*; Miwako proposes ordinary-language teaching, Ayumu accepts and Player offers help. The scene does not show Player initiating the embrace or establish misconduct, and the characters' historical framing is theirs, not external verification. [A:P2–36]

### EV-B0120-002 — modeling alone does not transfer a register

Ayumu's attempted formal speech breaks into her usual `ぽえ`/`にゃん` sounds. Chacha prompts Miwako to demonstrate first professional speech, then a master's welcome; Miwako is reluctant but does so for Ayumu, and the Player praises her. Ayumu tries and slips again. Kazumi says she often reads British literature and may know the maid style; Ayumu welcomes her teaching. Chacha's confidence and Kazumi's reading are character testimony, not an independent certificate of period expertise. [B:P1–43]

### EV-B0120-003 — successful role acquisition imports more than diction

After a week with Kazumi, Ayumu appears in a formal maid costume and register. Kazumi calls her historically perfect; Ayumu says she has discovered another valid way to be a maid and thanks the group. The Player/Coney are impressed. She serves tea they find extremely bitter and recommends substantial milk and sugar; Chacha gives a hard-water explanation as a claimed British custom. Neither the actual brewing process nor the historical generalization is independently verified. [C:P1–38]

### EV-B0120-004 — the enacted hierarchy fails a café-service test

Ayumu refuses Coney's request to tidy her desk, saying her contract is service to the master only. She defers to Chacha as a supposed noble lady and classifies the Player as a worker; Kazumi applauds this as period fidelity. The Player says it is unsuitable, and Coney notes café customers would mostly fall outside that hierarchy, then riffs on the possible appeal of an insulting maid. These are role labels and a *claimed* contract inside the lesson, not a reviewed employment agreement or independently established social class. Coney proposes no actual customer offering. [C:P39–58]

### EV-B0120-005 — reported event and incomplete return

At a later lesson Ayumu uses her usual speech again. The Player says the café event finished without trouble; the event itself is not presented. Ayumu says serious maid work is worthwhile but `ぽえ☆きゅん` fits her best. When Chacha asks for tea, Ayumu instantly calls her `チャチャお嬢様` and agrees; the Player calls their relation pseudo-master/servant, while Chacha does not object. The joke persists in this last scene, but no enduring labor contract or change in Ayumu's ordinary service obligations is shown. [C:P59–78]

## Interpretation and alternatives

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0120-001.** Ayumu's formal style is a learnable role, not a replacement self. Her initial resistance, week of teaching, competent demonstration and later preference for `ぽえ☆きゅん` make the chapter an example of repertoire expansion with retained self-description. [A:P26–36; B:P1–43; C:P11–23,61–67]

Rival: she was permanently transformed or forced into a new personality. She voluntarily accepts help and explicitly resumes her usual idiom. The opposite claim that the lesson accomplished nothing misses her formal performance and own recognition of another mode.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0120-002.** The lesson shifts from surface diction to an enacted social system. Kazumi's "perfect" result reveals a practical mismatch when the historical role-play prioritizes rank and a narrow service obligation over care for café customers. This is a claim about the *story's* constructed Victorian mode, not verified Victorian social history. [B:P12–43; C:P8–58]

Rival: flawless period mimicry is therefore flawless café service. The Player/Coney articulate the mismatch. A rival that Kazumi intended harm exceeds the evidence; she acts as a teacher who values fidelity.

**ETHICAL_THEMATIC_INTERPRETATION — CL-B0120-003.** Ayumu's refusal to clean Coney's desk shows she can mark a limit, but its rationale is a fictional rank/contract rule. A boundary can be real as a performed act without making the role's hierarchy equitable or legally operative; Chacha's playful preferential service outlasts the lesson. [C:P39–58,68–75]

Rival: the scene proves a real contract, class status or lasting domination. No actual contract is displayed and the Player explicitly calls it a pseudo-relation. A reverse rival that Ayumu cannot say no ignores her direct refusal.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0120-004.** The event's outcome is secondhand, narrower than the shown training result: the Player says it went without trouble, but no customer interaction, reception or full performance is inspected. Coney's insult-maid marketing thought is hypothetical. [C:P55–67]

Rival: the successful event verifies every customer accepted the hierarchy. It does not; neither does it establish a failed event, because the explicit report is positive.
