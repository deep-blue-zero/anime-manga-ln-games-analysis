# T7S_B0104 — My Notebook, My Love

## Result and entering boundary

Kazumi declines Rona's premise that friendship is a necessary condition for better idol performance. When Rona finds Kazumi's misplaced, privately authored novel, her first concern is to keep others from reading it; Musubi then reads despite Rona's warning, and Rona joins her. Rona stops Kazumi to say they have barely spoken and might perform better if closer. The Player privately wants Rona to break Kazumi's “heart wall” on his behalf. Kazumi disputes the premise. ### EV-B0104-003 — a bounded opening authored by Kazumi

Kazumi dismisses the novel as idle, unplanned writing. Rona says she felt Kazumi's feelings about idol activity through it; Kazumi warns that story and reality differ and that conflation is dangerous. Rona moves to a milder claim—that writing such a dreamer implies an understanding of her feelings—but Kazumi calls that overestimation and says the story is imagined. Neither Rona's intimacy claim nor Kazumi's modest downplay is omniscient proof of the fiction's origin. [B:P41–51]

Rona then changes the object of her request: she wants to encounter more of the *story Kazumi creates*, without claiming it as a direct report of Kazumi's life. Musubi asks for a continuation too. Kazumi hears their specific appreciation and promises to try, while saying she has not planned the plot. Rona proposes dinner to discuss favorite authors; Musubi joins. Kazumi accepts but suggests talking about idol activity, since she is an idol, not an author. They leave together. The Player, who says he was in the restroom, reflects that idols can connect through expression without his intervention and privately wishes to read the novel. He is not shown requesting or receiving permission. [B:P52–66]

## Interpretation and alternatives

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0104-001.** Kazumi's original refusal is a refusal of *required* friendship as a performance method, not a vow never to connect. She articulates a positive account of reflective solo practice. The Player and Rona initially treat closeness as a managerial or relational goal for her, and the Player even outsources crossing her “wall.” The ending is significant because Kazumi does not merely submit to that premise: she accepts a concrete dinner invitation and chooses idol activity as the topic. Friendship may become possible, but this chapter establishes only the selected interaction and her authorship of its terms. [A:P14–34; B:P52–66]

Rival: the novel proves Rona was right all along that Kazumi secretly wanted friendship. The readers' description of a friend-minded protagonist could express imaginative skill, genre choice, sympathy, desire, or autobiographical material. Kazumi explicitly rejects the equation of story and reality. Her later participation gives evidence of an opening, not retroactive access to the character's inner life.

**ETHICAL/THEMATIC INTERPRETATION — CL-B0104-002.** The chapter lets aesthetic recognition and privacy harm coexist. Rona initially grasps that the notebook should be kept from unauthorized eyes, yet reads alongside Musubi after warning her not to. Kazumi names the violation plainly; the fans' interruptions are not an apology, and her decision to continue writing does not grant every past or future reader consent. The work can change peer speech without becoming common property. [A:P36–41; B:P1–43,52–56]

Rival: Kazumi's happiness at their praise cancels the breach. Her objection at B:P32 and P37 is direct, and no explicit retraction or comprehensive forgiveness follows. The more precise account is mixed reception: their reading hurts her boundary and their response also moves her.

**THEMATIC INTERPRETATION — CL-B0104-003.** Rona's revised request marks the decisive difference between using a fictional protagonist as a key to Kazumi and asking to see more of her authored world. Kazumi's answer retains uncertainty: she will *try* to write, not deliver a finished volume or accept compulsory publication. Her invitation to discuss idol activity also refuses a fixed opposition between solitary thought and collective artistic practice. The Player's concluding generalization is his perspective, not proof that no management or privacy care is necessary. [B:P47–66]

Rival: because Kazumi labels her story “just imagination,” it has no bearing on her practice. That claim is her self-description and should be respected as a boundary against biographical extraction; it does not negate that writing is one of her expressive acts, received by peers in this scene. Conversely, fictional intensity does not demonstrate the quality of an unheard performance or the novel's full content.

Retrospective B0039 confirms that Musubi and Rona have access to such writing while the Player does not. B0078 shows another, differently situated privacy conflict. Neither later case supplies a chapter-to-chapter time edge, a universal access rule, or post-hoc permission for B0104.
