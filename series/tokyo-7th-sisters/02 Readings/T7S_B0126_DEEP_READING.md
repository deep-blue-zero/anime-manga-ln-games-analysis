# T7S_B0126 — Armchair Detective

## Result and entering boundary

Kajika and the Player find Saori unresponsive beside crumbled tofu, with a doughnut in hand and a peace sign; they infer murder without a shown check of her condition. Chacha says pursuing a culprit is pointless but leads a detective-style inquiry when they insist. Her reconstruction mixes clues with unsafe, often unconsented demonstrations: she has the Player pressed into a locker, pushes him into a river and pressures him toward a roadside collision test that does not occur. A tofu-and-doughnut trail eventually leads to Coney, who admits only exchanging her doughnut for Saori’s tofu; the group leaps from trade to guilt. Saori reappears alive and reports coming back to change after getting wet, then slipping on wet shoes and fainting. Chacha says she understood all along and treated the inquiry as relief from boredom. The apparent mystery illustrates how correct observations, unsupported causal stories and performative certainty can coexist. Its comedy does not certify safety after loss of consciousness, excuse risky treatment of the Player, establish a car impact or prove any homicide.

## Factual reconstruction by actor knowledge

### EV-B0126-001 — an unresponsive person is mislabeled a victim of murder

Kajika screams when she and Chacha find Saori motionless. The Player summarizes tofu fragments, a doughnut, peace-sign hand and wet hair with dry clothes; the selected native object is a broken tofu block. He and Kajika infer an attack, and he raises contacting police, but the chapter shows no pulse check, medical assessment or direct confirmation of death. Makoto and Hime imagine police would suspect the only man, leading the group to propose catching a “real culprit.” Chacha says that is pointless but agrees to an inquiry when asked for truth, assigning Player and Kajika as assistants. Her knowledge at this point is not independently demonstrated; her later “I knew” is retrospective self-report. [A:P2–60]

### EV-B0126-002 — a negative locker test still coerces an assistant

Kajika recalls water ending near a locker room and imagines Saori hid in a locker. Chacha orders the Player inside; when he says he cannot fit, she asks Kajika to push him, causing pain. Makoto identifies internal dividers and demonstrates that even a smaller person could not fit. This rejects one hiding hypothesis. It does not justify the painful test or establish what Saori actually did in that room. Makoto’s knowledge of the Player’s similar locker hints at her own intrusion but is not an authorized access right. [B:P1–23]

### EV-B0126-003 — a river demonstration generates another unverified story

Madoka says Saori vanished beside her after a splash. Chacha asks the Player to fall into the river; he refuses, so she distracts him and pushes him in. From Kajika’s vantage he appears to vanish, but he is submerged and calls for help. Chacha says little strength is needed and treats his survival as proof Saori would be fine; neither claim is a health evaluation. The demonstration establishes a possible sightline illusion, not that Saori entered the river in the same way. Hime later says she saw wet Saori and gave her a towel and tofu. [B:P24–73]

### EV-B0126-004 — roadside inference stops before the demanded experiment

Hime relays a neighbor’s story that his car seemed to strike a ghost but nothing was found. Chacha asks the Player to be hit by a car to test it. He refuses, then yields under guilt-laden accusation that he does not care about Saori; Chacha admits the experiment’s likelihood of answering is vague. Kajika finds bent grass along a route toward Nanasta before any collision test occurs. Chacha infers Saori was flung by a car and returned, but neither the driver’s original perception nor the grass proves impact. Saori’s later account never confirms that inference. [B:P74–108]

### EV-B0126-005 — an actual food exchange becomes a false accusation

Chacha returns to Saori’s doughnut and points to tofu crumbs near Coney’s mouth and soy sauce on her sleeve. Coney admits trading her doughnut for Saori’s tofu. The Player and others promptly treat that as a murder confession, despite Coney denying harm. The exchange is supported by her words and clues; the jump from food contact to assault is not. [C:P1–32]

### EV-B0126-006 — Saori returns and Chacha explains her game

Saori walks in, surprising everyone who has been calling her dead. She reports returning to change after becoming wet, then slipping because her shoes were still wet, falling and fainting. The precise earlier cause of her wetness and the neighbor’s road incident are not settled by this account. Chacha says she knew Saori was only down as usual, had suggested leaving her, and found the search an entertaining diversion. The Player names the locker, river and roadside risks he bore. No clinical evaluation of Saori after fainting, nor of the Player after river immersion, is shown. [C:P33–55]
