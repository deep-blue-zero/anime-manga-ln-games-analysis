# Tokyo 7th Sisters — B0005 deep reading

## A princess who leaves without being rescued: Hime adds idolhood by redistributing care

## 0. Result, scope, and integrity

B0005 closes the complete nine-episode native chapter `300050`, プリンセス・シタマチ. Its central opposition is deliberately misleading. 野ノ原ヒメ says that a person with something to protect has no need to be cute, as though family care, shop labor, rough speech, and idol femininity must exclude one another. The chapter does not reveal those duties or her masculine-coded manner as false. **It shows that Hime has made herself the household's indispensable protector and converted grief into a prohibition against a desire she has held since childhood. Her family and Nanasta do not rescue a passive princess; they take on enough work for an already active girl to choose another practice. Hime joins without surrendering `オレ`, the tofu shop, her siblings, or her inherited neighborhood voice.**

## 1. Descriptive reconstruction

### 1.1 The princess story already contains Hime's answer (`201000401`)

Hime opens by attacking the picture-book princesses her mother read to her. They are defined as terrifyingly cute, remain shut in a castle, and wait for someone else to take them out. She resents sharing their name. Her own conclusion then sounds hard and self-sufficient: a princess who does not leave and a person with something to protect do not need to be cute; no rescuer exists in reality. But the monologue does not end there. Hime imagines a place where even she could become cuter, a chance, and a `家族` who could help make that possible. [E01] The chapter's governing desire therefore precedes the recruitment plot. What remains unknown is not whether she wants change, but whether change can coexist with protection and who may participate in it.

The present first establishes Nanasta as an imperfect collective home. Haru can sleep in its cramped women's dorm when work runs late; Rona enjoys the three girls lying in a row; Haru treats enlarging the theater as their own work. [E02] Hime enters this family language as a tofu delivery worker, not as an unformed visual discovery. She knows storage requirements, handles the order, responds to Rona's panic without condescension, and makes the shy girl stand straighter with the injunction to eat tofu and `シャキっと`. Hime also names her blond hair and blue eyes as inheritance from her mother. [E03]

Coney's gaze initially strips away most of this. She inventories blond hair, small shoulders, and white skin, then turns the name `ヒメ` into “princess.” Haru imagines dress-up; Rona, for reasons not yet explained, asks to work with Hime. Hime rejects cute clothing and says a tofu-shop worker cannot be an idol, then leaves. [E04] The scene gives two different recruitment grounds: Coney/Haru respond primarily to visible suitability, while Rona responds to an effect Hime has already had on her. The latter eventually becomes decisive.

### 1.2 The shop makes “something to protect” concrete (`201000402`–`201000403`)

At the shopping arcade the group sees Hime moving continuously among customers, her father, and her younger brother and sister. She advertises several tofu products, adds okara for a customer, calls herself the shop's `看板娘`, and immediately receives sibling requests for food and clean clothes. This is not an abstract excuse invented when recruitment becomes frightening. She is a skilled worker and a functional center of the household. Rona proposes waiting until the rush passes; Coney instead approaches and, after Hime refuses again, declares an immediate test of her signboard-girl ability. The Player recognizes the battle-manga rhythm but allows it. [E05]

The next document resumes after Hime has been made to sing. The act itself is elided. Customers, father, siblings, Rona, and Haru call the result cute; Hime rejects the adjective. Yet her account of idol work is neither contemptuous nor ignorant. She describes cute clothing, song, and dance used to give fans energy, and calls that respectable work before saying it cannot suit her. Coney correctly detects knowledge and interest, then predicts Hime will become much cuter as an idol. Hime retreats behind the shop and shopping district she must protect. [E06]

The text does not let later success silently authorize this scene. The Player criticizes Coney's approach; Musubi explicitly says family circumstances make force inappropriate. [E07] The song reveals Hime's capacity and desire to observers, but the source supplies no prior assent to this “test.” Her later entry must remain a later decision.

### 1.3 A ride changes the recruitment relation (`201000404`)

The Player walks to save transport costs for Nanasta's performers. Hime notices, nearly rides past, then returns and offers him a place on the back of the old delivery bicycle. She further asks for a detour along a riverbank whose sunset she loves. [E08] This matters because the encounter begins with reciprocal recognition of labor: he is spending his body for the theater, and she uses the vehicle of her own work to help him. The Player is no longer arriving with a scouting party at her shop.

When he calls the sunset preference girlish, Hime bristles. The bicycle then supplies the route into grief. It belonged to her late mother, whom Hime remembers as exceptionally beautiful and as the person who promised that Hime too would become beautiful. Praise from anyone else now hurts. She hears `可愛い` and `女らしい` through two simultaneous responses: envy and a question—whether wanting that is permissible. She invokes younger siblings and lack of time, insists she is not suffering, and says she has become too `男勝り` to turn back. [E09]

Nothing here supports a diagnosis in which the rough self is simply counterfeit. Hime likes delivery work, the bicycle, the neighborhood, and caretaking. Her conflict is that she treats those real attachments as proof that another real desire is disallowed.

### 1.4 The HoloCom makes a rehearsal possible, but does not settle its own mystery (`201000404`–`201000405`)

The Player asks Hime to sing at the nearly empty riverbank. When she objects that a sudden solo is impossible, he projects a video of Nanasta's current performers from `このホロコン`, asking her to imagine a shared live. Hime asks whether he always carries it so carefully; he says an encounter might become everyone's chance and admits this is about all he can currently do. She agrees because she is already unsettled. [E10]

After the elided song, Hime calls the other girls cute, joyful, and radiant. The Player's answer changes cute from an entry qualification into a process: strict practice and singing before people make idols more beautiful than their slightly earlier selves. He validates her mother's prediction and, with an explicit hedge, uses pre-debut Nicole as an example. Hime begins, `あんたならオレを可愛く――`, catches herself, and again chooses shop/family duty over entry. [E10]

This is the Player's strongest intervention in the chapter because it offers experience rather than simply contradicting Hime's self-description. It is also qualified. “No one starts cute” replaces fixed essence with practice, but `女の子なら` later universalizes the desire, and the half-formed “make me cute” places considerable transformative authority in the manager. The carried HoloCom has a concrete enabling use and its exact prop depicts projected girl silhouettes. It is compatible with the B0001 prediction about a functional device, but neither text nor asset identity establishes that this is the same handed-down HoloCom or that the supposedly special contents are involved. The inherited-device thread therefore advances without closing.

### 1.5 Childhood prevents a false-mask reading (`201000406`)

A sepia-filtered memory begins with Hime's mother repeating that Hime will become cuter. Child Hime already uses `オレ`; her mother lightly asks whom that resembles, and Hime answers that it is obviously her father. The child then asks whether becoming cute will surprise him, and her mother assures her it will make him happy. [E11]

Chronologically, this comes before the loss around which adult praise hurts. The masculine first-person form and paternal verbal inheritance therefore did not arise only to suppress grief, nor did they prevent a childhood desire for beauty. Hime's speech and aspiration have coexisted from the start. The chapter's task is not to replace one with the other.

### 1.6 Care is redistributed, so the choice changes (`201000406`–`201000407`)

Hime returns from deliveries to find Rona, Haru, Musubi, and the Player tending the shop, the younger children, and the coming meal. Rona says she will help whenever she has time and asks again to work together. This time she explains why. Hime's command to stand straight showed Rona that Nanasta needs a companion who can restore others' energy; Hime also looked sad while calling Rona cute, so Rona recognizes a wish to become cuter. Rona explicitly identifies with that wish. [E12]

Hime admits it, but still refuses to impose the cost on her family. Her father then says that if she wants the work he will not stop her: the family is stronger than she thinks. He apologizes for failing to notice her feelings and treats his failure to buy cosmetics as one sign of that neglect. Her brother says he can help in the shop; her sister promises household help and asks Hime to become more beautiful. The Player says the desire is neither shameful nor bad. Hime accepts in her own idiom: since they have looked after her family, she cannot send them away; since the Nanasta group is hard to leave alone, she will look after them. She finally thanks everyone directly. [E12]

This is support rather than literal rescue. Others do not carry Hime out of her castle. They dispute her assumption that protection must be solitary, perform the work that demonstrates an alternative, and state their willingness to keep doing it. She then converts receiving care into a familiar active role—`オレがいっちょ、面倒みてやる`—without losing the gratitude beneath it.

Coney immediately proposes a guerrilla live on the shopping district's old special stage. Hime is startled by showing herself to everyone she knows, but once Coney frames it as showing her decision to the community that raised her, Hime tells them to call everyone. [E13] The tempo remains coercively fast; unlike the shop “test,” however, the source records Hime's assent to this specific public event.

After the live, the shopping district calls her its princess. Hime asks her father once more whether idolhood and becoming cute are truly allowed. He answers that she should go into the world with pride as his and her mother's daughter. Both Player choice branches are retained: one promises responsible support; the other accepts the father's teasing “defective daughter” language and provokes Hime before she says it gives her courage. They converge on Hime publicly naming herself the tofu shop's signboard girl and declaring that she will become cuter while aiming for the top of idolhood. [E14] She does not exchange the shop identity for the idol identity; she puts both in the same sentence.

An unidentified beautiful woman then buys tofu, says doing so may become difficult, and leaves. The Player finds her familiar and cold-looking; Coney suggests he might have wanted an autograph, then withholds the reason. [E15] The source establishes a teaser and information asymmetry, not the woman's name, career, or relation to Hime.

### 1.7 Debut enlarges family; costume pressure preserves the problem (`201000408`–`201000409`)

Before a streamed debut Hime again says she is not cute enough. Haru, Musubi, Rona, and the Player answer from their own first-performance experience and send her out; the performance is elided. Afterward Rona welcomes her, Hime calls her a new younger sister, and asks her to stop polite speech. The Player declares that everyone who enters Nanasta is family. Coney scales the institution's aim from one neighborhood stage to making the world absorbed in idols who exceed Seven Sisters. [E16]

The ending then refuses a completed makeover. Coney produces an extremely frilled, ribboned costume; Haru becomes the most enthusiastic participant; Hime calls it nonhuman clothing, tries to force herself into a rhetoric of resolve, and flees by bicycle. Musubi again says excess force is wrong. The Player knows she has returned to the riverbank. There Hime addresses her absent mother: becoming cute is difficult. [E17]

The joke is a meaningful boundary test. Hime chose idol work and two public performances, but she has not consented to every feminizing object or abandoned her discomfort with spectacle. Conversely, the final line does not revoke her choice. “Difficult” names an ongoing practice whose terms remain contested.

## 2. Analytical findings

### 2.1 Hime's castle is responsibility, not passivity

The picture-book princess is trapped because she waits. Hime appears to invert the figure by working, riding, delivering, feeding, and protecting. Yet her opening phrase `お城から出ないお姫さま` also identifies how indispensability can become enclosure. The shop is loved work and a real obligation; it is also the reason she declares every outside possibility impossible before asking whether the load can be shared.

The chapter's answer is neither departure from family nor romantic rescue. Nanasta members work the counter and household; father and siblings claim their own strength; the shopping district receives her new role as continuous with the old one. Hime leaves the castle because the people inside become co-agents.

### 2.2 `オレ` and “cute” are not mutually exclusive selves

Hime's language carries a stylized downtown/Edo shop register: `てやんでい`, `ベラボーめ`, `あいよ`, `らっしゃい`, `～でい`, and `オヤジ`, alongside `オレ`. The childhood memory explicitly routes `オレ` toward her father. This speech is familial, commercial, playful, and confrontational at once. It cannot be reduced to a post-bereavement disguise.

Her desire for cuteness is equally old. The child who says `オレ` wants to surprise her father by becoming cute. Adult Hime does not need to discover a hidden “real girl” beneath masculine language; she needs permission to stop treating plurality as contradiction. The chapter preserves her register through acceptance, performance, gratitude, and flight.

### 2.3 Praise hurts because it is relationally overdetermined

Hime does not simply believe she is visually unattractive. She says praise feels good and knows others praise her. The pain begins after her mother's death because the same words now recall a promise whose speaker is absent. Her `羨ましい` and `そんなんでいいのか` coexist: desire activates grief and a moral suspicion that beauty is indulgence beside care work.

This makes blunt reassurance insufficient. Coney, Haru, and the Player repeatedly call her cute before learning what the word carries. The more effective repair combines memory, practice, and labor: the Player affirms becoming rather than fixed essence; family releases her from sole responsibility; her father explicitly sends her out as both parents' daughter. Praise becomes usable only when its relationship and cost change.

### 2.4 Family is an enabling practice, not just a feeling

The opening asks for a `家族` who can make a chance possible. By the exit, family has three overlapping referents: the original household, the shopping district that helped raise Hime, and Nanasta. The chapter does not make the word meaningful through declaration alone. Each circle performs or promises labor—shopkeeping, cooking, childcare, training, encouragement, audience support—before or alongside naming kinship.

The Player's final “everyone is family” claim is therefore supported by practice but remains a future obligation. Nanasta has already repeated consent and privacy failures. Calling itself family raises the standard for responsiveness; it cannot convert managerial power into automatic permission.

### 2.5 Rona becomes the peer who recognizes an effect

Rona's investment is not a generic wish for another attractive recruit. Hime's brief `シャキっとしな` changes her state at first meeting. Rona later remembers the effect, observes sadness in Hime's own use of “cute,” identifies a shared desire for self-cultivation, and organizes material help before asking again. She recruits Hime for what Hime already does for others.

This extends B0004 without pretending Rona's own vulnerability is solved. Haru helped Rona distinguish her effect from Nicole resemblance; Rona now performs a comparable act of specific recognition for someone else. At the exit Hime calls Rona a younger sister and forbids deferential speech, giving the once-frightened recruit a chosen intimate place in another member's network.

### 2.6 “Becoming” is more adequate than qualification, but not neutral

The Player's riverbank proposition is important: cuteness can be made through strict practice and repeated public work, so present lack is not a gate. It aligns idolhood with temporal self-comparison—more beautiful than one's slightly earlier self—rather than resemblance to an ideal. This is a useful answer to both Hime's fixed disqualification and Rona's prior Nicole criterion.

It remains normatively narrow. The Player says the wish belongs to every girl, and Hime's interrupted question places him as the person who might make her cute. The narrative partially redistributes that authority to peers and family, but the final costume chase shows how easily collective encouragement becomes compulsory femininity. The chapter offers a process model, not a fully plural account of what Hime may become.

### 2.7 Consent remains local and temporal

The chapter contains at least five distinct participation states:

1. Hime refuses recruitment at Nanasta and at the shop.
2. Coney imposes a shop performance whose aftermath is shown but whose authorization is not.
3. Hime agrees to the quiet riverbank song after objecting to sudden solo performance.
4. Hime accepts idol work after demonstrated burden-sharing and explicit family support, then assents to the shopping-district live.
5. Hime completes a streamed debut but flees the proposed frilly costume.

The sequence is development, not retroactive permission. Musubi's repeated objections are an important internal ethical memory: she has experienced secret-based coercion and now names both family circumstances and excessive force. The Player improves when he listens privately and offers a low-audience rehearsal, but he also permits the first imposed test, reports Hime's private conflict to her father without shown permission, universalizes gender, and joins the machinery of rapid escalation.

### 2.8 The HoloCom supplies partial, not decisive, continuity

The exact B0005 page presents `adv_obj_0002`, a handset projecting three girl silhouettes, while B0001 presented the transferred object through `adv_obj_0026` and `adv_obj_0027`. The devices share a broad visual form, but HoloComs are explicitly common in this world. B0005 proves that a HoloCom carried by the Player can turn stored Nanasta video into a chance for a candidate to rehearse. It does not prove that this is the handed-down device, identify its special contents, or distinguish special inheritance from ordinary system function. `PRED-0002` gains a matching functional instance and remains open.

### 2.9 Textual voice and presentation limits

Hime's native text moves fluently between sales calls, rough comic challenge, compressed grief, embarrassed self-interruption, and direct gratitude without abandoning `オレ`. Her most revealing changes occur inside the same register: `オレは心のどこかで可愛くなりたい`, `あ、ありがとよ`, and the public fusion `野ノ原豆腐店の看板娘、野ノ原ヒメ`. Rona's hesitant phrasing becomes unusually sustained and declarative when she explains why Hime is needed. Musubi's `強引に誘うのはどうなのかな` and `強引すぎるのはよくない` provide explicit ethical counter-speech.
