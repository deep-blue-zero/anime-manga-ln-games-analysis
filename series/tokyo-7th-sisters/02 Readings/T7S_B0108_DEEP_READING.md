# T7S_B0108 — Wish You Were Here

## Result and entering boundary

Rona accepts a requested solo live although she first asks Haru and Sumire to join her and the Player offers to decline for lack of preparation time. Coney's rule that an idol must always be able to take a stage helps Rona articulate a real wish to try, but her work/part-time-job/rehearsal load then brings visible unsteadiness. The Player and Coney first propose easing the choreography. Rona says a solo stage might let her change rather than merely follow Nicole or lean on peers; Coney chooses to retain the routine while openly admitting that neither stopping nor urging on has a certain ethical answer. At the event a fan-owner has moved her from the expected `ビバド`-front space to a much larger mall stage. Rona freezes; Haru, Musubi and others call and address her specifically as Rona, while a crowd audibly calls for her. She enters and announces a song for joyful and sad listeners. The chapter cuts there. The beginning of a public address is not evidence of completed singing, technical success, absence of injury or durable self-confidence.

## Factual reconstruction by actor knowledge

### EV-B0108-001 — a chosen solo request under an absolute professional norm

Rona reports a request for a live at the end of next month in front of `ビバド`; Haru calls it a solo. Rona immediately asks Haru and Sumire to appear too. Coney says an idol who is wanted must be able to take the stage whether solo or in a unit. The Player points to the short lead time and suggests turning the job down. Rona says she fears failure but wants to try, invoking Coney while almost saying `ニコ…`. Sumire asks the Player to support her; he accepts the job, and Coney volunteers to train her. Rona's acceptance is explicit, but so is the social/professional pressure around it. The source gives a request and later acceptance, not a contract or verified pay/safety terms. [A:P2–27]

### EV-B0108-002 — practice receives help, then crosses a visible limit

The Player narrates daily rehearsal between Rona's part-time and idol work. Coney demands stamina for a solo stage, tells her to run another five kilometers and smile while it hurts. Friends bring company and honey-lemon food, yet Rona and Coney are too absorbed in correcting steps to greet them. Later even Coney is breathless. She calls a break; Rona argues she has not mastered the steps and can continue. The Player also asks her to pause. Rona grows unsteady at A:P58–60 and says at B:P3 she only felt dizzy and was not injured. This is evidence of an imbalance risk, not a clinical diagnosis, actual fall, measured exertion or completed five-kilometer run. [A:P29–61; B:P1–5]

### EV-B0108-003 — the supporters' safety dilemma is not solved by resolve

Coney offers to simplify the routine so Rona can focus on singing; the Player agrees and calls the present work/part-time/rehearsal schedule too hard. Rona refuses the reduction. She names Haru's recovery from failure, Musubi's search for herself and Sumire's newly serious purpose as comparisons. She still admires Nicole, but wants a solo effort of her own to change her frightened, dependent self. She fears failure and nonetheless chooses not to flee. This is more specific than merely obeying Coney's first injunction; it is also not proof the workload is safe. [B:P6–30]

Coney agrees to keep the choreography. In a separate exchange she asks the Player what an adult can do when someone pushes beyond a limit: prevent injury by stopping her, or invoke ideals and let excess continue? She says she does not know which is correct, asks to watch Rona a little longer, and the Player runs alongside Rona with a way to call Coney if something happens. Accompaniment and a contact channel mitigate isolation but are not medical evaluation or a tested workload rule. After he leaves, Coney asks absent `みんな` whether she is doing well or has grown up, recalls that an idol need not be an idol, and says she is lonely. The Player and Rona are not shown hearing that private admission. [B:P31–64]

### EV-B0108-004 — surprise scale, freeze and a caller-specific answer

At the event Coney sees that the requested `ビバド`-front live has shifted to a much bigger mall stage. The Player reports that a shopping-mall owner who is Rona's fan lent it; Coney says her taught choreography will not make adequate use of the size and sees a larger crowd than expected. Rona turns pale, retreats backstage and says she is shaking and cannot move, despite having chosen to go alone. The Player considers asking for a stage change but does not do so on page. Neither his report of the owner's fandom nor the visual background gives a full venue agreement, audience count or safety plan. [C:P1–27]

A call on the HoloCom brings Haru, Musubi and a montage of peers. Musubi acknowledges their words may not help but says they believe her. Haru names Rona's crying, attentiveness to others and effort, then asks her to hear people call specifically for `ロナ`. Musubi distinguishes this crowd expectation from expectations aimed at some other idol. Haru attributes Rona's courage to what she has cultivated, not a lucky miracle. Rona says the shaking has stopped and promises to show *her* stage. The mounted peer figures and transmitted speech show a remote supportive address, not proof every pictured member is physically at the venue. [C:P28–63]

## Interpretation and alternatives

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0108-001.** Rona's solo entry is a chosen experiment in self-authorship, not just a command from Coney: the Player offers decline, she voices fear and desire, then explains the wish in her own comparisons. Yet Coney's absolute idol norm and Rona's wish to prove she can change make consent socially loaded. [A:P11–27; B:P13–30]

Rival: accepting the offer proves she was ready and any later strain is merely the price of growth. The unsteadiness and proposed simplification defeat that shortcut. The opposite rival—that she had no agency—misses her specific refusal to be rescued from the attempt.

**ETHICAL/THEMATIC INTERPRETATION — CL-B0108-002.** The chapter asks how an adult can accompany aspiration without making either safety or ambition an unanswerable slogan. Coney explicitly rejects easy certainty; the Player shifts from forbidding the plan to running alongside with contact. This is a local accommodation under risk, not a proven safe protocol. [A:P52–61; B:P6–54]

Rival: because Coney agrees to continue, the story certifies pushing past limits as correct. Her own question denies that certainty, and no later health outcome is shown. A pure-abandonment reading also misses her break proposal, choreography offer and contact arrangement.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0108-003.** The peer call recalls B0004's separation of Nicole aspiration from Rona's personhood but works differently: it is not a performance imitation lesson or an instruction to stop being afraid. Friends name the specific care and effort already attributable to Rona; the crowd calls her name, and she reports that she can act. The evidence is her immediate transition to a public opening, not permanent cure. [C:P28–63]

Rival: the audience supplies external validation that could simply replace Nicole as another impossible standard. That risk remains, but the peers' statements are relational and specific, and the resulting action is Rona's own addressed promise. Nor do the words alone prove that a complete solo act follows.

**BOUNDED INFERENCE — CL-B0108-004.** Coney's private loneliness and half-spoken Nanasta thought expose a second unfinished arc under Rona's milestone. The B0002 maxim belongs to Coney's own unresolved adult role here; it is not advice given to Rona or a new Nicole disclosure to the Player. The interrupted clause and performance cut preserve two open futures. [B:P55–64; C:P64–80]

Rival: `いつか私が` certainly announces Coney's leaving or a succession transfer. No object or predicate completes the thought. The opposite claim that it means nothing discards the staged interruption and her preceding self-questioning, but its content stays unknown.
