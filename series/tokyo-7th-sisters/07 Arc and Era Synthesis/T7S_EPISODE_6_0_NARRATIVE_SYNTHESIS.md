# EPISODE 6.0 FINAL — narrative synthesis

## Unit result

Episode 6.0 closes the formation-era central conflict by moving self-authorship from individual declaration to institutional design. 777☆SISTERS begins as a successful but overextended handmade organization, accepts a worldwide Rainbow Dome live, and is coerced into Dream★Age partnership after its privacy, legal, venue, and industry vulnerabilities are deliberately exploited. The partner converts protection into control, numericalizes human value, surveils private deliberation, and tries to dissolve the group through fabricated scandal.

The group first fractures because its inherited principle—ask what you want—has been used too abstractly. Desire is not a universal answer, and freedom is not produced by leaving each person alone with a choice after institutions have narrowed the available world. The members return through different relational routes that preserve uncertainty, refusal, and later departure. The finale succeeds when workers, rivals, fans, managers, technology, and countervailing capital interrupt different control points. Nanasta subsequently expands its infrastructure while remaining independent.

The resulting state is not “purity beats business.” Idol work remains commerce, fantasy, bodily labor, administration, and exposure. Legitimate support is distinguished by whether it preserves the participant's ability to say no, revise a decision, and remain more than the role being produced.

## Character-state transitions

- **Haru:** moves from rejecting successor identity, through overconfident universal advice and its collapse, to a self-authored practice: she can speak truth for herself and try to push another person while the receiver owns the effect. Her injured live remains chosen and ethically costly; treatment follows.
- **Musubi:** moves from competent coordinator and watcher to recognition that “normal/right” has no meaning without an object and affected people. She accepts shared search, then makes collective refusal publicly legible.
- **Rona:** turns Nicole from an external standard into a horizon she can move beyond. She accepts change and conflict among desires without making either equivalent to betrayal.
- **Hime:** separates group-made joy from a lifelong idol vocation. She returns without pretending grief is solved or promising permanent participation.
- **Momoka:** names Nanasta management as her own desire and later trains managers. Her answer is not withdrawal from institutional responsibility but distribution of it.
- **Sumire:** admits love for the Player while refusing to make romance the whole reason for work. She begins business/design study so future adulthood has material routes.
- **Sui:** states the fear of being left behind and receives present accompaniment rather than a false guarantee of changelessness.
- **Shizuka:** converts qualification anxiety into accompaniment across uncertainty; the “cold wall” is crossed by action, not erased by proof.
- **Susu:** stays by choice, respects departure, and keeps Nanasta available for return. Her private collapse preserves the cost of that stance.
- **Sawara, Kajika, Shinju:** expose reciprocal overprotection. The younger sisters make time-bounded choices; Sawara trusts capacities she helped form rather than substituting her decision.
- **Player:** recognizes both organizational neglect and paternal authorship. He moves from sole shield to one participant who states desire, accepts possible loss, and builds shared infrastructure.
- **Gotoda:** is established as a harmed child, coercive operator, and coerced subordinate. His final refusal opens revision but does not absolve violence or close repair.
- **Natsumi:** exits an abusive competition by bounding idol work as a job, later returns to Nanasta as a trainee. The return does not invalidate the earlier refusal or prove Haru's words universally correct.

## Institutional and world-state result

Dream★Age's “invisible hand” is a distributed apparatus of media exchange, legal leverage, venue control, scheduling, surveillance, contract penalty, and employment access. Its power works by making a manufactured crisis resemble ordinary social necessity. The chairman's doctrine names the aim directly: replace unstable free action with controllable stimulation.

Nanasta's counterform is also distributed. Its emergency survival requires rival artists, workers able to stop machinery, fans as relays, managers, recovered technology, and Misonoo capital. The episode therefore does not establish that sincerity alone defeats institutions. It establishes that material support becomes legitimate when it protects refusal and plural authorship rather than purchasing them.

The expanded Nanasta is a qualified answer. More managers and trainees reduce single-person dependence; member teaching and study circulate knowledge. Financial scarcity, formal ownership, safeguarding, pay, privacy, contract review, and the exact voting/revision procedure remain unresolved.
