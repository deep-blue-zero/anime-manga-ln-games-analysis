# EPISODE 0.0 narrative synthesis

## Unit result

EPISODE 0.0 is a six-part memory album of the original Seven Sisters while active, with one internal childhood flashback. Five episodes isolate a member-centered backstage problem; the sixth gathers the group around a proposed HoloCom game and supplies a possible childhood prehistory for their smile ideal. The unit is read after EPISODE 4.0 AXiS but occurs earlier diegetically. It enlarges the historical record without replacing the later states already established.

The six memories form a backstage counterarchive. They contrast public images with tracksuits, money talk, social freezing, teasing, work discipline, and domestic play. The contrast does not expose the public identities as fraudulent. It demonstrates that idol personae are deliberately made modes operating alongside other real modes. The unit's recurring question is who authors the transition: the performer, a peer, a camera, an audience, or a commercial institution.

## Causal and narrative sequence

### 1. Mana makes the group available to home

Mana records a birthday video letter for her father. She introduces Rui, Kurt, Memoru, Mito, and Nicole by moving between their public profiles and private group knowledge. Nicole supplies material from outside the frame, takes over her own introduction, and forces Mana toward self-exposure with a shelved gravure photograph. Mana ends by saying she is happy.

The letter establishes the group's backstage life as familial evidence. It also establishes the camera as contested territory: a loving record can contain a refused image, and no single participant owns the finished representation.

### 2. Rui coordinates play, broadcast, and discipline

Rui leaves an elaborately narrated card game to host her radio program. In costume she inhabits the princely role and offers care to unseen listeners. Nicole and Memoru send prank mail exposing her tracksuit habits and accusing her of escaping after a win. Rui contains her anger on air, returns during a song break, and distinguishes play from work while disciplining them.

Her public role is crafted but continuous with her backstage ethic. Composure is skilled responsibility rather than evidence of emotional falsity. Intimates can read what strangers cannot, and can misuse that access.

### 3. Nicole tries to train Mito's access

Nicole says Mito's cold interview manner hurts other workers. Mito defends songs against explanatory surplus but accepts that she may have injured the interviewer. A mock interview shifts from forced romantic interpretation to first-kiss extraction. Mito reveals that Nicole suddenly kissed her in middle school to test a cliché; Nicole barely remembers.

Nicole then demands a smile. Mito cannot deliberately produce one, but reciprocal tickling grows into ensemble play, and Mito closes with a visible smile toward Nicole. Relational expression succeeds where compelled display fails. The result does not make a private smile a debt owed to public media.

### 4. Memoru defends authored commerce

Memoru records a fan-club video in explicit `営業モード`. She treats the idol/fan relation as an exchange of dream for money and calculates its value. Nicole asks for a supposedly raw Memoru and imposes comedy as the form authenticity should take. Memoru refuses and follows her own plan: a secret drink demonstration that becomes ensemble chaos.

The episode makes performance compatible with agency. Memoru's commercial mode belongs to her because she designed and endorses it; backstage access is not inherently more truthful when another person commands it.

### 5. Kurt acts when the demand is not self-display

After Nicole reveals Kurt's freezing before a young fan, the group sends her to buy snacks as exposure practice. Kurt freezes before the clerk. Nicole stages a robbery; Kurt immediately intervenes, then apologizes for the kick and introduces herself.

The crisis does not create agency from nothing. It changes attention from self-presentation to a concrete wrong. Kurt is shy and courageous in different relational structures. Nicole's deceptive method reveals that distinction while imposing unchosen risk on Kurt and the clerk.

### 6. Mito remembers a different win condition

A proposed Misonoo Electronics HoloCom game prompts the adult group to imagine themselves as game characters. Mito remembers asking child Nicole whether peace achieved by killing minor enemies could be peace for everyone. Nicole proposes making the enemies and demon king smile with Mito's song and forming an “absolutely invincible party.”

Adult Mito wonders whether this became the Seven Sisters; Nicole refuses or cannot confirm the origin. The sequence distributes authorship: Mito supplies the ethical objection, Nicole collective form and confidence, and the eventual group adds four other lives. Mito finally addresses the recipient and offers Nicole as someone who may make them a little happier.

## Character and relationship state

### Nicole

Nicole is a catalytic leader rather than a solitary origin. She repeatedly turns static situations into shared action and articulates the party form of the universal-smile ideal. She also treats intimacy as permission: she scripts Mito's description, exposes Mana's photograph, invades Rui's broadcast, minimizes a sudden childhood kiss, and stages a false robbery. Care and coercion are not alternative diagnoses; both are recurring features of her practice.

### Mito

Mito protects artistic autonomy, recognizes interpersonal consequence, resists compulsory expression, and participates in reciprocal play. Her childhood objection supplies the ethical premise of the group's founding image. As adult narrator, she is a custodian of Nicole's memory rather than merely its object.

### Mana

Mana mediates between parental home and chosen group. Her gentle idiom contains deliberate structure and interpretive authority. She can be happy in the group while objecting to a specific exposure.

### Rui

Rui's prince, tracksuit player, radio host, and disciplinary senior are continuous roles. Her competence lies in changing register without abandoning a stable care-and-work ethic.

### Memoru

Memoru understands persona as commercial labor and asserts the right to control its production. Calculation, affection, anger, and comic failure all belong to her; none alone is the hidden authentic self.

### Kurt

Kurt's social freezing is context-bound. Moral orientation can support decisive action where self-presentation fails. One successful encounter does not establish a permanent cure.

- Mana identifies Misonoo Electronics as the developer of the first HoloCom unit; Memoru describes the system as globally ubiquitous.

- Mana's father is the intended receiver of the birthday letter; receipt is not confirmed.

## Continuities and revisions

EPISODE 0.0 strengthens the later claim that the Seven Sisters' smile ideal was historically real. It was not invented after dissolution to justify inheritance. It also revises any singular-founder account: the decisive childhood fantasy is co-authored by Mito and Nicole, and adult group life is produced by all six.

The unit also sharpens later critiques of universal consolation. Nicole's wish to include even the designated enemy begins from a serious refusal of exclusion. Its repeated coercive implementation shows why successors must retain the aim while bounding claims over other people's interior lives.

Later Coney/Nicole analysis is recontextualized, not overwritten. The original group gave Nicole intimacy, collaborators, discipline, resistance, and joy; it was not only an abstract monument or a unilateral expression of her will. Her power to enliven people coexisted with boundary failures long before dissolution.

## Closeout

EPISODE 0.0 turns a legendary predecessor group back into coworkers and childhood friends. Its archive is valuable because it does not purify them. Public selves are made rather than fake; private selves are plural rather than final; intimacy is sustaining rather than automatically consensual; and Nicole's ideal is powerful without being solely hers. What survives is not an invulnerable icon but a contested, transmissible practice of making a party where the game's assigned enemies might also count.
