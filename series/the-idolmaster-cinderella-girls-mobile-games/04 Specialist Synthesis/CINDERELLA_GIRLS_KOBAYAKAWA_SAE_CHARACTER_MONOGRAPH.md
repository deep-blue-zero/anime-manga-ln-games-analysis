# THE IDOLM@STER CINDERELLA GIRLS — Kobayakawa Sae Character Monograph

## Status, purpose, and evidentiary boundary

This document reconstructs **Kobayakawa Sae / 小早川紗枝** from the preserved mobile-game evidence corpus. Its purpose is not to summarize the recurring “Kyoto girl” motif, list her traditional accomplishments, or flatten her into an archetype of graceful femininity. It attempts to model the person underneath and within that presentation: her motivational structure, work ethic, social intelligence, stubbornness, care behavior, family history, relationships, speech, self-presentation, ambitions, and the rules by which she tends to act when placed in unfamiliar situations.

The governing evidence packet contains 329 inventoried candidate source objects, 203 source objects with Sae's participation directly confirmed, 203 recovered complete scenes/dialogue sets, 2,244 derived Sae utterances, and 105 objective interaction counterparts. Deresute text is **COMPLETE_AGAINST_INVENTORY** for the final released categorized corpus. Mobage contributes 46 preserved card/profile dialogue objects and three complete Refresh Room scenes, but remains structurally incomplete: absence from the current Mobage packet cannot establish that a line, event, birthday scene, Idol Produce episode, Memory Episode, or other historical object never existed.

This monograph therefore treats repeated Deresute behavior across many years and content classes as the strongest basis for personality reconstruction, uses preserved Mobage material as longitudinal corroboration where possible, and marks claims that could still be altered by better Mobage recovery as provisional.

The 2015 anime is deliberately excluded. A later cross-media artifact can ask how the anime selects, compresses, or reinterprets Sae; it does not govern this mobile-game character model.

## Executive thesis

The most important correction to a superficial reading of Sae is that **grace is not passivity**.

Sae presents herself through Kyoto speech, kimono, Japanese dance, tea, flower arrangement, seasonal imagery, hospitality, controlled manners, and an almost continuously soft social surface. She can look like a character whose primary function is to embody inherited tradition. Yet her foundational motive for becoming an idol is almost the opposite of passive traditionalism: after her parents tell her, in effect, that a Kyoto woman need only be intelligible within Kyoto, she decides that this is boring and wants to test how far the arts she has inherited can carry her in the wider world. Her first important act is therefore a quiet act of **defiance**.

That defiance never develops into rejection of Kyoto. Instead, Sae spends her career proving that inherited form can be **portable, revisable, combinable, and self-authored**. Japanese dance teaches her body control for idol choreography; kimono knowledge becomes a professional resource for other idols; Kyoto aesthetics become material for modern staging; the same girl initially embarrassed by Western dance positions eventually treats idol dance and Japanese dance as different forms sharing an underlying physical axis. In later material she explicitly enlarges her goal from “proving herself outside Kyoto” to making what she has learned work anywhere in Japan and eventually the world (`DERESTE:STORY:3013205`).

Her other major correction is temperamental. Sae's softness coexists with **considerable stubbornness, competitiveness, social acuity, and strategic force**. She stays after lessons until she is personally satisfied and describes herself not as nobly diligent but as a `頑固者` who cannot stop until the result meets her own standard (`DERESTE:STORY:5102903`). She can assess a group's interpersonal structure with startling bluntness, identify who should lead and why, notice insecurity that others are hiding, read sulking as a request for belonging, or use exquisitely polite language to corner predatory adults without alarming the friend she is protecting. Producer's early judgment that she is `したたか`—not merely gentle but resilient, shrewd, and harder to push around than her appearance suggests—is repeatedly borne out.

The resulting character can be summarized as follows:

> **Sae turns form into agency.** She uses etiquette, beauty, ritual, dialect, craft, patience, hospitality, and traditional training not as walls that restrict her but as tools through which she chooses how to enter the world.

Her mature identity is therefore neither “traditional Kyoto girl” nor “modern idol who escaped tradition.” It is a synthesis: **a Kyoto woman who insists that tradition belongs to her strongly enough that she can take it somewhere new.**

# 1. The surface archetype and the first major correction

Sae's visual and verbal construction is extraordinarily coherent. Her earliest card material foregrounds Kyoto speech, habitual kimono, Japanese dance, tea, the vocabulary of `はんなり`, and imagery of flowers, moonlight, stages as `お座敷`, and idol life as a new kind of `花街`. Her first-person pronoun is `うち`; she calls Producer `プロデューサーはん`; her greetings and requests are saturated with Kyoto forms such as `おくれやす`, `よろしゅう`, `おおきに`, `堪忍`, `どす`, and `～まへん`.

The archetypal reading is obvious: elegant, old-fashioned, gentle, feminine, perhaps sheltered.

The corpus itself keeps only part of that.

Sae *is* culturally rooted. She does not secretly dislike Kyoto, resent traditional arts, or regard her upbringing as something to be discarded. She genuinely enjoys kimono, tea, Japanese dance, aesthetics, seasonal ritual, and the feel of Kyoto streets. But the character's first central conflict arises because **other people interpret those roots more narrowly than she does**.

In Memorial Commu 1 (`DERESTE:STORY:3013201`), her parents tell her that `京女は、京で通じれば、それでええ`—a Kyoto woman only needs to be understood in Kyoto. Sae's reaction is not a dramatic rebellion. It is a deceptively mild `そんなんおもんないな～て、思いまへんか～？`: isn't that boring?

That sentence is an excellent early key to her. Sae's opposition rarely needs to become loud in order to become real.

She wants to know whether the arts she has learned since childhood—Japanese dance, song, flower arrangement, tea ceremony—will work in a broader world. She wants to become known and then demonstrate to her parents that their daughter is someone who can be presented anywhere without embarrassment. She calls this motivation `うちの小さな意地`, her little stubborn pride.

So the apparently conservative character begins with a **universalizing impulse**. She values inheritance enough to test it outside the conditions that produced it.

That tension remains productive for the rest of her career.

# 2. Origin motive: curiosity, pride, and the desire to make inheritance travel

## 2.1 Curiosity is real, but insufficient on its own

The base-card Affection Max dialogue describes her entrance into idol work as initially close to sightseeing: she wanted to peek into the idol world and see what it was like. She jokes that she arrived as a `一見さん` and somehow became a regular.

Episode Commu `DERESTE:STORY:2100023` adds the deeper layer. Kyoto itself was not intolerable. She says she could have remained there. The problem was **unused possibility**. She had accumulated years of arts and discipline, and the idol world looked like a “綺羅星” world in which those abilities might become something else.

Memorial Commu 1 then reveals the personal edge: after the disagreement with her parents, testing her arts in the wider world becomes a small act of pride and proof.

Thus Sae's foundational motivation contains three elements:

1. **curiosity** — what is this glittering world like?
2. **translation** — can the things I already know work here?
3. **self-assertion** — can I prove that the boundaries other people have placed around me are too small?

These remain more durable than a simple fame motive.

## 2.2 Approval matters, but not as obedience

Sae clearly wants her parents' recognition. This should not be confused with wanting merely to do what they tell her.

The early conflict exists precisely because she acts against their preferred boundary. Later `美に入り彩を穿つ` material shows that she delays returning home because she believes she has not yet earned the right to face them. The anxiety is self-imposed: she left saying she would prove herself, so returning while she still feels “unfinished” seems like failure.

Shuko's intervention helps her understand that reconciliation does not need to wait for perfect achievement (`DERESTE:STORY:5102904`). Sae returns, reconciles, and eventually performs a triumphant homecoming LIVE.

By Memorial Commu 5 (`DERESTE:STORY:3013205`), her parents are openly supportive. Yet the disappearance of parental opposition does not end Sae's ambition. Her goal expands beyond them. She now wants what she learned before idoling *and* what she learned as an idol to work everywhere—outside Kyoto, outside the region, ultimately outside Japan.

This establishes that parental proof was a catalyst, not the terminal purpose.

# 3. Work ethic: soft presentation, hard internal standard

Sae's work ethic is easy to underestimate because she rarely performs labor through aggression or visible desperation. She often sounds relaxed even while describing intense effort.

The evidence repeatedly shows a person with a **high internal finishing threshold**.

In `DERESTE:STORY:5102903`, early Haguromo Komachi history reveals that after a joint lesson, Sae volunteers to clean up and lets Shuko go home. Shuko later returns and discovers her still practicing alone. Sae is not merely rehearsing because a trainer ordered her to. She continues until she can personally accept the result.

When Shuko later calls this `根性`, Sae resists the flattering interpretation:

> `うちが、自分に納得できひんと気がすまへん頑固者なだけや。`

She is simply a stubborn person who cannot settle until she is satisfied with herself.

This is one of the strongest personality claims in the corpus because it explains behavior that otherwise looks like generic “proper Japanese diligence.” Sae's persistence is tied to **self-judgment**. She does not merely want to avoid letting authority down; she needs the work to meet a standard she can inhabit without embarrassment.

Several recurring behaviors follow from this:

- private practice after official lessons;
- embarrassment at mistakes followed by immediate retry rather than withdrawal;
- careful physical technique;
- desire for a “perfect” LIVE when working with a group (`DERESTE:STORY:5100502`);
- concern with whether acquired form has truly become embodied—`体が覚えるまでが手習い`;
- long-term transfer of Japanese dance principles into idol movement;
- willingness to endure discomfort when she has decided that doing so means something.

There is a shadow side. Sae can turn pride into concealment. In the 2022 Kyoto drama material (`DERESTE:STORY:5304102`), she notices other people's physical needs while trying to hide her own fatigue because being the local Kyoto girl who complains about Kyoto heat would, in her mind, look bad. Fumika notices. Sae admits that this is, in a sense, forcing herself, but asks to be allowed to keep the face-saving fiction a little longer.

The same source therefore demonstrates both strength and limitation:

> **Sae's discipline helps her endure; her pride can make her endure past the point where transparency would be wiser.**

This becomes important when modeling her care for other people, because she is often better at detecting others' concealed strain than acknowledging her own.

# 4. Tradition as living technique rather than museum identity

Sae's most important thematic function within the games is not simply “represent Kyoto.” It is to explore what happens when embodied tradition enters a medium that demands constant transformation.

## 4.1 Japanese dance gives her a base—and a problem

Her first idol lessons are a literal collision of bodily grammars. Sae enters dance training confident because she has practiced Japanese dance for years. Instead she discovers that idol choreography asks her to open her legs, move in unfamiliar ways, and wear clothing that exposes movements her traditional training taught her to avoid. She is embarrassed enough to turn red and get corrected by the trainer.

Her response is characteristic. She does not conclude that idol dance is vulgar, nor that Japanese dance is useless. She decides that if she has entered this world she must learn its grammar.

Later card dialogue explicitly revises the initial separation: Japanese dance and idol dance are different, but both require a stable physical core. The skill transfer becomes real rather than decorative.

`DERESTE:STORY:5102905` likewise has Sae explain that the things she learned—beautiful movement, role creation, dressing—now function every day in idol work. The traditional arts are not a costume stored behind the new career. They are **infrastructure**.

## 4.2 She is culturally rooted without being culturally defensive

Sae is often delighted by Western clothes, unusual costumes, contemporary performance, unfamiliar locations, modern food, theater, film, and other people's styles. Her recurring response to difference is curiosity followed by incorporation.

That matters because a weaker version of this archetype might protect traditional identity by treating novelty as contamination. Sae rarely does that. Her preferred move is closer to:

> What is this new form asking of me, what do I already know that can help, and what will I become if I learn both?

This is why the 2018 Haguromo Komachi material is so central. Shuko has a very different relationship to Kyoto: modern, casual, initially alienated from its expectations. Sae is deeply attached to its forms. Yet the unit works precisely because Kyoto is large enough to contain both of them.

By the climax (`DERESTE:STORY:5102906`), they formulate tradition not as a fixed script but as one layer in a continuing act of creation. Their history and Kyoto shape them; their own hearts determine the new colors they add. Sae goes so far as to propose that their present should be carved into Kyoto's history too.

The gesture is ambitious. Tradition is not only something one receives from the past. **If one takes it seriously enough, one is allowed to contribute to what future people will call tradition.**

## 4.3 Her mature ambition is translocal

Memorial Commu 5 makes the trajectory explicit. Sae observes that beauty and `雅` exist outside Kyoto; therefore, what she values must not be limited to working in Kyoto. She wants the things she cultivated before idoling and the things she cultivated afterward to become valid anywhere.

The sequence ends with a role reversal toward Producer: after being led this far, she says the next turn is hers—`次はうちが手を引く番`.

Her growth is therefore not abandonment of a teacher/student structure but **reciprocity and self-direction**.

# 5. Social intelligence: the woman behind the soft surface

Sae is one of the clearest examples in Cinderella Girls of why gentle affect should not be mistaken for weak social judgment.

## 5.1 She watches people closely

Story/Event material repeatedly makes Sae an observer of small interpersonal facts:

- who has practiced late;
- who is too nervous to lead;
- who is overcompensating through pride;
- who needs food, water, clothing help, or rest;
- who is sulking because she feels excluded;
- who will finish what she starts even if she looks fragile;
- who is pretending not to be tired;
- when a friend is lying badly;
- when a supposedly harmless social interaction has predatory intent.

`DERESTE:STORY:5100502` is unusually explicit. Asked why she recommends Kanako as leader, Sae says she has confidence in her ability to read people—`人を見る目`—and then proceeds to analyze the group one by one. Sachiko wants authority but is too proud and will make Chieri/Kanako spend energy managing her. Chieri is diligent but too anxious to have leadership imposed on her. Mayu is kind, but Producer would suffer. Kanako lacks confidence but has more strengths than she recognizes.

The joke about her “Kyoto-trained aesthetic eye” does not diminish the accuracy. Her choices are functionally correct.

Later in the same project, Sae identifies Sachiko's hostility not as mere narcissism but as hurt over being excluded. She does not indulge the behavior: she tells her to stop grumbling, points out that being late was her own fault, rejects the ridiculous unit names, and then correctly names the underlying emotion—loneliness.

This combination is important:

> **Sae is empathic without being permissive.**

She can see the wound and still insist that the behavior around it change.

## 5.2 Her preferred influence is lateral, not command-centered

Sae explicitly says she is not naturally the type to stand in front and pull everyone behind her. This should not be mistaken for inability to lead.

Her preferred influence is often **social architecture** rather than command. She proposes the person who should lead, reduces friction around that person, notices emotional bottlenecks, manages troublesome personalities, and creates small rituals that make the group more functional.

She is therefore a strong example of *distributed leadership*: important to the group's shape without always needing the formal center.

When circumstances demand directness, however, she can become commanding very quickly.

# 6. `したたかさ`: strategic softness, controlled sharpness, and protective force

Producer's early description of Sae as `したたか` in the Haguromo Komachi flashback is particularly apt. The word should not be reduced to “cunning” in a negative sense. In Sae it describes a capacity to **survive, maneuver, and exert force without abandoning the social form she prefers**.

## 6.1 The smile does not mean surrender

Sae's sharpest remarks are often delivered through exactly the same soft register as her most hospitable ones. This creates a distinctive social effect: the listener may receive the criticism only after the sentence has already landed.

The early Sachiko scenes use this comically. She responds to boastfulness with questions that sound innocent but expose the ridiculous premise. She alludes to the saying about fools liking high places without needing to state the insult. She describes Sachiko as “cute” while redirecting or containing her. The linguistic surface stays warm; the propositional content can be merciless.

This is not evidence that every polite Sae line contains secret hostility—the familiar stereotype of Kyoto speech as perpetual coded insult would badly distort her. Most of her politeness is simply politeness. But **when she wants to apply pressure, she does not need to change dialects or become loud to do it**.

## 6.2 The 2024 hidden-camera scene: politeness as counterattack

Business Commu `DERESTE:STORY:7213037` is perhaps the cleanest demonstration of mature Sae under threat.

Yukari, after successfully imitating Sae's speech for a radio drama, is mistaken by men who are covertly filming and encouraging her into increasingly embarrassing behavior. Sae arrives, immediately recognizes the camera and the situation, and does not panic. She enters by reversing the imitation—introducing herself as Mizumoto Yukari—then plays along with the fiction of a harmless TV prank long enough to give Yukari a face-saving interpretation.

Her later lines are extremely polite and extremely threatening at the same time. She asks which television station the men belong to, says she is sad they did not invite her from the beginning, expresses great interest in seeing everything they filmed, and brings Producer and staff into the situation.

She never needs to say “I know what you were doing and you are trapped.” Everyone involved understands it.

Afterward she refuses to burden Yukari with the ugliness of the encounter. She returns immediately to ordinary companionship and suggests they go out together.

This scene crystallizes several features at once:

- fast situational inference;
- protective orientation;
- ability to weaponize public manners without abandoning them;
- face-saving care for the person she protects;
- controlled confrontation rather than impulsive aggression;
- confidence that social rules can be turned against people violating them.

Sae's grace is therefore not merely aesthetic. It can become **defensive technology**.

# 7. Caretaking and hospitality: attention as affection

Sae's care for other people is unusually concrete. She is less likely to announce abstract devotion than to notice a practical need and quietly handle it.

Typical actions include:

- fixing hair;
- dressing or redressing others in kimono;
- serving tea and sweets;
- cleaning shared spaces;
- waiting so someone does not return alone;
- noticing insufficient hydration;
- moderating younger idols' workload;
- finding food;
- straightening clothing;
- encouraging rest;
- learning enough about people's habits to anticipate what they will need.

In the 2022 drama shoot (`DERESTE:STORY:5304102`), Sae notices at breakfast that Fumika barely drank water. Later in the Kyoto heat she identifies dehydration risk before Fumika herself responds properly and hands her a sports drink. Fumika explicitly recognizes that Sae has been watching the group closely.

This should not be sentimentalized as limitless maternal instinct. Sae herself calls herself `世話焼き`, and other characters tease her about it, but her caregiving has boundaries and pride. She can expect competence, scold people who create preventable trouble, or refuse to infantilize them.

The 2024 maid-themed material (`DERESTE:CARD:101329` and `DERESTE:STORY:2101329`) deepens the theme through reciprocity. Sae remembers that her family home had household help who quietly made it possible for her to concentrate on lessons. As a child she thanked them without fully understanding the emotional value of being seen. Performing a service role lets her discover how powerful even a small acknowledgment can feel.

She converts that recognition into a mature principle toward Producer: she dislikes a one-way relation in which she only receives support. `してもらいっぱなしは、嫌`—she does not want to be helped forever without becoming someone capable of helping in return.

This is a central developmental direction throughout her corpus:

> **being cared for → understanding care → becoming deliberately reciprocal.**

# 8. Speech and linguistic identity

## 8.0 Evidentiary note

Sae is an unusually voice-dependent character, but the current packet has **no acquired audio**. There are 148 mapped audio containers, yet `audio_completeness` is `NOT_ACQUIRED`. This section therefore distinguishes two questions:

1. **linguistic/performed speech model** — strongly reconstructable from 2,244 Japanese line records;
2. **acoustic voice model** — pitch, breathiness, tempo, precise intonation, vowel quality, and actress-specific delivery remain open until audio is acquired and directly reviewed.

The source supports a very detailed model of what Sae says and how dialogue is orthographically/stylistically framed. It does not yet support fine-grained phonetic claims.

## 8.1 Kyoto speech is identity, not an accessory

The base card begins with Sae acknowledging that her `京ことば` will not come out of her speech and hoping the listener will think it elegant. This is not a temporary introductory gimmick. Across the complete Deresute line packet, the dialect remains one of the most stable components of her identity.

A simple string audit over the current 2,244-line derivative illustrates its density. These are raw occurrence counts, not linguistic-token analysis, but they demonstrate scale:

- `はん`: 965 occurrences across 825 lines;
- `うち`: 763 occurrences across 698 lines;
- `どす`: 393 occurrences across 370 lines;
- `まへん`: 206 occurrences across 201 lines;
- `さかい`: 143 occurrences across 143 lines;
- `おくれやす`: 63 occurrences;
- `おおきに`: 49;
- `どすえ`: 42;
- `よろしゅう`: 31;
- `どない`: 31;
- `いけず`: 22;
- `はんなり`: 19.

The point is not that Sae mechanically inserts catchphrases. The density establishes that the dialect is the **default grammar of self**, not a special-mode affectation.

This becomes plot-relevant in `Secret Mirage`. While acting a role that demands standard Japanese, Sae becomes distracted by how difficult the standard register feels (`DERESTE:STORY:5303702-5303703`). Her Kyoto speech is therefore represented as embodied enough that switching away from it increases cognitive load.

A generic “refined Japanese girl” voice would miss the character.

## 8.2 Pronoun and honorific ecology

Sae's `うち` is stable and important. It keeps even intimate or serious speech locally marked. She frequently attaches `はん` not only to Producer but to peers: `周子はん`, `ゆかりはん`, `幸子はん`, `智絵里はん`, and so on.

This creates a curious combination of closeness and courtesy. The honorific does not necessarily indicate distance; with Shuko and Yukari, `はん` survives into extremely intimate friendship. The listener therefore should not infer “formal relationship” merely from the honorific surface.

Producer is almost categorically `プロデューサーはん`. The stable address helps define their relationship: even when the content becomes teasing, romantic-coded, or personally dependent, Sae usually keeps the relational title intact. Intimacy appears through syntax, topic, physical metaphor, and implication rather than through abandonment of address conventions.

## 8.3 Sentence endings and invitation rather than command

Sae often builds requests and proposals through forms that invite participation:

- `～まひょ` / “shall we?”;
- `～くれはります？`;
- `～しておくれやす`;
- `～やろか`;
- `～どすか`;
- `～ますえ`.

In the line corpus, `まひょ` appears more than 150 times and `やろか` more than 100. These are rough string counts, but the pattern matches the scenes: Sae prefers to pull people into an action rather than issue naked imperatives.

Crucially, **semantic force and grammatical softness are separable**. When Sachiko needs to stop sulking, Sae can say `ぶつぶつ言わんと、はっきりし`. When Shuko needs to go home, she simply arranges the intervention and tells her to move. When malicious staff need to be cornered, she invites them politely to explain themselves.

The social power of Sae's voice often comes from this mismatch:

> the sentence sounds hospitable; the available choices have already narrowed.

## 8.4 Orthographic domestication of modern loanwords

One of Sae's most distinctive textual signatures is the frequent rendering of modern entertainment vocabulary in hiragana rather than standard katakana.

A raw-string count in the 2,244-line packet finds approximately:

- `あいどる`: 115 vs `アイドル`: 1;
- `すてーじ`: 29 vs `ステージ`: 0;
- `れっすん`: 21 vs `レッスン`: 0;
- `ゆにっと`: 31 vs `ユニット`: 1;
- `だんす`: 12 vs `ダンス`: 0.

Producer remains overwhelmingly written `プロデューサー`, so this is not simple global kana normalization.

The effect is subtle but analytically useful. Foreign modern terms are made to look visually softer and more assimilated into Sae's traditional-register text. Her speech literally **domesticates the modern idol lexicon into her own written voice**.

This aligns perfectly with the larger character architecture: she does not choose between old and new; she pulls the new into a form she can inhabit.

## 8.5 `ふふ` and the smile in text

Forms such as `ふふ`, `ふふっ`, and `うふふ` appear constantly. A raw audit finds more than 200 `ふふ` occurrences. Without audio, it would be wrong to assign every instance identical acoustic delivery. Functionally, however, the laugh often serves as punctuation.

It can mark:

- uncomplicated amusement;
- teasing;
- smoothing after criticism;
- affection;
- self-consciousness;
- a warning delivered pleasantly;
- delight in a small aesthetic detail;
- confidence before a playful trap.

The important generative rule is that Sae frequently **smiles around social force**. A writer who removes that cushioning will make her too blunt; a writer who interprets every smile as gentleness will make her too weak.

## 8.6 `いけず`: a relationally flexible keyword

`いけず` is one of Sae's most useful speech markers because its meaning depends strongly on relationship and context.

With Producer, it often becomes flirtatious or embarrassed deflection: he has placed her in a suggestive situation, praised her too directly, or understood the implication she hoped to leave indirect, so she calls him `いけず` rather than plainly naming the intimacy.

With Shuko, it can become mutual teasing and recognition of their shared Kyoto social play. In the Haguromo Komachi retrospective, Sae jokes that Shuko's meanness has rubbed off on her.

In adversarial situations, the same cultural resource can become sharper.

A generative model should therefore not treat `いけず` as a random dialect catchphrase. It is often **Sae's pressure-release word when another person has made the subtext too visible**.

## 8.7 Directness appears when stakes override elegance

Sae is indirect by preference, not by incapacity.

Under high stakes she can become remarkably plain. She tells Shuko that waiting for emotional readiness will accomplish nothing and they are going home. She tells Sachiko to stop grumbling. She tells overheated Fumika to drink. She tells Yukari why she trusts her after years of friendship. She tells Producer that the next stage of the journey is one she intends to lead.

The shift is diagnostically useful:

> **When Sae stops ornamenting and states the conclusion, the situation usually matters.**

## 8.8 Relationship-conditioned registers

### Producer

Baseline remains polite and teasing: `プロデューサーはん`, soft questions, requests, invitations, mock complaints about `いけず`. As affection rises, she adds physical and domestic imagery—walking together, tea, hair, hand-holding, mutual care, the fantasy of a quiet future—without usually dropping the polite Kyoto shell.

### Shuko

With Shuko she becomes faster, more reciprocal, more willing to tease, and less protective of a perfectly graceful image. Their dialogue has a ping-pong quality. Sae is comfortable being called stubborn, being baited, pushing back, and arranging matters for Shuko's own good without permission.

### Yukari

With Yukari the register is unusually domestic and tender. They exchange clothing expertise, hair care, massage, food, private secrets, and eventually explicit statements of chosen long-term friendship. Sae can be playful but is less adversarial than with Shuko. The relationship supports an everyday, mutual-care register.

### Younger/anxious peers

Sae often reduces pressure rather than increasing it. She names strengths, notices hidden exertion, gives concrete help, and uses a soft tone to make correction survivable. Yet she does not falsely reassure; she is capable of telling somebody where their behavior is creating problems.

### Threatening adults or bad-faith interlocutors

Her manners become more exact, not less. Questions become traps. Polite inclusion becomes containment. She preserves face for allies while removing it from the aggressor only as much as necessary.

## 8.9 Generative speech model

A plausible Sae line should usually satisfy several of these constraints:

1. Use `うち` naturally for first person.
2. Maintain Kyoto morphology throughout ordinary speech rather than decorating standard Japanese with one catchphrase.
3. Use `はん` relationally, especially with named peers and Producer.
4. Prefer invitation, suggestion, observation, or question before blunt command.
5. Allow gentle laughter to cushion social force.
6. Let teasing contain real information.
7. Use aesthetic or seasonal metaphor when she is reflective, but do not make every sentence poetic.
8. Preserve her willingness to become sharply direct when someone is in danger, behaving unfairly, or forcing a decision.
9. Render much modern idol vocabulary in the established hiragana style where source practice supports it.
10. Do not write her as permanently cryptic. Sae's indirectness is a tool, not an inability to say what she means.

### Do not

Do not write Sae as:

- a generic Kansai comic speaker;
- an emotionless traditional doll;
- a perpetually passive yamato-nadeshiko stereotype;
- someone whose every polite phrase hides an insult;
- someone who rejects modern culture;
- someone who cannot lead because she dislikes formal leadership;
- someone whose flirtation means the game has canonically resolved a romance;
- someone who automatically prioritizes harmony over principle.

Her most recognizable voice comes from the coexistence of **soft form, sharp perception, and stubborn interiority**.

---

# 9. Producer: teacher, witness, co-conspirator, and eventually someone she intends to lead

Sae's Producer relationship begins in apprenticeship language. She asks him to teach her the ways of idol life, calls him a possible `師範`, and asks him to cultivate her like a Kyoto flower.

The dependency is real but never total. Memorial Commu 1 shows her arriving in Tokyo under her own initiative. Producer does not invent the desire; he gives it institutional shape.

## 9.1 Confidence as the early relational need

The base trained card identifies a specific weakness: when it matters, Sae tends to take one step backward because her mother taught that restraint as proper Kyoto-woman conduct. Idol work demands the opposite—visibility. She asks Producer to help her gain enough confidence to shine rather than defer.

The request is significant. Sae does not ask him to replace her judgment. She asks him to help her **occupy more space**.

## 9.2 Intimacy through practical exchange

Much of their closeness develops through mundane embodied acts rather than declarations: tea, dressing, hair, lessons, walking, travel, rehearsal, physical fatigue, food, and hand-holding imagery. Sae often flirts by making an implication and then retreating into `いけず` when Producer recognizes it.

Some card material is openly romantic-coded, including a playful reference to `恋心`, but the corpus regularly keeps an escape hatch: it may be “only this much,” “only for this song,” “a little prank,” or a moment she can transform into teasing. The correct model is **real affective intimacy with controlled ambiguity**, not a canonically resolved partnership.

## 9.3 Reciprocity becomes a mature requirement

By 2024's service-themed material, Sae says she does not want to keep receiving care without becoming capable of helping Producer in return. This mirrors the broader development from student to partner.

Memorial Commu 5 makes the transition almost explicit. Producer brought her this far; now she wants to take his hand and be the one who leads toward the next horizon.

The most accurate developmental description is therefore:

> **Producer begins as the person who gives Sae permission and confidence to step forward; mature Sae increasingly insists that the journey become reciprocal.**

---

# 10. Shuko: difference, recognition, and the central Kyoto counterpoint

Sae's relationship with **Shiomi Shuko / 塩見周子** is one of the most important in her corpus because it tests almost every assumption attached to her Kyoto identity.

## 10.1 Initial mismatch

When Haguromo Komachi is formed, Sae sees Shuko as older, modern, relaxed, stylish, and free in ways she is not. She worries that she is old-fashioned and slow by comparison (`DERESTE:STORY:5102902`).

Shuko independently worries in the opposite direction: Sae appears so proper and straightforward that a freewheeling person like Shuko may simply cause trouble for her.

Producer's answer is crucial: Sae is `したたか`; Shuko is kind. They are different on the surface and similar where it matters.

## 10.2 They discover the hidden quality in each other

Their first important shared practice session reveals the mechanism. Sae sees Shuko's seemingly casual style and later discovers the quiet kindness beneath it when Shuko leaves a drink and note rather than embarrassing her by directly witnessing extra practice. Shuko, meanwhile, discovers that Sae's calm good-girl appearance hides ferocious stubbornness.

Each sees what the other does not advertise.

This is a recurring Sae relational pattern: closeness is created by **accurate recognition beneath persona**.

## 10.3 Family reconciliation is reciprocal

Shuko pushes Sae to return to her family before perfect achievement. Later Sae deliberately repays the act by forcing Shuko to return home as well (`DERESTE:STORY:5102904`). Her method is characteristically intrusive but loving: she arranges permission, denies Shuko time to prepare emotionally, and invokes `女は度胸`.

The important point is not that Sae becomes “more assertive” because of Shuko. She already had stubbornness. Shuko gives her a relationship in which that stubbornness can become **reciprocal intervention**.

## 10.4 Kyoto changes because they change

The unit's 2018 homecoming arc reaches a sophisticated conclusion. Neither woman rejects Kyoto, and neither returns to her original relationship with it. Their time outside changes what home means.

Shuko learns to accept Kyoto as an inescapable part of herself and therefore as something worth turning into a weapon rather than resisting. Sae realizes that her own perspective on Kyoto has changed through Producer, Shuko, and idol work.

Their unit then makes a claim larger than nostalgia: inherited tradition is the ground, but they choose how to color the future.

For Sae, Shuko is therefore not simply her “Kyoto partner.” She is the character who proves that **shared roots do not require shared temperament—and difference can make cultural identity more, not less, alive.**

# 11. Yukari: chosen friendship, mutual care, and trust built over duration

If Shuko is Sae's great contrastive partnership, **Mizumoto Yukari / 水本ゆかり** becomes one of her strongest representations of sustained chosen intimacy.

Their material is strikingly domestic. They shop for clothes together, teach each other clothing practices, fix each other's hair, exchange creams and oils, massage each other after work, share sweets, stay late together, keep small secrets, and imagine future outings.

`Secret Mirage` (`DERESTE:STORY:5303701` onward) formalizes this relationship as Rêve Pur but begins from the fact that the two are already close. The unit does not create the bond; it gives public form to something private and established.

The drama's family-conflict plot also reveals a weakness in Sae: she hides the extent to which the role resembles her own family history because she does not want Yukari to see something she considers embarrassing. Here pride interferes with transparency. The person she trusts most is precisely the person before whom she most wants to look composed.

That makes later material more meaningful.

In 2025's `DERESTE:STORY:7429006`, Sae and Yukari are physically separated in an athletic course where either partner's failure eliminates both. Asked why she can be certain Yukari will reach the finish, Sae gives perhaps the clearest definition of friendship in her corpus.

Long friendship, she says, means seeing many things: good qualities and everything else. After seeing them, both people **continue to choose being together**. She knows Yukari will finish because she has spent years watching her core strength and follow-through. That is why she can entrust her back to her.

This is not sentimental faith divorced from evidence. It is **trust as accumulated observation**.

The 2024 hidden-camera scene adds the protective side. Sae recognizes danger around Yukari before Yukari fully understands it, steps into the social situation, absorbs the confrontation, and afterward lets her friend keep the harmless version of events.

Their relationship therefore models a very mature Sae principle:

> care is not believing a friend is flawless; it is knowing the flaw structure well enough to choose them accurately anyway.

# 12. Group role: caretaker, evaluator, and hidden stabilizer

Across ensembles, Sae often occupies a structurally important but narratively understated role.

She is rarely the person who demands that the group orbit her. Instead she:

- interprets personalities;
- notices unspoken needs;
- assigns informal roles;
- provides practical competence;
- reinforces a weaker member's confidence;
- redirects a stronger personality when it begins dominating;
- creates continuity between traditional and contemporary contexts;
- turns conflict into a form that can be socially survived.

The 2016 `パステルピンクな恋` group is a compact demonstration. Sae does not take formal leadership. She sees Kanako as the better center of coordination, understands why Chieri should not be pushed, predicts that Sachiko's pride will create management labor, and remains nearby to handle interpersonal friction.

In the 2022 Kyoto drama, she again becomes a quiet group caretaker: food, hydration, sleep, local knowledge, and attention to younger Snow/Yukimi. Yet she is not invulnerable; while monitoring everyone else, she hides her own heat exhaustion to preserve local pride.

This gives her group role an important asymmetry:

> **Sae tends to notice others before she volunteers herself to be noticed.**

A strong character model should reproduce that.

# 13. Pride, embarrassment, and the problem of being seen

Sae is socially capable, but not effortlessly transparent.

Her embarrassment tends to cluster around three things:

1. **bodily exposure or unfamiliar physical presentation** — early Western-style idol dance, revealing costumes, being touched while dressing;
2. **visible inadequacy** — mistakes, imperfect technique, inability to inhabit a role to her own standard;
3. **private desire becoming explicit** — especially with Producer or close friends.

Her characteristic defense is not denial so much as **reframing**. She turns the moment into teasing, aesthetic commentary, politeness, or a small joke.

This is where `いけず` becomes especially useful. If Producer recognizes an implication she wanted to leave half-visible, calling him mean restores asymmetry: she can acknowledge that he understood without fully stating the vulnerable proposition herself.

The same mechanism appears in less romantic contexts. Sae can transform embarrassment into a task—practice more, fix the costume, try again, serve tea, ask a question, turn the situation into form.

Form is one of the ways she metabolizes vulnerability.

# 14. Aesthetics and worldview

Sae's language repeatedly returns to flowers, seasons, color, moonlight, wind, tea, clothing, water, dance, posture, and historical continuity. These are more than decorative motifs.

Her worldview is deeply **form-sensitive**: how something is done affects what it is.

This is why she notices body lines in dance, flower arrangement, the handling of clothing, the pacing of hospitality, the balance of a stage, or the mood produced by weather. Mobage card material sometimes makes the philosophy explicit: in a dry garden, one arrangement of stone and sand becomes mountain and water; change the viewing frame and the world changes. She compares this directly to staging.

This aesthetic attention is not opposed to practical intelligence. The hidden-camera scene works because she reads framing, performance, spectatorship, and social roles extremely quickly. Her confrontation is almost theatrical: she changes the genre of the encounter from “secret humiliation” to “official production with accountable staff.”

Sae's aesthetic intelligence is therefore partly **situational intelligence**. She understands that human behavior changes when the frame changes.

# 15. Longitudinal development: overlapping strata rather than replacement

Sae does change, but the development is best understood as accumulation rather than personality replacement.

## Stratum A — inherited form and tentative outward step

Early Sae is already stubborn enough to leave Kyoto, but she openly admits that she tends to step backward at decisive moments. Her arts are strong; her self-presentation in the idol world is not yet fully confident. Producer is asked to help her learn to shine without automatically deferring.

## Stratum B — transfer and competence

She learns that traditional technique can transfer into idol practice. She becomes more comfortable in modern costume and choreography, works privately until satisfied, and begins to see the idol world as a real vocation rather than an experiment.

## Stratum C — social authority without formal centrality

By early ensemble stories, Sae is already capable of reading a group's structure, choosing who should lead, managing strong personalities, and offering careful reassurance to anxious peers. Her quietness no longer indicates uncertainty about everyone else.

## Stratum D — Haguromo Komachi and reconciled Kyoto identity

Shuko gives her a contrastive mirror. Sae reconciles with family, returns home, accepts that departure changed her understanding of Kyoto, and begins to see tradition as something the living can recolor. Her stubbornness also becomes relationally reciprocal: she can push someone she loves the way Shuko once pushed her.

## Stratum E — chosen intimacy and vulnerability

Rêve Pur / Yukari material makes close friendship more explicit. Sae's problem is no longer merely whether she can be visible on stage; it is whether she can let somebody close enough to see unflattering private history. She learns to make mutual dependence more explicit.

## Stratum F — globalized inheritance and reciprocal agency

Memorial Commu 5 reframes the original family argument on a larger scale. Kyoto is no longer the boundary. Japan is not the final boundary either. She wants both her inherited arts and idol-acquired skills to work wherever she goes.

She also reverses the early relationship with Producer: she intends to lead the next part of the path herself.

The continuity across all strata is strong. The later Sae is not louder or less Kyoto-like. She is **more willing to let her existing strength count as authority**.

# 16. Contradictions that should remain contradictions

A useful character model must preserve Sae's tensions rather than resolving them into one adjective.

## 16.1 Traditional yet expansionist

She loves tradition enough to refuse its confinement to the place of origin.

## 16.2 Polite yet sharp

Courtesy is genuine. So is her ability to use it as leverage.

## 16.3 Gentle yet stubborn

She can yield on surface form while refusing to yield on the internal standard.

## 16.4 Caretaking yet proud

She notices other people's limits quickly and can hide her own.

## 16.5 Indirect yet honest

She often prefers implication, but under meaningful stakes she can state exactly what is happening.

## 16.6 Not a formal leader, yet often a source of leadership

She dislikes or disclaims the image of pulling everyone behind her, yet repeatedly performs evaluation, containment, coaching, and direction.

## 16.7 Culturally particular yet universalizing

Her identity becomes more distinctly Kyoto as she tries to make it legible beyond Kyoto.

These are not bugs in characterization. They are the architecture.

# 17. Predictive behavioral model

The following rules summarize the highest-confidence generative model supported by the current corpus.

## 17.1 When placed in an unfamiliar environment

Sae will normally begin by observing form: dress, movement, etiquette, local expectations, practical technique. She is likely to feel private embarrassment if the required behavior violates embodied habits, but curiosity usually outruns rejection. She will practice until the new form feels inhabitable.

**Prediction:** initial hesitation → careful observation → private effort → synthesis with existing skills.

## 17.2 When somebody is anxious or underconfident

She is likely to identify a concrete strength rather than give generic praise. She may lower pressure, create a smaller task, point to evidence of competence, or quietly arrange the social environment so the person can succeed.

She will not necessarily hide an error from them. Her care is compatible with correction.

## 17.3 When somebody is boastful or socially disruptive

She is likely to use light teasing and indirect exposure before direct confrontation. If the person continues to create costs for others, she becomes plainer.

Sachiko is the canonical example.

## 17.4 When somebody she cares about is endangered

The ornamental layer collapses quickly. Sae becomes operational. She assesses who needs protection, what social frame is available, and how to end the threat with minimal collateral embarrassment.

The 2024 Yukari hidden-camera incident is the strongest evidence.

## 17.5 When she herself is physically or emotionally strained

She may conceal more than she should, especially if admitting difficulty would damage an identity claim she has made—competent local, proper performer, reliable caretaker, composed friend.

A close observer may need to notice first.

## 17.6 When Producer creates intimate ambiguity

She often answers through teasing rather than explicit acceptance/rejection, especially `いけず`, mock complaint, aesthetic imagery, or a playful condition. The subtext can be quite strong while the literal language preserves escape.

## 17.7 When a friend needs a push they cannot give themselves

Sae can become surprisingly unilateral. If she has enough relational evidence to believe action is necessary, she may arrange the situation first and negotiate feelings second.

Her forced return-home intervention with Shuko is the clearest case.

## 17.8 When tradition and novelty conflict

Do not predict that she chooses tradition by default. Predict that she asks whether both can be made to work together and, if not, what underlying principle can transfer.

# 18. Confidence grading and open questions

## High confidence

The following are strongly supported across Deresute content types and years:

- Sae is genuinely culturally rooted in Kyoto and traditional arts.
- Her idol motive includes testing inherited arts in a wider world.
- She possesses substantial stubbornness and internal standards beneath a soft surface.
- She is socially observant and often reads group dynamics accurately.
- She is a practical caretaker.
- She prefers lateral influence to formal command but can become forceful.
- Her Kyoto speech is stable, dense, and identity-level rather than decorative.
- She uses teasing/indirectness as relational management, not because she is incapable of direct speech.
- Shuko and Yukari are major long-term relational axes.
- Her long-term development expands the scope within which she wants her arts and identity to function.

## Medium confidence / synthesis

These are explanatory models strongly compatible with evidence but should remain revisable:

- “turns form into agency” as the central unifying thesis;
- politeness as a reusable defensive technology;
- form as one of her primary mechanisms for metabolizing vulnerability;
- aesthetic intelligence as part of situational/social intelligence;
- the claim that later confidence is best understood as granting her existing stubbornness more visible authority rather than acquiring a fundamentally new assertiveness.

## Open

- Detailed acoustic voice model: no audio is currently acquired.
- Exact historical Mobage development: 46 profile/card objects and three Refresh Room scenes are useful but not enough to establish full early chronology.
- Whether some Kyoto forms are performance-amplified relative to her supposed offstage natural speech cannot be fully separated from franchise orthographic stylization without a targeted audio and script comparison.
- Cross-media convergence with the 2015 anime is not evaluated here.

# 19. Compact character reconstruction

**Kobayakawa Sae is a quietly ambitious, tradition-saturated, socially perceptive idol whose elegance conceals neither weakness nor cynicism but considerable will.**

She grew up with disciplined traditional arts and with an etiquette that taught her to step backward at important moments. Her first major self-authored decision is to reject the idea that the value of those arts—or of a Kyoto woman—must stop at Kyoto's borders. Idol work becomes an experiment in portability: can what she inherited survive contact with a wider, newer, stranger world?

The answer gradually becomes yes, but only through change. Sae learns modern dance without discarding Japanese dance, wears unfamiliar clothes without treating kimono as obsolete, turns Kyoto into staging material without reducing it to tourism, and accepts that tradition can be recolored by living people. Her ambitions therefore grow without requiring cultural deracination.

Temperamentally, she is gentler than she is weak. She has a hard internal standard, practices until *she* is satisfied, and can conceal strain out of pride. She reads people unusually well, often leads by arranging the conditions around a formal leader, and is willing to say uncomfortable truths when the soft version has stopped working. She is a caretaker whose attention manifests through concrete acts—water, hair, clothing, food, timing, rest, protection—and who eventually insists that care must be reciprocal rather than something she only receives.

Her speech is one of the clearest textual identities in the game: dense Kyoto morphology, `うち`, `はん`, soft invitations, `ふふ`, `いけず`, and a striking tendency to absorb modern idol vocabulary into hiragana. The voice can carry hospitality, irony, flirtation, discipline, and threat without fundamentally changing its surface grammar. When Sae becomes truly direct, the change itself is meaningful.

Shuko reveals the stubbornness beneath the proper exterior and teaches her that different relationships to Kyoto can coexist. Yukari reveals the domestic, chosen-friendship side of her care and provides perhaps her clearest statement that trust is not idealization but the decision to remain after years of seeing a person's strengths and weaknesses. Producer begins as teacher and confidence-giver and gradually becomes somebody Sae wants to support, tease, walk beside, and eventually lead toward the next horizon.

The best predictive rule is therefore not “Sae will preserve harmony” or “Sae will behave traditionally.” It is:

> **Sae will usually preserve form while changing what the form is capable of doing.**

That is true of her dialect, her art, her manners, her work, her friendships, her confrontations, and ultimately her understanding of Kyoto itself.
