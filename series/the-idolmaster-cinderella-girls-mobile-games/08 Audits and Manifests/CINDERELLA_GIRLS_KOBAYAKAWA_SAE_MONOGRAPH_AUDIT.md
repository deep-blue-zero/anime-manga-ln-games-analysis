# THE IDOLM@STER CINDERELLA GIRLS — Kobayakawa Sae Monograph Audit

## 0. Audit purpose and result

This document audits `CINDERELLA_GIRLS_KOBAYAKAWA_SAE_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md` against Sae's integrated mobile-game evidence packet. The 2015 anime is not used as the governing check. The task is to determine whether the game-corpus monograph is well calibrated on its own evidence boundary.

The audit asks:

1. whether the central **“form into agency”** thesis survives adversarial reading;
2. whether the monograph romanticizes tradition, grace, care, or social perception;
3. whether Sae's Kyoto speech has been described at the correct level—**character-scripted linguistic identity**, not unverified real-world sociolinguistics;
4. whether the generative model correctly predicts what happens when Sae must protect, confront, care, or violate her own preferred social form;
5. whether Haguromo Komachi/Shuko and Rêve Pur/Yukari support the relational claims made for them.

The overall result is:

> **PRESERVE WITH TARGETED REVISIONS.**

The central monograph is strong. In particular, the corpus supports that Sae is much more than a static “traditional Kyoto girl” archetype. She enters idol work partly because she refuses the proposition that a Kyoto woman's arts need only be legible in Kyoto. Her Japanese dance, kimono knowledge, etiquette, dialect, seasonal aesthetics, and hospitality repeatedly become **portable techniques** through which she enters new professional and social spaces. The corpus also strongly supports stubborn private standards, social acuity, protective force beneath a soft manner, practical care, and the importance of Shuko and Yukari as relationships that expose different dimensions of her.

The most important audit correction is that V1's central thesis is **slightly too celebratory** if read without its contradiction sections. Form does not only empower Sae. It can also become a **self-imposed constraint**:

- early etiquette teaches her to step back at decisive moments;
- pride keeps her from returning home until she imagines she has “earned” the right;
- her identity as a Kyoto local leads her to conceal heat fatigue because admitting it would look bad;
- stubbornness can push her beyond what ordinary prudence requires;
- care can become unilateral intervention, as when she arranges Shuko's return home before Shuko feels prepared.

The best revised thesis is therefore:

> **Sae turns form into agency, but her maturation also depends on learning when inherited or self-authored form has hardened into a cage.**

A second major correction concerns the monograph's claim that under threat “the ornamental layer collapses.” The strongest counterexample is the very scene V1 cites: the 2024 Yukari hidden-camera incident. Sae does **not** abandon elegance, indirection, smiles, or Kyoto-inflected courtesy. She weaponizes them. What collapses is not ornament but **ambiguity about purpose**. She becomes operational while preserving—and in some respects intensifying—the social form.

A third correction concerns dialect. The packet gives overwhelming evidence that Sae's scripted Kyoto register is stable and embodied. *Secret Mirage* explicitly makes standard-Japanese acting difficult for her, and another scene says the language was learned in childhood around geisha/maiko practice and has become `染みついてもうた`. But the analytical artifact should avoid silently claiming that every form is a documentary sample of contemporary real-world Kyoto Japanese. The corpus establishes **Sae's authored character register**, not sociolinguistic authenticity outside the fiction.

No major governing claim merits `REJECT`.

# III. Strongest preserved theses

## 1. “Grace is not passivity” — STRENGTHEN

The monograph is unequivocally correct on its first major correction.

Sae's early self-presentation is saturated with:

- Kyoto speech;
- kimono;
- Japanese dance;
- hospitality;
- cultivated manners;
- stepping back rather than pushing forward.

The base card even states that she tends to withdraw at important moments because her mother taught that as `京女の作法`.

Yet Memorial 1 gives the actual motive for leaving the protected frame. Her parents' proposition is:

> `京女は、京で通じれば、それでええ`

Sae's response:

> `そんなんおもんないな～て、思いまへんか～？`

She wants to know whether what she has learned can work in a broader world. This is not a loud rebellion, but it is real self-assertion.

The strongest formulation is:

> **Sae's soft form is often the way agency is expressed, not evidence that agency is absent.**

## 2. Traditional form is portable technique — STRENGTHEN

The corpus repeatedly makes inherited arts operational:

- Japanese dance provides posture and bodily control;
- its mismatch with idol choreography creates a learning problem rather than a rejection;
- dressing/kimono knowledge becomes useful to others;
- Kyoto aesthetics become staging resources;
- tea, flowers, seasonal awareness, and hospitality structure professional scenes;
- later Haguromo Komachi material explicitly treats tradition as something living people can recolor.

This is stronger than archetypal “cultural pride.” Sae treats tradition as **something that can be translated into new environments**.

## 3. Stubbornness is a central engine — STRENGTHEN

One of the cleanest self-definitions in the packet occurs after Shuko discovers Sae continuing to practice alone after the lesson is officially over.

Sae rejects the flattering interpretation of noble grit:

> `うちが、自分に納得できひんと気がすまへん頑固者なだけや。`

This is analytically useful because it explains why her effort can look effortless from the outside. She may speak softly, accept praise modestly, and maintain composure, while privately refusing to stop until she meets an internal standard.

The audit recommends making `頑固` even more structurally central in the revised monograph—but also preserving its costs.

## 4. Strategic politeness is real — STRENGTHEN

The 2024 hidden-camera scene (`DERESTE:STORY:7213037`) is unusually decisive evidence.

Sae:

- detects the camera;
- understands Yukari is being manipulated;
- gives Yukari a face-saving “TV prank” interpretation;
- imitates Yukari to enter the scene without breaking its social surface;
- asks which television station the men belong to;
- politely requests inclusion next time;
- asks to see everything they recorded;
- brings Producer and staff into the frame;
- protects Yukari from having to process the predatory intent immediately.

The crucial lines remain almost absurdly courteous:

> `お客はん方、どちらの局の方やろか♪`

> `次は初めから声をかけておくれやす。ちゃあんと、初めから……ね♪`

This is the best evidence that her manners can become **force without loss of form**.

# IV. Principal required revisions

## 1. Add the constraint side of “form into agency”

V1 already contains pride and contradiction sections, but the central thesis should explicitly admit that form is **ambivalent**.

Sae inherits forms that empower her:

- dance;
- etiquette;
- craft;
- dialect;
- hospitality;
- composure.

But she also inherits or develops forms that constrain her:

### Early self-effacement

Base card:

> `うち、肝心なときに、一歩ひいてしまうんどすー…`

The behavior is explicitly connected to learned etiquette.

### Family pride as self-imposed exile

Before Haguromo Komachi's reconciliation sequence, Sae refuses to return home because she does not feel sufficiently accomplished. Her parents' boundary is no longer the only constraint; Sae has internalized a standard that says she must become “worthy” before returning.

Shuko must tell her that if she wants to go home, she can simply go home.

### Local identity as concealment

During the Kyoto shoot, Sae monitors Fumika's hydration carefully but conceals her own fatigue. When Fumika notices, Sae admits the reason is largely face:

> `地元の人間が暑い暑い言うてたら、格好がつかんやろ？`

She promises she will speak up if it becomes unsafe, but the scene clearly shows identity standards encouraging unnecessary concealment.

The central thesis should therefore become:

> **Sae's growth is not merely learning to use form. It is learning which forms deserve loyalty, how to make them travel, and when pride in form must be loosened so it does not govern her from the outside.**

That is a stronger and less triumphalist model.

## 2. Revise “universalizing” into a temporally precise expansion model

The early Sae wants to test herself outside Kyoto. That is an expansive/translocal motive.

Later material supports broader claims—Japan, wider worlds, skills that should function wherever she goes.

Calling the initial motive **universalizing** risks importing the endpoint into the beginning.

Recommended longitudinal wording:

> Kyoto-bounded value → translocal test → reconciled Kyoto identity → increasingly broad ambition.

## 3. Revise social intelligence from “correctness” to high-success inference

`DERESTE:STORY:5100502` is strong evidence. Sae says:

> `こうみえてうち、人を見る目はあるつもりどす。`

She then analyzes possible leaders:

- Sachiko's pride will impose management costs;
- Chieri's anxiety makes forced leadership unwise;
- Kanako underestimates herself;
- Mayu is described through a comic aside about Producer.

The monograph says these choices are “functionally correct.” That phrasing is too categorical.

Reasons:

- some observations are comic rather than formal diagnosis;
- unchosen leadership counterfactuals cannot be directly tested;
- Sae herself sometimes self-corrects wording after seeing how another person receives it.

The better formulation:

> **Sae has unusually strong, usually successful social inference, and the narrative repeatedly validates her read of interpersonal pressure points. She is not omniscient.**

This preserves what makes her distinctive while avoiding a perfect-empath model.

## 4. Revise threat response: purpose clarifies, ornament may persist

V1's predictive rule says:

> “The ornamental layer collapses quickly. Sae becomes operational.”

The hidden-camera scene contradicts the literal wording.

Sae becomes highly operational, but she does so **through ornament**:

- smiles;
- politeness;
- role-play;
- indirection;
- Kyoto-inflected softness;
- social face.

What changes is not necessarily syntax or aesthetic surface. What changes is that every line now has a clear tactical purpose.

Revised rule:

> **Under threat, Sae's ambiguity of purpose collapses. Surface courtesy may remain or even intensify because form itself can be the instrument of control.**

This is one of the most important audit findings because it improves both behavioral and speech generation.

## 5. Make care's intrusive edge explicit

Sae's care is usually observant and practical. She understands that help can become burden; the 2021 hospital/event scene says this outright:

> `その応援も、押し付けるみたいな形になってもうたら、かえって重荷になってしまいますえ。`

But she is not uniformly low-pressure.

In the Haguromo Komachi family-return sequence, after Shuko once helped Sae go home, Sae decides it is now her turn to push Shuko. Shuko protests that she is not prepared. Sae answers:

> `待っとっても心の準備なんてできまへん。それよりも、飛び込んでみるんどす。`

This is care, but it is unmistakably unilateral.

The better model is:

> **Sae prefers care that preserves dignity and autonomy, but when accumulated relational evidence convinces her that avoidance itself is the obstacle, she can become paternalistic/managerial and act first.**

That edge should not be hidden because it is one of the ways her stubbornness enters relationships.

# V. Speech and linguistic-identity audit

## 1. Sae's scripted Kyoto register is extraordinarily stable — STRENGTHEN

Across 2,244 recovered lines, direct string counts include approximately:

- `はん`: 965 occurrences;
- `うち`: 763;
- `どす`: 393;
- `まへん`: 206;
- `さかい`: 143;
- `おくれやす`: 63;
- `おおきに`: 49;
- `よろしゅう`: 31;
- `いけず`: 22;
- `堪忍`: 14;
- `ふふ`: about 204;
- ellipsis on more than 1,000 lines.

This is far too dense and persistent to treat as an introductory gimmick.

## 2. But “natural dialect” needs a more precise model — REVISE

The corpus itself gives Sae a history of language acquisition.

In the Kyoto material she explains that geisha/maiko figures around her childhood practice environment influenced how she spoke:

> `姐さんたちが、練習してはったのを真似てたら、なぁ。`

She knows the register can sound grand:

> `大仰しいのはうちも分かってるんやけど、染みついてもうた言葉やから。`

This is an unusually useful statement.

The register is neither:

- a detachable idol branding trick;
- nor simply an unconscious, unmarked sample of “how everyone from Kyoto naturally talks.”

It is better modeled as:

> **a culturally acquired, embodied, identity-level register that Sae knows is marked, inherited, and partly stylized, but that has become difficult to separate from her ordinary self.**

That formulation is more source-faithful.

## 3. *Secret Mirage* strongly confirms embodied default — STRENGTHEN

Sae says:

> `今回のお芝居は京言葉を使われへんし、なかなか苦労してるわぁ。`

The narrative makes register-switching itself part of acting difficulty.

This is very strong evidence that Kyoto speech is not an optional seasoning added only when the writers want to remind us of Kyoto.

## 4. Do not convert fictional script evidence into external sociolinguistics — REVISE METHOD

The monograph should explicitly distinguish:

- **what Sae's game script does**;
- **whether those forms correspond to any particular real-life distribution of contemporary Kyoto Japanese**.

The first question is strongly answerable from the corpus. The second would require separate linguistic research and comparison.

Therefore future artifacts should say:

> “Sae's scripted Kyoto register…”

rather than implying documentary authenticity unless independently verified.

## 5. Hiragana-rendered idol vocabulary — STRENGTHEN written-voice claim, reject acoustic inference

The packet strongly supports forms such as:

- `あいどる`;
- `すてーじ`;
- `れっすん`;
- `ゆにっと`;
- `だんす`.

This creates a recognizable **orthographic integration** of modern idol vocabulary into her written persona.

It is analytically useful for text generation.

But hiragana versus katakana does not by itself prove a different pronunciation.

So:

- written-character voice significance — **STRENGTHEN**;
- acoustic significance — **OPEN/unsupported without audio**.

## 6. `ふふ` — PRESERVE

`ふふ` frequently cushions:

- teasing;
- care;
- mild superiority;
- amusement;
- flirtation;
- threat softened into social form.

Its function varies. A model should not treat every `ふふ` as coquettish or every smile as hidden aggression.

## 7. `いけず` — PRESERVE with relational scope

`いけず` can operate as:

- playful reproach;
- affectionate teasing;
- mild flirtational ambiguity;
- social criticism.

It is an important keyword precisely because its force depends on relationship and context.

The audit supports V1's flexible model and warns against mapping all 22 occurrences to a single romantic subtext.

## 8. Directness under stakes — REVISE linguistic formulation

The packet strongly supports increased **semantic/illocutionary clarity** when Sae cares about an outcome.

But clarity does not require blunt grammar.

The hidden-camera sequence demonstrates:

> very indirect/polite surface + completely unmistakable operational meaning.

A future speech model should therefore distinguish:

- **surface directness** — blunt syntax, explicit accusation;
- **illocutionary directness** — everyone understands exactly what Sae is requiring.

Sae is often high on the second while remaining low on the first.

# VI. Adversarial scene tests

## Test 1 — Base-card “one step back” etiquette

**Question:** does tradition only give Sae tools?

No. Sae explicitly says inherited etiquette makes her withdraw at important moments.

**Result:** form is both resource and constraint.

**Disposition:** **REVISE** central thesis to include ambivalence.

## Test 2 — Story/Event leadership analysis

**Question:** does Sae actually read groups well?

She identifies pressure points among Kanako, Chieri, Sachiko, and Mayu and helps place Kanako in a role that mobilizes her confidence.

**Result:** strong social acuity.

**Caveat:** not every diagnosis is empirically testable; some lines are comic.

**Disposition:** **STRENGTHEN** acuity, **REVISE** categorical correctness.

## Test 3 — Haguromo Komachi extra practice

Sae remains after the lesson, practices alone, and later calls herself a `頑固者` rather than a noble hard worker.

**Prediction from V1:** soft presentation hides a hard internal standard.

**Observed:** exceptionally strong match.

**Disposition:** **STRENGTHEN**.

## Test 4 — Sae refuses to return home

She wants to return but believes she is still too immature as an idol to face her parents.

**Prediction from a simplistic agency thesis:** independent pride should simply free her from parental boundary.

**Observed:** pride becomes a self-imposed barrier even after leaving Kyoto.

**Disposition:** **REVISE** form/pride model.

## Test 5 — Sae forces Shuko's return home

Shuko says she is not mentally prepared. Sae answers that waiting will not produce preparation and pushes her through the encounter.

**Prediction from “care preserves autonomy”:** Sae should wait.

**Observed:** she does not.

**Disposition:** **REVISE** care model; preserve unilateral-push rule.

## Test 6 — Hospital/event support problem

Sae says support can become a burden if imposed.

**Prediction:** she understands autonomy and emotional load.

**Observed:** yes, and she also apologizes that her wording may have been poor.

**Result:** social intelligence includes **self-correction**, not infallibility.

**Disposition:** **STRENGTHEN** empathy, **REVISE** omniscience.

## Test 7 — Kyoto heat

Sae notices Fumika's hydration from breakfast behavior and intervenes before Fumika fully registers the problem. Later Fumika notices Sae herself is tired.

Sae admits she is maintaining local pride:

> `地元の人間が暑い暑い言うてたら、格好がつかんやろ？`

**Result:** she is better at noticing others' limits than volunteering her own.

**Disposition:** **STRENGTHEN** pride/care asymmetry.

## Test 8 — Yukari hidden-camera incident

**Prediction from V1's literal threat rule:** ornament should collapse.

**Observed:** the opposite. Sae preserves social performance and turns it into a trap for the offenders.

**Disposition:** **REVISE** threat-response rule substantially.

## Test 9 — *Secret Mirage* standard-language acting

Sae explicitly says acting without Kyoto speech is difficult.

**Result:** dialect/register is embodied and cognitively default enough to become performance friction.

**Disposition:** **STRENGTHEN** linguistic-identity thesis.

# VII. Relationship audit

## 1. Producer — PRESERVE

Producer's early role as confidence giver is directly supported by the base Affection Max sequence. Sae asks him to help her develop the confidence required to stop stepping backward.

Later she increasingly wants reciprocity. The mature movement is not simple dependency reduction; it is a shift from:

> “teach/guide me”

toward:

> “I also want to support, pull, tease, and eventually lead you.”

The monograph is well calibrated here.

## 2. Shuko — STRENGTHEN central counterpoint

Haguromo Komachi does several things no other relationship does quite as densely:

- reveals Sae's stubbornness to someone initially inclined to read her as merely proper;
- reveals Shuko's care beneath casualness;
- places two different relationships to Kyoto in dialogue;
- gives Sae permission to return home before perfection;
- later lets Sae return the intervention in a much more forceful form;
- reframes tradition as something they can add their own colors to.

This supports calling Shuko the **central Kyoto counterpoint** rather than merely a close friend.

The audit's main correction is to make the relationship's care more dialectical: they do not simply accept one another; they sometimes **push each other past self-protective forms**.

## 3. Yukari — STRENGTHEN chosen intimacy

Yukari provides a different relationship function:

- shared domestic/room familiarity;
- performance imitation;
- secrets and embarrassment;
- mutual observation;
- the hidden-camera rescue;
- later explicit trust built from long-duration knowledge of strengths and flaws.

The monograph is justified in treating this as one of Sae's strongest examples of **chosen, sustained intimacy**.

The caveat is ranking language: “one of the strongest” is safer than asserting a single objective #1 friendship without a dedicated relational-comparison audit.

# VIII. Longitudinal audit

## 1. Early Sae is already agentic, but her inherited form sets the size of the first move

Her first step is outward, not anti-Kyoto. She wants to test whether her inheritance can work elsewhere.

## 2. Competence grows through transfer rather than replacement

Japanese dance does not vanish when idol dance arrives. It becomes a base from which she learns a new grammar.

This transfer logic repeats across aesthetics, dress, etiquette, and group work.

## 3. Family reconciliation revises pride, not cultural identity

Shuko's intervention matters because it teaches Sae that **return does not have to be earned through perfect completion**.

The result is not “Sae escapes tradition.” It is that the relation to family/Kyoto becomes less conditional on proving herself.

## 4. Later Sae increasingly accepts reciprocal agency

She wants to care back, lead Producer in turn, intervene for friends, and take what she has learned further outward.

The monograph's overlapping-strata structure is therefore **STRENGTHENED**.

# IX. Revised generative behavioral model

## 1. Unfamiliar environment

Likely sequence:

1. identify local form, etiquette, bodily requirement, or craft;
2. compare it—often implicitly—with existing embodied technique;
3. experience private embarrassment if it violates learned habit;
4. remain curious rather than defensive;
5. practice until she can inhabit it without feeling false;
6. eventually combine old and new.

## 2. Anxious person

Sae tends to:

- observe concrete behavior;
- name a real strength rather than offer generic praise;
- reduce unnecessary pressure;
- preserve dignity;
- correct when correction is needed.

But she can misphrase and adjust. Do not make her psychic.

## 3. Socially disruptive person

First preference:

- teasing;
- gentle reframing;
- indirect exposure of contradiction;
- making the better behavior socially easy.

If disruption persists, she becomes more explicit.

## 4. Threat to someone she cares about

**Revised rule:**

- operational purpose becomes immediate;
- she identifies face-saving options;
- surface courtesy may remain or intensify;
- she may use role-play, etiquette, or indirection as leverage;
- she minimizes humiliation for the protected person;
- she closes off the bad actor's room to maneuver.

Do **not** automatically switch her to blunt standard Japanese or loud confrontation.

## 5. Her own strain

She may hide difficulty when admitting it threatens an identity claim:

- competent local;
- proper performer;
- reliable caretaker;
- strong daughter/friend.

Close observers can catch this before she volunteers it.

## 6. Friend trapped by avoidance

Sae's respect for autonomy is not absolute.

If she believes she has enough relational evidence, she may:

- arrange the encounter;
- recruit Producer;
- remove escape routes;
- insist the friend “jump in” before feeling ready.

This is care plus stubbornness, not pure gentleness.

## 7. Tradition versus novelty

Her default is synthesis, but not because all combinations are easy.

She asks:

> What underlying technique can travel?

and:

> Which part of the old form is essential, and which part can change?

## 8. Speech generation

Default ingredients:

- `うち`;
- relational `～はん`;
- stable Kyoto-scripted morphology;
- soft invitation/request forms;
- `ふふ`;
- indirect teasing;
- `いけず` when relationship/context permits;
- hiragana-rendered modern idol vocabulary in written dialogue.

Under stakes, make **purpose clearer**, not automatically syntax blunter.

# XI. Final audited model

The strongest post-audit reconstruction is:

> **Kobayakawa Sae is a culturally saturated, quietly ambitious woman who uses form as a means of agency—but whose maturation also requires discovering when form has ceased to serve her and has begun to demand obedience from her.**

That second clause is the key audit improvement.

Her Kyoto upbringing gives her real power:

- embodied discipline;
- aesthetic vocabulary;
- movement technique;
- hospitality;
- patience;
- social control;
- a speech register capable of warmth, teasing, and pressure.

It also gives her or reinforces habits that can become restrictive:

- stepping backward at decisive moments;
- maintaining face;
- refusing to return home before imagined completion;
- hiding personal strain;
- equating internal standard with obligation.

Idol work does not free Sae by stripping those forms away. It gives her more opportunities to **choose which parts she wants to continue**, to translate them, and eventually to modify the conditions under which they govern her.

Her stubbornness is therefore not a hidden “tough girl” twist. It is one of the main engines of the character. It makes her work hard after the room empties. It can also make her too proud to yield. The same is true of care: she is exquisitely attentive to other people and genuinely understands that support can become oppressive, yet with people she knows deeply she can also decide that waiting is just another name for avoidance and push them through a door they did not choose to open that day.

Her social intelligence is strong but not magical. She notices details, models pressure points, and is often validated by outcomes. She also apologizes when wording lands badly and depends on accumulated observation rather than omniscience.

Her speech should be modeled as an **embodied fictional Kyoto register** with deep identity significance. She knows it is marked and somewhat grand; she can trace parts of it to childhood imitation around traditional arts; it has nevertheless become so naturalized that standard-Japanese acting imposes real cognitive friction. Its written texture includes dense Kyoto morphology, soft invitation, teasing, `ふふ`, `いけず`, and distinctive hiragana treatment of modern idol vocabulary. None of this alone licenses fine-grained acoustic claims.

Under threat, the best rule is not that Sae becomes linguistically blunt. It is that **her purpose becomes unmistakable**. She may smile more softly than ever while removing every available exit for the person causing harm.

That principle—**clarity of purpose inside continuity of form**—may be the most precise single behavioral refinement produced by this audit.

# XII. Final judgment

**Overall disposition: `PRESERVE_WITH_TARGETED_REVISIONS`.**

The V1 monograph is strong and should remain the current active-provisional character reconstruction until a deliberate revision is emitted. Its central thesis, work/stubbornness model, relationship analysis, and textual speech reconstruction are substantially supported.

The next revision should principally:

1. make form's constraining side part of the central thesis rather than a later caveat;
2. replace “universalizing” with a temporally precise expansion model;
3. lower the certainty language around social reads;
4. revise the threat-response rule so politeness can remain the weapon;
5. foreground intrusive/managerial care alongside tactful care;
6. distinguish Sae's fictional Kyoto register from claims about real-world dialect authenticity;
7. separate written hiragana styling from acoustic speech;
8. treat pride as both strength and self-confinement.

A later Sae audio pass would be valuable, especially for register-switching, prosody, `ふふ`, `いけず`, and the difference between soft surface and forceful illocution. Until then, the current textual voice model is strong while phonetic/prosodic reconstruction remains open.
