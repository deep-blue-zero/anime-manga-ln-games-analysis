## Current-unit story synopsis — unit-end spoilers

The figure Yūki met outside the apartment follows her inside. It looks and sounds exactly like her. It accuses her of becoming complicated, distressed and unrecognizable since she began to care about other people. Yūki remembers killing Tamamo at Royal Palace. She suspects perceptions she acquired in the games are letting her give a persistent sensory reality to this response. The figure challenges her to remember who she was before Hakushi taught her. (E001, S16 P0007–0049.)

In Maiden Race, her first game, Yūki sleeps through the initial alarm. Setsuna, who knowingly entered the event while unable to see much reason to stay alive, meets two sisters called Shizuku and Kanade. Shizuku says her younger sister thought the offer was an ordinary way to earn money. Shizuku goes first across the logs after the starting platform collapses inward. Setsuna wants to move but is frightened, then chooses to wake Yūki and help her. The two cross a series of timed obstacles by cooperating and sometimes separating. At the last surviving bridge, Kanade freezes. Shizuku cannot persuade her to move and the platform behind her is on a timer. Yūki goes onto the bridge, pushes Kanade to her death and crosses. (E002–E003, S18 P0007–0469.)

The final obstacle has fifteen locked doors for the twenty-two people still present. Yūki finds a key almost immediately and leaves before most of the violence over the remaining keys. A player who tries to follow her through the same door dies. Shizuku, unable to reach Yūki, blames Setsuna for waking her and shoots her when she tries to flee with a key. Setsuna almost lets herself be killed. The remembered figure of Yūki on the bridge gives her a model she longs to imitate. She fights back, kills Shizuku and crawls through a door. Beyond it, Yūki lends her a shoulder and listens to her confess. Setsuna then learns that Yūki sincerely thought the entire game was an elaborate entertainment production. Yūki's ignorance and genuine concern strike her as so incongruous that she laughs and decides she wants to live. Thirteen of the fifty beginners survive. Yūki continues to play after learning the truth. Setsuna joins the organization as an agent and seeks out the job of looking after her. In the current story Yūki's assigned agent is Setsuna, formerly Yukino Nanami. (E004–E006, S18 P0473–0796.)

Yūki refuses the phantom's offer to replace her with an undisturbed, supposedly stronger self. When it starts attacking, Yūki runs out of the apartment and calls her agent for help. Yūki asks to be put to sleep and sets out a plan for a private competition that can give the phantom binding rules. Her agent chooses how to interrupt the pursuit, then takes Yūki’s plan to Rinrin. Rinrin and the procurer create Snow Room, a puzzle race shared across two linked spaces. Hakushi plays the part of the opponent under the impression that she has been called to test a harmless version. She deduces that Yūki is probably the other player and is facing real danger. Yūki separately suspects that her former teacher is the person behind the phantom's activity. (E007–E010, S20 P0007–0357/P0712–0929.)

Yūki gets hurt while solving the puzzles and loses an arm when she guesses under time pressure. Hakushi reaches the final chest first and enters the right answer. Its independent trap drops her into a cushioned pit on her side, where she cannot climb out. Yūki realizes that the open floor is a trap, not the described penalty for a wrong answer, and claims her tracksuit, sneakers and hair ornament from the chest on her side. The phantom briefly takes Tamamo's form, accusing Yūki of abandoning her twice and contending that Yūki cannot sustain a real relationship. Yūki answers that the game itself exists only because others agreed to help her. (E011–E012, S20 P0897–1110.)

As soon as the clothes are found, a change Rinrin kept from the agent lifts the ban on violence. Hakushi's part has ended, but the phantom pursues Yūki to the door and holds her by the hair. She forces herself and the phantom into a marked zone reserved for someone wearing the clothes and asks her agent to fire. The agent chooses to do it. The shots wound Yūki and damage her tracksuit and hair. The phantom disappears from her view. The agent sees the hair pulled taut on the monitor and tries to explain it by the wind. Later, elsewhere in the building, she hears someone address her by her former player name, Setsuna, without seeing a speaker. (E013, S20 P1112–1374.)

Yūki gets home with her arm restored. She has the agent cut her damaged hair and plans to keep the patched tracksuit in her closet. She goes to thank Hakushi, who acknowledges that she missed the final trap. They still know different parts of what happened, and Yūki keeps the apparition private. At a crowded station Yūki finds she can get through with her functioning eye shut and without deliberately clicking. She thinks the ordeal has made her a better player, and finds this hard to accept when it began with killing her pupil. Walking home, she allows herself the apology she has chosen to reserve for after play. (E014, S22 P0003–0135.)

Yūki first mistakes a lethal game for a production, then asks others to make a production convincing enough to carry real stakes. Her talent runs through both stories. Chance, unequal information and the separate decisions of people who can help her determine what that talent can accomplish.
