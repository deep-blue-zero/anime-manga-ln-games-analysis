## Current-unit story synopsis — unit-end spoilers

In her third game, Hormone Town, Yūki is an outlaw. She thinks the rules favor the outlaws, who only have to leave town. She finds that her speed, instincts and previous experience of shooting in video games leave her poorly equipped against an expert. Hakushi, playing a sheriff, shoots and captures her alive. Yūki wakes in a wagon crossing the boundary with surviving sheriffs. Her earlier attacks have removed the people who need her bounty, Hakushi tells her. Hakushi offers to teach Yūki and predicts that she may one day want to live long enough to need the training. Yūki resents being treated as a copy of Hakushi's younger self. Hakushi concedes the point, gives her an address and lets her decide. Yūki goes to her.

Much later Tamamo comes to Yūki's apartment to claim the second lesson that Yūki had never meant to give. Yūki recognizes that she agreed to a condition and takes her on. Tamamo follows her closely, wants to stay near her and starts helping in the apartment. Yūki finds the attention both welcome and intrusive. After introducing Tamamo to the games and their supporting trades, Yūki asks Hakushi about teaching and her sense of responsibility for a pupil outside a game. Hakushi leaves the teaching method to Yūki and asks her to take the relationship seriously.

Kokone's referral brings Yūki to Rinrin, who kept playing for a time after losing her sight. Tamamo comes along. Rinrin appears to navigate the island with bells and extraordinary hearing. She has their boat sent away, then sets the visitors a real, dangerous trial. They must find a hidden boat and its key while she tries to stop them. Tamamo discovers usable signals from concealed items even without mobile reception and later helps find and restrain the person feeding Rinrin information. Rinrin had indeed acquired echolocation while playing blind, but another game damaged her hearing. She relies now on a limited aid and, for much of what the visitors see, on a procurer's descriptions. Her former agent removed her from play. She wants to teach what she can and have Yūki kill her. Yūki refuses that last demand. When Rinrin nearly makes the key irretrievable, Yūki intervenes and leaves her alive. Yūki, Tamamo and the procurer depart in the boat. Rinrin stays at the dock to arrange her own return.

Yūki teaches Tamamo to judge unfamiliar risks and adapt rather than try to become her. At the end of Yūki's fifty-sixth game, Crisis Shuttle, she clears the escape despite a fracture and worsening vision. Tamamo has reached her own eighth clear. The two live apart by then. Yūki asks Rinrin to teach her echolocation without repeating the island trial and unilaterally ends Tamamo's apprenticeship. She tells Tamamo that each of them needs to find a style of her own. Tamamo is too shocked to answer. The narrative returns repeatedly to their practical lessons and to Tamamo's growing recognition that defense suits her. She had hoped becoming like Yūki would make a self she hated disappear. After the separation she spends about two months building a way to play as herself.

In Royal Palace, Yūki's sixty-second game, ten teams compete through eighteen possible rounds of one-on-one duels. Yūki has lost sight in her right eye. She wins seven of her team's first eight bouts, usually forcing surrender, then loses the ninth after Shiro injures the other eye. Shiro knows where to press because her teammate Tamamo has told her. Yūki lets others represent the team until its lead vanishes. She volunteers again and learns to make meaningful use of echoes during the seventeenth match. She wins. With both teams at risk on eight wins, she faces Tamamo in the last round.

Tamamo patiently holds Yūki at an uncomfortable distance, times a greeting so Yūki will recognize her by voice, and takes away her sword. Memories of their shared practice help each of them anticipate the other. Severely injured, Yūki takes the fight to close quarters. She falters at the thought of killing Tamamo and understands why she really ended the apprenticeship. She wanted to avoid seeing Tamamo die and feeling answerable for it. Yūki eventually overpowers her and repeatedly stabs her while she is unconscious. The prose cuts away before a final ruling or a separate confirmation of Tamamo's condition. In the agent's car Yūki wakes with her left sight back and hears that she has cleared #62. She asks to walk the final stretch home. At the apartment, a figure she takes for a phantom of herself is clearest on the side where she has no vision. It asks to talk about how she is going to live.

## SBY-V05-E001 — The wrong distance in Hormone Town

The epigraph joins physical distance and interpersonal distance, but the first episode lets them fall out of alignment. Early Yūki can sense danger and act on it. The weak premise comes from familiarity with a model of equipment, including what certain obstacles can stop, and an overestimate of what a talented individual can infer under fire. Six starting rounds are an observed constraint for her, and capability also depends on resupply and taking ammunition. She confronts a real opponent whose quality would outrun any symmetry of costume or starting equipment. Hakushi's victory makes the novice's abilities specific before the novel asks what to do with them. S14 P0004 and S16 P0003–0067.

The outlaws' escape objective and the sheriffs' bounty threshold come through observations and Yūki's retrospective reconstruction. The counts of roughly a hundred and an eighty/twenty split are approximations inside that account. She initially thinks alive or dead could end the same way for a prisoner. The wagon and Hakushi's explanation discriminate a real rival outcome. Once the surviving sheriffs no longer need Yūki's bounty, one adversary's restraint can matter alongside a collective incentive. The novel neither supplies the whole roster nor shows what a sheriff with an unmet threshold would actually do at the gate. The fact of release is stronger than the exact rule Yūki or Hakushi takes to have motivated it. S16 P0027–0034/P0038–0057/P0064–0093.

## SBY-V05-E002 — Who can accept the invitation

The agent's long-term job in V04 makes the after-game recovery familiar. Hakushi at the wagon uses a different kind of authority. She can identify a beginner's mistakes because she has just exploited them. She begins speaking as if aptitude confers a duty to train, encounters 「私のお母さんか」 and an explicit accusation that she is projecting, then distinguishes the offer from the choice to act on it. The initial rough address and the sudden almost punctilious objection make it possible for Yūki to resist both a stranger's imposition and the role of a naïve girl. Hakushi's own former indifference to dying is intelligible as a reason to offer a choice. Yūki's reception has several possible motives on retrospective narration. Her acceptance at #3 does not contain the already formed 99-clear project. S16 P0096–0146, S02–03-I01 after prose.

The text-bearing guide S17-I01 explicitly names survival as a common pupil motive and shows possible wild variation in a teacher's benefit. The pictures and text invite a trade model with reciprocity, sentiment, material support and self-gratification among possible terms. Its marked claim that passing thirty without apprenticeship is nearly impossible expresses a practical professional expectation, beside named differences in actual paths and the trainee whose last stated count is eight and whose final status remains unconfirmed. It is a testable professional assumption with much lower evidentiary standing than a formal game condition or a survival statistic. The placement between the offer and the disciple's presence also gives the reader an opportunity to make a mistake about what advice can accomplish.

## SBY-V05-E009 — Rules, visible behavior and the pressure to yield

Royal Palace announces the central rules before allocating its test. There are seventy entrants in ten teams of seven. A double round robin gives each team eighteen pairings and ninety across the whole tournament. Seven teams clear and the operator says remaining members of the bottom three will be killed. One representative from each team brings one sword. The game ends each duel at surrender or outwardly apparent absence of vital signs. It specifies no prohibitions during the bout. Teams choose representatives by any procedure, can repeat them and can let the operator select at random when there is no volunteer. Refusal then counts as surrender. Fewer surrenders is the first tiebreak, another duel is the next. A team mathematically assured of clearing can leave and yields later pairings by default. Twelve wins are a sufficient bound from the stated arithmetic, not the only route. The main text later reports security at nine and ten in its actual pairing state. S21 P0278–0316/P0545–0555/P0732–0745/P0896–0905.

The first round’s screen preserves everyone on the board and displays team tallies, so surrender works at first. At 7–1 Yūki's team seems likely to stay safe. It becomes precarious after she stops playing. At eight wins late in the tournament, who lost by surrender matters. A team that loses through a death may still have a collective tiebreak chance that the dead member cannot experience. At the individual level concession preserves two bodies in round nine while ending one person's claim on a win. At the collective level it is one reason a near-threshold position may become unsafe. Yūki's claim that she has to kill Tamamo is the decision the tightening field brings her to make. Surrender remains an announced means to end a duel. No final ranking or enforcement is narrated and the team view cannot yield an exact casualty total. S21 P0545–0561/P0611–0652/P0715–0765/P0887–0905/P1015–1019, S23 P0007–0019.

## SBY-V05-E013 — Responsibility without a metric

「卑しい根性」 travels from Tamamo's explanation of her own replacement fantasy to Yūki's description of her prior desire for any death to happen out of her sight. Both could use the language of self-improvement to keep something else out of view. Neither has direct authority over the other's whole motive. One separation can contain a good pedagogical point and a decision to evade distress. The split becomes consequential in the length of time she cannot bring the blow down. At the closest distance she can hardly distinguish attacking another person from attacking herself. The prose withdraws an explicit grammatical subject from the inner invective. It makes the two available targets overlap without stating that Tamamo said or thought these words. S21 P0876–0883/P0917–0935/P1057–1092.

Yūki's evident physical upper hand and her reluctance do not lead to the earlier pattern of preserving the life of a subdued opponent. The internal speech reasserts her value as a player, then the repetitions of 「深く刺した」 retain the effort of doing what she has resisted. Their effect does not depend on forensic identification of a fatal blow. The narrative treatment strongly indicates Tamamo's death given the represented act and confirmed #62 clearance, while ending at the stakes of what Yūki did. No independent terminal witness or final adjudication closes Tamamo's individual case. Room viewing could disseminate a performance and still leave private motives unavailable. S21 P1057–1102, S23 P0007–0029.

## Exit

The stable conclusions are an independently verified referral, precise new impairments with different causes and outcomes, an actual two-way teaching relation with unequal terms, a declared local system that permits concessions and rewards some harms, and observable changes in how each student uses inherited knowledge. Yūki's #62 clear is explicitly reported by her agent and corroborated by waking outside with partially recovered function. No official roster or examination of Tamamo is shown.
