## Current-unit story synopsis

A speaker without a name would accept dying but refuses to remain beaten by a particular person. Scrap Building takes place three months after Candle Woods. Yūki wakes on a dark fifth floor for her tenth game and meets four participants who already know one another. Her participation count is higher than their elected leader Mishiro's eighth, but the others trust Mishiro. The five search by flashlight. They identify an unnamed sixth player dead in a fall, infer that visible openings are only part of the threat, and try to distribute the risk of leading. Mishiro doubts Yūki's reported experience and sees her unverified hunch as an attempt to avoid being exposed. A route that Yūki considers dangerous turns out to be passable. The four others discount her next warning, that lighting every floor will exhaust their batteries where they need light most.

On the third floor Kotoha sustains catastrophic injury after an apparent success in handling a fictional mine. The protective rope becomes involved in the accident. Mishiro's team decides against retracing its route. Giving up her lamp at Mishiro's insistence, Yūki goes alone, finds Kotoha conscious and carries her. She calls this credit-seeking and shows Kotoha one of the previously unexplained pieces of white paper. With Kotoha's fading lamp they descend and confront a black wolf-like animal on the second floor. It carries a severed arm, keeps back from the beam and withdraws after the light runs out. Keito and Chie have fled the animal and are hiding. They join Yūki, who finds the stairwell to the lighted first floor. Chie takes over carrying Kotoha, while Yūki goes back for Mishiro.

Mishiro is sheltering in the dark after the animal took her right forearm. She reviews her reasons for entering a game in which she could rank first. Yūki finds her and demands the apology Mishiro previously demanded from her. Instead, Mishiro gives voice to her humiliation and runs. Yūki, who intended to help anyway, is surprised and pleased by Mishiro's refusal. She intervenes to save her from the beast and gives her directions to the stairs. On the first floor Kotoha tells Chie that the supplied paper and white dresses suggest a compulsory vote and promises to choose someone else. At the exit the players learn that the least-contributing player according to their votes will die. Chie is selected and screams through the walls of the locked booths. Yūki's decision to submit a slip for every other player leaves the counted choice to the organizer's opening order. Four clear. Mishiro apologizes, then rages in her agent's car. She asks for an ordinary prosthesis and intends to improve enough to defeat Yūki.

Before Golden Bath, Hakushi describes the Wall of Thirty as a professionally observed threshold and recalls the experience as a curse. After suffering severe injury in her 29th game, Yūki feels her judgment and confidence have deteriorated over several games. Tsutomu Kaneko, the father of Kinko from Ghost House, finds Yūki and asks her to take a tracking capsule into the next game. He wants to locate and expose the organizers. He imagines Yūki as someone who would welcome release from the profession and offers financial and employment help. She cannot voice her refusal or her own goal and leaves with the capsule. By accident she swallows it when her agent gives her water for the ordinary sedative. She takes the sedative too.

Mikan wakes in a shower unit, finds a numbered gold shoe-locker tag and is drowned by other players after reaching the exit approach. The narrative begins again with Yūki waking in a similar unit, much later than the others. Yūki's tag is quickly put into the communal stock of an outnumbered team led by Azuma, a seventh-appearance player who initially attacks her in a misunderstanding. Azuma knows the baths, the steam-filled approach to the changing room and a larger group controlling access to the exit. The prizes for matching tags to lockers are shoes that protect against the electrified entrance floor. There are too few for every participant. When asked about a hypothetical offer of rescue from her profession, Azuma says she chose this work even if she lacked other viable paths. The two prepare to defend their outdoor bath, expecting an eventual bargain once enough players have died.

Past the steam and beyond a bamboo wall there is another bath. The reader gains the fuller map through Mishiro, who leads the entrance group and whose ally Riko has returned from defeating the Silver Bath group. Six of the remaining eleven try to reach the exit directly before the entrance faction gets back. Five, including Azuma, die. Yūki is the sixth. The pursuer who reaches her is Mishiro, eight months after their encounter in Scrap Building and now forty games into her own career.
