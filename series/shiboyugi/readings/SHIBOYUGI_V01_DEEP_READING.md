## Current-unit story synopsis — unit-end spoilers

**Ghost House, appearance 28.** Yūki wakes in a windowless, black-and-white bedroom in a maid costume. Five other costumed participants have reached a dining room. Kokutō has played once before. Kinko took on her parents' debt as her own responsibility, Beniya entered with a debt of her own, Momono says an easy-job promise brought her there without consent, and Aoi says she had no other option without naming it. Yūki warns that they must escape a building with lethal traps and says she wants to get as many of them out as possible. The group takes cues from the food she eats, stays close when it sets out, and fails to search the dining table until Kokutō realizes the key bundle is under the platter. Raising it triggers a projectile that kills Kokutō. Yūki secures the key and the other four elect to proceed. (S12 P0003–0094, P0141–0244, P0253–0387.)

They enter a six-sided room that closes behind them. After testing alternatives the five pull levers in successive configurations. The final one locks each player into a sector while blades descend. Yūki uses the key bundle to stop her own blade assembly and puts it through a gap that others can reach. Beniya gets free first among the others and diverts the key to Momono. Kinko loses her right hand freeing herself enough to reach the key after Momono. Kinko survives. Aoi dies. For Momono and Beniya, Aoi's apparent white stuffing introduces what Yūki calls the players' pre-game bodily alteration, 防腐処理. Kinko apologizes repeatedly and the earlier group formation breaks up. (S12 P0391–0613.)

The four reach an elevator with a 150 kg label and a one-use notice. Yūki proposes sharing the reduction needed to ride together. Kinko offers to stay behind and locks herself in an adjoining sauna. Momono initially restrains Yūki from intervening. Yūki convinces Momono and Beniya that their three-person load would probably still exceed the limit and uses an object recovered from a hallway trap to get Kinko out. After searching unsuccessfully for a less damaging route, the four share severe injuries and reduce their load enough to use the lift. Yūki carries Kinko. They emerge at an inner hall and briefly speak of returning to chores, food and unfinished obligations. (S12 P0617–0896.)

At the locked outer door two of three crossed-person lights are lit. Momono urges them to look for another reading and Beniya suggests the symbols might count obstacles. Yūki concludes a third person must die. Acting under her own rule to select the nearest person once she has decided a killing is called for, she kills Kinko. The third light goes on and the door opens. Yūki steps outside and apologizes. Momono and Beniya are last shown standing in shock at the open exit. Yūki is brought home, finds her body restored, gives the dead three minutes of attention, hangs up her repaired maid outfit and begins a private review. (S12 P0893–0954.)

**Candle Woods, appearance 9.** A remembered lesson warns Yūki against fighting a person who specializes in killing. The story then returns to an earlier Yūki, aimless and living in a neglected flat. Her agent fetches her in the night. In a fake forest Yūki finds herself among three hundred costumed rabbits with her mentor Hakushi, entering her 96th game after 95 clears. Hakushi leads an offensive against the thirty Stumps. The rabbits are meant to last a week. Each Stump is told to kill five rabbits during that week or die. Unbeknown to Yūki, the only experienced Stump, sixteen-year-old Moegi, has already forced novices to practise killing on members of their own faction. Three die. Moegi draws on the teaching of her mentor, Kyara, while feeling responsible for the deaths. (S15 P0003–0186, P0190–0337, P0345–0387.)

The start opens access to the maze and facilities. Experienced rabbits turn the numerical imbalance to their advantage. At base, Sumiyaka warns Yūki about a missing member of the apparent beginner group. Yūki learns the Stumps fear their leader from a questioning of the captive Kushieda which Hakushi has ordered kept free of violence. The Stumps suffer heavy losses. Airi, already reported to have four kills, tells Moegi about a mutilated Stump and a discarded rabbit costume. Moegi recognizes signs of Kyara. Hakushi separately warns the rabbits of a killer in their own ranks, then the hall fills with smoke. Yūki escapes. Moegi decides that Kyara could leave too few rabbits alive to meet the quota and turns on other Stumps as competitors, killing Hikawa after a request for assistance. She encounters a rabbit who defeats and kills her, revealed as Yūki at the shift in focalization. Moegi dies in tears as the need to act strong drops away. Yūki reads frustration in her face and is disturbed that her own ability overcame someone who wanted something from these games. (S15 P0341–0838.)

Yūki returns to check Hakushi despite the known risk and finds Sumiyaka dead and a devastated body she takes to be her mentor's. Kyara claims responsibility for the latter, admits hiding among novices and attributes her outburst to anger after taking the looser clothing. Yūki temporarily incapacitates Kyara, then learns that she has concealed body armor and is badly hurt in return. Kyara's insistence that Yūki shares her affinity for this world leaves Yūki unable to act on a ready defense of her life. Yūki declares she will finish Hakushi's 99-clear project. She uses that commitment to move again, works out that a usable weapon may be concealed in the smashed rabbit mascot, finds it and finally kills Kyara. Airi encounters her afterward, reports that she has met her quota and disarms. The coda counts Yūki, Airi and unexpectedly Hakushi as the three survivors out of 330. (S15 P0814–1183.)

**Lifetime Job.** Yūki wakes at home after Candle Woods repaired. The aim she voiced in the hall now makes staying in bed feel wrong. She counts the costumes she has kept and recalls two lost to mold, recovering her nine-clear total. The story steps back to the cleanup. With no further action after Yūki and Airi's encounter, the operators end the game on day three. Hakushi's agent addresses the apparently dead Hakushi, who stands and speaks despite her injuries. She has privately modified her body, says she deliberately entrusted the target to Yūki, retires after her 96th clear and declines archive access. The story returns to Yūki as she cleans, records past games and prepares for evening school. Her need for knowledge grows directly out of her choice to become a professional player. (S17 P0003–0145.)

## Candle Woods

### SBY-V01-E007 — Two briefings and two forms of group power

Hakushi received the rabbit mascot briefing and relays it to Yūki. Moegi hears the Stump tree explain terms and then openly filters the performance from what she reports. She knows about individual last-hit credit and the internal penalty before Yūki has proof that the stumps have weapons. The rabbits infer a clock from a visible countdown. Zero actually opens previously hidden entryways. The game uses cosmetic roles to organize attention and a quantitative quota to motivate hunters. The hunters' individual completion conditions make cooperation a means, not a pooled score. The rabbits' 150-death estimate under the assumption that all thirty Stumps individually meet their five-kill minimum helps mobilize an offensive. It places no limit on actual deaths. The true collapse later shows why a quota should be read as a minimum achievement for one player rather than a fixed limit on harm. (S15 P0123–0186, P0190–0248, P0341–0353, P1177–1181.)

Moegi faces 29 other beginners who can reasonably suspect she is part of the setup. She expects them to be outmatched if each tries to win alone, and anticipates the rabbits' counteroffensive without hearing them plan it. She adopts Kyara's violent instruction. Hikawa is among the first to comply under threat and Kabane becomes a victim, alongside two others whose agency and identity the prose does not fill in. The fact that later recruits simply copy earlier acts and require another act of coercion to change course qualifies Moegi's success. Some can fight later. That gain does not make her a capable general or relieve her of responsibility. She identifies herself as principal author of the deaths and invokes her guilt over small everyday excess change as evidence that she has to overcome hesitation. Her ruthless appearance is labour: she composes herself out of the group's sight, then later fears what an ineffective tyrant's own party may do. (S15 P0249–0337, P0483–0516.)

Hakushi bans violence in novice questioning of Kushieda. As Yūki relays it, the reason concerns how a large group behaves once violence becomes an ordinary resource. Experienced rabbits are still permitted to hunt Stumps, and the novice team's tickling has coercive force of its own. From the signs of fear Yūki infers the opposing faction's internal organisation and says she might have used similar means in Moegi's position. This makes any categorical moral attribution based on faction labels unstable. Hakushi's strategy uses a different supply of competence, responsibilities and time. The encounter samples Hakushi's warning in detail. Yūki's usual assessment of injury fails when Kyara rises from seemingly decisive wounds. Yūki selects a bounded repeated undertaking rather than the revenge or personal proof she considers as alternatives. ## Lifetime Job and the two mentor histories

### SBY-V01-E010 — A surviving mentor whom the reader has twice lost

The final chapter opens on Yūki elsewhere and rewinds to a point she does not see. Hakushi's agent knows the body is feigning death. The subsequent performance directly confirms an extraordinary survival. The agent does not know how the private alteration works, and Hakushi reports modification and deterioration rather than licensing a specific physiology or resurrection. This gives three distinguishable bodily routes: the organizers' treatment and later repair, Kyara's array of protection, and Hakushi's privately altered ability to survive apparent disintegration. Yūki's right eye functions at home. The illustrated profile separately lists an injured right iris and no impairment of sight at 27 clears. The profile gives no cause for that listing. (S15 P0842–0852, P0943–0965, P1177–1181, S17 P0007–0012, P0044–0085, S03-I01.)

The narration reports that the organizers ended the game on day three because they saw no point in continuing. The agent then appears in the cleanup. That is a rare ordinary preference disclosed in the same scene that confirms a near-mythical ability. The agent's claim that the operation wants stars provides interested testimony about the 30 wall. The text gives three different levels of access to this question: Sumiyaka's fear about her 29th appearance and subsequent death, Hakushi's memory and curse comparison, and staff rationale. ### SBY-V01-E011 — What Yūki does after declaring an ambition

Hakushi privately says she gave her purpose to her pupil and mentioned that transfer to Kyara, while remaining surprised at the words Yūki used. The page does not show Yūki receiving this plan from Hakushi, hearing the retirement or learning the method of the bodily alteration. The downstream behavior that S17 does show belongs to Yūki. At 5 p.m., two hours before her ordinary waking time, the thought of what the successor should do breaks an established pattern of a return to bed. She puts the meal aside to count her outfits, undertakes concrete household work, begins a notebook, buys hangers and addresses diet and exercise. Evening school is a practical choice because her narrowly cultivated ability did not teach her folklore, arithmetic or everything else on which high-level survival might depend. In the final running scene her self-conscious preparation in a purchased sailor uniform produces a public entrance into daylight she can make without the ordinary fear of looks governing her course.
