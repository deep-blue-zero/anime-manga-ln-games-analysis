# Shiboyugi V01–V10

Four people reach the exit of Yūki’s twenty-eighth game. Believing an additional death is required, Yūki kills Kinko there. Yūki steps outside and says a short apology. Momono and Beniya are last shown alive beside the open door. At home Yūki gives the dead three minutes of attention without apology or grief, checks her repaired body, hangs up the repaired costume and reviews what she did. The first book then shows where she acquired a reason to keep coming back. At the end of the tenth book she has reached ninety-three, returned seriously injured and become the object of a dispute among people who want to keep her alive or make her able to fight. Which of those goods directs the next act is unsettled. These books repeatedly make somebody finish a task before it is possible to know what the task will cost, who can answer for it, or whether its actual accomplishment makes anything else more possible. V01 S12 P0893–0954 and S15 P1033–1072/S17 P0127–0145, V10 S23 P0304–0376/S26 P0007–0069.

The lifework of survival yields distinct changes at different scales. It supplies some people with real income, new abilities and a reason to get out of bed. It redistributes their reliance on strangers, teachers and specialists and can make the choice it once gave them harder to exercise. The novels make this visible by following differentiated participants into work and domestic life, giving scenes information the people doing the work cannot all see, and resisting the conversion of somebody's count into an answer about her whole life. That is a contestable interpretation, not a unified moral diagnosis. Hakushi’s and Hitomi’s different departures, Airi’s private decision to stop, Koyomi’s refusal of a contest and the real cooperation between deeply unequal parties each have their own causes and effects. V01 S17 P0044–0119, V03 S23 P0060–0093, V04 S17 P0280–0438, V10 S23 P0451–0459 and S24-I01.

## One count and several histories

The series opens inside well-developed practice. The reader sees Yūki negotiate help in Ghost House, answers some of the questions the players ask about her, then encounters the earlier Candle Woods enterprise. The second position of Candle Woods changes the first. A norm of recording, keeping clothes and performing a particular ritual came to someone who used to live in a largely nocturnal and unfurnished life. She speaks ninety-nine when Kyara has made it hard to speak or act for any further reason. Yūki’s response can be a tactical need and a meaningful long-term choice together. She recovers a weapon she believes her mentor left for someone who could use it, survives and then makes a deliberate effort to know more about the ordinary world, starting with school. Evidence after the occasion gives the stated objective a much firmer basis than the moment’s speech. V01 S12 P0136–0170/P0893–0954, S15 P0095–0174/P0974–1073/P1110–1133, S17 P0013–0040/P0085–0094/P0127–0145. [The first volume’s two relevant career stages](readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md).

The order later supplies two more starting points. The novice of #3 meets a competent opponent who spares her under specific conditions and demands to train her. She objects to having a choice made for her and accepts once the offer becomes voluntary. #1 is shown later still. Some possible origins of exceptional composure can only then be evaluated. Yūki is strikingly able to move and to aid an injured participant. She also thinks the events are a production in which a push from a bridge will not kill the woman blocking it. Setsuna, who knows what an entry can cost, sees the very same action as an appalling and admirable instance of decisive conduct. She must rethink both when the partner she is first ready to blame for harm lends a shoulder, fails to understand her confession and explains what she saw. Yūki later chooses to keep playing after knowing more. Formal autonomy, knowledge of harm and competent action can each occur without the others. V05 S16 P0038–0146, V06 S18 P0331–0367/P0381–0469/P0679–0796. [Earliest apprenticeship](readings/SHIBOYUGI_V05_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v05-e002--who-can-accept-the-invitation), [the first game reconstructed](readings/SHIBOYUGI_V06_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v06-e005--two-incompatible-examples-in-one-person).

Sometimes the late disclosure changes what someone already knew before acting. The V09 phone scene provides a more complete account of the earlier #73.5 call. By the time Yūki declines to associate further after the violence at Tochinoki, her information includes the proposal for the 99th occasion that the audience had not entirely seen her receive. The actual damage at home and the disclosure of her earlier knowledge both matter. At a different point V10 adds the words Airi heard at Candle Woods. They help explain a sustained curiosity that did not seem to follow entirely from family need or available pleasure. Nothing about the placement of this memory entitles Yūki to know what outcome it had. Chronology is thus an account of how somebody can have a formative effect before she chooses to interpret it, and of when a viewer can justifiably change a judgment about a past action. ## Trust without agreement on the facts

The first novel uses a readable bodily description as a point of false finality. Yūki sees Hakushi grievously injured and believes she is dead. In the following sequence the position of the apparent corpse and its owner's actual survival can both be described faithfully. Her own agent recognizes a feint but lacks a complete technical account of it. Later, Koyomi supplies information Yūki previously had no way to check, and coming to the bar allows her to verify that someone who has received an almost unthinkable injury is recognizably the woman who taught her. The plot preserves the genuine effect of believing the mentor had died. Imitation by Etsusei presses the same problem into a formal murder puzzle, where a participant shifts from apparent victim through lethal concealment to offering the wrong kind of rescue. People infer that a corpse has to be a corpse in part because they have seen what violence did elsewhere. Other details, such as the effects of bodily devices and their removal, make it possible to act knowingly without arriving at every relevant conclusion. V01 S15 P0842–0852/P1177–1181 and S17 P0044–0085, V03 S17 P0138–0276/S19 P0130–0358/P0482–0529 and S21 P0003–0023/P0439–0455, S23 P0010–0056.

The same dependence on an account persists in ordinary life. A familiar tenant in a hurry says a slightly more marked good evening. A trained observer, Yayoi, detects the variation and has reason to worry about what it means. On a later occasion the words do precede an unusual request. Professional attention to appearance works in both directions, detecting real facts about occupation while generating a mutual error between an assassin and a professional player. Real help in the household and under severe attack makes trust develop even without correcting it. Ramona can answer somebody’s question about the research in limited terms and preserve what was genuinely at stake in the earlier coercion. The difference between refusal, silence, a prompt to supply another word and a full answer depends on who is entitled to ask. A particular education continues because a neighbor teaches something practical. Neither woman's professional concealment erases that benefit. V07 S19 P0076–0109/P0289–0376, S21 P0240–0315/P0420–0467, V08 S20 P0288–0345/P0529–0622/P0762–0788 and S22 P0051–0069. [Who learns what at Tochinoki](readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e012--the-city-as-an-arrangement-of-disclosures).

The format repeatedly makes the distribution of knowledge visible. A short wake-up chapter advances a clock without giving its first focal subject causal access to the last event. The writing can put the forbidden institutional term 運営 underneath the more discreet あれ as a ruby, separating two readings of what people near a classroom exchange could have heard. A recurring graphic presentation through an imagined recording frame gives form to the idea that virtuosity can be a performance for unseen observers. Printed labels, color and exact count at a separate publication position can add a detail without validating every inference about timing or a universal source of narrative voice. Formal credibility is not the same as procedural authority. Hakushi’s agent announces her 96th clear at Candle Woods in V01, and Yūki’s account in V05 supplies 96 again. The V06 narration supplies a conflicting 95. Evidence does not license the private-record explanation some characters could find congenial. The split attaches to the exact counts while leaving retirement, reused skills and an ensuing investigation demonstrable. ## An institution that can really rule and repeatedly lacks control

The institution bears on a person's immediate choice before it can reliably narrate its own mechanisms. In Ghost House, a lock can be turned into a private assessment of who has to die. The observed opening makes the action effective without certifying that the number of dead was the only possible explanation. Scrap Building requires a secret ballot and the result really condemns Chie, who has insisted that chance allotted her a limited opportunity to contribute. Yūki refuses to single out a participant and submits four slips simultaneously. She thinks her act might have decided the outcome. She never learns which name counted, and the exact effect depends on the other votes and the announced tie procedure. An external procedure makes it possible for a person to distribute or defer responsibility without taking away its effect. The option of a shared external measure of value and the apparent caprice of whom it actually selects are equally necessary to both episodes. V01 S12 P0871–0954, V02 S12 P0925–0970/P1002–1005/P1049–1118. [An actual vote and its conditions](readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e005--taking-a-secret-ballot-at-its-word).

At Royal Palace some costs can be read in advance. The actual team competition allows surrender, distributes opportunities unevenly over an eighteen-pairing schedule and counts fewer surrenders as a way to break ties. The same act that preserves a life in one round raises a later chance of team elimination. Shiro can make a skilled opponent yield. The story gives Yūki’s tally, the capacity she acquires and her decision under comparatively late constraints. Setsuna confirms Yūki’s clear as her assigned agent. Shiro later supplies separate evidence about Tamamo’s death and the favorable tiebreak. Each remains smaller than a restoration of a complete scorecard. A story in which the rules wholly force a killing would misstate a decision the participant made, just as a story in which individual temperament alone decides it would lose actual material incentives and limited access to cooperating bodies. V05 S21 P0278–0316/P0545–0561/P0732–0765/P0887–0905/P1048–1100, S23 P0007–0028, V07 S23 P0152–0178. [The value and price of conceding](readings/SHIBOYUGI_V05_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v05-e009--rules-visible-behavior-and-the-pressure-to-yield).

V10 widens that distribution to an unusual number of people. The original scale plate gives a commercial reason for bringing many competitors together, and the invitations, rooms and initial deadline actually create unequal windows to prepare. A private force can unbalance groups, yet Shiro’s early sense that the contest will stop once the unaccounted-for killer is dead gives way to an official notice that play continues. Reaching an escort changes an apparent prohibition into a test of the organization’s own declaration. An official with the appearance of a child grants Yūki’s appeal and makes the permission symmetrical. A route over exposed water and a player in a defensible position promptly make the equal formal condition materially unequal. On the ship some people begin trying to fulfill their own survival aims by turning on others in their faction. The book can show the lethal institution enforcing something its staff did not anticipate. It can also locate limits to its management of how people satisfy such a condition. V10 S17-I01, S19 P0001–0007/P0255–0262, S21 P0035–0054, S23 P0304–0376/P0427–0429/P0463–0542 and S26 P0007–0028. [Rules, material constraints and adjudication](studies/SHIBOYUGI_GAME_RULES_AND_ADJUDICATION.md).

The two common views of the organization both require revision. The fixed procedural map has support in clear boundaries, official messages and successful use of an appeal. It cannot do justice to disparate titles that hide one common event, variable information at invitation or the unanticipated consequences of making people compete across asymmetries. The omnipotent scheme would explain hostility but leave unclear why function, testing, practical aid and appeals have independent effects. Kūron’s account of the Wall of Thirty is a useful exact case. He supplies charts and an empirical denial of a special hazard, but no underlying data or method for the reader. Hakushi has heard of and remembers genuine irregularities, and can take the chart to be evidence while considering whether experienced players disproportionately notice surprising failures. A broad claim about fairness and a local event that looks unfair have different burden of proof. The ability to reject an aggregate explanation and still have a reason to take a specific institutional statement seriously is itself one form of skill. ## What life is being exchanged

Kokutō argues that selling an hour at a conventional job and gambling in the house are related ways to turn life into cash. It is a plausible analogy with much less force when time, injury, departure and knowledge have already been allocated differently. Kinko has volunteered for another person's debt. Beniya considers a future study plan because one payout will probably not cover her own. Momono would give up the reward for an exit. Airi keeps taking invitations after enough money makes it possible to leave. Sana can have a livable body and less practical ability to decline work under the same agreement. Her account of what would count as buying back a life after a required tally deserves more weight than a well-specified general rule’s claim to leave all players free. Procedures can actually provide opportunities for Airi, Yūki and some of their colleagues without creating the same kind of voluntariness for every case. V01 S12 P0187–0231/P0859–0891, V03 S12 P0145–0207, V09 S18 P0161–0184/P0335–0397 and S26 P0072–0111, V10 S24-I01. [Livelihood, spectators and the cost of care](studies/SHIBOYUGI_LIVELIHOOD_AND_CARE.md).

The business side does not belong wholly to the people it makes visible. Shiro can bow at the cameras in Royal Palace and suspect more attention could raise pay. Viewers and bettors supply two actual proposed sources of commercial income, and an unsigned explanation of larger games claims a possibility of disproportionately large return. It also says the typical games are small. A store of prizes lets Yūki finance particular private trials, but it takes an assigned account handler and a retired practitioner with another assessor’s knowledge to make her plan possible. A fellow player may occupy a very different position in an exchange. Kagura uses money, credit, hours of play and another woman’s professional access to recover Sana’s body and act on an obligation that can no longer benefit the departed in the ordinary material sense. With an apparently marketable object, what someone wants to take possession of and what a relationship lets her do come apart. A plausible critique of expendable labor and a recognition of meaningful mourning both follow from the same transaction. The story’s medical arrangements are an especially demanding instance of what it takes to stay capable. Early restoration of missing bits can make an observer think restoration is all the same. Some injuries receive useful replacement after practice, blindness creates a specific source of teaching, and a damaged limb once useful in daily life needs to remain present for other reasons. The move from three lost fingers to a later four-limb reconstruction needs a new association with a hospital, instruction and altered ordinary obligations. Examples include deciding with an artisan that the damage to the nerves does not presently justify replacing more of the arm, then agreeing after the operation to the shortening of the legs that followed the later attack. Both actions mean something, even though one gives input before alteration and the second survives a decision already taken. #86 interrupts the experiment of getting back to work with additional functional loss. What a limb can do, what somebody wishes it could do and the meaning of her assent continually pull away from each other. Ordinary things can indicate where a goal is still furnishing genuinely independent goods. A low rent and a kept closet name specific attachments. Learning a language and ordinary stories changes what a player can imagine, and teaching it does not require full knowledge of her other work. After leaving due to an actual security incident, Yūki keeps the lease and a specific reason to return. Later she assumes she may never finish school and says the learning still counts. The sailor outfit she handles while giving away her mementos touches the person she might have been able to become without giving school and work the same function. Another version of the costume at #93 is available for an entirely different purpose. One persuasive reading sees the time of crisis as the material cost of having chosen a goal too dependent on an employer. Another sees the goal making work, knowledge and relationships possible in the first place. The choices about books, clothes and residency supply the gains as well as the losses needed to adjudicate that tension. ## Keeping and passing on an ability

An unusually dangerous job makes it irresponsible to pretend that proficiency appears in a vacuum. Hakushi teaches someone enough to make her own practice and declines to have her view of the resulting conflict become an obligation. Etsusei can use a technical effect seen in recordings with genuine tactical consequence. She makes a different argument about what success in applying an example proves. Koyomi can recognize a situation she will do better to avoid and later stops when there is nothing to gain by imitating a kind of success she does not have. Kyara shows Moegi a skill in recognizing intent. Recognition of a teacher’s capacities can make the pupil feel most vulnerable to loss of her help. Mishiro receives aid even after refusing a display of subordination, and later does something similar in practice while placing a more explicit claim on the success of a grateful pupil. Riko’s attempt to fulfill that claim and Mitsuba’s attempt to learn without accepting an inherited grievance give different ways to remain a person who was helped. ## Violence and the kinds of attention it asks for

The first near-comic alteration of bodies is material world-building and a way for the operators to arrange what can be watched. The House’s novices ask why the actual dead body has no ordinary color or smell. Beniya reasons before she fully understands what that would imply about her own body. The image of white stuffing can reduce familiar kinds of sensory horror and facilitate new ones as the other characters realize it is part of their own working condition. In V03 excessive dispersal of the material lets people genuinely misclassify a body. Later mere survival of an altered body can be stronger grounds than what looks like a normally fatal wound. V10 Busutake’s own later recognition overrules emphatic earlier camera-mediated reporting. By then the possibility of seeming dead is a reason one character has to test whether an altered person remains an agent, while another has reason to misunderstand what she sees. Treating unusual physiology as a global rule of who cannot die, or as an excuse to dismiss the concrete costs, would defeat the novel’s own particular distinctions. A contrast within Yūki’s work gives a strong reason to separate harm from what its narration asks of the reader. In the early lift episode she says she can conveniently forget how people responded to being dismembered and that they deserve a degree of honor. The prose elides detailed reactions and retains evidence of decisions they make and what joint effort produces. At the door it emphasizes the ease of her murder and Kinko's initial charitable interpretation, preventing the accomplished escape from absorbing what it does to the person chosen. In V10 Yūki herself puts a mutilated body on display to frighten a wider group. Maya’s final perception of Yūki’s vitality, Airi’s normalization of the inscription, Busutake’s notice of the error and Yūki’s report from inside the tactic supply different appraisals. Yūki deliberately sets up the display. The spelling “Masacre” is her unintended mistake and provokes her own laugh. A competent act of violence can be at once presented as impressive, indicative of injury and intentionally displayed to make other people frightened. For Airi it becomes breathtaking that a person can repeatedly stand up. For Yūki its emotional absence remains strange. An uncomplicated portrait of giving oneself over to joy in killing cannot explain her attested practical reasoning. Declaring the scene only a tactical display cannot account for the changed feeling she reports or the changing social use of what she does. Institutional permission also fails as a complete moral explanation. That choice toward Kōen has a protective and a threatening part, and it relies on attracting her toward something the novels have treated as both paid professional option and system of coerced choices. ## When helping means choosing for someone

Airi furnishes a useful comparison because inquiry, allegiance and bodily vulnerability each change what she will do. She pursues the unknown motive of a colleague by trying to work out when what she sees is reliable. She is capable of accepting an arrangement that gives her an exact further objective without sharing the organizer's desired reward. When she would stake herself in a dangerous last exchange, Shiro prevents an action she thinks would compromise the survival of an ally and candidate. Maguma takes her actual immobilizing hold as evidence of a willingness to pay that price. A third person's analysis of a request supplies an actionable option where no one can truly share all the evidence. Subsequent rescue by Maguma comes from a new appraisal after the cost and the danger have changed. Honoring a person's earlier bid can coexist with protecting her later. Distinguishing an offered sacrifice from a license that expires at its immediate context allows each choice to remain intelligible. Setsuna and Yūki give the same problem years to develop in a different form. The recipient of help after the first game takes a job helping the woman who provided it. Later Yūki trusts a worker who still keeps that past to herself. In V02 Setsuna has actually aligned herself with those who want to see competitors stake everything and is prepared to keep outsiders from interfering. Her early formal cooperation and that hard position give force to material changes later. The closet and the hospital bring her to think about whether continuing has made the seeker lose meaningful attachments. In the actual exchange Setsuna gives the response of a competent provider and privately plans to reverse the disposition of the clothes. She thinks of supporting a pause and then accuses herself of having helped cause the present damage. Continued help, a privately reaffirmed commitment and a chosen refusal to speak can support both devotion and severe complicity. Their choices together in the Snow Room give one exact condition in which she can use her own judgment and follow Yūki’s call. The clothes and the clinical conclusion leave a more general decision about living and working together open. Several earlier episodes are too distinct to read the last outcome as a final judgment against all aid. Hitomi chooses a real kind of ordinary future after receiving disruptive knowledge.
