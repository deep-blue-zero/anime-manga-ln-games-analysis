# Rules, material constraints and adjudication

Shiboyugi makes a distinction between a disclosed goal, what the organizers actually enforce and what is practically possible. On its own, no one of the three accurately predicts who will survive. A door can open after a killing without proving beforehand that a killing is required. A technically legal path can be physically unavailable. An observer with useful surveillance equipment can state a result that the event’s recognized adjudicator has not certified and that another scene later disproves. The novels derive moral pressure from the time participants have to compete, cooperate or challenge an interpretation before a fact of enforcement becomes available. V01 S12 P0893–0954, V06 S18 P0473–0537, V10 S21 P0035–0054/S23 P0231–0248/P0304–0376/S26 P0026–0069.

Evidence here follows a particular claim at the time of decision. A prior statement of eligibility can avert a penalty, a current inference can support a local choice, and a final result may verify only some consequences. The record of those differences is worth a separate home because they change causal events and ethical judgments across volumes, including the due weight of a kill threshold or promise. Cases are selected where details of authority, practical access or enforcement change the interpretation of a decision. [Current scope and source register](../SHIBOYUGI_SOURCE_REGISTER.md).

## A room is already a partial statement

Ghost House gives apparently six matching positions, one shared means of protection, and a separate final exit with lamps. In the lever room clear material behavior is more helpful than standing. The crew both reasons about ways of distributing help and encounters delays that prescriptive speech cannot remove. Kokutō’s earlier correction of the search locates actual useful property and also ends in her death. The mechanism vindicates looking there while discrediting one inference about the safety of using it. This occurrence matters to the last threshold. Two lit marks and the experience of two deaths give Yūki one explanatory rule. Beniya proposes a rival rule tied to the group’s passed obstacles and Momono wants further work on the puzzle. Yūki kills the nearest person under the selection discipline she has adopted. The change of the third lamp and opening of the exit strongly warrant a relationship in the realized route. There is still a distinct question about the availability of another trigger, how long uncertainty could have been borne and why she instead commits another person’s life. Proving that the chosen act suffices gives no proof that every alternative was excluded. V01 S12 P0304–0384/P0391–0613/P0871–0954. [Ghost House](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md).

In Maiden Race bodies and time give conditions before any dependable account does. Movement of the start, collapse and disposition of the last bridge make standing still actionable. The players proceed one at a time. Kanade draws the first place, but the others let her delay. She starts nineteenth, after two bridges have collapsed. With less than twenty minutes on the original platform and others still to cross, Yūki decides to clear the obstruction and pushes her. The clock still has time. Yūki’s judgment under pressure causes an actual death. The later doors more plainly mark scarcity, but the participants know very little about the location of keys, whether one use closes off another person and who needs to make which move at once. Thirteen of fifty survive although fifteen doors are in principle usable, because two keys go unfound. Keeping distinct the number of available doors, the practical access to them and the success of named participants prevents a deceptively clean result. The actual #77 construction with marked one-use routes stages a later echo without a wholly identical objective or access problem. V06 S18 P0079–0173/P0381–0537/P0679–0761 (the final counts at P0758–0760), V09 S18 P0003–0098/P0105–0164/P0490–0617. [Maiden Race](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V06_DEEP_READING.md), [Gimmicky Mansion](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V09_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v09-e002--a-communal-search-becomes-a-scarcity-problem).

The paper visible before the last part of Scrap Building makes a vote plausible, with no particularly accessible early instruction about how the participants will allocate it. The procedure later delegates a notion of contribution to secret choice. Chie sharply identifies the issue that a game can randomly give different opportunities to contribute. The judgments she voices before the booth and the actual ballots are differently accessible. Yūki submits a slip for each of the others. The selected outcome depends on a process she also cannot see. One might call this an impartial allocation by one participant, a delegation of responsibility to the counting procedure, or both at once. This case manufactures democracy through a structure in which the decisive metric has little shared content and where silence at the booth can conceal action. The result establishes Chie as the victim and others as clears. It fails to supply a general reconstruction of other people’s ballots from prior words. V02 S12 P0925–0970/P1002–1005/P1063–1085. ## Who gets to cooperate

In Candle Woods hundreds of people receive an asymmetrical one-week problem. Most must survive, while the opposing group must meet individual killing requirements. Existing training, weapons, information and the treatment of costume make a simple numerical advantage misleading. Some rules come through a mascot; the usable timing and possibilities for props require different observation. Players turn unfamiliar costumes and an uneven social repertoire into apparent role credentials that can be exploited. The killers' quota makes the survival of vulnerable members of one team a threat to members of the other even in the absence of immediate contact. Moegi reads that threat as a threat from fellow Stumps, who might have to compete for a narrowing pool. Goal and relation therefore change without a declaration of new teams. The end is called on day three when only three survive, which shows power to close this event before the admissible interval rather than a universal waiting policy. V01 S15 P0095–0174/P0366–0412/P0443–0598/P0612–0688/P1162–1181, S17 P0044–0085. [Candle Woods](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md).

Golden Bath reserves recognizable objects and access bottlenecks at two related places. What Azuma’s smaller working group can see makes a locally reasonable arrangement until it learns that its tagged advantage is accessible from a different side. Yūki’s willingness to disclose a route does not establish what a specific trap will do to each body. The eventual institutional count can be assigned to gross attendance and prior equipment in one version of the event, with the causal fates of people who took distinct routes separately visible. The presence of an omitted or unexpectedly altered entry condition in #30 also needs different proof. A tracker was definitely received and later retrospective testimony supports interception in pregame examination. A late wake and an assigned agent's inability or unwillingness to specify the order support circumstantial inference of a personalized response. The exact operator, order and real out-of-game effect of the attempt remain unresolved. Cooperation among people competing on apparently common ground is fragile before any distinct actor intervenes. V02 S16 P0096–0250/P0332–0538/P0713–0808/P1043–1116, S18 P0025–0072, V09 S21 P0189–0194. [What the group can see](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e009--how-much-of-the-bath-does-a-player-know).

Cloudy Beach assigns a role whose failure after a week would lead to execution in Etsusei’s own account. She must actually kill at least three people within the week and let the others see their bodies. Ordinary players must leave on the rescue vessel. Etsusei stages her own apparent death, then boards the operator’s vessel before closure and uses equipment from it. The others see a concealed figure and distrust her. The few routes off the island and the devices other people discover and remove change which tactics will work. Three actions by Yūki are understandable only by taking the reported role carefully: she treats her own death by a trap as irrelevant to the killer’s credit, turns to terrain earlier made dangerous by Maguma, and stops once she can herself leave. The actual receipt of rescue and delay gives narrower evidence about enforcement than some clauses of Etsusei’s assignment. Working cooperation to deter the person suspected of killing can survive an incentives argument about surrendering someone else, and the effects of removing a device do not prove every device’s capability from the start. ## The apparatus can rule against a plausible agreement

In Halloween Night, the available sweets predict survival only for someone who understands when a valid transfer must be made. Haine tries to satisfy a demand already answered by a call of Trick. The costumed pursuer will not accept the late sweet. Yūki and Shion later have too little for simultaneous demands and can temporarily work together despite a genuine contest between them. The result gives the actors reasons to change their method as sweets are consumed. Looking only at the rule words would lose the time and state of the encounter. Looking only at their actions would obscure why some people could avoid a fight altogether. V04 S21 P0003–0068/P0183–0227/P0448–0665/P0813–0977. [A competition for supplies at different moments](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md).

Royal Palace openly gives some of the pieces implied elsewhere. Ten teams of seven can each try eighteen pairings, yielding ninety duels in the double round robin. The top seven teams can clear. People for each bout can be chosen again, volunteer, be selected by chance or refuse and incur surrender. At least twelve wins guarantees a rank high enough on the stated arithmetic, while the actual late pairing state can secure some teams earlier. Fewer surrenders breaks a tie first and a further duel is available after that. The weight of yielding changes with a team's place. Shiro wins her bout against Yūki by forcing surrender, with the latter actually able to see the point of yielding. For a later participant approaching the cutoff, a death can preserve a better ranking than the simple act of remaining alive. That pressure helps explain Yūki's choice in fighting Tamamo, especially with an independent loss of sensory access and slow developments in other pairings. It does not establish that an announced alternative vanished or that every participant knew the final ranking. Shiro's later information clarifies part of the actual result, without giving a final roster. Hakushi can manipulate timing on her own side, with effects makers have failed to tell her will be possible on the other's. When the no-violence marker is removed and the hazard at the exit becomes operable, the assigned agent is asked to make one severe choice personally. The rules make some of the situation discussable without providing common knowledge of the experiment or proving that everyone accepted the same risks. ## Procedure outside the official announcement

The relevant restriction sometimes comes from a continuing employment contract. Sana learns how her medical support and choice of whether to play are entangled. A playable future is economically valuable and its formal terms are, in her view, intolerable. A bid to leave that costs twenty clears means something specific for her and cannot be generalized from a claim that anyone might decline an invitation. Another medic or caretaker cannot simply cancel the obligation. The shortest view of agency as choosing one of two opportunities ignores how long it takes to establish a new viable alternative. The assistant who believes her role is to keep work and treatment available supplies necessary help and acts from a perspective Sana does not wholly share. Borrowing evidence about registration from one case without comparing the context would make this agreement sound far more voluntary than it is. V09 S18 P0161–0184/P0260–0397/P0458–0504, S21 P0105–0137, S26 P0090–0111/P0204–0257. [A medically conditioned decision](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V09_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v09-e003--two-meanings-of-having-a-choice).

The body can also be a means of exercising or circumventing a nominal rule. The #73 game Mossygrove supplies faction guidance and indicators that Maya uses to find opponents. Its result is countable from her position only through what those instruments display. The last presently required death fails to appear at the moment she expects. Keito’s successful evasion is distinct from a claim about whether every part of Maya’s preparation was technically permitted or someone else seriously enforced a claim to ownership. Jamboree offers another distinct limit. An employee asks where a participant is because the organizers have relied on practice and never had to turn an operational assumption into text. Positive cases of actual enforcement are stronger evidence of a particular scope than a single staff member’s estimate that suspicious acts are generally illegal. Phantom Thief declares 20 designated exhibits and 20 thieves. The briefing says that for each export, one randomly chosen guard from a force of 40 will be killed. When Yūki shoots through it, the dress falls over Kagura. Kagura eventually exports it in its damaged state. The event ends at 22:00 because every thief has died or left. The report gives nineteen of twenty exhibits protected, nineteen thieves dead and seven guards dead at that point. Kagura’s export and announced clear establish the fate of the twentieth exhibit and the one successful departing thief. A case of injury, loss in a duel or unexpected lifesaving aid has to be reconstructed independently of the event's aggregate scores. Busutake helps Yūki survive a secondary assault after playing a notably different part against the thief Chiyo. The observable features of that rescue do not supply the precise formal count assigned to the victim. ## One threshold between two ships

On Jamboree the selected 422 defenders and 48 killers arrive on a deliberately disjoint schedule. The fourteen dummy titles, the unequal starting equipment and the physical scale turn rational information-gathering into uneven control over who can plan a defense and for how long. Survivors have grounds to organize before having the same view of the attackers or the conditions for a clear. Captives tell Shiro that a week and a figure of 140 deaths are decisive. The scope includes anyone assigned to the survivor faction who dies, regardless of who caused death. The official termination verifies that enough eventually die. She believes she has found all 47 opposing bodies other than Yūki and concludes the latter is dead. The signal that play continues has greater evidentiary force for that state of the game than her live operational count. Yūki actually survives and reappears. A later inference about her mobility becomes stronger with subsequent observation, rather than making prior search certain. V10 S19 P0001–0007/P0089–0131/P0255–0262/P0605–0624, S21 P0016–0054/P0092–0115, S23 P0278–0376/P0427–0429. [The initial count and its limit](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V10_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v10-e008--the-first-hour-and-its-disputed-counts).

A staff member treats boarding an escort vessel as prohibited. The staff had planned to keep the vessels out of reach and had not anticipated the boarding. Yūki invokes the bounds of the game area and the earlier use of the boat at Cloudy Beach. The adjudicator recognizes her boarding on the basis that the vessel is in the area and that every player who reaches it must have the same chance to board. That changes what staff and organizers may do to stop someone. It does not supply equally safe water, bodies or arrival positions. Yūki is already in a defensible place and actually shoots a person trying to cross. Once the ship becomes untenable and the killer seems beyond easy reach, some people on the defender side move against supposed burdens among their own. Shiro's earlier trust in grouping comes under a material revision independent of the exact motives of every person who turns to it. V10 S23 P0219–0248/P0304–0376/P0427–0429/P0437–0446/P0463–0542. The aggregate report finds 187 dead and 283 alive of 470. 187 is consistent with the earlier 47 and the required 140. Yūki privately estimates how much she personally caused, and infighting is directly depicted in some instances. There is no complete account of the causal allocation, equipment proof or terminal conditions of everyone whose private plans the book has visited. After the three horns the adjudicator confirms Yūki's survival and calls for treatment. The survivors are transported ashore, with those still on the sinking ship rescued. Airi’s private career decision and the later competing claims over Yūki’s treatment each follow a different course. A success can close an administrative case while intensifying the work and authority conflict that follows. V10 S23 P0427–0459/P0463–0542, S26 P0007–0069. ## The reach of institutional reports

Hakushi hopes operator data can help distinguish organized unfairness at #30 from veteran observation under a high cost of error. Kūron says a study shows no statistical hazard there and offers graphs. She sees evidence against what she expected. The reader has neither the study data nor its scope and method. Even if the negative aggregate claim were certain, extra scrutiny of one player who carried an object and the sequence of entry conditions would still require case-specific accounts. The observed authorization about a boat shows at least one contested exception becoming governable. The stories about what a 99th game does concern a very different level of authority. Reported nomination, choice of allies, single or divided managerial benefits and Kūron’s claimed reasons for the threshold can orient actual actions without proving a future proceeding or conducted election. An apparent historical recording changes the possibilities for knowing more, but the subject who obtains it is not shown checking its contents. V01 S17 P0095–0119, V02 S18 P0025–0072, V08 S22 P0138–0177, V09 S16 P0010–0045/S24 P0008–0038, V10 S16 P0033–0097/S23 P0304–0376. [A rule’s weight before the test](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V09_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v09-e008--the-wall-of-thirty-as-belief-and-as-claim).

A generous account of the institution finds procedures, appeals and accountability in the operator's enforcement. Some endings and conditions genuinely change when a recognized official acts. A hostile account finds profitable control of incomplete information and conditions chosen to turn participants against each other. The early forced choices and actual economic frame give it support. Evidence that people uncover real alternatives, band together across those pressures or successfully demand equal permission makes it hard to treat the guard room as omnipotent. Deliberate asymmetry, changed practical access and differently proved outcomes give the strongest account when they are combined. A claim about permission, an explanation for failure and a judgment about human willingness then remain independently checkable in each case.
