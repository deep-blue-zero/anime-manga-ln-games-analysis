# Livelihood, spectators and the cost of care

The games make money, but the usefulness of money depends on where the characters stand. A first fee might settle a bill, confirm aptitude, furnish a reason to return or compensate for a life a relative has already lost. A sizable balance can coexist with an old apartment, absence from class or dependence on somebody else to make it accessible. By V10 long practice may require a hospital, daily tests and the work of a different provider who claims to preserve a kind of capacity a patient has spent years maintaining. The change from one source of solvency to another gives material shape to decisions often discussed as matters of will. V01 S12 P0171–0231/P0844–0887/P0925–0954 and S17 P0127–0145, V09 S26 P0090–0111/P0204–0279, V10 S16 P0344–0408 and S26 P0029–0069.

There is enough repeated evidence for a study distinct from game adjudication. Relative ability to refuse, money actually transferable, housework, medical repair and spectator dependence change what an invitation offers before any recognized rules of play apply. This study follows the repeated failures of exchange to make these different goods commensurable. It also tests a contrary possibility. A person can obtain real relief or a more sustainable life through one of these exchanges, with no need to treat material help as a mere pretext for control. ## Entry, ordinary work and private risks

Early participants name different relationships between payment and risk. Kinko says family debt must be repaid. Beniya treats a financial liability as her own business and begins to think about repeated earnings and better preparation. Kokutō speaks of the price an ordinary job asks someone to pay in time and proposes the trade to a person who already does it. Momono asks only to be able to go home when she learns the danger. Aoi’s narrower personal account and later end preserve the gap between a stated money motive and next available response. These people can be in the same room and receive equally framed instructions while having radically different reasons to accept, bargain or feel deceived. Kinko's father later confirms concrete failed business and guilt rather than turning all the first-room statements into general economic laws. V01 S12 P0171–0231/P0286–0294/P0893–0954, V02 S16 P0083–0169. [Work and debt in Ghost House](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md).

Young Yūki leaves casual employment after conflict and takes an opaque offer. One entrant is informed about the risk, one assumes ordinary work, and one mistakes what she sees for an attraction. An opportunity to walk away only changes agency if the entrant can recognize the choice. Later Yūki quite plainly wants this to be her profession and builds cleaning, study, physical preparation and reflection around it. Her preference for a small cheap room and keeping money available have a positive domestic context. Its loss after outside violence depends on safety, not the emergence of a desire to consume fine lodgings. In a wealthy recruit's case an eighteen-year-old's trip on foot can express an ordinary preference. The small choices matter exactly because each person's money is only one part of what she can do with a day. V01 S15 P0024–0061/P0157–0174/S17 P0127–0145, V04 S19 P0007–0019/P0596–0606, V06 S18 P0025–0074/P0724–0761/S22 P0025–0040, V07 S16 P0003–0043/S17-I01, V08 S22 P0041–0097, V10 S16 P0101–0118.

Airi gets financial security around her thirtieth game and keeps playing. A lingering question about other participants and a habit of accepting work can coexist at that point. In contrast, Kagura takes actual first payment of around three million yen after an opaque offer and later uses work to accumulate cash with an entirely different recipient in mind. Sana believes being able to walk is real relief and judges the lifetime constraint that secured it coercive. An arrangement to sacrifice twenty clears in a bid for freedom shows that what looks like one tally can represent material concessions of health, independence and future time. Recovery from financial desperation is possible in the depicted system. Taking money once leaves neither lawful consent to an undisclosed event nor one permanent motive to return. V03 S12 P0145–0207, V09 S18 P0161–0184/P0335–0397/S21 P0275–0407/S26 P0072–0111/P0143–0257, V10 S16 P0283–0293. ## Goods acquired with a game

Being paid as an individual can make another person's labor invisible. In Scrap Building an institutional verdict about effort consumes secret votes from people whose actual opportunity to contribute has been uneven. Chie treats individual contributions as a bad general measure of the allocation of risk. In the same volume a father privately offers another stream of support to someone he is using to get potentially consequential information. His hope for disclosure, real concern for a daughter and disbelief that an earned livelihood could matter to Yūki come from different circumstances. The novel lets a participant’s dependence and an outsider's need to understand her motivate sincere actions on both sides without giving either person the same estimate of the offer. V02 S12 P0925–0970/P1002–1005/P1063–1085, S16 P0083–0216 and S18 P0025–0072.

Sana and Kagura furnish another path for actual cash. After Sana's death the institution apparently expects to sell processed remains. Kagura cannot change what happened and wants to take charge of the funeral. She plays more to make the money available, relies on Kotono's loan and depends on Sana's assigned agent to make a bid practicable. Fee, loan, transfer and the funeral are separate acts that depend on different people. The service Kagura chooses may restore some personal relation to what she mourns. It also places the recovery of a friend's body inside the logic of acquiring a valuable item. The completed rite has a real independent value and gives no later information about repayment, a new job or continued enrollment. Outside the games, the Red Bear commission brings money into a different sort of allocation. A police officer poses a question beyond his lawful authority, one donor takes up neighborhood anger, another dislikes the leader from inside the group, and Hiyori spends money meant for a computer for a reason she does not supply to the beneficiary. An aggregate sufficient to commission someone is no shared story of why. V07 S21 P0446–0476/S25 P0003–0026/P0036–0075, V09 S21 P0325–0407/S24 P0638–0657/S26 P0196–0257. [Who makes the end of a commission possible](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e010--the-commissions-close-after-the-fighters-leave).

Some dividends belong to the organization. V03's unattributed handover plate asserts continued profit, growth and an ability to recruit in spite of a drop. Its frame raises the possibility that records say more about what a successor is meant to know than about realized internal control. V10’s original scale plate names two sources of money, viewer charges and betting-related commissions. It says at least nine in ten games feature around ten participants. It suggests that larger groups can bring audiences multiplied by preferences for individual players, while most fixed expenditures grow more slowly. It gives a financial reason for attempting a large game. Participants describe an old catastrophic event and reasons for caution. The invitation episode shows the concealment tactic. Almost all active players receive staggered invitations under one true title and fourteen dummy titles. The official tally ultimately counts 470 entrants. It does not show an audited balance sheet, actual revenue per player, a calculation of profit or the authorship of the original explanatory panel. The financial theory is materially stronger than a statement that killing automatically makes a reliable business. V03 S03-I01, V10 S16 P0079–0097/S17-I01/S19 P0001–0057/P0089–0131/S26 P0028. [What the scale report claims](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V10_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v10-e006--the-invitation-nobody-could-match).

A desire to improve a performance or avoid becoming one is rational in that frame. Airi tries an appearance change on advice about popularity and returns with little political attraction to the institution. Shiro bows to cameras at Royal Palace and treats her alias and visual bearing as instruments, connecting her projection of competence to an audience and a suspected payout. The later discussion of how to find talented people also gives appearance a role in recruitment, though it compresses examples that do not bear out a simple division between mercenary and violent people. Yūki funds places to develop her own underdescribed capacities. She does it through entrusted management of funds rather than through the imagined authority of public fame. Reading those acts as economic behavior requires keeping visibility, private payment and authority to use it distinct. V03 S12 P0139–0207, V05 S21 P0317–0431, V06 S22 P0025–0034, V09 S19-I01/S22-I01. [Positive attention and a negative public image](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V05_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v05-e010--being-recognized-in-another-teams-room).


## Labor bought with continued play

The material network includes personal trades before it resembles a single institutional policy. Setsuna actually transports a player, arranges a sheltered residence, connects work to the artisan and knows how to use someone else’s balance to pay unusual costs. The procurer can provide a practice space and help Rinrin make a trial physically workable. Yūki’s own funds supported the private island trial. Setsuna can access the accounts during an emergency and forecasts thrift after Snow Room. None of this gives a known price for that test or the general costs of running a game. It allows a more consequential claim. The money can let a player choose or improve a practice, while the staff or independent technicians still implement the practical alternatives. V05 S19 P0245–0280/P0814–0857 and S21 P0339–0362, V06 S20 P0103–0205/P0320–0357 and S22 P0025–0034, V09 S18 P0630–0636. [The procurer and the private commission](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V06_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v06-e008--work-on-the-other-side-of-the-wall).

The actual injuries matter to who can control that money and care. Three fingers lost at #30 require a durable trade relationship, maintenance and readiness to go back. Patients with an altered body also need remaining useful flesh, time to train or an unusual substitute when ordinary support fails. The same artisan refuses to make future function sound automatic, and recommends retaining living material when Yūki's arm fails to work predictably. As a specialist he can extend part of an ordinary life. Tests from the operator’s facility and fittings from an external craft may interact without one supplier controlling all of the terms. When Busutake appears after V10’s game, she has firsthand reasons of her own for seeking alteration and actual experience providing maintenance. A successful past rescue of Yūki and technical ambition later coexist. The claim that her way of proceeding can keep Yūki able to fight puts the normative aim of the proposed work above any price currently stated. V02 S18 P0049–0054, V03 S12 P0070–0146, V08 S18 P0003–0122/P0241–0347, V09 S24 P0610–0626/S26 P0037–0071, V10 S26 P0029–0069. [The veteran who retains her arm](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V08_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v08-e003--an-accommodation-the-practitioner-keeps).

V10 makes the possibility of availability itself a demanding form of work. There are working prostheses with a genuine learning interval, a material failure during #86, further repair and the need to keep many health measures in view every day. The hospital provides over nine-tenths of Yūki’s food in the period described. What looks like convenience can keep alive the capacity to do more, and at the same time impede the private habits and future choices by which the player once judged the success of that work. At first Yūki takes up school as an investment in her practice. Later she wants the institutional tie and the learning as things that are her own. As she relies on delivered food and prepares to give up her costumes, she thinks graduation may be beyond reach while its actual learning remains worth something. Care for a body, having time to live outside the institution and personally willing to do the work remain three different measures of an outcome. ## The chosen exit and the offered exit

V10’s retirement panel supplies three measures, preserving an agent while taking a break, a usual bar on return after deregistering and the option of altering memory. Hitomi can recognize a memory removed from her and still choose ongoing school and friendship. Rinrin's old agent determines she will no longer be invited, effectively cutting off the option that gave the older player what she needed. Setsuna leaves the field after a clear and later enters agent work. On entering that work her civil registration is erased and her face altered. She retains her memories and later chooses which player to serve. Sana's economic and bodily leverage under an agreement and the potential scale of retirement for Airi and Maguma require even more individuated accounts. Acknowledged legal or institutional options have explanatory force only when distributed over the lives to which they apply. This is one source of resistance to an exclusively economic reading. A commitment that can outlast the debt that created it is still a fact a model of the industry has to explain. V04 S17 P0280–0438, V05 S19 P0765–0805/P0858–0976, V06 S18 P0679–0796, V09 S18 P0161–0184/P0335–0397, V10 S19 P0171–0192/S23 P0451–0459/S24-I01. [The parallel forms of departure](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V10_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v10-e015--the-several-meanings-of-leaving).

The institution can price work, technical support or even coercion without fully determining what those prices buy. It lets some people obtain real forms of relief and hides other choices until the material threshold passes. It can welcome a prestigious future attempt and furnish daily assurance with no clear answer about where someone can now live or what harm beyond the next game might be acceptable. A wholly voluntarist view is implausible wherever a person does not understand the threat, receives treatment on restrictive terms or loses registration against her wishes. A theory of pure dependence would also miss people using their earnings to care, learn, travel quietly, continue for reasons unrelated to cash or take deliberate action to stop. Some answers from the novel are precise enough to overturn a claim about what a score or payment means. The remaining questions include actual payout schedules, comparative survival rates and who ultimately controls the two proposed treatments in the V10 hallway. They survive without taking the already represented transfers away from the people who did make them.
