# Mikkai, professional intimacy and succession

Some people in Shiro’s house can teach skills they made for themselves. Other people offer knowledge between compartments of a secret institution, tend the household or need a living candidate who can reach a highly contested number. Mikkai appears to each of them as a means of arranging different exchanges around a shared future. None owns an equivalent ability to make that future happen. The place where people eat together and recognize each other is also where a person becomes a candidate, a technique becomes available in a dispute, and one attachment is tested against a promise made elsewhere. Cooperation at sea reveals which conclusions about the group could be reliable and which applied only to its assembling. ## What people think they can agree to

The first shown discussion of a future arrangement takes place after a private offer of a ride to an injured colleague. Shiro treats an oral promise as a small requirement compared with the things she can tell Yūki. Kokone furnishes credibility to the introduction because Yūki already has evidence of her doing meaningful work. She describes her own work as service to players. An employee can care about someone and know less of the employer's actual conditions. Shiro admits that she wants to use somebody else's pursuit for her own and can think of the surviving connection as a thread. Yūki declines an apparently friendly promise because she would treat it as binding and wants the goal to remain a choice. Taking Kokone's phone number establishes a connection through someone Yūki chooses, even as it preserves the practical option of influence that Shiro sees. The same ordinary favor supports independently true claims about the proposer, the person with whom she can meet a future target and the person whose reassurance makes the meeting possible. V08 S16 P0009–0081/P0121–0145/P0162–0265. [The invitation and the limit Yūki sets](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V08_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v08-e001--the-invitation-offered-as-friendship).

Yūki declines the invitation at Shiro’s house. In a subsequent call she reserves her decision, then declares opposition after the Awahime incident. V09 gives the reader more of that same earlier call. Shiro tells Yūki that the contender at 99 can nominate participants, that the players can organize against the operator and that each person who clears can receive a share of management. Shiro’s comparisons and examples do not independently establish an authorized procedure. She wants someone whose strength and motives permit reaching that game and intends to gain power even if she begins with a share. Those claims and motives are more specifically evidenced than any observed actual succession. Previous family records and a discussion with Kūron guide her retrieval. Coercion and selective provision accompanied her first contact with him, while usable corroboration came from subsequent contact with insiders. The official’s reasons for keeping the threshold and Hakushi’s assent to part of the argument help explain the terms from another standpoint. At present nobody in the main chronology is shown making an actual nomination. Yukariko offers Shiro a disk said to contain evidence of a past occasion, with no portrayed viewing of it. Secret knowledge can therefore sustain a coalition even while crucial details are neither equally available nor demonstrated to the readers. ## The household, the owners of skills and selected outsiders

Maya lives within an arrangement she can call love. She welcomes her partner with familiarity and thinks praise is responsive to how useful she remains. Shiro makes different intimacies available to a lover, an agent, an employee and a prospective recruit. Maya’s jealousy has pragmatic grounds when the recruiter is looking for a closer relationship with somebody else. Her own desire for exclusive recognition is clearer than the exact reciprocal form of Shiro's affection. The ordinary table gives independent depth to the other alignments. Five sit at the original table: Shiro, Maya, Shiro’s assigned agent, Takami and Busutake. The agent can make her own portion. Takami leaves out meat and Busutake salts a plain tortilla. Kokone, a paid housekeeper who prepares and serves food and knows only part of the group's affairs, is invited to sit with them as a sixth diner. These small arrangements leave differences of work and freedom within what looks like being a part of the same home. The prospect of rising with a group gives some people a motive whose source is praise, strong company or space to continue a craft. It gives others a specific aim or permission to use something, which can fail separately. V08 S16 P0083–0118/P0151–0156, S18 P0126–0189/P0529–0543/P0719–0729, V09 S26 P0072–0087. Later, Kuromatsu, Takeda and Umesada join for a technical exchange, while Airi is invited as a candidate for #99 (V10 S16 P0123–0145/P0281–0292). [Who sits at the table on what terms](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V08_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v08-e002--domestic-division-of-access).

Awahime's way of reducing perceptible presence began before her membership and is important to her sense of having anything for which to be noticed. She supplies others with a simplified form and believes the transfer is internal. The moment she sees someone outside the group use a form of it, learning turns from recognition to a practical challenge to her claim. Shiro actually wants more public knowledge of the group and can imagine the spread as part of recruiting. The sources give Awahime’s belief about the instructional promise and Shiro’s eventual acknowledgment of publication. They give no agreed full text of the original arrangement or general law of skills. Awahime's large demand is a way of putting a price and a limit against disregard. Some motives for the specific amount are Shiro’s estimates. Takami’s disagreement with her chief, request to settle and continuing cooperation undo a simple relation between dissent and leaving. Shiro has already commissioned force. The result shows how distribution can go on being a practical advantage after it has weakened the human agreement that originally allowed it. Kokone casually tells Awahime where Yūki lives. She has reasons to be helpful and little access to the full conflict her disclosure enables. Awahime can seek leverage with another participant without arriving with a plan to hurt her. Shiro can order the hired group to stop after a shot, and regards the prior commission under a partial description in conversation with Yūki. The sequence makes a claim of control testable and establishes its limit. Yayoi’s and Yūki’s separate actions at the site demonstrate effects beyond the aims of all three. Actual recruitment returns to a form of bounded affinity. Maguma refuses membership despite skills and a career that make Shiro think she can reach the target. Busutake identifies another candidate with whom she already shares practical work. Airi feels real distrust, holds a decision for nearly two months and asks her to convey a willingness to cooperate. At the next meeting other applicants join for technical exchange, while her own intention becomes explicitly related to 99. Care, practice, financial means, persuasion and changing trust are specific prerequisites for her decision; praise alone fails. The meeting also contains a small refutation of her picture of Takami as forcing attention on others. After asking the visitor’s age, Takami brings her a soft drink. ## Demonstrated coordination and what it costs

Jamboree Ship gives many participants real reasons to accept Shiro’s direction. Defenders can concentrate resources and cross-check injuries and messages. V10 shows some actual solutions and assignments. It also shows the difference between a technical and a political repertoire. Airi employs a signed code learned inside Mikkai with Takami, but its usefulness ends at Maguma’s distinct membership position. She has practiced Awahime’s gait after the rupture and can rediscover a way to move with an injury that observers distinguish from that learned technique. Busutake can buy, teach, maintain or reuse practical resources without fully tying her professional ambitions to Shiro’s reward. She earlier lends lifesaving help to Yūki and later appears alive after a supposed death, making a separate claim to what her treatment can do. It would be too simple to treat a successful transfer of methods as proof of uniform allegiance. V09 S24 P0610–0626 and S26 P0037–0087, V10 S19 P0394–0456/P0557–0603, S21 P0058–0087/P0279–0295 and S26 P0046–0069. [Abilities with and without shared membership](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V10_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v10-e009--the-skills-nobody-brings-in-the-same-form).

The biggest disagreement in the common work is visible when Airi offers to be hit. Shiro tries to preserve an ally whose future makes the group possible and calls for another approach. Maguma the outsider thinks honoring her immediate choice is warranted and shoots. Airi considers herself a candidate whose life is too important for a leader to stake. The action is direct, its interpretation gives evidence about her view of coalition terms, and Shiro's fuller reason remains partly uncertain. Maya’s death exposes an even more difficult attribution of love and function. She wants to regain favor and acts to demonstrate the capacity Shiro has made precious. Takami reads Shiro's blankness and demonstration of upset as partly unfeeling, in the context of her own habit of watching large, violent events with considerable distance. Shiro shows real later anger in a related conflict. This does not make Takami’s estimate groundless or let one of them accurately speak for everyone involved. The faction ends in partial conflict that neither keeping valuable people alive nor the precise stake before a potential final game can wholly explain. V10 S21 P0183–0275 and S23 P0133–0178/P0268–0277/P0463–0542. [Where a leader's decisions become dangerous to her side](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V10_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v10-e014--who-is-left-to-blame).

There are two plausible models of that collection. One treats it as a patronage network where the leader’s project organizes distributed work and lets people trade participation for attention and resources. Offers, planned candidate preservation, favoritism and the late eruption of discord all support that reading. Another treats it as genuinely capable mutual assistance that participants can keep using after their competing purposes escape the leader's terms. Body maintenance, advice openly given by dissenters and the learned code that works at sea support its distinct value. Neither model warrants membership from a temporary order, gratitude from a costly warning or final outcomes from a stopped narrative. Independent support gives people reasons to remain and real grounds to object. The scarcity of a candidate later turns those same limits into a vulnerability.
