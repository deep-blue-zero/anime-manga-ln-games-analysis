# Riko

Riko (狸狐, リコ) is the player Mishiro rescues in Riko's first game. She acquires a workable body and a relation that gives her a reason to use it, knowing that her teacher hopes to have other people carry on her own purpose. She asks for signs of affection and can set those requests aside to get a job done. Her deference then enables a decisive responsibility at Golden Bath. There she makes choices of her own inside the mission she has accepted, and dies trying to stop a woman who barely knows who she is. This portrait follows the Japanese novel evidence through V10 and contains spoilers. The V02 illustrated front sheet's Latin “Kiko” differs from prose reading リコ, Riko, for the one attested person. (V02 S05-I01 and S16 P0533–0536.) [Original front plates and their limits](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e015--delayed-cover-and-front-plates)

## How the relation begins

Riko's own account of entering is that she was tired of everything and did not particularly care whether she lived or died. The novel gives no more specific account of a household, financial demand or sustained ordinary occupation. She loses much of her body during her first game, and the fact that she can stay alive makes the question of what she would do next even harder. Mishiro rescues her and arranges access to a craftsman because the ordinary treatments cannot restore what she lost. Riko remembers not being confident in this older player at first. Mishiro had been incapacitated by fear in the same game before later becoming effective and helping other participants. Understanding those different moments gives Riko firsthand grounds for thinking Mishiro can act under fear rather than assuming mastery is a permanent quality. Her own first game is Mishiro's thirtieth. The reader sees the rescue through her memory after already seeing the developed partnership in Golden Bath. She cannot remember the setting of every early conversation. (V02 S16 P0862–0865/P0985–1008.) [Riko's earlier memory and the timing of its disclosure](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e011--the-disciple-as-an-extension-of-the-person)

Mishiro often tells Riko about the humiliation that gave her the reason to challenge Yūki. Riko usually leaves her objection to herself, though she finally asks how such a thing can suffice as a reason for risking a life. Her answer to the teacher's question about self is equally concrete. She identifies with the body that remains and needs help imagining an agency that extends beyond it. Mishiro uses the example of somebody giving an order, then explicitly describes what it would mean to count the deeds of a person she had rescued and shaped as her own. She calls Riko her fifth such pupil and tells her she will take over in the event Mishiro loses. Riko realizes that Mishiro wants more than a subordinate's occasional help. She sees the offer of belonging as an effort to make a whole other person into a continuation of the teacher. It frightens her, and she understands why she accepts. The mission is the missing thing she recognizes she wants. (V02 S16 P0993–1034.) [What Mishiro asks, and how Riko hears it](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e011--the-disciple-as-an-extension-of-the-person)

This is an agreement made while Riko is materially dependent on others, and the unequal power helps explain why it feels so effective to both of them. Her recognition of its purpose is a limit on treating the pupil as oblivious. She actively desires to be useful and to receive direction from the person who saved her. The resulting willingness is intelligible without proving that Mishiro's stated ideal of full control has been realized. Comparative evidence comes from Mitsuba, a former pupil who says she received a similar assignment and later chose to leave. That difference also shows why the earlier teaching can have real value for both pupils even when only one carries the directive forward. (V02 S16 P1005–1034; V03 S17 P0372–0449.) [What different pupils retain from the same instruction](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V03_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v03-e005--more-than-one-succession)

## Affection, performance and practical independence

In Golden Bath Riko reports the recovery of valuable tags and asks for the usual reward. Mishiro strokes her head. Riko has gradually added requests for more tactile attention, and the practice has extended to ordinary occasions such as being on time to meet outside a game. Mishiro likes being held in such regard and also gets tired, has to say when the reward is over, and finds Riko more able to affect her than other people she thinks she can manipulate. Riko's habit takes shape through asking and getting varying responses. It is actual intimacy in the sense of personal touch and trust. Romantic character, preferences or an ordinary intimate history beyond this contact cannot be established from the passages. Riko's identification with her teacher thus has concrete small rewards in addition to the scale of the promise that names it. (V02 S16 P0533–0577.) [What happens between Mishiro and her pupil in an ordinary task](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e010--forty-plays-and-the-right-hand)

Later she reports a further recovery of tags. There is no reward request that time. She wants to get back to help secure the items and reasonably fears the opposing group will make a desperate effort to take them back. When Mishiro orders her to stay and entrusts her with overseeing the exit stock, Riko asks if it is really safe for Mishiro to go. Her concern is specific to the round number and the danger that might accompany it. She proceeds on the instruction. At this point real practical foresight and a desire to protect Mishiro show up alongside the preference to act on instructions. She can postpone gratification, propose what her next task should be and take over a resource everyone depends on. She is not simply following whichever instruction will bring her the most attention at the moment. (V02 S16 P0688–0722.) [How a pupil becomes responsible for the group](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e011--the-disciple-as-an-extension-of-the-person)

The ordinary glimpses are narrow but individualized. Exuberance when she has accomplished something punctuates use of polite forms with Mishiro, including the excited やりました and repeated names with さん. Firmness changes audience. When Yūki appears at the exit, she pushes a rougher register and demands to know what happened, while still referring to 御城さん with respectful familiarity. The narrator hears effort in how she puts force into her voice. From inside her at an earlier point we know she can hesitate to voice a genuine objection, even though her willing questions sometimes make it out. There is no useful generalization about her food, leisure, household routine or ordinary relationships beyond the described meetings and her chosen form of reassurance. (V02 S16 P0533–0577/P0688–0722/P0997–1007/P1042–1061.) [Riko's outward orders and inward assent](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e011--the-disciple-as-an-extension-of-the-person)

## A mission under incomplete information

Riko recognizes the player she has heard about long before Yūki can locate or name her. She asks what happened to Mishiro. She learns only that Yūki did not check. She does not need a death attestation to apply what she takes as permission to act at full strength and to defend access to the stored footwear. Earlier Mishiro's wording had marked the idea of use without naming a precise thing to be used, and Riko now supplies her own account of the command. This is again a place where an action can accord with an instruction without being mechanically triggered by it. Watching a stranger claiming the resource, receiving no reassurance about her mentor, and knowing the long promised contingency all bear on her decision. (V02 S16 P0717–0722/P1032–1034/P1042–1066.) [The confrontation from both participants' positions](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e011--the-disciple-as-an-extension-of-the-person)

Riko's alterations give her demonstrated force and mobility that Yūki does not expect from her appearance. The prosthetics referral and Yūki's tactile observations support an artificial arm. Yūki infers that the legs too have been altered, with exact mechanisms and inventory unknown. Riko then improvises to follow Yūki onto a dangerous part of the floor. Her ability to do so gives her another opportunity, yet she remains dependent on the physical conditions the building imposes. Yūki uses a newly available chance and Riko dies, with her exact moment of death unspecified. That result is firmly distinguished from the wider generalization Yūki voices about how long extensively altered players last. Riko is among the past figures Yūki thinks of when she must live and compete with different parts of her body later. That is the surviving player's reevaluation of evidence. (V02 S16 P1042–1119; V04 S21 P0378–0383/P0545–0552; V10 S16 P0159–0198.) [Bodily recovery and its limits in the source](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e011--the-disciple-as-an-extension-of-the-person)

Yūki has not heard Riko's name during the fight. In Cloudy Beach she recalls it, and the route by which she learned it has not been given. Mitsuba's refusal to inherit the mission further clarifies that Riko's actual response is a feature of Riko. In a similar role, she is likely to use directions from a trusted mentor as a practical basis for action, seek signs of approval when time allows and independently defer them when a task seems urgent. An unanswered question about that person's safety and a challenge to the role can make her press forward in the absence of full information. She can also recognize her teacher's vulnerability and propose help. The most consequential limit is the evidence from only one fully developed attachment. It cannot tell us whether she would respond the same way to another authority, or whether she would discover an ordinary purpose that did not rely on completing somebody else's.
