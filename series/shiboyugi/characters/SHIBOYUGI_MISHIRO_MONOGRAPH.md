# Mishiro

Mishiro (御城, ミシロ) first organizes a small group in Scrap Building, her eighth game. She requires the people she leads to account for their abilities and can manage shared resources. She also experiences somebody else's claim to precedence as danger in its own right. Eight months later Yūki encounters her leading a larger coalition in Golden Bath. By then Mishiro has lived through further bodily losses, established ways to help and use her pupils, and built a purpose around beating the one person she could not make acknowledge her superiority. In a remembered family scene she is called 一美, Kazumi. That is a recalled given name and a source of meaning to her. The novel does not give a verified complete civil name. This portrait covers the admitted Japanese evidence through V10, with spoilers.

## The name and the first group

Mishiro remembers being told by her mother that Kazumi was chosen in the hope she would excel at one thing, however small. She brings the memory forward as a curse when she is injured and alone. She also remembers her sister's birth as the beginning of repeatedly being held responsible for their quarrels, recalls serious violence she directed at that sister, and remembers how little sympathy other pupils had for someone she believes was unfairly treated at school. These scenes are embedded in Mishiro's effort to explain her present humiliation and the unfairness she sees in the ballot. They give her formative comparisons and actual self-reported harm. They do not provide the mother's or sister's competing account. Her assigned agent's invitation appeals precisely to a place where she could occupy a status no one else had reached. Faced with a demonstrably capable rival, she remembers how much she wanted that invitation to be true. (V02 S12 P0761–0792/P1063–1078.) [Mishiro's isolation and her chronology of blame](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e004--giving-up-an-advantage-and-choosing-to-return)

At the beginning of Scrap Building, familiar teammates agree to Mishiro's leadership. Rank is only one reason she can defend drawing them together. She has their existing trust, asks for relevant skills beyond game count, identifies decisions that require explicit coordination, and initially limits consumption of their usable light. She also reprimands the hesitant Kotoha for how she expresses uncertainty. When Yūki claims a higher count and makes a locally erroneous call, Mishiro has real grounds for skepticism. The private account of a later choice is more revealing. Mishiro believes she could have given the correct light-conservation instruction if Yūki had not said it first. As soon as it comes from her rival, agreeing feels like losing position. This is a specific failure of an otherwise competent leader, and ties together Yūki's credible early mistake and the unreasonable price Mishiro later pays for excluding her advice. (V02 S12 P0062–0139/P0185–0267/P0763–0792.) [The initial distribution of authority](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e001--who-can-claim-experience)

## What she can concede, and when

Mishiro calls the injured Kotoha finished as a player while recognizing she is still alive. She frames the proposed rescue as a use of scarce light and an exposure the capable survivors would take on. This hard limit distinguishes obligations she is prepared to accept from a denial that the other person still matters. After a separate attack costs Mishiro her dominant right forearm, she has to face the same dependency. Yūki asks for an apology and pronounces the superiority she thinks Mishiro needs to recognize. Mishiro can infer that even a contrived apology is likely to save her and that Yūki may help whether or not she gives it. Her response is to refuse and push past. When she privately names the feeling as 悔しい, the frustration is physically mobilizing. Yūki directs and helps her anyway. The apparently life-dependent bargain does not by itself produce an admission. (V02 S12 P0363–0412/P0761–0797/P0826–0909.) [Refusing the requested apology](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e004--giving-up-an-advantage-and-choosing-to-return)

The final ballot and exit bring two different concessions. Mishiro considers voting for Yūki and regaining rank, yet feels that doing so after being rescued would violate an obligation. She considers Kotoha expendable in terms of future play and then recognizes a kind of sympathy with her own injured state. After searching for procedural advantages and writing under the practical difficulty of losing her dominant hand, she tells the others that she voted for Chie according to the instructions. The plurality punishes Chie. That outcome still strikes Mishiro as placing responsibility on a convenient person, the very thing she resents in her childhood memories. The report of Mishiro's ballot has her stated authority, with the exact complete count unknown. (V02 S12 P1049–1078/P1097–1115.) [Mishiro's choices under the secret ballot](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e005--taking-a-secret-ballot-at-its-word)

Outside she asks whether Yūki's stated liking was intended to steer the vote. She listens to the answer, studies Yūki's face and voluntarily apologizes in the form she earlier refused. The admission costs her something and helps her orient the next decision. In the car she angrily vents in front of the one person allowed to see this side of her, then explains that she needed to acknowledge the result in order to reverse it. She requests an ordinary prosthetic arm. The agent points out the limits of replacement, and she makes improvement a requirement instead of expecting the body alone to restore what she lost. This reveals how easily it would be to mistake genteel composure for the whole person. Harsh private speech and audible regret belong to the same relation as her highly controlled public address. (V02 S12 P1116–1169.) [An apology and a changed course of work](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e005--taking-a-secret-ballot-at-its-word)

## The larger coalition and the pupil who matters

In Golden Bath Mishiro is within reach of an exit she could take alone. She remains beside the group's stock of footwear until there is enough for the people she commands. She expects to meet some of them in later games and sees betrayal as a strategic cost. She also thinks such an easy way out would be contemptible even if there were no later cost. Her choice of a seat where she and the exit resources can be seen serves both supervision and a public role. Later she leaves the position to Riko and takes a turn exposing herself because leadership would lose credibility if she never did. She understands display and accepts a real risk where that display is meaningful. The decision broadens the terms of usefulness she found so hard to preserve in the smaller group. (V02 S16 P0518–0533/P0688–0722.) [Mishiro waits for her coalition](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e010--forty-plays-and-the-right-hand)

Riko belongs to a relation Mishiro knowingly cultivates and cannot wholly control. Riko remembers that in their earlier game Mishiro at first reacted to the creature with intense fear. She progressively became effective and rescued a severely injured novice. Mishiro gives the newcomer access to a prosthetics artisan. She also explicitly proposes a mission: if she loses to Yūki, the pupil is to act in her place. For her, somebody whose survival, direction and skill she has helped bring about can make her own agency extend past her body. She tells Riko of earlier pupils and describes her goal as full control. This is a forthright project, with real dependency and a gap between its ideal and the actual result. (V02 S16 P0862–0865/P0985–1034.) [The bargain a pupil remembers](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e011--the-disciple-as-an-extension-of-the-person)

They often see each other away from games according to the direct narration in Golden Bath. Riko asks for a recurring tactile reward even after small off-game achievements. Mishiro provides it with both affectionate satisfaction and fatigue, internally recognizing that this particular pupil leads her around in a way other people do not. Extending care and treating another person instrumentally can occur together, and the arrangement enables Riko to ask for more than a direct service requires. Yet Mitsuba later describes accepting instruction for survival and leaving the arrangement, and acts on her own curiosity when she encounters Yūki. Her account supplies a real exception to complete domination and better evidence for the breadth of the succession project than for all of its results. The warm contact and psychological dependence shown with Riko do not establish romance. Missing ordinary interests and intimate history should remain missing. (V02 S16 P0533–0577; V03 S17 P0372–0449.) [The pupil who asked to be used and the pupil who left](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V03_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v03-e005--more-than-one-succession)

## The rival she both needs and misunderstands

In Golden Bath Yūki first learns that the veteran she is facing is Mishiro, that she has continued and that she can wield the repaired right hand. Mishiro's memories meanwhile supply the intervening sequence. The new prosthesis and part of her upper arm were lost during her ninth game, her seventeenth brought near immobilization after penalties, and the beast at her thirtieth confronted her with the fear she had acquired in the building. An ordinary dog's bark can cause her to shrink away. In the earlier game, seeing Riko lose an arm in a way that recalls her own injury helped her act despite that fear. The reader learns these past confrontations late. Yūki hears the claim of forty appearances but does not inherit all the circumstances that attached importance to her. (V02 S16 P0815–0837/P0858–0888/P0989–1004.) [The histories behind the changed comparison](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e010--forty-plays-and-the-right-hand)

At the first moment of reunion Mishiro could apparently have inflicted much more serious harm than she does. She uses the recovered right hand to strike in anger and attacks the way Yūki is carrying herself. The narrator gives the reader greater access to Yūki's paralysis and its uncertain causes than Mishiro has. Mishiro wants to defeat someone who can still justify the trials she has endured to reach this meeting. The idea of an impaired Yūki disappoints and enrages her. That desire is compatible with relief at Yūki's earlier survival, as she states outside the building, and with trying to kill her in the bath when the contest draws nearer to her expectations. She takes Yūki's poor performance as a betrayal of the person she had constructed in imagination. Yūki protests that she is human and is actually doing what she can. The argument gives Mishiro's aspiration a condition she cannot control, because the person she wants to surpass does not owe her a performance. (V02 S12 P1086–1096; V02 S16 P0822–0888.) [The two most visible ideas of the reunion](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e010--forty-plays-and-the-right-hand)

Mishiro uses what she has learned and her own configuration of physical resources before Yūki finds an answer to an action she remembers from their first meeting. Their last direct state together is Mishiro wounded and sinking out of action in the bath. Yūki acknowledges a good game and specifically does not check whether she is dead. Mitsuba later treats her as dead, without an independently shown confirmation. Yūki subsequently calls on the encounter when thinking about what bodily replacement costs and how a serious opponent differs from somebody incidental. Those recollections do not give Mishiro a new action. The similar-sounding earlier name 一巳 of another former player belongs to a different person, Hitomi. (V02 S16 P0952–0978; V03 S17 P0394–0412/P0435–0449; V04 S17 P0280–0392; V06 S20 P0659–0668/P0864–0873; V07 S23 P0661–0665; V10 S16 P0159–0198.) ## Written voice and a conditional portrait

Her formal self-reference わたくし and sentence endings including ですわ, ますの and ですこと mark the controlled mode from the first introduction onward. They can convey authority, reassurance, an apology or an accusation. The shift from ふざけるな to ふざけないでくださいまし in the shelter puts raw refusal and her habitual style into the same emotional movement. Her agent can use お嬢様 to tease precisely when her usual language breaks. In Golden Bath, なんてざまだ becomes なんてざまですの as her attention turns from the initial shock to expectations she can articulate. The register is written, and the family memory does not independently establish social class or every performance outside play. The very visible concern with appearance, a controlled place in the room, playful care of a familiar pupil and the recoil from an ordinary dog's bark give aspects of daily behavior beyond the immediate duel. Her leisure and sustained acquired tastes remain unknown. (V02 S12 P0066–0083/P0855–0868/P1126–1169 and S16 P0523–0532/P0536–0577/P0833–0852/P0863–0865.) [The cost of maintaining a commanding voice](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e004--giving-up-an-advantage-and-choosing-to-return)

In a comparable group, Mishiro is likely to ask what everyone can contribute, arrange visibility of command and try to turn special capacities into commitments. She can give protection and make herself responsible for a group while disliking an escape that invites the judgment she left others to pay. Advice offered by a perceived rival is unusually difficult for her to adopt in public, even when she knows its substance. An experience of real independence, or help given without exacting recognition, can make her choose acknowledgment and revise her preparation. The individual who wants to serve can also exceed the role she has assigned, as Riko and Mitsuba do in different ways. These constraints let the portrait account for both increasing skill and continuing vulnerability to the ranking she has made indispensable.
