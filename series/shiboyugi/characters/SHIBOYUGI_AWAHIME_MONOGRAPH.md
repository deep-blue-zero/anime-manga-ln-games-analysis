# Awahime

淡姫, with ruby アワ／ヒメ, is an experienced player whose first distinctive achievement grew out of a wish to pass unobserved. Mikkai, Shiro's group for sharing unusual skills, gives her a place to use and teach that achievement. She develops a form other players can learn and later finds it circulating outside the group. She tries to regain leverage over Shiro, threatens and attacks her directly, and decides that she can use a third person's importance to Shiro without harming that person. These are her own choices as well as responses to decisions made about her skill. The admitted Japanese account through V10 leaves her civil name, precise age and clinical endpoint unknown. V08 S20 P0164–0218/P0263–0284/P0352–0371/P0709–0756/P0789–0830, V09 S16 P0041–0043.

## How she came to take pride in disappearing

Awahime remembers feeling ill-equipped for intelligent, athletic, artistic and social distinction. These are her judgments about herself. She despises the ordinary platitude that jobs carry no difference in rank and takes its very existence as evidence of how deeply people care about status. The line of thought comes from a taxi driver's passing attempt to reassure her about an occupation she says she would rather keep private. She finds conversation at a salon, a clothing shop or in a taxi costly, and says 「あまり、人に言いたくない仕事をやってます。察してください」 to bring the inquiry to a close. Shiro later faces a similarly economical opening in 「まだるっこしいのは、嫌い」, though there the speaker has a definite demand of her own. Both encounters show Awahime doing something with few words. One is a controlled dismissal and the other announces an insistence on compensation. V08 S20 P0170–0180/P0352–0364.

Before play, she learned to avoid drawing notice. She trained her movement and attention to other people's lines of sight and altered her face and body toward an appearance she expected others to overlook. Her account calls much of the practice habitual and impossible for her to enumerate. In the street it let her escape unwanted attention and gave her occasions for mischief at other people's expense. Pride at making herself hard to detect coexisted with the sense that her one ability was something of which to be ashamed. A recruiter approached when she was walking around without deliberately concealing herself. Her ability had more use in the games than in the everyday life she despised. She thought she might have found her calling and remembers little sense of imminent danger in her first thirty or so games. She distinguishes settings in which eluding someone's attention is decisive from those in which movement can only be a subsidiary advantage. Her career and the particular confrontation with Shiro corroborate unusual skill. They provide no general guarantee of invisibility or immunity. V08 S20 P0363–0371/P0197–0212/P0709–0713. ## The value of praise and the scope of consent

The encounter she later calls unlucky takes place in her thirtieth game, Heavenly Sky. Shiro admires Awahime's unusual abilities and calls her a person worth getting to know. Awahime remembers the attention as the first time she had been praised in that way. She joined Shiro while the other members were Takami and Busutake, whose company she actually remembers enjoying. Mikkai's instruction improved her own practice before the time came for her to contribute. She voluntarily turned a limited part of her experience into a teachable walk, accepting Shiro's explanation that it would stay within the group. Other people could learn this version. Much of her own practice would not fit in it. Even during internal instruction she felt something of herself detach, a discomfort she made herself overlook. The history gives her positive reasons to assist Shiro and a basis for anger specific to wider circulation. It gives no identified romantic attachment to Shiro or other intimate history. V08 S20 P0709–0720.

When a player unaffiliated with Mikkai uses the walk, Awahime grasps how much of her identity she had invested in its distinctiveness. She compares the experience with being flattened and spread so thin that she can no longer recognize herself. Shiro confirms intentional promotion to recruit more members. She explicitly credits Awahime's skill as the basis when pitching Mikkai to Yūki, while giving the group credit for the resulting technique. Credits, ability to teach and permission to distribute are distinct. Awahime imposed a scope in her recollection and reiterates it at the dispute. Shiro says 「そうでしたっけ？」 upon being reminded and later invokes prior consent to provide the technique when accounting to Yūki. The evidence independently supports Awahime's contribution, voluntary initial sharing and actual public use. It leaves the exact original agreement and whether Shiro sincerely forgot open. Seeing Keito and later Airi use the derivative version confirms that something real has circulated. Neither has acquired everything Awahime learned to do. V08 S18 P0241–0307/P0525–0528/P0727–0729, S20 P0187–0191/P0720–0731/P0893–0905, V10 S16 P0300–0307 and S21 P0288–0294.

In the formal confrontation Awahime asks for three hundred million yen in exchange for standing down. She calls it compensation for an appropriated life and will let part be paid out of the success Shiro is pursuing. The suggestion that it matches lifetime earnings comes from Shiro. Awahime's attempt to restructure payment is an exercise of negotiation alongside the growing anger. She starts with 「あなた」 and later turns to 「お前」, pounding the table. Shiro contests her moral claim. Awahime attacks and Takami's birds get Shiro clear. Awahime herself later concludes that she might have obtained an apology or payment had she pressed her case with composure. She still wishes she had hit Shiro on the earlier occasion and privately imagines hurting her again. Self-review is present, and it coexists with a durable sense of injury and willingness to act on it. V08 S20 P0169–0218/P0222–0259/P0724–0732. ## Making her own approach

Awahime walks away believing quiet negotiation is exhausted and looks for leverage that would affect something Shiro actually cares about. She thinks through the possibility of abduction and rejects involving Yūki that way. Yūki is outside the dispute and could defend herself. If Yūki hears what happened, the account can still damage a partnership Shiro needs. Awahime uses what Kokone once told her about the apartment and intends to make the disclosure a bargaining instrument. There is a difference between choosing to extend a quarrel by approaching Yūki and deciding to injure her. Shiro later puts deliberate harm to Yūki forward as a possibility. The reader has the stronger evidence of Awahime's actual reasoning and of Shiro's commission to remove Awahime. Takami's later complaint about publicizing the walk also shows dissent inside Mikkai without independently establishing the exact scope of consent. V08 S20 P0219–0259/P0263–0284/P0889–0912, V09 S18 P0051–0090.

Awahime understands how much danger a person can sense face to face, but she has not realized the extent of Takami's surveillance or that the response to her will be so fast. The pursuers follow her into Tochinoki, where Yūki offers her a chance to talk and does not give her up. When threatened, Awahime recognizes the loss of outside communication and kills the three nearest attackers. She lets Yūki handle arrangements for the aftermath while herself intending to call Shiro. A separate shooter wounds her before she can complete the call. Her earlier confidence and the defeat are compatible once the novel has shown what she could do locally and what she could not see. She still gets into the building and uses her remaining strength to tell Yūki 「信用するな」 and 「私を騙した」. Yūki thereafter detects no breath, grip or pulse and initially judges her dead, then qualifies the judgment and awaits medical assessment. The source shows an emergency response and treats the event as a decisive break. It supplies no later clinical confirmation. V08 S20 P0626–0709/P0738–0756/P0762–0830/P0885–0892/P0935–0939, V09 S16 P0041–0043. [Different vantage points at the apartment](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V08_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v08-e009--four-views-of-the-same-address).

Her warning contributes to Yūki's actual decision to cut off contact. Yūki does not know the agreement's exact terms and does not need to settle them to decide what the attempt and Shiro's incomplete account mean for her own safety. By V10 other players still use the teachable movement and remember Awahime as a former member. Maya thinks of what happened to Awahime when she worries that her own usefulness to Shiro is running out. These are later consequences and readings, with Awahime's own last perspective confined to the apartment. V08 S20 P0897–0942, V09 S16 P0041–0043, V10 S16 P0307, S21 P0142–0157/P0288–0294. [Yūki's decision after the warning](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V08_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v08-e010--what-can-be-judged-from-an-incomplete-account).

Awahime ordinarily tries to minimize exposure and favors direct speech that ends a pointless negotiation. Recognition from someone she respects, past help and a bounded audience can make her willing to disclose even a skill bound up with her sense of self. A public use she considers unauthorized creates a conflict she wants named, initially accepts financial means of settling and later tries to force. She spares an outsider the immediate harm she is willing to direct toward a participant in the grievance, although she involves the outsider in the dispute. Her conduct is weaker evidence for any general code about other people's safety or the way she would respond if a future agreement were clear. The Japanese supports written wording and a marked difference between public restraint, private rage and the urgent address of a seriously injured speaker. Performance is outside this witness.
