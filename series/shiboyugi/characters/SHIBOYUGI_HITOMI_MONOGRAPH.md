# Hitomi

骨塚仁実, Honezuka Hitomi, is an evening high school student whose former name in organized death games is very likely 一巳, likewise read ヒトミ. Kokone addresses her by that name and Hitomi answers. This is a different spelling and reading from the similarly written 一美 recalled in another character’s past. The first portrait the novel gives of Hitomi is a civilian self she sincerely believes. Her body has more continuity with her early life than her memory does. What she does after regaining her memory matters as much as how she regains it. She keeps what she has learned, declines to resume lethal play, and later puts some of the same skills to use for people she already cares about. This portrait covers the Japanese novel through V10. Her last directly portrayed ordinary and protective choices are in V07. V10 places her past retirement alongside other forms of departure without adding a new episode in her life. V04 S17 P0068–0109/P0342–0392/P0415–0469, V07 S19 P0158–0215 and S21 P0082–0114, V10 S24-I01.

## A young person who learned to stop

Hitomi recovers her occupational history in a classmate’s apartment. A friend offered her the first game while she was looking for work after being fired. The stakes surprised her and the play itself came more readily than she expected. They found a teacher early and both made considerable money. Hitomi recalls watching people coming home from school or work and congratulating them inwardly on their efforts with contempt. Luck, aptitude and access to a good opportunity seemed to her to determine what effort could ever accomplish. She knew other players were dying. An advantage that repeatedly paid out made its own dangers feel theoretical. Years of ordinary employment, civil family identity and the need that took her to specific earlier work remain mostly unavailable. The recovered period does give a concrete prior job, access through friendship and an early feeling of superiority. V04 S17 P0348–0358/P0442–0445.

The friend’s reported death at her own thirtieth game changes what Hitomi will tolerate. Hitomi herself is close to thirty. She believes continuation at that point requires a commitment to staying a player until death, which she does not feel entitled to claim. Money has also distorted her idea of ordinary earnings, the route still tempts her, and surviving her friend feels wrong. She asks her agent to alter her memory so that she will not drift back. The decision reflects several actual concerns. It cannot make the nature of the Wall of Thirty a general fact. Hitomi has no inspected common denominator and does not say she saw the friend die. The novel gives the lived consequence of the report. The prospect that she might be unable to give up easy earnings is strong enough in her mind to justify giving up knowledge of a large part of her own life. V04 S17 P0348–0358/P0366–0375. [Hitomi’s restored access](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e004--hitomi-and-the-knowledge-in-the-body).

Before the recovery, she is pursuing a diploma, lives in a well-appointed one-room apartment and understands herself to be from a rich family that asked her to live independently and stopped contacting her. She is embarrassed by that account and scarcely remembers her parents’ faces. The narration follows the autobiographical knowledge she has access to then. Her withdrawal memory and situated responses afterward give good reason to distinguish that story from corroborated family history. A complete explanation of how the operator altered particular memories, what her family did or who supplied her material security is unavailable. Her later remark that her early life resembled Yūki’s points to recognizable likeness, not an independent report validating their whole histories. V04 S17 P0247–0257/P0348–0358, V06 S20 P0498–0522.

## Curiosity, skill and a breach of trust

Sorimachi Yūki becomes a puzzle at evening school partly because of things any classmate could see. She enters and leaves their social world unevenly, keeps personal affairs private, misses stretches of class and returns with small injuries. Hitomi searches for patterns, checks social media, goes through a classmate’s things and asks a teacher indirect questions. She can tell the normally close Kazami and Hiyori more than she would tell others. She still goes ahead when their alarm makes clear that her conduct is hard to explain as ordinary interest. At first she imagines an unusual stranger and has no conscious occupational category to attach to the impression. Her own felt urgency is real. Later she understands it as continued attraction to a world she thought she had left. Nothing in this sequence establishes a romance, and the book gives no romantic history or intimate preference for Hitomi elsewhere. V04 S17 P0068–0109/P0113–0156/P0247–0257/P0430–0438.

Two bodily surprises show why retrospection alone is inadequate to explain her behavior. While following Yūki after class, Hitomi feels her pulse settle in the very circumstances she expects would make it race. She can maintain apparently practiced distance and concealment, yet she misses the person behind her while attending to her quarry. The woman is Yūki’s assigned agent, Setsuna, a fact Hitomi does not know at the time. Hitomi doubts her claim of being simply a friend, does not identify the work from the unfamiliar alias, and reacts with an effective throw she cannot remember learning. Her shock afterward is specifically about her own competence. She makes the limited humane choice of dragging the incapacitated woman to the side of the road before leaving. She remains frightened and becomes less able to let go of the inquiry after reaching her own home. The pattern is selective steadiness in action, then uncertainty about why she can act that way. V04 S17 P0145–0184/P0247–0257/P0396–0411.

In Yūki’s room, Hitomi knows entry is a breach and later feels genuine relief when Yūki allows a way to leave. She says 「今までのこと、ごめんなさい」, acknowledges her curiosity, takes her hand off the closet and steps back. That spoken acceptance has an honest feeling behind it and fails to describe what she will do next. She uses the opening to go to the closet. During the struggle, a touch discloses artificial fingers, which prompt an unplanned question. When Yūki asks why she is so fixated, Hitomi answers 「こっちが聞きたいわよ」. Only the recognizable game garments give her the missing knowledge about both of them. The sequence separates a useful discrimination between perceptual clues, an abusive choice, an intended or even wanted retreat and the force of an appetite she cannot explain while acting. This is stronger evidence of continuing agency than calling her pursuit instinctive and leaving it there. It is also stronger evidence of impaired self-understanding than treating the apology as a calculated lie. V04 S17 P0280–0344.

## Keeping a past she no longer wants to live in

Hitomi tells Yūki that she used to play and that she stopped before a point where going on carelessly no longer seemed defensible. Seeing the artificial fingers and the marked eye in good light produces specific admiration. She considers this someone who crossed the threshold she would not cross. She does not fully know the limits or future of either injury, and her conclusion is grounded in the signs and account she actually receives. Yūki in turn says being able to give something up can be a talent. The exchange matters without becoming a promise of a friendship or a shared vocation. Hitomi returns to school, decides against playing again and declines a second erasure. Having already been unable to control what the blackout left behind, she now prefers to face the past. This is an actual second choice to retire in a different knowledge state. She considers offering to pay for damage and ultimately leaves the subject alone unless Yūki raises it. A more settled project for herself does not prevent ordinary self-protective evasion. V04 S17 P0366–0392/P0415–0438.

The call to Kirihara adds a different test of what memory now permits. Hitomi remembers the house telephone number without a saved contact. She has an old wish to know how her teacher is and calls despite the late hour. She first recognizes Kokone, realizes that she knew a different pair of twins before Kazami and Hiyori, and answers to 一巳. Asked how memory returned, she says there are circumstances and chooses to leave out the details. When she learns that Kirihara was killed despite having retired, surprise interrupts the expectation that leaving play had secured her teacher’s ordinary life. She asks to hear the circumstances. That decision reconnects her with people and knowledge, without evidence of a retaliatory operation or a return to the profession. Kirihara’s teaching and the unnamed friend’s recruitment are independent influences. Kokone’s family and work obligations can explain her access to Kirihara and anger without giving Hitomi prior knowledge of the killing. The history of the investigation appears later in V04’s disclosure than this call. V04 S17 P0442–0469, S19 P0273–0313/P0405–0408, S21 P0178–0197/P0237–0240.

## Different kinds of company

With Kazami and Hiyori in the cafeteria, Hitomi is familiar enough to know which is speaking when the two look and sound uncannily alike. The twins’ shared timing entertains and sometimes inconveniences her. She will discuss things they find unsettling and rely on ordinary reports about who has been in class. She hears them uninhibitedly disagree about cola, contributes a bit of knowledge about Mexican cola, and tells them she feels comfortable sitting with them. When asked what she means she answers 「褒めてんのよ」. The texture depends on being allowed to complain, tease or leave an explanation brief. The ice cream conversation records the twins’ topic and Hitomi’s pleasure in being with them. It does not establish her own enduring preference for ice cream. Later she says she does not walk with them on science and mathematics test days because they will show off. Her willingness to rely on them is active, with minor preferences about when she wants their company. V04 S17 P0068–0109/P0261–0263/P0285–0292/P0415–0438, V07 S19 P0164–0173.

Hitomi also initiates private walks and practical questions with Yūki. In V06 she questions what school offers somebody who will not 「足を洗う」, and learns more of Yūki’s choice to keep studying. Her interest now has a recognizable legitimate channel. In V07 she distinguishes school from 「本業」, asks about both, admonishes Yūki over her exams and asks whether feeling invulnerable with one eye is really a good sign. She lets Yūki explain her judgment and conditionally grants it, 「あんたがそう言うなら」. Hitomi can recognize an experience of knowing one is in good form without acquiring a witness’s knowledge of the private trial that preceded it. Her ease with this slightly distant classmate is different from unquestioned comfort with the twins, and Yūki’s quiet satisfaction during a walk should stay on Yūki’s side of the exchange. V06 S20 P0498–0522/P0864–0873, V07 S19 P0158–0203. [The later school relationship](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e006--hitomi-hears-a-difference).

That walk gives Hitomi an ordinary urgent decision in a different state from the one before she recovered memory. A garbled message arrives from the twins. Hitomi tests a possible help phrase against how the letters are entered, keeps the interpretation provisional and decides for herself to go directly because a call might be unsafe. She neither asks Yūki to act for her nor waits for perfect certainty. Later the twins actually apply bandages and Hitomi reports that she defended them and fought off a reprisal. Hear the fights as her account and the wounds and reciprocal care as witnessed. She chooses her words to protect work knowledge from the twins even when comparing the attackers’ behavior to the organization she and Yūki both know. Alone, Hiyori later contributes money to an already active harmful commission, and the injured friend she invokes is strongly identifiable as Hitomi. There is no evidence that Hitomi asked for it, learns of it or wants the same thing. Her defense, the twins’ care, Yūki’s limited knowledge and Hiyori’s concealed retaliation are separate relationships and acts. V07 S19 P0204–0215, S21 P0082–0114, S25 P0003–0015.

## Written voice and conditional conduct

Hitomi often addresses a familiar peer as あんた and uses わ, のよ and かしら flexibly. Directness can reassure as well as antagonize. 「知らないわよ」 in the cafeteria sends overconfident speculation back to the twins without weakening the bond. 「こっちが聞きたいわよ」 is the same capacity under far different pressure and breaks off an attempted explanation she herself lacks. At the apartment she can apologize sincerely before violating the boundary again. On the phone, 「師匠に代わってくれるかしら」 asks politely through an old household connection while leaving out the most private part of the story. 「聞かせて」 later replaces that circumlocution when the subject is Kirihara. With Yūki in their school routine, short questions and teasing give way to technical inquiry, a warning and qualified assent. In the emergency she lowers the informational risk by making the occupational referent indirect in front of school friends. These are choices about speech and withholding in Japanese writing. The text provides no performed voice. V04 S17 P0309–0334/P0415–0438/P0447–0469, V07 S19 P0164–0199 and S21 P0085–0108.

In a comparable everyday situation after memory recovery, Hitomi is likely to test an ambiguous sign against practical knowledge, speak candidly to a familiar person, and act without complete verification when friends could be in immediate danger. The message and matching later injury are the strongest example. She can also warn and then defer to a person whose own experience better answers the question. Different commitments sometimes constrain each other. She investigates for herself until the pursuit overrides an acknowledged privacy claim; she sometimes leaves a bill, apology or private story for someone else to raise. Her strongest current inhibitor to resuming lethal play is an expressly reaffirmed valuation of ordinary life coupled to acute knowledge of the prior risk and attraction. Retained capacity and willingness to protect someone off game do not constitute a request to resume. There is too little evidence to predict disclosure of earnings, response to a new romantic attachment, how she would react to Hiyori’s secret choice, or a change after V07. The character who goes directly toward a possible emergency differs from the one who knows she also needs to come back to the cafeteria. Both are evidenced.
