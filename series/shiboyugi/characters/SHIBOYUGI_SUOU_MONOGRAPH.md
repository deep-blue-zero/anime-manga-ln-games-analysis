# Suō

Suō provides for a retreat before she knows whether a job will go wrong. She nevertheless misidentifies an object that matters to the retreat and lets a personal quarrel interrupt it until Sango gets her moving. She can interrupt her partner's angry coercion for the sake of an assignment and then herself order more serious harm to the person they are holding. Around a superior who has injured her before, the same attention to contingency takes the form of securing time, hiding pain and looking for the least conspicuous decision. V07 S21 P0274–0315/P0377–0412, S23 P0074–0129.

## The pair and the assignment

蘇芳, Suō, is one of the four Red Bear members who take Ramona against her will. Sango is identified as her partner when they leave. Azuki and Ebina are the people kept outside to look out. Kōen, the deputy chief, is their direct superior in the group's profit-seeking operations. Neither a civil surname nor an exact age is established. Ramona hears two youthful voices while the two people in the room keep their faces partly hidden. The text supplies enough to distinguish the partners' actions and much of their dialogue. It does not reliably assign which one first greeted her or exactly which person mimicked her accent. Ramona differentiates the speaker who uses あんた from the one who uses おねーさん. Sango continues holding the bat and uses the first form after the other goes to look outside. Suō is identified in the next viewpoint as the person who checked outside and sent her partner word to withdraw. This joins the later names to the two roles without extending the distinction to every unattributed line. V07 S21 P0207–0247/P0248–0315/P0377–0412. [The address cues and Ramona's refusal](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e005--ramonas-refusal-and-the-cost-of-useful-information).

The objective is information from the institute, valued by someone outside the immediate team. When Sango reacts to Ramona's refusal as contempt, blows intended to terrify her and personal claims about hardship are displacing the task. Suō says 「カッカすんなよ。目的忘れたのか？」 and stops the escalation in that form. She grabs Ramona's hair, asks whether she has changed her mind under the newly displayed threat and keeps pressing for information. 「墓まで持ってけアホ」 turns Ramona's stated confidentiality into a threat to her life, with a mocking explanation pitched at the foreign student. Suō then seeks a password and, faced with silence, gives a curt order for Sango to break a leg. The arrival of a new sound interrupts them before the demanded act is carried out. The scene shows an ability to reassert purpose over her partner's anger, not opposition to violence in carrying out the purpose. Neither her assessment of what might make Ramona comply nor her team's capacity to obtain the desired information is borne out. V07 S21 P0241–0247/P0263–0315/P0414–0421. [The cost of a person withholding information](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e005--ramonas-refusal-and-the-cost-of-useful-information).

## Retreat and uneven teamwork

With an approaching motorcycle audible in the room, Sango defers to the partner who leaves to check. Suō tells her to be ready, sights an actual motorcycle and changes the plan by communicating the withdrawal. Her point of view shows a habitual provision for escape as something she considers good work, with a useful effect here. Her first readings of the phone that drops in the course of leaving are less sound. She assumes it is Sango's and has to take Sango's correction. She then recognizes Akane and links the disruption to Akane's known objection to the direction of Red Bear. Part of the explanation is right. Akane is there to stop them. The device belongs to and was used by Yūki in the earlier effort to locate Ramona. Suō instead attributes the object to Akane. Her knowledge of a dissenting member makes that mistake feel especially complete. V07 S21 P0319–0373/P0377–0412/P0414–0421. [Akane's actual part and the separate ownership of the phone](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e007--akane-distinguishes-obligation-from-assent).

Suō shouts at Akane and wants to continue the exchange. Sango urges her to drive and physically prompts the decision. Suō takes the team away. Even then she tries to keep Akane from going to police by minimizing what happened and threatening her if she identifies the others. Akane's account of why she came is different from Suō's treatment of her as a disruptive teammate. Yūki and Akane's actual rescue is established in the scene, and Ramona is later physically uninjured. Neither fact grants Suō's claim that nothing relevant had been done. Ramona has been restrained and threatened for information. As a team, Suō and Sango can hear useful correction and change course under threat while retaining different views of the same action. Their roles do not settle which one controls every decision. This original rescue concerns Ramona. Maguma's later cooperation with Yūki and Akane begins in another operation after Sakura's capture. V07 S21 P0319–0373/P0377–0421, S23 P0890–0946. [Intervention in the abduction](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e007--akane-distinguishes-obligation-from-assent).

## What a table with Kōen asks of her

The pair arrive half an hour early for an appointment at midnight to discuss their failure. Suō is comforted by slow piano music, excessive light and the sounds of dishes in a nearly empty family restaurant. She wishes the moment could stay that way. This is a particular and unguarded response to an ordinary setting even though the interview makes her anxious. She knows that Kōen often pays for the food and that an order here will appear to say something about their attitude after failure. She keeps her gaze up and composes her face when Kōen hurts her under the table. Her past experience includes multiple incidents of being hurt by the same person. She is experienced enough to hazard that the superior is not particularly angry. She orders something safely ordinary and sees Sango follow. There is no answer to whether it was the right order. The food itself is not named as a favorite. V07 S23 P0074–0103. [The leaders' uses of pressure](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e008--three-ways-to-explain-a-groups-purpose).

Suō gives the actual report and adds why she thinks the offense will not come to official attention. She waits with sweaty hands. Kōen tells them there will be no punishment for the failed job, says their client cannot be reached and gives the two part of an advance. Her direct physical abuse continues around the same decision. Suō has learned to distinguish Kōen smiling at optional cruelty and falling expressionless when she takes violence to be necessary. The present account of past patterns belongs to the person who has lived under them, and Kōen's immediate anger about Akane fits enough of it to alarm her. It does not let Suō read all the reasons Kōen has for retaining, hurting or paying her. In a later coercive business scene, Suō is again active under Kōen and actually handles the money taken from a civilian, teasing and humiliating him. This shows continuing conduct, separate from the failed attempt on Ramona. It supplies no individual fate for Suō after the group breaks up. V07 S23 P0104–0129/P0436–0464, S25 P0019–0026. [Authority and ongoing work](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e008--three-ways-to-explain-a-groups-purpose).

## Speech and conditional appraisal

With captives, Suō's おねーさん combines feigned familiarity and a chosen disparity of power. The long approach through possible rewards and consequences can give way to the bare 「足折れ」 directed to her partner. When there is a practical threat to their own side, 「見てくる」 cuts through the talk. Suō's 「石蕗！」 identifies a known peer rather than an anonymous enemy. Her rough language to Akane tries to deny the account Akane could give and to deter her from giving it. The 「お疲れさまです、紅艶さん」 that the pair give their superior and Suō's hesitant 「……はい……」 place her on the receiving end of authority instead. Her private appreciation of the restaurant and reluctance to ask a personal question about her superior coexist with little outward display of either. Printed register and narrated reactions supply audience and effect. The masked captors' combined teasing cannot be distributed line for line and the print does not supply an actual vocal performance. V07 S21 P0224–0235/P0279–0315/P0392–0412, S23 P0078–0108/P0445–0451.

In a task where Suō expects local resistance, she is likely to establish a backup, read practical signs and ask others to stay focused. That supports action under an interruption. A plausible account of who caused the interruption may lead her to fill in facts too quickly. Toward someone whose compliance she wants, she can prefer controlled threats while authorizing serious harm if a softer pressure fails. Around an abusive superior she can withhold signs of fear, follow custom, and use accumulated behavior as a guide to what can be said or done safely. Sango's successful opposition to her hesitation in the car and the failed guess about the phone are counterexamples to taking her ordinary competence as independent control of the group. The portrayal has little basis for extrapolation to a lawful client, a group of strangers whose values she shares or a situation where she can dispute Kōen without risking retaliation. Her family, occupations outside the group and any romantic or intimate life are unestablished. The restaurant is a concrete comfort without proof of a favorite meal or durable routine elsewhere. V07 S21 P0279–0315/P0377–0412, S23 P0074–0129/P0436–0464.
