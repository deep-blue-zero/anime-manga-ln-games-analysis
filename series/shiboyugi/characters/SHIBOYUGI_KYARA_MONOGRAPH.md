# Kyara

Kyara (伽羅, キヤラ) shelters and teaches younger players, kills beyond the demands of Candle Woods, and explains what she does to an opponent in terms of frustration with other people and the situation they have made for her. She relishes aspects of the confrontation even while insisting that killing provides no solution to her anger. The younger players in her household take different things from her and attach themselves to her on different terms. The first volume introduces her through their effects and Yūki's discovery. Later volumes disclose more of the household and the consequences of her reputation. This portrait covers the admitted Japanese novels through V10 and contains spoilers.

## The household the reader learns about last

Shion remembers encountering Kyara before she became notorious. At that point she sees a person whose way of surviving in secret resembles her own and feels that Kyara accepts her as she is. Kyara houses Shion, Moegi and Hizumi. Shion reports receiving both practical instruction and dubious lore, alongside conversations about how to live. She considers Kyara her teacher in effect without remembering that she ever formally asked to become her pupil. The account is a substantial independent instance of sustained help. It also says something about its narrator, for whom the house provided rare happiness. Kyara's part in making it available can stand without a deduction that she felt everything Shion felt or welcomed everyone on the same grounds. (V04 S21 P0628–0640.) [Shion's account of the home](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e012--shions-ways-of-making-and-losing-a-home)

Moegi is more openly invested in Kyara as the model for someone who can impose her will. Kyara later says she ordinarily kills everyone in a game and makes her second outing a verbal exception, saying she let Moegi live because she was a good girl. This teaches Yūki something about their relation. It is a self-reported claim about the earlier outing, with an interest in explaining why people do not recognize Kyara. It gives no complete series of death counts. Moegi's own knowledge is detailed enough to use Kyara's reported preference for loose clothes to identify an elusive attacker. She nevertheless fears the teacher would kill her if they met in Candle Woods or would leave no opponents available for her to meet her quota. Kyara denies intervening on the pupil's behalf and, when told Moegi is dead, gives little outward sign of what she feels. The choice to shelter and teach and the absence of protection inside the game are distinct. (V01 S15 P0534–0569/P0612–0618/P0883–0905.) ## Why she wants to be left to her own judgment

When Yūki finds Kyara, the older-hand's appearance and calm posture among the dead initially resist her attempts to categorize them. Kyara answers one practical point directly. She likes looser clothing and had changed out of an outfit she dislikes. Moegi's earlier recollection corroborates that preference. Her account that obtaining the replacement in the condition she wanted made her angry explains one occasion. It is too small a cause by itself for the scale of the subsequent killing. When Yūki presses her, Kyara describes acting out in order to endure anger until it passes, comparing it to drinking to suppress an anxiety. Neither the comparative alcohol reference nor a literary impression of her appearance establishes an ordinary drinking habit. Her argument is that violence distracts and tires her without fixing what gives rise to it. (V01 S15 P0861–0911/P0917–0933.) [The encounter after the two accounts of the maze](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e009--a-borrowed-objective-and-an-improbable-victory)

She quickly takes Yūki's look as another example of being regarded from above for what she does. Then she gives the stronger claim that the environment made her this way and the other people are effectively driving her to it. The first part is a desire to be understood, the second an effort to place responsibility elsewhere. Her willingness to call Moegi an exception makes it harder to regard the choices as uniform reactions forced on her by others. Her words to Yūki can express real anger and try to make the opponent accept the same explanation of them both. Her habitual register in this conversation makes the shift easy to follow. Sparse うん or ううん and わかんないかな repeatedly find out whether the other person will take the next step in her reasoning. It changes into open assertion about 私 and derision of what the other person pretends not to want. The novel gives extensive speech directed at a rival and brings the kinder-looking household conduct mainly through other people's recollection. Her written dialogue there remains much less recoverable. (V01 S15 P0867–0933/P1029–1045; V04 S21 P0630–0636.) [Written register and the pressure of Yūki's viewpoint](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e012--where-the-text-lets-a-voice-be-heard)

## Preparation, welcome competition and limits

Kyara presents herself as sufficiently new because Candle Woods is, on her own count, only her tenth game. She had concealed her standing inside the novice group and later uses a changed costume. A modification to her body helps her survive what Yūki first takes for a conclusive blow. She disputes the distinction Yūki tries to draw between this preparation and more familiar modifications to the body. The source demonstrates that the preparation functions in the encounter. It does not supply a full inventory, the craftsperson's identity or permission formally given in advance. The more important distinction is between foresight and compulsion. She anticipates danger and expends effort and bodily cost to meet it, even while representing much of what follows as anger she cannot govern. (V01 S15 P0443–0461/P0867–0888/P0983–1015.) [Evidence the witnesses understand at different times](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e008--the-spectators-advantage-over-both-viewpoints)

She speaks in comparative terms about the injured Hakushi, pointing out the veteran's failing condition and wondering why she did not leave after earning enough. Later she eagerly praises Yūki's nerve in action. Kyara claims their real similarity is in finding this world more congenial than the world above it. Her speech moves from observation to provocation as she tells the younger woman what they both must secretly feel about dying there. When Yūki answers by claiming Hakushi's unfinished goal, Kyara smiles in a way Yūki reads as interested rather than mocking. Hakushi, who survives Kyara's attack despite appearing dead to both women, later suggests that a teacher with pupils of her own might have appreciated what the vow meant. Her suggestion and Yūki's sighting together support genuine interest. The reason for that smile remains indeterminate. Nor does interest in a worthy opponent settle whether one must enjoy each killing to act as Kyara does. (V01 S15 P0936–0962/P1029–1081 and S17 P0048–0092.) [Kyara's challenge and Hakushi's later assessment](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e010--a-surviving-mentor-whom-the-reader-has-twice-lost)

The confrontation ends in Kyara's actual death. She has overcome apparent defeat once and Yūki briefly admits the risk of misjudging it twice. The narrative confirms the later outcome. That order matters because later people interpret her before and after relying on another person's apparent death or physical resemblance. Shion experiences her loss as the end of the household and still finds the report hard to accept. The original scene gives stronger evidence of death than that difficulty gives of survival. (V01 S15 P1146–1158; V04 S21 P0637–0641.) [The decisive confrontation at Candle Woods](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e009--a-borrowed-objective-and-an-improbable-victory)

## What remains of her in other people's choices

In Cloudy Beach Airi asks Hizumi directly about Kyara after seeing a disturbing resemblance. Hizumi discloses a pupil relationship while protesting that it does not equip anyone to read her as the same kind of person. The later account shows that Etsusei, a pupil of Hakushi, staged the misleading appearance. Etsusei knows of Hizumi's relation and partly chooses her as a target because she fears another Candle Woods. Kyara's legacy thus helps produce an accusation against the wrong person and a separate actual choice made by someone afraid of repetition. V04 gives Shion's household evidence, while V10 gives Airi a later association with a caramel-haired girl without naming Kyara. These are reception and influence, with different strength depending on who speaks. They do not establish that Kyara trained Etsusei or took any new action. Yūki's later right-eye problems also give the old encounter a bodily consequence after Kyara's death, with delayed causal and clinical interpretation in V04. (V03 S19 P0282–0319/S21 P0003–0023; V04 S19 P0359–0376/P0605–0618/S21 P0608–0616/P0628–0640; V10 S21 P0338–0354.) [More than one kind of succession](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V03_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v03-e005--more-than-one-succession)

Kyara is likeliest to enter a serious exchange when she perceives a person as capable of understanding her or as someone worth testing. The known cases include practical teaching over time, provision of a home, tactical concealment, a harsh response to perceived contempt and actual interest in a rival's commitment. Secrecy and available means condition what she can attempt, and obvious bodily ability does not make her invulnerable to a competitor who perceives a constraint she missed. A pupil's admission to her household gives little ground for predicting unconditional loyalty in play. Established ordinary particulars are few beyond her clothing preference and the fact of the shared home. Her civil occupation, family history, routine tastes, private attachments and romantic or intimate life are unrecovered. Those limits leave her claims about belonging and what she found in the games intelligible without treating them as a complete autobiography.
