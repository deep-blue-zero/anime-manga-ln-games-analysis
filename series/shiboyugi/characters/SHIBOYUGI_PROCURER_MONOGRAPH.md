# The unnamed procurer

The procurer turns someone else's plan into a place people can actually go, a test people can undergo, or help a participant can use. He has reservations about what his longtime acquaintance Rinrin wants from an island encounter. His private judgments are unusually searching about that purpose. He still puts the encounter together and helps her maintain it. Later he does ordinary work for the people he once helped deceive, then helps Rinrin add a consequential condition to another trial. What he supplies, what he understands and what he endorses are separate questions. V05 S19 P0765–0805/P0837–0857/P0946–0969, S21 P0212–0217/P0339–0358, V06 S20 P1123–1148.

## The man at the other end of the order

調達屋, chōtatsuya, is a professional designation. The man who bears it in these encounters has no disclosed civil or game name or age. His work is in the neighboring economy that sells materials and services to players. His stated range in the narration runs from fruit out of season to a different family register, with illicit requests a practical part of the trade. This wide description establishes how he is presented to prospective clients. It does not prove he fulfilled every kind of request named as an example. He is the person who can supply a venue or equipment and stay to assist. The artisan who fits replacement parts and the agents who control access to the games have other responsibilities. His participation in a private test is real without evidence that he can enroll someone or reverse retirement. V05 S19 P0769–0778/P0921–0949. [The actual division of labor](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V05_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v05-e005--a-performance-with-more-than-one-perceiver).

He has known Rinrin since before the current approach. He remembers a marked loss of spirit since retirement. A call from her after a long gap comes with a brightness that reminds him of the past. She already feels she has something to transmit to Yūki before she has met her and wants the procurer to help her do it without compromise. He thinks her hope is to hand on what she has as a practitioner, then ask for death. He considers the value of a rare skill to others and the history of the practice as it appears to him. He also wonders whether the hope he sees is the desire to be rid of intolerable circumstances, so close to despair that the two are difficult to tell apart. That is his own judgment, formed from years of knowing her and from her new request. Rinrin's actual intention is separately evidenced later. V05 S19 P0769–0805/P0858–0917. [Rinrin's performance and his independent reading of it](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V05_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v05-e005--a-performance-with-more-than-one-perceiver).

## Discretion, dependence and a change of client

He furnishes a remote island and the materials needed for Rinrin's trial. He also provides himself as help. From another building he follows her movements and relays practical descriptions to the auditory access she still has. That is work under pressure with incomplete information, requiring selection and brevity. He cannot supply complete awareness in time, and he knows it. He credits Rinrin's judgment, audacity and sometimes sheer luck for the margin his descriptions leave. Some of her apparently effortless acts are almost unsupported by him. His good assessment of the limitation is stronger than a claim that he or the material alone enables all of her success. He helps her pass off a current dependence as an ability that resembles what she once did. Rinrin explicitly credits him with the choice of a design that keeps the visitors from searching for the collaborator. By her account he contributes to how the deception works, as well as supplying the objects. V05 S19 P0765–0805/P0814–0857. [The concealed partner and his own design contribution](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V05_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v05-e005--a-performance-with-more-than-one-perceiver).

His reservations do not stop either the preparations or the personal work. He has something he might say and does not tell Rinrin to stop. The novel gives the reader both the thought and the decision. A professional promise to deliver what was requested, personal attachment to a longstanding client, agreement with what can be taught and uncertainty about what would help her are all compatible with some of it. A simple account of endorsement would lose his sustained scrutiny of her hope. An account in which he has no part in the risks would lose the material help and the design choice. The two visitors actually discover him and interrupt the relay. After Yūki declines the intended kind of closure and Rinrin stays behind, he pilots the boat that leaves with Yūki and Tamamo. Rinrin says she expects to be sent back later, but her own transport is unshown. The act of helping the visitors leave has its own evidence. It does not establish a pact to oppose Rinrin in other matters. V05 S19 P0790–0805/P0814–0857/P0880–0917/P0946–0969. [The limits of the test and the actual departure](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V05_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v05-e005--a-performance-with-more-than-one-perceiver).

The later relationship with Yūki takes a less secretive shape. He provides a site for her escape training with Tamamo, which Yūki checks and understands as nonlethal practice. He subsequently makes another location and varied equipment available for her direct instruction. The novice does not have to guess accurately about a secret helper to benefit. He can provide a constrained venue for a teacher who chooses her own goals and techniques. The employment relation is sparsely documented in these scenes, and no fee or discussion establishes why he takes the new work. The change of recipient and character of the actual resource supplied are nevertheless real. This is a strong contrast with asking him to keep another person's abilities hidden during the island test. V05 S21 P0212–0217/P0339–0358. [The actual work of training](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V05_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v05-e007--what-gets-repaired-and-what-becomes-a-practice).

## A further request from Rinrin

Snow Room places him in the control room with Rinrin and Yūki's agent. The two physically separate settings have managed effects and an imbalance in stakes. The agent controls actual parts of the presentation. The procurer's contribution is distinct from the operator's ordinary game administration and from the identity of the competitor. Yūki names his help among the practical relationships that let her take part. That is direct evidence of recognition by a beneficiary, without showing full knowledge of his private arrangements. When the rule that prohibited violence is lost, Rinrin tells the agent she had asked the procurer to add that late alteration without the agent's knowledge. The rule change really takes effect. The agent turns to him. He silently looks away. The omission from the agent's briefing and Rinrin's account of the commission are stronger than any explanation of his glance. He might be avoiding responsibility, protecting a client or reluctant to endorse what is being said. His words do not decide this. Silence also gives no evidence that he secretly opposed the installation. V06 S20 P0320–0357/P1095–1106/P1123–1148. [Work in the control room](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V06_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v06-e008--work-on-the-other-side-of-the-wall), [The alteration and the agent's objection](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V06_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v06-e012--what-the-completed-work-says-about-solitude).

## Speech, ordinary scope and conditional appraisal

His relay is evidenced as concise, useful verbal selection, with more of its effects shown through Rinrin than of its exact wording. He privately murmurs 「……一体、どうなっちまうのかね……」. The contraction and the question aloud supply an informal register at odds with the controlled appearance of his work. It is not a question he directs to the client. His private reflections similarly use rougher and sometimes blunt forms when considering how much Rinrin relies on daring. In V06 his response to the agent's look is a visible act of silence. There is insufficient direct dialogue to extend this to his preferred name for a client, a fixed self-reference, vocal performance or tonal habits in other everyday relationships. His usual work supplies information about travel, preparations, present margins of equipment and making practical help available across visits. Food and leisure preferences, living arrangements, family and romantic or intimate life remain unknown. V05 S19 P0769–0805/P0946–0969, S21 P0212–0217/P0339–0358, V06 S20 P1141–1143.

Given a request from a known client who can specify what a location, supply or practical assistant must make possible, he can be expected to turn the objective into actual resources and consider what observers need to learn or be prevented from learning. His work from both sites supports that inference. When a client's larger purpose appears doubtful, he may privately evaluate and help anyway. The island and the hidden late modification both support that possibility, with strong evidence of the acts and unequal evidence of motive. Less fraught supply for Yūki shows he can also put skills at another person's disposal without making a mystery of the relationship. There is too little evidence to predict his choice under an explicit demand to harm a person himself, a client's refusal, a price dispute or a case where he must publicly decide between two people he serves. A refusal, an independent account of who obtained his agreement or a second observed job in which he holds something back would be more diagnostic than one more anonymous item delivered. V05 S19 P0769–0805/P0837–0857/P0946–0969, S21 P0212–0217/P0339–0358, V06 S20 P1123–1148.
