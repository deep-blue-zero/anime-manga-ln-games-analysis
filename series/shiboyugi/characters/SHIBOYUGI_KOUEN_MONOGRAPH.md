# Nanahoshi Kōen

Kōen knows what it is like to feel that other people will go on humiliating a person without consequences. She has also studied hard, met a school goal, worked in a conventional company and chosen to leave a group that once made life easier. Sakura remembers a smile at the departure and later weighs what it might have meant. Her return to Red Bear brings a new use for those abilities and for the grievance she still recognizes. She means to make other people feel a pain they have ignored. Where they cannot resist her, she can enjoy causing it. Where opposition ruins the arrangement that sustains her, she can let the people she used to direct go their own ways and wonder about another place for herself. V07 S23 P0074–0129/P0436–0474/P0623–0633/P0890–0917/P0983–1148, S25 P0019–0033.

## Coming into and out of Red Bear

南々星紅艶, Nanahoshi Kōen, is named as Red Bear's vice-chief. The original V07 portrait prints Koen Nanahoshi. She entered as a private-school student, later left on the established route into another life, and returned about a year before the events in V07. She had worked in a foreign company's corporate environment. Suō recognizes a style and bearing consistent with that real history. The job title, exact age and first stages of her later financial independence are unknown. She is shown living alone in a fairly large house, without a pet, much later. These different circumstances let a reader situate her outside the single moment of authority as deputy. V07 S23 P0078–0087/P0623–0633, S25 P0031–0033, S09-I01. [The factions and their different routes through the group](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e008--three-ways-to-explain-a-groups-purpose).

Kōen privately describes herself as capable of considerable patience in study and work. In the same recollection she calls fools intolerable. As a child she believed a teacher had disciplined her unfairly and responded with a serious assault. She refused her parents' demand to apologize, ran away for about a week, and stayed with a person she had only just met. Looking back she can recognize the danger of that choice. Early in high school she attacked a classmate she found domineering and received a brief suspension. She connects that disruption to finding Red Bear. Sakura's separate memory preserves another part of the story. A newcomer in a prestigious school did not fit its atmosphere, and people welcomed her even though her circumstances were unlike many of theirs. Sakura remembers the shared activity, a decline in visible rebelliousness, improved school results, successful admission to the school Kōen aimed for and a memorable smile at her exit. The past violence is represented memory with Kōen's evaluations built in. Sakura's reception and memory of a departure are a partially independent control on making those evaluations her only history. Exact circumstances of the confrontations, family life outside the episode and the meaning of that smile to Kōen at the time are unknown. V07 S23 P0623–0633/P0983–0998. [What the two former companions remember](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e008--three-ways-to-explain-a-groups-purpose).

At work she hears a colleague dismiss an unnamed person's apparently self-directed death on the railway. She remembers having contemplated something of the kind herself. The stranger's unknown state evokes a real sympathy in her at the same time as the colleague's contempt evokes rage. She attacks with the purpose of killing, is stopped by others and learns later that the colleague lived. Some legal consequences follow, without a full record of them or her last dealings with the employer. Kōen knows she had tolerated similarly callous comments before and cannot say why she lost control that time. Her subsequent dissatisfaction becomes a mission. She recalls what understanding had been possible within Red Bear. She wants to use the organization to force otherwise indifferent people to experience the insult and pain she associates with being an outsider. This is a purpose she reaches and articulates after the incident. The sources do not make it the motive she already held when she first joined or left. V07 S23 P0436–0474/P0623–0633/P0988–0998. [The grievance and the changes it drives](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e008--three-ways-to-explain-a-groups-purpose).

## An efficient superior with uneven obligations

Suō and Sango know her as the direct superior on a profitable coercive job. After the failure they arrive early for a midnight meeting at a family restaurant. Paying for what they order is a known practice of Kōen's. She asks for an account from the people who actually did the work even after learning the story elsewhere. She considers whether Akane has given their names or gone to police, declines a penalty for this failure under the circumstances, says the expected client has gone silent and distributes the small advance already available. Separately she hurts Suō during the meeting and hurts Sango after a literal question about an idiom. A personal reward for having power over an aide can coexist with a business judgment that an employee ought to be paid. Suō's experienced reading distinguishes the smile that goes with optional aggression from the colder face she sees when Kōen thinks violence is required. Kōen's shift when thinking about Akane lends the distinction weight, without making Suō an infallible observer of another person's inward life. V07 S23 P0074–0129. [A report, payment and coercion at the same table](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e008--three-ways-to-explain-a-groups-purpose).

Kōen's sympathy for an unknown, socially dismissed person does not include the man whose distress now sustains another of her operations. She watches a civilian ask to be let go, finds the expression on his face funny and turns her attention to the factions' progress. She prefers not to have to attack people from her own team, but considers the separation necessary if its strength is to grow. Casual enjoyment of a powerless person's distress and belief in a meaningful cause are both attested. The reaction she wants her former group to produce in an outsider has become an end she authorizes and sometimes enjoys. Suō, Sango, Akane and the civilian have distinct roles and levels of choice in this structure. The person capable of rewarding a useful associate is also capable of making association dangerous, especially when the other's judgment of what the group should do conflicts with her own. V07 S23 P0104–0129/P0212–0250/P0436–0474. [The cost of Kōen's idea of unity](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e008--three-ways-to-explain-a-groups-purpose).

Sakura remembers welcoming the student on the premise that an excluded person might recover and later leave. Kōen now expects the group to act as a common instrument. The messenger who challenges Sakura voices that aim and declares her no longer chief. This is the messenger's public act on behalf of Kōen's faction, before there is direct evidence of Kōen's personal treatment of Sakura. Later Kōen receives the news and photograph of the capture and personally encounters the injured, bound chief, who is unresponsive. She twice inflicts additional violence and regrets getting no response. In her own view the photograph visible to the rival faction has effectively brought the internal conflict to an end. It does not show the full outcome for Sakura. Kōen's disappointment that the founder Maguma has come back exposes a limit to her judgment about who would consider the struggle their business. She can also estimate her remaining forces and recognize when their size no longer gives her a likely victory. The two assessments, personal grievance at interference and practical recognition of likely defeat, coexist. V07 S23 P0187–0250/P0607–0633/P0877–0917/P0983–0998. [What capture settles for Kōen and leaves open for Sakura](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e008--three-ways-to-explain-a-groups-purpose).

## The risk she imposes and the later act she chooses

The dispute has already involved Yūki and Akane in reaching Ramona, after Suō and Sango's abduction under Kōen. Their later movement on Kōen after Sakura's capture is a separate action, converging with Maguma's return. Maguma agrees to handle the group gathered outside Kōen's room while Yūki and Akane go to Kōen. Kōen uses rooms in the abandoned hotel often enough to know one of its chairs will bear her weight. She privately gauges that the numbers on her side are unlikely to prevail and that she is losing the capacity to keep her anger under restraint. This is an active choice of a known place and a failing strategy, rather than an abrupt transformation with no earlier sign of practical judgment. V07 S21 P0319–0373/P0414–0436, S23 P0890–0946/P0983–0998.

She receives the two visitors politely and insists that Yūki take part in a potentially lethal wager. She claims the building will come down on everyone if they refuse and shows an object she calls a means to do so. Yūki assesses the claim as highly doubtful, but cannot rule it out. No actual destruction, verified capability or chemical expertise can be inferred from the claim. Akane offers to stand in for Yūki, who declines. Kōen takes the first apparent risk herself and taunts the other player during the exchange. She asks why her life has come to this and returns to the impunity of people whose contempt she cannot abide. When she believes the next turn should carry the lethal consequence, she takes it against herself rather than aiming at Yūki. It has been made nonlethal without her knowledge. The choice is evidence of a willingness to take that outcome as she understands it. It still leaves alternative initial purposes for the challenge, such as controlling the encounter, terrifying the others or finding a place to die. Yūki herself cannot settle the explanation by looking at her. V07 S23 P1013–1134. [The forced wager and the uncertainty of its purpose](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e009--figured-risk-the-genuine-shot-and-a-conditional-offer).

After discovering the empty wager, Kōen makes a new, genuine attempt at a self-directed shot. Yūki strikes her just ahead of discharge. The shot misses and actually hits a picture in the room. Yūki disarms her and takes the weapon away when leaving. This shot and its outcome are witnessed, distinct from every empty turn in the wager. Kōen now asks to be allowed to die and says she is tired. Yūki will not presume she knows exactly what Kōen feels. She offers another venue instead, telling her to come to the games if she wants the real thing and explicitly offering a real lethal fight there. Kōen recognizes the industry from rumors and infers the woman is a participant. She wonders during the ride home whether she could make a life work there. The invitation changes the possible future that appears to her. She has not decided to enter, found a sponsor, been invited by the operator or entered a game. V07 S23 P1118–1148/P1152–1161, S25 P0027–0033. [The actual shot and the invitation](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e009--figured-risk-the-genuine-shot-and-a-conditional-offer).

## Going home and the other people's commission

The fight has ended and Kōen tells those still able to listen that everyone can do what they like. She wants the others told the same thing when they wake. There is no formal disbanding proclamation, but her order removes her demand that they act together. She returns to a mostly unused home. The narrative establishes a life that had involved sleeping at members' houses, business hotels or the deserted hotel as her work moved around. She has to remember where she keeps the key. At home nobody greets her. These ordinary conditions explain why knowing a stable address did not previously give other people a stable opportunity to find her. They do not show a settled preference for solitude, particular foods or leisure, or evidence of romantic or intimate history. V07 S25 P0019–0048. [The end of work and the return home](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e010--the-commissions-close-after-the-fighters-leave).

An individual police officer has privately initiated a paid commission against Kōen. Different contributors provide the means for their own reasons. Kōen's release of her people, the group's defeat and Hiyori's decisive payment belong to independent agents' courses. In the house Fuchidori Yayoi acts against Kōen, who loses consciousness, and Yayoi and Edomoto remove her. The narration calls the operation completed and explicitly says they carried out the assassination. Yayoi subsequently thinks of the commission as the killing of someone she knew. There is no further Kōen point of view, examined body, separate clinical confirmation or later individual appearance in the admitted volumes. The endpoint therefore has strong explicit narrative standing as an assassination, alongside the narrower scope of the attack actually shown. Nothing makes it the completion of Kōen's own earlier attempt, a decision by Yūki to kill her, or a reliable forecast of what she would have chosen after going home. V07 S25 P0003–0053, particularly P0033–0053. [Whose act completes the commission](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e010--the-commissions-close-after-the-fighters-leave).

## Japanese address and bounded prediction

「好きに頼みなさい」 at the menu is an ordinary imperative backed by payment, although Suō knows that freedom to choose can have a social cost. 「特に咎めることはしません」 makes the status of the assignment precise without supplying safety from Kōen's physical treatment. 「大鉈を振るう」 lets her resolve about a change in the group appear as a managerial decision and gives her the chance to make a subordinate's misunderstanding another amusement. At the hotel she opens with 「こんばんは」 and 「どうぞ、お入りください」. Formal hospitality makes it harder to hear where conversation ends and coercion starts. 「死なせてくださいよ」, later, is directed as a request to the woman who has just intervened. The decorum of a sentence and how much control she retains are separate. The sharply personal 「馬鹿が嫌い」 is part of Kōen's own account of her pattern. Suō's described expressions and the mimicry of gunfire show what particular listeners notice. The source is print, with exact register, pauses and narrated effects but no performed sound. V07 S23 P0088–0129/P0988–0995/P1013–1074/P1128–1148.

Under organized conditions where Kōen has information, capable assistants and a known target, she is likely to gather direct testimony, budget effort and direct it toward obtaining leverage. Someone she regards as indifferent to injury or as doing group harm may draw a far more personal response. Her history shows both holding that urge in check and losing restraint, without an exact prediction of which similar provocation crosses the threshold. Where she can hurt someone with little risk and regard them as subject to her, approval or usefulness offers inconsistent protection. First departure from Red Bear, long stretches of study and work, measured payment after failure, and later dismissal of her followers all qualify an account of uninterrupted obsession. A prediction about rules in a new profession, the durability of her reaction to Yūki's invitation or how she would relate to a person able to hold her responsible without threatening the life she wanted would exceed the evidence. V07 S23 P0074–0129/P0436–0474/P0623–0633/P0983–1148, S25 P0019–0033.
