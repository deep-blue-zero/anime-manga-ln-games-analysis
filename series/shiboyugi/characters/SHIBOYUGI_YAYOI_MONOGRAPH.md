# Fuchidori Yayoi

縁鳥八代衣, Fuchidori Yayoi, is eighteen when the reader follows her out of the Tochinoki apartments and into the profession she inherited. Her family prepares successors by forcing children to fight to the death. It gives her firsthand competence and a well-established prospect of inheriting its headship. She wants enough of a choice to see what else it might mean to live. She cannot be sure that an exceptionally watchful tenant is a colleague, but the apparent possibility gives her courage to ask how someone might make a life in repugnant work. She is also observed acting like a competent, protective neighbor in another kind of emergency. The shared capacity to perform different work helps explain why a mistaken professional recognition can become a real increase of trust. Her strongest direct scenes are in V07 and V08. Events after she is recommended to help the landlady remain outside the witnessed portion through V10. V07 S19 P0078–0142/P0234–0345/P0449–0476, S25 P0040–0053, V08 S20 P0529–0622/P0762–0788 and S22 P0051–0069.

## What she has learned to imagine

Yayoi first killed at five in a contest set up within the family to choose promising children. By twelve she performs family work proficiently, with the narration describing close to a hundred deaths. By fifteen she feels she can never get away. Her mother Yumiko actually heads the main family in the period described. This is a background of real control, with the family's far-ranging connections and its specific use for a designated successor narrowing her future. Yayoi privately considers whether leaving openly, simply disappearing, or using some leverage against the head might work. She thinks each approach would be stopped or create danger she could not match. A plausible argument that the family would stop her is still her appraisal, rather than a scene of attempted escape or a checked universal prohibition. The character's continuing action in work depends in part on that appraisal. Her own ability to judge risk gives it a serious basis, while fear and learned inevitability may further narrow what she pictures. V07 S19 P0234–0265. [An inherited life](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e003--an-inherited-profession-and-an-apparent-peer).

She accumulates unread books on a phone reader and likes fiction because she can project herself into another person's life. The return home from work through ordinary station crowds prompts this more specific preference. Her feeling that she is a caged bird adds form to the contrast with a high place from which the surrounding city briefly makes freedom seem possible. She can experience comfort there without mistaking it for an actual change of future. She chooses what to read, and in company on the windy rooftop she can offer cocoa she brought in a thermos. To draw direct character inference from her reserved outward manner alone would lose both the work she can do administratively well and her real need to imagine lives beyond it. Her private idea that she will go to hell gives an appraisal of what the future feels like. Her family's exact early domestic behavior, other interests and relationships beyond the witnessed roles remain open. V07 S19 P0244–0265/P0291–0345/P0358–0376/P0416–0427. [The value of a purpose](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e004--giving-a-motive-and-carrying-one).

## Edomoto and competent cooperation

Edomoto Tōhei comes from a branch family and supplies much of the investigation, preparation and transfer her work needs. She accepts his direct reports and the restricted reach of information shared on a particular job. It can make her feel competent while making it difficult to judge the action as a whole from where she stops. In one job she can tell a man is alive when they finish her part and his body is carried away. That situation is not solved merely by knowing her employer assigned an assassination. She can recognize that a body or evidence has been handled from what the collaborator did, whose authority and purpose reach farther in a different part of the work. She has reason to value a genuine wish of luck, ご幸運を, because preparation leaves the risk of contingency. When he uses 八代目 as if the hierarchy leaves no other address, she asks him to use simply one of her names. His awareness that it irritates her sometimes motivates his choice. She can criticize a piece of his acting once she sees its defects and hear practical arguments from him on the next job. Familial position, effective cooperation and actual warmth are visible through different acts. The observed exchanges supply no romance and no release from the work’s tenure. V07 S19 P0103–0142/P0217–0280, S21 P0446–0484 and S23 P0794–0840. [Working partnership in context](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e013--address-politeness-and-what-registers-conceal).

She recognizes a new commission concerns somebody she knows and still takes it on. The difficult victim is Kōen, whose activities appear genuinely harmful to Yayoi. She thinks killing her to stop them is enormously severe. After acting she can verify part of the practical finishing work and feel that she has done nothing to answer what the whole practice is for. Previous objections and the effect of a neighbor’s advice survive a genuine contractual act. Her provisional purpose is meaning in seeking meaning, enough to continue for now. It is an adoption without a conversion. The competence to do the next job does not require enthusiasm, a change of private feeling or approval of the social allocation of harm. In this instance a local finality to her work comes from completion of her part and the continuing joint assignment. The final death at a precise time is not supplied through her live observation of the victim. V07 S19 P0264–0280/S21 P0446–0484/S25 P0038–0053. [The attempt at a reason after an actual job](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e010--the-commissions-close-after-the-fighters-leave).

## A mistake about a colleague can support real reliance

She is watchful enough to identify a stranger in ordinary life as potentially dangerous through small patterns, but watchfulness leaves her with beliefs she has to test. When a quiet, practiced neighbor says こんばんは in an unexpectedly explicit way, Yayoi wonders whether she has been discovered. She repeatedly persuades herself it is likely coincidence and uses further observations. Yūki was rushing to class. Yayoi also notices aptness, seeming danger and the car that arrives from time to time, correctly infers work involving violence and then assumes its specific form from the subset of professions she knows. She wants Yūki to answer a question which is difficult to address with anyone at home. She risks violating the building’s understood protection of privacy by asking if Yūki is カタギ. Each responds to the other as if she has learned they have the same work. Neither extends that observation into mutually specific disclosure. Yūki is about to explain her own central motivation, the goal of ninety-nine clears, when Yayoi asks her to stop. She wants a direction she can explore. She lets the conversation end there and offers Yūki cocoa. Her information about the other tenant is weaker than what she receives from that conversation about her own life. V07 S19 P0076–0109/P0289–0443. [What shared vocabulary can hide](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e004--giving-a-motive-and-carrying-one).

During the later assault, she has real professional resources and uses them to secure two residents. She asks them to stay protected and try a working route to reach police if possible, and leaves them a way to defend themselves. She explains to Ramona that she and Yūki are 同族, allowing a useful reply based on the same mistaken classification. It supplies more sense of the local danger without causing Ramona to know exactly what either woman does. Yayoi recognizes that one member of the invading party really belongs to the same profession and subdues him. She then stays in room 101 with Ramona and the landlady. From the sounds and the disappearance of hostile presence she concludes that Yūki killed the three intruders. Awahime actually killed them. Yayoi counts ten attackers on site with six left, without knowing about a further pair away from the building. The actual protective act and mistaken conclusion about violence belong together. When Yūki leaves the apartment she tells the landlady to rely on Yayoi if need arises. That advice constitutes a new trust grounded in the crisis. A durable mutual recognition of work or final arrangement is unavailable. V08 S20 P0529–0622/P0762–0788 and S22 P0051–0069. [Four residents with unequal evidence](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V08_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v08-e009--four-views-of-the-same-address).

## Voice and expected limits

The direct question to Yūki about カタギ is followed by a very specific account of material cues, asked in politely advanced form while trying not to give away what it costs. Address with さん and the colloquial classification both remain useful. With Edomoto she wants a familiar name without the title 八代目 and can use sparse questions or reply directly about a failed performance. With Ramona, phrases such as 明かりをつけてはいけません and ここに留まってください are formal while decisive. They are directions spoken under acute pressure, with genuine authority from the speaker’s attention to a particular threat. In the unspoken inquiry into her life, she calls herself a caged bird and writes doubt and hope into consecutive possibilities. None of those interior questions is automatically available as her utterance. Any claim about performance would require another source beyond this text and illustration boundary. V07 S19 P0257–0265/P0314–0345, S21 P0451–0464, V08 S20 P0552–0576.

In a comparable professional assignment she can combine prepared support, awareness of nearby people and careful private reservations. She usually needs specificity before accepting another actor’s intention. A known neighbor with a live risk can prompt intervention independently of her employer. A true but limited answer and a dangerous mistaken inference remain possibilities when classifying an unrelated practice. She is not secure in a political or family capacity that would allow her to be known as an unconditional defector. The novel provides neither her completed departure, change of her mother's position nor a broadly attested pattern of romance. Fiction, cocoa, trained work, an attempt to find room for moral purpose and a small share of ordinary protection provide firmer ranges of behavior. V07 S19 P0244–0269/P0289–0376 and S25 P0040–0053, V08 S20 P0529–0622 and S22 P0051–0069.
