# Hakushi

白士 is the playing name of 白津川真実, Shiratsugawa Manami. A highly experienced practitioner of the organizers’ lethal games and the first sustained teacher of Yūki, she learns by looking for what could make her competence fail. She applies this scrutiny to her own body, rules and pupils. When she cannot continue her usual work, she reuses the same habit on the organization that once employed her. Her legacy is often described as handing on a number, ninety-nine clears. Her decisions make it more demanding than that. She gives a pupil a route to live and then tells her to disregard her judgment. She cares that another player might reach ninety-nine and accepts that she may decide to stop. Early experiences and the exact extent of her present knowledge have different evidentiary limits. This portrait covers the event order through her investigation of Kūron and V10’s later references to her, with spoilers throughout. ## A teacher’s early test

At the earliest detailed encounter she is already highly experienced. In Hormone Town, Yūki’s third game, the sheriff catches a talented outlaw who has an attractive but wrong model of what an obstacle can stop. The arrested woman survives. By the time Hakushi explains the result, the remaining sheriff group has no need of another death for its returns. She uses the available margin, notices that a question about a name drew the young woman’s civil name, and infers novice status before learning an exact count. That combines tested judgment of the body in front of her, professional vocabulary and an understanding of institutional incentives. Her initial declaration that she will make Yūki her pupil scarcely treats the other woman as a chooser. Yūki asks whether Hakushi is her mother and explicitly accuses her of projecting her own past. Hakushi pauses, acknowledges that she once did not care if she died and reformulates help as a means of remaining alive long enough for a choice to arise. She ultimately gives Yūki the choice of whether to take it. Yūki's later narration can propose several reasons why she agreed. Her assent does not contain the developed ninety-nine plan. V05 S16 P0003–0093/P0096–0146. [The early encounter](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V05_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v05-e002--who-can-accept-the-invitation).

It matters that her recollection of her own change and her later claim about young Yūki are separate. Hakushi says in the early encounter that she changed her estimate of dying. Later she tells Yūki she did not initially see the apathetic novice’s likely death as particularly objectionable. Elsewhere Yūki remembers being told that finding something to do had calmed her teacher’s own diffuse anger. These are windows into chosen practical reasons and retrospective distance. None dates an onset of anger, establishes family circumstances or explains all the interest in Yūki. Serious mentorship follows. Hakushi insists on recording each game, cultivating enough usable knowledge across several kinds of problem and recognizing small flaws before they become irreversible. She offers training to prevent a future failure she cannot specify, while competition continues to preserve some effects of chance. ## Candle Woods and the disputed count

At Candle Woods she is very near her own goal. She has taken three months away from play after the pool game and emphasizes being physically prepared. Yūki would ordinarily prefer to keep the rhythm of coming back. Hakushi's reply that she will know after the game avoids turning the choice into a universal prescription. She brings a folk tale to bear on whether the rabbit players may find something at the destroyed tanuki mascot, distinguishes expressly delivered rules from likely equipment and timing, and works among other survivors. She tests and occasionally exploits ways an opposing person can misread a corpse. She does so knowing much more about her manufactured durability than her own agent, let alone Yūki. Role and preparation still leave her open to defeat. Kyara's performance makes her judge that her own body and readiness have reached a limit. Yūki eventually finds a concealed weapon beneath the broken tanuki mascot and infers that her mentor left it as an opportunity. Hakushi later says she meant to entrust her aim to Yūki and was surprised by how explicitly her pupil took it up. Kūron connects the concealment to the act of entrustment. The recording of the events gives an intended beneficiary, without Yūki receiving a direct spoken appeal from Hakushi at the apparent death. It has real cost and also pursues an advantage against a powerful adversary. V06 and V08 use ninety-five in narration, while V09 has an original statistical-looking plate that gives ninety-five for the ranking and a prose account of ninety-five consecutive wins. One could model a public record that diverges from a directly shown result, or distinguish consecutive wins from a differently counted event. The prose and the plate supply no actual bookkeeping rule that makes either reconciliation safe. Hakushi’s actual exit choice and future work are clear independently of that unresolved difference. ## A pupil’s freedom and a practitioner's body

Koyomi’s disclosure makes Hakushi’s survival accessible to Yūki long after the reader has seen it. Yūki meets her at a magic bar after the encounter with Etsusei, whom Hakushi had also taught. Hakushi says Etsusei had independently investigated the extreme means by which she passed for dead. Etsusei understood reproducing the effect as evidence of learning the teacher's practice. For Yūki, damaging a body this way as a means to look like a gifted teacher mistakes the underlying discipline for one result. Hakushi declines to pick a deserving survivor. The opening 永世だ is completed by the question of whether Yūki would really change places with Etsusei. Yūki says no. Hakushi calls her no longer a pupil for whom an outsider's evaluation determines the answer. In talking with Koyomi shortly afterward, she both calls Yūki an idiot and says she still hopes for her success. She can recognize someone else's freedom while caring how that person uses it. This releases an authority claim in one conversation; it does not dissolve the continued ability of either woman to affect the other. V03 S19 P0482–0512/S21 P0344–0397 and S23 P0010–0093, V05 S19 P0193–0241. [The meeting at the bar](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V03_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v03-e011--the-answer-from-someone-at-the-bar).

The same bar gives an unusually concrete trace of the private cost of continued competence. She goes there often enough for familiarity and orders a drink. Her fingers cannot feel temperature while she guides the glass, and the alcohol reaches constructed organs. Yūki remembers at a comparable count that her teacher had become very extensively modified and appeared less often at games. In the later discussion of the wall of thirty, Hakushi herself recalls games #83 and #90 as the ones that hurt her badly. These details establish a sequence of practical adaptations and deteriorating participation. The artisan confirms he has made prostheses for her. The design and provider of each particular alteration remain uncertain. Her own statements about present capacity must answer to actual performance. The unusual condition does not settle ordinary moods, ongoing health or romantic life. Recurring bar visits, paying for Yūki's drink, considering a friend's retirement and declining a chance to watch private recording are concrete evidence of social and leisure behavior. Food, a family history, a romantic attachment and experiences beyond the story’s few glimpses are open. Respect appears vividly in someone whose strengths are unlike hers. Koyomi can recognize when to avoid an outing. Hakushi finds it easy to say 臆病者 to a drink companion and privately considers her a genius at detecting fatal risk, recalling that she stayed away from Candle Woods merely because it felt wrong. Koyomi's choice to leave produces the advice to use her money elsewhere. Her fearful refusal of even a suggested alternative investment supplies a comic check on the range of Hakushi’s advice. Etsusei's ambition, by contrast, makes one extraordinary performance the desired template. Yūki’s need to teach Tamamo asks for still another relation. Hakushi recommends applying the newcomer’s new sense of responsibility, knowing enough about her former pupil's individualism to call it a chance to take another person seriously. She hears the name Rinrin and advises bracing for the meeting without pretending to know her closely. Guidance at times consists of relinquishing the next action to someone who knows more. ## What a retired practitioner can still do

The Snow Room trial tests a distinction between someone deciding to play hard and someone planning all the harm that follows. For Hakushi it is a relatively safe mock game with imperfectly disclosed rules and an unknown opponent. The costume and penalty on her side are different. Her personal knowledge of Yūki, what Yūki has told her about Tamamo and Rinrin and the apparently unnecessary duplicate settings help her guess whom she is facing. While Rinrin is away, Hakushi keeps talking to draw Setsuna to the microphone and asks how closely her theory fits. Setsuna begins asking what Hakushi would do if it were true. The concrete risk Hakushi later evaluates is that winning on her safer side could cause Yūki to lose on the dangerous side. She submits an answer after inferring that Yūki agreed to the test. Other hazards, including the separate trigger set up by Rinrin, have their own source of authority. Her hypothesis of a phantom has the wrong person for one of the appearances. Her answer to the last puzzle is correct and the floor still drops under her because she overlooks a separate trap. Assessing the hidden consequence and best effort in this case therefore requires attention to the actual structure of both rooms, an old relationship and gaps in present information. V06 S20 P0337–0357/P0526–0624/P0712–0833/P0875–0934/P0993–1019/P1153–1168.

Her subsequent banter allows Yūki to question an account she would once have been tempted to accept. A story about deliberately surrendering the advantage is inconsistent with the retired player’s own reasoning when falling. She then acknowledges the error. She can describe a workaround retrospectively, while a solution involving Yūki’s physical leap remains something she herself could not do. At the same trial she treats the previously unknown older Rinrin with sustained politeness, even while finding the abrasive form amusing. Their attitudes are not evidence of a previous teaching bond. In a recognizable competitive exercise she is likely to look for what an apparent rule omits and to impose available pressure if the other party’s willing involvement is credible. Here that willingness is her inference and the misplaced attention can leave her exposed to a separate physical event. Her remaining speed of puzzle solving and current movement are evidenced separately. V06 S20 P0875–0934/P1153–1168 and S22 P0053–0107. ## The wider institution as an object of inquiry

Retirement makes available roughly two and a half years of research, by her account and narration, into the organization’s origin, purposes and authority. Hakushi says having time made the context of her earlier life newly worth knowing and grants the earlier goal continues to interest her. She had previously refused Shiro's offer of purportedly comprehensive institutional data for expertise. She tells Kūron she had no reason to know the material was true and accepts a direct conversation in which she can get answers checked. Even on meeting a person with the outward appearance of a child, she keeps the appointment serious once he identifies himself as Kūron, the head of the game organization. Narration credits her with learning its story and withholds most of what she found. Kūron still supplies new information about the dealings between Shiro and Yūki. He also offers a strong rival account. Seeking him out could be suicidal risk disguised as investigation, and the old ambition could not really be transferred to a pupil. Hakushi, who was willing to stop following actual loss, calls the interpretation badly wrong and suggests the succession struggle may predispose him to it. Her willingness to trade barbs, her refusal of the earlier deal and her actual investigative effort give substance to the stated preference for verification. Residual attachment is directly visible in the work and in her expectation of Yūki. The question of whether that attachment explains all of the inquiry survives Kūron's proposed reading and her reply. V08 S22 P0109–0177, V09 S24 P0008–0038. [Retirement and the direct meeting](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V08_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v08-e012--the-reward-and-the-contest).

She asks if the Wall of Thirty is an actual institutional or physical phenomenon. She has heard other practitioners describe unusual events and had one herself. When Kūron gives her graphs and denies setting special disadvantages, she reads the data skeptically and acknowledges it bears against what she had thought. He suggests that an experienced practitioner already handles ordinary kinds of failure, so the failures left available are liable to be surprises. She can see a relationship to what happened to her and wonders whether her pupil will follow the same course. Asking the question, changing the ranking of plausible answers and declining an earlier rumor are all better warranted than saying she found definitive statistics. The dataset and testing method do not reach the reader. She believes her investigation has established a great deal, but her access to that information gives no route to most of its contents. Her account with Kūron and her returned attention to an apprentice express a deliberate move from individual risk assessment to studying the institution’s claims. V01 S17 P0095–0119, V09 S24 P0008–0038/P0292–0322.

## Written register and the ordinary limits of the portrait

Hakushi appears commonly as 私, with clipped queries and お前 among those she teaches. 名前を教えろ and 私の弟子にしてやろう express an authority she assumes she can exercise in the early case. The narrator represents her pause and choice of words when the audience objects. At the bar, 外野の評価なんざ、気にするな uses a similar terse order to loosen dependence on her now. With a long-standing companion, きさま and 臆病者 land beside serious private respect. ごきげんよう to Kūron and sustained politeness toward senior Rinrin depend on the audience and circumstances. She is blunt with both people once the argument requires it. Her さあな when Koyomi asks about ninety-nine evades a specific question, while 私が望むのは真実だけだ gives a newcomer a condition for believing the institution. Expressions of age or generation, including 女の勘 in V01, are the writing’s means of locating her in a professional and social world. They do not supply birth date, accent or a recording. Yūki describes a low, soft and oddly carrying voice in Candle Woods. That is the limited published audibility attached to a particular encounter. V01 S15 P0096–0117/S17 P0078–0094, V03 S23 P0015–0025/P0049–0056/P0063–0093, V05 S16 P0096–0146, V06 S20 P0875–0934, V08 S22 P0110–0149.

A reported strong performance or good information is likely to interest her more when she can check it. A susceptible student would have grounds to expect specific criticism and useful alternatives, with no guarantee she would keep protecting the student from the implications of choosing. Yet she also intervenes closely when the other person's apparent lack of a future matters to her, misses a machine's actual effect through concentration on a plausible rule, and uses banter to protect herself after a mistake. A model of a wholly dispassionate analyst would lose all three. The exact threshold for her own preparation, physical intervention or withdrawal in a novel present-day emergency remains uncertain. At V10 she enters the story as a precedent in other people’s decisions and comparisons. That does not turn the ways she continued after earlier serious injury into her judgment about what Yūki ought to do in the hospital.
