# Kirihara

Kirihara loses both legs during a playing career and chooses a second trade out of a hobby she already practices. Her new life is connected to the people and skills she knew before. She teaches Hitomi early enough for the instruction to matter to a competitive career, works for Shion as a tattooist, and employs the runaway twins Haine and Kokone so that they no longer have to depend on active play to live. The privacy of her house and what a client has reason to fear she knows become consequential in another way. A great deal is known about what Kirihara gives people access to and what access she reserves. The main evidence about her own reasons is a wry refusal of Haine's expected explanation. V04 S17 P0348–0358/P0442–0469, S19 P0125–0134/P0161–0165/P0226–0244/P0405–0408, S21 P0178–0197/P0237–0240.

## Name, work and the continuity of the house

桐原佳奈美 is her civil name, Kirihara Kanami. 錐原, read キリハラ with the same sound and a different first character, is the player name under which Hitomi and others remember her. Her playing career was more than ten years before the events Yūki investigates in V04, a generation earlier than Hakushi and Koyomi's according to what Yūki has been told. The reported near thirty is a number of clears. There is no established age at retirement. Kirihara lost both legs to a severe injury as a player. She obtained replacements from the artisan who now cares for Yūki's own parts, and nevertheless retired after receiving them. Tattooing became her second career. The practice had interested her before it was work. This documents one durable interest transferable between kinds of life, without disclosing her first interest in body art, her earlier family situation or why she began playing. V04 S19 P0125–0134/P0161–0165/P0287–0291, S17 P0442–0469. [Yūki traces a retired practitioner](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e005--the-labor-of-getting-an-account), [The injury and career change](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e007--red-blood-death-and-affiliation-after-retirement).

Her former client relationship with the artisan and continued professional contact make her reachable years later. He says he sees her at regular examinations and can offer an address, even if he does not know whether she still lives there. He thinks a former player who moved into service work might know something, without possessing her actual answer. She has a large house in a residential area, with a gate, entryphone and a separate inner door. Yūki speculates about tattoo income or other money because the house appears expensive. The house itself and the existence of both employees establish living and working resources. Neither supplies an account of the income that let her maintain them.

The twins can open the gate, and handle visits and calls in the course of looking after the house. They tell the visitors that only Kirihara keeps the front-door key and that she does not like giving one to anybody else. Retaining the key while accepting the normal assistance of employees is evidence of control over one point of access, even with people who have lived and worked there for years. Yūki interprets it as the caution one would expect of a former player. That explanation is plausible. Kokone and Haine, who know the house, establish the habit rather than giving Kirihara's first-person reasons. They initially suppose she is asleep. Yūki's thought that Kirihara must be a night person comes from the time and the twins' reasonable guess, while Kirihara's actual unresponsiveness in this scene is due to her death. The evidence supports private domestic access more strongly than a sleep preference. V04 S19 P0125–0134/P0161–0165/P0226–0244/P0273–0292. [The house and its boundaries](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e007--red-blood-death-and-affiliation-after-retirement).

## Teacher, employer and tattooist

Hitomi began to play because a friend offered the chance while she needed work. Separately, she remembers a teacher providing the know-how that helped her and her friend win. Later in V04, once Hitomi has recovered memories she had chosen to lose, she calls to speak to that teacher. She already knows Kirihara had retired. Kokone answers from within the older relationship circle and tells her Kirihara was killed the previous month. Kirihara's help and Hitomi's wish to renew contact occupy different directions. The friend who recruited Hitomi is another person. The actual content of the instruction and the terms on which Kirihara agreed to teach remain undisclosed. Hitomi's experience supports the usefulness of the instruction without letting a later reader assign every success to Kirihara. V04 S17 P0348–0358/P0442–0469. [Hitomi's past and changed knowledge](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e004--hitomi-and-the-knowledge-in-the-body).

Haine and Kokone ran away before finishing compulsory education and turned to the games because participation brought concrete material support. Haine remembers Kirihara understanding their situation as a former player. Kirihara takes both into her household as employees, making it possible for them to stop regular participation. Haine has been employed there for years by the time of her account. They still have industry ties and their work is occasionally connected to its difficult customers. The act of employing them makes a less dangerous course possible while giving Kirihara the help she says she wants. Haine keeps the reasons for running away private even from Kirihara. Accepting help does not dissolve the limits the recipients set on disclosure. In Haine's direction the years of work give rise to a bond stronger in her eyes than family. After the death she returns to play with revenge as her dominant aim. Kokone loses the same employer, helps investigate, does different work around the outcome and eventually offers a contact useful to a surviving player. Their respective responses show the scale and variety of Kirihara's continuing importance to them and do not establish her own desired response to any future attack. V04 S19 P0226–0244/P0336–0345/P0547–0573, S21 P0178–0197, S23 P0010–0049. [Haine and Kokone's distinct courses](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e011--haine-acts-kokone-watches).

Kirihara's most informative direct speech comes in Haine's memory of asking why they were taken in. 「──ただお手伝いが欲しかっただけだよ」 offers a utilitarian explanation. The familiar あんたら and the following comparison to how many people she could employ make a serious debt possible to talk about without demanding a solemn display of gratitude. They can also keep Haine from knowing what she was asking about. Haine hears evasion and weighs embarrassment, sincerity and a whim. The practical motive could coexist with affection or care, and the fact that the help was sustained says more than a confident diagnosis of her private feelings. The print gives a brief, familiar explanation with sentence-final よ and さ directed to someone in her household. The much more ceremonial speech with which the twins identify their employer is theirs. There is insufficient direct speech with pupils, clients or peers to assign Kirihara their forms, a regular first-person pronoun or an acoustic register. V04 S19 P0233–0244, S21 P0180–0193. [The practical account of hiring](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e011--haine-acts-kokone-watches).

Shion remembers having commissioned her tattoos from Kirihara before becoming aware of the twins. The choice of a tattooist familiar with that client's identity and the visible results of the work survive the relationship. Haine's view that Kirihara had customers whose dealings could not be made public is based on her real employment and comes in an inward reflection on the death. It cannot establish awareness of every client's purpose. Shion later takes deliberate steps to remove people who could identify her, including Kirihara. In the investigation, the view that the killing was intended to silence a potential witness initially comes from what Keito and Yūki can infer. Shion's focal account independently supplies the purpose. It does not give a scene of a tattooist learning about Garbage Prison or a final decision to cooperate in an investigation. Kirihara is an actual provider of skilled work and a potential conduit for an identification because she knew a customer. Her knowledge of Shion goes further than that of many others. Its full scope and her willingness to use it are unknown. V04 S19 P0299–0313/P0405–0408, S21 P0187–0193/P0237–0240. [What the customer tried to eliminate](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e007--red-blood-death-and-affiliation-after-retirement).

## Death, aftereffects and a bounded account of her choices

Yūki, Keito and the twins enter and find her dead. The two household members recognize her and Yūki checks both artificial legs. The red blood and smell are unfamiliar to Yūki in a world in which current players receive a preparatory treatment. Keito offers a plausible time-and-tissue explanation. The body supports the absence of the usual effect in this case. It supplies no general expiration schedule or details about Kirihara's own view of the procedure. The substance of the death is known to the reader and to people who see the body before the point at which Hitomi calls her former teacher in story time. The narrative discloses the call first. After the death, Kokone knows of an old acquaintance of Kirihara's and arranges a new route for Yūki to seek instruction. The later meeting identifies that person as Rinrin. This is concrete evidence that another relationship continued to be useful, without amounting to access to either retired woman's private history with the other. V04 S17 P0442–0469, S19 P0273–0313, S23 P0039–0049/P0065–0067, V05 E004. [The death and its evidence](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e007--red-blood-death-and-affiliation-after-retirement), [The contact Kokone offers](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e014--two-returns-after-a-win), [The later encounter](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V05_DEEP_READING.md).

Kirihara responds to practical dependencies with arrangements that can last. A person facing the costs of play can receive transferable expertise, a place to work, or an alternative way to live when she has both something to offer and a reason to become involved. Kirihara is capable of taking substantial responsibility under those conditions and of keeping control over access to her private rooms. With someone inviting an explicit account of benevolence, the one attested reply is likely to permit a shared practical explanation while closing down a discussion of personal motive. These are stronger predictions in circumstances resembling Hitomi's instruction and the household than in an encounter with a stranger in imminent danger, an agent offering a return to play or a client caught in a crime. The decision to retire and make a living out of tattooing is established. Her current fighting ability, her thoughts during the attack, her broader ethics toward clients and the values she thought she was teaching are not tested. Her ordinary preferences beyond tattooing and the held key, family biography and any romantic or intimate history are unestablished. Direct evidence of her choosing how to teach, negotiating work across different clients or accepting a request that intrudes on the household would give a firmer basis for extension.
