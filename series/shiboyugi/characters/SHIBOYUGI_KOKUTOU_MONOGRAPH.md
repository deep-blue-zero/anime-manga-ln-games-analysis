# Kokutō

Kokutō (黒糖, コクトー) is the only participant besides the veteran Yūki to have entered a game before Ghost House. She puts her earlier experience to use by asking questions about the relevant survival odds and about the offer of help. The money she seeks is for living expenses. She speaks to a first-time player about the trade every wage earner makes with their life, then makes room for a frightened stranger to speak. Her same independence makes the group reconsider where it has searched. She finds what everyone overlooked and dies before it can help her. This is a bounded portrait of the Japanese novel continuity through V10, with spoilers. Kokutō's actions and dialogue are concentrated in V01.

## What two entries tell her

She says her previous game was two years ago and characterizes herself as nearly inexperienced after that interval. Earlier, once Yūki's greater experience is established, she immediately suggests that Yūki take the lead. This is a practical response to a difference in useful experience. Shortly afterward the veteran quotes survival of approximately seven in ten, and Kokutō asks what that figure means for beginners. Yūki concedes that the novices probably face worse odds. The two exchanges show discretion about whom to trust and a different question about what that trust can warrant. Kokutō can recognize an experienced lead while making her give up an overly reassuring inference. (V01 S12 P0073–0079/P0123–0128/P0199–0201.) [A veteran learns to lead](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e001--a-veteran-learns-to-lead)

Similarly, when Yūki offers assistance, Kokutō explicitly welcomes any help she might receive and asks what Yūki stands to gain. Yūki says that people who are helped may repay the advantage later. Kokutō points out that this depends on there being a later meeting, since nobody knows how many will survive. The exchange gives her reasoning a social use. She is testing compatibility of incentives while the group decides whether to rely on someone it has just met. Skepticism about the offered explanation leaves open genuine cooperation. Her decision to remain apart while the others hold on to Yūki as they move through the house is an observed difference in behavior. The narrative's suggestion that she can remain apart because she has played once already is Yūki's explanation. (V01 S12 P0134–0148/P0286–0294.) [Familiarity, trust and the risks in a collective search](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e002--earned-trust-and-a-dangerous-familiar-room)

## Work, money and how she talks to people

Kinko asks how ordinary work differs from Kokutō's reason for entering. Kokutō calls wage labor foolish and says hourly work is also a way of turning life into money. In that framing the game achieves the conversion sooner. She has no debts by her own account, and she still describes lack of money as pressing. This judgment gives the reader a criterion she uses to compare livelihoods, with no further history of what jobs she has done, what commitments she is trying to meet, or how regularly she chooses such danger. Indeed, the two years between entries counter the impression that she substitutes play for a day-to-day job as a matter of routine. (V01 S12 P0199–0209.) [Early reasons for play and the terms of help](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#ghost-house)

The dialogue makes that practical emphasis sound different with different people. She begins with polite deference in あなたのほうが経験豊富ですよね, then brings forward the missing comparison group in the survival statistic. With Kinko she mixes formal sentence endings with compressed colloquial turns such as つっても and はええですわ. She solicits the veteran's reaction with a direct ねえ. The narrative compares her manner and bearing to people practiced in disreputable business, while also imagining someone who might be putting it on. Those are impressions from outside. What she actually says next is observably considerate: when passing the turn to Momono, she checks whether the crying girl is able to speak. In a lighter moment Kokutō teases Yūki about the attention of the other girls. A reader can see fluency with blunt questions, a willingness to defer and an attention to another person's ability to answer even without a private view of her thoughts. (V01 S12 P0076–0079/P0123–0128/P0200–0212/P0286–0288.) [Written voice in the V01 cast](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e012--where-the-text-lets-a-voice-be-heard)

## The familiar room

While others wonder whether their method is wrong or their inspection should be more detailed, Kokutō names the dining room they have passed over because they spent so much time there. She makes the distinction that being familiar with the room has given them no reason to exclude it from the game's active area. Seeing no key at first, she apologizes for taking up their time and concedes that perhaps they would have found it already. Then, in the resumed break, she reconsiders the platter in the course of reaching for its food. She briefly forgoes the cookie and looks underneath. Her glee at the discovery is concrete. She finds the key, brings it to the others' attention and takes hold of it. The hazard kills her before Yūki's warning can affect the outcome. (V01 S12 P0304–0314/P0318–0356.) [The room everyone thought they knew](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e002--earned-trust-and-a-dangerous-familiar-room)

Familiarity helps make the missed location intelligible to Kokutō, but the discovery also gives her a new problem she has a short time to notice. Yūki recognizes the immediate danger and later questions whether Kokutō knew that vital objects are often placed next to traps, whether such knowledge had become usable for her, and whether a faster warning might have saved her. The text never enters Kokutō's mind in that interval. It shows the observational success and the fatal consequence without saying that pride, ignorance or a deliberate bargain about danger caused the loss. Her prior claim about the cost of work is no license to read that last action as wanting death. The group uses the key after her death, and Yūki later remembers the hazard as part of the house's design, a consequence rather than a new moment of Kokutō's personal development. (V01 S12 P0333–0384/P0743–0746.) [Observed discovery, inferred danger and the first loss](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e002--earned-trust-and-a-dangerous-familiar-room)

A bounded expectation is that when people present a convenient generality, Kokutō will ask whether it covers the people actually facing the decision. She can delegate practical authority when someone is better positioned, and offer an alternative when familiarity conceals a material possibility. Changed evidence can prompt a courteous retreat and then a fresh look. Her fatal exception warns against extrapolating those habits into a standing capacity to pause between noticing an opportunity and taking it. Her actual life outside the game, stable preferences, attachments and romantic or intimate history are unavailable. The cookie is a momentary choice, not a verified favorite. Her small courtesy to Momono and her teasing of Yūki provide distinct interpersonal evidence without extending to a history for any of those relationships.
