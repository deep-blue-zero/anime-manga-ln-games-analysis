# Kotoha / Kotono Shiori

Kotono Shiori (琴乃詩織, ことのしおり) uses 言葉, Kotoha, in play. In Scrap Building, her fifth game, she expects that a careful accumulation of money might buy a life outside the pressures she dislikes. She anticipates part of the game's assessment and loses both legs while taking a risk she thinks might count in her favor. The intervening period after that injury is only partly depicted. The next clear position the novels give her is a wheelchair user who has retired from play, reads in her own antiquarian bookshop and recruits for the same organization that taught her what it can make of someone else's need. When an injured recruit finds herself committed to a price she cannot pay, Kotono changes what she can do. This portrait covers the admitted Japanese V01–V10 evidence, with spoilers for V02 and V09.

## A quiet claimant of useful knowledge

Kotoha introduces herself to Mishiro's group as someone who thinks she can help with knowledge. Mishiro objects to the uncertain phrasing, and Kotoha apologizes and gives up the floor. Yūki initially imagines that her glasses and lowered gaze make her seem like a school librarian. The group can use her attention before it understands exactly what she knows. Early in the search she notices a piece of relevant equipment near a body. It takes Yūki to make others attend to what Kotoha is indicating. After the injury, the formal test of her orientation is remarkable for a more intimate reason. She gives Yūki her civil name when asked who she is. She supplies the in-game name after Yūki specifies it, remembers the immediate events, and accepts both Yūki's help and the return of her damaged glasses. That is the primary join between the name which Yūki knew before the injury and the person we follow in V09. The reason she wears the glasses is still unknown. (V02 S12 P0073–0084/P0166–0179/P0413–0443.) [Kotoha's contributions and Yūki's rescue](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e003--when-the-cost-of-help-includes-the-judge)

When they travel separately from the others, Kotoha asks about Yūki's return, her count and why she has a goal at all. She admits that she initially doubted Yūki's claim to experience. Having learned more, she infers that the veteran played before the recruiting surge which brought her own group together. The contrasting confidence behind these questions prompts Yūki to revise the first image of a timid participant. Kotoha's own reason for playing is expressed as アーリーリタイア. She wants the means to live in another, cheaper country and withdraw from a society she describes in terms of cynicism, manipulative ethics and belief that people get the outcomes they deserve. She sees an economic condition for that choice, and tentatively says she may have to live very frugally or find another route once injured. What she tells Yūki articulates a distinct understanding of ordinary freedom. It does not establish that she later emigrated or ever saved a specific amount. (V02 S12 P0448–0502.) ## Anticipating the ballot and choosing a confidante

Kotoha infers that an apparently incidental material is used for voting slips, suggests that the group's garments resemble ancient Greek clothing and observes that preceding dangers generally exposed the first person to meet them. The relative safety of the lower floor then looks like a sign that another assessment will come near the exit. Her historical analogy and the actual vote are related. The novel does not confirm that every visual similarity was planted by the designers, or that every physical hazard could always be addressed the same way. Kotoha's inference is useful because it affects how she anticipates the relevance of risk. She says she had been privately glad to lead on the third floor. If the group would later judge who had contributed, there was something to be gained by trying to do what not every participant could take a turn doing. She guesses enough of the game's design to act in a new way without knowing the complete scoring procedure. The risk leaves her unable to move by herself. (V02 S12 P0413–0435/P0928–0970/P0989–1047.) [Kotoha's different uses of the same clues](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e003--when-the-cost-of-help-includes-the-judge)

Chie's practical help gives their conversation a different footing. Kotoha waits until Keito is out of hearing, asks Chie to owe her a favor and tells her what she thinks the group will face. She reacts initially to Chie's lack of historical associations with irritation. Then she changes the explanation to what any of them can observe about the layout and finally admits that she had withheld her private guess. Even in a bargain she distinguishes a debt of gratitude for being carried. She tells Chie that she will not vote for her and asks for reciprocal treatment. She later reports having voted for Keito. The announced rule punishes Chie, and no one reveals a complete official count. In this instance Kotoha can be loyal to the person who helped and instrumental about when to disclose information for the same reason. She exits with the other survivors. Severe loss of function is an observed condition of her survival, rather than a prediction that she will never exercise initiative again. (V02 S12 P0925–0984/P1063–1085/P1097–1119.) ## A bookshop and a second place in the industry

Kagura later knows her as Kotono and has worked at the shop. On a visit she finds Kotono absorbed in a hardcover and surprised to be spoken to. Kotono dismisses the likelihood that an ordinary visitor would know the value of the unsupervised stock. The shop is a hobby rather than a thriving commercial business, and its books occupy her for their own sake. The attitude survives from the younger player who was privately annoyed when her companion could not follow the Greek analogy. The older Kotono is also much more comfortable letting dissatisfaction become audible. At closing after a difficult customer she takes out a drink, commands the younger woman to join her and complains about dealing with the public. The shared drink is an actual ordinary exchange, not evidence that she drinks regularly. This is when she says she met Yūki on her fifth game, respects a highly accomplished player and warns Kagura to leave a ghost alone unless she wants to be pulled back into that world herself. The reunion she imagines is not a report of renewed contact. (V09 S21 P0108–0137 and S24 P0326–0340, with V02 S12 P0955–0963.) [What everyday life discloses about the retired player](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V09_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v09-e007--what-kotono-owes-the-people-she-recruits)

Her retirement follows the game injury. Her move away from play does not produce the clean separation she once imagined. The operators commission her to introduce new players, and she approaches Kagura under a paid-work premise without explaining the nature of the work in advance. That omission is attributed directly in the later account. When Kagura returns from her first dangerous game and thanks her, an untroubled greeting and congratulations are what we see from Kotono. The gratitude is Kagura's own reaction after surviving rather than proof that consent was informed or that the procedure served everyone the same way. Kotono later says she thinks she introduced nine people and that only Kagura survived. The remembered total and the actual fees she retains are different evidence. Even her way of keeping the money matters to her portrait. She has heard that she could bank it and chooses to keep it as physical cash because doing anything else makes her uneasy. (V09 S21 P0275–0304 and S26 P0218–0232.) [The path from a first offer to an actual responsibility](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V09_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v09-e007--what-kotono-owes-the-people-she-recruits)

## When assistance changes the course

When Sana fails to appear at an appointment with Kagura, her own assigned agent has already brought her death notice to the shop. Kotono puts down her book and tells Kagura directly. She thinks this kind of outcome is part of the world they have chosen to work in, and cannot give a reassuring explanation of why Sana died before someone who seems to take the stakes less seriously. Yet when asked what will happen to the body she acknowledges not knowing, initiates a call to the agent and gives Kagura the phone. Sana's agent posits what the organization will probably do, largely out of the agent's jurisdiction, and eventually offers to help Kagura seek actual access to the sale. Kotono neither makes that ruling nor claims she can locate the body on her own. A specific concern she had not yet taken into account turns her prior contact into something another person can use. (V09 S21 P0127–0137/P0325–0344/P0377–0407.) [The different parts of help after the death](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V09_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v09-e007--what-kotono-owes-the-people-she-recruits)

On the day of the sale, Kotono cautions Kagura against exerting herself with a chest injury, scolds her for arriving ill prepared and actually lends access to a computer. While Kagura is trying to stay in the bidding, Kotono sees the younger woman's desperation and starts to offer resignation as comfort. When Kagura commits to a price beyond what she can pay, she realizes a further game is about to become a requirement Kagura has effectively taken on for herself. Kotono still does not understand why reclaiming a body is worth so much to another person. That limit coexists with a clear assessment: she brought Kagura into the games and bears some responsibility for the present exposure. Her answer is to turn her actual recruitment fees into a loan that can cover the shortfall. The direction and result are decisive. The narrative confirms that Kagura pays without entering one more match, the remains are delivered and a farewell is held. Kotono adds travel and funeral costs from her resources and asks to travel with Kagura. It is an uncommon excursion for her since the injury, with some visible excitement alongside the seriousness of what they do. (V09 S26 P0115–0157/P0181–0248.) [The observed effects of the loan and other practical aid](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V09_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v09-e007--what-kotono-owes-the-people-she-recruits)

She then accepts a delayed repayment precisely because repayment with another prize would defeat her purpose. The loan changes Kagura's immediate choice and the terms of how it can be repaid, without a confession that Kotono repudiates everything she has done as a broker. The actual loan and participation in the farewell are stronger than speculation about what she might do for a different recruit. Kagura's former work at the shop predates her first game in the main narrative. An original front plate describes her as a part-time employee, while her direct account says she did no ordinary work after becoming a player, and she speaks about finding a job afterward. Those observations do not yield a precise current employment schedule for the two women. Their close, sometimes impatient contact and the shared journey establish a relationship in practice. Romantic or intimate preference is unknown and the role of the two women in the farewell should remain distinct from Kagura's friendship with Sana. (V09 S21 P0275–0304; S24 P0239–0251; S26 P0218–0257; S06-I01/S09-I01.) ## Written voice and a bounded expectation

In Scrap Building she meets Mishiro's reprimand with an interrupted apology and asks Yūki politely why she came. In the same game she uses short plain questions during the private bargain with Chie. The contrast makes public hesitation and private directness concurrent features. At the shop, つきあえ is blunt enough to invite a companion rather than maintain customer manners, and 貸しだからね and the demand that follows it carries a practical seriousness about money that is audible even when she is helping. Her habit of imagining what young people fail to know occurs both in the earlier talk of history and the later impatience with Kagura's lack of equipment. The register is written and the contrast follows differences in authority and familiarity as well as changes in circumstance. (V02 S12 P0077–0084/P0448–0486/P0937–0970; V09 S24 P0326–0340 and S26 P0125–0150/P0220–0232.) [Companionable bluntness and material obligation](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V09_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v09-e007--what-kotono-owes-the-people-she-recruits)

Given a concrete puzzle with observable cues, Kotono is likely to develop a hypothesis, consider who needs to know and withhold it when premature disclosure offers no different outcome. Help received from a particular person makes private reciprocity more valuable to her than the public scoring everyone is told to apply. Given a risk she helped create for someone she already knows, she can express incomprehension or irritation and still act materially when she learns what would avert the danger. That is an expectation about her actual kinds of choices. The evidence does not tell us how evenly she would apply those obligations to other recruits, what she did soon after her injury, or whether she eventually changed her broader activity. Her reasons for initial entry, the days actually shown at the shop and the repayment condition all support a recognizable person in both roles.
