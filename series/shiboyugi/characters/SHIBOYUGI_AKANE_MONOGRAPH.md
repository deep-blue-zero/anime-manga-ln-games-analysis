# Tsuwabuki Akane

石蕗茜, Tsuwabuki Akane, lives in room 103 at Tochinoki and rides with Red Bear. She helps Yūki rescue a neighbor and discovers that the abductors are members of her own group. Belonging gives her independent access and clues that an outsider lacks. The same allegiance lets her declare the abduction unacceptable and later refuse to report her fellows to the police. She has a way to leave through university that several of them believe they do not have. She has to find a reason to care what happens to the group after that protectable future is available. Her answer becomes an active part in ending a conflict and a strange request for the older leader to include her in the consequences. V08 reports that she has moved away. The exact family troubles and her subsequent university life remain unwitnessed through V10. V07 S16 P0062/S21 P0186–0197/P0319–0421, S23 P0008–0070/P0543–0594/P0931–0946/P1172–1191, V08 S20 P0288–0333.

## The world she can navigate

She can ride a motorcycle in circumstances where she and Yūki urgently need an independent route. She knows how some of the people in Red Bear use vehicles and terrain and can allocate several kinds of work in a pursuit. The property she lives in permits a private ordinary life. A possible earlier insult from family is raised by somebody she has fought. Yūki gets an impression of a difficult life but interrupts the threatened words before hearing the name. We have direct knowledge of Akane’s living arrangement and her actual university admission, with a general wish and an ordinary goal. We have no given basis for an exact age from a private insult or for assigning the causes of her history. Much of her day beyond the apartment, the group, school and the nearby rider’s habits remains open. V07 S16 P0057–0064, S21 P0182–0197/P0319–0355, S23 P0357–0369/P0543–0594.

Her early task involves borrowing and giving things. On seeing a captive taken, she works with the person in a position to take immediate action. She helps locate the kidnappers, gives Yūki access to transport and supplies a phone whose light she believes Yūki needs. Yūki thinks she can work without it and keeps it with her. The interactions hold practical knowledge in different hands. Akane stays where sent and then recognizes Suō and the people with her. Hostility in the resulting exchange is genuine. She objects to kidnapping and responds to threats about the consequences of reporting. Her later statement 仲間は売れません describes a line about selling out members, even when their behavior has made them immediate adversaries. She can decisively help the victim and decline cooperation with a punitive authority in the same sequence. Cooperation with her can therefore depend on sharing a bounded purpose without receiving all kinds of loyalty equally. V07 S21 P0182–0197/P0319–0355/P0377–0422. [What she will and will not do for the group](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e007--akane-distinguishes-obligation-from-assent).

## Whose boundary matters

Akane remembers a group with 気品. Yūki recognizes that an ungentle activity and noise have already presented costs to other people. Akane says no one was hurt and in the same conversation concedes that a distinction between the group's line and society's can be acknowledged. The older standard remains useful to her because there are things on the other side of it she expects the group to refuse. For-profit abduction and a general readiness to target outsiders make her believe they are giving up their own stopping point. Yūki's objections help show an actual weakness in her account, and Akane keeps arguing about the limits of conduct with the person whose help she wants. Rejecting a new practice is a commitment independent of the full legitimacy of the older one. V07 S23 P0008–0070. [Competing purposes for a riding group](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e008--three-ways-to-explain-a-groups-purpose).

After making a request for protection, she later invokes Yūki’s promise when the risk arrives. The landlady opens an immediate opportunity to move. Akane apologizes and says she is the cause, then steals a fellow rider’s bike and rides aggressively with Yūki as an unhelmeted passenger. That choice and the earlier overlooked effect of roaring bikes on neighbors show limits to saying she always rejects harm outside the group. She has practical reasons to privilege immediate departure, and other people have independent reasons to help them. When a white-haired member alleges she has an exit to university and tries to share a painful earlier name or form of address, Yūki interrupts the latter exposure and asks only about the university. Akane describes admission to a national institution in an adjacent prefecture and qualifies any impression of academic brilliance by mentioning the department’s competition rate. She understands that people who feel they have no other destination resent the judgment of someone who can leave. That brings a real question. Does the person who wants to go still have standing to resist a change that will matter chiefly to those remaining? V07 S23 P0260–0369/P0543–0594. [Which kinds of help count on the way out](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md).

Akane asks Yūki for her view while expressly suspending the reason the latter may have to protect her. Yūki gives advice, saying she should oppose the change if she finds it objectionable. Akane actually helps get past the confrontation and backs the former chief's decision to retire the Red Bear name. At the hotel she continues to act despite being injured. She is sad about what is ending, in a different position from a leader who claims this is the natural result. Later she asks the former chief to honor a declaration about striking everyone by striking her as well. Maguma considers the statement figurative and tries to decline the request because of the present injuries. Akane persists and gives her wholehearted thanks afterward. The ritual matters to her as a particular way to accept inclusion in what happened. It does not make earlier coerced or incidental injuries expressions of assent. In the next period the apartment actually has one fewer tenant. Her admission and departure create a serious alternative, while matriculation, a lasting new group and her view of it lack direct follow-up. V07 S23 P0543–0594/P0931–0946/P1172–1191, V08 S20 P0288–0345.

## Register and conditioned conduct

She speaks frequently to Yūki with っす and ないっす, allowing informality within an address that acknowledges another person's position. The range includes 友樹さん and a request for help expressing real vulnerability. Her immediate response to Suō is rough and oppositional, as in こっちのセリフだボケが. Later the face-to-face disclosure of university admission puts colloquial dismissal, humility and an exact ありがとうございます at different places in the same dialogue. The final ありがとうございました to Maguma follows a requested physical act. It is evidence of what this person is making of the occasion, distinct from how an observer understands the ceremony. Other written effects such as a strained unclear sound at the first question reflect local surprise. The text provides changes of written register, without an independent recording on which to fix how they ordinarily sound. V07 S21 P0349–0355/P0395–0412 and S23 P0054–0070/P0551–0594/P1172–1191.

A sustained expectation in comparable circumstances is that she can help actively once she sees people close to her take a step she considers beyond their proper limit. She can criticize them directly and make use of experience these people taught her, even while wanting to prevent later punishment by someone else. The actual dependence on Yūki and Maguma qualifies a general theory of her independence. She also needs action she can take herself when she knows enough about a place and a person to reach them. Her intention to leave Red Bear, her support for ending it and the university offer give no separate completed resignation or confirmed enrollment. They give little basis for how she would behave in an unrelated group, under an unobserved family pressure, or in an intimate relationship. Ordinary interests include riding and a concrete wish to continue studying. The degree of interest in a subject, future occupation or actual romance is unavailable. V07 S21 P0319–0422 and S23 P0008–0070/P0543–0594/P1172–1191, V08 S20 P0288–0333.
