# Kaneko Tsutomu

Kaneko Tsutomu (金子努, かねこ つとむ, SBY-P021) is the father of the participant called Kinko in Ghost House. By the time he approaches Yūki he knows his daughter did not survive and has involved himself with people who want to make the games public. His ability to find and approach a participant in spite of institutional secrecy contrasts with something he cannot get from her. Yūki accepts an object but cannot tell him why a future in which the games end is not the future she wants. His proposal and his demand that she value her own life arise from his experience of losing a daughter. Together they show both the care with which he handles practical difficulty and the limits of his recognition of another person’s aims. This portrait covers the original Japanese novels through V10, with spoilers. The family testimony in V01 and one meeting in V02 supply the direct evidence about him, with restricted later confirmation about the attempted intervention.

## The earlier family event and a meeting with a partial witness

In V01 a young girl introduces herself by the written characters 金子 read as キンコ. The debt she came to repay is “親のこさえた借金.” She agrees a child does not necessarily have to answer for it. Her independent reason is that a debt incurred should be paid. That is the more immediate evidence of why she enters. When the father appears in the next volume, Yūki says he does not fit the picture she had made of parents who would transfer their failures to their daughter. He confirms that a family business failed and the debt was real. To the question of whether he made her enter, his “断じてない” turns into a qualification. By being wholly occupied with himself he may effectively have done something like that anyway. This is a distinction he makes about his own past conduct, taking responsibility for what the failure of attention could have enabled. Independent proof of the exact path by which Kinko heard of the games or entered them is unavailable. Yūki's confidence that the daughter would have acted by herself comes from the young woman's known temperament, and Yūki still entertains contact from the organizers. V01 S12 P0189–0199. V02 S16 P0123–0145. He is a middle-aged, formally dressed stranger when he makes contact after Yūki finishes her usual walk. He says he meant to wait until her business was done and apologizes for making her uneasy. At the damaged public bench, he declines the offer to move somewhere more comfortable because their conversation would be inappropriate in such a place. He touches his beard when deciding where to begin. He also thanks Yūki for looking after his daughter, while her own immediate association is with how much responsibility she bears for the girl’s death. He knows who to approach and that she is a regular who played with his daughter. Her player name is new to him, and he has not shown knowledge of what occurred inside the house. For Yūki the difference between a man with access to her address and one without access to those facts matters. The scene sustains actual uneven knowledge rather than exceptional competence at discovering everything. V02 S16 P0061–0124. ## What he asks, offers and withholds

“娘の無念を晴らしたい” names an acknowledged purpose before the more direct intention to destroy the organization. He describes the institution as depending on a hidden venue, backers and customers. To him, locating and exposing a game can undo that arrangement. He has brought an object for Yūki to take along. His account that his associates made the tracker and that it could yield the information needed indicates a project with assigned roles and strong confidence in its premises. The story shows actual delivery of the object to Yūki, then the actor whose body and reputation would be exposed having to decide what to do. Its technical performance, tracking result and the partners' later use of any signal are not observed.

Tsutomu starts out telling her that his own network enabled the meeting but he could not explain the exact route. When he says “わたくしども” while promising the rest of the plan will be done for her, she catches the plural. His face betrays his concern. He admits there are associates and that they made the capsule, and declines her invitation to explain further, apologizing again. Yūki imagines a victims' association and infers an instruction from them to withhold information. Concern about disclosure to a loyal player is plausible. The actual boundary is the one he draws in conversation. His choice of “仲間たち” also sounds more personal than an agency title. There is no equivalent claim by him to be their spokesman in all matters or to know the exact route used to find Yūki. V02 S16 P0108–0114/P0149–0173. [V02, secrecy and the intervention](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V02_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v02-e007--a-practitioner-who-cannot-say-her-purpose)

His next move is materially specific. If their purpose succeeds, they will provide for her living needs and his personal connections could introduce a new employer. Yūki senses that he has mistaken the problem. She tries to explain a distinction between forced entry and people who repeatedly choose to enter. When he asks what would be intolerable enough to motivate her, she cannot voice her own need for a life pointed toward ninety-nine. She evades the point and he stops asking for the specific personal reason. “失礼を承知で言います” introduces an insistently moral answer. Life-threatening entertainment belongs in fictional media, he says. Voluntary exposure to it in real life is “明らかに異常” and he thinks that anyone capable of repeatedly surviving it can find a viable way to live without it. The phrase about fiction expresses his preferred limit on representation versus practice. His own everyday reading and viewing tastes are unknown.

There is resignation and judgment in different parts of this exchange. He genuinely considers how she could continue to live and softens the risk of giving an immediate answer by telling her she may discard the object. He also recasts her pursuit as a transferable ability and denies the legitimacy she attaches to it. Yūki experiences the intrusion as an attack on a deeply held choice, with associations from her own past. Nothing indicates that Tsutomu intends those particular associations, and her actual responses never deliver the objection with that force. He can reasonably see a young person in danger and still fail to understand the terms on which she understands herself. She leaves with the object without agreeing to assist. V02 S16 P0170–0216. ## Consequences outside his view

Yūki accepts the game's invitation by her own slower decision after an automatic first answer, then accidentally swallows Tsutomu's capsule. Her later in-game question about Azuma's reaction to a proposed job gives Tsutomu no new knowledge. Azuma's wrath belongs to a separate person who also resents being treated as a candidate for rescue. Following Golden Bath, Yūki's agent strongly implies organized interference but refuses to identify the device's fate or fully explain Yūki's late waking. By V09 Yūki characterizes the failed attempt as having been defeated during the pregame physical examination. She deliberately leaves her own involvement out of that story. This is a participant subsequently describing the outcome as she understands it, not an independent report that Tsutomu received. V07 depicts a named Victims’ Association and an episode of institutional suppression without taking the reader back to the V02 park to identify the father as a confirmed member or report his fate. No information in the corpus closes that family history. V02 S16 P0217–0252/P0607–0635 and S18 P0025–0047/P0055–0067. V07 S19 P0451–0476 and S21 P0446–0484. V09 S24 P0158–0168. ## A conditional view of the father

When Tsutomu can place a participant's apparent dependency and practical circumstances in view, he is likely to propose concrete aid and ask cautiously for cooperation. He shows capacity to wait, acknowledge an unwelcome personal question honestly, and allow the other party time to decide. He may nevertheless move from deference to direct judgment if he believes the person is neglecting the value of her own life. He uses 私 about his own history and resources, more formal わたくしども when describing joint obligations, and repeatedly calls Yūki 幽鬼さん even when pressing her hard. His formal apology to a stranger and firm denial regarding his daughter have different objects. These are written choices, including hesitation as narrated. There is no performed voice or independent account of his habitual address to family or collaborators.

The largest exception to simply calling him a moralizing outsider is his actual attempt to provide means, accept scrutiny about his own fault and leave a route to decline. The largest limit to calling him a tactful reformer is that he hears what Yūki says she wants and nonetheless explains it as a departure from a worthy kind of life. His livelihood claims concern a failed family business and some present contacts, not a documented profession or demonstrated guarantee of employment. His earlier treatment of his daughter outside his own retrospective admission, any spouse or romantic history, other personal tastes and whether he ever changed his approach remain unavailable. One charged conversation gives a strong reading of his approach in this kind of loss and secrecy. Broader personal predictions would require a different encounter.
