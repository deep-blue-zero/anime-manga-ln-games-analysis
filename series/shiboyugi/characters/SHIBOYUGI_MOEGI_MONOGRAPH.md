# Moegi

Moegi (萌黄, モエギ) is sixteen in Candle Woods, her third game. She becomes the only experienced member of a group of novices and pushes them to act by reenacting the instruction she received from Kyara. She wants to become able to insist on her own desires after learning that doing what other people expect does not keep everything she values safe. Her ability to organize, her grasp of the game's incentives, her real responsibility for the harm she causes, and her desire to stop acting strong all belong to the same person. This portrait covers the admitted Japanese novel continuity through V10 and contains V01 and V04 spoilers.

## Before Candle Woods

Moegi approaches the games as a means of changing how she lives. In Candle Woods her thinking eventually states the premise directly. Being a good child can cost a person everything, and she no longer wants to be someone who can only cry. What she calls strength is 実行力, the capacity to put one's own desires into effect. She seeks instruction from someone who can do that without caring what it looks like. Kyara later tells Yūki that she spared Moegi during her own second game because Moegi was a good girl. That is what the teacher says to a stranger under entirely different circumstances. It accounts for the exceptional survival from Kyara's side without supplying an independent record of the meeting or the whole history of the mentorship. (V01 S15 P0646–0657/P0883–0897.) [Candle Woods's unequal views of the two mentors](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e008--the-spectators-advantage-over-both-viewpoints)

The reader only later learns from Shion that before Candle Woods, Kyara housed the three younger players Moegi, Hizumi and Shion. Moegi spoke most often with Shion, who was near her age and had also left home. According to Shion, Moegi repeatedly said she had obeyed her parents and lost everything anyway, and was now learning how to make her own wishes prevail. Here the position she takes toward a peer is open and repetitive where the same vulnerable subject is largely hidden from the players she commands. Shion regarded her as susceptible to dubious self-help and unlikely to last. The shared household and conversations extend the visible chronology of Moegi's pursuit. Shion's judgment is one person's retrospective reaction. The original nature of the family loss and the parents' own conduct are never specified. (V04 S21 P0628–0640.) [The household Shion remembers](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e012--shions-ways-of-making-and-losing-a-home)

## The novice who has to explain the game

On waking Moegi has the headache she associates with how players are brought to a game. She has meant to ask whether anyone else gets the same pain and keeps forgetting once play starts. This small frustration shows how quickly practical concentration takes over. She notices the artificial floor and false forest immediately, compares the gathering to a tentative new school class, and tries a little small talk to find out what the others know. She reads their uncertainty correctly enough to establish that they have no prior experience. She also thinks she can identify which girls are still in secondary school by their bearing, while having no firm basis for the guess. Both observations matter to a portrait of her cognition. She can make a useful provisional inference from reticence and can be overconfident about another kind of social reading. (V01 S15 P0191–0227.) [Two briefings and two sorts of leadership](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e007--two-briefings-and-two-forms-of-group-power)

She finds and listens to the explanation, discarding the showman's taunts to retain the operational terms. Each of thirty hunters has to secure five individual kills from the much larger opposing group, in a week. There is no team score. She understands that opponents have incentives to resist, and she correctly doubts that she could deal with their numerical advantage by going alone. That is her reason for trying to turn the other novices into collaborators. Her first appeal to work together meets silence and an accusation that she is part of a setup. She can imagine reacting the same way in their position. She then chooses to show them real harm and force them to take part, using what she remembers of Kyara's instruction. The narration keeps her private fear and embarrassment in view while her public orders grow direct. Means that actually secure compliance can still undermine future trust. (V01 S15 P0237–0240/P0249–0277/P0279–0329.) [Two different ways to make a group act](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e007--two-briefings-and-two-forms-of-group-power)

Her own appraisal is unusually explicit. She holds herself responsible for three deaths even though other hands inflicted injuries. The ordinary analogy is guilt at receiving too much change, which makes the distance from her desired self legible. Later she is still reluctant to step over a dead person, in part because of a superstition that doing so stunts growth. She despises that hesitation. Yet she takes satisfaction in the minutes in which she managed to behave as she believes Kyara would. The chapter supports a person who experiences moral distress and chooses to override it. It also supports calculated coercion, including her awareness that a forced choice excludes other possibilities. Genuine feeling does not relieve her of agency in devising the training. (V01 S15 P0309–0337/P0607–0616.) [The pressures the instruction leaves behind](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e007--two-briefings-and-two-forms-of-group-power)

## Two tests of the same ambition

Airi interrupts Moegi when she is close to doing harm to herself. Airi reports an altered body and missing clothing. Moegi begins by thanking her and asking a series of diagnostic questions. She recalls Kyara's dislike of tight clothing and pursues a detail about the victim's size. The inference that her teacher is there and active is much more informative than the bare suspicion of a disguise. It also overwhelms her. She tells Airi to flee, recognizes that escape alone will not meet the hunter's requirement, and raises her voice when Airi asks for information she does not have. The warning can express concern and shock at the danger to both factions. It does not establish how far Moegi would have tried to protect Airi after that encounter. She expects neither automatic protection from Kyara nor enough qualified opponents left to meet her own requirement. (V01 S15 P0498–0569/P0602–0619.) [What Airi reports, and what Moegi recognizes](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e008--the-spectators-advantage-over-both-viewpoints)

Her assessment of scarcity then changes whom she treats as a rival. Hikawa asks for equipment and offers cooperation. Moegi kills her. She learns afterward that Hikawa was carrying more than she disclosed. The hidden equipment leaves possible bad faith, while Moegi's own account says she had already decided to kill whomever she met. Even killings of hunters, which cannot count toward her official requirement, can remove rivals and provide resources. In one room she had tried to create group strength because she needed other people. Now she takes action against those she had tried to educate, in pursuit of her own standing and survival. The public threat posed by Kyara and the private pressure to act like her are concurrent explanations. (V01 S15 P0624–0657.) [Scarcity after Kyara's arrival](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e008--the-spectators-advantage-over-both-viewpoints)

Against Yūki, Moegi can recover from a surprise, remember facts about the encounter and recognize how poorly a straightforward fight is going. Perceived inferiority and lack of sight then constrain her choices. She cannot make herself advance and stays when moving might be safer. Her declaration that she is Kyara's pupil follows a stream of private encouragement and contempt for her own fear. At the point of defeat she privately realizes she can stop performing strength and cries. Yūki sees frustrated effort on her face and is furious that the result does not answer to what the girl had invested. Yūki can recognize another player's purpose without knowing which experiences brought it into being. Moegi dies without having met the individual condition she set out to fulfill. (V01 S15 P0706–0744/P0778–0808.) [The competitor whose commitment Yūki cannot explain](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e009--a-borrowed-objective-and-an-improbable-victory)

## Speech and the conditions of action

Her first questions contain pauses, soft requests and polite forms. Once she has chosen coercion, the public register includes abrupt bare imperatives such as 感じろ and less room for someone to answer. With Airi she returns to さん, thanks and requests for specific evidence before falling into urgent commands. The private repeated phrase なりふり構わない強者 names a person she wants to make herself become. By contrast, the desperate utterance 伽羅さんの弟子なんだ names an existing affiliation she draws on to endure a situation she cannot master. These are written registers and explicitly rendered thoughts. They give no performed tone beyond what the narrator describes. Ordinary traits recovered here include social affinity with a housemate, shame about small wrongs and the habits of attention that survive fright. Other preferences, private work or school, and romantic or intimate experience are unknown at this boundary. (V01 S15 P0195–0206/P0273–0275/P0331–0337/P0509–0567/P0720–0726; V04 S21 P0631–0636.) [Where the written voices change](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e012--where-the-text-lets-a-voice-be-heard)

A bounded expectation is that Moegi first asks what the others know, assesses the practical conditions and may imitate a more forceful authority when she sees time or credibility running out. Acts that look purely ruthless can be followed by a still potent sense of responsibility. When the ability to meet a personal goal seems scarce, the people she needed earlier can become competitors. An encounter with an evidently superior opponent, or assistance that lets her act without making a performance of mastery, changes the conditions of the prediction. No general capacity for coercive leadership outside the game follows from the first room. Her earlier life is known chiefly where she told someone what she wished to change.
