# Ramona Squier

ラモーナ・スクワイア is a university researcher and international doctoral student living at Tochinoki. The original V07 profile supplies the published Latin lettering Ramona Squier. She has lived in Japan for about six months when her neighbors’ lives start to overlap overtly with her work. She can help someone understand a test question and defend a boundary about institutional information in the same week. Speaking effectively in a second language means she has to decide how much sense a listener needs, even when that listener controls the terms of the conversation. Her actual choice in a kidnapping combines considerable fear and sustained discretion. Later she answers nearly the same research question from someone who comes to learn rather than to take, changing the extent of the answer without supplying protected specifics. Her relation to the games remains largely unknown to the neighbor she helps. Her last direct household scenes are in V08. V07 S07-I01/S16 P0061/P0075–0097/S21 P0201–0315/P0420–0442, V08 S20 P0288–0345/P0529–0576.

## Being understood at home

She has little spare money. She explains that clothing and possessions commonly come from marked-down stock and that she has no wealthy acquaintance to draw on. Her family is in her country of origin and, she says, poor. These last details are a reply to a threat, with a motive to make demands seem unprofitable. Actual familiarity with discounts and the simple setting independently make financial constraint plausible. Neither her age nor exact country follows from a neighbor identifying English in passages of her speech. In the apartment she can navigate ordinary Japanese while having difficulty with some written terms and sound choices. When Yūki says けじめ after her haircut, Ramona asks about an unfamiliar meaning and lets the other woman try out explanations. She hears approximate language and can speak to the speaker before a personal explanation is available. V07 S16 P0075–0097, S21 P0049–0075/P0207–0243, S07-I01. [Useful ordinary exchanges](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e001--apartment-thresholds-and-making-a-break).

She does not take all names as approximate in the same way. After demanding entry by calling レモン, the landlady returns a precise water-charge overpayment. Ramona corrects her to her face but says “Nothing” when asked to repeat herself. She tells Yūki separately that “lemon” can describe a defective or useless person. Yūki asks for a comparison and hears similarity. Ramona insists the pronunciations differ. She chooses which part of the correction to explain to each listener. The house is still a place in which time, money, dinner and questions can be shared. This separate conversation makes the consequence of the name intelligible to another tenant. V07 S21 P0047–0075, V08 S20 P0288–0345.

## Where words actively matter

Two visible kidnappers suggest that the secret of a research institute is worth more than her money. Ramona tries to establish their exact purpose and count who could still be nearby from what she heard. She notices a familiar object has been moved and thinks the people holding her may have searched her things. Access to a document or machine is a distinct possibility from having acquired the protected facts, used them externally or received them from her. She recalls a warning by the institute about pressure. The sentence 守秘義務があります is an actual memorized institutional answer, and she initially says she could be punished for speaking. One participant offers apparent understanding of poverty and shared gain. Ramona attempts to answer the argument, giving the other person reason to feel patronized and making the threat worse. A borrowed refusal therefore operates within a failed interpersonal inference. She repeats it under far worse conditions. V07 S21 P0201–0291. [Accounts of the threat and a possible reply](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V07_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v07-e005--ramonas-refusal-and-the-cost-of-useful-information).

After a request for a password she stays silent as the abductors make her think an assault is imminent. The silence gives no further information to them, and she also cannot be sure she could say the same phrase without her voice shaking. She considers whether she will survive and whether living after disclosure would still feel like being herself. Her internal question about whether she could stand future pain prevents a bland portrait of incorruptibility. She actually maintains her reserve until a rescue interrupts the attempt. She is not shown having to sustain the refusal under the specific serious injury she fears. When the danger changes she can answer Yūki’s conditional call and later declines to pursue a formal complaint. In the later encounter she is physically unharmed, but this does not retroactively make the coercion theatrical or prove that information on the premises never attracted attention. She trusts what she can see of the present injury and what has come back to her, enough to go home and be with people she knows. V07 S21 P0275–0315/P0365–0373/P0414–0433.

## Giving someone enough to learn

She performs real work in English tutoring even though the pupil approaches after a grave interruption. Yūki can show a low score and ask about language. Ramona explains the idiom “put food on the table” in familiar everyday terms and accepts that Japanese offers a related expression. As the roles reverse, Yūki asks about the research that made someone want the captive’s knowledge. Ramona first recoils, then chooses to talk within narrowed limits when the other woman says she can keep her secrets. She asks what to call a scab in Japanese, supplies an approximate description of an injury-treatment research topic and never specifies the full institutional connection. The juxtaposition with Edomoto’s separate commission strongly connects the research with the organizers’ treatment. Her neighbor lacks the same evidence and misses the connection. A capacity to name one accessible fact after a boundary is respected is different from a willingness to name the protected facts under coercion. The lessons have a later concrete benefit in Yūki’s promotion into the third year. V07 S21 P0420–0442/P0451–0467 and S23 P0003–0070, V08 S20 P0288–0345. [Help between neighbors](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V08_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v08-e009--four-views-of-the-same-address).

When communications and normal light fail during the later assault, she begins to use a phone for light. Yayoi sees that it would alert someone and intervenes. Ramona follows her emergency direction and receives an ordinary-looking object for defense. She asks how a neighbor would have them and receives the bounded explanation that she and Yūki are 同族, rooted in Yayoi's misconception of the two women’s professions. These are rational levels of reliance in an unfamiliar setting. Accepting an effective instruction and wanting more information about its source can coexist. There is no actual display of Ramona becoming able to fight or learning the complete organization. No later crisis tests whether she would reveal different parts of her own work if the stakes changed. V08 S20 P0529–0576.

## Speech, appraisal and relationship boundaries

The written voice gives Ramona's Japanese a cultivated use of polite forms, isolated choices of English and room for uncertainty about nuance. 誘拐……ですか？　これは is a question aimed at securing the real nature of an event. The captors make fun of its accent, represented in the page’s shift to their writing of ゆうかい and in the narrator's statement that they mimic her. It would be too much to infer a precise geographic or performed accent. Phrases such as 日本語でなんと言いますか can make the learner into a contributor. With Yūki she moves between making corrections and giving approximations; faced with violent demands and threat of future punishment, she holds with the protective formula until silence does its work. Beyond the apartment, we have her professional method in a few actual exchanges with people who do and do not respect the boundary. V07 S16 P0075–0097 and S21 P0053–0075/P0207–0243/P0275–0297/P0423–0442.

Given a similar cooperative request from somebody who understands the limited terms, Ramona can be expected to offer an accessible explanation, make material contributions and ask for missing language. An extractive request more plausibly produces a refusal, a change of terms or silence. The source records fear and misappraisal as reasons she cannot control every outcome that follows. Her picture of herself as someone who withholds important information remains effective under the observed test and avowedly uncertain when she imagines a different threat. A stable research aim beyond professional discretion, concrete ethical views about the games, affection beyond what she shares with neighbors and an actual romance are unavailable. Her working choices, fragile budget and practical reciprocity establish an ordinary life with points of contact with the lethal one, many of which she cannot choose. V07 S21 P0207–0315/P0420–0467, V08 S20 P0288–0345/P0529–0576.
