# Sana’s assigned agent

Sana's assigned agent is an unnamed organizer employee who becomes a point of contact between the institution and Sana's friend Kagura. After Sana dies, she explains what she thinks will happen to the remains, undertakes to make the auction accessible, helps receive Kagura after the game she enters to fund the bid and attends the farewell. Her offer of help comes after a direct objection to the risk Kagura proposes taking. The evidence for this woman is concentrated in V09 within the admitted Japanese continuity through V10. V09 S21 P0323–0407, S24 P0638–0657 and S26 P0236–0238.

## A report to the people who were waiting

The agent has learned about a meeting Sana was meant to attend. Kotono tells Kagura that the agent came to her bookshop in person to give the news. The narration treats preventing someone from waiting in vain as the probable reason. We see the agent's next step from the recipient's side. Kotono uses a number the agent previously gave her, hands the phone to Kagura, and the woman offers a brief conventional condolence as 「紗那のエージェントです」. Her promptness is part of the action. Her prior relationship with either the shopkeeper or Sana is thinly evidenced. Kagura only later encounters the agent face to face. This sets the bookshop visit and telephone acquaintance apart from the unnamed escort seen when Kagura and Sana went to the amusement park. V09 S21 P0311–0317/P0323–0344, S24 P0640–0645. [The friendship and the report](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V09_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v09-e007--what-kotono-owes-the-people-she-recruits).

Sana's agent answers Kagura's question about return to the family by describing several ways the organizers handle bodies. She says Sana's case probably differs and explains 「死亡後の処理は管轄外でして」. Her own assessment uses the surgery agreement, an apparent shortfall against the cost and Sana's status as missing to predict sale after postmortem processing. The reasoning is specific to Sana, and the actual auction and delivery later support the predicted disposition. The scene does not independently disclose accounting records or authorize the reader to assume every person subject to a contract has the same outcome. The agent also anticipates strong interest in the sale and acknowledges a degree of professional partiality, 「エージェントとしての贔屓目」. This is unusually personal wording amid an explanation of the market. It admits an investment whose character and history the novel cannot establish from the agent's statement alone. V09 S21 P0342–0362 and S26 P0152–0205/P0236–0238.

## Helping while still objecting

Kagura asks whether a player may buy the remains rather than letting them pass to an unknown viewer. Sana's agent considers it possible and explains that the auction calls for the entire payment, at a price she thinks exceeds Kagura's current means. Kagura proposes entering more games to save the money. The agent then asks whether Sana would really want her friend risking her body for this. It is a criticism of the means, framed around the friend, and Kagura takes it seriously enough to consider that the plan might be guilt or impulsive spending. The agent makes no claim to know Sana's answer. After Kagura identifies herself plainly as a friend wishing to mourn, the agent pauses, says she understands her decision and adds 「競売に参加できるよう、動いてみます」. The shift from anticipated institutional disposition to an undertaken intervention comes within the same exchange. V09 S21 P0361–0398. [How a personal decision enters the auction process](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V09_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v09-e007--what-kotono-owes-the-people-she-recruits).

At the Phantom Thief exit the agent joins Kagura's own assignee in physically carrying Kagura to treatment and confirms that the bidding arrangements have gone through. 「冷や冷やしましたよ」 describes how badly the outing worried her. She now gives Kagura practical reassurance, then tells the other agent 「とっととやめたほうがいいよ、こんな仕事」 when the colleague regrets that a talented player is considering leaving. She brushes off the characterization of her remark as a problem. The two hearers explain part of the difference in register. Kagura receives businesslike support and a term of personal concern, while the peer hears direct dissent. Meaningful help actually took place, despite the agent's view that leaving was preferable. When Kagura finally raises the money through Kotono she pays through Sana's agent. The agent attends the small funeral with both women. Neither a formal seat over disposal nor authority over bidding terms is established. The travel and ash dispersal later shown involve Kagura and Kotono. V09 S24 P0638–0657, S26 P0208–0247. [What changes after the game](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V09_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v09-e010--what-the-dress-carried-through-the-fight).

This agent speaks in written role formulas and precise limits with a bereaved caller, can ask an uncomfortable question when care conflicts with proposed risk, and then takes an available action without withdrawing the objection. One plausible explanation gives center place to professional responsibility for Sana and Kagura, while another emphasizes an attachment to her former charge extending beyond work. The record supports the individual concern and conduct under either reading. Her own training, the exact oversight she exercised while Sana lived, her private reactions beyond reported delivery, personal preferences and intimate history are unknown. The novel supplies the written register of this work and descriptions of its delivery. A prediction that she routinely departs from policy, might sponsor continued play, or always advises retirement would need another test with a different beneficiary or materially different choice.
