# Sana

紗那, with ruby サ／ナ, is a thirteen-year-old player whose agreement with the organizers requires twenty clears in return for life-saving treatment. Her friend Kagura calls her Sana, as does the admitted Japanese character plate in Latin letters. In a hospital memory her mother addresses her as 佐名, explicitly rubied さ／な. This is an evidenced family form of address. The novel supplies no full civil name. Sana wants to move through the world on her own terms and gains some of that freedom before she can finish the contract. At her fifteenth game she seeks an individual exit, accepts help from a more experienced player and dies before she can use it. The portrait covers the admitted Japanese continuity through V10, with the direct personal evidence concentrated in V09. V09 S04-I01, S18 P0161–0184/P0322–0385/P0462–0509 and S26 P0236–0247.

## Choosing a chance to stay alive

Sana tells Takami she had a severe cardiomyopathy for which an overseas transplant was indicated. The organizers privately offered to treat her. When Takami asks about public fundraising, Sana can explain why a family without successful media attention or effective support might not raise the money. According to Sana the organizers approached her without telling her parents, who had no part in agreeing to the games. To the outside world she is missing. She tells Yūki that she lives in a facility run by the organizers, does not attend school despite still being of middle-school age, and may go out only with permission and continued observation. Her speculation about an implanted transmitter makes sense of her conclusion that flight is impractical. The escort at the amusement park corroborates that movement can be supervised. It gives no evidence for a device in her body or the full scope of the contract. V09 S18 P0161–0184/P0340–0361, S21 P0311–0317. [The contract and the actual choices it leaves her](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V09_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v09-e003--two-meanings-of-having-a-choice).

Her memory of her mother's apology is unusually precise about what she felt. She remembers being cold toward the prospect that she would die while relatives came and went with sympathy, and would rather live even if it meant being alone. She does not describe being angry at her mother for giving her a sick body. By the time she remembers this during the fight with Hinoe, ordinary gains have changed the value of survival. She can eat without a tube in her wrist, walk outside under observation and have friends she knows through the games. She treats those as real pieces of an ordinary life. They coexist with an agreement she sees no viable way of escaping. Her participation demonstrates a constrained decision to take a medical chance and actual coercion once the undertaking sets the terms of her movements and labor. It leaves the exhaustiveness of the proposed medical alternatives and her family's independent understanding unknown. V09 S18 P0352–0361/P0477–0487.

## Friendship gives freedom a shape

Sana saves Kagura at Undead Mall, Kagura's third game. Kagura offers a two-night trip to a large amusement park to thank her. The outing is reported as a first experience for Sana and looks to Kagura like something she enjoys. She explains the looming escort in the matter-of-fact familiar 「私が逃げ出さないように、監視してるんだよ」. The girls continue to see each other outside play. At a restaurant Sana reacts sharply when Kagura says she cannot quite feel what it would mean to die and works chiefly to pay for leisure. She then teaches her. After recalling how little Kagura could do with an unfamiliar weapon, she asks about her passport and helps her get practiced. Kagura later displays real benefits from this training. She speculates that Sana found her too foolish to leave alone. The rescue, teaching and recurring ordinary company support a durable friendship, with that precise motive still Kagura's guess. V09 S21 P0308–0317, S24 P0239–0269. [Rescue, leisure and the informed choice to teach](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V09_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v09-e006--what-training-gives-kagura-and-what-it-cannot-settle).

She tells Yūki she would travel freely if she met the twenty-clear condition, then asks what Yūki will do after achieving ninety-nine. The question exposes a gap in the more experienced player's plans. With Kagura she gives the idea of traveling another form. Looking at a mock harbor she says 「私、海に還りたいんだよね」. She wants her ashes dispersed at sea so she can travel the world through marine life. Kagura makes a joke and Sana comes back with 「性癖って言うな」. The exchange takes place beside the mock harbor on the third day of their amusement-park trip. Kagura recalls it later while fighting Yūki. It shows the familiarity of the friendship. No sexual preference or romantic attachment follows from the joke and the witness supplies no broader intimate history for Sana. The outing, regular meetings and wish to travel are the strongest ordinary-life evidence, amid otherwise narrow knowledge of pastimes or tastes. V09 S18 P0370–0394, S21 P0311–0317, S24 P0496–0514.

## The survival she is willing to fight for

In Gimmicky Mansion, Yūki observes that an injury has conveniently allowed Sana to remain behind. Sana gives away that she understands what the question is about when Yūki produces a map. Sana has a different one, marked three of eight. She retains a useful memory of the house and thinks she knows where to go. Yūki draws out her admission that at the sight of the trap she saw an opportunity and took it, then explains what the injury costs. The common interest, two different maps and Sana's difficulty moving give them a reason to cooperate. Sana accepts being carried. They reach the intended room and part company, each with a different prospective exit. Sana says she has a sense suited to surviving danger and associates it with having lived close to death before playing. Her independent clue work and deliberate separation support practical initiative, even though injury turns her original plan into a poor way of reaching an exit on her own. V09 S18 P0267–0361/P0437–0465. [Two participants with different information](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V09_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v09-e002--a-communal-search-becomes-a-scarcity-problem).

Sana finds a hatch but hesitates over an unknown drop because she has just hurt herself by rushing. Hinoe reaches her and overpowers her initially. Sana remembers Hinoe speaking of money for leisure and inwardly insists 「譲れ」. Earlier she told Yūki that it is difficult to make sense of freely choosing to enter these games and qualified any blanket contempt for those who do. She has taught one such person. Now she uses the contrast in motives to claim that her own survival should take precedence. She actually disables Hinoe through close resistance. Inwardly she asks what she is staying alive for and comes back to the immediate fact that she wants to live. Her count of six still to go is consistent with fifteen as the current attempt and fourteen completed before it. She is heading back to the hatch when Mononobe shoots her. She is still moving after the shot, and Mononobe then kills her. The prior medicinal rescue, all her competence and the acute resolve counted here leave the agreement unfulfilled. V09 S18 P0322–0325/P0370–0381/P0458–0512. [The final contest over a single exit](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V09_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v09-e003--two-meanings-of-having-a-choice).

Sana's polite explanations to Takami and Yūki put 「私」 next to matter-of-fact qualification, and with Kagura she can move into ordinary teasing or a corrective question. The interior under pressure uses the same first person with much sharper syntax. It is written register and access to thought, with performance beyond this evidence. When Sana puts considerable weight on a concrete opening, she can act alone and quickly. A new risk can make her pause, and a compatible route makes her willing to rely on another person without handing away her own judgment. In close friendship she can answer a moral difference through help. Under immediate competition she is capable of directing anger at an opponent's motives. These few scenes support that range better than an unqualified forecast of timidity, altruism or resentment. V09 S18 P0162–0184/P0338–0385/P0462–0489, S24 P0239–0269/P0496–0514.

Kagura's later retrieval makes Sana's death materially certain. The agent forecasts a sale under the medical arrangement, a sale occurs, and Kagura receives the body, takes it with others to a funeral and carries out the sea promise. Those events satisfy what the friend said she wanted done with her ashes. Whether she would have approved the new games, the cost and the debt Kagura incurred in pursuit of that promise is unanswerable. V09 S21 P0330–0397 and S26 P0152–0257.
