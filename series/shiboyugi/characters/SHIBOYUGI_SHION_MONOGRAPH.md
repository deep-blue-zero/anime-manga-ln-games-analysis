# Shion

紫苑, read シオン, is a fourteen-year-old player with twenty-nine clears before Halloween Night. The original character plate prints “Sion.” No civil name is supplied. Shion has worked for a place where it might be possible to live openly with the desire she describes. The more she tries to escape exposure, the more people she makes willing to pursue her. It matters that she knows ordinary pleasures and forms particular attachments. She can enjoy a croquette bought from a woman she has known since childhood, depend on an agent whose company she would like to have, and linger over a chance to be understood by Yūki. Recognition does not give its giver safety. This portrait covers the admitted Japanese continuity through V10, with Shion’s life and terminal events disclosed in V04. V04 S19 P0380–0440/P0577–0592, S21 P0007–0038/P0608–0648/P0763–0977, S07-I01.

## The freedom she remembers and the one she hoped to find

In an earlier life not publicly narrated in detail, Shion holds that her parents knew how to hurt her through words. She recalls killing them as an unambiguous emotional victory that promised freedom. She does not give anyone a description of the precipitating family incident or a separate account of each parent. The force of the memory establishes how she makes sense of her first killing. It cannot establish which prior injury made it possible, what her parents believed, or that every later attack expresses the same grievance. The freedom proves brief in her own account. She wants to recapture the experience, discovers more ways of thinking about how to do so, and thinks of the persistence of that want as a curse. The games look like a possible home in which people are already prepared to risk death. She discovers a society with prohibitions and the power to exclude her, even there. Continued play and clandestine violence become simultaneous activities for her. V04 S21 P0608–0616.

Kyara gives her a material answer to the isolation. Shion lives with her, Moegi and Hizumi. She calls Kyara her teacher in effect and later 「我が師匠」 in thought, while she distinguishes the relationship from formally having become a pupil. Kyara teaches harmful professional skills and shares mundane, sometimes dubious pieces of knowledge. Shion can also ask her questions about how to live. She credits this relationship with a large part of remaining alive through twenty-nine contests. She talks most with Moegi, who is close in age and has likewise left her parents. Moegi’s repeated plan to learn how to act on her own wishes sounds unsustainable to Shion, whose affection does not make her optimistic about a friend’s prospects. Hizumi is harder for Shion to talk to. She repeatedly provokes her in the house and takes both their pursuit of one another and what she learned through it as a kind of communication. That interpretation belongs to Shion. It leaves Hizumi’s genuine anger and independent ideas about her own individuality intact. V04 S21 P0628–0640/P0763–0765, V03 S19 P0278–0319. [The remembered household](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e012--shions-ways-of-making-and-losing-a-home).

Shion calls that period the first happiness she can remember. It becomes a benchmark for later recognition. The deaths of Kyara and Moegi dissolve the household in her account. Even knowing what people say about Candle Woods, Shion sometimes imagines Kyara survived, without receiving a new communication from her. The reader has a more direct view of the outcome from V01. Shion keeps some actual contact with Hizumi for a while and later hears that Hizumi has died. V03 establishes the killing independently. By the present sequence Shion sees herself as the last one left. She takes improvements in social order to predict the exclusion of people like her, and at the same time makes repeated efforts to stay alive. It would misstate those efforts to treat the view of inevitable defeat as a settled desire for death. V01 S15 P1146–1154, V03 S21 P0017–0023, V04 S21 P0628–0648/P0751–0766.

## Ordinary attention under the risk of being known

Shion returns to the inexpensive croquette shop she has used since childhood. She notices what crowds and lack of room do to strangers, snaps at a rude man and lets him pass. She judges her own reflected face undistinguished and thinks it would make her an unlikely-seeming culprit. The same undistinguished face has a practical benefit when she wants to be unnoticed. She suspects pursuit after seeing a girl’s familiar face in a shop window but can first supply an ordinary reason for the recurrence. She waits until the crowd thins to distinguish one possible coincidence from a group actually following her. Public witness is a real inhibitor in this setting. It affects both her decision against acting on the first flash of anger and her estimate of the people following her. Her small shop habit and careful observations are directly given. No wider food preferences or routine domestic life are established. V04 S19 P0380–0417.

Garbage Prison accounts for how danger has reached an ordinary street. Shion privately recalls being seen doing what she calls a bad habit, killing a witness, being seen again and attempting to eliminate the remaining participants so that no identification could get out. Some escaped. The chain gives a definite reason for continued killing without supplying the exact first act or validating the reported eighty-to-three tally. Keito gathers partial evidence separately. Shion also kills Kirihara, from whom she previously obtained tattoos, because she regards the retired artisan as somebody able to link the arms to a person. The two had occupational dealings. Shion shows surprise at the people in Kirihara’s household and is not given their history with her. Once fear of exposure overrides the prior transaction, she assumes there are no recoverable leads left. Keito’s inquiry and outside pursuit make that assumption fail. An easily angered response in the street would by itself tell Keito almost nothing about these events. V04 S19 P0205–0225/P0273–0313/P0405–0416/P0444–0462, S21 P0237–0240. [Keito’s and Shion’s separate accounts](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e006--one-known-escalation-and-an-unknown-tally).

## Help that can be refused and then made possible

Shion earns money but is still a minor without the ordinary standing she would need to disappear. She does not attend school or live in a facility, and she acknowledges that her assigned agent has done considerable work to make that life possible. She had told the agent after Garbage Prison that she would retire. When she calls for a car, the agent cites that statement and refuses help. Shion makes the difference between professed independence and actual reliance audible. 「僕だ」 comes quickly, followed by the facts of pursuit and a demand for a car; her anger later breaks through when the agent treats the matter as somebody else’s problem. She improvises her first taxi ride as a pupil afraid of being late for class, gains transport by showing she can pay, and regrets first assuming she would not be followed into the car and then fleeing to a place with fewer people. Her capacity to assess attention and credibility survives those mistakes. Her own view of having money and almost no social credibility shows why a general claim that she can do whatever she wants would fail. V04 S19 P0417–0440/P0468–0499.

As she is about to be attacked, she tells the pursuers she will stop. Keito thinks the declaration may be sincere and still judges it too risky to rely on. The two assessments can coexist. Shion has a current reason to retreat and a current reason to say so. The agent then arrives and makes acceptance of a particular game into immediately effective protection. Shion chooses the opportunity while injured and under acute pressure. It is a reversal of conduct, with little basis for taking it as a considered endorsement of the entire career. On the way out she first claims she always thought the agent would come. The narration calls this insincere and she makes the more revealing choice to ask why. The agent invokes a bonus tied to thirty. Shion offers to pay for the meal the agent had mentioned, and the agent refuses with explicit disgust. Shion thinks the refusal makes sense. Actual life support, money as a possible motive, the desire to be owed companionship and the refusal of it are all different facts. Shion never learns the agent’s precise motivation or timing. V04 S19 P0489–0510/P0530–0545/P0577–0592. [The intervention and its conditions](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e008--when-an-invitation-makes-someone-protectable).

## Skill under disadvantage

Shion begins Halloween Night on her thirtieth appearance with a wound and confidence that she can manage one more time. A visible reputation and possible intimidatory effect of her two arm tattoos cannot replace fresh judgment. She tries an available sweet too soon, learns by encounter which items an enforcer will accept and gets real use from passing, withholding or reclaiming what she has. Injury changes the costs of each exchange. Other players whom she would normally consider easier opponents can act collectively and force her into situations in which mobility matters more than professional rank. She stops to weigh the worth of pursuing a person without any helpful stake. She also goes back to the site of Haine’s death even when there is no ordinary survival gain to the gesture. Practical restraint when action will cost and rage when the matter becomes personal are both legible at the same late stage. V04 S21 P0007–0038/P0084–0113/P0201–0363/P0499–0514/P0574–0604/P0652–0669/P0735–0774.

Haine enters through a separately available invitation to avenge Kirihara. Shion does not see the full household grief or the process that put Haine into the game. She receives an accusation, suffers a broken leg and does enough to contribute to a disastrous outcome for the other player. The costumed enforcer actually kills Haine after the late offer of a sweet. At first sight of Yūki, Shion claims she turned the tables on the person who sought revenge. This describes a role in the defeat and a view of who has prevailed. It cannot take over the actual final act. Her later argument about retaliation emphasizes the chance that the intended victim will fight back, and so reveals a real preoccupation with the adequacy of someone’s preparations. It is also self-serving. Her own attack on a weaker or endangered person can meet the same unexpected resistance she calls other people foolish for overlooking. V04 S19 P0547–0573, S21 P0201–0363/P0735–0744/P0778–0825/P0930–0948.

Shion spots signs of trouble in Yūki’s right-side vision and gains a real advantage from the inference. She then puts too much weight on the prospect the local success has revealed. She thinks such an opponent cannot compete at her level despite previous evidence that recognition can outrun ordinary sight. She is distracted and sees the surrounding threat late. Under severe injury she decides that pushing the other person into danger is preferable to depending on a proposal of temporary mutual retreat. Yūki anticipates that move from her past with Kyara. The inference is Yūki’s active strategy and happens to succeed here; neither lineage nor resemblance made it automatically true. Defeated and thrown back toward the pursuers, Shion disappears from Yūki’s sight. Yūki hears an apparently fatal impact and later attributes the final blow elsewhere. Kokone’s independent spectator account treats Shion’s defeat as settled. The combination strongly supports death and leaves the exact visible final act, body or complete administrative record outside what is shown. V04 S21 P0874–0948/P0959–0977, S23 P0016–0029. [Recognition and the false truce](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e013--recognition-and-the-false-truce).

## Recognition that makes a difference

At first sight of Yūki, Shion moves from rumor to a person capable of answering back. She calls the other woman someone she knows 「親の仇みたいに」 and names Kyara’s defeat as a reason she wanted to meet her. The simile indicates intensity. Yūki was not responsible for Shion’s parents. Shion also shows the tattoos when Yūki cannot place her and asks what Yūki thinks of a conventional claim about revenge. Her manner anticipates a simple disapproval, and Yūki’s distinction between the result and a feeling someone cannot easily displace surprises her. They disagree about who is easy to understand. Shion’s warning 「やめときなよ」 then reads distinctly from the threat. She argues that ninety-nine clears are an indirect means of dying and names her own anticipated ruin. Yūki says she would continue anyway. In her own restaging of the exchange Shion sees a mirror, feels understood and experiences a form of happiness recalling the household. This does not disclose all of either life to the other participant. V04 S21 P0763–0866.

The urgency of obtaining sweets interrupts that brief attachment. Shion can genuinely think 「こんな僕を認めてくれてありがとう」 and then privately intend to make the person who recognized her die in her place. Neither statement needs to be feigned. Her satisfaction in being understood and her decision to save her own life or have the remaining chance are independently represented, with changed conditions between the question and the attempted escape. A later helper who needs mutual loyalty should give far more evidentiary weight to the actual stakes and conduct than to a frank expression of closeness. No romantic or intimate involvement is established, and the teacher, housemates and professional opponent should keep their evidenced distinct meanings for a fourteen-year-old subject. V04 S21 P0847–0866/P0918–0948.

## Japanese speech and conditional conduct

Shion’s 僕 appears in spoken distress as well as inward address. 「僕だ」 opens a practical demand to the person who already knows her, while 「もうゲームには参加しない」 appeals publicly to people about to harm her. In the later car ride she can first boast through relief, then bluntly ask 「なんで来てくれたんだ？」 and offer a shared meal in return. Toward Yūki, あんた, the light mock-politeness of 「よくご存知で」, and the personal 「僕みたいに」 mark different effects. She can pose a genuine question about what people mean by revenge, ask whether her own view sounds strange, briefly warn an opponent in a register neither formal nor performatively fearsome, and then return to leverage. Taciturnity may indicate bodily exhaustion or waiting to be recognized. Neither the printed profile nor these shifts establishes a performed voice. V04 S19 P0419–0440/P0489–0494/P0577–0592, S21 P0778–0825/P0847–0866/P0930–0931.

Put in close variants within this story, Shion often reads the risk of being seen before deciding whether to act on anger. A plausible witness can stop an ordinary escalation, while exposure of her identity can instead trigger efforts to remove a source of identification. She can make good local revisions if she has time to compare evidence. Her errors increase when she treats the task of controlling attention as finished, takes lower rank for low risk or overcommits to a discovered advantage. Longed-for understanding can produce an honest pause and direct exchange. It puts little constraint on her conduct once independent survival costs become severe. These expectations pertain to the episodes where she supplies or acts on the relevant appraisals. The book leaves her choice under stable support without a competitive crisis largely unknown, except for what she remembers of one household. A future foster relationship, likely apology, further career, degree of enthusiasm for every aspect of violence and the effect of individual undisclosed childhood events remain unavailable.
