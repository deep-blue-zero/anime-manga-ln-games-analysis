# The unnamed prosthetics artisan

The unnamed prosthetics artisan (義体職人, 職人さん; SBY-P032) lives and works in a mansion in the forest. Yūki first needs his workmanship to resume her career. Over repeated visits she learns to recognize his pranks before he can surprise her and increasingly needs his judgment about what continuing that career will ask of her own body. His daughter Busutake eventually argues that the discipline he understands as replenishing a capacity cannot give Yūki enough to return. The craftsperson's choices before that dispute matter. He has recommended preserving vulnerable living tissue, made adjustments without a client's prior assent when the catastrophe was greater, and shared responsibilities with an organizer's hospital. An ability to give someone a body that can continue is a technical skill with a consequential set of priorities and permissions. This portrait uses the Japanese original novels through V10 and contains spoilers. The artisan's actual name, earlier years, and full contractual position remain undisclosed.

## A working place with an owner who plays

Yūki heard of the repair trade in her twentieth game and encountered a player with apparently artificial limbs at twenty-three. Her actual need arose after losing three left fingers in her thirtieth game. The artisan accepted the work readily, and the replacements restore a level of dexterity she understands to be comparable to her former fingers. They need regular maintenance, and close inspection can reveal their artifice, so she hides the hand in other contexts. V10 adds her recollection of postponing her immediate return to complete the artisan's “教練.” What he makes and what a player has to learn to do with it are separate conditions of a functional return. The source gives his abilities through repeated use and consultation. It does not assign to him the unknown maker of every prosthesis she has seen. V03 S12 P0089–0097. V05 S21 P0656–0669. V10 S16 P0019–0027. [V03, the first replacement account](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V03_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v03-e002--replacement-and-the-price-of-being-seen) · [V05, replacements in ordinary life](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V05_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v05-e007--what-gets-repaired-and-what-becomes-a-practice)

His early appearance is a loud laugh from inside a bag in the workshop. He saw it as a chance to make her jump. He explains it freely and savors the reaction. She observes an orderly workspace and respects it even as she learns the owner can hide himself in it. He asks to be called “おっちゃん.” Her continued “職人さん” preserves professional respect and their ongoing attachment without mirroring his construction of intimacy. She seeks an early check because of an anxiety unrelated to the condition of the part, which he at first takes for conscientious maintenance. Later he has built a concealed compartment and tries a similar entrance. Yūki locates him and calmly asks for the check. His surprise at the reversal is the practical consequence of an unusually familiar client whose perception he has previously been able to deceive. There is a routine physical relationship to the workshop too. He allows arrivals to let themselves into this isolated house. A wet-haired appearance in V04 suggests he has just bathed, and he welcomes a visitor who has inadvertently thrown tools and shelves into disorder while trying to anticipate another surprise. He excuses it once he hears the cause. “発端はわしみたいなもんやし” makes his earlier behavior relevant to the present mishap. His play is a grounded preference in how he greets customers. It supplies no general leisure profile beyond work. V03 S12 P0101–0146. V04 S19 P0072–0106. V08 S18 P0046–0069. [V03, entering the workshop](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V03_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v03-e002--replacement-and-the-price-of-being-seen) · [V08, at a familiar address](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V08_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v08-e003--an-accommodation-the-practitioner-keeps)

Their service relation also matters to his interests. He tells Yūki and Airi that the rising population of players after Candle Woods is welcome because it means more customers. Airi answers with the prospect of another mass death. The difference shows how recovery is visible from one place as business and from another as a new threat. His explicit commercial interest leaves ample room for personal concern, which he later shows. He makes no broad statement about the morality of playing. The black-and-white original interlude headed “〈職人〉について 幽鬼の知ること” identifies craftsmen as third parties with variably priced and variably good work. It describes a mutually beneficial relation with the organizers and suggests different explanations for the division of work. The heading identifies the source of the explanation as Yūki's available knowledge. It cannot settle what contract binds this craftsman, and his congenial behavior cannot settle what he charges each client. V03 S12 P0192–0203 and S13-I01. ## Referring, warning and withholding

Yūki's early assumption that the artisan has broad industry knowledge is partly disappointed. Other clients have already asked him about the same disastrous game. He says he knows nothing specific, is neither an organizer's employee nor an audience member by his account, and has not competed. His network seems thin to him until he recalls Kirihara, a former player who moved into ancillary work. The address he offers is useful. He distinguishes having met her at regular checkups from knowing whether she still lives there or has relevant information. This help and the limit on its scope are more probative than the final surprise at her house. He is strategically useful to a player despite a modest role in acquiring competitive intelligence. V04 S19 P0107–0134. [V04, the referral](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e005--the-labor-of-getting-an-account)

He can see changes in a different way with a known client. During maintenance he asks about the state of Yūki's right eye. She regards its appearance as an old effect of Candle Woods and registers his unusually sober face. She only later realizes that he took the observation seriously enough to contact her agent without telling her in the room. The agent says the artisan initiated the warning and has a medical examination prepared. The consequence is established by the agent’s direct involvement and by the examination route, although the exact terms on which the two contacts agreed remain unknown. The episode supplies concrete corrective initiative by someone whose replacement range does not include eyes. It also shows an established working relation that can make decisions about what to flag before the client knows another person has been told. V04 S19 P0138–0157/P0363–0376. V05 S21 P0664–0669. [V04, the eye](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V04_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v04-e009--the-eye-becomes-an-actual-limit)

When Yūki returns alarmed by her hand, he actually examines the three replacements and says in his assessment they work well. She would prefer a mechanical failure because it would be easier to undo. He can pursue the alternate explanation by email after asking if she wants a connection to the organizer’s hospital. He distinguishes testing the artificial fingers that he knows well from testing the living arm in a facility equipped to do it. Yūki asks about Hakushi's progression. He first says that he generally does not speak about a client's private matters. He allows himself to share a comparison with the master's pupil, and specifically distinguishes symptoms beginning earlier from alterations that began around eighty. That distinction helps Yūki think but does not turn a clinical comparison into an identical prognosis. Some earlier reports of his famous clients were hearsay to her. She now has this account from his own conversation. He shares limited professional history in light of an existing relationship and without claiming that his earlier knowledge explains what all players do. He later advises “まだ生身にしとくのがいいよ,” because current benefit from the replacement is slight and the transition would be irreversible. The service he sells would give him a replacement to make. His actual preference in this case is to keep the arm. V08 S18 P0068–0122/P0342–0345. ## What the daughter can do with the family trade

Airi instead finds the usually unlocked door shut. Busutake identifies the artisan as her father, opens the door, names Airi as her father's client and performs the ordinary check. Airi already heard the trade was passed down within the family and has thought of the father as reluctant to talk about his origins. The daughter says she was trained from early childhood. An older child was chosen to inherit and she was judged “不適格.” Her claim that nobody particularly objected to her playing does not identify the decision-maker or say why she lost eligibility. Her actual ability to undertake the inspection counters any assumption that exclusion from succession simply means incompetence. The father is offstage. Like the information about an earlier client, family disclosure comes primarily from someone who participates in the relationship and can be challenged about its implications. It establishes him as Busutake’s father and a practitioner in an inherited trade. Who trained her and how succession was decided remain open. V09 S26 P0027–0071. [V09, Airi's visit and discovery](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V09_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v09-e012--airis-affirmative-answer-without-shiros-ambition)

V10 begins after a different order of injury. He explains that all four extremities have already had portions replaced. On a whiteboard he draws a figure to explain the adjustment of the legs, which were not left with exactly symmetrical residual lengths. The arm replacements could be sized individually, but he decided coordination of the legs warranted making the residual lengths even. “事後承諾” names what he is asking for after the fact. Yūki immediately says she accepts it. He assigns damaged organs to the organizing side and tells her to discuss post-operative care with her attending physician. The artisan then asks if she still intends to compete. She does, and he anticipates a period of training. Care around the client tends to preserve or recover useful motion, and in this case includes an irreversible intervention before specific assent. One might see it as an emergency compromise intended to enable walking. It also exhibits a real treatment asymmetry when she has to learn what they have done. His later request for assent and her answer give information about this encounter only. V10 S16 P0007–0029. [V10, the first consultation](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V10_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v10-e002--what-it-takes-to-play-again)

After a later catastrophe, he has been called to the medical examination because he made so much of Yūki's body. He reassures the waiting agent about survival and separately expresses doubt about a complete return. A full-body replacement like Hakushi's enters his account as a possibility. He leads the worried agent toward the room so they can see Yūki. Then Busutake, herself a patient, calls him “お父さん,” asks whether he can really help, and states that compensatory fabrication will no longer allow this particular player to compete. He answers “なんやと？” and starts to grasp what she means before she declares she will take over and her companions attack. Two priorities come into open conflict. Her assertion that she can furnish the strength necessary to play is her claim, with some basis in what she does as a player. The father has the clearer established evidence of recovery and ordinary use across time. This scene does not settle either prognosis or show a further response by him after the assault. V10 S26 P0029–0069. [V10, the competing claim to her care](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V10_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v10-e016--a-second-custodian-for-a-damaged-body)

## Language and judgment through the last consultation

The artisan's written language includes “わし,” familiar “自分” addressed to Yūki, and forms such as “やさかい,” “でけへん,” and “おおきに.” These give his speech a recognizably Kansai-associated register without locating a birthplace. He can be exuberant in a prank, brief and plain while evaluating a part, and confidential while adjusting how much he says about a past client. With Yūki's agent he remains familiar and offers an immediate action after delivering unsettling news. His “おまえ” to his daughter and “なんやと？” during her challenge come in a more direct family register. The last scene marks a change of interlocutor and pressure. It supplies no baseline for their conversations at home. The source offers written expression and narration, no performed voice. The workshop, humor and admission of commercial interest give evidence of specific routines and tastes. The series does not show his romantic or intimate life, identify another parent, or give a stable food preference.

In an appraisal with sufficient ordinary function, a durable option and an irreversible more invasive choice, he is likely to test existing capacity and preserve what the client still has. He can move quickly toward more extensive restoration when loss is immediate and then distinguish the benefit he can supply from limits that need testing or another specialist. His concern can also reach past the instant authority a client knowingly gives him, as with the warning directed to the agent and the leg adjustment. Both he and the daughter can claim to want Yūki to move again. The disagreement over the purpose of the replacement is more precisely assessed than a universal character judgment. His repair requirements and case comparisons are grounded in years with clients, with no warrant here for predicting his conduct under the daughter's new authority claim, an emergency beyond his technical range, or a challenge to the underlying family succession.
