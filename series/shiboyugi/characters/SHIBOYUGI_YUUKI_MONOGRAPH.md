# Yūki

幽鬼 is the playing name of 反町友樹, Sorimachi Yūki. She earns a living in organized lethal contests whose operators control invitation, movement and much of her medical recovery. She has unusually good judgment about what she can do in a room with other people and a comparatively uncertain sense of what she wants after the job. She works toward ninety-nine clears. The objective becomes her own by being used: she draws it from another person’s example, acts on it in an emergency, then subordinates ordinary routines and sometimes other people’s claims to the career it makes possible. She also tries to acquire obligations that could matter outside it. Evening school, a chosen haircut, a small apartment and the particulars of her relationships carry evidence of that effort. This portrait includes spoilers through V10’s hospital scene, where she is alive after her ninety-third game and others are arguing over what sort of body she will need. It distinguishes what she knows in each situation from what readers learn at later publication points. ## Before the career had a purpose

Yūki left compulsory schooling without immediately going on to high school. Her description of her parents is that they left her to her own devices. At the first relevant period she does casual work, quits a previous job after a quarrel and responds to a recruiter’s attractive but opaque offer. Her practical indifference toward the qualification path people usually follow is already in evidence. An early curiosity about what sort of job she has been offered is important to how she later remembers it. Maiden Race, published in V06, follows an underinformed beginner rather than a child consciously choosing among clearly explained options. Yūki deliberately pushes Kanade off the last bridge to clear the way for the waiting players. She thinks the event is staged and neither understands the risk nor realizes she has killed someone. She actually helps another injured survivor, the girl the reader later knows became Setsuna. Learning what the game was does not wipe the attraction of having been competent in it, and Yūki chooses to remain after learning the consequences. The first decision to try a job, acting on the only way forward she sees under that false understanding, and actual career continuation after knowledge are three events. Yūki’s records do not give a secret formative biography that explains them all. V06 S16 P0018–0047, S18 P0173–0223/P0331–0367/P0428–0447/P0679–0796. [First game and its later disclosure](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V06_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v06-e005--two-incompatible-examples-in-one-person).

By her third game she is capable of threatening another player and can recognize the difference between winning a move and proving the ability to live a life that way. Hakushi defeats and restrains her and then makes a voluntary offer of instruction. The episode, disclosed in V05, prevents a reading in which #9 starts their relationship. Yūki questions Hakushi’s assumption that accepting a pupil is hers to decide. She accepts the voluntary offer after Hakushi respects that choice. Retrospective narration leaves the precise reason for accepting open. Her future use of the lesson is independent. Before Candle Woods, she is adept enough to have an irregular nocturnal job and can go days without talking, while her little room and routines show little ambition for anything else. She cares about which tracksuit to wear around people and feels guilty about a small accidental mess. She is neither globally unobservant nor globally self-sufficient. At home she gets up earlier than she otherwise would have, makes a count from the old outfits, starts a record and cultivates a usable life for a game professional. Privately Hakushi takes herself to be passing on an aim, a fact the reader is given even while Yūki thinks her dead. Yūki does not witness Hakushi’s survival and retirement and learns the living woman is still available much later. Their accounts are different kinds of truth about the choice. ## Cooperation, calculation and a limit on helping

Ghost House opens the series on an established #28, where Yūki is the person new players look to. She lets the inexperienced label themselves by putting up her own hand first. She adjusts politeness deliberately and treats a challenger’s distinction between average survival and a novice’s prospects seriously. Her account of 利他 is practical. She values gaining goodwill, leaving others alive who can help her and behaving in a way consistent with her own interests. She explicitly leaves them room to suspect that this could be manipulation. She also likes their contact, minds turning over a muffin she wanted, and feels something when frightened partners stop holding on to her. Each fact can matter at once. When Kinko removes herself from the group, Yūki acts on a specific weight limit in going after her, takes the actual burden of a maneuver and tries to address her exhaustion and guilt in their conversation. Affection, necessity and leadership do not collapse into a single motive. V01 S12 P0067–0170/P0187–0199/P0508–0573/P0624–0834/P0838–0891.

At the final door, the number of lit symbols persuades her an additional person must die. She acts before securing the group’s assent or testing other explanations at the door, and her internal procedure is to kill the nearest person once she has decided there must be a victim. Kinko is that person. The door opens. Other people were still considering alternatives, and opening the door establishes that the act was effective rather than that alternatives were impossible. Yūki apologizes too late for the victim to choose and later gives a specific private interval to remembrance. She also benefits from keeping hard choices mechanically simple under acute pressure. Both the real earlier work on Kinko’s behalf and the small private acknowledgment matter to how she can live with later conduct. Neither grants her authority to take the risk for Kinko. Scrap Building, her #10, exposes a shortcoming in Yūki’s authority among people who already trust someone else. The four other active players look to Mishiro; Yūki’s experience and expectation of battery exhaustion do not persuade them, especially after a previous caution proves unnecessary. She remains partially responsible for making a collective alternative work. She leads and helps people she dislikes when the later route and their vulnerability furnish a need. Kotoha and Mishiro’s reliance on her does not yield unquestioned authority or a joint career. When Mishiro returns as an experienced competitor and explicitly makes Yūki a target, what Yūki wants from the new encounter changes. She had experienced a run of difficulties and could barely say the 99 target aloud. Hearing that this personally effective opponent is on her own fortieth game restores Yūki’s sense of possible action. Yūki’s escape in her own #30 does not demonstrate what happens to every badly injured opponent afterward. She took an aid-providing leadership role when group survival helped her and fought to escape when that collaboration no longer operated. ## What changes as her body changes

The medical trajectory has separate thresholds. In #30 she loses three left fingers permanently. She delays the next entry to train with replacements and learns that levels adequate for daily use are insufficient for fighting. She has a regular relationship with an independent artisan before later relying on other providers. The right eye progressively worsens after the earlier Kyara injury and is explicitly vulnerable in the game with Shion; by the later #61 period she has lost its light perception. She suffers a separate temporary loss of useful left sight during Royal Palace, #62, where she discovers a form of perception she can use under stress. The restored left eye and lingering right loss join adaptive practice during the private Snow Room experiment and later official games. At #64, she produces no click and has an intuitive sense of range and motion despite her blind right eye. #65 still unsettles her, and succeeding with a restriction has not made every stimulus easy. ## How she lives and what she gives up

At sixteen she received help in taking room 107 in Tochinoki, an inexpensive apartment at ¥35,000 a month. In the period shown before Candle Woods, even when the hourly routine itself is simple, living from night to night has severely narrowed her activities. She is attached to distinct indoor and outdoor tracksuits, puts off hygiene and can feel embarrassed at an accidental breach of ordinary civic behavior such as littering. The admitted profile at twenty-seven clears lists seventeen, a birthday of April 18, convenience-store ice cream and night walks, and a dislike of daytime. The age on this profile does not date the first or ninth game. A nocturnal walk persists after she changes eating, cleaning and exercise habits. Accumulating money does not initially make her want a new apartment or much furniture. In the Ghost House, the desire to eat a muffin precedes the others' use of the partly eaten one as a food-safety check. Hands she judges warmer than her own provide local means of recognition. Each detail originates in a different setting and has a different range of application. ## Whose judgment can reach her

Hakushi’s authority changes as more becomes known. In #3 Hakushi defeats and captures Yūki, then spares her once the remaining sheriffs no longer need the bounty. Yūki challenges the assumption that Hakushi can decide who will be her pupil, then takes the voluntary offer. She adopts the 99 purpose after the older player seems to have died at Candle Woods. Hakushi’s actual survival does not nullify what Yūki did with her supposed death. Meeting her again in V03 means hearing directly that she can act without awaiting approval. Yūki requests the private Snow Room to confront an apparent double under rules she can enforce. Each player depends on knowing how the other actually works. Hakushi solves a safer counterpart puzzle and falls into a separate trap. Yūki realizes the drop can coexist with a right answer, takes the items in her own room and reaches the exit while the apparent attacker persists. In their later conversation she challenges the intentional-handicap story, asks how the trap could have been solved and elects to withhold the apparition. Tamamo’s desire for company and training gives Yūki another role. She accepts, takes precautions and teaches real things, helps to choose the terms on which to stop, then is grieved and ashamed when relation and obligation prove less terminable than contact. The retired Rinrin supplies knowledge the mentor cannot supply. Training proceeds after Yūki experiences an invitation as an intrusion, learns to use solitude and renews contact on different terms. Whether Yūki could alone have prevented what happened in Royal Palace cannot be answered from how much she blames herself. ## Japanese speech, action and silence

The writing often sets Yūki’s 私 beside small changes in address or politeness. As the last newcomer to speak in Ghost House, 幽鬼っていいます is a reduced polite introduction followed by a short よろしく. She moves through the plain questions 全員、最初からここにいたの？ and けっこう待ったのかな, attempts an apology, then chooses how much expertise to volunteer. The pattern can let her take the initiative without an overt claim to rank. When she publicly says 気に入った！ then gives Mishiro urgent directions, the imperatives support an actual intervention. The narration is separately clear that its recipient hears the voice as 明朗快活 and takes a risk by trusting it. That impression is evidence in the scene, distinct from a general voice specification. Late friendly forms such as また会ったね、藍里 and どう思う？ can belong to a potentially lethal encounter, and Airi hears a little affection in the latter. The choice to stop speech and act here is shared in effect, although their reasons differ. V01 S12 P0044–0065, V02 S12 P0884–0903, V10 S23 P0068–0092.

With Setsuna the register will convey both intimacy and professional distance in the same exchange. エージェントさん and a formal expression of thanks accompany ざっくりいっちゃってください and the explanation that nobody else can be asked. At the box handover, Yūki imagines the formula かしこまりました would ordinarily follow 処分していただけると助かります. The actual 承知しました comes only after a pause, apology and reiterated direction. Yūki’s mental ear for routine professional language is quite good. She fails to infer what the unusual answer costs the other person. With the landlady, それは、言えないんです holds the limit on disclosure. Moments later a polite promise contains くたばる, a rough joke about returning in time, whose reception she cannot determine. An unusually explicit こんばんは elsewhere in their building can precede a real request or merely signal that she is late. No one can infer the former by hearing the form on its own. V06 S22 P0010–0038, V07 S19 P0076–0109/P0291–0299, V08 S22 P0063–0097, V10 S16 P0378–0392.

The audience can also consist of nobody. After Snow Room, she says 悪いね on a walk after adopting “good game” within the ordeal. Her rule permits the words after the event and permits grief to exist after effective decision-making. The writing gives a work-conditioned permission to speak as well as an apology. It leaves no recipient’s response from which to infer forgiveness. This distinction also limits imitation. The evidence gives identifiable written phrasing, effects from punctuation and perspective, and occasional descriptions of how other people hear her. Ordinary pitch, accent and a general conversational recording are unavailable. V06 S22 P0124–0135. [After the game](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V06_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v06-e014--the-work-of-repair).

## What can be expected of her and where prediction fails

In a familiar cooperative contest she is likely to try to identify the operative constraint, take up useful work quickly and withhold a claim she has no leverage to enforce. If another person is facing live danger and assistance can actually be given, an offer, route or intervention is plausible even after distrust. What she does when only one beneficiary can meet an observed requirement needs the local rule and the alternatives she believes she has. Her rule about the nearest person in Ghost House is known from a severe example and cannot be extended into a generic ranking of whom she would save. In an ordinary conversation with a colleague she may give enough truth to continue the task while holding back biographical detail. Dangerous questions about the work are likely to be met with a short refusal, a partial explanation or a condition. She can earnestly seek the other person’s help all the same. The distinction between one uncoerced request and apparent authority can fail where she misunderstands the danger, especially in the novel’s account of her first game. #65's fear also survives skills acquired for low perceptual access. Its later reappearance or absence cannot be deduced from experience level alone. V01 S12 P0136–0170/P0893–0954, V02 S12 P0806–0909, V06 S18 P0331–0367/P0429–0469 and S22 P0097–0135, V07 S21 P0003–0075/P0420–0442, V08 S22 P0063–0097.

Nothing observed warrants a confident reconstruction of her behavior with a romantic partner. She has attachments to people and places and at one point likens her simultaneous fear and anticipation in facing a rival to the feeling of visiting a lover. That comparison can register the specific intensity of a contest. It supplies no relationship history, partner preference, sexual identity or ordinary pattern of intimacy. Similarly, anti-institutional behavior in particular emergencies, refusal of office, present membership in a livelihood and distress over one’s own participation need to remain specific. Her repeated inability to imagine the day after #99 is the clearest limit on claims about her future. The jobs she can still perform, the school she expects to lose, the sharply divided choices others wish to make for her and one still unfulfilled motive coexist at the last admitted moment.
