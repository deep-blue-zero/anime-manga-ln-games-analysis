# Sumiyaka

Sumiyaka (墨家, スミヤカ) is an established player known to Yūki. She reports entering Candle Woods for her twenty-ninth game and says plainly that the next career threshold frightens her. She is physically playful with a familiar colleague, can tell the same person when her own knowledge runs out, and takes the absence of a participant more seriously than anyone else in that conversation. The record of her behavior is concentrated in one occasion. The admitted Japanese novels through V10 establish her actual death and give no later action by her. This portrait contains V01 spoilers.

## An experienced peer who knows how to ask

Yūki remembers Sumiyaka being at twenty-three on an earlier occasion, and attributes considerable standing to her now. Her current report of twenty-nine is different evidence from Yūki's remembered number or estimation of relative rank. In their shared game the veteran Hakushi has told the group to avoid violent interrogation. Sumiyaka mocks how slow the procedure looks and invokes her own firsthand knowledge of more immediate results. Nevertheless she stays within the group arrangement. When challenged about an alternative herself, she tests the point playfully on Yūki and then gives a serious reason to trust Hakushi's advice. Experience makes her able to understand the criticized practice and accept an institutional constraint she would not have selected herself. (V01 S15 P0369–0387.) [The team under Hakushi's instruction](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e007--two-briefings-and-two-forms-of-group-power)

The physical teasing is familiar and somewhat intrusive. When Yūki asks her to let go, Sumiyaka changes how she holds her and directly asks to keep holding her because she is anxious. Sumiyaka's posture can initiate a joke or ask for company without changing her firm language. Being able to say what she needs is consequential. She explains the professional threshold known as the Wall of Thirty, advances her own objection to the idea that the organizers deliberately suppress accomplished players, and says she is afraid because she does not know what actually creates the danger. She can use playful contact to spend time with a colleague and still present the genuine source of uncertainty when asked. Neither the teasing nor Yūki's receipt of it establishes romantic reciprocity. (V01 S15 P0378–0415.) ## Practical curiosity and a missed danger

In the rest of that conversation, Sumiyaka follows the implications of Hakushi's group strategy and makes room for the possibility that their opponents have equipment not yet seen. Yūki asks for an expert answer to a technical comparison. Sumiyaka initially admits she is no specialist, then gives a qualified answer grounded in what she has encountered in games. The form matters more than turning her guesses into a rule. Her scorn for children's play and her openness about uncertainty are different aspects of someone who is comfortable stating where the advantages of her own experience end. (V01 S15 P0414–0438.) [The V01 reading of the briefing and available knowledge](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e007--two-briefings-and-two-forms-of-group-power)

Sumiyaka also sees that a caramel-haired woman who had been among the novice group is no longer there. She distinguishes the particular color from the candidate Yūki suggests. Yūki thinks this could be a simple misidentification and is surprised that Sumiyaka stays interested. Sumiyaka begins to tell Yūki to do something about the group, but is interrupted. The earlier perception turns out to have mattered once Kyara's concealment is revealed. It does not retrospectively give Sumiyaka certainty about the person, the disguise or the threat. In a setting where one unusually observed trait is more important than the prevailing category of novice, she allocates attention better than her younger colleague does. She still loses the chance to make use of it. (V01 S15 P0443–0461/P0861–0888.) [What each side can see before recognizing Kyara](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e008--the-spectators-advantage-over-both-viewpoints)

Yūki finds her body after the group disperses. Recognition is physical and the death is directly attested. Yūki attributes the killing to the missing woman, now understood as the marauding attacker. That is a strong working inference in this setting, with no independent witness to the exact act. Later, while discussing what she sees in Yūki, Hakushi compares the pupil's native sense with Sumiyaka's. The comparison establishes the older teacher's estimation. It cannot serve as a comprehensive capability ranking or account for Sumiyaka's death. No conclusion about the causes of the Wall follows from one known player dying just before thirty. (V01 S15 P0823–0832 and S17 P0105–0112.) [Two different judgments of a player's ability](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e010--a-surviving-mentor-whom-the-reader-has-twice-lost)

## What the concentrated portrait can support

Her speech to Yūki uses a rough familiar register, from そりゃねえ to a direct imperative when she asks for contact. She is comfortable switching from a joke to an argument without making either one ceremonial. She uses 私 and resumes the formal subject of work after describing fear. The text attributes the hoarseness of her speaking voice to alcohol and tobacco. This is narrated characterization, with no observed ordinary habit, brands, quantities or reasons for use. Interpersonal knowledge is unusually unequal. Yūki supplies affectionate attention, later recognition and the principal view of her actions. Sumiyaka expresses concern about their available knowledge and her own prospects without giving away family, civil work, relationships or an intimate history. (V01 S15 P0369–0415/P0428–0438/P0823–0828.) [The source's distinctions in written voice](../readings/SHIBOYUGI_V01_DEEP_READING.md#sby-v01-e012--where-the-text-lets-a-voice-be-heard)

Given a familiar partner and uncertainty that admits conversation, Sumiyaka is likely to use humor bluntly, offer a practical view and ask for steadiness when she needs it. She may look again at a neglected observation and give weight to a more experienced person's instruction without abandoning her own judgment. That expectation is conditioned by the relationship and situation shown. A stranger's response, an acute threat that closes the time for discussion, or a conflict between the rule and personal obligations could change it. Before the portrayed game, further expressions of fear and what she had done independently at twenty-three remain unobserved. Her ability to be teasing, vulnerable and productively suspicious in actual succession is the available differentiation.
