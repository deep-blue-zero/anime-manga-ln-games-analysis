#### Counterreadings that the cumulative account must retain

**“It was only a misunderstanding.”** This captures the incompatible motives but suppresses actions. Restricting association, withdrawing without consultation, publicly disclosing private words, and later inspecting a phone are not made nonexistent by a better explanation. The account must retain who did what and who bore the cost. [WY2-C01/C05/C11.]

**“Hime finally becomes authentic.”** This overstates the endpoint. She recognizes the sincerity of a particular wish and gains a way to remain without protective falsehood. She continues attractive role performance and shows ongoing limits in institutional learning. The development is more specific, and consequently more useful, than a complete conversion from falsehood to truth. [WY2-C02/C04/C06; i139–150.]

**“Mitsuki’s hostility was always love.”** The new investment evidence makes pure indifference untenable; it does not transform harsh treatment into a healthy expression of affection or certify the same motive for every act. The apology matters because the prior behavior is not retrospectively self-justifying. [WY2-C03/C08/C09.]

**“The photographs explain Kanoko completely.”** They make attention visible to a new observer. They do not give that observer, or the reader, Kanoko’s whole account. The discovery creates an evidentiary opportunity and a privacy problem at the same time. [WY2-C10/C11.]

#### Counterreadings retained rather than dismissed

**“Kanoko merely misunderstood the sister script.”** She does lack information and asks a meaningful definition question. But once Hime explains her private joy, Kanoko acts against a preference that is no longer reducible to misunderstanding a term. Removing the script may be her chosen strategy; it is not established as a way to restore Hime’s feelings. [C01/C04/C15.]

**“Sumika correctly diagnoses the feeling, so her intervention is justified.”** The romantic inference is strong, but several further propositions remain distinct: what harm will occur, what could prevent it, who may decide, and whether abandoning the attachment is either necessary or possible. The moment in which she considers accepting Kanoko’s liking makes this gap visible inside her own thought. [C02/C06–C08.]

**“Sumika is only pretending to be kind.”** Her work, reading, help and brief reconsideration are real evidence. They do not erase surveillance or coercive elements; the reverse is equally important. A mixed account better fits the actual sequence than either total benevolence or total fraud. [C05–C09.]

**“The flashback proves Kanoko’s right to be the only friend.”** It establishes that Hime helped create an intimate exception. It does not show a promise forbidding future relationships, and the current narrative gives Hime a further connection she explicitly values. Historical specialness and permanent exclusivity are different claims. [C11/C15.]

**“The earlier Hime simply teaches healthy honesty.”** She presses Kanoko to voice dislikes, but then overrides a truthful public confession with a false alibi. She makes private speech possible while controlling the public account. The contradiction is analytically productive and should not be polished away. [C13/C14.]

**“The photo supplement settles the entire consent issue.”** It settles one scene’s permission and gives a plausible relational basis for photo-based encouragement. It neither proves permission for every later act nor warrants treating all images as stolen. [C16.]

#### Counterreadings that remain analytically necessary

**“Sumika’s earlier intervention was justified because it produced the new bond.”** The endpoint does not license the route retroactively. Kanoko cries under the original demand; Nene criticizes that demand; Sumika acknowledges it was wrong and changes her conduct. The new bond depends on correction, not on demonstrating that the initial prohibition was secretly the right method. [C02/C07/C08/C13.]

**“Nene proves that love should always outrank everyone else.”** She claims her own earlier choice and rejects a particular controlling intervention. The source also preserves the costs to Sumika and the changed work relations. Authorship of a choice is not exemption from describing its consequences, just as consequences do not erase authorship. [C01–C03.]

**“Kanoko is no longer possessive because she has Sumika.”** Her ballot motive and redirected prohibition occur within the same volume. She gains a listener without renouncing Hime as the object of love or fully revising the exclusivity model. The genuine change is an enlarged capacity to be supported, not an established disappearance of jealousy. [C04/C08/C10/C13.]

**“The sister pact is merely another performance.”** It follows chosen conversation, explicit disclosure, accepted comfort, and a stated condition; subsequent service supplies initial follow-through. The performance has a real private basis. This does not make the bond romantic or remove the public pressures of its formation. [C11–C13.]

**“Hime’s welcome settles the exclusivity conflict.”** Hime welcomes the new friend without knowing the complete conflict. Her response provides relevant evidence about her preference, but not the informed adjudication of a claim she has not heard. [C05/C15.]

**“The summer chapter is only a joke and need not constrain the models.”** Its comic staging still depicts refusals, choices, shame, correction, work competence, and accepted accommodation. Those events are usable behavioral evidence. Conversely, an analysis that turns the chapter into a serious diagnosis of every gaze would overread the framing and understate its ambiguity. [C16–C18.]

**“The final rule guarantees nobody can leave.”** The announced desire for continued presence is clear; a tested enforcement mechanism or an actual refusal to let someone depart is not shown. The remaining tension should become a future question, not an invented institutional outcome. [C14.]

#### Counterreadings that must remain visible

**“Mitsuki refuses help because Hime cannot do the work.”** This has an initial evidentiary basis: Hime is learning, and busy service is not an unlimited training opportunity. It becomes inadequate as a complete explanation when another arrangement lets Hime contribute and Mitsuki’s own thoughts remain distressed. The source supports both the real learning need and the personal investment in being the teacher. [C02–C05.]

**“Hime is simply demanding more attention.”** She does want a personally meaningful response, but she also offers useful, less glamorous labor and a gift planned before the immediate confrontation. Her desire includes giving. Ignoring that direction would reproduce the very asymmetry the volume dramatizes. [C03/C08/C10/C11.]

**“The gift proves Hime does not take Mitsuki seriously.”** Hime remembers the birthday, admits uncertainty about taste, selects a wearable object, and includes Mitsuki in a shared design. These acts are evidence of attention. The gift can still fail to express the specialness Mitsuki wants. Failure to meet an unspoken romantic or exclusive expectation is not identical to absence of care. [C08/C10/C18.]

**“Because Mitsuki likes Hime, refusing the gift is secretly harmless.”** The refusal is a legitimate boundary, but the repeated role-only explanations and visible hurt remain consequential. Private affection is not an automatic exemption from describing what another person receives. Conversely, describing that hurt does not create a duty to accept an unwanted object. [C11/C18.]

**“The entire injury was a scam from the beginning.”** The new scene names recovery and a decision to continue the pretence. It supplies a temporal boundary the blanket claim erases. Original fraud remains unproved; later concealment is no longer merely suspected. [C06/C07.]

**“Hime would stay anyway, so the secret does not matter.”** The source has not shown her fully informed decision. Enjoying work and being offered an informed choice are different facts. The girls’ train conversation confirms that the recovery premise still matters to their deliberation. [C06/C07; O07.]

**“The real Yano is the harsh backstage person; the kind Ayanokōji is false.”** The school history directly contests that division. Ayanokōji is a practised desired self. The more adequate distinction is among aspiration, capacity, particular action, and the explanation another person receives. None of those becomes identical merely because the role is sincerely wanted. [C12/C15/C20.]

**“A smile solves Mitsuki’s social difficulty.”** The successful school exchange also contains a statement of fear, a request for clearer signals, and an interlocutor’s specific explanation. The form makes the message easier to receive; it does not replace its content or guarantee goodwill. [C13/C14.]

**“The warmer ending means the current fight has passed.”** The ending returns to an earlier encounter. It changes what the reader understands about Mitsuki’s wish, not what Hime has heard after the gift refusal. [C17.]

#### Counterreadings and disconfirmers

**“The reconciliation is false because the kiss creates another crisis.”** This discards the specific problem the letter answers. Hime’s wish to give help is acknowledged, Mitsuki receives it, and the anklet’s refused state changes. The later mismatch concerns a further meaning and enactment. Preserve both rather than choosing the warmer or darker scene as the only real one. [C06–C09.]

**“Hime asked for true feelings, so she asked for the kiss.”** The actual promise concerns telling, and Hime’s immediate request concerns words of liking. Neither supplies specific agreement to that contact. The source’s later asymmetry confirms why those distinctions matter. [C08/C09/C13.]

**“Mitsuki’s kindness was never sincere.”** The role’s desired-self history and Hime’s letter oppose that conclusion. Sincerity is established more securely than the reliability of every resulting act. The analysis should question action and boundaries without erasing the aspiration. [C01/C06/C11.]

**“Hime is simply an innocent recipient of pressure throughout.”** Her own insistence and physical escalation must be included. They do not create consent to the later kiss, but omitting them would flatten the earlier disclosure into a conversation freely controlled by Mitsuki. [C02/C03/C09.]

**“Hime’s discomfort proves either hidden reciprocal attraction or permanent inability to love.”** Both move beyond the source. Her behavior shows disturbance and avoidance; it does not provide a complete first-person romantic self-definition. The evidence permits several explanations and awaits her own account. [C13/C14/C19.]

**“Kanoko finally understands what happened because she sees Hime is upset.”** She asks and is not told. The scene is useful evidence of attention and concern, not transmission of the kiss. The same restriction applies to Sumika’s teasing about a visit. [C19; INF07/INF10/INF12.]

**“The recovery disclosure confirms the predicted Hime trust crisis.”** It supplies the trigger, but the expected explicit response is absent. Kanoko’s supplemental criticism cannot be moved into Hime’s mouth. The correct disposition includes negative evidence, not merely an unchanged label of “untriggered.” [C15/C20; [V06 §14](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/WATAYURI_V06_DEEP_READING.md#predictions).]

**“Hime quits only because the original contract is over.”** Those facts are true and form her stated explanation. The intervening private aftermath, recoil, and repeated liking question make a purely administrative account inadequate to the sequence. Conversely, the source has not yet supplied a complete psychological explanation replacing those practical facts. [C14/C18.]

**“A polite resignation means separation is complete.”** Politeness does not make the boundary trivial, but the page ends before its uptake. #### Counterreadings that materially affect future synthesis

**“Hime cries because she secretly loves Mitsuki romantically.”** The chapter supplies an explicit contrary self-account and direct statement. Tears support distress and personal attachment, not an erased qualification. A future change would require later evidence rather than rereading this refusal as a disguised yes. [C12/C20.]

**“Mitsuki finally obtains the answer, so the communication problem is solved.”** One information problem is solved. Their assumptions about what follows differ immediately afterward. That distinction prevents both dismissing the value of clarity and exaggerating it into a completed repair. [C17/C19.]

**“Kanoko becomes selfless and no longer jealous.”** Her changed goal and recognition of Mitsuki’s importance are significant. The matching-object pain, accusatory behavior and hit prevent a totalized conversion account. A developmental model must retain improvement and continuing risk. [C03/C07/C09/C11.]

**“Kanoko was simply right, so the confrontation was necessary.”** She identifies recipient burden but initially overgeneralizes from ordinary suitors and uses force. Later correction is needed, including correction of her own explanation. The final outcome also defeats a simple confession-ended → Hime-returns mechanism. [C07/C11/C19.]

**“Mai’s response proves the earlier deception caused no harm.”** A better present procedure cannot retrospectively make hidden information available. It supplies a new case of asking and hearing, while Hime’s explicit trust response remains a separate forecast question. [C16/C22; WY5-PR01.]

**“The helper is neutral because she wants the other person happy.”** Sumika’s supplementary self-surprise shows how a helper can presuppose the outcome she expects to console. Her insight is meaningful without proving a fully articulated romantic motive or invalidating all help she offers. [C21.]

**“Hime’s wish not to hurt justifies never answering.”** The manga shows the cost of speaking and the harm already caused by absence and uncertainty. It does not grant a painless alternative or present Hime’s immediate conclusion about the encounter as a universal narratorial rule. [C15/C17/C19.]

**“Returning for the birthday means she has returned to work.”** Hime preserved a birthday exception before leaving. V07 shows that limited return and a proposed later salon appearance, not rescinded ordinary-work cessation. [C01/C24.]
