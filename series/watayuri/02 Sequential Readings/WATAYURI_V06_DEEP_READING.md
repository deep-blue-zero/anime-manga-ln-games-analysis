## 1. The volume’s governing problem

Volume 6 makes a genuine repair and then exposes the limit of what that repair has settled. Hime learns that Mitsuki’s failed kindness was not an absence of affection. Mitsuki learns that admitting jealousy need not cause Hime to reject her, and that accepting Hime’s help can be part of their relationship rather than a defeat of her older-sister role. The birthday letter, the three-person matching ornaments, and the offered hand give that recognition concrete forms. These are not merely preliminary illusions that the ending renders worthless. They answer real injuries from Volume 5. But they do not establish that Hime and Mitsuki understand the same thing by liking, want the same romantic relationship, or have agreed to the same kinds of bodily intimacy. [V06/28/i046–050; V06/29/i053–082.]

The crucial distinction is between **permission to disclose a feeling**, **recognition that the feeling is sincere**, **agreement with the relation it seeks**, and **permission to enact it physically**. Hime asks Mitsuki to speak honestly, accepts the disclosure of jealousy, and refuses to abandon Kanoko as the price of that acceptance. Mitsuki subsequently gives her liking an unmistakably romantic expression by kissing Hime. The page shows no prior request for that specific contact and no affirmative reciprocal act by Hime. Mitsuki understands the event as the end of concealment and the renewal of a first love. The later chapter shows that Hime’s smiling response did not establish the mutual understanding Mitsuki believed she had obtained. [V06/29/i074–088; V06/30/i095–096, i125–126; V06/31/i130–151.]

This is not a simple movement from false performance to liberating truth. The volume explicitly rehabilitates the performed kind self: Ayanokōji is something Mitsuki has actually tried to become, not an empty mask behind which only jealousy is real. At the same time, honesty does not make every act harmless or every desired relation reciprocal. Hime herself presses an ill and frightened Mitsuki hard, including physical pressure during the confrontation, and later conceals her own distress while asking again for the language of liking. The analysis must describe those actions without treating either person’s vulnerability as unlimited permission to determine the other’s response. [V06/28/i041–045; V06/29/i061–079; V06/31/i130–131, i146–151.]

The workplace plot produces a related but distinct reversal. Mai finally acknowledges that the fracture presentation continued after recovery, apologizes, and asks Hime and Kanoko directly to remain. Hime initially agrees. Later, after the renewed interaction with Mitsuki, she invokes the healed injury and restored staffing as reasons to finish her ordinary work that day, while still offering to attend the postponed birthday event. The source strongly connects this change with the unsettled private encounter; it does not give a complete first-person explanation that licenses reducing her decision to a single named emotion. The volume ends with a declaration of intended departure, not with an accepted resignation, a completed final shift, a dissolved sister pact, or an enacted farewell. [V06/31/i133–139, i144–154.]

## 3. Sequential, form-sensitive close reading

### 3.1 Shift 27: visiting the person behind the obligation

#### 3.1.1 An explanation is offered, not a mind made transparent

The present sequence resumes with Sumika’s account of why Mitsuki entered Liebe. The important correction is that wanting to perform kindness can itself be sincere. Hime has treated the contrast between the warm older sister and the difficult private person as evidence that one of them must be false. Sumika offers a developmental account instead: the role is connected to a person Mitsuki wants to become. The explanation makes the private harshness less definitive, but it does not turn every difficult act into successful care. [V06/27/i005–008.]

Sumika also suggests a benevolent explanation for the earlier refusal of Hime’s assistance. That suggestion must remain attributed. V05 supplied more than one relevant condition: Hime was still learning; Mitsuki wanted her able to remain; Mitsuki also felt displaced when another worker taught her successfully. Sumika’s interpretation gives Hime a reason to reconsider without exhausting the motives the reader has seen. Treating the mediation as omniscient would erase precisely the conflict between a helpful aim and a possessive desired position that V05 established. [V06/27/i005–006; inherited WY5-C04/C05/C12.]

Hime does not simply accept the explanation and become serene. At home she returns to the gift refusal and the problem of knowing what another person actually feels. Her uncertainty concerns a particular person, not a philosophical rule that no performed emotion can be real. The birthday letter begins in this unsettled state. Its early appearance is a preparation image, not evidence that its complete later wording was already written at that exact moment. The finished letter becomes available as an object later; the manga does not display every stage of its composition. [V06/27/i007–009; V06/29/i063–072.]

The chapter’s first image has already introduced a different kind of knowledge: a childhood exchange in which Hime denies having a romantic favorite and values being with Yano. It should not be read as the current Hime answering the gift dispute. Its meaning is deliberately deferred until Mitsuki’s longer retrospective in Shift 30. Beginning with a past assurance and moving into current doubt creates an expectation that continuity will matter, without guaranteeing that the two people presently understand that continuity in the same way. [V06/27/i001; V06/30/i115–117.]

#### 3.1.2 Illness changes the practical setting

Mitsuki’s cold interrupts the planned birthday event. The cancellation and postponement have practical consequences for reservations and staff, while the café translates the absence into its school fiction. Mai tells Mitsuki to rest rather than treating her willingness to appear as sufficient reason to work. This is a real illness within the represented events. The existence of a separate fracture deception does not license assuming that every subsequent sickness is staged. [V06/27/i009–011.]

The visit arises through mixed incentives. Mai presents it as something an older sister’s partner should do and as an event that can later enrich the salon’s shared story. Hime worries about leaving work and about being unwelcome. She is persuaded partly by care, partly by role obligation, and partly by the renewed challenge to her conviction that she ought to be liked. Her characteristic self-affirmation is present even while she prepares to perform genuine, unglamorous care. Neither a pure-altruism account nor a merely narcissistic account fits the whole sequence. [V06/27/i012–016.]

Kanoko’s response matters in its restraint. Hime tells her that she is going to visit Mitsuki and asks her to handle work. Kanoko’s concern turns toward Hime possibly catching the cold. Sumika watches the exchange. The scene does not show Kanoko consenting to every development the reader will subsequently witness; she knows about a sick visit, not the later disclosure or kiss. Nor does her cooperation establish that the romantic asymmetry has disappeared. It is an actual instance of facilitating Hime’s activity despite an attachment situation that could make that activity uncomfortable. [V06/27/i016.]

The ordinary apartment also interrupts Hime’s expectations about Mitsuki’s presentation. Her thoughts about the building are comic viewpoint information rather than reliable evidence of family wealth. What becomes concretely relevant is not status but the fact that Mitsuki is ill at home without her parents immediately present, and that Hime can do something useful there. The sickroom removes much of the salon’s public scaffolding while leaving the characters’ learned habits intact. [V06/27/i017–024.]

#### 3.1.3 An apparent rejection acquires a reason

At the door, Mitsuki tells Hime to leave and resists letting her enter. Hime presses to leave supplies and assist. The later explicit concern about infecting Hime changes the interpretation of the initial refusal: it is not adequate evidence that Mitsuki simply does not want her. Yet a protective reason does not mean that no refusal occurred. Hime pushes against an expressed boundary, and Mitsuki’s eventual allowance is part of a negotiation rather than proof that the earlier words meant their opposite all along. [V06/27/i018–021.]

Once inside, Mitsuki tries to perform hospitality despite being the patient. Hime prevents the work from flowing automatically back in that direction. She handles the food and supplies and has Mitsuki rest. This small redistribution prefigures the larger repair: the person who normally insists on being the helper is placed in the position of receiving help. The care is not simulated merely because Hime knows that being helpful can also improve a relationship. Its objects and actions are practical. [V06/27/i021–024.]

Mitsuki’s thanks provide a useful counterexample to the claim that her ordinary speech contains nothing but aggression. Hime begins to recognize that blunt or incomplete explanation can make an act harder to understand without exhausting its motive. This is a local correction, not a reliable decoding key by which every future rebuke can be translated into affection. The earlier pattern of withheld explanation still matters, and Hime’s effort to interpret it accurately requires more than indefinitely charitable guessing. [V06/27/i022–023.]

Their memories of piano, tea, and playing in the closet reopen a relationship whose pleasures were not wholly invented by either child. The conversation nevertheless reactivates Mitsuki’s conviction that she broke that relationship. She apologizes and retreats toward ending the visit. The illness, remorse, and fear of another failed exchange are simultaneous conditions; there is no need to select a single one as the whole explanation. [V06/27/i025–027.]

Hime then states that she has not come only to perform a sick visit. She wants to talk and recover a workable connection. Her refusal to leave before understanding is both a claim for relational participation and pressure on an ill interlocutor. The chapter ends with Mitsuki unable to say how she should interact with Hime. It has opened a private conversation, not completed one. [V06/27/i028–030.]

### 3.2 Shift 28: the feared cost of an answer

#### 3.2.1 The kind self becomes a test Mitsuki thinks she has failed

Mitsuki’s interior account returns to Hime’s accusation that the salon kindness was a lie. She had hoped that acting as a kind older sister would make a different relation possible; the accusation makes that entire route seem discredited. Her reasoning is not simply that she has been caught deceiving someone. She thinks that her best available method of becoming the person Hime could safely like has failed. This distinguishes an aspirational role from a cynical disguise without granting the aspiration immunity from criticism. [V06/28/i031–036.]

Hime asks for an answer from the ordinary person rather than the older-sister role. For Mitsuki, however, that is not an easy subtraction. Removing the familiar form does not reveal a confident, fully articulated self waiting underneath. What becomes available is a person afraid that unfiltered feeling will hurt someone and that every possible response has a socially wrong version. Her hesitation is thus about both what she wants and what will happen if it is known. [V06/28/i033–041.]

Hime’s apology for calling the salon kindness false is substantial. It changes the terms of the conversation: she no longer requires Mitsuki to choose between being an actress and having genuine feeling. She instead asks what feeling is present in the performed conduct and in the refusal of the gift. An apology does not supply the missing answer, but it removes one overgeneralization that made an answer harder to give. [V06/28/i037–040.]

The direct question about liking concentrates several incompatible concerns into one short form. Hime wants assurance that the relationship means something outside paid or staged kindness. Mitsuki hears a demand that may expose the exclusivity and jealousy she has been trying to suppress. The same apparent yes-or-no question therefore has different anticipated consequences for each participant. The manga’s alternating close faces and hesitant balloons sustain that difference rather than making the pause self-evident. [V06/28/i040–044.]

#### 3.2.2 Hime’s demand is consequential and forceful

Hime grows angry at what she receives as continued evasion. She grabs Mitsuki’s clothing and pushes her back while insisting on an answer. These are observable actions, not merely an interpretation of facial intensity. The encounter is taking place while Mitsuki is unwell and has repeatedly tried to stop or retreat from the conversation. Hime’s fear of being excluded from the truth helps explain the pressure; it does not make the pressure disappear. [V06/28/i041–044.]

There is a real argument inside that forceful behavior. Hime points out that refusing to speak for fear of breaking the relation is itself damaging the relation. Silence is not a neutral protective space once it repeatedly leaves the other person unable to understand what she is being asked to continue. But the validity of that insight does not establish that any means of extracting a disclosure is justified. The volume supports analysis of both the communicative impasse and the costs of how Hime tries to break it. [V06/28/i042–045.]

Mitsuki’s subsequent recognition is also specific: her invocation of protecting the relationship has concealed her own fear of being hated. That does not mean every concern about harming Hime was fraudulent. It means that a self-protective motive has become visible within a supposedly other-directed explanation. The characters’ growth depends on differentiating those motives, not replacing “all care” with “all selfishness.” [V06/28/i045–046.]

#### 3.2.3 Jealousy is now declared, not merely decoded

Mitsuki says that she wanted to be Hime’s number one. She identifies Kanoko’s position beside Hime, Hime’s reliance on her, and the three-person matching as sources of distress. The crucial lexical change is the explicit naming of **嫉妬**, jealousy. What earlier volumes supported through reaction, task-retention anxiety, and gift refusal now has the character’s own acknowledgment. The confidence of the bounded current claim can increase without making that confession the explanation of every earlier action. [V06/28/i046–049.]

Hime initially defends Kanoko as a genuine friend, unlike the earlier schoolmates whose attention was tied to her façade. Mitsuki does not answer by denying that fact. She says that she knows, and that the genuineness is precisely why the relationship is threatening. This makes the problem more than misinformation. Better information about Kanoko’s sincerity would not by itself remove a wish to occupy the most important place. The scene therefore prevents a simple solution in which Hime only has to prove that Kanoko is good. [V06/28/i048–049.]

Mitsuki describes her own disclosed heart as ugly and expects rejection. That is her distressed self-evaluation, not the narrator’s final judgment and not a clinical diagnosis. The question is what follows from jealousy: whether it becomes an entitlement, a prohibition, a demand that Hime give someone up, or a feeling that can be named without receiving its desired exclusive outcome. The next chapter tests those possibilities. [V06/28/i049–050; V06/29/i053–056.]

The endpoint of Shift 28 is deliberately narrower than confession-as-cure. The concealed wish has been spoken; Mitsuki still thinks that speaking it proves she cannot become kind. Hime has not yet supplied the answer that will qualify that belief. Preserving that local sequence matters because the later acceptance is an actual response, not an emotion automatically generated by disclosure itself. [V06/28/i050.]

### 3.3 Shift 29: a real repair with an unagreed extension

#### 3.3.1 Hearing jealousy does not mean granting exclusivity

Hime’s response separates several propositions that Mitsuki had bundled together. She does not like the negative remarks about Kanoko. She nevertheless understands the disclosure as evidence that Mitsuki values her. She does not decide that the feeling makes Mitsuki unworthy of affection. And she does not agree to stop valuing Kanoko. This is an acceptance of the person and of the fact of a feeling, not an acceptance of the exclusive relationship the feeling seeks. [V06/29/i053–056.]

That separation is one of the volume’s strongest contributions to the relationship model. Hime does not have to choose between endorsing jealousy’s preferred outcome and denying that jealousy exists. She can acknowledge the discomfort and retain another friendship. Her response is particularly important because she wants Mitsuki’s approval: the source does not portray her as incapable of tolerating any disappointed preference from someone whose liking matters. [V06/29/i054–058.]

This new event resembles, but does not fulfill, the older forecast about an informed response to Kanoko’s exclusive project. The subject and information are different. Hime is responding to **Mitsuki’s** declared jealousy; she has not learned Kanoko’s abolition proposal or the complete earlier strategy. The new evidence strengthens a broader account of Hime’s nonexclusive valuing, while the original forecast remains untested under its actual condition. [V06/29/i055–056, i077–079; WY3-PR02.]

Mitsuki is not instantly reassured. A promise that she will not be hated seems hard to trust when she believes her own speech will predictably injure. Hime’s first appeal is to trust the promise; the more effective move is to make its basis concrete. She trusts Mitsuki’s wish to be kind without pretending that every effort has succeeded. The distinction offers Mitsuki something other than either total vindication or total self-rejection. [V06/29/i057–064.]

#### 3.3.2 The letter answers the one-way model of help

Hime retrieves the birthday letter. Mitsuki briefly reads the movement as possible departure; the return with the letter supplies a correction through action. An object that seemed incidental during Hime’s unsettled preparation now becomes a way to say something longer and more coherent than either character could manage in the escalating exchange. This is not evidence that written speech is intrinsically more truthful. It is a particular prepared form that makes the intended message recoverable. [V06/27/i009; V06/29/i062–066.]

The address to **綾小路お姉さま** is decisive. Hime does not have to strip away the café name to acknowledge the person. She can speak through the role while recognizing that the kindness was performed by Mitsuki herself. The letter’s gratitude is grounded in the actual work they have shared, including learning, busy service, and the expectations of sisterhood. It treats the role as a history of actions and attempts, not only as a stage name. [V06/29/i065–070.]

The letter also explains why earlier assistance was not enough. Hime has wanted to help in return, and refusal has hurt her. Her request that Mitsuki receive her kindness changes the direction of the obligation. The older sister need not remain the exclusive source of competence and care; telling Hime about difficulty can be part of the relationship rather than proof that the role has failed. This directly addresses the participation problem left unresolved in V05. [V06/29/i069–072; inherited WY5-C03/C05/C20.]

The sequence supports **WY5-PR02** under its original test. A real repair attempt explicitly recognizes Hime’s wish to contribute; later gratitude and accepted care confirm that this is more than a formal sentence with no uptake. It would be wrong to score the forecast only from the existence of a birthday gift, and equally wrong to withdraw all evidence of repair because a different conflict follows. The predicate is reciprocal-care acknowledgment, not permanent romantic success. [V06/29/i070–082.]

Hime’s argument about identity is equally precise. The kind Ayanokōji is part of Mitsuki because Mitsuki has actually wanted, practised, and sometimes accomplished those acts. The jealous self is also present. The answer is not to decide which isolated feeling is the only real person. Nor does it follow that every act counts as kind because the actor aspires to kindness. Aspiration, acquired capacity, particular conduct, and another person’s experience must remain distinguishable. [V06/29/i073–076.]

#### 3.3.3 Three matching ornaments and a shared promise

Hime fastens the anklet that previously became a point of refusal. The object’s change in use is concrete, but its relational meaning is not revised into the exclusive matching Mitsuki had wanted. Hime says that Mitsuki and Kanoko are the people before whom she can set aside her façade, and that the three-person matching remains three-person matching even if Mitsuki feels jealous. She gives the ornament within an explicitly retained plurality of important relationships. [V06/29/i077–079.]

The fastening itself should be described without inventing a formal permission exchange that the page does not show. Hime tells Mitsuki to remain still and approaches; Mitsuki reacts with surprise and does not repeat the earlier refusal. The source supports later wearing and acceptance in the scene, not a general right for Hime to handle Mitsuki’s body or property whenever affection is sincere. The distinction is useful because the chapter immediately proceeds through several different kinds of touch, each with its own context. [V06/29/i077–082.]

Their promise concerns telling each other their true feelings and becoming that kind of sisters. Hime’s invitation to speak even about jealousy makes the formerly hidden feeling discussable. It does not promise that every disclosed desire will be fulfilled. The offered hand visually associates present Hime with the person Mitsuki remembers from childhood. Mitsuki takes the opportunity to receive help and pulls Hime into an embrace; Hime is initially surprised, then the scene allows a calmer closeness and explicit thanks. [V06/29/i074–082.]

Mitsuki’s gratitude for being helped or found is a particularly important reversal of the one-way helper position. The later fairy-tale account will show that wanting someone to find her has a longer history. At this point, however, the action already stands on its own: the person who thought she must earn continued contact by being the impeccable helper accepts that Hime has something to give. That is a genuine developmental achievement before the chapter’s final complication. [V06/29/i079–082.]

#### 3.3.4 The kiss exceeds the demonstrated agreement

As Hime prepares to leave, she asks for the direct words of liking that still feel missing. Wanting to be someone’s number one and saying one likes her are not identical expressions for Hime. She seeks an affirmation she can recognize and enjoy, now from the private person rather than only from the salon sister. Mitsuki says that she likes Hime. The fact of sincerity is no longer the principal uncertainty. [V06/29/i083–085.]

Mitsuki then takes Hime’s face and initiates a kiss. The manga makes the contact clear, unlike the more ambiguous hair-and-face proximity that required restraint in V03. Hime’s widened eyes and subsequent close-up establish surprise. The sequence contains no prior request for this particular contact and no affirmative reciprocal act by Hime. These are evidence-bounded descriptions of the depicted encounter; they neither invent an unseen consent conversation nor require a speculative account of Mitsuki’s complete intention. [V06/29/i085–087.]

Mitsuki explains the kiss as her actual, unconcealed liking. From her perspective, this follows the promise to reveal rather than suppress her heart. But a permission to speak about jealousy is not permission for every bodily expression of desire, and a request to hear that one is liked is not a reciprocal romantic declaration. Her sincere understanding of the sequence does not establish Hime’s. The distinction is a central narrative issue, not an externally imported demand that the characters use a particular modern vocabulary. The next chapter gives Mitsuki’s interpretation; the last gives observable evidence that Hime did not share its assurance. [V06/29/i074–088; V06/30/i125–126; V06/31/i130–151.]

It is equally important not to rewrite the preceding repair as entirely false. Hime really did ask to help, Mitsuki really did receive the letter and care, and the anklet no longer occupied the same refused state. The kiss introduces an unagreed extension of the intimacy, not proof that every prior acknowledgment was a trick. A model that keeps only the warm reconciliation will miss the boundary problem; a model that keeps only the boundary problem will miss the real capacities and hopes through which the encounter became possible. [V06/29/i065–088.]

The chapter ends on Mitsuki’s declared refusal to hide, not on a shared statement defining the relationship. Hime’s fuller immediate response is withheld. A reader is invited to feel the release of Mitsuki’s confession while lacking the evidence needed to certify mutual romantic success. That gap is subsequently made explicit by the source itself. [V06/29/i088; V06/30/i125.]

### 3.4 Shift 30: Mitsuki’s romance and the story through which she understands it

#### 3.4.1 The next morning belongs to Mitsuki’s viewpoint

The recovery frame establishes a quieter ordinary environment. Mitsuki checks her temperature, speaks with her father, and explains that it is Saturday and that she is off work. He allows for more rest and notices the food in the refrigerator. His understanding is that a friend has visited and that his daughter is happy about it. The conversation supports ordinary parental concern and partial knowledge, not a complete family model or the proposition that he knows about the confession. [V06/30/i091–095.]

After he leaves, Mitsuki privately distinguishes Hime from a friend: she is the person Mitsuki loves. This is much stronger evidence of the romantic category than inferring it from blushing or the title of the series. It is still a directional self-account. It does not supply Hime’s classification, a mutual agreement, or a sexual-identity label that Mitsuki has not used. The father’s narrower interpretation and Mitsuki’s private correction demonstrate how the same visit can be present in different people’s knowledge in different forms. [V06/30/i094–097.]

The chapter’s delight is real within that perspective. The bed, pillow, remembered face, and repeated small movements give her happiness an everyday setting rather than a ceremonial one. Manga marks and expressions may support an account of buoyancy and absorbed attention; they do not establish an acoustic laugh, pitch, or duration. The greater analytical point is that her happiness does not require public applause. She believes that the private relation has changed. [V06/30/i095–097.]

#### 3.4.2 A first love is reconstructed through a rescue story

The childhood retrospective begins with classmates discussing their crushes and asking Mitsuki about hers. She has little connection to that conversation and does not supply a matching answer. This is evidence of her relation to these peers and this topic at that time. It is not sufficient to diagnose social incapacity, derive a lifelong orientation from disinterest in particular boys, or decide that she could not understand affection before Hime. [V06/30/i098–100.]

The illustrated rescue story supplies another imaginative vocabulary. A confined princess waits for a prince who can help her leave; the story gives Mitsuki a way to picture someone noticing and reaching her. Its significance is not that fiction has instructed her in a complete theory of romance. It offers a form for a need she experiences: to be found rather than remain solitary. Her association of the hoped-for rescuer with a liked person belongs to the retrospective account’s development. [V06/30/i101–102.]

Hime initially seems to occupy the princess position through appearance and popularity. Mitsuki tries to offer help, but the crowd and Hime’s social visibility make the approach difficult. When Hime later comes to the piano, the practical direction reverses. The apparently self-sufficient popular girl is the one who finds Mitsuki. This is why “Mitsuki only wants to rescue Hime” is an incomplete model even before the present letter. Her attachment also contains a wish to be reached and helped herself. [V06/30/i103–107.]

The actual Hime is not identical to the ideal princess. She can be blunt, deceptive, demanding, and interested in having Mitsuki do something for her. Mitsuki nevertheless wants more time together and proposes the duet in that context. The retrospective does not require ideal moral perfection as a precondition for affection. It also does not erase the later harm in their friendship: wanting a person whose imperfections one sees is different from reliably knowing how to sustain the bond. [V06/30/i108–110.]

The closet game makes the role reversal literal. Hime plays the rescuing prince; Mitsuki is the princess. Their negotiation about how to perform the game, Hime’s reluctance to occupy the expected princess role, and her distinction between public cuteness and private feeling make performance part of the attachment’s early language. Hime’s outward princess-like attractiveness does not determine the part she wants to play. The child Mitsuki’s hoped-for rescue is not reducible to a simple gendered allocation in which appearance fixes action. [V06/30/i111–114.]

This material also strengthens the already established temporal constraint: Hime’s deliberate presentation predates the childhood rupture. The new scene again shows her treating cuteness as something maintained so that others will like her. It would be inaccurate to make Mitsuki’s later betrayal the origin of all performance. The rupture changes the stakes and defensive interpretation of the practice, not the bare fact that it already existed. [V06/30/i113–116; inherited WY2-C02.]

#### 3.4.3 Shared enjoyment and romantic meaning do not arrive as the same statement

Mitsuki worries that Hime might someday become absorbed in a romantic favorite and become less available. Hime’s distinction between ordinary liking and romantic liking, followed by the assurance that being with Yano is especially enjoyable, brings relief. The words initially shown in i001 now acquire their surrounding question and Mitsuki’s reaction. Her own statement of liking belongs within that remembered exchange. [V06/30/i110–117.]

The present narration interprets the earlier attachment as love and associates Hime with the prince who came to find her. This is valuable evidence of Mitsuki’s present self-understanding. It should not be flattened into the claim that the childhood pair openly agreed on a romantic relationship. The scene preserves a possible asymmetry from the beginning: the same shared enjoyment can have different categories and imagined futures for the two children. [V06/30/i115–118.]

The retrospective moves through loss, later school fear, reunion, and the attempt to become kind. It does not repeat every earlier causal detail, so it must be read alongside the accepted V02 and V05 accounts rather than replacing them. Its new contribution is how Mitsuki connects those events into the interrupted and revived history of her liking. “A first love resumed” is her organizing interpretation; it is not evidence that Hime has been living the same romance during the intervening years. [V06/30/i119–124.]

The rescue language is especially revealing in relation to her older-sister practice. The person who wanted to make herself indispensable as a helper also understands the present visit as being saved from isolation. That does not make the helping false. It shows that receiving care is not an accidental humiliation contrary to her deepest wishes. The V05 problem was partly that the role she used to secure connection made that other direction difficult to accept. Hime’s letter and offered hand temporarily reconcile those directions. [V06/29/i069–082; V06/30/i118–124.]

#### 3.4.4 A delayed response prevents the reader from sharing her certainty without qualification

Near the end, the chapter re-presents Hime immediately after the kiss. Hime gives a brief acknowledgment, constructs a smile, and says that they will meet again at the salon. It is an actual response, but it is not a reciprocal declaration of romantic liking. Nor is it a direct rejection. Its ambiguity is preserved precisely because Mitsuki receives it through the happiness of having finally acted and spoken without concealment. [V06/30/i125.]

Mitsuki calls the birthday dreamlike and identifies it as her sixteenth. She anticipates continuation rather than waking from it. Those statements provide age and present expectation within the witness. They do not certify that the expectation will be fulfilled. The preceding withheld response and the subsequent shift to Hime’s presentation make the distance between confidence and shared knowledge a formal feature of the volume. [V06/30/i125–126.]

This chapter therefore deepens Mitsuki rather than issuing a verdict on the relationship. The source allows the reader to understand why the visit, the hand, the anklet, and the kiss feel like one continuous liberation to her. It simultaneously withholds the evidence that would make that continuity mutual. A responsible reading must be able to stay with her joy without adopting her inference as the only reality in the scene. [V06/30/i118–126; V06/31/i127–151.]

### 3.5 Shift 31: everything restored except a shared understanding

#### 3.5.1 Sunday is not the preceding Saturday morning

The chapter changes viewpoint and day. Kanoko explicitly refers to Sunday work at i129. Mai later thanks Hime for the visit **the day before yesterday**, at i132. This distinguishes the visit, Mitsuki’s Saturday recovery frame, and the Sunday return. The apparent contradiction that would result from treating all of them as the next morning is resolved by the actual lettering, not by inventing an off-page event. No external calendar is needed to establish this relative order. [V06/30/i093; V06/31/i129, i132.]

Hime’s subdued expression before greeting Kanoko contrasts with the smile she produces in conversation. Kanoko notices that something is wrong, asks about the visit, and receives a denial. The image of Mitsuki associated with the question links the undisclosed encounter to Hime’s current state, but Hime does not yet provide a complete explanation of what she feels or what exactly she intends to do. A claim that she has already named disgust, fear, attraction, or a settled romantic refusal would exceed the evidence. [V06/31/i127–131.]

Kanoko is not simply ignorant in the sense of noticing nothing. She detects the mismatch, asks a relevant question, and is kept outside the explanation. This is an important limit on Hime’s earlier promise to speak openly with the people closest to her. It is not evidence that the friendship was never genuine; it shows that closeness and a history of private candor do not ensure disclosure under every new pressure. [V06/31/i130–132.]

#### 3.5.2 The recovery deception is acknowledged, and Hime initially stays

Mai’s uncast arm is now visible. Hime is sufficiently preoccupied that the change must be pointed out. Mai says that the fracture healed earlier and that she had concealed the ability to return. She says she worked without the cast **yesterday**, because two workers could not manage the salon. The supplement separately recalls cast removal after Hime left for the visit. These are differently worded temporal reports; the mainline statement does not itself date the first removal to the visit day. [i134; S31.5/i157.] Sumika also apologizes and describes the group as having known and stayed silent. This is a new shared disclosure, not just the reader’s V05 knowledge repeated. [V06/31/i133–136.]

The scope of that knowledge must be exact. Hime is explicitly told about recovery and concealment. The scene does not separately narrate to her every detail of Mitsuki’s role in the original late-April retention arrangement. Sumika’s statement that everyone knew is evidence of her acknowledgment of collective complicity; it should not be converted into an independently depicted earlier knowledge moment for every employee. Nene’s precise prior knowledge remains a question of attribution unless a later source supplies it. [V06/31/i134–136; inherited WY5-C06.]

Hime’s actual reply is important counterevidence to the producer’s entering forecast. She does not raise an explicit complaint about having been kept uninformed or about trust. She minimizes the offense, refers to her role in the original injury, and notes that she had thought recovery might be due. When Mai directly requests continued work, Hime says that she had already hoped to remain and agrees. Kanoko follows with her own agreement. The source has supplied the disclosure trigger and an answer; it is not an untested forecast merely because the answer differs from the expected emphasis. [V06/31/i135–138; WY5-PR01.]

That does not establish that the concealment had no cost, that the original arrangement was justified, or that Hime will never think differently about it. Her later decision remains entangled with a private conflict she does not explain here. The exact conclusion is narrower: the first shown response does not meet the predicted explicit trust/choice criterion and lowers confidence in that expectation. The half-chapter actually gives more direct distrust language to **Kanoko**, not Hime; substituting the subject would produce a false success. [V06/31/i135–138; S31.5/i157.]

Mai’s new request also makes a procedural difference independent of Hime’s tone. The manager finally asks directly rather than preserving an obsolete recovery condition. Hime can now state a choice under the acknowledged staffing situation. Her initial yes does not make the earlier omission retroactively informed. Her later change of answer does not mean the initial yes was never spoken. Both belong in the agency and information records. [V06/31/i136–138, i153–154.]

#### 3.5.3 Public recovery can coexist with private retreat

Mitsuki returns with a visibly happy greeting. She is told that Hime and Kanoko will continue and that the cast situation has been explained. The information confirms an important part of what she wanted: Hime will remain at Liebe. It does not tell her that Hime is troubled by the visit. Her response is therefore intelligible without being adequately informed about the private state. [V06/31/i138–139.]

Service resumes successfully. Hime can name and recommend tea within the shared work, and Mitsuki praises her progress and offers further teaching. These scenes preserve actual competence gains rather than rendering every skill from V05 irrelevant once the romance plot advances. At the same time, cheerful service is not a direct measure of private comfort. The public pair remains legible to the guests while the reader sees that Hime’s participation carries a different burden. [V06/31/i140–143.]

Sumika’s teasing about the sick visit incorporates real care into the salon’s entertainment. The guests receive a visit story, not the private confession or kiss. This distinction prevents a common information error: because the scene alludes to the same day the reader witnessed, it does not follow that the characters teasing about it know everything that occurred. The public narrative can be both grounded in an actual visit and radically incomplete about its consequences. [V06/31/i142–143.]

Backstage, Mitsuki thanks Hime and reaches toward her. Hime recoils sharply and collides with the doorway. The action supports a current boundary or alarm response to proximity; it does not reveal its full explanation by itself. The source does not require the analyst to label the movement a definite attempt at a second kiss. What is visible is an approach, a flinch, surprise, and Hime’s rapid attempt to cover the disruption as an accident. [V06/31/i144–145.]

Hime then returns the exchange to the salon. She initiates hand contact and asks, in the public sister register, whether Mitsuki likes her. Mitsuki understands the question as a welcomed opportunity to express what she has already said privately. Hime’s grip and serious gaze, followed by release, make the interaction harder to assimilate to an ordinary romantic encouragement. Yet the exact internal question Hime is testing remains unspoken. The analyst may infer that she is trying to locate or contain the meaning of the liking, but must preserve that as an interpretation rather than an explanatory monologue the source supplies. [V06/31/i146–151.]

This reversal is formally sharp. Earlier, Hime wanted to hear true feeling from the person outside the salon; now she brings an unsettling private feeling back inside the public form. Earlier, Mitsuki feared that the role was fake; now she believes she can express genuine liking through it. They do not simply switch from one shared misunderstanding to a shared clarity. The function of the same register changes differently for each person. [V06/28/i033–040; V06/29/i083–088; V06/31/i146–151.]

#### 3.5.4 The endpoint is an announced departure, not its completion

The staff meeting initially appears to complete a restoration: Nene is back from illness, Mai’s arm is acknowledged as healed, Mitsuki has returned, and the girls have agreed to remain. The postponed birthday event can be scheduled. This is the moment at which the title’s promise of being back to normal becomes most vulnerable. Organizational readiness and actual private willingness are not the same state. [V06/31/i151–153.]

Hime first participates in discussing availability, then explicitly corrects what she said earlier. She offers to attend the birthday event but says that her other work will end that day. She invokes Mai’s recovery and adequate staffing as a suitable stopping point. The phrasing is polite and the posture composed. The effects are not small: the other workers are visibly shocked, and the declaration changes the expected continuity of the group. [V06/31/i153–154.]

Her public reason is not fictitious in the sense of referring to nonexistent conditions. Mai really has recovered; staffing really has improved. The interpretive issue is whether those true conditions fully explain the decision. The sequence from private aftermath, through concealment, recoil, and the repeated liking question, to the correction of her earlier yes strongly supports the relevance of the relationship problem. It does not establish precisely how Hime names that problem to herself. The more adequate reading is a **strongly supported connection with an incompletely disclosed motive**, not either pure administrative completion or a fully explained romantic rejection. [V06/31/i130–154.]

The boundary at i154 must remain firm. Hime has announced an intended work arrangement and departure. Mai has not yet accepted or refused it in the displayed source; Mitsuki has not yet received Hime’s full explanation; Kanoko has not yet made a corresponding employment decision; the birthday event has not occurred. No sister pact is shown dissolved. These are future questions at this freeze, not facts supplied by the volume’s last sentence. [V06/31/i154.]

The birthday letter supplies the missing direction of agency from V05: Hime does not want merely to be a beneficiary. She wants to do something that Mitsuki can recognize and receive. The subsequent problem is not solved by that achievement. Asking to give help, asking for honest words, receiving an embrace, and consenting to a kiss are not interchangeable acts. The source does not depict a single blanket agreement covering them. The reading therefore preserves both Mitsuki’s sincere self-disclosure and Hime’s unestablished agreement to its physical enactment. [AG10–AG14.]

### 9.2 Hime’s pressure must remain visible too

The early confrontation places a cost on Mitsuki’s ability to withdraw. Hime’s frustration with endless evasion is supported; the force she applies is also supported. Describing that pressure does not make the later kiss permissible in return, and describing the kiss does not erase the earlier pressure. An accounting in which one harm cancels another would weaken rather than clarify the source-grounded analysis. [AG03/AG05/AG07/AG13.]

### 9.3 The departure is a live boundary, not yet a settled rupture outcome

Hime can now appeal to the original temporary-work condition because recovery is acknowledged. She also changes her answer after initially accepting continued work. The agency claim is that she announces a different boundary; the source does not yet establish whether it can be carried out, whether she will give a fuller reason, or how the others will respond. No legal judgment about employment, a medical assessment, or a diagnostic explanation is needed to preserve these distinctions. The actual narrative supplies the relevant choices and missing outcomes.

### 11.1 “True feeling” is not a single solved variable

**本音** changes function across the volume. Hime initially asks for the feeling behind role conduct; Mitsuki fears that revealing it will make her unlikable; the repaired promise invites mutual speech; the kiss is described by Mitsuki as an enactment of that same truth. The recurrence is analytically important because it makes the later gap more than a failure to utter a magic word. The participants may agree that truth should be shared while disagreeing about what is being disclosed, what response it asks for, and which acts can follow. [JP02–JP09.]

The contrast between **友だち** and **好きな人** gives the analyst firmer grounds to classify Mitsuki’s attachment than expression alone. It does not automatically solve every instance of **好き** spoken by Hime. The method requires carrying the speaker and context with the word rather than assigning a single romantic meaning to every repetition. [JP08/JP10–JP12.]

### 11.2 Register is relational and situational, not a moral scale

Mitsuki’s ordinary address can accompany infection concern, awkward thanks, fear, jealousy, affection, or practical guidance. Hime’s polished sister language can carry genuine gratitude in the letter and later conceal unresolved discomfort before guests. More formal speech is therefore not necessarily less truthful, and rougher speech is not necessarily the privileged authentic self. What matters is the particular relation among wording, action, audience, and the knowledge available to the recipient. [JP01/JP04/JP12.]

The final employment statement is similarly consequential despite its courtesy. Hime does not need to abandon her practiced presentation for the announced boundary to be real. But its composed form also limits what the other workers can infer about the underlying private problem. Public legibility of a decision is not complete legibility of its cause. [JP14; O24.]

### 12.1 Repeated hands do not mean identical consent

The volume uses hands for different functions: pressure during confrontation, uncovering a face, fastening an ornament, inviting help, embracing, holding a face, recoiling from an approach, and testing contact in public. Those configurations should not be reduced to a single symbol of intimacy. Initiator, timing, spoken context, and the recipient’s reaction change. A literary account that notices only recurrence while ignoring those changes will misrepresent the relationship’s boundaries. [VIS04/VIS08/VIS10–VIS12/VIS18/VIS19.]

The most important contrast is between the offered hand that Mitsuki accepts and the later kiss that Hime has not specifically invited. Both can be connected to Mitsuki’s desire not to hide; only the source-specific interaction establishes what the other participant has agreed to. The formal continuity of a motif is not continuity of permission. [i079–088.]

### 12.2 A withheld response is part of the evidence architecture

Ending Shift 29 with Mitsuki’s face rather than a reciprocal declaration creates a gap. Shift 30 fills part of it with Hime’s polite response but embeds that response in Mitsuki’s hopeful recollection. Shift 31 then supplies Hime’s changed presentation, refusal to explain, recoil, and departure plan. The text does not suddenly make Mitsuki’s feelings unreal; it supplies evidence that her reading of Hime was incomplete. This is a difference between revising an inference and negating an observation. [VIS13/VIS16/VIS18/VIS19.]

### 12.3 Objects keep their practical identity

The letter is a message that can be prepared and read; the anklet is the previously refused wearable gift; the refrigerator contains supplies from an actual visit; the cast presents a factual bodily condition; the birthday schedule requires staffing decisions. Their symbolic importance grows from their practical uses. The evidence record should not turn the three-person design into three identical accessories, the cast into proof of original fraud, or the birthday exception into a completed event.
