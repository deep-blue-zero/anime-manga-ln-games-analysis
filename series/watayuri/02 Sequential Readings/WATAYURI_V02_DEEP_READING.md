## 1. The volume’s governing problem

Volume 2 does not resolve Volume 1 by revealing that the supposedly hostile person was simply kind all along. It shows two people who wanted a connection, injured each other while interpreting that connection differently, and subsequently mistook the injuries for evidence that the connection had never existed. Hime’s protective withdrawal from the childhood piano partnership looked to Mitsuki like abandonment. Mitsuki’s disclosure of Hime’s private words genuinely exposed her to rejection. The present reunion initially repeats their incompatible explanations: Hime sees the person who hated and betrayed her; Mitsuki sees the person whose affectionate language cannot be reconciled with leaving. Neither account is an adequate description of everything the volume depicts. [V02/07/i017–025, i034–044; V02/09/i071–084.]

The decisive problem is therefore not just **whether a statement is sincere**. It is also **who gets to decide what another person needs, which costs they may accept, and whether a relationship must be abandoned to protect its participants**. Hime’s attempt to make Mitsuki safe from public blame repeats the unilateral logic that helped destroy their first partnership. Mitsuki’s strongest corrective is not a demand that Hime become incapable of lying. It is an objection to Hime making that relational decision alone: if both want to continue, why must either leave? The sister role becomes viable when it can house an acknowledged mutual choice, rather than merely cover a conflict with proficient acting. [V02/10/i118–132.]

That repair is real but bounded. An apology occurs; the explanation of the childhood injury changes; the sisters continue; and the next chapter shows more comfortable cooperation. Yet Hime’s language of restored friendship does not appear to exhaust what Mitsuki hoped for. The same word for liking can now be accepted without its relational category being mutually settled. Meanwhile Kanoko’s understanding of Hime’s attachment remains incomplete, and Sumika’s discovery of the photographs on Kanoko’s phone opens another consequential asymmetry. The volume moves from an unresolved identity to an acknowledged bond, then from that bond to the question of **what kind of attachment each participant believes has been acknowledged**. [V02/10/i129–134; V02/11/i149–156.]

### 2.5 Necessary V01 synopsis and entering developmental state

At the V01 ending, Hime and Mitsuki occupy Café Liebe’s publicly recognized sister relationship, but its private meaning is unresolved. Hime’s familiar success at appearing adorable has not made her immediately competent at the café’s shared fiction or practical service. She has, however, improved order-taking by requesting the information she needs. ## 3. Sequential, form-sensitive close reading

### 3.1 Shift 07: the childhood partnership and the injury inside protection

#### WY2-O01 — Hime was already performing before the betrayal

The opening restores Yano’s name to a childhood setting and then supplies an extended account of the fifth-grade transfer. Hime’s popular, carefully managed presentation is already operating before she becomes Mitsuki’s piano partner. This temporal fact matters. The later exposure can intensify her fear of rejection without having invented her pleasure in admiration, strategic social presentation, or attraction to a comfortable life. A reconstruction that makes every instance of Hime’s performance a symptom originating in Mitsuki’s betrayal reverses the order shown here. [V02/07/i001, i005–011.]

The class also interprets Mitsuki before the reader has a complete basis for judging her. Her request that others stop obstructing the movement of desks has a practical object. Her manner is abrupt; classmates describe her correction and seriousness as irritating. Their irritation is real evidence about her social position, but it is not a neutral verdict that the underlying request is unreasonable. Hime must navigate a group that has already made an unpopular child’s precision into a social fault. [V02/07/i007–010.]

The early piano question is consequently not a simple opportunity to display a talent. Peers’ interest in Hime’s ability is connected to the existing accompanist. Hime denies being able to play, avoiding a comparison that might antagonize Mitsuki. In doing so, she preserves one impression by making another part of herself unavailable. The lie is strategic and unnecessary in a broader sense, but locally intelligible: she is managing anticipated relationships, not merely concealing an embarrassing inability. [V02/07/i009–011, i016–018.]

The color spread places the current café selves, their childhood selves, and piano imagery in one composition. It instructs the reader to hold temporal layers together before the prose has explained their connection. It does not show all four figures occupying one diegetic scene. The subsequent childhood narration should likewise be read as the represented past, substantially framed through Hime’s recollection and evaluations, not as unrestricted access to both children’s minds. [V02/07/i002–003.]

#### WY2-O02 — A duet creates an alternative to replacement

Hime’s encounter with Mitsuki at the piano allows a different relationship from the one implied by the classmates’ comparison. Her admiration for the playing is supported by the attentive visual sequence and her response, rather than simply by the fact that she offers a compliment. Mitsuki, meanwhile, responds differently when Hime’s own ability becomes visible. Her initially corrective manner does not exhaust her capacity for interest or pleasure. [V02/07/i012–016.]

Hime admits that her classroom denial was a lie and explains its interpersonal purpose. Mitsuki had also been reading the classroom situation as a possible attempt to replace her. Once they compare those assumptions, the problem can be reorganized: playing need not mean taking somebody else’s place. Mitsuki proposes a two-person accompaniment. Hime can participate without publicly turning herself into a rival, and Mitsuki can retain a valued activity while sharing it. The teacher’s acceptance gives that possibility an actual institutional form. [V02/07/i017–021.]

This is the volume’s first successful repair of a false either/or. The solution is not that one girl generously abandons what she wants. It is a structure in which both can participate. That is a strong basis for reading the later sister-role crisis alongside the duet, but not for treating the two settings as identical. The childhood arrangement is built around a concrete shared activity and recognized musical competence. The café arrangement initially arose through a much more pressured public sequence. [V02/07/i019–021; predecessor V01/02/i043–059.]

The passage also supplies low-stakes differentiation. Mitsuki learns the part quickly and treats practice seriously; Hime needs more work and wants room for conversation. Hime is not uninterested in piano merely because she dislikes relentless practice. Mitsuki is not uninterested in Hime merely because she often channels their time into a task. Their preferred balance of activity, companionship, and discipline is not yet openly negotiated. [V02/07/i022–029.]

#### WY2-O03 — Wanted exclusivity becomes an instruction about other people

When classmates invite Hime to the pool, Mitsuki invokes their practice commitment and then tells Hime not to be close to those people. That is a consequential association restriction, not just a neutral scheduling reminder. The scene connects her concern to how happy Hime appears with them. Hime’s explanation that she is acting for approval reassures Mitsuki in one respect, but Mitsuki also objects to lying. She wants reliable assurance of a special connection while distrusting the means through which Hime distinguishes public amiability from private preference. [V02/07/i023–025.]

There are several compatible pressures here: preparation for a shared performance, concern about classmates who have treated Mitsuki poorly, fear of being displaced, and an expectation of privileged access. The evidence does not require choosing only one. It does require preserving the difference between a desire for closeness and a right to regulate Hime’s friendships. Sympathy for Mitsuki’s isolation cannot make the instruction harmless. Nor does the instruction prove that all her investment is controlling. [V02/07/i024–025, i033–037.]

The home visit makes another difference visible. Hime admires the house and grand piano, enjoys the prospect of leisure in comfortable surroundings, and is disappointed when Mitsuki remains focused on practice. Her existing material aspirations are therefore not absent from this friendship. Yet interest in the house does not invalidate interest in its occupant. Treating an instrumental pleasure as proof that every affectionate response is counterfeit would repeat the all-or-nothing interpretation the volume is testing. [V02/07/i026–028; separately V02/S11.5/i159–161.]

A later ensemble mistake shows why Mitsuki’s technical concern cannot simply be discarded as a pretext. There is actual music to prepare, and a teacher stops the performance. The social problem is not that technical standards are imaginary; it is that the way those standards and visible asymmetries are interpreted becomes evidence for a story about coercion. [V02/07/i029–030.]

#### WY2-O04 — A protective lie solves the wrong person’s version of the problem

The teacher asks whether Hime is being forced or bullied into accompaniment. Hime explicitly says she wants to participate. This is a crucial evidentiary anchor: the source does not leave her preference entirely to an analyst’s inference. The classmates’ rescue interpretation is not the same as her stated wish, even though Mitsuki’s pressure and restrictive conduct give them something real to react to. [V02/07/i030–032.]

Hime accepts a renewed invitation to the pool, then returns to Mitsuki instead and telephones the classmates with a false family pretext. In private she explains that she did not intend to choose them over their time together. Mitsuki’s tears and relief, followed by a period of conversation without enforced practice, show that the activity is carrying attachment beyond technical preparation. The two are capable of enjoying each other when the immediate threat of displacement recedes. [V02/07/i033–037.]

The problem changes when Hime separately approaches the teacher and withdraws from the accompaniment. Mitsuki learns of that withdrawal from the teacher, not through a mutual decision with Hime. Hime’s narration explains that she was trying to prevent Mitsuki from becoming the villain in the classmates’ account. But her explanation to the classmates—that she could not keep up—does not communicate that protective purpose to the person whose relationship is being altered. Mitsuki hears a departure following a particularly reassuring private encounter. [V02/07/i037–041.]

Hime’s intended meaning and Mitsuki’s available evidence now diverge sharply. Hime thinks she is paying a cost for Mitsuki. Mitsuki sees Hime leaving and telling classmates what appears to be an incompatible story. Hime is protecting Mitsuki’s reputation through an act that removes Mitsuki’s opportunity to decide whether she would rather risk that reputation than lose the partnership. That is the agency problem returned to explicitly in Shift 10. [V02/07/i038–042; V02/10/i126–129.]

This does not make Hime’s protective motivation fictional or her injury self-inflicted. It makes the attempted protection relationally incomplete. Her skill at choosing statements for different audiences is not matched by a habit of telling the other participant which decision she is making on their behalf. The scene’s tragedy depends on the coexistence of genuine concern and a communicative practice that hides that concern from the person who most needs to understand it. [V02/07/i035–042.]

#### WY2-O05 — Explanation qualifies betrayal without erasing exposure

Mitsuki publicly contradicts Hime’s profession of affection for the classmates and repeats the private distinction Hime had drawn between them and their own time together. The disclosure is not invented by Hime’s later grievance. It happens on the page and exposes the discrepancy Hime had depended on keeping audience-specific. Her subsequent exclusion is narrated as lasting until graduation; the concert succeeds with Mitsuki playing alone, and Mitsuki transfers away before long. The exact reason for that transfer and a precise calendar are not supplied. [V02/07/i041–044.]

The new context qualifies a simple account of gratuitous malice: the disclosure follows a perceived abandonment and a collapse in trust. It does not establish that the disclosure was justified, accidental, or harmless. Mitsuki’s hurt and Hime’s resulting social injury are distinct facts. An analyst should not use one to cancel the other or turn reciprocal misunderstanding into a claim of equal responsibility for every action. [V02/07/i038–044; V02/09/i081–084.]

The final separation of their faces turns a shared history into an apparent opposition. It preserves the felt force of hatred at this point in the narration, but it does not give the reader a complete independent account of both girls’ enduring feelings. That fuller asymmetry remains to be exposed through Mitsuki’s viewpoint in Shift 09. [V02/07/i044; V02/09/i071–075.]

### 3.2 Shift 08: a private identity makes the public role uninhabitable

#### WY2-O06 — Recognition removes one explanation but does not remove the wish

The present resumes where V01 stopped. Hime now knows the person before her is Yano. Mitsuki’s remark about having been foolish to expect anything registers disappointment without yet explaining what she expected. Hime interprets Mitsuki’s earlier silence through her own history of betrayal: she imagines the other girl watching her without disclosure. That is Hime’s interpretation of withheld identity, not independent evidence of a revenge plan. [V02/08/i048–050.]

The train conversation gives Kanoko a reasonable initial answer: if this is the person who hurt Hime, there is no reason to keep pursuing her approval. The fact that the answer does not satisfy Hime matters. A purely numerical program of being liked by everyone should be able to treat one justified exception as relief. Hime instead remains caught by the particular person who had appeared kind and who now shares the identity of the childhood friend. The individual attachment has become harder to dismiss than her general account of popularity allows. [V02/08/i054–056.]

Her present responses are still shaped by anger and expectation. She wants to know whether Mitsuki has told Sumika about her; Mitsuki’s refusal to let the conversation proceed, and her claim to feel nothing, are heard against Hime’s expectation of regret or apology. Yet Sumika has not actually received the complete history. The scene separates a character’s certainty that others must already know from the information shown to have circulated. [V02/08/i057–060.]

#### WY2-O07 — Playing a quarrel is different from being unable to play anything else

Mai asks for professional performance, not proof of private friendship. The distinction is coherent as a workplace requirement, but it does not by itself make the required behavior available. Hime can still speak to guests; the difficulty is specifically sister interaction with the person she now sees as Yano. A routine tray correction no longer arrives merely as useful instruction. It activates the childhood relationship and the old experience of being judged or disliked. [V02/08/i061–065.]

Sumika initially frames the visible tension as a dramatic disturbance within the café fiction. Guests can enjoy a quarrel when it remains intelligible as a stage of the sisters’ relationship. Hime’s assertion that her elder sister feels nothing for her strains that framing, because the statement carries an immediate private grievance that neither actor can smoothly contain. The café’s fictional language has not stopped referring to real people just because it supplies a plot-shaped explanation. [V02/08/i062–068.]

Sumika’s public suggestion that Mitsuki likes Hime is an attempted intervention, not privileged narration. Mitsuki’s forceful denial is a visible failure of that intervention. The denial is consequential precisely because it has several audiences: guests see a disturbance; management sees a performance problem; Hime hears confirmation of personal hatred. Later pages complicate the last interpretation, but the public affront and its effect do not become unreal. [V02/08/i067–070; V02/09/i071–075.]

This scene is important counterevidence against making Sumika an omniscient or uniformly successful mediator. She notices a pattern and acts on it; that action can pressure the person she is trying to move and worsen the immediate situation. The capacity to read more than Hime reads is not the capacity to know every private history or predict every response. [V02/08/i057–059, i068–070.]

### 3.3 Shift 09: a second viewpoint and an incomplete rehearsal of repair

#### WY2-O08 — Mitsuki recognized Hime early and hoped for a chosen reunion

The opening of Shift 09 changes focalization. Mitsuki remembers the girl she cannot forget and entertains the possibility that she herself was the cruel betrayer. The thought is self-questioning, not a definitive omniscient adjudication. It nevertheless supplies something the V01 record did not have: direct access to an unsettled interpretation on Mitsuki’s side. [V02/09/i071.]

The next pages replay the first café encounter from her perspective. They are not a second introduction in present time. Mitsuki recognizes Hime, confirms the surname through Mai, and hopes that Hime has come specifically to see her. Hime’s evident ignorance of the café concept and failure to recognize her then defeat that hope. A scene that Hime originally experienced as meeting an impressive stranger was also, for the other participant, a hoped-for reunion. [V02/09/i072–075.]

This is a major retrospective correction to the current explanation, but not an error in V01’s freeze. The prior volume had left the onset of recognition and the reason for Mitsuki’s investment open. V02 now places recognition early and supplies a positive expectation before the disappointment. The result weighs strongly against treating her entire first-volume conduct as detached role obligation or a straightforward campaign of retaliation. It does not excuse her rebukes or prove that every subsequent act was motivated only by affection. [V02/09/i073–076; WY1-C05/C11.]

Her conversation with Sumika is revealing in another way. She actively pursues an opportunity to insist that she did not want the sister relationship, while listing Hime’s failures and the uncertainty of her seriousness. Sumika notices the excessive effort to state indifference. But she also warns that the surrounding people’s tolerance has limits. The scene connects legible emotional investment to real responsibilities; understanding why Mitsuki is upset does not mean the workplace must absorb any resulting behavior indefinitely. [V02/09/i076–079.]

#### WY2-O09 — The private confrontation reveals two different betrayals

Mai’s stated consequence is dissolution of the sister pledge, represented within the setting as a breakup. It is **not a threat to fire either worker**. Mitsuki objects to the disgrace and insists on doing the work properly; Hime questions why a person who dislikes her should continue. They are still arguing across different meanings of the same role. [V02/09/i078–080.]

In the requested private exchange, Mitsuki names Hime’s withdrawal from piano as the betrayal. Hime names the exposure of her private words and insists she would not have betrayed Mitsuki. Those are not two rival recollections of one simple action. They identify different actions as the decisive injury. Until that difference becomes available, each hears the other’s accusation as an incomprehensible denial of what happened. [V02/09/i081–084.]

Hime’s tearful question about why Mitsuki came to hate her is therefore an interpretive turning point even before an answer is completed. It exposes the premise organizing her behavior. Mitsuki’s startled reaction does not alone establish a complete alternative feeling, but the reader now has her earlier reunion hope with which to read that reaction. The scene tests a particular belief, not merely the general proposition that Hime lies. [V02/09/i073–075, i083–084.]

A customer’s interruption prevents a full verbal settlement. Mitsuki puts Hime out of the returning guest’s immediate view, handles the forgotten item and greeting, and observes Hime’s automatic ability to perform a pleasant response despite the tears. She then says Hime can do the role and promises support. There is a real assistance event and a commitment to continue, but not yet a completed interpersonal apology or mutually agreed account of the past. [V02/09/i085–087.]

This is a strong but bounded test of WY1-PR05. Workplace responsibility creates an opportunity for actual support during unresolved private conflict. It does not show that the role is the sole cause of the support: ordinary consideration and Mitsuki’s personal investment remain available explanations. What it defeats is the assumption that assistance must wait until the entire emotional dispute is settled. [V02/09/i085–087.]

#### WY2-O10 — Rehearsal succeeds by using a wish it cannot fully explain

The rehearsal does not improve merely because Hime memorizes more elegant wording. The initial performance is stiff. Sumika asks the actors to recover how they first saw each other and how they wanted to be seen. Hime recalls the kind elder sister and the desire for her approval. The role becomes easier when it can use an actual relational wish instead of treating acting as manufacture from nothing. [V02/09/i088–093.]

Kanoko reads the result as a restoration of Hime’s familiar talent and continues to place fault elsewhere. Hime’s interior account is more specific: she recognizes that she really wanted that elder sister to like her. This does not mean her whole previous public persona was secretly transparent. It means a particular performed desire is not adequately described as a lie. The friend who knows Hime acts does not necessarily know which part of a new performance has become personally important. [V02/09/i094–096.]

The chapter then changes the audience. Online accounts have already made the pair into a story in which Mitsuki is the villain and Hime is forced into sisterhood. A successful rehearsal in an empty workplace cannot by itself rewrite those accounts. The sequence distinguishes **performative competence**, **private understanding**, and **public reputation** as three different problems. Improvement on the first does not automatically close either of the other two. [V02/09/i097–098; V02/10/i101–112.]

### 3.4 Shift 10: the protective withdrawal is repeated, recognized, and interrupted

#### WY2-O11 — The audience can interpret the correction as more coercion

Management explains that the rumor did not begin only with the latest outburst. The rapid creation of the pair had already invited speculation; the visible quarrel gave that speculation further support. Sumika asks Hime to help restore the public relation and worries about the effect of hostile commentary on Mitsuki’s already rigid concern with doing the work correctly. This is a situated assessment of Mitsuki, not proof that she could never tolerate criticism. [V02/10/i101–103.]

Hime responds with a technically workable scene. She deliberately gives her elder sister a reason to correct her tray handling, then makes being taught into a sign of pleasure in their bond. The guests enjoy it. At this point a gesture resembling a service mistake can perform successful role labor, while the physical demands of service remain real. The scene cannot be adequately evaluated by asking only whether Hime holds the tray correctly. [V02/10/i104–106.]

The plan falters when Mitsuki becomes unexpectedly serious: she notices Hime’s effort, calls her a genuinely good girl, and questions why Hime is her younger sister. Hime cannot easily maintain the prepared exchange when the other actor changes its emotional terms. A guest’s attempt to reassure Hime that she need not act against her wishes makes matters worse. Hime’s insistence on doing what her elder sister wants is intended as loyalty, but an observer already worried about compulsion can read it as confirmation. [V02/10/i107–111.]

The volume is not saying that the guests are malicious for noticing discomfort. Nor does it say that all concern about power in this pair is imaginary: the V01 formation did involve pressure, although not in the simple direction the rumor assumes. The problem is that an incomplete public account has become sufficiently fixed that contrary behavior can be absorbed into it. Hime’s denial does not automatically become credible just because it is true about her present desire. [V02/10/i109–112; predecessor WY1-C03.]

The return of childhood classroom imagery marks Hime’s recognition of the pattern. People who imagine themselves defending her can make Mitsuki the aggressor, and Hime’s efforts to perform the expected response can perpetuate the error. The visual recurrence is tied to a specific social mechanism, not merely to a generalized flash of distress. [V02/10/i112, i118–123.]

#### WY2-O12 — Caring for Yano survives the belief that Yano hates her

Kanoko sees Hime’s distress and validates her ability. This is genuine support, but it does not answer Hime’s reason for being distressed. Hime is not simply upset that she performed badly. She cannot bear the recurrence of a story that makes Mitsuki the villain, even while she still believes Mitsuki dislikes her. When Kanoko asks why she cares about the person who betrayed her, Hime reconstructs that person as a serious, awkward, isolated child. The adverse category has acquired enough particularity to support concern. [V02/10/i113–120.]

That concern is not proof that every grievance has vanished. Hime continues to interpret Mitsuki’s distrust as hatred. It is precisely the coexistence of hurt and protection that defeats an account in which she helps only people who already provide gratifying approval. Her desire to protect this particular person has become costly and resistant to Kanoko’s more straightforward advice. [V02/10/i118–122.]

Mitsuki, meanwhile, has learned the actual content of the rumor from a customer’s apologetic explanation. The customer says he intended the two to get along; this is evidence of his stated intention, not proof that his intervention was helpful. Another customer urges him to own his earlier remark. There is no basis in this exchange for saying that Sumika scripted the customer’s intervention. [V02/10/i114–117.]

The doorway now reverses the earlier volume’s overhearing structure. In V01, a disavowal meant for Kanoko became Mitsuki’s evidence that Hime’s wish was false. Here, Hime’s effort to explain her concern for Yano to Kanoko becomes available to Mitsuki. Accidental audience overlap can reveal a sincere investment as well as conceal it. But hearing that investment does not instantly make it intelligible: Mitsuki asks why Hime would say such kind things while also calling her a liar and betrayer. [V02/10/i120–122; predecessor V01/05/i130–132.]

#### WY2-O13 — Saying “I want this” while relinquishing its symbol

Hime’s public declaration is not a polished reprise of the rehearsal. She states that she wants to be a sister and refuses to let continuing that relationship make the elder sister an object of blame. The exposed seriousness of the intervention alarms the staff and interrupts the guests’ ordinary participation. The action has a real reputational cost: Hime cannot ensure that this unscripted disturbance will preserve her universally agreeable image. [V02/10/i123–125.]

Yet the next action shows that her solution still repeats the childhood pattern. She removes her cross and offers it up. This is not a simple reaffirmation of the pledge by displaying its emblem; it is an attempted relinquishment. Mitsuki recognizes the contradiction: Hime says she wants to continue, then enacts separation to protect her. The object makes a familiar communicative failure immediately visible. [V02/10/i126.]

Hime’s explanation—that she lies because she likes the other person—should not be made into a universal ethical endorsement of deception. In context it explains a specific protective logic: a professed or enacted departure can conceal a wish to remain because she imagines departure will spare the other person harm. It also asks Mitsuki to reread the childhood reassurance and subsequent withdrawal together. The recalled piano-room image makes that request formal, not just verbal. [V02/10/i127–128.]

The scene therefore stops short of the easy resolution “Hime finally tells the truth, so everything is solved.” She states her desire but still proposes a solution that defeats it. Her sincerity is now more visible; her decision remains insufficiently shared. The breakthrough requires the other participant to reject the premise that this sacrifice is necessary. [V02/10/i124–129.]

#### WY2-O14 — Mitsuki refuses to have her preferred cost decided for her

Mitsuki’s answer makes the relational correction explicit. She asks why Hime gets to decide how other people will regard her and why she must quit if both want to keep doing this. That rebuke is not a general indifference to reputation—her earlier concern with disgrace and job failure argues against that. It is a preference within a concrete conflict: losing the relationship is not the price she wants Hime to pay on her behalf. [V02/09/i079; V02/10/i129.]

The two-person alternative returns. In childhood, a duet allowed Hime’s participation without Mitsuki’s replacement. In the present, openly acknowledging that both want the sister relation makes Hime’s protective exit unnecessary. The analogy is strong because the shared past is explicitly recalled, but the new event is not merely a replay: the first partnership failed to survive an audience’s interpretation, whereas the present confrontation makes that interpretation discussable between the partners. [V02/07/i019–021; V02/10/i126–131.]

Mitsuki states that she likes Hime and tells her to continue as her younger sister. The formulation combines direct personal assertion, blunt correction, and the vocabulary of the role. It is neither detached professional elegance nor a completely separate private confession outside the café. A fictional relation supplies a language in which the actual participants can say something consequential. That does not mean every observer understands the same thing. [V02/10/i129–130.]

Hime’s interior recognition is equally specific: she could have chosen the option she wanted. It is a release from the assumed necessity of deceptive self-removal, not a pledge that she will never manage an impression again. The later performance makes that limitation clear. What changes is the available decision, not the sudden disappearance of all her established habits. [V02/10/i131–132; V02/11/i144–150.]

#### WY2-O15 — An apology is accepted, but “liking” remains incompletely shared

Mitsuki connects the present act to what Hime may have been thinking in the past, apologizes for causing pain, and embraces her. The familiar childhood address accompanies the apology. This is substantially more than a successful public scene: a represented reinterpretation and a direct acknowledgment of harm occur within it. Hime’s subsequent statement that they are friends again supplies evidence of acceptance. The reading should neither downgrade all of this to acting nor overstate it as proof of permanent, comprehensive repair. [V02/10/i131–134.]

The private aftermath preserves a new asymmetry. Hime understands the change as the restoration of their former friendship and points out that Mitsuki should have said earlier that she liked her. Mitsuki’s disappointment at that formulation, her account of hoping Hime felt the same way, and the marked embarrassment surrounding her denial make a more particular or romantic investment strongly plausible. The evidence is not just an isolated blush. It is a sequence in which a declared friendship seems inadequate to the other person’s expectation. [V02/10/i133–134.]

The claim should still be formulated carefully. The volume has established personal investment and an affirmative statement of liking. It has strongly cued a mismatch in the kind of attachment meant. It has not established a mutually named romantic relationship, reciprocal romantic desire, or a comprehensive sexual identity for either character. Hime can sincerely value Mitsuki while classifying that value differently. Mitsuki can be serious about liking her while retreating from an embarrassing interpretation of what she has just said. [V02/10/i130–134; V02/11/i149–151.]

The public result is similarly bounded. Staff interpret the reconciliation as likely to improve the rumors, and the next chapter supplies visible signs of renewed guest interest. Neither establishes that every online report has been corrected or that no future customer will repeat the coercion story. Local recovery is evidenced; universal reputational control is not. [V02/10/i132–133; V02/11/i144–148.]

### 3.5 Shift 11: improved work, a new public reward, and a private archive

#### WY2-O16 — The institution now offers a prize whose meanings are not identical

The Blumen event introduces another relation between fiction and business. In the school setting, the selected person symbolizes the school’s flower, and the sister shares a related distinction. The explanation distinguishes that position from an office defined by practical duties or authority. In the café, the visitors’ participation is directed toward the actual staff rather than the larger imagined student body. These are the rules of this fictional institution as explained in V02, not claims about a real school election. [V02/11/i137–142.]

Mai emphasizes the setting’s internal significance; Sumika offers the practical explanation that paying customers will choose among the five familiar people. Their contrast is not simply sincerity versus cynicism. Mai is maintaining the immersive structure that makes the event meaningful, while Sumika translates that structure into an operation the novice can understand. The café needs both comprehensible work and a fiction that is not constantly dissolved into its cash transactions. [V02/11/i139–143.]

Hime’s difficulty remains recognizable. She has not absorbed the supplied written material and becomes overwhelmed by additional terminology. The discussion ends with a plan to read and ask about unclear points, not with demonstrated mastery of the event. Her progress in one interpersonal crisis has not made her automatically fluent in every institutional rule. The opening stamp-making correction similarly preserves ordinary differences in task precision after reconciliation. [V02/11/i137, i139–143.]

This material is relevant to WY1-PR04 but not enough to mark that practical prediction confirmed. A request for explanation receives cooperation, yet the volume does not show a sufficiently comparable completed event task establishing the predicted advantage over façade-only concealment. A willingness to ask and a successful practical outcome are separate stages. [V02/11/i139–143.]

#### WY2-O17 — Public support is recognized, and affection makes the role less stable in a different way

The consecutive ornamental presentations of Mitsuki and Hime show both workers now able to inhabit the attractive public scene. Dense service panels then reveal a busy workplace beneath that presentation. Hime is not merely enjoying an intimate tableau; she is carrying out repeated tasks amid a larger flow of visitors. Her question about the crowd allows Sumika to interpret the renewed interest as a response to the sisters’ reconciliation. Hime begins to see the guests as people supporting a bond, not only as people whose gaze threatens it. [V02/11/i144–148.]

That is a meaningful change in her audience model. It does not establish that all guests have benevolent motives or complete knowledge. It shows that Hime can understand visibility as a source of encouragement as well as a source of judgment. The same public that amplified a damaging interpretation can also sustain a more welcome one. [V02/11/i147–149.]

Hime then offers further help and openly says she is happy because her elder sister finally told her she liked her. Mitsuki’s embarrassment and close, lowered-scale response again destabilize an easy division between performed and private affection. Earlier Hime feared the role could not contain hatred; now Mitsuki has difficulty containing the implications of an apparently affectionate line. Successful reconciliation has changed the problem rather than eliminated affective leakage. [V02/11/i149–151.]

Kanoko and Sumika are shown witnessing this interaction, but their visible attention should not be converted into complete interior accounts. Sumika’s subsequent conversation establishes her curiosity about the pair and her impression that the earlier reconciliation was unusually genuine. Kanoko’s precise evaluation of the restored closeness is not verbally supplied in these final pages. The withheld interpretation is important to the endpoint and to the treatment of the inherited Kanoko prediction. [V02/11/i151–154.]

#### WY2-O18 — Holding a phone is not permission to inspect its contents

Mai proposes that Sumika consider a sister relation of her own, mentioning possibilities involving Mitsuki or Kanoko. The exchange creates prospective configurations, not completed pairings. Sumika’s concern about how little Kanoko speaks to her establishes limited interpersonal access. Familiarity with Hime does not transfer automatically to easy engagement with another coworker. [V02/11/i153–154.]

A call on Kanoko’s misplaced phone supplies a narrow, legitimate reason to handle the device. Hime asks that it be kept until the next day. Sumika then notices that it is not passcode-protected and jokes about finding a weakness, while Mai tells her not to do that. The ensuing access to the photographs is therefore an identifiable boundary crossing, not a neutral consequence of recovering lost property. Lack of a technical barrier does not depict the owner granting consent. [V02/11/i154–156.]

The final collection of Hime photographs gives Sumika evidence of exceptionally concentrated attention. It does not yet explain when the images were taken, what permission was given for taking or retaining each one, what Kanoko thinks the collection means, or what Sumika will do with the discovery. It would be a second unsupported inference to turn this endpoint into proof of a future threat or a fully diagnosed pattern of surveillance. The visible fact is the archive and Sumika’s access to it. [V02/11/i156.]

The page’s structure is consequential. The volume began by opening the missing history behind one person’s apparent hostility. It ends by revealing an object that makes another person’s investment visible while keeping that person’s account unavailable. Information has increased; interpretive closure has not. This is the correct mainline freeze, rather than an imagined scene in which Sumika confronts Kanoko or Hime learns what happened. [V02/07/i001–005; V02/11/i155–156.]

## 10. Written Japanese speech, address, and register

**Canonical destination:** `03 Longitudinal Ledgers/WATAYURI_JAPANESE_SPEECH_ADDRESS_AND_REGISTER_LEDGER.md`. These records concern visible writing and its interactional use. They establish no acoustic timbre, pitch, breathiness, vocal duration, or adaptation performance.

### WY2-JP01 — The grammar of a protective lie

Hime’s compact formulation **「好きだから嘘つくんだ」** makes liking the stated reason for lying. The relevant distinction is not that the lie secretly ceases to be a lie. It is that the intended relational purpose can differ from the apparent meaning of separation. In this scene she wants Mitsuki to understand an action that sacrifices participation to protect reputation. Read beside the removed cross, the line explains her logic but does not make that logic sufficient. Mitsuki must still challenge why the protective departure is necessary. [V02/10/i126–129.]

The line’s local purpose also limits its transfer into a character model. It is evidence that Hime may lie to protect a valued person; it is not a rule that all her lies are protective, that affection licenses deception, or that a listener must trust any lie once she supplies that explanation. The early classroom façade and material pleasures remain counterweights. [V02/07/i005–011, i026–028.]

### WY2-JP02 — One liking word, unequal relationship classifications

Mitsuki’s **「あなたが好きよ」** is an explicit affirmative statement. Hime’s later friend-language and Mitsuki’s disappointed reaction prevent the analyst from treating their shared vocabulary as proof of a shared category. The follow-up denial is not a clean retraction of every positive feeling; in sequence it is better read as an embarrassed resistance to how Hime understands or states that feeling, while the precise category remains incompletely named. [V02/10/i130, i133–134.]

The later salon exchange intensifies the problem: Hime happily refers to having been told she is liked, and Mitsuki asks what she means while visibly struggling with the public situation. Semantic recurrence, context, and response provide the evidence; a single blush would be weaker. A translation that silently makes every instance mean either exclusively friendship or exclusively romantic love would resolve a distinction the manga is still using. [V02/11/i149–151.]

### WY2-JP03 — Familiar address crosses the role boundary

The childhood **ひめちゃん** reappears in Mitsuki’s recognition and the quiet apology. Public sister addresses and stage-name usage continue, but the familiar name marks a relation that predates the institution. Its significance comes from the linked history and timing, not from a universal rule that a diminutive always proves romance. Hime’s movement between Yano and elder-sister address similarly records which interpretive relation is active: childhood grievance, present coworker, or desired sister connection. [V02/09/i073–075; V02/10/i118–132; V02/11/i149–150.]

### WY2-JP04 — Private speech is relationship-conditioned, not uniformly rough

Mitsuki can address Sumika politely while urgently protesting, speak much more bluntly to Hime during conflict, and resume refined guest-facing language at the threshold. The evidence therefore supports conditional register switching rather than a binary elegant-fake/rough-real voice model. Her corrected professional greeting during the customer interruption is especially useful because the private argument has not disappeared. [V02/09/i076, i081–087.]

Hime likewise alternates between public politeness, internally sharp commentary, vulnerable direct questions, and forceful public assertion. Her outburst in defense of the sister relation is not simply her ordinary cute performance at higher volume; the written force and exposed disagreement break its usual social aim. No claim about actual loudness beyond what the scene and lettering represent is needed. [V02/08/i064–066; V02/09/i083–084; V02/10/i124–127.]

### WY2-JP05 — “Work,” “serious,” and “play” carry different disputes

Mitsuki’s insistence that the job is not play concerns actual responsibility, but it also allows an uncertain personal relation to be defended in impersonal terms. Rehearsal complicates that defense: taking the performance seriously may require acknowledging a genuine wish, not eliminating it. In the childhood short, Mitsuki says they should practice rather than play while privately valuing simply being with Hime. The same task vocabulary can enforce standards and conceal a wish that is harder to state. [V02/09/i079–093; V02/S11.5/i159–161.]

### WY2-JP06 — The sincerity question is specific rather than globally solvable

Questions about what can be believed recur in the childhood partnership, present distrust, and supplemental tea conversation. They do not demonstrate that one final declaration will make every future utterance transparent. The supplement’s question about whether Hime’s reassurance is true or false is especially diagnostic: Mitsuki is not asking for a philosophical theory of honesty, but for assurance that this instance of companionship matters to Hime. [V02/10/i121–122; V02/S11.5/i160–161; predecessor V01/06/i149–154.]

### WY2-JP07 — Different explanations make the institution teachable

Mai’s formal event terminology and Sumika’s practical popularity-poll paraphrase are not interchangeable word choices. They address different needs: preserving the school fiction and giving a novice a usable explanation. Hime’s repeated difficulty demonstrates that accurate setting language can still fail as immediate instruction. Her agreement to read and ask questions is a prospective learning act, not proof that the terminology has already become fluent. [V02/11/i139–143.]
