## 1. The volume’s governing problem

Volume 5 places two propositions under pressure at once: **being kind is not the same as making another person dependent on one’s kindness**, and **performing kindness does not make that kindness unreal**. Mitsuki wants to become someone who will not repeat the damage she caused in childhood. Her older-sister role gives that wish a practical form: help Hime, teach her, and make their continued work together possible. Yet when Hime learns from someone else and offers to help in return, the success of this apparent objective becomes distressing. Mitsuki wanted Hime to be able to stay; she also wanted a particular place beside Hime. Those wishes are related, but the second cannot be satisfied merely by accomplishing the first. [V05/24/i064–078; V05/25/i084–106.]

Hime encounters the opposite side of this contradiction. She receives real help, wants to acknowledge it, and increasingly wants to contribute rather than remain the person who is helped. The repeated answer that Mitsuki assists her because she is the younger sister begins to sound like a denial of any connection outside the job. That interpretation is incomplete: the volume supplies strong evidence of private investment. It is nevertheless responsive to something Mitsuki actually does. Hime’s offered labor and personal gift are refused, while the acceptable form of intimacy is repeatedly explained through the café’s hierarchy. The reader can understand why Mitsuki is afraid without declaring Hime irrational for experiencing the result as rejection. [V05/22/i018–028; V05/24/i068–078; V05/25/i107–115.]

The gifts sharpen the difference between genuine affection and agreement about its meaning. Hime’s shopping is neither indiscriminate generosity nor a purely calculated purchase of approval. She budgets, considers appearance, attends to other people’s tastes, and prepares a particular birthday gift for Kanoko. Kanoko initially objects to being included among workplace obligations, then recognizes the more personal intention. Mitsuki’s later gift belongs to the same matching series. To Hime, extending that design to three people can express a larger circle of valued relations. To Mitsuki, the inclusion of Kanoko changes what the matching can mean. The object is not insincere simply because it does not signify the exclusive position its recipient wants. Nor does the giver’s sincerity oblige the recipient to accept it. The consequential failure lies in the gap between refusal, explanation, and the relationship each believes is being offered. [V05/23/i037–049; V05/25/i109–115.]

The final chapter prevents a simple solution in which Hime must discard the false Ayanokōji and find the true Yano underneath. It shows how a younger Mitsuki found relief in a setting that stated its rules, tolerated an awkward question, and offered a way to make goodwill legible. Her elegant, kind café self was an aspiration she practised, not merely a disguise placed over an unchanged hostile person. That history qualifies Hime’s equation of salon kindness with a lie. It does not show the present conflict repaired. The mainline reading ends at **i157**, returning to the earlier first encounter with Hime; the latest depicted present remains **i119**, immediately after the gift dispute. Increased reader understanding must not be substituted for a conversation the characters have not yet completed. [V05/26/i118–157.]

## 3. Sequential, form-sensitive close reading

### 3.1 Shift 22 — the tea must be more than an attractive answer

#### A shared product requires different kinds of knowledge

The opening training session returns to a problem that Hime’s growing popularity can obscure: success at the café includes knowing what is being sold. The recommended teas are attached to characters and sister pairs, but that fictional attachment does not replace flavor, aroma, serving, or preparation information. Mai asks the workers to make an actual product intelligible to a guest. Hime has difficulty moving beyond the immediate category of “tea” into differentiating descriptions; other staff supply details she has not retained or cannot readily express. Her weakness here is specific. It is not evidence that she lacks all discernment, social intelligence, or capacity to learn. [V05/22/i005–014.]

Hime’s self-estimation and Mai’s appraisal use different measures. Hime sees the quality of the impression she produces; Mai is also assessing whether a worker can be trusted to complete the operational sequence. The gap matters because the business is represented as doing well and contemplating expanded activity. A worker whose attractiveness brings people in can simultaneously create a need for other workers to carry more of the explanation and service. The apparent contradiction—valuable employee, incomplete competence—disappears once those responsibilities are kept separate. [V05/22/i005–012.]

The chapter also refuses to isolate Hime as the only novice. Kanoko can describe a tea’s appealing feature but misses a serving recommendation; Sumika supplies it quietly. Their new sister relationship has practical content in this correction. What differs is not that one novice is wholly knowledgeable and another empty, but how limitations become visible, are supplied by a partner, and enter the next interaction. This is further early evidence of workable SK cooperation, not a test of the new confidential-listener forecast: the occasion is ordinary service, not a privately disclosed Hime-related crisis. [V05/22/i013–014; WY4-PR02.]

#### A performance can succeed locally while leaving the knowledge problem intact

Hime converts difficulty into a charming exchange: rather than deliver a confident description she does not possess, she invites the guests’ responses and invokes the prospect of her sister’s correction. This is not simply an outright false factual claim. It is an effective reorganization of attention around her persona. But when customers disagree about the flavors and return the question to her, the technique runs out. A request for information cannot always be answered by making the lack of an answer entertaining. [V05/22/i015–017.]

Mitsuki supplies the concrete answer and joins it to the sisters’ image. Her rescue is both informational and theatrical. The public quiz-like framing prevents the failure from becoming an embarrassing exposure, while the correct explanation actually completes the exchange. The same event therefore supports two judgments: Hime’s persona remains useful, and someone else has done substantive work that the persona did not do. Calling the whole sequence “fake service” would miss the information delivered; calling it proof of Hime’s independent skill would assign Mitsuki’s contribution to the wrong worker. [V05/22/i018–020.]

The private correction enumerates the less glamorous duties that still have to be learned. It deserves to be heard as a real work standard, not translated automatically into disguised flirtation. Mitsuki is right that guest attraction alone is insufficient. The later chapters will expose trouble in how she organizes help, but that does not retrospectively make every standard she states unreasonable. This is the first important counterweight to an interpretation in which all friction is produced solely by concealed romantic feelings. [V05/22/i019–020; compare V05/24/i067–074.]

#### Gratitude seeks a person; the answer returns to the role

Hime’s gratitude is not exhausted by the public benefit. She recalls previous assistance and asks whether Mitsuki likes her. The question links today’s help to a particular relationship. Mitsuki’s response retreats to the fact that Hime is her younger sister and needs to learn the work. It does not give Hime a clear account of how role obligation and private desire relate. A reasonable statement about responsibility consequently becomes an insufficient answer to a different question. [V05/22/i020–022.]

The next public interaction compounds the problem. Hime makes the liking question playable before an audience; Mitsuki responds with visible embarrassment and a close embrace that also contains a rebuke. The customers receive an attractive sister scene. Hime receives evidence that this warmth can be produced in the salon, but she still does not know how far it extends outside that frame. Her audience skill gets a response without ensuring that she has understood it. Her approach also makes it harder for Mitsuki to answer outside the script: a public challenge about private feeling is not a neutral request for disclosure. [V05/22/i023–027.]

The last two pages require especially careful attribution. The contemptuous rejection imagined on i027 belongs to Hime’s model of what Mitsuki really thinks; it is not a newly spoken private insult. On i028, after Hime leaves, the reader sees Mitsuki alone, flushed, crouched before the locker/mirror, with a near-echo of denial. The wording must not be mistranslated into an unequivocal affirmative confession, and the bodily image alone does not determine one unique emotion. Nevertheless, the panel supplies private strain that Hime’s projected image does not contain. The later explicit liking at i112 further constrains a literal, exhaustive no-affection reading. The distinction is not “the thought is false, therefore everything is clear”; it is that Hime’s certainty outruns what she has actually heard. [V05/22/i027–028; V05/25/i112.]

The chapter closes with the sisters’ tea performing particularly well. That outcome belongs to the shared service and attractive interaction. It does not retroactively solve either Hime’s knowledge limitation or their affection-scope problem. The result can be commercially good, practically interdependent, and privately painful at the same time. [V05/22/i028.]

### 3.2 Shift 23 — being specially remembered is not the same as being a date

#### Ordinary consumption complicates the strategic-persona model

The shopping chapter supplies a comparatively low-stakes context without abandoning the same interpretive problem. Hime calls the outing a date in a social performance, then explains to Kanoko that this is not its literal classification. Kanoko’s hopes and the atmosphere she experiences do not cancel that clarification. A romantic reading of her private response is well supported by the inherited context and this chapter’s focalization; a mutually recognized date is not. The source gives the analyst both pleasure and a limit. [V05/23/i029–036.]

Hime’s wages are a concrete new resource. She has accumulated earnings and now takes pleasure in spending with Kanoko, but the money does not produce indiscriminate consumption. Her account of future wealth still matters, as does investment in how she appears to others. She differentiates types of purchases: inexpensive consumables, clothes legible to peers, and an accessory that can be concealed rather than publicly displayed. These distinctions are useful everyday evidence precisely because they are more particular than the general label “materialistic.” [V05/23/i033–039.]

The source also shows aesthetic pleasure that should not be reduced to a distant marriage strategy. Hime enjoys examining items, matching them to Kanoko, and anticipating reactions. She has preferences about what can be seen, what fits an image, and what will be amusing or attractive. None of this licenses a complete favorite-brand, food, or domestic routine inventory. It does establish that price, visibility, novelty, and another person’s appearance can receive different weights in a single outing. [V05/23/i037–043.]

Kanoko’s makeup links this ordinary context back to the workplace: Sumika’s earlier help has become usable outside the salon. This is a small sign of an enlarged network of practical influence, not proof that Kanoko has transferred her romantic attachment. Hime’s praise of Kanoko’s appearance can be genuine friendship and a source of romantic exhilaration for Kanoko without being romantic intention on Hime’s side. [V05/23/i037; compare V04/21/i136–138.]

#### The absent shopper and the misread gift

Hime’s brief disappearance is experienced through Kanoko’s attention to proximity. It interrupts the feeling that this is an especially intimate shared day. The later explanation shows that the interval was used to prepare something for Kanoko, not simply to abandon her for a more attractive pursuit. The formal delay matters: the reader temporarily shares Kanoko’s incomplete access to Hime’s intention, then receives a correction with the gift’s unveiling. Discomfort during an absence is evidence of Kanoko’s experience, not automatically evidence of the absent person’s disregard. [V05/23/i040–049.]

When Hime describes buying workplace gifts, Kanoko resists being absorbed into a general category. She does not want the pleasant public face she already knows is performed. She wants acknowledgment of the particular friendship in which that face can rest. The objection has a real historical basis in V03. It is not merely an irrational demand to be treated differently from everyone else in every respect. But the objection also arrives before she knows what Hime has actually prepared. Her accurate knowledge of Hime’s façade does not make her accurate about this gift. [V05/23/i044–047; V03/16/i147–156.]

Hime’s answer is concrete rather than only reassuring. The matching hair ornament has been selected for Kanoko’s approaching birthday, with earlier consultation folded into the surprise. Hime shows her own related ornament. A shared design makes the particular intention visible, and the explanation accounts for the earlier absence. Kanoko can revise her judgment because she is given evidence of planning, attention, and a private purpose. The scene is a successful clarification of a gift’s meaning, not merely a successful use of a flattering sentence. [V05/23/i047–049.]

That success must be kept within its category. Kanoko can accept that this is not a date while still experiencing the gift as specially precious. Hime can genuinely cherish a friend without promising romantic reciprocity or permanent exclusivity. The same chapter contains Hime’s express non-date clarification and the affectionate matching. Keeping both is more faithful than declaring one a disguise for the other. The later three-person extension will test the limits of what matching has promised. [V05/23/i032, i047–049; V05/25/i109–111.]

#### “Until the arm heals” remains an active premise

The train conversation returns to the original temporary-work arrangement. Mai’s recovery is still treated as a relevant future condition by the girls. Hime does not simply announce a settled resignation; hesitation and deferral are part of the exchange. Kanoko’s sadness at the prospect also prevents reducing the workplace to an unwanted rival for Hime’s attention. It now contains people and routines that matter to her, even while Hime remains her primary attachment. [V05/23/i050–052.]

This modest conversation acquires new importance after the late-April flashback. At this point in the reading, the recovery condition is an apparently ordinary uncertainty. After i087–090, it is also evidence that the information available to the girls differs from the information held by Mai, Mitsuki, and Sumika. The later disclosure changes the reader’s explanation of the conversation; it does not turn the girls into people who secretly knew all along, nor does it prove they had already chosen to leave. [V05/23/i050–052; V05/25/i086–090.]

### 3.3 Shift 24 — assistance is offered, but the older sister keeps the task

#### Gifts are received through different institutions

Hime’s gifts to the staff continue the shopping chapter’s gratitude. Mitsuki declines hers. The refusal is an action that needs description before explanation: it does not establish the full motive later revealed around the matching accessory, and the first staff gift is not identical to the birthday anklet. Hime’s attempts to explain the refusal to herself are therefore not privileged access to Mitsuki’s mind. The analysis should not collapse every declined gift into one already-known romantic reason. [V05/24/i053–055; V05/25/i109–114.]

The birthday schedule and planned ritual add another setting for giving. The source lists Mitsuki’s and Kanoko’s July dates and describes an event in which the sister’s words have special value. That makes birthday attention institutionally organized as well as personally meaningful. Hime initially sees a role she expects to be good at: producing appropriate words and a convincing celebration. This expectation should be compared with, not substituted for, the unscripted present she later chooses herself. [V05/24/i056–058; V05/25/i108–109.]

Rules for guest gifts delimit what can enter the salon and its fiction. They concern kinds of objects, appropriateness, and reception, not a general law that every affectionate gift must be accepted. Staff exchanges, customer gifts, and the birthday performance are related but nonidentical practices. Later, the fact that Mitsuki can receive a customer’s present professionally does not establish that refusing a personal matching object is insincere. Different relationships expose the recipient to different meanings and obligations. [V05/24/i058, i063–066; V05/25/i109–114.]

#### A genuine staffing problem exposes a rigid arrangement

Nene’s illness creates an actual operational disruption. Mai moves into kitchen work with Sumika’s assistance; the available salon staff must redistribute tasks. Sending Nene home and arranging the practicalities is a managerial act of care within the story. It should not become a medical or employment-law judgment imported from outside the source. Nor should the subsequent disclosure about Mai’s recovery cause the analyst to deny that the kitchen now genuinely needs coverage. An earlier deception and a present absence are different causes. [V05/24/i059–062; V05/25/i087–094.]

Mitsuki takes on a large amount of work and does not readily allow others to relieve her. She receives gifts, answers guests, handles orders, and intervenes in tasks Hime attempts. Some caution is justified: a novice cannot be assumed competent simply because she volunteers. The difficulty is that her inexperience becomes a reason both not to delegate and not to make time for learning. Hime’s offered assistance repeatedly returns her to the position of the protected younger sister rather than a colleague who can acquire responsibility. [V05/24/i063–070.]

The order-slip exchange is especially diagnostic. Hime recognizes the task and asks to do it; Mitsuki says there is no time to teach her and sends her back to her own service duties. Hime’s private frustration is not the wish to avoid work. She is trying to reduce the pressure she can see. The source therefore distinguishes lack of present mastery from lack of willingness. A model that predicts Hime always chooses the easiest attractive performance would miss the fact that she wants a less visible administrative task. [V05/24/i068.]

The first-year/older-sister distinction also acquires an operational rigidity not warranted as an immutable rule. Mitsuki takes over reception and invokes rank, but the next chapter will show Kanoko learning reception successfully. That later counterexample matters. The hierarchy can organize dramatic presentation without fixing who may ever learn a real task. The apparent protection of the junior can also protect the senior’s claim to indispensability. The latter remains an interpretive account until Mitsuki’s subsequent interiority supplies stronger support. [V05/24/i069–070; V05/25/i091–106.]

#### Service failure is not a metaphor only

The chapter depicts consequences beyond emotional inconvenience. An order is lost or forgotten, food is delayed, a guest’s time runs short, and management has to repair the service problem with an extension and an additional offering. These events prevent treating the workload conflict as only an allegory of romance. A particular allocation of tasks is not working reliably. The counterevidence to Mitsuki’s solution is practical, visible, and consequential for people outside the central pair. [V05/24/i067, i071–075.]

Hime’s contribution to the apology works on the guest’s affect, whereas Mitsuki emphasizes the failure of the work itself. Both identify something real. A pleasant reaction does not make the missing service disappear; conversely, helping a customer feel acknowledged is part of the repair rather than inherently worthless “lip service.” Their conflict is intensified by each contribution’s failure to count as the other’s answer. Hime’s capacity for reassurance does not qualify her for every task, but neither is it nothing. [V05/24/i072–074.]

The close of the workday makes the asymmetry explicit. Hime is distressed by being refused a helpful place; Mitsuki is concerned with keeping her within a arrangement she believes will make continued sisterhood possible. The request to Mai about maintaining the arrangement is not shown to Hime. Thus the two have different explanations for the same refusal: Hime sees an absence of personal trust, while Mitsuki is attempting to preserve a future bond. The next chapter reveals that this attempt already depends on information withheld from Hime. [V05/24/i076–078; V05/25/i086–090.]

### 3.4 Shift 25 — a correct memory does not guarantee a corrected decision

#### Mitsuki recognizes the earlier injury

The childhood material at the start of Shift 25 should not be treated as a new present event. It returns to Mitsuki’s understanding of the earlier partnership, the fear of abandonment, and her eventual recognition that she injured Hime. She sees that Hime had wanted to help; she connects that earlier intention to the present offers of assistance. The source therefore does not support the claim that she has simply forgotten everything learned in V02. Recognition and guilt are present. [V05/25/i079–085.]

The failure is more difficult than ignorance. Mitsuki wants to act differently and yet organizes the new opportunity around being the one who is kind, capable, and helpful. She can recognize Hime’s goodwill without making enough room for its action. This distinction is central to the reconstruction model: a person may explicitly understand a past mistake while still reproducing a related relational arrangement under a different justification. The pattern is not identical to the childhood disclosure, and its moral responsibilities should not be mechanically equated. It does concern another unilateral decision about how a valued bond will be preserved. [V05/25/i084–090, i098–114.]

#### Recovery is disclosed to the reader, not to Hime

The flashback is explicitly placed in **late April, before the Blumen event begins**. Mai says the cast has come off that day and she can finally return to the salon. Her first stated plan is to ask Hime to continue. This is important counterevidence to an account in which Mai had only one unchanging plan to coerce indefinite work through an invented injury. At this moment, the source supplies an available alternative: tell Hime the temporary substitution has ended and ask for a new choice. [V05/25/i086–087.]

Mitsuki objects. Her fear is connected to Hime not yet having the skills to stay comfortably and to the remembered loss of the childhood partnership. She wants more time before a decision that could end their working connection. Mai accepts a delay and expressly says she will continue pretending to have the fracture. The manager’s interest in retaining Hime through the contest and Mitsuki’s attachment-based project converge in one practical arrangement, despite not being the same motive. [V05/25/i087–089.]

Sumika names the deception as such. Mai minimizes the duration and offers to take the blame if the pretence is discovered; she leaves the younger sister’s situation to Mitsuki’s judgment. This is not Hime’s consent, and managerial willingness to absorb blame is not a substitute for it. The dialogue shows knowledge shared by Mai, Mitsuki, and Sumika. It does not establish that Nene knows, nor that the girls continuing to discuss recovery on the train have learned it. Sumika’s objection is real; the fact that the source does not show her telling Hime does not supply a uniquely determined motive for that silence. [V05/25/i089–090; V05/23/i050–052.]

The new source finding must be scoped precisely. **Continued injury presentation after recovery is now established. Fabrication of the original accident or the original injury is not.** The scene actually gives recovery and cast removal as the occasion for the new arrangement. A later deliberate falsehood cannot be used to declare every earlier injury report false. The correct current revision of WY1-C06 is therefore dated and differentiated, not the blanket substitution “Mai was never injured.” [V05/25/i086–090; inherited WY1-C06.]

This also recontextualizes the setting of subsequent work. Hime is being helped to become able to remain without being shown the condition under which she could already decide whether to remain. The benevolent description—give her the skills to choose confidently—coexists with control over when that choice becomes available to her. Neither the good intention nor the real value of skill erases the informational restriction. Conversely, the restriction does not mean every lesson or practical favor was fraudulent. The volume is examining their coexistence. [V05/25/i088–090, i098–104.]

#### Practical improvement arrives through a different arrangement

The return to the present identifies another day of Nene’s illness and a changed division of work. Sumika teaches Kanoko reception; Hime’s familiar customer-facing abilities complement that administrative contribution; Mitsuki is freed for other tasks. Rain and a less crowded session also affect the workload. The outcome is not a controlled comparison in which only one variable changes, but it is a meaningful contrast to the earlier refusal to delegate. [V05/25/i091–099.]

The new reception work is particularly valuable as counterevidence to rank-based closure. Kanoko does not cease being a first-year role simply because she learns an operational procedure. Hime can escort and reassure guests while Kanoko handles another part of their arrival. The task becomes a sequence shared by people with different present abilities, rather than a single package reserved for the experienced older sister. The first day’s problem was not solved by making every novice instantly as capable as Mitsuki. [V05/25/i095–099.]

Hime’s order-slip lesson supplies the long-missing practical test in her own learning trajectory. She asks specific questions about how the slip is used, the direction in which orders are written, and an apparent exception. The dialogue makes the procedure explainable. A significant attribution limit is necessary: **the judgment that Hime has not properly memorized the abbreviations belongs to Mitsuki’s interior commentary**, not to an explicit Hime speech of self-diagnosis. Hime acknowledges the abbreviation topic, asks questions, and participates in instruction; Sumika permits full menu names until the abbreviations are learned. [V05/25/i100; compare V05/24/i068.]

Sumika’s workaround changes the prerequisite. The task’s purpose is intelligible communication with the kitchen and the account, not the display of perfect abbreviated notation for its own sake. Hime can begin correctly without first clearing Mitsuki’s imagined total-memorization gate. The following pages report her new participation and a mistake-free session, with her and Kanoko’s expanded work acknowledged. This is practical outcome evidence, not merely the delighted applause at a theatrical rescue. It gives bounded support to WY1-PR04 under its original clarification/requested-help criterion; it does not prove that Hime explicitly confessed every weakness or that the accommodation alone caused the entire day’s success. [V05/25/i100–104.]

The sequence matters for Mitsuki precisely because it is good work. She recognizes that division is helping, that Hime is learning, and that the ostensible objective is being met. Her unease cannot be explained by inventing a new practical failure the source does not show. When she sees Kanoko’s closeness and Hime’s increasing competence, the question becomes who occupies the desired place beside Hime. Learning from another person is an organizational success and a private threat within the same scene. [V05/25/i100–106.]

#### A gift can enlarge a circle that someone wanted to narrow

The encounter after work does not begin as an attempt to punish Mitsuki. Hime waits, asks what is wrong, and offers a birthday gift selected earlier. The scene therefore gives her multiple forms of initiative: asking for an explanation, offering a personal object, and trying to recognize the person beyond the labor she performs. Her uncertainty about Mitsuki’s tastes is admitted in the account of choosing something wearable at work. That uncertainty is itself evidence that she is trying to give to a particular person she does not yet fully know. [V05/25/i107–109.]

The anklet repeats the design associated with Hime’s hairpin. When Kanoko’s barrette is added to the explanation, the matching becomes three-person rather than dyadic. The source’s immediate change of expression, Hime–Kanoko imagery, and the preceding replacement anxiety strongly support the interpretation that this relational configuration matters to Mitsuki. Her spoken refusal names the matching. The evidence is stronger than an isolated jealous-looking face, but it still does not authorize a claim that she possesses a fully articulated theory of exclusivity she has chosen to state. [V05/25/i101–106, i109–111.]

The parallel with Kanoko’s shopping protest is exact enough to be useful and different enough to resist flattening. Both recipients fear an insufficiently particular place. Kanoko can be reassured by learning that the gift was specially planned for her rather than merely a generic staff token. Mitsuki is upset by the form of specialness that is actually offered: sharing a design with Hime and Kanoko. The second case cannot be solved simply by repeating that the gift is genuine. Its genuineness and its failure to meet a private wish can coexist. [V05/23/i045–049; V05/25/i109–114.]

Mitsuki has a right to refuse a gift. Accepting an unwanted object is not a necessary demonstration of affection, and Hime’s pain should not be converted into an entitlement to acceptance. But refusal also communicates in a relationship. Mitsuki says she likes Hime and that the refusal does not mean rejecting her; she cannot make that distinction comprehensible enough for Hime in the scene. Hime receives a series of refusals—help, participation, and now the matching—and connects them to the repeated claim that everything is done because of the sister role. The effect is larger than the object alone. [V05/24/i068–078; V05/25/i111–114.]

This is where the long-open affection-scope forecast is actually tested. Hime explicitly contrasts the kindness possible in the salon with the connection she seeks outside it, and calls Mitsuki’s liking a salon-only lie. Mitsuki explicitly states liking while explaining her wish to be kind, help Hime, work together, and continue sisterhood. The discussion does not simply contain an ambiguous word; it makes the scope of that word the object of their dispute. WY2-PR01 is supported even though no final romantic category is mutually named and the conversation ends badly. A forecast about the necessary distinction is not a forecast that making the distinction will immediately repair the bond. [V05/25/i111–115.]

Mitsuki’s collapse into the conclusion that she has not changed is her current self-assessment. The volume has shown actual changes in understanding and actual capacities for care, as well as a damaging recurrence in the organization of help. The analyst should not accept the all-or-nothing self-verdict as a complete developmental judgment. Nor should the existence of progress be used to deny the injury in front of her. The rain and isolation give the conclusion emotional force without making it omniscient narration. [V05/25/i116; compare i079–085, i100–104.]

### 3.5 Shift 26 — Ayanokōji is a way of trying to become Yano

#### The present ends before the explanation does

Hime tells the others that she can continue the sister work while doubting that Mitsuki likes her outside it. This separates continuation of a role from restoration of trust. The statement should not be read as proof that she has calmly resolved the argument. She is articulating a narrower workable arrangement after a painful exchange. Sumika tries to counter the interpretation that salon kindness is only acting, identifying it with Yano herself. Her unfinished explanation does not establish that Hime has now been told Mitsuki’s complete romantic situation or the recovery secret. [V05/26/i118–119.]

This is the latest depicted present. The title and subsequent school scenes turn backward. The reader is then given an account of how the café self developed, but Hime is not shown receiving that entire account. The volume’s final explanatory generosity is directed first toward the reader. Its effect on the present relationship remains unobserved. [V05/26/i120–157.]

#### Rule clarity is not the same as wanting to police everyone

The school incident is easy to misread through a generic image of the inflexible rule-follower. Mitsuki observes widespread departure from the uniform rule and proposes changing the outdated rule. She is not simply demanding that classmates be punished for failing to comply. The teacher’s response makes institutional amendment difficult and threatens stricter practice instead; peers blame her intervention. What she sought as a coherent solution becomes socially damaging in its uptake. [V05/26/i121–123.]

Subsequent examples describe a world of apparently open invitations with unstated qualifications. Mitsuki experiences the discrepancy as a failure to identify the correct answer. That is her explanatory vocabulary. It deserves to be preserved, not replaced with a clinical diagnosis or a claim that she lacks feeling. Her distress is partly about wanting connection and fearing the harm caused when she gets it wrong. The source supplies desire, shame, anger, and care alongside difficulty with implicit conditions. [V05/26/i123–126, i145–146.]

The school is not depicted as a perfectly uniform enemy. Some invitations have at least a plausible inclusive intention, while another participant privately wants a more exclusive occasion. Mitsuki’s own refusal, wish to follow, and flight contribute to the interaction without making her the sole cause of its difficulty. Later apology and explanation further complicate a global account of rejection. An evidence-bounded model must represent different people’s different intentions rather than treating “the class” as one hostile mind. [V05/26/i127–131, i142–153.]

The piano scenes connect this uncertainty to the unresolved childhood rupture as she understood it then. This pre-reunion Mitsuki still asks what Hime meant and what she should have done. She does not possess the later V02 correction simply because the reader does. The volume’s two retrospective sequences therefore contain different knowledge states: the start of Shift 25 reflects the more informed present, whereas the school history preserves an earlier uncertainty. [V05/25/i079–085; V05/26/i125–126.]

#### The strange café is easier to inhabit because it explains itself

Mitsuki’s entrance into Liebe begins as flight and concealment, not a fully considered search for employment or roleplay. The threshold matters. She asks an awkward question about what kind of shop this is; Sumika does not punish the bluntness. She accommodates the need to stay and gives Mitsuki a place in the fiction. The response supplies a small but important counterexample to the expectation that an imperfect social approach must end in injury. [V05/26/i131–139.]

At first the school explanation confuses her. Once the guest role is made concrete, however, she can act: drink tea, be hosted, return. She is told what she is in this interaction and what the others intend to do for her. The fiction is not easier because it is objectively more realistic. It is easier because the participants articulate the frame instead of requiring her to infer every qualification. Repeated visits then establish actual comfort, not merely a single bewildered encounter. [V05/26/i136–140.]

This ease is not total transparency. When thin staffing and low customer numbers are mentioned, Mitsuki cannot be certain whether they are real business concerns or further elements of the setting. The same fictional organization that reduces one kind of ambiguity can create another. Her offer to come more often answers a concern she takes seriously, but the scene itself preserves uncertainty about how the concern should be classified. That early ambiguity anticipates the volume’s present-day problem: a role-mediated statement can be genuine without making every practical circumstance explicit. [V05/26/i140–141.]

Her attachment to the café consequently has more than one basis. It is a place of predictable hospitality, a place where her awkwardness can be met without rejection, and eventually a place where she can practise a desired way of interacting. It is not necessary to infer that she loves all rules or wants superior rank in every setting. The concrete attraction is a form of interaction she can enter and learn. [V05/26/i137–148.]

#### The “correct answer” is displaced by the practice of kindness

Another invitation goes wrong when one classmate quietly signals that this is not a day for Mitsuki to join. She withdraws and returns to the language of error. Sumika’s answer does not provide a definitive decoding manual for every future hidden intention. She identifies the supposed correct answer with other people’s unspoken circumstances and feelings—things not perfectly determinate or available even to an attentive observer. This changes the problem from “I failed to know an answer everyone else knows” to a question of how to act when interpersonal information is incomplete. [V05/26/i142–145.]

Mitsuki resists simple indifference because she fears hurting someone. That fear is evidence against construing her directness as the absence of ethical concern. Mai and Sumika suggest becoming kind in conduct and presentation rather than solving every hidden condition first. The advice includes performing a brighter self, beginning with a smile, so that others can interpret her with more generosity. It is a local practical proposal, not an established psychological law that smiling always makes people benevolent. [V05/26/i146–148.]

Their intervention also has limits. Telling someone to perform a more legible self can help them communicate, but it does not remove the need for others to state their intentions or accommodate uncertainty. The following school repair is therefore essential evidence. The volume does not validate the advice merely by showing a beautiful smile. It shows what is said with that smile and how another person responds. [V05/26/i149–153.]

The classmate apologizes, but Mitsuki’s initial literal response is heard as continued anger. When she changes presentation, she also explains that she is not angry at being turned away; she is afraid because she does not know when joining will be unwelcome. She asks for clearer guidance and says she wants to get along. The classmate, in turn, supplies the particular reason for wanting the other girl alone and distinguishes that occasion from rejecting Mitsuki’s company in general. The repair is reciprocal clarification, not a one-sided makeover. [V05/26/i149–153.]

This scene offers an important alternative to both extreme models of Mitsuki. She is not simply incapable of hearing another person, because the explanation changes the exchange. She is not simply misunderstood through no action of her own, because her initial wording and expression contribute to the perceived rebuff. The successful response involves a more legible expression of her actual wish and a listener willing to specify the situation. The same conditional structure should inform later reconstruction: improvement depends on what the other person can hear and what they are willing to tell. [V05/26/i149–154.]

#### The kind self is an aspiration, not a completed moral certificate

Rehearsal, uptake, and graduation lead to the invitation to join the café while entering high school. Mai and Sumika frame the work as an opportunity to become a kinder version of herself. Mai articulates the idea that continued performance can become part of the person performing it. Mitsuki names Ayanokōji as her kind self and connects the aspiration to not hurting important people or breaking valued relationships. These are source statements about self-construction and desire, not an empirical theory imported by the analyst. [V05/26/i154–156.]

The title’s name now has a more precise meaning. Ayanokōji is neither an entirely separate person nor a fraudulent shell to be discarded before Yano can be loved. She is a role through which Yano tries to make a valued capacity habitual. The private wish has shaped the public character. The present conflict shows that this project can fail in application: kindness becomes overly tied to providing help, and the junior’s autonomy becomes difficult to accommodate. Failure does not make the aspiration unreal; aspiration does not validate every act performed in its name. [V05/26/i155–156; V05/25/i087–114.]

The final image returns to Hime entering the café, an already established event now placed within Mitsuki’s hope that something important could exist again. This is retrospective recontextualization, not a second arrival after the gift argument. It strengthens the interpretation that the working role was invested with genuine desire before the current dispute. It leaves unanswered how Mitsuki will explain that desire to Hime, whether Hime will accept the explanation, and what will happen when the concealed recovery arrangement becomes known. [V05/26/i157; V02/09/i072–075.]

The volume’s two endings must therefore be read together without being merged. The latest present supplies a rupture and an incomplete counterinterpretation. The reading endpoint supplies the history that makes that counterinterpretation credible. The first is not canceled by the second. [V05/26/i118–119, i154–157.]

## 4. The recruitment supplement and edition paratext

### 4.1 Shift 26.5 — presenting the ideal and exposing the backstage

The four-page half-chapter belongs to the earlier recruitment period. Mai first sees Mitsuki’s appearance as a strong fit for the café and imagines what her participation could do for the business. Sumika then reminds her of Mitsuki’s school stage; the conversation shifts to graduation and waiting. This is an explicitly represented constraint in the characters’ recruitment discussion. It is not used here to state or verify Japanese employment law. The useful analytical fact is that an attractive candidate does not make Mai proceed immediately without considering timing. [V05/S26.5/i159–160.]

The discussion also distinguishes two ways of recruiting. Sumika suggests responding to what makes the candidate pleased; Mai wants Mitsuki to see and share the café they actually hope to make. She anticipates the problem of someone becoming a worker and finding the workplace different from what they expected. This is a consequential statement of managerial principle because the mainline has just shown a later case in which Hime is not told that a condition of her work has changed. The contrast does not establish that Mai’s earlier ideal was entirely insincere. It does establish that the ideal and later practice must be assessed separately. [V05/S26.5/i160–161; V05/25/i087–090.]

The backstage punch line gives that contrast a gentler form. Mitsuki encounters the ordinary presentation of the manager and Sumika, recognizes the work hierarchy, and becomes visibly flustered by the difference from the salon. The scene is identified as before Hime and the others arrived. It does not establish a new post-argument conversation or a decision to leave the job. Its comic point depends on a gap between expectation and backstage reality, but the mainline has already shown that learning about performance need not destroy attachment to the place. [V05/S26.5/i162; V05/26/i136–156.]

**Temporal classification:** the supplement is historical, associated with the middle-school/graduation recruitment period and explicitly before Hime’s arrival. Its short sequence combines recruitment discussion and backstage discovery; the precise placement of every panel against the mainline montage is not fixed beyond those anchors. It must not be mechanically assigned to the next day after V05/i119 or to a wholly new present state. [V05/S26.5/i159–162.]

### 4.2 Afterword, manual, cover, and bonus image

The one-page afterword at i163 discusses production, the middle-school reach of Mitsuki’s history, the café, and the continuation of the Hime–Mitsuki material. It is creator/production testimony. It can explain why this volume’s formal scope includes a substantial earlier period; it cannot serve as independent confirmation of a forecast about what the characters will do next. No later volume has been read to complete that statement. [V05/paratext/i163.]

The operation-manual page organizes recommended teas and their serving information. It is useful as a secondary, in-edition route for checking the chapter’s product distinctions, but it is not a new work scene. Its fictional voice and recommendation format do not automatically make every line a private favorite or a behavioral event. Likewise, the bonus Hime tea illustration gives a stylized recommendation and presentation rather than a dated encounter after the ending. Where the mainline depicts a knowledge gap, paratext should not be used to pretend the character has already demonstrated mastery in that scene. [V05/paratext/i165, i168; compare V05/22/i005–019.]

The cover and underlying-cover image partition Mitsuki and Hime through glass, rain, and reflection. The design is consonant with the volume’s difficulty of reaching a person across differently inhabited roles, but it is framing rather than evidence that this precise arrangement occurred in the story. The back-cover material is similarly an edition-level presentation, not an additional ensemble event. These images may be compared with mainline thresholds and rain without overriding the explicit chronology. [V05/cover; V05/paratext/i164, i167; compare V05/25/i107–116.]

### 9.1 Help has a direction, and that direction can become a boundary

In the work conflict, Mitsuki is not refusing all contact. She is often working very hard for the person she then refuses to let help. That matters because it locates the constraint more precisely: the desired relationship permits closeness in the form of care provided by Mitsuki, but is less able to accommodate Hime as a competent contributor. The issue is therefore not solved by establishing that Mitsuki does care. The form in which care is allowed to circulate is itself consequential. [AG07/AG11; O10–O16.]

The alternative is also not an absolute rule that every offer must be accepted. Some tasks require instruction and some gifts can be declined. The source’s diagnostic contrast is between a blanket practical prerequisite that blocks learning and a specific accommodation that lets the work be done. A responsible model preserves the reason for supervision while remaining able to detect when supervision has become entangled with a preferred personal role. [AG07/AG11/AG12; i068–070, i100–104.]

### 9.2 The concealed recovery is a separate agency issue

The late-April arrangement is not simply another awkward expression of love. It concerns a factual condition under which Hime understands her work. A person can value the café, enjoy earning money, and conceivably choose to stay, while still not having been given the choice represented to her as pending. The possibility that she would stay does not make withholding recovery inconsequential. The source has not yet shown her informed response, so the analysis must preserve both the restriction and the unanswered outcome. [AG05/AG09/AG10; i050–052, i087–090.]

There is no need to equate this with the childhood betrayal in every respect. The means, participants, information, and immediate harm differ. What recurs is a belief that the valued relation can be protected by arranging another person’s options before asking what she wants. Mitsuki’s reflection makes this recurrence more complex, not less: she sincerely wants not to hurt Hime, but still finds a unilateral structure attractive when direct choice might bring loss. [O12/O13; AG09/AG10.]

### 11.1 Register can conceal the question it appears to answer

Mitsuki’s private language is more direct than her elegant service presentation, but directness is not the same as complete disclosure. She can say clearly that she is helping a younger sister while leaving unclear why that is the acceptable direction of care. She can say clearly that she likes Hime while not explaining the meaning of three-way matching. The point is not that private speech is another wholly artificial mask. It is that a clear sentence can answer a narrower question than the listener is asking. [JP01/JP05/JP06; O03/O17.]

Hime’s register changes are likewise not a simple sincerity switch. Public playfulness can contain a genuine wish for affirmation, and private anger can contain a mistaken generalization. The distinction between address to the older sister and accusation directed at Yano helps make the desired scope visible. It does not mean that every statement uttered under the private name is therefore true. [JP02/JP06; O03/O18.]

### 11.2 A missing explanation is not always a missing feeling

The school repair shows that Mitsuki’s difficulty can concern expressing the relation between a feeling and a response. She is not angry in the way the apologizing girl imagines, yet her initial acceptance is heard as anger. Once she states the fear and the wish for friendship, the interaction becomes more intelligible. This earlier success prevents treating the current gift failure as a permanent inability to explain herself. It also makes the missing explanation in the present more consequential: the source has shown a workable alternative, not an automatically available skill under every stress. [JP13; i149–153; compare i111–115.]

### 13.6 Counterreadings that must remain visible

**“Mitsuki refuses help because Hime cannot do the work.”** This has an initial evidentiary basis: Hime is learning, and busy service is not an unlimited training opportunity. **“Hime is simply demanding more attention.”** She does want a personally meaningful response, but she also offers useful, less glamorous labor and a gift planned before the immediate confrontation. **“The gift proves Hime does not take Mitsuki seriously.”** Hime remembers the birthday, admits uncertainty about taste, selects a wearable object, and includes Mitsuki in a shared design. These acts are evidence of attention. The gift can still fail to express the specialness Mitsuki wants. **“Because Mitsuki likes Hime, refusing the gift is secretly harmless.”** The refusal is a legitimate boundary, but the repeated role-only explanations and visible hurt remain consequential. Private affection is not an automatic exemption from describing what another person receives. Conversely, describing that hurt does not create a duty to accept an unwanted object. [C11/C18.]

**“The entire injury was a scam from the beginning.”** The new scene names recovery and a decision to continue the pretence. It supplies a temporal boundary the blanket claim erases. Original fraud remains unproved; later concealment is no longer merely suspected. [C06/C07.]

**“Hime would stay anyway, so the secret does not matter.”** The source has not shown her fully informed decision. Enjoying work and being offered an informed choice are different facts. **“The real Yano is the harsh backstage person; the kind Ayanokōji is false.”** The school history directly contests that division. Ayanokōji is a practised desired self. The more adequate distinction is among aspiration, capacity, particular action, and the explanation another person receives. **“A smile solves Mitsuki’s social difficulty.”** The successful school exchange also contains a statement of fear, a request for clearer signals, and an interlocutor’s specific explanation. **“The warmer ending means the current fight has passed.”** The ending returns to an earlier encounter. It changes what the reader understands about Mitsuki’s wish, not what Hime has heard after the gift refusal. [C17.]
