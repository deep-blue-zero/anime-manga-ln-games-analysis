## 1. The volume’s governing problem

Volume 8 does not retract Hime’s answer. She does not discover that she secretly meant yes when she told Mitsuki that she could not return her romantic love. What changes is the assumption that this difference leaves only two possibilities: a false romantic relationship or a separation that ends their opportunities to hurt one another. The volume develops a third possibility—remaining together while the difference continues—and then tests how much practical work that possibility requires. Its achievement is not the disappearance of refusal. It is a relationship in which a refusal can become more specific without necessarily becoming a declaration that the whole relationship must end. [V08/37/i030–038; V08/39/i088–102; V08/41/i149–158.]

Hime’s departure has more than one motive. She does not want to keep hurting a person she values; she is uncomfortable receiving a kind of attention she cannot reciprocate; and she fears that repeated rejection will eventually turn Mitsuki’s love into hatred. The birthday letter makes her positive attachment difficult for the reader to deny, even while she publicly promises a continuation she still intends to refuse. Her later declaration of complete indifference is a new protective and self-protective lie, not the hidden truth beneath the earlier nonromantic answer. Mitsuki’s consequential act is to distinguish these two kinds of refusal rather than treat every unwelcome statement as false. The source supplies Hime’s own eventual affirmation that she wants to remain; persistence alone does not establish that desire on her behalf. [V08/37/i027–038; V08/39/i077–099.]

The return is therefore both a real repair and an incomplete method. Mitsuki recognizes that Hime’s performed kindness can express genuine care. Hime recognizes that the kind Ayanokōji presentation can also belong to the real Mitsuki. They agree to continue as sisters without becoming ordinary friends again or becoming lovers. Yet knowing this broad description does not tell either person whether a directed embrace, a shared room, bathing together, changing clothes nearby, holding hands, or hearing another declaration will be comfortable. The hotel trip translates a large emotional agreement into small situations in which the parties discover that they were not agreeing to the same practical assumptions. [V08/39/i094–102; V08/40/i109–124; V08/41/i149–157.]

The final conversation matters because it replaces a general test—“Do you accept me?”—with differentiated information. Hime can hold hands and accept occasional hugs while declining shared bathing and asking for privacy during changing. She can value Mitsuki without welcoming a declaration at every moment. Mitsuki can continue feeling attraction while refraining from a desired action. None of these distinctions makes every preceding intervention harmless: Kanoko again uses physical pressure, coworkers press Hime to stay for another conversation, and Mitsuki’s demand for explicit words does not make visible discomfort irrelevant. The volume shows a better way of proceeding emerging through imperfect conduct, not a sequence in which all helpful intentions already justify their means. [V08/38/i050–060; V08/39/i069–082; V08/40/i118–121; V08/41/i140–157.]

The last mainline page moves from the pair’s negotiated limits to a photograph of Kanoko’s smile, taken during her scene with Sumika. It suggests that the trip has also made something newly visible in another relationship. It does not label that development, supply a confession, or establish reciprocal romance. The reading ends with an actual return to shared activity, a locally workable HM agreement, and an open SK question—not with every bond finally classified. [V08/41/i157–158.]

## 3. Sequential, form-sensitive close reading

### 3.1 Shift 37: the birthday performance contains more than one truth

#### 3.1.1 The answer remains, and the work has not disappeared

The opening recalls the refusal and returns to its immediate practical consequences. Hime tells Kanoko that she followed the advice to answer Mitsuki properly. She still intends to leave. This is not evidence that she failed to understand what she said; her explanation is that staying as friends after rejecting a person would keep injuring that person. The scene places the interpretation of the answer, not the answer’s existence, in dispute. Her wish to prevent harm is real, but the proposed remedy is her judgment about what continued proximity will do to both of them. [V08/37/i001, i005–007.]

The café must also decide whether the birthday can proceed. Mai treats the scheduled party as work and as part of keeping a place to which Hime could return. The two purposes coexist: maintaining a business event is not the same as proving that the coworkers’ private relations are safe, and leaving a return route open is not the same as Hime choosing it. Mitsuki’s statement that she will participate in the joint birthday also concerns Kanoko, not only her own romantic disappointment. Mai offers a backstage alternative if necessary. This does not remove every pressure associated with working while distressed, but it prevents the scene from being reduced to an unconditional command to perform regardless of capacity. [i008–011.]

Mitsuki initially describes the rejection as something she expected. Kanoko sees the inadequacy of that description: the day’s new catastrophe is the apparent loss of Hime’s continued presence. This distinction preserves what V07 established about hope. A person may say she did not expect romantic acceptance and still have expected that the confession would not make the other person leave. The present pain is evidence against an exhaustive “I wanted nothing in return” explanation, not proof that every earlier statement of limited expectation was deliberately deceitful. [i009–010; V07/C08/C19.]

The actual party begins at i012. Hime’s private reflections then interrupt its outward order. She had wanted to restore a candid connection and discover what Mitsuki really felt. Now she sees that telling the truth can hurt a valued person. Her temptation to retreat into performance follows from this experience rather than from ignorance that an honest answer has value. The volume gives her a genuine dilemma: she cannot ethically manufacture reciprocal love, yet she cannot make the correct negative answer painless. Her conclusion that perhaps she should not have spoken honestly is an understandable reaction, not a settled narratorial verdict against honesty. [i012–014.]

When guests refer to Hime’s congratulations, Mitsuki tears up while speaking about Hime’s kindness. The customers have a plausible in-role way to read her emotion, but that reading does not include the private rejection. Sumika creates a reason to take Mitsuki away without fully dismantling the salon fiction. Her role intervention has practical care value: it gives a distressed colleague a break while preserving the event. It also illustrates the limits of audience evidence. A visibly moving birthday scene can be professionally legible to customers while being privately unbearable in ways they do not know. [i015–016.]

Hime’s response is to withhold the very assistance she would otherwise offer. She reasons that more kindness would deepen Mitsuki’s attachment and therefore prolong the injury. Here the relationship’s earlier achievement—Hime wanting to help rather than merely be helped—has become a source of hesitation. The problem is not an absence of care but a belief that acting on care will make matters worse. This should not be idealized into pure self-sacrifice: her own discomfort and fear also matter. It should equally not be flattened into indifference, because her attention to Mitsuki’s distress organizes the decision. [i017; V05/C03; V06/C06.]

#### 3.1.2 Kanoko asks for Hime’s choice, not simply Hime’s presence

The SK birthday exchange reveals the favor Kanoko asked of Sumika. Instead of requesting only a tribute directed toward herself, she wanted words that could support Hime. Sumika praises this as an especially generous choice. That praise is evidence of Sumika’s interpretation; it does not erase Kanoko’s continuing wish for Hime to return or her earlier possessive actions. What is materially new is the form of the intervention. Hime is urged to decide according to her own heart, not to remain merely because somebody else needs her to. [i018–022.]

The salon’s language of future paths lets Sumika address a real decision without explaining the private conflict to the guests. This is not a false message simply because it is delivered through a fictional school. Nor is it complete candor: the audience receives a different account of what the speech concerns. Its layered construction permits meaningful intervention, while leaving Hime to decide whether being publicly encouraged to choose freely feels liberating or like another indirect challenge to the decision she has already announced. [i020–022, i026.]

Kanoko’s own statement is especially important for the longitudinal relationship. She acknowledges that Hime was the reason she began working there, then distinguishes that origin from her current reason for continuing. She now enjoys Liebe herself. This gives Hime a new piece of information: leaving need not be justified as the only way to restore Kanoko’s preferred world, and staying need not be treated only as an obligation to the friend she brought into the café. Kanoko’s attachment has acquired another context in which she can want something for herself. [i023.]

The development does not amount to a complete account of Kanoko’s prior interventions. Hime is still not told that Kanoko once proposed abolishing sisterhood or tried to prevent the outcome Hime wanted in the Blumen contest. The current statement can be sincere without repairing every informational asymmetry. A V08 current-view revision should therefore preserve the older acts and their secrecy while recording that Kanoko’s stated basis for participation has changed. This is development, not retrospective acquittal. [i023; V03/C04; V04/C10; V07/C03.]

Sumika then returns attention to Kanoko herself, offering support and birthday recognition. Kanoko’s response shows that the effort has reached her. The scene has relational weight beyond a mechanically performed sister speech: Sumika has understood a request, acted on it, and also made the requester feel celebrated. But a grateful or embarrassed response does not settle a romantic category. The most secure conclusion is that their accepted pact is accumulating occasions of personal recognition. Its direction and eventual interpretation remain separate questions. [i024–025.]

Hime nevertheless feels the pressure inside the benevolent intervention. If she has chosen to leave because she thinks remaining cruel, then being told to choose what she really wants can sound as if everyone has decided her choice is not hers. The manga does not need to make the coworkers malicious for this tension to exist. Advice can recognize a genuine unspoken desire and still be experienced as a refusal to take an announced boundary at face value. The distinction will become sharper when Hime later articulates the desire herself. Until then, the coworkers’ confidence does not replace her agency. [i026.]

#### 3.1.3 Reusing the letter changes its meaning without making its care false

Hime enters the HM birthday exchange still intending to complete the exception and leave. The public letter is the earlier birthday letter, written before she understood the romantic meaning of Mitsuki’s liking. Reuse makes the same words encounter a different knowledge state. What once helped restore reciprocal care now exposes why reciprocal care and romantic reciprocity cannot be treated as the same achievement. [i027–030; V06/C06/C08.]

Her internal reconstruction is unusually explicit. She had worked to obtain Mitsuki’s liking and had wanted it to be as strong as her own. The word **両想い** appears in that reflection, but the sequence immediately distinguishes the mismatch in the kind of liking. Reading the isolated expression as an affirmative romantic confession would reverse the work of the adjacent panels. Hime recognizes a desire for mutual attachment; she does not thereby negate the nonromantic answer she has just given. [i031–033.]

The letter’s value also exceeds approval-seeking. Hime wanted to receive help, give help and remain important to Mitsuki as someone more substantial than a successful public presentation. The re-presented scenes trace her frustration when those wishes failed to meet a legible response. Her attraction to being liked remains part of the picture, but the specific longing to act within this particular relationship is not interchangeable with wanting any admirer. V08 strengthens a person-specific model without replacing it with a romantic one. [i029–034.]

The most consequential discrepancy comes after the letter. Mitsuki expresses a hope that they will keep helping one another and continue as sisters. Hime gives an outwardly affirmative response while privately maintaining that this is the last occasion. Her subsequent embrace is a genuine act she chooses, but it occurs inside a public performance whose projected future she does not presently intend to fulfill. It is therefore possible to distinguish sincere affection, misleading future assurance and specific accepted contact within one scene. The categories should not be forced into a single verdict of either wholly fake or wholly transparent. [i034–036.]

This also sharpens the audience problem. Guests can applaud an apparently intact sister relationship because the performance provides no reason for them to know Hime’s planned departure. Mitsuki, who knows about the departure, must receive the affectionate words and gesture under a different burden. The same performance can complete a professional responsibility, preserve public legibility and simultaneously make private loss more acute. Its success as an event is not evidence that the two have agreed on what happens after it. [i029, i035–036; V08/38/i039–044.]

The final piano sequence of the chapter gives Hime a less managed recognition: she does not want to leave Yano. The page does not need an external audience to authenticate that wish. It is available to the reader through Hime’s own focalization, while Mitsuki still cannot rely on knowing it. The musical scene is represented through keys, posture, notation and remembered relational meaning; no particular acoustic delivery is inferred. Its structural function is to make a positive desire explicit before the next chapter returns to the outward decision to go. [i037–038.]

### 3.2 Shift 38: the party ends before the argument does

#### 3.2.1 An actual completed event, not a restored private world

The chapter gives Mitsuki’s experience of the ending. The birthday has provided the very closeness she fears losing, but neither the letter nor the embrace has withdrawn Hime’s departure. Her concentration on the remaining time underscores the asymmetry established at the preceding endpoint: the reader has just learned Hime’s wish to remain, whereas Mitsuki sees someone still committed to leaving. Information available in Hime’s inner narration must not be distributed to Mitsuki simply because it would make her next decision easier to explain. [V08/38/i039–043.]

The departure of the guests and the closed shop at i044 establish something the earlier volumes withheld: the birthday performance is now actually completed. That is a real advancement of the event boundary. It is not yet an advancement to repaired employment or ordinary friendship. Mai asks whether Hime has reconsidered; Hime still says she cannot continue. The managerial question treats return as a decision still requiring a response, rather than inferring it from successful birthday participation. [i043–045.]

Repeated talk about having rejected Mitsuki hurts her, and she asks for it to stop. Hime agrees. This small exchange matters because the larger conflict can otherwise make only Hime’s discomfort visible. Mitsuki, too, has a specific limit about how painful information is repeatedly presented. That does not reverse Hime’s answer or create an obligation to reciprocate love. It does show that both participants have to distinguish a fact that is known from every possible repetition of it. The closing hotel exchange will develop a parallel distinction about repeated declarations of liking. [i046; V08/41/i156–157.]

Kanoko requests another conversation. Hime initially frames it as the last possible exchange and presses for Mitsuki’s response, then accepts a later opportunity. During the intervening work she attempts to leave. The later discovery is not merely that she walked outside: the announced willingness to wait and the effort to depart do not match. Her wish to avoid renewed pain supplies a motive, but it does not make the misleading reassurance disappear. [i047–054.]

#### 3.2.2 Kanoko’s useful criticism is entangled with renewed pressure

Kanoko wants Mitsuki to state that she does not want Hime to quit. Mitsuki instead reasons that Hime has made her wish to leave clear and that further pursuit can only burden her. Each is responding to a different part of the evidence. Mitsuki takes an announced decision seriously, which is not inherently a failure of care. Kanoko is also attentive to Hime’s positive attachment, which is not inherently a fantasy. The difficulty is that neither has direct access to every part of Hime’s current reasoning, and their different responses are shaped by their own positions in the relationship. [i050–054.]

When they learn that Hime is trying to go, Mitsuki reacts to the broken promise as another confusing lie. Kanoko argues that Hime is using such lies because repeatedly hurting Mitsuki has become unbearable. This criticism identifies a real relation between distress and evasive speech. It does not settle the responsibility for every deceptive statement. Hime remains an actor making choices; Mitsuki’s pain is not by itself a wrongful act that causes Hime to lose all responsibility for how she speaks. A useful causal insight can coexist with an unfair allocation of blame. [i054–056.]

The physical staging is consequential. Kanoko grips Mitsuki’s clothing and uses forceful proximity while criticizing her. This is not a newly depicted repetition of the earlier punch, and the record should not substitute one act for the other. It is nevertheless renewed physical pressure after V07’s specific apology for hitting. The prior apology can have been real without proving that Kanoko will now handle every attachment crisis without coercion. The new act belongs in the agency ledger as a different event, not as evidence that the apology never happened. [i055–058; V07/C07/C11.]

Kanoko’s comparison with ordinary suitors is especially harsh. She describes a confession followed by withdrawal after rejection as insufficiently attentive to the particular Hime who values Mitsuki. This is a situated argument about the history of this pair, not an adequate general rule that a rejected person must keep pursuing. The prior volumes supply substantial assistance from Mitsuki to Hime, so a total claim that she has received everything and given nothing would also exceed the record. Her failure here is narrower: she cannot yet see how to return the specific recognition and effort Hime previously gave her without treating continued romance as the desired outcome. [i057–060; V01/C05; V05/C03–C05; V06/C11.]

The accusation strikes Mitsuki at an established vulnerability. She has repeatedly struggled to infer intentions that others think obvious. Her response is not confident manipulation of an unwanted recipient; it is an admission that she does not understand what to do. This history helps explain the paralysis without making it a permanent incapacity or diagnosing it. The source also shows that she can change her interpretation when concrete remembered evidence becomes available. [i058–064; V05/C13–C15.]

#### 3.2.3 Remembered care supplies a new question, not permission to override an answer

After Kanoko leaves, Mitsuki recalls Hime’s earlier efforts to approach her despite apparently discouraging responses. The hand imagery gives that history a concrete shape: Hime had offered a connection, Mitsuki had failed to receive it, and the opportunity to respond had not been reducible to guessing correctly from a distance. The memory moves Mitsuki from an abstract demand to understand everyone’s feelings toward a particular obligation to attend to Hime’s demonstrated care. [i061–064.]

The distinction between a new question and an already justified answer is important. These memories give Mitsuki a reason to ask what Hime actually wants and to explain what remaining could mean. They do not prove that Hime must return to work, permit contact or change her romantic classification. The next chapter has to supply Hime’s own disclosure for a mutual decision to exist. Had the story stopped at Mitsuki’s renewed resolve, there would be no basis for treating pursuit as a successful repair. [i065–066; V08/39/i088–099.]

### 3.3 Shift 39: the lie that denies a relationship, and the truth that does not make it romantic

#### 3.3.1 The renewed conversation is not free of pressure

A childhood counterfactual opens the chapter: Hime wonders whether saying something differently might have prevented the old separation. This expresses her present interpretation of the past, not a newly established alternative history. It places the current decision beside the earlier injury without certifying that all the same causal conditions have returned. The present problem includes an explicit romantic mismatch that the childhood account did not establish in the same shared form. [V08/39/i067.]

Coworkers then try to delay Hime’s departure. She says there is no more to discuss and presents leaving as an ordinary return home. Mitsuki arrives and asks her not to go. Hime changes her explanation: she had supposedly only gone outside while waiting for the promised conversation. The shift is visible enough to support a strategic-evasion reading, but it does not make every element of her discomfort fictitious. She genuinely experiences the renewed requests as obstruction and repeated pressure after a refusal. [i069–074.]

Mitsuki’s demand becomes more specific. She wants Hime to continue at the café and rejects the argument that leaving is necessary for Mitsuki’s sake. That focus differs from demanding romantic reciprocation. Nevertheless, a valued person’s willingness to endure pain cannot automatically decide whether Hime must stay. The scene is ethically strongest where it asks Hime to stop treating Mitsuki’s future response as already known; it would be weakest if generalized into a right to reject anyone else’s work or contact boundary. The subsequent explicit positive choice is therefore indispensable evidence, not a decorative confirmation of what persistence supposedly entitled Mitsuki to obtain. [i075–076, i092–099.]

#### 3.3.2 Hime exposes a real façade in order to support a false total denial

Hime next discloses her practiced presentation to the coworkers who have not possessed the same access as Kanoko and Mitsuki. The disclosure contains truth: she deliberately manages how she appears and can be colder in private than her public manner suggests. She uses that truth to advance a much larger claim—that she does not care for Mitsuki at all and is merely avoiding the trouble created by a rejected admirer. The second claim does not follow from the first. [i077–082.]

The reader has unusually strong counterevidence because Hime’s earlier private narration has just established wanting to help Mitsuki, wanting her regard and not wanting to leave. A reading that accepts the new indifference claim as the final revelation of Hime’s authentic self would discard the volume’s controlled sequence of focalization. The more adequate account is that she weaponizes a familiar disclosure about her performance to make a separation seem less negotiable. Real self-exposure and a new lie can occur in the same speech. [i029–038, i077–082.]

Sumika’s objection shows how severe the words sound to someone hearing them. She sees a valued friendship apparently denied with deliberate cruelty. Her reaction is neither proof that the denial is true nor evidence that she already knows Hime’s whole private motive. It is a response to what has actually been said. This helps preserve the informational cost of Hime’s tactic: the intended audience is not simply a tool whose later feelings can be assumed repaired once Mitsuki understands. [i080–082.]

#### 3.3.3 Calling Hime a liar now protects what earlier exposure destroyed

The visual recall of childhood precedes Mitsuki’s declaration that the new account is a lie. The inversion is precise. Earlier, exposing Hime’s private words helped destroy her social security. Here, identifying a lie defends the reality of her care against a self-description designed to make that care unavailable. The same broad act—naming Hime’s deception—has a different object and consequence. It is not a claim that the original harmful exposure was secretly protective all along. [i083–087; V02/C01.]

Mitsuki grounds the challenge in conduct she has observed: Hime notices, helps and tries to protect important connections. She names the ordinary Shiraki Hime rather than treating the kind public person as an unrelated fictional character. This has explanatory force because it does not require every private complaint or instrumental ambition to vanish. Hime can be calculating and genuinely helpful; her façade can shelter real care as well as conceal less attractive motives. Mitsuki’s account would become excessive if it turned every Hime lie into benevolence. The present claim concerns this particular denial and the accumulated evidence that contradicts it. [i084–087.]

Most importantly, **the lie being rejected is not Hime’s nonromantic refusal**. Mitsuki does not prove that Hime has been lying about lacking romantic love. The ensuing conversation continues to accept the difference between their feelings. A future synthesis must keep this object of correction exact, because otherwise the scene could be misused to transform a valid refusal into a concealed invitation. The source’s own distinction is sharper than that. [i088–102.]

Hime then acknowledges that she is lying in order to separate. She asks why Mitsuki is preventing what appears necessary and how many more times she must hurt her. Mitsuki takes ownership of having confessed and of the pain that a truthful response can produce. She says she wants Hime’s real answer rather than a painless substitute. This revises their immediate relation: Hime no longer has to determine the acceptable cost of Mitsuki’s feeling entirely on Mitsuki’s behalf. It does not guarantee that Mitsuki will manage every later injury without resentment. [i088–091.]

#### 3.3.4 The fear underneath protection is finally spoken

Hime’s more vulnerable explanation concerns hatred. Repeatedly rejecting Mitsuki might eventually make Mitsuki hate her. The childhood memory gives that fear particular weight. Leaving through a lie becomes, in this account, a way to prevent the future conversion of care into rejection. The motive does not cancel concern for Mitsuki’s pain; it adds Hime’s own threatened position inside the relationship. A model that classified her departure as either wholly altruistic or wholly manipulative would miss this layered explanation. [i092–093.]

Mitsuki’s assurance arrives in the composed, kind presentation associated with Ayanokōji. Hime initially challenges the smile as something forced. Mitsuki reminds her that this effort to be kind was precisely what Hime had earlier recognized as real kindness. The episode returns to V06’s distinction between a performed manner and a false relation. Effort can be constitutive of care rather than proof that care is absent. At the same time, a reassuring performance cannot certify an unlimited future promise never to feel anger. The present scene supports a chosen disposition and an attempt to make it credible. [i094–095; V06/C01.]

The hand exchange then supplies affirmative action rather than only verbal inference. Mitsuki’s approach is received; Hime squeezes back. Hime states that she still wants to be sisters and does not want to leave Yano, then initiates an embrace. These are not words inferred from tears by an observer. They are new, positive choices that make continued participation mutually expressible. They must be recorded separately from the earlier coworkers’ requests and Mitsuki’s insistence; the latter did not already contain the former. [i095–099.]

#### 3.3.5 A renewed sister relationship is neither an erased refusal nor a finished permissions system

The immediate follow-through retains the asymmetry. Hime checks that Mitsuki understands the refusal. Mitsuki is pleased that she can say she likes Hime and still remain with her. Her statement about not expecting an answer should be read alongside the earlier evidence of hopes that exceeded her own initial account. It need not be treated as deliberately false, but it is not sufficient to rewrite the whole confession history as emotionally costless or expectation-free. [i100–101; V07/C08.]

Mitsuki then says that she would like to kiss Hime but has learned not to act on that desire. The line explicitly separates feeling, disclosure and action. Hime’s alarm shows that even announcing an unacted desire can affect the recipient. The promise to tell the truth has not settled whether every truth should be spoken at every moment or in every form. The manga makes this tension comic without making it trivial; the hotel’s final exchange will provide an immediate timing limit on an affectionate declaration. [i101; V08/41/i156–157.]

Hime describes the resulting bond as neither ordinary friendship nor a lover relationship, but their own sister relationship. This is a locally chosen description of how they will remain connected under different feelings. It gives the role more than a purely commercial meaning while stopping short of mutual romance. It is not a complete taxonomy of queer attachment, a permanent legal or moral contract, or a guarantee that the description will fit all future states. Its current force is practical: they have found a way to name continuation without pretending their desires are identical. [i102.]

The chapter therefore completes a real return negotiation. Later work and the trip substantiate that the agreement is more than a fleeting promise. But the source does not show a new employment contract, payroll change or administrative form, and none is needed for the bounded claim of resumed participation. The important distinction is between V07’s birthday-only return and V08’s chosen continuation followed by ordinary collective activity. [i098–102; V08/40/i105–119; V08/41/i158.]

### 3.4 Shift 40: a new agreement meets an unfamiliar setting

#### 3.4.1 A work trip is also an opportunity for ordinary enjoyment

The chapter opens with the hotel rather than the decision to travel, then supplies the planning retrospectively. This order should not be mistaken for two outings. The one-night, two-day trip serves work photography, with transport and accommodation described as business-covered. Those arrangements establish the characters’ stated practical opportunity. They do not independently establish a complete expense ledger, employment-law conclusion or unshown financial agreement. Six staff members participate in a setting that combines a job, shared enjoyment and personal proximity. [V08/40/i103–108.]

Hime’s pleasure in the opportunity is ordinary and concrete. Attractive surroundings, a trip whose principal expenses are covered and the chance to spend time outside familiar routine matter to her. Her response belongs to an everyday-preference model without becoming a total judgment that she returns only for material benefits. Her affirmative sister decision has already occurred before the trip is introduced. Chronology prevents the later perk from being used as the hidden complete cause of that decision. [i098–108.]

This scene also provides meaningful follow-through for the workplace. The story does not merely say the pair might work together someday. They are participating in a new assignment with coworkers after the birthday agreement. That supports resumed practical belonging without proving that the emotional problems have disappeared. The trip’s combined purposes will actually make those problems more visible: the same location provides attractive photographic settings, shared rooms and occasions of privacy that the café normally separates. [i105–119.]

At i109 Mitsuki takes Hime’s hand, releases it when addressed, and apologizes with a luggage explanation. Hime privately reads her as having become more candid. This is an observed contact-and-response sequence plus Hime’s interpretation, not an instance of Hime offering to carry Mitsuki’s bag or Mitsuki receiving practical help. The exchange cannot independently strengthen the earlier reciprocal-help findings; those retain their original evidence. Nor does this contact supply blanket permission for later acts. [i109; V05/C03; V06/C06.]

#### 3.4.2 Sharing a room does not answer every question about privacy

Rooming is proposed by sister pair and accepted. The relation that made continued café participation possible is now also an organizing principle for overnight space. This choice has a limited object: agreement to share a room. It should not be expanded into agreement to every form of contact, watching or intimacy that might occur there. The chapter’s point is partly that a familiar label can organize logistics without carrying a mutually understood set of personal permissions. [i110.]

The room’s ordinary details matter. Beds, a power outlet and a USB connection turn an emotionally loaded arrangement into practical decisions. They also prevent the setting from being read only as a predetermined romantic stage. Hime and Mitsuki are travelers attending to convenience as well as two people managing asymmetric attraction. The ordinary objects provide room for characterization: practical attention can remain competent even when interpretation of another person’s feelings is not. [i111.]

Changing clothes introduces discomfort that had not been specified when the room was accepted. Hime is aware of Mitsuki’s body and of the possibility of being looked at, but awareness alone does not establish reciprocal romantic attraction. Nor does Mitsuki’s conduct here uniquely establish a deliberate strategy to seduce Hime. Ordinary changing behavior, different privacy expectations, attraction and incomplete understanding remain relevant alternatives. The later conversation will give more explicit information than an interpretation based only on one glance or posture can supply. [i112–113; V08/41/i151–155.]

Hime’s problem is not solved by telling herself that a shared room is normal for female friends. The relation has ceased to be transparent under that default model: she now knows that the other participant’s interest is not exclusively friendly. This does not mean that friendship with a person who feels attraction is inherently impossible. It means that previously unexamined arrangements may require discussion once the participants know that the same situation carries different meanings for them. [i110–113.]

#### 3.4.3 Photography reveals both a credible role and the cost of a pose

The SK photography uses a real feature of Sumika’s life: reading. Her role does not require the invention of an entirely unrelated interest. Kanoko participates in making that familiar activity legible as a sister scene. The images can therefore be constructed work products and records of an actual interaction at the same time. Neither fact settles the full private relation. The later café review will make the particularity of Kanoko’s expression newly consequential. [i114–116; V08/41/i158.]

The HM photographs require a different negotiation. A proposed embrace is not accepted merely because they have agreed to remain sisters or because Hime herself initiated a hug during the return. Mitsuki says there has been too much touching. Mai withdraws the request and apologizes. This is a concrete instance of the institution accommodating a limit rather than automatically subordinating it to the production of an attractive image. The photographer’s desired pose is not treated as proof that a participant owes the pose. [i117–119.]

That response is particularly important after V01’s audience-mediated pressure. The same café can create coercive moments and later accept a refusal. Institutional analysis should track these actual procedures rather than assign a permanent essence of either exploitation or benevolence to the workplace. Here, the assignment continues without the disputed embrace. The result is useful counterevidence to an absolute claim that public sisterhood necessarily erases private boundaries. [i118–119; V01/C03/C04.]

Hime interprets Mitsuki’s refusal as protection of Hime’s comfort. That may be part of the explanation, but the source does not make the interpretation exhaustive. Mitsuki has her own relation to touch, desire and emotional strain; saying a pose is too much can also express her own limit. Hime’s gratitude is real evidence of how she receives the act, not direct access to every motive behind it. This becomes another example of benevolent mind-reading that will not suffice for the later bath arrangement. [i120–121.]

#### 3.4.4 An invented prior plan makes avoidance look like ordinary scheduling

The proposed bath arrangement confronts Hime with something the rooming agreement did not settle. Rather than immediately state that bathing together is uncomfortable, she says she already has a plan with Kanoko. The claim provides a socially ordinary explanation for separation. Its tactical value is that nobody has to be told that Mitsuki’s romantic interest changes how the situation feels. The cost is that the real boundary remains unspoken. [i122–124.]

Kanoko’s actual invitation then gives Hime the alternative she wanted. The temporal distinction matters: a subsequent invitation does not make the earlier claim about an already-existing plan truthful. It also does not mean the alternative is emotionally neutral. Kanoko has feelings and secrets of her own. Hime’s relative ease with her rests partly on a different distribution of knowledge, not on a complete and correctly classified comparison of the two friends. [i122–124; V08/41/i125, i135–136.]

The chapter ends by arranging a situation in which indirect avoidance has deferred, rather than resolved, the new problem. No party has yet jointly specified the relation between shared accommodation, bathing, changing and contact. Hime has found a temporary way out. The next chapter tests what happens when that way out no longer keeps the relevant people apart.

### 3.5 Shift 41: boundaries become usable when they become specific

#### 3.5.1 The same apparent ease can be produced by different knowledge

The opening returns to an earlier preparation moment involving Kanoko’s contact lenses and Sumika. It supplies information about the invitation’s context after Hime has already accepted it. This is not a new event after the room conversation or a second invitation. Sumika is asked to keep information private; Hime does not acquire the reader’s or Sumika’s fuller understanding of Kanoko’s desire. [V08/41/i125.]

In the bath sequence, Hime’s concern with gaze and privacy recalls the summer-uniform difficulty. The recurrence supports a context-sensitive model of discomfort, not a retrospective diagnosis or an inferred sexual identity. She can notice another person’s appearance, compare herself, feel embarrassed and wish not to be looked at without those responses uniquely identifying romantic attraction. The analysis needs only the depicted configuration—where attention falls, who is aware of it, and how the participants respond—to establish the scene’s relational stakes. [i127–134; V04/C16–C18.]

Hime experiences Kanoko as an easier companion because she does not classify Kanoko’s interest in the way she now classifies Mitsuki’s. The reader has substantially different evidence. This makes the comparison an information-state problem as well as a temperament problem. It would be inaccurate either to declare Hime foolish for not knowing an undisclosed fact or to endorse her assessment as an objective demonstration that one person’s interest is wholly nonromantic. Her comfort is a real state reached from incomplete information. [i135–136; V03/C02; V04/C13.]

Nothing here retrospectively grants permission for Kanoko’s earlier private actions or converts secrecy into harmlessness. Nor does the presence of an unspoken attraction mean that every ordinary shared activity is secretly an assault on Hime’s wishes. The source supports a narrower, important distinction: Hime’s choices about proximity are being made under unequal knowledge, and the eventual effect of fuller knowledge remains untested. [i125, i135–136.]

#### 3.5.2 A coworker’s comment can be corrected without a public crisis

Sumika’s comment about another participant’s body is checked by Mai, who invokes an ordinary norm against such conduct. The scene includes a source-used harassment classification; this document does not convert that remark into an independent legal determination. The practical observation is that a boundary is named and the comment is not defended as an unrestricted privilege of friendly familiarity. Sumika accepts the correction. [i137–139.]

The small exchange belongs alongside the more difficult HM negotiation rather than outside it as mere comedy. A shared group space does not dissolve each participant’s entitlement to avoid unwelcome evaluation. At the same time, the correction does not require expelling Sumika from the group or classifying every earlier joke as the same act. It supplies a local procedure: identify a problem, respond to it, and continue without treating the limit as an attack on the whole relationship. That is one of the volume’s recurring possibilities. [i137–139.]

#### 3.5.3 Asking for explicit words does not make visible withdrawal meaningless

Mitsuki’s arrival removes the separation Hime had tried to achieve through scheduling. Hime’s spoken reassurance and bodily retreat do not match comfortably. Mitsuki wants a clear statement rather than another situation in which she must infer a hidden wish from behavior. That request has a genuine basis in the pair’s history: Hime has recently denied real care, invented a waiting explanation and used an untrue prior plan to avoid a direct boundary. [i140–143; V08/39/i072–093; V08/40/i122–124.]

Yet the need for words is not a reason to disregard present unease. Approach can itself increase the difficulty of speaking. The scene permits two observations at once: Hime has not given an adequate verbal account of this particular limit, and her discomfort is sufficiently visible that proceeding as though the positive words settled everything is not a reliable method. The later repair will improve the available information; it does not retroactively make every earlier approach considerate. [i140–145.]

Hime leaves upset. She then develops a more suspicious interpretation of Mitsuki’s behavior, wondering whether the interaction was meant to make her feel something. This is Hime’s hypothesis in the aftermath of discomfort. It is not a direct depiction of Mitsuki deciding on a seduction strategy. The distinction between experienced effect and established intention must remain explicit, especially where an interpretive claim could otherwise turn every awkward action into a calculated test of Hime’s refusal. [i146–148.]

This problem differs from the original question of whether Hime likes Mitsuki. That question has acquired a positive, nonromantic answer. The immediate issue is what being liked permits in a shared physical setting. The broad return agreement did not answer it. A model that treated continued sisterhood as sufficient permission would reproduce the misunderstanding the chapter is exposing. [i140–148.]

#### 3.5.4 The anticipated approach is replaced by an actual apology

Back in the room, Hime anticipates another difficult approach. What Mitsuki actually does is apologize. The panel sequence must not be collapsed into a single uninterrupted action in which the feared event occurred. The contrast between anticipation and reality matters for Hime’s state: she has begun preparing for a more threatening or intrusive encounter than the one Mitsuki now offers. Her expectation is evidence of apprehension; the apology is evidence of Mitsuki’s subsequent conduct. [i149–150.]

Hime then initiates the conversation that follows. This initiative is an important continuation of the reciprocal-care theme. She does not only receive an apology or wait for Mitsuki to infer the correct policy. She contributes information about why the current arrangement has become difficult. The repair therefore has two authors, without equalizing all responsibility for the episode that made it necessary. [i150–153.]

The parties revisit what Hime meant by being unable to touch, and what later acceptance of Mitsuki’s feelings appeared to change. A broad statement made during an earlier crisis is being tested against a new context. Mitsuki has reason to want distinctions rather than a rule that changes invisibly; Hime has reason to discover limits she had not anticipated before sharing the room and bath setting. Neither should be modeled as possessing a complete permissions table that she maliciously withheld. The relevant improvement is an explicit attempt to construct one together. [i151–153.]

The conversation also resists a simple “communicate better” moral in which all discomfort is blamed on the person who failed to formulate it elegantly. The pages have already shown how fear, embarrassment and uncertainty make speech difficult. Making a limit verbal is useful; it is not what creates the limit’s legitimacy. A responsible reconstruction should predict both a need for clarification and a reason to pause when the other person visibly withdraws. [i140–155.]

#### 3.5.5 The final rules separate actions, contexts and timing

Hime initiates hand contact as part of explaining what is possible. The list that follows distinguishes holding hands, occasional hugs, bathing together and changing clothes nearby. Handholding is acceptable. Hugs are acceptable **occasionally**. Bathing together is not something she can manage. She asks for changing privacy, phrased more softly as a request to avoid it where possible. The wording therefore has differentiated force; it is not a uniform ban on all touch or a blanket acceptance of bodily proximity. [i154–155.]

The exact limitation of this list should be preserved. It does **not** contain a new, separately spoken kissing clause. Earlier in the volume Mitsuki expressly says she wants to kiss but refrains. Nothing in the new permission for handholding or occasional hugs extends that permission to kissing. This is a source-bounded inference from the separation of acts, not an invented quotation. The record should cite i101 for the kiss-restraint discussion and i155 for the later list, rather than make one page contain both. [V08/39/i101; V08/41/i155.]

The hand exchange also makes the limits more than abstract rules imposed from a distance. Hime demonstrates a form of contact she chooses while explaining forms she cannot accept. That combination counters the premise that any no must mean she wants no relationship. It equally counters the reverse premise that one yes commits her to everything adjacent to it. The relation becomes more workable precisely because the acts are not bundled together. [i154–155.]

Mitsuki responds by declaring affection. Hime says not now. The timing word is consequential: the response does not repeat the entire romantic refusal or withdraw the return agreement. It limits what she wants to receive at this moment, after an already demanding exchange. Mitsuki initially questions the limit, then accepts it. This is a small but observable instance of uptake. It is not proof that her desire has disappeared, that she will never need reminding or that the pair has become permanently skilled at these negotiations. [i156–157.]

The closing reflection places conflict inside continuing attachment rather than outside it. People with different wishes may be able to remain connected through adjustment. That is supported by this episode’s actions, not by a guarantee about all future relationships. The volume’s broad emotional movement—from leaving to prevent every future injury, toward staying while specifying manageable limits—has acquired a concrete local method. [i157.]

#### 3.5.6 The café coda opens another question rather than concluding it

The final page is a later café scene reviewing photographs from the trip. Mai notices Kanoko’s expression in the pictures associated with Sumika’s photography. Sumika’s surprised response makes the image newly significant for her. The scene shifts attention from what a participant thought she was doing at the time to what becomes visible in a record afterward. [i158; V08/40/i114–116.]

A photograph is not an independent detector of romantic feeling. It captures an expression in a specific interaction, and another character interprets its salience. The source now supports a question about Sumika’s attention to Kanoko and about the personal effect of their time together. It does not establish a new reciprocal romantic pair, a confession, or a transfer of Kanoko’s attachment away from Hime. The openness is the endpoint, not a gap that later-volume knowledge should fill in advance. [i158.]

This page also resolves the volume’s temporal endpoint. The mainline has moved beyond the hotel-room conversation to later shared café activity. That follow-through supports continued workplace participation after the new agreement. It does not supply a complete account of the journey home or an indefinitely stable future. The freeze belongs at i158, with the precise claim that the café review has occurred and the newly visible SK question remains undecided.

## 4. Separately bounded supplement and edition matter

### 4.1 Shift 41.5: learning a preference without making it a romantic answer

The clothing short follows Hime’s return to work, but its exact position relative to the hotel trip and the café coda is not fixed. The short adds a useful relationship-conditioned counterexample to a model that predicts only blunt rejection. [i160–161.]

Because the short’s fine chronology remains open, it should be attached to this developmental period rather than used as proof that every difficulty after i158 has already been resolved.

### 9.1 Return is an affirmative decision, not a victory over refusal

The birthday continuation cannot be justified solely by saying that Hime secretly wanted to stay. The reader receives that wish before the coworkers do; the gap is part of the story. The defensible claim of mutual continuation rests on i098–099, where Hime says what she wants and acts on it. Earlier pressure remains earlier pressure. The episode is not a general lesson that sufficiently determined friends may decide which refusals deserve respect.

At the same time, treating Hime’s first announced departure as the only possible authentic expression would erase her later agency. People can revise decisions after another participant supplies a different account of what costs they are willing to bear. The source’s ethical distinction is not between never questioning and always persisting. It is between clarification that eventually receives an actual new choice, and a claim that one person’s interpretation itself grants control over another’s future.

### 9.2 Specific permissions are not transferable goods

Hime’s birthday embrace, her return embrace and her later handholding do not create a cumulative entitlement to more intimate acts. Nor does sharing a room transfer decision-making over bathing or changing. Each event has an actor, setting and immediate meaning. The final list improves their coordination because it does not require one total answer to every possible manifestation of affection. Mitsuki’s refusal of a posed hug also belongs in this account: the person with romantic feelings can have limits rather than always being the requester.

### 9.3 An apology changes the available next action, not the preceding event

Mitsuki’s room apology is important because it contradicts Hime’s feared next encounter and permits a new conversation. It does not make the earlier approach harmless, prove deliberate wrongdoing or replace the missing details with a complete confession of motive. Similarly, Kanoko’s V07 apology for a strike remains a real local act despite V08’s new clothing grip. Repair must be evaluated in terms of its stated object and subsequent behavior, not used as either an all-purpose absolution or a promise whose imperfection makes it retroactively unreal.
