# Tōtsuki, meritocracy, coercion, and institutional legitimacy

## Thesis

Tōtsuki is neither a fraudulent school hiding empty elites nor a meritocracy redeemed by spectacular results. It concentrates real expertise, resources, networks, and opportunities while repeatedly making continuation depend on opaque discretion, inherited office, disproportionate stakes, and exceptional intervention. The series' institutional question is not whether excellence exists. It is who may author, refuse, appeal, remain, and recover when judgment is adverse.

## Selection and resource power

The academy begins by claiming the right to discard nearly everyone. Erina's palate, entrance gatekeeping, Elite Ten authority, and society resources turn taste into control of space and future (`01-108`-`02-181`). The donburi conflict shows that institutional rank governs facilities and legitimacy, not only grades.

Camp and buffet tasks can produce valuable learning, but the Shinomiya expulsion exposes remedy dependence. Megumi's future survives because Dōjima uses exceptional authority to create a mock Food War (`03-060`-`04-143`). A defensible outcome does not prove ordinary due process.

The Autumn Election improves public comparability while retaining selection, judge discretion, and heterogeneous criteria (`07-006`-`13-042`). Scarcity can make results meaningful without making them universal human rank.

## Food War as constrained procedure

Food Wars can convert diffuse conflict into explicit theme, stakes, tasting, result, and consequence. They can also encode coercion.

Eizan buys judges and removes tasting while preserving form; direct property force operates beside the match (`17-112`-`18-106`). Once actual tasting is restored, Soma wins 3-0 and the dorm order is rescinded. Isshiki then designs stronger safeguards: negotiated themes/dates, random external judges, and anti-contact rules (`18-086`-`18-178`).

Those reforms still operate inside a choice between dissolution and victory. Fairer comparison can remain nested within an illegitimate option set.

## Central: valid accession, illegitimate sovereignty

Six Elite Ten votes make Azami's directorship operative (`16-158`-`17-020`). The constitutional mechanism does not legitimate the program.

Central abolishes autonomous societies, reserves creation for a selected few, mandates reproduction, captures resources and transport, and uses expulsion as enforcement (`17-006`-`24-178`). It can teach excellent technique and win locally valid ballots. Skill and political legitimacy do not commute.

The Régiment responds with negotiated reciprocal stakes and public judging because ordinary remedies have failed. Its final 3-0 rebel course victory defeats Central's claim and prevents compulsory service obligations from activating (`24-098`-`30-184`). The victory is constitutional change by exceptional contest, not evidence that the institution possessed routine accountability.

## Post-Central succession

V31 enacts consequences: Erina becomes headmaster, Soma First Seat, rebels occupy Elite Ten seats, teachers return, and the academy resumes (`31-006`-`31-054`). Rank is more challengeable and missions more distributed.

The structure still relies on Nakiri inheritance, elite approval, and high-stakes assessment. Staff shortages created by the purge open access to Asahi under a false teacher identity (`32-010`-`32-038`). Repair without verification creates a new vulnerability.

The beach exam demonstrates both value and severity. Public operation, repair, procurement, price, and teamwork matter; collective Elite Ten forfeiture and unequal facilities remain blunt mechanisms (`32-108`-`32-190`).

## Noir and autonomy without consent

Monarca rejects mafia-client domination but converts chef autonomy into confiscation of inns, tools, and defeated labor (`31-073`-`31-150`). Noir repeatedly shows that freedom for the powerful cook can become conquest over everyone else.

BLUE includes underground cooks, widening the field beyond licensed prestige. Yet it supplies no stable common grammar for safety, provenance, or due process. Armed judges, weapons, unequal seeds, sabotage, abduction, and improvised rulings coexist with excellent cuisine (`33-026`-`35-081`). Inclusion is not legitimacy.

## BLUE as captured public authority

Mana combines parent, ill beneficiary, organizer, evaluator, and selector roles. She secretly rewrites Erina's route into a Noir gauntlet to solve a family problem (`35-009`-`35-044`). Expert suffering explains the decision without authorizing it.

Senior WGO judges improve technical description. Soma's 3-0 semifinal result is locally valid after both plates are tasted (`35-168`-`36-075`). The bracket's unequal procedure remains unrepaired.

The final record is also incomplete. `Présent` identifies Erina champion and Soma runner-up but gives no vote score or detailed judging (`36-153`-`36-157`). Canonical placement and procedural transparency are separate.

## Family as institution

Nakiri family governance assigns public and private roles before consent. Erina becomes evaluator and proposed cure; Alice's letters are burned; Mana's illness is concealed; Senzaemon builds the Jewel Generation around one child's risk (`16-158`-`19-087`; `35-091`-`36-117`).

The Jewel Generation works: plural peers help Erina avoid solitary collapse. Its covert recruitment and citizenship maneuvering remain ethically fraught. Later self-authored friendships do not retroactively consent to their arrangement.

Azami and Asahi re-enter the family, and Erina refuses another solitary departure (`36-197`-`36-224`). Inclusion may create conditions for accountability; it is not restitution to everyone harmed.

## End-state assessment

Tōtsuki ends under Erina with distributed adult outcomes: owners, teachers, researchers, travelers, reviewers, and working chefs (`36-209`-`36-224`). This is more plural than Central's prescribed reproduction.

No democratic redesign, transparent appeals system, complete reinstatement ledger, conflict-of-interest repair, or safe appointment record is shown. The end state is `pluralized hierarchy`, not reformed meritocracy.

The series' most legitimate evaluation occurs outside grand office. At the final diner meal, peers say a dish is bad; no one loses property, status, body, or belonging; the cook begins again (`36-238`-`36-243`). Institutional legitimacy is measured by whether adverse judgment leaves a future.
