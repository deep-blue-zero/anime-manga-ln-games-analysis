# SHOKUGEKI_SOMA_ANALYTICAL_METHOD

## Purpose

This document governs the initial Yukihira Soma character-modeling pass over a longitudinal sample of the original Japanese manga *Shokugeki no Soma*.

The project is deliberately narrower than a conventional full-series literary reread. Its principal goal is not to summarize plot, evaluate every supporting character, or produce an exhaustive thematic history of the work. Its goal is to construct a source-grounded model capable of answering a harder practical question:

> Given sufficient situational context, can we predict how Yukihira Soma would speak, behave, decide, joke, compete, teach, comfort, disagree, improvise, learn, respond to failure, and relate to another person in a situation that never literally occurs in the manga?

A successful model should reconstruct not only recognizable surface traits, but also the conditions under which those traits change.

The intended end state is therefore a behavioral and linguistic model rather than a descriptive character essay.

## Governing principles

1. The original Japanese manga is primary evidence.
2. Soma is the principal analytical object; plot is contextual support.
3. Dialogue is interpreted in context, not extracted as isolated catchphrases.
4. Personality claims are conditional hypotheses, not adjective lists.
5. Counterevidence is preserved rather than harmonized away.
6. Relationship-specific behavior is modeled separately from a generic baseline.
7. Earlier hypotheses remain revisable through the full sampled sequence.
8. Late volumes are used as holdout tests rather than silently informing the early model.
9. Exact Japanese wording, visual behavior, and source location remain recoverable from every major claim.
10. The method must remain compatible with a later full-series literary reread without requiring a new project root.

## Primary source boundary

Initial source set:

- V01
- V03
- V08
- V13
- V19
- V25
- V30
- V36

Read in numerical order.

The first six volumes form the principal model-construction set. V30 is a first longitudinal holdout. V36 is the final endpoint holdout.

No unsampled volume should be used to fill a gap unless it is later formally added to the corpus and recorded in the source inventory.

External summaries, fan wikis, translations, anime dialogue, and remembered series knowledge may be useful for orientation, but they do not establish a claim in this project. If later used for comparison, they must be labeled as secondary or supplementary evidence.

## Analytical priority hierarchy

For each sampled volume, analytical attention should normally be distributed in this order:

1. Soma's original Japanese dialogue.
2. Soma's actions and immediate decisions.
3. Facial expression, posture, gesture, gaze, timing, panel composition, and reaction shots.
4. Relationship-specific differences in his language or conduct.
5. Other characters' reactions to or descriptions of Soma.
6. Cooking choices insofar as they reveal cognition, values, pedagogy, or personality.
7. Plot context only insofar as it is necessary to interpret the above.

Plot summary is not prohibited. It is subordinated.

A sequential reading should not spend several pages reconstructing a contest when two or three paragraphs establish all context necessary to analyze Soma's decisions within it.

## What this method is not

This is not initially:

- a chapter-by-chapter plot synopsis;
- a complete thematic study of *Shokugeki no Soma*;
- an Erina, Megumi, Joichiro, or ensemble monograph;
- a food-history project;
- an attempt to transcribe every Soma speech balloon;
- a frequency-analysis project detached from pragmatics;
- an anime performance analysis;
- a definitive full-series synthesis.

Those may later be added as distinct analytical responsibilities.

# 1. Evidence model

## 1.1 Evidence classes

Use the following evidence labels when useful:

- `JT` - Japanese textual evidence: Soma's exact written dialogue or narration-relevant text.
- `VB` - visual-behavioral evidence: expression, gesture, posture, panel timing, gaze, body position, or action.
- `AC` - action/choice evidence: a decision or behavior whose meaning is not reducible to dialogue.
- `RS` - relationship/register evidence: a contrast caused by interlocutor, social role, or relationship history.
- `OC` - other-character evidence: how another character describes, interprets, fears, trusts, or reacts to Soma.
- `CC` - cooking-cognition evidence: planning, experimentation, adaptation, evaluation, teaching, or problem-solving revealed through food work.
- `CX` - contextual evidence necessary to interpret the scene.

Evidence classes are descriptive, not confidence ratings.

## 1.2 Claim status

Major longitudinal claims use the established transition vocabulary:

- `PRESERVE`
- `STRENGTHEN`
- `REVISE`
- `DOWNGRADE`
- `REJECT`
- `OPEN`

A claim should not be called an invariant merely because it appears strongly once.

## 1.3 Confidence

Use four confidence levels:

### HIGH

Supported across multiple sampled volumes and more than one social or emotional context, with no unresolved major contradiction.

### MEDIUM

Repeated or strongly supported, but dependent on a particular relationship, role, or class of situations.

### LOW

Based on limited evidence, one unusually strong scene, or an inference requiring later testing.

### OPEN

Plausible but intentionally unresolved.

## 1.4 Fact, inference, and interpretation

Keep these distinct.

### Observed fact

Directly visible or directly written in the source.

Example form:

> Soma answers in plain speech despite the interlocutor's institutional status.

### Inference

A proposition that best explains several observed facts.

Example form:

> This suggests that formal rank alone does not strongly regulate his default register.

### Interpretation

A broader conceptual model.

Example form:

> Soma may distinguish social hierarchy from earned culinary competence.

Later evidence may preserve the observation while revising the inference or interpretation.

# 2. Source locators

Every major evidence entry should be recoverable.

Preferred locator grammar:

`VXX / chapter or chapter title / CBZ image index / page region or panel description`

Example:

`V08 / Ch. XX / image 143 / upper-right panel`

If printed page numbers are visible and reliable, record them in addition to CBZ image index, not instead of it.

For double-page spreads, record both image indices when applicable.

Do not rely on an informal phrase such as "during the tournament" as the sole locator for an important linguistic claim.

# 3. Per-volume reading protocol

Each sampled volume receives four principal passes after source integrity has been checked.

## Pass A - contextual read

Read the volume in full before constructing the detailed Soma extraction.

Record only enough to establish:

- Soma's social position at the start of the volume;
- major immediate pressures;
- important interlocutors;
- whether the dominant mode is competition, cooperation, instruction, uncertainty, defeat, success, institutional conflict, ordinary life, or another condition;
- meaningful changes in relationship state;
- any event without which later Soma behavior would be misread.

Do not write the final model during this pass.

The purpose is to avoid mistaking an isolated joke, bluff, or provocation for a general disposition.

## Pass B - Soma extraction

Return to every high-information Soma scene.

For each important unit of evidence, capture:

- locator;
- short Japanese quotation or tightly bounded exchange when wording matters;
- interlocutor(s);
- speech function;
- register;
- visible behavior;
- immediate goal;
- emotional pressure;
- relationship context;
- preliminary interpretation;
- confidence.

Do not transcribe low-information dialogue merely to enlarge the corpus.

High-information material includes lines or actions that reveal:

- how Soma handles status;
- how he handles not knowing;
- how he reacts to being underestimated;
- how he reacts to genuine superiority;
- how he reacts to failure;
- how he supports another person;
- how he teaches;
- how he disagrees;
- how he provokes;
- what makes him angry;
- what fails to make him angry;
- how he behaves when someone is emotionally vulnerable;
- how he changes when the stakes become collective rather than personal;
- what kinds of uncertainty produce caution versus excitement;
- what he treats as serious even when his surface affect remains casual.

## Pass C - adversarial reread

Actively search the volume for evidence against the strongest current claims.

For every important model statement, ask:

- What scene in this volume most strongly contradicts it?
- Is the contradiction genuine or context-specific?
- Does the claim need a narrower condition?
- Did an apparent trait come from one relationship rather than Soma generally?
- Did the model confuse confidence with arrogance, simplicity with lack of thought, calmness with lack of emotion, or irreverence with hostility?

The purpose is not to force every claim into balance. It is to prevent confirmation bias.

## Pass D - model delta and prediction

At the end of the volume:

1. List the major claims that changed.
2. Assign transition states.
3. Update the relevant longitudinal ledgers.
4. State what is still unknown.
5. Generate explicit predictions for later sampled volumes.

Predictions should be falsifiable at the level of behavior or speech function.

Weak prediction:

> Soma will still be confident.

Better prediction:

> When confronted by a demonstrably superior technique, Soma will show curiosity and problem-solving interest before status anxiety, unless the situation is tied to a relationship or failure condition not yet represented in the sample.

# 4. Japanese linguistic model

The project should model Soma's Japanese as a system of social choices rather than a bag of catchphrases.

## 4.1 Self-reference and address

Track:

- first-person forms;
- second-person forms;
- surname versus given name;
- honorific presence or absence;
- nicknames;
- occupational or status titles;
- changes in address over time;
- unusual switches during conflict, intimacy, teaching, or formal encounters.

The relationship register matrix should preserve who elicits each pattern.

## 4.2 Politeness and register

Track:

- plain versus polite speech;
- mixed-register speech;
- deliberate or comic over-politeness;
- casual speech toward higher-status figures;
- serious register shifts;
- whether respect for expertise produces different language from respect for rank;
- whether emotional vulnerability produces more direct, less stylized, or otherwise altered speech.

Do not infer disrespect from plain speech alone.

## 4.3 Morphology and recurring constructions

Track recurrent identity-bearing features, including:

- sentence-final particles;
- contractions;
- colloquial negatives;
- imperatives;
- requests;
- invitations;
- modality;
- hedges;
- certainty markers;
- challenge constructions;
- evaluative expressions;
- repeated lexical choices.

Frequency is useful only when paired with function and context.

## 4.4 Pragmatics

Pragmatic modeling is more important than surface imitation.

For each recurrent situation, determine how Soma actually performs the act:

- disagreement;
- challenge;
- reassurance;
- apology;
- praise;
- criticism;
- teaching;
- refusal;
- request;
- admission of ignorance;
- admission of error;
- response to praise;
- response to contempt;
- response to another person's distress;
- acceptance of defeat;
- declaration of intent.

A future reconstruction should imitate these functions even when no canonical catchphrase applies.

## 4.5 Textual prosody

Manga lacks acoustic prosody, but it contains textual and visual timing.

Track when analytically useful:

- fragments versus full explanations;
- abrupt answers;
- pauses;
- ellipses;
- repeated words;
- laughter markers;
- balloon size and shape;
- emphatic typography;
- silent reaction panels;
- delayed response across panels;
- facial shift before or after a line.

This material may later be compared with anime audio, but the manga model remains independently authoritative for written characterization.

# 5. Behavioral model

Avoid trait scores such as "competitive 10/10" or "friendly 8/10."

Model personality as conditional rules.

Each major rule should take a form such as:

> Under condition X, Soma tends to choose response Y, unless modifier Z is present.

The model should cover at least the following domains.

## 5.1 Competition and status

Questions:

- What does winning mean to him?
- What does losing mean?
- How does he treat stronger versus weaker opponents?
- Does prestige change his behavior?
- What kinds of opponents provoke him?
- What kinds of boasting are playful rather than defensive?

## 5.2 Learning and curiosity

Track:

- response to unfamiliar techniques;
- willingness to ask questions;
- imitation versus transformation;
- how quickly he revises a plan;
- whether embarrassment inhibits learning;
- treatment of people who know something he does not.

## 5.3 Risk and uncertainty

Track:

- action threshold under incomplete information;
- experimentation;
- tolerance for failure;
- consequences that actually make him cautious;
- whether risk changes when other people bear the cost.

## 5.4 Hierarchy and authority

Distinguish:

- formal authority;
- institutional prestige;
- culinary competence;
- seniority;
- family authority;
- coercive power.

Do not collapse all of these into a single "irreverence" category.

## 5.5 Care and social support

Track:

- how he notices distress;
- whether he asks directly;
- whether he uses food, action, humor, challenge, reassurance, or presence;
- whether support becomes more verbal with particular people;
- when he gives another person autonomy versus taking over a problem.

## 5.6 Conflict

Track:

- provocation;
- insult tolerance;
- escalation;
- de-escalation;
- response to unfairness;
- response to contempt for cooking or cooks;
- response to attacks on friends or institutions he values.

## 5.7 Failure

Track separately:

- immediate affect;
- public behavior;
- private reflection if available;
- subsequent adaptation;
- self-blame;
- external blame;
- curiosity;
- renewed challenge;
- situations in which failure genuinely destabilizes him.

## 5.8 Emotion

Do not equate low melodramatic display with shallow emotion.

Track departures from baseline:

- anger;
- embarrassment;
- surprise;
- fear;
- disappointment;
- admiration;
- affection;
- concern;
- grief if present;
- determination.

Ask what produces the departure and how quickly he returns to baseline.

## 5.9 Cooking cognition

Cooking is one of the principal ways Soma's thought process becomes externally visible.

Track:

- problem decomposition;
- sensory inference;
- improvisation;
- constraint handling;
- use of accumulated diner experience;
- willingness to test strange combinations;
- adaptation after new information;
- teaching through demonstration;
- evaluation of another cook's intentions;
- distinction between pleasing a diner, defeating an opponent, and proving an idea.

Do not convert the project into a recipe catalog.

# 6. Relationship-specific modeling

A generic Soma model is insufficient.

Maintain distinct profiles for recurrent relationships when enough evidence exists.

At minimum, watch for differences involving:

- Erina;
- Megumi;
- Joichiro;
- peers at Polar Star;
- serious rivals;
- teachers and institutional authorities;
- strangers or ordinary diners;
- weaker or inexperienced cooks;
- people who openly dislike or underestimate him.

For each relationship or category, track:

- address terms;
- politeness;
- teasing intensity;
- willingness to expose uncertainty;
- willingness to praise;
- unsolicited assistance;
- competitive framing;
- explanatory patience;
- social distance;
- response to the person's distress;
- response to disagreement;
- changes over sampled time.

If evidence is sparse, mark the profile underdetermined.

# 7. Longitudinal design and holdouts

## 7.1 Model construction set

Use the following volumes to build and revise the provisional model:

- V01 - baseline construction;
- V03 - early contextual test;
- V08 - competitive model;
- V13 - failure and limit calibration;
- V19 - institutional and ideological pressure;
- V25 - collective action and mature rivalry.

After V25, freeze a provisional model snapshot before close-reading V30.

## 7.2 First holdout: V30

V30 tests whether the model generated from V01-V25 predicts later Soma.

Before detailed Soma extraction, record predictions for major scenes when feasible:

- likely speech act;
- likely register;
- likely emotional posture;
- likely behavioral choice;
- behavior considered unlikely.

Then compare against the actual source.

The test is functional, not verbatim. Exact wording is not required for a successful prediction.

After V30, revise the model only through explicit claim transitions.

## 7.3 Final holdout: V36

Freeze the post-V30 model before close-reading V36.

V36 tests endpoint stability and late-series change.

The final model must distinguish:

- stable kernel;
- genuine development;
- relationship-specific change;
- context-specific exceptions;
- earlier claims that failed.

# 8. Sequential character-reading artifact structure

Each sampled reading should normally contain:

1. YAML authority metadata.
2. Source identity and scope.
3. Minimal contextual state.
4. Soma-at-entry model snapshot.
5. Japanese voice evidence.
6. Behavioral evidence.
7. Relationship/register observations.
8. Cooking-as-cognition observations.
9. Emotion and pressure observations.
10. Counterevidence and adversarial findings.
11. Claim transitions.
12. Soma-at-exit model delta.
13. Predictions carried forward.
14. Evidence locator table.

No fixed word count is mandatory.

As a default, a focused character reading may be substantially shorter than a conventional full-volume deep reading. Plot synopsis should remain a minority of the document unless unusual context genuinely requires more.

# 10. Final character model

Canonical future filename:

[`SHOKUGEKI_SOMA_FINAL_CHARACTER_MODEL.md`](../../../provenance/drive-artifacts/DRIVE_ARTIFACT_REFERENCE_INDEX.md)

The final model should be organized for reconstruction use.

## 10.1 Character kernel

The dispositions that remain stable across the sample.

## 10.2 Context modifiers

Rules showing when the kernel changes under pressure, uncertainty, failure, hierarchy, competition, care, or collective stakes.

## 10.3 Relationship modifiers

Specific changes produced by recurring people or social categories.

## 10.4 Linguistic model

A practical description of how Soma sounds in Japanese, including what should not be imitated indiscriminately.

## 10.5 Generative constraints

Classify proposed behavior as:

- strongly expected;
- plausible and context-dependent;
- unusual but supportable;
- inconsistent with the observed model unless substantial new development is supplied.

Good reconstruction depends as much on ruling out implausible behavior as on producing recognizable behavior.

# 12. Future anime-audio calibration

A later optional audio phase may add approximately 30-60 minutes of carefully selected Japanese anime material after the manga model is stable.

Its purpose would be to add:

- acoustic prosody;
- pitch movement;
- speaking rate;
- pauses and breath;
- laughter;
- vocal contraction under seriousness;
- differences between relaxed confidence and genuine agitation.

Anime performance should calibrate the written model, not replace it.

# 13. Compatibility with a later full-series reread

If a full 36-volume literary reread is commissioned later:

- preserve this project root;
- do not rename or discard the Soma-model artifacts;
- add general volume artifacts under the same root using the distinct role `DEEP_READING`;
- retain the current sample documents as `SOMA_CHARACTER_READING` artifacts;
- extend source inventory to V01-V36;
- add thematic, institutional, ensemble, visual, food-culture, and full-series synthesis layers only when analytically justified;
- route any later contradiction through explicit claim transition rather than silently rewriting historical snapshots.

This avoids forcing a character-modeling artifact to become a general literary analysis after the fact.

# 14. Governing warning against archetype collapse

Do not begin with a finished list such as:

- confident;
- competitive;
- laid-back;
- irreverent;
- creative;
- friendly.

Those may ultimately be useful descriptive words, but they are hypotheses to be decomposed.

For each apparent trait, ask what mechanism better explains it.

Examples of distinctions the source must adjudicate:

- arrogance versus low status anxiety;
- irreverence versus rank indifference;
- emotional simplicity versus low self-consciousness;
- low emotional display versus low empathy;
- competitiveness for ego versus competitiveness for learning;
- recklessness versus unusually high failure tolerance;
- friendliness versus low social defensiveness;
- confidence versus accurate self-knowledge.

These are questions, not predetermined conclusions.

The method succeeds only if later evidence is allowed to produce answers the initial character stereotype would not have predicted.
