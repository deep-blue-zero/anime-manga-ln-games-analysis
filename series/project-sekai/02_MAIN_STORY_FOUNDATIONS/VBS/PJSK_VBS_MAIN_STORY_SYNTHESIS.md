## The causal model

Vivid BAD SQUAD forms when a common destination ceases to function only as a test of who deserves admission and becomes a project people can choose together. RAD WEEKEND initially gives An and Akito an absolute horizon, but the four do not arrive at that horizon through the same experience. An receives an admired paternal/community example; Akito finds the object of commitment after an earlier aspiration proved shallow; Kohane discovers musical desire through An; Toya begins outside classical music in opposition to paternal control and learns enjoyment through Akito. The different origins create the central problem of legitimacy.

The foundation does not solve that problem by pretending all four have always meant the same thing. It demonstrates how a borrowed or oppositional beginning can become a personally endorsed present desire. Kohane learns that wanting to sing with An is *her* wish, not a defective version of An's original dream. Toya learns that wanting to remain beside Akito and believing in his dream already count as a meaningful commitment, despite an origin in escape. The two experiential entrants become able to say what they want, while the two apparently certain aspirants learn what their partners contribute.

This is why there are two crises rather than a single novice-training story. The public debut asks whether Kohane can continue when support ceases to guarantee safety. The BAD DOGS rupture asks whether Toya can accept support without treating dependence as disqualification. Both crises concern relation and ownership, but their immediate fears differ. Kohane fears failure and burdening An; Toya fears that his very presence compromises Akito's serious dream. The first must choose to return after failure; the second must stop interpreting departure as the responsible form of care.

## Starting configuration: asymmetry supplies both resources and risks

An's confidence is rooted in an already functioning musical world. WEEKEND GARAGE provides work, hospitality, performances, familiar adults, and the history of companions who helped, fought, and sang together. She refuses technically promising partners who dismiss the benchmark, then chooses Kohane because their joint singing excites her. That decision is discerning in one dimension and underinformed in another: An hears weaknesses in rhythm but has not even asked whether Kohane has performed at events. Her promise of mutual aid is foundational, yet its initial implementation mostly flows toward the beginner (`PJSK:main:street_01_03:01:002:0027–0036`; `street_01_04:01:002:0005–0036`).

Kohane's initial self-account is more incapacitated than her behavior. She knows the classroom answer, fulfills an errand despite fear, researches an arrangement, and opens the café door again after fleeing once. The low-stakes evidence establishes a person who can act when attention is organized around a concrete reason, even while interpreting social hesitation as a global failure to change. Her musical growth begins through specific perception and repetitive practice before public confidence. An's recognition makes that work socially legible to her (`PJSK:main:street_01_01:01:001:0002–0016`; `street_01_02:01:001:0003–0034`, `004:0012–0016`).

BAD DOGS already provide a contrast to that new pair. Akito and Toya plan for audience familiarity, perform frequently, and correct technical details. Akito seems the uncompromising aspirant; Toya seems the capable, reserved support. Later disclosure revises those appearances without erasing the actual competence. Akito's steadiness depends on a partner who listens seriously to a dream others dismiss; Toya's competence conceals an unstable entitlement to participate. Their apparent self-sufficiency is a relational achievement neither initially states clearly (`PJSK:main:street_01_04:01:001:0007–0017`; `street_01_07:01:004:0002–0011`; `street_01_19:01:001:0021–0029`).

The pair structure therefore matters more than a list of four traits. Each experienced, publicly assertive aspirant relies on a quieter partner whose motive is mediated through the relationship. Each quieter partner has a contribution the louder partner values more than the contributor can yet understand. Formation requires making those reciprocal dependencies explicit enough to survive conflict.

## Recognition, gatekeeping, and the limits of visible proof

Akito's hard standard is neither empty cruelty nor authoritative access to another person's intentions. His soccer experience taught him that natural competence and a vague career idea do not equal full commitment; RAD WEEKEND gave him a goal he would prioritize absolutely. He then applies that history to a novice whose anxious, appreciative social presentation he reads as unserious. Because An shared the legendary encounter, her choice of Kohane feels like failure to meet a previously respected standard (`PJSK:main:street_01_05:01:002:0003–0028`). His emotional investment explains the judgment without making it accurate.

The sabotage is a crucial confound. Toya first obtains a fair hearing, and Akito agrees. Another musician, resentful of An as supposedly receiving favor through her father, independently interferes. Akito had told him not to interfere and is angry at the damage to the event. Kohane initially manages to sing, then freezes after the microphone and accompaniment fail. It is consequently wrong to reconstruct the result as a clean experiment proving the novice's lack of resolve (`PJSK:main:street_01_09:01:001:0002–0013`, `002:0023–0047`).

Akito's false confession compounds the problem. He considers himself the cause and regards explaining the distinction as making excuses. The girls' subsequent belief that he literally did it is therefore reasonable on their evidence, although false on the audience's. His apology after the rematch signals recognition and responsibility but leaves that belief intact until the actual offender confesses in15. The foundation preserves responsibility, action, and knowledge as three different things (`PJSK:main:street_01_10:01:002:0007–0034`; `street_01_13:01:005:0052–0057`; `street_01_15:01:004:0031–0042`).

The rematch also has two outcomes. Akito recognizes that the girls are not half-hearted and turns the new respect into rivalry. Toya sees the same evidence as proof that Akito now has better potential companions than himself. Public validation is not a universally stabilizing force; it enters each character's prior self-model. The victory that secures Vivids thus precipitates BAD DOGS' breakup. This is a causal continuity, not a replacement plot attached after the beginner succeeds.

## Self-authorship does not require solitary origin

Kohane's critical transition occurs in the café before the successful second performance. She initially says she was given a dream and was not serious enough. MEIKO and Len offer ordinary care and a frame for continuing to struggle; Miku asks what she herself wants and leaves the choice with her. Kohane then identifies the particular shared experience she values and decides to tell An (`PJSK:main:street_01_11:01:003:0009–0058`). Onstage later, she is still afraid but wants to deliver that excitement to listeners. The target now organizes attention and choice; it does not simply remove nervousness.

Toya's version requires revising a moralized origin story. Childhood lessons deny ordinary play and disregard fatigue; his mother's account of his father's professional sacrifice reinforces obligation. He begins street music because it opposes that system, then judges the beginning as evidence that his present ambition is impure. His description nonetheless says Akito gave him enjoyment, companionship, and a precious dream (`PJSK:main:street_01_18:01:001:0004–0022`, `0027–0058`). His self-condemnation excludes the very development his narrative reports.

Akito answers through reciprocal testimony. Toya's silent practice beside him after criticism was support; Toya's belief enabled his certainty; he does not want a substitute. Present desire and shared action become the criteria. Toya finally admits he wants to sing with Akito, and the partnership is renewed (`PJSK:main:street_01_19:01:001:0017–0054`). The resolution does not claim the parental system is repaired or that every ambivalence about classical music is gone. It establishes a different account of why Toya may remain here now.

An and Akito also change through these resolutions. An receives evidence of Kohane's independent initiative and supports it, including when Kohane wants to intervene for the boys. Akito must acknowledge that his own dream has depended on another person. Neither transformation requires their energy, competitive drive, or distinctive styles to disappear. Progress modifies how those traits work in relationship.

## Information and disclosure as causal conditions

The foundation repeatedly shows that psychological interpretation fails when the wrong information is supplied. An and Kohane's early dreamlike SEKAI encounter has poor subsequent recall; they do not begin their café acquaintance as fully informed returning companions. The girls lack Akito's invitation motive, later carry his false literal confession, and learn the correction only in15. Toya knows the girls' uncertainty and sincerity through private conversation before Akito grants the fair hearing. The audience learns Toya's private self-disqualification before Akito does.

Street singers contribute questions and re-framing, not a complete secret biography. Len's unsuccessful attempts to contact the busy boys do not mean they have met him. Akito first enters16; Toya19. MEIKO's glass contains oolong tea, and its misleading appearance prompts Akito to doubt literal interpretation of Toya's words. It does not prove what Toya secretly meant; that requires the subsequent disclosure (`PJSK:main:street_01_17:01:001:0023–0042`).

The phone route is ethically and analytically consequential. Toya permits Ken and the girls to hear his explanation but asks that Akito not be told. Ken has connected a call at Akito's request; Akito later confirms hearing it. A successful repair follows a disclosure that exceeds Toya's stated audience. The analysis must neither omit the boundary nor convert the resolution into a general rule approving such conduct. In addition, the detailed childhood flashback remains audience-richer than Toya's spoken summary. Accurate later scenarios must distinguish the information actually heard from the narrative images available to the reader.

## Four human baselines reusable by later monographs

**Kohane, `VBS-KOHANE-MS4`:** her main-story contribution is a conditional model of initiative. Specific interest, attachment, and a chosen desired effect can outweigh social fear; she still hesitates and minimizes herself in unfamiliar or praising situations. Her progression runs through willingness, supported practice, genuine collapse, and chosen return. A later monograph can reuse this substrate without treating her as either always helpless or suddenly fearless.

**An, `VBS-AN-MS4`:** she is a socially embedded initiator who evaluates partners through shared aspiration and lived musical response. Her protective confidence is tested and then made more reciprocal. Her ending proposal joins all four because the experience suggests a better route to the dream, not because a mystical authority commands a merger. A later monograph must retain ambition, practical café life, warm direct attachment, and provocative rivalry as distinct contexts.

**Akito, `VBS-AKITO-MS3`:** he combines intense commitment, audience-aware labor, context-sensitive courtesy, and harsh authenticity judgments. He can misread surfaces and obscure facts through blame-taking, yet also revise judgment, accept care, and articulate dependence. The repair establishes that demonstrated current desire matters more than a pure original motive. It does not license a universal “secretly kind” explanation for every antagonistic act.

**Toya, `VBS-TOYA-MS3`:** ability, restraint, and disciplined practice coexist with a fragile claim to belonging shaped by coercive training and negative self-comparison. His care often takes practical or quiet forms, but shame can produce decisive, hurtful withdrawal rather than passivity. At the endpoint he can own his desire to remain with Akito and acknowledge support from the girls. His family conflict and the wider development of emotional expression remain unresolved.

## Everyday language prevents a crisis-only model

The source supports different speech by interlocutor and context. Kohane's anxious repairs and polite speech coexist with excited arrangement talk, assertive disagreement in12, and an unsolicited naming proposal in20. An uses direct invitations, intimate first names, affectionate declarations, customer-facing service, and competitive teasing. Akito changes between courtesy to staff/hosts and rough familiar speech; in the repair he remains rough while saying emotionally explicit things. Toya speaks respectfully to Ken, concisely about technical work, hesitantly about his own legitimacy, and forcefully when finally stating desire. His dry disclosure of the BAD DOGS name in Akito's recollection also shows ordinary literal-minded participation rather than perpetual crisis solemnity (`PJSK:main:street_01_14:01:004:0018–0023`).

These textual observations do not establish delivery, vocal pitch, speed, accent, animation, or universal catchphrases. They support reconstruction constraints: resolve state and relationship first; then infer goal, behavior, and speech. A main-story baseline gives useful low/medium/high-stakes examples, but the task has not completed the broad evidence package or audit required for reconstruction readiness.

## Ending equilibrium and unresolved claims

Ready Steady names the shared wish after the boys' repair and joint singing. The next day's collaborative naming gives each human a role, while Ken's closing observation returns to the earlier account of companions singing, fighting, and singing again (`PJSK:main:street_01_20:01:004:0008–0040`). This circularity makes conflict part of the story of forming companions; it does not make every act within the conflicts justified.

The ending establishes a unit and a credible common project. It does not establish that RAD WEEKEND has been surpassed, that the four have equal skill or confidence, that conflict is permanently solved, or that Toya's home has changed. The inherited tensions are therefore concrete: sustaining the ambitious standard without misjudging others, permitting reciprocal dependence without self-erasure, translating first shared success into sustained work, and maintaining disclosure practices that do not require a crisis to reveal true wishes.

The integrated foundation preserves `VBS-CR-001` through `VBS-CR-010` and all historical state/relationship/epistemic transitions. Later routed evidence may strengthen or revise them under the live-service method. Until that evidence is actually interpreted and integrated, the authoritative VBS boundary remains the main-story ending, with final tuple `VBS-KOHANE-MS4 / VBS-AN-MS4 / VBS-AKITO-MS3 / VBS-TOYA-MS3`. Source-current material, other-unit event progress, and reconstruction-current readiness remain separate.
