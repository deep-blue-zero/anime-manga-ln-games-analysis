# Vivid BAD SQUAD main story P03 — Owning a wish after public failure

## Scope and phase claim

Canonical reading under the [governing methods](PJSK_VBS_MS_P01_DEEP_READING.md#scope-and-authority), [phase map](PJSK_VBS_MAIN_STORY_PHASE_MAP.md), and [source lock](../../01_SOURCE_LOCK_AND_INVENTORY/PJSK_ANALYTICAL_SOURCE_LOCK.md). Episodes09–13 were read fully in internal order and checksum-verified. [P02](PJSK_VBS_MS_P02_DEEP_READING.md) supplies the entering configuration. The phase retains its own endpoint: Vivids have earned Akito's recognition, but the false account of sabotage remains uncorrected for the girls, and Toya has just announced he will stop performing with Akito. It cannot borrow P04's reconciliation.

The debut exposes a difference between practiced participation under support and the ability to continue when the supporting structure fails. Yet the text does not prove that involuntary silence measures a person's moral worth. Akito interprets it that way, and Kohane initially adopts the accusation. Her recovery requires something more specific than becoming “tough”: she learns to treat singing with An as her own desire, eligible to survive another person's verdict. The next performance demonstrates that new stance while Toya turns the same vocabulary of resolve against himself.

The crisis must be read in order. Before the debut, Akito accepts Toya's request to hear the girls before deciding; his later behavior is not evidence that this concession never occurred. The sabotaging musician then acts independently, despite Akito's previous warning. After the interruption, Akito's surprise and angry accusation distinguish his intended musical defeat of the pair from the interference he did not arrange. The musician admits cutting the sound to “help.” Akito subsequently says he did it, making the girls' false belief understandable (`09:002:0030–0033`, `0056–0064`; `10:002:0007–0009`).

Akito's reason for the false confession is unusually explicit: he considers himself the cause and dislikes excuses (`10:002:0033–0034`). That is responsibility interpreted as refusing exculpatory explanation. It does not make the false literal statement accurate, erase the independent offender's agency, or turn the harsh attack on a shaken beginner into a neutral lesson. His approach increases the information damage just when it claims to avoid evasion. This is a durable reconstruction problem: an aversion to excuses can produce concealment of relevant distinctions rather than transparency.

Kohane's failure is equally precise. She does get her voice out before the interruption; the transcript then states that the sound and microphone stop. Her training included public a cappella practice, which she remembers, but retrieval of that fact does not immediately restore performance (`09:002:0026–0047`). Therefore “she could never sing before people” is contradicted, and “she lacked the technical ability to sing without backing” is too strong. The source supports acute overwhelm in an unfamiliar disrupted setting. It does not independently isolate every cause of that overwhelm.

An continues because she refuses to let the event be ruined and explicitly links persistence to the RAD WEEKEND goal. Akito accurately identifies that particular motive, then improperly treats An's capacity as a complete diagnostic standard for Kohane. Kohane recognizes a real dependence in herself—assuming things would be fine with An beside her—but accepts a broader self-condemnation as well (`10:003:0002–0016`). The reading should preserve both the genuine vulnerability and the excess in the verdict.

## Four human trajectories, including internal crisis states

**Kohane — `VBS-KOHANE-MS2 → VBS-KOHANE-MS3 → VBS-KOHANE-MS4`.** MS3 is the historical post-debut collapse: she cannot answer An, believes her effort was insufficiently serious, and frames the episode as proof she has not changed. This state is neither her whole personality nor erased by the recovery. The Street singers supply a different route. MEIKO offers honeyed hot milk and names worry as an attempt to open a new door; Len recognizes the effort in struggling; Miku asks what Kohane herself wants and explicitly says the decision belongs to her (`11:003:0004–0030`). Kohane recalls not abstract status but the pleasurable encounter of singing with An. She then says she wants to sing with An and make an event beyond RAD WEEKEND (`11:003:0041–0056`).

MS4 begins with that self-owned commitment and becomes behaviorally tested across12–13. She approaches An without waiting to be retrieved, admits uncertainty about skill and the wider scene, contradicts Akito directly, and chooses to face another audience. Onstage she explicitly remains frightened but directs attention toward giving listeners the excitement she received (`13:005:0011–0024`). The change is a different value ordering under fear, not fearlessness. Her haircut and changed eyewear are reported and associated with resolve by dialogue; their exact visual design and causal psychological effects are not established by transcript evidence.

**An — `VBS-AN-MS1 → VBS-AN-MS2 → VBS-AN-MS3`.** MS2 records the first serious injury to her protective confidence: after persisting onstage and defending Kohane, she cannot obtain a response and believes she failed the promise to help (`11:006:0006–0010`). She does not discard Kohane for failing. Nor does she simply wait indefinitely: she is about to go find her when Kohane returns. MS3 is the renewed partnership in which Kohane's independently voiced purpose confirms An's original choice. An supplies tactical support for the rematch and continues standing beside Kohane, but the goal is now voiced reciprocally. Her hostility to Akito remains partly based on false sabotage responsibility; musical recognition and teasing at13 do not settle the factual misunderstanding.

**Akito — `VBS-AKITO-MS1 → VBS-AKITO-MS2`.** He enters capable of accepting Toya's request to revise his procedure, then responds to the debut with harshness and a self-imposed blame claim. After Kohane's challenge in12, he still explains her new directness as An inciting her (`12:002:0002–0004`): another unsupported inference, not knowledge. At the rematch, Vivids' performance evokes RAD WEEKEND for him and makes continued dismissal untenable (`13:005:0026–0038`). He says the singing was good, acknowledges the resolve, and apologizes for the previous interference (`0052–0057`). MS2 is a recognition-capable rival with the high standard preserved and the novice-dismissal revised. He immediately turns that recognition into a desire to improve BAD DOGS' next set. This is competitive respect, not instant tender intimacy or abandonment of ambition.

**Toya — `VBS-TOYA-MS1 → VBS-TOYA-MS2`.** He continues mediating outwardly: securing a hearing for Vivids, attempting to explain the sabotage, interrupting Akito's attack, providing the next event slot, and inviting Akito to acknowledge the girls. His private comparison becomes explicit in13. His father calls street music childish, emphasizes lost classical prospects, and disparages a bad friend; Toya refuses to return to classical music and tries to defend Akito (`13:002:0003–0013`). Alone, he contrasts Akito's apparently pure dream with his own initial willingness to do anything except classical music. Vivids' success then confirms the girls' legitimacy while making him feel uniquely deficient (`13:005:0045–0047`). MS2 is self-disqualified withdrawal: at the phase endpoint he says he will no longer work with Akito. This is a new act, not yet an explained conversation between the boys. His detailed childhood training history and later repair remain outside the phase.

## Relationship transitions and asymmetric knowledge

`VBS-REL-KH-AN-2` is the brief disrupted-contact/protective-guilt state after the debut. `VBS-REL-KH-AN-3` succeeds it once Kohane returns with a first-person wish: protection remains welcome but no longer exhausts Kohane's relation to the project. `VBS-REL-AN-AK-1` and `VBS-REL-KH-AK-1` record the rupture under false sabotage attribution; `-2` for each records the post-rematch recognition and apology, with the unresolved attribution still attached. `VBS-REL-KH-TY-1` is strengthened by his repeated engagement and encouragement. `VBS-REL-AN-TY-0` gains the same event/practical contact without warranting equivalent emotional closeness. `VBS-REL-AK-TY-1` becomes the announced rupture at13's last spoken line; the audience knows more about Toya's reason than Akito.

The recognition process is not zero-sum psychologically. The event makes Kohane more legitimate in her own eyes and Akito's, while making Toya feel less entitled to remain. This explains why a local victory can trigger the next crisis without being retroactively false progress.

## Behavior and Japanese register under changing pressure

Kohane's language moves from apologies and inability to answer toward short, explicit contradiction: `中途半端じゃないよ`, `してるよ、覚悟`, and `絶対、もう逃げない` (`12:001:0027–0039`). The directness is goal-conditioned. Afterward she admits she was extremely nervous (`001:0053`), and after the second performance she still gives a hesitant polite public thanks (`13:005:0041`). A model should preserve both assertive conflict speech and residual social hesitation.

An's repair grammar combines reassurances, readiness to act, first-name intimacy, and a shift to joint challenge once Kohane states the wish. Her teasing of Akito follows his acknowledgement; it need not mean every injury has been resolved. Akito uses blunt second-person language, derogatory references to Kohane's size, imperatives, and authenticity judgments in the confrontation; his apology is short and direct once the evidence meets his standard. At the same time, he leaves to perform and tries to restore the event's energy. Value for the musical event and harmful interpersonal conduct coexist.

Toya's interventions are unusually clear relative to his general restraint—asking for a fair hearing, saying enough, and supplying concrete time/place—while sentences about himself repeatedly stop at `俺は……`. His refusal to his father is direct; his self-condemnation is private; the decision to quit is finally spoken without the full cause. These are different communication modes, not a universal inability to express himself. The transcript does not establish vocal timbre, intensity, or choreography.

## Themes, alternatives, and handoff

The door motif from02 becomes an explicit model for choosing another step in11. Miku does not promise that the next attempt will succeed; she separates ownership of a wish from other people's authority to invalidate it. The rematch then reconnects inward motive to outward musical contribution. Seriousness is demonstrated as persistence around a chosen relation and desired audience effect, while the text leaves intact the need for skill and work.
