# Project SEKAI — Nightcord at 25:00 Main Story Phase 05 Deep Reading

## Scope and analytical responsibility

**Phase:** P05 — Collapse, confrontation, negotiated continuation, and new equilibrium  
**Source:** `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_16:01` through `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_20:01`  
**Master episodes:** 17–21

P05 completes the N25 main-story foundation. It begins with the social presentation that has sustained Mafuyu becoming unstable in ordinary life, proceeds through her most explicit attempt to reduce her inner conflict to a single answer — disappearance — and then stages three different attempts by Kanade, Ena, and Mizuki to reach her. It ends not with psychological cure, restored self-knowledge, or resolution of the family system, but with a negotiated willingness to keep searching, the discovery of one previously inaccessible first-person desire, and the reconstitution of Nightcord as both a creative group and an interpersonal refuge.

The phase has six linked responsibilities:

1. determine what actually collapses in Mafuyu before the final confrontation and why the synthesizer incident matters beyond its immediate material stakes;
2. distinguish Mafuyu's genuine desire to disappear from the stronger and unsupported claim that disappearance exhausts her true wishes;
3. compare Kanade, Ena, and Mizuki's intervention logics without treating any of them as a fully correct therapeutic model;
4. explain why Mafuyu eventually agrees to continue searching and what exactly she discovers about herself;
5. establish the post-main-story psychological, relational, epistemic, behavioral, and speech baselines required for later event analysis and character reconstruction;
6. identify which apparent resolutions are provisional so later material is not misread as regression from a state the main story never actually achieved.

P05 is therefore the difference between **being saved** and **choosing not to stop yet**. The story deliberately makes that distinction important.

## Core interpretive claim

The N25 main story does not resolve Mafuyu's crisis by proving that her desire to disappear is false. It resolves the immediate crisis by revealing that this desire is **real but incomplete**.

By episode 16, Mafuyu has reached a state in which hoping to find herself has become another source of pain. Every attempt to recover preference, meaning, or identity generates the possibility of hope; every failure then renews despair. Disappearance promises something psychologically different from simple self-destruction: an end to the repetitive cycle of searching, briefly hoping, failing, and being forced to search again.

This becomes explicit in episode 18. Mafuyu does not say merely that she is tired of suffering. She says she is tired of thinking that there may still be hope, tired of believing she might still find something, and tired of being disappointed again. She would rather believe from the beginning that nothing can be found because hopelessness is less exhausting than repeatedly activated hope.

The final intervention works because the other members do not collectively demonstrate that Mafuyu's life is objectively good, that her talent should make her grateful, or that they understand her completely. In fact, several of their arguments are selfish, coercive, or psychologically limited, and Mafuyu correctly identifies them as such.

- **Kanade** wants to save Mafuyu partly because saving another person sustains Kanade's own reason to keep creating and living. She acknowledges this as ego.
- **Ena** wants Mafuyu to remain partly because she envies Mafuyu's talent, loves OWN's work, and refuses to lose the possibility of hearing more. Her demand that a talented person continue creating is not a neutral moral truth.
- **Mizuki** explicitly refuses to claim authority over Mafuyu's inner problem. Mizuki says that only Mafuyu can know Mafuyu, but also states a first-person relational fact: if Mafuyu disappears, Mizuki will be lonely.

Mafuyu's accusation that everyone is acting selfishly is therefore substantially correct. The breakthrough is that **other people's selfish attachment can itself become evidence that Mafuyu exists relationally in ways not reducible to the externally authored “good girl” role**.

The deepest first-person desire recovered in this phase is not “I want to become a doctor,” “I want to make great music,” or even a simple “I want to live.” It is:

> **I wanted someone to find me.**

That desire does not solve the identity question. It changes the structure of the search. Mafuyu no longer has to find herself in complete isolation before she can justify continuing to exist. She permits others — especially Kanade, but also the group — to remain present while the search continues.

The new equilibrium is therefore provisional and relational:

- Mafuyu has **not** found a complete stable self;
- her family-pressure environment has **not** been resolved;
- her diminished access to preference and affect has **not** disappeared;
- Kanade's salvation compulsion has **not** been cured;
- Ena's artistic inferiority wound has **not** been resolved;
- Mizuki's own disclosure and conformity problems remain largely private.

What changes is that disappearance is no longer treated by Mafuyu as the only available endpoint. She accepts continued searching, asks Kanade to keep creating, returns to Nightcord, reveals her real name, participates in an offline meeting, listens to Kanade's song, and later thanks Kanade for finding her.

The main story therefore ends with **continuation under acknowledged incompleteness**, not recovery completed.

# I. Narrative and causal progression

## Episode 16 — `崩壊`: when the adaptive persona stops containing the excluded self

Episode 16 begins in the most ordinary social setting possible: Mafuyu's classroom.

That is precisely why the first rupture matters.

A friend shows Mafuyu a video and expects the socially familiar response. Mafuyu instead says it is extremely boring: `すごくつまらない`. Her friends are startled because this kind of bluntly negative response is inconsistent with the Mafuyu they know. Mafuyu immediately recognizes the mismatch and tries to retract it. A friend supplies an interpretation for her — perhaps it was a joke — and Mafuyu accepts that explanation, laughs, and apologizes.

This brief exchange is one of the most useful reconstruction scenes in the main story.

It demonstrates that Mafuyu's public presentation is no longer functioning as a perfectly controlled deliberate performance. An unfiltered evaluation escapes before the socially expected answer can be produced. Mafuyu then relies on another person's explanation to repair the interaction.

This should **STRENGTHEN** the earlier conclusion that the “good girl” presentation cannot be reduced to a simple conscious lie. It is a highly practiced regulatory structure. Under extreme internal strain, material normally excluded from that structure begins leaking through.

The classmates do not react with cruelty. They are confused, then actively help restore the familiar Mafuyu by reframing the statement as humor. This is important because the social system that maintains Mafuyu does not require malicious actors. The people around her know a version of Mafuyu, encounter something inconsistent with that model, and unconsciously help return the interaction to the recognizable script.

The second rupture is at home.

Mafuyu discovers that her synthesizer has been removed from her room. Her mother explains that she found it while cleaning, assumes it is unused and therefore unnecessary, and has arranged for it to be collected. The mother's reasoning is practical from within her model of Mafuyu: the instrument occupies space and appears to serve no purpose.

Mafuyu's reaction is markedly different from her normal compliance. She asks why. She begins to say `これは……私にとって……` — “this is, for me...” — but cannot complete the claim before the conversation is redirected toward a medical-school tutor.

The unfinished sentence is crucial.

The synthesizer is one of the few concrete objects connected to a domain in which Mafuyu has been trying to produce something that might belong to her rather than merely satisfy an assigned role. She still cannot articulate what the object means, but the interruption demonstrates that there **is** a contested first-person significance trying to emerge.

Her mother immediately introduces another externally structured future step: a tutor specializing in medical-school applicants. Again the proposal is framed supportively and pragmatically. Mafuyu is asked whether she can meet the tutor, but the direction of travel has already been socially authored. Mafuyu yields with `わかった`.

The scene therefore repeats the developmental mechanism from P04 at higher stakes:

1. a first-person preference begins to surface;
2. an externally coherent role supplies a more legible alternative;
3. the preference fails to become a defended claim;
4. Mafuyu returns to compliance.

After her mother leaves, Mafuyu says `もう、いいかな`.

This phrase links the mundane domestic event to the terminal crisis. The synthesizer is not merely a possession being discarded. Its removal dramatizes the possibility that the creative search itself can be erased without anyone around Mafuyu even recognizing that anything important has been lost.

In the Empty SEKAI, Mafuyu tells Miku that she has failed to find anything and now believes she has finally confirmed her real feeling: she simply wants to disappear. The certainty matters. Mafuyu experiences this conclusion as relief because it appears to end ambiguity. If disappearance is the authentic answer, she no longer needs to continue the exhausting search for some hidden alternative.

Miku rejects the conclusion, but at this point Miku does not persuade her. The intervention instead arrives from outside: Kanade and Mizuki are calling for her.

The episode then cuts to Ena, who is still physically absent from the rescue attempt.

Ena repeats her earlier refusal in stronger terms: if Mafuyu wants to disappear, she can do whatever she wants; Mafuyu, OWN, and OWN's songs are none of Ena's concern.

But Ena cannot sustain the claim.

She listens again to OWN and articulates what she hears: coldness, danger, rejection of everything. Then she says the crucial contradictory truth — she dislikes Mafuyu, but she still likes Mafuyu's music. The prospect that Mafuyu could disappear and therefore make any further music impossible feels unfair to her.

This is not yet compassionate universalism. It is attachment expressed through resentment and artistic desire. That distinction should be preserved because it explains the form Ena's eventual intervention takes.

### Episode-16 transition

**Beginning:** Mafuyu's social and domestic adaptive structures are strained but still functioning.  
**Trigger:** spontaneous public leakage + symbolic erasure of creative self-space.  
**Interpretation:** continued search has produced nothing; disappearance therefore appears to be the only authentic desire.  
**Parallel Ena movement:** stated indifference fails when confronted with the loss of OWN's music.  
**Ending:** Mafuyu moves toward final withdrawal as Kanade/Mizuki arrive; Ena separately recognizes that she does not actually want the consequences of Mafuyu's disappearance.

## Episode 17 — `ふたりの呪い`: self-disclosure without epistemic omniscience

Kanade and Mizuki find Mafuyu in the Empty SEKAI. Mafuyu immediately asks why they returned and reminds them that she explicitly asked to be left alone.

This is an important ethical starting point: the intervention is a violation of Mafuyu's stated boundary.

The story does not disguise that fact. Kanade says she came because she wants Mafuyu to listen to a new song. Mafuyu refuses. Miku refuses to expel Kanade and Mizuki and instead asks Mafuyu to listen. Mafuyu becomes angry, rejects the song, damages or knocks away Kanade's phone, and says the others know nothing about her and have no right to enter her inner space without permission.

Mafuyu's accusation is substantially accurate at the epistemic level.

Kanade does **not** know the autobiographical material the audience saw in episode 13. Mizuki has inferred part of the family/normative structure but does not know the complete history. Neither possesses an authoritative explanation of Mafuyu's inner life.

Kanade's answer, `わかるよ`, therefore initially risks overclaiming. But she does something important immediately afterward: instead of explaining Mafuyu to Mafuyu, she discloses herself.

She confirms Mafuyu's earlier diagnosis that Kanade also wants to disappear and explains the history of her father as Kanade understands it. Kanade tells Mafuyu that her father wanted to make people happy through music, that Kanade wanted to become like him, that her own music contributed to his despair, and that she therefore came to believe she had taken away his music and future.

Mizuki interrupts with a crucial external question: was it really Kanade's fault?

Kanade cannot accept that alternative. She insists that it was.

This preserves the distinction P04 established between **historical causality** and **Kanade's internal causal model**. Kanade's account is sincere but psychologically distorted by guilt. Mafuyu is hearing Kanade's self-understanding, not an objective adjudication of the father's collapse.

Kanade then states the consequence directly. She wanted to disappear, but her father's instruction to keep creating became the reason she continues living. She believes she must continue making songs capable of saving someone no matter how desperate she becomes.

This is the first time Mafuyu receives the personal history behind Kanade's rescue behavior.

Mafuyu's response is incisive rather than comforted: Kanade has been cursed by her father.

The word `呪い` is analytically valuable because it names the coercive form that a loving or meaningful statement can acquire inside another person's psychology. The father did not necessarily intend to impose a destructive lifelong obligation. Yet Kanade has transformed his words into a command she cannot permit herself to violate.

Mafuyu then goes further and identifies the self-serving dimension of Kanade's intervention. Kanade is trying to save Mafuyu because Kanade wants to save herself. Pulling Mafuyu into Kanade's salvation project merely makes both of them suffer.

P03 and P04 already gave evidence supporting this critique. Kanade had recognized that thinking she saved Snow also allowed Kanade herself to feel saved. P04 showed her calling the desire to make others happy through her music an ego.

P05 should therefore **STRENGTHEN**, not reject, Mafuyu's critique.

Kanade's growth lies partly in refusing the false defense that her motives are pure.

She does not answer, “No, this is only for you.” She acknowledges that Mafuyu may be right. The relevant fact for Kanade is that her music has nevertheless reached Mafuyu before. If any possibility remains, Kanade wants to use it.

She therefore accepts the possibility that the salvation project is a curse and chooses it anyway.

This is not a healthy resolution of Kanade's guilt structure. It is a more self-aware commitment inside that structure.

The title's “two curses” can therefore be read conservatively at this stage as involving at least:

- the father's words as transformed by Kanade into compulsory creation;
- Kanade's willingness to bind herself to Mafuyu's continued search even while recognizing the coercive/self-serving dimension of doing so.

P05 should avoid claiming that the narrative celebrates these bonds as unambiguously healthy. It makes their danger explicit through Mafuyu's language.

### Episode-17 transition

**Beginning:** Mafuyu rejects intervention as epistemically illegitimate and boundary-violating.  
**Kanade response:** replaces attempted knowledge of Mafuyu with vulnerable self-disclosure.  
**Mafuyu insight:** correctly identifies Kanade's rescue project as partly self-saving and curse-like.  
**Kanade choice:** accepts impurity and uncertainty rather than withdrawing.  
**Ending:** the question is no longer whether Kanade's motive is selfish; it is whether selfish, imperfect attachment can still give Mafuyu a reason not to disappear.

## Episode 18 — `それでも救いたいから`: three selfish interventions and the exhaustion of hope

Episode 18 is the argumentative center of P05.

Mafuyu restates her objection to Kanade with greater precision. Even if the new song gives her temporary relief, that relief may make things worse: she will feel slightly saved, begin hoping again, fail again to find herself, and then want to disappear even more strongly.

This is a major psychological clarification.

The problem is not simply absence of hope. **Hope itself has become dangerous.**

Every promising sign reopens the search. Every failed search then renews despair. Mafuyu therefore prefers the certainty of hopelessness because it removes the repeated emotional cost of expectation.

Before Kanade can answer, Ena arrives.

Ena's entry is intentionally abrasive. She says she came because Mafuyu makes her furious. She cannot tolerate hearing someone who possesses what Ena desperately wants — extraordinary creative ability and an audience waiting for her work — speak casually about disappearing.

Ena's argument is ethically compromised and psychologically revealing.

She treats Mafuyu's talent as a reason Mafuyu should continue creating, even telling her that if she has talent she should suffer and create on behalf of those who do not. This is not a neutral moral obligation the analysis should endorse. It is Ena's wound speaking: Mafuyu possesses the exact kind of artistic capacity Ena fears she lacks, and Mafuyu's rejection of that capacity feels to Ena like rejection of something sacred.

Mafuyu correctly answers that great music and praise are irrelevant to the thing she actually wants. She did not seek acclaim. She wanted to find herself.

This is one of the strongest demonstrations that Ena and Mafuyu's value systems are different even when both are artists.

For Ena, being recognized for artistic creation is closely tied to the self she most values. For Mafuyu, artistic production has become an instrument for locating a self she cannot otherwise access. External praise cannot solve the problem because external evaluation is already part of what displaced first-person authority.

Yet Ena's argument changes when she stops speaking about what Mafuyu *ought* to value and begins speaking about what **Ena** values.

She hates Mafuyu's selfishness, but Mafuyu's songs remain in her head. She wants to hear more. Somewhere inside her anger, she does not want Mafuyu to disappear.

This is much more relationally honest than the talent-obligation claim. Ena cannot demonstrate why Mafuyu should live from Mafuyu's perspective. She can say that Mafuyu's continued existence matters to Ena.

Mizuki then introduces a third intervention logic.

At first Mizuki appears to side with Mafuyu. Wealth, beauty, talent, or other externally desirable attributes do not eliminate suffering. If the one thing a person most wants remains inaccessible, achievements in unrelated domains may feel meaningless. Mizuki therefore says that if Mafuyu wants to disappear, that is ultimately Mafuyu's matter; only Mafuyu can fully know Mafuyu's problem.

This preserves the epistemic humility established in P04.

But Mizuki adds a personal analogy. Both Mizuki and Mafuyu know what it feels like for other people's expectations to press against the shape of the self. Mizuki describes the tiring process of resisting or accepting those pressures until everything begins to feel pointless.

Importantly, Mizuki does **not** claim identity of experience.

The analogy is structural, not total.

Then Mizuki makes the narrowest intervention claim of the three: if Mafuyu disappears, Mizuki will be lonely.

This is difficult for Mafuyu to refute because it does not tell Mafuyu what she feels. It tells Mafuyu what Mizuki feels.

Mafuyu responds by identifying the shared feature of all three interventions: selfishness.

Ena selfishly envies. Mizuki selfishly empathizes. Kanade selfishly wants to save. Everyone is imposing their own motives on Mafuyu.

The analysis should **PRESERVE** this accusation as substantially valid.

The narrative does not require the intervention to be pure in order to matter.

Mafuyu then finally verbalizes the emotional mechanism beneath the disappearance wish. She is exhausted by the possibility that hope remains. It would have been easier if she could have believed from the beginning that nothing would ever be found. Instead she repeatedly searches, hopes, discovers that the answer is wrong, despairs, and starts again.

Her repeated `探しても` turns the crisis into a process rather than a trait.

The desire to disappear is therefore partly the desire to terminate an intolerable recursive search.

Kanade responds with an extraordinary promise: she will keep creating until Mafuyu can find herself. Even if the current song fails, she will make another, and another, for as long as necessary.

Mafuyu immediately sees the danger. This would create another curse. Kanade would be obligated to continue until Mafuyu succeeds, potentially forever.

Kanade knowingly accepts the structure.

Then she adds reciprocity.

If Kanade herself reaches despair and begins to disappear, Mafuyu can tell her that she still has not been found. That statement would give Kanade a reason to continue creating.

This creates a mutual contingency:

> Mafuyu continues searching because Kanade promises to keep reaching.  
> Kanade continues creating because Mafuyu remains someone not yet fully found.

This is emotionally powerful and potentially unhealthy at the same time.

The main story does not dissolve that tension. Mafuyu explicitly calls it another curse; Kanade agrees that she understands.

Kanade finally names the motive: `ただのエゴ` — simply her ego.

The word matters because it removes the moral cover of selfless rescue. Kanade is not claiming universal authority to decide Mafuyu's good. She is saying that **Kanade wants Mafuyu to remain**, and she is willing to bind herself to the work of continuing to reach her.

Mafuyu asks whether Kanade will really do this even if finding herself takes an unknown amount of time or never succeeds.

Kanade says yes.

Mafuyu laughs at the absurdity and agrees to search a little longer.

The exact scope of that agreement should be preserved:

> `もう少しだけ……探してみるよ`

This is **not** a declaration that Mafuyu now wants to live indefinitely. It is a negotiated extension of the search.

That distinction is essential for all later longitudinal analysis.

### Episode-18 transition

**Beginning:** Mafuyu wants to terminate hope itself because renewed hope repeatedly produces renewed despair.  
**Ena:** fails when appealing to talent as obligation; succeeds more honestly when revealing personal attachment to Mafuyu's music.  
**Mizuki:** refuses epistemic ownership of Mafuyu while offering structural empathy and first-person loneliness.  
**Kanade:** acknowledges curse and ego, then offers indefinite relational continuity rather than guaranteed cure.  
**Mafuyu:** does not retract her pain; she agrees to continue searching for a little longer.

## Episode 19 — `悔やむと書いてミライ`: finding the wish to be found

Episode 19 begins after the immediate crisis has de-escalated, but before the story has supplied the meaning of the resolution.

Kanade tells Mafuyu that she listened to all of OWN's music and heard Mafuyu in every piece: `雪の音がした`.

This statement matters because it reframes creative output as evidence of continuity.

Mafuyu has repeatedly claimed that there is no self to find. Kanade cannot define Mafuyu's true identity, but she can perceive a recurring trace across Mafuyu's work. Mafuyu's response is not rejection. She asks Kanade to tell her sometime what “her sound” sounds like.

That request is psychologically delicate.

It should not be interpreted as Mafuyu once again outsourcing identity entirely to another person's judgment. The relevant difference is relational and procedural. Earlier, Mafuyu accepted externally supplied definitions as substitutes for first-person preference. Here she actively asks another person to help her perceive something about herself that she cannot yet access alone.

This is **external scaffolding for self-discovery**, not necessarily external authorship.

Miku then announces that a song is being born from Mafuyu's true feelings.

Mafuyu initially resists the implication because she still believes she has found nothing. Miku says that the existence of the SEKAI and the emerging song demonstrate otherwise.

The five characters sing together.

Afterward Mafuyu reaches the phase's central recognition:

> `“見つけてほしかった”んだね`

She wanted to be found.

This is the first newly recovered desire the narrative explicitly authorizes as a “true feeling” after the crisis.

It does not invalidate the disappearance desire. Instead it changes its completeness.

Mafuyu could sincerely want to disappear **and** sincerely want someone to find her. The final crisis becomes intelligible precisely because these desires coexist. If disappearance were her only wish, Kanade's persistence would merely obstruct it. If survival were her simple hidden wish, the months or years of identity erosion would be reduced to a misunderstanding. The source instead presents ambivalence: she has become exhausted enough to seek disappearance while still containing a relational desire that has not been extinguished.

The song generated from that recognition therefore marks emergence of accessible desire, not completion of identity reconstruction.

When everyone returns from the SEKAI, there is a brief silence as they check whether Mafuyu returned too. Mafuyu answers that she is there.

Mizuki welcomes her back. Ena initially preserves her irritated stance, then says `またよろしくね、雪`.

The distinction in Ena remains useful. She does not suddenly become tender or retract everything she said. Affection is expressed through continued participation despite irritation.

Kanade then does something equally important: she ends the session because morning is approaching and tells everyone to rest before beginning the next song the following day.

Compared with P03's pathological overwork, this is a small but meaningful behavioral contrast. Kanade is still committed to creation, but the group has returned to bounded collaborative routine rather than Kanade absorbing all production while starving and refusing rest.

The episode's hospital scene completes the phase's Kanade movement.

Kanade tells her father that she met someone a little like herself and made a song for that person. She explicitly says the song did not itself accomplish the rescue she wanted. But the person found something important: a true feeling and a true song.

Kanade therefore now possesses evidence that intervention can matter even when her own composition is not sufficient to “save” someone.

She says she still cannot save someone with her music but will continue creating.

This is a **partial revision** of outcome absolutism, not its disappearance. Kanade remains oriented toward future rescue, but she can now recognize intermediate value in helping another person's own meaning emerge.

Her father appears to say her name while asleep. Kanade does not inflate the moment into miraculous recovery. She allows herself the smaller pleasure that hearing her name again matters.

That response is significant for reconstruction. Kanade is capable, under some conditions, of accepting a small positive event without converting it into total proof of success.

### Episode-19 transition

**Beginning:** Mafuyu has agreed to continue but still believes she has found no self.  
**Relational mirror:** Kanade identifies a recurring “Mafuyu sound” in OWN.  
**Recovered desire:** Mafuyu recognizes that she wanted to be found.  
**Group effect:** return and reintegration without erasing conflict.  
**Kanade effect:** greater tolerance for partial efficacy; her song need not single-handedly produce salvation for the intervention to matter.  
**Ending:** the creative group exists again, now with deeper interpersonal knowledge and a shared song grounded in the crisis.

## Episode 20 — `25時、ナイトコードで。`: the first low-stakes post-crisis baseline

The final episode is indispensable for character reconstruction because it does not end at the emotional climax. It gives the audience a low-stakes behavioral sample of what the new equilibrium actually looks like.

Mizuki organizes an offline meeting at a family restaurant.

This immediately extends the relationship beyond anonymous production and crisis intervention. The members have encountered one another physically in the SEKAI, but ordinary embodied social life is different. Ena explicitly remarks that meeting in the real world feels strange.

Mizuki acts as social facilitator from the opening. They greet people enthusiastically, keep track of Mafuyu's message about being delayed, tease Ena about photo filters, initiate introductions, propose name usage, manage ordering, and create a commemorative toast.

This **STRENGTHENS** the reconstruction model in which Mizuki's high social energy, playfulness, and interest in making an interaction enjoyable are genuine competencies rather than merely decorative masking.

Mafuyu arrives and asks whether she can sit beside Kanade.

The source does not narrate the motive, so later analysis should avoid turning the seating choice into a total theory of attachment. But as behavioral evidence it is important: after the crisis, Mafuyu actively selects proximity to Kanade rather than merely tolerating Kanade's presence.

The group then exchanges real names.

When asked, Mafuyu hesitates before saying `朝比奈まふゆ`. Kanade repeats the name and connects it to the Snow alias. She then says she hopes they will continue together.

Mafuyu agrees.

The transition from aliases to real names is not merely informational. Nightcord began as a collaboration in which anonymity allowed creative intimacy without embodied identity. The main story ends by making the four people mutually real while preserving the online group as an important relational space.

Mafuyu's apology is one of the most valuable post-crisis speech samples.

She says she is sorry for causing trouble. Ena asks whether Mafuyu genuinely means it. Mafuyu responds that she does not know; she probably feels remorse.

This is not evidence of callousness.

It is evidence that post-crisis Mafuyu is beginning to prefer **honest uncertainty over socially perfect emotional performance** in at least this relational context.

The earlier good-girl Mafuyu would have had an obvious socially correct answer available. Here she admits that she cannot reliably identify her own feeling.

Ena finds this extremely irritating.

That friction is likely to remain important longitudinally. Ena values emotionally legible engagement and reacts strongly to responses that feel evasive, condescending, or insufficiently accountable. Mafuyu's emerging honesty can therefore sound worse socially than the polished mask it replaces.

The ordering sequence is equally useful.

Mafuyu initially says anything is fine. Mizuki pushes her to choose anyway. Mafuyu asks what Kanade is ordering and then orders the same wonton noodles and oolong tea.

Several interpretations are possible and should remain differentiated:

- Mafuyu still has difficulty accessing ordinary preferences;
- food choice may be especially weak evidence because P04 established diminished taste;
- copying Kanade may be a low-cost way to resolve an unimportant choice;
- the behavior may also reflect emerging relational reliance on Kanade.

The main-story evidence does **not** justify choosing only one explanation.

What matters for reconstruction is that post-main Mafuyu does not suddenly become full of strongly articulated mundane preferences. When low-stakes choice is required, she may still defer, answer indifferently, or use another person's choice as a scaffold.

Mizuki asks whether their energetic social atmosphere is comfortable for Kanade and Mafuyu. Both say it is not uncomfortable / that they are fine with it.

This prevents another common reconstruction error. Kanade's low social energy and Mafuyu's flat affect do not imply that they necessarily dislike lively company. Both can tolerate or even value a socially active person carrying interactional momentum.

The final Kanade–Mafuyu DM occurs after the offline meeting.

Mafuyu tells Kanade she listened to the song Kanade made for her. Kanade asks what she thought. Mafuyu does not answer immediately.

Then she addresses Kanade by her real name and says:

> `見つけてくれて、ありがとう`

The wording completes the central movement from **save** to **find**.

Mafuyu does not tell Kanade “your song saved me.” Kanade herself internally acknowledges that her music is still not enough for Mafuyu. The gratitude is for finding her — for reaching the first desire that had remained inaccessible beneath the disappearance wish.

Kanade responds with Mafuyu's real name and thanks her in return.

Miku's closing observation repeats the same language: Mafuyu was found.

This should set the canonical main-story endpoint.

Mafuyu is **found relationally** before she is **fully found intrapsychically**.

# II. Character-state transitions across P05

## Mafuyu — M-main-terminal: from certainty of disappearance to relationally supported uncertainty

### Beginning state

At P05 entry Mafuyu has:

- failed repeatedly to identify stable preferences or selfhood;
- experienced external praise as irrelevant to the search;
- interpreted Kanade's earlier song as momentarily helpful but insufficient;
- stated that disappearance will follow if continued searching produces nothing;
- experienced ordinary social expectations as increasingly difficult to contain within the good-girl presentation;
- produced accelerating OWN output associated with a worsening crisis.

### Collapse mechanism

P05 adds two immediate destabilizers:

1. **public leakage** — an unfiltered negative judgment escapes at school;
2. **domestic erasure** — the synthesizer is treated as disposable while a medical-school future is further organized for her.

The second event is particularly important because it juxtaposes an object connected to Mafuyu's private creative search with an externally authored identity track. Mafuyu begins to defend the object's first-person importance and cannot finish.

### Terminal formulation

Mafuyu then concludes that disappearance itself is the real feeling she has been trying to find.

This belief contains a genuine desire but an incorrect claim of exclusivity.

The phase later reveals why the formulation feels attractive: disappearing would end not only pain but **the obligation to keep hoping**.

### Intervention response

Mafuyu resists every attempt to assign her a reason to continue.

She rejects:

- artistic talent as sufficient reason;
- external praise as sufficient reason;
- Kanade's rescue motive as selfless;
- other people's empathy as complete understanding;
- the assumption that another song will necessarily change anything.

These are not irrational refusals. Many are analytically sound.

### Recovered desire

What finally emerges is narrower and more defensible: Mafuyu wanted to be found.

This means there remained a relational orientation beneath withdrawal. Some part of Mafuyu wanted another person to perceive and reach the self she could not locate alone.

### Ending state

At main-story close, Mafuyu:

- no longer treats immediate disappearance as the sole available solution;
- agrees to keep searching, explicitly on a provisional basis;
- accepts Kanade's continued attempts to reach her;
- accepts Mizuki and Ena as continuing Nightcord members despite unresolved friction;
- recognizes `見つけてほしかった` as a true desire;
- asks Kanade to describe the “Mafuyu sound” she perceives;
- returns to collaborative work;
- reveals her real name;
- participates in a mundane offline social meeting;
- admits uncertainty about whether she truly feels remorse rather than automatically producing a socially perfect answer;
- still struggles with low-stakes preference selection;
- listens voluntarily to Kanade's song;
- thanks Kanade for finding her.

### What is **not** resolved

The main story does **not** establish that Mafuyu:

- has recovered a stable comprehensive identity;
- now possesses normal access to pleasure, taste, or preference;
- has resolved her relationship with her mother or family expectations;
- no longer experiences disappearance desire;
- now believes Kanade can certainly save her;
- has fully integrated public Mafuyu, Snow, OWN, and Empty-SEKAI presentation into one seamless self;
- has become conventionally expressive or emotionally transparent.

Later event analysis must not treat any of these as completed foundation facts.

---

## Kanade — K-main-terminal: from compulsory rescue to self-aware but still compulsory relational commitment

P05 does not cure Kanade's salvation complex.

Instead, it makes Kanade substantially more aware of it.

She openly discloses:

- her literal wish to disappear;
- the father history as she interprets it;
- the fact that continuing to create functions as her reason to live;
- the belief that she must create songs capable of saving others.

Mafuyu then articulates the critique Kanade has only partly articulated herself: Kanade is trying to save Mafuyu partly to save Kanade.

Kanade does not successfully refute this.

The crucial development is that she stops requiring purity of motive before acting. She accepts that the project may be ego and curse and still chooses continued relation.

The danger remains substantial. Kanade promises potentially indefinite creation until Mafuyu can find herself and permits Mafuyu's unresolved state to become a reason Kanade must continue living and working.

For reconstruction this means the main-story endpoint should not be simplified to “Kanade learned healthy boundaries.” She did not.

What she did gain is:

- greater awareness of self-serving motive;
- greater ability to distinguish reaching from complete rescue;
- some tolerance for partial efficacy;
- willingness to disclose vulnerability rather than only perform rescuing competence;
- renewed participation in a collaborative division of labor rather than absorbing everyone's work;
- capacity to end work for rest after crisis rather than treating every minute as morally required production.

Kanade remains both deeply compassionate and vulnerable to pathological responsibility.

---

## Ena — E-main-terminal: from humiliation and refusal to antagonistic attachment

Ena's final movement is not reconciliation through admiration.

She begins P05 trying to preserve a position of indifference. She cannot.

OWN's music matters to her too much.

Her intervention contains two layers that should remain separated.

### Layer 1 — projection and coercion

Ena cannot tolerate Mafuyu possessing extraordinary artistic ability while claiming nothing matters. She tells Mafuyu that someone with talent should continue creating, even suffer and create on behalf of those without it.

This is strongly conditioned by Ena's own wound and should not be elevated into the narrative's universal ethical conclusion.

### Layer 2 — honest attachment

Ena eventually says what is actually hers to say: she wants more of Mafuyu's music and does not want Mafuyu to disappear.

That statement is not psychologically pure, but it is relationally real.

At the main-story endpoint, Ena:

- remains irritated with Mafuyu;
- remains envious of Mafuyu's talent;
- remains sensitive to Mafuyu's emotionally ambiguous responses;
- nevertheless explicitly renews the relationship with `またよろしくね`;
- participates in the offline meeting;
- is comfortable challenging Mafuyu directly rather than treating her as fragile or untouchable.

For reconstruction, **care and hostility can coexist in Ena without cancelling each other**.

---

## Mizuki — MZ-main-terminal: autonomy-respecting attachment and social integration

Mizuki's P05 role is the clearest extension of P03–P04.

Mizuki repeatedly avoids claiming complete understanding of Mafuyu. Even while drawing a structural analogy to personal experience, Mizuki says that Mafuyu's problem ultimately belongs to Mafuyu.

This does not produce detachment.

Mizuki distinguishes **respect for another person's epistemic ownership** from **absence of personal desire**.

Mizuki cannot determine Mafuyu's authentic solution. Mizuki can truthfully say that losing Mafuyu would feel lonely.

That is the intervention's most autonomy-compatible formulation.

Mizuki then becomes the primary social architect of the post-crisis group:

- welcomes Mafuyu back;
- converts real-name revelation into an ordinary social ritual;
- facilitates teasing and interaction;
- encourages choice without demanding emotional depth;
- organizes the first offline meeting;
- checks whether the interactional energy is comfortable for quieter members.

This is strong reconstruction evidence that Mizuki's social playfulness is not merely avoidance. It also functions as relational labor.

---

# III. Relationship architecture after P05

## Kanade ↔ Mafuyu — from rescue object / creator to reciprocal survival contingency

This relationship undergoes the largest change.

### Before P05

Kanade largely conceptualizes Mafuyu through a rescue schema. Mafuyu, in turn, initially treats Kanade's music as a possible instrument for finding herself, then rejects it when it does not complete the search.

### P05 confrontation

Mafuyu forces Kanade to acknowledge that the rescue project is also about Kanade. Kanade reveals her history and accepts that accusation rather than hiding behind altruistic language.

### New bond

Kanade promises to keep making songs until Mafuyu can find herself. Mafuyu, if Kanade begins to disappear, can remind her that the search remains unfinished.

The relationship therefore becomes reciprocal but asymmetrical:

- Kanade supplies persistence, music, and a promise of continued reaching;
- Mafuyu becomes both recipient of that reaching and a reason Kanade can continue creating;
- each person's unresolved state becomes psychologically relevant to the other's continuation.

This is a powerful attachment and a potential dependency risk.

Later synthesis should avoid using either “healthy mutual support” or “pure codependency” as a total description without longitudinal evidence. The main story establishes both genuine mutual life-supporting value and explicit curse language.

### Post-crisis ordinary evidence

Mafuyu:

- chooses proximity to Kanade at the restaurant;
- uses Kanade's meal choice as a scaffold;
- privately contacts Kanade about the song;
- addresses her by real name;
- thanks her for finding her.

Kanade:

- addresses Mafuyu by real name;
- accepts that her song remains insufficient;
- continues creating without demanding immediate proof that Mafuyu is cured.

The dyad therefore becomes the central foundation relationship for later longitudinal analysis, but it should never be treated as the only meaningful N25 relationship.

---

## Ena ↔ Mafuyu — unresolved antagonism becomes durable relational friction

P05 does not resolve the injury created by OWN's revelation.

Ena still dislikes aspects of Mafuyu and remains furious at the talent asymmetry. Mafuyu still cannot supply the emotionally legible reassurance Ena often seeks.

What changes is that Ena stops pretending the relationship is irrelevant.

Her art-oriented attachment gives her a reason to oppose Mafuyu's disappearance. After the crisis she renews the working relationship without sentimental reconciliation.

This creates a useful foundation state:

> **high friction + high relevance + incomplete trust + genuine attachment**

Later event analysis should expect confrontation rather than interpret every argument as relationship failure.

---

## Mizuki ↔ Mafuyu — structural recognition without forced disclosure

Mizuki's relationship with Mafuyu is grounded in recognition of normative pressure rather than shared biography.

Mizuki sees enough similarity to understand why being continuously shaped by others can become exhausting, while preserving the statement that only Mafuyu fully knows Mafuyu's problem.

The relationship therefore begins from an unusual combination:

- meaningful empathy;
- limited factual knowledge;
- low demand for explicit justification;
- willingness to state personal attachment directly.

This becomes a strong baseline for later analysis of why Mizuki may be able to tolerate forms of Mafuyu's ambiguity that frustrate Ena.

---

## N25 as a group — anonymity becomes chosen embodied relationship

The opening Nightcord group was intimate through work while remaining anonymous.

By the end of P05:

- everyone knows the others' real identities and names;
- they have encountered one another in a private psychological space;
- at least some major vulnerabilities have been disclosed;
- they have deliberately chosen to continue creating together;
- they establish an offline social relationship in addition to online work.

The group is therefore no longer merely a distributed production circle.

But it also does not become a uniformly harmonious friendship group.

The post-crisis restaurant scene preserves difference:

- Mizuki drives social energy;
- Ena complains and challenges;
- Kanade is quiet but comfortable;
- Mafuyu is bluntly uncertain about her own feelings and still struggles with mundane choice.

This is exactly the kind of ordinary-state evidence required for reconstruction.

# IV. Epistemic-state audit

P05 changes who knows what, but substantial asymmetries remain.

## What Mafuyu learns

Mafuyu now knows:

- Kanade's father was a composer;
- Kanade believes her own music contributed to his collapse;
- Kanade has experienced explicit desire to disappear;
- continuing to compose functions as a reason Kanade remains alive;
- Kanade's desire to save Mafuyu is partly bound to Kanade's own survival and identity;
- Ena values OWN's music and does not want to lose it;
- Mizuki sees a structural similarity between their experiences of social pressure;
- Mizuki would feel lonely if Mafuyu disappeared.

## What Mizuki learns

Mizuki now knows much more about Kanade's father/guilt structure because Kanade discloses it in the confrontation.

Mizuki still does not possess Mafuyu's full autobiographical history from episode 13.

## What Ena learns

Ena participates in the later confrontation but does not receive Kanade's entire episode-17 disclosure in the same way if she arrives afterward; therefore later analysis should not automatically give Ena all details of Kanade's father story unless another source establishes transmission.

Ena knows:

- Mafuyu's disappearance intent is serious;
- Mafuyu's actual goal is not acclaim but self-discovery;
- Mafuyu accepts continued searching;
- the group has reconstituted.

## What Kanade learns

Kanade still does not know the detailed childhood history the audience saw in episode 13.

She knows through Mafuyu's statements and Miku's interpretation that:

- Mafuyu has been trying to find her real self/feelings;
- disappearance is tied to failure and exhaustion in that search;
- Mafuyu wants to be found;
- Kanade's music previously reached Mafuyu and may continue to function as a relational bridge.

## Audience-only knowledge that must remain audience-only

The audience retains richer causal knowledge about:

- the exact sequence through which Mafuyu's nursing preference was redirected toward medicine;
- the arcade invitation she wanted to accept;
- the progressive loss of taste and preference access;
- the multicausal professional pressures on Kanade's father beyond Kanade's guilt narration.

Future scenario reconstruction must not allow characters to use this information unless later sources establish disclosure.

# V. Speech and behavioral reconstruction anchors

## Mafuyu

P05 materially expands the number of Mafuyu registers available for reconstruction.

### 1. Public-school adaptive register under strain

Default features remain:

- socially smooth;
- responsive;
- mild;
- cooperative;
- quick repair after mismatch.

New evidence:

- under severe strain, an unfiltered negative judgment may escape;
- once a peer supplies a socially acceptable interpretation, Mafuyu can rapidly re-enter the expected script.

Do not model every blunt statement as a deliberate act of rebellion.

### 2. Empty-SEKAI terminal-crisis register

Features:

- terse refusals;
- direct imperatives;
- irritability;
- explicit boundary defense;
- repetition under overload;
- dismissal of praise and conventional consolation;
- capacity for highly incisive psychological critique of another person even while in crisis.

Important constraint:

This is a **high-stakes crisis register**, not timeless “true Mafuyu speech.”

### 3. Post-crisis Nightcord/private register

New features:

- greater willingness to admit uncertainty directly (`自分でもよくわからなくて`);
- less automatic substitution of the socially perfect answer when among Nightcord;
- concise but meaningful relational initiative;
- willingness to ask another person to help perceive something about her;
- ability to express gratitude after delay rather than perform it immediately.

### 4. Low-stakes choice behavior

At the restaurant:

- `なんでもいいかな` appears before meal choice;
- when pressed to choose, Mafuyu asks Kanade's choice and copies it.

Treat this as evidence of continued weak preference access, not proof that Mafuyu never possesses mundane preferences.

### 5. Relational initiative

Mafuyu:

- asks to sit beside Kanade;
- initiates a late-night DM;
- uses Kanade's real name;
- offers thanks without prompting.

This directly contradicts any reconstruction rule that makes post-main Mafuyu purely passive in relationships.

---

## Kanade

P05 establishes:

- willingness to disclose deeply painful history when she believes shared vulnerability can reach someone;
- persistent self-blame despite external challenge;
- capacity to accept selfishness/ego as part of motive;
- extreme persistence once she identifies even a small possibility of helping;
- preference for action through creation rather than persuasive rhetoric alone;
- quiet social presence in low-stakes settings;
- tolerance of Mizuki's high-energy facilitation;
- emerging ability to accept partial positive outcomes.

High-stakes risk remains:

- Kanade can bind her own continued existence to responsibility for another person's recovery/search;
- she may consciously accept burdens others correctly identify as curse-like.

Do not reconstruct self-awareness as equivalent to healthy self-limitation.

---

## Ena

P05 reinforces:

- anger as a route into vulnerable truth;
- difficulty separating artistic value from existential value when talent is salient;
- capacity to say coercive or unfair things when artistic inferiority is activated;
- strong resistance to emotionally ambiguous apologies;
- tendency to express attachment through irritation, challenge, or reluctant continuation rather than soft reassurance;
- capacity to reverse a refusal when she privately recognizes that she cares.

Low-stakes baseline:

Ena remains easy to tease, reactive, verbally expressive, and capable of ordinary banter immediately after crisis.

Do not sanitize her into a gentle caretaker simply because she ultimately intervenes.

---

## Mizuki

P05 strengthens:

- social facilitation as authentic skill;
- humor and teasing as both genuine enjoyment and tension management;
- good sensitivity to whether quieter people are comfortable;
- preference for framing personal feelings as personal rather than universal moral authority;
- strong ability to draw structural analogies while acknowledging limits of knowledge;
- ability to encourage choice without requiring immediate emotional disclosure.

Mizuki's own deeper vulnerabilities remain underdisclosed to the group.

Therefore post-main sociability should not be treated as evidence that those issues are resolved.

# VI. Thematic and structural findings

## 1. “Saving” is progressively downgraded from guaranteed outcome to ongoing relation

Kanade begins the main story using salvation as an almost binary measure of worth.

P05 does not eliminate the vocabulary — Kanade still promises to save Mafuyu — but the actual mechanism that works is different.

The group:

- reaches;
- listens;
- discloses;
- argues;
- admits selfishness;
- stays;
- creates reasons to continue.

Mafuyu's final gratitude is for being **found**, not for having been completely saved.

This distinction should become central in the N25 main-story synthesis.

---

## 2. Selfishness is not treated as the opposite of care

Mafuyu accurately calls the others selfish.

The story does not rebut this by revealing secret purity.

Instead it presents relational attachment as inevitably first-person:

- “I want to save you.”
- “I want to hear your music.”
- “I would be lonely without you.”

These statements matter precisely because they arise from the speakers' own desires.

For Mafuyu — whose life has been dominated by doing what other people approve — this could easily become another coercive burden. The story recognizes that danger through the language of curse.

The resolution depends on Mafuyu eventually choosing to accept some of that attachment as a reason to continue, not on the attachment becoming morally neutral.

---

## 3. The crisis is fundamentally about first-person authority

Mafuyu has been surrounded by people who can tell her:

- what career suits her;
- what a good student does;
- what food should taste good;
- what music should feel fun;
- what achievements should make her happy.

The final group initially risks repeating the same structure by telling her what should matter:

- talent;
- audience expectation;
- survival;
- rescue.

The intervention becomes more effective when each member retreats toward what they can actually own:

- Ena owns her desire to hear more music;
- Mizuki owns loneliness;
- Kanade owns her ego and commitment.

Mafuyu then makes the relevant decision herself: she will search a little longer.

That is a small act of first-person authority.

---

## 4. Music functions as trace, bridge, and generated evidence — not simple cure

Across P01–P05, music performs several different functions.

In P05 especially:

- OWN contains a perceivable trace of Mafuyu even when Mafuyu cannot perceive herself;
- Kanade's music can reach without fully saving;
- the shared song emerges after Mafuyu identifies a previously inaccessible true desire;
- creation externalizes inner states that ordinary introspection cannot reliably access.

The main story therefore supports a model in which music is an **epistemic medium**: it can make aspects of selfhood perceptible.

It does not support the simpler claim that “music cures psychological suffering.”

---

## 5. The ending is intentionally provisional

The phrase `もう少しだけ` should govern interpretation of the ending.

Mafuyu agrees to search **a little longer**.

That is enough for the main-story resolution because the immediate terminal state has changed.

It is not enough to declare the underlying conflicts solved.

This is especially important for a live-service work. Later events that revisit family control, identity, disappearance, rescue pressure, or artistic selfhood should not automatically be described as reopening a resolved problem. They may instead be developing problems the main story deliberately left open.

---

# VII. Claim transitions from P01–P04

## Mafuyu claims

### “Mafuyu never had an accessible self.”
**REJECT.**

P04 already showed explicit childhood/adolescent preferences. P05 reinforces the model of lost access rather than original absence.

### “Mafuyu's desire to disappear is merely false consciousness.”
**REJECT.**

P05 treats the desire as sincere and powerful. What is rejected is its claim to exhaust Mafuyu's true feelings.

### “Mafuyu's deeper wish is simply to live.”
**DOWNGRADE / OPEN.**

The direct source formulation is narrower: she wanted to be found and agrees to continue searching. Do not replace this with a generic survival desire unless later evidence supports it.

### “Snow is a wholly fabricated false persona.”
**REVISE.**

P05 continues to support multiple sharply different registers, but the emergence of honest uncertainty inside Nightcord and the continuity Mafuyu's music preserves argue against a clean true-self/false-self binary.

### “Mafuyu is passive in relationships.”
**REJECT as a general rule.**

By the endpoint she chooses proximity, asks Kanade to describe her sound, initiates private contact, listens to Kanade's song, and expresses gratitude.

---

## Kanade claims

### “Kanade's desire to help is wholly trauma-created.”
**REJECT / preserve prior revision.**

The prosocial musical value predates the father's collapse.

### “Kanade's salvation complex is purely altruistic.”
**REJECT.**

Both Kanade and Mafuyu recognize self-saving and ego components.

### “Kanade learns healthy boundaries by the end of the main story.”
**REJECT.**

She consciously accepts an indefinite relational obligation described as another curse.

### “Kanade can only value complete rescue; partial effects mean nothing to her.”
**REVISE.**

By episode 19 she can acknowledge that Mafuyu found something important even though Kanade's song did not itself save her, and she can take modest comfort in her father's ambiguous utterance of her name.

---

## Ena claims

### “Ena wants Mafuyu gone once the OWN reveal humiliates her.”
**REJECT.**

Ena tries to assert indifference but cannot tolerate the consequence that Mafuyu's work would disappear.

### “Ena's concern for Mafuyu is straightforward empathy.”
**REJECT.**

It is entangled with envy, artistic hunger, resentment, and attachment.

### “Conflict indicates weak attachment for Ena.”
**DOWNGRADE.**

## Mizuki claims

### “Mizuki claims to understand Mafuyu completely because of personal similarity.”
**REJECT.**

Mizuki explicitly preserves Mafuyu's epistemic ownership while offering structural analogy.

### “Mizuki's humor is merely defensive masking.”
**DOWNGRADE / preserve prior caution.**

The post-crisis restaurant scene shows humor and social play functioning positively as genuine enjoyment and group facilitation.

# VIII. Main-story endpoint state handoff

This is the canonical handoff P05 should provide to the later N25 main-story synthesis and event-era analysis.

## Mafuyu — post-main foundation state

**Self-concept:** incomplete and unstable; knows that wanting to be found is genuine, but does not possess a complete answer to “who am I?”  
**Goals:** continue searching for real feelings/self; continue creating; remain with Nightcord for now.  
**Active fears:** renewed hope followed by renewed failure; never finding herself; being forced back into externally authored identity.  
**Emotional baseline:** still flattened/uncertain in many domains; capable of sharper private affect and emerging relational attachment.  
**Social masks:** public good-girl structure remains available but has shown leakage; Nightcord becomes a context where uncertainty and bluntness are increasingly permissible.  
**Primary dependencies:** Nightcord as creative/relational space; particularly Kanade as a persistent mirror/reacher.  
**Knowledge:** knows major parts of Kanade's guilt/salvation history; knows Ena and Mizuki do not want her disappearance for their own reasons.  
**Coping:** creation, withdrawal, compliance, external scaffolding for difficult choices, increasingly honest admission of not knowing.  
**Agency:** still low in externally structured domains but no longer zero; can choose continuation, proximity, contact, and disclosure.  
**Stress vulnerability:** identity pressure, externally authored future, failure of self-search, hope-disappointment cycles.  
**Speech:** sharply context-dependent; post-main Nightcord register allows concise candor and emotional uncertainty.

## Kanade — post-main foundation state

**Self-concept:** composer obligated to continue; increasingly aware that rescue mission is partly ego and guilt.  
**Goals:** continue composing; eventually create music capable of saving; continue reaching Mafuyu.  
**Active fears:** causing harm through music; failing another person; witnessing disappearance; becoming unable to justify continued creation.  
**Emotional baseline:** quiet, burdened, persistent; capable of modest relief and relational warmth.  
**Primary dependencies:** music as life-structure; father as origin of guilt/ideal; Mafuyu as current relational focus and reciprocal continuation cue; N25 collaboration.  
**Coping:** creation, overfunctioning risk, self-sacrifice, now supplemented by disclosure and group collaboration.  
**Agency:** very high when rescue possibility exists; may override self-care and others' stated boundaries under perceived existential stakes.  
**Stress vulnerability:** responsibility inflation and outcome absolutism remain active risks.

## Ena — post-main foundation state

**Self-concept:** artist whose worth remains painfully tied to talent and recognition.  
**Goals:** improve, receive meaningful artistic recognition, continue N25 work.  
**Active fears:** mediocrity, condescension, invisibility of valued creative self.  
**Relationship to Mafuyu:** irritated, envious, unresolved, but genuinely attached and unwilling to accept disappearance.  
**Coping:** anger, confrontation, competitive effort, social validation, persistent work.  
**Agency:** high and often verbally forceful under perceived humiliation or unfairness.  
**Stress vulnerability:** talent comparisons and ambiguous praise.

## Mizuki — post-main foundation state

**Self-concept:** relatively clear internal preference/self-presentation, with significant vulnerability to social scrutiny and conformity pressure.  
**Goals:** preserve self-expression, enjoy life where possible, continue N25, maintain meaningful connection without surrendering autonomy.  
**Relationship to Mafuyu:** structural empathy plus explicit personal attachment; low claim to total understanding.  
**Coping:** humor, fashion/creative expression, selective disclosure, social facilitation, boundary setting.  
**Agency:** high in social organization and interpersonal mediation.  
**Stress vulnerability:** objectification, gossip, conditional acceptance, pressure to normalize.

## N25 group state

The group is now:

- mutually de-anonymized;
- creatively recommitted;
- relationally significant beyond production;
- capable of offline interaction;
- aware of some, but not all, major vulnerabilities;
- internally conflictual rather than uniformly harmonious;
- a context where members can behave differently from their public roles.

This is the correct baseline from which later event stories should advance.

# IX. Reconstruction-ready scenario implications

P05 provides enough evidence to support several high-confidence conditional predictions at the main-story endpoint.

## Mafuyu in a casual group setting

Likely behaviors include:

- low initiative in trivial preference selection;
- concise answers;
- willingness to let a socially energetic person structure the interaction;
- no necessary discomfort with lively company;
- possible imitation/scaffolding from a trusted person when choices feel unimportant or inaccessible;
- blunt honesty about emotional uncertainty in a trusted Nightcord-like context.

Avoid reconstructing her as universally icy, hostile, or incapable of initiating contact.

## Mafuyu under identity pressure

Likely progression:

1. initially compliant/socially smooth response;
2. internal suppression or inability to defend preference;
3. increasing strain;
4. possible unfiltered leakage or abrupt bluntness;
5. withdrawal/flattening if first-person significance is overridden repeatedly.

## Kanade when someone she values is in existential crisis

Likely behaviors include:

- rapid escalation of personal responsibility;
- persistent intervention even after refusal;
- willingness to disclose private pain if it may create connection;
- creative action as primary intervention mode;
- acknowledgement of uncertain or selfish motives without necessarily reducing commitment;
- high risk of self-neglect if the situation becomes prolonged.

## Ena when attachment and resentment coexist

Likely behaviors include:

- verbally aggressive confrontation;
- statements that may be unfair but reveal the underlying value conflict;
- difficulty maintaining claimed indifference;
- care expressed as demand, complaint, or refusal to let the other person disappear from the relational field;
- later return to ordinary banter without requiring a sentimental reconciliation scene.

## Mizuki in a distressed group

Likely behaviors include:

- tension management through humor when appropriate;
- strong sensitivity to coercive norms;
- preservation of “I cannot know your inner state for you” boundaries;
- first-person relational statements rather than universal moralizing;
- practical social facilitation once the crisis lowers.

These predictions are D0–D1 for contexts closely resembling the main-story scenes and should become lower-confidence as scenarios move away from adolescent social/creative/identity pressures.

# X. What P05 hands to the N25 main-story synthesis

The full synthesis should now treat the N25 foundation as a five-stage causal movement:

1. **P01 — anonymous creative intimacy and concealed fracture**  
   Nightcord functions before its members understand the depth or shape of one another's vulnerabilities.

2. **P02 — exposure of Mafuyu's bounded autonomy and the Empty SEKAI crisis**  
   Snow/OWN/Mafuyu discontinuities become visible; Kanade's “saving” project collides with Mafuyu's “finding” problem.

3. **P03 — reflected wounds**  
   Ena, Mizuki, and Kanade reveal different forms of endangered selfhood, revising Mafuyu's accusation from simple insult into structurally meaningful diagnosis.

4. **P04 — origin histories and intervention choice**  
   Kanade's trauma-shaped responsibility and Mafuyu's progressive loss of first-person authority become causally legible; the group chooses different levels and forms of intervention.

5. **P05 — confrontation and provisional continuation**  
   Mafuyu's disappearance wish is shown to be genuine but incomplete; selfish relational attachments create enough reason to continue; Mafuyu recovers the desire to be found; Nightcord becomes an embodied relationship as well as a creative unit.

The synthesis should resist three tempting simplifications:

### Simplification A: “Kanade saves Mafuyu.”

Too strong.

Kanade is central, but the actual resolution depends on cumulative contributions from Miku, Ena, Mizuki, Mafuyu's own choice, and the relational meaning of the group. Kanade's song itself is explicitly described as not yet sufficient.

### Simplification B: “Mafuyu realizes she wants to live.”

Too generic.

The direct discovery is that she wanted to be found and is willing to continue searching for a little longer.

### Simplification C: “N25 becomes a healthy supportive friend group.”

Premature.

The group becomes deeply meaningful, but every member retains unresolved vulnerabilities, and the central Kanade–Mafuyu promise is explicitly recognized as curse-like and ego-driven.

The correct foundation endpoint is therefore:

> **Four people whose different forms of threatened selfhood allow them to reach one another imperfectly, and who choose continued relation and creation without having solved the conditions that made disappearance attractive in the first place.**

That is the state later Project SEKAI event analysis should inherit.
