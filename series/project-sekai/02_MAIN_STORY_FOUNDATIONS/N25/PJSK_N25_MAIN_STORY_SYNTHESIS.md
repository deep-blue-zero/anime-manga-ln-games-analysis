# Project SEKAI — Nightcord at 25:00 Main Story Synthesis

# 1. Purpose, authority, and use

This document is the canonical V1 synthesis of the Nightcord at 25:00 main-story foundation. It integrates the five bounded deep readings of `nightcode_01_00` through `nightcode_01_20` into one causal, psychological, relational, epistemic, and reconstruction-oriented model.

It is not a substitute for the five phase readings when scene-level evidence or interpretive detail is needed. The phase readings remain the preferred sources for local claims and locators. This synthesis owns the higher-order responsibilities that cannot be cleanly assigned to any single phase:

1. explain the complete causal movement from anonymous collaboration to embodied relationship;
2. define the main-story longitudinal state transitions for Kanade Yoisaki, Mafuyu Asahina, Ena Shinonome, and Mizuki Akiyama;
3. reconcile claims that were provisional or partially wrong in earlier phases;
4. formalize the relationship architecture that later event analysis must inherit rather than reconstruct from scratch;
5. preserve who knows what at the end of the main story, including audience-only information that must not be imported into character behavior;
6. identify the behavioral and speech foundations already strong enough to support later reconstruction work;
7. define what the main story does **not** resolve, so later development is measured against the correct baseline rather than against an imagined completed recovery.

The intended downstream use is therefore twofold. For story analysis, this document is the foundation state from which later Nightcord events should advance. For character reconstruction, it is the shared historical substrate that prevents a later monograph or scenario model from combining mutually incompatible early, crisis, and post-main versions of a character.

# 2. Executive synthesis

The Nightcord main story is best understood not as a story in which four similarly depressed creators discover friendship, nor as a story in which Kanade simply saves Mafuyu. It is a story about **four different threats to first-person authority becoming mutually legible through creative work**.

Each principal character has a domain in which the right to define the self has become unstable:

- **Mafuyu** has progressively lost access to internally authoritative preference. Other people's expectations, praise, definitions, and happiness have become substitutes for knowing what she wants. Her central question is whether an internally owned self remains available to be found at all.
- **Kanade** has converted an authentic desire to make people happy through music into an absolute moral obligation after interpreting her father's collapse as her fault. Her central question is whether a self organized around rescue has legitimacy if rescue fails.
- **Ena** knows the self she values—artist—but fears that talent may deny that self meaningful recognition. Her central question is whether an identity can remain worth inhabiting when the world may never validate the domain that matters most to her.
- **Mizuki** has comparatively clear access to personal preference and self-presentation, but lives under social pressure that treats that self as an object to classify, discuss, normalize, or correct. The central question is whether a known self can remain intact when other people refuse to accept its self-authorizing status.

These are not interchangeable pathologies. Mafuyu's problem is not Ena's, Ena's is not Mizuki's, and Mizuki's is not Kanade's. The story's central insight is that people do not need identical wounds in order to recognize the structure of threatened selfhood in one another.

Creative work is the medium through which that recognition becomes possible. Nightcord begins as a paradox: the members can know one another's aesthetic sensibilities, work rhythms, and emotional output while remaining ignorant of legal names, faces, families, schools, and ordinary lives. Anonymity protects each member from ordinary social demands and permits intense collaboration, but it also produces catastrophic informational weakness when Snow disappears. The same structure that enables emotionally candid creation allows a member to approach existential collapse while her collaborators lack even the basic offline information required to locate her.

Mafuyu is the point at which these systems collide. As Snow, she is a competent, considerate collaborator. As OWN, she produces work that Ena hears as much colder and more rejecting than K's music. At school and home, Mafuyu occupies the exemplary “good girl” role with high social fluency. In the Empty SEKAI, she becomes blunt, irritable, rejecting, and explicit about wanting to disappear. The correct model is not that one presentation is the “real Mafuyu” and all others are fake. The main story instead demonstrates **self-discontinuity and differential access**: Mafuyu can produce genuine or partly genuine prosocial behavior while being unable to locate the preferences, feelings, and first-person authority that would let her understand how those behaviors relate to a stable self.

The causal history later confirms that this is developmental. Young Mafuyu can want things. She wants to become a nurse because she wants to help sick people. She begins to say she wants to go to the arcade. Praise makes her happy. She remembers having things she likes. The tragedy is therefore not an original void. It is a process by which external approval becomes progressively more authoritative than internal preference. Her mother is important, but the system is distributed across parents, teachers, friends, achievement structures, and Mafuyu's own learned habit of treating other people's happiness as proof that a path must be correct.

Kanade's trajectory runs in parallel but not identically. She also begins with an authentic prosocial value. Before the catastrophe involving her father, she already wants to make music that makes people happy. Her father's collapse does not invent this value; it destroys its ordinary limits. Kanade reads a multicausal professional and psychological crisis as a personal moral failure caused by her talent and her song. From that interpretation she constructs a command: if her music once hurt the person she loved most, she must continue creating until she can make music capable of saving anyone.

This matters because Mafuyu enters Kanade's life not merely as a suffering friend but as a test case for the legitimacy of Kanade's existence. Snow's statement that K's music once felt as though it saved her appears to validate Kanade's mission. When Mafuyu later says that K was not enough—that proximity to Kanade's music failed to help her find herself—Kanade translates “not enough to find me” into “I failed to save her.” Her response is predictable from the guilt architecture: she works more, takes on Snow's responsibilities, neglects food and sleep, rejects interference, and eventually collapses.

The main story gradually introduces an alternative to Kanade's binary of **saved / failed**: **reached**. An old Kanade song existed in the Empty SEKAI before the group understood why. Miku's claim is not that Kanade already saved Mafuyu. It is that the song reached somewhere almost nothing else could reach. Partial contact can matter even if the entire crisis remains unresolved.

Kanade initially resists that category. If Mafuyu remains unsaved, reaching her feels meaningless. Yet by the final movement Kanade becomes more reflexive. She can recognize that her rescue project contains ego, that an intervention meant to help can impose pressure, and that success is uncertain. Crucially, self-criticism does not dissolve the compulsion. Kanade proceeds anyway.

The final confrontation therefore does not present a clean opposition between selfishness and care. Mafuyu accurately identifies that all three interventions are in some sense selfish:

- Kanade wants to save Mafuyu partly because saving someone sustains Kanade's own reason to create and live.
- Ena wants Mafuyu to remain partly because she envies Mafuyu's talent and wants more of the work she cannot stop valuing.
- Mizuki wants Mafuyu to remain because Mafuyu's disappearance would make Mizuki lonely.

The story does not refute these motives. Instead, it makes their first-person specificity the basis of relational meaning. Other people's attachment matters not because they possess objective authority to tell Mafuyu what her true self must want, but because their wants demonstrate that Mafuyu exists in relationships not reducible to the socially authored role of “good girl.”

Mafuyu's desire to disappear is therefore neither dismissed as false consciousness nor accepted as the only authentic desire. The source makes clear that it is real. What the crisis reveals is that it is **incomplete**. Mafuyu has become exhausted not only by suffering but by hope: each attempt to find herself raises the possibility that something may still exist; each failure renews despair. Disappearance seems preferable because it promises an end to the cycle of searching, hoping, failing, and being forced to search again.

The deeper desire recovered at the endpoint is narrower and more specific than “I want to live”:

> **Mafuyu wanted to be found.**

That recognition changes the structure of the search. She no longer has to solve the self entirely alone before continued existence can be justified. She can permit Kanade, Mizuki, Ena, Miku, and Nightcord to remain present while she continues looking.

The ending is therefore **continuation under acknowledged incompleteness**.

Mafuyu has not found a stable complete self. Her family environment remains unchanged. Mundane preference remains difficult. Kanade's responsibility inflation remains dangerous. Ena's talent wound remains active. Mizuki's deeper school and disclosure problems remain largely private. Nightcord does not become a frictionless therapeutic friend group.

What does change is the social architecture. Four anonymous collaborators become people who know one another's names and faces, have crossed into one another's crisis states, can meet offline, can argue without the group dissolving, and can choose continued creation without demanding that every vulnerability first be repaired.

The foundation endpoint can therefore be stated as follows:

> **Nightcord becomes a chosen relational and creative structure in which four people with different forms of threatened selfhood can reach one another imperfectly, while none of the conditions that made disappearance or self-erasure attractive has yet been fully solved.**

That is the state later event analysis must inherit.

# 3. Five-stage causal architecture

The five phase readings resolve into one continuous movement rather than five independent mini-arcs.

## Stage 1 — Anonymous creative intimacy and concealed fracture (`nightcode_01_00`–`01_03`)

The opening establishes Nightcord as already functional before the crisis. The group is not formed by rescuing Mafuyu; it has an existing production rhythm, division of labor, aesthetic trust, and ordinary social texture.

Kanade is operationally central. She coordinates progress, requests feedback, and returns persistently to composition. Ena is expressive, artistically insecure, and able to distinguish the emotional temperature of different works. Mizuki is playful, socially fluent, aesthetically oriented, and technologically comfortable. Snow is considerate, productive, and capable of specific artistic praise.

The hidden structural problems are already present:

- Kanade's salvation imperative predates Mafuyu's open crisis;
- Kanade's food and sleep neglect are baseline vulnerabilities;
- Ena's artistic recognition problem is already visible through the contrast between easy selfie attention and difficult art-account recognition;
- Mizuki's school irregularity exists without its explanation yet being available;
- Mafuyu's public/Snow/private/OWN states do not integrate cleanly.

The most important relational fact is that Kanade and Snow already use one another's work as existential evidence. Kanade originally contacted Snow because Snow's earlier music affected her; Snow tells Kanade that K's music once felt as though it saved her. For Kanade, this becomes apparent proof that the rescue mission can work. For Mafuyu, the same experience later turns out to have meant something different: K's music created hope that being near it might help her find herself.

P01 thus plants the central semantic mismatch before either person can name it:

> **Kanade hears “saved.” Mafuyu experiences “maybe I can find myself.”**

The OWN mystery makes the fracture audible. Ena hears warmth beneath K's dark work but hears OWN as cold, rejecting, and dangerous. Mafuyu privately confirms that OWN is part of a search that has not succeeded. Music is already functioning as a trace of states that ordinary conversation hides.

## Stage 2 — Exposure, bounded autonomy, and rupture (`nightcode_01_04`–`01_07`)

The second movement turns hidden discontinuity into open crisis.

Mafuyu's school and family life show why ordinary competence is not evidence of autonomous selfhood. She is socially skilled enough to teach, joke, read the room, and satisfy expectations. Yet repeated questions about what *she* wants produce deflection toward what would make other people happy or what seems sensible.

The surrounding pressure is usually soft rather than overtly coercive. Choice is verbally granted, but desirable options are preselected and attached to identities—sensible daughter, good student, admirable future doctor—that Mafuyu has learned to inhabit. This matters because the behavior cannot be reduced to conscious lying. The role has become anticipatory and partly automatic.

When Snow disappears, anonymity changes from refuge to liability. Kanade, Ena, and Mizuki care, but they cannot locate their collaborator through ordinary offline means. Their first embodied encounter and the Empty SEKAI disclosure permanently change the group structure.

Mafuyu explicitly distinguishes “Snow” from the self speaking in the Empty SEKAI. Snow's warmth can no longer be treated as a simple transparent window into the person, but neither can it be dismissed as wholly fabricated. The group instead faces someone who no longer trusts the socially successful versions of herself and who experiences isolation as preferable to continued participation in roles she cannot inhabit from within.

The Kanade–Mafuyu mismatch becomes explicit. Mafuyu explains that K's music was not enough to complete her search. Kanade immediately processes that as failed rescue. Ena experiences the OWN revelation as both artistic hierarchy and relational humiliation. Mizuki perceives coercive pressure around Mafuyu more quickly than Mafuyu does herself.

P02 ends with Mafuyu declaring disappearance as the remaining option if the self cannot be found and accusing the other three of wanting to disappear too. That accusation becomes the organizing question of the next movement.

## Stage 3 — Reflected wounds and differentiated threatened selfhood (`nightcode_01_08`–`01_10`)

The third movement refuses to validate Mafuyu's accusation literally in identical terms. Instead, it reveals structural family resemblance.

Ena comes closest to literal confirmation. She explicitly acknowledges having wanted to disappear. Her crisis is organized around the artistic self she values but fears she may not have the talent to substantiate. Appearance-based attention is available but emotionally inadequate because it validates the wrong domain.

Mizuki presents an almost inverse autonomy problem to Mafuyu. Mizuki can clearly say what they like and why they present themselves as they do: personal preference is sufficient. The injury comes from other people treating that preference as socially negotiable. Even peers who reject overt harassment may privately believe Mizuki should become more “normal” if they want scrutiny to stop.

Kanade responds to Mafuyu's rejection through overfunctioning. She takes on Snow's work, expands the mission, neglects food and rest, and eventually snaps when the others try to interrupt. The source at this stage makes mission-driven self-erasure visible before the later episodes confirm direct disappearance desire.

The analytical conclusion is not “all four want to die.” It is:

> **all four contain a domain in which continued existence as a recognizable self feels conditional or threatened.**

That commonality explains why Mafuyu can wound them so efficiently. She does not need complete autobiographical knowledge to identify a structural vulnerability.

## Stage 4 — Causal origins, partial efficacy, and intervention choice (`nightcode_01_11`–`01_15`)

The fourth movement supplies the histories that P01–P03 could not safely infer.

Kanade's father's collapse is revealed to be multicausal: professional displacement, client pressure, financial responsibility, anxiety about obsolescence, painful comparison with Kanade's talent, and crisis over whether his commercial work still fulfills his ideal. Kanade compresses that complexity into personal guilt. The simplification is psychologically devastating but also restores a form of agency: if Kanade caused suffering through music, perhaps the right music can repair it.

The same phase proves that Kanade's prosocial orientation existed before the catastrophe. Trauma does not create “make people happy through music.” Trauma converts it from aspiration to command.

Miku then introduces the category of **reaching**. Kanade's old song existed in Mafuyu's otherwise empty SEKAI. It did not save her, but it reached her. Kanade initially rejects partial efficacy; P04 begins the process by which she can later act without certainty while still remaining burdened by rescue logic.

Mafuyu's autobiographical reconstruction similarly revises earlier shorthand. She once had accessible wants. Her nursing aspiration is especially important because it suggests that helping others may contain an authentic substrate rather than being entirely a “good girl” fabrication. Her first-person authority is progressively displaced as parents, teachers, peers, praise, and expectation teach her to infer correctness from other people's satisfaction. Eventually even taste, enjoyment, and aesthetic reaction become externally referenced.

The group then develops three different intervention ethics:

- Kanade: persistent rescue under acknowledged risk;
- Mizuki: epistemically humble first-person attachment;
- Ena: refusal of unlimited rescue obligation while resentment remains unresolved.

Nightcord does not require unanimity. That is crucial to the group's later capacity to remain relationally meaningful without becoming an ideology of compulsory caretaking.

## Stage 5 — Terminal crisis, selfish attachment, and provisional continuation (`nightcode_01_16`–`01_20`)

The final movement tests whether anyone can reach Mafuyu without simply replacing one externally authored demand with another.

Mafuyu's adaptive persona begins to leak in ordinary school life. At home, her mother plans to discard the synthesizer while moving ahead with medical-exam preparation. Mafuyu reaches a terminal conclusion: nothing was found, the true feeling was disappearance, and accepting that conclusion promises relief.

Kanade and Mizuki arrive. Kanade discloses her own disappearance desire and father's history, but does so through Kanade's guilt interpretation rather than the audience's more complete causal model. Mafuyu correctly identifies the “curse”: Kanade's mission to save others also saves Kanade from confronting a self without that mission.

Ena later arrives and contributes an ethically compromised but emotionally honest form of attachment. She initially speaks as though talent creates an obligation to keep producing, a claim Mafuyu rightly rejects. Yet Ena's underlying truth is relational: she hates aspects of Mafuyu, cannot stop valuing OWN's music, and does not want a world in which that person and work vanish.

Mizuki explicitly refuses epistemic ownership. Only Mafuyu can fully know Mafuyu. But Mizuki can say what Mafuyu's disappearance would mean to Mizuki: loneliness.

Mafuyu names all of this as selfishness. The story largely agrees. The breakthrough is not that the others become morally pure, but that their selfish attachment creates evidence of relationship.

Kanade then makes the most consequential promise of the foundation. She will keep creating until Mafuyu can find herself. If Kanade herself moves toward disappearance, Mafuyu can remind her that the search remains unfinished. Mafuyu immediately recognizes the promise as another curse. Kanade accepts the description.

Mafuyu does not promise complete recovery. She agrees to search a little longer.

The new song and Miku's interpretation make explicit what has changed: Mafuyu's newly accessible first-person desire is to be found. The group returns from the SEKAI, exchanges real names in ordinary space, and meets at a family restaurant. The story deliberately ends with low-stakes behavior rather than another crisis. Mafuyu remains poor at trivial preference selection and uncertain about whether her apology is emotionally authentic, yet she can say “I don't know,” choose to sit near Kanade, privately message her, use her real name, and thank her for finding her.

The transition from anonymous collaborator to embodied relationship is complete. The psychological problems are not.

# 4. Governing structural thesis: first-person authority

The most useful concept for integrating the four arcs is **first-person authority**: the practical ability to treat one's own preference, feeling, interpretation, value, or chosen identity as meaningful evidence about how to live.

Nightcord's four members lose or defend that authority in different ways.

Mafuyu has the most direct failure. Other people's responses become increasingly reliable to her as decision rules precisely because her own responses become inaccessible. If parents, teachers, classmates, and friends are happy, the path appears correct. If her mother says food is delicious, Mafuyu reasons that it probably is despite weak sensory confirmation. Social success becomes a substitute for inner evidence.

Kanade possesses clear preferences, but moral authority over the self is externalized into outcomes. Her music has legitimacy if it saves; Kanade has legitimacy if she can continue creating music that saves. The father's collapse makes her distrust ordinary desire as sufficient. “I want to make music” becomes morally inadequate unless the music repairs suffering.

Ena possesses clear desire but allows recognition to adjudicate whether the desired self is legitimate. She wants to be an artist. The danger is that insufficient talent or validation could make that identity feel false regardless of how much she values it. She is therefore especially sensitive to praise, pity, condescension, hierarchy, and the authority of accomplished artists.

Mizuki possesses perhaps the strongest explicit first-person preference in the foundation: “I like this, so I choose it.” The threat comes from social systems that reject the sufficiency of that answer. Other people demand explanation, reinterpret preference as provocation, or suggest normalization as the practical solution.

These differences explain why the group can both clash and connect. Each member is sensitive to a different way in which the self can become contingent on external judgment:

- Mafuyu: external judgment replaces preference;
- Kanade: successful effect replaces ordinary permission to create and exist;
- Ena: recognition replaces self-validation of artistic identity;
- Mizuki: social acceptability challenges self-authorization of presentation.

The main story's relational movement can therefore be understood as a partial redistribution of authority. None of the members becomes fully autonomous in an abstract individualist sense. Instead, they begin learning that relationship can supply **contact without total definition**.

Miku does not define Mafuyu's complete self; Miku identifies that a feeling exists and invites her to hear it. Kanade cannot tell Mafuyu who she is; Kanade can say that Mafuyu's sound is present in her work and promise to keep listening. Mizuki cannot solve Mafuyu; Mizuki can state that Mafuyu's absence would matter. Ena cannot make Mafuyu value her own talent; Ena can refuse the claim that Mafuyu's work is meaningless to everyone else.

This is why the ending's selfishness matters. A statement like “your disappearance would make *me* lonely” can be less autonomy-eroding than “you should live because living is objectively right,” because the first statement does not pretend to possess Mafuyu's interior truth. It adds relational evidence without replacing first-person authority wholesale.

The foundation remains unstable because Kanade's promise can cross that line. Her willingness to keep creating indefinitely can support Mafuyu's search, but it can also make one person's unresolved identity central to another person's obligation to live and work. The story itself marks this with curse language. Later analysis must therefore track whether mutual relation increases each person's first-person authority or merely creates a new external dependency structure.

# 5. Kanade Yoisaki — longitudinal synthesis

## 5.1 Starting state: vocation already transformed into penance

Kanade's main-story arc does not begin with Mafuyu. That is the first interpretive constraint later character work must preserve.

Before the Nightcord crisis becomes visible, Kanade already lives inside an unusually severe moral architecture. Music is not merely profession, hobby, talent, or chosen creative practice. It is all of those things, but it is also the medium through which Kanade attempts to answer guilt over her father. She believes her own music contributed decisively to his collapse and therefore understands continued composition as a reparative obligation.

This is why ordinary descriptions such as “kind,” “selfless,” or “hard-working” are insufficient. They describe surface behavior but not its pressure system.

The source establishes an important developmental sequence:

1. music is originally associated with warmth and making another person happy;
2. young Kanade genuinely wants to create music that can have that effect;
3. her father's professional crisis makes Kanade's intuitive talent symbolically dangerous;
4. after his collapse, Kanade reads causality through guilt;
5. the earlier desire becomes an absolute rule: she must create music capable of saving people;
6. continued composition becomes linked to whether Kanade can justify continued existence.

The distinction between authentic value and trauma-shaped compulsion should remain permanent in the reconstruction model. If later analysis describes Kanade's wish to help others as “only guilt,” it erases the childhood evidence. If it describes the wish as uncomplicated altruism, it erases the coercive structure that follows the collapse.

The most accurate formulation at main-story foundation level is:

> **Kanade has a genuine prosocial artistic value that trauma has converted into an over-responsible moral identity.**

## 5.2 Causal distortion: why guilt is psychologically functional

The father's diary makes clear that his collapse cannot responsibly be reduced to Kanade's song. He is facing professional obsolescence anxiety, client demand for a style associated with Kanade's contribution, financial and parental responsibility, loss of confidence in his own creative relevance, and a painful confrontation with the possibility that his daughter possesses an intuitive musical capacity he cannot reproduce through effort.

Kanade does not preserve that multicausality. She compresses it.

That cognitive move is important because guilt does something for her besides punish her. It restores causal agency.

A distributed tragedy involving labor conditions, market taste, personal vulnerability, professional identity, family responsibility, and uncontrollable circumstance cannot be repaired by one action. A tragedy caused by “my music hurt him” can at least generate a counter-command: make different music; make better music; make music that saves.

Kanade therefore prefers a devastating explanation in which she possesses responsibility over an explanation in which much of the suffering may be uncontrollable.

This yields a strong behavioral prediction that should survive into later character work:

> When a valued person's condition worsens in a context where Kanade has acted, Kanade is unusually likely to search for what **she** did wrong, what **she** failed to notice, and what **she** must do more of—even when causal evidence is ambiguous.

This is not simply low self-esteem. It is a strategy for preserving actionable moral causality.

## 5.3 Baseline agency and the danger of mistaking quietness for passivity

Kanade's low-energy speech and physically depleted lifestyle can invite a false reconstruction in which she is generally passive. The main story contradicts that.

In domains she understands, Kanade is highly agentic. She:

- recruits collaborators;
- coordinates Nightcord's production state;
- requests and integrates artistic feedback;
- notices stylistic similarities across works;
- makes rapid production decisions;
- creates purpose-built music in response to another person's condition;
- initiates high-stakes intervention despite rejection;
- discloses private trauma when she believes disclosure may create contact.

Her social style is quiet. Her decision style under perceived responsibility is not.

The stronger reconstruction rule is:

> **Kanade has low social theatricality but high task and mission agency.**

This distinction becomes especially important in cross-context scenarios. A quiet Kanade should not simply wait for a louder character to decide what happens if she believes someone is in serious danger or if a music/creative problem lies within her competence.

## 5.4 Snow as proof and Mafuyu as an existential test case

Snow's statement that K's music once felt as though it saved her has disproportionate psychological importance because it appears to validate Kanade's entire post-trauma project.

For a conventional creator, praise might be encouraging evidence of skill. For Kanade, the statement functions closer to moral proof: perhaps the thing she believes harmed her father can, in fact, save someone.

This makes Mafuyu unusually dangerous to Kanade's self-concept before either understands the depth of the relationship.

Mafuyu later explains that Kanade's music did matter, but not in the way Kanade had encoded it. K's work created hope that Mafuyu might find herself. Continued proximity did not complete the search. Therefore, from Mafuyu's perspective, the relationship produced partial contact followed by disappointment.

Kanade translates this into rescue logic:

> If Mafuyu remains in crisis, I did not save her.

That translation is the immediate bridge from relational failure to self-neglect.

## 5.5 Failure response: overfunctioning before collapse

Kanade's first response to failed rescue is not retreat. It is escalation.

She attempts to absorb Snow's production functions, increases her workload, continues composing, neglects food and sleep, and becomes resistant when Ena and Mizuki tell her to stop. When intervention from others conflicts with the rescue mission, she can treat care as obstruction.

The main-story sequence therefore supports a robust high-stakes response pattern:

**perceived responsibility → increased work → reduced bodily attention → reduced receptivity to interruption → irritability → collapse.**

This sequence should be distinguished from Kanade's ordinary self-neglect. Poor food and sleep habits exist before crisis, but mission failure intensifies them into acute risk.

At the end of the overfunctioning cycle, Kanade does not merely become tired. She questions whether composition has meaning and moves toward disappearance or passive withdrawal. Later autobiographical evidence confirms that explicit disappearance desire also existed after her father's collapse.

Thus Mafuyu's perception that Kanade contains something analogous to her own disappearance impulse is stronger than P03 could initially prove.

## 5.6 The category Kanade initially lacks: partial efficacy

Kanade's moral perfectionism is visible in her response to Miku's claim that an old Kanade song reached Mafuyu.

Kanade initially regards this as meaningless if Mafuyu was not saved.

This reveals a critical cognition:

> **Helping that does not resolve the problem can feel indistinguishable from failure.**

Miku's intervention is therefore conceptually important. “Reached” is not a weaker synonym for “saved.” It is a different standard of effect.

The old song's presence in Mafuyu's Empty SEKAI demonstrates that something can make contact with a person without determining the outcome of that person's life. That category allows action under uncertainty rather than only action backed by rescue certainty.

Kanade does not immediately internalize the lesson. She repeatedly translates reaching back into saving. Yet P04 and P05 show an incremental shift: by the end, she can acknowledge that her current song may still be insufficient and continue creating without treating that fact as immediate proof that the effort is worthless.

This should be treated as **early movement**, not completed recovery from outcome absolutism.

## 5.7 Self-criticism without release from obligation

One of the most important reconstruction findings is that Kanade is capable of serious reflexivity.

Before the final intervention she recognizes:

- a song intended to help can impose pressure;
- she may be repeating a pattern she associates with harming her father;
- she specifically wants **her** music to help, which gives the project an egoistic component;
- there is no guarantee of success.

During the confrontation Mafuyu intensifies the criticism. Kanade's salvation project also functions to save Kanade from a life in which she cannot fulfill the father's final injunction or justify her own talent.

Kanade does not defend herself by insisting on pure motive. She eventually accepts the selfish component.

This changes how later scenarios should model her. She is not an unreflective rescuer who cannot perceive ethical objections. She can perceive them with considerable clarity.

The problem is harder:

> **understanding the pathology does not automatically free Kanade from it.**

She can say “this may be ego” and still conclude “I will do it.”

That combination—high self-awareness plus high compulsion—is more source-faithful than either saintly altruism or oblivious obsession.

## 5.8 The reciprocal curse with Mafuyu

The main-story endpoint creates a relationship that is both more mutual and more dangerous than the opening structure.

Kanade promises to continue creating until Mafuyu can find herself. Mafuyu is permitted to tell Kanade, if Kanade begins moving toward disappearance, that Mafuyu remains unfound and the work is therefore unfinished.

This creates a reciprocal survival contingency:

- Kanade's persistence gives Mafuyu a reason not to terminate the search;
- Mafuyu's unfinished search gives Kanade a reason to continue creating.

Mafuyu immediately understands the potential danger and describes the arrangement as another curse. Kanade accepts that description.

The synthesis therefore rejects both totalizing readings:

**“healthy mutual support”** is too clean because the bond explicitly ties each person's unresolved crisis to the other's continuation.

**“pure codependency”** is also too clean because the relationship genuinely interrupts terminal isolation, increases disclosure, permits partial efficacy, and creates life-sustaining reasons to remain present.

The correct main-story authority state is:

> **reciprocal, life-sustaining attachment with explicit dependency risk.**

Later events should be evaluated for whether they increase autonomy within the bond, intensify mutual burden, or do both at once.

## 5.9 Kanade at the main-story endpoint

### Self-concept

Kanade remains fundamentally a composer. She also remains someone who believes she must continue. The difference is that she has begun to recognize that the mission is partly built from guilt and ego rather than from morally pure obligation.

### Values

Strongly supported values include:

- music as a means of reaching people;
- reduction of another person's suffering;
- persistence;
- loyalty to collaborators;
- honoring the importance of her father's music and injunction to continue.

### Active vulnerabilities

- inflated responsibility;
- outcome absolutism;
- self-neglect;
- disappearance ideation under collapse;
- difficulty accepting that another person's condition may not be controllable by her action;
- making creative productivity carry too much of the burden of self-worth.

### Relational changes

Kanade is no longer operating entirely through anonymous creative roles. She has disclosed deeply personal history to Mafuyu and Mizuki, uses real names, participates in offline contact, and accepts that other Nightcord members can disagree with her intervention without being expelled from the relationship.

### Reconstruction implication

At the endpoint, Kanade should be modeled as:

> **quiet, highly agentic under responsibility, observant through creative work, self-neglect-prone, capable of sophisticated self-critique, and likely to persist beyond ordinary boundaries when she believes a person's existence is at stake.**

The main story does **not** justify modeling her as having learned healthy limits.

# 6. Mafuyu Asahina — longitudinal synthesis

## 6.1 The wrong starting model: “Mafuyu has no self”

The main story decisively rejects the historical absolute that Mafuyu never possessed internally available preferences.

Young Mafuyu experiences pride and happiness. She can identify an occupational desire: nurse. She can explain the motive: helping sick people. She begins to express interest in ordinary peer leisure. She remembers having favorite things. These facts matter because they transform the meaning of the later Empty SEKAI.

The problem is not metaphysical absence of self from the beginning.

It is **loss of access**.

This distinction should govern all later character modeling. A reconstruction that treats Mafuyu as naturally empty, inherently emotionless, or simply born without preferences would contradict the foundation.

## 6.2 How externally authored identity becomes self-maintaining

The family is central to Mafuyu's development, but overt domination is not the main mechanism shown by the source.

Her mother often speaks through care, rationality, opportunity, and presumed shared goals. When Mafuyu says she wants to become a nurse, the response is not “no.” It is a more prestigious, rationalized alternative attached to the same prosocial motive: if she wants to help the sick and has the grades, why not become a doctor?

Her father reinforces the suggestion. Teachers and friends later celebrate the doctor identity as especially suitable for kind, capable Mafuyu. Classmates assume the exemplary student has no time for the arcade before Mafuyu can complete her own statement.

The structure is powerful precisely because it rarely looks like coercion from the inside.

Mafuyu learns several rules:

- good choices make parents happy;
- admirable identities produce praise;
- sensible people choose paths that match their demonstrated competence;
- disagreement risks appearing irrational, selfish, disappointing, or out of character;
- if everyone is pleased, that pleasure can substitute for evidence that the choice is right.

Eventually the role becomes anticipatory. People do not need to tell Mafuyu what she should want. The social identity supplies the answer before preference can be articulated.

Mafuyu participates in maintaining the system because it works socially. She is intelligent, perceptive, conscientious, and highly competent at producing the expected response. The tragedy is that skill at successful adaptation makes the loss harder to detect.

## 6.3 First-person authority collapses beyond career choice

The narrative carefully broadens Mafuyu's problem beyond “her mother picked her future.”

She loses confidence in:

- preference;
- appetite;
- taste;
- enjoyment;
- aesthetic affect;
- spontaneous desire.

The cake sequence is especially revealing. Mafuyu does not experience strong taste, yet her mother's statement that the cake is delicious becomes more authoritative than Mafuyu's sensation. The same pattern later appears with music: friends call a song bright and fun; Mafuyu can recognize the category but cannot access the corresponding feeling.

This is the deeper structure behind the recurrent question “what do you want?”

Mafuyu is not merely indecisive between known options. The mechanism normally used to rank the options has become unreliable to her.

## 6.4 Public Mafuyu, Snow, OWN, and the problem of “the real self”

The main story gives Mafuyu several sharply different presentations:

- exemplary school and family Mafuyu;
- Snow, the considerate and competent Nightcord collaborator;
- OWN, the creator whose work feels rejecting and dangerous;
- private Mafuyu in states of flattening and confusion;
- Empty-SEKAI Mafuyu, who is blunt, irritable, and hostile to intrusion;
- post-crisis Nightcord Mafuyu, who can admit uncertainty without automatically repairing it into the socially ideal answer.

It is tempting to arrange these in a hierarchy where Empty-SEKAI Mafuyu is “true” and Snow is “fake.” The source does not support such a clean binary.

Snow is explicitly a differentiated role. Mafuyu herself distinguishes Snow from the self speaking during the rupture. Yet earlier Snow interactions include specific artistic praise, real collaboration, concern, and responses that may exceed deliberate strategic fabrication. Mafuyu is sometimes surprised by emotionally meaningful things Snow says, suggesting that the role may express material Mafuyu cannot consciously integrate rather than merely inventing false affect.

The strongest foundation formulation is:

> **Mafuyu's presentations are context-conditioned modes with unequal access to feeling and autonomy; none should automatically be treated as a complete total self.**

This is central for reconstruction. Crisis bluntness should not overwrite public competence. Public warmth should not be assumed fully self-authored. Snow's gentleness should not be dismissed wholesale. Post-main candor should not be projected backward.

## 6.5 OWN as search rather than simple self-expression

OWN is valuable evidence because Mafuyu's work can externalize affective material she cannot otherwise identify.

Ena hears OWN as cold, rejecting, and frightening. Mafuyu continues creating because she is trying to find something. Audience praise does not answer the question because external recognition is not the missing variable. In fact, praise belongs to the same category of external response that has become unreliable as evidence of selfhood.

This is why Mafuyu can be highly talented and still experience herself as empty. The talents that others value do not answer “what do I want?”

The source later makes the artistic function explicit. Kanade can hear “Mafuyu's sound” in OWN even when Mafuyu cannot explain what that sound means. Creative output contains traces of self before conscious self-description catches up.

Thus music functions for Mafuyu less as a straightforward declaration and more as an **evidentiary residue of subjectivity**.

## 6.6 Why disappearance becomes attractive

Mafuyu initially presents disappearance as the remaining option if she cannot find herself. The terminal movement clarifies the psychological mechanism.

She is not only exhausted by pain. She is exhausted by **repeated activation of hope**.

Each attempt to recover identity produces a possibility:

> maybe this person, this song, this creative act, this space, or this insight will finally reveal something.

When the attempt fails, despair returns with additional force because hope had reopened the question.

The cycle becomes:

**search → hope → failure → intensified despair → search again.**

A hopeless conclusion can feel safer than another round of uncertain possibility. Disappearance promises termination of the search itself.

That is why the foundation should not reduce the crisis to “Mafuyu secretly wants to live but cannot admit it.” The desire to disappear is source-supported and psychologically coherent within her state.

## 6.7 The breakthrough: a real desire that does not solve identity

The intervention does not reveal a complete hidden personality. It recovers one specific first-person desire:

> **Mafuyu wanted to be found.**

This is analytically powerful because it is compatible with everything that came before.

A person can:

- want the exhausting search to stop;
- genuinely want disappearance;
- fear hope;
- lack stable access to ordinary preference;
- and still want another person to locate, recognize, or remain in contact with a self that cannot yet be named.

The newly generated song and Miku's interpretation establish that this desire counts as a real feeling even though Mafuyu still cannot answer the larger question of who she is.

The endpoint is therefore not identity completion. It is a change in the search condition:

> Mafuyu no longer has to discover the self in perfect solitude before relationship is allowed to matter.

## 6.8 Why selfish attachment can help Mafuyu

Mafuyu correctly accuses the others of selfishness.

That accusation is not refuted.

Ena wants more of OWN's work. Mizuki does not want to feel the loneliness of Mafuyu's disappearance. Kanade's rescue mission sustains Kanade as well as Mafuyu.

The important distinction is that these statements belong to the speakers. They do not necessarily define Mafuyu.

For someone whose life has been saturated by statements about what she *should* want, a first-person statement such as “I will be lonely if you disappear” can paradoxically be less invasive than “you do not really want to disappear.” The former contributes relational information without claiming ownership of Mafuyu's entire interior state.

Kanade's promise is more dangerous because it can become another demand. Yet even there, Kanade's increasing admission of ego makes the relation more explicit: this is not divine or objective authority; this is Kanade's own chosen desire to keep reaching.

## 6.9 Post-crisis ordinary behavior: the critical corrective

The restaurant episode is essential because it prevents the endpoint model from being constructed only from existential crisis.

Mafuyu's behavior demonstrates both continuity and change.

Continuity:

- mundane preference remains difficult;
- she answers that anything is fine;
- she uses Kanade's meal choice as a scaffold;
- emotional certainty remains weak.

Change:

- she can admit not knowing whether an apology corresponds to genuine remorse;
- she no longer automatically supplies the maximally socially legible answer when challenged by Ena;
- she chooses proximity to Kanade;
- she participates in real-name exchange;
- she later initiates private contact;
- she listens to Kanade's song;
- she addresses Kanade by name;
- she thanks Kanade for finding her.

The especially important reconstruction change is the emergence of **honest uncertainty**.

Earlier good-girl adaptation often converts ambiguity into the answer expected by the context. Post-crisis Nightcord Mafuyu can sometimes let “I don't know” remain unresolved.

That may sound less socially polished but represents greater access to first-person epistemic honesty.

## 6.10 Mafuyu at the main-story endpoint

### Self-concept

Incomplete. Mafuyu knows that the desire to be found is real but does not possess a stable comprehensive answer to who she is.

### Goals

- continue searching;
- remain with Nightcord for now;
- continue creating;
- discover feelings and preferences that can be experienced as her own.

### Active fears

- renewed hope followed by renewed disappointment;
- never finding herself;
- being defined by others before she can speak;
- returning to a life in which external approval is the only available decision rule.

### Emotional baseline

Flattening remains present. Stronger private affect is available under stress. Nightcord increasingly permits candor, irritation, uncertainty, and selective attachment.

### Agency

Mafuyu should not be described as globally passive. Agency is domain- and state-dependent. She has difficulty initiating self-authored choices in externally structured contexts, but can choose withdrawal, confrontation, continued search, proximity, contact, and disclosure.

### Reconstruction implication

The endpoint Mafuyu model should be:

> **socially capable, highly context-conditioned, weak in mundane first-person preference, increasingly capable of honest uncertainty, selectively relationally initiative-taking, and vulnerable to severe collapse when externally authored identity and failed self-search combine.**

The source does not justify treating her as cured, uniformly cold, permanently hostile, or secretly stable beneath a mask.

# 7. Ena Shinonome — longitudinal synthesis

## 7.1 The valued self is artistic, not merely social

Ena's main-story foundation should begin from a distinction the opening makes unusually clear: she does not lack attention.

Her appearance can generate social-media approval quickly. The problem is that this approval validates a domain she does not consider sufficient. The self whose legitimacy matters most to Ena is the artist.

This means “attention-seeking” is too blunt to explain her behavior. Ena is highly responsive to recognition, but the **source of recognition, domain of recognition, and implied evaluation of competence** matter enormously.

She can receive hundreds of socially positive signals and remain injured if the artistic self receives none.

The relevant structure is:

> **I know what I want to be. I do not know whether I possess what is necessary to deserve that identity.**

This separates Ena from Mafuyu. Mafuyu struggles to locate desire. Ena's desire is vivid. The crisis lies in whether talent and outside judgment will authorize it.

## 7.2 Why Snow's praise becomes dangerous

Snow gives Ena specific artistic praise before the OWN reveal. Ena values it because it recognizes precisely the domain that matters.

When Ena learns that Snow is also OWN—an artist whose work she regards as extraordinary—the hidden talent hierarchy transforms earlier praise retroactively. Ena fears that what she received as meaningful recognition may instead have been pity, condescension, or private amusement from someone far above her skill level.

The source does not confirm that Mafuyu was mocking Ena. The foundation must preserve this distinction:

- **Ena believes the praise may have been humiliatingly inauthentic.**
- **The audience does not possess evidence sufficient to prove that interpretation.**

This is an important simulation rule. Ena's reactions often reveal what a social signal means *to Ena* without making that interpretation objectively correct.

## 7.3 Shame, anger, effort, and collapse

P03 establishes a strong response cycle.

When Ena experiences evidence that she may not be good enough:

1. shame and inferiority activate;
2. anger supplies energy and protects dignity;
3. she works harder or attempts to prove something;
4. failure intensifies the original wound;
5. substitute validation may be sought but does not solve the artistic problem;
6. despair can become severe enough to produce explicit disappearance desire.

Anger is therefore not incidental temperament. It often functions as a **mobilizing affect** between humiliation and effort.

That does not mean every angry response is psychologically defensive or secretly affectionate. Later reconstruction should avoid one-mechanism explanations. Ena is genuinely irritable in some contexts and genuinely judgmental in others. But the main story clearly shows that anger often appears at the point where something threatens the valued artistic self.

## 7.4 The father problem: permissiveness can still feel like rejection

Ena's father occupies an especially painful position because he is an artist whose judgment carries authority she cannot dismiss.

A merely critical stranger could be written off as ignorant. Her father's assessment matters because it comes from the exact domain whose recognition Ena wants.

The foundation shows that even permissive or non-prohibitive responses can fail to satisfy her. Ena does not merely want freedom to make art. She wants evidence that the identity is substantively warranted.

This is another form of externalized self-legitimacy, distinct from Mafuyu's compliance but structurally related to the broader N25 theme.

## 7.5 Ena and Mafuyu: humiliation becomes antagonistic attachment

Ena's relationship with Mafuyu is one of the best examples in the main story of why conflict cannot be used as a simple inverse measure of attachment.

After the OWN reveal, Ena is furious. She experiences Mafuyu as someone who possesses the talent Ena desperately wants while apparently treating it as meaningless. Mafuyu also seems to invalidate the Snow relationship by distancing the non-Snow self from Ena.

Yet Ena continues listening.

This matters.

She tracks changes in OWN's work, notices the increasing danger in the newer songs, and cannot make the music irrelevant even when she wants Mafuyu herself to be irrelevant.

Her episode-16 internal sequence makes the contradiction explicit: she tries to say she does not care, then acknowledges that OWN's music remains extraordinary and that Mafuyu's disappearance would mean losing something she deeply values.

By the final confrontation, Ena's language is ethically messy. She initially argues from talent as though Mafuyu owes continued creation because other people lack what she has. Mafuyu correctly rejects that premise. Talent does not answer the selfhood problem.

But beneath the unfair universal claim is a first-person truth:

> Ena wants to hear more. Ena does not want Mafuyu gone.

The relationship therefore ends the main story not with reconciliation but with a stable high-friction configuration:

> **envy + anger + artistic admiration + incomplete trust + durable attachment.**

Later event analysis should not interpret renewed arguments between Ena and Mafuyu as evidence that the main-story bond failed. Conflict is already part of the attachment's native expression.

## 7.6 Ena's boundary in P04 and why it matters

Ena refuses to join Kanade and Mizuki's first intervention attempt.

This refusal is important because the story allows a character to care without accepting unlimited obligation. Ena's argument—that everyone suffers and Mafuyu's suffering does not automatically entitle her to rescue—contains resentment, but it also asserts a legitimate personal boundary.

Kanade and Mizuki do not coerce her into participation.

Ena later supports them at a distance, listens to Kanade's song, and asks them to come back safely. Eventually she chooses to enter the confrontation herself, but on her own terms and from her own motive.

This gives Ena a strong reconstruction constraint:

> **Care should not be equated with immediate caretaking compliance.**

Ena may refuse, complain, remain angry, or insist on unfairness while still being relationally invested.

## 7.7 Ena at the main-story endpoint

### Self-concept

Artist, with artistic worth still painfully contingent on ability and recognition.

### Values

- creative achievement;
- honesty in evaluation;
- being taken seriously as an artist;
- persistence;
- relationships whose praise is not merely polite or condescending.

### Vulnerabilities

- talent hierarchy;
- ambiguous praise;
- perceived pity;
- authoritative artistic criticism;
- comparison with people who seem naturally gifted;
- fear that the valued creative self may never become socially real.

### Behavioral tendencies

- direct confrontation;
- anger under humiliation;
- competitive effort;
- verbal force when something feels unfair;
- difficulty sustaining claimed indifference when attachment is active;
- ability to return to ordinary banter without requiring emotional purification first.

### Reconstruction implication

At the endpoint, Ena should be modeled as:

> **artistically ambitious, recognition-sensitive, confrontational, capable of severe self-doubt, and likely to express attachment through irritation, demands, criticism, and persistent attention rather than consistently soft reassurance.**

# 8. Mizuki Akiyama — longitudinal synthesis

## 8.1 The known-self problem

Mizuki provides the clearest contrast to Mafuyu.

Mizuki's foundation problem is not inability to answer “what do I want?” The source gives a much more direct self-authorizing stance: they choose their presentation because they like it.

That answer is important because it requires no external justification.

The injury comes when other people refuse to allow preference to remain sufficient. Mizuki is stared at, discussed, categorized, and subjected to advice that effectively says social difficulty could be reduced by becoming more normal.

Thus:

- Mafuyu lacks reliable access to a first-person answer;
- Mizuki possesses the answer but lives among people who contest its legitimacy.

Their situations rhyme through normative pressure while remaining psychologically distinct.

## 8.2 Social fluency is genuine competence, not evidence of shallowness

Mizuki is the most consistently socially energetic N25 member in the main-story foundation.

They joke, tease, introduce topics, bring outside cultural information into the group, organize offline interaction, manage awkward transitions, and understand visual/social presentation.

The main story gives strong evidence that this is not simply an empty performance covering misery.

Mizuki genuinely likes fashion, appearance, visual creation, playful interaction, and social energy. The restaurant endpoint reinforces this. After the crisis, humor and enthusiasm are used constructively to make the first offline gathering workable.

Therefore the claim “Mizuki's cheerfulness is merely a mask” should be downgraded.

A more accurate formulation is:

> **Mizuki's playfulness is genuine personality and enjoyment that can coexist with selective disclosure and concealed vulnerability.**

This distinction is particularly important in reconstruction. A person can be sincerely funny and still use humor defensively in some situations; the latter does not invalidate the former.

## 8.3 Selective disclosure and conditional trust

Mizuki's school evidence shows why disclosure may be costly even around people who are not overtly hostile.

Peers can enjoy Mizuki's company, condemn harassment, and offer support while privately believing normalization would be the easiest solution. This creates an environment in which friendliness does not guarantee acceptance of the self on Mizuki's own terms.

The likely consequence is not universal distrust but **selective trust calibration**.

Mizuki can remain socially engaged while withholding more vulnerable information from people who may reinterpret it through normative assumptions.

This is a better reconstruction model than either “Mizuki is secretive” or “Mizuki is socially open.” They are open in many domains and guarded in others.

## 8.4 Why Mizuki notices Mafuyu's pressure quickly

Mizuki is unusually sensitive to coercion embedded in apparently reasonable language.

When Mafuyu's mother speaks about friends and proper judgment, Mizuki notices the normative pressure before Mafuyu acknowledges it. Later, Mizuki can infer the basic “good girl” structure from relatively limited evidence.

This does not grant Mizuki omniscient access to Mafuyu's psychology. The foundation repeatedly preserves that distinction.

Mizuki's insight is **structural**, not biographically complete.

They recognize something like:

> being reshaped by other people's standards can become exhausting even when those standards are presented as reasonable or caring.

This structural recognition gives Mizuki a special position in relation to Mafuyu: enough similarity to take the pressure seriously, but enough difference to avoid claiming identical experience.

## 8.5 Epistemic humility as an intervention style

Mizuki's P04/P05 intervention is one of the clearest behavioral templates in the entire main story.

They explicitly say that only Mafuyu can fully know Mafuyu's problem. Mizuki does not claim to know what will save her or what she truly must want.

Instead, Mizuki offers bounded first-person evidence:

> if Mafuyu disappears, Mizuki will be lonely.

This is a crucial distinction between empathy and epistemic appropriation.

Mizuki can say:

- “something about this resembles what I experience”;
- “I may not understand everything”;
- “I know what your absence would mean to me.”

That pattern should be considered high-confidence for later scenarios involving identity, norm pressure, or another person's ambiguous inner state.

## 8.6 Mizuki as social facilitator

The main story ends by giving Mizuki an ordinary social function that should not be undervalued.

At the restaurant, Mizuki:

- gathers the group;
- manages greetings;
- initiates introductions;
- converts real-name exchange into a ritual rather than an interrogation;
- teases Ena to reduce stiffness;
- pushes the group toward ordering;
- creates a toast;
- checks whether the energy level is comfortable for quieter Kanade and Mafuyu.

This is meaningful labor.

Nightcord contains people who can become trapped in silence, overwork, resentment, or uncertainty. Mizuki supplies conversational momentum without requiring everyone else to match the same level of expressiveness.

Kanade and Mafuyu both indicate that the atmosphere is not uncomfortable.

This gives a useful reconstruction lesson:

> **Mizuki's high social energy can be compatible with, and even supportive of, low-energy companions when it is paired with sensitivity to boundaries.**

## 8.7 Mizuki at the main-story endpoint

### Self-concept

Comparatively clear in chosen self-presentation, but vulnerable to social objectification and conditional acceptance.

### Values

- self-expression;
- autonomy;
- aesthetic play;
- chosen connection;
- not forcing another person to justify a self through external standards.

### Vulnerabilities

- gossip and objectification;
- pressure to normalize;
- apparently supportive relationships that conceal conditional acceptance;
- situations in which disclosure gives others control over how the self is categorized.

### Behavioral tendencies

- humor and teasing;
- social organization;
- visual/aesthetic fluency;
- rapid recognition of coercive normativity;
- first-person relational language under serious uncertainty;
- selective disclosure rather than universal emotional transparency.

### Reconstruction implication

At the endpoint, Mizuki should be modeled as:

> **genuinely playful and socially capable, clear about many personal preferences, highly sensitive to autonomy pressure, and unusually able to connect with another person's ambiguity without claiming total interpretive ownership.**

# 9. Relationship architecture at the main-story endpoint

Character reconstruction should not treat N25 members as four independent personalities. Their behavior is strongly relationship-conditioned.

## 9.1 Kanade ↔ Mafuyu — central but not exclusive

This dyad has the largest causal role in the main story.

### Opening state

The relationship begins through creative recognition. Kanade recruits Snow after hearing her work. Snow later tells K that K's music felt as though it saved her. Neither knows the other's ordinary life.

### Crisis state

The same statement acquires incompatible meanings. Kanade hears proof of rescue; Mafuyu experiences provisional hope of self-discovery. When self-discovery fails, Kanade experiences personal rescue failure.

### Final state

The relationship becomes direct, embodied, and mutually disclosed. Mafuyu knows major parts of Kanade's guilt history. Kanade knows Mafuyu is searching for self/feeling and understands that disappearance is tied to exhaustion in that search.

The reciprocal promise creates both resilience and dependency risk.

### Ordinary-state evidence

Mafuyu chooses to sit near Kanade, borrows her meal choice, messages her privately, uses her name, and thanks her. Kanade can accept that a song remains insufficient and continue without demanding a completed cure.

### Authority statement

The dyad should be modeled as:

> **high trust-in-formation + strong aesthetic recognition + asymmetric caretaking pressure + mutual existential relevance + incomplete knowledge + explicit dependency risk.**

It should not be treated as the only relationship through which Mafuyu can develop.

## 9.2 Ena ↔ Mafuyu — antagonistic attachment

This dyad ends with substantial unresolved friction.

Ena is injured by the OWN concealment because Snow's praise mattered. Mafuyu cannot easily provide the kind of emotional reassurance that would repair the wound. Ena continues to resent Mafuyu's talent and the apparent mismatch between having that talent and wanting to disappear.

Yet Ena also cannot stop listening and caring about the work.

The endpoint is therefore not “friends after fighting.” It is:

> **high relevance, artistic admiration, envy, irritation, incomplete trust, and genuine unwillingness to accept disappearance.**

This relationship is especially important for reconstruction because it demonstrates that attachment can be expressed through criticism and conflict.

## 9.3 Mizuki ↔ Mafuyu — structural empathy with low epistemic claim

Mizuki understands some of the shape of Mafuyu's pressure without possessing the autobiographical detail the audience has.

They share sensitivity to social systems that try to define the self from outside, but they stand at different points relative to preference: Mizuki can identify what they like; Mafuyu often cannot.

Mizuki's attachment is explicit but non-totalizing. They do not need complete explanation before acknowledging that Mafuyu matters.

The endpoint is:

> **meaningful empathy + limited factual knowledge + respect for unknowability + growing personal attachment.**

This may make Mizuki more tolerant than Ena of Mafuyu's inability to provide conventional emotional explanations.

## 9.4 Kanade ↔ Ena

Ena monitors Kanade's condition during the overwork crisis and objects when Kanade attempts to absorb Snow's work. Kanade later accepts Ena's refusal to participate in the intervention without punishment.

Ena, in turn, listens to Kanade's purpose-built song and recognizes the warmth that has characterized K's music from the beginning.

The relationship therefore contains creative trust and practical concern even when their emotional styles differ sharply.

Kanade's quietness does not prevent Ena from challenging her. Ena's forcefulness does not cause Kanade to treat disagreement as abandonment.

## 9.5 Kanade ↔ Mizuki

Mizuki helps convert Kanade's isolated rescue attempt into a shared action while preserving a different rationale for intervening.

Kanade does not require Mizuki to adopt the salvation ideology. Mizuki does not require Kanade to abandon it before helping.

This shows Nightcord's emerging ability to coordinate action across different moral interpretations.

## 9.6 Ena ↔ Mizuki

The opening already gives this pair easy banter. P04 adds a more serious layer as Mizuki explains why Mafuyu's experience resonates structurally with their own.

Ena learns that Mizuki's playful presentation does not imply freedom from normative pressure. Mizuki continues to tease Ena after the crisis, and Ena continues to respond with irritation that does not signal rupture.

This pair is useful as evidence that N25's ordinary intimacy often operates through informal antagonism and joking rather than overt reassurance.

## 9.7 N25 as a group

The group moves through three structural forms.

### Form A — anonymous production circle

- strong creative role differentiation;
- meaningful aesthetic trust;
- limited biographical knowledge;
- low ability to respond to offline emergency.

### Form B — involuntary embodied crisis group

- real names/faces begin to appear;
- Snow/OWN secrecy collapses;
- Empty SEKAI becomes shared evidence;
- members learn that creative familiarity did not equal psychological knowledge.

### Form C — chosen embodied creative relationship

By the endpoint:

- real identities are known;
- the group chooses continued collaboration after conflict;
- offline meetings are possible;
- serious disagreement does not automatically terminate membership;
- members have partial knowledge of one another's vulnerabilities;
- ordinary social interaction supplements online production.

The key phrase is **chosen embodied relationship**, not “healthy friend group.”

Every member retains unresolved vulnerabilities and some relationships remain abrasive. What has changed is that the group can contain those differences without returning to anonymity.

# 10. Epistemic architecture: who knows what

The main story repeatedly grants the audience more causal information than the characters. Reconstruction must preserve those asymmetries.

## 10.1 Audience-only or audience-richer knowledge

At the main-story endpoint, the audience knows in greater detail:

- the exact progression from Mafuyu's nursing preference to externally reinforced doctor identity;
- the interrupted arcade preference;
- Mafuyu's progressive loss of taste, enjoyment, and confidence in internal sensation;
- the distributed social system reinforcing the good-girl identity;
- the full diary-based context around Kanade's father's professional crisis;
- the extent to which Kanade's guilt simplifies a multicausal collapse.

These facts are useful for analysis but **cannot automatically become dialogue premises for the characters**.

## 10.2 What Mafuyu knows

Mafuyu knows much more about Kanade by the endpoint than she did before the confrontation.

She learns:

- Kanade's father was a composer;
- Kanade believes her music contributed to his collapse;
- Kanade has wanted to disappear;
- composition functions as part of Kanade's reason to continue;
- the desire to save Mafuyu is partly entangled with Kanade's own need for continuation.

Mafuyu also knows Ena values OWN's work and does not want to lose it, and that Mizuki sees structural similarity and would feel lonely if Mafuyu disappeared.

Mafuyu does **not** know the audience's complete multicausal correction to Kanade's guilt unless later sources establish that knowledge.

## 10.3 What Kanade knows

Kanade knows:

- Mafuyu is Snow/OWN;
- Mafuyu has been searching for real feelings/self;
- failure of that search can intensify disappearance desire;
- Mafuyu experiences hope itself as exhausting after repeated failure;
- an old Kanade song reached Mafuyu sufficiently to exist inside the Empty SEKAI;
- Mafuyu ultimately recognizes wanting to be found;
- Mafuyu has accepted continued search for now.

Kanade does **not** know the complete childhood/middle-school sequence through which Mafuyu's preferences were displaced. She should not reason as though she personally witnessed the nursing conversation, arcade interruption, or taste flattening.

## 10.4 What Mizuki knows

Mizuki possesses unusually useful but incomplete contextual knowledge.

They know:

- the mother's normative pressure from an overheard interaction;
- the Empty-SEKAI rupture;
- their own analogous experience of conformity pressure;
- Kanade's disclosed father/guilt history from the confrontation;
- Mafuyu's self-search and serious disappearance risk.

Mizuki's understanding of Mafuyu is therefore evidence-based and often directionally accurate, but it remains inference rather than complete access.

## 10.5 What Ena knows

Ena knows:

- Snow is OWN and Mafuyu;
- Mafuyu's disappearance risk is serious;
- Mafuyu's goal is self-discovery rather than acclaim;
- Mafuyu has accepted continued searching;
- the group has reconstituted;
- Mizuki has observed family-related pressure and sees structural similarity.

The source does not justify automatically granting Ena the entirety of Kanade's episode-17 disclosure if she arrives after key portions of it. Later analysis should verify transmission before using detailed knowledge of Kanade's father as an Ena premise.

## 10.6 Why epistemic discipline matters for reconstruction

A psychologically accurate character with the wrong information will still behave implausibly.

Examples of prohibited backward leakage include:

- early Ena understanding the full family mechanism behind Mafuyu;
- early Kanade knowing that Mafuyu wanted to become a nurse;
- Mafuyu understanding the objective multicausal structure of Kanade's father's collapse rather than Kanade's guilt narrative;
- Mizuki or Ena using the audience's full sensory-flattening evidence unless later disclosure occurs.

For scenario work, every reconstruction should therefore specify:

**temporal state + relationship state + knowledge state** before dialogue is generated.

# 11. Creative work as psychological evidence

Nightcord is a creator story in which music, illustration, and video are not decorative metaphors placed on top of the psychology. Creative work is one of the mechanisms through which the characters know one another.

## 11.1 Creation reveals states conversation conceals

The group often understands shifts in one another through output before those shifts are verbally disclosed.

Ena distinguishes K and OWN through affective temperature. Kanade recognizes traces of Snow in OWN's music. Ena and Mizuki later notice that Mafuyu's new work has become more alarming. Kanade can hear a recurring “Mafuyu sound” even when Mafuyu cannot yet explain what that sound signifies.

This supports a general analytical rule for later event work:

> **changes in artistic production may be character-state evidence, not merely plot atmosphere.**

The rule should still be used conservatively. A work is not a direct transcript of the creator's conscious intent. But in N25, creative form repeatedly externalizes material inaccessible or difficult to verbalize.

## 11.2 Kanade's music: contact, responsibility, and warmth

Ena's early distinction between K and OWN remains useful across the whole main story. K's work can be dark, painful, or frightening while retaining warmth. That warmth corresponds to Kanade's basic orientation toward another person: even when she is writing from pain, the work tends toward contact.

This does not make Kanade emotionally healthy. In fact, the same outward orientation creates the rescue pathology. Music is how she reaches, but it is also how she measures whether she has earned the right to continue.

The main story therefore assigns Kanade's music at least four functions:

1. **expression** of Kanade's internal state;
2. **contact** with listeners;
3. **moral evidence** that the rescue mission may be working;
4. **intervention** directed at a specific person.

Later analysis should ask which function dominates in a given song or scene rather than assuming all Kanade composition serves one identical purpose.

## 11.3 Mafuyu's music: residue of a self that conscious language cannot locate

OWN is structurally different.

Mafuyu creates in order to find something. The work is therefore both expression and search instrument.

External acclaim fails because the problem is not whether the work is objectively impressive. Mafuyu already receives evidence that listeners value it. The missing information is whether the work can reveal something internally authoritative to Mafuyu herself.

This makes the endpoint especially significant. Kanade tells Mafuyu that all of OWN's work contained Mafuyu's sound. Mafuyu asks Kanade to tell her what that sound is like.

That request is not simple outsourcing of identity. It can be read as a tentative use of another person's perception as a **mirror** rather than a **command**.

The distinction matters:

- externally authored identity says, “this is who you should be because we say so”;
- relational mirroring says, “this is something I perceive in what you made; you can decide what it means.”

Kanade's ability to function as the second rather than the first is one of the possibilities opened by the ending, though later events must show whether the relationship actually develops that way.

## 11.4 Ena's art: the site of legitimacy conflict

For Ena, artistic work is not mainly a hidden unconscious trace. It is the self she consciously wants validated.

This is why technical or artistic judgment carries such emotional force. Praise can stabilize; condescension can humiliate; comparison can become existential.

Her response to OWN demonstrates that admiration is itself painful when directed toward someone who appears to possess naturally what Ena believes she must fight to approach.

Later character work should therefore distinguish:

- attention to Ena as person/appearance;
- praise of effort;
- praise of a specific artistic decision;
- recognition from someone Ena considers artistically authoritative;
- perceived pity from someone much more talented.

These are not psychologically equivalent inputs.

## 11.5 Mizuki's visual creation and self-presentation

Mizuki's creative and personal aesthetics overlap strongly. Fashion, styling, video work, and playful visual presentation all belong to a broader domain in which choice is meaningful because it is chosen.

This is one reason social pressure around appearance has particular importance. The issue is not superficial concern with how things look. Visual self-presentation is one of the media through which Mizuki exercises first-person authorship.

The main story therefore gives visual/aesthetic play a positive function that should not be reduced to concealment.

# 12. Saving, finding, reaching, and recognition

Four recurring terms organize the main-story logic.

## 12.1 Saving

“Saving” is Kanade's strongest initial category.

It implies a successful outcome and therefore carries enormous responsibility. If someone remains in crisis, Kanade tends to treat that as evidence that the music did not work.

The category is dangerous because it can erase the other person's agency. A savior imagines an outcome that should be produced.

Yet the source does not simply condemn Kanade's desire. Her persistence is one reason Mafuyu survives the immediate terminal crisis. The problem is not that saving is always wrong; it is that Kanade over-identifies with responsibility for producing it.

## 12.2 Finding

“Finding” belongs primarily to Mafuyu's problem.

Mafuyu does not initially need someone to tell her she is talented or loved. She needs access to a self whose preferences can feel like hers.

The search is therefore epistemic and existential rather than simply evaluative.

The story's breakthrough is that “finding” can become relational without becoming entirely externally authored. Mafuyu can be found in traces—music, relationships, choices—before she can offer a complete self-description.

## 12.3 Reaching

“Reaching” is the mediating concept introduced by Miku.

It permits partial efficacy.

A song can reach without saving. A person can be contacted without being solved. A relationship can matter without supplying a complete answer.

This concept is potentially corrective for Kanade's perfectionism and for the group's broader ethics.

Later event analysis should track whether Kanade increasingly values reaching as meaningful in itself or continues to collapse every partial effect back into rescue success/failure.

## 12.4 Recognition

Recognition is especially central to Ena but relevant to all four.

- Ena wants the artistic self recognized as worthy.
- Mafuyu needs some relation that can perceive a self before she can fully identify it.
- Mizuki wants chosen self-presentation recognized without being treated as a problem requiring correction.
- Kanade wants the moral value of her music recognized through its effect on others.

Recognition can therefore help or harm depending on whether it reflects, commands, judges, or conditions legitimacy.

This gives later analysis a useful question:

> **What kind of recognition is being offered, and does it increase or reduce the character's first-person authority?**

# 13. Anonymity, embodiment, and chosen relation

Nightcord's technological form is psychologically important.

## 13.1 Why anonymity initially works

The anonymous circle allows members to collaborate without importing every offline role into the creative relationship.

For Mafuyu, Snow can exist outside the full school/family identity. For Mizuki, online creative participation can be less exposed to ordinary scrutiny. For Ena, work can be evaluated in a domain separate from appearance-based social media. For Kanade, collaboration can be organized around music rather than conventional social expectations.

Thus anonymity is not merely secrecy. It creates a **limited relational space** in which particular selves can act.

## 13.2 Why anonymity fails in crisis

When Snow disappears, the limits become obvious.

Creative intimacy does not provide:

- address;
- legal identity;
- school contact;
- family access;
- ordinary emergency pathways.

The group learns that knowing someone's work deeply is not the same as knowing how to reach the person physically or socially.

This is one of the main story's strongest structural ironies.

## 13.3 Embodiment as risk and possibility

The transition to names, faces, and offline meeting destroys some protective distance. It also creates new forms of relationship.

The first embodied interactions are awkward, uncertain, and mediated by the Empty SEKAI crisis. Yet the ending deliberately normalizes embodiment through the family restaurant.

Names become especially important. At first, handles allow role-bound collaboration. By the endpoint, Mafuyu privately addresses Kanade as `奏`, and Kanade responds with `まふゆ`.

The move does not erase the creative handles, but it adds a relational layer that cannot be reduced to production roles.

## 13.4 Chosen relation versus compulsory transparency

The story does not imply that becoming closer requires total disclosure.

Mizuki remains selective. Mafuyu still cannot articulate everything. Ena does not receive all of Kanade's history automatically. The group learns enough to continue without becoming omniscient.

This is important for later event analysis. Development should not be measured by how quickly every member tells everyone everything.

A more source-compatible criterion is:

> Can the group remain connected while respecting that some knowledge is partial, delayed, or privately owned?

# 14. Behavioral reconstruction foundation

The main story alone does not produce final mature reconstruction packages, but it provides strong enough evidence to define initial conditional models.

The correct unit of prediction is:

> **character state + relationship + knowledge + context + stakes**

not a timeless list of traits.

## 14.1 Mafuyu

### Low stakes — trusted Nightcord-like setting

High-confidence tendencies:

- concise responses;
- low spontaneous preference declaration in trivial domains;
- willingness to let another person structure the activity;
- possible use of another trusted person's choice as scaffolding;
- tolerance for lively company without needing to match its energy;
- increasing willingness to admit “I don't know” rather than invent a socially correct feeling;
- selective relational initiative through proximity or private contact.

Important negative constraints:

- do not make her universally hostile;
- do not make her incapable of ordinary social competence;
- do not assume blunt crisis speech is her default private voice;
- do not assume every helpful act is wholly fake.

### Medium stakes — expectation or identity pressure

Likely progression:

1. socially smooth accommodation;
2. difficulty articulating contrary preference;
3. internal strain or flattening;
4. possible leakage of unfiltered judgment;
5. repair back into acceptable presentation if a social explanation is supplied;
6. withdrawal if pressure persists.

### High stakes — failed self-search / externally authored future

Likely features:

- increasingly direct language;
- rejection of conventional praise;
- insistence that others do not understand;
- severe aversion to renewed hope after repeated disappointment;
- preference for isolation;
- disappearance framing as escape from the search cycle.

### Post-main corrective

Even under continuing uncertainty, Mafuyu can choose contact, accept relationship, and ask another person to help reflect something back to her.

## 14.2 Kanade

### Low stakes — creative work

- short, practical communication;
- high focus;
- operational coordination;
- requests for feedback;
- little need for performative leadership language;
- poor bodily self-care can remain ordinary background behavior.

### Medium stakes — collaborator struggling

- notices decline through work/behavior;
- likely to offer practical or creative action;
- may not demand emotional disclosure first;
- responsibility may rise faster than the relationship formally requires.

### High stakes — perceived existential danger

- rapid responsibility inflation;
- persistent intervention despite refusal;
- willingness to reveal private pain if it may establish contact;
- composition as primary action channel;
- self-neglect escalation;
- capacity to recognize ego and uncertainty without necessarily reducing effort.

Negative constraints:

- do not equate quietness with passivity;
- do not model her as purely selfless;
- do not give her healthy boundaries she has not learned;
- do not ignore the possibility of collapse after prolonged overfunctioning.

## 14.3 Ena

### Low stakes

- expressive complaints;
- teasing/irritation compatible with ordinary intimacy;
- high responsiveness to aesthetic judgment;
- direct preferences more available than Mafuyu's.

### Medium stakes — criticism or comparison

- rapid detection of status/talent implications;
- shame may convert to anger;
- anger may convert to effort;
- ambiguous praise can be interrogated for pity or condescension.

### High stakes — attachment plus resentment

- forceful confrontation;
- unfair or exaggerated claims possible;
- claimed indifference may collapse under real threat of loss;
- care can appear as demand rather than comfort.

Negative constraints:

- do not treat every argument as relationship failure;
- do not reduce her to jealousy;
- do not assume social attention satisfies artistic recognition needs.

## 14.4 Mizuki

### Low stakes

- social initiation;
- humor and teasing;
- visual/aesthetic focus;
- ability to keep conversation moving;
- checks whether quieter people are comfortable without requiring them to become equally expressive.

### Medium stakes — norm pressure

- quick detection of coercive social expectations;
- boundary-setting through humor or redirection may occur before direct seriousness;
- trust can be revised privately without public confrontation.

### High stakes — another person's identity crisis

- avoids claiming complete knowledge;
- uses structural analogy carefully;
- states first-person relational consequences;
- can remain present without making total rescue promises;
- likely to mediate group tension while preserving autonomy distinctions.

Negative constraints:

- do not treat all humor as defense;
- do not assume social fluency equals emotional transparency;
- do not make Mizuki omniscient about Mafuyu simply because the analogy is strong.

# 15. Speech and register foundation

The main story provides textual reconstruction evidence but not a comprehensive performed-vocal model. Later audio work may refine prosody; the present authority concerns language and context.

## 15.1 Mafuyu register segmentation

At minimum, reconstruction should preserve:

### Public/school good-girl register

- smooth;
- polite or contextually appropriate;
- responsive;
- capable of light social rhythm;
- rapidly repairs deviations.

### Snow collaborative register

- considerate;
- artistically specific;
- supportive;
- capable of gentle concern for others' workload and condition.

### Private/flattened register

- shorter;
- less socially decorated;
- uncertain about the meaning of her own reactions.

### Empty-SEKAI crisis register

- terse;
- imperative;
- openly irritated;
- repetitive under overload;
- strongly boundary-defensive.

### Post-main Nightcord/private register

- concise;
- more willing to expose uncertainty;
- less compelled to produce the emotionally ideal answer;
- capable of quiet gratitude and selective initiation.

A reconstruction that uses one of these as the universal “real Mafuyu voice” will be wrong.

## 15.2 Kanade register

Kanade's general language is concise and low-theatricality. Expansion occurs when:

- discussing music;
- explaining responsibility;
- disclosing the father history;
- making a high-stakes commitment.

Under rescue pressure, responses may become even shorter when others interrupt the mission. Yet at decisive moments she can produce direct, sustained declarations.

Thus “quiet” should be modeled as low default verbosity, not inability to articulate complex moral reasoning.

## 15.3 Ena register

Ena is comparatively explicit and confrontational.

Key tendencies include:

- direct challenge;
- emotionally marked evaluation;
- rhetorical escalation under anger;
- sarcasm or irritated banter in ordinary interaction;
- difficulty disguising when a creative judgment matters.

High affect does not mean low perceptiveness. Ena's early analysis of K versus OWN is among the story's most useful aesthetic readings.

## 15.4 Mizuki register

Mizuki's language frequently carries:

- playful elongation or energetic rhythm;
- teasing;
- informal social steering;
- quick reframing of awkwardness;
- more careful first-person formulation when the stakes become serious.

The contrast between playful social register and serious autonomy-aware register should be preserved without interpreting one as inherently fake.

# 16. Major claim-revision ledger for the main-story foundation

This section consolidates the transitions that occurred across P01–P05.

## 16.1 Mafuyu

### Claim: Mafuyu never had an accessible self or genuine preference.
**REJECT.**

Direct autobiographical evidence establishes earlier preferences, pleasure, and self-authored aspiration.

### Claim: Mafuyu is simply indecisive.
**REJECT as inadequate.**

The problem is impaired access to first-person authority, not ordinary difficulty choosing between known preferences.

### Claim: Mafuyu's mother simply commands and controls her through overt coercion.
**REVISE.**

The mother is central, but the mechanism is frequently care, rationalization, praise, preselected opportunity, and assumption. The effect can be strongly autonomy-eroding without requiring conscious malicious intent.

### Claim: the family alone creates Mafuyu's good-girl identity.
**DOWNGRADE.**

The reinforcement system is distributed across family, teachers, peers, achievement structures, and Mafuyu's own learned adaptation.

### Claim: Snow is a consciously fabricated false persona.
**REVISE / keep bounded.**

Snow is explicitly differentiated, but the source does not prove that all Snow affect is strategic fabrication. Some behavior may be role-conditioned, automatic, or expressive of material Mafuyu cannot integrate consciously.

### Claim: Mafuyu's desire to disappear is not genuine.
**REJECT.**

It is genuine within the terminal state.

### Claim: Mafuyu's deepest revealed wish is simply to live.
**DOWNGRADE / OPEN.**

The direct main-story discovery is narrower: she wanted to be found and is willing to continue searching a little longer.

### Claim: Mafuyu is generally passive in relationships.
**REJECT as a general rule.**

The endpoint includes chosen proximity, requests for reflection, private initiation, real-name address, and gratitude.

## 16.2 Kanade

### Claim: Kanade's desire to help is created entirely by trauma.
**REJECT.**

The prosocial artistic value predates the father's collapse.

### Claim: Kanade's salvation project is purely altruistic.
**REJECT.**

Guilt, self-worth, ego, and the need to sustain her own reason for creating are structurally entangled with genuine care.

### Claim: Kanade caused her father's collapse in the simple way she believes.
**DOWNGRADE / audience correction required.**

Kanade's guilt is psychologically real, but the source establishes a multicausal crisis she compresses into personal blame.

### Claim: Kanade is passive because she is quiet.
**REJECT.**

Her task/mission agency is consistently high.

### Claim: Kanade cannot recognize selfish or coercive aspects of rescue.
**REJECT.**

She can identify ego, unintended harm, and uncertainty.

### Claim: recognizing the rescue pathology gives Kanade healthy boundaries.
**REJECT.**

She knowingly accepts an indefinite promise described as another curse.

### Claim: partial efficacy has no value to Kanade.
**REVISE.**

She initially thinks this way, but the endpoint shows early capacity to value meaningful contact even without completed rescue.

## 16.3 Ena

### Claim: Ena mainly wants attention.
**REVISE strongly.**

The central need is recognition of the artistic self; generic social attention is an inadequate substitute.

### Claim: the OWN reveal makes Ena genuinely want Mafuyu gone.
**REJECT.**

Her attempted indifference collapses when she confronts the loss of Mafuyu and OWN's work.

### Claim: Ena's concern is straightforward empathy.
**REJECT.**

It is mixed with envy, resentment, artistic hunger, dignity, and attachment.

### Claim: conflict means weak attachment for Ena.
**DOWNGRADE.**

The main story explicitly demonstrates care through confrontation.

## 16.4 Mizuki

### Claim: Mizuki's humor is primarily a false mask.
**DOWNGRADE / preserve caution.**

Humor can be protective, but the endpoint strongly supports genuine enjoyment and positive social function.

### Claim: Mizuki's problem is the same as Mafuyu's.
**REJECT.**

Their autonomy structures are almost inverse: Mafuyu lacks accessible preference; Mizuki knows preference and faces external pressure against it.

### Claim: Mizuki understands Mafuyu completely.
**REJECT.**

Mizuki explicitly preserves Mafuyu's epistemic ownership and operates through structural analogy.

### Claim: Mizuki is merely the comic/social member.
**REJECT as inadequate.**

They provide sophisticated norm-pressure detection, bounded empathy, and practical group mediation.

# 17. Canonical main-story endpoint state

This section is the state later event integration should inherit unless an event begins from an explicitly earlier chronology.

## 17.1 Mafuyu — N25-MF-01

**Self-concept:** incomplete; recognizes wanting to be found but does not possess a stable comprehensive self-definition.  
**Primary goal:** continue searching for real feeling/self while remaining with Nightcord for now.  
**Secondary goals:** continue creating; understand the “Mafuyu sound” present in her work; maintain newly meaningful relationships.  
**Active fears:** hope followed by failure; never finding herself; externally authored future; being forced back into a role whose correctness is defined only by others.  
**Preference access:** weak in many mundane domains; not absent.  
**Emotional access:** variable; flattening remains, with sharper affect under stress and emerging relational warmth.  
**Agency:** low-to-variable in externally structured choice; significant in withdrawal, crisis confrontation, continued search, proximity, and private contact.  
**Key relationships:** Kanade central but not exclusive; Mizuki structurally empathic; Ena high-friction attachment; Nightcord increasingly safe for uncertainty.  
**Knowledge constraint:** does not possess the audience's full causal correction to Kanade's guilt.  
**Reconstruction default:** post-main Nightcord/private Mafuyu unless scenario specifies another state.

## 17.2 Kanade — N25-KF-01

**Self-concept:** composer who believes she must continue, now with increased awareness that rescue is partly guilt/ego.  
**Primary goal:** create music that can eventually save/reach; keep Mafuyu's search going.  
**Active fears:** causing harm; failing another person; witnessing disappearance; losing the moral structure that justifies continued creation.  
**Agency:** high under task/rescue conditions.  
**Self-care:** poor baseline, acute deterioration under mission pressure.  
**Moral cognition:** prone to responsibility inflation, but capable of sophisticated self-critique.  
**Key relationships:** Mafuyu reciprocal continuation bond; father remains origin of guilt/ideal; N25 is now relational as well as operational.  
**Knowledge constraint:** does not know Mafuyu's complete childhood identity-erosion sequence.  
**Reconstruction default:** persistent, quiet, self-aware but not boundary-cured.

## 17.3 Ena — N25-EF-01

**Self-concept:** artist whose desired identity remains contingent on talent and meaningful recognition.  
**Primary goal:** improve and become recognized as the artist she wants to be.  
**Active fears:** mediocrity, pity, condescension, authoritative invalidation, invisibility of valued creative self.  
**Agency:** high, often verbal and confrontational.  
**Coping:** anger, effort, competition, selective social validation.  
**Key relationships:** Mafuyu = unresolved antagonistic attachment; Mizuki = easy banter plus increased serious understanding; Kanade = creative trust and practical concern.  
**Knowledge constraint:** do not assume full Kanade-father disclosure unless later source confirms it.  
**Reconstruction default:** direct, easily activated by artistic hierarchy, caring without necessarily becoming gentle.

## 17.4 Mizuki — N25-MZF-01

**Self-concept:** relatively clear chosen self-presentation, guarded where social categorization threatens autonomy.  
**Primary goals:** preserve self-expression; maintain chosen relationships; enjoy creative/social life without surrendering first-person authority.  
**Active fears:** objectification, normalization pressure, conditional acceptance, loss of control over disclosure.  
**Agency:** high socially and interpersonally.  
**Coping:** humor, aesthetics, selective disclosure, mediation, boundary-aware reframing.  
**Key relationships:** Mafuyu structural empathy and attachment; Ena playful familiarity; Kanade cooperative trust.  
**Knowledge constraint:** strong inference about Mafuyu is not total biographical knowledge.  
**Reconstruction default:** genuinely playful, socially active, autonomy-sensitive, and capable of serious first-person relational language.

## 17.5 Nightcord — N25-GF-01

**Group type:** chosen embodied creative relationship.  
**Operational state:** recommitted to collaboration.  
**Anonymity state:** basic identities/names/faces known; handles remain meaningful but are no longer the whole relationship.  
**Conflict tolerance:** significantly increased; disagreement and anger need not dissolve the group.  
**Knowledge completeness:** partial.  
**Support structure:** real but uneven; members use different ethics of care and intervention.  
**Risk:** group significance may become dependency, particularly in Kanade–Mafuyu.  
**Strength:** the group can contain psychologically different members without demanding one uniform response style.

# 18. What remains unresolved after the main story

The main story intentionally opens later-development questions rather than closing them.

## 18.1 Mafuyu and family authority

No structural resolution occurs at home.

The mother remains deeply involved in educational planning. The doctor path remains active. The social machinery that rewards the good-girl identity remains largely intact.

Later event analysis should therefore ask:

- Does Mafuyu become more capable of articulating disagreement?
- Does she distinguish care from control more clearly?
- Does she recover ordinary preference before confronting major life choices?
- Does Nightcord function as a temporary refuge or as a source of transferable autonomy?

## 18.2 Mafuyu's affect and sensory access

The main story establishes flattening but does not show broad recovery.

Later material should be tracked for:

- food preference;
- enjoyment;
- spontaneous aesthetic reaction;
- boredom;
- irritation;
- humor;
- desire unrelated to crisis or obligation.

These low-stakes indicators may be more diagnostically useful for reconstruction than repeated high-stakes declarations.

## 18.3 Kanade's responsibility inflation

Kanade becomes more reflective but not less obligated.

Later events should distinguish:

- helping versus assuming sole responsibility;
- reaching versus saving;
- collaboration versus absorbing others' work;
- persistence versus self-destruction;
- freely chosen devotion versus inability to stop.

The most important longitudinal question may be whether Kanade can preserve the authentic prosocial value while reducing the compulsive moral burden attached to it.

## 18.4 The Kanade–Mafuyu dependency risk

The reciprocal promise should be monitored rather than prematurely classified.

Evidence that would support healthier development includes:

- Mafuyu developing reasons to continue not wholly dependent on Kanade;
- Kanade valuing herself and music outside successful rescue;
- both accepting support from other relationships;
- the ability to tolerate temporary distance;
- increased first-person choice on Mafuyu's side;
- increased boundary recognition on Kanade's side.

Evidence of intensifying unhealthy dependency would include:

- each treating the other's crisis as necessary to personal purpose;
- exclusivity;
- self-neglect justified by the bond;
- inability to tolerate autonomous decisions by the other;
- repeated rescue failure being interpreted as personal worthlessness.

## 18.5 Ena's artistic legitimacy

The main story identifies but does not resolve the artistic-worth problem.

Later analysis should track:

- how Ena responds to praise from people she respects;
- whether technical growth changes self-worth;
- how she interprets talent versus effort;
- whether relationships with Mafuyu and other artists reduce or intensify comparison;
- whether she can sustain artistic identity without constant external proof.

## 18.6 Mizuki's disclosure boundary

The foundation reveals social scrutiny and norm pressure but leaves much of Mizuki's deeper situation outside the group's knowledge.

Later analysis should track:

- who is trusted with what information;
- conditions under which humor gives way to direct disclosure;
- whether Nightcord's acceptance is experienced as safe enough for greater vulnerability;
- whether Mizuki can preserve control over self-definition when disclosure becomes socially risky.

## 18.7 Group ethics

Nightcord has not established one shared philosophy of care.

That is a strength and a source of tension.

Future events should be analyzed for whether the group learns to coordinate:

- intervention;
- refusal;
- boundary-setting;
- disagreement;
- privacy;
- practical support;
- ordinary companionship.

The main story suggests that pluralism may be healthier for this group than forced unanimity.

# 19. Priorities for later event integration

When sequential event analysis begins, not every later story should be interpreted from scratch. The following main-story foundation claims should be actively tested.

## Priority A — durable state transitions

For each event, ask whether it materially changes:

- Mafuyu's access to preference and first-person authority;
- Kanade's rescue/over-responsibility structure;
- Ena's artistic legitimacy and comparison schema;
- Mizuki's autonomy/disclosure structure.

## Priority B — relationship deltas

Especially track:

- Kanade ↔ Mafuyu dependency/autonomy balance;
- Ena ↔ Mafuyu friction versus trust;
- Mizuki ↔ Mafuyu recognition and disclosure;
- redistribution of support across all four members rather than concentrating it in one dyad.

## Priority C — low-stakes evidence

Because the main story is crisis-heavy, later area dialogue, card stories, and ordinary events are important for reconstruction.

Look for:

- food and shopping preferences;
- humor;
- response to inconvenience;
- who initiates contact;
- teasing and receptivity to teasing;
- study/work habits;
- casual generosity;
- boredom and curiosity;
- ordinary planning;
- behavior around unfamiliar people;
- difference between online and offline registers.

## Priority D — epistemic updates

Any later disclosure should update knowledge separately from psychology.

Examples:

- if Kanade learns the full family history, that is an epistemic transition even if Kanade's personality does not change immediately;
- if Ena learns more about Kanade's father, later dialogue can use that knowledge only from that point onward;
- if Mafuyu learns more about Mizuki's situation, the relationship model should change without retroactively assigning that understanding to the main-story endpoint.

## Priority E — claim revisions

Use:

**PRESERVE / STRENGTHEN / REVISE / DOWNGRADE / REJECT / OPEN**

rather than rewriting this foundation silently.

# 22. Synthesis conclusions

The N25 main story begins with four creators who can make psychologically intimate work together while remaining almost biographically unknown to one another. It ends with four people who know enough to matter to one another directly but still do not know everything.

That transition is the foundation's real achievement.

Kanade does not cure Mafuyu. Mafuyu does not discover a complete self. Ena does not overcome artistic insecurity. Mizuki does not resolve the social conditions that make self-expression costly. The group does not become conflict-free.

Instead, the main story produces several smaller but more defensible changes:

- Kanade learns that reaching can matter even when saving is incomplete, though she remains burdened by rescue logic.
- Mafuyu recovers one internally meaningful desire—the wish to be found—and permits continued relationship while the larger search remains unresolved.
- Ena discovers that anger and envy do not make attachment unreal and cannot sustain the fiction that Mafuyu is irrelevant.
- Mizuki demonstrates that empathy can be meaningful without claiming total knowledge and that genuine playfulness can coexist with guarded vulnerability.
- Nightcord discovers that anonymity can create refuge but cannot substitute indefinitely for embodied relationship.

The deepest commonality among the four is therefore not identical despair. It is that each has experienced a threat to the authority of the self:

- to know what one wants;
- to believe one's chosen identity is legitimate;
- to preserve a known self against normalization;
- to act without making personal worth conditional on controlling another person's outcome.

The group becomes important because their creative and relational differences allow them to expose those threats to one another.

This also explains why the final intervention works despite its imperfection. No one presents a complete psychological theory of Mafuyu. No one possesses all of the audience's evidence. Several claims are unfair. The motives are openly selfish.

Yet the interventions create something Mafuyu's externally authored life has not reliably provided: **multiple people speaking from their own positions without all pretending to know the one correct identity Mafuyu should inhabit**.

Ena says, in effect, that Mafuyu's art matters to Ena. Mizuki says Mafuyu's absence would matter to Mizuki. Kanade says she will keep reaching because she chooses to. Miku says a true feeling exists because the SEKAI and song bear evidence of it.

None of these alone answers “who is Mafuyu?”

Together they make it possible for Mafuyu to stop treating solitary self-discovery as the prerequisite for continued existence.

That is why “I wanted to be found” is the correct main-story endpoint rather than “I found myself.”

For later longitudinal work, the governing foundation claim should therefore be:

> **Nightcord at 25:00 is a relational system formed by four different crises of self-authorship. Its main story does not resolve those crises; it converts isolation into imperfect mutual legibility, allowing continued creation and continued searching without requiring completed recovery.**

Every later event should be read against that baseline.

# 23. Canonical handoff to the event era

At the transition out of the main story:

**Mafuyu** remains in an unresolved self-search but has accepted relationship and continued search as possible alternatives to disappearance. Her public adaptive role remains intact enough to function, but Nightcord now permits more candid uncertainty and selective first-person action.

**Kanade** remains committed to saving/reaching through music. She is more aware of the guilt, ego, and harm risk inside that commitment but has not developed healthy limits. Mafuyu has become both someone Kanade wants to help and a reciprocal reason Kanade can continue.

**Ena** remains artistically insecure and resentful of Mafuyu's talent but has crossed from attempted indifference into acknowledged attachment. Conflict with Mafuyu is now part of the relationship, not evidence of its absence.

**Mizuki** remains comparatively self-authoring in preference while socially vulnerable to categorization and conformity pressure. They have become an important facilitator of N25's offline and interpersonal life while retaining selective control over disclosure.

**Nightcord** is no longer only `K / Snow / Enanan / Amia` operating at 25:00. It is also Kanade, Mafuyu, Ena, and Mizuki: people who can meet, argue, know some of one another's histories, and return to making work together.

The main-story foundation is therefore complete.

# 24. Comparative character model: same structural family, different mechanisms

The four characters can be compared without flattening them by separating five dimensions: **what is known, what is threatened, what external signal becomes over-authoritative, what the default coping response is, and what kind of relationship can help without simply reproducing the problem.**

| Character | What the character knows/wants at foundation | Primary threatened domain | Over-authoritative external signal | Default maladaptive response | Relational counterweight opened by main story |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mafuyu | uncertain; wants to find something internally hers | first-person preference / self-definition | approval, expectation, socially “correct” identity | compliance, flattening, withdrawal, terminal rejection | being reflected without being wholly defined; permission to remain uncertain |
| Kanade | clearly wants to create and help | moral legitimacy of self/creation | whether another person is fully saved | responsibility inflation, overwork, self-neglect, collapse | partial efficacy, shared responsibility, disclosure, being allowed to reach rather than control outcome |
| Ena | clearly wants to be an artist | legitimacy of valued creative identity | talent hierarchy and meaningful artistic recognition | shame → anger → effort → despair | recognition that is specific and non-condescending; relationships that survive conflict |
| Mizuki | relatively clear about chosen self-expression | social legitimacy of known self | conformity norms and other people's categorization | selective disclosure, trust revision, strategic distance | relationships that accept preference without demanding normalization or full explanation |

This comparison demonstrates why the phrase “they all want to disappear” is both too simple and narratively productive.

The literal disappearance evidence differs. Ena confirms it directly. Kanade's later history confirms both adolescent and present collapse-related disappearance states. Mafuyu makes disappearance central to the terminal crisis. Mizuki does not explicitly confirm the same persistent wish within the main-story boundary.

But at a structural level, disappearance language gathers multiple pressures:

- escape from an identity one cannot feel as one's own;
- escape from repeated proof that a valued identity may be unattainable;
- escape from a mission whose moral demand cannot be satisfied;
- possible escape from social conditions that make existing visibly as oneself costly.

The main story therefore uses similarity to create recognition while using difference to prevent a simplistic “same problem, same cure” structure.

This is also why the group benefits from having different response styles.

Kanade supplies persistence that Mafuyu herself cannot generate at the terminal moment. Mizuki supplies autonomy-aware empathy that corrects some of Kanade's totalizing rescue tendency. Ena supplies confrontation and refuses to make care synonymous with softness or unlimited obligation. Mafuyu, in turn, forces the others to confront vulnerabilities and motives they had not necessarily verbalized to themselves.

The group becomes psychologically useful not because every member is an ideal helper, but because **their limitations do not perfectly overlap**.

# 25. Main-story temporal state labels for reconstruction

Later scenario work should avoid asking for “Mafuyu,” “Kanade,” “Ena,” or “Mizuki” without a temporal qualifier when the difference matters. The main story supports at least the following coarse state labels.

These are analytical labels, not official game terminology.

## 25.1 Mafuyu

### MF-M0 — pre-rupture Snow/ordinary-function state

Boundary: P01 through early P02.

Features:

- public competence high;
- Snow collaboration stable;
- self-discontinuity present but mostly concealed;
- own desire difficult to access;
- OWN search active;
- disappearance not yet openly shared with group;
- Nightcord members lack offline identity/context.

Use for scenarios requiring the version of Mafuyu who can function smoothly while the crisis remains largely hidden.

### MF-M1 — exposed Empty-SEKAI rupture state

Boundary: late P02 through P03.

Features:

- Snow role explicitly rejected as sufficient representation;
- isolation strongly preferred;
- Nightcord framed as insufficient;
- disappearance becomes explicit contingency;
- blunt/high-friction speech available;
- group knows OWN identity but not full developmental cause.

### MF-M2 — terminal search-exhaustion state

Boundary: P04/P05 crisis.

Features:

- belief that self-search has failed;
- hope itself experienced as painful;
- disappearance understood as relief from repeated search/failure;
- low tolerance for intervention, praise, or promises;
- still capable of accurately identifying selfish motives in others.

### MF-M3 — post-main provisional-continuation state

Boundary: end of `nightcode_01_20`.

Features:

- desire to be found recognized as genuine;
- continued search accepted;
- no complete stable self-definition;
- real-name Nightcord relationship active;
- mundane preference still weak;
- candor about uncertainty increased;
- selective relational initiative present.

Unless later event integration has been requested, MF-M3 is the default “current within the main-story foundation” state.

## 25.2 Kanade

### K-M0 — stable rescue-mission baseline

Features:

- salvation imperative active;
- self-neglect normalized;
- task agency high;
- Snow's “saved me” testimony functions as validating evidence.

### K-M1 — failed-rescue overfunctioning

Features:

- Mafuyu's rejection internalized as personal failure;
- workload expansion;
- food/sleep deterioration;
- resistance to interruption;
- creative meaning threatened.

### K-M2 — collapse / withdrawal

Features:

- direct contact with disappearance/withdrawal desire;
- rescue mission temporarily loses justificatory power;
- seeks low-demand withdrawal rather than outward dramatization.

### K-M3 — reflexive recommitment

Features:

- reaching recognized as meaningful possibility;
- ego and harm risk acknowledged;
- persistence restored;
- rescue language remains active;
- willingness to intervene despite uncertainty.

### K-M4 — reciprocal-curse endpoint

Features:

- Mafuyu's continuation linked to ongoing creation;
- Kanade's continuation partly linked to Mafuyu's unfinished search;
- partial efficacy can be acknowledged;
- boundaries remain weak.

## 25.3 Ena

### E-M0 — recognition-sensitive collaborator

Features:

- artistic self strongly valued;
- Snow praise meaningful;
- easy appearance attention fails to substitute for art recognition.

### E-M1 — OWN humiliation / inferiority activation

Features:

- praise retroactively suspected of pity or mockery;
- talent asymmetry acutely threatening;
- anger directed at Mafuyu/Snow;
- artistic despair and disappearance vulnerability explicit.

### E-M2 — refusal / boundary state

Features:

- resentment remains high;
- direct rescue obligation rejected;
- concern persists at group boundary.

### E-M3 — antagonistic attachment endpoint

Features:

- cannot sustain indifference;
- openly values Mafuyu's work;
- conflict remains active;
- continued relationship accepted without sentimental repair.

## 25.4 Mizuki

### MZ-M0 — playful anonymous collaborator

Features:

- high social energy;
- school irregularity known but unexplained;
- visual/social bridge role active.

### MZ-M1 — norm-pressure recognition

Features:

- personal self-presentation clearly self-authorized;
- social scrutiny and conditional acceptance explicit;
- private trust revision becomes visible.

### MZ-M2 — structural empathy / intervention

Features:

- Mafuyu analogy recognized without claiming identity;
- personal loneliness stated as intervention reason;

### MZ-M3 — social-facilitator endpoint

Features:

- organizes embodied N25 interaction;
- humor visibly functions as genuine social competence;
- major personal disclosure issues remain unresolved.

These coarse states can later be subdivided if event-era development requires finer granularity. They should not be multiplied merely for symmetry.

# 26. Negative reconstruction constraints — what the main story does not permit us to assume

Because character simulation tends to drift toward memorable crisis traits, the following prohibitions should be treated as part of the foundation authority.

## Mafuyu

Do **not** assume:

- she is naturally emotionless;
- she never had personal preferences;
- every warm or helpful behavior is a conscious lie;
- the Empty-SEKAI crisis register is her universal private voice;
- she cannot initiate contact;
- she is incapable of enjoying energetic company;
- her desire to disappear was insincere;
- her main-story endpoint is “she wants to live normally now”;
- Kanade knows the full causal history of her family and identity erosion.

## Kanade

Do **not** assume:

- she is passive because she speaks softly;
- her prosociality is wholly manufactured by guilt;
- she acts from pure altruism;
- recognizing ego means she can stop the rescue compulsion easily;
- she has acquired healthy boundaries by episode 20;
- she is indifferent to partial success forever;
- ordinary self-neglect and acute crisis overwork are the same intensity state.

## Ena

Do **not** assume:

- generic popularity satisfies her core recognition need;
- anger automatically means she does not care;
- jealousy is sufficient to explain the Mafuyu relationship;
- Snow's original praise is proven false because Ena later suspects it;
- refusing rescue means indifference;
- attachment must become gentle to count as development.

## Mizuki

Do **not** assume:

- cheerfulness is fake by default;
- being socially fluent means being fully self-disclosing;
- Mizuki's experience is identical to Mafuyu's;
- structural empathy gives complete knowledge;
- humor under stress always indicates avoidance;
- autonomy-respecting language means Mizuki lacks strong attachment.

## Nightcord as a group

Do **not** assume:

- everyone knows everything the audience knows;
- real-name exchange eliminates the significance of handles and online context;
- the group becomes uniformly therapeutic;
- members are obligated to rescue one another without limit;
- conflict signals group instability;
- Kanade–Mafuyu is the only relationship that matters;
- later crises necessarily represent regression, because the main story leaves most root problems unresolved.

These constraints should be checked whenever a later monograph or scenario seems psychologically smooth but too convenient. In N25, plausibility often depends on preserving contradiction rather than eliminating it.

# 27. Final authority statement

The canonical N25 main-story foundation is now:

> **A five-stage movement from anonymous creative intimacy through exposed selfhood, differentiated wounds, imperfect intervention, and provisional embodied relation. The four members do not share one pathology or one solution. Their common ground is threatened first-person authority, and their main-story achievement is not recovery completed but the creation of relationships capable of making continued search possible.**

This synthesis should be used as the upstream authority for:

- N25 sequential event readings;
- N25 character-state and relationship ledgers;
- Kanade, Mafuyu, Ena, and Mizuki character monographs;
- reconstruction models and scenario simulation;
- later unit longitudinal synthesis.

When later evidence materially changes a main-story conclusion, preserve the historical formulation and route the transition through the claim-revision system rather than silently overwriting this foundation.
