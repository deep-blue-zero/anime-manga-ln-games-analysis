# Project SEKAI — Nightcord at 25:00 Main-Story Phase Map

## Purpose

This document maps the complete 21-episode Nightcord at 25:00 main story into analytically coherent blocks. It is a reading crosswalk, not an official arc list and not a replacement for the source pipeline's authoritative episode order.

The segmentation was fixed after reading all 21 canonical Japanese main-story records in sequence. Boundaries follow causal continuity, psychological state transitions, relationship changes, and changes in the story's governing question. Equal episode counts were not a goal.

The five phases are intended to support two downstream responsibilities at once:

1. literary and structural analysis of the N25 main story; and
2. reconstruction of Kanade, Mafuyu, Ena, and Mizuki as state-dependent characters rather than flattened composites.

## Phase map

### P01 — Anonymous collaboration, salvation premise, and concealed fracture

**Source episodes:** `nightcode_01_00`–`nightcode_01_03`  
**Master episodes:** 1–4  
**Story IDs:** `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_00:01` through `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_03:01`

**Primary responsibility:** establish the pre-crisis system.

This block introduces Kanade's guilt-derived imperative to create music capable of saving others, the Empty SEKAI and Miku's request that Kanade save an unidentified nearby person, the ordinary working structure of Nightcord, and the members' offline/online splits. The block closes when Kanade recognizes that the anonymous creator OWN resembles Snow's earlier music and the source confirms privately that Mafuyu is OWN and is using that music to search for something she cannot find.

The governing question changes at the end of P01. Before the close, the problem is a mystery embedded in a functioning anonymous creative circle. After it, the problem becomes Mafuyu's concealed psychological crisis.

### P02 — The “good girl” structure, disappearance, and exposure

**Source episodes:** `nightcode_01_04`–`nightcode_01_07`  
**Master episodes:** 5–8  
**Story IDs:** `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_04:01` through `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_07:01`

**Primary responsibility:** expose the contradiction between Snow/Mafuyu's socially legible identities and her private state.

Episode 04 shows the externally admired “good girl” system around Mafuyu and her growing inability to locate a desire that feels like her own. She withdraws into the Empty SEKAI. Episodes 05–06 turn the others' concern into a search and then an involuntary first offline meeting. Episode 07 resolves the mystery violently: Mafuyu admits she is OWN, rejects Nightcord, states that Kanade's music was insufficient, declares that she may disappear if she cannot find herself, and accuses Kanade, Ena, and Mizuki of wanting to disappear as well.

P02 ends with the group expelled from the SEKAI and with every member's prior model of Snow destabilized.

### P03 — Reflected wounds and Kanade's collapse

**Source episodes:** `nightcode_01_08`–`nightcode_01_10`  
**Master episodes:** 9–11  
**Story IDs:** `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_08:01` through `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_10:01`

**Primary responsibility:** test Mafuyu's accusation against the other three members.

The phase is organized as a sequence of refracted wounds rather than three unrelated character spotlights.

- Ena interprets Mafuyu's concealment through her own artistic inferiority, dependence on recognition, and resentment of talent.
- Mizuki's school experience reveals the pressure created by being treated as an object of scrutiny and by nominally supportive peers who still expect conformity. Mizuki recognizes that Mafuyu's apparent indifference to pressure may also have been false.
- Kanade converts Mafuyu's statement that her music was “not enough” into renewed self-punishment, attempts to absorb Snow's entire production role, neglects food and rest, pushes the others away, and reaches the point of questioning whether continuing to compose has meaning.

This phase ends at Kanade's creative and psychological nadir. The story is now ready to revisit the causal histories that produced both Kanade's salvation imperative and Mafuyu's loss of self.

### P04 — Origins, recognition, and the decision to intervene

**Source episodes:** `nightcode_01_11`–`nightcode_01_15`  
**Master episodes:** 12–16  
**Story IDs:** `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_11:01` through `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_15:01`

**Primary responsibility:** reconstruct causes, reinterpret the crisis, and assemble a response.

Episode 11 gives the full causal history of Kanade and her father: her father's professional insecurity, the role of Kanade's talent in intensifying it, his collapse, Kanade's discovery of his diary, and the guilt logic by which she turns “make music that makes people happy” into an obligation to save anyone through music. Episode 12 does not erase that compulsion; instead, Miku gives Kanade evidence that her music reached Mafuyu even if it did not solve her condition. Kanade chooses to act again.

Episode 13 reconstructs Mafuyu's gradual loss of self through rewarded compliance, redirected aspiration, social expectation, and loss of affective access to taste, pleasure, and preference. Episode 14 lets Mizuki and Ena interpret the crisis from their own positions and converts abstract concern into a desire to see Mafuyu again. Episode 15 brings the decision together: Kanade commits to making a song for Mafuyu, Mizuki chooses to accompany her, Ena initially refuses, and Kanade explicitly recognizes the ego involved in her desire to save someone while acting anyway.

P04 therefore ends not with resolution but with an intervention plan and a more self-aware version of Kanade's motive.

### P05 — Collapse, confrontation, negotiated continuation, and new equilibrium

**Source episodes:** `nightcode_01_16`–`nightcode_01_20`  
**Master episodes:** 17–21  
**Story IDs:** `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_16:01` through `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_20:01`

**Primary responsibility:** resolve the foundation crisis without pretending the characters are cured.

Mafuyu's public mask begins to fail and her mother unknowingly treats the synthesizer as disposable while redirecting her future again. Mafuyu concludes that “wanting to disappear” is her true feeling. Kanade and Mizuki enter the SEKAI; Ena follows after admitting that she cannot accept losing Mafuyu's music despite resenting her.

The confrontation is deliberately plural rather than a single therapeutic argument. Kanade offers recognition through shared despair and an unconditional commitment to keep making music; Ena offers anger, envy, and insistence that Mafuyu's creative existence matters to someone; Mizuki offers non-coercive recognition of Mafuyu's autonomy while admitting that her disappearance would be personally painful. Mafuyu finally reveals that the most intolerable part is not simply pain but repeated failed hope that she might still find herself.

Kanade's answer is a negotiated burden: she will continue making music until Mafuyu can find herself, and Mafuyu can become one of the reasons Kanade continues if Kanade herself approaches disappearance. Mafuyu agrees only to keep searching “a little longer.” The resulting song reveals a more precise desire: she wanted to be found.

The final episode establishes a new but fragile equilibrium. The members exchange real names and meet at a family restaurant. Mafuyu remains uncertain about ordinary preference and affect; Ena remains irritated; Mizuki mediates; Kanade and Mafuyu establish a more direct bond. The main story therefore ends with continued searching and relationship rather than cure.

## Why these boundaries are preferred

### 03 → 04

`OWN` ends by confirming to the audience that Mafuyu is OWN and that she is searching for something she cannot find. `私はいい子` begins a new causal inquiry into the social system producing that condition. This is a clean shift from concealed mystery to exposed psychological structure.

### 07 → 08

`消えたがってるくせに` is the rupture: Mafuyu rejects the group and accuses the other three. `凡人の絵` begins the aftermath by testing that accusation against Ena. The narrative question changes from “what is happening to Snow?” to “what does Snow's accusation reveal about everyone else?”

### 10 → 11

`足りない音` is the completion of the reflected-wounds movement and Kanade's nadir. `幸せになる音楽を` shifts from present collapse to historical reconstruction. It supplies the causal evidence needed to understand why Kanade responds to failure through compulsive rescue.

### 15 → 16

`消えてしまう前に` completes the intervention decision and the song. `崩壊` begins the terminal crisis: Mafuyu's social mask fails, her synthesizer is threatened, and she moves from “if I cannot find myself, I may disappear” to “wanting to disappear was my true feeling.” This is the clearest transition into final confrontation.

## Analytical rules for downstream readings

- Individual episodes remain evidence locators; the phase is the primary interpretive unit.
- A phase deep reading must not borrow later-state knowledge to simplify an earlier character state.
- Mafuyu's public, Snow, OWN, private, and later post-crisis presentations must not be flattened into a single timeless personality.
- Kanade's desire to save others must be tracked both as compassion and as a guilt-linked compulsion; later self-awareness revises but does not erase the earlier structure.
- Ena's anger and envy should not be treated as mere hostility; they are repeatedly tied to recognition, artistic legitimacy, and fear of being unnecessary.
- Mizuki's lightness and humor must be treated as real behavior without assuming they exhaust the underlying state.
- The ending is a new equilibrium, not resolution of the underlying psychological problems.
