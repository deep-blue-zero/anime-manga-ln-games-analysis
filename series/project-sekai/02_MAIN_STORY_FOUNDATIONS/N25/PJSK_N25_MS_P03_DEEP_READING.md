# Project SEKAI — Nightcord at 25:00 Main Story Phase 03 Deep Reading

## Scope and analytical responsibility

**Phase:** P03 — Reflected wounds and Kanade's collapse  
**Source:** `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_08:01` through `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_10:01`  
**Master episodes:** 9–11

P03 tests the accusation Mafuyu made at the end of P02: that Kanade, Ena, and Mizuki also want to disappear. The phase should not treat that accusation as a single proposition that is either uniformly true or uniformly false. Its responsibility is to determine what each character's private evidence actually supports and to distinguish literal disappearance wishes from broader forms of self-erasure, escape, or identity collapse.

The phase is deliberately structured as a sequence of three private refracted wounds:

1. Ena converts Mafuyu's rejection into a crisis of artistic inferiority and explicitly admits that she has wanted to disappear for a long time.
2. Mizuki is subjected to curiosity, gossip, and superficially supportive pressure to conform; rather than explicitly endorsing Mafuyu's accusation, Mizuki recognizes that Snow's earlier claim not to care about social pressure was probably false.
3. Kanade converts Mafuyu's statement that K's music was “not enough” into proof of personal failure, attempts to absorb Snow's entire production role, neglects food and rest, rejects help, and reaches the point of questioning whether continuing to compose has meaning.

P03 therefore asks a more precise question than “were they all secretly suicidal?”: **what does each member want to escape from, what part of the self becomes endangered under pressure, and what behavioral strategy do they use when that pressure becomes intolerable?**

The phase ends at Kanade's psychological and creative nadir and at the beginning of the retrospective inquiry into her relationship with her father. P04 owns the completion and interpretation of that causal history; P03 should not use later material to turn the episode-10 flashback into a settled explanation prematurely.

## Core interpretive claim

P03 partially validates Mafuyu's perception but revises its apparent literalness. Mafuyu is not demonstrated to possess infallible access to the others' inner lives. Instead, the narrative shows that she has recognized a family resemblance among four different forms of endangered selfhood.

Ena's case is the most literal. Her artistic self is central to the identity she values, yet she believes she may never possess the talent required for that self to receive meaningful recognition. Easy attention to her appearance provides immediate validation but cannot substitute for artistic recognition. When creation becomes another proof of inadequacy, she explicitly admits that she has wanted to disappear for a long time.

Mizuki's problem is nearly the inverse of Mafuyu's. Mafuyu struggles to locate an internally owned desire beneath accumulated expectations; Mizuki knows what is wanted and can state it plainly. When asked why they dress as they do, Mizuki answers that they like it. The injury comes from other people refusing to let that preference remain self-authorizing. Mizuki is watched, discussed, categorized, and advised that the simplest solution to harassment would be to become more “normal.” The threat is therefore not absence of self but social pressure to surrender an already known self. Mizuki does not explicitly confirm a wish to disappear. Instead, the episode gives a guarded `さあ……どーだろね` and then turns Mizuki's attention back toward Mafuyu: someone who claimed not to care may have been hurting too.

Kanade's case is different again. P03 gives no explicit statement that she wants to disappear. What it shows is an identity organized so strongly around the obligation to make music that saves others that one failed rescue threatens the reason for continuing at all. Her response to failure is not withdrawal from work but pathological intensification: take on more roles, make more songs, sacrifice sleep and food, reject interference. She moves toward self-erasure through mission rather than through an articulated desire for nonexistence.

The three episodes therefore transform Mafuyu's accusation from a dramatic insult into a diagnostic problem. The common structure is not identical symptomatology. It is that each member has a domain in which continued existence as a recognizable self feels conditional:

- **Ena:** “Can the self I value ever be worthy of recognition?”
- **Mizuki:** “Can I remain the self I know myself to be without being made into an object or pressured to conform?”
- **Kanade:** “If my music cannot save anyone, what justifies my continuing to make it—and what remains of the self built around that obligation?”
- **Mafuyu:** “Is there an internally owned self or desire left for me to find at all?”

That distinction is crucial for later reconstruction. The members are drawn together partly because they can recognize forms of pain in one another, but their vulnerabilities and coping strategies are not interchangeable.

## I. Narrative and causal progression

### Episode 08 — Ena: humiliation, artistic worth, and explicit disappearance desire

`凡人の絵` opens immediately after Miku has expelled Kanade, Ena, and Mizuki from the Empty SEKAI. The first response is collective safety checking. Ena calls for Kanade repeatedly and is audibly relieved when Kanade answers; Mizuki likewise checks that everyone returned safely. This matters because the P02 rupture has not dissolved the group's concern for one another. Even in shock, the first operational priority is whether everyone is safe.

Their interpretations then diverge. Ena returns immediately to Mafuyu's concealment and frames it as humiliation: Snow hid that she was OWN, listened while Ena praised OWN, and may therefore have been privately laughing at her. Mizuki is more cautious. Mizuki does not claim to understand Mafuyu and explicitly says it is not clear that Mafuyu was mocking Ena. Kanade barely participates. She hears Mafuyu's statement that K's music was “not enough” and internalizes the word `足りなかった`.

The group call already demonstrates three stress styles:

- Ena externalizes injury through anger and accusation.
- Mizuki uses humor, qualification, and social mediation to keep the exchange from hardening too quickly.
- Kanade collapses inward and converts another person's statement into a judgment on her own adequacy.

Ena insists that Mafuyu can work alone because OWN already produced a highly successful piece without them. The wording is angry, but the admission that follows—Mafuyu can produce something like that alone—reveals the inferiority wound underneath it. Her anger is not evidence that she no longer cares about Snow. It is evidence that the relationship mattered enough for the hidden talent asymmetry to become humiliating.

Alone in her room, Ena replays Mafuyu's P02 statement that the non-Snow self has nothing she wants to discuss with Ena. Ena then supplies an interpretation that is psychologically plausible for her but not source-confirmed as Mafuyu's actual motive: someone talented enough to make OWN must have no interest in an ordinary or mediocre artist.

The flashback to Snow's earlier praise of Ena's work makes the structure clearer. Snow had described Ena's illustration as gentle, delicate, somewhat lonely, and beautiful; Ena had accepted that praise with visible relief. After the revelation, Ena cannot simply retain it. She retroactively converts the praise into mockery. The source does not establish that Mafuyu's earlier praise was false. What it establishes is Ena's difficulty trusting recognition from someone she perceives as vastly superior once an asymmetry becomes salient.

Her immediate coping strategy is competitive overwork. She will draw, catch up, and make Mafuyu regret looking down on her. Anger becomes fuel for agency. This is a recurringly useful reconstruction pattern: when Ena feels humiliated, she does not necessarily retreat first. She may attack the problem through effort, confrontation, and a vow to prove the other person's judgment wrong.

But effort does not relieve the wound. Ena can identify technical competence in the drawing—perspective and draftsmanship are not collapsing—yet still judges the result as not “good.” This is important because her crisis is not simply ignorance of technique. She can perceive a gap between technical correctness and the quality she wants, and that gap becomes evidence of feared inadequacy.

Her father enters at exactly that point. Ena challenges him as a “genius painter” and asks whether seeing a talentless daughter draw is unpleasant. His response is ostensibly permissive: she may do as she likes; everyone has the freedom to draw; lack of talent does not mean someone is forbidden to draw. But the response does not challenge Ena's premise that she lacks talent. To Ena, permission to participate is devastatingly weaker than recognition that she might become good. She is not asking whether mediocre people are allowed to make art. She is asking whether the artistic self she values has the capacity to become worthy.

Ena hears his answer as another talented person's condescension. Whether the father consciously intends condescension is not fully established within this phase, but the psychological effect is clear. Ena generalizes from the interaction: talented people look down on her. Mafuyu is rapidly assimilated to the same schema.

Akito's brief appearance provides a useful family baseline. He recognizes that the argument is probably another conflict with their father, refuses to be pulled into Ena's outburst, and nevertheless tells her to sleep properly if she is going to keep drawing. The exchange is abrasive but not indifferent. Ena is comfortable lashing out at him; Akito responds with irritation and practical concern rather than emotional escalation.

After the confrontation, Ena tries again to create and reaches the edge of despair. At that point she shifts to another source of validation: a selfie. The response is immediate. Comments praise her appearance within moments, and Ena observes that people will look at her this easily.

This scene should not be reduced to “Ena is vain” or “Ena likes social media attention.” The contrast is the point. Recognition of the visible, socially consumable Ena is abundant and cheap. Recognition of the artistic Ena she most wants affirmed is scarce, uncertain, and tied to the possibility that she may lack the talent she values. The selfie gives her proof that she can be seen while simultaneously reminding her that this is not the kind of being-seen she wants most.

Mafuyu's accusation then returns in memory. Ena finally answers it directly: yes, she has wanted to disappear for a long time. Her next question—why was Mafuyu able to understand that?—changes the meaning of the earlier confrontation. Mafuyu's cruelty and perceptiveness coexist. Ena feels injured by Mafuyu, but the person she experiences as humiliating her has also recognized a despair Ena apparently does not display openly to Nightcord.

This is the strongest direct confirmation of Mafuyu's accusation in P03. It should nevertheless be modeled as Ena's state-specific admission, not automatically converted into a claim that every ordinary Ena interaction is governed by active disappearance intent. The phrase establishes a durable high-stakes vulnerability; it does not erase her ordinary competitiveness, sociability, anger, humor, or capacity for enjoyment.

### Episode 09 — Mizuki: known self, social objectification, and guarded recognition

`ボクはボクでいたいのに` is structurally different from Ena's episode because Mizuki's problem is not uncertainty about the self they value. The episode opens with Mizuki entering school cheerfully, joking about being summoned for remedial work, and demonstrating that academic ability is not the issue. Mizuki can intuitively solve material they have not formally conceptualized. Their irregular attendance is therefore not presented as simple academic incapacity.

The social atmosphere is more revealing. Other students whisper about Mizuki as a rumored object of curiosity, remarking that they might not have noticed “if they did not know,” while assuming Mizuki cannot hear. Mizuki hears perfectly and internally dismisses it as routine. Later, older students actively seek Mizuki out because they want to see the unusual first-year student for themselves. Their language treats Mizuki as spectacle: someone whose appearance must be inspected, categorized, and discussed.

Mizuki initially considers avoiding them, then decides waiting would be more annoying. When directly asked why they dress that way, the answer is unusually important for the N25 autonomy architecture: `ボクがこの格好、好きだからです。ただそれだけ`—because I like dressing this way; that's all.

This is almost the inverse of Mafuyu's P02 response to “what do you want?” Mafuyu redirects desire through other people's happiness and ultimately cannot identify an answer. Mizuki can identify preference immediately, state it clearly, and treat it as sufficient justification. The problem is external: other people refuse to accept that preference as self-authorizing.

Mizuki's response style is also characteristic. They do not offer the older students a vulnerable explanation, debate legitimacy, or disclose pain. They answer lightly but firmly, use socially appropriate politeness, and leave. This is assertiveness wrapped in playfulness rather than either submission or explosive confrontation.

The classmates who walk with Mizuki appear supportive in the moment. They call the seniors' behavior cruel and offer to listen if anything happens. Mizuki thanks them. Then, at the shopping mall, Mizuki overhears the same classmates saying that if being targeted is unpleasant, Mizuki could simply dress “normally” at school. One adds that standing out naturally makes people curious; another describes Mizuki as a good person who simply cannot match the group.

This is more psychologically significant than open mockery. Open hostility can be rejected as hostility. The classmates perform sympathy face-to-face while privately accepting the normative premise behind the harassment: Mizuki's refusal to conform is itself part of the problem. Their offer of emotional support therefore has a hidden condition. They will sympathize with the pain but not fully affirm the self-presentation that attracts social sanction.

Mizuki does not confront them. The immediate response is private dismissal: if that is what they really think, they should not speak as if they understand. The episode title then becomes explicit in Mizuki's own language: `ボクは、ボクでいたいだけなのに`—I only want to be myself.

The statement is a critical reconstruction anchor. Mizuki's central wound here is not a lack of self but the social cost of self-continuity. They know what they like. They know how they want to appear. They can articulate the principle. What they cannot control is whether others will turn that self into spectacle, rumor, or a problem that ought to be normalized away.

Mafuyu's accusation then returns. Unlike Ena, Mizuki does not say “yes.” The answer is `さあ……どーだろね`—roughly, “who knows?” or “I wonder.” That ambiguity should be preserved. It would be an analytical error to convert it into a direct confirmation equivalent to Ena's.

What Mizuki does next is more revealing. They recall checking on Snow and Snow replying that such pressure did not really bother her. Mizuki now calls that answer a lie and wonders whether Snow was actually suffering too.

This marks a relationship transition. Mizuki uses their own experience of being watched, misunderstood, and told to conform as a model for reinterpreting Mafuyu. The insight does not require their wounds to be identical. In fact, the difference makes the recognition more interesting: Mizuki knows their own self and resents people who will not accept it; Mafuyu has been shaped by expectation to the point that she struggles to identify a self. Both understand the violence that can hide inside apparently reasonable social expectations.

Mizuki's capacity for this inference also strengthens the P02 finding that they are unusually sensitive to normative pressure. But the episode adds an important constraint: Mizuki's outward sociability is not proof of full trust. They can be cheerful with classmates, accept invitations, thank them for support, and still withhold the vulnerable core of the problem. When apparent allies reveal a conformist assumption, Mizuki does not immediately seek clarification. They distance internally.

For reconstruction, this means that bright affect, teasing, shopping, fashion interest, and social fluency should not be treated as superficial “mask” in the sense of being unreal. These are authentic preferences and competencies. The guardedness lies in selective disclosure and in how much pain Mizuki allows others to see.

### Episode 10 — Kanade: mission intensification, self-neglect, and collapse of meaning

`足りない音` turns the word Mafuyu used against Kanade—“not enough”—into an internal command. The opening makes the causal conversion explicit. Kanade judges the current sound insufficient, recalls Mafuyu saying K's music was insufficient, and concludes that nobody can be saved.

This is the most severe example so far of Kanade translating another person's state into a verdict on her own obligation. Mafuyu's original statement was that the music made her feel slightly saved but did not allow her to find what she sought. Kanade compresses that complexity into failure: if Mafuyu remains unsaved, the song was inadequate; if the song was inadequate, Kanade must make more and better songs.

One week later the result is visible to Ena and Mizuki. Kanade fails to respond during collaborative discussion, has difficulty tracking what Mizuki sent, and dismisses questions about sleep with `大丈夫` and `平気`. When the question of Snow's vacant production roles arises, Kanade does not propose finding a replacement. She announces that she will write lyrics, arrange, mix, and continue composing herself.

Mizuki correctly identifies the workload as unrealistic. Kanade's answer is revealing: there is no time, and she must keep making more songs. The mission is not bounded by ordinary production capacity. It has become urgent enough that the appropriate response to loss is to absorb additional labor.

When Ena tries to object, Kanade says `邪魔しないで`—do not interfere / don't get in my way. This is a marked departure from her ordinary quiet cooperativeness. It should therefore be classified as a high-stress register rather than a stable interpersonal trait. Her attention has narrowed so far that concern from collaborators is recoded as obstruction of the mission.

Ena and Mizuki do not retaliate. Mizuki notices that Kanade is no longer really hearing them and recommends leaving her alone for the moment. Ena agrees. This interaction is useful relationship evidence: the others are already willing to monitor Kanade's condition and modulate pressure rather than simply demand productivity from her.

The private continuation shows the physical cost. Kanade becomes dizzy while sitting and only then realizes that she has eaten nothing since the previous day. Even after noticing, her first thought is that there is no time to eat. This is not ordinary absentmindedness alone. Within the episode's causal frame, bodily needs have been subordinated to the rescue obligation.

Kanade then notices a new OWN piece. Her aesthetic reading remains precise even in collapse: it sounds like being at the bottom of a dark sea beyond the reach of light. Mafuyu is physically absent and relationally withdrawn, but the artistic channel remains psychologically legible. OWN continues to function as involuntary or indirect communication of Mafuyu's state.

Listening returns Kanade to the P02 conversation. She now identifies a self-serving component in her prior salvation narrative: she decided that her song had saved Snow and, through that belief, felt saved herself. This is an important increase in moral reflexivity. Kanade is capable of criticizing her own motives rather than framing herself as purely altruistic.

But the self-critique does not liberate her. It threatens to deepen the compulsion. She initially responds that she must still continue: if she cannot make a song that saves someone, then she is—she does not finish the sentence. The incompletion is significant. The source does not explicitly say “then I should disappear,” and P03 should not insert words Kanade does not speak. What it does establish is that the possibility of failing the rescue mission reaches directly into her sense of what she is permitted or required to be.

For the first time, however, the compulsion meets exhaustion strong enough to produce a counter-question: does continuing to make music have any meaning?

That question is Kanade's P03 nadir. Her default response to failure has been more work. Now even work loses its validating function.

The episode then turns to her father. Before the traumatic causal history is completed, P03 gives one crucial corrective to an overly simple trauma model. Young Kanade already wanted to make music that would make listeners happy. She admired her father's ability to make her mother happy through music and wanted to become a composer like him. The desire to help through music therefore predates the later catastrophe.

This matters enormously for character reconstruction. Kanade's current salvation imperative should not be modeled as a completely artificial trauma implant laid over an unrelated personality. A genuine prosocial artistic value existed first: music can affect people; making someone happy is meaningful; creating can be a form of care. Later events appear to have transformed that value into an absolute obligation, but P03 does not yet own the final account of how.

The flashback also establishes a talent asymmetry. Kanade's father is an established composer facing professional pressure and creative stagnation. Middle-school Kanade can hear his problem and spontaneously produce an arrangement he immediately recognizes as excellent. Kanade herself treats the solution as intuitive and may not grasp how striking her ability appears from his position. His final `奏は、すごいな` carries emotional weight, but the episode ends before the consequences are shown.

P03 must stop there. It supports the facts that father and daughter once shared a warm musical aspiration, that Kanade cared for him practically during his professional struggle, and that her talent became salient during his crisis. It does not yet establish a complete causal chain from that moment to his hospitalization or to Kanade's later guilt. P04 is responsible for that transition.

## II. Character-state findings

### Ena Shinonome

P03 substantially strengthens the model of Ena's artistic identity.

**Artistic recognition is not interchangeable with generic attention.** Ena can obtain immediate praise through selfies and appearance, but this does not resolve the central wound. The scene is constructed to show how easy one kind of visibility is compared with the recognition she wants for art.

**Perceived condescension is a major trigger.** Once a more talented person is imagined to be looking down on her, Ena rapidly becomes angry, confrontational, and competitive. This can occur even when the evidence of contempt is ambiguous. Under shame, she tends to interpret asymmetry through a humiliation schema.

**Anger often converts vulnerability into action.** Her first response is to draw harder, catch up, and prove the other person wrong. This makes Ena relatively high-agency under some kinds of interpersonal injury. The agency can be productive, but it can also become compulsive and self-punishing.

**Technical self-awareness does not protect against global self-condemnation.** Ena can recognize that perspective and drawing structure are not broken while still judging the work as fundamentally inadequate. She is capable of specific craft judgment and global emotional judgment at the same time.

**Her disappearance desire is source-explicit at this phase.** `もう、ずっと消えたいよ` should be treated as strong high-stakes evidence. It should not be generalized into the claim that Ena is always actively oriented toward disappearance; the statement emerges at the intersection of artistic despair, paternal invalidation, and failed substitute validation.

**Family conflict is recurrent enough to be familiar.** Akito's “again with Dad?” response indicates that this pattern is not novel. Ena's abrasive interaction with Akito also shows that direct anger can coexist with an underlying relationship in which practical concern is ordinary.

### Mizuki Akiyama

P03 turns Mizuki's earlier sensitivity to social expectation into a concrete autonomy model.

**Mizuki has accessible first-person preference.** They know what they like and can state it without needing external justification. This is a major contrast with Mafuyu.

**The injury is social jurisdiction over self-presentation.** The problem is not merely being insulted. Mizuki is repeatedly treated as something other people have the right to inspect, classify, discuss, and advise into conformity.

**Direct boundary-setting is possible without maximal confrontation.** With intrusive seniors, Mizuki uses polite, almost playful language, gives a concise self-authorizing answer, and exits. This is a useful medium-stakes behavioral template.

**Cheerfulness and fashion interest are not merely fake presentation.** The episode shows genuine enjoyment of cosmetics, shopping, joking, and social exchange. A reconstruction model should not flatten these into defensive camouflage just because Mizuki is also guarded.

**Guardedness is selective disclosure.** Mizuki can accept companionship and offers of support while withholding the deeper pain. When peers reveal that they privately endorse conformity, Mizuki does not confront them with vulnerability; they privately withdraw trust.

**Mafuyu's literal accusation remains unresolved for Mizuki.** The `さあ……どーだろね` response is deliberately ambiguous. Current authority should preserve that ambiguity rather than upgrading it to Ena-level confirmation.

**Mizuki can identify suppressed pain in another person through structural analogy.** Their conclusion that Snow was probably hurting despite saying she did not care is one of the first strong examples of empathic inference based on lived social pressure.

### Kanade Yoisaki

P03 strongly advances Kanade from “quiet rescuer” toward a state-conditioned model.

**Failure produces escalation before withdrawal.** Kanade responds to Mafuyu's unmet need by increasing workload, not by initially giving up. She attempts to absorb writing, arrangement, mixing, and composing simultaneously.

**Responsibility crowds out delegation.** Mizuki suggests finding someone to replace Snow's role; Kanade rejects the time cost and takes the responsibility herself. Under guilt, she prefers impossible personal burden to distributed problem solving.

**High-stress speech contracts sharply.** `大丈夫`, `平気`, `そんな時間はない`, and finally `邪魔しないで` show a terse, low-mitigation register that appears when attention narrows around obligation. This should not be substituted for her ordinary speech style.

**Interoceptive and bodily needs can become secondary to mission.** Kanade notices hunger only after dizziness and still frames eating as a time cost. This is direct evidence for self-neglect under severe goal fixation.

**She can scrutinize her own altruism.** Kanade recognizes that believing she had saved Snow also allowed her to feel saved. This prevents a simplistic saint model. Her care is sincere, but the rescue mission also regulates her own guilt and identity.

**The rescue imperative appears to be a distorted continuation of an earlier genuine value.** Young Kanade wanted to make music that made people happy before the later family catastrophe was fully shown. Trauma appears likely to absolutize the value rather than create it ex nihilo.

**P03 does not establish a literal wish to disappear.** Instead, it establishes something adjacent but distinct: if music cannot save people, Kanade's purpose and self-authorization become unstable. Her unfinished sentence should remain unfinished analytically.

### Mafuyu Asahina

Mafuyu is physically absent from most of P03, but her role changes from crisis object to diagnostic mirror.

**Her accusation is perceptive but not uniformly literal.** Ena directly confirms it; Mizuki gives an ambiguous answer; Kanade exhibits severe self-erasure without using disappearance language. Mafuyu therefore appears able to recognize hidden distress, but her crisis rhetoric compresses different structures into one absolute formulation.

**OWN remains psychologically communicative even when Mafuyu refuses interpersonal contact.** Kanade reads the new work as something like the bottom of a lightless sea. This suggests that Mafuyu can externalize internal state artistically while remaining unable or unwilling to sustain direct relationship.

**The Snow role remains epistemically unstable, not disproven wholesale.** Ena assumes prior praise was mockery. Mizuki independently concludes that Snow's claim not to care about pressure was probably false. The latter strengthens the claim that Snow's self-report can be socially adaptive and unreliable. Neither establishes that Snow's affection, aesthetic judgments, or collaborative care were universally fabricated.

## III. Relationship architecture

### Mafuyu ↔ Ena

P03 reveals that Ena's anger depends on Mafuyu's evaluation mattering.

If Snow's opinion were irrelevant, the OWN revelation would be surprising but not humiliating. The injury exists because Snow had praised Ena's art and because OWN represents a level of creative achievement Ena fears she cannot reach. Ena therefore retroactively recodes intimacy as possible pity or ridicule.

The relationship is now organized around an unresolved authenticity question: **did Mafuyu actually value Ena's work and company, or was that only Snow-role behavior?** P03 does not answer it. Ena's certainty that the praise was false is evidence about Ena's wound, not proof about Mafuyu's earlier mental state.

At the same time, Mafuyu's ability to perceive Ena's disappearance desire gives the relationship an unwanted intimacy. The person Ena believes has invalidated her artistic self has also recognized a despair she does not openly advertise.

### Mafuyu ↔ Mizuki

This dyad deepens through analogy rather than direct interaction.

Mizuki experiences social pressure that is both different from and structurally related to Mafuyu's. Mizuki knows the self they want to preserve; Mafuyu struggles to find one. But both encounter social systems that disguise normative pressure as ordinary reasonableness: “why not dress normally if being stared at bothers you?” and “of course a sensible daughter will think properly about the path prepared for her.”

Because Mizuki has experienced the gap between outward lightness and inward pain, Snow's earlier “I don't really care” becomes legible as possible suppression. Mizuki is therefore beginning to reinterpret Mafuyu less as a bizarrely different person and more as someone whose adaptive presentation may have hidden strain.

This is a strong foundation for later relationship-specific reconstruction: Mizuki may notice pressure and defensive normalcy in Mafuyu earlier than people who take socially appropriate language at face value.

### Kanade ↔ Mafuyu

The P02 saving/finding mismatch becomes a self-destructive loop inside Kanade.

Mafuyu said K's music made her feel slightly saved but did not allow her to find herself. Kanade hears “not enough” and converts it into a demand for more output. She does not yet revise the premise that her role is to save. She concludes that the current quantity or quality of saving music is insufficient.

This is a critical relationship asymmetry. Mafuyu's need cannot be met simply by Kanade maximizing the behavior Kanade already knows. Yet Kanade's guilt initially makes precisely that move.

OWN complicates the separation. Mafuyu refuses Nightcord contact, but Kanade still listens closely enough to her music to perceive emotional texture. Their relationship remains active through art even while direct reciprocity has broken down.

### Kanade ↔ Ena and Mizuki

P03 provides the first clear evidence that Ena and Mizuki monitor Kanade as a person rather than only as the group's composer.

They notice poor responsiveness and probable sleep deprivation, recommend rest, and object to her attempt to absorb Snow's entire workload. After Kanade snaps, they correctly recognize that continued pressure is unlikely to help and back off.

Their response is imperfect but significant. Nightcord is no longer merely a production arrangement in which each member supplies output. Concern can interrupt task discussion. This provides the relational substrate on which later intervention can be built.

### Ena ↔ father and Akito

Ena's father is a central trigger because he occupies the exact authority category that hurts most: an accomplished artist whose judgment cannot be dismissed as uninformed. His permissive answer does not provide the recognition Ena seeks and is experienced as confirmation that she lacks talent.

Akito's role is different. He does not adjudicate her artistic worth. He recognizes the familiar conflict, complains about the noise, and tells her to sleep. This is practical sibling concern delivered through rough speech. Later reconstruction should preserve both abrasiveness and familiarity rather than turning their relationship into either pure hostility or sentimental support.

### Mizuki ↔ school peers

The classmates are neither simple friends nor simple antagonists in this evidence boundary. They can enjoy Mizuki's company, invite them home, condemn overt harassment, and offer to listen. They also privately believe Mizuki should conform if they want the harassment to stop.

That mixed structure is important. Mizuki's guardedness is not generated only by obvious enemies. It is sustained by the experience that even kind people may accept the social assumptions that make self-expression costly.

## IV. Epistemic state

P03 is dominated by **private knowledge that does not become group knowledge**. This is essential for simulation chronology.

### Audience/source-confirmed by the end of P03

- Ena explicitly says she has wanted to disappear for a long time.
- Ena uses appearance-based social-media attention as an immediately available but emotionally inadequate substitute for desired artistic recognition.
- Mizuki is routinely scrutinized and discussed at school because of self-presentation.
- Mizuki clearly states that the presentation is chosen because they like it.
- Mizuki's apparently supportive classmates privately think conformity would reduce the problem.
- Mizuki does not explicitly confirm Mafuyu's disappearance accusation.
- Mizuki concludes that Snow's apparent indifference to pressure may have been false.
- Kanade has spent roughly a week escalating output after Mafuyu's rejection.
- Kanade attempts to take over Snow's production responsibilities and neglects food and rest.
- Kanade recognizes a self-serving component in her prior belief that she had saved Snow.
- Kanade questions whether continuing to compose has meaning.
- Young Kanade already wanted to make music that made listeners happy.
- Kanade's father was a composer under professional strain and recognized Kanade's exceptional intuitive musical ability.

### What Ena knows privately

Ena knows that Mafuyu's accusation reached something true in her: she has wanted to disappear. She also knows how strongly she fears lacking artistic talent. She does **not** know whether Mafuyu's prior praise was actually false; that is Ena's interpretation.

### What Mizuki knows privately

Mizuki knows the social cost of their self-presentation and the conditional nature of some peer support. Mizuki has begun to infer that Snow's “I don't care” responses may conceal pain. Mizuki does not disclose the full contents of this reflection to Ena or Kanade within P03.

### What Kanade knows privately

Kanade knows that she had converted Mafuyu's earlier testimony into evidence that her music could save people, and now recognizes that this belief also saved Kanade psychologically. She knows she is failing to meet her own standard and has begun questioning the purpose of continuing. Ena and Mizuki see overwork and irritability but not the full internal logic.

### What remains unresolved

- Whether Mafuyu's earlier praise of Ena was sincere, role-conditioned, or both.
- Whether Mizuki actually experiences a persistent wish to disappear, rather than recognizing the structure without endorsing the literal claim.
- What Kanade's unfinished `誰かを救える曲を作れないなら、わたしは……` would have become if verbalized.
- The complete causal chain between Kanade's talent, her father's creative crisis, his later condition, and Kanade's guilt.
- Whether Mafuyu consciously understood the differences among the three people she grouped together, or spoke from crisis-driven overgeneralization.

## V. Reconstruction-relevant behavioral and speech findings

### Ena: shame-to-anger-to-effort cycle

A useful P03 Ena sequence is:

**perceived humiliation → anger/accusation → competitive vow → intensive effort → failure/invalidation → despair → substitute validation.**

This is not a universal algorithm, but it is strongly evidenced under artistic-status threat.

Speech becomes blunt and aggressive when she feels looked down on. She uses language like `馬鹿にしやがって`, raises her voice at family members, and directly attributes hostile motives. This means a high-confidence scenario involving condescension toward Ena's art should not default to quiet hurt. Anger is likely to appear early.

But the anger should not be mistaken for indifference. The more deeply an evaluator matters, the more threatening their supposed contempt can become.

### Mizuki: playful boundary, private hurt, selective trust

Mizuki's public response to intrusive but nonviolent strangers is a useful medium-stakes template:

- recognize the intrusion;
- answer with surface politeness/playfulness;
- assert the preference clearly;
- refuse the premise that further justification is owed;
- leave rather than prolong the confrontation.

When the injury comes from nominal friends, Mizuki behaves differently. There is no explosive confrontation. The hurt is processed privately, and trust is revised internally.

Mizuki's bright speech style remains active even around stress, but internal monologue is markedly less buoyant. This provides direct evidence that spoken affect cannot be used as a one-to-one measure of emotional state.

### Kanade: overfunctioning under guilt

Kanade's high-stakes response to perceived failure is overfunctioning.

She takes responsibility inward, increases workload, narrows attention, dismisses bodily needs, and rejects assistance as delay or interference. Dialogue contracts to short declaratives and refusals. The pattern is closer to compulsive task continuation than dramatic emotional display.

This gives strong predictive guidance for scenarios involving responsibility for another person's suffering: Kanade is likely to ask what more she can do and to overestimate the amount of burden she personally ought to carry.

A reconstruction must also include the possibility of collapse after prolonged overfunctioning. P03 shows that the same mission that drives extreme persistence can become vulnerable to a single question—what if the mission itself is impossible?

### Mafuyu: perceptive compression under crisis

Mafuyu's P02 accusation should now be modeled as **perceptive compression** rather than either omniscience or random cruelty.

She accurately reaches Ena's hidden disappearance desire, activates a real but more ambiguous wound in Mizuki, and targets a self-erasing structure in Kanade even though Kanade's evidence does not take the same literal form.

Under extreme stress, Mafuyu can therefore say something psychologically penetrating in an absolutized form that erases distinctions. Later scenario reconstruction should preserve both aspects: she may see vulnerabilities others miss, but her crisis formulation should not automatically be treated as objective clinical truth.

## VI. Thematic and structural findings

### 1. “Disappearance” is becoming a family of escape structures

P03 refuses to make N25 psychologically homogeneous. The narrative repeats Mafuyu's phrase but changes what it means in each episode.

Ena wants escape from a self she fears will never become artistically worthy. Mizuki wants relief from a society that makes self-expression costly. Kanade approaches self-erasure by subordinating body and identity to an impossible rescue duty. Mafuyu wants escape from a life whose expectations have made internally owned desire inaccessible.

The repetition creates solidarity without sameness.

### 2. Recognition is the central currency of the phase

Each episode turns on a form of being seen.

Ena is seen easily for appearance but not in the way she wants as an artist. Mizuki is seen too much, as spectacle, but not recognized on self-defined terms. Kanade's deepest crisis comes from believing she misread Mafuyu's recognition of her music as evidence of successful salvation. Mafuyu, absent from direct interaction, is nevertheless “seen” through OWN more accurately than she can tolerate being known interpersonally.

The phase therefore distinguishes visibility from recognition. Being looked at, praised, or evaluated is not equivalent to being understood.

### 3. Autonomy problems can be opposite and still rhyme

Mafuyu and Mizuki are the clearest example.

- Mafuyu: externally competent, internally uncertain what she wants.
- Mizuki: internally certain about a valued preference, externally pressured to abandon or normalize it.

Both are harmed by expectation, but the intervention each would need is different. Telling Mafuyu “just be yourself” assumes the self is accessible. Telling Mizuki “ignore what others think” trivializes the real social consequences of already being themself.

This distinction should become a governing principle for later character modeling.

### 4. Prosocial values can become coercive when absolutized internally

Young Kanade's wish to make people happy through music is not pathological. It is warm, relational, and modeled on a loving family bond. The later Kanade of P03 has converted a related value into something much harsher: she must make songs that save people, and failure removes the justification for rest, food, delegation, and perhaps continued creation itself.

The story is therefore not simply opposing selfishness and altruism. It is showing that even care can become destructive when it becomes an absolute criterion for one's right to exist or stop working.

## VII. Claim transitions from P01–P02

### Mafuyu's claim that the others want to disappear

**ENA — STRENGTHEN / DIRECTLY CONFIRMED WITHIN THIS STATE.**  
Ena explicitly states that she has wanted to disappear for a long time.

**MIZUKI — OPEN / QUALIFIED.**  
Mizuki does not confirm the literal claim. The episode confirms substantial pain around social scrutiny, conformity, and self-preservation and shows why Mafuyu's statement lands, but the correct authority remains ambiguous.

**KANADE — REVISE.**  
P03 does not show an explicit disappearance wish. It reveals a related self-erasing structure: Kanade's body, workload, purpose, and self-worth are subordinated to the obligation to save through music. The claim should be reformulated around conditional self-worth and mission-driven self-neglect rather than literal equivalence to Ena.

### Ena's anger as defense of artistic dignity and recognition

**STRENGTHEN.**  
P03 shows the full cycle from perceived humiliation through competitive overwork to despair and substitute validation. Generic “jealousy” is insufficient.

### Mizuki's sensitivity to coercive normativity

**STRENGTHEN.**  
P02's reaction to Mafuyu's mother is now grounded in a direct parallel from Mizuki's own school life.

### Kanade's salvation schema as a vulnerability

**STRENGTHEN SUBSTANTIALLY.**  
Mafuyu's rejection produces workload absorption, bodily neglect, social narrowing, and collapse of creative meaning.

### “Kanade wants to save people only because of trauma”

**REJECT AS TOO SIMPLE / REVISE.**  
Young Kanade already wanted to make music that made people happy. Later trauma may transform this value into compulsion, but the prosocial artistic orientation predates the completed causal history.

### “Snow is entirely fake”

**REMAIN OPEN / DO NOT USE AS CURRENT AUTHORITY.**  
Mizuki concludes that Snow's claim not to care about pressure was false, which strengthens role-conditioned self-report unreliability. Ena believes Snow's praise was fake, but P03 provides no independent confirmation of that belief. Do not generalize one unreliable self-report into total falsity of the Snow relationship.

## VIII. Phase state handoff

### Beginning state

- Mafuyu has rejected Nightcord and remains in the Empty SEKAI/OWN mode of solitary searching.
- Ena is angry about the OWN revelation and perceived humiliation.
- Mizuki is unsettled and uncertain what Mafuyu's accusation means.
- Kanade has internalized “not enough” as failure to save.

### Pressure and new information

- Ena confronts artistic inferiority, paternal evaluation, and the emptiness of appearance-based attention.
- Mizuki encounters repeated social objectification and discovers that supportive classmates still endorse conformity.
- Kanade spends a week intensifying work, assumes Snow's tasks, neglects bodily needs, and listens to Mafuyu's new OWN piece.

### Behavioral responses

- Ena attacks the problem through anger and overwork, then reaches explicit despair.
- Mizuki maintains outward playfulness and boundaries but revises trust privately and empathizes with Mafuyu through analogy.
- Kanade overfunctions, rejects help, and eventually questions the mission that organizes her identity.

### Relationship consequences

- Ena remains wounded by Mafuyu precisely because Mafuyu's artistic recognition matters to her.
- Mizuki begins understanding Mafuyu's socially adaptive “I'm fine” presentation from inside a parallel structure of norm pressure.
- Kanade's saving/finding mismatch with Mafuyu becomes an acute self-punishing loop.
- Ena and Mizuki begin functioning as people who monitor Kanade's wellbeing, not merely as collaborators awaiting output.

### Epistemic consequences

- The audience now knows that Mafuyu's accusation reached literal truth in Ena, structural truth but unresolved literalness in Mizuki, and a distinct self-erasing vulnerability in Kanade.
- Much of this remains private. Nightcord does not yet possess a shared psychological model of itself.
- Kanade's family history has begun to open but remains causally incomplete.

### Ending state

P03 ends with Kanade at the deepest crisis yet shown: the strategy “make more music until someone is saved” has failed to restore certainty, and she questions whether continuing to compose has meaning. The narrative has also revealed that her original aspiration was not simply guilt-driven—young Kanade genuinely wanted to make people happy through music.

This creates P04's governing problem:

> **How did an ordinary prosocial artistic value become an obligation severe enough that failure to save another person threatens Kanade's reason to create, and what can that history teach her about Mafuyu's need?**

P04 must complete the father-history causal chain, then determine how Kanade, Ena, and Mizuki move from private recognition of their own wounds toward a decision about whether and how to re-enter Mafuyu's crisis.
