# Project SEKAI — Nightcord at 25:00 Main Story Phase 02 Deep Reading

## Scope and analytical responsibility

**Phase:** P02 — The “good girl” structure, disappearance, and exposure  
**Source:** `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_04:01` through `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_07:01`  
**Master episodes:** 5–8

P02 converts the concealed problem established in P01 into an explicit interpersonal crisis. Its analytical responsibility is fivefold:

1. establish the social and family structure surrounding Mafuyu without reducing it to a simple true-self/fake-self binary;
2. show that Mafuyu has difficulty accessing desire as an internally owned category, even while she remains highly competent at satisfying socially legible expectations;
3. expose the practical weakness of Nightcord's anonymity when Snow disappears;
4. move Kanade, Ena, and Mizuki from anonymous online collaborators into embodied acquaintances with real names and shared physical experience; and
5. end the Snow/OWN mystery by confronting the group with a Mafuyu who explicitly rejects the Snow role, Nightcord, and Kanade's prior understanding of having “saved” her.

The phase should not use P03–P05 to decide whether Mafuyu's accusations about the others are correct. At the end of P02, those accusations are hypotheses delivered by a distressed but perceptive character, not yet established facts.

## Core interpretive claim

P02 reveals that Mafuyu's central problem is not best described as ordinary indecision or simple dishonesty. She lives inside a system in which being admirable, helpful, reasonable, and successful is continuously rewarded, while “what do *you* want?” has become an increasingly inaccessible question. The pressure does not generally appear as overt command. It appears through praise, care, preselected opportunities, assumptions about what a sensible person would choose, and Mafuyu's own highly practiced capacity to turn those expectations into smooth voluntary behavior.

This is why the episode titled `私はいい子` is more complicated than “Mafuyu pretends to be a good girl.” Her school self is socially fluent enough to notice friends' motives, joke with them, teach them patiently, and manage the rhythm of a study session. Her Snow self can collaborate competently and respond gently. The source does not demonstrate that every prosocial behavior is false. What it demonstrates is that Mafuyu cannot reliably distinguish socially successful behavior from autonomous desire. When her classmates ask what she wants, she redirects the answer toward everyone else's happiness; when her parents tell her to choose freely, their preferred educational path is already present; when Mizuki notices the pressure implicit in her mother's advice, Mafuyu initially translates it back into a benign and reasonable formulation.

By the end of the phase, that socially adaptive system has reached failure. Mafuyu retreats to the Empty SEKAI, where the absence of ordinary demands feels preferable to remaining embedded in roles she cannot inhabit from within. When the others arrive, the rupture is not merely disclosure that Snow is OWN. Mafuyu explicitly distinguishes “Snow” from the self speaking to them now, says that the non-Snow self has nothing to say to Ena, and rejects the idea that Nightcord or Kanade's music has solved the problem. The result is a destructive but clarifying transition: the group loses its previous model of Snow, and Mafuyu finally expresses aversion, exhaustion, isolation, and the possibility of disappearance in language that is unmistakably her own current state.

The phase therefore replaces the mystery “What is Snow hiding?” with a more difficult question: **what kind of relationship can exist with someone who no longer trusts the socially functional versions of herself, and whose search for an autonomous self has become tied to whether she continues to exist at all?**

## I. Narrative and causal progression

### Episode 04 — Admired competence and the disappearance of first-person desire

`私はいい子` begins by showing why Mafuyu's ordinary life appears successful from the outside. A teacher wants to submit her essay to a competition because it was well received. Mafuyu accepts modestly. Classmates celebrate her achievement, ask her to tutor them, and describe her as kind, unarrogant, academically and athletically capable. None of these interactions is openly hostile. On the contrary, Mafuyu is liked.

Her social competence is also real at the behavioral level. She recognizes immediately that her friends' sudden praise probably precedes a request; she jokes with them about it; she agrees to help; she notices when the work has become difficult enough that a break is reasonable; and she returns the group to task afterward. A reconstruction that depicts public Mafuyu as stiff, robotic, or obviously dissociated would already be wrong. She can perform ordinary peer intimacy smoothly and responsively.

The first fault line appears when her friends notice something Mafuyu herself does not seem prepared to answer: she helps with what *they* want but rarely says what *she* wants. When asked to name her own desire, Mafuyu pauses and answers that she wants everyone to spend their time as they like, because their happiness makes her happy.

The safest interpretation is not “this is a lie.” The more important point is structural: Mafuyu answers a first-person desire question by routing desire through other people. Even the answer “what I want” becomes “what I want is for everyone else to get what they want.” This is perfectly compatible with genuine care, but it does not demonstrate access to an independent preference of her own.

The episode then places Mizuki in a position to hear Mafuyu's mother discuss those same friends. The mother's language is caring and prudential rather than openly punitive. She tells Mafuyu that if helping friends is burdensome she should say no, but then recommends spending time with people who can compete with her academically because doing so will help Mafuyu improve. The explicit principle is “value your own time.” The implicit sorting criterion is that relationships should contribute to advancement.

Mizuki immediately hears the second layer. Mizuki paraphrases the advice as something close to “choose your friends” and describes the pressure of being told that a particular value system is simply what one is expected to do. Mafuyu does not become angry at the mother in response. Instead she first reframes the remark into the most charitable reasonable version: perhaps her mother only means that she should sometimes study with people who are stronger academically.

This exchange is important for two characters. For Mafuyu, it shows how external expectation can be metabolized into reasonableness before it is experienced as pressure. For Mizuki, it establishes unusual sensitivity to the difference between explicit permission and implicit norm enforcement. P02 does not yet explain the full source of that sensitivity, but it is observable before the later spotlight on Mizuki.

The family dinner makes the structure more explicit. Mafuyu's future as a doctor is treated as her existing dream. Her mother has already ordered university materials from institutions the parents think are suitable. Both parents say that Mafuyu is free to choose—even an overseas university—but the freedom is immediately framed by the expectation that she will “think properly,” because she is their sensible and admirable daughter.

The analytical point should be stated carefully. The source supports **bounded or conditional autonomy**: choice is verbally granted, but the desirable horizon and the identity of the “good chooser” are already strongly supplied. It does not, in this episode alone, establish malicious intent. The parents appear to understand themselves as supportive. That distinction matters because Mafuyu's condition is more psychologically plausible if the pressure works through trusted care and praise rather than through a cartoonishly obvious oppressor she could simply reject.

Once alone, Mafuyu finally expresses irritation: `うるさいな`. The line follows a montage of praise and invitations to “choose,” culminating in the question of what she wants. She then asks herself whether she has anything she wants at all and answers that she does not know. Her stated desire becomes spatial rather than aspirational: she wants to remain in the SEKAI.

The Empty SEKAI therefore functions here as relief from demand. It is not yet a positive life project. It is valuable because it is empty enough that Mafuyu does not have to immediately produce another socially correct answer.

### Episode 05 — Snow's disappearance exposes the cost of anonymity

`消えた雪` begins with Kanade still listening to OWN. Her reading of the music has sharpened: cold, sharp, fragile, and expressive of wanting nobody and wanting to be alone. The source continues to let Kanade understand Mafuyu aesthetically before she understands her biographically.

The episode also returns briefly to Kanade's father. Kanade brings practical supplies and flowers to the hospital, learns that he had recently begun remembering and talking about her, and then leaves quickly rather than staying. The scene preserves the P01 interpretation that Kanade's relationship to her father is not simply tender grief. Contact with the possibility of recognition is psychologically difficult. She continues to care for him through task-like behavior while avoiding prolonged confrontation with what happened.

That distinction is useful for reconstruction: Kanade can be extremely reliable in concrete care while remaining avoidant of emotionally destabilizing contact.

At 25:00, Snow fails to appear. At first Ena and Mizuki normalize the absence. Snow attends school and may simply have fallen asleep. Kanade is more uneasy, but the group continues working. A week later, however, all three are openly worried.

Here Nightcord's anonymous intimacy becomes an operational weakness. They care enough to worry about accident, illness, or disappearance, but they do not know Snow's face, legal name, address, or ordinary social network. Ena explicitly notes that Snow rarely talks about herself. Mizuki possesses one fragment of offline context—the mother's conversation—but does not have enough identifying information to convert concern into contact.

This is the inverse of the refuge established in P01. Anonymity allowed people with difficult ordinary lives to collaborate without explanation. It also means the group has almost no emergency access to one another.

The shared `Untitled` file bridges that gap. It is owned by Snow and dated to the day she disappeared. The group plays it not because they are seeking a supernatural event but because it is the only new trace left by someone they are worried about. Their concern, rather than curiosity alone, causally leads them into the Empty SEKAI.

Kanade's memory of Snow saying that K's music once felt as if it saved her is foregrounded immediately before the search. This connects Snow's disappearance to Kanade's deepest vulnerability. Snow is not only a missing collaborator; she is the person who had functioned as proof that Kanade's mission could succeed.

### Episode 06 — Embodiment, names, and the SEKAI as shared evidence

`誰もいないセカイ` performs two transitions at once: the characters enter Mafuyu's interiorized space, and the anonymous team becomes physically embodied to itself.

Their behavior under uncertainty gives unusually useful reconstruction evidence.

Kanade is comparatively calm and task-oriented. She asks how Ena and Mizuki arrived, establishes the common `Untitled` mechanism, and proposes searching the area. Most strikingly, when Ena complains about how eerie and empty the place is, Kanade says she does not find it especially uncomfortable. P02 cannot yet turn this into a complete explanation of Kanade's affinity with Mafuyu, but it is a meaningful parallel: the emptiness Mafuyu chooses as refuge is not wholly alien to Kanade's own emotional habitat.

Ena responds more visibly to uncertainty. She wants location information, complains about the space, and oscillates between anxiety and practical/social thoughts, including the joke that the place would make a photograph likely to attract attention. This is consistent with P01: emotional expressiveness and social-media awareness remain accessible even when circumstances become bizarre.

Mizuki continues to use humor and genre language in the unfamiliar space, comparing the situation to an isekai opening and imagining how a normal first offline meeting should have happened at a restaurant or cafe. This gives stronger evidence that Mizuki's levity persists under situational stress. It still does not prove that humor is *primarily* a defense mechanism; it may simultaneously be temperament, social skill, enjoyment, and regulation.

Mizuki also initiates the transition to real names. Ena has already inferred Kanade's name from her email address; Mizuki then volunteers `暁山瑞希`, invites Kanade to use it, and pushes Ena to disclose `東雲絵名`. Kanade accepts the new register immediately and without ceremony. Ena is embarrassed but reciprocates.

This scene is more important than a cute “first offline meeting.” It changes the relationship ontology. The three are no longer only K, Enanan, and Amia. They now possess embodied evidence that the people behind the handles are real, physically present, and available to direct first-name address. The anonymity boundary that previously protected separation begins to collapse before Mafuyu is encountered.

Miku then explains that the place is a SEKAI formed from “that person's” feelings, that `Untitled` connects the spaces, and that they must find and save the person so that the person's true feelings can be found and a song can emerge.

Kanade's response to the word `救う` is especially important. For Ena and Mizuki, Miku's explanation is strange and difficult. For Kanade, “save” maps immediately onto an existing psychological command. Miku's request is therefore not received neutrally across the group. Kanade has a pre-existing schema waiting for it.

The episode ends when Mafuyu's voice appears.

### Episode 07 — Snow ends as a stable shared fiction

`消えたがってるくせに` is the rupture that justifies the P02 boundary.

The first thing Ena notices is not identity but register: the person sounds like Snow, but the atmosphere of her speech is different from Nightcord. Mizuki initially responds with the familiar Snow relationship—relief, concern, questions about food and whether she has been trapped. Mafuyu rejects that relationship in extremely compressed language: `うるさい`, `来ないで`, `ひとりにさせて`.

This is the strongest evidence so far that Snow is a context-bound role or presentation. Mafuyu herself later makes the distinction explicit: `雪じゃない私は、あなたと話したいことなんてないから`—the self who is not Snow has nothing she wants to talk about with Ena.

That still does **not** prove that every warm Snow behavior was consciously fabricated in the ordinary sense of strategic deception. What changes in P02 is the strength of the role distinction, not our certainty about its mechanism. Mafuyu can now name “Snow” as something separable from the current speaker. Whether Snow consists of deliberate performance, internalized automatic compliance, partially genuine attachment, dissociated access to feeling, or some mixture remains unresolved at this phase.

Kanade asks whether Mafuyu wants to continue alone as OWN and then tells Ena and Mizuki the identity conclusion she had privately reached. Mafuyu confirms it. Ena's reaction is immediate and personal. She does not first ask about technical authorship; she asks why Mafuyu allowed her to praise OWN without disclosing the identity and whether Mafuyu thought she looked stupid.

This is a direct continuation of Ena's P01 recognition vulnerability. OWN is a creator Ena admired as overwhelmingly talented. Snow is a collaborator whose specific praise of Ena's work mattered. Discovering that they are the same person produces not only jealousy but a humiliation narrative: Ena imagines herself being observed from a position of hidden superiority while she enthusiastically praises the superior creator. Mafuyu's answer—that the non-Snow self has nothing to talk about with Ena—intensifies that relational injury.

Mizuki attempts mediation rather than escalation. Mizuki asks whether Mafuyu could remain in Nightcord while also creating as OWN and emphasizes how abrupt the separation is for the others. Mafuyu responds that Nightcord is no longer necessary because it was “not enough.”

The phrase has different meanings for Mafuyu and Kanade. Mafuyu says that K's music once felt as if it saved her, so she stayed near K hoping she might find what she was searching for. She did not. From Mafuyu's perspective, “not enough” describes failure of the search for self. From Kanade's perspective, it immediately becomes `救えて……なかった……？`—“I didn't save her?” Kanade translates Mafuyu's existential search back into the success/failure metric of her salvation mission.

This is the first major collision between the two organizing concepts established in P01: **Mafuyu is trying to find; Kanade is trying to save.** The two projects overlapped just enough to create the relationship and now diverge enough to injure both participants.

Mafuyu then states the stakes with unprecedented clarity. If she can still find herself, she will discard everything in order to do so; if she cannot, she believes disappearing is the only option left. The source therefore moves the problem beyond dissatisfaction or burnout. Mafuyu's continued existence has become conditional on recovering some sense of self.

Ena reacts angrily to the language of disappearance. Mizuki urges discussion and says Mafuyu is acting strangely. Mafuyu turns that judgment back on the group: why is she uniquely “strange” when K, Ena, and Amia all want to disappear more than anyone?

At the end of P02, this statement must remain analytically suspended. Kanade and Ena visibly react. Mizuki becomes silent, then tries to insist on being happy, and Mafuyu tells Mizuki to stop because Mizuki understands what she means. Mizuki's `……へぇ` is not a clean denial. The scene strongly suggests Mafuyu has perceived hidden vulnerabilities, but P02 alone does not establish the content or correctness of each diagnosis. That is explicitly P03's responsibility.

Mafuyu finally says that she is tired and asks Miku to remove them. Miku complies, while the closing line continues to ask for Mafuyu to be saved. The phase therefore ends with an unresolved contradiction: Mafuyu demands isolation; the SEKAI itself has brought the others into contact with her; Miku honors Mafuyu's immediate request to expel them while still framing relationship/intervention as necessary.

## II. Psychological state at phase end

### Mafuyu Asahina / Snow / OWN

P02 establishes the first robust causal model of Mafuyu's crisis, but it should remain narrower than a complete diagnosis.

**Access to desire is impaired.** Mafuyu can identify tasks, standards, and socially appropriate actions with exceptional fluency. She has much greater difficulty answering what she personally wants. The episode does not merely show parental pressure; it shows that Mafuyu herself no longer has reliable access to preference when directly prompted.

**Compliance is highly automatized.** At school and home, Mafuyu does not look visibly forced. She can joke, smile, teach, thank, and reassure. The problem is therefore not that she is perpetually on the verge of visibly breaking character. Her adaptive presentation is practiced enough to operate as normal social behavior.

**External expectation is converted into internal reasonableness.** When Mizuki identifies the controlling implication in the mother's advice, Mafuyu initially reformulates it benignly. This suggests that pressure is not experienced only as an external enemy. The standards have been internalized into the language of what a sensible person naturally does.

**The Empty SEKAI offers relief through absence.** Mafuyu wants to remain there because it is empty of the social demands she cannot distinguish from herself. This is withdrawal, not yet recovered autonomy.

**The P02 crisis register is direct, terse, and hostile to relational demand.** Mafuyu uses short imperatives, little mitigation, and explicit refusal. This is valuable speech evidence, but it should not be elevated into the one “real Mafuyu voice.” It is the voice of Mafuyu in an exhausted, identity-threatening confrontation after a week of retreat.

**OWN is an active search strategy.** Mafuyu creates alone because Nightcord and K's music did not allow her to find what she seeks. OWN should therefore not be modeled simply as “her true artistic taste.” At this phase it is instrumental to a desperate search for self, even though the work clearly externalizes something psychologically authentic enough for Kanade and Ena to hear.

**Disappearance is now an explicit contingency.** Mafuyu says that if she cannot find herself, she has nothing else left. Any high-stakes reconstruction from this temporal state must treat existential withdrawal as source-supported rather than as fan inference.

### Kanade Yoisaki

P02 strengthens Kanade's profile in two directions that can look contradictory but are compatible.

First, she is behaviorally competent under uncertainty. She organizes the search for Snow, handles the bizarre SEKAI situation calmly, asks clarifying questions, proposes exploration, and accepts the transition to real names with little social friction. Quietness is again not passivity.

Second, her salvation schema creates a severe interpretive vulnerability. Snow's earlier statement that K's music “saved” her had become evidence that Kanade's mission could work. Mafuyu's “not enough” statement is therefore not processed only as information about Mafuyu. Kanade immediately translates it into failure to save.

The hospital scene also preserves emotional avoidance around her father. Kanade provides practical care and visits reliably but retreats when the possibility of direct recognition becomes salient. Her care style can therefore combine intense responsibility with avoidance of emotionally unstructured contact.

At phase end, Kanade has not yet collapsed; that belongs to P03. But the trigger is now in place: the person whose testimony validated Kanade's reason for composing has withdrawn that validation in a form Kanade interprets as personal failure.

### Ena Shinonome

Ena remains direct, expressive, and comparatively easy to read at the surface level, but P02 shows that anger often protects a more specific vulnerability.

She worries about Snow during the disappearance. In the SEKAI she is visibly unsettled and wants concrete explanations. Once Mafuyu reveals that she is OWN, Ena rapidly converts confusion into anger because the disclosure reorganizes an earlier scene of admiration into one of possible humiliation.

Her question is essentially relational: *what did you think of me while I was praising you without knowing who you were?* That is not reducible to generic envy. Ena fears being looked down on by the talented person whose recognition she valued.

This gives an important reconstruction rule: threats to artistic dignity and perceived condescension can produce fast, confrontational anger even when the underlying relationship matters to Ena.

### Mizuki Akiyama

P02 makes Mizuki more psychologically informative without yet exposing the full background.

Mizuki is the first Nightcord member to identify the coercive implication in Mafuyu's mother's apparently reasonable advice. The language Mizuki uses—values being pushed onto someone, an expectation that “of course” one should behave a certain way—shows sensitivity to normative social pressure rather than merely dislike of strict parents.

Mizuki continues using humor in the Empty SEKAI, initiates real-name intimacy, and tries to mediate the rupture rather than taking Ena's confrontational route. When Mafuyu accuses the group of wanting to disappear, however, Mizuki's response is qualitatively different. The initial claim of being happy does not fully resolve the accusation, and Mafuyu directly asserts that Mizuki understands what she means.

The correct P02 conclusion is not yet “Mafuyu has exposed Mizuki's secret.” It is narrower: the source deliberately marks Mizuki's response as nontrivial and connects it to the earlier sensitivity to imposed normativity. P03 must determine how much of that connection is justified.

## III. Relationship architecture

### Mafuyu ↔ family

The relationship is defined less by open hostility than by high expectation embedded in care.

Mafuyu's parents praise her, provide opportunities, discuss her future, and repeatedly tell her she may choose. At the same time, they preselect educational possibilities, assume medicine is her dream, define “proper” thought, and reinforce her identity as the sensible daughter who will not make thoughtless decisions.

The relationship therefore places pressure on refusal. To reject the proposed path is not merely to select a different university or career; it risks contradicting the admired identity through which the family understands Mafuyu.

P02 does not establish that the parents knowingly intend this effect. Current authority should distinguish **psychological effect** from **parental intent**.

### Kanade ↔ Mafuyu

The dyad becomes structurally unstable.

Kanade had interpreted Snow's “saved me” statement as evidence of successful contact. Mafuyu explains that the experience was only sufficient to make her hope she might find herself by staying near K. When that search fails, she concludes that K and Nightcord are insufficient.

They therefore discover that they have assigned different meanings to the same relationship:

- Kanade: “my music reached/saved someone”; 
- Mafuyu: “being near this music might help me find myself.”

Neither interpretation is wholly false, but they are not equivalent. The mismatch is now a central relationship problem.

### Mafuyu ↔ Ena

This relationship takes the sharpest direct injury in P02.

Snow's prior artistic praise mattered to Ena. OWN is an artist Ena admired. The revelation that they are the same person creates a hidden-status asymmetry that Ena experiences as humiliating. Mafuyu's statement that the non-Snow self has nothing to say to Ena further threatens the authenticity of their previous bond.

At phase end, Ena does not possess a stable answer to whether Snow's earlier warmth was real. Her anger should therefore be modeled as involving envy, wounded recognition, and perceived relational invalidation simultaneously.

### Mafuyu ↔ Mizuki

Mizuki has observed more of Mafuyu's offline pressure than the others because of the overheard conversation with Mafuyu's mother. Mizuki also understands the coercive subtext more quickly than Mafuyu says she does.

During the rupture, Mizuki repeatedly attempts to preserve dialogue. Mafuyu, however, singles Mizuki out as someone who already understands the accusation about wanting to disappear. This creates a new asymmetry: Mafuyu may see through something Mizuki has not disclosed, while Mizuki possesses family-context evidence that the others lack.

### Kanade ↔ Ena ↔ Mizuki as an embodied group

Episode 06 changes the group from usernames to people with real names. Mizuki drives the transition, Ena is initially embarrassed, and Kanade adopts first-name address immediately.

This matters downstream because post-P02 relationships can no longer be modeled as purely online anonymity. Even if they return to Nightcord handles, the members now possess faces, names, physical co-presence, and a shared supernatural event. The social boundary has irreversibly changed.

## IV. Epistemic state

At P02's end, the earlier asymmetry around OWN is largely destroyed but new asymmetries replace it.

**Source/audience-confirmed within the P02 evidence boundary:**

- Mafuyu is Snow and OWN.
- Mafuyu has difficulty identifying a desire of her own.
- Mafuyu experiences the Empty SEKAI as preferable to ordinary social demand.
- Mafuyu says K's music initially gave her hope of being saved/finding something, but that this was insufficient.
- Mafuyu states that if she cannot find herself, she believes disappearance is the only remaining option.
- Mafuyu accuses Kanade, Ena, and Mizuki of also wanting to disappear.

**Kanade knows by phase end:**

- Snow is OWN.
- Snow/Mafuyu has rejected Nightcord and wants isolation.
- Mafuyu's testimony about being saved was not a completed rescue in Mafuyu's own understanding.
- Mafuyu is searching for a self/feeling and links failure to disappearance.
- the Empty SEKAI is formed from the feelings of the person they were asked to find, strongly connecting that space to Mafuyu.

Kanade does **not** yet possess the family-history explanation that the audience saw in episode 04.

**Ena knows by phase end:**

- Snow is OWN.
- Snow's non-Snow presentation is dramatically different from the collaborator Ena knew.
- Mafuyu says she no longer needs Nightcord and may disappear if she cannot find herself.

Ena does **not** know the family conversation Mizuki overheard and has not seen the private dinner/bedroom sequence available to the audience.

**Mizuki knows by phase end:**

- all of the group-level revelations above;
- that Mafuyu's mother expressed strong normative preferences concerning friendships;
- that Mafuyu initially minimized that pressure;
- that Mafuyu now believes Mizuki understands the accusation about wanting to disappear.

**Important unresolved point:** the source ending does not justify assuming that the other three have yet learned Mafuyu's legal name from Mafuyu herself. They know Snow/OWN and encounter Mafuyu physically, but the name-exchange scene in episode 06 covers Kanade, Ena, and Mizuki. Avoid importing later name knowledge backward unless a locator confirms it.

## V. Behavioral and speech evidence

### Mafuyu

P02 sharply expands the register model.

**School/public peer register:** warm, modest, lightly teasing, patient, socially observant, capable of easy reciprocal friendliness.

**Family register:** affectionate and reassuring on the surface, compliant, low-conflict, quick to accept proposed reasoning.

**Snow register:** gentle, collaborative, hedged, reassuring, and oriented toward other people's effort.

**Private overload register:** sparse and affectively flat, with irritability surfacing once external interaction stops.

**Empty-SEKAI rupture register:** extremely terse; direct imperatives; little mitigation; repeated desire for solitude; blunt rejection of relational claims; low tolerance for repeated questions.

These are state/context registers, not five unrelated personalities. Reconstruction should choose among them based on temporal state, relationship, and pressure.

### Kanade

- remains concise but functional in uncertainty;
- asks direct causal questions rather than emoting first;
- takes practical leadership without social dominance;
- accepts first-name intimacy without visible embarrassment;
- has unusually strong pattern recognition through music;
- responds to the concept of “saving” as personally charged language;
- can perform reliable concrete care while avoiding difficult emotional contact with her father.

### Ena

- expresses fear, irritation, and confusion openly;
- wants concrete explanation when events become strange;
- can joke or think about social-media response even under uncertainty;
- reacts rapidly and confrontationally to perceived humiliation or condescension;
- frames hidden-status asymmetry in interpersonal terms (“what did you think of me?”), not only competitive terms;
- worry for a person can coexist with anger toward that same person.

### Mizuki

- uses humor and pop-cultural framing under uncertainty;
- initiates social intimacy and lowers awkwardness;
- notices coercive or normative subtext in otherwise polite language;
- mediates conflict through questions and alternative arrangements rather than immediate accusation;
- can continue a playful register under stress but becomes notably less glib when Mafuyu targets a concealed vulnerability.

## VI. Thematic and structural function

### Choice versus a prewritten identity

P02's most important social theme is that permission to choose can coexist with a heavily prewritten answer.

Mafuyu is repeatedly told that she may decide, but she is also repeatedly told who she is: talented, kind, sensible, capable, destined for demanding success, the daughter who thinks properly. When she cannot locate desire, other people's expectations remain available as ready-made substitutes.

The problem is therefore not simply “Mafuyu is forbidden to choose.” It is that the self who would choose has become difficult for her to access independently of the roles that receive approval.

### Anonymity becomes embodiment

P01 showed anonymity as refuge. P02 shows its two failures.

First, the others cannot find Snow when she disappears. Second, once they enter the SEKAI, anonymity can no longer contain the crisis. Real names, bodies, registers, concealed authorship, and private psychological states begin colliding in the same space.

Nightcord survives P01 partly because members can selectively present what belongs in the collaboration. P02 asks whether a relationship can survive after those partitions fail.

### Saving versus finding

The conceptual difference becomes explicit.

Mafuyu's testimony is not that Kanade's music did nothing. It gave her enough relief or contact to make her hope that staying near K might help her find something. But Kanade hears the failure of that search as proof that she did not save Mafuyu.

That mistranslation is psychologically consequential. It allows one person's unresolved identity search to become another person's moral failure.

### The Empty SEKAI as negative space

Mafuyu wants to stay where there is nothing. Kanade says the emptiness is not especially uncomfortable. Ena and Mizuki find it eerie and unpleasant.

This does not yet prove identical psychology between Kanade and Mafuyu, but it gives the space relational meaning. The environment that expresses Mafuyu's emptied self is also a place Kanade can tolerate unusually well, while the more socially expressive members immediately feel the lack of ordinary stimulation and human presence.

### “You want to disappear too” as the next analytical test

Mafuyu's accusation transforms the story's structure. The crisis can no longer be treated as one pathological member surrounded by healthy helpers. The source deliberately causes Kanade and Ena to flinch and Mizuki to become guarded.

Whether Mafuyu is projecting, perceiving, or doing both remains unresolved at P02. The next phase exists to test that claim rather than assume it.

## VII. Reconstruction implications

### Low-stakes Mafuyu

Do not use the Empty-SEKAI rupture voice as the default for ordinary scenarios. In school or familiar low-stakes social contexts at this temporal stage, Mafuyu can be warm, helpful, gently teasing, perceptive about practical needs, and socially smooth.

The hidden risk is not obvious coldness. It is weak access to autonomous preference. If a low-stakes scenario asks “where do you want to eat?” or “what do you want to do?” Mafuyu may be more likely to accommodate others, infer what will satisfy the group, or provide the expected sensible answer than to spontaneously state a strong preference.

### Medium-stakes autonomy pressure

P02 suggests that Mafuyu may initially rationalize pressure rather than confront it. Advice framed as reasonable, caring, or obviously correct can be absorbed without immediate resistance. The emotional cost may appear later in private rather than during the interaction itself.

This is crucial for reconstruction. A model that makes Mafuyu instantly call out controlling behavior because the analyst recognizes it as controlling would give her knowledge and assertiveness she does not yet possess.

### High-stakes Mafuyu

When role integrity collapses and she perceives continued relational demand as an obstacle to finding herself, Mafuyu can become blunt, exclusionary, and willing to sever relationships. Her speech becomes shorter and more imperative. She prioritizes isolation and the search for self over preserving others' feelings.

However, this is a state-conditioned crisis mode. It should not be treated as proof that ordinary warmth is always fake or that she is universally indifferent to people.

### Kanade under uncertainty and rescue pressure

Kanade's general uncertainty response is calm and analytic. Her response changes when the situation is framed as someone needing salvation. Then the problem connects to guilt and identity.

A scenario involving an unknown emergency may therefore elicit efficient problem-solving; a scenario in which someone explicitly says Kanade failed to help them can produce much deeper destabilization than an ordinary criticism of competence.

### Ena under perceived humiliation

Ena is especially reactive when admiration is transformed into evidence that she may have looked foolish or inferior. She is likely to confront rather than quietly withdraw, particularly when the issue touches artistic legitimacy or the authenticity of another person's recognition.

### Mizuki under ambiguous social pressure

Mizuki is a strong detector of coercion hidden inside politeness and of expectations presented as “what everyone naturally does.” In unfamiliar group situations Mizuki can use humor to create social momentum, but conflict involving identity pressure is more likely to reduce that lightness and produce careful mediation or guardedness.

Status: OPEN. Their reactions make the accusation analytically serious, but P02 does not yet prove it. P03 is explicitly responsible for testing the claim.

## IX. Phase-end handoff

At the end of P02, the system has moved from concealed fracture to open rupture.

**Mafuyu:** can no longer be modeled only through the Snow presentation. She has explicitly separated Snow from the self now speaking, identified Nightcord as insufficient to her search, declared the Empty SEKAI preferable to ordinary life, and made continued existence contingent on finding herself. Her ordinary social competence remains source-supported and should not be overwritten by the crisis state.

**Kanade:** remains operationally competent but has received a direct injury to the belief that Snow proved her music could save someone. Mafuyu's “not enough” becomes “I did not save her” in Kanade's own cognition. P03 must track what this does to her behavior and self-neglect.

**Ena:** has learned that Snow is the admired creator OWN and experiences the concealment as both talent asymmetry and relational humiliation. Her anger now has a specific causal basis rather than being treated as generic abrasiveness.

**Mizuki:** has demonstrated sensitivity to coercive normativity, initiated the group's first-name embodied transition, mediated the confrontation, and reacted ambiguously when Mafuyu asserted that Mizuki understands the wish to disappear.

**Nightcord as a group:** the anonymous operating system has been broken open. Three members now know one another by real name and face; all three know Snow is OWN and is in severe crisis; their prior confidence that they understood their collaborator has collapsed.

**Narrative handoff:** P03 must test Mafuyu's accusation against Ena, Mizuki, and Kanade in turn. The analytical question is no longer only “what is wrong with Mafuyu?” It is whether the group can respond to her without confronting the hidden wounds that made Nightcord attractive to each of them in the first place.
