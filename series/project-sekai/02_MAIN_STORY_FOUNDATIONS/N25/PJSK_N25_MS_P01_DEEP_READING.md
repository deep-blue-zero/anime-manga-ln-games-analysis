# Project SEKAI — Nightcord at 25:00 Main Story Phase 01 Deep Reading

## Scope and analytical responsibility

**Phase:** P01 — Anonymous collaboration, salvation premise, and concealed fracture  
**Source:** `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_00:01` through `PJSK:main:nightcode_01_03:01`  
**Master episodes:** 1–4

This phase establishes the pre-crisis operating system from which the rest of the Nightcord main story departs. Its responsibility is not merely to introduce four creators. It establishes five pieces of infrastructure that later analysis must preserve:

1. Kanade's pre-existing guilt and salvation imperative;
2. Nightcord's anonymous but functional creative relationship structure;
3. the contrast between online presentation and offline/private state for all four members;
4. the Empty SEKAI as an externalized problem already linked to someone close to Kanade; and
5. the concealed fracture around Mafuyu/Snow/OWN, including the fact that Mafuyu herself cannot clearly articulate what she is searching for.

P01 ends before Mafuyu's family system is examined directly. Claims about why Mafuyu became this way therefore remain deliberately limited here. The phase can establish that a severe self-discontinuity exists; it cannot yet fully explain its origin.

## Core interpretive claim

The opening movement presents Nightcord as a paradoxically intimate form of anonymity. The four members do not initially know one another's faces, real names, or ordinary lives, yet they have developed a reliable collaborative routine and enough aesthetic trust to make psychologically intense work together. Their anonymity protects separation between ordinary identity and creative identity, but that same separation also allows profound distress to remain structurally hidden.

Kanade enters this system already carrying a self-punitive mission: because she believes her music destroyed her father's capacity to make music, she has converted composition into an obligation to create songs capable of saving anyone. Mafuyu is already the person most directly entangled with that mission. As Snow, she tells Kanade that Kanade's music once felt as though it saved her; as OWN and in private, she is producing radically colder work in an attempt to find something she cannot locate. Kanade hears the connection before she understands it.

The phase therefore sets up the central danger of the foundation story: Kanade and Mafuyu are not simply “helper” and “person who needs help.” Each is already using music to solve a problem of existence, and each risks turning the other into evidence for whether that project can succeed.

## I. Narrative and causal progression

### Episode 00 — Kanade's mission exists before Nightcord's crisis

The opening begins with Kanade's childhood memory of her parents and her father's music. Music is first coded as warmth, care, and the capacity to make another person happy. That original aspiration is then shattered by her father's collapse. Kanade's adolescent interpretation is explicit: her own music harmed him because it was not music capable of making people happy. From that premise she constructs an absolute obligation to make songs that can save anyone.

The important point for reconstruction is that Kanade's “saving” language is not introduced as a later response to Mafuyu. It is already the organizing principle of her life before the main plot begins. She does not merely prefer helping people. She treats continued composition as something she *must* do.

Two years later, the behavior produced by that belief is already visible. Kanade works through severe sleep deprivation, responds minimally when Snow tells her not to overdo it, and immediately returns to the thought that she has to keep composing. Her internal logic is conditional and punitive: if she cannot make music that makes someone happy, then her own position becomes difficult to justify.

The Empty SEKAI encounter then attaches an unidentified “that person” to Kanade's pre-existing rescue structure. N25 Miku tells Kanade that the SEKAI was formed from that person's feelings, that the person cannot find their true feelings in its current empty state, and that Kanade should save them. The story is therefore not giving Kanade an alien task. It is presenting a task that fits almost dangerously well into an obligation Kanade already carries.

This distinction matters later. Kanade's willingness to pursue Mafuyu cannot be read only as spontaneous compassion generated by friendship. Compassion is present, but the source has already shown a powerful psychological mechanism that makes “someone nearby must be saved” almost impossible for Kanade to ignore.

### Episode 01 — Nightcord as a functioning anonymous system

`真夜中のサークル` shifts from Kanade's private origin to the social system that has formed around her music. Public listeners describe Nightcord's music as piercing, frightening, and capable of digging into the listener. The creators are known externally as a mysterious four-person circle whose identities are obscure.

Inside the group, however, the atmosphere is mundane and operational rather than gothic. The members exchange progress reports, files, deadlines, technical feedback, and casual complaints. Their division of labor is legible:

- Kanade/K is the principal composer and functions as a project coordinator;
- Snow handles lyrics and also discusses arrangement and mixing;
- Ena/Enanan supplies illustration;
- Mizuki/Amia produces the MV and visual treatment.

Kanade knows where each member is in the production process and asks Snow to review her rough composition. This is useful reconstruction evidence because it establishes Kanade's agency style outside crisis. She is quiet and terse, but not passive. Within a task domain she coordinates, tracks progress, requests opinions, and makes decisions efficiently.

The episode also establishes ordinary behavioral differences. Ena openly lives on a reversed sleep schedule and complains; Mizuki is animated, playful, technologically comfortable, and visually oriented; Snow presents herself as conscientious, considerate, school-attending, and concerned about the others; Kanade is intensely work-focused and neglects food and time. None of this requires a crisis to appear.

The group's anonymity is equally important. They collaborate closely without knowing basic offline facts. This means the relationship is both real and bounded. They can know one another's aesthetic sensitivities and work habits better than they know one another's biography.

### Episode 02 — Parallel offline lives and the first visible discontinuities

`白いミク` deliberately separates the members into offline vignettes.

Mafuyu leaves home as an apparently affectionate, reliable daughter who respects her curfew. Nothing in this scene by itself marks the interaction as deceptive. That is methodologically important: later evidence will complicate the presentation, but P01 should not retroactively turn every polite statement into a consciously performed lie.

Ena struggles with an illustration, then turns to a selfie account where validation arrives quickly. The contrast is sharp. She receives immediate praise for her appearance while the account attached to the artistic work she values does not grow. This establishes a fault line that later resentment will exploit: Ena has access to recognition, but not in the domain where she most wants recognition.

Mizuki receives a call about attendance and treats the need to return to school with breezy annoyance. The source establishes absenteeism and a casual, humorous presentation, but P01 alone does not explain why Mizuki avoids school. Later analysis must not backfill the full explanation into this scene as if the audience already possessed it.

Kanade receives a hospital call concerning her father, then reads listener comments describing her music as dark but comforting and helpful. Her reaction is relief: if the song can save or accompany someone, then the work has value. This is one of the clearest demonstrations that audience effect is not merely career feedback for Kanade. It is moral evidence about whether her reason for continuing is succeeding.

Immediately afterward, Miku's fragmented request to “come quickly” reconnects that private mission to the still-unidentified person in the SEKAI.

### Episode 03 — The concealed fracture becomes legible

`OWN` transforms the phase from setup into mystery resolution.

Ena introduces the group to OWN, a new creator receiving rapid attention. Her description is analytically precise. She compares OWN to K because both can externalize things that are difficult to put into words, but distinguishes their affective temperature: K's work may be dark and painful yet retains warmth, whereas OWN's work feels cold, rejecting, and frightening.

This contrast gives the story an aesthetic vocabulary for Kanade and Mafuyu before the characters can articulate their psychology directly. Kanade's music seeks contact. OWN's music presents withdrawal and refusal.

Mafuyu, as Snow, praises Ena's art in unusually specific language. Ena visibly values the praise. This matters because the later rupture will not occur between emotional strangers; Snow has already occupied the position of someone whose aesthetic approval reaches Ena's core insecurity.

Kanade then recognizes similarity between OWN and Snow's old music. Snow denies authorship plausibly: the arrangement and lyrics appear unlike what she makes for Nightcord. Kanade cannot prove the connection. The exchange also reveals that Kanade and Snow are the oldest connection in the group. Kanade originally created an account and contacted Snow after hearing her earlier work; Ena and Mizuki joined afterward.

Snow asks why Kanade remembers that old music, and Kanade says it left a strong impression. Kanade then asks why Snow agreed to work with her. Snow answers that Kanade's first song felt as though it had saved her.

For Kanade, this is enormously consequential. It appears to validate the central mission created after her father's collapse: she *has* made music that saved someone. Kanade's private relief after Snow logs off is therefore not ordinary pride. Snow has inadvertently become evidence that Kanade's self-imposed reason to live and compose might be achievable.

The final private Mafuyu scene destabilizes Snow's own statement. Mafuyu repeats the idea of being saved as if she does not understand why she said it. She dismisses public reactions to OWN and states that, despite continuing to make such music, she still cannot find what she is looking for. The source thus confirms the identity concealed from the other members while also showing that Mafuyu does not possess a stable explanatory account of herself.

That is the correct stopping point for P01. The audience now knows the group contains a hidden crisis, but the social mechanism that produced Mafuyu's condition has not yet been exposed.

## II. Psychological state at phase end

### Kanade Yoisaki

**Stable evidence in P01**

Kanade is introverted, terse, highly task-focused, musically observant, and capable of sustained autonomous work. She notices formal musical details readily and responds to other creators primarily through the work itself. Within Nightcord she is not socially dominant, but she is operationally central.

Her most important psychological feature is the belief that she must create music capable of saving people. This belief is inseparable from guilt over her father. Composition functions simultaneously as vocation, penance, duty, and a reason to continue existing.

Kanade's self-neglect is already normalized: inadequate food, disordered sleep, and continuous work are not emergency deviations introduced later. They are baseline vulnerabilities.

**Phase-end pressure point**

Snow's statement that K's song once saved her gives Kanade an emotionally powerful success case. That makes Snow unusually salient before Kanade knows the full crisis. If that success case is later invalidated, Kanade's own self-concept is exposed to direct damage.

### Mafuyu Asahina / Snow / OWN

P01 establishes multiple presentations but does not yet justify flattening them into a simple “true versus fake” binary.

As Mafuyu at home, she is warm, compliant, punctual, and reassuring. As Snow, she is considerate, encouraging, collaborative, and able to provide specific aesthetic praise. As the private creator OWN, the work is experienced by others as cold, rejecting, and desperate. In private after the Snow conversation, Mafuyu's language is markedly flatter and more dismissive.

The most important evidence is not merely that she uses different registers. It is that she appears estranged from her own emotional statement. After telling Kanade that K's music felt as if it saved her, Mafuyu privately asks what she is talking about. This suggests that Snow's expression cannot simply be classified as a calculated falsehood. At this point, the safer interpretation is self-discontinuity: Mafuyu can produce socially and emotionally meaningful language whose relation to her accessible internal state is unstable.

OWN is explicitly part of a search. Mafuyu continues making the music because she has not “found” something. P01 does not yet establish exactly what has been lost or why.

### Ena Shinonome

Ena's artistic identity is already organized around recognition scarcity. She is technically self-critical and emotionally invested in visual art, while social-media approval is easier to obtain through selfies than through the work she values. That gap is a source of pain rather than a satisfying substitute.

She is direct, expressive, and willing to state aesthetic judgments with more detail than the others. Her description of K and OWN shows meaningful perceptual sensitivity: she can identify not just whether a work is impressive but the emotional relation it seems to establish with the listener.

Snow's specific praise matters to Ena. This creates a relationship vulnerability that will become important if Ena later learns that Snow concealed something she regards as relevant to the authenticity of that praise.

### Mizuki Akiyama

Mizuki's P01 presentation is energetic, playful, aesthetically oriented, technically competent, socially fluent, and deliberately light. Mizuki jokes about school, deadlines, technology, and the others' habits, helping keep the Nightcord working environment low-friction.

The source also establishes irregular school attendance, but P01 does not yet supply enough evidence to explain it. The correct reconstruction stance is therefore conservative: absenteeism is known; the deeper social meaning is not yet current knowledge at this point in the analysis.

Mizuki appears to have the broadest casual social network and the easiest access to trend information, illustrated by learning about OWN through a niche friend. This contrasts with Kanade's isolation and contributes to Mizuki's role as a social/visual bridge.

## III. Relationship architecture

### Kanade ↔ Mafuyu/Snow

This is already the most consequential dyad in the group.

Kanade recruited Snow after hearing her earlier music. Snow later says that K's first song felt as though it saved her. Kanade remembers Snow's old work well enough to recognize a stylistic trace more than a year later. Their connection is therefore founded on mutual aesthetic recognition before either possesses the other's offline identity.

The asymmetry is crucial. Kanade understands Snow's “saved me” statement as evidence that her mission can work. Mafuyu herself does not seem able to integrate the same statement into a stable self-understanding.

### Snow ↔ Ena

Snow offers Ena specific artistic validation; Ena receives it with visible relief. The relationship at this point appears supportive, but the value Ena places on recognition makes the bond fragile under conditions of perceived condescension, concealment, or unequal talent.

### Mizuki ↔ group

Mizuki functions as a social lubricant. Jokes, enthusiasm, casual check-ins, and visual-production competence help make an otherwise intensely nocturnal and psychologically heavy collaboration feel ordinary. P01 provides little evidence of explicit emotional dependence on any one member, so stronger claims should remain open.

### Kanade ↔ group as coordinator

Kanade's communication is short but reliable. She knows each member's production state, distributes attention to work products, and solicits feedback. This is a useful counterweight to reconstructions that mistake low verbal expressiveness for low agency.

## IV. Epistemic state

At the end of P01, knowledge is sharply asymmetric.

**Audience/source-confirmed:**

- Mafuyu is Snow.
- Mafuyu is also OWN.
- OWN is part of Mafuyu's attempt to find something she cannot locate.
- Mafuyu's accessible private state does not align cleanly with Snow's warm presentation.

**Kanade knows or strongly suspects:**

- Snow's old music resembles OWN.
- Snow's statement that K's music “saved” her is psychologically significant.
- An unidentified nearby person is connected to the Empty SEKAI and needs help.

Kanade does **not** yet possess proof that Snow is OWN, and she does not yet know that Snow is the person Miku means.

**Ena and Mizuki know:**

- OWN is a striking new creator.
- Snow is an online collaborator with the familiar Snow presentation.

They do not know the OWN identity connection or Mafuyu's offline identity.

This asymmetry must be respected in any scenario set at P01. Later knowledge about Mafuyu's family, OWN identity, Mizuki's school experience, or the Empty SEKAI confrontation cannot be imported backward.

## V. Behavioral and speech evidence

P01 already supplies enough low-stakes material to avoid building the characters solely from later crisis scenes.

### Kanade

- concise, low-energy spoken and typed responses;
- technical focus and rapid return to work;
- comfortable asking for artistic feedback;
- coordinates production without theatrical leadership language;
- notices others' work states;
- minimizes her own exhaustion;
- becomes more verbally expansive only around music or the salvation imperative.

### Mafuyu/Snow

- polished, reassuring, socially smooth phrasing;
- frequent concern for others' effort and condition;
- specific praise rather than generic flattery when discussing Ena's art;
- at home, similarly gentle compliance;
- in private, much shorter, colder, more dismissive language;
- the contrast should be modeled as context/state conditioned rather than as two unrelated characters.

### Ena

- openly complains and expresses fatigue;
- gives direct aesthetic judgments;
- quickly reveals insecurity through comparison;
- responds strongly to specific artistic praise;
- uses easy social-media validation while simultaneously devaluing it relative to artistic recognition.

### Mizuki

- high conversational initiative;
- teasing and playful exaggeration;
- frequent positive affect markers;
- casual technical competence;
- comfort discussing trends and visual aesthetics;
- light treatment of school attendance problems without enough P01 evidence to decide how much of that lightness is defensive.

## VI. Thematic and structural function

### Music as contact versus music as refusal

The K/OWN contrast gives the story a formal way to express psychology without requiring diagnostic exposition. K's music is described as painful but warm; OWN's as cold and rejecting. Both externalize what ordinary language cannot.

This sets up a central question: does art communicate because the creator understands herself, or can art communicate what the creator herself cannot consciously formulate?

Mafuyu is already evidence for the latter. Other people can hear a coherent despair in OWN before she can name what she is seeking.

### Anonymity as both refuge and failure mode

Nightcord's anonymity makes collaboration possible across incompatible ordinary lives. It allows Kanade's isolation, Ena's insecurity, Mizuki's absenteeism, and Mafuyu's social presentation to coexist without requiring explanation.

But anonymity also prevents the members from recognizing when the person behind a stable username is deteriorating. The system is emotionally real yet informationally thin.

### Being saved versus being found

Kanade begins with the language of saving. Miku speaks of helping someone find their true feelings. Mafuyu speaks of failing to find something. These are not yet equivalent concepts.

P01 therefore establishes a tension that later phases must resolve: Kanade initially interprets value through *saving*, while Mafuyu's problem is already framed by the source as *finding*.

## VII. Reconstruction implications

A reconstruction model built only from this phase could already simulate a useful pre-crisis baseline, but it must be temporally bounded.

### Low-stakes scenarios

Kanade should be modeled as quiet but operationally decisive around creative work; Ena as expressive and evaluation-sensitive; Mizuki as socially initiating and playful; Snow as considerate, polished, and collaborative. Their ordinary Nightcord interactions are not dominated by visible crisis.

### Medium-stakes creative disagreement

Kanade is likely to move toward technical/problem-solving language rather than interpersonal confrontation. Ena is more likely to verbalize frustration and comparison. Mizuki is likely to diffuse tension through social maneuvering or humor. Snow is likely to remain outwardly calm and supportive at this stage.

### High-stakes extrapolation

Confidence should be limited. P01 has not yet exposed how Mafuyu behaves when the Snow presentation collapses, why Mizuki avoids school, or how Ena responds to direct identity-threatening humiliation. Kanade's compulsive rescue structure is much better established than the others' crisis behavior.

## VIII. Claims and confidence

### Strong claims

**C-P01-01 — Kanade's salvation motive predates Mafuyu's main-story crisis.**  
Status: STRONG. Directly established in `nightcode_01_00`.

**C-P01-02 — Kanade's self-neglect is a baseline vulnerability, not merely a later crisis symptom.**  
Status: STRONG. Repeated sleep, food, and work evidence across `nightcode_01_00`–`01_02`.

**C-P01-03 — Nightcord is a functional and trusted creative team despite minimal offline knowledge.**  
Status: STRONG. Directly established by the production routine in `nightcode_01_01` and Snow's explicit description of them as collaborators/companions despite unknown faces and names.

**C-P01-04 — Mafuyu's presentations are already discontinuous before the open rupture.**  
Status: STRONG. Snow/ordinary-home presentation contrasts with the private OWN scene and her inability to integrate her own “saved” statement.

**C-P01-05 — Ena's central vulnerability concerns recognition of the artistic self rather than lack of attention in general.**  
Status: STRONG. The selfie/art-account contrast and her response to Snow's specific praise support this distinction.

### Provisional/open claims

**C-P01-06 — Snow is primarily a consciously fabricated mask.**  
Status: OPEN / DO NOT ASSERT. P01 proves discontinuity, not the degree of deliberate fabrication. Mafuyu's own surprise at her “saved” statement argues for caution.

**C-P01-07 — Mizuki's humor is primarily a defense mechanism.**  
Status: OPEN. P01 establishes the humor and absenteeism but not their causal relation.

**C-P01-08 — Kanade's desire to save others is purely altruistic.**  
Status: REJECT as an adequate formulation. Compassion is present, but P01 directly links the imperative to guilt, obligation, and self-worth.

## IX. Phase-end handoff

At the end of P01, the story's state is:

**Kanade:** has a guilt-derived rescue mission, has received apparent proof that Snow was once reached by her music, and has developed a strong but unproven suspicion connecting Snow to OWN. She does not yet know the Empty SEKAI is Mafuyu's.

**Mafuyu:** maintains a competent social and Nightcord presentation while privately creating as OWN and failing to find an undefined missing self/feeling. She appears unable to integrate some of her own emotionally meaningful statements.

**Ena:** values Snow's artistic recognition and is already vulnerable to talent comparison. She does not know Snow is OWN.

**Mizuki:** functions as the most socially energetic Nightcord member and has known school-attendance irregularity, but the deeper reason remains outside current evidence.

**Nightcord as a group:** operationally stable, relationally real, biographically anonymous, and unaware that one member's hidden creative identity is tied to an escalating existential search.

**Narrative handoff:** P02 must explain the social structure around Mafuyu, convert the concealed OWN problem into disappearance and direct encounter, and test what happens when the anonymous boundaries that made Nightcord functional suddenly collapse.
