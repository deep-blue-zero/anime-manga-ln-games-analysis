# Leo/need P03 — Serious music and negotiated protection

## Scope and causal map

Canonical reading, master episodes 10–12; all 234 records. The [previous phase](PJSK_LEO_NEED_MS_P02_DEEP_READING.md) establishes the practicing duo and unresolved refusals. The [evidence index](PJSK_LEO_NEED_MAIN_STORY_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md) retains all source ranges. The durable transition is Shiho's explicit request to join, reached through a musical trial that does not finish on its original terms.

The instrument-shop encounter converts Saki's persistence into a conditional offer: perform the assigned piece together next week and Shiho will consider joining. Assisted preparation makes the challenge attainable. Saki then conceals a cold, becomes dizzy and interrupts the session. Concern replaces assessment; Ichika voices the fear that Shiho disliked her, Saki asks what happened, and Shiho explains that she withdrew to stop classmates extending their disparagement to her friends. Ichika rejects the premise that protecting her requires excluding her from the choice. Shiho calls her own wish a weakness but asks to join. That is a negotiated change in affiliation, not merely a prize for error-free playing.

## Why the trial matters, and why passing it is insufficient

Saki's desire for a personally chosen synth, and willingness to spend accumulated New Year money, makes her commitment concrete before Shiho offers the test. Ichika's suggestion that price does not guarantee suitability and that they consult staff supplies practical counterweight to Saki's spending impulse (`PJSK:main:leo_01_09:01:002:0002–0006`). The source establishes a purchase plan and request for staff assistance; it does not specify the instrument's exact model, price or every completed transaction detail.

Shiho initially states that she does not intend to form a band, although she sometimes substitutes as bassist at the live house where she works. She then relates a middle-school band experience: she wanted serious rehearsal, the others were engaged in unproductive socializing, conflict followed and she was expelled. This is her account, with no independent view from those bandmates. It nonetheless supplies a clear value condition for her present offer: shared music requires sincere work (`PJSK:main:leo_01_09:01:002:0019–0043`). Saki's persistence matters because she does not take Shiho's first refusal as sufficient evidence that affiliation is unwanted; Shiho's subsequent condition matters because Saki cannot simply decree what participation will cost her friend.

The source refuses to let either relational warmth or competence erase the other. Ichika/Saki work intensively with Miku/Luka, request specific help, and are described as keeping up during the session. Shiho notices the practice. But Saki becomes unwell before they complete the piece. Shiho's “any mistake and no band” warning never receives a clean literal fulfillment or enforcement. When she immediately thinks of an ambulance and later blames her own pressure, care takes priority over the standard she had set (`PJSK:main:leo_01_10:01:003:0002–0005`; `PJSK:main:leo_01_11:01:001:0005–0021`). The development is not abandonment of seriousness. It is recognition that the people making music matter within its demands.

The ensuing disclosure reveals a second, distinct cause of refusal. Shiho enjoyed solitude and declined classmates' invitations; being left alone was itself acceptable to her. The injury became intolerable when gossip extended to Ichika and others. She tried answering back, perceived escalation, and chose separation to prevent further collateral targeting. She withheld the explanation because she expected Ichika would come check on her and thereby intensify it (`PJSK:main:leo_01_11:01:001:0030–0051`). Authentic preference for solitude and protective withdrawal both exist. Neither cancels the other, and neither is equivalent to the earlier band's disagreement about practice.

Ichika responds by saying that others' judgments will not change her wish to remain Shiho's friend. This restores an option Shiho's private protection plan had removed: the supposed beneficiary can choose continued association knowing the risk. Shiho does not announce that all risk has vanished; she warns them and then asks to join. Her self-description as weak marks how hard she finds permitting her own desire when it may expose friends to harm (`PJSK:main:leo_01_11:01:001:0052–0066`). The friendship becomes more reciprocal because care no longer operates entirely through unilateral exclusion.

## Psychological state by member

**Shiho.** Musical seriousness is stable; trust in these partners and permission for her own attachment change. Her private question after the shop—whether it is all right to be with someone again—already separates desire from expressed refusal (`PJSK:main:leo_01_09:01:003:0002–0003`). Childhood memory is activated by what she first takes for a shooting star, then identifies as an airplane. This oscillation supports hope followed by self-correction without proving that she has no hope. At exit she joins while still using defensive language, preserving her need to avoid sentimental exposure. The major change is behavior and relational permission, not a new extroverted temperament.

**Ichika.** She helps translate enthusiasm into preparation, asks for additional instruction and succeeds in keeping pace. She credits Saki for persisting where she believes she would have given up. Her desire becomes more personally explicit: she wants to be with Shiho again and says she will remain her friend regardless of gossip (`PJSK:main:leo_01_09:01:002:0048–0053`; `PJSK:main:leo_01_10:01:001:0063–0065`; `PJSK:main:leo_01_11:01:001:0026`, `0055`). Yet she initially apologizes for not noticing Saki's condition, preserving the tendency to evaluate care through what she failed to prevent. Her friends' agency and privacy make complete prevention impossible; the narrative does not establish that she should have known a concealed condition.

**Saki.** Commitment is expressed through money, effort, questions and persistence. She also overprioritizes the opportunity to reunite: she hides feeling unwell and tries to finish while dizzy. The scene provides direct evidence of this one decision under unusually high relational stakes, not a universal habit of dishonesty. She calls the illness a cold, rests, improves somewhat and accepts the instruction to recover before pursuing Honami (`PJSK:main:leo_01_11:01:001:0008–0018`, `0069–0074`). Her wish to resume action quickly and the others' braking response establish a pacing problem to carry forward; they do not license a diagnosis or a medical prognosis.

**Honami.** She is outside the negotiation but witnesses the three moving together and infers that they may have reconciled. She wants that for them, yet experiences loneliness, contradicting her proposition that their happiness without her should be enough (`PJSK:main:leo_01_10:01:003:0006–0013`). This is private affective evidence; she does not learn the trial's terms or outcome on the page. Her exclusion from the conversation is now sharper because the other three are becoming an explicit group.

## Relationship and epistemic transitions

The Ichika–Shiho relationship changes from painful, unexplained restricted contact to acknowledged care with explicit acceptance of social risk. Saki–Shiho likewise moves from repeated declined approaches to negotiated participation. Saki's childhood persistence gives the present exchange a recognizable pattern, but Shiho's willingness is not merely coerced capitulation: she introduces the criterion and ultimately voices her own request. The trio's first established shared norm joins serious practice to care for bodily limits. Honami remains outside it.

## Speech, ordinary behavior and thematic mechanisms

Saki makes large commitments exuberantly, then accepts Ichika's shopping correction without a fight. Shiho's teasing comparison of her persistence to Shizuku's supplies bounded sibling familiarity, not knowledge of Shizuku's separate narrative. Ichika moves from thanking Saki to calling a shop attendant; practical action accompanies emotional hope. The roof conversation includes Saki's wrong constellation name, Ichika's correction, memories of Honami becoming annoyed when the others would not learn and Saki's first melody offering. Honami's remembered mild irritation is an important counterweight to an endlessly placid caregiving model (`PJSK:main:leo_01_09:01:002:0028–0033`; `PJSK:main:leo_01_10:01:001:0030–0036`, `0050–0058`).

The melody is not yet a finished band composition; Ichika says she no longer writes lyrics and asks to hear future melodies. Creative possibility is present before the central social problem is resolved. Stars connect shared memory to future intention, while the mistaken airplane preserves the difference between wanting a sign and actually seeing one. Honami's lonely witness provides the counterpoint to the duo's growing hope: a partial group's gain can reveal the remaining person's self-exclusion more painfully.

Shiho's decisive speech is a request to be allowed to play with them, preceded by warning and self-reproach. This matters more than extracting a stock “cold but kind” style. Her care can be immediate and practical under bodily threat; her disclosure can be reluctant when exposing attachment; her musical direction can be precise. Those are different conditions, all textually supported. No independent performance quality, voice tremor or tears is asserted beyond the record.
