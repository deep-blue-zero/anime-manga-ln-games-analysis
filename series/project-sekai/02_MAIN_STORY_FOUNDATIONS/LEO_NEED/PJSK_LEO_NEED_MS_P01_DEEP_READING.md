# Leo/need P01 — Return makes the fracture visible

## Scope and causal question

Canonical phase reading, master episodes 1–4. The [phase map](PJSK_LEO_NEED_MAIN_STORY_PHASE_MAP.md) explains the complete foundation; the [evidence index](PJSK_LEO_NEED_MAIN_STORY_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md) supplies checksum and exhaustive scene coverage. This reading uses all 394 records in `leo_01_00–03`. Later explanations of the estrangement are unavailable at this cutoff. The question is how a friendship that still matters to everyone can appear to Ichika and Saki to have ceased functioning.

Childhood establishes differentiated participation rather than an undifferentiated happy quartet. Ichika proposes playing Miku's music and tentatively asks to repeat it; Saki supplies immediate enthusiasm and the wish to continue together; Shiho finds the effort worthwhile; Honami supplies practical knowledge, binoculars and a star chart. These roles are already complementary before the fracture (`PJSK:main:leo_01_00:01:001:0008–0035`). The opening narration retrospectively places the belief in permanence against an interrupted present. It does not explain the interruption's causes. That withholding is structurally important: viewers possess evidence of past intimacy before they can evaluate present refusal.

## Narrative and causal structure

The present begins with Honami's interrupted acknowledgment and Shiho's short departure. Ichika tries to speak, fails to find an opening and imagines graduation ending the remaining possibility of reunion. The dusty guitar case associates stalled music with stalled collective life; it is not evidence that her love of music disappeared. Her Miku listening is active and privately absorbing even when playing has lapsed (`PJSK:main:leo_01_00:01:004:0002–0007`, `005:0004–0019`, `006:0003–0005`; `PJSK:main:leo_01_01:01:001:0002–0007`).

The opening SEKAI encounter gives a counterclaim to Ichika's pessimism: School Miku and Luka say the place comes from the group's feelings, that true feelings can become song, and that the friends have a common feeling. But Ichika returns thinking she slept and experienced a strange dream. Accordingly, the encounter provides narrative possibility without immediately providing usable certainty or repaired relationships (`PJSK:main:leo_01_00:01:007:0009–0017`, `0033–0044`). The final sequence shows four separate responses to stars; shared attention is audience evidence of continuing resonance, not a conversation in which they learn one another's wishes (`007:0047–0064`).

Saki's unexpected return creates an apparent missing ingredient. Her class introduction announces energetic participation and anticipated youth; Ichika expresses uncomplicated happiness at seeing her. Yet Saki assumes that restoring her physical presence restores access to the four-person group. Ichika already knows that access is impaired. The contrast is strongest when Saki publicly identifies the four childhood friends while Ichika privately hesitates over “everyone” (`PJSK:main:leo_01_01:01:003:0008–0034`).

Episode 02 immediately tests this assumption. Shiho and Honami are pleased that Saki has returned, then leave. Warm concern and contact refusal occur together; the refusal cannot responsibly be flattened into lack of affection. Ichika describes the situation as high-school busyness and difference from childhood, although internally she hoped Saki's return might change it. This substitutes a less painful general explanation for an account she cannot yet fully give (`PJSK:main:leo_01_02:01:002:0008–0036`). Saki's subsequent ordinary activities do not erase the disappointment. The bubble-tea outing is enjoyable, but recalling the old band and Honami's apple pie makes the absence of two members nameable (`PJSK:main:leo_01_03:01:002:0016–0030`).

By episode 03, Saki asks whether something happened during her hospitalization, then withdraws the question and asks about music. Ichika reads this as Saki having noticed and sparing her a demand for explanation. That is Ichika's inference about Saki's tact; Saki's exact reason for changing topics is not narrated. The music exchange supplies the practical occasion for selecting Untitled and entering the unfamiliar school together. Shared musical interest thus creates contact with a new environment after social invitations have failed (`PJSK:main:leo_01_03:01:007:0002–0017`, `008:0001–0005`).

## Psychological states

**Ichika — attachment with inhibited initiative.** Her active goal is continuing friendship, while her working belief is that she cannot repair the present herself. She attempts greetings and practical small talk, then retreats; she imagines irreversible separation before trying a direct question about its causes. But she can state her happiness about Saki plainly. Her difficulty is conditioned by threatened relationships and uncertain responses, not a universal inability to express affection (`PJSK:main:leo_01_00:01:005:0004–0013`; `PJSK:main:leo_01_01:01:003:0015–0022`). Admiring Miku's freedom provides a contrast to her own felt immobility. It is a self-comparison, not an objective claim that she lacks agency in every domain. She manages listening, playlists, companionship and concern for Saki's return to activity.

**Saki — expansive participation with a missing history.** The wish for youth includes new lipstick, club participation, fashionable food and time with friends. These are specific ordinary pleasures after hospitalization restricted her experiences. They should not be reduced to decorative cheerfulness. She also worries that joining a sports club may inconvenience others, indicating that awareness of bodily and social limits coexists with enthusiasm (`PJSK:main:leo_01_00:01:007:0058–0064`; `PJSK:main:leo_01_03:01:002:0004–0019`). Her energetic self-description that she is recovered is a participant statement, not independent medical certification. At this cutoff she recognizes loneliness and suspects omitted history; she does not know what happened to either friend.

**Honami — outward accommodation with inhibited loyalty.** She greets Ichika, reacts warmly to Saki and tells her to care for her body, but withdraws toward classmates. Her conversation with them provides a revealing gradient: she quietly corrects the instrument from guitar to bass, deflects the question of how she knows, then changes the subject as negative talk continues (`PJSK:main:leo_01_02:01:004:0017–0030`). This supports tension between attachment/knowledge and willingness to declare affiliation. It does not yet establish why she fears affiliation or prove that the current classmates have explicitly forbidden it. Their description of Saki as a nice person further prevents prematurely treating them as a uniformly hostile group.

**Shiho — active musical life and restricted contact.** Her bass and stated practice/part-time-work commitments distinguish her withdrawal from Ichika's dusty guitar. She gives short answers and leaves, but her reaction to Saki's return contains relief. Other students call her intimidating or difficult to approach; those are social judgments, not sufficient personality descriptions. Ichika quietly disputes the judgment (`PJSK:main:leo_01_01:01:002:0018–0022`; `PJSK:main:leo_01_02:01:002:0012–0015`). Whether her solitude is chosen, defensive, circumstantial or some combination remains OPEN here.

## Relationship topology and epistemic limits

Ichika–Saki becomes the only currently easy human dyad within the quartet. Their affection, humor and shared memories survive absence, yet the dyad contains an asymmetry: Ichika knows that Honami/Shiho have been distant, whereas Saki arrives expecting ordinary reunion. It also contains uneven self-evaluation: Saki acts as though time together can be arranged, while Ichika doubts her power to help. The pair's joy is real and its insufficiency is jointly acknowledged; describing them as entirely unhappy would erase episode 03's ordinary pleasure.

Ichika–Honami and Ichika–Shiho remain damaged in access rather than conclusively emptied of trust or care. Saki–Honami and Saki–Shiho receive warm reunion signals without sustained conversation. Honami's peers can interrupt contact, but no recorded present rule bans the old friends. The original quartet persists as a desired topology, not an operating group. Miku's confidence in a common feeling must not be promoted into an observed mutual agreement.

## Behavior, language and theme

Ichika's hesitant openings to Shiho differ from her direct thanks and affectionate complaint to Saki. Saki's mock-formal greeting to “Hoshino-san” plays with their existing intimacy; it is not her default register to a stranger. Familiar nicknames—Icchan, Hona-chan, Shiho-chan—carry the continuity Saki expects. Honami's apologetic departures and alternate explanations make language a means of managing affiliation. Shiho's brief practical answers make refusal effective but leave motives unspoken. These are textual interaction patterns; no pitch, delivery, timing or vocal warmth is inferred from punctuation (`PJSK:main:leo_01_01:01:003:0014–0022`; `PJSK:main:leo_01_02:01:002:0020–0025`).

Stars connect the common past to privately continuing attention without solving present access. Music works similarly: a remembered shared practice and a present personal pleasure precede its possible use as a meeting place. School is not just background; separate classes and the presence of peer groups structure who can speak to whom and when. The opening contrast is therefore between enduring significance and disrupted everyday contact, not simply between authentic childhood and inauthentic adolescence.

## Continuity, alternatives and handoff

Initialize `LN-CR-001`: refusal is evidence of restricted contact, not sufficient evidence of absent affection. Initialize `LN-CR-002`: Ichika's present inhibition is relationship-conditioned; the childhood initiator and direct gratitude to Saki are counterevidence to an invariant passivity claim. Initialize `LN-TH-001`: shared stars mark continuing but uncommunicated attachment. These are phase-bounded claims; the causes of Honami/Shiho's refusal remain OPEN.

A charitable interpretation of Ichika's busyness explanation is that she lacks a clear causal account and hopes not to hurt Saki; a less charitable reading is that she postpones a necessary conversation to avoid discomfort. The text supports both protective concern and avoidance without requiring a single purified motive. Similarly, Saki's topic change can be tact or uncertainty about whether to press, and Honami's subject change may interrupt disparagement without publicly defending friendship.

At exit, the new fact is **shared movement beyond stalled ordinary invitations**: Ichika and Saki enter the unexplained school together after openly acknowledging that being only two feels lonely. Neither friendship refusal is resolved.
