# Leo/need P02 — Shared contact becomes chosen practice

## Scope and narrative map

Canonical reading of master episodes 5–9, all 408 ordered records. [P01](PJSK_LEO_NEED_MS_P01_DEEP_READING.md) supplies the prior state; the [evidence index](PJSK_LEO_NEED_MAIN_STORY_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md) supplies complete source coverage. Four friends arriving in one place is an opportunity, not a reunion already achieved. This phase tests whether emotional resonance, a common song and sympathetic support can overcome estrangement. They can initiate practice; they cannot yet secure Honami or Shiho's continuing participation.

Episode 04 brings the four together, explains SEKAI through School Miku and gives recognizable instruments physical presence. Episode 05 shows an interrupted first attempt, Miku's successful participation and the refusal of Saki's band proposal. Episode 06 juxtaposes Honami/Shiho's failed conversation with Ichika's proposal to begin as two. Episode 07 converts that proposal into learning with Miku/Luka. Episode 08 punctures easy optimism: Honami refuses again, privately misses the others, and Ichika's retrospective memories establish incomplete access to both withdrawal histories.

## Causality: enabling contact is not obtaining commitment

The four arrive separately enough that no human has successfully organized the meeting. Honami and Shiho followed the song after encountering each other; Ichika/Saki did likewise. Miku offers touch as evidence that she is not merely an image. Recognizable instruments support her claim that the place is formed from their feelings, but the girls' reactions remain differentiated: Ichika's astonishment, Saki's eagerness, Honami's uncertainty and Shiho's practical concern about work all continue (`PJSK:main:leo_01_04:01:001:0011–0035`, `0046–0054`). A shared environment does not homogenize personality or priorities.

The first performance is not unqualified consent to be a band. Shiho asks how to leave; Miku conditions the return instructions on hearing one complete performance. Honami also agrees reluctantly. That leverage should remain visible even though the narrative presents Miku as helpful. It creates the occasion for musical recontact, but cannot prove freely chosen continuing membership (`PJSK:main:leo_01_04:01:001:0058–0078`). The subsequent refusal supplies the decisive limit: once a song has been played, both still say no to the band (`PJSK:main:leo_01_05:01:001:0035–0057`).

The first attempt stops because Ichika/Saki lag behind Honami's rhythm. Shiho tells them to listen to the drums and objects to half-hearted performance. Honami mediates by suggesting another attempt. Miku then participates; Honami experiences her as adjusting to them, while Saki experiences a lead she can follow. These are transcript reports of participant perception, not an independent analysis of the arrangement or audio (`PJSK:main:leo_01_05:01:001:0003–0030`). The scene preserves two truths: Shiho's musical dissatisfaction is concrete, and supported coordination can reveal pleasure without resolving the social conflict.

Ichika's most important decision occurs after this failed recruitment. She proposes starting with Saki, explicitly acknowledging that two are insufficient for the desired full group but that she can answer Saki's feeling now. Her internal resolve shifts from an all-or-nothing question—can the old quartet return?—to an action available under uncertainty. The initial motive is heavily other-directed: if Saki will not give up, Ichika will work beside her. That dependence on Saki's persistence should not be mistaken for a fully articulated independent desire yet (`PJSK:main:leo_01_06:01:004:0013–0022`).

The duo's practice matters because it changes what another invitation would mean. Saki admits that piano experience does not equal synth fluency; Ichika names the limits of her guitar skill. Luka gives a specific account of the different key touch and encourages slower, gradual work. Saki links improvement to matching Shiho. They begin acquiring capability rather than relying entirely on remembered intimacy (`PJSK:main:leo_01_07:01:001:0022–0024`, `0055–0065`). But episode 08 demonstrates that capability is not a universal remedy: Ichika's renewed invitation to Honami is refused before musical competence becomes the issue.

## Four states and the work each is doing

**Ichika.** Recognition that the encounter was real comes through the sensation of playing and repeatable access, followed by deliberate return. She begins to act: proposes the duo, practices, approaches Honami with an invitation. Her hesitation remains, but it now exists within initiative rather than preventing all initiative. She still regards the band as a possible return to childhood and interprets old roles as unchanged after one performance. That is a hopeful personal reading, not proof that the years of separation changed nothing (`PJSK:main:leo_01_06:01:001:0003–0010`). Her memories in episode 08 show that she had attempted questions and practical support during middle school; her final self-blame is an assessment of failed outcome, not evidence of literal total inaction (`PJSK:main:leo_01_08:01:003:0022–0039`).

**Saki.** She persists after rejection, accepts a provisional two-member form and commits to learning. Musical joy gives hope an observable basis. Her imagined future imitation of a moved Shiho is playful anticipation, not evidence that she understands Shiho's actual decision process (`PJSK:main:leo_01_08:01:001:0004–0007`). Her statement to Luka that she does not know what happened preserves the knowledge gap despite optimism. She accepts practice demands readily but has not yet demonstrated a sustainable pacing strategy; the source here warrants eagerness and discipline in learning, not a forecast about health.

**Honami.** Her retained drumming ability, immediate mediation and memory of the old song contradict a wholly severed musical attachment. The scene with Shiho makes the social pressure more precise. She begins explaining that Shiho's work is at a live house rather than somewhere dangerous, but when asked whether they are close she remembers being asked whose side she is on and claims merely to have heard it by chance (`PJSK:main:leo_01_06:01:003:0022–0038`). The audience now has an explicit fear-associated memory fragment. It still lacks the full circumstances and cannot ascribe those past words to the present classmates.

Honami's apple-pie encounter is crucial ordinary evidence. She is embarrassed by the quantity, asks Ichika not to tell everyone and chats briefly about the shop. This is familiar, specific ease within a damaged relationship, not a sudden restoration of it. After refusing the invitation she wonders how long she will continue this way and connects the dimmer stars to being without the others. That private thought decisively supports longing while leaving its inhibition unresolved (`PJSK:main:leo_01_08:01:001:0029–0046`, `002:0002–0006`).

**Shiho.** Her expectations for coordinated, serious playing remain clear. She also initiates an unexpectedly vulnerable question to Honami: if the place is made from their feelings, does Honami have the same feeling as Ichika and the others? This is not a confession of Shiho's own wish, but it makes her attentive inquiry incompatible with simple disinterest (`PJSK:main:leo_01_06:01:003:0008–0019`). Her disparaging response after Honami's evasion and her sharp refusal of her sister's school approach expose irritation and privacy management. Shizuku notices lower spirits, but Shiho denies any problem; no disclosure of the cause occurs (`003:0040–0049`). Preserve this as bounded sibling evidence, not an MMJ or cross-unit developmental conclusion.

## Relationships and knowledge: asymmetries now do causal work

The Ichika–Saki dyad becomes collaborative. Initiative is less unilateral: Saki's enthusiasm starts the recruitment, Ichika proposes the viable first step, and both work with instructors. Their shared disappointment is no longer the only thing binding them. Ichika–Honami shows intermittent ease without restored public affiliation; Honami allows familiar ordinary conversation but rejects continuing band participation. Shiho–Honami contains observation, inquiry and frustration. Neither can presently turn knowledge of the other into repair. No stable trio or quartet exists at exit.

School Miku and Luka are both emotional facilitators and practical collaborators, with differentiated styles. Miku supplies an initially forceful opportunity, then asks whether the two will abandon what they want. Luka jokes about strict training and follows with patient, concrete instruction. Their reading that important friends may be difficult to speak to because they matter is tentative, not omniscient exposition; Luka asks whether something happened and Miku uses a possibility formulation (`PJSK:main:leo_01_07:01:001:0034–0049`).

## Behavioral and thematic interpretation

Practice supplies low- and medium-stakes evidence: Ichika asks whether an unannounced visit is acceptable; Saki treats the invitation as sufficient and is eager to thank Miku; both ask for help once the skill gap is named. Saki's playful imitation shows person-specific intimacy with Shiho. Honami's quantity-of-pie embarrassment and request for discretion give a small, ordinary trust boundary. These details prevent a crisis-only model in which everyone speaks continuously in declarations about friendship.

At higher emotional stakes, Ichika converts concern into a bounded offer, while Honami manages access by apology and external obligations. Shiho's practical musical directives and clipped social language should not be fused: the former specifies a real coordination problem; the latter withholds an unexplained motive. School Luka's technical comments can be retained as textual musical instruction, whereas claims about pitch, rhythmic execution or instrumental quality beyond the dialogue would require uninspected media.

SEKAI's strongest structural function here is to make a latent connection actionable without automatically solving it. Familiar instruments externalize memory; the retained song lets previously separated people coordinate briefly; patient tuition lowers an actual skill barrier. But the band still requires decisions the space cannot make for them. Stars likewise remain interpretive rather than causal magic: Honami's “unseen” stars register felt absence; this does not establish that the physical sky in the ordinary world changes with her psychology.
