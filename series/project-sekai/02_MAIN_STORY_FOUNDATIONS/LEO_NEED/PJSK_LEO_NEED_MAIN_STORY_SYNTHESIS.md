## The conflict is not the disappearance of affection

Leo/need begins with durable significance and failed access. Childhood cooperation was already differentiated: Ichika initiated a Miku-song band, Saki amplified the opportunity, Shiho invested in the challenge, and Honami supplied care and practical knowledge. Their promise of staying together expressed a shared wish, but it was not a procedure for handling social injury or changed circumstances (`PJSK:main:leo_01_00:01:001:0008–0035`).

During Saki's absence and class separation, contact became dangerous or uncertain in different ways. Shiho genuinely preferred solitude and disliked unserious rehearsal, but her estrangement from the childhood friends also aimed to prevent classmates' disparagement spreading to them. Honami's broadly offered sympathy was interpreted as disloyalty; support from Ichika became another object of scrutiny, and Honami eventually restricted affiliation to current classmates to avoid repeated exclusion. Ichika experienced the resulting distance without adequate explanation and increasingly interpreted herself as unable to act. Saki returned without the intervening history and treated physical reunion as though it might restore the group's everyday availability.

These are four different positions in the same damaged relationship, not four versions of one misunderstanding. Private affection can remain while protective behavior harms a friend, and knowing that affection remains cannot by itself remove its competing pressures. The source establishes this through Shiho's direct disclosure and Honami's explanation, while the earlier phases preserve why Ichika and Saki could not yet know them (`PJSK:main:leo_01_11:01:001:0030–0064`; `PJSK:main:leo_01_12:01:002:0027–0086`).

## The repair requires three changes working together

**First, contact must become a chosen, repeatable activity.** School SEKAI gathers them and offers a familiar song, but the initial performance is partly secured by Miku withholding return instructions until they finish. Both reluctant members still refuse the band afterward. The stronger beginning is Ichika's proposal to practice with Saki despite not being able to assemble the quartet. The duo acquires skills and a continuing reason to meet without pretending the missing members have consented. Creative labor translates attachment into an action available under uncertainty ([P02](PJSK_LEO_NEED_MS_P02_DEEP_READING.md); `PJSK:main:leo_01_06:01:004:0013–0022`).

**Second, care must permit another person's informed choice.** Shiho's trial gives musical seriousness practical evidence, but Saki's illness interrupts it. Concern and disclosure replace the original pass/fail terms. Ichika can then answer Shiho's reason for withdrawing: she chooses friendship even if others speak badly about them. Shiho's warning and request to join preserve risk and desire together. She is not reassured that harm is impossible; she no longer monopolizes the decision about whether the relationship is worth that risk ([P03](PJSK_LEO_NEED_MS_P03_DEEP_READING.md); `PJSK:main:leo_01_11:01:001:0050–0066`).

**Third, the person trying to repair things must include her own wish.** The Honami crisis tests two incomplete remedies. Saki removes her request to protect Honami's present friendships and suffers; Shiho attacks the inconsistency in Honami's behavior but injures her and regrets it. Ichika initially treats the problem as deciding which friend's feelings to honor and evaluates herself as useless when she cannot satisfy both. Luka's question, her friends' memories and the old lyric notebook let her recognize a sustained desire she already has. She can invite Honami into a valued experience and say plainly that she wants to be together. Honami receives the possibility of belonging despite fear and past hurt, rather than having to establish that she has always been a perfect friend ([P04](PJSK_LEO_NEED_MS_P04_DEEP_READING.md); `PJSK:main:leo_01_17:01:001:0016–0031`; `PJSK:main:leo_01_18:01:003:0023–0063`).

The complete repair is consequently distributed. Saki's care matters, Shiho's question about mistrust matters, Ichika's welcome matters, Honami's answer matters, and the current classmates' response matters. The story neither canonizes one ideal mediator nor makes every action equally helpful. Shiho's final apology explicitly acknowledges harmful means even as Honami credits the confrontation with prompting recognition. Saki's willingness to stop pressuring remains a genuine ethical change even though renunciation alone fails to satisfy their relationship.

## Why the school ending is part of the climax's meaning

SEKAI provides protected contact, familiar instruments, tuition, shared memory and a space whose visible conditions the singers describe as responding to feelings. Miku/Luka facilitate and participate; they do not supply a complete biography of each girl or act as universally omniscient interpreters. The wish becomes song only after the friends have apologized, accepted the invitation and recognized their desire together. The song is a consequence and articulation of repair in the narrative sequence, not evidence that music independently cured fear.

Honami's next-day conversation then tests the proposition maintaining her avoidance. Her current classmates do not demand exclusive loyalty: they thank her for telling them, explain their concern, apologize for their remarks about Shiho and welcome future contact. She can belong to the band while retaining school friends. This directly revises her prediction about these particular people. It does not prove that the earlier exclusion was imagined, that all groups would respond well, or that delayed disclosure was a simple moral failing (`PJSK:main:leo_01_20:01:003:0004–0018`).

The band's name distributes contribution across all four: Shiho offers the star association, Honami searches terms, Ichika contributes the spelling, and Saki links need to everyone's importance and the slash to a shooting star. Their familiar playful differences remain. Ichika's lyrics for Saki's melody provide a further distinction: beyond needLe's feelings-derived emergence, the friends now have an ordinary piece of creative work to practice. The future is specific enough to begin but not narratively guaranteed (`PJSK:main:leo_01_20:01:005:0009–0033`).

## Event-entry baseline for the four members

### Ichika — `LN-ICHIKA-MS-P04`

Her core attachment is person-specific: she values Saki's enlivening presence, Shiho's conviction and Honami's responsiveness. Her self-concept has shifted from failed helper toward someone whose own wish to be with them can justify a direct invitation. Agency is recovered and strengthened, not invented for the first time; the childhood band origin, earlier attempts to help Honami and practical initiative during the duo period remain historical counterevidence to literal helplessness.

She initiates a band step, seeks instruction, makes practical suggestions, creates the rooftop invitation and resumes writing lyrics. Under relational uncertainty she hesitates, attributes failure to herself and can defer when told everything is fine. With Saki she can be direct, teasing and grateful; with the reunited group she can offer creative work while asking not to have it read before her. Calmness, embarrassment and intensity about friends coexist. Her music-listening pleasure and practical attention to purchasing make ordinary life recoverable beyond crisis scenes. Future events should test how far this more affirmative agency travels, rather than assume all self-doubt has vanished.

### Saki — `LN-SAKI-MS-P04`

Her drive is participation after absence: school life, friends, food, fashion, sport and music are all concrete opportunities she missed or wants to enjoy. Brightness is a genuine mode of engagement and can also coexist with pain. Her decision to stop invitations demonstrates that the familiar persistent enthusiast can restrain herself for another person's perceived good; the tears show that restraint does not cancel wanting.

She mobilizes plans, jokes, asks for help and invests materially and practically in music. Under the high stakes of reunion she conceals a cold and overexerts herself; at the ending she says she is trying to be careful and receives continuing reminders. Her health and sustainable pacing remain open developmental constraints. Her absence also generates guilt about not supporting Honami, which must be treated as Saki's self-assessment rather than a finding of fault. A plausible foundation-based reading must allow her to be excited about mundane things, embarrassed by earnest gratitude, serious about learning and sad without treating these as contradictions to one fixed cheerful persona.

### Honami — `LN-HONAMI-MS-P04`

She combines care with a historically learned fear of social punishment. Broad sympathy, practical help, retained drumming, apple-pie pleasure and interest in stars predate the repair. Her fear does not prove the kindness was false. Nor does kindness absolve the harm of her withdrawal. The ending lets both truths be spoken: she hurt people while trying to avoid another isolation, and they still want her.

Her new agency is measurable: she accepts the band's invitation and initiates a difficult classroom conversation, then invites the possibility of introducing Shiho and welcoming classmates to a future live performance. This breaks the exclusive-affiliation rule in one important context. Fear of being disliked, shame and hesitant language remain relevant vulnerabilities. Her star knowledge and remembered annoyance at friends' failure to learn constellation names protect against depicting her as endlessly agreeable. Her small request to conceal the amount of apple pie provides an ordinary discretion boundary distinct from the crisis secret. No later employment, household-care relationship or cross-unit identity is imported into this baseline.

### Shiho — `LN-SHIHO-MS-P04`

She remains serious about practice, capable of solitary musical work and irritated by uncommitted rehearsal. Her solitude preference was real; it was not merely a disguise to be discarded at reunion. What changes is her permission to be with these friends and her willingness to disclose why she kept them away. Care becomes more reciprocal after Ichika chooses the risk and Shiho asks to join.

Her directness has different functions across contexts: precise musical correction, teasing, urgent practical care for Saki, injurious accusation when frustrated, private self-reproach and explicit apology. Her acknowledgement of harm forbids treating bluntness as infallible honesty. She also praises earned progress, contributes the band's star-based name and is embarrassed by affection. Her sister recognizes unusual low spirits, but no main-story scene establishes that Shizuku learns the private causes. Future interpretation should preserve musical ambition at the level actually present—serious band practice and prospective performance—without assuming a completed professional program from later knowledge.

## Relationship and knowledge baseline

The group is a four-member band with active, differentiated reciprocal care. Ichika–Saki has easy longstanding intimacy and mutual encouragement; Ichika–Shiho now includes informed acceptance of social risk and mutual recognition; Ichika–Honami has direct disclosure and affirmed belonging. Saki–Honami contains remembered care, the painful relinquishment and mutual apology. Saki–Shiho combines persistent invitation, negotiated seriousness, teasing and bodily-limit concern. Shiho–Honami includes disagreement, actual verbal injury, regret and repair. These histories should remain available when later events resemble only one of their aspects.

The four directly share the final apologies, membership decision and song experience. They do not all witness every private scene. Honami misses Shiho's initial disclosure in episode 11; Saki/Shiho do not demonstrably hear the entire earlier rooftop exchange in episode 18. Miku/Luka know and assist the ongoing repair, but the text does not transmit every autobiographical detail to them. Current classmates receive an elided account and learn of the band; they are not shown learning about SEKAI. Tsukasa and Shizuku have bounded family-presence/concern evidence only. Other-unit secrets, relationships and chronologies remain unavailable.

## Motifs and limits passed forward

Stars move from shared childhood attention to private continuing resonance, absent companionship, a renewed shared experience and a collaboratively chosen identity. They do not mean the same thing in every scene, and ordinary-world visibility is not treated as mechanically controlled by emotion. Music moves from personal listening and dusty instruments to assisted recontact, disciplined practice, negotiated partnership, the feelings-derived song and new authored work. School moves from the setting of affiliation pressures to the place where a revised expectation is actually tested.

The foundation supports a unit whose togetherness is both affective and practical: they want one another, accept imperfect repair and make something together. It leaves serious questions open: whether Saki can pace herself reliably; how Honami handles future distrust; whether Shiho can make criticism effective without injury; how Ichika's self-authorized agency survives other pressures; how musical standards, available time and different goals will be negotiated. It does not establish that later conflict is impossible, that any character is reconstruction-ready, or that every relevant event has already been interpreted.
