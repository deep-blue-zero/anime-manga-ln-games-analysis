# Leo/need P04 — Repair must include the person who wants to stay

## Scope, continuity and narrative map

Canonical reading of master episodes 13–21, all 710 records. This is one extended causal phase because each attempted solution changes how the next must be understood. [P03](PJSK_LEO_NEED_MS_P03_DEEP_READING.md) ends with Shiho joining, Saki recovering from a cold and Honami privately lonely. [Source coverage](PJSK_LEO_NEED_MAIN_STORY_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md) is exhaustive. Later events and later retrospective interpretations remain outside the cutoff.

Honami first explains her exclusion history to Ichika. When this reaches the trio, Saki stops inviting her in order to respect her current friendships. Saki's visible and continuing grief demonstrates the cost of that solution. Shiho confronts Honami, correctly identifies a problem of mistrust but injures her with a reductive accusation; she then acknowledges that failure to Ichika. Ichika's friends and old lyrics recover evidence of her own active attachment. With Miku/Luka's assistance she creates an invitation grounded in Honami's pleasure and their shared history. Honami asks whether someone who has hurt them can still belong; Ichika affirms that they want her. Reciprocal apologies, Honami's explicit assent, the song and the next day's classroom disclosure make reunion both emotionally articulated and socially tested. Naming the band and proposing an original song give the repaired relationship a practical future.

## 1. Exclusion made affiliation feel dangerous

Honami approaches Ichika because she has not seen Saki for a week and fears another hospitalization. Her concern gives access to a conversation she normally avoids. Ichika explains the prolonged cold and reports a visit, including Tsukasa's effort to cheer Saki with a self-made musical. This produces a brief moment of their familiar ease before the band invitation returns (`PJSK:main:leo_01_12:01:002:0002–0018`). The Tsukasa information is Ichika's report, not a directly observed scene in this source. It does not establish his unrelated unit goals or give him knowledge of Honami's crisis.

Ichika apologizes for not doing more when Honami struggled. Honami rejects that attribution and tells the missing story. She had listened sympathetically to multiple classmates' complaints because she did not want them to carry painful feelings alone. One classmate construed this as talking behind people's backs and presenting a pleasing face to everyone. Others then construed Ichika's support as Honami portraying herself as a victim, extending scrutiny to the friend who tried to help. Honami told Ichika not to approach and claimed reconciliation with her classmates (`PJSK:main:leo_01_12:01:002:0027–0067`).

The source preserves the difference between listening to a complaint and endorsing every judgment within it. The accusation of duplicity is the classmates' interpretation; Honami explicitly describes a different original motive. At the same time, the retrospective scene supports a genuine interpersonal risk in indiscriminate listening: people may expect exclusive allegiance even when she intends general care. An analysis should neither endorse their accusation as fact nor treat her desired kindness as proof that communication can never go wrong.

Her present rule is explicit: she fears mockery, negative talk and being ignored again, so she has decided to associate only with current classmates to avoid being seen as pleasing everybody. She ends by saying she no longer understands what friendship is (`PJSK:main:leo_01_12:01:002:0071–0086`). This is broader than reluctance to join a band. She has converted a painful history into a restricted model of allowable social affiliation. The original decision also protected Ichika from being discussed, but the present explanation plainly includes protecting herself from repeated isolation. A single pure motive would misrepresent the source.

## 2. Renunciation protects an expressed boundary but does not settle desire

In the trio's discussion, Shiho initially knows only that Honami was excluded, not its full cause. She says she tried speaking with her too. After Ichika reports the explanation, Shiho criticizes Honami's avoidance and the hurt it causes, yet also acknowledges that her own earlier conduct gives her no clean position of superiority (`PJSK:main:leo_01_13:01:002:0017–0043`). This self-qualification is analytically essential: she recognizes a similarity between their withdrawals even while objecting to Honami's present response.

Saki offers a different remedy. She remembers Honami immediately retrieving Shiho's treasured hat from a river despite getting soaked, understands her as caring, and decides to stop band invitations. Saki reasons that she was absent when Honami suffered and should not now intrude into the friends who have given her a current place (`PJSK:main:leo_01_13:01:002:0045–0064`). Her hospitalization is thus more than a period of missed fun. It becomes a basis on which she questions her right to reassure or make requests. That conclusion is Saki's morally serious self-assessment, not proof that illness made her culpable.

She goes to tell Honami so that Honami will not keep worrying about their invitations. She apologizes for being self-centered and encourages her to treasure her present friends. Afterward Saki cries despite insisting that the choice is right; days later Ichika/Shiho still see her as dispirited (`PJSK:main:leo_01_14:01:003:0003–0010`, `005:0002–0027`, `006:0003–0007`). Her willingness to refrain is a real change from repeated insistence. But her grief demonstrates that outwardly removing the request does not eliminate the attachment. The text tests a repair that protects Honami's apparent preference at the cost of Saki's own unvoiced continuing wish.

This is not evidence that respecting a refusal is inherently wrong. Saki lacked a workable account of how to acknowledge both their wishes without denying Honami's fear. The later invitation succeeds by reopening a conversation around reciprocal wanting, not by proving that persistence must always override another person's no. The narrative's stakes require preserving that distinction.

## 3. Confrontation reveals a contradiction and reproduces injury

Shiho approaches Honami separately after several more days. She admits that she also kept the others at a distance, then asks what Honami wants and whether Saki's pain matters to her. The useful question is whether continued avoidance actually protects the people Honami values. The harmful escalation is the inference that fear of injury means she does not care about them at all. Honami's remembered accusations intrude into the conversation; she cannot answer freely and repeats that she does not want the old isolation again (`PJSK:main:leo_01_15:01:002:0017–0052`).

Shiho adds that Honami is also failing to trust her present friends. This identifies a proposition the ending will test: no present classmate has demanded exclusive association, yet Honami acts as though they will repeat the old group's response. But Shiho ends by telling her she cares only about herself. Her private reflection immediately qualifies that verdict: she thinks Honami kept silent partly because she did not want to worry them and asks why she can only use hurtful words (`PJSK:main:leo_01_15:01:002:0055–0059`, `003:0002–0005`). A reading that extracts the accusation while omitting the regret would convert a flawed intervention into the story's authoritative diagnosis.

Shiho later tells Ichika she failed to speak well and hurt Honami. Practicing bass alone to clear her head is a concrete coping behavior; it does not establish that instrumental work solved the conflict (`PJSK:main:leo_01_15:01:003:0009–0015`). Ichika affirms Shiho's ability to act on what she thinks right. Shiho reciprocates by recalling that Ichika used to intervene first in quarrels, sometimes before understanding them, and says she may now be thinking so much that she cannot move. The conversation supplies the next repair mechanism: neither participant must perform perfect wisdom before their friend can recognize a usable strength (`003:0017–0030`).

The failed confrontation therefore has two consequences. Honami retains the question about trusting current classmates, but also carries intensified shame. Ichika receives evidence that her identity as unable to act is incomplete. The ending will use the first insight without making the injury disappear: Shiho explicitly apologizes for her harshness, and Honami says the anger helped her face the issue. Both evaluations belong in the record.

## 4. Ichika moves from judging her usefulness to stating her wish

School Luka has already asked Ichika what she wants when she cannot respect one friend's feelings without failing another. Her analogy says that there is no single correct sound independent of those who value it; what sound would be best for this group? This is an invitation to consider their own desired relationship, not a rejection of technical standards or an omniscient solution (`PJSK:main:leo_01_14:01:001:0004–0018`).

Ordinary trio practice in episode 16 shows positive affiliation still functioning amid the unresolved conflict. Miku attributes Ichika's progress to her own work; Shiho gives deserved praise; Saki asks to practice again to earn similar recognition. Shiho worries about further overexertion and Saki says she is trying to be careful. These are modest but concrete counterexamples to Ichika's assertion that she can do nothing and to an exclusively crisis-driven understanding of the group (`PJSK:main:leo_01_16:01:001:0002–0019`, `002:0002–0008`). The pacing concern is acknowledged; durable mastery of it is not established.

Saki then confirms Shiho's recollection of Ichika: generally calm, she would act quickly when the others were involved. Their example of Ichika climbing a tree to help Saki and becoming stuck too prevents “rescuer” from becoming a claim of effortless competence. Saki credits her with maintaining the group's balance. Ichika's lyric notebook gives private material evidence of the sustained wish beneath that activity: earlier words about staying together, initially embarrassing to her in their directness (`PJSK:main:leo_01_16:01:002:0012–0026`, `003:0007–0012`).

Even then she tells Miku that she has done nothing for them. Miku does not supply another assignment to prove usefulness. She recognizes that Ichika has already named the true feeling and asks her to accept and value it. Ichika describes each friend in person-specific terms and says she wants the four to play together. She qualifies that it may not absolutely have to be a band, while explaining the happiness of their shared performance (`PJSK:main:leo_01_17:01:001:0007–0031`). This qualification prevents the band from becoming a mystical exclusive cure. It is the specific practice in which their togetherness has been experienced and can be pursued.

Miku/Luka offer to think with her, and the star chart suggests Honami's continuing place in SEKAI. The source does not show the complete plan being formulated or make every singer's knowledge of Honami's biography explicit. The important change is that Ichika can now approach with a positive request she owns, rather than only apology for failing everyone or an attempt to choose whose feelings should win.

## 5. Affirmed belonging, reciprocal apology and an external test

Before Ichika arrives, Honami tells her current classmates she might like to see stars, immediately retracts it as strange, and faces their concern about her low spirits. She recalls Shiho's point about distrust. When Ichika asks for time after school, the classmates say they have other activities and that it is fine (`PJSK:main:leo_01_17:01:003:0002–0032`). Their response matters before the climax: the anticipated social veto is not observed even when Honami's old friend approaches publicly.

Ichika takes Honami to the roof in SEKAI. She evokes the ordinary park's adventure at night and the group's joking constellation errors. Honami laughs, including a remembered moment of Shiho inventing a bass-shaped constellation. Ichika then states the wish directly: she wants all four in the band, wants Honami to smile, and wants them to laugh together (`PJSK:main:leo_01_18:01:003:0014–0029`). The invitation offers a valued experience before demanding a defense against accusations. It does not deny that Honami has injured them or pretend the fear is absent.

Honami recalls former classmates, Saki's relinquishment, Shiho's accusation and Ichika's wish. She says she chose the least painful path for herself and hurt those caring about her, then asks whether she is really allowed to be with them. This is a shift from managing group membership by avoidance to asking whether acknowledged imperfection is compatible with belonging. Ichika answers that it is, because they want her with them. Miku/Luka bring Saki/Shiho into the scene; Ichika is surprised, so their arrival is not safely described as her fully disclosed prearranged group ambush (`PJSK:main:leo_01_18:01:003:0034–0072`).

Episode 19 completes what episode 18 alone does not. Honami apologizes to the group and explains the fear of repeating isolation in high school. Saki and Ichika apologize for absence or failed support; Shiho apologizes for harsh speech. Honami credits Shiho with helping her face herself. Ichika repeats the invitation, Honami explicitly accepts, and she recognizes that she wanted to be with them all along (`PJSK:main:leo_01_19:01:001:0003–0034`). The promise not to leave her alone becomes a commitment by friends, not a factual guarantee that future loneliness or conflict is impossible.

The song follows this reciprocal articulation. Miku/Luka explain that feelings are becoming song; Miku asks to sing with Ichika, Ichika welcomes her and credits both singers. After the performance the members describe familiarity, warmth and pleasure, and Untitled's name becomes needLe (`PJSK:main:leo_01_19:01:001:0036–0067`). The transcript contains a music-video cue but no lyric transcription or independent musical analysis. The claim here is that the narrative places the song after shared acknowledgment and acceptance. No lyric exegesis, arrangement analysis, unseen gesture or performed emotion is supplied.

The next day, Honami initiates disclosure to her current classmates. The intervening account is elided, but their response explicitly acknowledges her past experience and thanks her for telling them. They explain that they were worried and unsure how to ask, apologize for disparaging Shiho, welcome an introduction and express interest in attending a future live performance (`PJSK:main:leo_01_20:01:003:0004–0016`). The exact verbatim content of Honami's account is not recoverable. In particular, their knowledge of SEKAI is not established. What is established is that she can maintain the present friendships while acknowledging and pursuing the old ones. She no longer has to enact the exclusive loyalty rule she constructed from the earlier injury.

Her thought that it was simple and she should have spoken sooner is retrospective relief mixed with self-criticism. It does not prove that speaking to the earlier exclusion group would have produced the same response, or that her fear was trivial. The successful comparison is between her prediction about these classmates and their observed reaction in this specific conversation.

The three friends secretly watch, showing continuing concern but also room for awkwardness and teasing. Ichika is pleased that Shiho may now be understood; Shiho dismisses personal importance and becomes embarrassed by the affectionate statement. In the planning scene, Shiho proposes a star-based name, Honami researches terms, Ichika helps settle the spelling and Saki gives the slash a shooting-star meaning. Saki also hears “need” as everyone being necessary. These are the characters' naming contributions and associations, not an independent etymological claim (`PJSK:main:leo_01_20:01:003:0019–0026`, `005:0004–0024`).

Ichika has written lyrics for Saki's earlier melody. She is embarrassed to have them read in front of her but wants the group to play the song; Shiho proposes it for their first practice as Leo/need. This is ordinary authored work, distinct from the narrative's description of needLe emerging from their feelings. The ending establishes a new composition initiative and planned practice, not a completed public concert, professional trajectory or future success (`PJSK:main:leo_01_20:01:005:0025–0033`).

## Relationship topology and communication

The quartet is now a chosen and named band rather than only a childhood relation or shared wish. Its capacities are distributed: Saki mobilizes participation, Shiho articulates demands and acts under conflict, Honami offers responsive care and practical knowledge, and Ichika joins them through a direct statement of mutual wanting. These are tendencies from this foundation, not permanent job descriptions. Each also receives help: Saki needs pacing and room for grief; Shiho needs permission and forgiveness; Honami needs affirmation and a disconfirming social experience; Ichika needs recognition of her existing agency.

Ichika–Honami changes through disclosure and invitation; Saki–Honami passes through painful relinquishment into reciprocal apology; Shiho–Honami includes a genuine injury followed by apology and acknowledged benefit, not an unqualified victory for harshness. Ichika–Shiho gains mutual recognition of strengths and defects. Ichika–Saki retains ordinary humor while Saki's account helps Ichika recover self-understanding. Saki–Shiho's teasing and performance demands continue within explicit care about Saki's limits. The quartet's trust is more reciprocal because asking, objecting, admitting fear and apologizing become available forms of staying together.

Honami's current classmates are a separate relationship. Earlier gossip about Shiho was real, but the ending shows concern, correction and welcome. The analysis should preserve both facts, not rewrite them as always perfectly understanding or identical to former aggressors. This new bridge remains local: the story does not show public school-wide reconciliation, every classmate learning the past or every rumor disappearing.

## Speech, themes and revision

Ichika's central speech change is from repeated negative self-assessment to first-person affirmative desire. She still uses hesitant openings and blush-adjacent embarrassment is not asserted as a visual fact; what is shown textually is her request not to read lyrics in front of her. Saki shifts between playful group energy, deliberate apology and crying while saying she is all right. Honami's classroom disclosure begins with asking for conversational space, a direct contrast to earlier excuses to leave. Shiho can move from accusation to private regret, then concise explicit apology, without abandoning musical directness. Miku and Luka respond to current interlocutors with encouragement, questions, teasing and practical help; their School manifestations are retained separately.

The central thematic revision is that care cannot reliably operate by deciding alone what another person must lose. Shiho's earlier protective withdrawal, Saki's relinquishment and Honami's exclusive-affiliation rule have different motives and consequences, but all limit reciprocal choice. Successful repair includes the speaker's own wish and the other's ability to answer. The story does not require abandoning considerate behavior; it requires that concern cease to erase the people doing and receiving it.

Stars and music turn remembered togetherness into a shareable present experience. The roof is useful because it gives Honami pleasure and a way to remember her place before she must justify herself. The ordinary sky after the song and the next-day school conversation then prevent reunion from remaining confined to a special environment. Naming the band distributes authorship of the new identity across all four; the slash marks the group's own reading of a shooting star. Ichika's lyrics for Saki's melody establish that creative work will continue after the feelings-derived song.
