# MORE MORE JUMP! main-story synthesis — Learning to receive the hope one gives

## Authority and integrated proposition

This canonical synthesis was produced after the five complete [phase readings](PJSK_MMJ_MAIN_STORY_PHASE_MAP.md). It owns the shared historical causes of Minori, Haruka, Airi, and Shizuku's formation, so later character monographs can reuse them without four independent retellings. Root review reconciled the five phase histories with the shared ledgers before establishing this foundation authority. The source lock is frozen to `PJSK_SOURCE_20260822T184634Z_EVENT_0213`; actual interpretation stops at human main `mmj_01_20`. The 21 files and 1,891 records are fully read and checksum-verified; the phase map carries exact coverage, locator syntax, governing methods, and the prospective-integrity audit. A reference such as `20/001:0051–0064` is an exact source range under `PJSK:main:mmj_01_20:01`.

MORE MORE JUMP! forms because four people become able to recognize one another as contributors to a shared vocation despite unequal skills, unequal public recognition, and professional failure. The story's initial formula is that an idol gives listeners hope for tomorrow. Its developmental work makes that relation reciprocal: the person who gives hope also needs to receive it, and a recipient can demonstrate an effect that institutions, self-blame, or comparison have obscured. This does not establish that effort always succeeds. The last audition fails. Haruka needs physical help after the successful live. Airi and Shizuku do not repair their former institutions. The new group creates a place in which help and improvement can continue.

## The initial system: one aspiration, three different obstructions

Minori's aspiration begins as an answer to helplessness. Her childhood account connects ordinary mishaps and a dropped relay baton with the fear that effort changes nothing. Haruka's televised statement produces renewed action: she decides to go to school and apologize (`00/001:0001–0041`). It later supports repeated audition applications and solitary rehearsal. The narrative does not demonstrate objective extraordinary bad luck or guarantee that classmates would have blamed her. It demonstrates what she believed and how hope altered her response.

The three experienced idols arrive with different relations to recognition. Haruka has received considerable recognition but privately believes she has harmed someone through her defining message and can no longer approach the stage. Airi wants to be recognized and employed as an idol but is institutionally redirected toward variety work; the redirection wounds her even though she has enjoyed, studied, and succeeded at that work. Shizuku's apparent ideal recognition as beautiful center gives her increasing opportunities while colleagues dismiss her labor and relationships deteriorate. Their losses cannot be collapsed into an undifferentiated “idol industry is cruel” account. The institutions matter through specific selection, role-assignment, scheduling, and comparison practices, while personal responses and responsibilities differ.

Minori initially sees their outward positions far more readily than these private conditions. Yet she does not simply refuse to accept retirement. Before the group has formed, she expressly decides to respect Haruka as a girl with a chosen path and to pass on what she received (`01/004:0015–0024`). Her later attempts to help Haruka must be understood against that prior respect and her explicit worry about intruding, not as uncomplicated insistence that her favorite idol resume performing.

## The causal mechanism of formation

### From aspiration to a teaching relationship

Minori's persistence alone cannot diagnose her weaknesses. The rooftop encounter supplies expertise and a reason for it to be shared. Haruka protects the novice's use of the space; Shizuku refuses Airi's negation of the dream; Miku's account of learning from Rin prompts Minori to ask for instruction. Shizuku invokes Airi's own norm that an idol cannot abandon an aspiring child (`02/001:0054–0065`; `03/001:0024–0066`; `04/001:0004–0018`). Airi agrees under a failure condition and immediately replaces a generic self-introduction with specific evidence of persistence (`04/003:0013–0030`). The first shared structure is thus practical and conditional. Minori gains teachers without yet gaining professional status, and the teachers act on values their current disappointment has not completely erased.

### From denied identity to accountable recognition

The first Stage live does not fix that disappointment. Enjoyment brings the lost work closer, causing different kinds of pain. Airi's exclusion from idol stages, Shizuku's longing for trustworthy collaboration, and Haruka's remembered blue view emerge side by side (`06/001:0045–0060`). Airi then reduces Shizuku to the very innate qualities whose emphasis has been hurting her. Because Airi originally taught Shizuku that heart mattered more than appearance, this attack damages a foundational source of trust, not merely an interchangeable friendship (`08/002:0006–0019`; `08/003:0007–0039`).

Repair requires more than restoring confidence. Rin and Minori first help Airi own the desire to be an idol despite external classification (`09/001:0011–0034`; `09/002:0010–0042`). Airi then has to see Shizuku accurately and acknowledge what she herself did. The theater scene makes her name Shizuku's effort while admitting a shared envy with the people she confronts. Minori's restraint prevents defensive support from becoming action Airi herself recognizes as contrary to her idol identity (`11/002:0026–0049`). Shizuku receives the particular recognition missing from flattering descriptions of her beauty: someone has seen her work, intention, and endurance.

Their renewed partnership rests on two reciprocal judgments. Shizuku calls Airi the idol who gave her hope and taught her dream; Airi identifies Shizuku as her own ideal and asks to continue together (`12/001:0010–0041`). These statements do not make their strengths identical. They permit difference without turning difference into a verdict that one must be illegitimate. Shizuku does not return to the group that mistreated her. Choosing a new partner and leaving an old institution are compatible parts of the outcome.

### From impossible responsibility to supported action

The analogous pattern becomes harder with Haruka. Mai's account discloses that encouragement interacted with fearful overpractice and injury; Haruka subsequently made it her responsibility to ensure her message would never hurt anyone. She tried to compensate through more work until she could not move toward the stage (`13/003:0038–0074`; `15/001:0014–0055`). Mai's apology is insufficient because Haruka continues to protect Mai by withholding her private explanation, and because the problem is not only a misunderstanding between two speakers. Haruka reports prior consultation and repeated attempts to return (`14/002:0022–0044`; `15/002:0002–0010`).

Minori's answer changes as she learns this. She first worries that her previous enthusiasm may have hurt Haruka. She asks for help rather than assuming her motive makes her wise. The Stage singers let her see the audience view from another position, and Miku suggests that she express her own gratitude through a performance. Airi and Shizuku test the proposal's practical and emotional weaknesses, join it, and make rehearsal useful to the intended recipient (`16/001:0031–0106`; `17/001:0031–0063`; `17/002:0010–0030`). The resulting performance is not solitary heroic inspiration: its capacity depends on the novice's purpose, the seniors' expertise, singer support, and a shared place to rehearse.

Even then Haruka trembles and initially declines to climb up. Minori's detailed account of dance, friendships, affection, and a dream supplies evidence against Haruka's global belief that she has nothing to give. Miku asks what Haruka wants; she says she wants to see the view again. With Minori's hand, the singers' help, and the seniors supporting her onstage, she can enact that wish while still physically unsteady (`18/002:0057–0101`). The exact achievement is supported access plus acknowledged benefit. This is a new route to agency, not proof that symptoms, guilt, or every future stage are resolved.

### From a successful act to durable membership

The four's shared song confirms a common intention inside the story's SEKAI mechanism. Haruka then announces her wish to return to idol work. But membership is decided in the ordinary world after another audition rejection. By retaining this failure, the ending separates institutional selection from actual value to specific recipients. The seniors have already experienced Minori as an idol who gave them hope; they continue training her despite the original failure limit (`19/001:0002–0039`; `20/001:0002–0022`).

Haruka proposes the group and must expressly tell Minori that she belongs. Minori first imagines herself as its fan, then cites her lack of credentials and weaker skills. The answer is shared work and mutual accommodation, not denial of the gap (`20/001:0027–0064`). Thus the character who enabled others' return still needs their recognition to inhabit her own place. The group name integrates Minori's repeated “more” with a new plural capacity to cross the barriers that halted the seniors (`20/001:0065–0083`).

## Relationship topology and knowledge constraints

The final named group `MMJ-REL-GROUP-05` contains six differentiated dyads, detailed in P05. Minori–Haruka remains an admiration relation that has gained reciprocal aid and membership. Minori–Airi keeps rigorous instructional asymmetry while adding an ethical reversal in which Minori can stop her teacher. Minori–Shizuku combines instruction, validation, and invitations toward familiar address. Airi–Shizuku carries a preexisting history, explicit rupture, accountability, and a repaired mutually desired partnership. Haruka–Airi moves from friction into exacting professional collaboration; Haruka–Shizuku moves from professional acquaintance into received aid and chosen work. The dyads share a group without becoming interchangeable “close friends.”

Mai remains a separate, bounded relationship (`MMJ-REL-HRMAI-01`). She both inspired and wounded Haruka within a history of admiration, friendship, and shared work. At the end she knows Haruka intends to return and expresses relief; she is not shown receiving the final group name or all the private explanation about retirement. Her present injury outcome stays OPEN.

Knowledge must move through actual scenes. Minori cannot understand the professionals' causes before disclosure merely because she has watched their careers. Airi does not initially know Shizuku's full present harm. Haruka's private solo memory in episode 18 does not become everyone else's knowledge. Miku and Rin receive Minori's account and witness support, but their names and friendly confidence do not grant unlimited human knowledge. The Stage singers are not shown at the final rooftop naming; they should not automatically speak its exact contents at an immediately contemporaneous scenario boundary.

## Speech and ordinary behavior as foundations, not decorative traits

The Japanese text repeatedly connects register to relationship and pressure. Minori's awe and formal requests coexist with intensely informal internal excitement. Haruka explicitly permits peer address early; Shizuku later permits dropping senior titles after membership. Minori initially hesitates over both changes (`02/001:0036–0038`; `20/001:0086–0090`). Those are temporal permissions, not a fixed honorific table valid for every point in the story. Haruka continues to use senior-directed courtesy toward Airi at formation.

Airi's directness can discourage, teach, defend, apologize, or organize. The repeated surface force must be interpreted through its goal and interlocutor. Shizuku's gentle phrasing can preserve work relations, oppose a harmful judgment, assert that she will not return, or invite support. Haruka's polite control becomes especially clear when she refuses to transmit blame to Mai; the later explicit wish to climb the stage is significant because it displaces obligation with first-person desire. Minori's final evidence to Haruka is specific autobiographical testimony rather than only repeated slogan.

Ordinary evidence already distinguishes the four: application preparation and catchphrases; reading and privacy; stretching, photograph review and video-based practice checks; work-schedule and towel management; offered udon after disappointment; collaborative naming with mistaken English and quick correction. Stage Miku's ribbon adjustment and Rin's thanks are local practical care (`20/002:0002–0007`). These scenes prevent a purely climax-based portrait, but they remain too narrow to certify mature everyday reconstruction across family, school, strangers, leisure, and unfamiliar stakes.

## Themes, limits, and event-era questions

The main foundation supports four thematic entries: hope as renewed but uncertain action (`MMJ-TH-001`); institutional categorization and unequal visibility (`MMJ-TH-002`); reciprocal recognition with accountable repair (`MMJ-TH-003`); and collective belonging despite unequal skill and rejected credentials (`MMJ-TH-004`). The blue sea is a documented recurring local motif (`MMJ-MOTIF-001`): remembered from the audience-facing career, reencountered as spectators, viewed from a changed position, and finally approached together. Its narrative function is firmer than an exact theory of who creates every light. Neither transcript references to performances nor the music-video marker establish observed audiovisual qualities.

The foundation must resist several convenient closures: hope is not a guarantee of success; Airi is not merely a bitter person who never enjoyed variety; Shizuku is not a passive beauty with no chosen ambition; Haruka is not simply cured by a song or solely responsible for Mai's injury; Minori is not a fully trained peer merely because membership is equal. The text retains effort, difference, harm, and practical uncertainty inside its affirmative ending.

Subsequent interpretation should ask how this group obtains work, develops Minori's skill without making her belonging conditional on perfection, sustains Haruka's returned agency under actual professional pressures, accommodates Airi's desired performance identity and existing strengths, and lets Shizuku act beyond imposed appearance. Former relationships, ordinary-life breadth, and the durability of care are open evidence responsibilities. They are not predictions of specific later events.
