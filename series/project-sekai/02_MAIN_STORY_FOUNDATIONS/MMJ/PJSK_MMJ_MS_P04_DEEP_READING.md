# MORE MORE JUMP! P04 — The cost of hope, recipient testimony, and a supported return

## Scope and phase rationale

Canonical reading of every canonical record in `mmj_01_13–18`, master episodes 14–19. This longer phase deliberately preserves one continuous causal problem: why Haruka left, why apology alone is insufficient, and what changes when she can receive support. [P03](PJSK_MMJ_MS_P03_DEEP_READING.md) supplies the entering states; [phase map](PJSK_MMJ_MAIN_STORY_PHASE_MAP.md) supplies source lock, governing methods, and locator expansion. All retrospective scenes are disclosed within this block, rather than smuggled into earlier participants' knowledge. The exit is supported presence on the stage and recognition of hope delivered; final unit formation remains future.

## Causal structure: a crisis of responsibility, not simply forgotten desire

The three practice together but worry about Haruka's unexplained statement. Airi reports that speculative media articles had not persuaded her; before the outburst she had considered Haruka's stated wish to study plausible (`13/001:0002–0013`). An encounter with former ASRUN member Mai produces information that publicity could not. Mai had admired Haruka, joined the group, grown beside her, and later developed a slump in which anticipated mistakes and criticism undermined both singing and dancing (`13/003:0019–0048`).

Haruka encouraged her with hope and offered positive feedback. Mai reports that she then overpracticed, including after a trainer told her to stop, damaged her throat, and was told that recovery might take years or longer. She experienced that as the end of her idol career and accused Haruka of lying and taking away her time and idol life (`13/003:0045–0074`). This is a source-character account of injury and medical advice, not an independently established diagnosis or general statement about treatment. Nor is it a simple story in which Haruka ordered harmful practice: Mai describes the trainer's stop instruction and her own subsequent actions. The encouragement is part of the causal history; total culpability does not follow.

Mai has regretted the accusation and seeks to apologize. Airi and Shizuku encourage direct communication; Minori worries that bringing them together could worsen Haruka's pain. Shizuku invokes her own experience of the pain of misunderstanding (`14/001:0004–0016`; `14/002:0005–0009`). The ensuing rooftop meeting is partly arranged without full disclosure to Haruka—Airi later calls it like an ambush (`14/002:0036`). This practical defect must remain visible even though the helpers mean well.

Haruka apologizes to Mai, denies that Mai caused her retirement, and repeats the school-life explanation. After Mai leaves, she admits the episode caused her self-disqualification but insists she will never tell Mai because she does not want Mai held by the past (`14/002:0022–0044`). This is protective withholding, not a completed shared understanding. Haruka maintains that she has nothing to give. Minori counters with her own received hope; Haruka responds that she cannot stand on the stage even if she wants to (`14/002:0046–0055`). Persuasive testimony has not yet removed the practical barrier.

The following retrospective sequence shows Haruka initially trying to compensate by working harder for fans. Trembling knees accompany performances; responsibility expands into the demand to prevent anyone being hurt. At the stage entrance her legs will not move, while Mai's accusation returns (`15/001:0014–0055`). Her obligation to give hope becomes entangled with the fear that giving it will pressure another person. She reports consulting her agency, hospital, specialists, and books, trying repeatedly, and finally deciding to accept and relinquish the career (`15/002:0002–0010`). The prior help-seeking makes “she had simply never tried” false. The text gives no diagnostic label, no basis for judging all such help ineffective generally, and no license to regard the later performance as a universal cure.

Minori's first response is self-reproach over unknowingly discussing idol work and practicing before Haruka. She wants to lighten Haruka's distress but wonders whether intervention would violate her wish to leave (`15/004:0002–0009`). She goes to the SEKAI and tells Miku and Rin the story of hope's concrete effects on her life. They let her view their live from the stage edge. This changes her access to Haruka's treasured blue view (`16/001:0015–0065`). Miku says it may express someone's feelings; Minori infers that it may be Haruka's and wants her to see it even if she cannot return to idol work (`16/001:0075–0094`). The inference motivates an action, but its exact ontology remains uncertain.

Miku proposes that Minori perform because the wish is hers to deliver. The novice consequently becomes the initiating performer, with Airi and Shizuku later joining. The seniors identify concrete hazards: the desired view requires stage access, and a beloved old view might increase pain (`17/001:0031–0033`). Minori does not yet have a complete solution. Her commitment is to help Haruka face forward even if she remains retired, and to try again if one live does not reach her (`17/001:0046–0052`). Airi calls the plan unformed but recognizes her own analogous impulsive intervention; Shizuku links renewed hope to Airi's prior action. Their aid becomes practical: checking stage dimensions, rehearsing, correcting broad gestures for distant viewers, relaxing breathing for pitch, and reviewing the recording (`17/002:0010–0030`).

Haruka agrees to see a nearby beautiful view without first knowing it is the SEKAI (`18/001:0004–0010`). There she hears Minori frame the performance as gratitude and support. Observing Minori's effort recalls her own first solo: the fans' blue lights once gave her confidence and enabled her to deliver in return (`18/002:0031–0052`). Reciprocity is remembered, not newly invented in her history. Yet when invited onto the stage she still says she cannot; her legs shake (`18/002:0057–0062`). The live is not an instant transformation.

Minori then offers a hand and gives concrete testimony: learning to love dance, forming continuing distant friendships through concerts, joy in fan contact, and the dream of becoming an idol (`18/002:0063–0073`). Stage Miku asks what Haruka wants. Haruka explicitly says she wants to climb up and see the view; the singers help her take Minori's hand. Once there she still cannot stand steadily, so Shizuku and Airi support her arms and tell her to rely on others (`18/002:0075–0093`). Finally, looking at the view and hearing Minori's thanks, Haruka recognizes that she had delivered hope (`18/002:0094–0101`). The change is an accepted desire, assistance, and counterevidence to global self-disqualification, embodied in a supported act. Independence of support and complete symptom remission are not shown.

## Behavior and Japanese register

Haruka's private register shifts from obligation (`届けなきゃ`, `誰も傷つかないように`) and self-negating prediction to an explicit first-person want (`ステージに上がりたい`) and tentative recognition (`ちゃんと、届けられてたんだね`) (`15/001:0030–0055`; `18/002:0083,0101`). Her respectful register toward Airi and Shizuku coexists with protective firmness about what she will not tell Mai (`14/002:0040–0041`). The speech change is a meaningful behavioral shift under this particular pressure; it is not proof her formal restraint disappears everywhere.

Minori is no longer relying only on the inherited slogan. The recital of dance, friendships, joy, and aspiration uses concrete recipient testimony to contest “nothing delivered” (`18/002:0065–0073`). She still repeatedly says `がんばる` and `もっと`, now conditioned by gratitude, a chosen recipient, and explicit help. Airi's practical imperatives translate feeling into observable performance tasks; Shizuku's advice explains bodily tension, breathing, and pitch from within the narrative (`17/002:0017–0022`). Actual sound quality, prosody, choreography, camera work, and the visual realization of the blue scene are unverified: transcript descriptions and characters' evaluations are the evidence here.

## Themes, alternative readings, and revision

`MMJ-CL-001` / `MMJ-TH-001` require REVISE/STRENGTHEN: hope can renew action without guaranteeing good outcomes; taken as a duty to keep pushing or ensure nobody is hurt, it can join an injurious cycle. The story does not establish that encouragement itself is uniformly harmful, that Mai's accusation is the whole truth, or that good intention removes responsibility. It exposes how different recipients and circumstances alter its consequences.

`MMJ-CL-007`: Haruka's public retirement explanation is incomplete; the barrier includes guilt and reported bodily inhibition after prior attempts at help. `MMJ-CL-008`: supported desire and concrete evidence of benefit interrupt global self-disqualification without certifying complete recovery. `MMJ-CL-005` is STRENGTHENED as a motif of audience reciprocity; exact light ontology stays OPEN. The blue sea is first recalled, then seen from a shifted vantage, then jointly approached. Minori interprets the lights as the hope Haruka gave; Miku earlier offered a possibility, not a fully specified metaphysical rule.

The helpers' ambush-like meeting, uncertain stage plan, and partly concealed destination remain real limitations. The source also explicitly returns a decisive choice to Haruka through Miku's question and Haruka's expressed want. Neither a wholly coercive reading nor a frictionless care narrative fits all these steps. The exit restores an avenue of action through others; what that avenue can sustain in an actual unit is the next phase's question.
