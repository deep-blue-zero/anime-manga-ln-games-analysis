## Narrative and causal structure

Minori's opening account makes hope a change in what one can do tomorrow. She remembers misfortune, anticipated blame after dropping a relay baton, and an avoidance day at home. A televised Haruka performance and promise of possible better days prompt her to return to school, apologize, and try again (`00/001:0001–0041`). Neither classmates' actual anger nor supernatural bad luck is independently established. What matters causally is her interpretation of events and the new response available after hearing Haruka.

Years later, Minori applies for auditions and rehearses alone. She interrupts her own temptation to stop practicing by imagining how hard Haruka must work (`00/004:0002–0007`). Her first SEKAI encounter contains unfamiliar claims about feelings and song; after returning she treats it as a strange dream and resumes rehearsal (`00/005:0007–0033`). The spectator sees Haruka decide to quit while Minori still organizes her future around becoming like her (`00/005:0036–0043`). This is a deliberate information asymmetry, not evidence of Minori denying a fact she knows.

The fiftieth rejection precedes news of ASRUN's dissolution and Haruka's retirement. Minori's response is important: proximity to a desired idol does not simply override recognition of the person's autonomy. She reasons that Haruka is a girl before she is an idol, decides to accept her chosen path, and redirects the hope she received toward future recipients (`01/004:0015–0024`). Her later fan excitement does not erase this initial decision.

The rooftop brings together different practical needs: Minori wants rehearsal space; Haruka seeks somewhere to read away from the attention surrounding her return; Airi wants Shizuku to answer rumors about her group. The encounter is not an already formed friendship circle. Haruka directly challenges Airi's accusation of eavesdropping and attempted eviction, protecting Minori's prior use of the space (`02/001:0054–0065`). Airi then subjects Minori's ambition to industry knowledge: only three first screenings from roughly fifty applications, no second-screen success, and an ambitious agency target (`03/001:0002–0022`).

Shizuku counters Airi's dismissal with Airi's own normative identity: an idol should give hope rather than negate a girl's dream (`03/001:0024–0030`). Minori responds with a promise to persist, quoting Haruka's ideal. Stage Miku's account of learning dance from Rin supplies Minori with a practical inference: she can ask experienced performers to teach her (`03/001:0047–0066`). Shizuku accepts within her work availability; she invokes Airi's earlier obligation toward aspiring idols, and Airi agrees only until the next failed audition (`04/001:0004–0018`). Hope thus creates a request and a provisional institution of teaching, rather than replacing work with optimism.

The first lesson replaces a generic catchy introduction with observable evidence of persistence. Airi gives blunt evaluation and a usable criterion; Shizuku explains why sustained solitary work is not universal (`04/003:0013–0030`). Airi's earlier contempt and her later competence belong in the same model. The closing reminder of unanswered rumors and passersby's uncertainty about her idol status keeps the arrangement from becoming premature resolution (`04/003:0035–0048`).

## Behavior and Japanese textual register

Minori's rehearsal count, complete choreography imitation, enthusiasm for catchphrases, hurried morning, stretching, and desire to use all teaching time are ordinary evidence (`02/001:0003–0006`; `04/003:0005–0014`). Her internal language repeatedly intensifies excitement while her spoken requests hedge and stumble. `遥ちゃん` is an idol/familiar form she briefly corrects to `桐谷さん`; Haruka then explicitly removes the requirement for polite speech. This negotiated change matters more than treating an honorific as a static personality marker.

Airi uses `アンタ`, imperatives, sharp rhetorical questions, and score-based evaluation. The force depends on action: she can dismiss, demand clarity, or structure instruction with similar directness. Haruka counters her with polite but pointed sentences (`02/001:0055–0065`), while her self-introduction to school is controlled and formal (`01/004:0004–0005`). Shizuku often supplies permission and reassurance through questions and gentle sentence endings, but `その子の夢を否定しないであげて` is an unambiguous intervention (`03/001:0026`). The transcript establishes wording, not vocal pitch, comic timing, physical acting, or the actual quality of choreography.

## Themes, alternatives, and revision

`MMJ-CL-001` / `MMJ-TH-001`: hope is initially an enabling relation between a received expression and renewed action. It is not proof that effort determines outcomes. PRESERVE Minori's continuing susceptibility to disappointment; REJECT a reading in which she simply ignores Haruka's personhood to keep an idol fantasy intact. Her acceptance in episode 01 is explicit.

`MMJ-CL-002`: Airi's instruction and public classification are now a productive tension; the cause remains OPEN. `MMJ-CL-003`: Shizuku's support has agency and historical grounding, while her silence with Airi requires later evidence. Haruka's recurring rooftop presence permits an inference of continuing interest in the people or activity, but neither that motive nor a wish to return to idol work is established at this cutoff.
