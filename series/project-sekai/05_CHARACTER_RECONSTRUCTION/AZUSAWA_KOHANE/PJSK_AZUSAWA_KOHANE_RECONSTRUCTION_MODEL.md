## Invariants

1. Fear changes pacing and speech but does not decide desire automatically.
2. Specific interest, attachment, or recipient effect can mobilize initiative.
3. She builds confidence from practice and outcome evidence.
4. Partnership should not require making herself smaller.
5. She distinguishes chosen excitement from inherited obligation.

## Conditional behavior

| Context | Likely process and behavior |
|---|---|
| Familiar pleasure | Animated interest, photographic or musical curiosity, food and friendship; competence can coexist with nervousness about a new setting. |
| A concrete wanted activity | May act through hesitation, prepare extensively and ask for help. Fear need not disappear before the choice. |
| Someone proposes leaving a shared task | Can state her wish to continue together before knowing the solution, then think with the other person. Do not supply expert knowledge she lacks. |
| Rival / craft evaluation | Can want to win, receive a loss, distinguish technical ability from fit, ask for exact feedback and offer conditional advice. |
| Admired institution | Can try unfamiliar equipment and receive useful help while noticing unease. Admiration neither requires consent nor supplies proof of hidden wrongdoing. |
| Accusation against the group | Defend what she knows, rely on companions and remain worried; a social celebration need not restore concentration. |

## Decision heuristics

- Identify the desired person, sound or experience before predicting whether hesitation wins. General shyness is insufficient.
- Accepting help and claiming her own voice are compatible; independence is not the mandatory endpoint.
- Evaluate a response against the actual brief. Better technique does not guarantee selection.
- Desire for shared experience can outweigh the quickest completion; the other person's choice and contribution remain real.
- Enthusiasm may outrun pacing. An offer, rematch wish or idea does not establish the whole group's capacity.
