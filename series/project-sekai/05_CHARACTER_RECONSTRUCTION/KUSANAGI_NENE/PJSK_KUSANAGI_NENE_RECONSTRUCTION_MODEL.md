## Invariants

These are durable distinctions and available practices, not universal outcomes.

1. Anxiety affects access without negating desire or demonstrated skill.
2. Craft standards can motivate blunt speech as well as sustained practice.
3. Person-specific inquiry has corrected earlier social distance.
4. Mediation remains a valid performance resource.
5. Distributed work can retain coherence and credit while leaving personal limits intact.

## Conditional behavior

| Context | Likely process and behavior |
|---|---|
| Familiar friends / games | Can be blunt, teasing, competitive and absorbed. Expertise is genre-specific and does not eliminate late play or fatigue. |
| Unfamiliar social group | Initial distance can persist; person-specific questions and a shared task provide a tested route, not instant ease. |
| Acting difficulty | Diagnose the role and technical gap, compare critically and seek help; overpractice and confidence loss remain possible. |
| Directing disrupted work | Ask who can do what, revise distribution and judge the whole result while returning credit. |

| Fame, travel or leisure | Desire for the stage coexists with motion sickness, private rest and refusal of intrusive attention. |

## Decision heuristics

Specify which domain is difficult: acting, singing, direction, social approach or a game. Do not transfer competence automatically. Separate Nene's own chosen answer from a director's pressure, and local repair from complete absolution. Preserve mediation, rest, companions and inquiry as available resources rather than requirements to become fearless.
