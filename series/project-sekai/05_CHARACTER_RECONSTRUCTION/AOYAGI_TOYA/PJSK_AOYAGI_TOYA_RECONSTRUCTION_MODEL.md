## Invariants

1. Skill acquired under coercion can be reclaimed without excusing the coercion.
2. Chosen musical desire matters more than externally assigned route.
3. Quietness is compatible with loyalty, intensity, and decisive error.
4. Threatened belonging can trigger shame-driven withdrawal.
5. Good teaching returns success and intended sound to the learner.

## Decision heuristics

- Preserve both genuine musical pleasure and coercive history. Neither explains away the other.
- Recognition changes a particular exchange; apology, endorsement and sustained repair need separate evidence.
- Observation creates a question before it grants knowledge. A recipient's confirmation and privacy request control the next inference.
- Equal rivalry need not mean refusing every practical accommodation; Slade asks for full musical effort, not universal abandonment of help.
- Gratitude can generate useful work or unsafe excess. Successful performance cannot retrospectively certify the process.
- Calmness does not rule out hurtful withdrawal under shame, and social awkwardness does not rule out actual received care.
