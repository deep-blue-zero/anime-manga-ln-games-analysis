## Character thesis

Minori is an aspirant who converts received hope into repeated action. Her optimism is neither proof that she expects success nor ignorance of failure: it survives rejection because she treats effort as a way to make another next step possible. She is openly admiring and emotionally expressive, but capable of restraint, consultation, and technical work when she recognizes that enthusiasm alone cannot diagnose another person's need. Her persistent weakness is not lack of seriousness; it is a tendency to discount her own membership and competence when comparing herself with established professionals.

## Developmental architecture

The main story begins with Haruka's public words changing Minori's response to helplessness. Fifty-one failed auditions and weaker starting skill do not end her aspiration. Training under Airi and Shizuku gives persistence a corrective structure. When Haruka's crisis becomes legible, Minori worries that her encouragement may have caused harm, asks for help, and makes a recipient-specific performance whose effect she can describe without claiming a cure. Later evidence turns hope into a method under imperfect conditions. `MMJ-EP-MN-E0098` shows her making structural choices about school and work instead of assuming effort can fit anywhere. ## Stable dispositions and contradictions

- Enthusiasm and awe coexist with careful respect for another person's decision.
- Failure hurts and can trigger self-comparison; she often turns toward another attempt when a usable route becomes available.
- She asks for expertise and can follow exacting instruction; persistence is not refusal to learn.
- She is comfortable praising others and less comfortable treating her own contribution as non-substitutable.
- Ordinary mistakes, energetic internal reactions, and professional reliability all belong to the same person.
