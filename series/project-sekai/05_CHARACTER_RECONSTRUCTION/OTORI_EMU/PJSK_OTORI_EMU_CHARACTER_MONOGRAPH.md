# Otori Emu — provisional character monograph

## Authority snapshot

Preserve independent `WXS-EP-EMU-E0104` for shared stewardship, admitted grief and outside learning with intended return. ## Character thesis

Emu pursues shared joy through places, bodies, people and institutions. Exuberance is genuine, but she can conceal grief or conditions that others need to choose knowingly. She learns to test intended feeling against actual receipt, share stewardship and argue for a bounded institutional experiment. That learning coexists with impulsive overreach, fear and grief. The best current explanation keeps her willingness to ask, revise and return authorship alongside occasions when she acts before a recipient has agreed.

## Developmental architecture

The main story begins with a beloved stage under threat and Emu trying to protect it through relentless recruitment and positivity. She initially withholds the full institutional terms, leaving companions unable to choose the risk knowingly. When burden becomes shared, the group can act on a vision that is hers without treating her as its only labor source. Family authority persists after the first success; “saving the park” is not a permanent institutional resolution.

EVENT_0104 makes the independent troupe possible through shared stewardship: Emu can preserve the park's audience purpose while accepting organizational transition and group choice. EVENT_0124 maps the troupe's dreams onto a continuing voyage rather than fixing everyone at the park. EVENT_0131 makes her acting method explicit. She starts from an intuitive role feeling, maps it into trained bodily choices, solicits differentiated viewpoints, compares intended feeling with observed reaction, revises variables, and retests transmission successfully. The confirmed Mikazuki production validates work, not universal acting mastery.

## Stable dispositions and contradictions

- Brightness can express pleasure and conceal distress; neither explains every scene.
- She notices responses and can test interpretations, but does not read minds or know all causes.
- Place loyalty supports both preservation and changed means, including outside learning.
- Initiative can open a possibility or outrun resources and permission.
- Affection, institutional power, personal authorship and consent remain different relations.

## Relationship conditioning

Her grandfather's memory is grief and a continuing value, not a command to remain physically fixed. Brothers, Hinata, management and staff hold different kinds of affection, knowledge and power; shared stewardship does not place all decisions in Emu's hands. Ryō coauthors the institutional proposal. El authors her own plan and chooses her own confrontation; Emu's contribution cannot absorb either.

Tsukasa, Nene and Rui are recipients and critics as well as collaborators. Nene can request practical limits and privacy, Rui can identify consequences, and Tsukasa can preserve an improvised idea she cannot recall exactly. Mafuyu's changing expressions support attention, not mind-reading. The bereaved girl can choose whether to hear an account. Olivia supplies translation and transport by choice. Wonderland singers remain locally differentiated rather than universally informed.

## Current state and open limits

Emu combines performance craft, practiced institutional argument, enduring park attachment, admitted grief and spontaneous advocacy. She can ask permission and can overstep instructions. E0164 has a local implementation; El's project has permission to prepare; the Wonder! audition and larger park future remain open. R4 describes this conditional textual model, not infallible empathy, universal consent practice or managerial mastery.
