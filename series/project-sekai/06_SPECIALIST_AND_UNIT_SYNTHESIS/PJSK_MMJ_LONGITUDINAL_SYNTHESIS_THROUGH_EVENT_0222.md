## Central argument

MORE MORE JUMP!'s persistent idea of hope acquires meaning through increasingly specific decisions about how another person might receive it. The group begins because experienced performers can receive something important from an inexperienced aspirant, while she needs their expertise and recognition to inhabit her own membership. Subsequent work makes that reciprocity practical: instruction, shared promotion, managed space, negotiated production, chosen disclosure, audience research and competitive collaboration. Hope is therefore neither a possession of the most accomplished member nor a promise that the recipient will succeed.

Professional growth makes this ethic harder to apply. A production can reach more people while exposing more private life; a rival can help while still wanting to win; a strong performance can depend on overextension; a supportive group can choose unequal stage emphasis. The four do not solve these tensions by abandoning their purpose. They repeatedly distinguish the purpose from a particular method and ask what can be changed, by whose decision and at what cost. At the cutoff, this process has produced an adopted semifinal strategy, not its performance or result.
