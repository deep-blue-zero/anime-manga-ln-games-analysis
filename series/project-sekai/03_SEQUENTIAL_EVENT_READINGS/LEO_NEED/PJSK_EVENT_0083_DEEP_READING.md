## 3. Recognition creates an opportunity and a workload

Saki researches the company; Shiho plans to ask the venue manager for verification. The former occurs, the latter is not completed merely by being proposed. His suggestion of a solo concert accelerates a step Shiho thought would require more skill. The four choose to attempt it, but Shiho explicitly prevents Saki's excitement from turning interest into an actual signing offer (`PJSK:event:0083:01:003`; `PJSK:event:0083:01:004`).

Honami's research turns aspiration into a task model: roughly an hour and eight songs in her plan, with venue, equipment, promotion, reservations and ticket work, against earlier shorter sets. This is a character's practical estimate, not a universal concert rule. Shiho proposes testing the seven available songs without relying on the unfinished new composition and insists on stable, all-out performance rather than barely playing through. Ichika's simultaneous voice/guitar load matters. After four songs, Honami notices Ichika's breathlessness, Saki's timing and her own hands; she offers a break. Others offer help and acknowledge Honami's burden. Yet each already has consequential work: publicity research, reservation design, sound/lighting preparation, CD/jacket work and rehearsals. Honami's later offer to take all administration would displace her drumming and is challenged by the others. All four are caught in a shared capacity problem, although Honami's wish to compensate for perceived musical inadequacy makes over-absorption especially tempting (`PJSK:event:0083:03:001`; `PJSK:event:0083:03:002:0020–0048`).

## 5. Choosing what professional ambition may cost

The director introduces a live example: recently signed Gimme!!, whose ability and presence impress different members for specific reasons. Shiho recognizes a level she must catch up with; Honami feels the force of professional qualification. The director then argues that musical quality alone may not distinguish Leo/need commercially. Their youth, contrasted with his report of Gimme!!'s average age, could sell the story of high-school girls committing themselves to a dream. His sports analogy frames youthful striving as an audience hook. The members understand the commercial logic without thereby consenting to it (`PJSK:event:0083:06:001:0019–0113`).

Honami's objection is precise. The four began from wanting to remain together; through each other's feelings they came to want their performance to move listeners. Saki's composition, Ichika's lyrics and Shiho's playing embody that purpose. They do not want youthful effort to substitute for the music as the primary object of attention. Asked whether she would preserve that purpose even at the cost of becoming professional, Honami insists on achieving both. She does not reject all cost, promotion or professional ambition; she asserts the right to decide which costs to pay. Her belief is that an unwanted selling point would compromise their conviction and consequently their best performance. Shiho explicitly agrees and clarifies that becoming professional is a means to musical impact, not a sufficient end (`PJSK:event:0083:07:001:0004–0044`).

The director's private response preserves friction. He still thinks every competitive resource is useful in a difficult market, while acknowledging that using one can destroy something valuable. He revises his initial quiet/weak impression of Honami and sees a potential band anchor. His call to his boss about Leo/need is actual, but its outcome is absent; a possible future formal offer remains prospective. The band does not hear this entire private reassessment. Nor does his respect prove the market argument objectively resolved (`PJSK:event:0083:07:001:0047–0058`).

Afterward Honami shakes, fears she endangered the opportunity and apologizes. Saki offers her hand; all three affirm support, with Shiho explicitly denying that her agreement was merely protective politeness. Courage here includes delayed fear and a need for reassurance. Honami has kept the band's shared purpose available while others were tempted to accept an unwanted compromise. Improved scheduling then permits renewed intensive practice; Shiho explicitly wants to compensate for Honami's reduced drum time. A later all-out set is actually completed and Miku approves, after which refinement remains necessary. Honami's tentative thought that she may have helped them move forward is a situated correction to self-minimization, not proof of permanent confidence (`PJSK:event:0083:08:001`; `PJSK:event:0083:08:002`).
