## 2. The debut commission asks for difference rather than repetition

Solis confirms Leo/need's JAM festival booking and commissions the debut song. Shindo asks Ichika and Saki to compose together because Saki's warm, companionable writing and Ichika's direct but developing work might produce music beyond the band's existing pattern. The request is commercially consequential: an outside partner will fund large advertising if the result succeeds. `PJSK:event:0128:01:001:0048–0073`; `PJSK:event:0128:02:001:0009–0049`.

The commission does not declare either creator complete. It deliberately tests whether two different expressive systems can make one public first impression.

## 3. Exchange without receipt creates two incompatible songs

Ichika and Saki exchange chord progressions and separately write melodies. Each sincerely tries to include the other's stated aim: Ichika wants connection remembered directly; Saki wants encouragement that stays beside pain before moving forward. Their results fail because each translates the other's words through her own preferred affect and then revises toward instructions she still does not understand. `PJSK:event:0128:04:002:0011–0030`; `PJSK:event:0128:05:001:0015–0061`; `PJSK:event:0128:05:003:0002–0068`.

The conflict is substantive rather than a simple communication mistake. Both have creator commitments, and neither can make the work honest by mechanically yielding. Completion and even friendship feel threatened, but the event rejects the premise that difference itself was failure.

## 4. School singers model interpretive help without claiming authorship

School MEIKO interrupts the spiral with rest and a bounded confidence in their history of repair. In the SEKAI, Miku, Luka, and MEIKO receive both unfinished pieces as meaningful, ask what each creator is trying to express, and help Saki trace her melody through the band's separation and renewed forward movement. Ichika recognizes the missing act: the singers do not overwrite a person's intention; they receive it, ask into it, and help it become sound. `PJSK:event:0128:06:001:0001–0030`; `PJSK:event:0128:06:002:0015–0068`.

Preserve `LN-REL-SCHOOL-VS-MS` at very strong I2. MEIKO explicitly limits the singers' role to helping another person's feelings become music. Their causal contribution is substantial, but the human creators author the song and no cross-manifestation or global singer identity follows. `PJSK:event:0128:07:001:0051–0071`.

## 5. `LN-EP-ICHIKA-E0128` — connection becomes arrangement practice

Ichika identifies her own unacknowledged priority, apologizes, and chooses a method suited to their present capacity: Saki retains primary expressive direction while Ichika receives, questions, arranges, and carries that intention toward listeners. This is not absence of Ichika's feeling; the wish to deliver Saki's feeling becomes her authored contribution. `PJSK:event:0128:07:001:0005–0049`.

Advance `LN-EP-ICHIKA-E0101 → LN-EP-ICHIKA-E0128`. Recursive connection purpose becomes an executed composition rule: find the intended recipient and feeling, make the creator's distinctions audible, and contribute structure without replacing the source. The result does not prove universal mediation skill or equal coauthorship in every future collaboration.

## 6. `LN-REL-ICHIKA-SAKI-E0128` — repaired asymmetry with a future promise

The completed song uses Saki's central emotional intention and Ichika's arrangement, structure, and delivery choices. Saki explicitly names the asymmetry and asks that they later make a work in which both aims truly collide; Ichika accepts. `PJSK:event:0128:07:001:0007–0038`; `PJSK:event:0128:08:001:0002–0049`; `PJSK:event:0128:08:002:0031–0036`.
