## 4. An audience and a medium change what performance needs

Honami's preparation is concrete: several phone tripods, multiple angles, a laptop and software practice. The group permits use of its phones and acknowledges the labor. Repeated technically acceptable takes lack the live momentum the band wants. With visible recipients, Ichika can direct gratitude toward people usually concealed near stage equipment. The singers feel addressed and return thanks; the band then checks the recording and finds the desired result (`PJSK:event:0076:05:003:0054–0092`; `PJSK:event:0076:06:001:0002–0031`). This is causal audience orientation, not a claim that intensity alone replaces skill or that camera work is irrelevant.

Editing creates a second real choice. Shiho prefers original brightness because darkening might interrupt concentration on the music; Saki prefers darkness because it clarifies sadness. Ichika accepts the force of both reasons, then argues for darkness: the interruption can be an expressive realization available specifically to video. She gives Honami final decision responsibility. Honami selects that direction because visual expression can supplement what a recording cannot carry exactly as a live performance. Shiho reconsiders the intended audience, including people who are not already deeply invested in music, and agrees (`PJSK:event:0076:06:001:0076–0102`). This is deliberation with competence-sensitive authority, not majority victory or suppression of Shiho's standards.

The compositional choice and editing choice are related but not identical. Ichika later leaves one musical passage unadorned because an unrepeatable phrase should carry her friends' unadorned smiles. Thus the event does not endorse either effects or naturalness as a universal rule. The represented aim determines the use of technique (`PJSK:event:0076:07:001:0002–0008`).
