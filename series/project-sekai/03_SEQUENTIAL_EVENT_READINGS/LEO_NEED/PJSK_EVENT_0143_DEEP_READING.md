## 2. A profitable default becomes an authorship conflict

The proposal is materially attractive. Leo/need's debut sells twice the estimate; Miku's version reaches roughly eight million views and directs listeners toward the band. The company therefore asks for both Leo/need and Virtual Singer versions of every future release, and a Shuwa-Deli Cider tie-in arrives on that condition. Ichika does not dispute those benefits or the tie-in's fit. Her objection begins as an unresolved discomfort with turning one successful arrangement into an automatic rule. `PJSK:event:0143:01:001:0010–0037`; `PJSK:event:0143:01:001:0043–0064`.

Shindo separates two professional responsibilities. As producer, he names reach, sales, and preserved musical identity; as manager, he refuses to let the band proceed while Ichika cannot explain the conflict. He distinguishes whim from conviction, says company preference is not compulsory obedience, and gives a deadline for a considered answer. This is neither managerial surrender nor coercion: the commercial argument remains real while authorship requires an owned decision. `PJSK:event:0143:01:001:0065–0088`.

## 3. Origin memory becomes evidence for present choice

Ichika reconstructs how an accidental Miku video exposed her to many creator-shaped voices, styles, images, and songs carried by the same public character. Admiration becomes action: she asks to learn guitar, recruits Saki, Honami, and Shiho, and helps form their first uneven but joyful ensemble. Creator pages, another child's recommendation, a second video site, and searchable tags expand both her musical world and peer contact. `PJSK:event:0143:03:004:0002–0016`; `PJSK:event:0143:04:003:0013–0036`; `PJSK:event:0143:04:005:0002–0010`; `PJSK:event:0143:05:002:0013–0019`; `PJSK:event:0143:05:004:0002–0027`.

Miku-mediated music also remains available during Saki's hospitalization and the four friends' separation. A remembered song does not provide factual assurance, but Ichika hears it as encouragement, renews her hope that the four can reunite, and chooses continued support for Saki. The past therefore functions as a causal history for why publication in Miku's voice matters to her; it is not nostalgia substituted for a decision. `PJSK:event:0143:05:006:0002–0026`.

## 4. Ichika derives a song-specific rule rather than a universal ethic

Ichika distinguishes childhood public/software Miku from the School SEKAI person she now knows as friend and mentor. Both histories matter, but they do not become one shared memory or identity. Saki's comparison helps Ichika articulate the operative issue: because the relation matters, she wants to publish a Virtual Singer version only when the band genuinely wants Miku to sing that song. Ichika expressly recognizes other legitimate creator practices, including frequent use or voice-specific selection. `PJSK:event:0143:06:001:0002–0045`.

The band accepts that this is Ichika's conviction and supports rejecting the all-song mandate. Ichika does not immediately convert that answer into acceptance of the tie-in; she keeps the specific commission open because the finished direction might not support the same sincere invitation. That separation—general rule first, particular work second—is what makes the method repeatable. `PJSK:event:0143:06:001:0047–0069`; `PJSK:event:0143:07:001:0012–0028`.

Ichika then accepts the specific tie-in because its shared-moment theme can truthfully carry both Leo/need's time together and her time with Miku. `PJSK:event:0143:08:003:0002–0028`.
