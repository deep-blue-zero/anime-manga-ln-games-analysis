## A successful company acquires something to fear losing

The opening matters because it shows a working ordinary life before the crisis. Rui chooses equipment and stages a fictional black knight; the humans receive Wonderland audience responses and meet Len, who missed the show while attending to puppet trouble. Tsukasa acknowledges wire fear but connects his effort to audience smiles; Nene limits repeated performance rather than being indefinitely available. Rui later describes the pleasure of freely making shows, and Nene recognizes his happier appearance. Belonging is already experienced here. The accident does not make Rui care for the first time (`PJSK:event:0003:01:001:0002–0004`; `PJSK:event:0003:01:002:0002–0040`; `PJSK:event:0003:01:003:0002–0020`; `PJSK:event:0003:01:004:0005–0021`).

The contest gives existing aims a common production task without making them identical. Tsukasa anticipates recognition and notices the opportunity for Nene's acting aspiration and Rui's resources. Nene assents conditionally on Emu being comfortable. Emu reports that her grandfather opposed ranking different shows; Rui hypothesizes a policy change, and she chooses the event in terms of additional smiles. That choice does not prove that she has rejected the grandfather's principle. The promised television role is conditional on winning a three-show audience-vote contest. Their ghost-park adaptation is a fictional production whose final invitation includes the dead; it may resonate with the troupe's concern for participation, but it is not literal biography or evidence of supernatural events in their park (`PJSK:event:0003:02:002:0003–0036`; `PJSK:event:0003:02:003:0003–0029`).

The under-stage pulling effect first appears practicable. Tsukasa accepts it, Rui describes mats and a safety device, and a rehearsal receives approval. The accident happens afterward: a still-moving zombie robot catches Tsukasa's clothes, unbalances him and pulls him into a fall. His temporary unresponsiveness prompts urgent concern; he wakes, Rui identifies malfunction and accepts having assumed the device sufficient, and Tsukasa reassures him while requesting more interesting work. This separates artistic willingness, precautions, apparent test success and actual equipment behavior. None entails the others (`PJSK:event:0003:03:001:0002–0040`; `PJSK:event:0003:03:001:0042–0067`).

Tsukasa later reports that a hospital found no particular problem; no clinician speaks directly. Rui becomes distracted and abandons a proposed wire sequence after briefly elaborating it with evident imaginative momentum. His explanations invoke tempo and visibility, and he replaces physical pulling with mime. The problem is not that mime is inherently artistically inferior. It is the discrepancy between his expressed preference, earlier absorption and present withdrawal. Nene's objection to Tsukasa's ten-zombie proposal independently preserves a real danger judgment; the story's concern about Rui's inhibition does not make her caution mistaken (`PJSK:event:0003:04:001:0004–0014`; `PJSK:event:0003:04:001:0023–0044`; `PJSK:event:0003:04:001:0048–0080`).

## Confrontation diagnoses too much; help-seeking makes the question answerable

Tsukasa correctly notices that something changed after the accident, then overstates what he can know when he accuses Rui of lying. Rui's anger and subsequent inability to identify the problem support limited self-understanding rather than verified deliberate deception. Emu and Nene interrupt, and Tsukasa apologizes for shouting before leaving to cool down. Compared with the main-story rupture, disagreement does not become a final rejection of the company: an apology, a pause and independent assistance interrupt escalation. Yet the accusation itself remains harmful conduct, even when motivated by wanting Rui's best work (`PJSK:event:0003:05:001:0002–0035`).

Nene then supplies something the argument could not. She describes diminished enjoyment, Rui asks for her view and accepts that their observations may expose something he cannot recognize. She privately fears his renewed isolation, doubts her ability to help and nevertheless proposes consulting the singers. Rui agrees because they still want a good show together. This is additional evidence for the agency already present when Nene challenged his withdrawal in main episode17; the new situation lets her accompany Rui's vulnerability instead of only defending her own return (`PJSK:event:0003:05:001:0038–0077`; `PJSK:main:wonder_01_17:01:001:0037–0057`).

KAITO's questions bring the contradiction into Rui's own account. Rui reports childhood ideas and repeated rejection, including peers unwilling to jump from a wall even with a trampoline below. Those peers' refusals do not establish irrationality or bad faith. Nene's private middle-school memory contextualizes her regret; her spoken reassurance instead stresses Rui's care for performers. Her claim that trusting him made dangerous-looking staging safe remains her judgment. It is not an engineering certificate (`PJSK:event:0003:06:001:0005–0050`).

Rui says shows with these receptive actors matter most to him. KAITO asks whether that very place has become precious. Rui tentatively recognizes that Tsukasa might leave like earlier companions, and that he does not want to lose the place where he can work fully. The transition is precise: from being unable to explain restraint to recognizing a fear attached to an already valued company. It occurs for Rui, rather than only supplying the reader with a new interpretation; Nene and KAITO hear his recognition. Rui's immediate verdict that this disqualifies him as director is his self-condemnation. KAITO instead treats wanting companions and not wanting them hurt as compatible with making a show, and proposes carrying those feelings through shared work. Rui receives that alternative to disqualification and proceeds toward renewed direction. Nene supports that prospect; predictions of Tsukasa's acceptance are still predictions at this moment (`PJSK:event:0003:05:001:0046–0049`; `PJSK:event:0003:06:001:0051–0056`; `PJSK:event:0003:06:001:0075–0099`).

The separate Emu–Tsukasa exchange exposes the complementary pressure. Emu likes how Rui's work creates excitement and smiles, connecting it to her grandfather's shows. Tsukasa shares the appreciation, but is also angry at himself: by getting hurt, he believes he has made the director hold back. His image of a true star as someone who inspires unlimited direction redirects established ambition toward a collaborator while risking self-erasure. It does not establish objective responsibility for the malfunction or mean his body must answer every proposal. Emu receives this account; Rui, Nene and KAITO are not shown hearing it. Tsukasa resolves to explain himself better the next day (`PJSK:event:0003:06:001:0102–0122`).
