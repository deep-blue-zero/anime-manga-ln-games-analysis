## 3. Growth makes a future separation feel near

Tsukasa follows through on EVENT_0081's intended consultation: he asks Emu's brothers about renewed promotional or other outside performances as a way to grow and improve the home show. They recognize his aim but explain why the park cannot presently provide it. Promotion has already increased attendance; existing visitors expect the troupe at home, while outside appearances require a business purpose and scheduling expenditure. Tsukasa accepts the force of those obligations. The brothers distinguish personal sympathy from managerial decisions and warn that an eventual choice may be necessary; after he leaves they consider a mutually beneficial response (`PJSK:event:0082:01:001`). Neither a permanent ban on growth nor a covert dismissal decision is established.

The workshop is a concrete alternative, reached through searching and Asahi's recommendation. Tsukasa asks the group to support about a month of additional study and promises to maintain existing duties. Nene/Rui's concern about stamina and the next performance is not opposition to his ambition. The early chapters show continued home rehearsal and useful shared techniques, not an inevitable collapse from overwork (`PJSK:event:0082:01:003`; `PJSK:event:0082:02`). His exuberance does not eliminate ordinary coordination or the need to ask about limits.

Emu's support is initially sincere. Unease appears when outside learning becomes frequent, successful and attractive. A declined food outing makes absence tangible; a habitual assumption that Tsukasa will help eat Rui's vegetables briefly places him at a table he has not joined. Nene's interest in learning outside and Rui's word for loneliness help Emu name her feeling. The melting star-shaped dessert gives the scene a local image of precious time passing while her attention is elsewhere (`PJSK:event:0082:03:002`). This is analytical motif interpretation, not a new N25 motif claim.

Her problem is not that she suddenly wants her friends to fail. She feels that their growth makes parting closer, then judges herself for having difficulty cheering them on. Privately, the upcoming performance threatens to demonstrate exactly what frightens her; publicly, she continues to sound enthusiastic (`PJSK:event:0082:04`; `PJSK:event:0082:05`). Card 0571:01 makes the distinction concrete: seeing happy visitors does not initially supply its usual encouragement, but direct contact with children who love the show produces a bounded lift and renewed practice motivation (`PJSK:card:0571:01:001:0025–0064`). Cheerfulness is neither absent nor a reliable index of a resolved conflict.

## 4. Support creates conditions for a choice

Nene and Rui compare observations, treating opposition to Tsukasa as a hypothesis rather than a fact. Rui thinks sincere joy can coexist with the newly vivid prospect of separation. His interpretation is perceptive but is not yet Emu's disclosure. The Wonderland singers likewise do not claim they can settle her feelings for her: KAITO distinguishes her own organizing work from help restoring a mood in which other thoughts become possible. Len proposes doing something she loves; Rui recognizes the potential in revisiting her own park, while Nene initially doubts whether such a familiar place can help (`PJSK:event:0082:04`; `PJSK:event:0082:05`).

The outing combines pleasure with context. Rides affect participants differently, and fatigue brings rest and drinks. A popcorn route passes through the busy park, making the troupe's earlier contribution visible in visitors' enjoyment. Real-world food cannot simply be eaten by the remote singers: buying it to bring into SEKAI is proposed separately (`PJSK:event:0082:06:002:0066`). Rui's planning is a means of attention and companionship; it is not presented as guaranteed emotional treatment.

At the saved fish coaster, Emu connects the present families' pleasure with what her friends helped protect. She recognizes that her dream is getting closer, then chooses to return the support they gave her. She explicitly says loneliness about parting will probably never disappear, while still wanting to encourage their dreams (`PJSK:event:0082:07:001:0009–0060`). The causal turn is a changed way to act with conflicting feelings, not disappearance of attachment, completed achievement of her own dream or a settled departure plan.

Nene thanks her for telling them and acknowledges having felt lonely too. Rui also admits imagining separate futures and wanting to support everyone despite sadness. His brief remark about looking for coexistence is audible, but he withdraws it when Nene asks: the others do not receive a worked-out strategy (`PJSK:event:0082:07:001:0061–0074`). Emu subsequently learns that concern motivated the outing and thanks Len (`PJSK:event:0082:07:001:0078–0083`). Tsukasa is absent from this disclosure; next-day cheerful feedback does not automatically transfer it to him.

The performance then tests her chosen orientation. Tsukasa plays Peter Pan in a story Emu loves and had wanted to stage. Rui identifies a specific embodied choice—springing footwork even during speech—as making the flying character convincing; Nene and Emu respond to its effect. Emu privately affirms her love of his acting and hopes he can realize the wider dream of making people throughout the world smile, while her own dream remains rooted in Phoenix Wonderland (`PJSK:event:0082:08:001:0025–0065`). These are her recognition and hopes, not proof of inevitable worldwide success or an instruction she has delivered to Tsukasa.

The next day, Tsukasa both wants to keep attending outside learning and begins to acknowledge the pleasure of acting with these four. A remembered managerial warning interrupts him; he turns to the next home performance. All four want to improve it. The brothers' joint Wonder Stage/Phoenix Stage proposal creates a new possible opportunity within the park (`PJSK:event:0082:08:003:0002–0039`). Card 0571:02 distinguishes this from a prior night-show collaboration and a competition: the group is excited, but details remain awaited. The joint performance has not happened (`PJSK:card:0571:02:001:0014–0026`).

## 5. The cards qualify learning and helping

### 5.1 Tsukasa: expressiveness needs a role's life behind it

Asahi's introduction opens access to experienced peers who recognize Tsukasa's previous work yet welcome him as another learner. His formal thanks shifts to familiar exchange as they explicitly loosen status distinctions for this setting (`PJSK:card:0574:01:003:0004–0023`). That welcome is compatible with demanding evaluation. The director asks all participants to communicate their actorly individuality through the same role and lines; Tsukasa interprets the naming challenge as a need to earn recognition (`PJSK:card:0574:01:003:0033–0054`). The director remains unnamed in this witness.

Card 0574:02 prevents a falsely smooth account of improvement. Tsukasa's request to show multiple versions and his account of his school reputation amuse the director, who allows two versions. The initial actual acting is nevertheless sharply criticized for lacking the role's background and life. Tsukasa realizes that frequently performing his own scripts had supplied that understanding implicitly; it did not automatically transfer to unfamiliar roles. He reports gradually recovering and winning the Peter Pan role (`PJSK:card:0574:02:001:0035–0067`). His surface confidence and willingness to ask are resources for learning, not proof that he began with every required skill.

Continued correction distinguishes the character's defiant words from inner despair at being unable to win. Tsukasa accepts it and asks peers to practice during their break (`PJSK:card:0574:02:001:0012–0023`; `PJSK:card:0574:02:001:0070–0072`). The nickname Pegasus is observable; his idea that it penalizes slow progress and peers' idea that it signals favor remain rival interpretations (`PJSK:card:0574:02:001:0024–0031`). The core's shoeshiner simulation similarly supplies an interpreted experience of invisibility and loneliness, which he uses to deepen the hero's fear and trust alongside resolve (`PJSK:event:0082:02:003`). Neither imitation of a circumstance nor one successful part yields exhaustive knowledge of a life.

### 5.2 Nene: receiving help can coexist with doing one's own work

Nene's private response includes admiration, competitiveness and concern about what her current confidence depends on. She recalls the pleasure of childhood theatre learning and an unfinished, uneasy memory of becoming a lead; she can now stand onstage with the group, yet fears a setting of strangers. She actually rehearses a conventional self-introduction and searches online, without reaching a convincing image of success, then retrieves old lesson notes (`PJSK:card:0575:01:001:0014–0055`). This is active but limited preparation, not refusal to learn or resolved stranger anxiety.

The second half confirms completed review and her perception that she can reach higher notes, alongside frustration that Tsukasa has advanced more. She wants to hear peers' professional experiences but distinguishes freely shared workshop teaching from contacts and conversations he earned himself. Her hesitation is partly a fairness concern, not only shyness (`PJSK:card:0575:02:001:0002–0020`; `PJSK:card:0575:02:003:0002–0006`). Rui names their different social strengths, acknowledges her efforts and permits reliance while improvement remains possible. His teasing costs her a moment of embarrassment; it does not erase the practical support (`PJSK:card:0575:02:003:0008–0018`).

Nene asks Tsukasa directly, specifying only what he can share. He agrees. When the bell postpones the talk, she suggests waiting until Emu can join; he accepts (`PJSK:card:0575:02:003:0021–0042`). The request and permission occur, but that later full discussion is not shown. There is no completed workshop enrollment or demonstrated permanent independence from familiar support.

### 5.3 Rui: the helpful observer also needs support

Rui privately wants a path that preserves both collective presence and everyone's dreams, but fears the time limit is nearer than expected. His feelings disrupt work on directing a scene, prompting him to seek company (`PJSK:card:0572:01:001:0014–0031`). With MEIKO and Luka he describes emotional interference without disclosing all its causes, briefly wishing someone else could control it, then rejecting abandonment of his own responsibility. MEIKO declines to press the private matter and offers shared thought and concern without claiming to substitute for him. He reports feeling calmer (`PJSK:card:0572:01:002:0011–0038`).

Luka and MEIKO report that KAITO also finds waiting difficult when he wants to relieve others' suffering, and that MEIKO sometimes rebukes his worrying because it affects Rin and Len (`PJSK:card:0572:01:002:0026–0034`). This is attributed testimony, not omniscience or an Empty-KAITO model. Card 0572:02 then supplies an explicit causal link: Rui credits their earlier invitation to rely on them for making his later consultation about Emu feel natural. MEIKO confirms that he also helped with KAITO's question; details of that directing solution remain unshown (`PJSK:card:0572:02:001:0040–0054`). Support circulates through people whose own needs remain active.

### 5.4 Len and the singers: a successful rescue does not erase disappointment

During a Wonderland play, Rin loses her wand. MEIKO improvises an in-role cause, Miku recruits audience encouragement, Rin adapts her own lines, Len and KAITO continue the climax, and Luka's late return with the prop becomes comedy. The show succeeds, yet Rin remains unhappy about failing a moment she had practiced seriously. The others recognize both the need for reflection and the group's successful response (`PJSK:card:0573:01:001:0013–0059`). Fictional magical power belongs to the play; collaborative improvisation is the manifestation behavior evidenced.

Len initially considers following her despite advice to wait, worrying that encouragement might make things worse. He discovers the other singers also watching anxiously. A repeat performance is considered, but Miku raises the possibility of added pressure. Len explicitly remembers the approach to Emu and proposes something Rin loves (`PJSK:card:0573:02:001:0009–0046`). Their playful human train sets off toward attractions and Rin laughs (`PJSK:card:0573:02:001:0049–0075`). That laugh is the observed outcome. The complete attraction circuit, lasting relief and successful retry are not established.
