## 4. A different question for the director

Rui asks the organizers whether he can seek advice before completing a script. Tom agrees, reads the drafts and listens to the problem. He does not dismiss Rui's technique, effort or audience ideal. Instead, he asks whether Rui suppresses his own inner experience while concentrating on the emotion he wants to give a recipient. Rui's assertion that he can be stubborn is not the answer: insisting on a desired audience effect differs from allowing one's own raw feeling into the work. Tom also cautions that excessive self-display can become self-indulgent. This is advice for Rui's particular creative limit, not a universal demand for autobiographical art or proof that suffering is required for depth. `PJSK:event:0172:06:002:0002–0065`.

Rui recalls isolation, the pleasure of making shows and companions, and fear of separation. These memories activate established history, including EVENT_0144's loneliness integration; they do not rewrite that history or make his friends ignorant of every prior difficulty. Asked directly about *グッバイ・ゴースト*, Tom says that his lost parents and friends are present in it and that life can continue through such expression. That is a personal account of creative origin, not a one-to-one key for all characters in the play. Rui recognizes that he has drawn on experience before but applied a brake to unprocessed feeling because it might repel an audience. He now chooses to expose more of that feeling while retaining his wish to reach others. `PJSK:event:0172:06:002:0070–0110`.

The later writing does not erase his practical vulnerability. He works through the night until almost noon, has barely eaten and accepts his friends' visit. Nene and Emu choose accessible food because they expect that elaborate preparation would fail; they include pleasure without confusing candy with adequate nutrition. Tsukasa brings rest aids and later insists on sharing lunch when Rui proposes only a drink. Rui accepts care, but a stable healthy routine is not demonstrated. `PJSK:card:1196:01:001:0002–PJSK:card:1196:01:002:0043`; `PJSK:card:1198:01:001:0002–0058`; `PJSK:card:1197:02:002:0035–0044`.

## 5. The performed trial and its unfinished balance

The final presentation occurs at THE CENTER THEATRE before workshop participants and teachers. It is not a booked public Arcland production. Rui thanks the other three for rehearsing alongside their acting course. They recognize an unusual show while retaining concern for the familiar audience aim. In the play, Tsukasa's monster enjoys frightening people, disguises itself as human and meets Emu's bard. Musical apprenticeship initially serves self-interest, then makes other people's pleasure meaningful. Nene's hunter confronts the danger that this change has not wholly removed. The rehearsal discussions and peer comments attribute the monster's awakening to restrained timing, pauses and light focus. They show deliberate collaborative craft in the text; the analyst has not heard Tsukasa's delivery or measured its audience effect. `PJSK:event:0172:07:002:0035–0119`.

The monster's admission of cruel pleasure is paired with despair that it did not choose its nature. Rui asks for a relatively flat delivery because the line concerns that dead end, not simply an emotional outburst. When the bard still claims it as a pupil, the monster wants to stay with people despite not being wholly changed. It offers violent guarantees and accepts the hunter's shot; the hunter then treats its wound and remains to watch its conduct. The ending leaves future coexistence uncertain while removing complete solitude. This bargain is staged fiction. It does not establish that actual Rui regards himself as a dangerous being, wants injury, must purchase friendship through sacrifice, or teaches that violence is a proper admission price for belonging. The play's unresolved form lets him express the difficulty of connection without supplying a complete social solution. `PJSK:event:0172:08:001:0009–0097`.

The instructors value the finer psychology but divide over the result. Sakaki finds its theme interesting and its treatment insufficiently worked through; Tom appreciates its earnest heat while accepting the imbalance. Both locate a problem in fitting this raw work to Arcland's audience. Rui acknowledges that the brief is not fully met and treats the trial as something to develop, not a victory that cancels the criticism. Tom counsels patience with a lifelong task. Tsukasa and Nene see Rui's satisfied expression but cannot hear the earlier private exchange among critics. The final Sakaki–Ohara memory gives the reader a prior professional connection; Rui is not shown learning it. Preserve `WXS-REL-RUI-OHARA-E0119` rather than making this hidden recommendation a new direct exchange. `PJSK:event:0172:08:001:0101–0148`.

Rui later tells Wonderland Luka that the work divided its recipients and did not fit all its conditions, yet he is glad he attempted it.
