## 4. Akito's causal model of RAD WEEKEND


The event's strongest contribution is Akito's attempt to explain **why** RAD WEEKEND became legendary.


He begins by rejecting a simple skill explanation. Elite performers exist elsewhere, and he has attended events with musicians comparable in technical quality to the RAD WEEKEND participants. Yet those events did not generate the same experience.


His first hypothesis is relational and dynamic:


`each team collides with the others -> competition raises everyone -> the whole room enters a mutually escalating atmosphere`.


He calls the missing factor a kind of `高めあう空気` / synergistic atmosphere.


That distinction matters for reconstruction. Akito is not merely a grind-focused character who sees every deficit as “practice more.” When raw skill fails to explain an observed phenomenon, he constructs a model about interaction effects.


## 5. Turning the hypothesis into an experiment


VBS decides to host an event rather than merely attend more shows. The design has an explicit investigative function:


- recruit performers who genuinely want to surpass RAD WEEKEND;
- choose people strong enough to create pressure on one another;
- approximate the number/order of teams;
- run full-order rehearsals, unusually early for an event;
- observe whether the desired “heat” emerges;
- revise based on the result.


This is unusually diagnostic Akito material because it shows **experimental cognition inside artistic practice**.


He is willing to test his idea in public-facing work rather than preserve it as a romantic belief.


## 6. Recruitment: seriousness is more important than prestige alone


The event repeatedly tests what qualifies someone for the experiment.


Arata is an obvious recruit because of his extraordinary ability and explicit RAD WEEKEND goal.


EVER becomes important because losing to VBS at STAY GOLD changed their orientation: their leader says VBS showed them the strength of pursuing an almost impossible goal. This is one of the first clear demonstrations in this tranche that VBS is not only inheriting inspiration from RADder; it is already **producing new aspirants in the local scene**.


Kotaro is the hardest case. He previously helped ruin an event and admits that his earlier music-making was casual. By EVENT_0059, however, he has been training seriously, entering events, and now says he wants to surpass RAD WEEKEND partly so that he can become someone he can respect.


Akito does not accept him sentimentally. He explicitly warns that the event level will be high and tests whether the commitment is real. Once convinced, he allows Kotaro into the experiment.


The decision rule is therefore approximately:


`current imperfect skill + demonstrable serious commitment + willingness to be tested` can be more valuable than static status.


## 7. The first rehearsal falsifies Akito's initial theory


The rehearsal is analytically important because **everyone performs well and the hypothesis still fails**.


EVER provides a strong opening. Kotaro surprises the group with much improved performance and crowd-handling instincts. Arata is overwhelming. VBS gives a strong closing performance.


Yet the desired RAD WEEKEND atmosphere does not emerge.


This creates a useful negative result:


`strong performers + correct order + shared goal + competitive intent != sufficient condition for RAD WEEKEND heat`.


Akito does not discard the entire model immediately. He distinguishes between an approach that may still be directionally correct and missing conditions that have not yet been understood.


That is much more diagnostically valuable than a simple “we failed, train harder” response.


## 8. KAITO's pancake analogy and model revision


EVENT_0059:06 gives the revision route.


Akito is frustrated both by the failed atmosphere and by Arata's increasingly distracted presentation. He reads Arata as highly capable but opaque: serious in practice, yet sometimes looking elsewhere mentally.


KAITO first corrects Akito's interpersonal model. Akito had just misread KAITO's disappearance as selfishly going to eat ice cream; KAITO was actually preparing pancakes because Akito looked mentally exhausted. KAITO suggests that Akito may likewise be misreading Arata.


The pancake analogy then broadens:


**good ingredients are not enough; how they combine matters.**


Akito and the others infer that the performers may not know each other well enough for the desired synergy to occur. The intervention is not simply “rehearse harder” but “learn each other's reasons, histories, and stakes.”


This is a major expansion of Akito's performance logic:


`competitive pressure` is retained, but `interpersonal understanding / shared stake legibility` becomes another necessary condition.


## 9. Arata and Soma: the hidden relationship beneath performance opacity


The hospital visit to Soma explains Arata's apparent distraction.


Soma is Arata's close friend and former musical partner. An accident left Soma physically unable to pursue the same path. Arata's current pursuit of RAD WEEKEND is therefore not a purely individual ambition.


Akito notices that Arata has been talking about VBS frequently to Soma, and later sees that Arata's emotional investment includes carrying Soma's unrealized wish.


This reframes Arata without romanticizing burden:


- he is independently ambitious;
- he is also performing with another person's interrupted future in mind;
- his mental absence is not lack of seriousness;
- it reflects unresolved concern and a parallel question about what “seeing the city” means.


The event thereby teaches Akito that another performer's visible intensity cannot be understood from technique or competitive posture alone.


## 10. “Look at the city” as an open epistemic problem


Taiga's instruction to Arata—`RAD WEEKENDを超える気なら、この街を見ろ`—remains unresolved.


Arata has interpreted it literally and behaviorally:


- attended events;
- met musicians;
- walked the streets;
- learned alleys;
- observed the scene.


He still does not understand the instruction.


Akito does not solve it for him. Instead, EVENT_0059 expands the evidence around the phrase:


- local performers motivate one another;
- legends survive in shared stories;
- VBS influences EVER and Kotaro;
- Soma's memory of RAD WEEKEND persists despite being physically removed from the scene;
- ordinary customers and venue relationships respond to events;
- Vivid Street is a network of people, not merely a physical location.


The correct current state is therefore **OPEN**, not “the answer is community” as a prematurely closed thesis.


## 11. The live event: partial success and a better model


The final event does not recreate RAD WEEKEND. That restraint is important.


However, something qualitatively different happens compared with rehearsal.


EVER opens hard and establishes trust with the audience. Kotaro receives the handoff and consciously shapes his MC/performance to pass momentum onward. Arata receives a room already elevated by the previous acts and pushes it higher. VBS then takes responsibility for burning through the accumulated heat as the closer.


The later Akito card clarifies the insight: what matters is not four isolated good performances in sequence. Each act must **receive, transform, and transmit** what the preceding act created.


This suggests a stronger causal model:


`shared goal + credible mutual challenge + interpersonal knowledge + audience continuity + explicit handoff responsibility -> emergent escalating heat`.


Akito still knows this is not enough to surpass RAD WEEKEND. But he concludes they have genuinely taken one step.


That is the right scale of success.


## 12. Akito reconstruction consequences

- forms causal hypotheses from repeated performance observation;
- tests hypotheses rather than protecting them from falsification;
- becomes frustrated by ambiguity but continues searching for mechanisms;
- evaluates recruits by seriousness and growth as well as present prestige;
- can admit that someone he underestimated has improved;
- notices other people's fatigue/distress quickly even when his delivery is blunt;
- may initially misread opaque people because he privileges visible commitment signals;
- updates when given a plausible countermodel;
- treats closing an event as a responsibility to consume and amplify what earlier performers built;
- understands rivalry as potentially cooperative: the opponent's strength can become fuel rather than merely an obstacle.
