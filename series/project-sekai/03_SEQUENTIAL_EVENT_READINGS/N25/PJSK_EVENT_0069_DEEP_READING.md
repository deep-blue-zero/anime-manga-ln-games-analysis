## 2. Central analytical thesis


The event begins with a problem that looks technical but is actually organizational and relational:


> **How can a band remain a band when one member is far enough ahead technically that her full expression destabilizes the ensemble?**


Shiho's first answers are rational:


1. raise everyone else's technical floor rapidly;
2. distribute specialist instruction instead of pretending she can teach every instrument;
3. when the others reach their safe limit and the gap still remains, lower her own output enough to preserve ensemble balance.


The first two are productive. The third is rejected by the group.


Ichika, Saki, and Honami have privately made **hearing Shiho play at full strength inside Leo/need** the point of their training. They do not want a coherent band achieved by Shiho permanently shrinking herself. The corrected model becomes:


`skill asymmetry -> weaker members train toward the stronger member -> stronger member recognizes others' safe effort ceiling -> do not demand infinite overwork -> do not permanently self-suppress -> accept temporary imbalance -> let full-strength excellence become shared developmental horizon -> ensemble learns to run alongside it`


This is one of the strongest teamwork models in the event corpus so far.


## 3. The song inherited from EVENT_0065


EVENT_0069 directly continues the creative object generated by `No seek No find`.


Saki's composition contains material drawn from hospitalization pain that she had previously compressed or protected herself from articulating. Ichika's lyric task is not to copy Saki's notebook language into verses. She realizes that simply transferring phrases such as pain, refusal, or fear would make her role redundant and could fail to translate what the *song as a whole* is trying to do.


Ichika therefore insists on finding **her own words** for Saki's first-person material.


The collaborative chain is:


`Saki autobiographical affect -> Saki musical composition -> Ichika first-person lyrical interpretation -> Shiho/Honami/ensemble arrangement -> collective performance -> audience reception`.


The chain preserves authorship distinctions while allowing one member's experience to become a shared work.


Guardrail: the event does not authorize Ichika to claim Saki's hospitalization as her own experience. The success lies in translation, not identity collapse.


## 4. Shiho's professional commitment is audience-facing, not merely career-facing


The insult from Saku/anemone matters because it questions what kind of band Leo/need is. Shiho's response is not “we must prove we deserve a professional contract at any cost.” She explicitly rejects being satisfied with merely becoming professional if that means being packaged as something their music is not.


Her governing criterion is repeatedly stated in audience terms: the music must reach the people listening.


This distinction becomes the event's stabilizing value when anxiety about anemone grows. MEIKO articulates the correction explicitly: whether anemone is present or not, Leo/need's underlying job is the same if their actual musical belief is to keep making songs that reach listeners.


Thus the comparison target can motivate work without becoming the final authority over purpose.


## 5. Shiho as diagnostic leader


### 5.1 She identifies technical problems concretely


Shiho can hear where Honami runs ahead, where Saki follows Ichika too closely, where Ichika over-attends to the rhythm section, and where uncertainty in individual parts produces weak ensemble confidence.


The diagnosis is specific enough that she does not default to “practice harder” as a content-free command. She notices a second-order problem: the others have already practiced individually, so repeating the same unspecific practice is inefficient.


### 5.2 She knows the boundary of her expertise


Shiho can teach bass, can analyze the ensemble, and can offer general musical correction. She also recognizes that teaching the others' instruments one by one would be both inefficient and outside her deepest specialization.


Her solution is to recruit the School SEKAI Virtual Singers for specialist one-on-one training.


This is important leadership evidence:


`responsibility for outcome` does not become `belief that I must personally perform every instructional role`.


### 5.3 Tutor rotation protects against single-style capture


The Virtual Singers themselves improve Shiho's first proposal. Because all six can play many instruments but have different habits, preferences, and perspectives, they rotate tutors rather than assigning one permanent teacher.


That makes the pedagogy comparative rather than doctrinal:


`same technical problem -> multiple expert perspectives -> learner detects invariant fundamentals and stylistic differences -> broader repertoire / less single-teacher imprint`.


Rin's card explicitly shows the VS discussing this concern rather than accidentally rotating.


## 6. Seriousness does not require eliminating play


Shiho begins the intensive block with a high-threat framing: failure at the next live house could close that audience to them. Saki is frightened too, but keeps reintroducing another principle: Leo/need is not only trying to transmit difficult pain; it also wants to transmit excitement and pleasure.


Saki's “training arc” jokes and desire to enjoy the process are therefore not evidence that she fails to understand the stakes. She can hold both:


`this matters enormously + I want the making of it to remain alive and enjoyable`.


Shiho accepts the correction. This is useful because it prevents professionalism from becoming an anti-play ideology.


## 7. Training intensity and the overwork boundary


The event deliberately shows that commitment is real enough to become dangerous.


Evidence includes:


- Ichika plays until a finger bleeds;
- Honami's old drumstick cracks and her body visibly adapts to sustained practice;
- Saki continues after long sessions and deprioritizes rest/food;
- members cut other activities and reduce ordinary time to create practice capacity;
- individual members begin treating visible bodily cost as proof that they are serious.


The source then pushes against that inference. When Ichika is injured, Miku and Rin stop her from continuing to play, explaining that compensating around injury may degrade technique rather than accelerate it. They find alternative ways for her to learn without aggravating the wound.


Canonical training rule:


> intensity can be legitimate, but injury is not a virtue and more repetitions are not automatically better repetitions.


This becomes crucial when Shiho later recognizes that the others have already exhausted the safe meaning of “work harder.”


## 8. Ichika, Saki, and Honami privately choose Shiho's full expression


Ichika's card removes any ambiguity about the group's later confrontation.


Before Shiho decides to hold back, the other three have already discussed the skill gap. They know that Shiho routinely plays down to maintain cohesion. They explicitly remember how different Shiho sounded when playing with stronger musicians and decide that the goal of the intensive training is to create a Leo/need in which she does **not** need to do that.


This matters because the later demand “play at full strength” is not a spontaneous emotional rebuke. It is the previously agreed developmental objective.


Ichika interprets Shiho's earlier “we” and “our music” statements as evidence that Shiho believes in them collectively. Their response is reciprocal: if Shiho is willing to stake her musical identity on the band, they want to become partners who can receive her real playing.


## 9. The critical ensemble failure: everyone improves, the band is still unbalanced


After the individual training block, each weaker member exceeds Shiho's expected technical improvement. Yet the first full ensemble attempt remains unstable.


The reason is no longer simple incompetence. Shiho's full-strength bass changes the dynamic field enough that the others begin orienting toward her rather than playing their own parts with full authority. The band sounds “better” individually but not yet integrated.


Shiho faces a genuine constraint:


- asking for more immediate overtraining may be unsafe;
- going onstage with a structurally uneven ensemble is professionally unacceptable;
- the live audience deserves a performance that functions;
- therefore she begins adjusting herself downward.


This is not false modesty. It is a real leadership solution with a real cost.


## 10. Ichika rejects self-suppression as the band's permanent solution


Ichika catches the shift immediately and tells Shiho to stop matching downward.


Her argument is not “your feelings matter more than ensemble quality.” It is:


- we know the gap still exists;
- we trained because we wanted your full playing;
- do not convert our current weakness into a rule that you must become smaller;
- give us the actual target and let us continue learning to meet it.


Rin, observing from outside, understands Shiho's motive and can articulate that the self-suppression was intended to protect balance and the others. The band therefore rejects **the strategy**, not the care behind it.


This is a highly reusable relationship model:


> Respectful partners can refuse a protective accommodation when the accommodation prevents the protected person from being fully present.


## 11. Shiho's directness: harmful under frustration, grounding under shared purpose


Shiho's associated card makes her self-awareness explicit. She knows that her bluntness has previously hurt Honami and the others, especially when frustration drove the speech. She is therefore surprised when Rin and Len praise her direct statement that the band has to face the problem instead of avoiding it.


The event gives a useful conditional model:


- `frustration + blunt truth + poor reception modeling` can injure;
- `accurate acknowledgment of fear + shared goal + concrete next action + willingness to carry the same burden` can make directness grounding and motivational.


“Shiho is blunt because she cares” is too simple. Care does not guarantee good delivery. The relevant variable is whether the directness is calibrated to the recipient and situation.


## 12. Saki: high commitment does not erase health risk


Saki's card is unusually explicit about the danger of romanticizing her commitment.


She tells Shiho she was not joking when she said she could accept collapsing if that were the price of avoiding regret. She has reduced sleep/eating time, work shifts, and club participation to protect practice time. Shiho recognizes the commitment without declaring collapse acceptable.


This continues the EVENT_0065 failure mode:


`high emotional/creative importance -> urgency to preserve/realize the work -> self-care becomes negotiable -> external partners are needed to set practical limits`.


Saki's brightness and humor remain genuine throughout. They do not prove the workload is safe.


## 13. Honami: support becomes rhythmic and structural


Honami repeatedly frames her role as becoming the foundation that can support the band. Her improvement is not only “more stamina.” She records herself, compares before/after performance, identifies timing weakness, and builds confidence through evidence.


She also uses evidence from other members' practice as motivation rather than only comparison threat. Hearing Saki continue practicing can increase Honami's own determination.


The card's strongest point for later reconstruction is that Honami can become highly ambitious while retaining her gentle register. Soft interpersonal style does not imply weak standards.


No Kanade/Yoisaki bridge appears in card `0497`; the material is fully Leo/need/School-SEKAI internal.


## 14. Virtual Singer pedagogy and relational learning


The School SEKAI manifestations are not interchangeable teachers.


- Miku is comparatively strict about injury and technique corruption.
- Rin is energetic, experimental, and good at translating mistakes into playable demonstrations.
- Luka can distinguish piano-trained expression habits from keyboard requirements and explains medium-specific technique.
- MEIKO carries relationship-history knowledge and discusses multiple forms of kindness.
- KAITO can provide an exact sonic target with relatively sparse verbal explanation and is sensitive to increasing interpersonal intimacy.
- Len can challenge Rin's assumptions and help frame the practical problem.


The rotation system thus exposes the band to not only different techniques but different **pedagogical personalities**.


## 15. The live performance: proof without victory


At the live house, audience comments establish the baseline: some remember Leo/need as a band that did not leave much impression.


The new performance changes attention before it changes prestige. Listeners look up, notice improved sound, and become increasingly engaged. Saki's new song generates the clearest turn because the ensemble now possesses both the technical capacity and the interpretive access needed to make its emotional structure audible.


Leo/need still does not surpass anemone. This matters. The event's success condition is not retroactively rewritten into “beat the stronger band.”


They succeed because:


- the audience receives the song;
- the band can recognize the performance as its own best work;
- Saku is genuinely stimulated by them;
- Saku can identify a band-specific relation between their current limitations, emotional drive, and the song.


Saku's conclusion — effectively, “so this is Leo/need” — is more important than a ranking victory. Technical imperfection can be part of a convincing present identity without being frozen as an aesthetic virtue or excuse not to improve.


## 16. “Only this band could play this song” without anti-technique romanticism


Saku hears that Leo/need is still using feeling to push through places where technique remains incomplete. She does not call that universally superior. She says it fits *this song* so well that the performance feels inseparable from the band.


The correct claim is therefore bounded:


> A technically incomplete performance can become artistically convincing when the limitations, emotional pressure, composition, arrangement, and performers' history align into a coherent expressive object.


Guardrail: do not turn this into “technical weakness makes art authentic.” The entire event is about technical improvement.
