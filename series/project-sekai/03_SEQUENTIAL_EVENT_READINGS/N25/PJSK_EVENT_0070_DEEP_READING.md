## 2. The initial problem: technically credible criticism remains painful and useful


Yukihira's critique is not dismissed as jealousy, cruelty, or ignorance. Ena is told that her picture has improved technically but that the critic cannot feel `魅力` from it. Because the criticism is professionally credible, it reaches the exact vulnerability EVENT_0053 left active: Ena has recommitted without a guarantee that continued effort will make her work good enough.


The event therefore does not solve Ena by telling her that technique is irrelevant. It asks a harder question: **what kind of improvement counts as improvement if recognizability, finish, and correctness can increase while person-specific expressive force decreases?**


## 3. Honami's goal initially justifies a technique-first pedagogy


Honami needs to draw animals that kindergarten children can recognize. Ena responds rationally to the stated task:
- close observation;
- tracing and simplified shapes;
- correction of proportion and recognizable features;
- repeated practice.


This works. Honami becomes more technically legible. The problem is that the resulting pictures no longer feel as specifically Honami-like.


That distinction matters for reconstruction. Ena is not anti-discipline. She can diagnose a concrete recipient/task requirement and teach the relevant fundamentals. The later revision is therefore **not technique -> spontaneity**. It is:


`task requirement -> fundamentals -> detect person-fit loss -> recover intended expression -> integrate technique without erasing authorship`.


## 4. The child's crocodile: correction can destroy first-person ownership


The decisive external case occurs when a child's crocodile is corrected into a more recognizable drawing and the child rejects it: `これは、わたしのワニじゃない`.


The event gives Ena a direct demonstration that visual accuracy is not always the actual target. A drawing may function as a representation of **what this subject was like to this particular person**. Correcting the image before understanding that relation can make it objectively cleaner and subjectively false.


Ena's teaching rule changes accordingly. Instead of asking only “what should a crocodile look like?”, she asks Honami to draw **Honami's crocodile**—the frightening animal remembered from Honami's own encounter. The result is technically strange but communicatively successful.


## 5. Multiple valid perceptions can coexist


Ena later draws the same wolf through different child perspectives: scary for one child, cute for another. The point is not relativism in which any drawing is equally competent. The point is that representation includes a relationship between object, perceiver, and intended communication.


The mural succeeds because its mismatched drawings visibly contain different people. The diversity is not noise to be standardized away; it is part of why Ena can call the whole thing `すごくいい絵`.


## 6. School-SEKAI Len independently cross-validates deliberate distortion


Card `0502` provides a useful independent model. Len wants a picture to feel “cool,” so he alters features rather than reproducing objects neutrally. When too many “cool” elements compete, he learns subtraction rather than simply adding detail.


Deferred reconstruction rule:


`desired affect -> identify causally expressive features -> deliberately exaggerate/distort -> remove competing elements -> preserve recognizability of the intended personal style`.


This keeps EVENT_0070 from being reduced to an abstract “art is subjective” statement. Expressive distortion can itself be technically reasoned.


## 7. Futaba and the imitation trap


Futaba's history supplies another convergent case. Imitating admired work can improve surface performance while decreasing motivation and distance from one's own desired picture. Her teacher's question—whether the resulting work was actually what Futaba wanted to draw—provides a direct analogue to Ena's current problem.


The event therefore distinguishes:
- learning from another person's technique;
- reproducing another person's surface solution;
- identifying what one wants one's own work to do.


Only the first is reliably compatible with self-authored growth.


## 8. Ena's endpoint: self-authored aesthetic judgment becomes admissible evidence


After the mural, Ena revisits the criticism that her work lacks charm. She does not invalidate the critic. Instead, she can now look at a technically flawed picture and say:


`私は好きだな、この絵`.


Her final goal is correspondingly reformulated:


`私が納得できる絵が描けるように——頑張っていこう！`


This is the state transition. EVENT_0053 allowed Ena to continue without certainty that talent would be enough. EVENT_0070 adds a usable internal criterion: **her own aesthetic approval is legitimate evidence, even while technical criticism remains real.**


That is not “ignore professionals” or “whatever I like is good enough.” It is an integrated two-source model:


`external technical/professional evaluation + first-person aesthetic/expressive judgment -> self-authored practice`.


## 9. Post-event persistence confirms the transition


Card `0499` is important because the change survives the event climax. Ena later teaches apple observation carefully—weight, contour, texture, shine, color—showing that fundamentals remain active. At the same time, she recognizes that drawing with Honami and Emu lets her return to a more basic `純粋に、絵を楽しもう` orientation.


This prevents a false binary between serious improvement and enjoyment. Ena can pursue professional development and also recover non-instrumental drawing pleasure.


## 10. Honami: humiliation history, recognizability, and owned style


Honami's card makes her earlier drawing shame causally explicit. Past ridicule made exposure aversive. EVENT_0070 does not turn her into a confident illustrator; it lets her discover that her malformed images can still be recognizably **hers** and can work because of what they communicate.


Her later formulation that `『わたしのワニ』が一番いい` is strong first-person evidence. Leo/need members likewise recognize her pictures through personal association even when literal object recognition fails.


Deferred Leo/need reconstruction value is therefore high:
- embarrassment remains compatible with initiative;
- technical weakness does not erase distinctive symbolism;
- recipient function can motivate work outside one's competence comfort zone;
- Ena's approval becomes a remembered permission source without replacing Honami's own judgment.


## 11. Emu: playful pedagogy and non-expert creative facilitation


Emu does not claim art expertise. She asks Ena for instruction, then turns elementary shape construction into a playful drawing-song method that children can adapt. Her contribution is not technical authority but embodied/social facilitation.


This strengthens the WxS model in which Emu often converts procedural material into participatory play. It also demonstrates that a non-expert can help another person access agency without pretending to own the domain.
