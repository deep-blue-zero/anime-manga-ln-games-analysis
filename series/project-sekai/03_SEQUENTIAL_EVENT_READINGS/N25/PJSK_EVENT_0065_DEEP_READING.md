## Executive finding


EVENT_0065 is a high-value creative-development event because it separates **having a sincere feeling** from **having actually reached the full affective truth of that feeling**.


Saki begins with a composition method that has previously worked: identify an experience or current feeling and put it into music. After Leo/need fails to connect with a new live-house audience, she initially treats the problem as one of audience fit and message targeting. That produces a plausible but shallow solution: if the audience is lonely, write a song that tells lonely people they are not alone.


The song still fails in Saki's own judgment because she has silently edited the source experience. She remembers hospitalization as loneliness plus later reassurance, but avoids the material she most fears reopening: anger at her body, envy of ordinary peers, terror of being left behind, resentment at missing normal adolescence, guilt about burdening her family, and the fear that her life might remain permanently restricted.


The decisive creative transition is therefore:


`recipient model + safe autobiographical summary -> inadequate emotional representation -> recovery of disowned/avoided affect -> integration of pain and received care -> recipient-oriented but first-person-authentic composition`


The event does **not** teach that darker emotion is inherently deeper or that cheerful songs are lesser art. Len's card explicitly protects the earlier bright song as a real expression of Saki's excitement that simply mismatched this audience. The stronger rule is that a work becomes thin when the creator substitutes a simplified emotional thesis for the more complicated experience actually generating it.


## 1. Initial creative baseline


Core `0065:01` establishes two parallel creative trajectories.


Ichika is learning voice-synthesis and composition from the bottom up. She can make Miku sing a melody and refine parameters, but she still struggles to turn an imagined musical shape into actual sound. She admires Saki's ability to write songs that feel recognizably like Leo/need.


Saki, by contrast, believes she has stabilized a working method. She describes drawing on:


- her experiences;
- what she currently feels;
- the emotional purpose of the song.


This is real growth, not false confidence. The event's later correction should therefore be read as a **deepening of an already valid method**, not a revelation that Saki had never put genuine feeling into music.


## 2. New venue as a controlled audience mismatch


The new live house is important because the failure cannot be reduced to bad performance.


The band observes before playing that:


- the audience is quieter and less anticipatorily exuberant;
- the room has a darker, more inward atmosphere;
- the venue regularly hosts unusually strong acts, including peers already receiving agency attention.


During Leo/need's set:


- the band itself performs well;
- Saki judges the others' playing unusually strong;
- the audience nevertheless disengages;
- some leave for drinks;
- the new bright, participatory song receives especially poor response.


This gives a useful distinction for later reconstruction:


`technical execution quality != audience resonance`


and


`creator sincerity != universal recipient fit`.


## 3. anemone and Saku as diagnostic contrast


anemone's performance provides the controlled comparison.


Their song is quiet, painful and cool-toned, yet Saki experiences it as strangely warm and relieving. Audience members who were indifferent to Leo/need become attentive enough to cry.


Saku's feedback is blunt:


- Leo/need is cute;
- the second song was somewhat better;
- the third song produced no emotional response in her;
- the venue's audience probably does not want what Leo/need currently offers.


The event does not simply endorse Saku's taste as objective truth. Shiho immediately notes that Leo/need has connected elsewhere and that venue compatibility is real. Len later independently likes the rejected bright song.


Saku therefore functions as a **high-value discrepant recipient**, not universal aesthetic authority.


## 4. Saki refuses the easy venue-fit exit


Shiho initially offers a reasonable strategy: if the venue is a poor fit, Leo/need does not have to force itself into it now.


Saki refuses to stop there because ignoring the reaction would also mean ignoring the people who were actually in the room.


Her question changes from:


> Where should our current music be performed?


into:


> Why did this specific audience receive almost nothing from it, and can I learn to reach them without abandoning Leo/need?


This is a meaningful professionalizing move. Saki does not treat existing fans as proof that no further recipient modeling is necessary.


## 5. First hypothesis — target the audience more directly


Saki researches posts associated with the venue and notices recurrent descriptions of loneliness and relief through live music.


Her first model is reasonable:


`lonely audience -> needs companionship / emotional accompaniment -> write a song that says you are not alone`.


She identifies an earlier successful song written for specific recipients and correctly notes that it also contained separation and consolation.


This is a **partially correct causal hypothesis**. The failure comes from how she attempts to instantiate it.


## 6. Protective autobiographical editing


Saki deliberately reaches for her hospitalization memories as relevant source material.


At first she accesses manageable components:


- painful examinations;
- seeing peers discuss school trips;
- missing Ichika and the others;
- wanting ordinary shared adolescence;
- present-day confidence that things can improve.


She builds a clean emotional arc:


`I was lonely -> people stayed with me -> therefore I can tell lonely listeners that a better future is waiting`.


But the music feels thin even to Saki.


This matters: she is not being externally told that her work is fake. **Her own creative ear detects that the emotional model is incomplete.**


## 7. The hidden layer of the hospitalization memory


Len's diagnostic contribution is modest but precise: if the intended experience is there and the music still feels emotionally underloaded, perhaps Saki has not recovered everything she actually felt.


Saki's old medical diary unlocks the missing layer.


The remembered state is much more severe than the sanitized version:


- other children get school trips, clubs and ordinary after-school life while she repeatedly returns to hospital;
- she feels left behind by her peers;
- she asks why her body is different;
- she resents having endured injections and examinations without getting better;
- she feels guilty for burdening Tsukasa and her parents;
- she fears she may remain hospitalized, alone, and unable even to enter high school;
- she explicitly pleads not to be left alone.


The important psychological finding is not simply that Saki once suffered. It is that later Saki has been **protectively compressing** this state.


She recognizes two reasons:


1. remembering it hurts;
2. saying it aloud would hurt people who love her.


A third creative constraint follows: she has implicitly assumed that putting such feelings into a song would merely make listeners sad.


That protective rule made the composition emotionally incomplete.


## 8. Pain is not the endpoint


The recovered memory does not terminate in despair.


Ichika's cloud photograph, and the playful exchange about eating matching soft-serve-shaped clouds under the same sky, becomes the remembered relational counterforce. Saki then broadens the memory to include Tsukasa, her parents, Honami and Shiho as people who repeatedly communicated some version of:


- you are not alone;
- we are still with you;
- there can still be something enjoyable ahead.


Thus the final song is not:


`I was miserable, therefore sadness is authentic`.


It is:


`I know the texture of being excluded, frightened, angry and alone; I also know what relational continuity felt like from inside that state; therefore I can offer companionship without pretending the pain is simple.`


This is why the finished song can sound painful and warm simultaneously.


## 9. Saki's revised composition rule


Saki's endpoint self-formulation is unusually explicit:


- she had thought she understood loneliness after recalling only part of the past;
- that understanding was shallow;
- she needed to face the painful, unpleasant and unwanted feelings as well;
- future composition should involve more serious self-examination.


Best reconstruction rule:


`identify intended recipient -> identify personally relevant experience -> test whether remembered affect is complete or protectively simplified -> recover contradictory/aversive layers -> translate the whole emotional structure into music -> preserve recipient purpose without replacing first-person truth with a generic message`.


This substantially increases Saki's future songwriting-model depth.


## 10. Guardrail — do not downgrade Saki's bright songs


Len's card is essential.


After hearing the rejected song, Len says he genuinely likes it. He hears Saki's excitement and invitation:


`fun things are happening here; come join us`.


KAITO agrees that the song successfully expresses Saki's excitement and that venue mismatch mattered.


Therefore EVENT_0065 must **not** generate the claim:


> Saki's earlier bright songs were shallow or insincere.


Correct distinction:


- the earlier song authentically expressed one real Saki state;
- it poorly matched this venue's recipient state;
- the later song demonstrates a broader emotional range and more complete autobiographical excavation.


Creative development is additive rather than self-repudiating.


## 11. Audience modeling versus pandering


Saki researches the audience and intentionally asks what kind of emotional support they need. This could be misread as formulaic pandering.


The event rejects that route by making her first audience-designed song fail.


What finally works is not copying anemone's style or mechanically feeding the audience a predicted sentiment. It is using the audience problem to identify a **real first-person experience that overlaps with the audience's condition**.


So the event's creator/recipient model is:


`recipient attention constrains the problem; personal truth supplies the content`.


Neither half is sufficient alone.


## 12. Saki's overwork pattern


The event also gives important load-management evidence.


When a creative problem feels both personally meaningful and group-critical, Saki can:


- work for hours without noticing time;
- sleep unintentionally rather than choosing to stop;
- resume immediately after waking;
- notice dizziness and consciously consider stopping;
- continue anyway because she fears the emotional/creative state is time-sensitive;
- work through the night;
- present the finished song while visibly unstable from exhaustion.


This should not be flattened into generic recklessness. She knows others told her not to overdo it and can articulate the safety rule. Under high creative salience, however, she can override that rule because she believes the current affective access may disappear.


After completion, she accepts the group's transfer of responsibility and rests.


## 13. Leo/need's distributed-care model


The band does not respond to Saki's struggle by removing agency from her.


Honami:
- wants to reduce Saki and Ichika's workload;
- delivers food tailored to cognitive exertion;
- deliberately avoids interrupting Saki's process.


Shiho:
- worries strongly;
- offers consultation without demanding it;
- deliberately chooses trust over preemptive control;
- reframes the post-composition work as the other members' turn.


Ichika:
- sends an ordinary sky photograph at exactly the kind of moment that historically helped Saki;
- later asks for Saki's raw notes and explicit first-person account because generic empathy is not enough for the lyric task.


The relationship rule is:


`care -> preserve creator agency -> offer targeted support -> monitor load -> accept transferred responsibility when the creator reaches a stopping point`.


## 14. Ichika — pain-sharing as lyric method


Card `0470` gives Ichika a strong creative/relational model.


She first realizes that she cannot write lyrics adequately because she knows only a small portion of the experience that generated Saki's song.


Rather than treating Saki's private history as material she is entitled to, she asks permission.


She explicitly says she wants Saki to share:


- the painful parts;
- the hated examinations;
- what it felt like to be in the hospital room;
- the whole emotional load.


Her purpose is not curiosity. She wants to become capable of writing words that stand beside the music rather than merely describe its topic.


This creates a useful creative-relationship rule:


`insufficient second-person knowledge -> request consent for deeper first-person testimony -> receive pain as entrusted material -> translate without claiming ownership of the experience`.


## 15. Saki–Ichika relational continuity across time


The cloud/soft-serve motif becomes a compact longitudinal object.


During hospitalization, sharing images of the same sky allowed Saki to feel as though she and Ichika were in the same place despite physical separation.


In the present:

## 16. Shiho — trust versus protective control


Card `0473` makes Shiho's dilemma explicit.


She does not want Saki to reopen painful hospitalization material and is alarmed by the visible overwork. But she also recognizes that Saki chose the professional path herself and that serious composition sometimes requires difficult first-person engagement.


Shiho's decision rule becomes:


`if Saki's self-authored goal is clear and the work is developmentally meaningful, prefer vigilant trust + available support over unilateral stopping, unless danger crosses a stronger threshold`.


The event retains tension here; it does not prove that every episode of Saki overwork should be indulged.


## 17. Honami — distributed practical contribution


Honami initially worries that composition/lyrics place too much burden on Saki and Ichika.


Her response is not to force herself into a role she cannot yet perform. She asks where contribution is useful and accepts concrete responsibilities:


- schedule management;
- arrangement/phrasing discussion;
- food/support logistics;

This is useful for reconstruction because it distinguishes Honami's care from passive emotional reassurance.


## 18. Len and KAITO as bounded creative supports


Len's useful intervention is diagnostic, not prescriptive. He does not tell Saki what the hidden emotion is; he suggests that an unexpressed answer may remain.


KAITO hears that the draft expresses the stated theme but feels as though the emotion is not fully loaded into it. Later, he treats his melodic opinion explicitly as one possible opinion rather than authority.

## 19. Saku as foil rather than villain or oracle


Saku is blunt enough to hurt Saki and later speaks dismissively about Leo/need as a plausible marketable "high-school girl band" when rejecting an agency's proposed packaging of anemone.


Yet her core creative position is coherent:


- she writes from what exists inside her;
- she resists external packaging that would displace anemone's desired musical identity;
- her direct feedback forces Saki to distinguish venue compatibility from deeper expressive range.


Do not model Saku as uniquely correct. Her feedback is one recipient's sincere response, and her own market/identity conflict becomes another case of artist–audience–industry tension.


## 20. Performance-system implications


EVENT_0065 adds a useful performance model distinct from VBS `0059`:


`performer skill + song quality + recipient history/state + venue culture + message/affect fit -> live reception`.


A technically good set can fail if the event's audience has no reason to enter the offered emotional frame.


Conversely, adapting to a room does not require aesthetic surrender. Leo/need's answer is to expand expressive capability while preserving its own identity.

## 22. Reconstruction implications


For future Saki modeling, avoid the fandom-level shortcut `bright girl who treasures ordinary life because she was sick`.


The more diagnostic structure is:


- ordinary life is precious partly because she has direct knowledge of exclusion from it;
- brightness is genuine, not a mask in the simple sense;
- she can nevertheless protect others and herself from the darkest part of her own history by compressing it into a more reassuring narrative;
- difficult artistic problems can motivate deliberate re-entry into that avoided material;
- once she reopens it, she seeks to transform rather than merely discharge it;
- high creative salience can drive serious self-neglect even though she understands her medical/physical limits;
- receiving ordinary care from friends is not peripheral to her creativity—it is part of the experiential basis from which she constructs hope.
