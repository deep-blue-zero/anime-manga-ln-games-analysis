## 2. Warmth is evidence of a relation, not a sufficient solution

Kanade can find a phrase that conveys softness while doubting the composition’s intended function. The doubt concerns the relation between warmth and rescue: she now believes she encountered real warmth in Mafuyu’s mother, yet mutual attachment did not make their attempted cohabitation work. She repeatedly marks this as what she saw or felt. The opening therefore does not establish a transparent, exhaustive account of either mother or daughter, and it does not reverse the failed return recorded in EVENT_0165. Luka asks what could rescue Mafuyu; Kanade admits she does not know. `PJSK:event:0192:01:001:0002–0006`; `PJSK:event:0192:01:001:0023–0044`.

The grandmother’s call enables an actual visit to Kanade’s mother’s grave. Kanade had forgotten the usual season amid composition; she cleans, brings carnations and accepts one of the sweets associated with hospital visits. Her grandmother notices a gentler expression than the earlier apparent self-blame that made approach difficult. Kanade attributes the change to friends who make music, support her outside work and scold overwork. Her private reassurance to her mother that she now takes care must remain a self-report beside the later cards’ interrupted sleep and distraction. It is not a retrospective proof that EVENT_0161’s collapse created a durable safe-work system. `PJSK:event:0192:02:002:0003–0027`; `PJSK:event:0192:02:002:0032–0052`.

Kanade distinguishes previous avoidance of painful warm memories from a gradual ability to recall them: her father’s birthday cake, a reading voice, cooking and smell. The dying mother’s remembered words wish Kanade happiness beyond their physical separation. Their value is real for Kanade, but the scene is remembrance, not a present conversation or supernatural confirmation that the mother eats the offered food. The grandmother gives her sweets for friends and apologizes that her own health prevents living together; Kanade thanks her ongoing calls. Care already crosses separation without requiring constant cohabitation. `PJSK:event:0192:02:002:0061–0094`.

Asked about her troubled expression, Kanade describes a friend’s mother as well-intentioned and the friend as wanting to respond. Her grandmother suggests that deep attachment may produce fear of losing one’s place, whether through growing apart or death. The repeated possibility language matters: this is an analogy based on Kanade’s account, not direct access to Mafuyu’s mother. Kanade recognizes fear in losing her mother, her father’s condition and the prospect of losing Nightcord, yet still asks why Mafuyu fears separation when she need not leave her mother now. The grandmother supports helping a dear friend conditionally, then explicitly wishes Kanade a life of her own. Kanade receives the words before she understands their full relevance. `PJSK:event:0192:03:001:0002–0021`; `PJSK:event:0192:03:001:0029–0047`.

## 4. The offered alternative remains an option

Kanade initially tells herself that successful rescue would merely return her to composing for someone else, yet that answer does not settle her bodily unease. Mafuyu notices her poor color, requests rest, offers to inform the others and asks whether she needs anything. Kanade denies that something happened and withdraws a possible question. Alone, she imagines Mafuyu happily reunited with family and no longer needing her music. This is a counterfactual imagined result, not an actual family reunion or completed song. `PJSK:event:0192:07:001:0002–0025`; `PJSK:event:0192:07:002:0002–0015`.

Ena and Mizuki request a private conversation if Kanade feels able. Kanade uses their message to rebuke herself for thinking about herself: she must rescue Mafuyu. The speech shows how a supposedly generous duty can also interrupt self-examination, without proving conscious manipulation. Her peers do not simply reinforce that rebuke. They distinguish their limited access, the family’s own authority and the absence of a certain answer before proposing an alternative. Ena agrees that a family able to speak and repair hurt would be desirable, then asks why it must happen now. `PJSK:event:0192:07:002:0018–0040`; `PJSK:event:0192:08:001:0003–0019`.

Ena revisits her inability to stop Mizuki’s departure. She now thinks immediate forced discussion, when neither understood their own feelings, might have inflicted more harm. Mizuki agrees that she lacked room to receive anyone’s words and says time is needed to decide a next action. These are reflective counterfactual judgments, not claims that an actual extra conversation occurred or that every rupture requires waiting. Their suggestion is distance for a time, not permanent estrangement. Ena’s more recent recognition of previously unseen feelings toward her father supports the difficulty of changing an outlook; she expressly distinguishes her earlier dislike from Mafuyu’s attachment. `PJSK:event:0192:08:001:0026–0060`; `PJSK:event:0192:08:001:0079–0087`.

Kanade resists because she believes both people want togetherness and because they are family. She then privately identifies that preference as her own: if they love each other, she had assumed living together would be happiest. Mizuki allows that continued confrontation might also change things and that they cannot know. Kanade thanks them for an option she could not have reached alone. They have not decided whether or how to tell Mafuyu, because the proposal could change her life. Ena also asks Kanade to value herself, saying rescue is not her only happiness and offering to listen. The ending reconnects those words with the grandmother’s wish and Luka’s question, then commits Kanade to thinking, not to a settled answer. `PJSK:event:0192:08:001:0073–0075`; `PJSK:event:0192:08:001:0097–0145`; `PJSK:event:0192:08:001:0151–0169`.

The I3 threshold is this newly explicit conflict and accepted task of consideration. The evidence does not support a successor whose state is independence, recovery or abandonment of rescue. Conversely, retaining only the unchanged E0116 description would omit a consequential new limit on how Kanade understands recipient-directed help: she can now see a collision between her desired family form, her own sustaining role and alternatives offered by trusted peers.
