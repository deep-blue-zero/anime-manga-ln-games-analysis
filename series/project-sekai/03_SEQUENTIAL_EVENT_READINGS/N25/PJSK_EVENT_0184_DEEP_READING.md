## 4. A recovered childhood perspective produces pleasure, then a low grade

The next oil-painting task is **hands**. Seeing Futaba begin recalls Ena's childhood wonder at her father's work. In an actual historical camping scene, her mother praises a butterfly study and encourages her to show him. Ena instead becomes absorbed in his painting and asks why he has used red where she sees none. He says he painted what he wanted and describes painting as a world of one's own. This memory predates later disqualification and the E0127 conversation; it neither retracts the father's hurtful judgment nor constitutes a new present reconciliation. `PJSK:event:0184:06:004:0002–0017`; `PJSK:event:0184:07:001:0003–0025`.

Ena remembers his hand as making a world that looked magical to her. She explores scale, blur, red and mixed colors instead of correcting every deviation toward literal appearance. The image's magic is her expressive comparison, not a supernatural event. Her judgment remains active: these particular deviations serve this particular remembered view. She finds making the painting so pleasurable that she wishes it could continue. Empty Miku and KAITO observe her working, but this is still textual observation, not an inspected painting or proof of universal audience effect. `PJSK:event:0184:07:001:0028–0069`.

The result is **C**, not a high grade; no second-round class rank is given. Ena can say both that she made a satisfying work and that its result makes her fear 東美 is beyond reach. She finally states the desire directly and refuses to abandon it. Yukihira confirms that current skill is insufficient, distinguishes the technical score from the work's expressive attraction, and offers conditional guidance if she chooses to proceed. His assessment is that the possibility is not zero, not that entry is probable or assured. Ena accepts the work ahead. The established teacher relation receives very strong I2 rather than a redundant new mentor identity. `PJSK:event:0184:08:001:0002–0072`.

At home Ena makes the choice concrete by deleting **her selfie account**. The illustration account remains. She describes selfie approval as a habitual retreat when painting becomes difficult, thanks the departed account and tells Nightcord she will aim for 東美. This is an actual costly boundary, not the disappearance of every desire for recognition or a promise never to use social media again. Later she acknowledges an empty feeling. The story does not require every artist to erase a hobby or prove that avoidance can no longer recur. `PJSK:event:0184:08:002:0002–0039`; `PJSK:card:1276:02:002:0020–0064`.
