## 1. Scope, authority, and analytical decision

This artifact is the canonical V1 deep reading for EVENT_0026, `カーネーション・リコレクション`, within the Nightcord at 25:00 sequential event-analysis program. It inherits the fully integrated EVENT_0019 authority state and reads the complete documentary envelope rather than treating the eight event chapters as sufficient by themselves.

The reviewed envelope is:

- core event: `PJSK:event:0026:01-08`;
- associated cards: `PJSK:card:0237:01-02`, `0238:01-02`, `0239:01-02`, `0240:01-02`, `0241:01-02`;
- linked/unlock-dependent area: `areatalk_ev_night_04_001`, `_002`, `_003`, `_005`, `_006`, `_011`, `_012`, `_013`.

The release is classified:

- **Tier A - Developmental**;
- **I3 - state-changing release**.

The decisive transition belongs to Kanade. EVENT_0002 had already revised her rescue method from unilateral output toward reaction-seeking, listening, creative transformation, and reassessment. EVENT_0014 demonstrated that she could support another person without claiming complete causal knowledge. EVENT_0019 showed that she could ask others for help when solitary compositional control failed. None of those releases, however, had altered the most dangerous underlying architecture of her Mafuyu-centered mission: if a song does not save, the result remains morally insufficient, and Kanade's own continued existence is implicitly tied to eventually producing the successful rescue.

EVENT_0026 makes that architecture explicit, pushes it to a crisis point, and then partially revises it.

The event first intensifies the old rule. Kanade produces repeated demos, discounts Mafuyu's partial affective reactions, denies herself the right to grieve her father, and eventually tells Mizuki that if she cannot quickly make music capable of saving Mafuyu, she has no right to remain here. The rescue project is therefore not merely a vocational goal or altruistic preference. It is still a **penitential condition of self-authorization**.

The development occurs when Kanade recovers a positive part of her father that guilt had obscured. Her father's music was not only part of the causal chain Kanade associates with his collapse. It was also music made so that his wife and daughter would smile; it carried warmth, gentleness, ordinary family happiness, and an enveloping quality Kanade recognizes as missing from her recent work. Once that inheritance becomes available again, Kanade asks a different compositional question:

> not only **“How do I stop Mafuyu from disappearing?”**, but **“What do I actually want Mafuyu to become able to experience?”**

Kanade distinguishes her own pain at Mafuyu's disappearance from a positive destination for Mafuyu. She remains aware that “I do not want her to disappear” is partly her own ego. She then imagines a Mafuyu who can find herself, feel many things, and someday smile from genuine warmth or happiness.

Episode 8 supplies D0 confirmation that this new orientation reaches Mafuyu differently: Mafuyu smiles without initially noticing it and can identify that the song made her feel a little warm. Card `0237` then proves persistence beyond the climax. Kanade explicitly says she did not reach “saving,” but nevertheless recognizes the smile as a real step forward.

That is enough to change future response distributions and justify a new global Kanade state:

**`K-E0026-01 — positive-destination rescue / persistent-penance state`.**

The second half of the label is essential. EVENT_0026 does not cure Kanade's guilt, eliminate her salvation mission, teach healthy self-care, or persuade her that she did not cause her father's collapse. Card `0237` shows her still normalizing extended periods without food or water. The correct transition is therefore **broader efficacy and a more person-centered destination layered over still-dangerous guilt and self-punishment**.

## 2. Executive synthesis - from rescue as negation to rescue as imagined life

EVENT_0026 is built around a deceptively simple creative problem: Kanade's music can make Mafuyu feel something, yet Kanade still judges it inadequate.

This matters because by the current authority boundary Mafuyu is no longer the completely unreadable person Kanade first encountered. EVENT_0002 established that Mafuyu can possess affect before she can name it, that negative or ambiguous reactions count as first-person data, and that creative externalization can help recover distinctions. EVENT_0019 added tentative positive salience and ordinary curiosity. Kanade therefore already possesses evidence that music can reach Mafuyu without instantly “saving” her.

Yet episode 1 shows that Kanade continues to process those partial results through an outcome-absolutist filter. Mafuyu can say a song creates a hazy or difficult-to-identify feeling; Kanade hears “not enough.” She has already produced several songs and immediately begins another. Her internal standard remains binary enough that the existence of some reaction does not significantly reduce rescue pressure.

Luka's intervention in episode 2 attacks the missing variable. She does not tell Kanade to compose harder, more beautifully, or more sincerely. She asks where the song is meant to take its listener. If Kanade cannot imagine a destination, then emotional intensity alone can leave the listener wandering.

The metaphor of the maze is particularly important for Kanade because her rescue mission has historically been strongly **negative** in structure:

- do not let someone disappear;
- do not fail to reach them;
- do not repeat the catastrophe associated with her father;
- create until rescue occurs;
- continue because stopping would mean accepting guilt without compensatory action.

These rules specify what must not happen. They do not yet provide a sufficiently concrete account of what Mafuyu's *good life* would look or feel like from Mafuyu's side.

The event then forces Kanade through memory rather than abstract ethics.

Her hospitalized father no longer recognizes her and speaks happily about the child he expects to have. Kanade receives a devastating image of paternal happiness temporally located before the daughter he no longer remembers. Her first interpretation is entirely consistent with the old guilt system: that was the smile before Kanade ruined him. She tells herself she has no right to grieve. Personal pain must be converted into more production.

Mizuki interrupts the behavior without pretending to possess a philosophical solution. They pull Kanade into ordinary contact, help her search for the remembered carnation place, and gradually draw out the causal structure of her distress. When Kanade finally says that failure to save Mafuyu would mean she has no right to remain, Mizuki correctly recognizes composition as self-punishment. Importantly, Mizuki also recognizes that simply saying “it was not your fault” will not work. Kanade's guilt is not an information deficit that can be corrected by one reassuring proposition.

The recovered carnation memory then does something more useful than contradiction.

Kanade remembers not only the later tragedy but an intact family scene. Her father plays for his wife and daughter. His criterion for success is modest and relational: if the music makes the people he loves smile, that is enough. Kanade realizes that she had effectively locked this memory away because remembering the happiness itself was unbearably painful. At first the memory hurts precisely because it was good.

By the end of episode 6, however, she can classify it as **her precious memory**. This is a major change in memory use. The family past no longer exists only as prosecution evidence in the case against Kanade. It can also contain affection, aesthetic inheritance, sensory detail, and a model of what music was for before trauma converted creation into obligation.

Episode 7 turns that recovered memory into a compositional rule.

Kanade recognizes the gentle, enveloping feeling in her father's music and the music-box sound inherited from him. She asks what state she wants Mafuyu to reach. She first encounters the old answer—“I don't want Mafuyu to disappear”—and immediately subjects it to the ethical self-critique already developed in the main story: that desire is partly Kanade's ego because Mafuyu disappearing would hurt Kanade.

Instead of abandoning involvement because the motive is impure, Kanade asks beyond it. The answer is not “Mafuyu should become grateful,” “Mafuyu should like my music,” or “Mafuyu should become the person I imagine.” It is broader and more experiential: Kanade wants Mafuyu to find herself, recover the capacity to feel different things, and someday genuinely smile.

The new song is therefore different because the imagined endpoint is different.

Mafuyu's response in episode 8 is small by ordinary narrative standards and enormous by the current N25 state model. She says the song was probably good, is observed smiling before she notices the smile herself, and can identify a little warmth. Luka names this as the place Kanade wanted to reach.

The event does not pretend this is salvation. Card `0237` is explicit: Kanade herself says she did not reach “saving.” But for the first time, that statement can coexist with **“we moved forward.”**

That is EVENT_0026's developmental achievement.

## 4. Episode 1 - partial affect is still coded as failure

The opening immediately establishes that EVENT_0002's methodological breakthrough did not fully revise Kanade's moral standard.

Kanade presents another demo. Mafuyu cannot name a clear reaction. Ena notes how many songs Kanade has already produced. Kanade's answer is essentially that number does not matter: if the song does not reach Mafuyu in the required way, she must keep making more.

Mizuki recommends rest. Kanade does not reject rest as such, but converts even rest into a brief interruption before another promised demo.

Privately, Kanade acknowledges evidence that should matter. Some recent songs have produced something in Mafuyu: chest tightness, a less oppressive feeling, or an indistinct reaction. But Kanade's evaluative conclusion remains that this is not enough. The target is still “a song that saves.”

Mafuyu's internal perspective creates an important asymmetry. She does experience something. She can tell that Kanade's work is not neutral to her. What she lacks is category access. The event therefore begins with:

`real affective contact + poor Mafuyu identification + Kanade outcome absolutism`.

This protects against two bad readings:

1. Kanade is not literally failing to affect Mafuyu;
2. Mafuyu is not secretly receiving a complete cure she simply refuses to admit.

The actual problem is the interpretation and destination of partial efficacy.

## 5. Episode 2 - Luka asks where the song goes

Ena provides useful cross-event testimony before Luka intervenes. She remembers earlier Kanade songs as staying close to the listener and recalls the EVENT_0014 song as something that made her chest tighten and made her want to draw again. Kanade therefore already has evidence that her music can change another person's state without “saving” in the absolute sense.

Kanade nevertheless wonders whether she needs to recover the extreme absorbed condition in which she previously composed consequential work.

N25 Luka appears and refuses to solve the problem for her.

Luka's critique is precise: the song is beautiful, but it does not seem capable of leading Mafuyu out of the maze. When Kanade asks what is missing, Luka asks where Kanade wants the listener to arrive.

This is a compositional question and a psychological question at once.

Kanade's old schema contains enormous motivational force but weak positive teleology. She can imagine rescue as the removal of catastrophe. She has more difficulty imagining the texture of Mafuyu's life after rescue.

Luka therefore introduces:

`emotion/intensity -> direction -> imagined destination -> compositional form`.

Her role should not be exaggerated. She does not know Mafuyu's final answer, does not decode Kanade's trauma, and does not prescribe the destination. She identifies the missing question and leaves Kanade to answer it.

That epistemic restraint is consistent with the N25 Virtual Singer ontology developed since EVENT_0014: manifestation can produce useful perspectives without granting omniscience.

## 6. Episode 3 - being forgotten and denying the right to grieve

Kanade visits her father.

His memory has regressed far enough that he speaks to Kanade as a stranger and describes the child he and his wife are expecting. He explains the name “Kanade” through their shared love of music. The scene is painful not because the father is hostile, but because he is warm while occupying a time before he remembers his own daughter as existing.

Kanade responds gently in the moment. Afterward, the old guilt architecture takes control.

She tells herself she has no right to grieve. The logic is severe:

- father suffers because of Kanade, in Kanade's model;
- therefore sadness about losing recognition is self-centered;
- time spent grieving should instead be spent creating;
- creation must save Mafuyu and others;
- successful rescue functions as the only legitimate answer to guilt.

This is stronger evidence than generic “Kanade blames herself.” It shows guilt regulating **permission to have emotions**.

The rain imagery externalizes the state without needing a one-to-one symbolic claim. Kanade walks through rain without properly protecting herself. Mizuki notices both the physical behavior and the expression beneath the socially sufficient “I'm fine” response.

Mizuki's intervention is practical and relational rather than interpretively authoritarian: get Kanade out of the rain, create a pause, provide another context.

## 7. Episode 4 - inherited sound is not only inherited guilt

In the café, Kanade tells Mizuki that she herself knows the recent song is insufficient and repeats Luka's maze/destination formulation.

Mizuki suggests seeking clues through unfamiliar experience, explicitly building on the EVENT_0019 lesson that new contexts can restore compositional movement. This is a useful example of the group developing **shared procedural memory**: a prior event's support method becomes available later.

The music box becomes the key object.

Mizuki notices that music-box-like sounds recur in Kanade's work. Kanade recognizes the connection to her father. The point is not merely that the father influenced her taste. It reopens the possibility that Kanade inherited something desirable from him.

Before this event, father-related musical continuity is easily swallowed by the guilt narrative: Kanade's musical talent becomes part of the story of how she believes she destroyed him. EVENT_0026 begins to recover a second inheritance:

- gentleness;
- warmth;
- music made for specific loved people;
- the wish to make someone smile;
- sensory/aesthetic habits embedded in Kanade's own composition.

This is the bridge from trauma history to positive destination.

## 8. Episode 5 - `ここにいちゃいけない`: rescue as right to exist

The search for the carnation place initially seems like ordinary memory work. White carnations connect Kanade to her mother, and Mizuki willingly spends the rainy day helping search.

The scene also preserves EVENT_0019 continuity for Mizuki. “Someday” remains dangerous language because Mizuki is still wondering whether a future with N25 will exist. Even while actively caring for Kanade, Mizuki can fear that current companionship will become past tense.

Kanade eventually discloses the father visit.

Her conclusion is the event's clearest statement of the old pathology. The father before Kanade was smiling. Kanade believes she took that smile away. Therefore she must quickly produce a song that saves Mafuyu. If she cannot, she says she has no right to remain here.

This statement connects previously separate observations into one governing chain:

`father catastrophe -> self-blame -> guilt -> denied grief -> compulsory creation -> rescue as moral repayment -> successful rescue as permission to continue existing`.

Mizuki recognizes the punitive structure. They understand that Kanade is effectively using creation to punish and justify herself.

Equally important, Mizuki recognizes the limitation of reassurance. “It wasn't your fault” is unlikely to dissolve a system Kanade has used to organize her life for years. This is epistemically mature support. Mizuki does not mistake a correct external proposition for a psychologically effective intervention.

Instead, Mizuki offers counterevidence within Kanade's own domain: Kanade's music has already saved or reached people, including an earlier Mizuki. The point is not to prove the debt fully paid. It is to introduce evidence Kanade's all-or-nothing accounting excludes.

## 9. Episode 6 - the happiness Kanade locked away

Kanade finally recovers the family memory associated with the carnation place.

The childhood “field” turns out to be a large flowerbed remembered at child scale. This is an elegant correction because the memory is not false. Its spatial interpretation changed with perspective. The event repeatedly uses this structure: facts can remain while meaning changes when the observer's position changes.

The family scene is ordinary and happy. Kanade's father plays guitar. Her mother responds warmly. Young Kanade says she loves his music. The father frames success not through status, commercial legitimacy, or rescue in grand terms, but through the smiles of the people he loves.

In the present, the memory is initially unbearable.

Kanade realizes she had locked it away herself because remembering the happiness made the subsequent loss more painful. This is a significant distinction. Her memory problem is not merely accidental forgetting. It is a form of protective exclusion: positive memory became intolerable because it intensified grief.

By the end of the episode Kanade can say that she is glad she remembered because it is **her precious memory**.

That marks a new relationship to autobiographical evidence. The past can now contain:

- what Kanade lost;
- what she loved;
- what she inherited;
- what her father valued;
- not only what she believes she destroyed.

The event does not remove the guilt narrative. It pluralizes the memory archive that narrative had monopolized.

## 10. Episode 7 - defining a positive destination

Kanade returns to composition with the recovered memory available.

She hears her father's music differently: gentle, warm, enveloping. She recognizes a quality absent from the recent demos produced under escalating rescue pressure.

Then she asks the decisive question: **what does she want Mafuyu to become?**

The first answer is the familiar one: she does not want Mafuyu to disappear.

Kanade immediately notices the problem. Mafuyu's disappearance would hurt Kanade, so non-disappearance alone can be framed as Kanade's ego. This preserves the main-story ethical advance rather than resetting Kanade into naïve savior certainty.

She keeps asking.

She wants Mafuyu to find the self she has been searching for. But “find herself” is still abstract. Kanade then imagines a more phenomenological destination: Mafuyu able to feel many different things, able to experience warmth, able someday to smile genuinely.

This matters because the destination is not simply a behavioral performance. Kanade is not asking Mafuyu to display a smile for reassurance. The model is:

`recovered feeling capacity -> self-authored experience -> possible genuine smile`.

The smile is evidence of life becoming experientially richer, not the goal as surface compliance.

Kanade's father's old criterion—music that makes loved people smile—therefore returns transformed. Kanade does not simply imitate him. She integrates that positive relational image into the already more reflexive rescue ethic she developed with Mafuyu.

## 11. Episode 8 - warmth before understanding

The new song is presented to Mafuyu in the Empty SEKAI.

Kanade initially falls back toward the old evaluative habit. Mafuyu's reaction is quiet enough that Kanade fears the song failed.

Then the group notices Mafuyu is smiling.

The strongest evidence is that Mafuyu does not initially know she is doing it. This avoids the possibility that she is merely supplying a socially expected reaction to reassure Kanade. When asked how she feels, Mafuyu still cannot provide a rich explanation. She can, however, identify a small degree of warmth.

This is exactly the sort of evidence the post-EVENT_0002 model predicts: affect can precede conceptual access.

Luka tells Kanade that this is the destination she was seeking. The line should not be read as “mission accomplished.” The more important effect is that Kanade now has a different form of success evidence.

The old binary was approximately:

`not saved -> failure -> make another song`.

The new possibility is:

`small authentic warmth/smile -> real movement toward a positive destination -> continue without pretending rescue is complete`.

Kanade later thanks Mizuki and recognizes that Mizuki expanded her world. The event therefore ends with a relational correction to the isolated savior model: Kanade's own development was made possible by someone else noticing, interrupting, searching, accompanying, and offering perspective.

## 22. Theme and motif integration

### 22.1 Maze and destination

### 22.2 Grief versus penance

Kanade's statement that she has no right to grieve exposes a central ethical distortion: guilt turns ordinary mourning into forbidden self-indulgence and converts pain into compulsory productive repayment.

The event does not resolve this. It gives Kanade a second way to hold the past.

### 22.3 Positive memory as usable inheritance

The carnation memory matters because happiness itself had become intolerable. Recovering it allows Kanade to inherit values from the father without first acquitting herself of guilt.

### 22.5 Flowers, scale, and memory

The “carnation field” becoming a flowerbed is not a debunking of memory. Childhood scale made the place enormous. Adult perspective corrects geometry without invalidating emotional truth.

This supports a broader methodological guardrail: later perspective may revise interpretation without making the earlier experience fraudulent.

### 22.6 Persistence: virtue and pathology

Kanade's refusal to give up helps produce the new song and is admired by Mizuki. The same persistence is also bound to self-punishment, denied grief, and bodily neglect.

The event therefore must not be moralized as “never give up.” Its stronger formulation is:

This extends the N25 curse/continuation dialectic.
