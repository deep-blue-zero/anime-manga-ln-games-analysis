## 2. Ena turns counterfactual guilt into a bounded claim of her own

Ena initially treats the rupture as a problem that a better prepared expression might have prevented. She replays whether different words, less surprise, or prior knowledge would have kept Mizuki present. That counterfactual reasoning does not give her authority over Mizuki's account: when Kanade and Mafuyu notice the absence, Ena explicitly refuses to tell them what is not hers to disclose. She can report that something happened and accept their help without transferring the guarded content. `PJSK:event:0150:01:001:0061–0093`; `PJSK:event:0150:02:001:0045–0088`; `PJSK:event:0150:05:001:0020–0057`.

Rui offers neither permission nor prohibition. He gives a personal analogy in which another person's forceful persistence became valuable to him, then marks that history as his rather than a rule for Ena and Mizuki. Ena takes hope from the possibility without gaining certainty that pursuit is correct. This preserves both action and epistemic humility: the event authorizes Ena's specific relational bid, not a universal right to breach withdrawal. `PJSK:event:0150:04:001:0015–0086`.

Ena's decisive change is not discovery of a flawless response. She gives up the fantasy that one correct sentence could erase the rupture and instead accepts responsibility for what she can author: she wants Mizuki as a friend, will try not to cause harm, and cannot honestly make her care disappear. The claim is about Ena's own attachment and effort, not a diagnosis of Mizuki or a demand for identity proof.

## 3. The group supports a search without taking the disclosure

Kanade and Mafuyu know that Mizuki has stopped joining Nightcord and that Ena's distress is connected, but they do not know the guarded content. They offer presence, search labor, and practical coordination on those limited grounds. An and other school contacts contribute sightings and access rather than an identity interpretation. The search therefore distributes care while keeping the account's authorship unavailable to helpers who have not received it. `PJSK:event:0150:05:001:0020–0057`; `PJSK:event:0150:06:003:0026–0043`.

That boundary matters because Mizuki's withdrawal is acute. Alone, Mizuki reports inability to want or enjoy anything and says both that disappearing feels impossible and that living is unwanted. The text demonstrates severe despair and passive suicidal ideation language, but depicts no attempt, plan, method, or clinical diagnosis. Later ordinary contact and return must not be read backward as proof that this state was minor or permanently resolved. `PJSK:event:0150:06:002:0002–0033`.

## 5. The confrontation ends by restoring choice

Ena pursues, asks to be heard, and argues with Mizuki's attempt to decide that separation would be better for both of them. Mizuki names the continuing cost directly: kindness may still trigger scrutiny, rumors may implicate friends, accepting care may remain painful, and fear may repeatedly make belonging feel undeserved. Ena cannot refute those future risks. She can only refuse Mizuki's unilateral claim about Ena's own wishes and state that surprise did not change whom she regards as a precious friend. `PJSK:event:0150:07:003:0020–0063`; `PJSK:event:0150:08:001:0001–0075`.

The exchange becomes reparative only when insistence stops short of taking the final decision. Ena says what she needed to say and states that she will not stop Mizuki if Mizuki still leaves. Mizuki then articulates both sides rather than suppressing either: fear and another wish to flee will probably return, yet Mizuki wants to believe continued presence is possible and wants to remain friends. Ena answers by returning the choice to Mizuki. Mizuki chooses to stay, and Ena invites that chosen return to the whole group. `PJSK:event:0150:08:001:0076–0130`.
