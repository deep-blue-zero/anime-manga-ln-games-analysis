## 4. Event-level causal structure


EVENT_0058 begins as a school obligation and gradually converts obligation into chosen participation for multiple characters.


The blue-team captain is the event's ethical hinge. His previous cheering loss taught him that an obsession with defeating the rival team displaced the actual recipients of encouragement. His current goal is therefore not merely “win.” He wants students to feel energized, glad to belong to blue, and able to remember the festival as genuinely enjoyable.


This recipient-centered revision matters because it creates the bridge to Rui. The cheering competition is show-like without being a professional show: Rui must work with inexperienced performers, school safety rules, limited time, unfamiliar space, and peers who have no prior reason to trust his eccentric methods. The captain has sought him out precisely because a prior WxS performance convinced him Rui could turn a desire to move an audience into a concrete production.


Mizuki enters from the other side. Cheer squad is initially a bad draw: conspicuous, collective, and school-centered. The captain's sincerity and the visible accumulation of effort make the activity meaningful enough that Mizuki's relationship to it changes.


Rui then reaches his own obstacle. He devises a stronger staging idea but cannot say it. The practical objections are real—time, complexity, apparent danger—but he eventually identifies that those are not the whole cause. The deeper problem is an old expectation: if he says what he actually wants to make, ordinary peers may move to “the other side” and leave him isolated again.


Mizuki recognizes the problem because its structure is familiar. They do not solve the production; they tell Rui that his social world has already changed enough that he can try crossing toward the group. Rui then presents the idea, the captain tests his confidence and safety plan, and the group voluntarily accepts the risk.


The ending validates not spectacle for spectacle's sake but **recipient-oriented spectacle built through reciprocal trust**. Blue fails to win the overall sports festival, yet wins the cheering contest with a record vote total and succeeds in energizing the losing team.


## 5. Mizuki: school participation without assimilation


### 5.1 Starting condition: constraint, visibility risk, and instrumental attendance


Card `0429` prevents a sentimental rereading of Mizuki's participation.


Mizuki is at school for a full day partly because of attendance requirements and explicitly finds it tiring. When cheer-squad selection is announced, the internal response is immediate:


`悪目立ちしそうだし、やりたくないな……`


The problem is not simple laziness. Being made conspicuous inside a school collective is aversive. Selection by lottery therefore feels like especially bad luck.


An's behavior matters here. She voluntarily trades into the role after noticing an anxious classmate who does not want it. Her reasons combine empathy with genuine interest and a desire to share the festival with Mizuki. This gives Mizuki a familiar safe person without making An responsible for changing Mizuki's mind.


Mizuki initially reframes the obligation pragmatically: active participation contributes to attendance and may be less boring than sitting through class. That instrumental motivation should remain part of the model.


### 5.2 Why the activity becomes meaningful


The shift begins when Mizuki encounters the captain's accumulated work and goal.


In `0058:02`, Mizuki explicitly acknowledges discomfort with the “みんなで” atmosphere. Yet the captain's nearly year-long preparation, willingness to solicit criticism, and focus on making the team feel encouraged turn the group activity into a concrete creative problem rather than an abstract demand to “participate normally.”


That distinction is crucial.


Mizuki does not become someone who suddenly enjoys compulsory school collectivity. They become someone who can **choose investment once a role offers real creative agency, trusted collaborators, and a purpose they can endorse**.


This is fully compatible with earlier school-avoidance evidence.


### 5.3 Creative competence outside N25


Card `0429:02` is unusually useful reconstruction evidence.


Rui gives Mizuki a functional production problem: create fire imagery for the cheering performance. Mizuki does not merely make something aesthetically pleasing.


They:


- ask what the fire is doing narratively;
- gather source material rather than improvising solely from style;
- ask Toya for useful Three Kingdoms references;
- study Liu Bei's characterization;
- connect the visual progression to dramatic function;
- storyboard how the image enters the character's field of view;
- move the fire from forceful/principled energy toward warm, enveloping encouragement;
- repeatedly revise materials and treatment;
- coordinate with Rui on staging;
- look for music whose dynamics fit the intended emotional curve.


This expands Mizuki reconstruction beyond “fashionable video editor.” Their craft reasoning can be **narrative, symbolic, research-driven, and iterative**.


Ena's recognition in `0058:08` provides valuable outside validation. Ena recognizes the projection as Mizuki's work despite it being unlike their usual output and notices that the blue flame looks cold yet somehow warm. This is exactly the effect Mizuki had deliberately designed.


### 5.4 Bounded help-seeking from Kanade


When Mizuki cannot find suitable music, they contact Kanade privately.


The request is carefully bounded:


- the project is personal and unrelated to N25 work;
- Mizuki asks whether Kanade happens to know a track fitting the storyboard;
- they explicitly say it is fine only if Kanade feels like responding.


This is diagnostically important relational behavior. Mizuki can ask for help while protecting the other person's freedom not to provide it. Kanade responds quickly with a useful music link, which Mizuki receives with obvious delight.


Do not inflate this into evidence that Kanade became part of the cheer production. Her role is a bounded creative assist.


### 5.5 Endpoint: genuine enjoyment is admitted, not inferred away


The most important card endpoint is Mizuki's own conclusion:


`応援団に選ばれちゃった時は、とんでもないはずれくじだって思ったけど……`


followed by:


`結局、なんだかんだ楽しんじゃってるな`


This is first-person authority.


Later, during the N25 debrief, Mizuki again distinguishes the attendance-credit incentive from the experience itself and says the event was genuinely fun. They even attend the cheer-group barbecue afterward.


The correct transition is therefore not:


`school avoidance -> school belonging solved`


but:


`identity-threat-conditioned school discomfort + pragmatic attendance -> bounded, role-fit collective investment -> genuine enjoyment while guarded self remains undisclosed`.


That is a **STRENGTHEN** of the existing Mizuki model, not a successor state.


## 6. Mizuki and Rui: outsider kinship becomes active social intervention


EVENT_0058 is one of the clearest current-event demonstrations of why the Mizuki-Rui relationship cannot be reduced to “two weird kids who used to hang out on the roof.”


### 6.1 Rui's residual fear


Rui has joined WxS and can participate in ordinary school interactions, but `0058:04-05` proves that the old wound remains behaviorally active.


He devises an ambitious staging improvement and repeatedly delays telling the cheer group. He recognizes the contradiction himself:


- he genuinely wants to make the best possible performance;
- he has a concrete plan;
- he believes he can make it safe;
- yet he keeps searching for reasons to postpone the conversation.


His self-diagnosis is unusually explicit:


`まさか、自分がここまで臆病だとは思わなかったよ。 ……本当に、染みついたものは厄介だね`


and then:


`僕が僕のやりたいことを話したら、 またみんな、あちら側に行ってしまうんじゃないかと`


This is not ordinary shyness. “Saying what I actually want to make” is linked to the learned expectation that peers will separate themselves from him.


### 6.2 Why Mizuki sees it


Mizuki notices that Rui is about to say something and stops. When they confront him, Rui admits the fear directly.


Mizuki's response is simple because they do not need the full history explained:


`類は、もう大丈夫だよ`


They invoke the school festival as evidence that Rui can now move not only toward the rooftop side but toward `みんなのところ`.


This is a high-value relationship transition in **function**, even if no new mature relationship ID is required. Mizuki is not only Rui's fellow outsider or nostalgic safe person. They can use shared outsider knowledge to evaluate when his current social environment has become safe enough to risk self-expression.


### 6.3 Important asymmetry with Mizuki's own disclosure conflict


Do not use this scene to claim Mizuki believes “just tell everyone; people will accept you.”


That would contradict EVENT_0039.


Mizuki encourages Rui because they have observed **specific evidence**:


- Rui already crossed into broader school participation during the culture festival;
- this cheer group asked for his expertise;
- the captain explicitly values his directing;
- Rui's problem concerns proposing a production idea, not disclosing identity-sensitive guarded information;
- Rui has a concrete safety plan and role authority.


Mizuki therefore distinguishes a situation in which disclosure is warranted from their own unresolved one.


That distinction strengthens, rather than weakens, the EVENT_0039 model of context-sensitive disclosure safety.


### 6.4 Relationship consequence


Preserve `REL-CROSS-MIZUKI-RUI-E0007`, but **strengthen** it from static outsider-kinship/safe-return evidence toward:


`mutual outsider recognition + calibrated social re-entry support`.


Rui has previously functioned as a safe person for Mizuki. EVENT_0058 shows reciprocity: Mizuki now performs the same kind of structural recognition for Rui.


## 7. Rui: fear persists after growth


EVENT_0058 is a major Rui reconstruction source because it rejects a simplistic recovery model.


### 7.1 Joining WxS did not erase the old expectation


Rui tells KAITO in card `0428` that he thought he had already overcome this problem because of Tsukasa and the others. The cheer event proves otherwise. A “cowardly” part remains.


This means later Rui should not be modeled as permanently immune to rejection anxiety merely because he has close friends.


The trigger is specific:


- unfamiliar or only partly familiar peers;
- a proposal that exposes his authentic creative appetite;
- an idea that appears dangerous or excessive before explanation;
- a context where other people's acceptance determines whether the idea can exist.


### 7.2 His fear produces caution, not paralysis alone


KAITO's card dialogue offers an important corrective to Rui's self-criticism. Fear can also make a person careful.


The event supports that empirically. Rui does not simply pitch “jump from the school building.” He works through:


- collision protection;
- lifelines / shock absorption;
- individual physical ability;
- prior experience;
- personalized practice plans;
- maximum-efficiency rehearsal;
- the compressed schedule needed to make the change viable.


His creative risk appetite therefore coexists with serious safety engineering.


### 7.3 Conditions that allow Rui to act through fear


The successful proposal depends on several conditions:


1. **Mizuki names the social fear without mocking it.**
2. **Rui has role legitimacy** as the group's entrusted director.
3. **The captain asks for evidence of confidence and feasibility**, rather than demanding conformity.
4. **Rui can promise safety** and explain rehearsal cost.
5. **The group chooses voluntarily** after hearing the risk.
6. **The project has a recipient-centered purpose** Rui genuinely shares.


This should become a future Rui simulation rule. Under high creative stakes, he may conceal or soften an idea when he anticipates rejection; he is more likely to expose it when trust is specific, competence is recognized, and he can make the risk legible.


### 7.4 The outcome is social, not only artistic


The performance succeeds artistically, but the more important Rui consequence comes afterward.


Ordinary classmates identify the extreme introduction as “something Kamishiro would devise,” initially worry about safety, and then hear the participating students explain that Rui had planned for safety and supported them carefully.


The event therefore lets Rui's eccentricity become **socially legible through trusted testimony** rather than erased.


He does not become normal. His difference becomes compatible with peer trust.
