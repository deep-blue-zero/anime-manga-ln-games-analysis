## 2. Nene: fear is not incapacity


Nene initially reacts to shipwreck/island uncertainty with realistic catastrophic projection: no communications, uncertain rescue, unfamiliar forest, possible animals, food/water insecurity, and discomfort without her ordinary technological environment. This is not evidence of generalized helplessness. She continues tracking concrete risks, catches bad assumptions (notably food safety), and participates despite fear.


The banana sequence is diagnostic. Nene is placed at the physically riskiest point of the human tower, explicitly fears imbalance/falling, notices that the others are also straining, and chooses to extend herself because she wants to contribute rather than remain the only member who has done nothing. Her courage is therefore **relational and task-directed**, not fear extinction.


The monkey-capture sequence sharpens the model. MEIKO reframes stage performance itself as repeated confrontation with fear: uncertain reception, possible failure, returning to stage after withdrawal, improvising when NeneRobo failed. Nene accepts the unfamiliar task because the group makes risk bounded and because prior performance history proves she has already acted while afraid.


**Reconstruction rule:** `unknown/threat -> anticipatory fear and risk scanning -> reassurance becomes useful only when grounded in concrete team support -> contribution motive -> action while fear remains -> post-success relief and updated self-estimate`.


Do not model Nene as suddenly adventurous or fearless after this event.


## 3. Experience transfers into acting


The most important performance result occurs after the survival episode. Rehearsing a princess who must act despite fear, Nene produces what Rui and the others identify as her strongest/most convincing fear performance so far. Nene herself connects the role more directly to the recent experience.


This establishes a useful acting principle for later WxS synthesis:

The event therefore contributes not only relationship/team evidence but performer-method evidence.


## 4. Unknown as threat versus unknown as possibility


Nene explicitly contrasts herself with Emu and Rui. Where they often approach the unknown with curiosity/excitement, Nene's default is more threat-sensitive. Card 0450 turns this into a conscious aspiration: she would like to become better at enjoying or investigating the unknown because her dream will require entering unfamiliar situations.


Rui and Emu correctly resist her self-description as simply cowardly. Their evidence is behavioral: she has repeatedly done frightening things when they matter. Nene's endpoint is modest—she does not claim transformation, but permits the possibility that she is less incapable than she assumes.


This is high-value reconstruction because it separates:


- **fear sensitivity** from **avoidance ceiling**;
- **low novelty appetite** from **low courage**;
- **social shyness** from **competitive/game initiative**.


Linked area `_001` is especially useful: Nene actively hunts for a high-score arcade player because a strong opponent makes games more interesting, showing that a domain she values can reverse her normal social reluctance.

## 6. Rui: perspective-taking as craft


Tsukasa card 0451 contains unusually useful Rui/Tsukasa acting cognition. The exercise forces the actor to distinguish **object as humans name it** from **object as the role could know it**.

`surface observation -> hypothesize another agent's conceptual world -> strip unavailable concepts -> rebuild description from accessible sensory/categories -> use resulting constraint for role imagination`.

## 8. Emu and novelty


Emu's apparent fearlessness should not be reduced to stupidity. She explores aggressively and can create risk, but she also finds food/resources, rapidly builds affiliation even with the monkeys, shares what she gathered once conflict is resolved, and frames ordinary outings as deliberate relationship-building opportunities.


Her novelty orientation is socially generative: she tends to transform unfamiliarity into play, participation, or connection. This contrasts productively with Nene's threat-first perception without establishing one response as universally superior.


## 9. Tsukasa and persistence


Tsukasa continues to organize adversity around duty and show-readiness. Even after the scheduled performance becomes impossible, he rehearses immediately upon waking because an eventual substitute performance may occur. This is consistent with existing performance ethics rather than a new state.


The event also supplies comic counterevidence against reading confidence as competence: he can make bad heuristics about wild mushrooms, exhaust himself chasing monkeys, and remain dramatically loud under absurd conditions. He is persistent and motivational, not infallible.
