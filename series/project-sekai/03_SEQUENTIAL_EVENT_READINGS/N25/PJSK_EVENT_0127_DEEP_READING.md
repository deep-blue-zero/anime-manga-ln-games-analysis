## 2. The application question exposes a missing future

Ena is practicing more efficiently and can identify incremental technical progress, but the art school's university-preparation course forces a decision she cannot reduce to effort. Her father's retrospective exhibition makes professional life look impossibly distant, and Yukihira's question—why she wants art university—reveals that “become better” does not yet supply an owned life direction. `PJSK:event:0127:01:003:0003–0028`; `PJSK:event:0127:02:001:0005–0029`; `PJSK:event:0127:02:004:0002–0023`.

Yukihira responds with an off-site lesson at the father's exhibition. He does not promise admission, talent, or success. The assignment is to look rather than borrow a verdict.

## 3. Seeing requires Ena to suspend inherited explanations

At the exhibition Yukihira distinguishes reported metadata from Ena's own perception: knowing that a peony was seen on the night of her birth does not answer what the painting contains. Ena initially resists because the father's accomplished work intensifies comparison, then deliberately examines it with Rin and Len. Across the chronology she detects a turn from illuminated nights to compressed, desperate work. `PJSK:event:0127:05:001:0013–0038`; `PJSK:event:0127:05:001:0041–0096`.

The rough paintings resemble Ena's own period of compelled, panicked production. That analogy lets her infer suffering without collapsing their situations into identity. In *Peony Blooming at Night* she sees meticulous light held inside a roughly painted darkness and names both pain and possible rescue. `PJSK:event:0127:05:001:0097–0136`; `PJSK:event:0127:06:001:0001–0036`.

## 4. Hidden history revises the image of effortless authority

Yukihira reports that the father once worked without recognition, depended on his wife's support, considered leaving painting after Ena's birth, and made the peony as a presumed final work. He carefully marks the limit of his knowledge: he does not know exactly how the father found his answer. Ena remains with the paintings rather than turning that report into complete biography. `PJSK:event:0127:06:001:0038–0065`; `PJSK:event:0127:07:001:0030–0078`.

The father later supplies first-person authority. He had alternated between work others requested and work others did not understand; holding newborn Ena made protection feel necessary, and the peony gathered despair, joy, love, and the small light she represented. Completing it showed him that he could not live without painting, so he chose to bear rather than escape its difficulty. `PJSK:event:0127:08:001:0021–0071`.

## 5. `E-E0127-01` — an owned vocational commitment

Ena tests the same counterfactual for herself. A life with shopping, conversation, selfies, and occasional easy illustration remains imaginable but unlivable as her life because abandoning serious painting would make her unable to forgive herself. She then states the decision to her father: she will keep drawing and wants to become a painter despite absent talent guarantees and expected pain. `PJSK:event:0127:08:001:0073–0104`.

Advance `E-E0070-01 → E-E0127-01`. EVENT_0070 made technical judgment and first-person aesthetic judgment co-authorities; EVENT_0127 adds a self-authored vocational rule under full uncertainty. The operative is commitment to pursue and prepare, not admission, professional success, stable confidence, healthy pacing, or proof that suffering authenticates art.

## 6. `REL-FAMILY-ENA-FATHER-E0127` — direct knowledge and bounded permission

This is the first reciprocal conversation that makes his artistic struggle and her chosen future mutually usable. `PJSK:event:0127:08:001:0016–0071`; `PJSK:event:0127:08:001:0094–0118`.

Advance `REL-FAMILY-ENA-FATHER-E0014 → REL-FAMILY-ENA-FATHER-E0127`.
