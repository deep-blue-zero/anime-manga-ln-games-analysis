## Executive thesis


`close game/OFFLINE` is not analytically important because gaming reveals a hidden alternate personality. It is important because the game tournament removes many of the narrative supports normally surrounding these characters and makes their micro-decisions legible.


For Nene, gaming is simultaneously expertise, refuge, competitive self-evaluation, social vulnerability, and eventually a form of contribution. The event closes by revising her own earlier shame about using games while unable to perform: gaming had sometimes functioned as escape, but the same accumulated competence can now support friendship, public confidence, ethical action, and other people's enjoyment.


For Toya, exceptional technical skill coexists with a durable prosocial ceiling on optimization. He can dominate a puzzle game with calm precision and withstand deliberate interference, yet in a survival game he instinctively protects familiar people even when doing so harms his own competitive position. His kindness is not lack of competitiveness: once the illegitimate threat is removed, he explicitly ends cooperation and fights Nene seriously.


Akito and Emu provide complementary diagnostic contrasts. Akito enters unfamiliar domains through preparation, live updating, opponent monitoring, and partner protection. Emu enters them through fearless sensorimotor experimentation, extremely rapid embodied learning, and a persistent assumption that shared play should remain fun. Neither is reducible to 'serious' versus 'silly'; both become highly functional competitors through different cognitive routes.


## 1. Tournament structure as a behavioral stress test


The refurbished arcade deliberately chooses heterogeneous activities rather than a conventional esports bracket:


1. print-sticker presentation judged by heterogeneous observers;
2. `ぷよぷよ通`, a high-speed competitive puzzle game;
3. `フィールドバトル`, a team survival/shooter game allowing temporary cooperation and betrayal.


This prevents one narrow skill from determining the whole event. Each round changes the success condition:


- social presentation and recipient modeling;
- procedural speed, planning, error management, and pattern knowledge;
- tactical perception, resource management, coordination, threat assessment, and ethics under adversarial pressure.


The result is unusually valuable for reconstruction because the same people can be compared across domains rather than inferred from a single competence display.


## 2. Nene — expertise without social ease


### 2.1 Technical observation begins before competition


Nene notices the maintenance quality of arcade hardware in concrete terms: button response, aiming calibration, cleaning, and controller usability. She distinguishes a poorly maintained familiar arcade from the refurbished tournament venue almost immediately.


This is not generic 'gamer knowledge.' Her attention is operational and embodied. She knows what bad calibration does to score and survival, tests controls before committing, and notices maintenance cues other patrons may ignore.


Reconstruction rule:


> When Nene enters a domain she knows well, she first evaluates the reliability of the interface/environment before evaluating herself.


### 2.2 Competence does not erase public inhibition


Nene is willing to enter because the prize matters, but strangers watching her play still create tension. She dislikes conspicuous public gaming even though gaming itself is one of her strongest domains. Print-sticker posing is embarrassing enough that she initially resists both the pose and later distribution of the image.


This preserves a useful distinction:


`domain confidence != social exhibitionism`.


She can be technically assertive while remaining personally self-conscious.


### 2.3 Competitive self-evaluation is strict but reality-based


After Toya defeats her decisively at Puyo, Nene does not protect her ego by dismissing him or the game. She identifies the scale of the skill gap, accepts his praise only cautiously, and immediately asks for a future rematch because remaining defeated bothers her.


The card follow-up makes the learning style explicit:


- researches stacking patterns;
- practices independently;
- enters ranked play;
- reads opponent habits and mistakes;
- uses practical disruption rather than copying Toya's exact style;
- accepts Toya's offer to teach fundamentals while understanding actual matches as the fastest learning route.


She privately experiences the interaction as unexpectedly friendship-like. Thus rivalry becomes a socially safer path into cross-unit connection than free-form small talk.


### 2.4 Nene as tactical leader


In `Field Battle`, Nene becomes much more directive:


- teaches Emu how to loot and equip;
- establishes stealth rules;
- tracks sound and likely enemy position;
- manages ammunition and healing resources;
- chooses when to avoid combat and when to engage;
- converts Emu's accidental exposure into a distraction opportunity;
- uses dead angles for headshots;
- recognizes prohibited exploit use;
- proposes temporary cooperation once the rule-violating team becomes the shared threat.


Her language becomes short and functional because the context rewards compressed instructions.


This is a strong simulation rule: in a known technical problem with immediate stakes, Nene's social hesitation can collapse rapidly into precise task leadership.


## 3. Nene — gaming as refuge, shame, and reintegrated competence


The event's most important Nene material comes after the tournament. She tells WxS Miku that when she could not perform and was 'smoldering,' games supported her emotionally. At the same time, she sometimes hated herself for playing so much because she interpreted it as turning away from reality.


The event does not retroactively claim the escape function was imaginary. Instead it adds a second truth:


- games could provide avoidance and refuge;
- the competence accumulated there was real;
- that competence can later be recontextualized into chosen social participation.


At EVENT_0066 she uses gaming to:


- help a struggling arcade regain attention;
- make an unfamiliar event exciting for spectators;
- protect fair play;
- form a new gaming friendship with Toya;
- teach Emu;
- win something that benefits the rest of WxS;
- create smiles in a public setting.


Reconstruction formulation:


`withdrawal-era coping / self-judged escapism -> retained genuine interest and expertise -> socially reintegrated competence with contribution value`.


Guardrail: this does not mean all avoidance becomes healthy simply because the same hobby later becomes useful.


## 4. Emu — embodied learning and fun-first cognition


### 4.1 Extremely fast sensorimotor adaptation


Emu repeatedly performs better than her own lack of formal game knowledge would predict.


Examples include:


- producing a substantial Puyo chain because she played repeatedly with Shosuke;
- evading shooter fire through rapid jumping before understanding formal tactics;
- adapting quickly to racing, fighting, crane, and rhythm games after brief explanation;
- clearing a high rhythm-game difficulty on first exposure with only a few misses.


Her competence route is not Nene's explicit systems knowledge. It is closer to:


`demonstration / immediate feedback -> bodily trial -> rapid pattern acquisition -> enthusiastic repetition`.


### 4.2 Low embarrassment cost


The print-sticker round shows Emu treating a socially awkward task as straightforward creative play. She uses `わんだほい`, Phoenix Wonderland associations, decorative effects, and expressive posing because her goal is to make the judges smile.


Her recipient model is simple but sincere: if the image carries visible fun, some viewers will respond to it. The three judges' different reactions also preserve the limit of this method: recipients are heterogeneous, and no single affective strategy guarantees universal approval.


### 4.3 Fun is an ethical assumption, not mere frivolity


When cheating emerges, Emu's first formulation is not about procedural legality but the shared emotional contract of play: games should be something people enjoy together, and cheating makes other people sad.


That is consistent with her wider smile-oriented ethics without reducing her to naivety. She can accept tactical instruction, competition, winning, and losing; what she resists is a mode of competition that destroys the shared activity.


## 5. Toya — elite procedural skill and prosocial limits


### 5.1 Hidden game expertise


The tournament reveals that Toya's arcade history extends well beyond crane games. His ranking handle and Puyo performance indicate genuinely high-level procedural competence.


His puzzle-game behavior includes:


- extremely rapid but accurate placement;
- deep chain planning;
- calm tolerance of delayed resolution;
- enough confidence to remove his hands from the controls once a planned chain is locked in;
- thirteen-chain execution even after deliberate physical interference.


His own explanation is characteristically unromantic: it is simply accumulated practice, comparable to singing.


### 5.2 Serious competition without ego hostility


Toya praises Nene's defense after defeating her, accepts her rematch request immediately, and later offers to teach foundational patterns because he wants a strong rival with whom he can improve.


He does not treat instruction as surrendering advantage. Competition and mutual development are compatible.


### 5.3 Prosocial ceiling on optimization


In the survival game, Toya saves Nene and Emu despite their being direct opponents. He explicitly recognizes the strategic irrationality and says the genre may not suit him because seeing someone he knows about to be shot feels bad.


This is not inability to compete. After the cheating team is eliminated, Toya tells Nene the temporary alliance is over and that he will no longer hold back.


The more precise rule is:


> Toya can compete intensely inside a legitimate frame, but salient interpersonal harm can override local win optimization; once the legitimacy problem is removed, full competition resumes.


### 5.4 Boundary after interference


When a competitor deliberately spills a drink onto Toya's hand during Puyo, Toya completes the game rather than escalating emotionally. Afterward he allows the tournament to proceed so the event will not be ruined, but gives a clear warning that there will be no next time.


This supports a recurring Toya pattern:


`low-reactivity surface + continued task execution + later explicit boundary`.


It should not be modeled as passivity.


### 5.5 Gaming skill does not compete with his chosen musical identity


Akito's card records a professional-gaming scout approaching Toya after the tournament. Toya declines because he already has something he must do. This is diagnostically useful: extraordinary aptitude in a domain does not automatically acquire motivational priority.


Capability and commitment remain separate variables.


## 6. Akito — preparation, mask control, and partner protection


### 6.1 Public/private register switching is legible to outsiders


Emu notices immediately that Akito's participant-introduction politeness differs from his ordinary register. Nene already understands that this is simply how he behaves with patrons/outsiders.


The event therefore supplies clean cross-unit evidence that Akito's social mask is observable and context-conditioned rather than a secret inner personality.


### 6.2 Preparation for an unfamiliar domain


Akito is not a habitual gamer at Toya's level. Once he agrees to enter, however, he practices several relevant games because Toya is looking forward to the event and because he wants the performance-related prize as well.


His Puyo play is correspondingly intermediate rather than expert:


- conservative clearing;
- basic opponent-pressure response;
- rapid improvement during live play;
- frustration when cascading pressure exceeds his learned control.


He later finds shooter/survival play more intuitive.


### 6.3 Adversarial monitoring while Toya performs


Akito detects the suspicious team's behavior before the proof is obvious. When one member approaches Toya during his match, Akito intervenes socially before physically escalating, offering to send a video afterward while preventing access to Toya's space.


When the drink spill becomes obviously intentional, his anger spikes immediately.


Reconstruction rule:


> When a partner is task-locked, Akito externalizes threat monitoring that the partner cannot spare attention for.


This is a high-value relationship behavior beyond generic 'protective friend.'


## 7. Nene–Toya — rivalry as a low-friction friendship channel

2. decisive skill comparison;

4. explicit request for rematch;
5. temporary cooperation against illegitimate play;
6. immediate return to legitimate rivalry;
7. post-event practice together;

Provisional relationship formulation:


`shared technical interest + serious non-hostile rivalry + reciprocal skill recognition + bounded mutual help -> low-pressure gaming friendship`.


Because neither WxS nor VBS yet has a mature unit foundation, this remains a deferred route rather than a mature longitudinal relationship state.


## 8. Competition, legitimacy, and the cheating team


The antagonistic team matters because they are not depicted as incompetent people who cheat because they cannot play. They are genuinely skilled and have drifted toward exploitative victory-seeking.


The event supplies a small ethical model of competitive play:


- difficulty and loss are part of the pleasure structure;
- legitimate constraints make achievement meaningful;
- exploits and physical interference remove the meaning from victory;
- restoring ordinary fair competition can restore enjoyment.


Nene's formulation is especially important: winning a two-versus-one situation without exploits would have produced real pride because the result could be owned as one's own accomplishment.


Thus the event distinguishes:


`outcome possession` from `achievement ownership`.


This aligns with the broader franchise interest in self-authored effort without importing it into N25 absent a source bridge.


## 9. Arcade as community infrastructure


The arcade is not a neutral contest venue. The younger owner rebuilt a declining inherited business because it carried family memory. The tournament is an attempt to reintroduce the space to the neighborhood.


By the end:


- spectators discover the venue;
- younger patrons say they may return;
- former cheaters become ordinary regulars in follow-up material;
- Akito and Toya compare the venue's lively but comfortable social atmosphere to Vivid Street;
- Nene and Emu receive a year of free play, encouraging continuity rather than a one-off prize interaction.


This makes EVENT_0066 a useful companion to EVENT_0064. Both treat a commercial urban space as a relational environment sustained by repeated participation, memory, competence, and informal social recognition.


Do not collapse the two spaces: Vivid Street has a deeper intergenerational artistic history, while the arcade's community is newly reconstituting itself.
