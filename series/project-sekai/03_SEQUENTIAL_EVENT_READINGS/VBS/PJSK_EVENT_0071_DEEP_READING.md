## 2. Toya's problem is not musical ignorance


Toya begins with deep musical competence. He can hear his father's meticulous harmonic construction and the melancholy carried by the work. He can write clean, well-arranged melodies. The problem is that Vivid BAD SQUAD needs a track that makes performers feel compelled to sing and gives their bodies/imagination somewhere to go.


Toya experiences a gap between **correct construction** and **self-authored communicative force**.


That gap should not be reconstructed as lack of talent. It is a conflict between an inherited discipline built around faithful interpretation and the producer role's need to decide what Toya himself wants a new track to do.


## 3. Sampling becomes permissible through a respect model


Toya initially experiences sampling as potentially destructive because classical training taught him to preserve composer intent. Soma does not answer by rejecting respect. He redefines sampling as a visible form of respect:
- one borrows because the source matters;
- influence is acknowledged rather than hidden;
- existing material can become part of something new;
- everyday sound can also enter composition.


This is a high-value reconstruction correction. Toya does not develop by becoming anti-classical. He can reclassify an inherited moral constraint and reuse its underlying value.


## 4. Why compose at all? Contribution desire is the engine


Soma's strongest question is not technical: **why does Toya need to be the one who writes the track?**


Toya's answer is relational. Akito is waiting for his music. Kohane is growing rapidly. An can pull an audience. Akito can support the whole stage while preserving his own performance. Toya has become confident enough to stand beside them as a performer, but he now wants to be useful in forms he has not yet mastered.


This introduces an important Toya rule:


`belonging/security -> awareness of companions' growth -> desire to contribute beyond current role -> self-imposed new-domain challenge`.


Soma calls the desire greedy without treating greed as morally suspect. Wanting more ways to matter can be prosocial.


## 5. Kohane provides the first successful recipient model


Kohane independently begins track-making for An for essentially the same reason: she wants to contribute and to give form to gratitude.


Technically, her draft is rough. Toya says so. Yet he can hear it as distinctly Kohane's because its samples, gentleness, and excitement carry the history of An bringing her into music.


Kohane can explicitly state what the track contains: gratitude, happiness, excitement, and the life opened by meeting An.


This becomes the model Toya lacked. The question is not only **what feeling should the audience experience?** It is **whose heart am I trying to reach, and what do I need that person to receive from me?**


## 6. Classical training is reclaimed rather than discarded


VBS KAITO identifies the skill Toya just used: Toya can infer a creator's intended feeling from structure and background because classical study trained him to research and read a composer's circumstances and intentions.


The childhood form of that training was coercive. The competence itself is not therefore fraudulent.


Toya recognizes that work performed under an oppressive relationship can leave real skills that later become self-authored tools.


Deferred reconstruction rule:


`inherited/coercive training -> retain genuine competence -> reject imposed destination -> redeploy competence toward chosen relationships and goals`.


This is one of the event's most important anti-flattening constraints.

This fulfills Arata's earlier criterion: an effective track can make a performer feel **this is the song I have to perform**.
