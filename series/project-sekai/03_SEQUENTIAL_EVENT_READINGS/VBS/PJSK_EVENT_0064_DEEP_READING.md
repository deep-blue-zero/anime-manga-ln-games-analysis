## 2. Core causal reconstruction — An and Vivid Street


### 2.1 Childhood An is already high-agency, socially fluent, and musically ambitious


The flashback does not construct a timid child who is later transformed by street culture. Young An is already energetic, practices for hours, greets adult residents easily, absorbs recommendations, boasts that she will surpass her father, and treats music as an ordinary social language.


This matters for reconstruction: Vivid Street develops an existing high-energy social/competitive disposition rather than creating it ex nihilo.


### 2.2 Nagi is simultaneously sister-figure, coach, competitor, and civic model


Nagi's relationship to An has several separable functions:


- affectionate older-sister intimacy and play;
- precise embodied vocal coaching;
- competitive play and teasing;
- willingness to redirect conflict into music rather than domination;
- routine responsiveness to other people's problems;
- explicit explanation of why the street's people matter.


An later states that she knew many versions of Nagi — kind, funny, playful — while the performer Nagi remained distant enough to function as an object of awe. This creates a key developmental asymmetry: An has long wanted to become *like* Nagi, but had not previously translated admiration into an explicit desire to *surpass* her.


### 2.3 Adult responsiveness creates a childhood attachment injury without implying lack of love


The repeated broken-practice promises are not trivial background. From a child's perspective the pattern is legible as:


`I am promised time -> another adult/community need appears -> my adults leave -> the street repeatedly wins`


An eventually states the child-level conclusion directly: if her father really cared, he would keep the promise and stay with her.


The event refuses a cheap correction in which the child is simply "wrong." Nagi first validates that An has reason to be angry, apologizes, and only then supplies missing causal context. The adults genuinely love An *and* repeatedly choose obligations to other people. Both facts remain true.


### 2.4 The runaway episode is a belonging crisis


An's declaration that she hates her father and everyone in the street is produced by accumulated displacement, not by an underlying anti-social preference. Her flight to the unfinished Kamiyama building is diagnostically useful: pride and anger initially sustain independence, but fear, hunger, sensory discomfort, family food memories, and loneliness rapidly expose the attachment substrate beneath the rebellion.


Nagi finds her not through omniscience but through a model of An's behavior: An is unlikely to ask for help, will seek a hidden place, and had previously expressed curiosity about the construction site. This is strong evidence for Nagi's person-specific observational competence.


### 2.5 Nagi's intervention converts "the street steals my family" into "the street is extended relational infrastructure"


Nagi explains the street through concrete reciprocal histories rather than abstract civic rhetoric:


- residents taught and encouraged Nagi's singing;
- the street mediated Ken/Taiga rivalry into partnership;
- residents cared about An before birth;
- local adults helped her family materially;
- a pierced young resident once returned a lost toddler An;
- another resident's relationship with young An influenced her life direction.


Young An then reinterprets the same people who had seemed to take her father away as people who also continually hold *her* in a web of care.


The durable mechanism is therefore not generic community sentiment. It is:


`repeated distributed recognition + practical aid + musical correction + remembered personal history + reciprocal obligation -> place attachment as relational security`.


## 3. The meaning of `街を見ろ`


EVENT_0064 does **not** close Taiga's instruction to a single answer. It gives several constrained hypotheses.


### 3.1 The city is made by people, not venues


An explicitly reasons that Vivid Street would be a different city if the people were different. The event repeatedly moves attention away from buildings and toward residents, performers, shopkeepers, audiences, informal mentors, children, and memory.


### 3.2 Performance atmosphere is socially generated


VBS Virtual Singer material reinforces that event "air" is produced by people whose purposes differ: competitive events create a different atmosphere from collaborative events because performers enter with different stakes and intentions.


This supports a broader candidate model:


`people + histories + motives + mutual expectations + audience memory -> locally specific performance atmosphere`.


### 3.3 Walking the city is insufficient if treated as sightseeing


Kohane recreates Taiga's walking route with the others. They collect facts about shops and residents but do not suddenly solve the instruction. The negative result is valuable: `街を見ろ` is not satisfied by visually touring landmarks or memorizing trivia.


### 3.4 Toya and Arata prevent a sentimental overread


Toya says he loves the city because it gave him a place to belong, music, Akito, and people who accepted him through a single shared interest. He is therefore evidence that the street can become "home" through chosen participation rather than birthplace.


Arata, by contrast, cannot straightforwardly say he loves it. The street contains RAD WEEKEND, Soma, hard training, loss, rupture, departure, and uncomfortable stasis. His ambivalence demonstrates that the city's meaning is relational and historical, not automatically positive.


**Status of `街を見ろ`: OPEN, materially narrowed.**


## 4. An reconstruction model


### 4.1 Core rule — relationally grounded local confidence


An's ease in Vivid Street is not context-free extroversion. It rests on unusually dense local knowledge and reciprocal recognition. She knows residents, businesses, histories, tastes, routes, reputations, and unwritten norms; the residents know her history in return.


This predicts especially high social confidence when:


- she can act as host/guide;
- she has local knowledge others lack;
- the setting is music-adjacent;
- the relationship can be folded into an existing network of belonging.


### 4.2 Admiration can suppress competitive framing


An has no difficulty declaring RAD WEEKEND a target, yet recognizes that she had not internally treated Nagi herself as someone to surpass. Her admiration preserved Nagi as a protected apex image.


The card resolves this into a new VBS-deferred decision rule:


`admired model -> initially imitation/aspiration -> recognition that continued growth beside Kohane and toward the shared goal requires converting reverence into competitive reach`.


This is distinct from EVENT_0060, where An overperformed an admired *role*. Here the issue is not impersonation but an internal ceiling placed around a beloved person.


### 4.3 Attachment to the city is active, not decorative


An's city-love appears behaviorally as:


- concern about local residents;
- immediate conversational familiarity;
- willingness to help;
- pride in showing the city to Kohane;
- desire for Kohane to love it too;
- tendency to translate new VBS activity back into the street's social ecosystem.


### 4.4 Ordinary-life evidence


The envelope also adds:


- large breakfast / high-energy eating pattern;
- strong interest in games and competitive play;
- old gaming history with Nagi;
- fashion shopping and rapid "use it now" enthusiasm;
- running/fitness initiative;
- strong physical-affection response toward Kohane when emotionally pleased;
- ghost fear plausibly linked to the childhood construction-site episode, without making that episode the sole cause.


## 5. Nagi reconstruction model


Nagi is unusually diagnostic despite being historical/absent from present VBS life.


### 5.1 Social conflict method


When two rappers are approaching physical violence, Nagi:


1. reads the actual status injury beneath the fight;
2. uses humor without minimizing seriousness;
3. redirects the conflict into the shared domain both care about;
4. creates a public but legitimate rematch structure;
5. praises real ability afterward;
6. still requires repair toward people harmed by the disruption.


This is not simple peacemaking. It is **identity-respecting redirection**: preserve competitive pride while changing the medium through which it is expressed.


### 5.2 Coaching method


Her vocal coaching is concise, embodied, immediately testable, permissive of failure, and followed by positive reinforcement. She provides enough mechanism for An to try the skill rather than only supplying evaluation.


### 5.3 Care method


With runaway An, Nagi validates grievance first, then adds missing context. She does not demand forgiveness, deny the broken promises, or claim that adult obligations automatically justify the child's hurt.


### 5.4 Limitation/guardrail


Nagi's civic altruism also participates in the pattern that hurts An. The story therefore does not authorize modeling her as costless benevolence. Her care for the broader street can create opportunity costs inside intimate relationships.


## 6. Ken / Taiga / older-generation structure


The older generation is shown as embedded civic infrastructure rather than isolated celebrity performers. They advise younger musicians, help venues, intervene in disputes, understand contract/rights issues, substitute for failed headline acts, and function as locally trusted adults.


Ken's parenting model is materially complicated:


- genuine pride, affection, coaching, food/family warmth, and worry;
- repeated failures to protect promised one-on-one time from community obligations;
- belief that distributed street mentorship is itself valuable for An.


The event does not resolve whether that tradeoff is optimal; it shows why An ultimately interprets it positively.


The final Ken reaction to An speaking excitedly about Nagi is deliberately guarded. Do not infer content beyond observable hesitation/silence.

## 8. Kohane reconstruction increments

Her attachment therefore develops through a different path from An's:


`encounter -> chosen musical participation -> relationships -> historical curiosity -> place attachment`.


This makes her a useful comparison case for what "belonging to the city" can mean.
