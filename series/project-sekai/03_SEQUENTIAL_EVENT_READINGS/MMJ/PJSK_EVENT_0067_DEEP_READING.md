## 2. Central analytical thesis


The event's strongest distinction is between **technically correct performance** and **performance whose movement is causally generated by a performer-specific desire to reach someone**.


Minori's first major improvement is real but unstable because she is unconsciously converging on Haruka's elegant movement grammar. Airi recognizes the problem because she once made a structurally similar mistake while competing with Shizuku: she treated another idol's visible advantage as something she should reproduce rather than asking what could make *Airi* compelling.


The event therefore establishes a performance-development rule:


> `technical model -> imitation can improve form -> imitation eventually exposes a person-fit ceiling -> identify the performer’s own audience-directed impulse -> let that impulse alter motion naturally -> refine the resulting movement technically without erasing its causal origin`


The final Minori is not simply “more polished.” In several details she is less tidy than the imitation phase, but more compelling because the movement now reads as Minori trying to make a specific distant viewer notice, receive, and share her energy.


The event emphatically does **not** claim that technique is unnecessary. Airi immediately continues correcting Minori's balance and axis. The point is that polish should develop *around* authentic expressive causality rather than replace it.


## 3. Airi as performer, coach, and producer


### 3.1 The training camp is also a production


Airi's proposed live-streamed training camp is not an impulsive publicity stunt. She designs a structure serving several simultaneous purposes:


- increase physical and dance competence;
- create group coordination problems rather than only solo drills;
- expose Minori to unplanned situations where rote performance cannot carry her;
- give viewers an entertaining object rather than merely broadcasting repetitive practice;
- produce promotional visibility for the coming solo concert;
- preserve sufficient safety and logistical control for the experiment to be sustainable.


Airi's older variety-show experience matters here as transferable production knowledge even where she does not romanticize the circumstances in which she learned it. She contacts a former `ワッハッハTV` broadcast writer for professional consultation instead of assuming that having once appeared on television makes her a complete producer. Being quoted and treated as an ordinary professional client is itself meaningful to her: a formerly exploited junior talent can now purchase expertise on her own terms.


### 3.2 Physical drills as performance diagnostics


The weighted banana-boat exercise combines balance, core stability, destabilization, and the requirement to maintain an idol-facing expression. It is not generic conditioning. The task asks whether bodily control survives when the performer cannot devote full attention to looking composed.


The modified team watermelon exercise similarly converts entertainment into a coordination test. Members must trust one another, communicate under restricted information, and turn incomplete verbal/nonverbal guidance into action. Shizuku's well-established directionality weakness remains real, but the event shows an important countercondition: when another person physically guides her and the spatial problem is made relational rather than abstract, she can move quickly and competently.


### 3.3 Airi's own failure history is part of her coaching authority


Airi's flashback with Shizuku explains why she recognizes imitation so quickly. Young Airi sincerely believed that an idol's heart and effort should matter, yet repeatedly watched attention accumulate around Shizuku's effortless aura. Her response was not passive resentment. She experimented with cosmetics, fashion, nutrition, physical training, and presentation in an attempt to obtain the “sparkle” she thought Shizuku possessed.


This history should not be flattened into either “Airi was jealous” or “Airi learned effort beats talent.” The useful reconstruction structure is:


`credible comparison target -> visible advantage appears causally decisive -> intense compensatory experimentation -> frustration when imitation cannot reproduce the effect -> search for a person-specific performance route`


Airi's later coaching authority is partly the authority of someone who has already exhausted the wrong strategy.


### 3.4 Audience modeling: “I want this person to notice me”


Airi's decisive intervention is to make Minori imagine actual spectators rather than perform toward an abstract correctness standard. The second-floor audience is especially useful because distance creates a concrete communication problem: subtle movement that looks elegant in a mirror may not tell a far-away viewer that the performer has seen and is reaching toward them.


When Minori imagines a particular fan and genuinely wants that person to receive her energy, her body begins making larger, more direct choices. She lifts differently, reaches farther, changes emphasis, and becomes visually legible as *Minori*.


The phrase structure `私を見て` should not be reduced to narcissism. In this context it means something closer to:


> “Notice me so that what I am trying to give you can actually arrive.”


The self-display and the recipient orientation are inseparable.


## 4. Minori: upward comparison, imitation, and authentic enlargement


Minori is hardworking enough that correction produces visible improvement quickly. The danger is precisely that she is capable of copying a strong model. Haruka is physically and professionally close, highly admired, and technically elegant; Minori therefore drifts toward Haruka-like movement without deciding to impersonate Haruka.


This creates a useful reconstruction distinction:


- **poor learning** would be failure to observe or reproduce anything;
- **technical learning** is successful acquisition of useful form;
- **identity-preserving performance development** additionally requires that acquired form remain subordinate to Minori's own expressive motive.


Minori is especially responsive when the task is translated into a prosocial goal. “Fix your form” helps, but “make that far-away person know you are reaching them” reorganizes the performance more deeply.


Her final state in this event remains unfinished. Airi continues to identify instability and excess forward weight. The important result is not mastery but discovery of a viable developmental direction.


## 5. Airi's endurance-performance card as cross-validation


Shizuku's associated card supplies unusually strong independent support for Airi's theory. During a long endurance-dance television segment, Airi remains at full visible intensity even after others begin rationing energy. She later explains that her regulating image was not simply “I must win.” She imagines the future broadcast audience and refuses to make the person watching receive an obviously miserable performance.


This matters because it demonstrates that Airi's advice to Minori is not retrospective coaching rhetoric. Audience imagination has functioned as a durable self-regulation strategy in Airi's own body under fatigue.


Reconstruction rule:


`physical depletion -> retrieve audience/stage image -> convert recipient salience into motivational energy -> sustain outward communicative commitment`


Guardrail: this does not imply limitless stamina or that audience orientation makes overwork harmless.


## 6. Shizuku: competence and directional weakness can coexist


Shizuku remains physically capable, flexible, poised, and experienced. Her modeling history contributes posture, line awareness, and bodily control. None of that resolves her severe directionality problem.


The blindfold/team material is therefore diagnostically useful because it shows that the weakness is not generalized motor incompetence. With immediate interpersonal guidance, she can act quickly. Future reconstruction should not make “Shizuku gets lost” erase her real physical and professional competence.


Her historical view of Airi is also important. She recognizes that Airi's stage presence is not a consolation prize for lacking Shizuku's visual aura; it is a real, hard-won performance strength that Shizuku herself admires.


## 7. Haruka: procedural competence and distributed support


Haruka contributes mature practical knowledge around nutrition, training, pacing, and bodily preparation. She can physically demonstrate, explain, and stabilize the others without needing to occupy the central interpretive role.


This is particularly useful in contrast with Airi. Haruka is the strongest immediate technical model for Minori, yet Airi is the more relevant diagnostic coach because she has personal experience with the exact failure mode Minori is entering. Expertise is therefore **problem-matched**, not globally ranked.


The post-event labor distribution further matters: Minori, Haruka, and Shizuku take responsibility for afterparty planning rather than leaving Airi to produce, train, promote, and administrate everything. Airi accepts the distribution. Her competence should not be reconstructed as an inability to delegate.


## 8. Private leisure versus professionalized leisure


The linked area material preserves lower-temperature evidence that would be easy to lose in a purely developmental summary.


Airi's history means pools, games, food, and other ostensibly recreational spaces can carry professional associations. The group's plan for a private pool outing is therefore meaningful not because “Airi learns to have fun” in a global sense, but because an activity often experienced as work can be reclaimed as chosen leisure with companions.


The fan recreation of the “success prayer smoothie” likewise shows a performer/audience ritual becoming participatory culture. Airi's instinct is neither to reject the fan version nor leave it untouched: she wants to make the ritual healthier and tastier, turning received fan practice back into producer-side iteration.


Airi's concern that the Virtual Singers may overdo balloon inflation also preserves her ordinary safety-monitoring register below the more glamorous producer role.
