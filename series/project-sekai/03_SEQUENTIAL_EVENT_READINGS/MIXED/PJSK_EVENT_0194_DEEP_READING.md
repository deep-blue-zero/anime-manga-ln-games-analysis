## 2. Participation is chosen, and the rules produce the conflict

Tsukasa reports learning from an immersive play in which he could observe a character’s quick note-taking at close range. Rui has watched shows during the recharge after TCT. Their initial conversation is already active professional curiosity, not evidence that this game first teaches them to observe performance. The newly formed Real Game Club attracts them with a Valentine adventure. Rui has helped with its devices and underlying programming, not authored the whole game. Five-person entry turns a prospective pair into a mixed group when Mizuki, Toya and Akito arrive. `PJSK:event:0194:01:002:0002–0011`; `PJSK:event:0194:01:003:0016–0047`.

The invitation is not a frictionless collective wish. Toya wants an unfamiliar experience and Mizuki wants company, while Akito refuses participation and does not accept that he must do everything Toya chooses. The rare chocolate prize changes his answer. Toya notices the orange peel’s possible fit with coffee; Akito consents reluctantly. His specific reward motive and continuing discomfort remain important when he later performs competently. Wonderland Luka observes the activity through the local phone context after her nap; she is not publicly embodied as another school participant. `PJSK:event:0194:01:003:0050–0086`; `PJSK:event:0194:01:003:0095–0096`.

The game uses wearable devices for assigned roles, instructions, HP and MP. Zero health or failure to finish within an hour ends the attempt. Dramatic names and acting modify attacks, with the opponent actors judging the effect. The staff call that fair; the players recognize its subjectivity. Rui becomes a wizard, Mizuki a thief, Toya a priest and Akito the hero. Tsukasa privately receives a comic idler role concealing a member of the villain’s army. Its optional betrayal can wipe out the party, yield an individual win and extra cake, or result in his own removal if discovered. He is also free to remain loyal. These are rules of an actual school game, not new magical abilities or predetermined real identities. `PJSK:event:0194:02:001:0005–0034`; `PJSK:event:0194:02:001:0043–0084`.

Tsukasa initially rejects betraying his companions, then finds the role dramatically attractive because it can change the ending. He privately explains his predicament to Wonderland Luka. Her advice treats the game as an improvisation in which his response can follow the situation; she does not order a particular betrayal or know a fixed outcome. His desire for an exciting part is visible before any benevolent final interpretation. `PJSK:event:0194:02:001:0085–0117`.

The first village is a whiteboard and the monsters use visibly modest props. Tsukasa responds by committing himself to the part rather than withholding effort until the production meets professional standards. Akito’s plain attack fails the acting requirement and he must try a named move. Success produces praise and teasing that he does not simply welcome. The rule makes participation expressive, while his response preserves the difference between performing and wanting to be made a spectacle. `PJSK:event:0194:03:001:0002–0024`; `PJSK:event:0194:03:001:0038–0078`.
