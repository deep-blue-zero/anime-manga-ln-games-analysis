# Project SEKAI — EVENT_0007 Deep Reading
## KAMIKOU FESTIVAL！ — Mizuki, chosen belonging, and movement from rooftop observer to participant

## 1. Analytical responsibility and triage decision

`EVENT_0007 — KAMIKOU FESTIVAL！` is the first post-main-story mixed event in the N25 longitudinal sequence that produces a durable global state transition for Mizuki Akiyama. It therefore receives a full deep reading rather than a focused integration checkpoint.

The event is mixed-unit in cast and setting. Its immediate social field includes Mizuki, An, Kohane, Akito, Toya, Rui, Tsukasa, Nene, and several Wonderlands×Showtime Virtual Singer/contextual presences. Ena is physically absent for most of the festival, but remains a major relational bridge because Mizuki looks for her, recognizes Akito through her, describes the Ena relationship to Akito, and later brings the festival experience back into the whole Nightcord group. Kanade and Mafuyu are likewise absent from the event core but become important in the associated Mizuki card, where Mizuki explicitly imagines future ordinary-life participation with all four N25 members.

The event's principal N25 question is not simply whether Mizuki has fun at a school festival. It is:

> **Can Mizuki participate in collective school and social life without surrendering the self-authored presentation that has made school a site of scrutiny and pressure?**

The answer is a qualified yes. EVENT_0007 does not resolve the underlying stigma, disclosure problem, school-attendance instability, or sensitivity to being categorized. Instead, it establishes a new behavioral possibility: Mizuki can accept belonging when it is chosen, relationally safe, and compatible with self-authored difference. The class T-shirt sequence makes this unusually explicit. Mizuki first refuses the standardized group marker, later reveals pain around not simply being able to belong, and finally accepts the shirt only after claiming the right to alter it so that it becomes cute in Mizuki's own way.

This is why the release is **Tier A — Developmental / I3 — State-changing** rather than Tier C characterization. The decisive evidence is not a single happy afternoon. It is the persistence of the change after the festival: card `0139` has Mizuki describe the present as enjoyable, identify An as a friend who feels safe despite incomplete disclosure, treat N25 as people close to the category of `仲間`, propose future offline festival activity with all of them, and privately consider helping with next year's preparations so that the others can enjoy it.

### Governing delta

Before EVENT_0007, school is primarily modeled for Mizuki as a location of scrutiny, norm enforcement, strategic absence, and selective social contact. Mizuki has chosen relationships and strong social skill, but the main-story foundation leaves their school self comparatively defensive and structurally outside ordinary group belonging.

After EVENT_0007:

- scrutiny and disclosure risk still exist;
- irregular attendance remains real;
- Mizuki does not become generally conformist or school-enthusiastic;
- but school and collective events are no longer modeled only as hostile territory;
- chosen people can convert a threatening setting into a tolerable or enjoyable one;
- Mizuki can accept shared-group symbols while modifying them rather than erasing difference;
- N25 becomes imaginable as an ordinary-life companionship, not only a creative/crisis relationship;
- future school participation becomes something Mizuki may proactively choose for relational reasons.

The resulting global state transition is:

> `MZ-M3 → MZ-E0007-01 — provisional social re-entry / belonging-without-assimilation state`.

## 3. Starting state: avoidance is self-protection, not lack of social appetite

Episode 1 immediately corrects an easy but shallow reconstruction of Mizuki's school behavior.

An calls because Mizuki has forgotten that the Kamiyama festival is happening. Mizuki's initial response is casual, but the moment attendance becomes a real option the source supplies the actual constraint: memories of students whispering that Mizuki is “the rumored kid” and asking why Mizuki dresses that way. The internal conclusion is not “school events are boring.” It is that renewed staring, commentary, and unwanted attention would be troublesome.

This distinction matters. The main-story foundation already established that Mizuki possesses unusually clear self-authored aesthetic preference. They are not unsure why they dress as they do. The threat is external: other people insist on making the presentation into a social problem requiring explanation, normalization, or classification.

An responds in a notably competent way. She does not demand that Mizuki disclose the fear. She says that the festival contains many costumes and unusually flashy clothes, so someone would have to dress very conspicuously to become the center of attention. Mizuki correctly reads the subtext:

> `（……杏、ボクに気つかってくれてるな。 『今日なら大丈夫だよ』って）`

An is saying, in effect, *today the environment gives you cover*. Mizuki then decides that continuing to drag the old experiences around would be uncool and chooses to attend.

This establishes a decision rule that should enter later reconstruction:

> Mizuki's withdrawal from a setting can be reduced when the **identity threat is lowered without demanding self-erasure**.

That is different from reassurance like “nobody will judge you” or “just ignore them.” An changes the practical social conditions and leaves the decision to Mizuki.

The episode also shows that Mizuki's social appetite is intact. Once present, they immediately notice cute food, enjoy the festival atmosphere, converse energetically, and adapt easily to new people. There is no evidence that Mizuki needs to be taught how to socialize. The problem is access to social spaces under conditions of scrutiny.

### Kohane and voluntary stepping aside

When Kohane arrives, Mizuki quickly infers that Kohane may be nervous because an unfamiliar person is present and that Kohane probably came because she wanted to spend the festival with An. Mizuki yields space.

This is important because it prevents a second flattening: “Mizuki avoids because they assume everyone rejects them.” Here Mizuki is accurately reading another person's interpersonal desire and voluntarily making room. Their social sensitivity is high. They can withdraw for prosocial reasons as well as defensive ones.

Episode 8 later confirms An noticed what happened and understands that Mizuki stepped away for Kohane's sake. Mizuki tries to deny the interpretation lightly; An does not turn it into a confrontation. This is part of why An becomes a safe relationship: she notices Mizuki's social maneuvers without forcing them into confession.

## 4. N25 enters Mizuki's imagination before it enters the festival

Episode 2 is deceptively important for the N25 group model. After seeing Rui with Tsukasa and Nene, Mizuki wanders past a haunted house and begins imagining how the other Nightcord members would react.

- Ena would probably be frightened.
- Mafuyu might not react visibly.
- Kanade's reaction is uncertain enough that Mizuki wants to see it.

Then the thought consolidates:

> `（……ニーゴのみんなで文化祭まわったら、 どんな感じなんだろうな）`

This is not yet a plan. It is more important than that in one sense: it is an **ordinary-life projection**.

The main-story foundation established N25 as an anonymous production circle that became an embodied relationship because of crisis. EVENT_0002 further established a candid-affect creative container and real-world outing capacity. EVENT_0007 shows Mizuki spontaneously carrying those people into an unrelated mundane social imagination. They are not thinking only “what would everyone contribute to the song?” or “how can we help Mafuyu?” They are wondering what it would be like to go through a haunted house together.

That is the first signal that the eventual group-state change will be about **ordinary companionship**.

The same episode also gives the first Rui contrast. Mizuki observes Rui practicing happily with other people and thinks his expression has softened relative to the past. The realization is simple:

> Rui found companions.

Mizuki chooses not to interrupt. This response contains warmth without possessiveness. Their prior bond was partly built from mutual outsiderhood; Mizuki does not require Rui to remain isolated in order for that bond to retain meaning.

## 5. Ena's absence and presence: the sibling bridge to Akito

Ena is not a core participant in the festival event, but the story repeatedly positions her as a bridge through which Mizuki enters another social cluster.

Mizuki hears the name “Shinonome Akito,” combines the surname with the first name, notices a family resemblance in atmosphere, and directly approaches him. When Akito confirms he is Ena's brother, Mizuki introduces the Ena relationship as:

> `絵名とは……まぁ、それなりに深い仲って感じ？`

The wording is playful and deliberately imprecise, but it is still notable. Mizuki is willing to describe the Ena bond to Ena's brother as something with real depth.

Mizuki also admits that if Ena had attended, they would have liked to tour the festival together. This matters for the later N25-group interpretation: the eventual “let's all go next time” proposal is not manufactured only in the associated card after the fact. The desire for Ena's ordinary presence is already active during the core event.

Akito and Toya then become low-stakes cross-unit acquaintances. Toya invites Mizuki to see Tsukasa's play rather than leaving them alone. Mizuki accepts rapidly but still checks whether they would be intruding. Once admitted, Mizuki physically pulls Akito along, jokes, laughs loudly at the absurd play, and slots into the new social microgroup with very little friction.

For reconstruction this is high-value evidence:

- Mizuki can socialize fluently with unfamiliar peers after minimal introduction;
- they can initiate nicknames/teasing very quickly;
- they monitor whether inclusion is genuine before escalating familiarity;
- once included, they are not shy or observationally passive;
- their social inhibition is therefore highly **context-conditioned**, not a generalized interpersonal deficit.

Ena's own global psychological state does not change in EVENT_0007. The correct integration is relational: Mizuki's attachment to Ena is strengthened as ordinary-life desire, and Ena's family relationship becomes a new cross-unit route.

## 6. Toya's first-person-authority history as a cross-unit mirror

Episode 4 contains one of the clearest cross-unit validations yet for the project's central N25 synthesis framework.

Toya explains that his father's classical-music training was so strict that he wanted to escape, but guilt about disappointing a parent prevented action. Tsukasa told him:

> `お前の人生なんだから、お前がやりたいようにやればいい。 嫌なものは、嫌と言っていい`

The content is almost a distilled first-person-authority proposition:

- it is **your** life;
- you may act according to **your** desire;
- you may say that something you hate is hateful.

Toya says this permission enabled him to leave the imposed path and eventually meet Akito. Mizuki's response is inward and immediate:

> `（……変えてくれた、恩人、か）`
>
> `（ボクも、いろいろ助けられたっけ）`

This does not prove that Mizuki and Toya have identical histories. They do not. Toya's family structure, classical training, and route of exit are distinct. What the scene validates is a franchise-level structural motif: people can become trapped when externally authored expectations override first-person preference, and relational permission can help make preference actionable.

That is analytically valuable because the N25 first-person-authority thesis is no longer only an N25-internal abstraction. EVENT_0007 provides a direct cross-unit resonance without forcing equivalence.

Mizuki then volunteers to help Toya find Tsukasa because Toya had included them in the play outing and that inclusion made Mizuki happy. Again, the social response is reciprocal rather than dependent. Mizuki does not merely receive rescue from more conventional peers; they convert inclusion into helpful agency.

## 7. The class T-shirt wound: wanting belonging is not the same as wanting conformity

Episode 5 supplies the conceptual center of the event.

Mizuki encounters classmates who had previously been overheard saying that if Mizuki dislikes criticism, they should simply dress “normally,” that standing out naturally attracts attention, and that Mizuki is a good person but cannot match everyone else.

In the present conversation, the classmates are superficially friendly. They notice Mizuki is not wearing the class T-shirt and then dismiss the matching shirts as lame and embarrassing. Mizuki's private response is:

> `お揃いはダサい、かぁ`
>
> `そんなの——お揃いになれるから言えるんだよ`

This is a crucial correction to any model that treats Mizuki as simply anti-conformity.

Mizuki does reject **coerced sameness**. They reject the proposition that acceptance requires abandoning self-authored presentation. But this scene shows that the desire to remain different does not eliminate the desire to **belong**.

The classmates can call matching shirts lame because their right to be part of the matching group is secure. Mizuki experiences the issue from the other side: ordinary group sameness can have emotional value precisely when one's membership is not taken for granted.

The distinction is:

> **conformity:** alter yourself to become acceptable to the group;
>
> **belonging:** remain yourself while being allowed to participate in the group.

EVENT_0007 is not about Mizuki discovering a hidden wish to become ordinary. It is about discovering that collective participation can have value when it does not demand self-erasure.

This becomes explicit in episode 8 when An brings Mizuki's class T-shirt again. Akito calls the shirt unfashionable. Mizuki agrees it is not really their style—but this time the conclusion is not refusal. Mizuki says:

> `だからちょっとアレンジさせてよ。 着るなら、やっぱりカワイく着たいしね♪`

They will wear the shared marker **if allowed to make it their own**.

This is the event's strongest symbolic action. Mizuki does not choose between individuality and membership. They modify the membership symbol so that both can coexist.

For longitudinal reconstruction the resulting rule should be explicit:

> When collective participation permits self-authored variation, Mizuki may actively seek belonging. When participation is conditioned on erasing or explaining difference, withdrawal and guardedness remain likely.

## 8. Rui and Mizuki: from “lonely companions” to surviving kinship after social expansion

Episodes 5–7 provide the deepest Rui–Mizuki relationship history yet integrated into the N25 corpus.

### 8.1 Their original bond

The flashbacks show that Rui and Mizuki met on the school rooftop as socially peripheral students. Rui knew Mizuki's name but asked for it anyway because he wanted to hear Mizuki say it personally. This is a small but revealing autonomy gesture: he privileges self-presentation over rumor.

Mizuki initially treats him as strange and questions why someone who thinks the festival looks enjoyable remains on the rooftop rather than joining it. Rui cannot simply join. Both are in versions of outsiderhood.

Later, Rui repeatedly appears on the rooftop and waits for Mizuki without a formal promise. When Mizuki wonders whether they are merely useful material for Rui's shows, Rui says everyone becomes material to him—not Mizuki uniquely—and accepts the criticism without pretending he will stop orienting toward performance.

Rui describes solitude as something that gave him time for shows and ideas, and then adds:

> `屋上にいる孤独な仲間`

A companion in loneliness.

Mizuki identifies the contradiction but accepts the idea enough to wonder whether they might eventually find companions who are **not** lonely.

This is not simply a cute retrospective. It explains why Rui and Mizuki can understand one another without demanding full normalization. Their friendship originated from shared social marginality, but the bond is not defined by total disclosure or rescue.

### 8.2 The current relationship is not threatened by new companions

In the present, Mizuki notices that Rui's expression is different and that he genuinely enjoys being with Tsukasa and Nene. Mizuki says they are relieved.

Rui, in turn, infers that Mizuki has found people too. Mizuki initially resists the label `仲間`:

> `仲間、って言うと、ちょっとしっくりこないけど`
>
> `でも、そんな感じの人達に会うことはできたかもね`

This is important wording. The event does not instantly transform N25 into a perfectly named friendship group in Mizuki's mind. The category remains slightly uncomfortable. But the old question from the rooftop—whether they might find “non-lonely companions”—now has a partial answer.

Mizuki then says:

> `嫌なこともたくさんあるけど、 今は、ここから見える景色も、そんなに嫌じゃないよ`

The external world has not become harmless. The same school still contains students who comment on Mizuki's appearance. What changed is the **relational meaning of the view**.

Rui and Mizuki thus move into a new relationship form:

> shared outsider history + mutual recognition of each other's new companions + persistent kindred understanding without requiring exclusive isolation.

This should be logged as a real cross-unit relationship state, not merely a historical footnote.

## 9. An and Mizuki: safety without complete disclosure

Card `0139:01` makes An one of the most important new cross-unit relationships for Mizuki at this stage.

Mizuki thanks An for calling on festival day and asks why she bothered when Mizuki had not helped with preparations. An gives a simple answer: it was a festival, and not participating would have been a waste.

Mizuki considers whether An called because of some school-role obligation, then concludes she did it simply as a friend. More importantly, Mizuki explicitly thinks:

> they have only known An since high school;
>
> Mizuki has **not properly told An everything about themself**;
>
> nevertheless, being with An feels reassuring/safe.

This is a significant relationship constraint for future reconstruction. Mizuki's guarded disclosure does not mean they are incapable of trust until every secret has been revealed. They can experience real safety and friendship under partial disclosure when the other person does not pressure them to justify their existence.

An later notices that Mizuki is talking unusually much about themself—the rooftop years with Rui, N25, current life. Mizuki says:

> `相手が杏だから、かもね`

Maybe because it's An.

The relationship should therefore be modeled as:

> easy everyday friendship + tactful identity protection + incomplete disclosure + high felt safety.

It is bounded in the present corpus; it should not be inflated into Mizuki's deepest attachment. But it is clearly more than a casual classmate acquaintance.

## 10. EVENT_0007 changes what N25 means to Mizuki

Card `0139:02` is essential because it transfers the festival development back into the Nightcord relationship rather than leaving it isolated in school life.

Mizuki enters a Nightcord session visibly energized. Kanade notices the change. Mizuki recounts the festival, including finding Akito, and teases Ena for sleeping through the event.

Then Mizuki proposes:

> `次は一緒に行こうよ。 ていうか、ニーゴのみんなで行きたいな♪`

This is the explicit realization of the episode-2 thought experiment.

Mizuki explains that while walking around the festival, they kept imagining the others:

- how Mafuyu would react to the ridiculous play;
- how Kanade would react to the haunted house;
- how Ena would photograph the cute cotton candy;
- and how the day would likely have been more fun with all of them there.

This is a real group-state change because Mizuki is now recruiting N25 into **future ordinary life**.

The responses matter:

- Ena says she might look next year.
- Kanade says she has become interested.
- Mafuyu says that if she has time, she might go.
- Mizuki asks about Mafuyu's school festival too.
- Mafuyu says she will tell them when it happens next year.

This is not a binding promise that all four will actually attend. The epistemic/relationship authority should remain narrow. But the group has moved from “people who sometimes meet offline because of the crisis and production” toward “people who can imagine optional future social experiences together.”

Mizuki's final private thought supplies the durability evidence:

> `（来年は、ボクも準備手伝おうかな）`
>
> `（みんなが来てくれた時、 思いっきり楽しんでもらえるように、ね♪）`

The festival changes future-oriented behavior. Mizuki is considering helping to create the school event that they previously forgot, avoided, and did not prepare for—specifically because chosen people might come.

That is why a new global state ID is justified.

## 12. Ena-specific integration

Ena does not receive a new character state in EVENT_0007, but the event should not be treated as irrelevant to Ena.

Three changes matter:

1. **Mizuki wants Ena present in ordinary life.** During the festival, Mizuki explicitly wishes Ena had come and considers how she would react to attractions.
2. **Mizuki's relationship with Ena becomes legible to Akito.** Mizuki describes Ena as someone with whom they have a fairly deep relationship and learns ordinary sibling-adjacent information through Akito.

Ena's own response is characteristically bounded: she mocks, pushes back at Mizuki's teasing, but says she might attend next time. Nothing here resolves Ena's artistic wound, Mafuyu antagonistic attachment, or family issues. No `E-*` global state transition is justified.

A relationship micro-update is sufficient:

> Mizuki↔Ena now has stronger evidence for **ordinary-life desire and social inclusion**, not only creative partnership and teasing familiarity.

## 14. Linked-area baseline: development persists as ordinary contact

The nine linked area conversations are analytically valuable because they show that EVENT_0007's mixed cast is not merely assembled for one festival plot.

### Toya

Mizuki can joke freely with Toya, and Toya's literal sincerity produces comedy. Toya later invites Mizuki to lunch because Akito is absent. This is an especially useful counterexample to “Mizuki only socializes deeply online.”

### Akito

Mizuki repeatedly provokes him with the “little bro” address while also reading his trust in Toya and comparing his serious attitude to Ena. The banter is reciprocal enough to persist.

### Tsukasa

Mizuki can exploit Tsukasa's vanity playfully, ask sincere questions about his troupe, and infer that Rui's increased freedom is related to meeting Tsukasa. This gives Mizuki a broader map of Rui's new social world.

### Attendance guardrail

One linked area is especially important for preventing overreading. Toya says he has been running into Mizuki often lately, and Mizuki jokes that perhaps they are now a serious student. Internally, Mizuki notes that they need enough attendance to meet the quota.

Therefore:

> EVENT_0007 improves **willingness/possibility of participation**; it does not establish stable enthusiastic attendance.

The state is “provisional social re-entry,” not “school reintegration complete.”

## 15. Epistemic architecture after EVENT_0007

### 15.1 Mizuki knows

Mizuki now directly knows that:

- An intentionally considers their vulnerability to unwanted attention and can create safer participation without demanding disclosure;
- Rui has found companions with whom he is genuinely happy;
- Toya was helped by Tsukasa's permission to reject an externally imposed life path;
- Akito is Ena's brother and Toya's trusted partner;
- N25 occupies enough relational space that Mizuki repeatedly imagines them in ordinary festival life;
- collective participation can feel good without requiring complete sameness;

## 17. Thematic and motif integration

### 17.1 Rooftop: observer-space changes meaning

The rooftop begins as a place from which Rui and Mizuki watch other students participate. It is an outsider-space that offers relief from social demand.

In EVENT_0007 it becomes a comparative memory surface. The same view is now interpreted differently because both have acquired relationships elsewhere.

The building does not change. The view does not change. **Relational position changes the meaning of the view.**

The rooftop therefore evolves from:

> exclusion / observation / mutual isolation

into:

> reflective continuity / recognition of change / temporary retreat that no longer requires permanent withdrawal.

### 17.2 Matching T-shirt: conformity versus membership

The shirt motif passes through three stages:

1. **refusal** — Mizuki does not wear it;
2. **wound** — classmates call matching lame, and Mizuki thinks they can say that because they are able to match;
3. **self-authored acceptance** — Mizuki agrees to wear it after customizing it.

This is one of the cleanest symbolic enactments of the project's first-person-authority framework.

### 17.3 Observer → participant

The event title `ボクの文化祭` is earned through position change. Mizuki is no longer only watching other students have a festival. The festival becomes *Mizuki's* because Mizuki participates, helps, connects, accepts invitation, and anticipates future contribution.

### 17.4 Naming `仲間`

Mizuki's hesitation around the word matters. Development is not represented by instantly assigning a friendship label. The word remains slightly wrong, but the underlying relation has become strong enough to approximate it.

This is a good reconstruction warning: characters may have relationships that function in a category before they comfortably use the category label.

### 17.5 Future anticipation

“Maybe next year I'll help with preparations” is a major motif-level change because it introduces **future-oriented investment** in a setting previously associated with avoidance. Anticipation itself becomes evidence of integration.

### 17.6 Cross-unit first-person authority

Toya/Tsukasa supplies independent evidence for the broader theme: externally authored life paths become livable when a person can reclaim the right to say “I want” and “I don't want.” This should remain cross-unit validation, not proof that all units tell the same story.

### 17.7 Audience co-creation

Tsukasa's card gives Mizuki a new creative idea: audience response is not merely post-hoc evaluation but can be part of the work's realization in live performance. This may later matter to how Mizuki thinks about video, reception, and collaborative production.

## 23. What later events must test

EVENT_0007 creates a positive developmental movement, but it should generate hypotheses rather than permanent optimism.

Later integration should ask:

1. Does Mizuki's increased social participation persist outside exceptional festival conditions?
2. Does the school environment continue to punish visible difference?
3. What happens when chosen relationships become entangled with disclosure pressure?
4. Does N25 actually develop the ordinary-life companionship Mizuki imagines here?
5. Does Mizuki's willingness to prepare future events materialize, or is it later constrained by school avoidance?
6. How does the Rui relationship change once both are less isolated?
7. Does An remain safe specifically because she does not demand full disclosure, and what happens if she receives more information?
8. Does the “belonging without assimilation” solution hold when stakes are higher than clothing customization?
9. Does Mizuki's audience-co-creation insight reappear in creative practice?
10. Most importantly: does growing attachment make the undisclosed issue easier to share, or more frightening because there is now more to lose?

The final question is especially important. EVENT_0007 increases the value of belonging. That can reduce isolation—but it can also increase the stakes of rejection. Later Mizuki development must therefore be read for both possibilities rather than assuming social integration straightforwardly solves the underlying vulnerability.
