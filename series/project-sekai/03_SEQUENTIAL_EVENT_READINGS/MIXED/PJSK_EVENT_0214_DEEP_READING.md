## 3. A rescue uses different strengths and retains danger

Akito meets **大輝**, a former soccer-club junior who stopped playing after an accident. Rehabilitation has left ordinary walking/daily life possible but running limited; the story does not depict recovered competitive ability. His older brother **俊輝**, Akito's friend, invited him into manga-making during that difficult period. The younger brother now illustrates while the older writes, an editor is involved, and debut is only a possibility. 俊輝's strained refusal to join them worries Akito. Separately, Interstice MEIKO finds a clouded fragment and is pulled inside while trying to hear it. A later tree alert brings the four humans back; they hear her call and enter. `PJSK:event:0214:02:001:0002–0047`; `PJSK:event:0214:02:002:0010–0027`.

Changing forest, sea and other scenery initially resembles uncontrolled transport. Akito recognizes motifs from the manga, but ownership remains a hypothesis. Facing a lion-like monster, he proposes diversion rather than fighting it, enables the others to retrieve MEIKO and jumps a fissure first so he can catch them. Ena freezes. His appeal to her ambition to become a painter combines a harsh imperative with a concrete offered hand; she jumps, receives help and thanks him. Practical dependence is actual here, not reducible to verbal teasing. `PJSK:event:0214:02:003:0013–0109`.

A pen nib shows both brothers discussing analog drawing in hospital. The younger prefers the method his brother taught despite the difficulty; digital work is an available alternative, not a lesser art. Nene applies gaming knowledge to terrain, patrols and concealment. She is frightened and says so. Others accept her guidance because of observed aptitude and prior leadership, not because fear has disappeared. A second object, manga ink, shows the brothers approaching an editor together. `PJSK:event:0214:03:001:0010–0048`; `PJSK:event:0214:03:001:0052–0094`.

When another guarded route cannot easily be bypassed, Airi invites Nene's hesitant idea. Nene proposes a diversion with a fallback and assigns roles according to physical ability. A second monster defeats the initial plan. She briefly blames herself, then deliberately applies an already acquired capacity to think beyond a setback. She runs toward MEIKO, coordinates a timed sideways leap and causes the two pursuers to collide. The group escapes; Nene's post-crisis collapse in tension and thanks remain part of the outcome. This is consequential application and improvisation, not infallible planning or a new proof that dangerous choices are always justified. `PJSK:event:0214:03:001:0103–0136`; `PJSK:event:0214:03:001:0154–0211`.

## 4. Hearing the hidden wish creates a task, not its solution

MEIKO follows the muffled voice into the sea and retrieves a protected manuscript. She understands its submersion and the interfering waves as possible concealment of a painful feeling. She cannot move and suspects that holding the manuscript is responsible; she nevertheless stays long enough to hear the speaker's love of manga and fear of coming to hate 大輝. The others retreat at her urging and return with her when the fragment episode ends. She reports the words, and Akito identifies his older friend through what he knows. His account of years of drawing, technical pride and rapidly surpassed skill explains the comparison without making the younger brother culpable. `PJSK:event:0214:04:001:0003–0100`.

MEIKO asks the humans to help in their world while she considers what can be done with the fragment; she does not know how much she can accomplish. Akito recognizes the matter is personal, then chooses to ask his friend. The others attempt to monitor the park conversation from too far away to hear; Nene questions eavesdropping. Street MEIKO, in Akito's pocket, actually hears it. Its result is failure: 俊輝 has decided to stop manga rather than hate his innocent brother, and Akito cannot find a reply. Neither magical access nor sincere concern supplies an immediate successful intervention. `PJSK:event:0214:04:001:0103–0111`; `PJSK:event:0214:04:003:0002–0022`; `PJSK:event:0214:05:001:0007–0026`.

At home, Ena recognizes the cycle of comparison, self-disgust, irritability and displaced anger. She remembers that her mother and Akito were trying to help and explicitly apologizes to Akito for that conduct. He acknowledges that she had not said it, tells her to say it to their mother too and teases her about growth. The mother's receipt is not shown. This precise new acknowledgment earns the sibling successor; it does not erase already founded care or establish a universal change to their style. `PJSK:event:0214:05:001:0031–0068`.

Ena then asks to speak to 大輝. Airi explicitly permits him not to answer unwanted questions. He blames himself and has offered to stop drawing; Ena rejects that self-blame and seeks the desire to draw beneath the pain. Her proposal first recovers a real memory: 俊輝 introduced manga because his hospitalized brother smiled while reading his work. But remembering that joy does not settle the problem. He still wants to draw and still says he cannot continue. The story preserves the inadequacy of encouragement that merely recalls an original love. `PJSK:event:0214:05:003:0003–0037`; `PJSK:event:0214:05:005:0011–0058`.

## 5. A different way to compete survives an actual rejection

Airi acknowledges both brothers' effort and the pain of unequal results. She describes her own concrete adaptations: studying talk, building variety skills, using larger movement and a carrying voice. This is situated testimony, not a promise that effort eliminates every difference. Akito offers his own belief in continued struggle. When 俊輝 asks whether he can compete equally, Airi leaves the outcome with him and insists that trying is necessary to find out. He decides to develop his own strengths and submit another manga. Ena offers optional practical assistance so he can concentrate on that search; he accepts. `PJSK:event:0214:06:001:0005–0057`.

Several days of work follow. Ena supplies background drawings and learns from the changing compositions. Airi, Nene and Akito prepare character-shaped food using the monster design as a form of appreciative fan art. Their different kitchen capacities matter: Airi gives practical instructions, Nene struggles with seaweed adhesion, and Akito handles assigned details. 大輝 asks to assist as a fan of his brother's work; 俊輝 accepts and they distribute tasks around Ena's existing contribution. The hurt has not been solved by removing the younger brother or declaring his talent irrelevant. `PJSK:event:0214:06:002:0002–0034`; `PJSK:event:0214:06:003:0002–0030`.

The editor rejects the new work. Story and expressive choices receive praise, but battle drawing still fails to communicate clearly enough for the magazine. 俊輝 nevertheless feels he has tested his own full effort and found a way to compete, while accurately accepting that he has not yet reached the standard. He still envies his brother's drawing. The brothers choose to pursue separate manga careers, with another joint work conditional on both eventually becoming professionals. Rejection, residual envy and future uncertainty make this resolution more specific than either a success fantasy or abandonment. `PJSK:event:0214:07:002:0005–0028`.

The clouded fragment is now clear and ascends; Interstice MEIKO identifies a new SEKAI. The four's light is then absorbed by the tree. She interprets their common determination and strengthened feelings as relevant, but states that future reunion is not guaranteed. Their guesses about the new world's interior and 大輝 entering it are speculation. No brother is shown discovering it. `PJSK:event:0214:07:003:0002–0050`.
