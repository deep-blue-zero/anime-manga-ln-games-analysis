## 2. Shared causal reading

### 2.1 Designing for one friend's fear can expand access for others

Kohane preferred a café, but the class vote selects a haunted house. She accepts the result with curiosity, remembers to tell VBS that preparation may delay practice, and invites them. Toya accepts the school obligation; Akito proposes making up practice time and permits occasional leisure. Their response does not require her to abandon school participation to prove musical seriousness (`PJSK:event:0075:01:001:0002–0010`; `PJSK:event:0075:01:002:0010–0018`, `PJSK:event:0075:01:002:0048–0053`).

An's fear becomes visible through hesitation, denial and bodily reactions. Kohane explicitly says An need not enter: being together at the festival would already make her happy. An nevertheless wants to support what Kohane is making (`PJSK:event:0075:01:002:0020–0047`). Kohane's subsequent design problem is therefore not how to force a reluctant friend into an unavoidable test. It is how to make a desired shared activity more accessible.

The solution has multiple authors. Minori proposes a cute/fearful contrast, drawing on advice she received from Airi while initially disguising the name as a friend. Fairy-tale motifs lead Kohane to notice that the hunter offers a way to act against a frightening opponent. Guests can choose vulnerable story roles or a hunter role with a toy gun; the class values both the alternative experience and replayability (`PJSK:event:0075:01:003:0022–0059`). An is the initiating recipient in Kohane's private reasoning, but the design serves a wider audience.

### 2.2 Musical experience becomes ordinary competence through collaboration

Shiho starts by wanting the project not to obstruct band practice. Her friends' initiative makes her choose to invest more (`PJSK:event:0075:02:001:0004–0012`). When simultaneous suggestions overwhelm the class representative, she recalls Honami's report of a difficult class discussion, explicitly distinguishes that earlier disagreement from the present logistical confusion, and proposes listing tasks before allocating them. Clear speech is locally useful without being universally welcomed: she says others often find her wording too harsh (`PJSK:event:0075:02:002:0018–0035`).

The three contribute different capacities. Minori asks what the costumes should make guests feel, credits others for teaching her, and helps the team choose varied wolves. Shiho enjoys painting convincing trees, worries that her realism clashes with the rest, and accepts classmates' proposal to make increasing realism part of the route's atmosphere. Her difference is incorporated rather than suppressed; she then teaches the technique. Kohane uses event-flyer practice and her own camping photographs, suggests testing several versions, and is praised for now sharing more of her thinking (`PJSK:event:0075:02:002:0054–0086`, `PJSK:event:0075:02:002:0088–0124`). These are concrete transfers of learned practice, not proof of effortless universal competence.

Card 0528:01 makes the cooperation material: Shiho anticipates lending completed trees for publicity, costume makers explain the first wolf, and Kohane wants photographs that represent everyone's work well (`PJSK:card:0528:01:001:0009–0050`). In the later reflection, Kohane and Minori publicly credit their respective groups; Shiho privately credits Leo/need and recognizes that enjoying the festival with classmates once seemed unimaginable (`PJSK:event:0075:08:001:0044–0086`). The shared conversation does not disclose the whole content of Shiho's inner comparison.

### 2.3 Good intentions and preparation do not prevent a practical failure

Kohane volunteers to replace an ill classmate only until noon, because VBS is expected later. The class treats illness as a reason to rest; Shiho's equipment-check responsibility prevents her taking the shift (`PJSK:event:0075:03:007:0020–0036`). Kohane then learns she is wearing the especially frightening wolf, reserved for guests who want stronger scares. Her discomfort with its appearance coexists with understanding that those guests also deserve an enjoyable experience (`PJSK:event:0075:04:001:0006–0023`).

A stuck zipper prevents changing out of the costume. Minori tries, moves the task into better light, and goes for tools. Kohane thinks to notify her friends of delay. Their early arrival interrupts that attempt; An sees the frightening appearance before Kohane can explain. Rin first needs voice recognition too, and Toya asks whether the figure is Kohane (`PJSK:event:0075:04:003:0002–0025`; `PJSK:event:0075:04:004:0025–0058`). The misrecognition is not evidence that the friendship lacks care. It depends on costume, timing, expectation and panic.

Kohane pursues An to correct the misunderstanding, while An interprets pursuit as an extended haunted-house scenario. Each action then reinforces the other's immediate concern. Rin's navigation is also fallible in a crowded school; she apologizes, and Kohane refuses to blame her (`PJSK:event:0075:05:001:0003–0017`; `PJSK:event:0075:06:001:0032–0036`). Card 0528:02 later establishes that Akito and Toya made unanswered calls as well as searching. A failure to notice the phone is not a failure to attempt contact (`PJSK:card:0528:02:002:0018–0025`).

### 2.4 Repair depends on different people seeing different parts

Haruka recognizes Kohane by voice, notices frightened guests and temporarily frames the intrusion as promotion. She does not yet know the full problem; she asks whether An was meeting her there, and tries unsuccessfully to warn Kohane about continuing in costume (`PJSK:event:0075:05:002:0013–0032`). In another room, Emu improvises a continuation of the class play that makes the unexpected wolf intelligible to the audience. Honami is impressed, but Emu still wonders what happened; effective improvisation does not confer recognition of the person inside (`PJSK:event:0075:05:003:0009–0021`; identity explanation follows in `PJSK:card:0530:02:002:0002–0007`). Airi and Shizuku turn a fall near their stall into sales patter and provide practical reassurance, without knowing An's whereabouts (`PJSK:event:0075:05:004:0010–0033`).

The search becomes distributed: Minori tells Shiho, Shiho asks Shizuku for sightings, and Shizuku recruits Mafuyu. Mafuyu later identifies Kohane from the described costume, states who requested help, and directs her back toward worried friends (`PJSK:event:0075:05:004:0035–0057`; `PJSK:event:0075:06:001:0050–0058`). Honami's separate suggestion is explicitly what she herself might do while afraid, not knowledge that An actually chose such a refuge. It helps Minori consider Haruka's nearby classroom (`PJSK:card:0530:02:002:0010–0028`). Haruka's drink and deliberately playful cat-register encouragement support Minori's continued search, tailored to her known response (`PJSK:card:0530:02:003:0035–0047`).

Shiho's conversation with An supplies the crucial interpersonal correction. An imagines Kohane disappointed in her. An then entrusts an apology to her (`PJSK:card:0529:01:002:0015–0054`). This is Shiho's person-specific inference, supported by what the audience sees of Kohane's self-blame; it is not omniscient access to all of Kohane's feelings.

Minori removes the headpiece but the emotional problem remains. Kohane worries that her aim of making others happy has failed. ### 2.5 Accessible participation does not mean eliminating all fear

An and Kohane apologize to each other. Kohane explains the attraction accurately, including why the frightening costume exists. The group allocates its single hunter role to An, giving her a way to respond while keeping the game's constraints. Kohane promises to protect her if needed (`PJSK:event:0075:07:002:0002–0037`).

Minori's comic wolf produces laughter; subsequent encounters let An experience effective action, though surprise remains frightening. Kohane has also asked Shiho to provide some challenge for Akito's taste. The realistic final wolf exceeds both An and Akito's composure. Akito's claim that a known artificial creature cannot scare him is thus immediately qualified by his own response. Kohane exchanges roles with An and acts within the attraction's rules to get the group out (`PJSK:event:0075:07:003:0014–0084`). This is specific protective agency, not proof that she is globally fearless or that An has become dependent in every context.

Afterward the three guests give distinct positive responses. An says the experience was frightening but enjoyable because Kohane protected her. Toya appreciates the activity; Akito grudgingly praises the final creature while maintaining comparison with his own class's attraction. Shiho explains that recorded wolf sounds informed her imitation (`PJSK:event:0075:07:003:0087–0109`). Later An describes failed self-reassurance before the unexpected encounter and considers a future park attraction with Haruka. Fear is not cured, and a proposed future outing is not a completed one (`PJSK:card:0531:02:001:0036–0066`).

### 2.6 What persists includes both shared memory and mistaken reputation

As the classroom is dismantled, Shiho argues that an ending does not erase the day's memories. Minori relates this to concerts and their audiences. At the after-festival performance, Shiho extends that thought: Leo/need is creating memories for everyone present, not only the four musicians (`PJSK:event:0075:08:001:0019–0043`; `PJSK:card:0529:02:002:0051–0057`). Schoolmates report surprise at the music's quality; Honami's classmates compare it favorably with an earlier show. These are bounded audience reports, not objective measures proving universal reception (`PJSK:card:0529:02:002:0034–0047`).

The final newspaper preserves something else: a public narrative that the chaotic wolf appearances were bold planned promotion. Students infer daring personality from that account, while Kohane protests (`PJSK:event:0075:08:002:0003–0020`). The interventions were effective enough to change perceived meaning, but reception cannot establish original intention. A reconstruction must retain both Kohane's real initiative during preparation and her embarrassment at a falsely attributed stunt.
