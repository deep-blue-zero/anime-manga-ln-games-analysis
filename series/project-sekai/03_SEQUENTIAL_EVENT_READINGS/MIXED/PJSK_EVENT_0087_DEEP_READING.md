## 3. Gratitude, belonging and supported agency

Toya remembers the Tenma home as a place of relief when his own childhood home offered little rest; he immediately qualifies that present conditions have improved. Later he tells Rui about his father's renowned musical position, enforced classical study and few opportunities for play. Initial fear of being left at the Tenmas gradually became anticipation through Tsukasa's company. Saki was often absent through childhood illness; occasional three-person play must not be inflated into constant shared upbringing. In one recalled instance, after severe paternal scolding, Tsukasa performs and Saki draws Toya into watching; he recalls becoming absorbed and smiling (`PJSK:event:0087:01:001:0035–0045`; `PJSK:event:0087:06:002:0072–0109`).

The party is not emergency rescue from his current family. Saki explicitly distinguishes her own dislike of eating alone from what Toya might feel; her invitation offers an easy refusal. Toya wants to attend and later initiates repayment without either sibling demanding it. He already expects them to appreciate any considered gift, yet wants to make them happier. Rui's account of unexpectedly moving handmade Valentine presents changes Toya's willingness to risk cooking; Rui also says feeling can be conveyed in other ways (`PJSK:card:0604:01:001:0008–0030`; `PJSK:event:0087:01:001:0011–0025`; `PJSK:event:0087:02:004:0006–0057`).

Toya's first failure reveals practical unfamiliarity, not a failure of seriousness. Separating eggs, recognizing the endpoint of mixing and interpreting recipe photographs require knowledge that “beginner” language does not supply. He distinguishes a genuinely unpleasant result from his own low tolerance for sweets. He plans to clean up before his parents return and seeks help despite concern about burdening busy people. His first attempt already teaches him which actions he cannot perform or understand (`PJSK:event:0087:03:003:0002–0057`; `PJSK:event:0087:03:005:0015–0043`).

The second failure is worse because Miku's confidence is mistaken. Toya voices sensible doubts about minimal mixing and changed quantities, then accepts her answers because he believes he has a competent teacher. He initially attributes the failure to his own misunderstanding of her instructions. The source therefore limits an “earnest but simply incapable” account: situational trust and bad instruction contribute. His private discouragement concerns the available deadline and the recipients' pleasure, not a permanent declaration that learning is impossible (`PJSK:event:0087:05:001:0012–0049`; `PJSK:event:0087:06:002:0032–0054`; `PJSK:event:0087:07:003:0013–0025`).

Rui's invitation to recall childhood changes the emotional conditions for another attempt. Toya notices that those memories still encourage him and chooses to continue before KAITO offers new instruction. Correct teaching then makes that choice productive. Rui later recognizes a successful, still slightly ungainly cake; technical improvement and recipient feeling are both real. Toya gifts Rui a separately wrapped chocolate variation, and at the party openly credits many helpers. He also prepares a less-sweet matcha cake for himself. Care for others coexists with his own taste and participation (`PJSK:event:0087:06:002:0110–0123`; `PJSK:event:0087:07:003:0028–0051`; `PJSK:event:0087:07:005`; `PJSK:event:0087:08:002:0007–0046`; `PJSK:event:0087:08:004:0045–0064`).

At the ending, Toya privately contrasts childhood loneliness with his present irreplaceable partner and companions. The older refuge remains valuable alongside them. Do not turn that gratitude into a settled debt balance, a first discovery of belonging, a cure of all family conflict or a replacement of VBS by the Tenmas (`PJSK:event:0087:08:004:0074–0097`).
