## 2. A personally received story is chosen, not imposed as a duty

Honami sometimes attends neighborhood meetings in place of her busy parents. A regular reader cannot attend the coming picture-book session, so she volunteers to read a book she loved as a child. Saki recalls Honami reading to her during hospitalization and receiving comfort; this is distinct from the present community audience. In *The Dog Who Chased a Star*, the dog leaves to find a wish-granting star for a sick boy, helps others along the way, fails to find the literal star, but receives a chain of returned help that leads to medicine. The boy calls the dog the star; the dog credits everyone. This is a fiction recounted and performed inside the story, not an assertion about real medical outcomes or automatic reward for kindness. `PJSK:event:0158:01:002:0009–0035`; `PJSK:event:0158:01:004:0002–0022`; `PJSK:event:0158:02:002:0017–0038`; `PJSK:event:0158:08:001:0024–0039`.

Honami's worn, star-stickered copy retains her child's wish to give the fictional dog stars. Ichika and School MEIKO interpret the likeness between the dog's care and Honami's, which Honami receives with embarrassment and pleasure. Honami clarifies to Nene that she first wanted to act because she remembers being moved by the reading, then wants children to receive a possibility of that experience. Her care for the audience and her own desire coexist; neither turns her into a flawless savior. `PJSK:event:0158:02:002:0034–0074`; `PJSK:event:0158:04:002:0016–0037`.

## 3. `LN-EP-HONAMI-E0158` — recipient-sensitive story transmission

Honami rehearses but notices fluent delivery alone feels insufficient. She tries the reading with her brother Haru and his friend; Haru warns that younger children, including those brought unwillingly, may lose attention. His candid criticism is a useful audience hypothesis, not a judgment that all children are alike. Honami seeks voice-acting study, meets Nene, and explicitly asks for help within Nene's available capacity. Nene teaches articulation and emotional delivery and insists on rest when Honami presses ahead. `PJSK:event:0158:03:002:0002–0015`; `PJSK:event:0158:03:003:0002–0025`; `PJSK:event:0158:03:005:0012–0033`; `PJSK:card:1123:01:001:0002–0012`.

With Nene and Rui, she adds subordinate effects to the projected book: forest sounds, gentle snow, a recorded dog voice and tail shadow, and star-shaped paper. Rui tests the device and reduces snow that would compete with the reading, divides remote operations between the readers, and cannot attend the actual session. Leo/need hears rehearsal and offers recipient-specific gifts and support. The rehearsal's positive reaction is only a trial, not the child-audience result. `PJSK:event:0158:05:002:0002–0033`; `PJSK:event:0158:05:004:0002–0018`; `PJSK:card:1126:01:001:0002–0053`; `PJSK:card:1125:01:001:0002–0050`.

The two pivot to a staged search for an offscreen dog, using the recorded bark and tail image, Nene's improvisation, and Honami's timing to make the children active discoverers who then want the book. During the reading they respond to danger, relief, snow, and stars. Officers and children give direct positive feedback afterward; some ask for another reading. A child later reports that a copy was bought for her and that she rereads it at home.
