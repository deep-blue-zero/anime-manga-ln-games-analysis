# GENSHIN_FURINA_THIRD_PARTY_EVIDENCE_PASS

## Scope

This pass adjudicates the 870 normalized explicit-name candidates. The raw candidate count is not a count of meaningful judgments. Duplicate renderings, logistical mentions, and aggregate Talk context are excluded. The purpose is to identify what other people can legitimately tell us about Furina and where their access fails.

## Neuvillette: inconsistency without dismissal

Neuvillette's judgment that Furina is not superficial or foolish but inconsistent is the most analytically useful pre-reveal third-party statement. It recognizes a discrepancy between visible behavior and underlying seriousness without inventing the explanation.

Direct evidence supports all components:

- serious work and sacrifice;
- frivolous or theatrical surface;
- inconsistent public calibration;
- practical dependence;
- genuine institutional concern.

The statement is retained as a high-confidence external formulation, not as a complete definition.

## Arlecchino: accurate detection, incomplete motive

Arlecchino accurately detects fear, concealment and lack of demonstrable divine power. Her scrutiny is important because she does not grant the social premise everyone else accepts.

Yet her inference is incomplete. She sees that Furina cannot produce the expected plan and treats this as evidence of negligence or fraud. She does not know that Furina's nondisclosure is the condition of the hidden plan.

The correct use is:

> Arlecchino is a high-quality witness to cracks in the performance and a low-access witness to the ethical reason for preserving it.

## Traveler: cross-state witness

The Traveler has unusually broad access:

- public Furina;
- courtroom opponent;
- prophecy conversation;
- solitary collapse;
- near-confidant;
- interior history witness;
- post-crisis friend and artistic collaborator.

The Traveler's reading of Furina's work claim as sincere carries high weight. So does the ability to witness differences between forced and chosen performance.

However, the Traveler's role in the trap and trial complicates the relationship. Later friendship should not retroactively convert the trial into uncomplicated rescue.

## Paimon: witness to leakage

Paimon notices what Furina cannot fully conceal:

- tears;
- nervousness;
- harshness followed by concern;
- domestic awkwardness;
- ordinary taste;
- social reintegration.

Paimon is especially valuable for the boundary between performed explanation and visible affect. She should not be used to establish the hidden reason for affect before Furina explains it.

## Navia: consequences and reconciliation

Navia's evidence is less a personality diagnosis than an ethical counterweight. Her father and Poisson show how Furina's public system could wound people even while serving a larger salvation plan.

Later warmth and cooperation are meaningful precisely because Navia has grounds for anger. They do not erase those grounds.

## Clorinde: ordinary welcome

Clorinde's continued inclusion of Furina is evidence that Furina can be valued outside the divine role. Her invitation creates a social frame where Furina can accept risk safely. Clorinde's behavior is a practical argument that competence, friendship, and play can coexist without consumption.

## Charlotte and the public truth problem

Charlotte's wish to reveal the real Furina is sympathetic. It also recreates a familiar problem: someone else wants to decide how the public will use Furina's identity. Furina's reluctance does not make truth unimportant. It establishes that public truth-telling and survivor authorship can conflict.

## Public opinion

Public opinion provides evidence about the role's environment, not Furina's internal happiness. The public expects:

- confidence;
- spectacle;
- divine certainty;
- courtroom drama;
- recognizable celebrity performance.

Those expectations causally shape Furina. Public love is therefore both support and pressure. The trial demonstrates how quickly the same public can demand proof and withdraw recognition.

## Candidate rejection criteria

The pass rejects candidate records that only:

- name Furina in quest logistics;
- repeat identical text across CodexQuest/Talk;
- include Furina through aggregate scene membership;
- state a title without judgment;
- infer intimacy from co-presence;
- refer to public reputation as though it were private fact.

## Third-party conclusion

The external record converges on a stable proposition:

> Furina appears more frivolous, confident and self-centered than the serious, fearful and duty-bound person available to observers with greater access. The discrepancy is real, but no observer's partial view should be allowed to erase either side. Hostile witnesses overread fraud; sympathetic post-reveal witnesses risk overreading pure martyrdom.
