## Major transition sequence

### 1. Audience-constructed authority

Furina begins as a person whose public fluency depends on an intelligible stage. She is not socially empty. She is highly responsive to expectation and capable of turning expectation into command. Silence reveals the dependency before the source explains why it exists.

### 2. Error becomes procedural memory

The Lyney defeat does not cure pride, but it changes later trial conduct. Furina becomes more willing to admit uncertainty and to allow others' evidence to alter public conclusions. The humiliating event remains emotionally active, so growth and vulnerability coexist.

### 3. Duty becomes disconnected from explainable agency

Under prophecy pressure, Furina's state is defined by an impossible split: she sincerely works for Fontaine and believes in rescue, but cannot demonstrate the mechanism or share the truth. She is central to the plan and ignorant of its technical operation.

### 4. Moral injury and role collapse

Poisson makes the cost concrete. Furina's private state deteriorates into apology and desire to stop, while the public role becomes more automatic. The trial destroys the social identity but cannot compel disclosure.

### 5. Retrospective origin and embodied fusion

The interior history shows that divine theatricality began as an imposed adaptive technique. Five centuries of repetition turned it into habit, craft, preference, pride, humor, and reflex. The end of the assignment therefore cannot simply reveal a pristine pre-role self.

### 6. Negative freedom

Immediately after the crisis, freedom means not being needed. This protects Furina from renewed consumption, but also removes the only stable source of social purpose.

### 7. Positive freedom

Through the troupe, Poisson, and friendship, Furina begins choosing bounded obligations. Usefulness becomes compatible with autonomy because she can define scope and exit.

### 8. Integrated repertoire

Later play and professional work show a person who still dramatizes, fears, directs, judges, seeks companionship indirectly, and enjoys audiences. Recovery changes the conditions under which these traits operate rather than deleting them.

## Stable traits across states

- rapid reading of audience and social atmosphere;
- theatrical imagination;
- sensitivity to embarrassment and public legitimacy;
- risk aversion toward uncontrolled bodily danger;
- strong capacity for sustained duty when collective stakes are clear;
- indirect expression of loneliness and attachment;
- interest in law, rules, stories, and the form of public experience;
- ability to recover function by reframing a scene;
- difficulty receiving care without translating it into debt, service, or role.
