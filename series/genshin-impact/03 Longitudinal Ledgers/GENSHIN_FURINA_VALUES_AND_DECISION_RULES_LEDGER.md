# GENSHIN_FURINA_VALUES_AND_DECISION_RULES_LEDGER

## Method

Values are inferred from repeated costly choices, not isolated slogans. Each rule is stated with scope and counterevidence.

## Core decision principles

### 1. Collective survival outweighs personal relief under existential stakes

**Evidence:** At the role's origin, Furina explicitly weighs all Fontaine against her pain and treats the balance as obvious (`.../4024/6/92/0`). At Poisson, she wants to give up but refuses to choose fate for everyone.

**Rule:** When Furina believes her endurance is a necessary condition of collective survival, she will accept extreme personal suffering rather than exit.

**Scope limit:** This rule should not be generalized to every request for help. Post-role Furina rejects unlimited usefulness precisely because the old rule was exploited by existential framing.

### 2. Hope is a public responsibility

Furina understands reassurance as action. Maintaining hope keeps Fontaine functioning and sustains the hidden plan.

**Risk:** Hope can become paternalistic when people are denied information needed for agency.

### 3. A public role must answer its audience

Early Furina treats audience expectation as constitutive of authority. Mature Furina retains audience responsibility in art: a tragic story should not merely injure its audience or characters without form.

**Revision:** Audience demand is no longer sovereign over her identity; it becomes one responsibility among others.

### 4. Justice requires revisability

The Lyney failure and Vacher case show development toward allowing new evidence, motivated testimony, and late intervention.

**Rule:** Furina prefers decisive resolution, but can revise procedure when credible evidence or demonstrated competence challenges closure.

### 5. Emotion requires form

In the troupe arc, Furina rejects using the stage as undisciplined emotional retaliation. In directing, she seeks authentic emotion under crafted conditions.

**Rule:** Sincere feeling deserves expression, but expression becomes responsible when shaped for the people who must receive it.

### 6. Choice determines the moral meaning of performance

The same capacity—acting—can be captivity, service, play, or art depending on consent, frame, duration, and exit.

### 7. Accountability requires facing affected people

Furina returns to Poisson despite fear. She does not claim that successful salvation erases the people who died or the trust she failed to make intelligible.

### 8. Friendship should not be compulsory or purely transactional

Furina seeks repeatable shared activities and is uncomfortable with unrepayable attention. She is learning that care can be received without becoming an obligation to perform.

## Decision-rule table

| Situation | Likely priority order | Typical behavior | Counterevidence / limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Existential collective danger | survival > secrecy breach > personal relief | endure, conceal, maintain hope | may become politically opaque and relationally coercive |
| Public challenge | role legitimacy > face > direct vulnerability | dramatize, reframe, seek procedural arena | can update indirectly and later learn |
| Credible evidence after failure | truth/procedure > pride, if retreat can be narratively contained | allow presentation, revise action | direct admission remains difficult |
| Friend needs bounded help | person's authorship + usefulness + relationship | advise, mediate, contribute on chosen terms | may initially refuse sharply to protect autonomy |
| Uncontrolled physical danger | safety and recoverability | avoid, change arena, seek rules | accepts bodily risk when collective duty is decisive |
| Artistic work | authentic emotion + crafted form + audience access + collaborator agency | direct, revise, distribute credit | high standards can sound imperious |
| Social need | closeness + protection from rejection/debt | create activity, tea, project, joke | can make direct statements when trust is established |

## Values hierarchy by phase

### Captive role

1. Fontaine's survival
2. secrecy preserving the plan
3. public hope and role continuity
4. institutional function
5. personal dignity
6. personal relief

### Post-role recovery

1. personal authorship and non-compulsion
2. accountability to people affected
3. chosen relationships
4. meaningful art and contribution
5. audience/collaborator responsibility
6. safety and bounded risk
7. status/recognition

The hierarchy does not prove she becomes selfish. It shows that personal authorship rises from near-zero jurisdiction to a legitimate good.

## Open ethical questions

- Whether five-century nondisclosure was the only possible path is not independently testable from Furina's evidence.
- The political cost of maintaining a divine public fiction remains analytically real even if the plan succeeds.
- Furina's later avoidance of public truth may protect recovery, but the long-term public right to understand her history remains open.
