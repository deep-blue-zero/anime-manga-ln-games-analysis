# GENSHIN_FURINA_SELF_EVALUATION_AND_REGULATION_LEDGER

## Scope

This ledger tracks how Furina evaluates herself, processes failure, regulates emotion, and responds to being seen. It does not assign a clinical diagnosis.

## Self-evaluation regimes

### Role adequacy

For most of the five centuries, Furina evaluates herself against one absolute standard:

> Did I preserve the appearance required to keep hope alive?

This makes small cracks morally enormous. Ordinary embarrassment becomes potential catastrophe. The standard is perfectionistic because the believed consequence of failure is national death.

### Public competence

Furina wants to appear intelligent, informed, elegant, and decisive. Trial failure is painful because it threatens both status and the role's credibility. She is especially reactive when an adversary references a prior mistake.

### Moral adequacy

Poisson changes self-evaluation from "did I perform well?" to "people died while I maintained hope." Her private speech becomes apology and meaninglessness. She is disappointed in herself even though the hidden plan remains outside her control.

### Post-role worth

After the crisis, Furina fears that without divinity and public utility she may not be worth attention. The apartment withdrawal and reluctance to meet former colleagues reflect this uncertainty.

### Chosen artistic adequacy

Later she evaluates herself through craft, collaboration, and whether a performance serves its people. This is a more plural standard: she can contribute without being the whole reason the world survives.

## Regulation strategies

| Strategy | Example | Benefit | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Theatrical reframing | turns tears into opera response, pursuit into fandom | restores fluency and face | blocks direct support and may appear unserious |
| Proceduralization | turns duel into trial, emotion into artistic problem | creates rules and recoverable roles | can avoid direct relational confrontation |
| Humor/teasing | converts perceptiveness or embarrassment into play | reduces intensity, preserves affiliation | may invalidate others or hide need |
| Compartmentalization | keeps private fear separate from public god | enables long-term function | produces isolation and identity blur |
| Work/investigation | responds to prophecy doubts with continued effort | sustains purpose and moral legitimacy | cannot substitute for knowledge/control; encourages overwork |
| Withdrawal | post-crisis apartment, avoiding Palais | protects from role reactivation | increases isolation and purposelessness |
| Activity-mediated contact | tea, projects, roleplay | allows closeness with bounded expectations | direct requests remain difficult |
| Artistic transformation | shapes emotion into story/performance | integrates experience and communicates | can become another role if consent is lost |

## Shame and repair

Furina's shame is socially organized. It intensifies when:

- she is ignored;
- confidence is publicly defeated;
- tears are visible;
- practical powerlessness is exposed;
- past error is invoked by a hostile person;
- attention seems directed only at the role;
- she cannot repay care.

Her usual repair is not plain confession. She reframes, jokes, performs, redirects to procedure, or later offers a practical contribution.

Growth appears when she can repair without restoring superiority:

- admitting imperfect memory;
- apologizing to Paimon;
- facing Poisson;
- acknowledging choice and fear;
- distributing credit.

## Guilt

Furina experiences at least three guilt forms:

1. **epistemic guilt:** public false accusation/being wrong;
2. **role guilt:** failing to appear capable enough to sustain trust;
3. **survivor/office guilt:** people die while she remains symbolically responsible.

The Poisson collapse is the strongest evidence for the third. Her apologies are not calibrated to actual causal control; they express the burden of being the person who promised hope.

## Fear

Furina is fear-sensitive rather than globally timid.

She fears:

- uncontrolled physical confrontation;
- Arlecchino and bodily threat;
- public exposure;
- a crack in the secret;
- ridicule of the powerless ordinary self;
- being needed without limits;
- being cared for in ways she cannot repay.

She nevertheless acts under fear when:

- collective stakes are decisive;
- accountability requires presence;
- a bounded artistic/community commitment is chosen;
- rules make risk recoverable.

## Loneliness

Loneliness is often expressed indirectly:

- a table is too lonely;
- she follows after refusing;
- she invents tea/activity reasons;
- she asks to be invited again;
- she worries others wanted the role, not her.

Profile story 89205 shows that freedom is not solitude. Furina's recovery requires learning that relationships can exist without ontological dependence.

## Receiving care

Furina's discomfort with unrepayable attention likely arises from two historical asymmetries:

- the public gives adoration to an image, not reciprocal knowledge;
- Furina gives hidden sacrifice without receiving informed care.

Ordinary care therefore raises questions of debt and worth. She often answers through gifts, food, professional service, or shared activity.

## Mature regulation

The mature corpus does not show complete ease. It shows more options:

- direct but qualified honesty;
- boundary assertion;
- apology;
- chosen retreat;
- reversible performance;
- friendship activities;
- professional work;
- humor that affiliates rather than only defends.

## Reconstruction guidance

A plausible mature Furina under stress will often:

1. read the room quickly;
2. dramatize or joke to regain control;
3. seek a rule, role, or bounded task;
4. resist direct helplessness;
5. become more honest if the interlocutor remains safe and does not seize control;
6. choose responsibility when stakes and jurisdiction are clear;
7. withdraw if "being needed" begins to resemble indefinite consumption.
