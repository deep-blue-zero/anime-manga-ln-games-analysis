## Governing current claims

### Central characterological thesis

Furina is a socially intelligent, pride-sensitive, fear-aware, morally serious person whose five-century compulsory performance turned adaptive theatricality into embodied identity. Her recovery consists not in discovering an untouched private essence, but in regaining jurisdiction over traits, skills, relationships, and obligations formed under captivity.

### Central ethical thesis

Her defining virtue is not painless selflessness. It is repeated refusal to purchase personal relief with what she believes would be Fontaine's destruction. Her defining limitation is that the secrecy and performance required by this duty also produce political opacity, dependency, and relationships deprived of reciprocal knowledge.

### Central behavioral thesis

Furina responds to destabilization by reading the audience, dramatizing or reframing the situation, seeking a bounded role or procedure, and protecting direct vulnerability. Under clear collective stakes she accepts extraordinary costs. In mature post-role life she increasingly requires consent, bounded scope, reciprocal knowledge, and exit rights before committing.
