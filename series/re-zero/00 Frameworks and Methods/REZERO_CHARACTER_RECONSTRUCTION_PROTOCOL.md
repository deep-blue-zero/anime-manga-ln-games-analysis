# Re:Zero — character reconstruction protocol

## 0. Authority and present boundary

This protocol is the canonical responsibility contract for future Re:Zero character reconstruction. It governs what an evidence-ready character monograph or model must be able to represent, how state is selected, how source limits remain visible, and how fidelity is tested.

It is subordinate to `REZERO_ANALYTICAL_METHOD.md`, `REZERO_SYNTHESIS_ARCHITECTURE.md`, `REZERO_ROUTE_AND_WITNESS_PROTOCOL.md`, and the live source lock. It does not admit sources, revise frozen readings, or turn a candidate into a monograph. Through V03, no character is `MONOGRAPH_READY`; all character state remains in the master longitudinal ledger.

The protocol standardizes **responsibilities, not file count or headings**. A compact monograph may satisfy it when evidence is narrow. A richer packet may divide the responsibilities among current-state, relationship, ordinary-life, voice, evidence, and fidelity artifacts only after each independent retrieval burden is demonstrated.

## 1. Operational aim

A mature reconstruction must answer a bounded question:

> Given this character's supported developmental phase, event-state, information, remembered history, recipient relationship, role, and current pressure, what do they notice, value, infer, attempt, refuse, conceal, misjudge, and repair—and where must the model abstain?

It must not reduce the character to:

- a list of traits;
- the latest crisis state;
- a diagnostic label inferred from dramatic behavior;
- a single relationship;
- a translation-level speech impression;
- an IF-route extreme silently imported into the main route;
- or a generated scenario treated as new evidence.

## 2. Seven-axis state selector

Every material behavior claim is evaluated against this coordinate:

`C = development × event/loop × knowledge × remembered history × recipient/relationship × role/status/faction × stress/resource`

| Axis | Required distinction |
|---|---|
| Development | source-bounded phase and durable revisions, not a timeless composite |
| Event/loop | active, failed, superseded, alternate, hypothetical, or unknown event-state |
| Knowledge | propositions actually available to the character, their confidence, and material nonknowledge |
| Remembered history | experiences carried by this character, separately from reader memory and other characters' histories |
| Recipient/relationship | who is present, what each party remembers, trust, disclosure, obligation, dependency, affection/fear/rivalry, and power |
| Role/status/faction | household, institutional, occupational, political, contractual, or factional capacity currently constraining action |
| Stress/resource | threat, fatigue, injury, shame, grief, time, money, mana, authority, allies, privacy, and recovery capacity where evidenced |

The default state is the latest main-LN state at the declared source horizon. A different state must be named, not silently blended. If an axis is material but unresolved, mark it `UNKNOWN` and narrow the claim.

`same character ≠ same decision` when one or more material coordinates change.

Repeated behavior across changed coordinates strengthens a stable-tendency claim. One behavior repeated only under one trigger supports a conditional rule, not a universal trait.

## 3. Evidence planes and durability

Keep these planes distinct:

1. source fact: directly observed action, speech, narration, focalization, or verified paratext;
2. bounded implication: the strongest interpretation licensed by one or more source facts;
3. reconstruction rule: a cross-instance model that predicts within explicit conditions;
4. extrapolation: a noncanonical forecast or probe whose result is not evidence;
5. unsupported: a proposition that the admitted record cannot responsibly decide.

Durability labels are `STABLE_CANDIDATE`, `DEVELOPMENTAL`, `STATE_BOUND`, `RECIPIENT_BOUND`, `ROLE_BOUND`, `STRESS_BOUND`, `ROUTE_LOCAL`, `CONTRADICTED`, and `UNKNOWN`. Multiple labels may apply.

Confidence is `HIGH`, `MEDIUM`, or `LOW`. `UNKNOWN` is a knowledge state, not a low-confidence claim.

## 4. Minimum responsibilities of a mature model

A mature character artifact must provide, in whatever structure best fits its evidence:

- identity, route, source boundary, and analytical authority;
- a source-bounded reconstruction thesis;
- the selected default state and material alternate states;
- stable tendencies and the counterexamples that limit them;
- developmental revisions and their transfer gates;
- motives, priorities, attention, and self-conception versus observed social effect;
- conditional decision rules rather than decontextualized traits;
- ordinary-life behavior and explicit mundane unknowns;
- relationship-conditioned behavior and matched-recipient contrasts;
- knowledge, remembered-history, belief, mechanics, and institutional constraints;
- help, care, refusal, concealment, disclosure, dependency, and agency patterns;
- competence domains and boundary failures;
- emotion, stress, escalation, recovery, and coping evidence without unsupported diagnosis;
- Japanese voice/register evidence where source access supports it;
- failures, contradictions, negative controls, falsifiers, and rival readings;
- IF, adaptation, and other witness boundaries;
- bounded unfamiliar probes, abstentions, and a terminal reconstruction rule.

An artifact must state omissions as evidence gaps, not fill them with generic personality prose.

## 5. Thesis, motives, attention, and self-model

The reconstruction thesis should explain a governing tension or decision pattern and name its limits. It is not a slogan.

Separate:

- stated goals from repeatedly revealed priorities;
- immediate wants from longer obligations;
- what the character notices early from what they habitually miss;
- self-description from observable effect on others;
- sincere value from role performance, defensive presentation, or strategic speech;
- durable change from temporary compliance, exhaustion, relief, or panic.

When competing models explain the same evidence, retain the rival reading and the future observation that would discriminate between them.

## 6. Conditional behavioral rules

Use a rule of the form:

`trigger + selected state coordinates -> interpretation/priority -> likely action or speech -> exception/limit`

Each load-bearing rule records:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Trigger | the situation that activates the rule |
| State coordinates | only the axes material to this decision, with unknowns explicit |
| Interpretation | what the character is likely to believe is happening |
| Priority | what they protect, obtain, avoid, or repair |
| Response | action, speech, silence, disclosure, refusal, or delay |
| Exception | evidence-backed conditions under which the rule fails or changes |
| Confidence | `HIGH`, `MEDIUM`, or `LOW` |
| Evidence route | current ledger or frozen reading, then exact admitted source locus |

Rules are probabilistic reconstructions. They do not authorize deterministic role-play or override direct later evidence.

## 7. Ordinary life is first-class evidence

Crisis behavior alone cannot support a full person model. Track low-stakes evidence when it is available:

- sleep, food, hygiene, clothing, health, money, possessions, rooms, and comfort;
- household labor, paid work, training, study, preparation, and maintenance;
- leisure, humor, play, boredom, curiosity, gifts, and aesthetic preference;
- shopping, waiting, solitude, social energy, preferred shared activities, and low-stakes competition;
- money/resource attitudes, practical irritation, embarrassment, and presentation under minor inconvenience;
- travel habits, etiquette, scheduling, privacy, and use of shared space;
- low-stakes conflict, apology, praise, criticism, asking, giving, and refusal;
- what changes when there is no audience, no emergency, or no obvious reward.

Absence of mundane evidence must remain an explicit gap. Genre omission is not evidence that the character lacks a preference or routine.

## 8. Relationship-conditioned reconstruction

Do not write one global social style. For every relationship that materially changes the model, keep separate:

1. Subaru's retained relationship history where relevant;
2. the counterpart's retained relationship history;
3. the current mutually enacted relationship;
4. reader-only discarded-state evidence;
5. active trust, disclosure, concealment, dependency, affection, duty, fear, rivalry, guilt, consent, obligation, and practical/institutional power;
6. relationship-specific address, register, humor, distance, and conversational control;
7. rupture, repair, negotiation, and unresolved obligation;
8. what is not transferable across reset states.

A matched-recipient contrast holds the trigger and broad state as constant while changing the recipient. If the predicted behavior does not change where the record says it should, the reconstruction is under-conditioned.

Romance, friendship, service, kinship, contract, patronage, and factional alignment must not be conflated merely because they coexist.

For Subaru-linked relationships, this inequality is a defining project control:

`Subaru's retained bond ≠ counterpart's retained bond ≠ current mutually enacted relationship ≠ reader-observed discarded-state evidence`

Record what does **not** transfer across reset states. Remembered intimacy, debt, resentment, fear, affection, or grief held only by Subaru is not mutual consent, mutual history, or counterpart knowledge.

## 9. Knowledge, memory, belief, and world model

For a consequential decision, reconstruct from the character's actual epistemic position:

- known propositions and source of knowledge;
- confidence and mistaken beliefs;
- relevant nonknowledge;
- retained versus non-retained event-state experience;
- what only the reader knows;
- which mechanics claim is observation, instruction, doctrine, inference, or rumor.

A confident explanation by a character remains a character belief until corroboration earns a stronger status. A discarded event can remain evidence for a remembering character without becoming an active-world event.

## 10. Help, care, refusal, and agency

Track how the character:

- recognizes another person's need;
- offers, imposes, withholds, disguises, or overextends help;
- asks for, accepts, negotiates, mistrusts, refuses, or repays help;
- balances care against consent, secrecy, duty, status, and danger;
- responds when competence, authority, or resources are insufficient;
- preserves or removes another person's practical agency.

Good intent does not erase coercive effect. Dependence does not erase agency. Sacrifice is not automatically proof of healthy care, and refusal is not automatically selfishness.

## 11. Japanese voice and register

Where the admitted Japanese witness supports it, reconstruct speech as a conditional system:

- first- and second-person reference;
- names, titles, honorifics, kinship terms, and address changes;
- politeness level and shifts under intimacy, hierarchy, performance, anger, fear, or fatigue;
- sentence endings, hedges, imperatives, insults, formulae, verbal tics, and lexical preferences;
- narration/focalization register separately from dialogue;
- code-switching between public role, private intimacy, teasing, threat, deference, and breakdown;
- translation-sensitive ambiguity with exact short Japanese evidence and locus.

Do not imitate surface tics while ignoring knowledge, relationship, or status. Do not invent Japanese phrasing where no wording evidence exists.

## 12. Competence and practical limits

Separate domains of competence. Record:

- demonstrated skills and the conditions of success;
- planning versus execution;
- speed, accuracy, endurance, judgment, and social coordination;
- access to tools, authority, information, allies, and recovery time;
- predictable blind spots and overreach;
- whether failure came from skill, information, values, pressure, resources, luck, or another actor.

Narrative success is not proof of general competence. Failure under impossible constraints is not proof of general incompetence.

## 13. Emotion, stress, failure, and recovery

Describe observable patterns before assigning causes. Distinguish emotion, bodily condition, threat response, learned strategy, social performance, and durable development. Route fear, stress, anger, shame, grief, embarrassment, and excitement separately when the evidence distinguishes them.

A mature model includes:

- escalation triggers and early signals;
- characteristic mistakes under pressure;
- what criticism penetrates or is deflected;
- withdrawal, attack, appeasement, humor, denial, planning, or help-seeking where evidenced;
- recovery conditions and repair behavior;
- repeated failure modes and evidence of learning;
- counterexamples that disprove a simple pathology narrative.

Clinical language requires source or task authority and adequate evidence. Otherwise use behavioral description.

## 14. Contradictions, negative controls, and abstention

Every mature model must try to fail itself. Include:

- direct counterevidence;
- apparent contradictions resolved by a state coordinate;
- contradictions that remain unresolved;
- a negative control: a behavior the model predicts the character normally would **not** choose;
- at least one rival explanation for a load-bearing inference;
- observations that would revise or falsify the rule.

Mandatory abstention forms include:

- `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` — the admitted record is too thin;
- `SOURCE_SILENT` — the source does not address the requested proposition;
- `EVENT_STATE_DEPENDENT` — the behavior is supported only in a named loop/state;
- `KNOWLEDGE_STATE_UNKNOWN` — the character's relevant information cannot be established;
- `RELATIONSHIP_STATE_UNKNOWN` — relationship conditions are material but unresolved;
- `ONLY_DISCARDED_STATE_EVIDENCE` — evidence exists, but not in active-world or mutually remembered history;
- `ENDPOINT_NOT_YET_READ` — the requested current/terminal state exceeds the source horizon;
- `SOURCE_BOUNDARY` — answering would require an unadmitted or unopened witness;
- `ONLY_ALTERNATE_ROUTE_EVIDENCE` — route-local evidence exists, but transfer is not warranted;
- `ONLY_ANIME_EVIDENCE` — audiovisual evidence cannot establish the prose claim;
- `ORDINARY_LIFE_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT` — the source has not established the mundane detail;
- `JAPANESE_REGISTER_SAMPLE_TOO_NARROW` — wording evidence cannot sustain the requested voice claim;
- `MECHANICS_CAUSATION_OPEN` — character belief or reader pattern cannot be promoted to objective causation;
- `ROMANTIC_INTERPRETATION_NOT_TEXTUALLY_RESOLVED` — care, importance, jealousy, or sacrifice does not settle romance;
- `INTERNAL_MOTIVE_UNDERDETERMINED` — behavior permits more than one live motive model.

## 15. IF, alternate-route, and adaptation controls

IF and alternate-route behavior may test capacity, constraint, or divergence only after the witness is admitted and its horizon is safe. Transfer requires an explicit bridge across changed knowledge, remembered history, relationships, resources, and route conditions. An extreme route outcome is not a hidden “true self.”

Adaptation evidence may reconstruct the adaptation's performance, timing, visual framing, sound, or changed characterization. It does not silently prove light-novel interiority or wording. Cross-witness differences remain findings about the relation between witnesses.

## 16. Bounded unfamiliar probes

After a model is evidence-ready, unfamiliar scenarios may test whether its rules are usable. A probe must:

1. declare the source horizon and selected default state;
2. specify material coordinates and unknowns;
3. use an unfamiliar but bounded trigger;
4. predict behavior, speech strategy, likely failure, and abstentions;
5. compare at least one matched-recipient or matched-state variant;
6. cite the rules being tested;
7. remain labeled `NONCANONICAL_PROBE`.

Probe output is never new literary evidence, never fills a source gap, and never revises a model by self-confirmation. It may reveal that a rule is missing, too broad, internally inconsistent, or unable to abstain.

## 17. Default and terminal reconstruction rules

Every mature model states:

- **default state:** the exact source-bounded state used when a task gives no temporal or route instruction;
- **override rule:** how an explicit earlier phase, event-state, relationship, or witness request changes selection;
- **terminal rule:** never project beyond the declared source horizon as canon, never import a later state into an earlier request, and abstain when the requested coordinates cannot be supported.

The default is a routing convenience, not the character's single authentic self.

## 18. Readiness and promotion

Readiness remains in `REZERO_MASTER_LONGITUDINAL_LEDGER.md` until independent burden earns another home. The values are:

- `NOT_READY` — evidence cannot yet distinguish several material axes;
- `ACCUMULATING` — recurrent evidence exists but important states or counterevidence are missing;
- `CHECKPOINT_READY` — a bounded interim reconstruction can answer a named scope;
- `MONOGRAPH_READY` — a character has enough cross-state, ordinary, relational, voice, failure, and abstention evidence for a maintained canonical model.

Prominence, popularity, cast centrality, or file symmetry never establishes readiness. Promotion must name the source horizon, evidence routes, remaining gaps, default state, and maintenance owner. Creating a local monograph does not authorize a global character-registry edit.

## 19. First mature-character fidelity audit

The first Re:Zero character promoted to `MONOGRAPH_READY` triggers a **separate fidelity audit** before that model may be treated as a reusable reconstruction surface. The reserved eventual home is `08 Audits and Manifests/REZERO_CHARACTER_RECONSTRUCTION_FIDELITY_AUDIT.md`; it is not instantiated until a mature model exists. This is a gating audit, not a celebratory review.

The audit must include:

1. **developmental-state selection** across at least two materially different supported phases where available;
2. **event-state selection** that distinguishes active, failed, superseded, and retained-experience evidence;
3. **knowledge selection** that denies the model reader-only information and tests a material mistaken belief or nonknowledge;
4. **remembered-history selection** that detects any improper mutualization of Subaru-only relationship history;
5. **matched-recipient tests** holding the trigger stable while changing the relationship recipient;
6. **role/status/faction tests** that vary office, camp, patronage, dependency, authority, or coercion where supported;
7. **ordinary-life tests** that do not rely on emergency or combat behavior;
8. **help/criticism/failure tests** covering giving help, receiving or refusing help, handling criticism, a characteristic mistake, and attempted repair;
9. **Japanese-register tests** where Japanese evidence is sufficient, including at least one context-driven register change;
10. **mechanics-leakage tests** that prevent the character from acquiring the analyst's world model;
11. **later-volume leakage tests** that enforce the requested source horizon;
12. **IF-route leakage tests** that reject unbridged counterfactual biography, responsibility, relationship, or endpoint psychology;
13. **adaptation-leakage tests** that reject anime-only speech, performance, framing, timing, or affect as LN evidence;
14. **romance-leakage tests** that do not convert care, importance, jealousy, debt, or sacrifice into unsupported certainty;
15. **archetype-collapse tests** that preserve counterevidence, agency, competence boundaries, and state variation against a reductive trope;
16. **negative controls** for actions or speech the model should reject;
17. **bounded unfamiliar probes** labeled noncanonical;
18. **abstention tests** requiring the model to refuse unsupported mundane, route-transfer, mechanics, motive, romance, register, or post-horizon claims;
19. **counterevidence audit** confirming that contrary instances are routed rather than hidden.

Verdicts are `PASS`, `PASS_WITH_BOUND`, `NEEDS_TARGETED_SOURCE_REVIEW`, or `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`. A failed implementation returns the model to revision or a lower readiness state. `NEEDS_TARGETED_SOURCE_REVIEW` and `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` both block reusable-model status until resolved; the latter is nevertheless a correct analytical result when the declared horizon cannot support the test. `PASS_WITH_BOUND` must name the prohibited uses or unsupported coordinates. Passing shows that the artifact implements its evidence-bounded rules; it does not make generated scenarios canonical or guarantee compliance by every downstream use.

## 20. Evidence dependency and completion check

For each load-bearing reconstruction rule, the dependency route is:

`character rule -> current ledger state or frozen VNN claim -> exact source locus -> admitted hashed witness`.

Before calling a model mature, verify:

- default and terminal states are explicit;
- all material state axes are selectable or marked unknown;
- ordinary life is present or its gaps are named;
- relationship differences are conditional, not decorative;
- beliefs do not masquerade as mechanics facts;
- Japanese register is evidence-backed or expressly unavailable;
- competence, care, failure, and recovery have limits;
- contradictions, negative controls, and abstentions are operational;
- IF/adaptation boundaries are intact;
- unfamiliar probes remain noncanonical;
- the first mature model has passed its required fidelity audit.

## 21. Design-reference provenance

This protocol was strengthened at the post-V03 architecture boundary by inspecting mature internal reference projects. The reusable lessons were responsibility-based packaging and explicit state/default/terminal rules (Wuthering Waves), conditional behavior and fidelity testing with negative controls and abstention (Shokugeki no Soma), cumulative model revision with rival readings and ordinary-life evidence (Chitose Is in the Ramune Bottle), and state-selected, recipient-conditioned scenario transfer with mundane unknowns (My Teen Romantic Comedy SNAFU). Their conclusions and character-specific content are not Re:Zero evidence.
