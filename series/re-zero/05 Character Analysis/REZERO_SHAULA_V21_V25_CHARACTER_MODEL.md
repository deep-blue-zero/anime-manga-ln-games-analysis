# Shaula through V25

## 1. What two days are worth

Four centuries of waiting have given Shaula remarkably modest expectations. She tells Subaru that at first she would have been happy just to see him. Then he turned up. Of the four days she counts, he slept for two. In the other two she could speak to him and touch him. Subaru had asked about years. Her answer in days takes him by surprise. Those two days have changed the terms on which she could bear to see him go. When he asks her for a piece of information that could end their stay, her first answer is a joke. Her next answer is to put her hands over her ears. She can explain her refusal perfectly well once she gives up trying to make him stop asking. The woman who would agree to die at his word wants to keep something he is entitled to ask her. Both answers are hers. V24 part0014 B0027–0065, B0096–0192.

She is good at waiting in a particular sense. She can do the job that has been left for her. She can give the same account of who she is to anyone who will listen. Neither ability tells us how much of the time passed pleasantly. In the private passage late in V25, she remembers talking and walking with her master. For someone who describes herself as forgetful, she thinks the rules that kept her in this place may also have kept those memories from slipping away. Even she cannot settle whether trust sustained the long watch or whether she went on because she had already been doing it for so long. When he finally arrives, she discovers that he could stay. Better still, she might *go with him*. That small change of direction opens a possible life beyond the job. Her later pleasure in the fact of having waited can coexist with her earlier grief at the thought of doing it again. These statements belong to different moments, including a farewell of uncertain standing. V25 part0017 B0067–0099, B0191–0215, B0242–0272, B0324–0331.

Shaula has a social life proportionate to the brief chance she gets. She insists to Subaru that her hairstyle is a scorpion tail, likes food she did not cook, gets properly frightened, wastes other people's patience and can see considerable promise in taking a child who has hurt them under her wing. She can be insensitive about that same child's apparent death. In one return she embarrasses herself in front of Subaru and scarcely notices that Meili was worried too. In another she is delighted to find that the task of looking after Meili includes her. At the siege she fires beautifully, takes pleasure in being watched and reaches the limit of what beauty and accuracy alone can accomplish. Write the pressure and the person who is actually there. A generic centuries-old sentinel supplies none of those choices. V22 part0009 B0242–0261, part0012 B0608–0627, part0014 B0351–0370. V23 part0013 B0037–0067. V24 part0010 B0329–0373, part0012 B0093–0127.

At the end Subaru wants one more of her familiar answers. Outside, with Beatrice, Meili and Julius there, the giant creature brushes his tear with a fang. Earlier in the apparent white encounter she has used her tongue and found something to say about the taste. Later still a tiny animal will take his ear in its claw. The three contacts are materially different. The book lets the reader feel how readily Subaru would put a single person behind all three. It also specifies where the remembered person is absent. That is the limit governing use of this portrait. V25 part0017 B0218–0242, B0273–0317, B0324–0339, part0018 B0003–0038.

## 3. A star-keeper with her own methods

The first introduction is conducted at considerable range. In two attempts Subaru dies before he can ask the person behind the shots who she is. On the successful approach he is in danger from a monster when the barrage that has made the tower so frightening becomes his means of survival. For a moment the marks on the page make it hard to give a verbal account of the rate of fire. He hears someone say she has found him. At their later meeting there is a pause that is easy to lose in recollection. Shaula inspects him, murmurs a count of three with no supplied object, and very slowly changes expression. Only then does she thoroughly flatten him and Beatrice. The young woman named on the page of reunion and the unseen source of the earlier deaths are the same star-keeper. The reader takes a substantially different path to knowing that than she does. V21 part0011 B0806–0820, part0012 B0091–0169, part0014 B0379–0414, B0545–0624. V22 part0009 B0485–0508.

Shaula assigns authorship of the means with cheerful precision. Her master told her to obstruct the approach. The method, she says, was hers. She gives the attack its showy foreign name, Hells Snipe, then introduces the Dimension Gate. Her pride is intelligible as a craftsperson’s. It can be dangerous to anyone trying to correct her assumptions. Meili points out that the earthworms are dead. Shaula laughs at the suggestion that the same result could befall her master. If the target had died it would only prove, in her account, that he wasn't him. Subaru is in the room with two experiences that refute the reasoning. He cannot use either to change her mind in this exchange. An interpretation that grants Shaula knowledge of his returns converts a disturbing little circular argument into a display of secret omniscience. It also gives her credit for a distinction she never makes in the field. V21 part0014 B0585–0605. V22 part0009 B0485–0532.

At close range she is happy to claim other people's words, provided she likes what is in them. Subaru's remark about being unprepared to deal with a beautiful woman earns the stipulation that he did, after all, call her beautiful. Julius's polite questions produce an elaborate turning away, complete with a sound effect she supplies herself. The old injunction against talking to anyone else gives her an excellent opportunity to play at being mysterious. When Subaru gives permission to answer, she first checks whether he really means it, then takes evident relief in dropping the act. She also cares how the people newly allowed to talk to her call her. An honorific makes her blush. She would prefer plain Shaula. Julius finds his own courteous compromise. Being called a thing is unwelcome. She says her master gave her this name, and the prospect of a second woman receiving it from him troubles her. Asked about the historical Sage's achievements, she can confidently identify herself as Shaula and remain uncertain about the public attribution of those deeds. Julius brings the reputation to the tower. She has been inside it all this time. There is quite a lot of personal taste in the person who is also happy to call herself an instrument. V22 part0009 B0105–0194, B0510–0532.

The coin with a man's face on it makes a poor test of her recognition. She can inventory hair and the usual features with a straight face while everybody else sees a difficulty. What she relies on is a distinctive smell, black and disagreeable in her description, as familiar as any sign in her world. Washing will do little for the problem Subaru and the others actually have. She identifies her old master as Flugel, includes Reid and Volcanica among the people she knew and is capable of receiving news about them. Ram tells her how Reid died. Shaula briefly lowers her eyes, then wishes it had been the dragon. She does not arrive with an infallible chronicle of the intervening centuries. ## 5. The company she can keep

Her response to a changed Subaru can be generous for a reason that is also terribly partial. In E024 she can imagine him forgetting things and still believe the master should be free to do what he likes. She offers a kind of continuity when the person in front of her cannot recognize himself in other people's accounts. He experiences relief with his face obscured from the girls.
