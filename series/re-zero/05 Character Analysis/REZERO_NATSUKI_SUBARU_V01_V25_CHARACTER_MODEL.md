## 1. Governing interpretation

Subaru wants his presence to matter to the people he loves. Small, usable work can satisfy that wish. He learns household tasks, makes clothes and toys, practices with a whip, uses practical arithmetic and negotiates when he can, remembers details of other people's comfort and asks specialized allies to do what he cannot. Under a threatened relationship or fear of arriving too late, he can demand stronger proof that the person needed is him. An achievement may then seem worth more than his life, the recipient's preference or another participant's opportunity to object. That pressure makes selfishness, duty, affection and competent work coexist. It gives no single hidden motive for every joke or rescue. B01–B07, B15–B21, B26–B31.

The earlier model traced an idealized father who could make a room respond and a boy who tried to turn that response into a transferable act. His playfulness and appetite for attention survive the loss of friends and school withdrawal. The constructed V10 home lets him receive affection from the represented Kenichi and Naoko without having to succeed first. He defends those received answers in the tea encounter and later borrows Naoko's lesson with Emilia. His use of the lesson in V13 carries real comfort and a continuing gap. He insists on her continuity and then physically initiates a kiss whose recipient response he cannot confidently interpret. Care, promise keeping and control are separable within the same person. B01, B07–B08, B13–B14.

Sanctuary puts help into an ordinary relationship rather than a contest for uniqueness. Otto's choice to return after an offer of payment for silence defeats Subaru's account of him as a reluctant self-interested merchant. Subaru first mistakes the word for a name, laughs in a way Otto can hear as ridicule, then takes responsibility for the misunderstanding and thanks him. At another point he accepts an imperfect telling to Otto while keeping Return by Death and the tea private. A friend can give usable help without receiving his entire experience. During the latest Sanctuary attempt, Subaru can ask a representative he then addresses as Ryuzu-san for guidance despite having authority to command an ordinary copy, and ask Beatrice to choose him after she has repeatedly refused. His need and pressure stay visible even when she freely takes his hand. B09–B16.

Pristella develops real authority and practical skill. Received knighthood and repeated training make the label useful, and pain, inadequate intelligence, the need for a shield and another speaker's decision still define its reach. In the city he warns Rachins without revealing how he knows and needs Beatrice to propose ways of coping with the affected crowd. His public address through the metia opens vulnerability to strangers and asks people to sustain one another. The girl's reply in a shelter is available to the reader. Subaru has no private transcript of her response. With Reinhard he accepts a division of labor, while his private resolve to return if his allies die again puts his own life outside the rescue. B17–B24.

The person who says that experience has made him a better collaborator can still take control away from others. Subaru's choice to withhold the artificial Echidna's occupation of Anastasia's body aims to prevent her own camp from stopping the journey. This is a chosen strategy with a foreseeable consultation cost. In V21 he tells Petra he wants to be the person Rem sees and praises first. He can imagine a stronger rescuer while still electing to go himself. The practical wheelchair and diary-assisted bedside telling are useful care. Neither demonstrates that he alone may provide it. The same Subaru later fears Patrasche's current presence because he remembers the deaths under dense miasma. The memory is probative of his fear without incriminating this Patrasche or giving Ram the events for which he feels shame. B25–B33.

Memory loss makes his self-comparison more explicit. The new Subaru knows Japan and his name and inhabits a trained, marked body. He lacks much of its local autobiography and cannot read local writing or retrieve trained whip procedure simply on demand. Others' accounts give him a capable predecessor to admire, then fear. The Meili murder involves his body's hands during a missing interval. His terror at the evidence and the inscription cannot identify an agent. His subsequent cover-up, purposeful harm to Patrasche in an earlier discarded segment and his rush at Ram have their own positive evidence. The new self also takes a warning seriously, returns an apology, goes to catch Emilia and accepts help in rescuing Meili. Describing all those acts as a single imposture would erase both accountability and agency. B35–B42.

The apparent solution in V25 first repeats the problem. After the V24 fall, Subaru hides the terms of his death tests from Beatrice and measures the current self against an imagined version that would never fail or tire. Beatrice asks for his story, calls him an ordinary boy and sees his failing legs when he tries to stand. The distance between his words and her observation gives him another person's account in a moment when he has stopped trusting his own. His own books, and the represented meeting of the two histories, let each recognize how much the other has already done while frightened and inadequate. Integration preserves the later attachments and the earlier ones. His restored confidence is partly a performance with an audience. It does not resolve Roy's case, or tell him how to bring Shaula back. B43–B51.

His last exchanges make the bounded change practical. Beatrice asks to share pain and joy. That offer can change how he distributes a burden, while the original uptake from Meili lacks a demonstrated agreement and has a finite bodily effect. He sees participating Louis's fear in the Corridor and tells her he will withhold his help. During the later attempted evacuation he grabs physical Louis while carrying Rem, cursing as he acts to protect them. He wakes on the plain with physical Louis already straddling him and licking his face. He pushes her away, checks for survival and then finds Rem breathing quietly. Earlier in the tower, Ram rebukes him for turning grief over Shaula into anger at another person, and he receives her correction. When Rem wakes, his grief and longing coexist with a choice to answer her actual question. Their relationship can now be addressed to someone who can reply, and still awaits the new answer he wants. B48–B55.
