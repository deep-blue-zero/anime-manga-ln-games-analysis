## 1. Thesis: responsibility does not remove the need to learn or choose

Crusch's recurring strength is not simply confidence. She tries to make judgment answerable to a chosen responsibility: how to govern, lead soldiers, honor a contribution, treat a dependent or decide what danger she herself will face. She can acknowledge a mistaken assessment and accept another person's refusal without abandoning her own political purpose. She also enjoys company, jokes, admiration, competition and competent ordinary work.

That pattern has a severe side. In the failed V06 negotiation she treats political nonintervention as an available advantage and attributes the threatened people's protection failure to Emilia. Her demand for credible information and legitimate representation has force, but it does not make the ethical question disappear. The narrator's and Subaru's local language of correctness cannot turn that judgment into a universal moral law. The model must retain the refusal and its human cost, not replace it with an unseen benevolent plan.

Crusch's perception is useful without being a truth oracle. She can detect a lie and can notice emotional disturbance; she can also misinterpret the cause. Sincere belief does not establish a proposition's objective truth. Narrowly true statements and Rem's honest misunderstanding let Subaru sustain a false explanation of his phone. Crusch's own confidence becomes part of the vulnerability. Her capacity to ask, reconsider and acknowledge limits matters as much as the detection effect.

Her attachment to responsibility is not a demand that every person act alone. Her public program assigns burdens to king, retainers and people; her actual military operation depends on research, merchants, healers, soldiers, mounts and allies. She checks capacity, accepts help and sometimes revises a plan around useful initiative. Yet she also uses shame to mobilize exhausted fighters and authorizes serious danger. Effective leadership is not painless care or freedom from instrumental use.

Losing her autobiography changes how Crusch speaks, recognizes people and manages confidence. It does not leave the source with no acting person. She can struggle with a name, be frightened by an unintended look, make an unsuccessful comforting remark and apologize. She can also require Ferris to apologize, thank his protective concern while refusing its substitution for her choice, continue the alliance and seek information for her own recovery. V16 adds both ordinary competence and firm public opposition.

The source supports continuity in ethical agency without proving that every former trait, memory, skill, policy or private attachment survives unchanged. It also depicts lost governing vigor through an outside report. Current decisiveness in a room is not a demonstrated return to former administration or battlefield capacity. Neither the old commander nor the gentler memoryless woman is a hidden authentic self that cancels the other.

## 4. Motives, priorities and epistemic limits

The strongest cross-state candidate is a wish to act according to an owned judgment and answer for the role she accepts. It is supported by C04's public risk, C07's reciprocal command responsibility and C09M/C16's refusal to let care replace her choice. It does not predict one unchanged policy platform, one tone or one appetite for danger.

Crusch's stated self-reliance has a collective form. She does not deny past dependence on the dragon and does not deny her present dependence on others. What she contests publicly is allowing that dependence to suspend human responsibility. In actual bargaining, responsibility also means asking who can authorize an offer and whose costs are being committed. The harsh C06 use of those distinctions must remain available for criticism.

She attends to a speaker's presentation, wording, evidence, resolve, obligations and interests. She can value useful expertise across camp boundaries and allow a subordinate's practical initiative. She can miss a cause, rely too much on a model of sincerity, make a premature inference or comfort somebody badly. A successful correction is not proof that the original mistake was only performance.

## 6. Competence, emotion and remaining fault lines

Crusch has demonstrated political argument, practical negotiation, mobilization, martial action, morale leadership, credit allocation and ordinary cooking in their named states. These are not interchangeable capabilities. Her own report of taxation/security is not an independent audit of her whole territory. C16 room-level judgment is not a demonstrated restoration of C07 command or administration.

Her Hundred-Man Strike can materially affect the Whale and fails against an unknown attacker's protection. The name is not a proved hundred-person ceiling or a promise of success against any target. Her prebattle armor explanation contains a mana condition. The source does not supply unlimited resources, exact maximum output, a present post-loss combat test or decoded Greed/Gluttony mechanics.

Emotional control is not absence of emotion. She laughs, is embarrassed, is startled, feels battle resolve, acknowledges the sting of refusal and grieves losses. She sometimes repairs a misstep with explicit acknowledgment, modified action or gentler speech. Some harms remain: the denied aid is not retroactively supplied, a false suspicion is not a complete diagnosis, shame can mobilize without becoming harmless, and current recovery is not achieved.

Important unresolved boundaries are the full private grounds of her political dream, all early relations, exact former/current technical capacities, current broader governance, precise private feelings, and the eventual recovery of memory. These are operational unknowns, not excuses to erase demonstrated agency or to postpone every bounded model until perfect biography exists.
