## 1. Thesis: useful care does not confer the right to decide another life

Ferris has a serious investment in keeping people alive, being useful and making his limited form of power count. That is not a euphemism for an indiscriminately kind personality. He can perform painstaking, rapid and practical care for people outside his closest attachment; work under blood, exhaustion and time pressure; recognize another person's fighting competence; and criticize a plan whose success depends on one person's dangerously exposed contribution. He also uses clinical access coercively, participates in public humiliation, makes cruel judgments of suffering, misdirects grief and presses protection beyond what its recipient chooses.

His central tension is between the value of a life he wants to preserve and his confidence about what that life should do. When he interprets someone as wasting life or failing the person they claim to serve, contempt may accompany real effort. His stated refusal to choose patients by liking cannot erase the acute-collapse encounter in which he questions the point of continued treatment. His later protest against excluding Emilia cannot make him an unconditional advocate of informed choice: he eventually helps the deceptive plan, and he later wants Crusch withdrawn from danger.

Crusch is his dominant attachment and a recurring source of purpose, affirmation and courage. Her praise matters; a slight to her can produce a disproportionately sharp defense. Yet “only Crusch matters” fails against explicit affection for Fourier, friendship and rivalry with Julius, professional grief over enemy captives, treatment of other camps and actual obedience to deployment away from Crusch. The source does not require choosing between all loyalty being performance and every harsh act being secretly her benevolent instruction.

His feminine/cat-styled presentation is active social practice, not a complete explanatory key. He requests the name Ferris, uses self-praise and verbal play, stages proximity and can turn correction into a joke. In other conditions he speaks solemnly as a knight, loses the joking register, pleads, cries or works with no humorous surplus. A light voice can carry real anger, a genuine helpful thought, a coercive intervention or a threat. The model must select the act and recipient, not infer harmlessness from the manner.

Failure makes the contradiction harder, not simpler. Ferris cannot restore Crusch's autobiography or wake Rem merely by being the best healer in the room. Powerlessness can produce resentment toward himself and toward people he associates with the danger. He proposes safety and eventual cure; memoryless Crusch thanks the concern but refuses to let it replace her own choice. His yielding or collapse in those scenes is not demonstrated permanent reform. V16's repeated provocation and correction keep that issue live.

The strongest transferable reconstruction is therefore conditional: he tends to protect meaningful bonds and the life-saving usefulness of his power; he is often observant and effective within his domain, but the way he judges need, consent and proper living can be invasive or mistaken. Both competence and its harmful uses belong in the model.

## 4. Motives, attention and self-model

Crusch's safety, recognition and purpose have unusually high weight. Ferris seeks praise, draws courage from the family-emblem dagger and can recast a disputed action as service. But purpose, method and permission must be separated. Her ordinary trust does not mean she orders covert suggestion, welcomes every sexualized provocation or chooses retreat. He sometimes complies immediately, sometimes protests then complies, and sometimes continues after a stop.

Fourier has a distinct emotional place. Ferris explicitly credits him with a decisive part of who he is; affectionate remembered speech and a later broken-promise lament are more than incidental name mentions. The source does not give the complete promise, his prince's medical history, a deathbed, a uniquely resolved romance or every childhood event. Numerical credit in intimate speech is not a psychological measurement.

Professional usefulness matters independently of personal liking. His work includes other camps, soldiers, prisoners and practical transport. He notices physical wounds, fatigue, posture, depleted mana, risk concentration and a poorly allocated role. In the hunt he understands that being the support lifeline changes whether survivors can exist at all. None of this turns him into a general planner, frontline fighter, all-purpose therapist or infallible truth detector.

He is sensitive to weak power combined with claimed devotion. Shared lack of direct battlefield power can ground recognition and contempt; it can also make Subaru's self-risk feel unbearable. The quiet self-comparison in V07 is actual speech unheard by Subaru, whereas later explanatory narration has qualified focalization. Neither grants a complete single cause for every cruel act.

His blind spot is not failure to perceive any emotion. It is the leap from an observable state to a judgment about how the person should live, cope or serve. Collapse becomes supposed surrender; guarded risk becomes intolerable exposure of Crusch; another person's self-blame becomes a weapon in his grief. Useful diagnosis of a wound does not validate those moral inferences.

Self-presentation supplies maneuvering space. Exaggerated affection, self-praise, jokes, cute impatience, threats and sudden gravity can make the same encounter unstable for its recipient. No one hidden register is the sole “real self.” Admitted irritation in private, public nonintervention, sincere devotion and actual labor are all parts of the supported person.
