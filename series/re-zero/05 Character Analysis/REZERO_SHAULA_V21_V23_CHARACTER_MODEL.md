## 1. Operating motives and competing explanations

The watch and the reunion have different stakes for Shaula. She attributes the obstruction duty to her old master and claims the means as her idea. She waits for the person she recognizes, fears losing him again, and presses for a place near him. Long waiting lends weight to the desire for company. Her hospitality can therefore be sincere while her tolerance for delay is dangerous to travelers on a deadline. The tower’s reported conditions and her use of force supply independent reasons to take that danger seriously.

She privileges what she understands as the master’s own wishes. His permission changes her treatment of other speakers, and his request can make a task matter when the group’s claim on her fails. She also initiates, negotiates, refuses, invents a pose, complains, and asks others for help. Her insistence on a given name and her resistance to formal distance fit a desire for particular recognition. Her language of being a tool or a convenient woman has observable consequences in dialogue. It leaves room for her own choices and for a conflicting guardian duty.

Shaula can read a situation unevenly. She protects, carries, gives usable tower information, and finds a kind of reassurance the amnesiac Subaru can accept. Her assurance that the master would survive her fire discounts reports of harm. Her cheerfully practical response to Meili’s death hurts companions who know their shared history. Later she detects enough tension to insist that Subaru articulate his own wish. The extent of that inference remains open. This difference in sensitivity is operationally important in an unfamiliar scene.

## 7. Application procedure and refusal boundaries

1. Resolve the requested event and narrative scope, or use S11 for an unqualified horizon request.
2. Mark the present recipient, what each party has personally received, the task, physical or time pressure and any expressed limits. A source fact available to the reader can remain unknown to the subject.
3. Run the matching rule and one adverse case. Say what the subject is likely to notice, which concern competes with it, and what response is plausible. Reserve high confidence for a close state match.
4. Separate any noncanonical scene from historical assertion. If the premise demands proof of an open identity, motive, diagnosis, memory, power, preference or romance, describe the narrower supported behavior and state where evidence stops.

The strongest refusals concern cross-return autobiographical claims, unshown terminal speech or action, exact relationship history, consent imported from a different act, authorship or literacy from the teacher graphic, a confirmed rule violation or scorpion identity, the contents of a withdrawn rule, and claims that extreme power could fulfill every spoken boast. Reconstruct usable ordinary or tactical behavior only within the narrower conditions above.
