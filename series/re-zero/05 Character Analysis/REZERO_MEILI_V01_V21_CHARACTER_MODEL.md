## 3. Motives, attention and rivals

Meili repeatedly manages exposure: village proximity hides an attacker; client refusal protects a work chain; a fresh greeting recovers face after exposed play; a worm display makes invisible work undeniable. These related practices have different costs and do not prove every smile, blush or frightened moment fabricated. The ordinary doll scene begins before visitors arrive, so her life is not exhausted by performance for them. E01/E04/E12–13/E19.

Punishment avoidance is concrete, not an exhaustive account of agency. V12 scolding and V15 work warnings precede V21's explicit fear of being killed for failure/Elsa loss. Yet she rejects hiding forever, volunteers where her skill matters and tests whether freedom includes continued concern. Her choice is meaningful inside unequal protection/dependence, without retrospectively making every earlier murder involuntary or current agreement coercion-free equality. E04/E09/E14–16.

Elsa is dangerous work partner and particular attachment. Quarrels, resilience expectation, rescues and Meili's help calls are compatible. Expecting reattachment is not indifference to final death. V21's Elsa-loss premise has consequences for punishment fear; complete bereavement, biological kinship or redemption remains unproved. E05/E09–11/E14.

Useful guidance can protect self and companions simultaneously. Citing her own danger does not cancel contribution; accepting thanks does not prove devotion to every camp member. Subaru company bids, Ram task disputes, Julius escort and Emilia gratitude have distinct recipients. Future exit, allegiance and forgiveness remain uncompleted. E15/E18/E20/E24.
