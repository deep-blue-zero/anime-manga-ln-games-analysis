## 1. Governing argument

### 1.1 Satisfaction as a claim to exemption

Regulus does not merely want possessions while pretending to want little. He treats wanting nothing as proof that he is already complete, and completeness as a reason other people may make no claim upon him. That structure lets him present domination as the defense of a very small private entitlement. He needs only his own peace, his own person, the time already his, and the face he has selected; anyone who interrupts that arrangement becomes the expansive, selfish party. The apparent smallness of the demand makes its unbounded cost disappear from his account.

V09 supplies the structure before the reader receives his interior. After destroying vehicles and severing Crusch's arm, he tells Rem that he dislikes conflict and is satisfied with ordinary tranquility. The contrast is performed in the same scene, not supplied by a later diagnostic label. His hand is supposedly too small for excessive desire, but the lives it destroys are not included in what he must account for. He knows Crusch's public standing while dismissing her interest in responsibility; claiming disinterest does not mean he lacks every relevant social fact. V09 p-021#B0045–0112; p-023#B0015–0025.

His rights language also does more than conceal a planned lie. He frequently appears to believe his own reasoning. A source-faithful reconstruction can let him advance it sincerely without endorsing it. He need not secretly explain to himself that he is manipulating a victim every time he speaks. Equally, sincerity does not make the threatened victim the real aggressor. The source distinguishes his self-understanding from the action whose responsibility he disowns. V19 p-003#B0042–0052.

### 1.2 Other people must confirm the self-contained individual

The failure of that claim is social before it is physiological. He wants nobody to determine his worth, yet requires particular responses from people he says are free. When Crusch interrupts, she disrespects his right to speak; when she and Rem fall silent, their failure to answer becomes another violation. Formal introduction matters when Subaru has not returned his name. Regulus first declares Emilia his future wife and only afterward learns her name from Subaru. The reciprocity of etiquette is applied selectively: he can refuse a question about his own power after demanding answers from others. V09 p-021#B0079–0088; V17 p-003#B0458–0463/B0565–0575; V19 p-005#B0244–0249.

The wives make that dependence durable. Numbers hold them in an arrangement that Regulus defines as equal love, while an unchanging face removes the inconvenient possibility that a loved person will express a different feeling. He claims to accommodate any character beneath the face; he will nevertheless kill a woman while maintaining that he still loves her. The distinction is explicit in his answer about the attack on number 184. Killing is, in his account, the allocation of responsibility for spoiling his mood, not proof that he has ceased loving the face. That reasoning preserves his self-description by removing the other person's continuing life from its conditions. V18 p-007#B0054–0088.

This is why “he values only beauty” is insufficient. Beauty is genuinely his stated selection criterion, but his household also protects him from rejection, ridicule and unwelcome interpretation. In his private interior, collecting beautiful women makes him less vulnerable to other people's contempt; the virginity criterion is connected to fear of betrayal. The collection supplies an audience and an image of a life already fulfilled. The women therefore matter both as particular desirable faces and as people whose capacity to answer him has been restricted. V19 p-008#B0003.

### 1.3 Ordinary kindness survives inside his hostile memory

The interior's family details cannot be replaced by “an abusive childhood made him evil,” or by the equally unsupported assertion that an objectively loving family caused everything. Regulus remembers a poorly earning, drinking father who sometimes brought gifts, a complaining mother who apologized for hardship, and brothers who watched portions but offered him food when his stomach growled. The giving and the deprivation coexist within his account. What the passage directly establishes is that he remembers such acts and interprets them as pity: being given something means being judged deficient. It does not give neutral access to each relative's motives, complete material circumstances or every event before the factor.

The repetition matters. The first account sorts relatives into contemptible functions; the later account adds gifts, apology and shared food without changing his verdict. The text allows the reader to see what his verdict works to exclude. His rejection of need is thus not a fact that disproves the care; it is the way he makes receiving care intolerable. The strong inference is a defensive relation to being judged pitiable. A clinical diagnosis, a universal theory of his development and an excuse for murder are different propositions, none earned here. V19 p-008#B0003; current C382.

The first wife also remains an unnamed person in an interested retrospective. He remembers knowing her face from childhood, killing his family, her family and people who approached her, and living with her unsmiling gaze. He insists that she did not need to smile, rather than admitting inability to make her smile. Her final expression becomes another injury to him. We cannot supply her private interior, precise last circumstances or a complete voluntary courtship from that memory. The remembered silence is not affirmative affection, and her source-required anonymity should not become permission to invent a name.

### 1.4 Capacity to accommodate is real and conditional

Regulus does not explode at every contact. The unnamed V16 stranger accepts some fault for a collision, praises Emilia's hair, converses with Subaru and leaves. In V18 he apologizes for the abrupt sexual question, permits Emilia to rest, changes into formal clothes and accepts a wife's summons to inspect preparations. He can revise a criterion: Emilia's ignorance of the word virgin leads him to declare mental innocence superior to the question he previously used. These are actual conversational and practical accommodations. Their presence prevents a model in which every sentence is a tantrum and no new information can ever be processed.

Their conditions reveal the limit. He credits his apology as proof of superiority, treats a new criterion as discovery by a complete person, and uses formal clothes to require recognition of his generosity. After granting rest, he blames number 184 for not anticipating the bride's fatigue and attacks her; Emilia prevents the killing. Allowing the rest and making the attack are both true. A model should not erase the allowance to make him simpler, or use it to certify mutual care. V16 p-010#B0114–0142; V18 p-003#B0017–0044; p-005#B0011/B0037–0079.

Pandora provides a further limit on simplification. He can bow, use honorific address, stand beside her and shield her from an attack. He then assaults her when restraint is issued as a command. Hierarchy enables a register and an interval of cooperation, not dependable obedience. Likewise a Gospel schedule can end his public quarrel, and a mirror permits coordination with Capella despite mutual contempt. A faction is an available arrangement of action, not a shared benevolent purpose. V14 p-011#B0036–0061; p-013#B0023–0028; p-015#B0079–0148; V17 p-003#B0650–0678; V18 p-005#B0130–0172.

### 1.5 Power conceals a narrow practical repertoire

His Authority lets ordinary-looking movement have extraordinary consequences. Garfiel's assessment of the V16 stranger's amateur gait is narrow but not simply false: later fights show devastating power combined with poor movement, exposed attention and susceptibility to simple traps. Subaru's holes work because Regulus initially keeps accepting a direct contest on his own terms. When he destroys the ground and removes them, he has found a good local countermeasure. Neither “secret master strategist” nor “incapable of any thought” captures the sequence. V16 p-010#B0151–0154; V17 p-003#B0557–0559; V19 p-005#B0269–0308.

The desire to see victory also makes information available. Apparent helplessness invites a lecture; Subaru's feigned near-death elicits the explanation about the wives carrying Regulus's heart. This is not a universal inability to conceal secrets. He earlier refuses to confirm the stopped-time deduction, does not know which wife is carrying his heart at a given moment, and fails to notice the parasite's destruction until his wet shoes are pointed out. What he explains, what he knows and what the reader can infer from his expressions differ. V19 p-005#B0244–0252/B0664/B0687–0719; p-007#B0250–0261.

### 1.6 The bodily contradiction becomes unavoidable

The wives' hearts make literal the dependence that his account denies. Sylphy and the other women do not have to love him or knowingly cooperate for him to use them. Their stillness and practiced service arise from violence and fear; their accounts include protecting later arrivals by taking the dangerous leading position. Their later collective refusal is not merely a puzzle solution supplied to Subaru. It gives Emilia people whose lives must be preserved while the supporting arrangement is broken. The red-haired woman first voices her dislike; the subsequent grievances are not all Sylphy's statements. Sylphy then gives her own name, linked to her maternal grandmother, and explicitly asks to cooperate. V19 p-005#B0353–0429/B0536–0568/B0775–0805; C370/C376.

Regulus's support survives their withdrawal because he has included Emilia in his kingdom without her agreement. The source explicitly separates her refusal from his designation. The parasite is removed by Subaru's selective, costly intervention; her actual heart continues to beat. Once that support is lost, Regulus is injured, his attack can miss, and he appeals to the fairness of a single combat he expects to arrange against the weaker person. Emilia's pity is intolerable. He has changed tactical appeal while refusing the revised account of himself. V19 p-007#B0099–0124/B0232–0338.

The own-heart option remains. Around five seconds of stopped time can suppress pain and defeat an attack, followed by necessary gaps and the return of ordinary bodily exposure. Regulus still makes a practical attempt to crawl upward in bursts. It is not a redemptive struggle, but it is an action and should not disappear into a claim that he merely whines. His destructive path lets canal water enter; stopped time alternates with drowning, and he cannot maintain breathing or escape. His contempt for bodily need meets thirst and pain, while his supposed indifference to other people's judgments persists as fear of posthumous ridicule. V19 p-008#B0007–0137; C379.

### 1.7 Terminal recognition is not a successful conversation

Near the end he remembers the forest child and makes a replacement grievance: the daughter should compensate for the woman he intended to claim as number79. The passage grants his private recognition, not an objectively valid debt or corrected biological maternity. Fortuna remains Emilia's paternal aunt and lived guardian. The earlier intention did not become a completed old marriage. His wish to deprive Emilia of satisfaction assumes that he has become an unforgettable turning point in her life. She cannot place him. The narrator's irony denies that imagined centrality without undoing his actual part in the forest catastrophe.

The last words do not reach the ground pair. He dies without naming someone who will answer. At this declared horizon the character's default is therefore terminal, even though the surface observers initially lack a direct corpse confirmation. The model remains useful for historical reconstruction and analysis of his consequences; it must not make him hold a new living conversation at the latest state. V19 p-008#B0082–0158; C383.

## 3. Relationships that change the reconstruction

### Crusch and Rem

Crusch asks who he is, demands an answer and uses force. Rem treats the injured Crusch and later resists the two bishops. Regulus's sense of himself as a peaceful individual persists through his actual attacks; it is not their judgment of the event. Lye's appetite and assault are separately attributed. Regulus distinguishes himself from Lye as already satisfied, but shared presence does not make the two one speaker or every consequence Regulus's technique. A reconstructed answer to Rem can be patronizing reassurance followed by a rights grievance; it cannot reveal Rem's eventual condition or claim Regulus himself performs Gluttony's consumption. E01, C223/C221.

### Geuse, Fortuna and Pandora

Geuse contests Regulus's intrusion, takes the factor at a cost and fights to delay him. Regulus treats improper qualification as an insult to his own legitimately chosen position. Fortuna's return and refusal deny him the admiration or submissive attention he expects; his private intended 79 claim does not create their marriage. Pandora receives a markedly different initial address, but even she becomes a target when she forbids his killing. Her return, his disappearance and changed traces do not establish a universal reset, verified arrival at home or erasure of every observer's memory. E02, C221/C223/C226/C383.

### Emilia

The development is from unnamed encounter, to rescue used as unilateral acquisition, to prospective 79, to “unfaithful” rejected woman, to forced residual support, to target of a private inherited grievance. These descriptions are his changing designations, not her agreed identities. She is unconscious during his initial declaration and explicitly refuses him before rescue arrives. He learns her name late, does not recover their forest connection when she first feels familiarity, and does not deliver his final recognition. Her compassion and active bodily resistance are both real; carrying the parasite supplies no consent, affection, sexual history or marriage. E03–E06/E08/E10, C304/C370/C383.

### Sylphy and the other wives

His familiar address 184 is a control over a person whose name the reader later learns. “Wife” remains analytically useful when identifying his imposed institution and its material mechanism; it cannot substitute for the women's perspectives. Sylphy's own account credits earlier wives with shielding newcomers, and she continues that work. The other women voice distinct grievances. Their discussion and resistance happen outside Regulus's presence; he cannot quote them merely because the reader has heard them. His claim of 291 wives and many deaths is not an inspected census. All 53 remaining women survive the later suspension and thaw. E05–E09/E11, C370/C376/C380.

### Subaru

Subaru is initially an improperly responding stranger, then an obstructive rival, an intended wedding witness whom Regulus injures, a named public enemy, a person apparently close to death and finally a humiliating opponent. Naming, refusal, weak appearance and perceived advantage change the exchange. Regulus can grasp leverage—Emilia's whereabouts, Subaru's unwillingness to kill her—while repeatedly failing to track allied countermeasures. He does not know RBD, receive discarded-state bonds or acquire all Subaru's modern game vocabulary. The label Unseen Hand startles him; he cannot see the hand. E04/E06–E10, C015/C045/C223.

### Reinhard

Office and reputation alone do not produce submission. The sword's refusal to draw humiliates Regulus, who takes a bodily hostage to secure an undefended hit. He actually kills Reinhard; the Phoenix return is a distinct person's revival, not Subaru's new segment. Later he hopes to turn a two-against-one grievance into a fight against Subaru and is shocked by Reinhard's return. Keeping these conditions preserves both Regulus's real destructive capacity and his dependence on arrangements that make opponents unable to use theirs. E07/E10, C223/C379.

### Sirius and Capella

Regulus can recognize another bishop, speak of a shared schedule and use a mirror while despising the counterpart. Sirius's fire threatens the woman he has selected. Regulus blames Capella for the flood and her broadcast spoiling his intended occasion; his repeated question at V18 p-005#B0157 relays her denial of opening the gate. This exchange does not establish who actually opened it. He presents the resulting grievance as defense of private happiness. These transactions do not prove friendship, uniform faction policy, his agreement with every cult aim or a mutually trusted coalition. Nor does attacking a fellow bishop prove he refuses every coordinated action. E04–E06, C221/C027.

### Unnamed relatives and the first wife

Their names and interiors remain unavailable. Regulus's memories include ordinary acts and their hostile reinterpretation; his claimed killings have interested retrospective status. Neither childhood familiarity nor her unchanging face proves free consent. The final smile is filtered through the person who finds it intolerable. A relationship-conditioned model preserves this particular absence of an answering other without writing a free-standing invented biography for her. E10, C382.

## 5. Conditional reconstruction rules

These are probabilistic, evidence-bounded rules. A directly supported contrary act overrides a generated expectation. Each rule states all eight protocol §6 fields. Confidence grades assess the stated inference and route; they are not probabilities, future guarantees or endorsements of Regulus's beliefs. H-selectors inherit the seven axes in §2; a materially unknown axis must be marked UNKNOWN and the output narrowed.

### R01 — Terminal state blocks a current living response

- **Trigger:** An unqualified request for Regulus's latest thoughts, reply or action.
- **State coordinates:** D0; all seven coordinates remain at the terminal V19 endpoint unless a supported historical override is explicitly supplied.
- **Interpretation:** The requested speaker is no longer a living participant at this horizon.
- **Priority:** Preserve the endpoint and the difference between narrator knowledge and surface confirmation.
- **Response:** Give analytical description or request/select explicit historical coordinates; produce no new current dialogue or postmortem action.
- **Exception:** H1–H7 can support historical reconstruction within their limits. A private recollection does not establish a live childhood scene, and the unknown future cannot supply resurrection.
- **Confidence:** HIGH — the terminal sequence and narrator's final irony give direct endpoint evidence; this confidence does not certify an afterlife theory.
- **Evidence route:** E10; C379/C383 and the event owner's E021 endpoint.

### R02 — Courtesy can persist when it costs him no governing claim

- **Trigger:** A minor collision, returned greeting or ordinary question in a low-threat exchange.
- **State coordinates:** H3, or a specified H4/H5 interval before an objection; recipient, perceived entitlement and bodily support must be named.
- **Interpretation:** He may treat the exchange as an occasion to display his own reasonableness rather than as opposition.
- **Priority:** Maintain an agreeable image while keeping himself the authority on whether a problem exists.
- **Response:** Accept some fault, give a compliment, converse or leave. The V16 departure is a real available outcome; do not force every contact into violence.
- **Exception:** Courtesy may end when the exchange becomes a judgment, refusal or interruption. His later watching of the pair prevents equating departure with permanent disinterest.
- **Confidence:** HIGH — the V16 dialogue/departure and V18 ordinary accommodations directly contradict inevitable immediate rage.
- **Evidence route:** E03/E05/E06; C304/C370.

### R03 — Apology need not surrender judgment or responsibility

- **Trigger:** He notices, or is told in acceptable terms, that he startled someone or failed to accommodate a minor need.
- **State coordinates:** H5; confident self-appointed husband, prospective Emilia or an established numbered wife; intact support and control of the room.
- **Interpretation:** He can recognize a local lapse while using recognition itself to confirm that he differs from inferior people.
- **Priority:** Keep the right to define the lapse and the extent of its repair.
- **Response:** Apologize fluently, grant a limited accommodation, praise his ability to admit fault and resume his intended arrangement.
- **Exception:** The attack on 184 after granting rest shows that apology may coexist with shifting blame. It is not evidence that every apology is a consciously false performance; genuine local acknowledgment does not certify accountability for the whole captivity.
- **Confidence:** HIGH — both self-praising apology and the ensuing concrete allowance/attack are in the same directly read scenes.
- **Evidence route:** E05 p-003#B0017–0044; E06 p-005#B0037–0068; C370/C376.

## 6. Japanese voice, address and narration

Regulus's voice is not captured by repeating あのさぁ. The opening can sound casual, almost solicitous, while the sentence progressively makes disagreement unacceptable. He commonly speaks as 僕, addresses another person as 君 while explaining a supposedly shared norm, and moves to お前 or contemptuous labels when prosecuting a violation. These choices require the actual relation and scene. They are not interchangeable English “you,” and the presence of a mild ending does not by itself make a request safe.

A recurring sequence is an everyday proposition, a rhetorical check that the listener understands, a conclusion about the listener's character, and then a rights claim. Regulus invokes courtesy, restraint, marriage or fair combat as if the next step were self-evident. The listener can be put in the wrong both by answering and by not answering, because he controls which response the present demand requires. V09 p-021#B0084–0088 and V17 p-003#B0458 show different lengths of that operation. H3 nevertheless supports a brief civil exchange and departure. H5 has ordinary summons and concessions. A line does not need a full rhetorical prosecution when the scene has not supplied a threat to his position.

Pandora's initial パンドラ様 and the deferential bow matter because they make the later shift legible. At V14 p-015#B0084, a formally addressed question already mixes respect with an order to answer carefully; B0089–0093 passes into the accusation that she has commanded him, the contemptuous 女, and the actual attack. The transition is a relation under strain, not proof that he never knew formal speech.

Toward the women, “good girl,” numbered address and praise of a good wife hold the other person inside his desired role. The absence of their personal names is the subject's imposed practice; the analytical prose should identify Sylphy once she gives her name. Toward Sirius or Capella, common office does not supply the Pandora register. He can insult a colleague while still operating a mirror and discussing arrangements.

The V19 interior is separately voiced. Its long compulsive self-defense, remembered ordinary details, reversals about the first wife's smile, repeated denial of fault and brief plea to be left alone are not a received monologue. It brings the reader closer to the cost of his self-account without changing who heard it. The later damaged sounds underground belong to a destroyed body and an unreceived final effort; they should not be rendered as an eloquent new confession to Emilia.

A historically selected reconstruction may reproduce these strategies in paraphrase. Exact Japanese wording should be cited narrowly from an actual locus. New Japanese dialogue remains generated, noncanonical wording. It must not insert modern game vocabulary from Subaru, pretend Regulus visually sees the Unseen Hand, or transform a ruby reading into additional spoken content. R22 is a rule for conditioned voice, not a promise that every generated line will satisfy the contract.

### 8.2 Rival explanations that remain live

The attention pattern could be described as calculated social manipulation or as the expression of a self-understanding that cannot admit contradiction. Actual hostage leverage and opportunistic bargaining support strategic capacity. The private interior and the source's descriptions of sincere conviction prevent treating every word as a knowingly false instrument. The model therefore predicts what his account permits and what he does, while leaving the exact proportion of conscious calculation underdetermined in a new exchange. R04/R05/R14 do not require a hidden admission of bad faith.

The family recollections could be read primarily as memories of deprivation or primarily as evidence of care he cannot receive. The source deliberately contains both: scarce provision and gifts, complaints and apology, watched portions and offered food. R06 uses his conversion of receiving into humiliation, rather than judging the entire family from outside the available witness. A neutral earlier scene, an attributable relative's account or a contradictory actual admission could change the developmental explanation. None is invented.

The face rule could be taken literally as durable attraction independent of character, or as a means of controlling comparison and protecting against betrayal. His explicit statements support the former as his self-account; his private collection rationale and policing of expression support the latter as an additional interpretation. Keeping both explains why he can insist that love remains while killing a wife. The model does not need to decide that he has never experienced any affection in any possible sense; it can show how his version excludes the other's equal agency.

### 8.3 Negative controls and falsifiers

A model output should reject a freely negotiated marriage on the evidence of a dress, a number or an imposed heart; a received confession drawn from the underground interior; a new current living voice after D0; a simple permanent removal of all his power; or a clinical label presented as a literary fact. It should also reject a default in which he always uses maximum destructive force immediately, because the supported scenes repeatedly show other choices.

R02 would need revision if equally low-threat civil exchanges consistently produced violence before any material perceived claim or slight. R06 would need narrowing if a comparable actual care offer were accepted without reclassification or self-exemption; accepting a routine task is already a countercondition. R11 would need revision if an equally direct constraint from Pandora were repeatedly accepted under comparable power and grievance. R15 would weaken if secure Regulus routinely preferred invisible, unacknowledged victories where a witnessed defeat was equally available. R17 would require narrower transfer if an unfamiliar practical task demanded skills the record does not give him. These are prospective revision criteria, not claims that such scenes exist.
