# Louis Arneb through V25

## 1. The face she knows by touch

Louis comes to herself on a white floor. She puts her palms against her face. There is no mirror in this place and very little to handle besides her own face and body. The touch is familiar enough for her to know whose face she has. Only after she has spent a little time doing this does she look at the boy in front of her. A reader who remembers her first interest in him might expect her to ask how it felt. This Louis already knows. She screams. A few pages later someone with exactly the same face wants the full report. How did it look, sound, smell, feel, taste? This other Louis has been waiting. Their histories part at a single decision. One of them went. V25 text/part0011.html B0182–0207, B0352–0387.

There is an earlier, quieter way to see what a life means to Louis. Her brothers supply other people's completed experiences. In the white room she grades them. Parents, friends, partners, money, the conditions in which a child grows up all enter her reckoning. Even the highest-scoring life leaves her dissatisfied on some particular. She can see what someone should have done. There is no way to reach back into the record and do it. The narrator compares her position to looking over a player's shoulder and criticising the play. The frustration has an object well before Subaru arrives. She wants to exercise a choice in the first person, keep the good result and undo a bad one. Somebody's likable family cannot confer that power. V25 part0011 B0003–0074, B0115–0148.

Her wish to be happy is sincere. What she thinks it entitles her to do to other people is also sincere. A successful meal deprives someone else of a name or a lived history. She can recognise why a young man regrets leaving his parents without a farewell, and use the accuracy of that recognition to make him doubt who he is. When her experiment finally gives her a history that is hers alone, the experience refuses to behave like a meal. It cannot be put back on a shelf. What was enviable in someone else's past becomes unbearable in her own. The other Louis, still measuring somebody else's life, gets even the meaning of her sister-self's refusal wrong. V24 part0011 B0028–0057, B0167–0217, B0517–0580. V25 part0011 B0203–0237, B0390–0453.

The last physical girl offers a different starting point. She is named, touches someone, makes sounds and rolls on grass. No passage on the plain opens her thoughts. A request to write what she knows, privately wants, regrets, calls Subaru or plans for Rem at that moment returns **SOURCE_SILENT**. A request about her earlier boast, the participating self's fear, or the waiting self's anger can be answered by selecting that earlier person and occasion. V25 part0018 B0315–0342, B0351–0368, B0408–0446.

## 3. Who gave the girl a name in the square

Otto has good reason to think that “Gluttony” has more than one bearer before he has good reason to count three. He met the earlier bearer himself a few hours ago and now confronts Lye, who has given a different name. Across the city, Roy names himself to Julius and Ricardo. Roy's recital of appetites can sound like a cast list once a reader knows the family. His final 兄様 is addressed to Julius. A reader who assigns that line to a younger sister because of the kinship word has lost both the Archbishop’s capacity to use the language of victims and the force of the insult to Julius. Roy can put a particular young man's brother into his mouth. That does not put the young man's brother back into the world as Julius knew him. V19 item/xhtml/p-002.xhtml B0013–0022, B0342–0354, B0382–0397. For Julius's and Joshua's distinct losses, V20 p-010 B0197–0217, B0304–0329 and V21 text/part0009.html B0048–0060. Joshua's particular assailant remains unidentified by that bedside evidence.

Lye thinks he can pick out someone his sister would like and can predict her scolding if he returns with nothing better than snacks. This tells us what he expects of her. He is the one who imitates Rem's manner of introducing herself, tries the name Felt, and suffers the consequences when it fails. Later a large man attacks Dynas and Otto, licks Otto’s injury and calls attention to what is being done in front of the group. Before Otto has asked a question, the speaker applauds the clever ruse she imagines he and Felt have played with a false name. When Otto asks if the newcomer is Gluttony, she exclaims at his ability to speak despite his injury. He points out the vanished Lye and the clothes the new figure is wearing. After hearing him, she tells him she can see the value of a place at table with him. Beatrice will draw a further conclusion about the occupant only after the form changes and the girl introduces herself. Each step increases Louis’s interest in the wounded man. None relieves his need for treatment. V20 item/xhtml/p-003.xhtml B0081–0091, B0253–0267, B0348–0359, B0533–0550, B0626–0668.

Felt gets a different answer in the same encounter. Louis takes using a false name to be evidence of tactical cleverness. She revises what she thinks Felt herself knows once the girl insists that Rom gave her the name and that it has been hers for fifteen years. Louis persists in the claim of an earlier name, without saying what it was. The attack on the premise that a person knows her own name is pointed precisely because the history Felt adduces is true to her life. Louis has a large stock of stolen memories to consult. It fails her on this very small, particular question. The audience sees the large form give way to a girl with extraordinarily long fair hair. She gives her office and the name Louis Arneb. Beatrice infers that the occupant has changed as well as the appearance. The attribution rests on the sequence of actions, testimony, visible transition and self-introduction. A voice sample on its own would have put this at the door of an unidentified adult man. V20 p-003 B0632–0688 and original item/image/p094.jpg.

A finger against the tip of the raised metia suffices to make the next decision social. Louis says she has seen the object and that it cannot fire again. She claims the choice to leave, turns her back on people who have good reason to pursue, and lets the wounded make pursuit expensive. Which companion one eats with matters to her, she says. Take that detail literally as a preference stated here. There is no meal among friends in the square, no invitation Felt accepts, and no promise to behave the same way if the second shot is demonstrably available. Even the flattering form of her farewell tells the party that she is grading their decision. Earlier she was the one who mistook the nature of Felt's knowledge. She can exploit a situation with incomplete information. V20 p-003 B0683–0708.

## 4. Borrowing the feeling, keeping the opportunity

Before Subaru, the people Louis knows most intimately as providers are the brothers on whom her access depends. Lye is selective about flavour. Roy's broad appetite includes the experience of eating in quantity. They make their sister presents of experience. She can recognise the labour and the family idiom of the gift and resent how thoroughly its selection remains with them. She calls Lye お兄ちゃん and Roy 兄様 in this family account. At Priestella an injured stranger gets お兄さん. So does Subaru. V24 part0011 B0605–0616 brings the distinction into one exchange. She says she asked the brothers, says their eating determines what she can choose, and informs Subaru of their interest and knowledge of his whereabouts. Subaru works out whom she means and fears that they have come to the tower. Later he predicts what Lye will do, says he felt no family love in her conduct, and speculates about why the brothers persist. Those are his expectations and moral judgment. The dependence does not establish domestic affection, a shared command channel operating at all times, unconditional obedience or the fulfilment of every request. V25 part0011 B0007–0049, part0012 B0073–0083, B0747–0754.

The first arrival of the Subaru who knows his friends gives her an unexpected feeling of kindness toward him. Someone her brother has eaten had good reason to love this boy. Louis lets that person's remembered tenderness direct her address, then takes advantage of the access it affords. When the same tenderness becomes irksome after division, she throws away the borrowed memory carrying it. She can handle the source of this feeling like a possession. The distinction matters because the Louis who returns with a history of dying would give a great deal to be able to do the same thing with her own past. V25 part0011 B0075–0091, B0159–0179, B0415–0425. The later description of eaten memory resisting the waiting half gives the reader a reason to take the persistence of an attachment seriously. It does not give every appearance of tenderness the same origin or guarantee that Louis can control every recalled voice. V25 part0011 B0363–0375.

It is possible to feel something and deliberately use it at the same moment. Louis finds Subaru interesting, acquires terms from his life, delights in the oddity of the experience and makes a plan to take the life for herself. Subaru as a boy she could be kind to, as a puzzle and as a potential source of an infinitely revisable future occur in quick succession. If a recipient hears a gentle intimate voice, that may be exactly what the borrowed feeling sounds like. When the waiting self later offers to deceive people who love him, the desired object is stated with painful clarity. Her sincerity makes that offer worse for him. No scene requires an exchange of romantic attachment. Subaru's refusal and his account of whom he cares for deserve the same evidentiary weight as her petition. V24 part0011 B0558–0595. V25 part0011 B0075–0116, B0133–0179, B0247–0263.

Louis discovers an impossibility in the record. In RZ-V25-C688, K1003 and M639 the first-person side of the comparison needs an explicit owner. The speaking girl has no personal memory of having killed Subaru. In the history she takes from him there *is* an experience of Subaru being killed by “us.” For Louis, a person remembering his own deaths was already strange. To find a death at “our” hands that she herself never experienced is stranger still. She recognises that these are things Subaru actually lived. Subaru's record contains his deaths. A failure to remember them on his part would destroy the puzzle that prompts her thinking. The plural does not identify which brother or individual hand did the killing. She tries out vocabulary from his world. Words he has used or thought in the year she can reach can tell her quite a lot about him, without opening his whole pre-summoning childhood. On separation she specifies the short reach and leaves its cause unknown. V25 part0011 B0088–0114, B0352–0358.

She gives the unusual access a theory. Might he hold Pride? What has he already done with other Factors? If she eats the appropriate person, will the Authority come with him? Even in her excitement she cannot supply the mechanism for simply taking what she wants. She changes the project. She will become the bearer, then consume him as a different person. Her soul is bound up with the Factor in an exceptional bodiless place. She actually divides it. Two voices, of the same sound and intonation, speak at once. The narrative qualifies the comparison with her brothers’ capacities. The projected benefits of a future freely selected by Louis remain a project. As an account of how she decides, this is more revealing than treating her boast of being able to do anything as a rule of the world. When a particular route to a benefit fails, she can abandon her theory of the route and stay loyal to the benefit she intends to take. V25 part0011 B0108–0180.

### Which skill belongs to whom

暴食 is their shared title. The brothers’ 美食 and 悪食 and Louis's 飽食 distinguish appetites without making the three people interchangeable. 日食 and 月食 also need their own attribution. The inline graph 蝕 in the V25 original is an image at part0011 B0063, B0065 and B0069. The prose elsewhere names the solar and lunar uses, which can be rendered solar and lunar eclipse. In the solar application an eaten form can be expressed and can influence the user's disposition. The lunar use brings eaten capabilities to a bearer acting in the bearer's own form. V25 qualifies typical use by these three people. An initially bodiless Louis tends toward the former, the brothers toward the latter. Earlier Roy demonstrates a borrowed technique. Later Lye considers how he can combine techniques and judges Roy's and Louis's aptitude against his own. His judgment, the weight of another personality and the problems of communicating all count against a perfect common inventory with instant mastery. These statements do not establish any present power of the girl on the grass. V20 item/xhtml/p-002.xhtml B0236–0265. V25 part0011 B0055–0074, part0012 B0747–0754, part0015 B0122–0130.

## 5. A private conversation conducted with someone else's memories

There are two distinct meetings before the separation. In V22 the Subaru who knows his friends thinks of a way to pursue the question of Reid, then hurries away. His footsteps are all the reader hears. The people who find him later have to deal with the loss. In V25 he explains that he went to the library to look for Reid’s Book and found it empty. V22 leaves the internal encounter out of view. The later confrontation in V24 begins with a Subaru who wants that history back. His way in is Reid's Book. In V25 the retrospective supplies the earlier theft and lets an attentive reader identify the girl who argues in V24 as the half who waited. Her counterpart knows something of that encounter from being inside Subaru. They did not each live it from the host’s position in the Corridor. V22 part0015 B0253–0309. V24 part0010 B0889–0956. V25 part0010 B0394–0399, B0443–0456, part0011 B0075–0091, B0363–0375.

Louis tells Subaru how he feels about his parents. She gets enough of it right to hurt him. He left without saying goodbye, is troubled by a farewell reached on his own terms, and still feels a duty he cannot discharge. She can put her finger on his guilt so accurately that he cannot dismiss her as someone who knows nothing. She embraces him from behind as she lays claim to this understanding. He tells her not to touch him, shakes off her arm and puts some distance between them. The gesture is an explicit physical refusal. His reaction to her words also allows the reader to see why this access feels like an intrusion. Knowing something true about the son gives her no qualification to speak for his parents. She cannot report how those two people on Earth are doing now. The moment supplies a limit on reproducing this conversation with another person. An available salient memory is an advantage she can use. Access to a stranger's current private condition requires separate evidence. V24 part0011 B0022–0069. V25 part0011 B0352–0358.

Her account of Od Lagna uses the homely image of washing a rag so it can serve again. She says what she takes the function of this place to be, equates two institutional descriptions for the purpose at hand, and explains why she thinks interference is unlikely. Subaru hears an account by a resident of the white room. He cannot see the whole system demonstrably working in front of him. She asks which occurrence he is living through. He says this is the fifth time. This question conveys some knowledge of recurrent death and a need for him to supply information. It establishes no universally observable counter, transmissible lesson about the taboo, or fifth freshly added death. V24 part0011 B0079–0136. In later company Subaru can report a voice or use “Gluttony” to conceal how he knows an event from a failed history. His explanatory choice does not retrospectively put a disclosure into Louis's mouth. V24 part0011 B0686–0706, part0013 B0052–0068.

There is a trap for anyone trying to complete Louis's charming sentences. She argues from appetite to love, then breaks off mid-utterance. The ensuing 愛してる comes from Subaru’s memory of words heard on the preceding loop. He anticipates what Louis might have said. She does not finish with the words his memory supplies. V24 part0011 B0132–0145.

She also knows that Subaru is unlikely to kill her with his bare hands. He puts his hand at her throat. His resolve slackens. A small push is enough to remove his hand. Later Louis herself takes his right arm and guides his hand back to her own neck while urging him to choose. The plate beside this second contact shows her smiling near his wrist, with part of his alarmed face visible. It depicts how she makes use of a choice he has already found difficult. Her account of needing a stored form for effective fighting is more limited than a general claim that she has no force here. This encounter is a victory in judging this particular adversary and a failure in something she very much wants from him. V24 part0011 B0132–0167, B0225–0238 and images/00010.jpeg.

When she likens restoring memories to the death of the Subaru in front of her, she accurately identifies the cost he most fears. She calls herself the creator of his present self and invokes the obedience owed a parent. She has never witnessed the proposed restoration in a comparable person. Asked for proof, she cannot give him any. The argument has an interest behind it. If he becomes a separate person in his own estimation, she may be able to eat him again. What she needs is his decision. Her switch into a cold, serious register on the proposition that memory makes the person is part of its force. A listener would be mistaken to discount every insight because she wants something. He would be equally mistaken to grant the forecast as a law because one insight is sound. V24 part0011 B0167–0217, B0256–0278, B0322–0339, B0493–0517.

She can press him exactly where devotion and self-neglect meet. By this time he really does need to include himself among the people worth saving. She can take that truth and use it to try to sever him from his former self. She invokes Emilia, Beatrice and Rem and supplies versions of the ugly motives she says are his. Those staged speeches show which charge Louis expects to hurt him. They do not give the analyst privileged access to what all four people actually think. The position from which he sees her can resemble the last sight in Meili's Book. Resemblance to a victim's perspective is a source for his response here, without naming the agent behind the violence in that other episode. V24 part0011 B0208–0217, B0220–0278, B0298–0339. V23 part0013 B0155–0208.

At the decisive instant the listener starts speaking to somebody she cannot see. She asks whom he is addressing. Rem's figure and characteristic address are available to him and to the reader. The figure asks something of her hero that includes conserving his own life and helping the one nearest him. Louis calls it their place and says she had expected interference there to be impossible. Her failure to perceive this exchange matters more than the theatrical ease with which she made the identification. She tries to resume her interrupted argument. When the desired division fails she loses the coaxing manner and demands to know what spoiled it. Eating the same person twice is the obstacle she gives. The later comment on an eaten memory resisting her provides an explanation at the level of the intrusion. It does not turn this into the waking Rem's choice or give Louis transcripts of Subaru's inward conversation. V24 part0011 B0369–0394, B0418–0465, B0467–0517. V25 part0011 B0363–0375.

Finally she drops much of the pretext. She tells Subaru that his life would suit her, promises to fool the several people to whom he matters, and extends her hands. The original illustration shows that open gesture, alone. He refuses an actual petition for his life. He has no obligation to grant it because he recognises the hunger that produced it. As he looks for a way out, she names the brothers she says she asked for help. In this E028 exit his wish to leave is effective. The later participant can eject him in a distinct encounter under a different pressure. Confusing these exits credits both halves with one continuous conversation and erases the other person's choice in each. V24 part0011 B0517–0557, B0561–0595, B0604–0630 and images/00012.jpeg. V25 part0011 B0311–0345.

## 13. Rival accounts and the end of the usable evidence

The starving child reading accounts for an abundance of records that cannot make one life hers. It also accounts for the peculiar pleasure she takes in finding something new and for her long boredom in a place with no mirror.
