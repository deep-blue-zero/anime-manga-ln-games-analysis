## 1. Reconstruction thesis: a chosen reason can change the use of a feared power

Theresia’s life changes as she finds people and purposes for whom she can act with a power she initially experiences as a threat. She fears injuring others before the Sword Saint inheritance. The inheritance then gives family and kingdom a role through which to demand action from her. She can defeat a loved brother without wanting the victory, receive his comfort without becoming able to fight, and later see how poorly her judgment of his continued practice understood his values. Fear, shame and a real failure coexist with the coercive circumstances and the kingdom’s protection of its public symbol.

Her flower meetings make another kind of relationship possible. At nineteen she has little energy for the household flowerbed, yet returns to a private field she seeded without watering. She is drawn to Wilhelm’s intensity, irritated by his manners, eager for more conversation and afraid of being left behind when he changes. Her outward ease covers near-tears and attraction. When he becomes distressed, she initially lacks an answer, then approaches and uses their familiar question. Her capacity for care develops through a particular relation; she is neither born socially omniscient nor incapable of helping.

Wilhelm’s metaphorical resemblance to a sword matters because he resists the complete control she associates with understanding actual blades. That interest supplies no literal ability to read human minds. The questions about flowers and sword practice carry curiosity, wanted contact, fear and hope before they lead to a changed answer. Afterward, the pair talks more freely. His development helps her while remaining his own development.

The rescue makes her powerful without making the decision costless. She chooses to wield the sword, endures the battlefield’s horror and kills. Her wish to save Wilhelm includes private possessiveness. After his hostile response, she ends the flower visits and gives her strength a wider protective purpose. Those motives do not collapse into a wholly selfless national duty or a hidden desire for domination. Her continued longing for him and her chosen public combat remain together.

The ceremonial contest also preserves both people’s choices. Theresia wants to drop the sword and embrace Wilhelm, yet the encounter ends in his genuinely trained victory. She asks what protection will mean for her, steps forward and returns love. The defeat releases a way of living; it does not remove the blessing. Married life includes accepted protection, humor, irritation, wanted words and an ability to control the Death God blessing that the frightened child lacked. She knows Wilhelm loves her through his conduct while continuing to want a spoken answer.

Her later protective decision returns her to the role she had left. She accepts the expedition, rebukes Wilhelm’s blaming of their son and deliberately wounds Wilhelm to prevent pursuit. Her plan protects people she loves by constraining the man who wants to accompany her. The promised return and closure do not occur as planned. Loss of the Sword Saint blessing leaves real sword skill but does not secure survival against the unidentified visitor.

The final response supplies a real gain within a strict limit. Theresia recognizes an aged husband, waits for his difficult words, wipes his tears and reciprocates. She still regards her family with love and hopes they will manage the problems left behind. Her first-sight question remains unasked. Her body then disappears into ashes, before the new accusations and rejection. The model preserves the farewell’s reality, the life that made it meaningful and the recovery it cannot perform.

### 1.1 Stable candidates and state-dependent revisions

The strongest cross-phase candidate is particular attachment joined to an effort to decide what protection can mean. Flowers and an available private space repeatedly matter; so do the sword, family burdens and Wilhelm’s words. Their meaning changes. The sword begins as danger and imposed identity, becomes a chosen means of rescue and public protection, recedes during marriage, then becomes a requested resource under renewed family pressure.

Teasing and direct questions recur with Wilhelm, while childhood distress, guarded courtship, a firm departure decision and the last breathless exchange have different pressures. Concern can lead her to receive comfort, approach another person, accept protection, withdraw from painful contact, use overwhelming force or restrain somebody she loves. A rule has to select the relevant relation and available body before predicting which response is plausible.

Her own retrospective contains judgments that later change: that practice below her gifted level is futile, that everybody is moving forward without her, that only she deserves blame, that being strongest can let her protect everything. These are consequential self/world models, not the analyst’s final verdict. Her genuine final sense of having been loved does not erase the earlier costs or verify every hope for the surviving family.

## 4. Motives, attention, knowledge and remembered history

### 4.1 What she wants and what she sees

Theresia’s early wish is to avoid causing the harm her birth-given blessing makes frighteningly easy. Avoiding practice is one way she acts on that wish. The inherited Sword Saint role then makes her gifted assessment unavoidable even in a test she resists. She can see the brother’s openings while failing to understand why he still loves practice. Mechanical superiority and understanding a person’s values have different results.

The first deployment brings a damaging asymmetry. Other people see a reassuring military symbol. She experiences fear and feels that weakness is forbidden. The eldest’s actual welcome lets her disclose what she had withheld; she revises her judgment of him. His death does not erase the comfort or prove that the child’s receipt of help was a selfish deception. The kingdom’s subsequent concealment of the failed deployment protects its own symbol as well as her public position.

At nineteen, Theresia notices both sharpness and vulnerability. A ruined district offers recognition for her own unused role; flowers grow without her care; Wilhelm’s dedication can look both deficient and compelling. She masks distress, wants to deflate his intimidating confidence and feels his absence or short conversation as a frustration. Her wish to understand him develops alongside the wish to be wanted by him.

She initially fears the answer changing because it might leave her behind. When it does change, she finds joy in his development. This is a real correction to her expectation. It does not establish a future rule that she always welcomes every change by every loved person.

### 4.2 Protection, possessiveness and responsibility

Choosing to rescue Wilhelm gives Theresia an urgent reason to act that the public title had not supplied. She knows the danger and carries fear into the battlefield. The inward claim that he is hers is part of that moment. It should remain beside the later broader decision to protect family, others and kingdom, rather than being erased to make her motivation perfectly impersonal.

Her later married decision repeats the effort to change a situation through a protection she can supply. She sees Heinkel’s frustration, Luanna’s unresponsive condition and Reinhard’s burden, then judges herself the one who failed them. Accepting the expedition gives her a concrete action. It also makes her willing to restrict Wilhelm’s pursuit by a wound whose continuing effect she understands.

The promised return matters to interpretation. She does not leave intending permanent abandonment, nor does the shallow wound mean no injury. She intends to close it when their work is done. The plan’s failure leaves a material and relational cost. Wanting another person safe and leaving that person free to choose are not identical achievements.

### 4.3 What she knows, what she does not receive

Earlier phases receive their own experiences rather than a retrospective complete biography. The child does not know the later brothers’ deaths, romance or intentional blessing control. The woman at the field does not yet have the married household’s remembered terms. The departing wife does not know the visitor or the family’s later accusations.

The narrated succession recognition is positive but bounded. In the fog, Theresia identifies Reinhard as the new bearer. The text leaves alternatives for the recognition’s basis: an inherited awareness like the uncle’s or her recognition of Reinhard’s extraordinary talent. The model can preserve her certainty without deciding that mechanism. It supplies no intention by Reinhard to kill her.

The platinum-haired visitor is not named in this recovered episode. Theresia judges the incongruous presence dangerous, drops the undrawable Reid, attacks with an ordinary long sword and then loses consciousness after the new voice. The sequence gives actions and a profound nonknowledge. It does not give her the analyst’s complete account of death, reanimation or control.

At the final response, Theresia recognizes Wilhelm and the passage of time. She tentatively senses Heinkel and Reinhard nearby and imagines their coming to see her off as loving. Her hope that they will manage the remaining problems is genuine. They have not yet performed the later rejection. The model must not turn a hopeful interpretation into a secretly informed approval of that rupture.

Wilhelm’s old injury account, Julius’s private report, Subaru’s idealization and the later partial disclosure have their own speakers and recipients. They help the analyst reconstruct information conditions. They do not create a newly shared acquaintance between Theresia and Subaru, a received husband’s full account in every relative, or autobiographical knowledge of another person’s discarded lives.

### 4.4 Own history and unrecorded history

Her narrated recollections supply enough for distinct phases without a complete biography. Unknown friends, routines and private conversations stay unrecorded; she is not modeled as unable to remember her own life simply because the analyst cannot name them. The loyal female attendant and unnamed uncle retain their source-local anonymity.

T9 requires a separate restraint. The controlled body’s familiar technique does not establish a continuous, freely choosing observer inside it. Conversely, the final direct response must not be dismissed merely because an earlier scene withheld ordinary recognition. The model selects what the source actually shows at each stage.

At T11 there is no available current living reply. This is the operative endpoint even when a downstream request proposes a plausible speech. ## 8. Competence, power and practical resources

### 8.1 Distinct capacities and their histories

Death God danger precedes Sword Saint inheritance. Early accidental harm is frightening precisely because intent does not provide the child a simple safeguard. Later narration explicitly says that, since being united with Wilhelm, she has become able to control the wounding power by her own will. The deliberate shoulder wound is a demonstration of that later condition. Her promised closure remains a proposed future act, not a performed cure.

The Sword Saint blessing supplies gifted assessment and extraordinary action. It does not make her want practice, understand every person, make a first battle possible or know how to comfort somebody. The white lines in her vision belong to a shown martial experience. Reading every human motive as if it were a blade is a metaphorical temptation the model rejects.

Actual practice and retained skill also matter. Wilhelm wins the ceremonial contest through training despite her wanted release. Theresia’s own earlier field experience leaves bodily sword capacity that persists when the blessing disappears on the last expedition. After dropping the locked Reid, she can still attack with an ordinary long sword. Losing the gift does not erase all skill; retained skill does not guarantee survival.

The ceremony uses a ceremonial sword. The final expedition and Reinhard’s later intervention have their own Reid conditions. Defeat, marriage, retirement, inheritance, permission to draw a blade and present bodily agency cannot replace one another as a single “power level.”

### 8.2 What strength fails to supply

The inherited role cannot supply a willing child’s answer or preserve a whole army when she cannot act. A successful rescue cannot secure Wilhelm’s grateful reception. A great public fighter can still be afraid, socially uncertain and unable to solve a loved person’s problem. The retired wife’s chosen return can still end in loss.

Her reassurance that she is the strongest swordswoman after Wilhelm has a recipient and a purpose at departure. It is not an omniscient ranking of every later person. Similarly, Wilhelm’s appraisal of the corpse and his awe before Reinhard are situated perceptions. They supply evidence about this encounter rather than a complete cosmological hierarchy.

Kingdom decisions, a public symbolic burden, Wilhelm’s actual search duty, household dependencies and available allies constrain the choices. Superior bodily force does not let Theresia perform every office, disclose everybody’s information or cure Luanna. The narration supplies no complete money/mana/tool inventory for every earlier phase.

At T10 she has little strength left and directs it into touching Wilhelm’s face. At T11 no living body is available. These are operative limits, even when a requested speech or action would fit an earlier remembered style.

## 9. Care, criticism, failure and recovery

Theresia receives real help. テムズ notices fear and makes room for confession; the attendant remains when she feels worthless; Wilhelm supplies a relationship in which she feels wanted and later protected. None is the sole cause that erases all other people or all her own choices.

Her care also has failed or costly forms. She initially judges her brothers’ practice through gifted superiority and later recognizes that this missed what they loved. She freezes in the first battle, withdraws after losses and cannot initially approach a distressed Wilhelm with a ready answer. These failures remain in the model even though she later acts powerfully.

The garden intervention shows a smaller recovery of action. She stops, fears adding to his emotional hurt, then approaches because losing Wilhelm feels worse. A familiar question and touch open a response without supplying a complete explanation in advance. His changed answer matters to her, and her own questioning changes afterward. This is usable positive evidence about learning within a relationship.

Criticism does not always produce calm disclosure. Wilhelm’s accusation after rescue hurts enough that she ends the visits. She inwardly denies mockery, yet sees no useful verbal defense in that encounter. The later contest and romance have their own actual meeting; the model does not invent an apology at the field to make the earlier rupture disappear.

Married affection includes direct resistance. She rebukes blame, insists on her decision and intentionally restricts pursuit. Her good intention cannot substitute for Wilhelm’s preferred choice. His objections remain spoken; her promised return and wound closure remain unperformed.

The V08 anecdote supplies further contrary material: Wilhelm reports that angry Theresia physically floored him and that he then forcibly embraced her until the anger dissolved. The source presents his recollection and a comic exchange, not a full jointly witnessed conflict or her general assent. V20’s positive relief during affectionate contact is another actual condition. Both belong in the model; neither licenses a rule that force is always harmless or that every offered embrace is refused.

Self-blame can shift after another person’s apology or a new family crisis. It is evidence of how she allocates responsibility, not a reason for the analyst to repeat her most severe judgment as a complete moral or causal verdict. The source does not authorize a clinical diagnosis from the recurrent command, withdrawal, fear, possessiveness or grief.

The final farewell is a real partial resolution. She can recognize love, give words and receive the words she wanted. She still leaves an unasked question and problems she hopes the others can handle. The later family catastrophe supplies a direct adverse test against an all-healed ending, without invalidating her final affection.
