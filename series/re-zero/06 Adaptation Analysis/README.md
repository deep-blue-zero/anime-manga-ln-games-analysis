# Re:Zero — adaptation analysis

This directory owns analysis of anime and other audiovisual adaptations as **distinct witnesses**.

No adaptation analysis is present at bootstrap.

## Admission requirements

Before an anime pass becomes canonical, record the actual audiovisual witnesses in the source inventory or a later adaptation-specific source lock: video identity, language/audio tracks, subtitle witnesses, episode numbering, and any extraction/manifest references used for retrieval.

## Analytical responsibilities

For each reviewed episode or adaptation unit, track as relevant:

- main-light-novel/source mapping;
- retained, omitted, compressed, expanded, and reordered material;
- changed focalization or information disclosure;
- voice performance and delivery;
- pauses, silence, screams, laughter, breathing, register, and paralinguistic cues;
- music and sound design;
- framing, blocking, camera movement, editing, color, lighting, expression, gesture, and visual symbolism;
- pacing and duration effects;
- changed ambiguity;
- adaptation-original connective material;
- whether the adaptation materially alters a character, relationship, mechanism, or theme reading.

## Cross-witness discipline

A good adaptation analysis can conclude that the anime produces a different emphasis or interpretation without declaring either witness invalid.

Do not use anime performance to rewrite what the light-novel prose literally says. Do not use later light-novel knowledge to claim that an earlier anime-only viewer should have inferred a fact the episode did not provide.

## Prospective option

If adaptation analysis is performed alongside the novel reading, freeze an episode-level viewer horizon before consulting source material beyond what that episode adapts. This permits two clean questions:

- what does the anime itself support at this point?
- what changes when compared against the already reviewed light-novel source?

Do not collapse those questions into one retrospective answer.
