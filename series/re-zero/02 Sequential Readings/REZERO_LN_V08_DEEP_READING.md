# Re:Zero — Japanese Light Novel Volume 08 prospective deep reading

## 0. Evidence boundary

V08 makes the ability to cooperate answerable to failure. Subaru does not simply forget the lessons of the Whale hunt: he requests help, values specialist work, accepts correction, shares an increasingly difficult battle with Julius and revises an operation to prioritize endangered civilians. These changes are consequential even though the attempt ends in his death. Conversely, cooperation does not automatically make every participant informed, every prediction correct, or every sacrifice acceptable to the person asked to perform it.

The central causal error is unusually instructive. The party verifies that Petelgeuse's first body is dead, then encounters different bodies wielding the same Authority. A collective “Sloth” office is a reasonable intermediate hypothesis, but it is not the explanation the volume finally establishes. The narrator identifies one parasitic mental entity, and Subaru becomes its next body. Correctly killing a body did not certify destruction of its occupant. The earlier observations need reinterpretation, not deletion.

Emilia supplies a second correction. She is not merely an absent recipient who can be given a perfect rescue and then asked to forgive. She has already tried to protect Arlam, has encountered rejection, has rebuilt its barrier and, when called by Ferris and Ram, fights alongside Puck. Subaru sees her act but does not complete a reunion conversation. Her agency is directly evidenced; her forgiveness, the reason for her tear and the post-death future are not.

## 4. Subaru: requesting help does not settle responsibility

### 4.1 Earned trust is not a demand for causal omniscience

Subaru bows and asks for help while admitting that he cannot explain his odor completely (`p-001#B0072–0126`). The allies accept his intent through achieved results, Crusch's judgment and their own relationships. Tibby's question is legitimate: he did not fight the Whale. Mimi mediates trust in her judgment and offers protection rather than producing evidence he has personally seen. Julius similarly asks what grounds the bait estimate. The resulting agreement is not a magically complete sharing of Subaru's discarded experience.

The improvement matters. Subaru can tell useful observations without narrating the power that made them available. His advisers do not require an impossible total disclosure before acting. Wilhelm's advice to lift his face and see the people he addresses turns help from an abstract mass into named contributors. Yet Subaru's “100 percent” bait confidence draws certainty from a past encounter while his own earlier planning admits that changed deployment can change the enemy's behavior (B0056–0059; B0131–0146). A true memory is not a guaranteed future script.

The initial ambush succeeds because roles are designed around limits. Subaru can see the hands but cannot personally win the sword fight; he can direct only a manageable number of evaders. Mimi and Tibby collapse the cave, Wilhelm cuts the body, Ferris checks death and the other forces track bases. Subaru refuses Petelgeuse's invitation to a personal duel, not the operation's responsibility. His weakness remains part of an effective plan instead of disappearing as a reward for resolve (`p-003#B0089–0137`; `p-005#B0029–0074`).

### 4.2 Protection and revenge remain different impulses

Subaru declares that everyone should return alive. The narrator marks the ideal as naive against ordinary battle expectations, but its ethical function is not merely to pretend risk does not exist: knowing death's terror makes him resist treating other lives as disposable (`p-001#B0225–0251`). He later proposes live capture, and Julius agrees where it does not require sacrificing their own people (`p-007#B0118–0128`). These are actual attempts, not merely sentimental speeches.

Counterevidence remains. Subaru still wishes suffering and regret on the buried enemy (`p-003#B0110`), still places his own life first on the betting table after Ferris objects, and still treats a perfect result as a qualification for meeting Emilia. A protective priority does not mean absence of retaliatory desire. A failed promise of universal survival does not mean every useful cooperative change was fraudulent.

The earlier face-ranking joke also preserves self-hatred after victory: Petelgeuse is first, Subaru himself second, not Julius as Julius jokingly suggests (`p-001#B0186–0190`). Peer-directed friction and self-directed rejection are related pressures, not the same feeling or recipient.

Ferris rejects a successful result as proof that the plan was safe. The objection concerns a single-point failure in Subaru's survival and the intolerable position of watching someone make that gamble (`p-005#B0120–0145`). Subaru understands the criticism without instantly acquiring a different self-valuing reflex. This is a precise limitation to development: insight changes available judgment before it reliably changes every habitual choice.

### 4.3 The dead are not proof that caring has stopped

Captured followers' self-killing first makes Subaru fear his own numbness to other people's deaths. His terror of his own repeated death has not worn away; the feared change concerns his response to others, not indifference to dying himself. Wilhelm misreads the doll metaphor as a comment about obedient pawns rather than receiving the whole private concern (`p-007#B0224–0243`). Neither the misunderstanding nor Subaru's coolness supplies a diagnosis.

Radian and four companions then make loss unmistakably allied and particular. Julius names Radian; Subaru associates the name with the fox-man scout. The other four remain unnamed, not interchangeable nonpeople. Ferris collects a ring and hair while others secure the area; their functional response is not evidence they do not grieve. Tibby cries and Mimi comforts him. Subaru's belief that only he has frozen in horror is an inadequate reading of their work (B0481–0530).

The next casualty account is corrected within the scene: the five dead scouts plus seven newly reported losses appear to make twelve, but Ferris saves one still-living critical patient, leaving eleven dead. The new seven comprise five instant deaths and two subsequent battle losses; they are not the earlier scout team's five counted again. Saving that patient does not undo Radian's death. Subaru has to learn both the value of the saved one and the weight of those who cannot be saved; a simple binary between total victory and worthless care misdescribes Ferris's work (`p-009#B0261–0325`; `p-010#B0066–0083`).

### 4.4 Fighting can mean borrowing judgment

Wilhelm's `強く在れ` differs from merely telling Subaru to acquire superior force. The instruction to fight includes thinking with others and asking for knowledge and strength. Subaru's renewed action after actual losses is stronger evidence than an untested post-victory declaration. His acceptance of weakness is nevertheless qualified “for now,” and he still treats the dead as his own sin and resists asking others to lighten that burden (`p-009#B0326–0431`).

The text therefore supports two concurrent readings: greater capacity to remain engaged with help under adverse results, and persistent punitive responsibility. A later model must account for both enacted delegation and continued self-disposability. Permanent incapacity to seek help and a completed cure would each misdescribe these scenes.

The fourth body's death sharpens the distinction. Subaru personally designs the lethal trap, but “without anyone's hand” at `p-019#B0035` cannot erase Patrasche's enabling work or the Ulgarm's bite. It marks the absence of a human ally delivering the decisive combat result and his first experience of this kind of personally caused killing. His trembling and the narrative “curse” of that experience are not automatically a newly implanted magical formula. He must continue fighting without pretending the act has no personal cost.

## 5. Julius: practical trust and the conversation that does not arrive

### 5.1 A real earlier apology leaves a real residual problem

V07's reciprocal qualified apology is not revoked by V08's friction. Julius accepts Subaru's expertise, asks him to report, recognizes a useful envoy role and promptly apologizes when “fortunate” sounds callous about Emilia's danger. Subaru can recognize kindness rather than automatically reinterpret it as contempt (`p-007#B0077–0100`, B0133–0144, B0395–0398). These are observable recipient-specific changes.

Ferris nevertheless identifies continuing unconscious aversion. Subaru's duel wounds are explicitly physically healed, while phantom pain and emotional resistance remain (`p-005#B0114–0117`). Ferris's first quiet comment is not heard; the later direct warning is (`p-010#B0085–0094`). A holder transition matters: Subaru can act on what Ferris now tells him without being blamed for an earlier unheard aside.

Two attempted further conversations fail. The first is overtaken by the hallucination/Ram incident; the second contracts into a safe remark about tactical coordination (`p-010#B0124–0141`; `p-014#B0008–0018`). Subaru also reflexively refuses Julius's offer of spirit-arts advice before recognizing the bias. Asking Julius to accompany the estate visit is a concrete corrective act, but it is not the missing full apology.

### 5.2 Six individual obligations, not an effortless all-purpose power

Julius wants the name `精霊騎士` to recognize his contracted quasi-spirits. His explanation connects six still-developing partners and his vow to become worthy of their future flowering (`p-012#B0284–0302`). When Petelgeuse later proposes discarding them to make his body usable, Julius refuses on his own side even if they might one day reject him (`p-023#B0012–0013`). The relationship is not a disposable inventory.

Ia's covert protection saved Subaru twice before its contractor is disclosed. Subsequent assignment is explicit, and Julius explains response and command limits: call her name, do not demand complexity or power beyond her capacity (`p-014#B0016–0023`). Her barrier later saves Subaru without preventing every burn and abrasion. Nect's emergency networking also has a cost: Subaru is overwhelmed, and Julius cannot stop the group rescue to retune it immediately. Affinity may explain the unusually deep connection, but remains an expert hypothesis.

The spirits do not give Julius unrestricted knowledge of Subaru's memory. Ia's ejection provides a reason to follow him at the ending; it does not reveal ten deaths, the taboo or an omniscient explanation of possession. Six bright spirits and Julius's appeal later help Subaru regain a brief opening, not a completed exorcism.

### 5.3 Entrusting a life makes refusal consequential

In the village battle Subaru asks Julius to take the followers while he draws the bishop, entrusting each other's survival to the other's task. Julius accepts after a brief hesitation (`p-017#B0137–0194`). Their capacities are unequal but complementary: Subaru cannot defeat the ordinary followers; Julius cannot see the hands. Practical trust is stronger than a claim that they have simply ceased disliking each other.

At the end Subaru asks Julius to kill him. Julius refuses, invokes his knightly and spiritual commitments and insists on considering another answer. Subaru narrows their contract to saving Emilia, calling the maneuver unfair himself (`p-023#B0044–0056`). The chapter title's fulfillment is consequently not uncomplicated contractual virtue. An agreed protective end is pressed into a request that wounds its other participant.

Julius asks about the conversation Subaru meant to have later. It will not happen in this attempt. His final acceptance of responsibility and cold blade at Subaru's neck do not prove an explicitly narrated beheading: the source supplies the placement and terminal death, not a described final cut (B0095–0127). They show that affection, commitment and resolve have become costly, not that the whole relation has reached a clean reconciliation.

## 6. Ferris: the hand that cannot make every outcome gentle

Ferris explicitly rejects selecting patients by personal liking or judging which lives deserve healing (`p-005#B0133–0141`). The capture attempts support that account: he tries to restrain and save enemies who use suicide formulas. His anger is not merely that they denied him useful intelligence; their use of their own lives violates the value he places on life. Concern about Subaru understanding the Cult coexists with these attempts, rather than making him uniformly merciful in speech or uniformly exterminatory in practice (`p-007#B0213–0222`, B0262–0268).

His expertise has limits. The first patient he “saves” after the ambush was critically injured, not dead. The bodies of Radian's group cannot be recovered to life. Yet Ferris later emerges bodily intact from an explosion and says that he died once, while his clothes remain destroyed because healing cannot regenerate them (`p-015#B0028–0039`). This directly qualifies any blanket “Ferris cannot heal the dead” rule. It does not establish that he can revive every other person's irreversible corpse. Self-regeneration, ordinary resuscitation, restoring a destroyed heart, memory erasure and clothing repair must not be homogenized. The source has not provided a complete account of his self-preservation mechanism.

Ferris also turns biological knowledge against a living opponent. He detects Kety's special formula through contact, pretends to negotiate cargo compensation, then forces the water in Kety's body to incapacitate him. He says that prior direct interference makes another touch unnecessary (`p-014#B0088–0105`). Paralysis does not block the hands, and the capture ends in an explosion; a superior medical technique is not a universal tactical guarantee.

The final scene makes the same access rule horrifyingly personal. Subaru has actually received treatment, enabling Ferris's remote intervention. Subaru asks, Ferris weeps and acts, and blood/viscera are lethally heated (`p-023#B0061–0087`). Ferris's refusal to enjoy it is explicit, as is his anger that a power promised to a prince and devoted to Crusch has been used this way. The prince reference is not a new independently specified biography for every earlier unnamed memory.

Subaru consents to being stopped, but Ferris has not received the premise of repeatable death. Subaru privately regrets making him violate the purpose of his hands more than he regrets his own pain. This is not a solved ethics problem in which one nod transfers all costs to the dying person. Wilhelm objects, Julius regrets his refusal and Ferris says that he too will resent it. No participant can be judged as if they knew the restoration mechanism.

Earlier, Subaru comforts Ferris by recognizing the one life actually saved (`p-010#B0066–0083`). That reversal of care is real; it does not cure either person's self-worth. Ferris's teasing can regulate fear, pry into a relation and conceal a covert capture in successive scenes. His ordinary `フェリちゃん` style, private `私` and final `スバルくん` require their recipients and stakes; none is an isolated “true personality” cancelling all the others.

## 7. Emilia, Ram and the people the operation would protect

### 7.1 A blank letter is evidence of what was received, not of who caused it

Ram attacks a force she understands as hostile. Her information is not simply “Subaru returned with strangers”: a sealed Karsten letter arrived blank, the lion crest marks its apparent origin, the forest has been unnaturally quiet for two days, her eye cannot obtain useful information there, and armed outsiders approach. Wilhelm confirms a blank letter's local no-dialogue/hostility convention (`p-012#B0028–0046`). Ram's wrong conclusion follows intelligible evidence and limited access.

Explaining the alliance changes her response. It does not explain the blank page. Subaru proposes an actor trying to divide the camps, but neither a swap nor a culprit is independently witnessed. Rem had written the intended letter because Subaru's literacy was insufficient; that earlier fact and the actual blank delivery are distinct observations (`p-001#B0211–0214`; `p-012#B0085–0092`). No named conspirator, memory-erasure cause or recovered written original is proved.

Ram holds the messenger in the house for hostage exchange. Subaru considers whether her attempt to seize him could have been a rescue from hostile captors, but it remains his interpretation of her conduct, not her full confession. Her readiness to die rather than be humiliated is directly expressed; the exact imagined captive arrangement is not. Her attack is her own judgment, not an order Emilia secretly gave (B0080–0105).

The apparent “no injuries” result is also corrected rather than harmonized away. Wilhelm's sleeve and wind graze are disclosed after the broad public account, and he has hidden the injury to ease the incident (B0001–0002, B0064–0065). Ram's short contest with him does not establish full equality: she later says a fit Wilhelm would beat her within ten seconds, while her own stored mana has been spent (`p-014#B0027–0034`). Rhetorical “circles” of decline are not measured power ratios.

### 7.2 Real practical care does not finish the sister question

Ram combines wind with a hallucinogenic drug. Her suggestion that tea has trained Subaru's resistance is explicitly a joke, not a new poisoning history or proof that he escaped unaided (`p-012#B0071–0078`). She then organizes, supplies information, invokes estate authority and helps move the wounded. The same person can be sarcastic, defensive, frightened, coercive and useful under different conditions.

Her Roswaal report identifies `聖域` as his destination and starts with a `ガーフ` reference. This is attributed location information, not a directly observed visit or complete Garfiel history (B0103–0105). No actual conversation with Roswaal authorizes his supposed intentions.

Nor is Ram's memory of Rem directly retested. Rem's name in Subaru's hostage speculation is internal; his later spoken murmur is not heard, and he declines to repeat it (`p-014#B0035–0042`). The narrator's sister reference and Subaru's sense that Rem now matters as much as Emilia cannot be converted into a demonstrated Ram response to Rem's name. The old V06 erased-sister denial remains historical evidence with its own conditions, not an accusation that current Ram knowingly lies.

### 7.3 Arlam's consent cannot be manufactured by a correct rescue goal

Subaru initially intends to speak quickly enough that the villagers will not choose refusal. His present mabeast explanation borrows the true two-month-old incident as cover for a different current danger. A familiar but unnamed young villager challenges it. The adults already fear Cult attention connected to Emilia's candidacy; they are not conveniently ignorant people awaiting the protagonist's account (`p-012#B0137–0177`).

Their fear and prejudice must both remain visible. The Cult threatens them, but they direct blame toward Emilia rather than the attackers. That is not justified by their need for safety. At the same time, a noble's household cannot assume that earlier gratitude for Subaru's rescue gives unlimited informed consent to its current operation.

Ram resolves the immediate impasse by invoking the lord's authority and promising compensation for all losses. Subaru initially objects to the rough method, then supports it and promises to negotiate with Roswaal. The villagers comply reluctantly, explicitly far from persuaded (B0205–0219). Material protection and coercion coexist. Compensation is a promise, not an observed payment or proof of a comprehensive national legal rule.

Their subsequent labor matters. The youth corps checks names, villagers collect minimal belongings, and fifteen wagons with capacity of roughly seven each are allocated (B0231–0234). Those figures describe capacity, not exactly 105 completed evacuees. Compliance is not total passivity, but active work after a constrained decision does not retroactively make the decision freely informed.

### 7.4 Emilia's care has already been refused before Subaru sees it

Ram reports that Emilia offered refuge, tried repeatedly to convince villagers, rebuilt the forest barrier against possible mabeasts, and stayed worried over the blank letter until exhausted. She is asleep after work, not doing nothing while Subaru rescues everyone (B0244–0264). The distinction between `拒絶` and `否定` marks a refusal to allow her offered contact and person into the exchange, not just disagreement with a proposal.

The earlier `まだ見ぬ` half-elf phrasing at B0170 belongs to Subaru's not-yet-reunited focal position; it cannot establish that the villagers have never seen or spoken with Emilia. The later account explicitly describes dialogue. Retaining both passages prevents an attractive but false “they have never met her” explanation of prejudice.

In the battle Emilia independently opposes the Cult. Ferris and Ram have called her; Ferris has warned her about the invisible attack, and Puck proposes ice-mist detection. She combines that warning, her own physical ability, ice terrain, childhood skating competence, kicks and casting. Puck's deceptive double and timing are consequential. The fight therefore neither reduces her to Puck's puppet nor turns her into an isolated unlimited combatant (`p-021#B0053–0138`).

Subaru's wish to prevent her fighting has to be corrected by Ferris: the person protected is not someone who only stands behind. This is not proof that every exposure of Emilia to harm is acceptable. It is a concrete refusal of the assumption that Subaru's protective goal grants exclusive authority over her action.

Her tear after the kill is observed; its cause is explicitly unknown. Subaru imagines pain at killing and makes it his own sin, then notices her apparent surprise at the tear (B0151–0158). ## 8. The ensemble, institutions and finite protective capacity

### 8.1 “Insurance” is an arrangement, not providence

Subaru's withheld insurance is finally identified: Anastasia and Russell help circulate a notice recruiting merchants with wagons, with Roswaal named employer and cargo bought at the merchants' own price (`p-001#B0197–0215`). This uses commercial networks, available vehicles and a clearing road. It is not Otto secretly arranging everything; Subaru explicitly finds no Otto among the arriving traders.

The familiar man who introduced Otto in a discarded state does not remember that encounter. Subaru covers his mistake as a private remark (`p-007#B0318–0321`). The man's presence supports current recruitment, not active mutual merchant history or a claim about Otto's death.

Subaru deliberately conceals the Cult threat because the traders might flee or expose the operation. Julius gives case-dependent pragmatic approval; the others' nonobjection is not a public mandate or full consent. Twenty knights are then allocated to fifteen wagons, materially reducing forest strength (B0301–0313, B0356–0366). The roughly “half” language and rounded larger cohort count must not be converted into a fabricated exact twenty-five-person detachment.

Kety later demands compensation for the concealed threat and proves to be an infiltrator. His identity does not make every merchant's grievance fraudulent. Ferris detects him through a specific physical trace, not by establishing that wanting more payment proves Cult membership. Unscreened recruitment and hidden cargo give the attackers a route in; the explosion removes the very resource on which evacuation depends (`p-014#B0054–0125`; `p-017#B0001–0009`).

The fallback to mansion defense is therefore not an arbitrary abandonment of a good plan. An option has been physically destroyed. Protecting noncombatants also constrains the coalition in a fight against similarly sized forces. Administrative preparation, information and material infrastructure are causal, even when swordplay dominates the page.

### 8.2 Wilhelm: a chosen sword with blind spots

Wilhelm's continued action after revenge is vigorous and voluntary. He supports Subaru, offers instruction, accepts a deceptive ambush despite his chivalric preference, isolates a dangerous enemy from civilians and refuses to abandon Subaru when gravely wounded. The narrator also marks his confidence in Subaru and Ferris surviving the first explosion as somewhat excessive (`p-017#B0058–0098`). Trust can sustain coordination without being an infallible safety estimate.

His answer to invisibility is expertise, not newly acquired sight. Dirt trajectories reveal negative space, timing predicts pursuit and movement exploits the user's limited skill. He accumulates wounds while doing it. Kety then exploits attention concentrated on invisible attacks by using a visible dagger for self-killing and a blast. The expert's failure is conditional and intelligible; it does not prove either that experience is useless or that the enemy's power has no counters.

Wilhelm's new marriage anecdote—Theresia knocking him down when angry, his forceful embrace until she calmed—is a limited self-account in banter (`p-007#B0172–0179`). It complicates a sanitized courtship portrait but does not furnish the entire marriage, certify every interaction consensual, or explain the Reinhard rupture. Eight-Arms Kurgan's reported death at Garkla and Wilhelm's remembered mutual near-fatal duel are another bounded testimony, not a present new fight or resurrection (`p-001#B0281–0287`).

### 8.3 The Iron Fang do not react to death as one person

Tibby is now directly identified, wears a monocle, asks grounds for confidence and participates in the combined cave attack. His desire for a safer job coexists with unexpectedly offensive capacity. Mimi's teasing judgment is not an authoritative cowardice rank. Hetaro's absence from the new forest action does not mean he has become incompetent or idle.

Their mundane work differentiates them: Tibby tends equipment and takes spoils; Mimi's corpse-destruction impulse attracts rebuke; Ricardo offers rough physical encouragement to Ferris; Tibby grieves Radian's group while Mimi comforts him. Loot entitlement is asserted, not proved universal law (`p-005#B0166–0178`; `p-007#B0064–0092`, B0250–0260, B0377–0384, B0527–0530).

Tibby's remark that wanting ruin is a luxury suggests a perspective on despair, but does not identify his past trauma. Mimi's failure to offer the same remark does not show she lacks painful experience. No exact ages, triplet taxonomy, slavery story or total shared private history is supplied.

Radian is a named scout with a limited observed role, not a ready-made complete character. His team's death shows that precautions and expertise can fail against an unrecognized mechanism. Other participants' grief and practical recovery work make the loss more than fuel for Subaru's development; the text names and shows people whose work and relationships were already present.

### 8.4 Patrasche is not a costless extension of Subaru

Wilhelm identifies her as a Diana type and describes broad terrain competence. Forest performance corroborates part of the account, not every listed environment. She survives the wagon blast, independently positions for a tackle, probably chooses cover before Subaru's instruction, and continues after bleeding wounds and a head strike (`p-010#B0054–0061`; `p-017#B0225–0309`).

Subaru notices her questioning look before redirecting into danger, interprets continued cooperation as entrusting her life, apologizes for needing more and still rides her wounded (`p-019#B0038–0044`). Those are relational and ethical costs, not a command power that makes her feel no pain. The narration explicitly resists treating his shouted name as literal strengthening magic. Her final contact as he dies is perceived comfort, not proof that she knows RBD.

## 9. Mechanics: a sequence of models rather than a retrospective encyclopedia

### 9.1 Body death, office multiplicity and one mental occupant

The evidence develops in stages:

| reached evidence | warranted model at that point | what is not yet established |
|---|---|---|
| first body cut apart and medically checked | that body is dead | all Sloth destroyed, no other compatible host |
| red-haired female uses same Authority/register | plural dangerous bodies; collective office is a working hypothesis | shared-mind ontology, possession, automatic resurrection |
| special formula found separately from poison | bodily preparation is relevant to investigate | the complete formula's purpose or identity with suicide mechanism |
| different bodies seek the same missing book | repeated continuity/ownership pattern | exact transfer trigger, range or number of unused hosts |
| Ia expelled; Subaru speaks/acts as Petelgeuse | hostile occupation of Subaru's functioning body | all host eligibility and prevention conditions |
| narrator identifies one parasitic mental entity | collective-separate-Sloths model rejected | permanent destruction of that entity after Subaru's death/return |

The bodies are not copies that dissolve like the Whale's mana-dependent duplicates. The first corpse remains, is dismembered and exhumed; later physical bodies have their own wounds and deaths. Neither verified first-body death nor Ferris's competent examination was simply false. The faulty inference lay in equating body and occupant.

“Fingers” likewise changes scope. Subaru first imagines ten groups from human finger names, then considers individual lieutenants carrying the Authority. Provisional base counts and the later “about forty” estimate are planning models, not a verified organization chart. Petelgeuse finally claims that the old Fingers are exhausted and Subaru is a replacement. The actual possession corroborates compatibility in this case; it does not independently prove every historical decade, all ten prior preparations or an unlimited replenishment rule (`p-010#B0014–0028`; `p-017#B0040–0044`; `p-023#B0001–0013`).

Petelgeuse's unsuitable-body judgments about Ferris, Wilhelm and Julius are attributed testimony. Julius's existing spirit bonds and Ia's ejection make spiritual occupancy relevant, but the full causal bridge is not experimentally settled. `精神体` establishes the narrated parasitic mental entity; it must not silently import a complete later spirit taxonomy.

### 9.2 Seeing is an advantage with a tactical counter

Subaru again sees seven hands from the first body. Other people are not retroactively given that perception. Wilhelm predicts and exposes paths through thrown dirt; Emilia detects interference in ice mist; Puck uses force and deception. Each counter has prerequisites and failure points.

Later bodies produce many more hands: the fourth approaches sixty or seventy, but initially controls them worse, reduces to roughly twenty and then changes strategy (`p-017#B0200–0211`). The progression does not prove an exact fixed maximum or measured body-to-count law. Raw direct hands are relatively slow in the described matchup, making wide evasion possible; thrown masonry is much faster and massed badly aimed projectiles still deny safety. The enemy also throws its own body between hands, paying in precision, straight-line constraints and further wounds (B0253–0284).

Subaru cannot grab a hand even while it grips him; that does not mean no physical counter exists. Wilhelm's dirt method exploits displacement rather than staining an inherently touchable limb. Self-transport can crush the user's own shoulder or worsen injuries; no blanket owner immunity has been shown. Seeing the power is not the same as acquiring it or being safe from its secondary effects.

### 9.3 Different mental effects and different magical access

Ram's hallucination affects Subaru despite his earlier resistance to the Whale's mental attack. The subjective minutes/outer seconds, false Patrasche presence and looping road establish sensory deception, not a spatially relocated world. Ia burns the flower within the hallucination; Julius then transmits a group counter and others break theirs (`p-010#B0143–0245`). This defeats any universal mental-immunity claim without explaining the earlier erasure-memory exception away.

Nect connects gates within range and blurs consciousness boundaries; too much depth can mix senses. Julius tentatively attributes Subaru's unusually painful overload to high affinity, not weak moral resolve. A fast rescue has a real receiver cost. The account does not establish unrestricted access to every memory, every creature or every distance (`p-012#B0302`; `p-014#B0001–0005`).

Ferris's biological mana interference is a different access relation, with demonstrated prior-treatment dependency. The Cult poison stones and the special Finger trace are also separate formulas. None can be merged with Subaru's old inert Ulgarm curses merely because all involve embedded structures. No new safe Shamac cast, completed gate course or old-curse removal is shown. Julius's suggestion of tuition is not actual training or cure.

### 9.4 Gospel, trial, scent and the limits of doctrine

The actual captured Gospel has unreadable script and a later blank portion. Ferris labels conversion-through-delivery a rumor; Wilhelm gives a stronger irreversibility account; Subaru challenges the inference from Petelgeuse's exceptional madness to all recipients. Handling the book does not visibly convert Subaru or Tibby, but that is not proof of universal safety. Later bodies' pursuit of this book supports continuing possession/priority, not independently decoded predictions (`p-003#B0066–0087`; `p-007#B0015–0036`).

Petelgeuse's Pride proposal and new `魔女因子` term are his claims. No conferred Pride office, demonstrated new Subaru Authority or independent reading of his supposed absence from the book occurs. Nor does calling Emilia a vessel make the Cult's abusive theological premise true.

The bishop's reliance on fog to block the road corroborates a Cult–Whale operational connection, and his lack of knowledge of the kill helps the coalition. It does not identify the full command chain or prove that Whale equals the Gluttony bishop. The reported Greed destruction of Garkla gives historical scale, not a named new incumbent or witnessed power.

Subaru's Ulgarm trap supports attraction to the bishop as well as to himself in this encounter. A removed barrier stone permits the attack and then repels the dog when raised; its warmth warns of nearby mabeasts (`p-017#B0344–0370`; `p-019#B0001–0044`). This is not obedience or shared Cult allegiance. The dog remains dangerous to Subaru; its exact motive and every other species' response remain untested.

At death the private `愛してる` returns without an explicit speaker identity (`p-023#B0125–0127`). Similarity to earlier utterances is a pattern, not proof unifying the voice, scent, taboo limbs, return provider, Witch factor and Sloth. No new disclosure-targeting experiment resolves the taboo.
