## 3. Volume identity and macrostructure

The source identifies itself as numbered Volume 1. A source-verified repository arc identity is **not yet assigned**. This artifact therefore uses global volume identity `V01` and preserves `arc: UNASSIGNED_PENDING_SOURCE_VERIFICATION`.

The prose is organized around a prologue, five numbered chapters, and an epilogue. Structurally, the prologue presents the terminal scene of the first fatal event-state before Chapter 1 supplies the path into it. The volume then repeats and alters a bounded span of events through three deaths and a fourth surviving sequence.

For analysis, the repeated span is divided into four local event-state identifiers. These labels are repository metadata, not terms claimed by the novel:

| event-state | bounded description | terminal state |
|---|---|---|
| `V01-E1` | arrival; encounter with the silver-haired girl and Puck; badge search; first loot-house arrival | Subaru and the silver-haired girl die in the loot house; prologue is this endpoint |
| `V01-E2` | first restoration; Subaru reaches the loot house through Rom/Felt and negotiates for the badge | Rom, Felt, and Subaru die during Elsa's attack |
| `V01-E3` | second restoration; Subaru encounters the silver-haired girl before the theft, then the alley attackers | Subaru dies after misapplying knowledge from a prior state |
| `V01-E4` | third restoration; Subaru explicitly models the phenomenon, recruits/encounters Reinhard, reaches Felt earlier, and changes the loot-house sequence | Elsa is repelled; Subaru, Emilia, Felt, Rom, and Reinhard survive the immediate conflict |

This structure is analytically more important than a conventional chronology because the same apparent “relationship” or “fact” can have different memory ownership in different states.

## 4. Executive analytical state at the V01 boundary

V01 establishes a story in which **information can persist across otherwise reset local histories, but information is neither complete nor cost-free**. Subaru's retained memories give him real tactical and social leverage. They also create false assumptions when he forgets that other people do not share the same history, and they carry sensory and emotional burdens from events that the surviving world no longer contains.

The volume's most important character finding is not that Subaru is fearless, naturally heroic, or secretly omniscient. He is frightened, often inexperienced, sometimes self-dramatizing, and repeatedly tempted by genre scripts that reality disproves. What becomes durable by the end of V01 is his refusal to treat remembered obligations as erased merely because the people who helped him no longer remember doing so. His formulation that `受けた恩義は消えてなくならない` is the clearest statement of that one-sided ethical continuity. (`第五章; p-022.xhtml`)

The successful fourth state is also not a solo victory. It depends on Subaru's retained information and warnings, Emilia and Puck's combat/healing capacity, Rom's resistance, Felt's choice to seek help rather than simply escape, and Reinhard's arrival and overwhelming force. V01 therefore supports **distributed causal credit** more strongly than a “Subaru solves the loop” model.

Several large mysteries remain deliberately open: what causes the restorations, who or what controls any checkpoint, why Subaru experiences them, the limits on repetition, the political meaning of Emilia's badge and Reinhard's epilogue discovery, Elsa's employer, the basis and history of half-elf/Witch stigma, and the deeper mechanics behind several magical explanations.

## 5. Event-state reconstruction and information flow

### 5.1 `V01-E1`: arrival, genre-script framing, and the first fatal endpoint

Subaru arrives with no source-established explanation for how or why. His first response is not pure bewilderment. He rapidly samples environmental evidence: language, currency, visible infrastructure, local technology, nonhuman inhabitants, and his own supplies. He recognizes that his Japanese money and cards have no obvious use, inventories his possessions, and tests whether an expected “special ability” exists. (`第一章; p-003.xhtml–p-005.xhtml`)

This is a useful baseline because it prevents a crude “Subaru is stupid because he thinks in isekai tropes” reading. His cognition has two simultaneous layers:

1. **practical observation**, often fast and adaptive;
2. **genre-script projection**, which supplies hypotheses but also creates unjustified expectations of a summoning purpose, exceptional power, and a designated heroine role.

The alley attack exposes the gap. Subaru initially treats the encounter with game-like confidence, but pain, numerical disadvantage, and a knife force immediate recalibration. He can abandon bravado and grovel when survival requires it. His fear is therefore not absent beneath the performance.

The silver-haired girl and Puck rescue him. She heals him, allows Puck to support his recovery, and then tries to recast that aid as self-interested exchange: `私のためだったの`. (`第一章; p-007.xhtml`) Puck calls her behavior `趣味が悪い` when she gives the name “Satella,” and the narration marks unusual emotional distance at the naming moment. V01 later supplies enough evidence to identify that name as false/loaded, but **not enough to establish why she selected it in E1**.

She also interrupts an urgent search for her stolen badge to help a lost child. That decision costs time and matters causally. It will later recur under a different event-state, making it stronger evidence for a stable preference than a one-off act under Subaru's influence.

At the loot house, Subaru and the silver-haired girl find Rom dead. Elsa attacks. The prologue and Chapter 1 converge on Subaru and the girl dying in the loot house. Near his earlier alley danger Subaru's narration identifies a particularly intolerable prospect not simply as fear of pain but as ending `何もない空っぽのまま`—ending while still empty. (`第一章; p-003.xhtml`) V01 does not yet justify turning that sentence into a timeless theory of his personality, but it is a strong early signal that usefulness, consequence, or meaningful attachment matters to his self-evaluation.

### 5.2 `V01-E2`: retained information works, but only locally

Subaru reappears near the fruit seller with his physical condition restored and memory intact. People around him do not act as though the prior state occurred.

The alley encounter provides the cleanest early test of retained information. Subaru has not become physically stronger between E1 and E2. He now knows about the knife and the attackers' pattern, and this changes what he can do with the same body. Foreknowledge lets him dismantle the encounter that previously overwhelmed him.

That success is immediately dangerous if generalized too far. The loot house produces intrusive recollection: the dead Rom, his own wound, the silver-haired girl's death. He has shaking legs, dry mouth, a pounding heart, tinnitus-like ringing, and a flashback presentation before entering. (`第二章; p-011.xhtml`) These are source-observed phenomena. This artifact does **not** assign a clinical diagnosis.

Subaru meets Rom and Felt alive, uses his cellphone as a bargaining object, and attempts to outbid Elsa for the badge. His choice is explicitly loss-seeking in the ordinary economic sense: `俺は損がしてぇんだよ`, followed by `貸し借りはきっちり返す`. (`第二章; p-011.xhtml`) The cellphone is valuable enough that Rom warns Subaru its value may exceed the badge. Subaru nevertheless treats repaying the silver-haired girl's aid as worth a deliberately bad exchange.

Elsa arrives and negotiates. When Subaru says the badge will be returned to its original owner, Elsa's behavior changes. She says her employer does not require the badge to be in hand and attacks after the transaction is no longer serving the employer's objective. (`第二章; p-015.xhtml`) The strongest V01-safe inference is that **preventing the original owner from simply retaining/recovering the badge may matter operationally**. That is not yet direct evidence of the employer's ultimate motive.

The ensuing fight provides another information-versus-capacity test. Subaru recognizes Elsa's preference for an abdominal strike from the previous death and partially evades one attack. That does not make him competitive with her. Felt saves him from the first lethal line, Rom fights, and all three ultimately die. Retained information improves local survival odds without solving the larger causal system.

Elsa also describes Felt's extraordinary movement as `風の加護` and says she is “loved by the world.” (`第二章; p-015.xhtml`) Felt's speed is observed. The explanatory label is Elsa's claim and remains uncorroborated as a complete mechanics account at the V01 boundary.

### 5.3 `V01-E3`: the first major misuse of cross-state knowledge

After the next restoration, Subaru encounters the silver-haired girl before the theft and calls her “Satella.” Her response is angry and unambiguous about the social meaning of the name: `嫉妬の魔女`, `禁忌の象徴`, a name people recoil from using. (`第三章; p-018.xhtml`)

This establishes several things while leaving others open:

- “Satella” was not an ordinary self-introduction that Subaru simply misunderstood;
- the name carries strong public taboo;
- the silver-haired girl knows that taboo;
- Puck's E1 reaction is retrospectively intelligible as disapproval of her naming choice;
- **why** she chose the name in E1 is still unresolved.

Felt steals the badge during the disturbance. Subaru then encounters the same three alley attackers and makes one of V01's most instructive mistakes: he treats them as though his prior victory should have altered their behavior. They do not remember it. He is stabbed and dies.

The failure falsifies any simple equation of retained information with mastery. Knowledge can be actively harmful when Subaru attaches it to the wrong holder or event-state. It also validates the repository requirement to track knowledge provenance by person and state.

Before dying, Subaru attempts to attend to information rather than simply surrender to panic. That adaptive turn matters, but it should not be romanticized. The state ends in another painful death, and the next chapter shows that the accumulated experience has not become psychologically trivial.

### 5.4 `V01-E4`: explicit `死に戻り` theory and a successful branch

On the third restoration, Subaru finally names his working model `死に戻り`. (`第四章; p-021.xhtml`) He verifies that wounds and inventory have reset and that the fruit seller has no memory of the prior encounters. He learns the local date as `タンムズの月十四日`. The cumulative pattern now supports a robust descriptive claim: **after Subaru's death, he has repeatedly reappeared at a prior local state with his own memories retained and surrounding conditions restored.**

The description is stronger than the explanation. Subaru also speculates about time travel, looping, and game-like autosave behavior. V01 does not establish:

- that the whole universe literally travels backward in time;
- who or what creates the restoration;
- how a return point is chosen;
- whether the return point can move;
- whether the phenomenon is unlimited;
- why Subaru retains memory;
- whether anyone else could retain memory under conditions not observed here.

Subaru himself rejects the assumption of unlimited retries. He explicitly says previous returns do not prove another will occur and considers the possibility of a limited number of uses. He also rejects using a life as disposable reconnaissance because the deaths hurt and because failure to return would be terminal. (`第四章; p-021.xhtml`)

This matters for longitudinal modeling: V01 does not depict a character who becomes brave because death has become cheap. It depicts a character whose information advantage is inseparable from a death he still regards as costly and frightening.

#### 5.4.1 The moral decision not to abandon the prior-state dead

E4 gives Subaru a rational-looking escape route. He understands that Elsa can be avoided if he does not involve himself with the badge, and that Rom and Felt are thieves/fences with no memory of any debt to him. He briefly considers walking away.

The text then forces a correction to his pre-isekai self-image. Subaru remembers that, in front of a computer, he had mocked the kind of feeling now moving him. (`第四章; p-021.xhtml`) In embodied circumstances he cannot make “they no longer remember me” equivalent to “the obligation never existed.” By Chapter 5 the principle is stated directly: `受けた恩義は消えてなくならない`. (`第五章; p-022.xhtml`)

This is not proof of pure altruism. Subaru also wants recognition, a smile, a satisfying self-image, and a connection to the attractive silver-haired girl he has already cast in “heroine” terms. V01 repeatedly makes those mixed motives visible. The stronger conclusion is that **his self-dramatizing motives coexist with a costly reciprocity norm that survives asymmetric memory**.

#### 5.4.2 Social learning becomes as important as tactical learning

E4 also shows learning outside combat.

Subaru uses the dying clue `衛兵` from E3 to call for a guard and meets Reinhard. He changes his presentation in the slum, deliberately dirtying himself so that residents will read him as less alien. He uses prior knowledge of Felt's location, bargaining style, and likely route. He catches inconsistencies when Felt tries to misdirect him, then repairs the interaction by acknowledging that his own unexplained knowledge gives her good reason to distrust him.

The adaptation is not perfect. He still speaks in game/flag language and still takes social shortcuts. But compared with E1, he is increasingly treating **other people's information states** as variables rather than assuming the world is organized around his own memory.

#### 5.4.3 Changing timing changes the branch

Subaru prevents Felt from spending time hiring slum interference against the badge owner. This changes when the silver-haired girl reaches the loot house. The branch is therefore not a fixed replay whose events occur regardless of intervention.

That point weakens Subaru's earlier temptation to describe recurring encounters as quasi-mandatory “events.” Some recurrence can be explained by stable incentives and starting conditions: the same attackers choose a conspicuous outsider as a target; Felt has the same theft assignment; the badge owner pursues the same stolen property. Once those conditions are altered, timing and outcomes move.

The source therefore supports **branch mutability** more strongly than metaphysical fate.

#### 5.4.4 The final loot-house coalition

Elsa attacks. Subaru's warning allows Puck to intercept an assassination line that had succeeded in earlier states. Emilia, Puck, Rom, Subaru, and Felt each contribute under different constraints.

Puck's ability is limited by mana and time. He eventually disappears from active combat. Emilia continues to fight and heal. Subaru is not an equal combatant and at points freezes under remembered fear. Rom can resist but is outclassed. Felt is instructed to flee, but instead chooses to seek help. She finds Reinhard, whose arrival changes the force balance decisively.

Reinhard overwhelms Elsa with extraordinary force, but Elsa survives and stages a final surprise attack. Subaru uses his retained knowledge of her abdominal-strike tendency to interpose Rom's club and block a killing line. He is injured through the guard but survives after Emilia heals him.

The final outcome is thus a **network effect**:

`retained information -> changed timing/warnings -> coalition survives long enough -> Felt seeks help -> Reinhard arrives -> Elsa is repelled -> Subaru's retained attack-pattern knowledge prevents a final reversal`.

Remove any one of several links and V01 does not establish that the same outcome follows. The volume strongly disfavors assigning total causal credit to the person who remembers the loops.

## 6. Subaru Natsuki — V01 bounded character model

### 6.1 Baseline before the repeated states

V01 gives a narrow but useful Earth-side baseline. Subaru is seventeen, a third-year public high-school student, and `不登校気味`. The narration says avoidance of unpleasant decisions became habitual enough that absences accumulated and his parents were hurt by it. (`第一章; p-003.xhtml`)

This matters because “quickly commits to action in another world” is not simply the revelation of a timeless heroic essence. V01 places decisive crisis action against a recent pattern of avoidance in ordinary life.

He is also more physically trained than his lack of real fighting experience initially suggests. He mentions middle-school kendo and claims grip strength over seventy kilograms and an approximately eighty-kilogram bench press, attributing some training time to truancy. (`第二章; p-011.xhtml`) These are self-reported numbers, not independently measured facts. The broader behavioral fact—that he has trained and is physically stronger than a completely sedentary baseline—is corroborated by his performance against ordinary attackers.

His starting inventory is mundane: near-dead cellphone, wallet/cards/coins, food/snacks, tracksuit, shoes. V01 gives him no demonstrated automatic combat “cheat” at arrival.

### 6.2 Genre literacy: cognitive scaffold and distortion

Subaru's game/anime/light-novel vocabulary is not ornamental. It actively structures perception.

It helps him:

- generate hypotheses quickly;
- notice repeated patterns;
- conceptualize branching outcomes;
- treat information as a resource;
- search for exploitable “flags” or event dependencies.

It also hurts him:

- he expects special summoning privileges without evidence;
- he initially role-casts the silver-haired girl as a designated heroine;
- he overestimates how much repeated events behave like scripted game events;
- he misapplies prior-state history to people who do not remember it;
- he sometimes treats people and danger as functions in a scenario before embodied cost corrects him.

The V01-safe conclusion is not “genre literacy is false.” It is **a fast but noisy model generator** whose hypotheses must be tested against the world.

### 6.3 Bravado as performance rather than stable fearlessness

Subaru talks big, jokes, sexualizes situations, and repeatedly narrates himself through protagonist/heroine/game language. V01 also shows him groveling when a knife appears, shaking before the loot house, freezing under Elsa's attack, experiencing dry mouth and intrusive sensory recall, and admitting he does not want to die again.

These observations are compatible if the bravado is partly a self-regulation strategy: he performs confidence to hold fear, embarrassment, and uncertainty at a usable distance. That is a **corroborated inference**, not a direct diagnosis of motive.

The rival reading—that he is simply inconsistent—cannot be eliminated. V01 is too short to decide whether humor under pressure is a stable coping style, youthful theatricality, genre habit, or some mixture.

### 6.4 Adaptive learning

Subaru learns quickly when the information is correctly scoped:

- knife foreknowledge changes the alley fight;
- the word `衛兵` changes whom he calls for;
- social presentation changes slum cooperation;
- bargaining knowledge changes his approach to Felt;
- Elsa's abdominal pattern changes his final defensive choice;
- timing intervention changes Emilia's arrival and the coalition structure.

His failures are equally informative:

- knowing the thugs does not mean they know him;
- knowing Elsa's preferred attack does not make him strong enough to defeat her;
- knowing likely events does not make them causally fixed;
- knowing a prior-state relationship does not give him mutual intimacy in the surviving state.

The pattern supports **high adaptive potential under feedback**, not omniscience or effortless strategic competence.

### 6.5 Reciprocity, mixed motives, and self-conception

The strongest V01 Subaru thesis is his treatment of remembered debt.

In E2, he deliberately accepts a bad material exchange because he wants to repay the silver-haired girl's help: `俺は損がしてぇんだよ`; `貸し借りはきっちり返す`. (`第二章; p-011.xhtml`) In E4, after the original benefactor no longer remembers helping him, he still states that received obligation does not disappear. (`第五章; p-022.xhtml`)

This is stronger than saying “Subaru likes Emilia.” It identifies a principle that survives the loss of reciprocal memory.

But V01 supplies material counterevidence against pure selflessness:

- he wants to be praised;
- he wants the beautiful girl's attention and smile;
- he frames himself as a protagonist and her as a heroine;
- he enjoys the possibility of a meaningful special role;
- he wants to repair an “empty” self-image;
- he sometimes wants to avoid danger and must talk himself back into action.

The mixed-motive model fits more evidence: **reciprocity and attachment become real enough to impose costs, while self-image, attraction, and narrative self-dramatization remain active motives rather than disappearing.**

### 6.6 Fear, retained suffering, and the refusal to make death cheap

V01 repeatedly shows that remembered deaths have consequences for Subaru's functioning. At the second loot-house approach he has shaking, flashback-like imagery, dry mouth, and ringing ears. In later encounters, remembered violence contributes to freezing and nausea. (`第二章; p-011.xhtml–p-015.xhtml`; `第三章; p-018.xhtml`)

The text itself summarizes his experiential conclusion: all three deaths were, literally, “painful enough to die from,” and he does not want another. (`第四章; p-021.xhtml`)

This is crucial to future revision discipline. If later volumes change his risk behavior, the V01 baseline is **not** “death is free because he returns.” The V01 baseline is “death may provide information, but it remains painful, frightening, uncertain, and potentially non-repeatable.”

### 6.7 Current bounded Subaru model

At the V01 boundary, the best-supported formulation is:

> Subaru is a socially and strategically improvisational seventeen-year-old whose genre literacy, physical training, desire for significance, reciprocity norm, attraction, and fear all compete inside the same decisions. Repetition gives him information, not invulnerability. His most consequential V01 development is learning to use asymmetric information while refusing to let asymmetric memory erase obligations he personally experienced.

This is a V01 model, not a whole-series personality essence.

## 7. Emilia — V01 bounded character model

### 7.1 Identity withholding and the false name “Satella”

In E1 the silver-haired girl gives the name `サテラ` after hesitation. The narration marks distance, and Puck condemns the choice as poor taste. (`第一章; p-007.xhtml`)

E3 supplies the missing social referent: “Satella” is the name of the `嫉妬の魔女`, described as a taboo symbol whose name people avoid. (`第三章; p-018.xhtml`) Bystander reaction and Emilia's own anger support the social taboo independently of Subaru's earlier ignorance.

At the end of the surviving state she identifies herself as `エミリア`, adding `ただのエミリアよ`. (`第五章; p-024.xhtml`)

V01 therefore establishes:

- the E1 name was knowingly loaded/false;
- Emilia is aware of the taboo;
- her true name is Emilia;
- the true name is withheld until the successful state endpoint.

V01 does **not** establish why she chose “Satella.” Distrust, testing, self-protective distancing, self-deprecation, or some other motive remain hypotheses. Do not import a later explanation.

The source's front-matter character presentation visually labels the silver-haired girl `???` while naming several other characters, reinforcing that her identity is intentionally withheld from the V01 reader. That is **paratextual presentation**, not an in-world fact.

### 7.2 Repeated prosocial action under time pressure

Emilia's strongest stable-tendency evidence comes from the lost child.

In E1 she is urgently pursuing a stolen badge but stops to help the child. She frames the decision partly through anticipated regret: recovering the badge while knowingly abandoning the child would leave her unable to feel satisfied. She wants both outcomes rather than accepting the trade-off.

In E4, where Subaru has not recreated the same interaction with her, she independently helps the same child again. The repeated behavior under comparable urgency is much stronger evidence than a single “kind act.”

The bounded inference is that **helping a vulnerable stranger is a robust preference for Emilia even when it imposes a cost on another urgent goal**.

### 7.3 Self-interested rhetoric and possible self-concealment

Emilia repeatedly narrates her help as self-interest. After healing Subaru she says the aid was `私のためだったの`. Later, after healing Rom, she again insists `これも私のための行為よ`. (`第一章; p-007.xhtml`; `第五章; p-024.xhtml`) Puck explicitly says she is not straightforward/honest about this tendency.

A simple “she is secretly pure altruism” reading goes too far. Her own explanation is not wholly false: she does want to avoid regret, recover information, and protect her goals. The stronger model is that **she resists presenting herself as a benefactor and repeatedly translates prosocial behavior into self-interested language, even where the action clearly benefits others at real cost**.

Whether that is modesty, defensiveness, learned social strategy, or something else remains open.

### 7.4 Stigma and self-presentation

Emilia identifies herself as a half-elf and anticipates that the category may matter negatively. Rom remarks that silver hair stands out in a bad way. Later, Rom and Felt react strongly to the combination of silver hair and half-elf identity, while Emilia distances herself from resemblance to the Witch and says she is inconvenienced by it. (`第五章; p-022.xhtml–p-024.xhtml`)

V01 supports a **social stigma** around the resemblance/association. It does not yet supply enough history to explain its origin, institutional extent, or metaphysical basis.

### 7.5 Competence, limits, and dependency

Emilia is a capable spirit user and healer. Puck is a major combat resource, but his manifestation consumes mana and normally follows a daytime schedule he jokingly describes as approximately nine-to-five. (`第一章; p-007.xhtml`) In the final fight Puck's available mana and time run out, and Emilia continues under reduced support.

She is therefore neither helpless nor self-sufficient in every circumstance.

Emilia also says she has a “trump card” with severe consequences, but V01 does not show it. Its nature remains `OPEN`.

During Reinhard's serious combat preparation, Emilia says ambient mana turns away from her and her own capacity drops. (`第五章; p-024.xhtml`) The observed correlation is strong; the exact system mechanism remains incomplete.

### 7.6 Relationship with Subaru at the surviving endpoint

The decisive V01 relationship fact is asymmetry.

Subaru remembers a full prior encounter in E1, her aid, the lost-child episode, joint searching, and multiple attempts to repay or save her. Emilia remembers only E4.

The epilogue makes this explicit. When Reinhard asks what Subaru is to her, Emilia answers `行きずり` and explains that she has no memory of meeting him before the recent encounter. (`エピローグ; p-027.xhtml`)

Therefore V01 does **not** support describing them as mutually developed intimate partners, close friends, or a reciprocal romance. Subaru's side of the relationship has accumulated across discarded states; Emilia's has not.

Her true-name disclosure and gratitude are meaningful within E4, but the relationship remains young and structurally asymmetric.

## 8. Felt — V01 bounded character model

### 8.1 Material position and ambition

Felt is a slum-based thief who treats bargaining, theft, and information as practical survival resources. She distrusts implausibly generous offers and catches Subaru when his knowledge exceeds what he can plausibly explain.

She also rejects passive acceptance of the slum as a permanent life. Her statement `アタシはこんな路地裏で一生を終わらせる気なんてさらさらねー` identifies strong upward/escape ambition. (`第四章; p-021.xhtml`)

The text places that ambition inside material precarity. It does not require the reader to excuse theft, but it makes a purely trait-based “Felt is greedy because she is selfish” model inadequate.

### 8.2 Transactional surface, non-transactional attachments

Felt's speech and bargaining are bluntly transactional. She prices risk, challenges asymmetrical information, and wants to maximize the value of the stolen badge.

Her behavior toward Rom and endangered strangers complicates a flat selfishness model.

Rom has cared for her since she was very young and behaves as a family-like protector. Felt's choices show that bond matters beyond money. In E2 she physically saves Subaru from Elsa's first lethal line and later dies in the fight. In E4, when Subaru tells her to escape, she does not simply secure her own survival; she seeks help and brings Reinhard.

The repeated evidence supports **strong attachment and situational prosocial risk-taking beneath a hard transactional presentation**.

### 8.3 Cross-state debt owed by only one side

Felt's E2 rescue creates a clean example of asymmetric moral continuity. In E4, Felt does not remember saving Subaru. Subaru does.

His later insistence that he owes her is therefore not repayment inside a mutually remembered exchange. It is repayment according to the memory-holder's own moral ledger.

This pattern parallels his obligation to Emilia and may become important if later volumes test whether Subaru distinguishes remembered debt from current consent or current relationship claims.

### 8.4 Epilogue significance remains open

When Reinhard sees Felt with Emilia's badge and asks about her name, family, and age, his behavior changes sharply. He takes custody of her over objection and treats ignoring the situation as a greater wrong than the theft issue. (`エピローグ; p-027.xhtml`)

V01 clearly signals **hidden significance**. It does not explain it. Any specific lineage, political role, or institutional conclusion would exceed the V01 horizon.

## 9. Rom — V01 bounded character model

Rom operates the loot house as a fence/stolen-goods intermediary inside an informal economy. That role does not make him normless.

He:

- evaluates Subaru's cellphone and warns that its value may exceed the badge;
- treats Felt's bargaining rights as real rather than simply selling her out;
- provides hospitality and information;
- acts as Felt's protector;
- fights Elsa at lethal risk.

V01 therefore supports an informal criminal institution with internal norms of trust, valuation, and relationship obligation rather than a simple “lawless slum” abstraction.

Elsa calls Rom a member of the `巨人族` during combat. His extraordinary size/strength are directly observed; the taxonomic label is a character assertion that V01 does not independently systematize.

## 10. Reinhard — V01 bounded character model

Reinhard first enters E4 because Subaru has learned to call for a `衛兵`. The alley attackers identify him as `剣聖`, and his combat ability later strongly supports that he occupies an exceptional martial position. He also describes his family/title as unusually weighty.

### 10.1 Power is overwhelming but not context-free

Reinhard can dominate Elsa in direct combat. His full attack has enormous environmental effect. Yet V01 also gives constraints/conditions around that display:

- he says his own sword cannot be drawn for the present opponent, so he uses another weapon;
- Emilia says his serious combat preparation affects ambient mana and therefore her own ability to operate;
- Elsa survives the confrontation and escapes.

The bounded conclusion is **exceptional combat capacity with incomplete mechanics**, not unlimited omnipotence.

### 10.2 Institutional discretion

Reinhard's behavior toward the alley attackers and the slum shows discretion rather than mechanical enforcement. He recognizes why ordinary people may avoid dangerous intervention and later treats the badge theft as a matter where complainant/evidence conditions matter.

In the epilogue that discretion changes when Felt and the badge trigger a higher-order concern. His decision to take Felt is clearly significant but unexplained. V01 therefore introduces a tension between ordinary law-enforcement discretion and another institutional duty that is not yet legible to the reader.

## 11. Elsa — V01 bounded character and threat model

Elsa is a hired killer/mercenary associated with the name `腸狩り`, which Reinhard recognizes as dangerous. Her own speech and actions show a fixation on abdominal injury and the sensory experience of killing. This artifact records the behavior without assigning a diagnosis.

### 11.1 Employer objective

In E2, Elsa has a finite payment pool from an employer and says the employer does not require the badge to be physically in hand. (`第二章; p-015.xhtml`) When Subaru intends to return the badge to its owner, negotiation gives way to violence.

**CORROBORATED INFERENCE:** the employer's operational goal may be satisfied by preventing the original owner from retaining/recovering the badge rather than by simple possession.

**OPEN:** employer identity, ultimate purpose, political affiliation, and whether Elsa knows the complete objective.

### 11.2 Threat characteristics

V01 demonstrates:

- extreme close-combat skill;
- multiple concealed weapons;
- a cloak/tool capable of defeating at least one magical attack and described as single-use in context;
- fast adaptation in confined space;
- willingness to kill witnesses when the transaction fails;
- survival of Reinhard's overwhelming attack;
- a final surprise attempt after appearing defeated.

Subaru's repeated abdominal-strike observation is actionable but not enough to make her predictable in general.

## 13. Institutions, status, economy, and practical agency

### 13.1 Royal capital and social scale

Emilia identifies the city as the royal capital of Lugunica and estimates roughly three hundred thousand residents. (`第一章; p-009.xhtml`) The number is a character-supplied estimate, not a census independently verified by V01.

The city visibly contains humans and nonhuman/demihuman people, formal guards/knights, commercial streets, and an economically marginalized slum.

### 13.2 Currency and informal valuation

V01 introduces copper/silver/gold currencies and `聖金貨`. Rom supplies relative-value information and uses his fencing experience to appraise Subaru's cellphone as a `ミーティア`-like object. (`第二章; p-011.xhtml–p-015.xhtml`)

The exact economy is not reconstructed here. What matters analytically is that value is relational and information-sensitive: Subaru's apparently useless Earth object becomes an extraordinary bargaining asset because local observers cannot reproduce its function.

### 13.3 Slum constraints

The slum is materially poorer, dangerous, and partially governed by informal networks. Subaru discovers that visible presentation affects whether residents cooperate with him. Felt's theft work and Rom's fencing business occur inside that structure.

V01 does not justify romanticizing the slum as a community outside law or condemning every resident through Felt/Rom's criminal activity. The stronger institutional claim is that **formal law, practical enforcement, material alternatives, and informal trust are not identical things**.

### 13.4 Badge and higher-order political significance

The badge bears a dragon design. The epilogue identifies that dragon as emblematic of `親竜王国ルグニカ`; its red jewel reacts brightly when returned to Emilia. Reinhard addresses her as `エミリア様` and internally/through narration confirms she is `選ばれている`. (`エピローグ; p-027.xhtml`)

This is enough to establish that the badge and Emilia's status have political/institutional significance.

It is **not** enough to specify:

- the exact selection process;
- the office or title at stake;
- the role of the dragon in constitutional order;
- why Felt triggers Reinhard's concern;
- whether badge reaction is legal proof, magical authentication, or something else.

All remain open for later source evidence.

## 14. Mechanics and world-model claims

### 14.1 Death-triggered restoration pattern

**Observed regularity:** Subaru dies three times and after each death reappears at the same local checkpoint-like situation near the fruit seller. His wounds and carried-state changes are restored; his memory persists.

**Observed information asymmetry:** people reencountered after restoration do not behave as though they remember prior-state interactions.

**Observed branch mutability:** intervention changes timing, interactions, survival, and causal outcomes.

**Subaru's model:** `死に戻り`, time reversion, looping, game-like save behavior.

**OPEN:** actual mechanism, provider, metaphysics, checkpoint rule, limits, universality, and causal ontology.

The repository therefore adopts `death-triggered restoration pattern` as a descriptive phrase when mechanism-neutral wording is needed, while preserving `死に戻り` as Subaru's own V01 label.

### 14.2 Spirits, mana, and magic use

Puck identifies himself as a spirit and says manifestation consumes `マナ`; he normally returns to a crystal vessel at night and describes an approximate nine-to-five active window. (`第一章; p-007.xhtml`)

Emilia uses spirit arts and healing. Rom describes `ミーティア` as a class of tools that can allow magic-like functions for people whose `ゲート` is not open. (`第二章; p-011.xhtml`) His classification of the cellphone is function-based and may be wrong in ontological terms; the conversation is still valuable evidence for local conceptual categories.

Puck tells Emilia she can call him by spending `オド` in an emergency. (`第一章; p-009.xhtml`; `第五章; p-022.xhtml`) V01 does not define `オド` sufficiently for a full system model.

### 14.3 Reinhard and ambient mana

Emilia says that when Reinhard truly prepares to fight, ambient mana turns away from her, reducing her ability to operate. (`第五章; p-024.xhtml`) The battle gives the statement immediate operational relevance.

The V01-safe state is:

- observed: Emilia's mana access changes around Reinhard's serious attack preparation;
- explained by Emilia: ambient mana favors/turns toward Reinhard rather than her;
- open: deeper mechanism, scope, whether this is unique to him, and what formal system rule governs it.

### 14.4 Felt's `風の加護`

Observed: Felt moves with extraordinary speed/agility.

Elsa's explanation: `風の加護`; “loved by the world.” (`第二章; p-015.xhtml`)

Status: **character explanation, not yet independently corroborated system fact**.

## 16. Japanese wording and register notes

This is not a full sociolinguistic audit. The following phrases materially affect interpretation.

### `死に戻り`

Subaru coins/uses `死に戻り` as his explanatory label after repeated deaths. (`第四章; p-021.xhtml`) The compact Japanese term can tempt later readers to treat a named ability as already objectively defined. V01 supports the **name as Subaru's model**, not a complete narrator-certified mechanism.

### `俺は損がしてぇんだよ`

Literally foregrounds wanting to take a loss. (`第二章; p-011.xhtml`) In context Subaru is not confused about value; he is asserting that repayment can rationally justify an economically losing exchange. This is central to the reciprocity model.

### `貸し借りはきっちり返す`

The language of loans/debts makes reciprocity explicit rather than leaving his motive at generic affection. (`第二章; p-011.xhtml`)

### `受けた恩義は消えてなくならない`

The important subject is **received obligation/grace (`恩義`)**, not whether the benefactor remembers giving it. (`第五章; p-022.xhtml`) The line directly supports one-sided moral continuity across reset histories.

### `私のためだったの` / `これも私のための行為よ`

Emilia repeatedly reframes helping others as action “for myself.” (`第一章; p-007.xhtml`; `第五章; p-024.xhtml`) The recurrence matters more than any one translation choice. Her rhetoric makes self-interest and care coexist rather than giving the reader a simple altruist label.

### `嫉妬の魔女` / `禁忌の象徴`

These phrases establish the social taboo attached to “Satella” in V01. (`第三章; p-018.xhtml`) They do not yet establish the full history or metaphysical truth about the Witch.

### `ただのエミリアよ`

Emilia's true-name disclosure strips the identification down to “just Emilia.” (`第五章; p-024.xhtml`) V01 supports reading this as a deliberate contrast with the loaded false name; larger identity symbolism remains open.

### `行きずり`

In the epilogue Emilia describes Subaru as essentially a passing/accidental acquaintance and explains that she only remembers meeting him very recently. (`p-027.xhtml`) This is a critical anti-hindsight term: whatever Subaru feels, the surviving mutual relationship has not accumulated the discarded states.

### Register tendencies

- Subaru's speech is dense with game/anime/meta slang and exaggerated self-performance.
- Felt's `アタシ`, contractions, and blunt bargaining style distinguish her rougher informal voice.
- Rom uses elder/dialectal speech patterns.
- Emilia is comparatively careful and often defensive in how she frames benevolent action.

These are V01 observations. A durable speech profile requires longitudinal evidence.

## 18. Counterevidence and rival readings

### Rival reading A: “Subaru is simply heroic/altruistic.”

**Support:** he repeatedly accepts severe cost for other people, honors debts they do not remember, and returns to danger after recognizing an escape route.

**Counterevidence:** he openly wants praise, attraction/heroine payoff, self-image repair, and special narrative significance. He sometimes wants to flee and must overcome that desire. His motives are mixed.

**V01 disposition:** **REJECT the pure-trait version; preserve costly prosocial commitment as a major tendency.**

### Rival reading B: “Subaru is a coward.”

**Support:** knives break his bravado; he shakes, freezes, vomits/feels nausea, and wants to avoid the loot house and death.

**Counterevidence:** fear does not stop repeated reentry into danger; he can act while frightened; caution sometimes prevents worse strategic errors.

**V01 disposition:** **REJECT cowardice as a sufficient model.** Fear sensitivity and inexperience coexist with value-driven action.

### Rival reading C: “The loop is fixed fate.”

**Support:** several encounters/events recur; Subaru himself wonders whether some events are compulsory.

**Counterevidence:** intervention changes escape routes, meeting timing, coalition composition, and who survives. Recurrence can often be explained by stable starting incentives.

**V01 disposition:** **DOWNGRADE metaphysical-fate interpretation.** Branch mutability is directly demonstrated.

### Rival reading D: “Emilia is purely selfless.”

**Support:** repeated aid to strangers and healing at cost to her own urgent goals.

**Counterevidence:** she explicitly frames action in terms of her own regret, information needs, and objectives. Those explanations need not be false just because they coexist with kindness.

**V01 disposition:** **REVISE to prosocial action with self-interested framing and mixed practical motives.**

### Rival reading E: “Felt is simply selfish/criminal.”

**Support:** theft, aggressive bargaining, profit orientation, disdain for passive slum acceptance.

**Counterevidence:** strong Rom attachment, rescue of Subaru in E2, decision to seek help in E4.

**V01 disposition:** **REJECT as sufficient.** Material precarity, ambition, attachment, and prosocial risk-taking all matter.

### Rival reading F: “Subaru and Emilia are already mutually close by the end of V01.”

**Support:** Subaru has accumulated strong attachment and Emilia gives her true name with gratitude.

**Counterevidence:** Emilia explicitly says `行きずり` and has no memory of the discarded-state history.

**V01 disposition:** **REJECT mutual-accumulation claim.** Relationship history is sharply asymmetric.

### Rival reading G: “Death makes Subaru strategically stronger because retries are cheap.”

**Support:** each death yields information that can improve later action.

**Counterevidence:** deaths remain excruciating; retained trauma impairs him; he doubts retries are unlimited; he explicitly refuses to throw away a life for information.

**V01 disposition:** **REVISE.** Retained information is useful precisely under a condition of costly, uncertain repetition.
