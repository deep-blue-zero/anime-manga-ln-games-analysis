## 3. Event-state and narrative form

`V04-E1` is a volume-local episode label on the continuing `V03-E1` world-line, not a new restored branch. Approximately a month has passed since Subaru's arrival in the world; subjective elapsed time is longer. The village/capital interval is not dated precisely enough to manufacture a daily chronology.

The prologue presents Subaru being beaten without its full institutional context. Repeated rising initially resembles the persistence that made previous rescues possible; the narrator immediately withholds the assumption that his anger is justified (`p-002#B0026`). Chapter 5 returns to the same confrontation and denies that repetition is generating a relationship or meaningful progress (`p-022#B0203–0205`). The recurrence is formal, not supernatural. The reader must revise an apparent heroic-persistence script without calling the earlier rescued lives unreal.

V04 also repeatedly separates **how an arrival occurred** from **why a person chose to be there**. Priscilla asks Subaru's reason for riding with her (`p-011#B0202–0228`); Emilia asks why he came to the castle, not the route he used (`p-013#B0140–0148`). Subaru is better at narrating circumstances than answering the relational question. At the endpoint, tactical political chatter again attempts to displace a conversation about trust (`p-024#B0019–0033`).

Focalization ceases to follow Subaru exclusively. Ferris/Wilhelm's carriage conversation reveals a perceptive but incomplete reading of his eyes. After he leaves the assembly, Reinhard's interiority and Felt/Rom's exchange remain available to the reader. The epilogue reveals motives and orders that Subaru does not receive. His absence is an information boundary and a structural rebuke to the assumption that important action stops when he cannot see it.

## 4. Ordinary usefulness is real, not a failed version of heroism

Radio exercises, potato attendance stamps, Puck designs, mayonnaise, village visits and small crafts have made Subaru valued. He regrets being regarded as an inventive knowledgeable person rather than simply a great rescuer (`p-003#B0033–0046`). That complaint exposes a hierarchy in his own recognition needs; it does not establish that the village is ungrateful. Emilia explicitly sees his effort. She also acknowledges that she previously drew a social line herself and now enjoys contact with villagers (`B0025–0030`, `B0047–0055`). Both have ordinary agency beyond emergency roles.

The promised village date has occurred: Emilia recalls handholding on it (`p-006#B0014`). This is mutually remembered continuity, not another erased promise. But she denies that she had already been in love, and their different interpretations of his flirting remain visible (`p-003#B0034`, `B0057–0059`). Warmth and shared time are established; reciprocal romance is not.

Subaru's wage-funded return to Kadomon fulfills a modest promise. The merchant cautions him about exchange rates and exploitative sellers, then adds a gift fruit (`p-008#B0024–0036`, `B0062–0067`). A workable debt ends in renewed goodwill rather than ownership. The fruit subsequently connects Subaru to Priscilla, but that contingent benefit does not prove a cosmic reward system.

Nor is Subaru uniformly tactless or incapable of inference. He notices Wilhelm identifying Emilia despite the recognition-blocking cloak (`p-003#B0184–0193`), asks a sensible question about separating royal and ritual offices (`p-015#B0055–0062`), and redirects conversation when Emilia wants to avoid Priscilla's attention (`p-010#B0303–0308`). These counterexamples prevent his later humiliation from becoming a timeless intelligence deficit. His judgment worsens particularly when inclusion, status and romantic worth are threatened.

## 5. Care, secrecy and the conversion of promise into entitlement

### 5.1 Political exclusion and medical inclusion

Emilia wants a voice in decisions about herself (`p-003#B0104`), yet repeatedly limits Subaru's participation. Those limits combine political standing, concern for his body and an increasingly justified fear of reckless behavior. They are not all reducible to arbitrary rejection. She has already negotiated access to Ferris's exceptional healing; Roswaal reveals the effort she wanted kept quiet (`p-005#B0077–0096`). Medical travel is not permission to join the selection.

The protective pattern nevertheless deserves scrutiny on both sides. Emilia's supervision and later treatment plan restrict Subaru's choice, and the source explicitly registers that he feels excluded from deciding his future (`p-024#B0074–0084`). Care can be genuine and paternalistic at once. The asymmetry of the immediate conflict remains: he knowingly breaks her requested waiting and no-magic conditions, while she continues arranging treatment rather than using injury to demand service.

### 5.2 Death as private utility

Subaru's fear of another threat is grounded in real experience. The badge attack and estate disaster make anticipation understandable. But no attack at this particular ceremony has been established. He admits the secure castle is not the core issue: he cannot bear to be uninvolved (`p-011#B0070–0078`). His self-authored purpose becomes helping Emilia, with his unique usefulness located where only restoration could undo catastrophe. The narrator explicitly calls this an unrecognized distortion that includes his own death in the calculation (`B0079–0080`).

The desire for a nonpainful special gift also persists. Learning about blessings does not remove his wish for an advantageous otherworld role (`p-005#B0184–0187`). V04 does not say he wants disasters to happen; it shows that his account of personal value has become dependent on being indispensable when they do.

### 5.3 Consenting counterpart versus self-appointed representative

Emilia asks Subaru to let her trust him. He responds affirmatively without understanding her anxiety, then engineers his escape (`p-011#B0029–0046`). He avoids the route that would let her refuse him remotely and expects his dangerous arrival to pressure her into keeping him (`B0114–0116`). This is agency bypass, not merely inadequate etiquette.

At the assembly, Emilia has just defended her own claim to fair treatment. When asked what Subaru is to her, she tries to answer; he ignores her request to wait and declares himself her first knight (`p-021#B0079–0097`). Copying the gesture of sworn service does not supply the missing appointment. His sincere wish to be foremost in her life is not her consent to a public office.

Julius's critique has separable components. Training, effective protection and the harm to Emilia are pertinent to the role claim. His claim that birth fixes a person's proper reach, and his hereditary qualification doctrine, are class-bound arguments, not narrator-certified moral law (`B0134–0149`). Subaru is right that birth is not something an individual can change. That does not authorize self-appointment or erase his subsequent demeaning of others' labor. Julius's final question about the face Subaru is causing Emilia to make attacks the contradiction between declared care and present effect (`B0168`).

### 5.4 A real information barrier does not explain every silence

In the earlier walk, Subaru knows his feeling but cannot confess because resolve and courage fail; no supernatural penalty appears (`p-008#B0099–0108`). Puck's telepathy allows private conversation, not demonstrated unrestricted knowledge of Subaru's entire memory. Wilhelm reads repeated exposure to death from Subaru's eyes, but does not learn the restoration mechanism (`p-005#B0029–0042`). Perception is not disclosure.

The later bedside impasse is more severe. Subaru invokes Emilia's initial rescue as the root of his obligation. She correctly identifies **her** first meeting as the loot house, not the alley rescue retained from an overwritten state (`p-024#B0170–0188`). Her inability to recognize that memory is not ingratitude. Nor is his memory fictitious merely because it is not mutual.

Attempting to explain that prior contact produces a stopped-time warning (`B0189–0197`). No heart squeeze, embodied hand or actual pain occurs this time. The restriction therefore remains semantically responsive but variably enforced; it is not a deterministic ban on a particular spoken label. The later recital of bodily ordeals does not provide a clean experimental threshold either. Intent, wording and what the hearer could infer are not controlled independently. No new odor measurement is supplied.

The reader consequently knows why a fully candid explanation is unavailable. But the same scene also shows Subaru expecting Emilia to infer everything without being told, refusing her attempts to understand, and avoiding her face. External censorship and self-protective evasion coexist; neither should erase the other.

### 5.5 Debt rupture without retrospective cancellation of care

Emilia's anger is a challenge to Subaru's model of her as endlessly soothing and maternal. He struggles to recognize that her distress and refusal also belong to the person he loves (`p-024#B0111–0118`). She distinguishes requested help from actions she never asked for, and trust from an exemption from promises (`B0119–0157`). Her desire to understand persists into the question about why he helps at all.

When that attempt fails, Subaru lists actual sacrifices and turns their causal importance into exclusive ownership of every good result. The claim that nobody would have survived without him suppresses the contributions the prior readings established. More decisively, he calls Emilia's debt unrepayable (`B0223–0235`). The narration marks this as betrayal of his root feeling, not revelation that every previous rescue was a calculated purchase.

Emilia accepts the transactional frame only to propose repaying and ending it. Her move from familiar address to his full name registers distance (`B0242–0250`). This is a real active-world rupture, not a declaration that no bond ever existed or that reconciliation is impossible forever. She has still arranged care with Ferris, Crusch's household and Rem. Subaru's final solitude is an experiential and formal judgment; it is not evidence that literally nobody will help him.

Her public request for fair consideration and private hope to be treated as an ordinary girl share a concern: being seen as herself. His insistence that she is uniquely special has romantic meaning different from racial stigma, but still fails to answer that hope (`B0258–0266`). Do not equate affection with persecution; do not assume flattering idealization necessarily grants subjecthood.

## 6. Rem, Ram, Beatrice and Roswaal after the estate checkpoint

Rem's trust persists in routine. She enjoys praise, volunteers companionship and treasures Subaru's imperfect written thanks. She can also insist that Emilia is right about outsiders at the ceremony (`p-011#B0008–0012`). Her behavior is not unconditionally undifferentiated submission. Yet she contrives an absence that lets him leave and wishes he had invited her (`B0084–0100`). The shift from Ram-centered penance toward Subaru-centered attachment remains a dependency risk rather than a demonstrated cure.

There is an independent political cause: Roswaal has instructed Rem not to obstruct Subaru, regardless of Emilia's wishes (`B0101–0109`). Rem and the reader receive this order; Subaru is not shown receiving it. The enabling cannot be assigned solely to her romantic investment. Ram's warning to keep Rem from overdoing things and the reversal of their usual travel roles broaden the sisters' ordinary configuration, but do not explain Ram's loyalty history or remove the mana dependence established in V03 (`p-005#B0129–0144`).

Beatrice's discreet farewell sustains the contrast between abrasive presentation and care; Subaru himself qualifies the loneliness he thinks he sees as possibly wishful (`p-005#B0147–0154`). It supplies no new contract rule. Her absence from the capital is not abandonment evidence.

Roswaal's sponsorship is active and controlling. His hidden crest claims Subaru as an employee when Priscilla playfully claims him; he lets Subaru stay despite Emilia's objection. His private permission to Rem and prior gate instructions confirm engineered opportunity. But his surprise that Priscilla brought Subaru from outside the castle is a negative control against total foresight (`p-013#B0114–0139`; `p-026#B0061–0067`). The endgame of those orders is not disclosed. V03's private Dragon-killing aim remains current without becoming a secret everyone knows.

## 7. Political legitimacy, practical power and five unlike camps

### 7.1 Qualification does not settle the political question

Marcos and Miklotov explain a centuries-old covenant between Farsale Lugunica and Volcanica. The Dragon History Stone's directive and the extinction of the royal family frame the search for five qualified maidens (`p-013#B0209–0231`; `p-015#B0048–0067`). Jewel luminescence is observed. The history of divine rescues and the interpretation of the stone are institutional accounts. The source has not independently shown every historical miracle or proved that obeying the stone must yield a good result. Reinhard acknowledges prior dispute about fusing ritual and sovereign office.

The ceremony itself supplies incompatible but operative standards: magical qualification, possible hereditary continuity, class education, institutional authorization, popular acceptance, material power, and personal willingness. Felt's qualifying jewel does not tell her to consent. Emilia's qualification does not eliminate prejudice. Candidate affiliation does not automatically make Al a royal-guard knight in Julius's doctrine.

After the speeches and Felt's acceptance, the Council declares a three-year interval ending one month before the covenant-renewal rite, five named candidates, selection through the nation's collective will together with jewel brilliance and dragon guidance, and an obligation to sustain the kingdom within each candidate's reach (`p-021#B0431–0455`). This is not a fully specified modern ballot system. Subaru is absent when the declaration occurs; Reinhard and Ferris subsequently give him an account (`p-022#B0003–0026`).

### 7.2 Emilia: force for the possibility of fair judgment

Roswaal first proposes reducing Emilia to a disposable fifth candidate and stages a threat that permits Puck's intervention. Miklotov detects the performance; Puck and Roswaal concede it (`p-019#B0304–0366`; `p-021#B0017–0034`). The apparent assassination attempt cannot remain an uncomplicated finding of literal murder intent after that disclosure. The power involved is nevertheless real.

Emilia names her Elior forest background and contract with Puck, takes up the local label `氷結の魔女`, and openly acknowledges a coercive demand. She demands fair consideration, not an automatic crown, and refuses to use the spirit to seize it (`p-021#B0035–0059`). Her self-assessed inexperience does not mean she has no platform. Bordeaux apologizes for his insult while explicitly warning that public prejudice remains (`B0063–0072`). The speech wins a local acknowledgment, not a national transformation.

Puck's `終焉の獣` identification and association with the four great spirits expand his public status. His support includes a threat of overwhelming violence constrained by Emilia's wishes. The gentle everyday companion and dangerous political capacity coexist. No later form, disaster or undisclosed contract clause is imported.

### 7.3 Crusch: inherited standing against inherited dependence

Crusch initially makes and repairs a public mistake about the meeting being a banquet (`p-015#B0011–0025`). Her authority does not require infallibility. Her defense of Ferris's freely chosen clothing also links dignity to an individual's suitable self-presentation rather than conformity (`p-017#B0269`; `p-019#B0001–0008`).

The presumed continuity candidate instead proposes ending reliance on the dragon. She acknowledges benefiting from the covenant, criticizes dependence and weakness in unpredicted crises, and says king, retainers and people must bear what they have outsourced (`p-019#B0019–0051`). This is a political independence platform, not evidence that she shares Roswaal's private aim to kill the Dragon. Its practical viability and costs remain untested. Ferris's formal affirmation, followed by familiar affection, shows an authorized advocate who need not repeat every argument.

### 7.4 Priscilla: favored rule, prosperity and coercion

Priscilla's rescue encounter refuses Subaru's assumed damsel-and-gratitude script. She can defend her own body, split fruit and choose how to classify a bet. Her acceptance of his invented rock-paper-scissors hand is discretionary, not proof that he has discovered a repeatable way to defeat fate (`p-008#B0313–0371`; `p-010#B0003–0053`).

She reports inheritance from her late nominal husband Leip Barielle. The hostile epithet `血色の花嫁` does not establish that she murdered him (`p-019#B0081–0101`). Marcos independently confirms prosperity under her administration; Al attributes success to intuition. This supports an effective territorial record, not the universal claim that the world cannot disadvantage her (`B0154–0157`). Her political offer is reward for those who obey and exclusion for those who do not.

Al is chosen for Priscilla's interest, not because he won her tournament; he says he reached the final four (`B0131–0145`). He can restrain her, admit inability to defeat Reinhard and create face-saving exits. He is not simply an obedient echo, though he also enables and threatens on her behalf. Priscilla's amusement, the fruit gift and Al's request all figure in admitting Subaru; one exclusive secret motive is not established.

### 7.5 Anastasia: acquisitiveness framed as sustained stewardship

Anastasia identifies a lowest-stratum Kararagi origin, commercial ascent and adoption of the admired founder Hoshin's name, not genealogical descent (`p-019#B0171–0194`). Her desire grows with access: she wants a country and promises that what becomes hers will not be discarded. Julius interprets this as ambition plus attachment, tempered by business judgment, and connects her skill to the kingdom's fiscal crisis (`B0213–0255`). The alignment of self-interest with public prosperity is an explicit program, not an already demonstrated national result.

Her soft diction and relaxed presentation coexist with cutting exclusion of Emilia and dismissiveness about Subaru's safety. Gentleness of register cannot be used as a proxy for benevolence. Conversely, her frank ambition is not by itself proof of predatory bad governance.

### 7.6 Felt, Rom and Reinhard: consent gained inside coercive conditions

Felt's new dress does not transform her into a compliant noble. She calls Reinhard's treatment confinement, says her original clothes were hidden and refuses the crown; he invokes national prosperity (`p-017#B0017–0027`, `B0160–0164`). His kindness and rescue ability do not erase coercive state service.

The white jewel response establishes qualification. Gold hair, red eyes and the reported abduction of Ford's daughter fourteen years earlier produce a **possible** royal identity. Reinhard explicitly lacks confirmation; the terminal roster itself says unconfirmed (`B0123–0155`; `p-021#B0446`). Do not convert a highly suggestive hypothesis into a name, age or proven ancestry.

Rom's capture reveals the practical relation between role and force. Marcos refuses a command from a candidate who has declined candidacy. Rom then stages contemptible begging so Felt will reject him and escape; Reinhard recognizes the protective strategy. Felt sees through it, refuses to abandon family, accepts participation and commands Reinhard to cut the shackles (`p-021#B0277–0386`). The first mutually enacted knight relationship occurs at this command and acceptance, not just at his earlier declaration.

Her choice has agency without being uncoerced: the institution has placed a loved one's release inside acceptance of the role. Her platform of dismantling nobility, knighthood and the kingdom is met by Reinhard's pledge to support reconstruction too (`B0401–0427`). It is not established as indiscriminate extermination. The counterpoint to Subaru is precise: Felt obtains a retainer's action by accepting and defining a relation; Subaru declared a relation while bypassing its supposed principal.

## 8. The duel and the epilogue: revision without absolution

Subaru's tactical trick with the wooden swords and sand fails immediately. The gulf is real; improvised cunning that helped in the forest is not automatically portable to a trained knight at arm's length (`p-022#B0115–0133`). His anger narrows the situation to one blow against Julius. Even Emilia's audible call does not stop his use of Shamac, which surprises but does not defeat Julius (`B0236–0288`). Julius's sweeping claim that no guard knight could be affected remains broader than the single demonstrated encounter.

The narrator calls the event a public lynching. The crowd moves from cheers to discomfort and horror; Ferris retains the authority to stop it but does not. The account of spectators staying from responsibility does not make spectatorship equivalent to effective restraint (`B0217–0230`). Reinhard had objected and urged correction by instruction rather than violence (`B0080–0085`). The guard is not one identical mind.

Other candidates invoke procedure, personal confidence or amusement against Emilia's intervention. Felt notices that the messenger's alarm needs explanation. Emilia eventually runs despite the pressure (`B0136–0192`). Her public rejection of unauthorized service has not removed concern for Subaru's life; she calls him a friend.

The epilogue revises the apparent motive. Julius preempted younger knights who might have killed Subaru for insulting their status. Ferris and Marcos understand the calculation, and Julius acknowledges choosing the task he could perform while leaving Ferris able to treat Subaru (`p-026#B0029–0043`). Protective intention is supported, not merely a flattering reader theory. Personal irritation is also admitted (`B0097–0102`).

This is not complete exoneration. Marcos calls the conduct unknightly and excessive; five days' suspension, exclusion from barracks/castle and temporary surrender of the sword follow (`B0007–0015`, `B0048–0058`). The source does not test that brutal public punishment was the only possible prevention of retaliation. A system in which status insult plausibly produces lethal reprisal is itself part of the evidence. Treatment repairs bones and teeth, not necessarily the mind, gate or relationship. Subaru does not receive the protective rationale here.

Julius later values Emilia's costly generosity and includes Subaru in his wish that people flourish. Friendship with Ferris survives open competition for different candidates (`B0078–0113`). Rivalry, care and institutional coercion coexist. A simple kind-knight/cruel-knight reversal would lose the point of the added perspective.

6. **Roswaal as omniscient controller:** multiple covert orders support planning; surprise about Priscilla and Marcos's unresolved inquiry bound the finding. Dragon-target, scope of knowledge and full intended outcome remain testable unknowns.
7. **All authority is mere birth privilege:** birth arguments operate, but skill, money, territorial performance, spirit force, magical qualification, family choice and formal acceptance also change outcomes. No single criterion explains all five camps.
8. **New magical failure proves permanent incapacity:** Shamac fails against Julius; prior success remains historical. Gate worsening and future safety need a specialist assessment in narrative, not promotion of V04's V05 advertisement.
