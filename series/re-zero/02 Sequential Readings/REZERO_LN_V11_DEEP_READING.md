## 0. Governing claim and evidence boundary

V11 makes the desire to save everyone depend on whether Subaru can recognize people as more than the parts he has assigned them. His earlier family-trial answer remains meaningful, but does not make him consistently receptive, truthful or safe. He interrupts Emilia's suffering and comforts her, yet presses to replace her, permits the discussion to be ended through sedation, and departs before she can answer him. He respects Ram's request for private time with Rem, yet expects their recovered sisterhood to produce the response he wants. He accepts that Frederica's suspicious behavior requires another explanation, yet repeatedly turns a working inference into certainty. He can receive Otto's friendship only after discovering that his account of rational advantage has omitted a real friend's reason to help. These are changes in particular judgments, not a single lesson either wholly learned or wholly lost.

The new returns intensify that problem. Bodies and material arrangements can be restored while fear, guilt and remembered attachment accumulate only for Subaru. His rescuers' voluntary risks are genuine, but survival at their expense becomes intolerable to him. He begins treating an ending as something that must be erased and his own life as its inexpensive price. Captivity then exposes a further danger: wanting to die may cease to be only a strategy for another attempt. Restored limbs after the rabbit attack do not restore his ability to inhabit the present. The prose does not let repeated death stand for immunity to pain or preparation stand for adequate control.

Echidna supplies a profoundly consequential alternative to solitary knowledge: Subaru can say what he has endured and be heard. That relief is real. So are her curiosity, leverage, limits and selective explanations. The successful disclosure occurs in her dream; her theory of effectively unlimited returns is expressly conjectural and conditional. Subaru's new confidence exceeds what has been independently demonstrated. His intention to exploit the power against its apparent provider can sustain rescue, but also deepen the belief that the only permissible cost is himself. His renewed resolve does not prevent an unforeseen waking-world disaster.

Other people remain causal participants rather than a reward for his inner progress. Petra warns the adults to flee, pulls Subaru out of danger, treats his wound, recalls his earlier rescue and insists on helping in return. Frederica and Ram exploit their long familiarity to negotiate a way around an oath, then supply distinct combat and evacuation capacities. Otto refuses a profitable silence, finds Subaru by reasoning about the absence of animal voices, and coordinates assistance that Subaru did not imagine. Villagers choose to guide and defend the person who has helped them. Patrasche acts under bodily and navigational limits, not as a guaranteed vehicle. Their losses cannot be reduced to lessons arranged for Subaru, even when his memory is the principal surviving account.

## 4. Household care: expectation, refusal and specific people

### 4.1 Ram can receive a sister without recovering the past

Ram's unease at a decoy joke recalls a real surviving forest episode involving Rem, not an automatically remembered discarded loop. Subaru interprets the unease as the imperfect repair of history after Rem's erasure (`p-001#B0455–0478`). The observation supports a residual gap; it does not establish an exhaustive erasure algorithm. Subaru then actually tells Ram about Rem on the road. At the bedside Ram recognizes their resemblance and kinship but later reports an inner blank where Rem should be (`p-003#B0058–0059/B0208–0214`). Newly received testimony and touching the body are not restored autobiography.

Subaru's response initially respects a boundary. Ram quietly asks to be alone with Rem; he leaves, although he wants the encounter to matter (`p-003#B0092–0099`). He later turns his desire into an obligation: she should protect Rem because she is the sister who loved and was loved. Ram rejects his appropriation of their tragedy through his guilt, and, during the attack, proposes sacrificing Rem and Beatrice for the camp (`p-003#B0225–0232`; `p-005#B0129–0148`). Her hypothetical account of what Rem would consent to is not Rem's current answer. Subaru's hurt does not make either speaker's imagined Rem authoritative.

Ram's emotional blank is not emotional emptiness. Her strained report, refusal to let Subaru own the encounter, familiar exchanges with Frederica, care for injured Roswaal and later intervention against Garfiel disclose distinct attachments and judgments. Her loyalty to Roswaal matters, but she expressly distinguishes his instruction to help Subaru from her own choice to recruit Otto (`p-001#B0360–0366`; `p-003#B0199–0214`; `p-011#B0132–0141/B0424–0438`). Treating every act as a mechanically dictated patron order would miss what she herself differentiates.

### 4.2 Frederica is not vindicated by a friendly face alone

Frederica's patient teaching, patrols and worry are first Petra's account; her astonishment at the transfer is her own observable response. Subaru gradually relinquishes the assumption that she deliberately sent Emilia into a trap. Her actual defense of Petra and fight with Elsa make the simple betrayal account substantially harder to sustain. They still do not disclose the private letter's instruction, the crystal's intended use or the original directing party (`p-003#B0043–0050/B0165–0191/B0238–0249`; `p-005#B0027–0038/B0174–0215`).

Her oath has a history: she dates leaving Sanctuary and entering service to ten years, seven months and thirteen days. She describes the oath as a personal commitment without compulsory force, unlike contract or covenant (`p-003#B0133–0143`). Subaru's demand that invested time be discarded when it obstructs something important fails to secure agreement. Ram's apparent threat of forcible removal instead provides the excuse Frederica accepts. Their coordinated solution depends on familiarity and tacit understanding, not Subaru's solitary mastery of negotiation (`p-003#B0247–0282`). The oath category does not explain every implementation: Echidna has separately altered memory, and that magical act cannot be disproved by a general word for personal commitment.

Frederica's care also has an explicit boundary. Carrying Subaru during escape is emergency assistance, not permission for the intimacy he jokes about; she refuses the peacetime implication and later the imagined bath. Full transformation preserves her voice and reason and closes only some wounds. Her voluntary delaying action does not establish either complete healing or the capacity to defeat Elsa (`p-005#B0073–0076/B0183–0215`).

Subaru's Shamac imposes severe pain and depletion, yet Elsa continues throwing projectiles through the blind interval. Ram corrects his first inference of magic immunity: an effective blind response is not absence of a sensory effect (`p-005#B0056–0070/B0094–0100`). The later new mabeast attack likewise challenges his expectation that the mansion's disaster must always be Elsa alone. Ram identifies the lion-headed creature as ギルティラウ; Petra points out its intact horn. The renewed horn-obedience account does not explain this instance. Subaru and Ram connect it to the vanished girl from the earlier Ulgarm incident, but her present identity, control method and employer are not directly observed (`p-005#B0247–0291`). Elsa's later Meili reference adds a name, not a newly witnessed girl or a complete command chain.

### 4.3 Petra gives help, not an adult romantic contract

Petra's apprenticeship includes clothing, forms of address, work and small mistakes. Her shift from the village nickname for Ram to formal senior-maid address expresses a role she wants to inhabit, not adulthood conferred by self-description. Under threat she tells the adults to escape, grasps Frederica's tail to enable rescue, pulls a frozen Subaru aside, treats his wound, and argues for the people left in the mansion (`p-003#B0021–0029`; `p-005#B0013–0037/B0161–0164/B0253–0256/B0302–0318`). The adults do not own the scene's entire practical intelligence.

She directly rejects Subaru's claim that he only exposes her to danger. Her recollection is of the surviving forest rescue, including what she later heard about his injuries; it does not grant her knowledge of the loops. She wants a turn at helping. Her playful correction from casual assent to maidly politeness gives him a moment of ease without making the surrounding danger unreal (`p-005#B0312–0336`).

The handkerchief is likewise more than a generic lucky item. It is a gift, a promise to return, an actual bandage and a reminder of someone else's concern. Subaru protects that meaning when Echidna asks for it. Echidna later attributes real protective force to the giver's wish and takes a limited, incompletely explained interference as payment; the physical cloth remains when Subaru wakes (`p-005#B0302–0312`; `p-024#B0257–0279`; `p-025#B0011`). Neither that claim nor Petra's affection establishes engagement, adult consent or a universally tested shield.

## 5. Emilia, Sanctuary and the cost of replacing another person's answer

### 5.1 Disclosure can improve while consultation still fails

E014 changes Subaru's conduct: he publicly admits his qualification and completed first trial rather than repeating the old concealment. The new information does not produce immediate consensus. Garfiel opposes his substitution and Ryuzu supports the importance of Emilia's credit. Emilia gives her own refusal: the trial is her responsibility and a present political opportunity, and she does not want Subaru hurt again (`p-001#B0097–0164`). Distress and responsibility can coexist with a choice; her refusal cannot be replaced by Subaru's inference that she secretly wants to stop.

Nor is Garfiel's changed advice necessarily a current betrayal. Subaru remembers a later conversation from the discarded sequence; the Garfiel now addressed has not had it. Subaru notices the discrepancy but initially argues as though the earlier response were a shared commitment. The new hostility later gains scent and attitude explanations, without making every response mechanically predictable.

Ram ends the exchange through the sleeping aroma, without a represented informed agreement from Emilia. Subaru accepts this as useful and later leaves before Emilia wakes, substituting a letter, Otto's care and Garfiel's undertaking for an actual departure discussion (`p-001#B0165–0175/B0407–0446`). His wish not to explain or seek assent is part of the choice, even though warning the mansion is urgent. Publicly disclosing one fact is not the same as making the whole course of action accountable to her.

E015 supplies another route of pressure through Otto's explicitly gathered reports. Roswaal proposes liberation first so a larger search for Subaru can be organized; Emilia's trial attempts resume under that promise. This is not evidence that she knowingly abandons an imprisoned Subaru. Puck's absence and her unavailable past remain unresolved. Echidna's later lack of interest derives from Subaru's observed failed attempts, not a demonstrated immutable future in which Emilia can never pass (`p-008#B0603–0652`; `p-024#B0178–0198`).

### 5.2 The barrier is not everyone's prison in the same way

Ryuzu narrows the earlier mixed-blood explanation to equal human and demi-human blood concentration. Frederica's human mother and mixed father make her an exception to the capture condition, not the discoverer of a route every resident could use. Garfiel confirms that they have different fathers and surnames. His anger at her not returning in ten years supplies his account of abandonment, not the complete explanation of her choice (`p-008#B0253–0279`).

This remains distinct from political confinement. Subaru is bound, gagged and hidden, despite being able to cross the magical boundary. Garfiel says he cannot simply kill him because of the reaction from people attached to him; Subaru is at once perceived threat and political deterrent (`p-008#B0418–0438`). Meanwhile, the apparent outside refuge rests on a false assumption: Garfiel's tiger form pursues across the barrier (`p-013#B0076–0090`). That action disproves the plan's practical limit. It does not, by itself, settle Garfiel's exact ancestry or distinguish every possible exception mechanism.

### 5.3 Residents are not one liberation preference

Food, future livelihoods, older residents' care and continued patronage matter in the release discussion. Otto distinguishes formal Emilia credit from the beneficiaries' support; Garfiel's resentment of Roswaal coexists with practical dependency and even bandaging the disliked man (`p-001#B0205–0252/B0366`). A bargain is not a proof that every inhabitant wants the same future.

The rescue develops that distinction. Otto first fears an explosive reaction to Subaru's detention; the villagers nevertheless learn what happened and choose a coordinated alternative. Their light chain reduces Patrasche's real uncertainty in the night forest, and five poorly armed youths volunteer to buy seconds. Their decision is not adequately described as either passive obedience or an inevitable riot (`p-011#B0442–0477`). The later slaughter does not erase that agency, nor does their freely offered help establish Subaru's right to consume it without grief or disagreement.

## 6. Captivity, friendship and the limits of restored composure

### 6.1 Apparent calm can conceal a closed interpretation

After death14, Subaru first feels relief that the checkpoint has not advanced, then reproaches himself for being glad at a moment when Emilia is suffering. He adopts an ordered care sequence; the narrator notes his unrecognized loss of facial emotion (`p-008#B0015–0048`). Otto asks whether he is all right, not whether he has time. His concern is that Subaru looks too calm. Subaru recasts the warning as confirmation that he can think clearly, despite Otto's explicit distinction between appearing calm and acting calmly (`p-008#B0069–0104`). The offered care is real; its intended meaning is not initially received.

Garfiel later accuses Subaru of the same selective attention he hates in Roswaal. His miasma evidence does not establish Witch Cult membership: Garfiel himself admits that unusually strong miasma can occur in ordinary people, and he identifies the proposed trial substitution and Subaru's attitude as further causes of action (`p-008#B0382–0403/B0456–0458`). Subaru's odor-based model explains part of the changing response, not every motive or a universal linear accumulation law. Neither moral suspicion nor the apparent legibility of a scent makes coercive detention consensual.

### 6.2 Kept alive is not the same as cared for

The three-day captivity is bodily and relational. Blindfolding, binding and enforced silence restrict orientation and speech. The caretaker maintains intake and checks excretion without responding, cleaning his face or giving companionship. Being touched and fed can deepen loneliness rather than relieve it (`p-008#B0528–0551`). Calling the caretaker a will-less doll is initially Subaru's impression, not yet an independently established account of its nature.

His thoughts move beyond strategic recovery of a failed route. The text expressly allows that his wish for death may now be unrelated to Return by Death (`p-008#B0552–0564`). Intrusive recollections and his search for pain to interrupt them do not show invulnerable endurance. An interrupted self-killing, speculative escape through death and the actual death count must remain different records. Garfiel's prevention keeps a politically useful captive alive; Beatrice's earlier physical intervention has different stated and uncertain motives. Neither intervention means Subaru has willingly accepted the present.

### 6.3 A friend is not a failed calculation of advantage

Otto's rescue has costs Subaru has overlooked: pursuit, filth, fatigue and refusal of Garfiel's offer of safety and payment for silence. Subaru initially explains the act as self-preservation or an improbable commercial gamble. The explanation fails because Otto had a safer choice (`p-008#B0653–0706`). Otto then explicitly calls him a friend. The comic mishearing of `友人` as an unfamiliar person's name dramatizes a missing category in Subaru's account, not the arrival of a new character (`p-010#B0001–0015`).

Subaru's laughter first wounds Otto's expectation, but he clarifies that he is laughing at his own foolishness, acknowledges the friendship and thanks him. This is an actual received repair in E015, not an analyst-supplied assumption that all his banter is affectionate or harmless (`p-010#B0017–0038`). It restores enough energy to act without making his head instantly work well or his body cease to be weak. His invented “plan B,” further risk at Roswaal's room, overlooked transport problem and persistent death-first reasoning remain adverse tests.

Otto's ability is neither omniscience nor command. He finds suspicious silence where animal voices should be, negotiates for directions, and warns that offended animals may deceive him. An animal's route can be unusable by a human. Complex political information comes through a human collaborator, not automatically through insects (`p-011#B0009–0024/B0064–0081`). The rescue depends on Ram's judgment, the villagers' coordination and Patrasche's bodily work as well as Otto's gift. Subaru learns that “I have not thought of a reason” is not evidence that another person lacks one.

## 7. Beatrice and Roswaal: who controls an answer

### 7.1 Saving the survivor can be experienced as another loss

Beatrice intercepts the door Subaru expects to lead to Rem, pulls him into the library, and begins repairing his severe injuries. To him, this can preserve the wrong present: he fears a checkpoint advance beyond the people already lost. His accusation that she could easily have rescued everyone is not a controlled test of her reach, timing or willingness. His dread of this outcome becoming fixed is also not evidence that the checkpoint has actually moved (`p-005#B0460–0530`).

He rejects healing and attempts self-killing; Beatrice physically stops and disarms him. The narrator distinguishes this third choice of suicide from the earlier mansion decision and the attempted recovery of Rem. The attempt here is aborted. Neither the value Subaru assigns to a timely death nor his belief that assistance has ruined everything is an endorsed verdict on the act of keeping him alive (`p-005#B0534–0563`).

The black book found among the broken ladder's pieces reclassifies the scene for Subaru. It is not the Petelgeuse copy left in the Sanctuary wagon. Beatrice consults it and claims that all her former actions, including their ordinary exchanges and help, followed its instructions. She names her mother's connection as her purpose and rejects Subaru in tears (`p-005#B0569–0588`; `p-007#B0001–0028`). Her words are actual evidence of what she asserts. They do not give the reader the book's contents or independently establish that every apparent attachment was unreal.

Subaru's last action complicates his own despair: he grabs Elsa's ankle to delay her approach to Beatrice, even after the claimed rejection. His right wrist is severed and the fatal blade follows (`p-007#B0043–0059`). That action does not solve their relation. It does show that his willingness to protect her has not simply vanished into a calculation that this world is worthless.

### 7.2 A more exact question produces a narrower answer

In E015 Subaru asks Roswaal the specific question he now fears: is Beatrice a Witch Cult member? Roswaal renews a truth-telling oath, allowing silence but professing not to lie. He says she is not a member and distinguishes her book from the inferior, ambiguous Cult Gospels. He describes it as a completed book, one of two extant, nearest the `叡智の書`, rather than supplying a demonstrated reading of its contents (`p-011#B0172–0225`). This is testimony under the renewed oath, not an independent experiment or an analyst's license to merge all black books.

Subaru can be relieved without dropping the next question. Lack of Cult membership does not by itself explain the book, her choices or her contract. Roswaal then explicitly denies being Beatrice's contract partner. Their interests coincide; her library obligation binds her to another person, whose identity he tells Subaru to ask her about (`p-011#B0269–0282`). This corrects Subaru's received premise. It does not justify editing that premise out of the V10 freeze, where it was all he had.

The forgotten message matters as a practical memory failure. Roswaal repeats the old instruction to let Beatrice ask a question; Subaru realizes he had failed to recall it after dying and throughout E014. Roswaal adds that he should affirm he is `その人`, and predicts that she will then help because of her contract (`p-011#B0251–0269`). The strategy has not been tried by V11's endpoint. Whether it tells the truth, what it asks Subaru to undertake and how Beatrice can choose are unresolved. A method for obtaining help is not already an ethically or personally settled bond.

### 7.3 The reader learns more than Subaru, but not everything

Roswaal's answer to whether he is an enemy changes the grammatical direction: `君たちは、私の味方だよ`—he identifies them as his allies, not himself as theirs (`p-011#B0303–0306`). The asymmetry should not disappear in a reassuring paraphrase. His expectation of Subaru, direction of Ram and control of information remain consequential even where his practical advice is useful.

The final independent viewpoint strengthens suspicion without completing its explanation. As the shadow reaches his room, Roswaal calmly takes an unnamed black book from under his pillow, holds it as if holding someone beloved, and addresses Subaru's next attempt. The narration repeatedly says nobody hears him (`p-025#B0107–0130`). The book resembles the newly important class of books; its exact identity, contents and predictions are not shown. His language strongly suggests expected repetition or another prospective account. It does not specify knowledge of the death trigger, retention of discarded lives, the employer behind Elsa, or the person to whom his devotion is addressed. Subaru cannot use any of this private evidence as if he witnessed it.

## 8. Witch explanations, causal conditions and earned uncertainty

### 8.1 The second tea really relieves a burden

The rabbit death defeats Subaru's belief that preparation might make another death bearable. Reconstitution restores organs and limbs; remembered consumption prevents ordinary recognition and action. In the tea space he is unexpectedly coherent. Echidna attributes the stability to the prior tea's work on his Witch Factor, while linking its usable continuation outside to remembering the encounter (`p-015#B0017–0039`; `p-016#B0020–0046`). His changed state is observed. The complete causal account is hers, not a proof of permanent psychological repair or a newly manifested combat power.

Subaru then risks the forbidden statement. It is completed; the scene continues without the familiar pain or an injury to Echidna. She says she had already seen the experience before hearing it, then asks what he thought and felt. He repeats the statement, cries, and slowly gives an account she does not hurry (`p-018#B0096–0146`). This is a real recipient and a real opportunity to articulate experience. Memory access is not perfect understanding: she specifically wants the account from his mouth and later does not initially understand the fear about Rem.

The successful local test does not establish permission everywhere. It does not prove the penalty can no longer reach anyone, identify its complete intent or guarantee that another recipient will be safe. Subaru himself recognizes the place-and-witches limitation on waking and calls the account of RBD's properties a hypothesis (`p-025#B0017–0018`). Typhon's statement that she vaguely heard from inside Echidna supplies limited additional receipt evidence, not a declaration that every witch knows every detail (`p-018#B0433–0435`).

Echidna's manner also resists a single therapeutic label. She offers patient listening and some explanation, yet intimidates Subaru over guest etiquette, presses him to drink before clarifying the bodily provenance he has forgotten, displays curiosity about dangerous outcomes and warns against entrusting a living heart to a dead witch. Subaru's relief and her self-interested knowledge desire can both be true (`p-018#B0007–0034/B0136–0146/B0402–0414`; `p-024#B0224–0228`).

### 8.2 “Probably unlimited” is not an audited law

Before answering the quota question, Echidna explicitly says she can offer only conjecture and has a vague prediction about the principle. Her first answer is `おそらく、ない`. She adds a condition: if the Witch's fixation ends, the power to reject death ends; she does not know whether such an occasion will arise (`p-018#B0191–0208`). Subaru's internal conviction that he will not be abandoned is expressly without evidentiary grounds. Later confident formulations must be read under that qualification.

Her account gives the power a different beneficiary from Subaru's desired outcome. The proposed provider protects him from a fatal impasse; losses to other people need not count. Rem's unrecoverable condition is the decisive counterexample to assuming that a usable restart preserves everyone's opportunity. Subaru embraces the inferred implication that he must save everyone else himself (`p-018#B0222–0256`). The account explains his renewed strategy and fear. It does not experimentally identify checkpoint selection, a retry quota, the ontology of superseded worlds or an infallible guarantee of success.

That renewed strategy carries a contradiction. He refuses removing RBD because of the people it has enabled and may enable him to save, not because he likes suffering. But he can now call repeated expenditure of his own life a cheap purchase. Sharing the burden has reduced one isolation without removing his demand that all costs be borne by himself (`p-018#B0174–0181/B0260–0269`; `p-025#B0020–0021`). The ensuing shock is evidence against treating the tea as an achieved final independence.

### 8.3 Soul danger, moral judgment and healing remain different mechanisms

Echidna warns that ordinary death is not available in the invited mental world, but severe injury to the psyche can persist when the body is rejoined. Subaru recognizes that this can be more dangerous than another external defeat (`p-018#B0405–0411`). The warning distinguishes physical restoration from the condition of the person returning.

Typhon's handshake removes an arm without pain, which she treats as a sign that Subaru is not an evildoer. His fragmentation then expresses, in her explanation, his sense of being a guilty person. Her tenderness is sincere in presentation, but it does not make the action safe, the contact fully informed or the moral verdict objectively infallible (`p-018#B0439–0472`; `p-020#B0001–0008`). Her appearance and wish to meet him do not prevent catastrophic incomprehension.

Minerva's punches actually restore the fragmented spirit body while the dream terrain is devastated. Her tears and anger are directed toward injury and the world that permits it. She calls for no further injury, but Subaru has not negotiated an enforceable undertaking never to be hurt. Echidna later identifies her as the safest of the five for him, a relative personal comparison, not a universal claim that all her effects are harmless (`p-020#B0009–0028`; `p-022#B0001–0051`). Echidna then restores the tea setting by an observed dream-world act. None of these events adds a death or proves that all mental injuries are painless.

The unplanned arrival of Typhon before Daphne also limits Echidna's claimed control. She says the souls were collected and may use her as a vessel; the exchange occurs, but not initially in the intended order. Control over a dream's furnishings is not demonstrated absolute command over every other subject within it (`p-018#B0384–0389`; `p-022#B0037–0055`).

### 8.4 Daphne can be argued with without becoming a moral equivalent

Daphne's account joins hunger relief to predation. A large whale offers more food and multiplying rabbits an endless supply; a being that eats cannot demand exemption from being eaten. Subaru can recognize the necessity of food without accepting this as a justification for the resulting slaughter. She distinguishes her own hunger from the rabbits' suffering and initially does not recognize the later human names assigned to the creatures (`p-024#B0020–0081`). This is not simple benevolence that happened to go wrong, nor only a wish to cause pain for its own sake.

Their common language initially fails to produce common values, but the narrative does not leave communication absolutely impossible. Subaru argues that her principle of eating to live must also allow killing to survive. Daphne accepts the consistency claim and gives him practical information (`p-024#B0083–0100`). That is a particular successful bridge, not proof that she has adopted his concern for all victims. His final provocation produces a new non-appetite response, qualifying even his impression that she has no interests beyond hunger (`p-024#B0111–0121`).

The resulting countermeasure remains prospective. Echidna describes division, common origin, shared hunger and the need to eliminate the whole set. Daphne adds one shared consciousness, no intelligent surviving reserve strategy, and attraction to total mana, so a strong magic user could gather them. Their accounts are substantial creator/knowledge-holder evidence; Subaru has not yet executed a bait-and-extermination plan (`p-018#B0340–0350`; `p-024#B0089–0100`). No source here identifies the snow's cause, proves every missing resident was eaten, or establishes why the rabbits arrived at this particular time.

### 8.5 A paid memory agreement is not blanket ownership

The tea's end has an actual cost and an incomplete explanation. Subaru asks to settle payment because retaining the encounter is indispensable. He refuses the implication that an ordinary-looking cloth is freely disposable: its return is part of a promise to Petra. Echidna says the physical cloth will not vanish, attributes a protective power to the giver's wish, and collects confirmation of that wish plus a small interference. She also explicitly limits her power over Subaru and the wish despite this being her domain (`p-024#B0242–0279`).

The resulting waking test supports retained memory and retained material cloth; it does not disclose the interference's full effect or prove that Petra's concern has been stolen or destroyed. Likewise, a higher threshold for another tea is a condition on the strength of knowledge-desire, not an instruction that a more violent death is required. That is Subaru's distressed interpretation of the difficulty. An encounter during another trial is a separate proposed route, not a third tea already earned (`p-024#B0215–0239`).

## 10. Ordinary life as explanatory evidence

The pocket handkerchief offers a new bridge to Subaru's actual remembered home life. He has carried one since his mother taught the habit, offers it to Frederica when she cries, and blushes at her response. It is explicitly a different cloth from Petra's wrist gift (`p-003#B0157–0162`). This matters without a heroic outcome: a learned domestic routine makes a small act of consideration possible. It does not transmit his trial farewell to the real Naoko or prove that his parents know his current circumstances.

Subaru also invokes late-night anime as the reason the hour does not trouble him. The moon, stars and wind in the clearing attract his sensory attention and a secret-base joke (`p-008#B0204–0224`). These are supported ordinary preferences and language resources. They do not establish the astronomy hobby in V09's internal escape-family premise, nor its marriage, children or future biography. Likewise, his locust, amoeba and horror-film analogies show the repertoire with which he tries to understand an unfamiliar threat, not independently verified zoology or a universally successful strategy (`p-018#B0335–0346`; `p-024#B0096–0099`).

Petra's kitchen, washing and cleaning panels frame skilled labor as something being learned. The narrative adds Frederica's teaching and forest patrol, Petra's formal address, embarrassed warmth and ability to act under instructions. Her politeness correction while holding Subaru's hand is not incidental to the danger: the daily role continues to matter to her during it. Ram's criticism of tea and Frederica's answer also reveal a relationship whose familiarity cannot be reduced to a single combat alignment (`p-fmatter-002`; `p-003#B0021–0050/B0199–0205`; `p-005#B0331–0336`).

Care appears through material detail rather than declaration alone. Food and drink include a welcomed shared cup, a threatening host's cup, forcibly administered cold soup and Otto's offered water. Cleaning a face after rescue brings modest relief; keeping a person supplied without conversation has not made captivity relationally bearable. A cloth can be comfort, a promise, a bandage, a magical claim and an incompletely explained price without those roles becoming identical (`p-003#B0122–0143`; `p-008#B0538–0551/B0598–0600`; `p-018#B0007–0034`; `p-024#B0257–0279`).

Otto's dirty clothes have explanatory contrast because he ordinarily keeps a merchant's presentability: he has already told Subaru why appearance matters to his work. Noticing the departure from that baseline helps Subaru recognize the cost of help (`p-008#B0653–0661`). The oil, formal audience, fee, repairs and payroll issues are still not reported settled. An actual friendship does not retroactively pay a merchant or make professional ambition counterfeit.

Mundane limitations persist alongside magic. Ram's inexperience tending Roswaal's injury lets Garfiel's bandaging stand out as care for someone he dislikes. The rescue party has no available healer; a damaged Gate makes Subaru's spell costly; fatigue follows confinement; one dragon poses a three-passenger problem; animal directions can lead to burrows; a forest route needs light. These details prevent determination or newly received affection from becoming a substitute for bodies, materials and other people's competence.
