## 6. The family trial: what changes, and whose knowledge it is

### 6.1 Four evidence planes, not an actual return home

The named parents are 菜月賢一, Kenichi, and 菜月菜穂子, Naoko (`p-014#B0752`; `p-015#B0022`). The chapter initially withholds the familiar setting's cause. Subaru's room, posters, games, physical play and breakfast give apparently ordinary continuity, while the approach of school time produces a distinct tightening of attention. Only the later debrief explicitly identifies the construction as memory-based fiction (`p-020#B0065`). The analysis must retain both presentation and explanation rather than pretending the reader began with every answer.

| evidence plane | what it can support | what it cannot support |
|---|---|---|
| remembered/developmental baseline | Subaru's narrated childhood, school withdrawal, family habits, imitative self-model and remembered missed sendoff | every parental private thought, a clinical diagnosis or universal reliability of all recollection |
| generated conversation | how Subaru now answers a plausible father/mother, receives affection, cries, apologizes and chooses a farewell | actual Earth parents hearing these words or present reconciliation there |
| Echidna's account | admitted memory construction, alternatives for unexpected information, declared pass condition and observed first-trial result | omniscient independent certification of every parental statement or a complete theory of the real world's access |
| real parents after disappearance | Subaru explicitly accepts his words were not conveyed and they may be worrying | present location, conduct, knowledge, death, forgiveness or later reunion |

Subaru's defense of the parents' particularity is meaningful without refuting the construction. Echidna offers unrealized experience or remembered letters as possible sources of unexpected responses, and wish-shaped behavior as another explanation (B0067–0069). He insists that these are his parents rather than generic consoling figures (B0075–0081), yet ultimately acknowledges they were not really there and did not receive the conversation (B0106). Neither “mere hallucination with no value” nor “an authentic message delivered across worlds” fits all this evidence.

### 6.2 Performance becomes a strategy before it becomes shame

Previously the deadline's passing brought relief; now pain interrupts that pattern. Continuing parental routines coexist with his harsh judgment of himself. No narrated expulsion or parental abandonment causes this absence.

His history supplies an escalation, not an innate essence (B0324–0408). Childhood competence and being Kenichi's remarkable son let him expect first place. When other children surpass him, exaggerated entertainment and risky antics attempt to preserve attention. Friends withdraw; he becomes isolated while continuing a conspicuous persona at home. At high school he imitates Kenichi again and is ignored rather than formally expelled. Truancy begins as occasional escape, becomes habitual within a short interval, and remains frightening because affection does not stop. He wants condemnation to make withdrawal and self-rejection cohere. This is a narrated self-model with real remembered events, not proof every self-condemnation is fair or a clinical account of all causation.

The model also revises V01's protagonist/heroine register. Subaru did not begin performing only after arriving in a genre-like world; the new setting gives an old attention strategy different materials. Nevertheless genre knowledge sometimes supplies useful analogies and jokes, not only defense. A rule that every joke is false bravado would fail the breakfast, family play, ordinary apology and genuine shared pleasure tests.

### 6.3 Kenichi's expectation is neither perfection nor rejection

Kenichi playfully wrestles with Subaru, enters despite no answer, stages an unpleasant shaken-cola prank, boasts, and can be insensitive in public (`p-015#B0004–0017/B0107–0119/B0195–0218`). Adults ask about school and Subaru retreats; one adult apologizes, so the episode is not a universally hostile neighborhood. These details stop affection from turning Kenichi into an infallible therapist or an ideal masculine standard the analysis itself imposes.

He says school is not the whole world and allows room to think while objecting to giving up (B0250–0270). His own reported truancy/regret makes advice situated, not an oracle who knows Return by Death. He refuses to abandon Subaru (B0442–0450). The exchange reframes expectation as continuing attachment and encouragement, not an unavoidable demand to be first (`p-017#B0033–0045`). Subaru can remain his father's son without existing only as a poor copy. Kenichi's instruction is also explicitly about recipients: the deliberately foolish family persona is adjusted to time, place and audience, whereas Subaru copied it to people with whom that relationship did not exist (`p-015#B0478–0482`). This is a concrete voice mechanism rather than a claim that blood predetermines personality.

Emilia's remembered words help Subaru recover an actionable reason to face his past (B0283–0313). Rem's remembered declaration/hero/zero support then joins them (B0410–0419). The family trial is not independent proof he could reach the same answer without these prior relationships. He tells Kenichi about two “first stars” and love for both women (B0457–0470). His father's conditional advice not to hurt either is not their absent consent or a recognized multi-partner arrangement.

When future care for his parents is invoked, Subaru cries because he believes he cannot come home and repay them (B0498–0517). The perceived irreversibility is his belief, not a narrator-certified interworld prohibition. Kenichi explicitly does not know why he is crying (`p-017#B0001–0012`). Accepting the embrace is not complete disclosure, but unlike earlier all-or-nothing self-rejection, it lets care remain real without having earned or explained it perfectly.

### 6.4 Naoko's apparent obtuseness and practical attention

The breakfast's peas, pilaf, tomatoes and improvised compromises show conflict and care at low stakes. Naoko presses a food she cannot consistently eat herself, while the men expose their own hypocrisy (`p-015#B0034–0065`). Her daytime program goes unwatched. Mayo habits are a shared family context: Subaru joined in to match his parents yet defends an actual acquired taste (`p-017#B0070–0098`). Belonging's influence does not make every preference fake.

Subaru and Kenichi describe Naoko as obtuse, but her later account recognizes exhaustion from imitation (B0140–0163). That contradiction revises their view without certifying what the real absent mother now knows. She distinguishes resembling Kenichi from needing to be nothing but his replica (B0167–0182). Subaru decides not to hide his farewell under the excuse of protecting her (B0195–0213). He gives a limited account of a long, dangerous absence and people who help him—not a complete death-return explanation.

He respects her wish not to have her crying face watched (B0223). Her born-love/given-love account asks him to pass care onward, potentially to hypothetical future children, rather than repay his own birth (B0226–0228). It does not import Rigel/Spica into the current route. Nor does the “smile at the end” homework excuse any intervening means (B0237–0239): it sits inside a relational refusal of punitive repayment, not Roswaal's consequentialist permission structure.

The final `いってらっしゃい` / answer supplies the sendoff he failed to reciprocate on the convenience-store night (B0255–0263). The response happens now in the trial. The old absence, real parents' uncertainty and moral significance of the unanswered farewell are not rewritten out of history.

### 6.5 Passing one trial is not mastering the arc

The school is a memory-shaped stage: an empty classroom and Echidna, not observed social reintegration (`p-019`; `p-020#B0010–0021`). Echidna allows either accepting the past or resolutely rejecting it as a passing response and declares Subaru has passed the first trial (B0042–0050). Her knowledge-growth ideology remains her view, and she is disappointed that Rem's earlier support already supplied the central answer (B0055–0061). Subaru can thank her without granting omniscience; she does not understand his gratitude fully (B0106–0107).

There are three trials (B0092). Subaru does not complete the other two. His desire to leave Emilia her own achievement does not settle whether she wants this particular ordeal; Echidna's unheard doubt remains reader-only (B0118–0125). Waking Subaru says his recollection seems intact, yet the interlocutor is printed as `────` (B0134). Later he remembers substantive facts while still lacking who told him (`p-024#B0087–0090`). Partial restoration inside the trial is not complete waking recovery.

He wakes Emilia from visible distress, holds her and respects withdrawal. Her cries for her father/Puck and “not me” do not disclose the trial's full content or prove she rejects Subaru as a person (`p-020#B0139–0171`). Puck does not answer here. Neither her pain nor his success licenses assigning her his childhood history, pass criterion or preferred solution.

## 7. Patronage, trust and the costs of taking over

Ram uses a newly specified hypnotic aroma to help Emilia rest; ordinary administration would be harder with Puck present, and the text distinguishes it from the earlier hallucination scent (`p-022#B0007–0008`). Subaru keeps Otto outside the camp's irreversible secrets (B0018–0024). Protection and exclusion coexist; it is not proof Otto is ignorant of every operational matter.

Roswaal admits the result exceeded expectation (B0037), offers knighthood conditional on leaving Sanctuary (B0045), and welcomes Subaru as a singular accomplice while excluding Emilia from the conversation (B0058–0079). Subaru objects and controls his anger; an offered office is not a performed appointment or Emilia's personal assent. Ram is told to attend, not speak (B0111). This patronage relation includes actual information control, not merely Subaru's exaggerated suspicion.

The direct Rem test finally happens: Ram does not know the name, and narration confirms forgetting (B0102–0108). The earlier V06 denied sister, V08 unheard murmur and V09 deferred question retain their separate horizons. Ram's loyalty now cannot be described as choosing Roswaal over a remembered sister at this meeting. There is no equivalent direct Roswaal answer about Rem.

Roswaal admits deliberate avoidance of the expected Cult danger and credits Subaru's response (B0120–0145). Subaru rejects a result-based excuse for losses; those losses must still be separated by event state and operation. Roswaal's “truthful” posture expressly permits withholding (B0149–0156). His confidence in Subaru has an unexplained basis, not a disclosed knowledge of death-return. Subaru then agrees to keep the admission from Emilia (`p-024#B0008–0014`). The objection is real, and the complicity remains real.

Roswaal asks whom Subaru met in the tomb; Subaru deliberately hides his own trial while also failing to recover the conversant's identity (B0029–0038). These are different kinds of omission. The later lie to Emilia attributing trial knowledge to Roswaal is conscious deception, not something the amnesia forces (B0080–0090). Emilia asks to be believed rather than indulged (B0097–0103). Subaru considers taking over if she refuses, but she has not actually ceded the role. Days later she refuses his proposal to pause attempts during his absence, and he accepts her answer (B0177–0186). A person can respect one refusal and evade another.

Negotiation also produces an accomplishment not reducible to psychology. Three days later50 evacuees leave in seven wagons while Emilia remains as assurance before the barrier is removed (B0125–0165). Garfiel's reluctance includes the elderly residents' welfare, finite fields and dependence on Roswaal's supplies; it is not mere petty obstruction. Ryuzu and other intermediaries matter. Roswaal approves through Ram after the arrangement has been achieved. Actual family reunion is narrated on arrival eight hours from Sanctuary (B0320–0323).

Garfiel distinguishes who frees the boundary from who gets camp credit: the beneficiaries need release, not necessarily Emilia's heroic reputation (B0243–0264). He then asks whether forcing painful past-confrontation is what she wants (B0266–0283). Subaru recognizes a conditional blind spot and intends a frank discussion. Neither the question nor that intention proves an unspoken wish or completed conversation. Asking Garfiel about outside ambitions exposes Subaru's freedom-based premise; Garfiel rejects the premise without supplying a full desired future (B0289–0295).

Garfiel hands him a second necklace matching Frederica's (B0305–0312), not the same physical stone changing custody and not a tested protective device. Otto's oil, audience, repair and compensation remain pending despite repeated delay (B0131–0137). Subaru later calls him a camp-retained merchant; Otto objects (B0331–0332). Unilateral naming does not hire him. Nevertheless an actual backup/report plan is agreed before Subaru's mansion visit (B0327–0329).

## 9. Conditional person and relationship increments

Subaru's new developmental account predicts attention to distinction, approval and being needed, but not one invariant response. With parents he can now receive affection and cry without furnishing a complete explanation. With Emilia he can listen, accept one refusal and respect a private motive while still hiding material information. With Beatrice he mistakes needed help for entitlement to her answer and is explicitly resisted. With Otto he appreciates mixed motives, apologizes for one snap, forgets a meal and imposes a role Otto rejects. With Roswaal he objects to instrumental sacrifice while entering a secrecy arrangement. With Garfiel he receives an unwelcome criticism and revises an intended conversation. With Rem, an actual reply remains impossible. These are different conditional policies, not decorative examples of a single “savior complex.”

Emilia's emerging pattern is also not passive trust. She requires an appearance apology, asks not to have Rem's importance hidden, postpones her own electoral explanation, independently obtains the route, offers protection, demands transfer information, answers the villagers herself, and elects to keep trying. Fear and a plea for Puck coexist with those capacities. Treating her request to be trusted as agreement to every concealed means would repeat the error being analyzed.

Frederica's declared care, professionalism and reader-only letter compliance must survive an unresolved motive review. Garfiel's violence, slur, corrections, Ryuzu care, material stewardship, interest in liberation and critique of vicarious help cannot be reduced to either aggressive fool or secretly faultless guide. Ram's patron obedience and direct loss of Rem recognition remain separate from her old reader-known sister history. Beatrice's withholding is an enacted boundary with emotional cost, not proof all her possible assistance is freely owed.

The final mansion search is the strongest adverse control on premature completion. Subaru notices unnatural silence and open room doors in a clean mansion, knows retreat to Otto is the safer move, and consciously continues (`p-024#B0353–0365`). His priority for Rem combines vulnerability and known location with self-recognized rationalization; Frederica is ranked differently partly because he assumes competence and suspects her (B0368–0377). Neither neutral triage nor a global ranking of women's human value is established. The attack proves an actual danger, not that every feared collateral event has occurred. Seeking information while dying shows recurring instrumental return thinking; it does not erase the earlier wish to live, new backup or capacity to receive care.
