## 4. Executive V03 state

V03's decisive shift is from **private replay to distributed present-state action**. Subaru still profits from erased histories, but he succeeds only when those histories are converted into testable mechanics, disclosures that do not require the forbidden secret, current evidence other people can assess, and complementary agency. Emilia regulates his collapse, Beatrice supplies and tests mechanics, Ram allocates roles and locates Rem, Rem heals and fights, villagers provide knowledge/resources, children supply both motive and a material tool, and Roswaal delivers the final overwhelming force.

The curse model closes substantially. Ulgarm bites implant dormant formulas; a hungry animal later activates its own formula to drain a target's mana. Killing that feeding animal stops the drain. A single inactive formula can be removed, but numerous overlapping formulas become too tangled to resolve quickly and remain inert after their operators die. The puppy-like vector is also a mabeast pack leader, and the terminal interlude proves the pack was controlled: every inspected animal's horn had been broken, making it obedient to the breaker. The brown-braided “rescued” child introduced the puppy, led the children through the severed barrier, and then disappeared. She is the leading mabeast-handler agent. Her identity, sponsor, exact target, and motive remain open.

V03 also gives the twins' crisis an internal history. Oni twins are condemned because they split the species' normal two horns. Prodigious Ram spared both at birth, then lost her only horn protecting Rem when black-robed, Witch-odor-bearing attackers destroyed their settlement. Rem's momentary relief at the break became a seven-year guilt system: she treats herself as an inferior replacement for Ram's lost life and converts service, vigilance, and self-sacrifice into penance. This explains the speed and extremity of her judgments; it does not absolve them.

Finally, success is not a clean bodily reset. Subaru retains scars, nearly exhausted mana, a gate damaged by repeated forced activation, and intertwined curse residues. He also retains fear, moral discomfort about killing, and a tendency to hide cost behind performance. The fifth-day morning is therefore a genuine victory without becoming a cost-free endpoint.

## 5. Event-state deep reading

### 5.1 Trauma, performed normality, and Emilia's indirect access

Restoration returns Subaru to the mansion-arrival bed and the twins' repeated greeting. He remembers deliberately killing himself but explicitly doubts that another restoration would follow another death. V02's strategic use of death therefore does not become confidence in an unlimited resource. (`Chapter 1; p-005; p-007#0053–0055`, `#0323–0331`)

Subaru identifies two operational goals—gain the household's trust and defeat the curse user—but his competence has a cost. Retained procedures and preferences again make him suspicious to Ram and Rem. Roswaal continues surveillance while privately ruling out a Subaru/Elsa partnership and instructing Ram to stop Rem from independently escalating, partly because violence around Emilia could damage political relations. (`p-007#0059–0135`, `#0238–0262`)

The cheerful week is not recovered stability. Subaru cannot sleep or retain food, vomits repeatedly, treats all free time as optimization, and consciously tries to “perform Natsuki Subaru.” Puck detects a disordered gap between exterior and interior and predicts that the performance will wear through. (`p-007#0266–0359`)

Emilia cannot receive the forbidden causal history, but she can respond to present evidence. She offers a lap pillow, does not demand an explanation, and says `大変だったね`. The wording validates experienced burden without claiming knowledge she lacks. Subaru cries, sleeps, and becomes able to interact with the twins without incapacitating fear. This is regulation and relational access around the disclosure barrier, not disclosure of `死に戻り` itself. (`p-007#0365–0490`; `p-011#0006–0018`)

### 5.2 From human-assassin theory to mabeast vector

Beatrice distinguishes curse formula from activation: before activation the implanted structure can be removed; after activation it cannot ordinarily be stopped. Strong curses require preparation and contact between practitioner and target. She names herself, Puck, and Roswaal as capable removers and explains that Emilia and the twins lack relevant experience. (`Chapter 2; p-008#0137–0188`)

Subaru correctly narrows common exposure to Arlam village but adds unsupported assumptions: a human practitioner, probably a recent outsider, working as royal-selection assassin. He then intentionally contacts every conspicuous villager. The test is methodical but ethically compromised: he uses children as social cover and privately acknowledges their potential as shields. (`p-008#0189–0210`; `p-011#0019–0071`)

The decisive contact is not one of the humans. A puppy bites the same place in each village loop. Beatrice detects and removes a real curse formula at that wound, converting the repeated bite into causal evidence and falsifying Subaru's initial human-contact model. (`p-011#0107–0149`; `p-013#0118–0178`)

Roswaal's altered departure demonstrates branch mutability without revealing its full cause. After an unspecified troubling report he flies to check on Garfiel, entrusts the mansion to the twins, and separately entrusts Emilia to Subaru because he senses danger. Subaru reads the change as the effect of his actions; the terminal interlude later identifies the report as a probable lure. (`p-013#0041–0093`; `p-031#0035–0048`)

### 5.3 Ordinary promises create public obligation

At night the village is searching for seven missing children. The forest barrier is broken, allowing mabeasts into a zone that normally separates them from villagers. Rem reports that mabeasts are mana-bearing enemies of humanity traditionally said to have been created by the Witch. The claim is important cultural/world-model testimony, not narrator certification of origin. (`p-015#0001–0053`)

Subaru persuades Rem to cross the barrier not through inaccessible loop facts but by naming Petra, Luca, Mild, and other children, their ordinary aspirations, and his promise to repeat radio calisthenics. The chapter's `勇気` is fear-filled action: he is shaking, knows his weakness, and proceeds because relationships make nondecision morally costly. (`p-015#0054–0111`)

Six children are found alive but severely drained. Rem can stabilize them but cannot remove active curses. Petra's first words identify a seventh child deeper in the forest. Subaru's desire to “pick up everything” risks losing already recoverable lives, so he assigns work by capacity: Rem sustains the six and transfers them to the villagers; Subaru attempts only to buy time for the missing child. (`p-015#0115–0176`)

Trust changes through verifiable action. Subaru acknowledges that Rem can track his Witch odor, promises future mutual explanation, and deliberately converts an erased-state haircut promise—originally a cover for surveillance—into a present-state proposal. Rem accepts, safely transfers the children, returns, and sees Subaru shielding the seventh child. Her focalized interlude later states that this evidence lets her stop suspecting him. (`p-015#0177–0229`; `p-023#0057–0079`)

### 5.4 Layered curse, oni history, and coercive odor as a tool

Subaru kills one large Ulgarm using his clothed arm as bait, then discovers a coordinated pack. Rem returns and protects him. A puppy-sized leader casts destructive earth magic; after Rem shields Subaru, Subaru pushes Rem clear of the pack and is nearly dismembered. The reciprocity that changes their relationship is bodily and dangerous, not merely verbal. (`p-015#0233–0440`; `p-017`)

Healing keeps Subaru alive, but multiple bites have planted too many overlapping formulas for timely removal. Beatrice explains their function as feeding connections: when a hungry Ulgarm activates its formula, it drains the target's mana; killing that animal stops the meal even though ordinary activated curses otherwise cannot be cancelled. The children's drains stopped because their feeders died. Subaru's unknown set of feeders creates the apparent half-day death sentence. (`p-020#0047–0152`)

Rem enters the forest alone to kill every possible feeder. Her inserted focalization supplies the underlying compulsion. Oni horns draw ambient mana and amplify combat; twins split the normal pair and are treated as `忌子`. Ram's prodigious one-horn power spared the twins, but the comparison erased Rem's independent self-worth. When Witch-associated attackers burned the settlement and broke Ram's horn while Ram shielded her, Rem's involuntary relief became guilt. She has since tried to live as the inferior replacement for Ram's lost future. (`p-021`; `p-023#0001–0042`)

Oni transformation gives Rem rapid healing and immense force but lets battle instinct overwhelm reason. Even in frenzy she recognizes Ram and retains her rescue objective strongly enough to kill the Ulgarm actively draining Subaru. Ram's horn is permanently absent; she retains powerful wind magic and `千里眼`, but both are resource-limited. Her remote sight works by borrowing the vision of wavelength-compatible creatures and leaves her physically exhausted and exposed. (`p-015#0390–0440`; `p-020#0191–0232`, `#0300–0368`, `#0572–0722`)

Subaru exploits disclosure enforcement as an attractant. He begins to reveal `死に戻り`; the world freezes from his perspective, the black form now manifests a more complete arm, and the Witch odor intensifies enough to draw mabeasts. A second attempt produces both arms: one grips his heart while the other touches his cheek with a comforting/violating warmth. Later, even the circumlocution “my heart was gripped” triggers pain and odor. The enforcer therefore reacts to semantic disclosure, not only one fixed phrase, and its embodiment appears to escalate. Agent, objective, and relation to restoration remain unverified. (`p-020#0393–0457`, `#0657–0676`; `p-024#0073–0086`)

The resulting plan is clever but cannot be called controlled. Ram exhausts her mana after killing seventeen Ulgarm; the pack tactically drives the pair over a cliff; Subaru must carry Ram; the leader moves the pack on earth magic; and frenzy-state Rem attacks Subaru while demanding Ram's return. Mutual rescue repeatedly substitutes for the plan's missing margin. (`p-020#0467–0656`)

Ram proposes striking Rem's horn. Subaru throws Ram so Rem will instinctively catch her, then uses the opening; fear shortens his first strike and makes it miss. The Ulgarm leader's earth attack accidentally creates a second opportunity, and Subaru's broken blade hits the horn. Ending transformation by attacking its power organ works, but only through knowledge, sister attachment, fear acknowledged rather than erased, and chance. (`p-020#0723–0798`; `p-024#0006–0029`)

### 5.5 All-in victory and its residues

Rem treats her hesitation and Subaru's wounds as a crime demanding solitary punishment. Subaru rejects the premise that guilt authorizes her to monopolize risk: insufficiency should lead to consultation and combined strength. He then repeats the same dangerous pattern by making himself bait so Ram can carry Rem toward safety. The volume critiques unilateral sacrifice while repeatedly relying on it. (`p-024#0031–0160`)

Subaru's performed confidence collapses once the sisters cannot see him; he cries from pain and calls for his parents and Emilia. Trapped by the leader and pack, he independently casts `シャマク` and bites a child-gifted `ボッコの実` to remain mobile after mana discharge. Sensory deprivation allows a stab and temporary escape, but the leader still recovers and nearly kills him. (`p-024#0164–0212`; `p-026#0001–0070`)

Roswaal returns and uses `ウルゴーア` fire magic to kill the giant leader and every Ulgarm gathered in Shamac. Emilia, despite Puck and Beatrice trying to keep her uninvolved, had predicted that cornered Subaru would cast and told Roswaal to watch for that signal from above. She therefore becomes an effective ally through present-state knowledge and institutional leverage without learning the forbidden secret. (`p-026#0071–0105`)

The epilogue makes future-making the opposite of penance. Rem holds Subaru's hand because she gives him the care that once helped her; Subaru distinguishes actual Ram from Rem's counterfactual idol, identifies Rem's concrete contribution, and says `レムがいてくれてよかったよ。ありがとう`. They propose mutual dependence and conversation about tomorrow. Rem's crying smile marks a real reorientation, not the disappearance of seven years of guilt or the guarantee of independent self-worth. (`p-027#0029–0220`)

Emilia thanks Subaru and agrees to a village date defined as shared time and shared memory. Yet their agency asymmetry remains: she is angry at being restrained and excluded, while Subaru privately admits he would exclude her again under the same danger. The bond advances, but protective paternalism is not solved. (`p-029#0001–0066`)

Victory retains material and moral residue. Subaru bears scars, nearly depleted mana, a damaged gate, and inert curse structures. He is disturbed by killing and by solving coexistence through extermination. His decision to remember that discomfort resists turning survival into proof that every means was clean. (`p-027#0084–0108`; `p-029#0067–0092`; `p-031#0021–0034`)

## 6. Character and relationship revisions

### Subaru

Subaru moves further from deterministic replay toward hypothesis testing and coalition building. His best decisions translate private history into evidence other people can evaluate; his worst decisions still instrumentalize himself and others, hide cost behind performance, and overvalue an “all-in” solution.

The volume rejects fearlessness as a heroic essence. Courage is produced by fear, relationship, and action; his first horn strike misses specifically because fear limits commitment. He remains dependent on parents, Emilia, the household, villagers, and children, and that dependence is analytically positive when shared rather than concealed.

His moral growth is also incomplete. He recognizes the children as persons through their ordinary futures and retains discomfort about animal killing, while still using children as imagined cover, using the disclosure punishment against his own body, and deciding unilaterally what dangers Emilia should be allowed to share.

### Emilia

Emilia's repeated prosocial tendency becomes practical discernment. She sees the mismatch in Subaru's performance, creates a low-information care channel, predicts his cornered behavior, and turns that prediction into rescue by instructing Roswaal. She does not need discarded-state knowledge to be causally important.

Her present-state agency is nevertheless constrained by Puck, Beatrice, Subaru, and physical limits. Puck withholds Subaru's curse; Beatrice restrains her; Subaru plans to repeat exclusion. Her gratitude and accepted date are real current-state reciprocity, not validation of those constraints.

### Rem

Rem's competence, violence, care, and impulsivity become one historically conditioned model. Witch odor is a learned danger marker after settlement destruction; community protection and Ram's safety are overriding goals; comparison with Ram produces chronic insufficiency; guilt turns danger into a chance for penance. These conditions explain why surveillance can become torture or self-sacrifice rapidly.

Current-state trust is genuinely rebuilt. Rem observes Subaru's labor, regulation after Emilia's care, village knowledge, willingness to name insufficiency, and rescue of the seventh child. She no longer treats odor as dispositive and risks herself for him. The reorientation should not be simplified into romance or cure: her closing willingness to lean on Subaru may replace one external evaluator with another unless future evidence shows growing self-authorship.

### Ram

Ram is not merely the competent or cold twin's inverse. She monitors, restrains Rem, allocates risk, repairs the barrier, feeds villagers, searches despite severe physical cost, and uses sister authority as a responsibility. Her `お姉ちゃん` response reveals the durable intimate bond beneath formal `姉様` hierarchy.

Her direct loyalty to Roswaal remains stronger than her own self-priority and may outrank Rem. The terminal interlude shows that being called an indispensable pawn is desired recognition. Her nightly dependence is medical/magical: Roswaal transfers balanced mana through the horn scar to sustain a body that can no longer exchange enough mana itself.

### Beatrice

Beatrice is the central mechanics expert and a repeated caregiver. She removes Subaru's first formula, helps remove the children's formulas, completes healing, discloses the apparently fatal residue problem, and leaves the library to act. She often cites Puck or rational necessity while assuming emotional burdens herself. V03 strengthens care behind abrasive language without closing contract history, motives, or the ominous possibility Roswaal briefly considers.

### Roswaal

Roswaal's capacity and strategic opacity both grow. He flies, uses high-level fire magic, transfers all four common mana types with extreme precision, sponsors Emilia, manages the twins, and resolves the pack when others cannot. He is also deliberately lured away, infers royal-selection sabotage, and privately names Emilia's victory as necessary to his own aim of killing the Dragon.

Calling Ram and Rem indispensable `駒` is not disguised affection; it is his explicit integration of value and instrumentality. Ram's consent to this role is real within her loyalty and bodily dependence, but does not erase the power asymmetry.

## 8. Institutions, factions, status, and practical agency

The estate's patronage has concrete territorial obligations. A broken forest barrier, village reporting failure, harmed children, and Ulgarm incursion expose Roswaal to responsibility. Ram repairs the barrier and tends villagers; Roswaal directly restores order. The village is neither scenery nor a passive beneficiary: its members search, lend Subaru a sword, transfer the rescued children, and provide the social/material resources that make the final rescue possible.

The mabeast incident is organized. Horn-breaking creates obedience, the unknown girl introduces the vector and crosses the barrier, and a report draws Roswaal toward Garfiel. Roswaal classifies this alongside Elsa as opposition around the royal selection, but V03 does not identify one sponsor, prove that the same faction hired both agents, or establish Emilia as the direct operational target.

Oni society supplies a coercive status order: twins are `忌子`, ability determines worth, and horn loss strips standing. The attack destroys that society before alternative practices can be observed. Rem internalizes its performance hierarchy even after leaving; Ram is more detached from horn status but materially disabled by its loss.

Roswaal's estate remains a layered dependency structure. He provides refuge, employment, political purpose, overwhelming force, and the mana required to sustain Ram. Ram gives direct ownership-like loyalty; Rem serves primarily because Roswaal protects Ram; Emilia's candidacy advances Roswaal's hidden Dragon-killing objective. Benefit, gratitude, dependency, affection, and instrumentality coexist.

## 10. Ordinary-life evidence

Ordinary life is a causal layer, not relief between “real” events. Emilia's lap pillow and listening make inquiry possible. Work routines allow Ram and Rem to compare Subaru's conduct. Radio calisthenics and children's future plans motivate entry into the forest. A haircut promise becomes a test of whether discarded familiarity can be remade through consent. Steamed potatoes enact Ram's village care. A village sword, children's gifts, and one `ボッコの実` materially enable survival. Written jokes and thanks make rescue a continuing obligation. The proposed date creates a future measured by shared memory rather than by crisis debt.

These details also prevent clean heroic accounting. Subaru initially treats children as cover; the village neglects barrier checks; Emilia's helpers restrain her; Rem turns care into penance. Reciprocity is real because it develops through imperfect actors, not because each act is pure.

## 11. Japanese wording and register

- `大変だったね`: Emilia acknowledges burden without claiming the forbidden explanation; its nonspecificity is the point.
- `恋心`: Subaru names his attachment to Emilia; it motivates action but does not certify reciprocity or healthy risk calibration.
- `勇気`: explicitly experienced as terrifying; the volume opposes courage to surrender, not to fear.
- `精霊の祝福がありますように`: Emilia's send-off becomes a practical relational anchor, later supplemented by her prediction to Roswaal.
- `鬼がかってますね`: begins as Subaru's improvised intensifier and returns from Rem as a shared phrase at the future-making turn.
- `姉様` / `お姉ちゃん`: Rem's switch from hierarchical reverence to childhood intimacy arrests Ram's “correct” sacrificial retreat.
- `ツノナシ`: an oni status insult and a literal bodily condition; Ram uses it matter-of-factly, while Rem organizes guilt around it.
- `駒`: Roswaal's unsoftened term for indispensable pieces; Ram treats this instrumental recognition as desirable.
- `──いしてる`: only a clipped audible fragment; neither `愛してる` nor its speaker may be promoted as direct fact.
- `龍を殺す`: Roswaal's private statement of purpose; exact referent and reasons remain open.

## 12. Causal threat model

The strongest V03 reconstruction requires six linked but nonidentical layers:

1. **restoration/disclosure system** — Subaru returns at the mansion checkpoint; disclosure enforcement manifests and intensifies odor, but provider and enforcer identity remain open;
2. **embodied stigma** — Rem and Ulgarm react to Witch odor for different reasons, turning a hidden mechanism into social and physical risk;
3. **curse feeding** — bites implant operator-specific formulas that later drain mana for Ulgarm consumption;
4. **human command layer** — a disguised girl introduces the leader, crosses the barrier, and probably commands horn-broken Ulgarm;
5. **political diversion** — Roswaal is lured toward Garfiel; royal-selection sabotage is plausible but not established as the sole purpose;
6. **social correction** — present-state care, testimony, complementary skill, village resources, and reciprocal rescue change who believes whom and who survives.

This model rejects both “natural animal accident” and “ordinary villagers were the assassins.” It also resists merging the unknown handler, V01's Elsa employer, the Witch-cult attackers, the restoration provider, and the disclosure enforcer into one actor without evidence.
