## 0. Governing claim and evidence boundary

V12 tests whether saving people means securing a future containing them, or acknowledging that their feelings, choices and injuries matter on the way there. Subaru does not simply cease caring. His attachment becomes more exacting, his own life more expendable, and other people's lives harder to accommodate without trying to control them. He refuses to accept Emilia's dependency as the romantic fulfillment he wanted. He also withholds information, declines a friend's offered hearing, treats Ryuzu's compelled cooperation as a resource and spends the copies' bodies to reach Emilia. Recognizing another person's significance and respecting that person's agency remain different achievements.

Beatrice makes the difference particularly difficult. Subaru discovers that her book has long been blank and that her apparent obedience has concealed years of unsupported waiting. He asks for an intelligible plea for help, hears one, and initially assumes that this has made rescue possible. Beatrice answers that she has wished for help before. Some visitors wanted her power; others really wanted to save her. Their arrival did not settle what would make her life worth continuing. Her death request is actual, but does not reduce her to wanting nothing except death: she protects Subaru, fights outside the library, and spends her last action removing him from the attackers. His later choice of her hand is a resolution to return to a particular person, not an already completed rescue or an agreement that he will make her his first priority.

Roswaal and Echidna expose two distinct ways to instrumentalize the same resolve. Roswaal manufactures isolation through snow, familiar prejudice and the community's memory of Puck's earlier cold wave. He wants Subaru to discard everything but one attachment. Echidna offers the knowledge and company Subaru has lacked, but values the possible routes as objects of inquiry. A promised desirable destination need not mean a desirable path. The danger is not simply that either speaker tells an obvious falsehood. Roswaal has enough information about repetition to plan around it without knowing its death trigger. Echidna can speak true descriptions of her offer while withholding the cost most material to accepting it. Neither correct information nor help genuinely received makes a relationship fully informed.

The second trial removes Subaru's certainty that his death disposes of the world he leaves. It does not prove that parallel worlds persist. The constructed scenes show valued people grieving and trying to save him; their force depends on the possibility that his life mattered to them, not on certifying a cosmological model. Echidna expressly distinguishes the constructed examination from the unresolved mechanism of RBD. Subaru's fear that a later person might not be the person he wanted to save therefore remains a serious identity problem without becoming an established answer about the universe.

## 4. Love, bodily danger and the shadow's rescue scene

The opening love-chant is not negotiated reciprocity. Lack of felt malice does not prevent the loss of agency, engulfment and erasure of the people around Subaru. The narration names the Witch of Envy (`p-001#B0095–0097`), and the face glimpsed beneath the veil matches Emilia (`p-003#B0170–0177/B0208`). Subaru's subsequent possession hypothesis is explicitly conjectural and uses his Petelgeuse analogy (`p-003#B0213–0217`). The naming and visual match are stronger new evidence than V11's anonymous ending, but do not establish an entire embodiment or punishment mechanism. No witnessed disclosure test isolates the cause of the shadow disaster.

Garfiel actually rescues Subaru and refuses to use him as disposable bait (`p-001#B0032–0054/B0159–0179`). Ram's reported aid and the swallowed residents remain different from directly witnessed bodies. More than twenty Ryuzu lookalikes are actually assembled and then sacrificed; the later count of twenty-one bodies and Garfiel's fatal wounds are not an independent account of how the copies were made (`p-001#B0194–0213`; `p-003#B0036–0058`). Garfiel can show protective resolve in this state without having remembered or been absolved of E015's actions. His death refutes the immediate impression that the tiger's first success has won the encounter.

Inside the shadow Subaru still has a body when the wrist cloth warms and restores his coherence (`p-003#B0118–0131`). The light buys a short opportunity and shapes a dagger; Subaru uses that opening to stab himself, with an actual fatal outcome (`p-003#B0150–0178`). The result is not a demonstrated weapon for killing the Witch. Nor does it prove that physical cloth was entirely consumed, that the cloth alone knows the future, or that Echidna spoke a new instruction in the shadow. Subaru credits her preparation and imagines her foreknowledge; the text marks that foresight as probable, not independently tested (`p-003#B0133–0165`).

The scene also keeps fear within deliberate action. Subaru's knowledge of past deaths does not make him indifferent to pain. His later wager against Garfiel includes trembling hands and stomach pain, even when his expression unnerves the other man (`p-006#B0580–0598`). The label of a madman is Garfiel's response, not a clinical diagnosis or proof of fearless competence. Subaru's plan to spend his own life can be calculated without being emotionally costless or causally limited to himself.

## 5. Ryuzu: made bodies, acquired persons and compelled cooperation

### 5.1 What the hidden facility actually establishes

Subaru retrieves the original crystal and uses it at the hidden entrance; its glow and the vanished wall reveal the chamber containing Ryuzu Meyer (`p-004#B0176–0183`). The crystal-contained girl is distinguished from the copies, whose representative says that only the girl inside is the original (`p-006#B0106–0118`). The image's curled figure is supplemented by the prose's simple one-piece clothing; the picture does not independently establish death, sleep, age or an unclothed body.

Bilma describes pseudo-Od cores, formulas and embodied mana (`p-006#B0133–0145`). The claim that Od is proof of a soul is reported doctrine, not the analyst's solved ontology. Her account distinguishes successful vessels from the failed project of housing Echidna's entire memory, knowledge and personality: capacity fails even when a small body can exist (`p-006#B0178–0210/B0254–0263`). The proposed soul-compression adjustment was not fully tested before the reported end of the project (`p-006#B0277–0283`). Neither ongoing automatic production nor a described failure proves limitless resources, a birth personally witnessed by Subaru, or the exact status of every departed copy.

Bilma's individuality is acquired, while the first management copies received assigned role knowledge (`p-006#B0111–0126/B0291–0298`). Tea need not sustain her body to be a meaningful preference; she actually drinks and enjoys it (`p-006#B0267–0272`). Names, habits and interpretation of a task matter alongside artificial origin. Subaru says that he would not rank lives by their manner of birth, but cannot honestly promise that learning the origin leaves his feelings unchanged. That tension is more discriminating evidence than either calling every copy an empty object or treating every copy as the same already-described person.

### 5.2 A command right is not consent or complete identification

Greed's apostle is described as a holder of command authority over the copies, not a modern Cult office (`p-006#B0307–0325`). Subaru reports the two disguised tea drinks; Bilma infers that taking in a Witch's part supplies the mark. This is an expert inference from the reported procedure, not a controlled causal experiment or a new willing formal soul contract. Garfiel's command right is acknowledged; the complete route by which he gained it and the full contents of his trial remain unreceived. Frederica later reports one known childhood entry and the grandmother's rescue; she explicitly lacks knowledge of subsequent entries (`p-009#B0085–0094`).

Subaru recognizes the pressure attached to his questions and nonetheless commands secrecy, temporarily setting aside the guilt he feels (`p-006#B0353–0363`). His refusal to excuse his conduct merely because he expects to discard the state is important. So is the conduct itself. Piko actually obeys the local direction to leave (`p-006#B0369–0371`); this does not prove unrestricted control, conflicting-holder priority or freely chosen agreement. Later, Subaru spends the copies' bodies in combat and orders self-explosions. His final promise that the six may do as they like after reaching the tomb does not turn their compelled defense into prior volunteer consent (`p-016#B0434–0450`).

The surveillance network explains particular reports and interceptions without becoming omniscience. The initial guide's identity as Piko is Subaru's provisional naming, not a mark that securely identifies every identical face. Bilma's invitation to repeat the name question tomorrow is agreed but not answered in this segment (`p-006#B0378–0390`). Garfiel's rejection of names for the copies and insistence on the one grandmother express his defensive valuation, not an objective finding that all other bodies lack worth (`p-014#B0100–0103`).

## 6. Ordinary help and an evacuation that is not informed safety

### 6.1 Received care does not settle whom Subaru can trust with weakness

Emilia steadies herself after E017's first waking, sees Subaru's distress, wipes his tears and embraces him (`p-003#B0238–0267`). At night she thanks him for courage and asks him to continue cheering her (`p-006#B0441–0450`). This is actual help received before her later dependent state; the later catastrophe does not show that these exchanges were worthless. Subaru still conceals his qualification and successful trial, partly from fear of how she will interpret it (`p-004#B0078–0084`). His statement that he lacks the standing to seek Echidna is a judgment about preparation, not proof of a magically revoked qualification.

Otto waits with a book and a separate piece of lagmite ore, combines commercial calculations with concern, and offers to listen (`p-006#B0469–0490`). Subaru understands the offer but refuses because he knows the friend will risk himself. This is not a case in which nobody offered ordinary help. The new Otto is not automatically the Otto who declared friendship and died in E015. Concern is locally renewed; exact commercial settlement, employment and standing consent to dangerous assignments remain absent. Subaru's later belief that only Echidna is available narrows actual alternatives through his own protective withholding.

Actual rest, tea, sleep allocation and companionship remain explanatory. Subaru drinks because he is thirsty; the copy drinks for preference. He declines privileged bedding while others' age and vulnerability shape accommodation. He sleeps briefly, reunites with the living Patrasche and interprets her familiar response; none of these moments grants an animal the prior segment's memories (`p-006#B0060–0075/B0462–0499`). Chosen research and departure then follow partial recovery, not a permanent cure.

### 6.2 Agreement and misinformation coexist

The mansion's Frederica and Petra are actually alive when Subaru arrives. His absence is short on their clock and long in his experience. He delays visiting Rem because he fears losing the resolve to act (`p-007#B0003–0041`). Thanking Petra for the cloth is a true acknowledgment of help without giving her the RBD or self-stabbing account (`p-007#B0044–0049`). Her happiness is not consent to every use of the gift or knowledge of its later function.

Frederica agrees to evacuation, receives Rem into her arms and undertakes to protect the party. Subaru deliberately describes the threat as surviving Cult attackers, borrowing plausibility from the real earlier attack rather than revealing Elsa (`p-009#B0011–0027/B0110–0129`). The agreement is actual; its informational basis is defective. Meili and Elsa follow the targets rather than respecting the house as a fixed killing site (`p-011#B0027–0038`). Earlier action and a new route are not sufficient to make safety durable. The text does not reveal the commissioner, and Subaru's false Cult explanation is not evidence identifying one.

Petra negotiates a promised outing, Subaru accepts, and she names the commitment (`p-009#B0035–0039`). The child's request and his assent should not be erased merely because no adult romance is warranted. Her word for the outing comes from the surviving V03 date, not a forgotten segment. Subaru's quiet intention to make the promise anew after another return is not heard future consent or a memory already held by the next Petra (`p-009#B0045–0048`). Her reported death makes the obligation and the lost anticipated ordinary day more specific; it does not turn the promise into engagement.

Frederica also begins to articulate her own reason for leaving: making a place outside may be a way to create a possibility for her brother (`p-009#B0071–0081`). Her phrasing is tentative and partly discovered in speaking, not a completed plan that has already solved the community's future. Her request that Subaru help Garfiel is actually made, but Subaru avoids answering (`p-009#B0100–0107`). Her account of the old trial does not give him Garfiel's complete present motives, even when he confidently supplies what he thinks she hoped for.

## 7. Beatrice: a blank book, an actual plea and the limits of rescue

### 7.1 Asking is not sufficient when the available answer is intolerable

The first successful door and Beatrice's expectation of a visitor do not establish that she wants Subaru's particular rescue plan. Subaru insists that she should behave like the confident helper he remembers, then snatches her book despite resistance (`p-009#B0176–0189`). Discovering blank pages and hearing that they have remained unchanged for years strongly revises the old all-actions-directed claim (`p-009#B0195–0221`). It does not show that all earlier care was false. Indeed, his hope that she helped through her own will is newly plausible precisely because the supposed continuous instructions were absent.

Beatrice herself introduces the promised visitor, describes waiting for instructions that never identify that person, and asks Subaru to kill her (`p-009#B0237–0259`). She wants release from the bond; death is the means she names. Subaru throws the book and argues that a clear request for help would let him respond. She makes that request and reaches out. When he assumes a readily available rescue has followed, she returns to the death request and withdraws (`p-009#B0261–0328`). The exchange is not proof that asking for help has no value. It is proof that the listener cannot decide what the asking must mean solely from his desire to supply a satisfactory answer.

Beatrice says others came before: both people seeking her power and people moved by compassion (`p-009#B0366–0372`). Her accusation that Subaru left her alone for four hundred years is the unbearable demand of belated rescue, not a literal finding that he personally caused four centuries of confinement. She asks to be chosen first, to have the bond overwritten and to be held (`p-009#B0374–0402`). Subaru cannot honestly give that priority over Emilia and Rem. The proposal is a real relational condition; no demonstrated spell establishes that first rank is the sole universally valid technical method of contract replacement. Her insistence that she herself would condemn abandonment of her role shows internal commitment as well as constraint (`p-009#B0387–0393`).

The claimed prohibition on a spirit's self-death remains Beatrice's rule account (`p-009#B0408`). Her reason for entrusting the end specifically to Subaru is interrupted, not delivered in full (`p-009#B0417–0422`). The analysis cannot replace that missing answer with a complete love confession, a contractual designation or later knowledge.

### 7.2 Book categories and chosen protection

V11 Roswaal calls Beatrice's book a completed copy, one of two extant books nearest the original Wisdom (`V11 p-011#B0218–0225`). V12's recollection calls it short of completion (`p-009#B0159–0160`), and the later Roswaal calls his own the second genuine rather than degraded copy (`p-016#B0250–0257`). The older freeze accurately preserved its source. A possible reconciliation distinguishes completion relative to inferior Cult copies from proximity to full Wisdom, but that is an interpretive hierarchy, not permission to normalize away the wording tension. Beatrice's observed blank pages are also different from Petelgeuse's unreadable script. Neither the owner's belief that her future has ended nor another owner's confidence establishes a fully decoded, infallible predictive system.

Elsa explains Door Crossing's use of closed doors and defeats the available selection by leaving doors open; Beatrice's response and narration support the account (`p-011#B0020–0026`). That supplies the missing local access method without making all library shelter impossible. Beatrice then casts the large Shamac before Subaru can spend his incomplete Gate, sparing him and leaving an escape gap (`p-011#B0053–0069`). The spell is hers, not a newly successful safe use by him. She is carried out while asking to be released, yet holds his clothing; the action is neither a completed freely negotiated rescue nor evidence that she has no protective attachment.

Outside, she stands between Subaru and Meili, deploys Minya and shatters Elsa's body (`p-013#B0018–0057`). The apparent death does not last. Elsa is later intact while her clothes retain the damage, explicitly denying simple immortality and attributing her persistence to someone's blessing (`p-013#B0104–0110`). The observed repair is strong evidence; its complete nature, donor and limits remain unknown. Beatrice pushes Subaru clear of the returning attack, is fatally pierced and dissolves without ordinary blood. Her last magic coincides with the blue crystal's flash and his transfer (`p-013#B0081–0094/B0128–0159`). The preserved injury proves movement rather than RBD. Subaru still does not know exactly which part of the transfer the stone performed and which depended on her final act.

## 8. Roswaal, Emilia and the controlled production of dependence

### 8.1 A declaration can be real without being a healthy fulfillment

Emilia actually says that she loves Subaru and needs nothing else (`p-014#B0174–0177`; `p-016#B0002–0012`). The scene is not discarded because the speech is imaginary. It is dangerous because she has lost the ability to engage the surrounding reality and because exclusive dependence replaces the wider concerns of the person he values. Subaru wants the offered intimacy, yet recognizes that he has been promoted into the absence of Puck, not simply chosen after a freely developed reciprocal exchange (`p-016#B0025–0058`). His insistence that the moment is not genuine fulfillment should not become a universal verdict that Emilia can never love him or that every feeling she expresses is a lie.

Subaru's first snow assumption is corrected by temporal reasoning: resentment cannot explain weather that helped create the resentment (`p-016#B0065–0076`). He admits that his conclusion includes a wish to trust Emilia. Roswaal's later admission supplies stronger evidence than that wish (`p-016#B0143–0145`). The scheme deliberately exploits the half-elf's stigmatized appearance, Garfiel's ready accusation and Arlam's memory of the surviving earlier Puck cold wave (`p-016#B0297–0313`). A shared communal memory becomes usable evidence for blaming the wrong person. Prejudice here is an actively manipulated causal resource, not merely an abstract topic surrounding an individual breakdown.

The missing letter remains an actual delivery problem. Subaru placed it, but never checked receipt. Garfiel does not know of it; Emilia does not give the answer Subaru expects; Roswaal appears puzzled when asked (`p-014#B0079–0084/B0148–0156`; `p-016#B0324–0326`). This does not identify an interceptor or prove that a delivered letter alone would have prevented the collapse. Subaru's wish to spare distress is real, but an unverified message is not consultation.

### 8.2 Killing the obstacle is not proof of mutual acceptance

Ram says that she will stand before an attack on Roswaal. He uses that position to pierce her and Garfiel together (`p-016#B0148–0174`). His gentle pledge of his soul and her response do not establish informed advance consent to being killed. Garfiel tries to heal her while his own body pushes toward transformation; the rescue attempt continues after her fatal injury has already made it ineffective (`p-016#B0177–0194`). Roswaal crushes his head before full transformation. The passage supplies both Garfiel's skill and his choice to spend it on another person; it does not erase V11's different evidence that his full tiger form could speak, reason and close some wounds. The local attempt to heal Ram is not identical to the body's self-preservation response.

Roswaal says the deaths create privacy and an opening against Garfiel. He then claims Subaru feels no sadness because he expects to undo the result (`p-016#B0211–0235`). The narration explicitly gives Subaru anger, grief and other conflicting feelings. Roswaal identifies the belief in replacement, not the whole emotional truth. His book-based knowledge of repetition becomes information Subaru actually receives, unlike V11's private coda. It remains partial: he does not know that death is the trigger (`p-016#B0330–0345`). The conversation's absence of a black hand is an observed local nonpenalty, not global permission for causal RBD disclosure. Subaru's felt clutch is explicitly a shock-induced impression (`p-016#B0237–0241`).

Roswaal cannot test whether he has already been subject to a repeat and denies retaining this experience into the next version of himself (`p-016#B0345/B0401–0410`). He is not a second confirmed carrier of discarded history. His willing unresisted death to the Rabbit demonstrates how far his plan can spend his own current life without continuity; it does not establish his entire four-century identity, ultimate purpose or the truth of parallel-world replacement. His final prescription is to discard every attachment but one (`p-016#B0414–0425`). Subaru's later determination to keep the others is a material refusal of that prescription even while his means remain troubling.

### 8.3 The final lap pillow does not restore the earlier scene

Rabbit wounds, blood loss and cold leave Subaru dying. He reaches the tomb through compelled defense, then accepts Emilia's lap pillow (`p-016#B0434–0487`). Emilia does not notice the injuries or the blood; the narration distinguishes failure of perception from deliberate refusal to care (`p-016#B0489–0491`). Her desire for his exclusive effort still contains an objection to a Subaru who ignores everyone else (`p-016#B0483–0485`). Even in this narrowed state, the person is not reducible to a single demand.

Subaru knows danger is approaching but cannot tell Emilia to leave because he wants her company at his end (`p-016#B0493–0503`). This complicates his previous protection and her dependency without equating his choice with Roswaal's whole scheme. The kiss follows the loss of light in his eyes. E018 confirms that death prevented carrying the full final sensation (`p-018#B0011–0017`). A remembered narrative image and the illustration do not establish consciously received reciprocal consent at that terminal moment. The earlier V03 lap-pillow care remains real historical care; the present resemblance does not make this a repetition of its outcome.

## 9. The second trial and the proposed price of certainty

### 9.1 Constructed aftermaths can injure without proving their ontology

Subaru seeks Echidna and initially mistakes collapse for the requested invitation. The command instead presents an impossible present (`p-018#B0080–0110`). The first detailed scene is the aftermath of his old self-stabbing for Rem. Emilia grieves, Wilhelm attempts resuscitation and Ferris repairs the wound but reports the soul absent (`p-018#B0141–0213`). Those are represented acts in the constructed trial, not a new current-world medical test or a universal refutation of Ferris's earlier bounded revival account. The next detailed aftermath of the possessed Subaru's requested death gives Julius grief and a wish to have called him friend (`p-018#B0245–0288`). That utterance is not a new mutual declaration received by current Julius.

The cliff scene gives Ram anger and Beatrice tears without naming Subaru the promised visitor (`p-018#B0317–0342`). The Puck/Reinhard scene presents a drawn Dragon Sword, overwhelming victory, regenerative world imagery and the malicious limit of being only a hero (`p-018#B0346–0395`). These can inform Subaru's interpretation of how others value him. They are not verified later combat, a new power audit, restored current Rem or actual Felt hearing the news. The trial's unknown information and repeated grief make its status frightening, not certain. The prose keeps imagination and continuing worlds as alternatives (`p-018#B0415–0420`).

Echidna subsequently calls the examination constructed from remembered conditions and expressly denies that its represented world is thereby an independently actual one (`p-021#B0063–0065`). She then refuses the different inference that RBD itself therefore erases every world: time reversal and hypothetical parallel overwrite remain possible, with no method supplied to distinguish them (`p-021#B0069–0078`). This is a meaningful limit on the apparent expert. Neither Subaru's condemnation of himself nor Echidna's inability to decide grants the analyst a cosmological answer. Emotional injury is actual even where the truth of the depicted aftermath is unresolved.

### 9.2 Rem's remembered answer is more than a comforting likeness

The apparent Rem offers love, complete forgiveness and permission to hand everything over. Subaru nearly accepts. Remembering the earlier Rem's refusal to let him abandon his effort interrupts the offered kiss (`p-018#B0490–0560`). He distinguishes knowing a person from recognizing the shape and voice he wanted. His stated permission to show weakness only to Rem remains his restrictive self-model, not proof that Emilia, Otto or anyone else has never cared. Nor does rejecting blanket surrender mean that ordinary rest or shared responsibility is illegitimate.

Carmilla is actually identified as the apparent visitor; she denies physical transformation and attributes the appearance to what the viewer wants to see (`p-020#B0002–0018`). The illustration's blur therefore need not be a literal change of bodies or Rem's arrival. Her attention-capturing Faceless Bride affects Subaru during anger as well as desired comfort: he forgets breathing and his heartbeat before Echidna intervenes (`p-020#B0027–0038`). The danger does not become a completed new death/return. The person who supplied the image is also reluctant, deceived by Echidna's instructions and later capable of retaliating despite hesitant speech (`p-021#B0263–0287`). Timidity is not equivalent to consent or inability to oppose someone.

### 9.3 Truth, disclosure and the difference between a future and its path

Subaru first asks Echidna for the account that would let him forgive her. She offers that false account hypothetically and then refuses to pretend it was what happened: Carmilla's part was her instruction (`p-021#B0013–0033`). He has genuinely been helped by intervention without the choice of method becoming harmless. His decision to continue the conversation and refuse the tea is a bounded acceptance, not total trust or a new consumed cup (`p-021#B0047–0058`).

Echidna proposes a formal soul-linked partnership; Subaru moves toward acceptance, and Minerva interrupts before the joining gesture establishes a completed contract (`p-021#B0141–0196`). The proposed relationship is separate from the earlier limited memory agreement and the already acquired command mark. No inference that refusal abolishes those earlier conditions is warranted. Minerva then exposes the missing cost account. The familiar claim that Echidna must lend her own manifestation to each other Witch is explicitly admitted to be a lie; actual simultaneous presence disproves the restriction (`p-021#B0208–0219`). Her claims about dominion transfer or Sekhmet defeating the other five remain attributed, unperformed rules and comparisons.

The declared price is experiencing Subaru's unknowns, not a demonstrated extraction of memories. Carmilla distinguishes speaking truths from disclosing all relevant truths. Sekhmet supplies the crucial qualifier: eventually (`p-021#B0243–0297/B0316–0336`). Subaru can want the promised future while still needing to know whether the path is needlessly destructive. The long uninterrupted monologue (`p-023#B0001`) stages the pressure in form as well as argument. Echidna denies forcing returns or deliberately sending him down a wrong path, yet admits that curiosity can draw her away from the best path and offers to restore lost trust afterward. She promises support for his damaged heart while valuing the variations that can damage it. Her insistence that he needs her is not independently established by the availability of her knowledge.

Subaru does not reject every bargain containing self-interest. He asks about Beatrice. Echidna admits ordering the wait, without fixing the exact physical place, then explains that she wants to learn whom Beatrice chooses. There was no predetermined correct visitor and no deadline (`p-025#B0053–0085`). Beatrice was made for an unspecified earlier purpose and later assigned another; the book/library task and open-ended waiting supplied a life purpose as well as an experiment. Echidna's pleasure in all results, including four hundred years of no choice and a wish for death, makes the incompatibility concrete. The creator's unspecified original purpose and the complete contract terms remain open; neither is supplied from later canon.

Subaru refuses Echidna's formal offer and decides to take Beatrice's hand (`p-025#B0090–0101`). That is not a spell that saves Beatrice, a present accepted first-priority promise or a passed trial. The seventh Witch arrives before a further result. Typhon welcomes “Tella”; Echidna shows hatred; the veiled face is not newly exposed (`p-025#B0105–0122`). The scene leaves the encounter unresolved. It does not yet supply Envy's explanation, the full relations among names and embodiments or Subaru's next waking state.

## 10. Conditional person, relationship and ordinary-life increments

Subaru's V12 change is not a linear movement from selfishness to altruism or from instability to permanent recovery. He can refuse comforting dependency while seeking an unqualified confidant, defend the reality of another person's choice while coercing substitutes, and reject one promised future because of the people consumed along its way. His fear remains physiological. His guilt can acknowledge acts he truly chose and also expand into the unproved claim that every failure exists because he is inadequate. Reconstruction must preserve both the responsibility and the excess, rather than endorsing his self-condemnation or excusing the actual commands.
