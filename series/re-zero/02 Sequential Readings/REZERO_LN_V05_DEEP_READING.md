The V05 opening gives approximately two and a half months when Subaru looks back on arrival (`p-006#B0004`). It does not supply a precise common-world calendar. The reliable local anchors are three days since the rupture at the initial training/street sequence (`B0110`, `B0235`), the following morning's unrest warning, and testimony identifying the reset immediately after Rem's sales and Subaru's request for all the fruit (`p-019#B0073–0077`). The reference to four days' acquaintance at departure (`p-021#B0019`) does not justify inventing an additional restoration or exact elapsed-hour schedule.

E1's detour and inadequate carriage differ from E2's earlier direct-road availability. In E1, the next-day road is fog-blocked and all long-distance vehicles are allocated; in E2 an earlier available vehicle can use the road (`p-013#B0173–0197`; `p-019#B0198–0201`). Rem's care then forces a slower journey and overnight stop. Geography, timing, resources and the patient's condition change the choice set; these are not interchangeable replays.

The prologue's captive Subaru is located retrospectively inside E2 when chapter 5 repeats the introduction (`p-024#B0172–0195`). It is not an extra capture or an opening event before the host-house continuation. The first village visit similarly requires retrospective rereading: apparent emptiness, mud and obstructing objects become ignored corpses and blood (`p-016#B0227–0241`). The text stages Subaru's refusal to recognize, not supernatural disappearance and reappearance of villagers.

Rem's extended E2 focalization creates a second interpretive center. The reader learns what care costs, what she desires, what she resists, and how she fears that her earlier passive comfort failed him. She rejects Ferris's reduction of the collapse to public failure while noticing scent change without knowing its cause. Subaru cannot be granted this unspoken interiority merely because he was present in the carriage. Her perspective also lacks his death memories. Neither can serve as a transparent account of the other.

## 4. Subaru: the crisis needed to prove the self

### 4.1 Improvement without the kind of improvement he wants

Training produces better breakfalls, not a newly competitive swordsman. Wilhelm immediately points out their limited value against a killing cut and refuses the flattering fiction that wanting strength produces it (`p-011#B0004–0014`; `p-013#B0005–0022`). Subaru's knowledge that he cannot become Julius's equal in a few days does not make training purposeless; it does expose its use as activity that avoids the harder question of what he should do.

The same pattern survives in quieter competence. He continues literacy study, notices public commercial excitement, thanks the innkeeper once he needs assistance, constructs a workable private-carrier option, and recognizes the limits of his bargaining skill (`p-010#B0050–0061`; `p-014#B0044–0051`, `B0131–0135`). He is not globally incapable. But money without route knowledge, price knowledge, transport, a weapon or a willing expert does not create independence (`p-014#B0019–0034`). His dependence on Rem is materially demonstrated before it is romantically interpreted.

Two different evaluations unsettle him. Crusch treats effort and eventual evaluation as separable; Ferris accepts weak swordsmanship because another professional contribution supplies worth (`p-010#B0170–0175`; `p-008#B0159–0165`). Subaru can concede weakness as a fact yet cannot accept a useful identity that does not compete at the center of heroic status.

### 4.2 Good advice does not arrive unchanged

Crusch urges recipient-facing action: looking down and inward cannot deliver an act to another person. Her proposed standard is effort toward what one can actually do, not a guarantee of victory (`p-010#B0232–0251`). Subaru hears something else. The cue to consider his own contribution becomes an exclusive weapon that will prove him right to Julius, Reinhard and Emilia (`B0270–0287`). The next morning makes the distortion explicit: death and battle are welcome as the stage on which his value can be burned into others' eyes (`p-011#B0020–0025`).

Wilhelm's criticism interrupts this exuberance but does not settle it. Subaru responds to news of danger with relief at escaping the conversation and anticipation of the opportunity (`p-013#B0034–0053`). The carriage monologue reaches the adverse conclusion that Emilia ought to be in danger so he can establish his worth (`B0255–0268`). This is no longer merely failing to anticipate that she might need others. His self-model needs her lack; no evidence establishes his wish as the attack's cause or makes the ensuing dead deserved punishment.

V04's sincere care and recognition hunger remain simultaneous. Subaru tries to relieve Rem's fear, knows his assurances lack evidence, feels gratitude and attraction, and still assumes his previous successful rescue should make her yield (`p-013#B0112–0123`, `B0298–0308`). The narrower diagnosis is not “all help was fraud.” It is that genuine attachment is increasingly organized through a coercive demand to be necessary.

### 4.3 New understanding becomes another defense

Kadomon's prejudice helps Subaru understand the material force of Emilia's request for fair consideration (`p-008#B0061–0065`). Yet the thought quickly returns to the special person who alone knows her, then to resentment that she did not tell him everything and has treated him as peripheral (`B0072–0095`). The passage itself admits that he does not know her history or royal purpose and does not want to know how she currently regards him. Learning the obstacle does not constitute learning the person.

Otto supplies an important negative control. He supports Emilia because he recognizes the difficulty of being misunderstood, without first receiving Subaru's private rescue history (`p-016#B0028–0053`). This should comfort Subaru if his sole goal is her support. Instead he cannot thank Otto or digest his own discomfort. The conflict is diagnostic: another possible ally threatens the fantasy that only Subaru can stand with her. Otto's particular past remains unknown; no later explanation is needed to establish the present counterexample.

At the estate, Subaru initially converts the village into proof that he was valued. Recognition of Petra's corpse breaks that defense (`p-016#B0195–0204`, `B0241–0266`). He then searches for any survivor partly to be told that the tragedy is not his fault (`p-018#B0042–0068`). Rem's death is not a metaphysical consequence of his wish; the guilt he feels and the evasions he performs remain psychological evidence. The source's harsh inward critique must not erase the killers' agency.

### 4.4 Responsiveness is not recovery

E2 begins in collapse: disorientation, uncontrolled laughter and tears, inability to stand, and desperate attachment to a living Rem. Petelgeuse later claims Subaru is consciously feigning madness to obtain sympathy (`p-026#B0009–0030`). The narration does support self-protective refusal to hear and acknowledge sealed knowledge. It does not establish that the original distress, exhaustion or inability to integrate the deaths was unreal. Petelgeuse's boast about authentic madness is neither a clinical authority nor a complete account of Subaru.

Rem's calls and struggle gradually reach him; he finally speaks her name (`p-026#B0133–0166`). Her immediate mutilation and the bishop's mockery then produce another mode of responsiveness: hatred. He demands that he personally kill Petelgeuse, uses the enemy's remembered details to resist isolation, and keeps his awareness through pain (`B0206–0217`, `B0261–0302`). This is meaningful change in attention, not a return to a healthy baseline.

The E3 reunion confirms both sides. Subaru can answer, touch, accept comfort and feel joy that Rem lives; her response to her name matters precisely because he has experienced its absence (`p-028#B0171–0222`). Yet he converts restoration into a mission of killing, with no narrated negotiated plan or completed reparative conversation. The final shared walk explicitly juxtaposes the joined hands and opposed hearts (`B0230–0262`). Attachment and revenge coexist; the book does not award a recovery simply for the return of fluent speech.

## 5. Rem: refusal, possession and the limits of self-spending

### 5.1 Care need not deny a flaw

Rem can call Subaru pathetic and still choose to remain because she wants to (`p-006#B0204–0214`). Her affection is not proof that she cannot perceive his failures, and she is not merely obeying an order. She capably sells Kadomon's stock at the stated prices, protects Subaru from Ferris's influence, teaches and manages transport (`p-008#B0117–0133`; `p-010#B0004–0035`). These are distinct kinds of labor, not one abstract devotion.

Her comfort can also sustain avoidance. The narrator calls the lap-rest a muddy peace in which Subaru need not think (`p-006#B0228–0236`). Rem later admits that she enjoyed having him to herself despite knowing he was troubled; she wonders whether waiting passively to receive his pain was itself serving her possessiveness (`p-021#B0057–0072`; `p-023#B0002–0014`). That is her self-critique, not evidence that she caused a collapse whose actual precipitant she cannot know.

### 5.2 A real refusal delivered coercively

When danger is reported, Rem initially asks Subaru to honor treatment instructions. He invokes the children's rescue as precedent, pushes past her and announces a shared return without her agreement (`p-013#B0112–0123`). Her eventual outward compliance does not end the disagreement. At Fleur she covertly induces sleep through purported gate treatment, leaves him paid accommodation and money, and goes alone (`B0314–0355`, `B0377–0397`). The letter, entirely in the script he can read, asks understanding rather than forgiveness and promises to return (`p-014#B0010–0012`).

This is costly independent action against Subaru's immediate demand, not a loss of affection. It also overrides his agency through concealment and bodily influence. The parallel with Ferris is deliberately uncomfortable: she detects a therapeutic power being used to make him receptive, then uses mana and intimacy to bypass his refusal herself. Different ends and stakes matter; benevolent intention does not turn either intervention into informed consent.

Subaru reads the accessible letter as another verdict of worthlessness, not as proof that someone is planning for his continued life (`p-014#B0009–0027`). His interpretation does not erase her concern. Nor does his later tragedy automatically prove that her particular protective plan was the only sound alternative.

### 5.3 The other relationships inside the devotion

E2 broadens Rem beyond Subaru. His arrival has made her work more meaningful, eased standing beside Ram, changed her manner toward Roswaal and Beatrice, and increased conversation with Emilia (`p-023#B0058–0064`). This is genuine ordinary and cross-recipient development, not only a crisis declaration. It remains partly mediated by Subaru and does not establish self-worth independent of being needed.

Her account of Emilia is especially important. Intellectual acknowledgment of innocence coexists with an affective association between half-elves and the Witch Cult that destroyed her home. She had maintained a mechanical guest/servant distance; Emilia had seemed to avoid unnecessary contact in response (`B0045–0057`). Emerging shared interests and jealousy now make simple liking or disliking impossible. Rem does not become an uncomplicated embodiment of tolerance because Subaru values her. Ram's resentment at being told to rank Emilia above Roswaal is another privately received signal, not the same reason for distance (`B0046–0049`).

The sisters' synesthesia likewise has limits. Strong emotion may leak across long distances, but Rem's deliberate sending is more constrained by range and energy, and both must be awake (`p-013#B0061–0097`; `p-023#B0021–0022`). Rem's later interpretation that Ram suppresses a signal is not receipt of a full tactical report. Concern for Ram focuses her so strongly that she loses attention to the surrounding anomaly (`B0155–0168`). Attachment is a resource and a narrowing condition.

### 5.4 Rejecting the satisfying wrong outcome

Rem recognizes pleasure in Subaru's dependence even while knowing that this state is not what he wants (`p-021#B0047–0048`). She imagines escaping with him and Roswaal's money, then rejects the fantasy: it would burden Ram, violate entrusted loyalty and, most decisively, leave Subaru unrecovered for her benefit (`p-023#B0072–0103`). She can distinguish possessing his helpless presence from wanting the person who once reached her.

That distinction revises an all-enabling model, but not into completed autonomy. The same passage locates her value in serving and spending herself. The remembered gift is something she cannot repay even by exhausting everything she has (`p-019#B0166–0170`). Her refusal of Petelgeuse's cheap label for her feeling preserves ownership of its meaning, yet her offered return still consists of all she has received plus her entire self (`p-026#B0123–0126`). Her agency and her self-expenditure are not mutually exclusive findings.

### 5.5 Combat, apparent death and the last act

The ambush's apparently fortunate survival of Subaru is retrospectively credited to Rem throwing him clear at the cost of her own wounds (`p-024#B0051–0054`). Horn amplification, flail work, ice and improvisation are formidable, but combined physical and magical pressure defeats her. Huma's barrier does not fully stop the fire; her remaining body is not an unlimited resource (`B0073–0114`). Her anguish when Subaru is taken even includes the lost possibility of dying for him (`B0131–0139`), exposing the danger within an otherwise real rescue effort.

She survives that battle and reaches the cave. Calling her dead at the burned apron would be premature; the cult's own report says her fate is unknown (`p-024#B0235`). Later Petelgeuse and Subaru again treat her ruined body as a corpse. She nevertheless reaches him and uses the expansion of frozen blood to break his handcuffs, injuring but freeing his wrists (`p-026#B0334–0370`). The final explicit death follows the request that he live (`B0388–0394`). This extraordinary last expenditure is not sufficient evidence of a repeatable resurrection ability or a second distinct death-and-revival mechanism.

Her final rescue succeeds at its immediate objective. It does not deliver a full moral lesson into Subaru unchanged: he hears the life-preserving request, but carries hatred forward. Rem in E3 has none of the dying woman's experience. Her restored body and affectionate response cannot be treated as mutual remembrance of the cave.

## 6. The host house: standards, knowledge and misrecognition

### 6.1 Crusch's generosity has boundaries without becoming a trap

Crusch separates treatment under a preselection contract from future dealings between public rivals. Departure ends the existing arrangement rather than entitling Subaru to reopen it at will (`p-013#B0127–0138`). He experiences this as betrayal and sabotage. Her next act defeats that simple model: she respects his decision and offers the fastest available transport, with Wilhelm explaining it as a parting provision for incomplete treatment and training (`B0173–0188`, `B0204–0239`). Respecting a choice does not certify its wisdom; a contract boundary does not prove imprisonment.

In E2, Crusch considers curse or political intimidation as possible causes, accepts Ferris's negative examination and apologizes for being unable to help further (`p-019#B0115–0136`). Her trust in him does not give either access to Subaru's discarded history. Their compassion and error can coexist. Rem then leaves of her own initiative, not because Crusch has expelled a useless patient.

The medical consideration remains exact-content OPEN. V04 Ferris explicitly said that an intangible, already learned price could not be returned; V05 speakers continue discussing the entitlement to receive contractual consideration (`p-019#B0153–0156`). This later wording does not establish a new payment, a refund, or that the prior information was never supplied. With no contract text or content disclosed, the current model preserves the earlier explicit testimony and records the limits rather than harmonizing a hidden bargain.

### 6.2 Ferris's specialty, influence and value judgment

Actual treatment circulates water mana through Subaru's damaged gate. It is unpleasant, induces nausea and fatigue, and remains an incomplete course. The damage is directly related to forcing depleted mana through doping; the book does not quantify a permanent destruction caused by Julius's beating (`p-008#B0170–0186`). Ferris's weak swordsmanship and strong medical contribution also expose a viable division of labor that Subaru cannot yet comfortably accept.

Ferris admits using treatment to influence Subaru, with a small concern for him, a friend's feelings and primary loyalty to Crusch among the motives (`p-010#B0004–0010`). The friend is not named in this exchange. Rem explains that entrusting one's mana makes the speaker's words easier to accept (`B0033–0035`). This supports real coercive influence, not an inferred complete permanent personality rewrite. Crusch is not shown ordering it.

E2 exposes another limitation. Ferris can repair bodily wounds but cannot explain or cure the mental collapse. His view that one apparently lacking a will to live need not be treated expresses a severe professional ethic; Rem perceives seriousness rather than pure personal malice (`p-019#B0085–0109`). But he does not know the losses, death or restoration that produced the presentation. The judgment is made under an information deficit, not narrator-certified evidence of culpable surrender. His aptitude for increasing receptivity is not equivalent to the ability to repair trauma, and his later wish for Subaru's recovery complicates an all-cruel model (`p-021#B0010–0012`).

### 6.3 Wilhelm and the refusal of quick transformation

Wilhelm's controlled training does not humiliate by inflicting major wounds. He repeatedly offers to stop, then makes the distinction between learning to fall and fighting to win (`p-006#B0030–0038`; `p-011#B0035–0043`). His claim of ordinary talent is paired with half a life of practice, not an inspirational promise that a single realization can produce mastery (`p-013#B0011–0026`). His wife has occupied his thoughts; her present status and their history are not disclosed. Subaru's initial assumption of a currently happy marriage remains an assumption.

The contrast with Subaru is not that desire and emotion have no place in skill. Wilhelm explicitly denies usually swinging with an empty mind. What matters is sustained work rather than the demand that a beautiful story make the answer encouraging. His regrets about cruel lies and an earlier moment remain bounded hints (`p-013#B0007–0009`; `p-021#B0017–0024`). No later biography is imported.

### 6.4 Reinhard's attempted repair and its limit

Reinhard visits, apologizes for failing to intervene, calls both men friends, discloses Julius's sanction, and offers mediation (`p-006#B0116–0170`). Subaru recognizes the sincerity and rejects the proposed settlement. For him, calling the duel meaningless and proposing to put it behind them removes its irreducible pride stakes (`B0171–0185`). That response is evidence of his need to preserve meaning in injury, not proof that Reinhard's good intent is false.

Neither party supplies the whole causal account. Reinhard's expectation that Julius regrets the duel is his inference; Subaru doubts it. The preemptive protective rationale remains undisclosed to Subaru. His new knowledge of punishment changes one belief about institutional impunity without magically resolving humiliation or creating consent to the original harm.

## 7. Politics beyond the assembly and the institution of fanaticism

### 7.1 Posted equality and unequal public knowledge

The selection notice gives candidates and the broad schedule without explaining the jewel's qualification rule. Kadomon can rank familiar names and question unfamiliar origins while not knowing Crusch's full independence speech (`p-006#B0305–0312`; `p-008#B0001–0017`). Formally even publication does not give everyone the same recognition, credibility or starting position. The official notice has not reached Fleur when rumors already shape transport decisions (`p-014#B0067–0071`).

Kadomon is concerned, generous and affectionate toward family; he also voices a half-elf slur and identifies feared appearance with danger. His apology for speaking excessively does not retract the underlying prejudice (`p-008#B0019–0058`). The account of the Witch's four-hundred-year history is inherited social knowledge and fear, not a witnessed complete history. His capacity to recognize environmental causes of resentment does not dissolve the fear that maintains exclusion.

Otto's sympathy prevents universalizing Kadomon's stance. It also shows that there can be support for Emilia outside Subaru's exclusive claim. Conversely, sympathy is not proof that a merchant has become a sworn retainer or will accept every risk. Otto's request to turn back at the frightened ground dragon is a practical boundary. After initially grabbing him, Subaru accepts it, apologizes, lets him keep the fee and proceeds alone (`p-016#B0085–0118`). This is a real exception to his pattern of overriding refusal.

### 7.2 Money, trade and organized resources

The traveling merchants do not simply lack benevolence. Routes, cargo, capital demand and local danger make a short paid detour less attractive than longer-term opportunities. Arms prices are rising in the capital; Otto has exchanged iron goods for oil just as his intended northern market becomes inaccessible (`p-014#B0054–0069`, `B0182–0189`). He reports a Gusteko border closure and guesses a selection connection; the causal explanation is not verified policy. Rem's money is enormous enough to solve his loss, but its availability does not give Subaru the driver's expertise or erase risk.

Crusch is actively collecting people, goods and vehicles for a coming event related to Wilhelm's service, not yet named (`p-010#B0094–0106`; `p-019#B0198`). That is concrete capacity and operational preparation, not proof that her dragon-independence program has been implemented. Her correction of the popular first-battle story—she repelled rather than killed the beast and regards defying her father as shameful—also separates successful reputation from her own evaluation of process (`p-010#B0111–0126`). She says she is twenty (`B0135`).

Marriage is a real institutional constraint. Candidate marriages are prohibited during the selection; postselection proposals and private advance arrangements remain possible (`p-010#B0131–0159`). Crusch first leaves the exception unsaid while teasing Subaru, then apologizes and supplies it. No candidate engagement is thereby established. Nor does the rule specify the missing electoral mechanism.

Felt's next planned movement is reported by Reinhard: his family estate, education and training of new employees (`p-006#B0130–0133`). It supplies neither a new Felt command nor her independent account of consent. Anastasia's expansion is merchant reputation; Priscilla's widowhood recurs in discussion. Neither gains a new tested policy outcome or universal metaphysical endorsement.

### 7.3 Petelgeuse: performance does not exclude organization

The self-identified Witch Cult bishop of Sloth performs extreme bodily distortion and self-injury, yet can issue precise operational instructions. He receives reports, reallocates survivors of a damaged finger unit, orders ambush-site cleanup and survivor pursuit, reports prior clearing of other people, abandons a cave and retains a scheduled trial day (`p-024#B0212–0248`; `p-026#B0218–0231`). Commands and reports demonstrate organization without proving every order completed. Fanaticism is not the absence of logistics.

His categories are self-sealing. A ground dragon is praiseworthily diligent while working, legitimately killed for obstructing the operation, then contemptibly slothful because dead (`p-024#B0223–0230`). Rem is praised for devotion, mortally violated, treated as dead, mocked for failure, and made an accusation against Subaru. These moves do not reveal a consistent objective moral law. They reveal the power to redefine every result as evidence for the speaker's framework.

He senses concentrated favor on Subaru and speculates about Pride. He claims acquaintance with the other bishops and says his Gospel records neither Subaru nor this day's events (`p-024#B0255–0265`). The black book is physically present; its content and authority are mediated through him. Unrecorded does not objectively mean unimportant, and survival by rescue does not prove membership. His commands do establish that trial preparation preceded Subaru's capture, not what the trial is or whom every attack ultimately serves.

The visible cult gives a present institutional object to Rem's remembered attackers. It does not identify the mabeast handler, Elsa's employer, Garfiel, or Roswaal's private Dragon target. Similar stakes, Witch vocabulary and adjacency do not supply an evidentiary bridge.

## 10. Rival explanations and falsifiers

1. **Disaster vindicates Subaru's indispensability.** The danger is genuine, but he arrives too late, lacks crucial knowledge and repeatedly cannot save anyone alone. Need for action is not proof that his earlier entitlement or refusal of care was right. An informed cooperative plan that respects another's choice would be a new test, not retroactive vindication.
2. **All affection is selfish possession.** Rem rejects an escape that would secure her exclusive comfort, and Subaru can release Otto from danger and appreciate living Rem. These counterexamples defeat a total fraud model. The narrower issue is how care becomes entangled with exclusive value, dependency and avoidance.
3. **Rem has completed an autonomy arc.** Her refusal, wider ordinary relations and rejection of helpless possession are material changes. Self-worth through service, the unrepayable gift and willingness to exhaust every part of herself remain explicit. Durable self-regard under limits and other relationships would strengthen a broader development claim.
4. **Ferris is either only malicious or medically infallible.** He heals many, has a distinct contribution and later hopes for recovery; he also manipulates a patient and makes severe will-to-live judgments without the actual trauma history. A later account of his motives or successful mental care cannot be supplied now.
5. **Petelgeuse's insight makes his diagnosis authoritative.** He identifies a defensive evasion that the interior narration supports, but uses it to blame the victim for violence he commits. Accurate local observation does not validate his account of love, culpability, madness, Gospel or identity.
6. **The same endpoint proves deterministic repetition.** Rem's location, the children's body locations, route, arrival timing, captivity and estate destruction differ. Similar widespread death is not an unchanged script. What would remain fixed under informed intervention is still untested.
7. **The giant beast is definitively named by familiar imagery.** Prior Puck evidence makes the hypothesis strong. A direct name, recognition, witnessed transformation or disclosed contract would be the needed next evidence; no such confirmation occurs here.
8. **Responsiveness and handholding complete recovery.** The final narration expressly joins warmth to hatred and distinguishes their directions. Later sustained deliberation, recipient-facing care or mutual repair could revise this endpoint; they cannot be assumed from an answered name.
9. **Subaru's guilt caused the massacre.** He wishes for an opportunity and later feels culpable. Cult organization, weapons and actual killers supply a different causal plane. The book's critique of instrumentalizing danger does not make every imagined catastrophe an enacted spell or remove the assailants' responsibility.
10. **Unshown means absent forever.** Emilia, Beatrice, Roswaal and other candidates lack new direct action in parts of this volume. This bounds the present reading; it is not evidence that they have no independent activity, no surviving future, or no later testimony.
