## 0. Governing claim and evidence boundary

V13 changes the terms of help without making help innocent. Subaru begins to accept that his life matters to people other than himself, lets Otto hear a crisis he had tried to carry alone, and wins a battle he could not have made possible without other agents. But the volume repeatedly separates what a helper wants to give from what the other person has actually asked for. Emilia does not want her trial taken away. She wants someone beside her effort and, later, a particular hand held until a particular morning. Subaru's encouragement is sincere and his departure has a caring purpose, yet neither fact fulfills that requested promise. Emilia's eventual affection for the writing he leaves does not erase her insistence on apology.

The same distinction prevents collective victory becoming a new version of the solitary savior's fantasy. Otto is not Subaru's obedient instrument. He helps devise the wager, disagrees with Subaru's protective withholding, chooses an unasked delay battle and keeps some cooperation terms secret even after surviving. Ram joins through her own judgment and maintains her attachment to Roswaal while pursuing her own method. Puck acts after ending Emilia's contract, under private terms the narrative does not deliver. Patrasche remembers her own first-day defeat and chooses the final charge. These agents enlarge Subaru's possibilities precisely because he does not possess their entire histories, motives or plans.

Garfiel's conversion is correspondingly more than a defeated antagonist conceding that the hero was right. He admits defeat before admitting his interpretation was wrong. He distinguishes those questions explicitly. His trial then changes what he can hear in a remembered departure, and what he thinks his mother's love meant. The result is a decision to help people who need him, not proof that his earlier injuries never mattered or that the future welfare of his grandmothers is guaranteed. His new followership preserves a condition on that future. Nor does a reconstructed Frederica become the actual sister's newly delivered testimony.

The volume also makes two kinds of inaccessible experience visible without equating them. Otto's childhood voices once prevented family language from reaching him; written accommodation and later filtering made a shared world possible. Subaru recognizes friendship only after his way of protecting Otto has excluded him. Emilia's missing memories, meanwhile, make the continuity of her own identity frightening. Subaru applies a lesson from his mother to that fear, but still wants to look impressive and cannot yet provide a complete answer for himself. These are particular histories of understanding and misunderstanding, not a clinical diagnosis, a universal cure by confrontation or an equation of every pain.

## 4. Seven Witches: receiving valuation without accepting their bargain

### 4.1 Name, face and claimed history do not collapse into one identity proof

Carmilla distinguishes the hostile Envy from the person she calls that girl; Typhon's familiar Tella address remains personal, not a complete taxonomy. Echidna attributes a second personality to an incompatible factor (`p-001#B0047–0075`). The analysis can report this fictional explanation without converting it into a clinical diagnosis or an experimentally established law governing every Witch factor. The Witches initially do not even know which personality they face.

Satella's affection produces relief before Subaru consciously accepts anything. He objects that he has no shared history that would justify the bond. Her later claims about bringing light, companionship and affection meet his explicit lack of recollection; an involuntary recognition-like feeling does not independently certify a prior encounter or time loop (`p-001#B0102–0135`; `p-003#B0014–0018`). The face becomes visible only near departure. Silver hair, violet eyes and likeness carry narrative weight but do not settle possession, complete genealogy or every relation between Emilia, Satella and the earlier shadow.

The requested futures differ. Satella asks to be killed someday; Subaru promises to save her (`p-003#B0311–0327`). Their hand contact and departure do not transform those different statements into agreed killing terms or a completed rescue. Nor is that hand the formal hand-contract Echidna had offered.

### 4.2 A right to choose and a life worth protecting are not the same principle

Minerva presses the grief other people would experience; Subaru hears this through a fear that they are accusing him of performing his suffering for attention, and Minerva explicitly denies that reduction (`p-001#B0148–0158`). His larger claim that only he pays is already contradicted by spent copied bodies and by the people who value him. Yet the scene does not simply place seven benevolent therapists opposite an irrational patient.

Subaru bites his tongue. Typhon protects his chosen act; Minerva attempts bodily repair, discounts some mental-wound reasoning and harms Typhon while intervening; Sekhmet restrains Minerva in support of the opposed choice. Typhon's fracture of Minerva's arm is not an objective moral verdict. Minerva later heals that arm herself. Daphne's coffin can restrain without its own deliberative head, making it useful in the local conflict; this is not a proved universal immunity or all-Witch power ranking (`p-003#B0026–0051/B0129–0150/B0182–0210`).

The decisive change is Subaru's actual wish to live. He need not select only unending self-erasure or giving up everyone else. This third course is a declared direction and an enacted acceptance of some help, not proof he will never risk himself again. He later explicitly leaves RBD possible (`p-003#B0129–0170/B0230–0294`; `p-010#B0078`). His later gate sacrifice and pressure on others belong in the same model as this real change.

### 4.3 Familiar voices are effective aid with particular provenance

Kenichi, Naoko, Julius, Wilhelm, Beatrice, Rem and Emilia are heard through familiar voices, not brought into the tea as actual current recipients (`p-003#B0085–0104`). Carmilla's explanation of this aid and her debt motive occur during a Witch-side exchange while Subaru is absorbed in his own distress. Later thanks for the voices establishes a relevant receipt, not complete access to every private causal aside. Other Witches' local immunity to her effect is not a universal test of everyone under every feeling.

Subaru refuses the renewed formal offer despite acknowledging genuine assistance. Echidna's useful disclosure about Garfiel's outside fear and first trial is actual; it does not make her disinterested or omniscient. He thanks different helpers without accepting a common moral framework (`p-003#B0237–0276`). The volume ends by exposing her personal malice toward Emilia, a direct counterexample to Subaru's earlier confidence that the examiner would not give an impossible or unfair challenge.

## 5. Real care, mistaken withholding and the patron who commissioned the attack

### 5.1 Patrasche's rescue is not an ordinary mount's unlimited service

Subaru wakes outside the tomb. Patrasche's blood, damaged clothes/dragging traces and narration establish her costly rescue, while Otto reports her prior restlessness and departure. The rescue is current evidence; it does not retrospectively identify every older unexplained waker. Otto then qualifies his role: communication is not exact translation. His approximation of her embarrassment and concern is an interpreted nonhuman response, not literal human speech or romantic assent (`p-005#B0015–0071`).

Subaru cries when he begins to understand her care. Otto sees that weakness too. This matters against the earlier self-rule that only Rem may see it, without requiring the earlier rule to have been insincere or the new openness permanent. Otto's continued commercial interest and conditional possibility of leaving are expressly real limits, not exposed lies beneath pure friendship (`p-005#B0071–0106`).

Subaru nevertheless expects the current Otto to act like the man who saved him in E015, using his own retained history. The new friend address is an initiative, not proof Otto remembers that older state. Later current mutual friendship must be established by the new words and actions actually delivered.

### 5.2 Actual entry tests separate lost standing from bodily courage

Subaru attempts the tomb and vomits/retreats under rejection. Narration confirms lost trial standing; later narration attributes it to the Witch's displeasure. The practical fact is stronger than mere deservingness anxiety. But it is not an absolute solid wall: he later enters through continuing pain, citing his weak gate for a less destructive reaction than Roswaal's. The causal explanation remains bounded to that account and the observed difference, not a matched fresh experiment on Roswaal (`p-005#B0163–0197`; `p-019#B0007–0018`).

The facility later opens through the original blue stone and Alma acknowledges apostle standing. Trial qualification and apostle access are therefore not the same permission. Neither surviving permission automatically proves a commander's priority over another holder.

### 5.3 Roswaal's admission has a defined scope

Roswaal explicitly admits commissioning the mansion attack, and Subaru actually receives it (`p-005#B0315–0333`). This is a genuine answer to the commissioner question, not a mere new suspicion. But Subaru's subsequent expansion to the badge theft, village attack and entire recruitment script is marked by his own inferential language (`p-007#B0054–0068`). No complete new confession establishes all those links.

Roswaal wants Subaru to override Emilia's will in order to save her life. Subaru objects, even while lacking a working alternative. Roswaal's declared impossibility of saving both sites is his proposition to force a single attachment, not an independently proved theorem. His four-century-eye claim reaches Subaru and creates an identity problem; no soul-transfer mechanism is shown.

Subaru's disclosure of lost qualification genuinely surprises Roswaal. The patron can plan around repetition without knowing carried feelings, every present divergence or the death trigger. No new RBD disclosure test validates global safety, and no lack of visible penalty during partial discussion supplies the missing mechanism.

## 6. Shared planning, differentiated representatives and unshared subplans

### 6.1 The wager is an accepted contest, not an accomplished moral settlement

Subaru proposes making this state the last contest rather than treating another retry as rescue. He does not swear never again to use RBD. Winning means saving both sites and obtaining Roswaal's abandonment of the book for Emilia's future; losing means obedience. Roswaal actually accepts and announces snow and mansion attack three days hence (`p-010#B0054–0069/B0073–0117`). No hand seal, soul ritual or magically tested enforcement is shown.

Subaru distinguishes practical future cooperation from forgiveness. He explicitly says he will not forgive Roswaal, while the patron himself calls an attempted evil evil. The fact the current fatal harm could be prevented would not erase the pain Subaru retains from discarded states, require Ram or other victims to forgive, or make the wager already won. The bare face and requested clown makeup change presentation, not a diagnosed inner identity or healed moral relation.

Otto helped devise the plan. His contribution is not revealed by pretending Subaru thought of everything. Ram also distinguishes her channel: she saw the wager through Clairvoyance but could not hear it (`p-012#B0021–0034/B0073–0084`). Narration grants her awareness of a bet, not the exact linked person's identity, a complete audio transcript or the RBD trigger. Later Ram and Otto agree to a delay battle without telling Subaru. Their cooperation terms are not entirely disclosed even afterward.

### 6.2 Alma: asking a person rather than exploiting an available command

The facility opens after trial qualification is lost. Subaru can still call on apostle standing. When the representative offers command as a way to compel truthful information, he refuses: he wants to speak to a person, not interrogate a copied vessel (`p-012#B0135–0178`). This is an actual changed choice against V12's deliberate secrecy command and spent copy bodies. It is not complete freedom from coercion: his discussion of liberation still contains an asymmetry of force if met with force.

Subaru initially assumes every representative opposes liberation because his retained captivity supplies that pattern. The actual interlocutor says otherwise. She accepts releasing the barrier while individuals decide whether to leave. Later, when Subaru finally asks her name, he receives Alma. Her refusal of outsider pity is equally important. Subaru can offer help if she wants change; he does not get to certify her unhappiness merely because he has learned her origin. The civil-registration joke does not establish an actual law, implemented citizenship or emancipation (`p-012#B0188–0212/B0252–0272`).

Alma attributes limited daily operating time to mana/pseudo-Od embodiment and explains the first four representatives' rotation. Shima has been removed from that duty for ten years after entering the tomb to rescue child Garfiel against the creator's oath. Alma has not met her since and admits uncertainty about her motives. These are attributed accounts with different levels of access, not interchangeable pieces of one universal copy memory (`p-012#B0244–0297`).

### 6.3 A shared grandmother office is not a single individual

Bilma, Alma, Delma and Shima are the named representatives; Meyer is the crystal-contained original. Delma later joins Shima in front of the tomb. The earlier provisional Piko address and every old forest/caretaker appearance are not retrospectively identified merely because more names are known. Garfiel treats the first four as the same grandmother role while also distinguishing the separated Shima. The family arrangement is real and complicated, not proof there is only one subject (`p-027#B0002–0020`).

His treatment of ordinary copies is different. He calls them dolls and uses the command right without the restraint he reserves for his grandmothers. During the search he explicitly resists compelling Shima herself, but commands ordinary bodies to find her and Emilia (`p-015#B0041–0044/B0175–0177`). The distinction demonstrates his selective moral boundary; it does not prove the bodies he devalues have no interests.

Copy surveillance is also concrete rather than omniscient. Reports from stationed 'eyes' captured the group's original arrival. The absence of a Shima sighting leads Garfiel to infer a blind spot near the facility, not to receive magical coordinates (`p-015#B0199–0209`). By contrast, Otto uses living creatures' reports and environmental cooperation. Both information systems require placement, interpretation and limits.

## 7. Emilia: requested support, memory continuity and the unexcused promise

### 7.1 Asking her past does not mean he has disclosed his own

Emilia first offers Subaru comfort and hears his proposal to substitute for her trial. She refuses substitution and says what she wants: his presence beside her effort, watching and cheering, not removal of the task (`p-007#B0084–0161`). He leaves without a sufficient answer and later judges himself unchanged. That total self-condemnation is not this reading's verdict; he has actually received care, but has repeated a recognizable failure.

He subsequently asks whether she is willing to share the trial past. She begins. This consultation is new and meaningful. It is also not full reciprocal disclosure: Subaru intentionally hides his own first-trial success and the tea, misattributing his information (`p-012#B0352–0376`). Later he speaks of gratitude for the examiner without telling Emilia or Garfiel he himself attempted the trial. Their uncertain interpretation of that claim must remain possible.

Emilia's chronology is explicitly approximate. She recalls being six or seven, frozen roughly a century and awake for six or seven years; her body grew during that interval. Subaru selects numbers to compute114 chronologically, an eighteen-like appearance and fourteen-like experiential age (`p-012#B0392–0411`). Those are focal comparisons using uncertain inputs, not an exact birth record, psychiatric measurement, universal elf growth law or test of consent capacity.

Her political purpose is newly delivered. People remain frozen in her forest, and Roswaal brought the insignia and promised royal power could make their rescue possible. She fears that a personal goal is selfish beside supposedly noble candidates. Subaru answers that a smaller number of beneficiaries does not invalidate the aim and that her public fairness can be genuine too (`p-012#B0447–0474`). The answer need not erase the patron's manipulation, establish all rival candidates' true goals, prove the magical thaw method or make her solely a pawn recruited for Subaru. That last claim is Subaru's inference.

### 7.2 Puck leaves; a memory lid opens; the complete cause remains undelivered

Emilia cannot recall what she saw in the trial. Narration treats the missing memory as real, not an unwilling confession. Subaru's belief that Echidna would not administer an impossible trial is his hypothesis, later challenged by the actual examiner's hostility. Received comfort brings real sleep, not a newly tested aroma intervention (`p-012#B0423–0439/B0479–0489`).

Subaru provokes Puck by putting his hands at sleeping Emilia's neck. The source explicitly says he does not tighten them. Puck's frost actually injures his hands and a voice comes from the green neck crystal. The intrusion is therefore actual even though a strangling or death is not (`p-012#B0522–0541`). His ensuing private interview is not delivered before the viewpoint shifts. The narrative gap must not be filled from later knowledge or presumed clauses.

Emilia hears a direct thought-voice, sees a diminished Puck and experiences the crystal cracking. He cancels their contract unilaterally. She faces a constrained, abrupt choice under partial information, not an already negotiated informed release from every consequence. The crystal splits and loses its light, and narration confirms the contract is gone (`p-013#B0017–0063`; `p-015#B0001–0010`). This does not prove Puck's soul annihilation: he later helps from the other stone.

Memories really return, but the cause and original sealer are still questions Emilia asks. The silver-haired woman becomes Fortuna, explicitly mother-like rather than biological mother established; the man's identity and the name Juice are not fully explained. The dream shows protective concealment, apology and professed love. It is one source of Emilia's 'liar' refrain, not proof she has recovered her entire past or caused everything she fears (`p-015#B0110–0138/B0241–0249`; `p-019#B0070/B0182–0186`).

### 7.3 The empty hand has a specific obligation behind it

Emilia initiates the handhold to verify herself amid unfamiliar memories. She asks Subaru to hold it and stay until morning; he agrees. Later she wakes to an empty hand while the moon still precedes the promised morning (`p-015#B0095–0108/B0130–0138`). Otto's later report that Subaru held her hand all night is a report, not a reason to overwrite her observed absence. The tomb conversation explicitly confirms Subaru did release her hand.

She does not merely ask for gentler reassurance. She asks why he will not be angry: does his endless consolation mean he expected her failure? The text allows care to relieve immediate pain and still intensify the fear of being underestimated. This does not establish anger as universally healthier care; it establishes her particular question (`p-019#B0027–0054`).

Subaru's harsh answer actually wounds her. He then avows love, refusing to make failure the reason to abandon her. Those sentiments are genuine, but narration also calls their argument childish and unproductive. Emilia rejects being treated as a doll who should make the face he wants. She says she did not ask him to keep hurting himself and asks again why he broke what she did request. He admits the breach but refuses to explain why (`p-019#B0083–0178`). A love declaration cannot be substituted for that unanswered accountability.

### 7.4 A live kiss and a transferred maternal lesson do not settle every term

Subaru remembers Naoko's principle about not deciding a life from its beginning or middle and applies it to Emilia's fear of becoming someone else as memories return. He passes on the principle without identifying the speaker as his mother. This is a genuine transferred lesson, not a real parent's present reply. Subaru himself says the homework is not fully understood (`p-019#B0182–0198`; `p-021#B0001–0007`).

He explicitly does not know whether that is resignation or deliberation (`p-019#B0201–0217`). The analysis must not replace this with enthusiastic verbal agreement or invent an unequivocal spoken refusal. The source licenses a live physical kiss with uncertain assent, not a settled universal consent rule.

For Emilia it is the first; Subaru compares it with the death-tasting kiss he retains from the old state. This difference does not give her the older memory. She later asks to postpone their important conversation until the trials and other problems are done, and he agrees (`p-027#B0083–0105`). Her partial understanding of his stated reason, blushing or affectionate care is not already a reciprocal love confession or an accepted multi-partner arrangement.

### 7.5 The wall actually reaches her, but the apology is still owed

Garfiel sees Subaru's private encouragement before its intended recipient. Emilia then finds Puck drawings and childlike local writing covering the chamber, including encouragement, expectations and affection. She infers that yesterday's absence was used for this effort, in the period he would not explain. The material message finally gives her a reason; it does not retroactively keep the promise (`p-027#B0120–0153`).

She touches the writing affectionately and explicitly refuses forgiveness until he apologizes. The scene is neither a completed oral apology nor evidence that all the previous objections were misunderstanding. Subaru has found a way to support the trial he cannot accompany, and still has something to repair.

Emilia enters her renewed first trial. The remembered forest is not yet the final snow scene. Echidna appears in a place where she was not an original resident, and narration identifies personal hostility toward Emilia rather than generic half-elf prejudice. Her abuse contributed to earlier failed attempts, but was not their whole cause. The hostile daughter label does not establish literal genealogy. Emilia answers in her own name and chosen ice-witch description, borrowing Subaru's defiant gesture and reclaiming the troublesome-woman label. ## 8. Otto and Ram: chosen labor, social accommodation and finite bodies

### 8.1 Otto's childhood changes the causal account of his present choices

Otto's birth blessing first makes voices incomprehensible and inescapable. It prevents human speech and affection from reaching him in their intended form. His parents seek help, and the family attention he remembers as shifting toward his brothers coexists with remembered love. The fictional account does not authorize a diagnosis or a claim that real deaf people lack feeling.

His elder brother recognizes another channel and teaches writing. The written thanks moves his parents; their tears move him. Communication accommodation, family action and his own work precede later mastery. By eight he can filter; by ten he decodes nonhuman voices' meaning and recognizes the blessing. These are distinct changes, not a power he has consciously understood since birth (`p-016#B0003–0037`).

The social failures remain part of the model. To avoid making his younger brother a liar, he proves the gift by summoning town insects, causing stigma rather than trust. At fifteen he uses it to rebut a false sexual accusation, exposes the powerful daughter's other partners and is targeted by her hired assassin. Flight and his father's network lead to apprenticeship, then traveling work at sixteen. This old assassin is not identified as Elsa or linked to the mansion commission. Exact mundane tastes, private hobbies and current family contact remain gaps (`p-016#B0037–0055`).

Fulfhu is an old companion, not a disposable wagon resource. After the oil/iron failure, losing his dragon or burdening family feels unacceptable. His sense that the first ten years exhausted his allowance of family help is self-imposed, not a demonstrated demand by his parents. The commercial debt and the moral debt are related in his self-model without becoming the same legal obligation (`p-016#B0056–0067`).

### 8.2 Gratitude, friendship and helping an earlier self are simultaneous motives

Ricardo's rescue gives Otto a reason to cry with relief at being alive. The handkerchief and privacy matter alongside the commander's military success. The 'third birth' is a figurative reorientation, not a death/return. He owes Ricardo and the commander Subaru different debts, and the current delay is explicitly undertaken for a friend too (`p-016#B0072–0119`).

Otto's impatience with Subaru's protective withholding is therefore not just a demand to be paid. Subaru has not asked him to help, and Otto hears in that omission a verdict that he is worthless or only someone to protect. He offers to listen even to an unordered account. Subaru accepts, but the narrative specifies that RBD and tea remain excluded (`p-009#B0046–0108`). Otto does not need to become omniscient to make a real choice.

His later interior account supplies another motive: he recognizes being unable to convey the private words that shape his world. Helping Subaru also helps the earlier Otto who was not understood. This self-directed motive does not expose friendship as false. It makes a single profit/saint interpretation inadequate. Nor does recognizing a parallel reveal the actual RBD mechanism to him (`p-016#B0149–0163`).

The roadside violence has its own limits. Otto's punch becomes a mutual dirty struggle; its effect on Subaru's exhausted thinking is actual but does not establish informed consent to the initial hit or universal confrontation therapy. His later plain full-name friend address differs from routine polite merchant banter. The emotional effect requires this recipient and state.

### 8.3 Distributed information and borrowed power have costs

The forest battle is not an order Subaru has planned in full. Emilia's disappearance destroys the intended meeting. Otto moves Shima away and adapts; he and Ram keep the delay battle secret from Subaru. His skill includes water/wind footsteps, pickpocketing Garfiel's key, environmental traps and animal cooperation. Garfiel first ignores him, then recognizes an opponent, and that changed attention itself becomes tactically exploitable (`p-015#B0264–0356`).

The blessing forces comprehension of many will-bearing living creatures. Voices from creatures on or in trees, soil and stone are not proof that inanimate rocks themselves converse. Prolonged processing produces actual headache, nosebleed and tinnitus; Otto discovers the limit while already dependent on surveillance to continue escaping. Cooperation is requested, not demonstrated universal mind control (`p-016#B0130–0148`).

For Al Dona, cooperating creatures share mana and Otto's gate channels a one-shot spell beyond his ordinary capacity. The spell's force is actual, as is its failure to defeat Garfiel. Ram then identifies the relevance of Garfiel's earth-spirit blessing. That criticism does not prove Otto knowingly ignored information he already possessed, or that every earth effect is useless (`p-016#B0196–0222`; `p-018#B0004`).

### 8.4 Ram's own method does not mean unbroken resources or total allegiance reversal

Ram remains Roswaal's maid and acts for his wish in her own way. Her current cooperation is not an automatically complete political defection. She trusts Subaru's timing and Otto's approach sufficiently to join, while retaining an undisclosed condition she requires Otto not to reveal afterward (`p-018#B0008–0043`; `p-025#B0041–0045`).

Her initial advantage over the full tiger is real. So are forehead bleeding, the broken-horn activation limit, depleted strengthening mana, knee damage and blood vomiting. A final spell prepared during the exchange is not free merely because it helps end the battle. Otto's beast-language briefly interrupts a nonrational tiger; Ram does not know the message. He is thrown into bushes with immediate survival uncertain. Her ironic death phrase must not become a certified casualty (`p-018#B0067–0107`).

Later both arrive alive, Otto carrying Ram. This resolves their immediate fate without making the earlier risks imaginary or showing full bodily recovery. She cares for Garfiel, rejects his marriage proposal, criticizes his shift from blaming others to punishing himself and asks him to test his past rather than merely submit. Her rough service demand, tenderness and continued Roswaal attachment must all remain available. None is a blanket permission for fatal use.

## 9. Garfiel: mediated suspicion, trial interpretation and collective defeat

### 9.1 Shima is the demonstrated miasma channel

Garfiel says the grandmother told him of Subaru's smell; narration explicitly identifies Shima as the one who detected it. She herself does not treat that cue as sufficient proof he is Witch-connected (`p-015#B0021–0028`). This revises the current evidence route. It does not prove every older claim fabricated, a permanent inability for Garfiel to sense it himself, or an infallible Cult classifier.

His ordinary keen nose remains actual. Facility odor impairs it; insects and sounds complicate vision/hearing; he resorts to boot tracks. Different channels and changed suspicion are necessary conditions, not superficial corrections to the same universal detector model.

### 9.2 Wanting the mother happy is not the same as simply hating her

Subaru obtains fragments through several people but acknowledges he does not know Garfiel's interior. His accusation of mother-hatred is admitted conjecture and is denied. Intrusive questioning and an actual arm grab force the issue; this pressure is not neutral, uncoerced therapy (`p-022#B0087–0124`).

Garfiel wanted his mother happy. He interpreted mixed children as burdens she deserved to leave, yet the trial showed an apparent landslide death after departure. If she never reached happiness, his separation cannot even be justified by its intended benefit. He would rather have been allowed to hate her than hold that unresolved loss. His protective conclusion is to become the barrier himself, preventing others from suffering outside (`p-022#B0124–0171`). That is a stated care-driven restriction, not a newly cast barrier, the real mother's verified motive or permission to forbid Emilia's chosen trial.

His current full-tiger form makes the killing moment something he need not consciously face. Narration explicitly calls that abdication weakness rather than care. This present reason-loss does not erase the older V11 full-tiger speaking/healing counterexample. Transformation conditions must remain state-selected, not forced into a universal formula.

### 9.3 The 'fair' brawl is constructed from several unequal contributions

Subaru casts Shamak despite Ferris's warning. Narration says the gate collapses and this is his last self-cast magic. Bodily healing later does not demonstrate a repaired gate. This is a specific capability loss, not a Subaru death or proof every possible external magical aid is gone (`p-024#B0023–0038/B0108–0109`).

Frederica's blue crystal, not Otto's pickpocketed Garfiel key, adheres to the shoulder and drains mana. Puck is narratively identified as the assist. The drain clears Shamak, forces human reversion and continues weakening Garfiel's blows. The private contract/interview explaining how Puck got there is not delivered. Continued help after Emilia's contract ends rules out equating cancellation with total soul death, without establishing a new accepted full contract (`p-024#B0040–0059/B0084–0085`).

After the gate's loss Subaru senses a different internal power, aims and lands an invisible fist. He later names it Invisible Providence. Severe retching/exhaustion follows, and Garfiel still stands. One performed strike does not prove all Petelgeuse powers, number/range limits, repeated costless use or a demonstrated factor-acquisition procedure (`p-024#B0129–0157`; `p-025#B0003–0013`).

Patrasche supplies the final charge. Her motive includes a current first-day defeat she remembers, not revenge for an erased-state murder. Otto, Ram, Puck, Subaru and Patrasche contribute different necessary conditions. The resulting 'power of numbers' is not merely a comic excuse for a solo hero's victory. It directly defeats Garfiel's claim that one protector can indefinitely contain every threat. It also leaves real costs and unresolved rescue work.

### 9.4 A first-trial answer is not forced agreement with every victor

Garfiel first admits defeat, not that all his beliefs were wrong. Ram pushes him toward a new trial rather than a punitive ending. Within it, the remembered family scene is initially silent because its words were not accessible to his old memory. He cannot physically change the past. Then speech becomes audible and an interactive Frederica addresses him while the representation pauses. He asks whose memory he is seeing. The expanded scene has causal meaning without becoming a perfect outside camera (`p-025#B0183–0257`).

The mother gives the pendants as beautiful gifts and intends to bring the father back. Garfiel's scar is narrated as self-inflicted after the earlier trial, linked to suppressing the remembered kiss at that spot. The sister proxy challenges the claim that maternal love was absent. It also argues that, if misfortune happened, other people would have been told. Garfiel infers the actual sister and Ryuzu knew. This is not current Frederica independently corroborating either her knowledge or the mother's fate (`p-025#B0236–0282`).

Asked what he wants, Garfiel chooses helping those who need him because they reminded him of maternal love. His first trial resolves; he refuses the remaining trial role, which Emilia claims. He thanks Subaru and follows him as commander while retaining the condition that opening Sanctuary must not make his grandmothers unhappy. Ram's disclosure that Garfiel is fourteen this year reframes Subaru's interpretation without diagnosing immaturity or absolving violence. The ending supplies actual alliance and a named interim developmental turn, not a complete ordinary-life model or proof the future promise has been fulfilled (`p-027#B0021–0082`).
