## 3. Event-state, memory and narrative order

### 3.1 Two actual arrivals, not a return for every discontinuity

Subaru first recognizes that the Whale victory and the council around him survive (`p-002#B0003–0040`). He had expected the earlier stall checkpoint; the present participants see a lapse of seconds, not his preceding death. This is `V09-E1 / RZ-E-012`. The successful hunt, already enacted bargains and deployment resources are available; the V08 terminal operation and its witnessed entrustment are not automatically current shared history. Subaru nevertheless retains enough of that failure to change the operation.

The behind-the-scenes presentation in chapter 2 moves backward within this attempt. Beatrice's door transfer is spatial. Nect's release, temporary occupation and the private taboo interval do not kill Subaru. The blast near the Whale's corpse leads to unconscious sleep, a private dark dream and waking on Emilia's lap, not another return (`p-015#B0459–0537`). Those differences matter: a chronology that counts every darkness, narrative rewind or near-death phrase would fabricate both deaths and knowledge transfer.

The final chapter later discloses an actual completed self-killing. Subaru remembers stabbing his throat; blood, pain and loss give way to an explicitly named return with the still-unwaking Rem before him (`p-026#B0049–0054`). This ends RZ-E-012 at death twelve and establishes `V09-E2 / RZ-E-013`, a post-ambush bedside checkpoint. His second attempt is interrupted by recognition that repeating the action cannot reach the event he wants to undo (B0055–0060). It is not death thirteen.

The reached record therefore has twelve actual Subaru deaths and thirteen represented aliased event segments. The narration does not timestamp every conversation in the final chapter against the retrospectively disclosed return. The current bedside scene is post-return; it is unnecessary and unsupported to invent a replay of the whole five-person meeting, which ally witnessed the self-killing, or an independently verified second-by-second autosave rule. Subaru's “immediately before” game analogy is his interpretation of the checkpoint, not a revealed selection algorithm.

### 3.2 Keeping an experience is not making its witnesses remember

The new attempt gives several strong positive tests without granting global continuity. Radian is actually alive and fights in the rear action (`p-010#B0342`); this is not a resurrection of the corpse previously observed by Subaru. Otto actually appears alive after captivity, and for Otto the encounter is their first meeting (`p-010#B0001–0041`). Julius's surprise at Subaru's familiarity is part of the evidence, not a disposable joke. Old unseen merchant fates remain unverified.

Subaru's terminal guilt and desire to avoid another requested killing now motivate disclosure of his own host eligibility and a nonlethal containment plan. Ferris and Julius receive the present operational information; they do not acquire the former lethal scene. Likewise, Subaru's anger at Petelgeuse includes atrocious acts that this present body has not yet performed in mutual history. That asymmetry does not erase the enemy's present lethal conduct, but it prevents a falsely omniscient opponent model.

Later memory loss works differently again. Rem's body remains warm, breathing and unwaking, while the people tested cannot remember her. Crusch remains known to others but cannot remember her own past (`p-024#B0034–0048`, B0080–0127). Neither is merely a new return, bodily fog annihilation or possession. Subaru's exception is directly consequential; its universal cause is not solved.

### 3.3 Giving the reader more does not give Subaru more

The private rejection of the invading Petelgeuse is focalized from the invader, not transmitted wholesale to Subaru (`p-011#B0211–0254`). Subaru subsequently explains expulsion through shared punishment and a contest of endurance (B0256–0268). The reader has seen an additional selective rejection. Both must remain in the causal record; neither a successful counter nor proximity to a scene grants its complete information.

The morning convoy interlude likewise supplies Rem's direct encounter with two named bishops after the reader has encountered Emilia's nonrecognition. This is narrative disclosure order, not a new attack occurring after the evening confession. The later consultation infers Gluttony through the Whale affiliation and analogous memory effect; it does not thereby receive every name, utterance and ability that Rem witnessed. The event ledger owns sequence and durability; the knowledge ledger owns these actual receipts and nonreceipts.

## 4. Protection, information and the person's right to act

### 4.1 Changing Ram's evidence changes Ram's action

Subaru does not defeat the earlier ambush by declaring that Ram is irrational. He uses the capability that made her dangerous as a route for communication. Ram's clairvoyance takes the perspective of a compatible other creature, with matching and local-attention costs; it is not unlimited all-seeing perception. Subaru supplies a legible board in the script she helped teach him, retracts the mistaken letter implication and takes responsibility (`p-005#B0003–0105`). Different evidence permits a different decision.

The sealed blank letter is still received; its cause is not established. Subaru calls it a Cult trap, but the source does not independently prove that accusation. The reader also has a live rival: the earlier intended writing was associated with Rem, V06 had shown material as well as remembered traces disappear, and V09 again removes her recognition. A writer-erasure connection is an interpretive hypothesis, not a demonstrated letter mechanism or a theory Subaru explicitly receives here; no before/after physical-letter test settles it. His use of Ram's fear is practically effective and not entirely candid. Emilia and Ram had also differed about how to treat the messenger: Emilia's willingness to let him stay rather than make him a hostage is a present choice, not a guarantee that every ally already shares one policy.

The expenditure distinction is equally explicit. Subaru acts without Roswaal's prior permission (`p-005#B0138`); Ram's emergency support and the lord's name make action possible, not every payment formally authorized. The source has not completed a Sanctuary visit or established the cause of Roswaal's absence. Emilia learns the ore-rights bargain involving her homeland from Wilhelm's explanation, not through a previously mutual political decision (`p-002`, Emilia viewpoint). Being a candidate and being represented do not imply having been informed.

### 4.2 The villagers are neither prejudice cured nor passive cargo

Emilia's earlier offers of refuge had been refused. Recognition-blocking clothing, limited contact and the village's stereotypes leave her actual conduct largely unreceived. Her self-description that no relationship has been won is not the whole social record: Petra remembers exercise, stamp gifts and ordinary shared time. The children's choices provide concrete counterevidence to collective rejection (`p-002#B0259–0548`; `p-005#B0140–0252`).

Subaru asks the adults to consider a particular person rather than merely a half-elf category. The pepper preference and children's memories do different work from an abstract claim that prejudice is wrong: they make an unknown neighbor available to attention. He kneels and asks for time and a chance to know her. The responding youth helps him up; the adults' warmth is real. It is not evidence that all racism has disappeared, that his status as their prior rescuer exerts no pressure, or that the still-unexplained evacuation is already fully informed consent.

Ram supplies the practical briefing and compensation work after that emotional breakthrough. Resources remain scarce enough that enemy-owned wagons are used and six children are placed with Emilia. The observed six do not revise an older child count into an unwitnessed death. A nickname resembling a village office likewise does not establish that its bearer is the formal headman. Specific labor, roles and counts cannot be replaced by a sentimental total of “the village.”

### 4.3 Ferris's dissent survives the successful outcome

Ferris directly challenges excluding Emilia from a battle about her own danger (`p-007#B0032–0053`). She has reason to participate and actual ability. Subaru admits an unexplained bad feeling and a selfish component, while Wilhelm defends a protective masculine ideal. Ferris backs off; this is not a source-certified demonstration that the objection was mistaken.

Puck helps make the exclusion feasible. He takes extra mana from Emilia to keep her asleep while the plan is arranged (`p-007#B0273`), and the men present a conveniently urgent evacuation explanation. Later Subaru explicitly wants the children's genuine attachment to keep Emilia from leaving even if she discovers the deception (`p-008#B0018–0025`). The affection is not fake, but its planned use is restrictive. When she tries to act, the children hold her and Petra invokes making Subaru cry; Emilia could physically pull free but does not (`p-015#B0230–0305`). That is an enacted limit on practical agency, not just a reader's suspicious interpretation.

Nor does it make Emilia inactive. She reassures frightened children, holds and bodily shields them during the wagon's lurching, keeps track of combat through Puck and prepares cold magic against an unknown pursuer. Wilhelm's earlier refusal of her offered help distinguishes a task he assigns from her actual lack of capability. Her frustration at merely being protected is explicit. The reader must hold these facts together: constrained information and options, real protective labor, and an eventual rescue she did not completely direct.

### 4.4 Risk can be transferred in the name of a better future

The cargo crisis reveals a still sharper choice. Puck says that he cannot stop the explosion but can contain it. Subaru rejects the feasible protection because exposing Puck's great-spirit form may reopen fear between Emilia and the villagers, with political consequences (`p-015#B0397–0408`). The alternative is retained as a last resort, not denied to exist. It would therefore be false to say that Subaru takes the bomb because absolutely no other life-saving method is available.

He instead uses the frozen Whale corpse and fallen tree as shelter. Patrasche anticipates his need, carries him and shields him; both suffer burns. This is real courage and inventive use of resources, but also a choice to accept severe bodily risk to avoid another kind of damage. The successful result does not prove a general rule that reputation must outweigh immediate safety, or that the dragon's costs were freely negotiated. Political care, personal care and informed choice do not become the same thing simply because the protagonist survives.

## 5. Julius and the practice of trusting someone still disliked

### 5.1 The further conversation actually happens

The last attempt's uncompleted talk is not completed by Subaru remembering that he wanted it. Julius now asks for trust in an actual exchange (`p-010#B0409–0446`). He acknowledges the beating while retaining his belief that there was a reason for it; he also recognizes that his self-justification does not answer Subaru's experience. This is not a wholesale retraction or a newly complete explanation of the protective rationale. The acknowledged difference nevertheless opens a conversation instead of requiring Subaru to pretend the hurt has disappeared.

Subaru names his resentment, including jealousy of the hand-kiss that preceded their real conversation and the bodily humiliation that followed. Julius's having held back does not make the injury negligible or consensual. Subaru also recognizes a capacity he cannot honestly deny. He entrusts himself because his own shame knows Julius's skill and knighthood, not because a moral formula requires liking the person who can help him. Julius accepts that entrustment.

The text does not leave the relation at an eternally deferred possibility. Julius explicitly calls Subaru `我が友` (`p-010#B0499`), and Subaru later invites him to a celebration (`p-013#B0141`). These are positive new evidence. Subaru's later “dislike forever” answer, avoidance and inability to put the relation into a simple formula are counterevidence to uncomplicated affection, not a reason to delete the friend address (`p-015#B0541–0548`). The planned ordinary celebration has not occurred. A state-selected model must represent earned reliance, acknowledged dislike and a changed future invitation together.

### 5.2 A borrowed viewpoint is not a borrowed lifetime of training

Nect's dangerous overlap becomes a deliberately intensified five-sense link. Subaru supplies the sight of the Hands; Julius supplies movement, sword practice, mana and contracted partners. Julius closes both eyes on his own initiative to avoid competing visual inputs, leaving Subaru's perspective as his combat view (`p-011#B0036–0039`). This was not a rehearsed step in Subaru's original proposal. Mutual trust is enacted through another person's judgment, not through turning Julius into a puppet.

The asymmetry remains physical and practical. Subaru feels Julius's pain and senses his mana, but does not acquire years of reflexes or swordsmanship. He slips and requires protection while serving as the essential observer. Julius adapts through trained bodily capacity. The doubled sensory burden and their reluctance to prolong it prevent Nect from becoming a costless improvement over ordinary perception (`p-010#B0472–0499`; `p-011#B0003–0070`).

Subaru recognizes effort behind what he had treated as effortless talent. His regret over wasted time is meaningful self-reassessment, but it overstates the absence of his own useful practice. Throwing a red magic stone enables Julius's strike, and the final pursuit makes use of learning not to shut his eyes when a blow approaches. Ordinary acquired capacities become valuable without giving Subaru an unearned sword technique. Even Julius cannot accompany the later pursuit: serious wounds and depleted mana defeat his effort to seem fit (`p-013#B0131–0143`).

### 5.3 Contracted partners have a claim on the relationship too

Ia is explicitly fire-aligned here, not merely a red image that the analyst translates into an element. Julius apologizes for the earlier covert assignment, while Subaru later asks whether she resents being passed between people. Her protective concern and Julius's report of her preferences belong in the partnership model, without supplying an exhaustive individual biography or a fully documented consent procedure (`p-002#B0150–0184`; `p-013#B0131–0139`).

The final spark uses an uncontracted quasi-spirit, an incomplete incantation and Subaru's almost exhausted mana (`p-015#B0026–0038`). Its deliberately borrowed, patchwork character matters. It is neither proof that he has mastered fire independently nor evidence that contributing through help is no contribution at all. Julius's formal bonds also have causal significance in the occupation model; they are not disposable decorations around the “real” swordsman.

## 6. Emilia, Beatrice and the difference between an offered hand and an imposed answer

### 6.1 Emilia's self-worth is directly narrated, not guessed from a tear

Emilia's viewpoint changes the evidence available for interpreting the rupture. She experiences authority without adequate confidence, prejudice without enough ordinary contact, and protection that refuses an ability she knows she possesses. Her initial thought that Subaru has abandoned her coexists with a prior wish to be treated as an ordinary girl. The children's testimony then reveals that he returned and cared about her being alone (`p-015#B0217–0279`). It is a partial discovery of the covert plan, not receipt of all its calculations or of his retained deaths.

The crucial distinction is explicit: she does not simply disbelieve his care; she cannot believe that she has enough value to receive it (B0252–0259). Anticipated rejection makes preemptive separation seem safer than building something that can later collapse. This is strong private motivational evidence. It does not retrospectively make every criticism of Subaru false. His breached promises, public self-appointment and imposed expectations remain actual parts of the V04 conflict. Her description of abandoning him as betrayal is also her self-judgment, not the analyst's automatic allocation of all blame to her.

The earlier V08 tear's precise cause is not independently replayed from her viewpoint. The new interiority provides a bounded pattern that makes isolation and longing intelligible; it does not grant a uniquely certified explanation of every older expression or make Emilia remember discarded encounters.

### 6.2 A declaration becomes an answer only when someone receives it

Subaru first answers the urgent question with a declaration before carrying the bomb away (`p-015#B0414–0432`). Later, while resting on Emilia's lap, he asks how much she has heard. She says almost nothing: Julius left the explanation to him (B0574–0579). Her correct inference that he helped arrange the operation does not make her an informed observer of every scene.

Subaru refuses the easy claim that his earlier selfishness was merely a temporary person who was not really him. He had believed she would accept his self-flattering performance as help. He explicitly admits using her and apologizes, while preserving his genuine desire to help her (B0583–0594). Emilia hears and acknowledges this account. He then answers the repeated why with love again (B0597–0604). Those are enacted speech acts, not a rescue's automatic moral reward.

Emilia responds by naming the traits through which she expects rejection: ancestry and appearance, social inexperience, and what she calls a selfish reason to seek the throne. The reason itself is not disclosed. Subaru's attention does not require her to have first won a universal argument about those traits. His desired `特別扱い` becomes a specifically welcomed distinction rather than the exclusion she has known; she says so and reciprocates the hand contact (B0612–0640).

The positive change should not be weakened into “nothing has happened because she has not said she loves him.” Equally, it should not be inflated into a reciprocal romantic declaration. Subaru asks her to take time; she thanks him, smiles and cries (B0643–0657). The subsequent ordinary wagon scene preserves his embarrassment and relief at Petra's presence (`p-017#B0018–0024`). Neither has become effortlessly fluent in intimacy.

### 6.3 Beatrice is not saved by being told that her refusal is invalid

The library encounter supplies a contrasting form of care. Beatrice takes Subaru through a door at Puck's request; Puck is not thereby the caster of an unlimited teleport. She knows about the Cult without the source explaining every channel of that knowledge. She refuses to leave (`p-007#B0138–0220`). Subaru treats her being a small girl alone as enough reason to override her answer, grabs her and dismisses the contract she treats as binding. Her hesitation and frightened ambivalence are not consent.

The reader then hears her private appeal to her mother and sees a black-bound book after Subaru has been expelled. He does not receive that final speech. The book is not independently identified as a Gospel, and the mother is not named. The scene warrants both a more emotionally specific Beatrice model and continued abstention about its complete cause.

Puck cannot simply solve the refusal. He tells Subaru that he will not do the reconciliation with Emilia on Subaru's behalf, yet accepts secrecy and extra mana-taking from Emilia; he also remains constrained around Beatrice's contract. These are differentiated relations and choices, not one universally wise protective function. Subaru finally leaves Beatrice largely because the practical deadline wins, not because the scene demonstrates a completed conversion to respect for all refusals. Later acceptance of Emilia's support can revise his behavior without erasing this counterexample.

## 7. Occupation, private contact and the standard for a real ending

### 7.1 Do not substitute a lost treatise for the mechanism being tested

Julius's account of ancient soul transcription is a hypothesis-generating comparison, not a demonstration that Petelgeuse copied a soul in the same way. The text describes a lost technique and the desirability of kinship in that account; Subaru's more categorical inference should not turn it into a universal blood-only host rule (`p-002`, council discussion). The practical question is how this particular occupying spirit can use prepared bodies and Subaru.

Ferris removes two different hazards from the living Kety: an explosive bodily formula and a foreign mana structure in his Gate (`p-010#B0058–0073`). He claims that removing the latter disqualifies the prepared host. The later exhaustion of usable Finger stock corroborates the result without providing a controlled transfer test into Kety. Most importantly, Kety is alive. “All Fingers eliminated” cannot be translated into ten verified biological deaths when one has been deliberately neutralized without killing him.

The source then endorses a more specific occupation account. Petelgeuse is a spirit forcing an irregular contract on a spirit-user-qualified but formally unbound human. A provisional arrangement can be overridden; an existing formal spirit contract prevents this takeover. Julius's bonds explain his exclusion, and Petelgeuse's reaction corroborates the classification (`p-011#B0185–0199`). This is stronger than leaving everything a conjecture because Subaru says it. It is narrower than a complete taxonomy of spirits, all possession, every Gate implant, or the Cult's proposed use of Emilia despite her bond with Puck.

### 7.2 Seeing, sharing and cutting are different capacities

The current original body gains many more Hands when distributed Witch-factor portions return (`p-010#B0279–0282`). That same-body change is a causal discriminator against merely counting new bodies. More than a hundred are observed, but there is no fixed universal maximum. During the final pursuit only seven are available for attack after damage and the allocation needed for movement (`p-015#B0005`). “Seven” is not evidence that the whole underlying power has reset to an initial global capacity.

Mimi and Tibby explicitly cannot see the limbs. Wilhelm's negative-space skill is not generally reproducible by Julius and Ricardo, whereas Julius can use Subaru's supplied view through Nect. The enhanced six-attribute blade cuts the Hands, but the subsequently raised earth wall repels it (`p-010#B0330–0334`, B0448–0499; `p-011#B0090–0108`). The observed counter is real and the universal boast is bounded by an immediate counterexample. Neither magic nor Authority becomes one infallible ranking chart.

Subaru throws a magic stone rather than curing his Gate or independently mastering a new spell. His ordinary practice supplies timing and accuracy, Julius supplies the attack, and six partners supply the blade's condition. Ferris's later remote intervention uses prior treatment access to overheat internal mana and immobilize both occupants (`p-011#B0131–0170`). It is coordinated through a mirror conversation; that does not prove that the mirror itself is the universal magical wire or that the procedure works over unlimited distance.

### 7.3 The successful explanation is not the whole observed cause

The prologue already associates Subaru's wished-for body, contact and response with seven sin labels (`p-001#B0021–0027`). This is affective/self-descriptive language inside a disorienting encounter, not seven demonstrated powers, offices or independently tested compatibilities. Its lonely love address belongs to the same record without identifying a complete supernatural provider.

Subaru deliberately begins the prohibited explanation after Nect has been released (`p-011#B0201–0208`). There is no successful full disclosure to Julius, no remaining sense-sharing channel that silently transmits the private encounter, and no tested rule that every external listener would now be safe. Subaru's present wish to survive is explicit; he is not requesting another killing from Ferris.

The subsequent scene belongs to the invader's experience. Petelgeuse expects reunion and receives rejection: he is not the person addressed by the love, and he is told to disappear (`p-011#B0211–0254`). The reader has direct selective speech; the beloved other is strongly correlated with Subaru by the sequence. The shadow does not independently name herself, reveal her face or explain all links among Satella, the taboo, return, scent, factors and Authorities. Petelgeuse's naming Satella and claiming previous recognition do not complete that identification for the narrator.

Subaru returns to the same cliff believing the shared penalty and endurance contest drove Petelgeuse out. His own explanation also explicitly hedges the connection among the Witch, return and arrival (B0256–0268). A causal account should preserve both his operational interpretation and the reader's additional observation, rather than giving him knowledge of the rejection dialogue or reducing the whole scene to pain. The temporary “shattering” imagery is not final extinction: Petelgeuse reappears.

Later a dark contact follows nonfatal unconsciousness, not a death. Subaru desires the unnamed figure while another beloved voice calls him toward waking; he promises to come and receives “waiting” as a reply (`p-015#B0480–0516`). The dream is described as forgotten on waking. This is evidence of private reciprocal address and its memory boundary, not an awake appointment, an available summoning method, a second completed death or a certified single provider of every supernatural effect.

### 7.4 The source first qualifies an ending, then certifies one

Julius's first apparent finishing strike does not destroy the heart before transfer. A later strike is narrated as burning the mana-aggregate, Od-consuming spirit itself, yet the process does not finish before the cliff crushes its damaged body (`p-011#B0112–0134`; `p-011#B0291–0303`). The pursuing remains have dead flesh while the spirit's own death is still approaching (`p-013#B0213–0226`). These are different moments, not proof that the earlier soul damage never happened or that every finishing claim is equally unreliable.

Oil and a tiny borrowed spark burn the remnant without immediately ending it. Petelgeuse regains lucid intention: he shifts from obtaining Subaru's body to killing a perceived threat to his Witch and Cult (`p-015#B0059–0075`). Subaru exploits his attachment to the Gospel and the wagon's wind-protection boundary, uses the courage learned from Wilhelm's practice, and knocks him out. He writes the ending in blood on the book's blank later pages. That is a human inscription in learned script, not a spontaneous fulfilled prophecy or newly acquired authorship over fate (B0077–0117).

The robe then catches in the wheel. The narration explicitly says that destruction of the body also dissipates the inhabiting evil spirit's life; the last Hand vanishes before reaching Subaru, signifying real extinction (B0120–0142). This is adequate evidence for Petelgeuse's local entity end. It does not demonstrate an acquired Sloth office, a new Authority in Subaru, the destruction of every Cult faction, or the harmlessness of all surviving books.

Petelgeuse's final need for touch, care and reciprocated love also changes Subaru's response. The narration supplies deprivation and obsession; Subaru pities him while refusing forgiveness. His inability to read the final complex expression is explicit. Sympathy neither excuses the attacks nor transforms a fragment of motivation into a complete four-hundred-year biography. Nor can Subaru's own repudiation of the shadow's “love” settle what that private voice actually feels.

## 8. Otto, Patrasche and the resources outside a heroic self-image

### 8.1 A merchant is not interchangeable with his wagon

Otto's earlier race for commercial advantage put him in the captors' path. His release produces a present first acquaintance, not a confirmation that Subaru's discarded experience is their shared contract. Ricardo's initial precautions and the subsequent judgment that Otto is safe are specific responses to possible infiltration, not evidence that every merchant is either an innocent object or a disguised enemy (`p-008#B0001–0085`; `p-010#B0001–0073`). Kety's own covert viewpoint, in contrast, establishes an independently acting infiltrator before occupation.

Paid inventory work makes Otto's commercially interested observation decisive. He had seen Kety's fire stones at recruitment while checking other traders' loads for route advantage; the ordinary manifest otherwise agrees. Julius acknowledges that he searched for an enchanted trap and missed a physically hidden load (`p-013#B0058–0098`). That is not a complete absence of inspection. Nor does “physical” make the exploding magic stones ordinary gunpowder. Subaru's error is to have treated the first wagon blast and the Finger's bodily suicide formula as one explanation.

Three infiltrators' wagons had been taken into the column. The later search proves that the dangerous load is beneath the floor in Emilia's wagon: Ia identifies its position, Puck freezes the floor, and Subaru retrieves a patterned mabeast-skin sack full of heating stones (`p-015#B0376–0396`). Otto's knowledge, the spirit's detection, Puck's manipulation and Subaru's physical retrieval are different causal contributions. The initial estimate that the stones could destroy seven or eight wagons is not a measured universal radius or a license to invent a chain-reaction explanation of the V08 losses. Heating and an ignition sequence are observed; the trigger and an exact countdown are not explained.

### 8.2 Bargaining is still agency under desperate conditions

Otto offers a way to pursue the convoy when ordinary road travel cannot make up the head start. His terms are an audience with Roswaal and purchase of his oil at the price he names. Subaru agrees immediately (`p-013#B0105–0129`). Actual payment, the audience and unlimited authorization to charge Roswaal are not shown. Otto's unsold stock and livelihood give the bargain stakes beyond comic greed.

There is a textual tension worth retaining. Otto initially promises to take Subaru to the objective or catch the suspect wagon (B0112), then later says that actually catching up was not a condition (B0164). The latter cannot silently erase the former. Subaru's insistence and serious plea put pressure on Otto, while Otto's negotiated terms and execution retain his own practical agency. A profitable agreement need not be free of desperation or inconsistent phrasing.

`言霊の加護` supplies conversation with living creatures. The narrator gives a broad account, and birds, insects and dragons supply actual route information (B0156–0160). It does not give command over every creature, complete foresight of hazards or knowledge without available informants. The same shortcut competence had already helped him arrive early enough to be captured. In the pursuit, a collapsing bridge, rough terrain, bodily discomfort and limited acceleration remain real costs. The wind blessing is not an all-speed guarantee of a smooth ride.

Subaru still conceals the exact pursuer from Otto, and afterwards evades the question with an animal-sloth explanation (`p-013#B0228–0235`; `p-015#B0144–0151`). Otto's collaboration is important without being fully informed about the enemy. The route succeeds through his knowledge and driving, two dragons' effort, and Subaru's rear defense, not a discovery that the original self-sufficient hero fantasy was correct.

### 8.3 The dragon carries a body, not an abstract promise

Patrasche takes initiative before a command, endures wounds and keeps making decisions that Subaru's novice riding does not specify. During the final blast she curls over him and takes burns herself. Emilia gives first aid and Ferris later treats her; the source says her life is not in danger, not that the risk was unreal (`p-015#B0436–0478`; `p-015#B0529–0537`). Otto's female dragon, フルフー, is a distinct participant, not another name for Patrasche. Their differing sizes and temperaments need not be forced into an unverified breed comparison.

Subaru's wish to receive Patrasche as the Whale reward is conditional; title or ownership has not been formally transferred in the scene. Two days' acquaintance and intense shared danger explain felt attachment without proving shared return memory or a name-induced power increase. Accepting aid, noticing injury, asking for more and receiving loyalty coexist. The resulting debt belongs in a relationship model as well as a transport calculation.

## 9. The domestic fragment: a different premise does not make happiness meaningless

`ナツキ・レム` presents Subaru and Rem as a married couple in Kararagi's Banan, with Rigel and the infant Spica. Its characters explicitly count eight years since they abandoned their other commitments and fled together (`p-019#B0046–0052`; `p-019#B0083–0108`). That premise is incompatible with the active route's decision to resume the fight. The fragment is neither the current couple's actual future nor an extra represented Subaru return. No external IF or WN has been admitted merely because the main volume contains this scene.

Its bounded evidence nevertheless matters. Work and housing had to be secured. The first pregnancy came before stable planning, the later child was planned, and the parents divide ordinary care while managing public embarrassment, jokes, play and neighbors (`p-019#B0024–0044`; `p-019#B0120–0138`). Subaru's gentle handling of the infant and Rem's inability to settle every anxiety prevent a crisis-only or flawlessly idyllic account.

Rem's anniversary question concerns whether she was selected as a compromise. Subaru gives an explicit exclusive declaration and devotion; she distinguishes trusting him from believing herself worthy of remaining with him (B0099–0154). Pregnancy had made a tangible bond feel harder to lose. Happiness and insecurity therefore coexist over years inside this scenario. This is not proof of present-main-route marriage consent, that children ethically solve attachment fears, or that abandoning others was costless. The parents' teasing and their use of Rigel's embarrassment also preserve a power difference inside affectionate narration.

The star-name account places Subaru's name-origin homework, star encyclopedias and naming preferences inside the same fragment (B0159–0162). These may concern a pre-divergence past, but the route-specific container alone is not an automatic bridge into the current biography. The main-route ordinary-life table must retain that qualification unless corroborated. Similarly, Rigel's prohibited horn use is a represented feature of this family, not a demonstrated general inheritance law for oni children.

Subaru infers why Rem grew her hair, and the artist's family-design sheet explicitly references Emilia (`p-019#B0205–0207`; `p-029`, image `p323`). The sheet strengthens the design-level relation without changing current Rem's hairstyle or assigning the main-route Subaru an exclusive spouse. Its question-marked Wilhelm-hairstyle note remains a design comment, not an independently established motive for an actual current grooming routine.

The fragment's warmth should not be dismissed because its premise is not active. It demonstrates a locally represented capacity for ordinary reciprocal happiness and an unresolved need for reassurance. What it cannot do is bypass the act of choosing and the changed history that make those conditions possible.

## 10. Memory loss, protective refusal and present choice

### 10.1 The other operation has a different casualty boundary

Ferris's report of no fatalities in the current Cult operation does not certify the simultaneous patient convoy's safety. The morning interlude shows Rem and Crusch traveling with badly wounded people. Crusch's carriage is itself a response to others' concern about her injuries. Her effort to reassure Rem includes admitting her own inability to resolve another person's worry, and her self-critical remark about smiling complicates the image of uninterrupted confidence (`p-021#B0003–0040`). The cause of that older regret is not supplied by this remark.

The attack destroys wagons, dragons and patients. Rem physically rescues Crusch while the driver is beyond reach. The white-haired attacker remains unharmed by collision and by Crusch's wind-magic/gift-combined strike; his clothing is unmarked as well (`p-021#B0045–0082`). This establishes an unusual effect, not its decoded mechanism or immunity against every possible attack. The embedded account of Crusch's first battle against `大兎` is new bounded historical evidence, not an invitation to import that creature's later taxonomy.

The two attackers identify themselves as Greed, `レグルス・コルニアス`, and Gluttony, `ライ・バテンカイトス` (B0112–0113). These names are heard by Rem and given to the reader. Regulus's rights, private property and avowed satisfaction contrast with the people he mutilates; the claimed desire for peaceful ordinariness does not make his behavior nonviolent. Gluttony's appetitive account is different again. He explicitly says that the object of interest is the Whale's successful hunters, not revenge for a dead pet (`p-023#B0009–0018`). The Whale is not thereby another body of this bishop or a demonstrated complete control system.

Rem has little mana after treating Crusch. She still gathers ambient mana through her horn and attacks, choosing a self-description as Subaru's `介添え人` rather than finishing the formal household title (`p-023#B0020–0044`). Her hope that her hero will judge the attackers is neither an achieved outcome nor absence of her own agency. The last wish that he feel something on learning of her loss is poignant evidence of attachment; “last moment” alone is not a bodily death certificate.

The later report confirms that half the returning force was dead or wounded, not that half was necessarily dead. Iron Fang escaped on its deputy's orders and returned with capital reinforcements after the attackers had gone (`p-024#B0075–0076`). This advances Rem's earlier inference about Hetaro's absence. It does not identify every motionless knight's fate, erase the separate Whale casualties or make the whole expedition a casualty-free victory.

### 10.2 Two kinds of lost memory are not one missing person

Subaru reaches the capital after the attack. Rem's external injuries have been healed, but she will not wake. Her body is warm and breathing; Ferris finds no bodily abnormality that explains the condition (`p-024#B0003–0048`; `p-024#B0119–0127`). Emilia and the children actually fail to recognize her name. Subaru remembers her and insists that her contribution is not an imagined story. Ram's own memory remains an explicit question at the final horizon (`p-026#B0071–0074`), not a test already completed by an earlier ambiguous remark.

Crusch has instead lost her own past while remaining recognized by others. She learns names again, receives support and still feels uncertainty about the person she is expected to be. Her reattached arm and transformed register are distinct from the memory injury. Calling her “like another person” records Subaru's disorientation, not the death or replacement of the actual Crusch.

The consultation links the memory cases to a bishop and compares Rem's lost recognition with the Whale fog. That causal attribution is reasonable and stronger than an arbitrary suspicion, but the characters have not learned every activation condition. The rare `眠り姫` condition supplies a clinical analogue: the participants report unending sleep without aging or hunger and no known awakening. The narration explicitly leaves its connection to Rem conjectural. Neither Rem's years-long agelessness nor a universal no-feeding rule has been tested here.

Subaru wonders whether Crusch's continuing will expresses the soul she spoke of earlier. The question is philosophically important to him; it is not experimental proof of where identity resides or of exactly what Gluttony has consumed. Personal continuity and causal metaphysics require different evidence standards.

### 10.3 Ferris's grief does not remove Crusch's choice

Ferris tries familiar banter with the altered Crusch while hiding serious distress. He also attacks Emilia with blame and directs an expressly malicious retort at Subaru. Subaru objects to making the target of persecution responsible for the persecutors' acts. It is Crusch herself who demands Ferris apologize (`p-024#B0088–0110`). Her lost past has not eliminated the capacity to recognize a wrong accusation and intervene. The apology occurs, though its flippant delivery does not demonstrate a complete change of feeling.

Ferris then proposes ending the alliance to protect Crusch from further danger. Subaru's anger does not prevent a quiet request for his reason. The ore-rights incentive remains, but Subaru cannot guarantee that continued cooperation will keep Crusch safe (B0134–0146). Ferris's desperation reveals a power limit behind the familiar presentation: his best healing cannot restore the memory injury. His promise to fix it later is sincere effort, not an already available solution.

Crusch thanks him and refuses to be simply led. She wants to understand and choose for herself, despite knowing very little about her previous life (B0163–0177). This is not a claim that she already has all information. It is a present decision to keep learning rather than cede the decision because she is injured. Care, concern and a refusal of substitutive protection are mutually present.

Emilia and Crusch actually agree to continue the alliance. Ferris adds a temporary secrecy condition while his camp decides how to respond to Crusch's condition, and Subaru accepts (`p-024#B0179–0190`; `p-026#B0001–0006`). Formal settlement of every prior expenditure does not follow. Anastasia is absent; the expectation that she will exploit the information is a political forecast, not witnessed betrayal. Emilia still needs to speak with Roswaal and reunite with the Sanctuary group.

### 10.4 Wilhelm discloses a reason that gratitude had not supplied

Wilhelm's military help and service are genuine. His argument for continuing the alliance is nevertheless not purely an altruistic wish to aid Subaru and Emilia. He apologizes for having concealed a personal reason and shows an old shoulder wound that has reopened (`p-026#B0008–0041`). His explanation links it to `死神の加護`: proximity to its bearer strengthens the wound's effect, allowing a previously closed injury to open again. This is expert testimony with an observed bleeding scar, not an independently mapped radius or a law that no such wound ever closes at any distance.

He identifies the person who gave him the wound as Theresia, his wife and the prior Sword Saint. His stated purpose is to verify what the reopening means by pursuing the Cult. The source has not yet shown Theresia alive, revealed a corpse mechanism or completed the family history. Nor does his familiarity with the sleeping-condition report identify its particular patient. The earlier courtship and belated words remain evidence, but revenge against the Whale is not an adequate description of every motive after it dies.

Wilhelm dismisses the importance of his own life in this explanation; Subaru objects. That response makes their relation more than a one-directional transfer of strength from invulnerable mentor to weak student. The new personal stake qualifies a pure release-through-revenge model without cancelling the protective work Wilhelm actually performs.

### 10.5 Being the only rememberer does not make Subaru the only carer

Subaru's actual self-killing is a major counterexample to a clean, permanent recovery narrative. He is prepared to surrender the successful operation to regain Rem; the new bedside checkpoint denies the intended reversal. His subsequent estimate that even the old council point was already too late, and that he has no method to defeat the new attacker, describes current feasibility under his knowledge. It is not a metaphysical theorem that Emilia and Rem must forever be traded against each other (`p-026#B0049–0061`).

Emilia finds him exhausted, not sleeping and repeatedly returning to Rem's room. She does not claim to understand the forgotten person or promise that everything will certainly be well. She asks to share the worry and wants to help because the person matters to Subaru, not because she has somehow recovered their former bond (B0063–0095).

Her offer corrects a specific equation: only Subaru remembering Rem does not mean that only Subaru can care about Rem or attempt her rescue. He recognizes his isolation, asks Emilia to turn away so he can cry, and accepts her later embrace from behind the chair (B0101–0117). This is actual received support with a request for privacy, not an instruction to perform invulnerable heroism. His vow to bring Rem back closes the narrative, but the recovery is not accomplished (B0118–0132).

The final state can therefore preserve both capacities: Subaru's self-disposability remains dangerous, and he can be reached by a form of care that neither claims his private knowledge nor abandons the person he remembers. Neither “fully cured” nor “learned nothing” predicts this sequence adequately.
