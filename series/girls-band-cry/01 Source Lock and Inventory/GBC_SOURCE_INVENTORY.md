# Girls Band Cry - V2 Source Inventory

## 1. Purpose

This document inventories the primary-source materials available for the V2 reread of the thirteen-episode television anime.

It is a source-control artifact, not a literary interpretation. Its job is to answer:

- what source units exist;
- where they live;
- what each episode archive contains;
- which modalities are available for analysis;
- which extraction or access caveats remain;
- and whether the corpus is complete enough to begin the sequential V2 reread.

The governing analytical method is `00 Frameworks and Methods/GBC_ANALYTICAL_METHOD_V2.md`.

## 4. What an episode bundle contains

Although the Drive filenames say `screenshots.zip`, the manifest establishes that the archives are multimodal analysis bundles.

Each validated episode bundle contains or was built to contain:

1. dense timestamped screenshots;
2. contact sheets;
3. two subtitle files, including the Japanese analysis ASS;
4. complete episode MP3 audio copied into the episode analysis directory before archive rebuild;
5. supporting analysis metadata generated by the extraction pipeline.

The manifest reports for the inspected episodes:

- `missing_required_files: []`;
- `analysis_validation.valid: true`;
- `audio_validation.valid: true`;
- `audio_bundle_validation.valid: true`;
- `archive_contains_audio: true`.

The complete MP3 audio is approximately 23:41 per normal episode, 48 kHz stereo, roughly 160 kb/s, and was validated against source duration with zero duration difference for the episodes surfaced during this audit.

## 5. Japanese subtitle source

The analysis bundles use an embedded ASS track titled `Japanese (analysis filtered)`.

The manifest identifies the language as Japanese where metadata is populated. The filter reports no Chinese lines in the resulting analysis track for the inspected episodes.

Historical source preparation for Episode 1 compared the Simplified-Chinese/Japanese and Traditional-Chinese/Japanese source ASS variants and found all 451 Japanese subtitle events identical in text and timing. A Chinese-only timing discrepancy in one vendor-call line did not affect the Japanese layer. The V2 analysis therefore treats the Japanese analysis ASS as the dialogue-navigation text while checking characterization-sensitive claims against the audio.

## 6. Audio source

The extraction pipeline selected audio `relative_stream_index: 0` for every episode represented in the manifest.

The selected source audio is FLAC, 48 kHz, stereo. The analysis bundles contain complete MP3 transcodes synchronized to episode duration.

Some source MKVs expose a second FLAC stream. The manifest does not identify the semantic role of that second stream. Phase 0 therefore records the following open provenance note:

> The selected stream is the historically used analysis audio and validates correctly, but the identity of any second FLAC stream should not be invented from stream order alone.

If a future claim depends on alternate-mix content, commentary, or a materially different audio track, that stream must be identified directly before use.

## 7. Visual evidence density

The screenshot archives provide unusually dense visual coverage for a television-anime analysis corpus.

For E01-E12, the manifest records between 676 and 974 screenshots per episode and between 34 and 49 contact sheets. E11 is the densest inspected bundle at 974 screenshots / 49 sheets; E12 is the least dense at 676 / 34.

This is sufficient for:

- composition and blocking;
- facial and body-state comparison;
- recurrent props and spatial motifs;
- costume and stage-state changes;
- many forms of CG acting analysis;
- visual continuity and shot-sequence reconstruction.

It is not identical to continuous motion.

## 8. Full-motion evidence

The canonical Drive source folder does not presently expose full episode MKVs or a standardized set of targeted MP4 clips as direct children.

Historical V1 work used selected full-motion clips for some especially important performance and confrontation scenes.

V2 policy:

- the validated bundle is sufficient for the default sequential reread;
- complete audio remains available inside the bundle;
- motion-sensitive claims must be phrased at the level the evidence supports;
- when choreography, gesture timing, camera movement, lip/voice synchronization, instrument interaction, or transition timing is load-bearing, use a full-motion clip if available or request/create a targeted clip before elevating the claim to high confidence.

## 9. Hardsub/font warning

## 11. Known gaps and cautions

### 11.1 E13 V1 prose gap

The available historical conversation export contains the user's E13 analysis request but no corresponding main assistant deep-dive turn. V2 must therefore treat E13 as a fresh primary-source deep reading.

### 11.2 Bundle completeness versus runtime accessibility

Archive completeness is established by the extraction manifest. That does not guarantee that every future connector/runtime will automatically unpack a ZIP in place.

Before each episode analysis, confirm that the archive can be materialized or otherwise inspected in the active runtime. If not, retrieve/mount the ZIP first. This is an operational-access issue, not a source-corpus gap.

### 11.3 Supplemental materials

Selected historical clips and any official material outside the thirteen TV episodes are not automatically inside the locked base corpus. They require explicit admission under `GBC_SOURCE_LOCK.md`.

### 11.4 ED/epilogue

The TV ending sequence is inside the series boundary, but its literal versus symbolic status is an interpretive question. The source inventory does not pre-decide it.
