# Girls Band Cry - V2 Source Lock 1.0

## 1. Lock declaration

The V2 sequential reread is locked to the thirteen-episode television anime as represented by the validated episode-analysis bundles in the canonical Girls Band Cry primary-source Drive folder.

**Lock ID:** `GBC-V2-SOURCE-LOCK-1.0`

**Primary-source folder ID:** `1T3fHW4OB5scED1Ymr76zlBD8Y-Lz3yZP`

**Episode coverage:** E01-E13, complete.

This lock defines what may establish local episode truth during the sequential reread.

## 2. Base canonical source unit

For each episode, the canonical working source unit is:

`GBC_ep##_screenshots.zip`

Despite the filename, the extraction manifest validates the archive as a multimodal bundle containing dense visual evidence, Japanese subtitle material, and complete synchronized audio.

For ordinary sequential work, the evidence stack is:

1. Japanese analysis ASS for dialogue navigation and exact textual recovery;
2. complete synchronized Japanese episode audio for delivery, voice, pauses, overlap, laughter, crying, breathing, shouting, register realization, and music;
3. dense screenshots and contact sheets for composition, acting poses, blocking, gaze, spatial relationships, props, visual motifs, and scene reconstruction;
4. targeted full-motion video when a conclusion materially depends on continuous motion or audiovisual synchronization.

## 3. Authority hierarchy inside the lock

When sources disagree or differ in evidentiary capacity, use this order:

### Tier P1 - original audiovisual evidence within the locked episode

- heard Japanese dialogue;
- heard vocal performance;
- score/song/performance audio;
- visible action, staging, framing, and movement.

### Tier P2 - Japanese analysis ASS

Use for:

- line recovery;
- timestamp navigation;
- wording searches;
- lexical/register analysis;
- lyric/dialogue discrimination where styles are informative.

If the subtitle and heard audio materially diverge, record the discrepancy and prioritize the performed audiovisual event for what was actually uttered.

### Tier P3 - derived screenshots/contact sheets

Use for static and sequential visual evidence. Do not infer micro-timing that a still sequence cannot establish.

### Tier P4 - admitted targeted full-motion clips

Use when supplied/retrieved from the same episode source or another source known to represent the same broadcast/BD content. Record filename and timestamp boundary.

### Tier H - historical analysis

V1 episode responses and the V1 full-series synthesis are historical hypotheses only. They cannot establish V2 source fact.

## 4. Prospective-reading lock

The main body of `GBC_E##_DEEP_READING.md` must respect local episode knowledge.

Future episodes may not be used to erase earlier uncertainty.

Later information belongs only in the explicitly labeled retrospective section.

This rule is particularly important for:

## 5. Language lock

Japanese is the characterization authority.

English translation may aid exposition, but any major claim about:

- politeness;
- dialect;
- honorifics;
- pronouns;
- address;
- lexical repetition;
- sentence endings;
- ambiguity;
- insult intensity;
- confession wording;
- apology;
- emotional register;

must be grounded in Japanese wording and, when meaningful, the audio realization.

The Episode 1 source comparison established that the Japanese layer of the two original bilingual subtitle variants was textually and temporally identical, so the Japanese analysis ASS is suitable for V2 navigation.

## 6. Voice-performance lock

Because the V2 monographs must support reconstructing character voice and behavior, written subtitles alone are insufficient.

For every major character scene, inspect qualitative audio evidence when available:

- pace;
- pitch placement;
- loudness;
- breath;
- strain;
- hesitation;
- clipping/compression of phrases;
- overlap/interruption;
- laughter;
- crying;
- intoxication effects;
- stage voice versus conversation voice;
- listener-specific shifts.

Do not invent acoustic measurements that have not been measured. Qualitative descriptions should remain qualitative.

## 7. Motion-sensitive claim rule

Screenshots are dense but not continuous motion.

A claim is **motion-sensitive** when its force depends substantially on:

- exact gesture timing;
- acceleration/deceleration;
- continuous camera movement;
- body-to-body timing;
- choreography;
- instrument-performance synchronization;
- animation timing;
- edit-to-beat relationships;
- a vocal line's exact synchronization with a gesture or cut.

For a high-confidence motion-sensitive claim, prefer full-motion evidence. If only screenshot + audio evidence is available, state the narrower claim that those sources justify.

## 8. Music/performance lock

Songs are part of the primary dramatic text when they occur in the episode.

The episode analysis may examine:

- lyrics present in the source/subtitle material;
- vocal interpretation;
- arrangement as audibly presented;
- member interaction;
- stage blocking;
- audience relation;
- reuse or recontextualization of earlier songs.

External official lyrics, scores, isolated stems, interviews, or release metadata are not automatically part of Source Lock 1.0. They may be admitted as supplemental evidence later, with provenance recorded and without overwriting what the episode itself establishes.

## 9. Supplemental-source admission rule

A supplemental source may be used only if all of the following are recorded:

- source identity;
- provenance;
- relationship to the TV episode;
- reason it is needed;
- whether it is primary, secondary, or paratextual;
- whether it changes the source boundary or merely improves access to the same event.

Examples:

- a user-provided MP4 clip cut from the locked episode: access-equivalent P1 evidence, not a boundary expansion;
- an official lyric sheet: supplemental primary/paratextual evidence;
- creator interview: secondary/paratextual evidence;
- fansub commentary: interpretive secondary evidence only;
- V1 prose: historical analytical evidence only.

## 10. Explicit exclusions from base Source Lock 1.0

Unless separately admitted, the following may not silently determine V2 episode conclusions:

- manga adaptations;
- game material;
- novels or prose tie-ins;
- later franchise continuation material;
- interviews;
- social-media statements;
- fan interpretations;
- English-subtitle wording where Japanese materially differs;
- V1 conclusions;
- the assumption that a symbolic ED image is literally diegetic.

This exclusion is methodological, not a judgment that such sources are unimportant.

## 11. Audio-stream provenance note

This remains an open provenance detail, not a blocker.

## 14. ED/epilogue classification rule

For ending-sequence claims use one of:

- `LITERAL` - directly depicted as ordinary diegetic continuation with minimal interpretive leap;
- `STRONGLY_IMPLIED` - not fully explicit, but the sequence strongly supports a diegetic reading;
- `SYMBOLIC` - formal/metaphorical content that need not correspond literally to an event;
- `OPEN` - evidence does not justify stronger classification.

A sequence can contain more than one class simultaneously.

## 15. Character-reconstruction evidence rule

A character-model proposition becomes `STABILIZED` only when it has adequate support across contexts or a compelling repeated trigger-response pattern.

The source lock prohibits turning one vivid crisis scene into an unqualified personality law.

Every stabilized behavioral rule should carry:

- supporting episode locators;
- trigger/context;
- relationship dependence;
- counterexample or explicit absence of known counterexample;
- confidence;
- development/time boundary.
