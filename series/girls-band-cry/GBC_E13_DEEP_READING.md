## Archival source: Assistant Turn 48 — Verbatim post-Episode-13 ED/epilogue analysis


Yes. The strongest available evidence confirms that the ED is intended as **post–Episode 13 material**, not merely a symbolic montage of the characters’ personalities. A post by ED visual creator **Tejima nari**, reproduced in contemporary commentary, states that it was drawn as “an epilogue after Episode 13.” The official no-credit ED had already been published on April 13, 2024, meaning the anime was quietly showing viewers its future destination well before the finale aired. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://note.com&sz=128)note（ノート）+1](https://note.com/shuhei_sa/n/n20de7f09a8ae)

That said, I would distinguish two layers:

1.  **A broadly literal epilogue:** Togenashi Togeari undertakes a self-organized road tour after its agency departure.

2.  **Symbolic visual narration:** the threads, past Nina, the changing phone display, and some collage transitions express what that future means rather than documenting events naturalistically.


The still bundle is enough for the main reading. An isolated clip would mostly refine the timing of the final hand-grab and thread transformations.

# What online commentary broadly agrees upon

The two detailed Japanese readings I found identify essentially the same narrative:

-   Nina has resumed studying, although not necessarily the same university-prep path she abandoned.

-   Each member is at home but remains magnetically drawn toward music.

-   Nina sees a poster advertising a Diamond Dust national tour.

-   She sends the others a proposal—implicitly, “Let’s tour too.”

-   The five load their instruments into a yellow van.

-   They travel together, apparently organizing their own small-scale tour.

-   Miura looks after Momoka’s cat and Tomo and Rupa’s pets.

-   The van breaks down.

-   Subaru cuts her hair during the journey.

-   Nina and Tomo quarrel and reconcile through a Mont Blanc dessert.

-   Nina’s family sees evidence of her travels.

-   At the end, the music on Nina’s phone changes from Momoka’s 「空の箱」 to Togenashi Togeari’s 「誰にもなれない私だから」. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://note.com&sz=128)note（ノート）+1](https://note.com/shuhei_sa/n/n20de7f09a8ae)


Where commentators diverge is the agency question. One interprets Miura’s continued involvement as possibly suggesting that the resignation did not ultimately go through; another thinks they almost certainly left but retained a personal relationship with her. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://note.com&sz=128)note（ノート）+1](https://note.com/shuhei_sa/n/n20de7f09a8ae)

My reading is closer to the second position:

> **The ED is most coherent as the band having left Golden Archer while refusing to turn that institutional separation into a personal severance from Miura.**

That fits the series better than a secret reversal of their decision.

# The ED as a second ending to Episode 13

The spoken ending of Episode 13 says, in effect:

> We lost.
> We may have made the wrong choices.
> We are still going to continue.

The ED then shows what **continuing** actually looks like.

It is not:

-   sudden chart success;

-   a major-label rescue;

-   a sold-out arena;

-   Diamond Dust’s defeat;

-   or vindication through a public verdict.


It is a secondhand van, self-financed travel, mechanical failure, arguments, convenience-store food, pet care, bad driving and small acts of repair.

Episode 13 rejects the agency’s institutional road. The ED does not replace it with fantasy. It shows the band having to make a road materially.

That is why the van travels first along a thin line and then along a path made from the members’ intertwined colored threads:

> **Once the external career structure disappears, their relationships and instruments become the infrastructure carrying them forward.**

The road does not preexist.

They generate it.

# Nina studying again: the end of all-or-nothing freedom

The ED begins with Nina surrounded by notebooks, vocabulary materials and what appears to be a red memorization sheet. Online readings reasonably identify this as studying, although the precise objective—university entrance, a high-school equivalency credential, general study or something else—is not established. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://note.com&sz=128)note（ノート）+1](https://note.com/shuhei_sa/n/n20de7f09a8ae)

The thematic meaning is stronger than the literal educational destination.

Nina’s original idea of commitment required destroying every alternative:

-   quit preparatory school;

-   reject family money;

-   return the bankbook;

-   cut off retreat;

-   prove music is the only possible path.


The ED shows that choosing music did not have to mean rejecting learning forever.

She can study because study is no longer solely:

-   her father’s plan;

-   a weapon against her bullies;

-   proof that she did not lose;

-   or the respectable future she was forced to preserve.


It may now belong to her.

That represents a major maturation of the “cut off every retreat” philosophy she inherited from young Momoka:

> **Autonomy does not require Nina to amputate every capacity associated with the life she rejected. She can take something back without going backward.**

The reflection of uniformed Nina beside her supports this. Past Nina remains present, but present Nina is not simply restored to that old life.

# The five separate rooms: music after crisis

The first portion shows every member engaged in ordinary private life:

-   Nina studies and listens to music.

-   Momoka handles her guitar, old CDs and demanding cat.

-   Subaru plays a video game but turns toward her drumsticks.

-   Rupa reads with her bass nearby.

-   Tomo lies down while her fingers imitate keyboard movement.


Online commentary reads this as each member being unable to stop thinking about music even during downtime. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://note.com&sz=128)note（ノート）+1](https://note.com/shuhei_sa/n/n20de7f09a8ae)

I would sharpen that:

> **Music has ceased being only an emergency response. It has entered habit.**

Earlier, they turned to music because:

-   Nina could not endure silence;

-   Momoka was attempting a final farewell;

-   Subaru needed resistance to acting;

-   Tomo and Rupa needed movement after professional stagnation.


Here, no one is in acute crisis. They still reach toward their instruments.

The band is becoming a life rather than merely a rescue mechanism.

Yet they are initially isolated in separate panels. Music exists in each room, but the group remains dispersed. Nina’s message activates the transition from private inclination to collective movement.

She remains the catalyst—but now the others knowingly choose to answer.

# Diamond Dust’s national tour: rivalry transformed into propulsion

Nina sees what appears to be a poster for Diamond Dust’s nationwide tour and immediately runs.

This could have reproduced her earlier pattern:

> They are succeeding.
> We lost.
> Therefore our existence is invalid.

Instead, she turns the sight into a proposal:

> They are touring.
> Then we should move too.

That is a genuine development.

Diamond Dust remains:

-   rival;

-   provocation;

-   unresolved personal history;

-   and an external measure against which Nina still reacts.


But comparison no longer produces only:

-   outrage;

-   despair;

-   demands that Momoka condemn them;

-   or compulsive online searching.


It produces action.

Nina does not need Diamond Dust to be false in order for Togenashi Togeari to move.

And importantly, the scale is different. Diamond Dust has a formally promoted **national tour**. Togenashi Togeari has a yellow van and a plan seemingly proposed by text message.

The ED does not pretend they have become equal institutionally.

It gives them an equivalent verb:

> **Both bands travel.**

Diamond Dust follows the major professional route. Togenashi Togeari constructs a rough independent route at its own scale.

---

# Nina and Momoka’s handshake: a new founding compact

Before departure, Nina and Momoka shake hands beside the van.

This recalls several earlier gestures:

-   the Episode 1 middle finger;

-   the Episode 2 pinky promise;

-   the Episode 8 confession;

-   the five-member pinky ritual before Episode 11’s performance.


The handshake is less romantically or rebelliously coded than those gestures. It looks almost contractual.

That is appropriate after leaving the agency.

They have rejected one formal contract, but they still require a compact of responsibility:

-   Nina proposes movement.

-   Momoka appears to invest materially in the van.

-   Both understand the financial and practical risk.

-   Neither is promising victory.

-   They agree to go.


It also places them on somewhat more equal ground.

Nina is no longer only:

-   the fan;

-   the rescued listener;

-   or the younger singer Momoka directs.


Momoka is no longer only:

-   the musical origin;

-   the experienced adult;

-   or the person whose decision defines the future.


They are partners agreeing to undertake something foolish together.

The gesture remains intimate, especially given the series’ Nina–Momoka grammar, but it is not exclusively romantic. It is also the re-founding of the band outside institutional protection.

# Rupa taking the passenger seat: the band exceeds the Nina–Momoka dyad

One particularly good comic detail is that Nina initially occupies the front passenger seat beside Momoka, but Rupa removes her and takes the position herself.

The practical explanation is obvious: Momoka is an unreliable driver, while Rupa is experienced and competent. Commentary also connects this to Episode 7’s road trip, where Rupa had to take over driving responsibilities. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://note.com&sz=128)note（ノート）](https://note.com/shuhei_sa/n/n20de7f09a8ae)

But it also has structural value.

The series’ emotional center is Nina and Momoka, and the ED preserves that. Yet the band cannot be organized entirely around their dyad.

Rupa inserts herself as the practical co-pilot because:

-   intimacy does not equal competence;

-   being most emotionally important to the driver does not mean being most useful in the front seat;

-   and the other three members are not passengers inside Nina and Momoka’s story.


This is Episode 9–12’s five-member correction expressed through blocking.

Nina starts the journey.

Rupa helps keep it on the road.

# The thread becomes the road

The ED begins with a red thread extending from Nina’s phone while 「空の箱」 is displayed.

The line passes through:

-   Momoka;

-   the other members;

-   their instruments and equipment;

-   and eventually several distinct member colors.


Those threads intertwine into the multicolored road beneath the van.

This is the ED’s central image.

The red thread can certainly suggest:

-   bond;

-   causality;

-   destiny;

-   and, around Nina and Momoka, romantic symbolism.


But the full sequence makes it larger than a single “red string of fate” pairing.

The thread’s narrative route is:

> Momoka’s old song
> → Nina’s phone
> → Nina’s survival
> → Nina’s encounter with Momoka
> → Subaru
> → Tomo and Rupa
> → five instruments
> → one road.

「空の箱」 is therefore not merely the Nina–Momoka love object. It is the initiating signal that becomes a social network.

The old Diamond Dust song does not remain a relic. It creates the conditions for a new band to move.

And because the road is composed of their differently colored threads, the ED rejects assimilation:

> They travel together without becoming one identical person.

That aligns precisely with the title:

> **Because I cannot become anyone else.**

Their inability to become one another is not the obstacle to ensemble.

It is what makes the ensemble necessary.

# Miura caring for the pets: separation without enemy-making

Miura appears to be caring for:

-   Momoka’s cat;

-   Tomo and Rupa’s snake;

-   and their lizard or gecko.


This clearly means the relationship has survived the agency departure.

I do not think the most persuasive conclusion is that the band secretly stayed under contract. The episode’s resignation would lose much of its force if quietly withdrawn in an ED that never clearly states so.

The more thematically coherent reading is:

> **They left the agency, but Miura did not become an enemy.**

That is a major endpoint for a story whose characters repeatedly confuse:

-   disagreement with betrayal;

-   institutional conflict with personal malice;

-   compromise with falsity;

-   and separation with total erasure.


Miura can believe that leaving is professionally foolish and still help them.

Togenashi Togeari can reject Golden Archer’s structure and still trust Miura with living things they care about.

This is the mature form of Nina’s Episode 12 lesson about **本音** and **建前**, mixed motives and adult relationships. A bond does not have to fit one category:

-   manager or stranger;

-   ally or opponent;

-   inside or outside.


Miura may no longer represent them professionally, but she remains part of the relational world the band created.

---

# The family image and the unsmoked cigarette

Nina’s family sees a picture or online update from the tour. Her father appears ready to smoke, then does not light the cigarette.

That follows Nina’s Episode 10 request that he reduce his smoking. Online commentary also reads the shot this way. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://note.com&sz=128)note（ノート）](https://note.com/shuhei_sa/n/n20de7f09a8ae)

The moment confirms that reconciliation was not a temporary episode-resolution.

Nina’s voice continues to reach home.

Her family does not control the tour, and may not even have been warned about it. But they can see the life she chose. Her father changes a small bodily habit because of something she said.

This creates a quiet parallel with music:

-   Momoka’s song entered Nina and altered her life.

-   Nina’s words enter her father and alter an ordinary action.


Connection does not require living under the same roof or accepting the same future.

---

# The van breaks down: independence has material consequences

The vehicle eventually emits smoke and stops.

This may be the ED’s most important correction to its own freedom fantasy.

Leaving the agency does not mean:

-   no more compromise;

-   no more logistics;

-   no more money problems;

-   no more authority;

-   or pure music unconstrained by infrastructure.


It means the members themselves now face:

-   transportation;

-   repairs;

-   budgeting;

-   equipment;

-   accommodation;

-   scheduling;

-   and interpersonal blame when something goes wrong.


The agency absorbed some of those burdens. Independence returns them to the band.

The ED does not depict this as proof they were wrong. It makes breakdown part of the road.

That matches the song’s final principle:

> 「傷つき堕ちるたび　歌えばいい」
> Whenever you are hurt and fall, you can sing again.

Falling is not abolished by the right decision.

It becomes something around which continuation must be organized.

---

# Subaru’s haircut: practical change and self-authorship

During the journey, Subaru appears to compare her long hair with Nina’s shorter hair after bathing. Rupa then cuts it dramatically shorter.

Online commentary proposes two compatible readings:

-   short hair is simply easier to wash and dry while touring;

-   it may also represent Subaru’s renewed commitment to the band and possible separation from the actress-image inherited from her grandmother. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://note.com&sz=128)note（ノート）](https://note.com/shuhei_sa/n/n20de7f09a8ae)


I think the practical explanation is intentionally primary. That prevents the act from becoming a heavy-handed “I have cut away my past” symbol.

But the symbolism remains.

Subaru’s long hair belongs to the highly managed visual identity of:

-   actress training;

-   famous grandmother;

-   inherited femininity;

-   and the socially polished Subaru who could enter any room correctly.


She allows Rupa—not her grandmother, school or professional stylist—to change that body.

The haircut is:

-   self-chosen;

-   improvised;

-   performed within the chosen group;

-   and adapted to the life she is actually living.


It does not prove she has ceased acting. It shows that acting no longer has exclusive authority over what her body should look like.

# Nina and Tomo: conflict becomes repairable routine

The ED shows Nina and Tomo apparently quarreling. Later, Subaru seems to direct Nina toward a solution: Nina gives Tomo an expensive Mont Blanc dessert, which Tomo loves.

This is a perfect postscript to Episode 9.

The band has not become conflict-free.

Nina remains:

-   intrusive;

-   impulsive;

-   loud;

-   and liable to irritate Tomo.


Tomo remains:

-   severe;

-   easily angered;

-   and willing to say that Nina’s ideas are terrible.


What has changed is the scale assigned to conflict.

The disagreement does not become:

> We are incompatible.
> The band was false.
> Someone must leave.

It becomes:

> Nina annoyed Tomo.
> Nina needs to repair it.
> Tomo can be reached through a particular act of care.
> The others help translate.

The Mont Blanc is comic, but it reflects a serious relational skill:

> **Love is not merely understanding another person abstractly. It is learning the concrete forms in which she can receive repair.**

Subaru again mediates. Rupa and Momoka watch. The whole ensemble participates in keeping two difficult people connected.

---

# The tour’s shifting day, weather and color

The van crosses:

-   bright summer sky;

-   afternoon;

-   sunset;

-   rain or heavy cloud;

-   night;

-   and dawn.


This compresses a journey without presenting a single climactic destination.

There is no final venue shown as the end of the road.

That is deliberate.

The agency offered a ladder:

> release → promotion → sales → tie-in → professional growth.

The independent road is represented horizontally rather than vertically.

They do not visibly ascend toward one institutional summit. They move through changing conditions.

This is a different career philosophy:

> not “reach the top and receive the verdict,”
> but “continue traveling, performing and accumulating witnesses.”

It does not reject ambition. It changes the temporal unit by which success is understood.

# Nina sleeping: she no longer has to generate every movement

Near the end, Nina sleeps in the van with earphones in. The others look down at her, apparently take a group photograph including her, and then continue preparing to move.

This is easy to overlook, but it is important.

Nina has spent the series as the person who cannot permit stopping:

-   chase Momoka;

-   force the band to continue;

-   demand a goal;

-   insist upon the festival;

-   insist upon Diamond Dust competition;

-   refuse the agency’s retreat;

-   generate the next decision.


In the ED, she can sleep.

The band remains around her.

She does not have to keep screaming to prevent everyone from disappearing.

The final group image includes Nina even though she is unconscious and not performing for the photograph. She belongs when she is not actively producing force.

That may be one of the clearest signs that Togenashi Togeari has become a home.

---

# Past Nina offers the phone; Momoka takes present Nina’s hand

The final symbolic sequence is the ED’s richest image.

A school-uniform version of Nina reaches toward present Nina with the phone. The device signifies 「空の箱」:

-   the song she heard while trapped in her former life;

-   the voice that told her she was not wrong;

-   and the artistic object through which she survived.


Present Nina reaches toward it.

But Momoka takes her hand or wrist.

One reading, reflected in online commentary, is that Momoka has become the living connection through which Nina moves beyond the past rather than remaining alone with the song that saved her. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://note.com&sz=128)note（ノート）](https://note.com/shuhei_sa/n/n20de7f09a8ae)

I would go further.

The scene answers Episode 8:

> 「私はあなたの思い出じゃない」
> “I am not your memory.”

Past Nina offers a memorial object. Present Nina might simply keep reaching backward toward the moment of rescue, endlessly replaying the song and asking it to settle her life.

Momoka instead touches **Nina**, not the phone.

This creates several reversals:

### Nina is no longer only saved by recorded Momoka

The living Momoka is now beside her.

### Momoka no longer preserves Nina as her younger self

She draws present Nina forward rather than enclosing her inside the schoolgirl image through whom Momoka recovered her past.

### Their relationship is no longer contained inside 「空の箱」

The song initiated the bond. It does not exhaust it.

The hand-grab is certainly intimate and can sustain the romantic or yuri-charged reading of their relationship. But the primary structural statement is broader:

> **They choose the living relationship over remaining transfixed by the artifact through which they first found one another.**

# From 「空の箱」 to 「誰にもなれない私だから」

At the end, Nina’s phone changes from 「空の箱」 to the ED song, accompanied by an image of all five members.

That completes the transmission chain that has structured the series:

> Momoka’s experience
> → 「空の箱」
> → Nina’s listening
> → Nina’s survival
> → Nina and Momoka’s encounter
> → the band
> → a new song
> → a shared future.

The old song is not deleted or repudiated.

It has done its work.

Nina began as someone who needed to hear:

> You are not wrong.

The ED song’s mature answer is different:

> 「大逸れてないよってただ聞きたくて」
> I only wanted to hear that I had not strayed completely off course.

> 「今日も変われないままだっていいんだ」
> Even if I remain unchanged today, that can be all right.

> 「君と歌っていたい」
> I want to sing with you.

> 「このままで歩いて行くんだ」
> I will keep walking as I am.

The movement is away from objective vindication and toward sustainable relation.

The phone no longer displays the song proving Momoka’s past was right.

It displays the song expressing what the five are doing now.

# Whose song is 「誰にもなれない私だから」?

One online reading treats the lyrics especially as Momoka’s growth after 「空の箱」: she stops defining rock through opposition, accepts Diamond Dust’s different path and decides to continue singing as herself. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://note.com&sz=128)note（ノート）](https://note.com/kanisuky/n/n4bf5518d377f)

That is persuasive, particularly for lines about:

-   being unable to become someone else;

-   being wounded by ordinary conversation;

-   discarding crossed lies;

-   continuing despite being hated;

-   and wanting to sing with “you.”


But the visuals deliberately distribute the song.

It belongs simultaneously to:

-   **Momoka**, who can neither return to old Diamond Dust nor become its opposite forever;

-   **Nina**, who cannot become the compliant daughter, ideal student or socially agreeable victim;

-   **Subaru**, who cannot become the actress imagined by her grandmother;

-   **Tomo**, who cannot become an easy collaborator by lying about what she hears;

-   **Rupa**, who cannot become the person she was before loss.


The title does not mean:

> I am alone because no one understands me.

It means:

> **Because none of us can become the person another system prescribed, we need a form of togetherness that does not depend upon becoming alike.**

That is Togenashi Togeari.

# What the ED says about leaving the agency

Read as a post-agency epilogue, the ED’s answer is neither:

> Leaving the agency was obviously correct.

nor:

> They were immature and will inevitably return.

It says:

> **They accepted the consequences and continued.**

They have:

-   less infrastructure;

-   more practical burden;

-   continuing arguments;

-   an unreliable vehicle;

-   no visible proof of commercial success;

-   and no final destination.


They also have:

-   self-directed movement;

-   a functioning five-person relationship;

-   Miura’s continued human support;

-   Nina’s family connection;

-   the ability to repair conflict;

-   and songs that now belong to them.


The agency departure is therefore not vindicated by instant success. It is made meaningful through the life they construct afterward.

That repeats Nina’s Episode 12 development:

> 「そう思えるようにするんです」
> “We’re going to make it something we can believe was right.”

The ED is the first evidence of them doing exactly that.

# Final interpretation

The ED is not merely “Togenashi Togeari goes on tour.”

It is a compact second-season premise and a summation of the first:

> **Five people begin separately, each unable to stop reaching toward music. Nina sees Diamond Dust moving and converts rivalry into a proposal rather than despair. The song that once connected Nina only to Momoka extends through all five members, becomes a road, and carries them through the unglamorous material life of an independent band. They remain quarrelsome, impractical and vulnerable to failure, but conflict no longer automatically becomes farewell. At the end, Nina stops reaching only toward the recording that saved her because the living people produced by that song are beside her. 「空の箱」 becomes 「誰にもなれない私だから」: inherited music becomes collective authorship.**

So yes: reading it as the post-agency future materially improves the finale.

Episode 13 does not end at “we quit the agency.”

The ED supplies the real final clause:

> **We left—and then we kept going.**

No isolated clip is necessary for that conclusion. The clip would be most useful only for a frame-by-frame study of the final past-Nina/Momoka hand transition and the exact rhythm by which the five threads merge into the road.
