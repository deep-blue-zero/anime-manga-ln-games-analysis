# Girls Band Cry E10 - V2 Deep Reading

## 1. Episode thesis

Episode 10 is about **reconciliation without rollback**.

The episode puts two apparently separate disputes into the same structure. Momoka is approached by a professional scout who loves the old Diamond Dust and dislikes its current form. Nina is approached by the family she left after her father failed to support her against the school. In each case the easy version of reconciliation would require one side to erase the earlier conflict: Diamond Dust was either right or wrong; Nina's father either loved her or betrayed her; Nina either returns home or remains independent; Momoka either accepts the market or defends her old sound.

E10 refuses those binaries.

Momoka can say of Diamond Dust's adaptation:

> `それを間違っているとは思ってません`
>
> E10 03:24.650-03:26.780

and immediately continue:

> `ここにいる４人は それでも勝負したい`
>
> `私のやりたかった歌で挑戦してみたいと`
>
> `その言葉に乗っかってみようと思っています`
>
> E10 03:33.490-03:42.590

The current Diamond Dust can have made a legitimate survival choice, found an audience, and still be the rival against which Momoka wants to test another possibility. Recognition does not require surrender.

Nina's family conflict resolves by the same logic. E10 finally gives Nina direct evidence that her parents love her. Her father also performs the alliance she had wanted from him by pressing the school and telling her:

> `お前は被害者なんだ`
>
> E10 14:02.250-14:03.830

But the episode does not retroactively make his earlier failure harmless. He never gives Nina a clean verbal apology for not taking her side at the time. Nina does not resume the university plan, revoke her musical commitment, or accept paternal authority over her future. Instead she tells her sister:

> `私は あの歌で飛べたの`
>
> E10 15:40.720-15:43.020

and later:

> `でも 今の私が一番好き`
>
> `胸張って言える これが本当の私だって`
>
> E10 16:07.290 onward

The mature movement is therefore not from rebellion to obedience. It is from needing a total verdict about the past toward being able to inhabit a chosen present while admitting that love, failure, gratitude, anger, and autonomy can coexist.

That movement matters for authorship as much as family.

Momoka asks Nina to write lyrics for the song that E09 left unfinished. Her phrasing is deliberately recursive:

> `見てみたいんだ 「私の歌」が書いた私の歌を`
>
> E10 04:19.170-04:22.920

The person once reached and partially constituted by Momoka's song is being asked to author what comes next. Nina initially cannot do it because words, as she says, become something outside the self once given form:

> `言葉ってさ 外にあるんだよ`
>
> E10 04:49.360-04:51.780

The episode then links that blockage to the family relationship Nina has tried to terminate through a letter and avoidance. It does not provide the completed lyric text, so V2 cannot claim that a particular family proposition becomes a particular lyric, rhythm, chord, or arrangement decision. What E10 can establish is one step earlier:

> **lived conflict becomes an authorship catalyst.**

Nina returns from Kumamoto saying:

> `私の作った歌 今度歌うから`
>
> E10 19:48.840-19:52.220

and finally tells Momoka:

> `はい できました いい歌が`
>
> E10 22:06.520-22:09.230

The listener has become a writer.

The episode's governing formulation is therefore:

> **E10 separates reconciliation from restoration. A relationship can become livable without declaring its earlier injury unreal; support can cease claiming ownership without disappearing; a rival's compromise can be legitimate without ending competition; and Nina can return home without returning to the self she had to leave. The same movement turns transmitted art into shared authorship: Momoka's song helped Nina become someone who can now write back.**

## 3. Prospective state entering E10

### Nina

At the E09 boundary Nina has:

- explicitly chosen music and declared withdrawal from preparatory school;
- survived deidealization of Momoka and Diamond Dust;
- rejected being used as Momoka's preserved younger self;
- named her attachment to Momoka as `告白` without establishing conventional couple status;
- begun converting dependence into contribution through guitar practice;
- shown that negative technical evaluation can hurt without automatically ending effort;
- retained a recurrent tendency toward categorical compression under high identity stakes;
- retained an anti-instrumentalization ethic that remains more reliable as a critique than as perfect self-regulation.

Her family wound remains incompletely integrated. E07 established that the university route was partly self-authored as proof that rejecting the school's settlement would not destroy her future. E10 therefore cannot be read as merely `parents versus rebellious child`.

### Momoka

At E10's opening Momoka has:

- returned behaviorally to professional possibility;
- directly revealed that her protection of Nina was partly projection onto `あの頃の私`;
- accepted Nina's rejection of that preservation contract;
- continued to show strong activation around concrete craft even when her abstract professional self-model is pessimistic;
- recognized Diamond Dust's choice as more legitimate than Nina's earlier purity binary allowed;
- retained rough/direct intervention styles whose moral status depends on function and jurisdiction, not surface softness.

The new test is whether projection correction produces a less proprietary relationship to Nina and whether professional competition requires renewed moral condemnation of Diamond Dust.

### Subaru

Subaru remains a positive band participant and decision architect, but E10 need not center her. The most diagnostic question is whether she continues helping structure conditions for another person's decision without converting herself into final moral authority.

### Tomo

E09 has just overturned CP2's conservative boundary. Tomo can convert present inadequacy into permanent judgment. E09 also shows the opposite capacity: she can re-enter criticism as continuing contact and can initiate a bounded compositional objection to Momoka without rupture.

E10 therefore cannot score Tomo by asking whether she is `harsh`. It must ask what function a harsh line performs and whether it moves or closes work/relationship.

### Rupa

Rupa enters with a narrow but meaningful trauma boundary:

- her catastrophic family loss is established;
- E08 established calm public regulation under racism and an explicit need for rock as another expressive channel;
- E09 kept her strongest psychological reading uncertainty-bounded;
- no source yet licenses turning bereavement into an explanation for her entire temperament.

E10 has the opportunity to show whether the loss generates a specific present decision rule rather than a total personality theory.

### Ensemble

The five-person band has survived:

- ideology conflict;
- professional-goal disagreement;
- Momoka's threatened exit;
- Nina/Momoka projection rupture;
- Tomo's severe criticism;
- ordinary domestic friction.

Its corrective functions are increasingly distributed. E10 tests whether found-family attachment becomes a substitute that lets Nina avoid biological-family conflict, or whether the band can support her by refusing to become an escape hatch.

# 4. Sequential audiovisual reading

## 4.1 00:00-02:24 - Professionalization begins with merchandise, not transcendence

E10 opens at the merch booth rather than with a grand artistic declaration. The band sells `トゲトゲラバーバンド`, towels, and cheki-style interactions while asking whether they are actually popular.

This is an important continuation of E05-E06's material logic. Togenashi Togeari does not enter professional visibility by escaping commerce. It enters through labor that turns name, face, relationship, and small objects into revenue and audience contact.

The comic distribution of fan attention also matters. Rupa's natural popularity, Nina's sales energy, and complaints about the strange band name keep the scene from becoming either a cynical indictment or a triumphalist success montage. Professional identity is being assembled through mundane work.

Then Miura presents a Golden Archer business card.

The sequence establishes CP2-P25's basic domain before any ideology appears: professional opportunity arrives because the band has become legible enough to scout, but it immediately creates new questions about audience proof, label leverage, artistic direction, and representation.

## 4.2 02:24-03:23 - Miura arrives as an admirer with a potentially narrowing story

Miura says she likes Diamond Dust but not the current Diamond Dust:

> `あれは 元のバンドの良さが完全に失われています`

and tells Momoka she wants the music Momoka had aimed for to be properly realized.

This is not yet exploitation. Miura's admiration is sincere enough within the scene. But her offer comes with a ready-made narrative about who Momoka is: authentic old Diamond Dust displaced by an inferior present form.

That makes Miura structurally interesting after E08. Nina and Momoka have just dismantled a relationship in which each partially needed the other to preserve a past self. A professional representative who loves a remembered Momoka could, in principle, reproduce another preservation pressure.

E10 does **not** establish that Miura will do so. That remains an OPEN institutional risk. The source's immediate diagnostic fact is Momoka's answer.

## 4.3 03:23-03:43 - Momoka refuses nostalgia as the price of professional support

Momoka says the industry told Diamond Dust that its old sound would not work and was out of step with the era. Then:

> `それを間違っているとは思ってません`

She adds that the current Diamond Dust has in fact been accepted by fans.

This is one of the most important Momoka developments since E08.

She does not need to call the market stupid in order to defend her own music. She does not need to call her former bandmates false in order to compete with them. The existence of audience acceptance becomes evidence she is willing to admit even when it complicates her preferred artistic history.

Then she defines her present commitment positively:

> `ここにいる４人は それでも勝負したい`
>
> `私のやりたかった歌で挑戦してみたいと`
>
> `その言葉に乗っかってみようと思っています`

`乗っかってみよう` is revealing. Momoka does not say `I finally know I was right`. She says, in effect, that the other four want to test this music and she will ride that willingness.

Current rule:

> **Momoka can recognize a rival strategy as legitimate while still choosing competitive falsification of her own pessimistic professional model.**

This strongly confirms CP2-P12 and supports P09/P13.

## 4.4 03:43-04:08 - The festival turns audience into bargaining leverage

Miura explains that a strong festival result will give the agency a stronger basis for arguing to the record company that Togenashi Togeari's current musical direction should be preserved. A visible fan base changes the negotiation.

The important distinction is between **freedom** and **leverage**.

A professional contract would not erase:

- record-company preferences;
- market expectations;
- audience numbers;
- promotional demands;
- scheduling;
- financial dependence.

Instead, success can improve the band's negotiating position inside those structures.

That is a much more mature industry model than `independent = free / professional = controlled`.

E10 therefore strongly confirms CP2-P25:

> **professionalization intensifies institutional constraint while potentially changing who has leverage inside it.**

## 4.5 04:08-04:24 - Momoka makes Nina an author

Momoka asks Nina to write the lyrics for the song under development:

> `この前の曲の詞`
>
> `仁菜が考えてくれないか`

and explains:

> `見てみたいんだ 「私の歌」が書いた私の歌を`

This is the clearest prospective authorship handoff so far.

The phrase cannot be reduced to `Momoka delegates a task`. The quotation marks around `「私の歌」` refer back to the relational transformation in which Nina became, for Momoka, a living encounter with what her own song had once carried.

E08 made Nina reject being a museum object for Momoka's lost younger self. E10's answer is not to eliminate influence. It converts influence into **authorship**.

Momoka is no longer asking Nina to stay the person her older song produced. She asks what that person will now write.

This strongly advances:

- differentiated contribution;
- Momoka's movement from private auteur toward shared authorship;
- the anti-ownership model of artistic transmission.

It does not yet prove strict conflict-to-composition because the resulting lyric text is withheld.

## 4.6 04:24-05:12 - Nina is frightened by the externality of words

Nina says she has something she wants to write but cannot produce the lyrics. Her explanation is unusually explicit:

> `言葉ってさ 外にあるんだよ`
>
> `言葉にした瞬間`
>
> `自分の中にあったものが外に出て`
>
> `形になっちゃうんだよ`

An unspoken feeling can remain contradictory and revisable. A lyric becomes an object:

- repeatable;
- judged by others;
- detached from the immediate speaker;
- capable of meaning more or less than the originating feeling;
- something whose consequences the writer must inhabit.

This connects directly to Nina's broader problem with verdicts. She often wants language to settle reality—`間違ってない`, `負けてない`, `無理`—yet here she is afraid of the same property in authorship: once language takes form, it can begin deciding what the feeling `is`.

Tomo's response is characteristically unsentimental. She objects to Nina speaking as though someone who has never written lyrics can lecture people who have spent years making songs. The line is abrasive but bounded to the work/history at hand; it does not reproduce E09's `一生` forecast.

## 4.7 05:20-06:30 - The father enters Nina's independent world and Nina hides

Nina's father comes to Kawasaki looking for her. At work, Nina ducks out of view in cap and mask while he asks after `井芹という子`.

The scene is visually important because Nina's independence does not make her immune to regression in the father's presence. The person who can stand onstage, confront Momoka, challenge Diamond Dust, and choose professional music becomes physically concealed when her father enters the room.

The source supports a state change, not a global claim that Nina `is a child around him`. The precise result is:

> **paternal contact activates a domain in which Nina expects dialogue to become control before dialogue has begun.**

## 4.8 06:30-07:14 - Professional responsibility becomes Nina's avoidance argument

At Subaru's, Nina says:

> `通じないんだよ 話`

and insists their values are too different. She says she has no time for this because she must write lyrics, the festival is close, and everyone's future is involved.

Subaru answers:

> `なんか逃げてるようにしか聞こえないけど`

The construction is tight: Nina says she cannot address the family wound because the band needs the lyric; the episode has already shown that the lyric itself is blocked by feeling Nina cannot externalize.

This does not prove an omniscient one-cause psychology in which `father issue = writer's block`. It does establish that the episode formally couples the two problems and makes Nina's professional urgency function as an avoidance rationale.

Subaru's line is a modest P15-adjacent example, but not a strong decision-architecture test by itself. She names the interactional problem rather than dictating Nina's final substantive conclusion.

## 4.9 07:14-07:38 - Nina's sister turns Nina's anti-evasion ethic against her

Nina's sister is angry about the letter rejecting future financial support. Her most important instruction is:

> `本当に言いたいことがあるんなら`
>
> `逃げてないで直接言いなっせ`
>
> E10 07:34.360-07:36.030 onward

Nina's letter can be sincere and still be one-way communication. She announced an endpoint while protecting herself from the other's answer.

That behavior is especially significant because Nina has repeatedly condemned people who decide privately what a relationship or situation means and then present the result as finished fact.

E10 therefore adds a useful Nina self-counterexample:

> **anti-evasion and anti-instrumentalization are values Nina can articulate before she applies them consistently to herself.**

The episode does not turn this hypocrisy into total moral equivalence between Nina and her family. The point is narrower: a valid grievance does not exempt Nina from reciprocal process.

## 4.10 07:50-09:30 - The band refuses to become a hiding place, but does so coercively

Nina discovers that Momoka has changed the apartment lock. She objects:

> `住んでるのは私ですよ`
>
> `新しい鍵をください`

The objection is valid. The apartment is Nina's home. Controlling access to it is not transformed into pure benevolence merely because Momoka has a good reason.

The band then coordinates to send Nina to Kumamoto. Work is cleared; Tomo and Rupa provide the trip; Subaru supports direct conversation; Momoka says Nina cannot use Momoka's home until she meets her father.

This is an important correction against a sentimental `found family is always non-coercive` model.

The band's technique includes:

- withholding access;
- pre-arranging logistics;
- exerting collective pressure;
- making a judgment about what Nina needs.

What differentiates it from the coercion Nina fled is **not** absence of force. It is the demanded endpoint. The band does not require Nina to:

- forgive her father;
- return to school;
- accept the university path;
- resume financial dependence;
- leave music;
- declare her family right.

It requires contact with an unresolved interlocutor and then leaves the substantive answer to Nina.

This strengthens the corpus-wide claim that coercive technique and benevolent intent must be analytically separable.

## 4.11 08:16-08:42 - Rupa turns bereavement into one narrow rule

Rupa says she once planned to call her family, work ran late, and she assumed tomorrow would still be available. It was not.

Her conclusion:

> `思っているより 時間やきっかけはないものですよ`
>
> E10 08:37.590-08:41.180

This is the first secure direct causal bridge between Rupa's bereavement and a present social rule.

The rule is narrow:

> **Do not assume that a relational opportunity remains indefinitely renewable.**

It does **not** license:

- `Rupa is calm because her family died`;
- `grief explains all her patience`;
- `she understands everyone because of trauma`;
- `her maturity is the product of bereavement`.

Tomo's `自分語りはうざいよね` interrupts the solemnity, but motive should remain open. It can function as abrasion, tone deflation, familiarity, or protection against sentimental overexposure; E10 does not securely identify which interior intention governs it.

## 4.12 08:50-09:30 - Momoka identifies the unresolved relational wound

Momoka says:

> `お前が一番引きずってんのはさ`
>
> `受験でも いじめでもないだろう`
>
> `父親が味方してくれなかったことじゃないのか`
>
> E10 09:16.460-09:19.340

This is strong social perception, but it is not neutral therapy. Momoka then uses the diagnosis to compel action:

> if that is not the issue, you should be able to meet him;
> if you can meet him, go meet him;
> until then, you cannot enter my home.

E08's projection rupture therefore does not produce a simple `Momoka stops controlling Nina` result.

Instead, E10 helps separate two mechanisms:

1. **projection-linked protection** — Momoka tries to preserve her lost younger self inside Nina;
2. **Nina-specific intervention** — Momoka can accurately identify Nina's avoidance and still overstep Nina's immediate access/choice in trying to correct it.

That distinction strongly confirms CP2-P10 while preserving a counterexample to any claim that projection correction equals perfect jurisdiction.

## 4.13 09:42-12:08 - Home begins with the old argument still intact

Nina returns to Kumamoto. The visual movement is a reversal of E01's outward journey, but the source does not frame it as ideological retreat.

The first father/daughter conversation immediately demonstrates why.

Her father sees Nina's professional direction as a violation of the bargain under which she left home. Nina hears his argument as another claim that he can define what her independence was allowed to mean.

The father points to the family's material role:

- school withdrawal procedures;
- the move;
- housing connections;
- the legal reality of Nina being a minor;
- adult guarantee/support.

His key formulation is:

> `大人の庇護のもとで 管理下にあるということだ`
>
> E10 11:53.870-11:57.250

`庇護` and `管理` sit together. Protection and management are linguistically bundled.

Nina immediately maps that bundle back onto the original wound:

> `だから言うこと聞けって言うんだ`
>
> `いじめなんかなかったことにしろって言うんだ`
>
> E10 12:01.710-12:04.300

This is the family conflict in compressed form. Her father's evidence is `we supported you materially`; Nina's evidence is `when truth and future-security conflicted, you did not act as my ally`.

Both can be facts. Neither cancels the other.

## 4.14 12:08-12:34 - Nina arrives intending dialogue and rapidly closes it again

Nina says she came intending to talk and be understood, but after the opening exchange:

> `やっぱり無理だよ`

and shortly afterward:

> `無理だよ`

This is diagnostically useful for CP2-P01.

It is not a clean full confirmation because E10 does not provide a matched low-stakes comparison in the same domain. But it is clear high-stakes evaluative compression: a renewed bad opening rapidly becomes a global forecast about the possibility of dialogue.

That does not mean the forecast is irrational. The history gives it real basis. The modeling point is that Nina again moves quickly from a bounded interaction to a terminal-seeming conclusion when the issue touches identity, family recognition, and past invalidation.

## 4.15 12:34-14:04 - The school scene revises the institutional record without erasing ambiguity

The next day Nina's father takes her back to the school.

The source provides two forms of institutional evidence that must not be collapsed.

First, the written apology document is shown visually. The readable portion includes hedged language such as `いじめと思われる行為` and an apology for failures of grasp/handling. The exact small print should not be over-transcribed beyond what the source resolution securely supports.

Second, the school's representative says the facts were checked and the relevant student admitted them, but verbally retreats when the father presses whether the school accepts responsibility and the bullying category.

The strongest V2 formulation is therefore **not** simply `the school admits the facts but refuses to say bullying`.

It is:

> **the institution moves toward acknowledgment through a hedged written record and factual confirmation while still verbally resisting full categorical recognition and responsibility.**

That distinction matters because institutional self-protection can operate through gradations of wording rather than absolute denial.

## 4.16 13:50-14:04 - The father finally performs the alliance Nina wanted

When Nina tries to stop the confrontation, her father says:

> `仁菜がそんなことを言う必要はない`
>
> `お前は被害者なんだ`

This is a major relationship event.

The father is now publicly taking Nina's side in the exact domain where she experienced his earlier failure as betrayal. He is willing to press the institution after Nina herself wants the scene to end.

But repair must not be rewritten as restoration.

The source does **not** give:

- an explicit paternal apology for the original failure;
- a statement that Nina's account had always been believed;
- a reversal of every prior parental judgment;
- evidence that the lost time or damage no longer matters.

> `もう戻りたくもない`

## 4.19 15:50-16:18 - `自分を殺そうとしてた` must stay source-bounded

>
> E10 15:50.560-15:54.480

At the same time, E10 does not establish a specific suicide attempt, plan, or clinical history. Within the surrounding argument Nina is describing suppression of the self she regarded as real: endurance, compliance, and living according to a socially acceptable identity at the cost of her own judgment.

## 4.20 16:07 onward - `I am not wrong` begins yielding to `I like who I am`

- `私は間違ってない`;
- Diamond Dust victory;
- Budokan;

The mature proposition is:

The secure result is:

# 5. Nina - reconstruction state after E10

## 5.1 Reconciliation does not reverse self-authorship

Nina can now accept evidence that her family loves her without accepting their authority to define her future.

This matters because early Nina often treated incompatible propositions as though only one could survive. E10 allows:

- father loves Nina;
- father failed Nina;
- family materially supported Nina;
- material support carried management claims;
- Nina hurt family members;
- Nina's departure remains meaningful;
- Nina can apologize;
- Nina does not have to return to the old path.

That is a major gain in tolerance for moral coexistence.

## 5.2 The most important self-verdict is no longer only `not wrong`

`でも 今の私が一番好き` does not replace Nina's correctness language globally, but it creates an independent source of legitimacy.

Her leap may be flight or fall. If the value of the choice depended only on successful outcome, this uncertainty would collapse the argument. Instead Nina says the present self is one she can affirm.

Current rule:

> **Nina is beginning to evaluate choices partly by whether they permit an inhabitable/authored self, not only by whether later victory certifies the earlier judgment.**

## 5.3 Nina still compresses high-stakes failure rapidly

The family conversation begins badly and Nina quickly reaches `無理だよ`.

This prevents E10 from being misread as sudden perfect nuance. Nina's mature insight is growing alongside the same old compression mechanism.

The episode is therefore developmental, not substitutive.

## 5.4 Irreversibility remains attractive but is not identical to sincerity

Nina says there is no ground to return to and that she does not want to return.

This partially supports CP2-P08, but E10 does **not** show Nina explicitly choosing cost *because* cost proves sincerity. She accepts irreversibility and rejects fallback, yet her stated reason is identity/self-inhabitation rather than an explicit `the more it costs, the more real it is` rule.

This distinction should remain in the model.

## 5.5 Nina's anti-evasion ethic is recursively applied

Her sister correctly observes that Nina used a letter and hiding to avoid reciprocal response.

Current rule addition:

> **Nina can use principled finality as a shield against the very reciprocal process she demands from others. External correction may be necessary before she sees the asymmetry.**

## 5.6 Authorship becomes explicit

Nina is no longer only:

- Momoka's listener;
- Momoka's vocalist;
- the person saved by `空の箱`;
- the younger participant trying to carry more instrumental weight.

She is now explicitly the writer of `私の作った歌`.

That is one of the strongest character-model transitions in E10 because it moves her from interpretation of inherited meaning to production of new meaning.

## 5.7 Speech/register delta

E10 strongly supports partner/home-conditioned register variation.

Nina's family-facing speech contains Kyushu/Kumamoto forms and local rhythm, including `まだ何かあると`, in a context that is not simply uncontrolled rage. Her sister's speech is even more strongly region-marked (`話したと`, `しなっせ`, etc.).

This supports CP2-P06 and the existing V2 caution:

> regional speech is relationship/home/state-conditioned; it is not a mechanical anger meter.

Professionalization is therefore a new arena of governance, not escape from governance.

Emotional repair is materially enabled.
