# Girls Band Cry E02 - V2 Deep Reading

## 1. Episode thesis

Episode 2 turns the emotional breakthrough of Episode 1 into a test of whether Nina can remain in ordinary relationship after the crisis that made intimacy easy.

The result is harsher and more diagnostic than the V1 shorthand "Nina fears invalidation." E02 shows that Nina does not only resist other people when they define her from partial evidence. **Under shame, uncertainty, or dependence, she performs the same operation on herself: a local failure becomes a total verdict about identity, future, and belonging.** Momoka's criticism that Nina is reading Subaru too suspiciously becomes, in Nina's mouth, a prophecy that she will never become close to anyone. A damaged lamp becomes evidence that she will live forever in darkness. Her cognitive danger is therefore not merely sensitivity to external invalidation. It is a more general tendency to convert bounded facts into permanent existential conclusions when the stakes touch self-worth.

At the same time, the episode refuses to make this totalization equivalent to stupidity. Nina can recognize other people's care almost immediately after she has rejected it. Her problem is not an inability to understand what happened. Her problem is that recognition reaches shame before it reaches repair. She leaves the restaurant, understands within minutes that Subaru was trying and Momoka was thinking about her, and then turns that insight into self-attack rather than walking back inside.

Momoka is also sharpened. E01 established practical care and direct apology after a conceded misjudgment. E02 reveals the other branch of her repair behavior. When Momoka believes her criticism is still correct, she does not apologize for it. She repairs through continued presence, practical intervention, and a reframing of Nina's difficult traits as artistic material. Her care is therefore not simply gentle responsiveness. It has an intrusive edge. She recruits Subaru before Nina has agreed, engineers social contact, follows Nina into a private corridor during conflict, and later enters Nina's apartment using knowledge of the hidden key. Yet when Nina gives a clear, reasoned refusal of the band, Momoka stops asking and accepts the separation. The emerging rule is not "Momoka ignores boundaries." It is more precise: **when Momoka sees a workable path or believes someone needs intervention, she often acts ahead of full consent; explicit, legible refusal can stop her.**

Subaru enters as the first major stress test of Nina's authenticity model. Her warmth is visibly polished and effortful. E02 does not establish that it is fake. Nina's error is therefore narrower than "she cannot detect sincerity." She treats visible accommodation by a socially smoother person as evidence of pity, asymmetry, or performance, and then treats performance as evidence that the relationship cannot be equal or spontaneous.

Finally, E02 gives Momoka's philosophy of music a concrete practical grammar. Cooking, learning, and band formation are all described as processes in which heterogeneous things proceed in parallel, collide, fail, and eventually make something an individual could not produce alone. Music is not proposed as a cure that will make Nina agreeable. It is proposed as a form that can make the "difficult" self transmissible without first sanitizing it.

The episode's central V2 formulation is therefore:

> **Nina's deepest early danger is not simply anger at being misjudged. It is totalization: turning partial judgments, failures, or social friction into final verdicts. Momoka's counterproposal is not self-improvement before belonging, but creation as a structure capable of holding what Nina cannot yet regulate relationally.**

## 3. Prospective episode state

At the start of E02, only the following can be treated as stable local knowledge.

Nina and Momoka have shared one extraordinary night and one performance. They have not actually agreed on a band. Nina still frames her future principally through education and proving that leaving school did not mean defeat. Momoka has abandoned the closure plan she announced in E01 enough to remain in Kawasaki and consider music, but she has not yet established housing or employment. Their intimacy is real but extraordinarily young.

E01 leaves several reconstruction hypotheses rather than facts:

- Nina appears highly sensitive to premature definition from partial evidence.
- Nina remains morphologically polite even while angry with Momoka.
- regional language appears connected to home, familiarity, and activation rather than simply anger.
- Momoka often expresses care through logistics.
- when Momoka accepts that she judged Nina wrongly, she can apologize directly and revise the judgment.
- Momoka may act before Nina has fully consented when she believes she understands what should happen next.

E02 must try to falsify these rules.

The episode also introduces Subaru. At this point the viewer does not know whether her friendliness is spontaneous, trained, strategic, defensive, false, sincere, or some combination. Her attendance at an actors' school is context, not a verdict.

# 4. Scene-by-scene close reading

## 4.1 00:00-02:34 - Domestic anticlimax: intimacy without agreement

E01 ended with a public musical union. E02 opens by showing Momoka asleep in Nina's apartment, taking food labeled with Nina's name, and still lacking stable practical arrangements. The episode immediately refuses to let performance substitute for ordinary life.

Nina's first spoken complaint is:

> `何度言ったら分かるんですか`
>
> "How many times do I have to tell you?"

Momoka answers with an untroubled morning greeting. Nina is managing the limited resources and boundaries of a tiny apartment; Momoka behaves as though their relational temperature already permits casual domestic trespass. This is comic, but it is also the first E02 instance of the care/boundary problem that will matter later. Momoka is unusually comfortable collapsing distance once she has decided a relationship exists.

The practical questions follow immediately: apartment, job, housing, money. E01's musical climax has not repaired Momoka's material life. That matters for the series-level materiality thesis. Artistic decision is already embedded in rent, food, temporary lodging, employment, furniture, and transportation.

The episode's first major misunderstanding then makes explicit that Nina and Momoka assigned different meanings to E01's shared gesture. Momoka says:

> `やるって言っただろ`

Nina replies:

> `言ってません`

Nina clarifies that she meant separate spheres: she would study and Momoka would do music.

> `それぞれの立場ってことです`
>
> `私は勉強 桃香さんは音楽`

This is a major relational correction. Nina's climactic pursuit in E01 did not mean that she had suddenly accepted collective authorship. Her preferred form of solidarity is still parallel rather than interdependent: *you resist over there, I resist over here.*

Momoka, however, has interpreted the experience as relationally generative. She has already begun imagining a shared project. That asymmetry helps explain why Momoka will later seem intrusive. She is acting from a relationship-state model that is further advanced than Nina's explicit consent.

### Falsification result: E01 did not create a band agreement

Any E01 interpretation that treats the final duet as a completed mutual band decision is rejected. What it created was relational possibility and a shared symbolic vocabulary. E02 opens by showing that the parties disagree about what those meant.

## 4.2 02:34-05:19 - University as counter-verdict; band as conflictual co-creation

At the preparatory school, Nina says:

> `いい大学に入らなきゃいけないんです`

But almost immediately qualifies it:

> `別に 特別行きたいわけじゃないんですけど`

The distinction is central. University is not yet an affirmative vocation. It is an instrument by which Nina hopes to force a different interpretation of her trajectory.

Momoka continues to invite her into a band. Nina answers:

> `無理に決まってるじゃないですか`

The phrase `muri ni kimatteru` sounds absolute, but E02 does not establish that this is the same moral modality as E01's `dekimasen`. Here Nina has obvious practical reasons: study demands, inexperience, and a future plan she believes she must preserve. V2 should not turn every categorical Nina statement into the same psychological mechanism.

Momoka's reply is important because she values Nina before Nina is useful in conventional musical terms:

> `私さ いいボーカルだと思うんだよね 仁菜って`
>
> `いい声だし 何より心がいい`

She then gives her first explicit philosophy of a band:

> `バンドってさ`
>
> `言いたいことがたまりに溜まってるやつが`
>
> `集まってやるものだと思うんだよね`

A band is not defined by agreement or even friendship. It is a gathering of people with accumulated things they need to say. Momoka describes romance, anger at the world, being moved, and loving or hating oneself. Her later description of Diamond Dust is deliberately ugly: backbiting, informing, tension, swallowed resentment, inability to breathe the same air, and eventual explosion.

Nina points out the obvious:

> `一つもいいとこ言ってませんよ`

Momoka answers:

> `でもまあ 楽しいんだよ`

Then states the positive principle:

> `自分だけじゃできないものを 自分以外の人と生み出すっていうのは`

Creating with other people something one cannot create alone is worth the friction.

This is the first direct primary-source support for a Momoka worldview that will matter throughout V2:

> **collectivity is not valuable because it eliminates conflict; it is valuable because conflict and difference can become productive conditions for outputs unavailable to the individual.**

That is stronger than a generic "bands are friendship" thesis and weaker than the mature full-series claim that conflict literally becomes composition. E02 has stated the philosophy; later episodes still need to prove it formally.

## 4.3 05:19-06:47 - Groceries, cooking, and Momoka's practical epistemology

Momoka takes Nina shopping, pays, and explains the gesture in practical terms: she is older, and the food can count as lodging compensation. Nina asks whether it is really all right. Momoka avoids turning the act into emotional debt.

> `本当にいいんですか`
>
> `一応年上だし`
>
> `宿泊代`

This strongly supports `MOMOKA-R01`: concern tends to become logistics before sentimental declaration.

Cooking then becomes an explicit analogy for songwriting. Momoka describes multiple operations moving at once - cutting ingredients while water heats, preparing sauce while things simmer - and finally converging into one whole.

This is not merely cute thematic mirroring. It reveals how Momoka conceptualizes craft. She thinks in coordinated processes rather than single inspired acts. Creation is sequencing, concurrency, timing, and integration.

Nina responds to the domestic lesson not with gratitude alone but with competence shame:

> `でも 私何もできてないんだなって`

Momoka's answer gives a practical learning philosophy:

> `もう そういうのはぶつかるしかないんだよ`
>
> `困った しまった そうなのか`
>
> `その繰り返しで覚えるしかない`

One learns by collision: trouble, surprise, recognition, repetition. Momoka does not promise Nina that independence will become painless once properly explained. She normalizes iterative failure.

This will become important in the later dark-room collapse. Nina experiences the broken lamp as proof of permanent incapacity. Momoka's epistemology has already supplied the opposite model: a failure is a local event from which one learns, not a final ontological verdict.

### Character reconstruction contrast

Nina under shame tends toward totalization.

Momoka under practical difficulty tends toward iteration.

That difference is more precise than "Nina is emotional, Momoka is mature." Momoka can be highly emotional and rash. The relevant contrast is how each character *interprets failure* in this domain.

## 4.4 06:47-09:48 - Family order, failed protection, and the ethics of prudent advice

The domestic warmth of cooking opens into Nina's description of an "ideal" family system. The rules are explicit and formal:

> `一つ 夕食は必ず自宅で家族揃って食べること`
>
> `一つ リビングではスマホの使用は禁止とする`
>
> `一つ 毎週日曜の夜は家族会議を開くこと`

Dinner together. No smartphone in the living room. Weekly family meetings.

Nina says her parents took pride in being that kind of household and that the family was known at school. The important issue is not that these rules are inherently abusive. On their face they can signify attention, structure, and family cohesion. The contradiction is that a family capable of formalizing togetherness did not protect Nina when protection became socially costly.

Nina initially tries to shut the subject down. Momoka reads her expression and asks about bullying. Nina then supplies the strongest prospective evidence yet about the injury behind her self-verdict. A socially powerful girl assembled allies before targeting her; the isolation escalated into harm; Nina says she was hospitalized; and even then school and family preferred not to make the matter into a larger public disturbance.

> `反撃できないようにしっかり仲間作ってから 仕掛けてくるんです`
>
> `仲間使って無視して 危害加えてきて`
>
> `私 病院に運び込まれたのに`
>
> `学校どころか 家族まで騒ぎにしないほうがいいって`

This materially strengthens the V1 claim that `I am not wrong` is defensive scar tissue against an imposed social account. It still does not give the complete incident, but it now gives direct evidence that the institutions around Nina prioritized containment over her experience of justice.

Momoka interrupts:

> `ストップ`

The interruption is framed by the overcooking pot. It prevents the scene from becoming an unlimited extraction of trauma. V2 should be cautious about romanticizing this as perfect therapeutic restraint - the immediate reason is literally cooking - but the effect is still important. Momoka has heard enough to respond without demanding every detail.

Her response is not "forgive," "move on," or "be positive." It is:

> `やっぱり歌ったほうがいいと思うぞ`
>
> `今の話だけでも ５曲は作れる`

What happened can become material. The proposition can sound exploitative if abstracted from the relationship: pain becomes content. In context, however, Momoka is offering Nina a route to authorship. The experience need not remain only something that school, family, or aggressors define.

### The father's advice

The remembered father says:

> `それだけ将来の選択肢が狭まるってことばい`
>
> `なりたいものができてから後悔しても`
>
> `遅かとぞ`

Dropping out narrows future options; regret may arrive too late once Nina discovers what she wants to become.

The episode does not show this proposition to be factually foolish. It is exactly the kind of advice a concerned parent may reasonably give. The ethical problem lies in context. Nina experiences it after a failure of protection, so responsibility for containing the consequences of others' wrongdoing is shifted back onto her.

Nina answers by making university into proof:

> `だから 大学入ればいいんでしょ`

Then states the core negative motive:

> `大学にも行けなかったら終わりですから`

Momoka repeats the crucial word - "the end?" - and Nina clarifies:

> `ただ 私がこのままだったら`
>
> `負けてるみたいじゃないですか`
>
> `いじめてた連中にも 親とか先生にも`

If she remains as she is, it will look as though she lost - to the bullies, to her parents, to teachers.

The important V2 distinction is now explicit:

- Nina was plausibly wronged.
- her father's future-oriented advice can still contain practical truth.
- the family's handling of the injury can still have failed ethically.
- Nina can still be organizing her future reactively around defeating their interpretation.

This is the earliest strong support for the broader series distinction among being wronged, being right, making a prudent decision, and refusing regret.

## 4.5 09:48-13:30 - A clean refusal, respectful separation, and the pinky protocol

When Nina finally gives a reasoned refusal, Momoka says:

> `分かった`
>
> `じゃあ もう誘わない`

Nina apologizes. Momoka responds:

> `謝ることじゃないってば`

This scene is one of the most important falsification tests for the emerging care/control model.

Momoka has pushed repeatedly. She has imagined the band, praised Nina's voice, and made a strong case for music. But when Nina's refusal becomes explicit and legible as a choice about her future, Momoka stops. She does not threaten abandonment, weaponize the previous night, or insist that Nina owes her because of the performance.

Therefore `GBC-V1-C030` should not become "Momoka disregards consent." The stronger model is conditional:

> **Momoka frequently moves ahead of consent in setup and intervention, but explicit reasoned refusal can stop her.**

The moving sequence continues the distinction. Nina returns the guitar because she cannot play it. Momoka accepts it and thanks her. The gift from E01 is not treated as an irreversible contract.

At the river, both characters attempt a separation that preserves relation. Momoka asks only to hear Nina sing once more, tells her to study, suggests they can eat together again, and promises to write another song Nina can love. Nina tells Momoka to continue music and to use the middle finger against those who dismissed her.

Then Nina modifies the gesture:

> `中指立てたくなったら 小指立ててください`
>
> `私もそうするので`

Momoka accepts:

> `分かった`

This is one of E02's most useful reconstruction events. Nina does not say "call me when you're lonely," "remember me," or "I need us to remain connected." Instead, she converts an existing shared sign into a protocol: when outward defiance is needed, substitute a private sign that points back toward the other person.

That supports a new provisional rule:

> **When direct dependency language is difficult, Nina may encode attachment into a shared gesture, object, or action.**

The sign is relational without requiring Nina to describe herself as dependent.

Nina then sings `Kara no Hako` by the river. The song's key claims - no single correct answer, no definitive defeat, remaining oneself - now operate in a third position. In E01 Momoka's rendition was a declared farewell and Nina's attachment turned it back toward continuation. In E02 Nina performs the song as a parting gift inside a relationship neither person is declaring dead.

The same song is therefore already moving among ownership positions and functions. That strongly supports `GBC-V1-C063` and the broader art-as-transmission claim.

## 4.6 13:30-14:19 - Crowds without relation

The episode then removes Momoka from Nina's immediate space and lets Nina live the future she said she wanted.

She attends preparatory classes. She commutes. She studies. Other students talk nearby. None of this is depicted as catastrophic failure. The problem is social absence.

Nina realizes:

> `誰とも喋ってない`
>
> `私 今週誰とも喋ってない`

She has text messages. She has classmates. She has physical proximity to hundreds of people. None of those count as having spoken to someone.

The visual grammar supports the distinction. Contact sheets place Nina inside crowded classrooms and stations while compositionally separating her from the conversational groups around her. The apartment then becomes dark and quiet.

This matters because E02 is not arguing that Nina secretly "wanted a band all along" in a simple sense. It is demonstrating that her individualist defense has costs she can feel even before she has a vocabulary for what kind of relation she wants.

## 4.7 14:19-18:11 - The lamp, Subaru, and Nina's suspicion of visible social effort

Momoka re-enters through a practical offer: an unused lamp. Nina immediately accepts.

This strengthens, but also narrows, the E01 help-seeking hypothesis. Nina is not generally incapable of accepting assistance. What becomes painful is assistance that exposes incompetence, dependence, or social asymmetry. A lamp can be accepted; being made to feel like a deficient social object is harder.

Momoka arrives with Subaru.

Subaru introduces herself with bright energy:

> `安和すばるでーす`
>
> `よろしくね`

Nina answers:

> `井芹です`

The acoustic contrast is marked: Subaru's introduction is substantially more energetic than Nina's clipped surname-only response. But V2 should not call this "fake" or "real" on volume alone. What matters is that Subaru is obviously doing social work. She projects warmth, immediately uses `Nina-chan`, generates possible shared topics, and keeps conversational momentum alive.

At dinner she probes for common ground: bands, Vocaloid, older singers, live shows, festivals. She uses affirming responses such as `ii jan ii jan`. Nina's answers remain sparse, and her visible comfort decreases.

Nina eventually withdraws with a polite formula:

> `すみません ちょっと`

Momoka follows and tells her Subaru is trying to establish a point of connection.

> `接点持とうって頑張ってるんだよ`

Nina seizes on the word "trying":

> `頑張るって何ですか`
>
> `無理して話してるってことですか`

This is more precise than a blanket rejection of social performance. Nina interprets effort as evidence that the interaction is not naturally reciprocal. The asymmetry matters. Subaru is socially fluent, informed, and visibly accommodating Nina's lack of knowledge. Nina reads that accommodation as condescension:

> `なんか 上から目線で腹が立ちます`

Momoka says this is Nina's assumption and explains Subaru's actor-school background. Nina responds by accusing Subaru of broadcasting how considerate she is.

The text therefore supports a narrower reconstruction rule:

> **Visible accommodation from a socially smoother person can trigger Nina's suspicion that the relationship is pitying, patronizing, or strategically performed.**

This is not yet proof that Nina rejects all performance. In fact, the episode gives no positive evidence that Subaru's warmth is insincere. Nina may accurately detect construction while making an unjustified inference about motive.

That is the first strong support for `GBC-V1-C008`: authenticity cannot be equated with effortlessness.

## 4.8 18:11-18:53 - The decisive falsification: Nina defines herself

Momoka says:

> `なんだそのひがみ`

Nina denies envy. Momoka then gives a bounded warning:

> `そんなふうに人のこと見てたら`
>
> `いつまで経っても誰とも話せないぞ`

The criticism concerns a pattern of interpreting other people. It is not a claim that Nina is metaphysically unlovable.

Nina converts it into exactly that:

> `ですね`
>
> `私 一生誰とも仲良くなれないんだと思います`
>
> `性格悪いですから`
>
> `一生一人なんだと思います`

This is the key E02 falsification of `NINA-R01`.

In E01 it was tempting to formulate the problem as an "invalidation trigger": other people use partial evidence to define Nina's whole life, and she explodes. E02 shows that the underlying mechanism is broader because Nina herself commits the same cognitive transformation. A local social criticism becomes a permanent identity verdict. She is not merely defending herself against totalization; she has internalized totalization as a way of making meaning under shame.

A stronger model is:

> **When a local judgment or failure touches shame, Nina is prone to collapsing the distinction between behavior, identity, and future. External invalidation is especially potent because it activates a structure she can also turn inward.**

Her exit also provides crucial speech evidence. She says `gochisosama deshita`, returns thanks for the lamp, and leaves. High emotional intensity has not erased politeness toward Momoka and Subaru.

This strongly falsifies any rule of the form "anger makes Nina drop polite speech." Register depends on interlocutor and relationship, not emotion alone.

Momoka's reaction after Nina leaves is not apology:

> `たく バカだな`

Then she says Nina has the most important thing despite failing to recognize it. Momoka believes the criticism was substantively necessary. This is the second important test of `MOMOKA-R02`: direct apology is not her universal repair behavior.

## 4.9 18:53-19:57 - Insight becomes self-attack, then displaced conflict

Outside, Nina almost immediately recognizes what she could not tolerate inside the restaurant:

> `何やってるんだろう`
>
> `せっかく声かけてくれて`
>
> `せっかく私のこと考えてくれてるのに`

Subaru went out of her way to speak to her. Momoka was thinking about her. Nina's social inference is not permanently fixed. She can update quickly.

But the update does not produce repair. It produces self-punishment:

> `バカじゃないの`

The repeated phrase roughens:

> `バカじゃねえの`

This supports a new rule distinct from totalization itself:

> **Nina can achieve rapid post-conflict insight, but while shame remains high she routes that insight into self-attack before interpersonal repair.**

Her register then changes sharply when a stranger becomes involved:

> `うっせえな`
>
> `うるせえ`

and finally the regional outburst:

> `せからしかー`

The audio energy rises significantly compared with the later dark-room collapse. Visually, Nina is crying, physically expansive, and using the lamp object inside the confrontation.

This gives V2 the strongest speech correction so far:

- with Momoka and Subaru, even intense conflict can retain polite morphology;
- in self-talk, Nina can shift into rougher contracted forms;
- with an antagonistic stranger under maximum disinhibition, rough speech and regional language become available together;
- regional language also appears naturally in family/home contexts.

Therefore dialect is neither a pure intimacy marker nor a pure anger leak. **Relationship and state jointly condition it.**

## 4.10 19:57-21:59 - Broken lamp, existential darkness, and Momoka's intrusive rescue

Back in the apartment, the lamp fails.

Nina says:

> `一人じゃ 電気もつけられない`

Then immediately globalizes the failure:

> `一生暗いままなんだ`
>
> `私 ずっと闇なんだ`
>
> `闇なんだ`

This is the second independent demonstration of the totalization mechanism within minutes. The lamp is not merely a symbol imposed by the direction. Nina herself performs the symbolic leap verbally: *I cannot make this light work -> my whole future is darkness.*

The visual sequence reinforces rather than creates the cognition. The apartment is rendered in cold blue darkness; Nina collapses toward the floor; the object meant to solve isolation has become evidence of incompetence.

Momoka and Subaru then enter.

Momoka says:

> `やっぱりやろうよ バンド`

and reveals that she knows the key hiding place:

> `鍵の隠し場所ぐらい変えとけよ`

This must be counted as a boundary crossing. The fact that the scene is benevolent does not erase that. Momoka has used private access knowledge to enter Nina's apartment because she believes intervention is warranted.

The event therefore strengthens the care/control seam. But it does so alongside the earlier counterexample: when Nina explicitly refused the band for a reasoned future plan, Momoka stopped asking. The emerging profile is not domination. It is **interventionist care with imperfect consent discipline**.

Momoka then returns to Nina's voice:

> `言ったろ 仁菜の歌声が好きだって`

Nina asks, quietly:

> `どうして そんな嘘言うんですか`

The audio matters here. The line is much quieter than her earlier challenges. It reads less like aggressive accusation and more like wounded disbelief - an interpretation supported by scene context and relative intensity, though not reducible to the measurement.

Momoka answers:

> `嘘じゃないよ`

Then describes Nina as twisted and difficult but unable to lie to herself, weak yet absolutely unwilling to bend herself.

> `でもそれは 自分に嘘をつけないからだろ`
>
> `弱いくせに`
>
> `自分を曲げるのは絶対に嫌だからだろ`

Finally comes the most important Momoka self-disclosure of E02:

> `それはさ`
>
> `私が忘れていた 私が大好きで`
>
> `いつまでも抱きしめていたい`
>
> `私の 歌なんだ`

Nina's difficult self-honesty is something Momoka says she had forgotten, loved, and wanted to keep embracing: "my song."

This is now direct evidence for the projection/identification hypothesis, but the exact formulation matters. Momoka does **not** say "you are my younger self." She identifies Nina with a lost artistic value or self-relation represented as `watashi no uta` - my song.

Therefore `GBC-V1-C032` should be strengthened and revised:

> Momoka explicitly identifies Nina's refusal to lie or bend with something Momoka has forgotten in herself and names that value "my song." A literal younger-self projection remains an interpretation to be tested later.

Nina rejects the praise:

> `そんなの`
>
> `そんなのいらない`

Momoka laughs and calls her troublesome. Then gives the episode's creative command:

> `いいからそのめんどくさいの`
>
> `全部ぶつけなよ`
>
> `歌に`

Put all of that difficult material into song.

This is not normalization. Momoka does not say that Nina is secretly easy, nice, or socially well adjusted. She says the difficulty itself can be given form.

That is E02's strongest artistic proposition:

> **music is a container for difficult affect and contradiction, not a reward granted after the person has become socially acceptable.**

Subaru's presence matters here too. The scene is not sealed into a Nina/Momoka dyad. The person Nina has just rejected is physically present in the room during the repair. Even before Subaru becomes a full mediator, the episode makes Nina's return to relation triadic rather than purely romanticized rescue by Momoka.

## 4.11 21:59-23:41 - Ending song as commentary, not literal monologue

The ending lyrics articulate several tensions that closely match E02: inability to become someone else, inability to say one is lonely, ordinary conversation hurting, fear of making no one happy, expectation surviving anyway, throwing away misaligned lies, remaining unchanged for now, wanting to sing with "you," and continuing to sing after falling.

The lyrics are highly relevant paratext within the locked episode source. They should not be treated as a verbatim Nina interior monologue. This supports, but does not independently prove, the episode thesis that belonging does not require prior normalization.

# 5. Character reconstruction observations

## 5.1 Iseri Nina

### Baseline evidence

E02 confirms that Nina's baseline is not high-volume aggression. In routine contexts she is restrained, formal, and often spatially small. She studies, follows household procedures, asks whether receiving groceries is really acceptable, and uses polite Japanese as the default interpersonal shell.

Her desire for connection is also stronger than her behavior makes obvious. The isolation sequence demonstrates that she notices the absence of spoken relation even while pursuing the independent plan she defended at the river.

### Stress-state evidence

E02 supplies the clearest early escalation chain:

1. perceive visible social accommodation;
2. infer asymmetry or patronage;
3. challenge the motive;
4. receive bounded criticism;
5. globalize criticism into identity verdict;
6. withdraw while retaining formal politeness;
7. recognize others' good intentions rapidly after separation;
8. convert insight into self-attack;
9. displace arousal into stranger conflict;
10. collapse into quiet helplessness;
11. require external re-entry before she can rejoin relation.

This should remain provisional rather than being treated as a fixed script, but it is already much richer than "Nina gets angry."

### Relationship-conditioned evidence

With Momoka, Nina can challenge intensely while retaining `desu/masu` morphology. Their familiarity permits blunt content without automatically collapsing formal surface grammar.

With Subaru, Nina is initially even more clipped: surname-only self-introduction, minimal answers, reluctance to meet the offered warmth.

With herself, her language roughens during shame spirals.

With an antagonistic stranger, she shifts into overtly rough forms and regional language.

With family memory, regional language is natural and not necessarily dysregulated.

### Worldview evidence

University is currently a counter-verdict rather than a positive vocation. Nina wants the result to prove that bullies, parents, and teachers did not successfully define her as a loser.

She also appears to believe that genuine relationship should not require visible manufacture. The E02 Subaru scene suggests a tacit standard that real connection ought to feel spontaneous and equal. Because this standard is not yet stated directly, it should be recorded as a strong inference rather than an explicit belief.

### Voice evidence

E02 materially strengthens the relationship-conditioned speech model:

- default polite morphology;
- rough contractions in self-directed escalation;
- rough stranger-facing commands under high arousal;
- regional features both in family/home speech and maximum disinhibition;
- extreme emotional content does not automatically erase politeness with Momoka/Subaru.

### Embodied evidence

Nina contracts in classrooms, restaurants, and private shame. During the street sequence she expands physically and becomes diagonally dynamic. In the apartment collapse she returns to the floor and darkness. Her body therefore tracks not simply arousal but the direction of arousal: confrontation expands; shame contracts.

### Negative evidence

E02 directly contradicts several tempting simplifications:

- Nina is not unable to recognize care. She recognizes it quickly after leaving.
- Nina is not incapable of accepting help. She accepts food, the lamp, and Momoka's practical instruction.
- Nina does not simply "drop politeness when angry."
- Nina's suspicion of Subaru does not prove Subaru is insincere.
- Nina's university goal is not yet evidence of genuine academic aspiration.

## 5.2 Kawaragi Momoka

### Baseline evidence

Momoka remains socially loose and practically oriented. She occupies Nina's apartment casually, shops, cooks, arranges moving, and thinks in terms of next actions.

Her care is concrete: food, lamp, transportation, moving, introductions, and future meals.

### Stress and correction

E02 provides a crucial counterexample to an overly simple E01 repair rule. Momoka does not apologize after the restaurant confrontation because she does not believe the substance of her criticism was wrong. Instead, she maintains connection and later returns with Subaru.

Thus the more useful rule is conditional:

- if Momoka concedes misjudgment, direct verbal apology is available;
- if Momoka retains the judgment, repair tends to occur through presence, practical action, and reframing rather than retraction.

### Care and control

Evidence for interventionist behavior:

- she has already recruited Subaru while Nina has not agreed to join;
- she repeatedly proposes the band;
- she engineers peer contact partly for Nina's benefit;
- she follows Nina during withdrawal;
- she enters Nina's apartment through a known hidden key location;
- she returns to the band proposal during Nina's collapse.

Counterevidence to domination:

- she stops inviting Nina after the explicit university refusal;
- she tells Nina there is nothing to apologize for;
- she accepts the guitar back;
- she preserves ordinary future contact without making it conditional on the band.

The current model should therefore stay at **interventionist care with a tendency to act ahead of consent**, not "controlling personality" as a global trait.

### Worldview evidence

E02 gives unusually explicit philosophy:

- learning comes from repeated collision with reality;
- creation coordinates multiple heterogeneous processes;
- band work is conflictual but worth it because it enables something impossible alone;
- difficult experience can be converted into artistic material;
- self-honesty is more artistically valuable than easy agreeableness.

### Voice evidence

Momoka's rough casual register remains stable, but E02 separates intensity functions. The restaurant warning is forceful. The final `watashi no uta nan da` is acoustically quiet and intimate. Rough vocabulary is therefore not a sufficient proxy for emotional aggression.

### Negative evidence

- E02 does not support the idea that Momoka always pushes past a clear no.
- E02 weakens a universal self-erasure model because Momoka accepts the guitar back and reclaims future musical activity.
- E02 does not prove that her identification of Nina is specifically a literal younger-self projection.

## 5.3 Awa Subaru

### Baseline evidence

E02 introduces Subaru as high-energy, socially polished, and proactive. She gives her full name, uses an elongated friendly introduction, calls Nina `Nina-chan`, and generates conversational topics rather than waiting for commonality to appear.

### Performance and sincerity

The actor-school context makes trained presentation a plausible component of her social style. But E02 gives no evidence that the friendliness is malicious, empty, or false. The correct V2 formulation is therefore:

> **Subaru's warmth is visibly effortful/polished; E02 does not justify equating construction with insincerity.**

That point is already analytically important because Nina makes exactly that mistake.

### Negative evidence

E02 does not yet establish Subaru as the band's mediator, reveal her family constraint, or show a stable stress/repair pattern. Those V1 claims remain open.

# 7. Speech and voice delta

## 7.1 Nina

### Politeness survives high affect

E02 strongly confirms that polite morphology is not simply a low-arousal mask that disappears when Nina becomes upset. During the restaurant conflict she uses formal or polite forms even while challenging Momoka's interpretation and condemning herself. Her departure remains formally courteous.

This matters for reconstruction. A simulated Nina who automatically becomes grammatically rough whenever angry would be inaccurate.

### Roughness is interactionally selective

The strongest roughening occurs in self-talk and stranger conflict:

- `baka janai no` becomes `baka janee no`;
- stranger-facing `usseena / urusee` appears;
- the regional `sekarashikaa` arrives at maximum public disinhibition.

The contrast suggests that Nina's register is governed by at least two variables:

1. relationship/interlocutor;
2. state/arousal.

Neither is sufficient alone.

### Regionality

E02 adds both ends of the distribution:

- father/family memory contains natural regional speech without crisis;
- maximum street disinhibition also produces regional speech.

The best current formulation is:

> Regional forms are part of Nina's underlying home-language repertoire. Standard polite Japanese is strongly available in chosen interpersonal regulation; familiarity, home context, and disinhibition can all increase access to regional forms, but there is no one-to-one equation between dialect and emotion.

### Catastrophic syntax

Nina's self-condemnation repeatedly uses permanence markers and total scope: `issho`, `zutto`, categorical identity claims. This linguistic pattern should be tracked longitudinally. The content is not simply "I feel bad now" but "this local event reveals what my whole life is."

## 7.2 Momoka

Momoka remains casual and compressed, but E02 shows a wide energy range inside the same broad register.

Her restaurant correction is acoustically forceful. Her final self-disclosure becomes much quieter without suddenly adopting formal language. Emotional intimacy therefore changes force and pacing more reliably than it changes her basic casual morphology.

Her care language remains practical and elliptical: pay for food, put things in the refrigerator, bring the lamp, change the key hiding place, use the difficult feeling in song.

## 7.3 Subaru

E02 provides only a baseline, but it is distinctive:

- elongated, upbeat full-name introduction;
- rapid use of `Nina-chan`;
- affirmative conversational backchannels;
- active search for common interests;
- visible comfort filling silence.

The actor-school context should be logged as a possible source of trained presentation, not used as proof of falsity.

# 9. Music and performance analysis

## 9.1 `Kara no Hako` changes ownership position again

E02's river performance is analytically important because the song is no longer simply Momoka's farewell object or Nina's private survival object.

Nina sings it *to Momoka* during an attempted separation. The lyrics about there being no single correct answer and no defeat are therefore placed between them as a shared vocabulary.

The song has now served at least three functions:

1. Momoka's E01 performance: declared final song / attempted closure.
2. Nina's E01 reception and joint performance: refusal of that closure.
3. Nina's E02 river rendition: relational parting gift that preserves connection without forcing the band decision.

This strongly supports art-as-transmission. Meaning changes as the song moves between singer, listener, and relationship.

## 9.2 Momoka's cooking analogy

Momoka explicitly compares cooking's simultaneous tasks and final integration to songwriting. The significance is methodological: she thinks of composition as managing distinct processes into a whole.

That provides conceptual groundwork for later collective authorship, but V2 should not pretend E02 has already proven the mature five-member musical architecture.

## 9.3 Music as channel rather than cure

The final instruction `put all of that troublesome stuff into song` is the episode's strongest music thesis.

Nina is not asked to eliminate anger, suspicion, shame, or contradiction before becoming artistically useful. Music offers those affects a form with audience-facing structure. The ethical promise is limited but important: difficult inner material can become communication instead of remaining only self-punishment or displaced confrontation.

This is a stronger and more specific claim than "music heals."

# 10. Visual-form analysis

## 10.1 Domestic warmth versus institutional coldness

The cooking/family sequence uses a warm small apartment as the present space and cuts into colder school-memory imagery. The formal contrast matters because the family being described is itself organized around domestic togetherness. Warmth as appearance is therefore not automatically proof of protection.

The episode repeatedly asks whether a social form - family dinner, friendly conversation, polite advice - actually performs the ethical function its appearance implies.

## 10.2 Crowds without connection

The prep-school and station compositions place Nina among people but not with them. The direction distinguishes social density from relationship. This gives visual form to her line that she has not spoken with anyone all week.

## 10.3 Restaurant blocking

Subaru and Momoka frequently form the conversationally fluent side of the table while Nina sits opposite, receiving topic probes. The geometry amplifies Nina's perception that she is being socially managed by people who already know how to interact.

Her withdrawal to a bright, flat restroom/corridor space strips away the warmer restaurant context and turns the conflict into direct interpersonal exposure.

## 10.4 Expansion and collapse

After the restaurant, Nina's body moves through distinct spatial states:

- contracted, crying self-rebuke;
- physically expansive street confrontation;
- return to cold dark apartment;
- collapse to the floor with failed lamp.

This is useful character evidence because it shows that "anger" is not one bodily mode. Outward conflict expands her. Shame and helplessness collapse her.

## 10.5 Re-population of the dark room

Momoka and Subaru entering the dark apartment is an obvious rescue image, but V2 should retain the boundary complication: they enter using a hidden key. The scene visually and ethically contains both care and intrusion.

The final room is also triadic. Subaru's presence prevents Momoka's speech from turning Nina's recovery into an exclusively dyadic salvation narrative.
