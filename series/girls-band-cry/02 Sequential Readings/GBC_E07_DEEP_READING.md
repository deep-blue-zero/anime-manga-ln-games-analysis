# Girls Band Cry E07 - V2 Deep Reading

## 1. Episode thesis

Episode 7 asks what it means to make a choice once every available path has become capable of disproving you.

E06 converted Nina's musical desire into an explicit proof project. She wanted a goal, professional recognition, Budokan, and ultimately a result capable of establishing that Momoka's song and Nina's refusal had not been wrong. The new five-person sound gave the group a reason to continue negotiating that ambition, but it did not give them a shared theory of success. E07 makes the missing costs concrete.

For Nina, music now competes with an existing bargain. Her grades have fallen and she has been missing preparatory school. Her older sister reminds her that Nina came to Kawasaki declaring that she would enter university by her own effort rather than accept the institutional compromise through which her family might have secured a recommendation. The university route is therefore not only her father's script. Nina herself turned it into proof that she could reject the school's handling of her injury without sacrificing her future.

For Momoka, professional music now competes with a different bargain. She can keep making music so long as no new professional attempt is allowed to become a verdict on whether her work is good enough. When she announces that the Suwa performance will be her last with the band, her stated reason is stark:

> `私はこのバンドを背負って プロを目指す気にはなれない`
>
> `私の作る曲じゃ プロの世界では通用しないからだ`
>
> E07 15:17.990-15:27.460

This is not merely E06's `I have no goal`. It is a negative professional conclusion.

Mine, an older musician from Momoka's early Tokyo life, then supplies an interpretation Momoka herself does not state directly:

> `嫌なんだろうね 結論が出るのが`
>
> `続ければ続けるほど 結果ははっきりしてくるからね`
>
> E07 16:32.020-16:39.990

One failed attempt can still be called bad luck. A second failure becomes harder to keep separate from ability. Mine's conclusion is that Momoka is afraid precisely because music matters enough for such a verdict to wound her.

V2 should preserve the evidentiary distinction:

- **fact:** Momoka says her songs will not work in the professional world and announces withdrawal;
- **strong in-text interpretation:** Mine says Momoka fears a conclusion that repeated testing would make harder to evade;
- **V2 synthesis:** Momoka is attempting **defensive foreclosure** - converting uncertainty into a self-authored defeat before the outside world can deliver a second defeat with greater authority.

That synthesis does not replace E05's guilt/jurisdiction model. It explains another layer of the same post-Diamond-Dust structure. Momoka has reasons not to make Nina's correctness tribunal her own goal. She also has reasons not to submit her own authorship to another professional test at all.

Mine provides the alternative adult model. She does not say professional commitment removes fear. She still loses sleep and appetite. She once recognized that becoming a teacher and keeping music as a hobby would be the more prudent route. Yet she remained because:

> `私にとってステージは 言いたいことが言える場所`
>
> `大好きな場所`
>
> E07 17:57.860-18:04.240

and therefore fear and pain are costs she accepts rather than evidence that the path is wrong.

Nina's response is important because she does not answer Momoka's withdrawal by immediately forcing Momoka to reverse it. She asks `それでいいんですか`, receives `それでいい`, then turns to Mine, listens, leaves the group, walks alone, and repeats:

> `決めた`
>
> E07 19:06.470 onward

This is a major post-checkpoint development. Threatened relational closure still activates Nina, but the activation is no longer only pursuit, interruption, or control of the other person's timing. E07 gives an alternative branch: **under attachment threat, Nina can convert urgency into a decision about her own stake.**

That decision is not moderate. At the end of the episode she announces:

> `予備校やめます`
>
> E07 23:29.270

The source establishes the declaration. It does not yet establish completed administrative withdrawal, abandonment of all university possibility, or reconciliation with her family. The distinction matters. E07 is not a triumphant proof that Nina has finally found the objectively correct life. It is the moment she redistributes risk. She stops protecting the university bargain as the institution that would certify her earlier refusal and makes the band bear more of the future she is willing to test.

The five-person band undergoes the same movement on a smaller scale. It has merchandise before it has a final name, accepts another live before its internal professional conflict is settled, argues technically in rehearsal, travels together, hears each other's histories, performs together even after Momoka announces she will leave, and acquires the name `トゲナシトゲアリ` only at the edge of the stage. The name is not the product of a successful consensus session. It is an abrupt public solution after every private proposal has failed.

The public performance then reveals that collective identity is still under negotiation. Nina says:

> `みんなきっと似たようなこと思ってるんです`
>
> E07 21:06.800

She again converts her own proof project into a claim about everybody. Tomo cuts her off:

> `うざいですよ`
>
> `自分語りは`
>
> E07 21:20.770-21:25.190

Nina answers simply:

> `はい`

and the band plays.

That exchange may be more important for five-person governance than the band name itself. Tomo's criticism is blunt, public, and behavior-specific. Nina does not treat it as rejection or relational exit. Tomo does not leave after delivering it. Their disagreement becomes a limit placed on Nina's attempt to make the band an extension of her autobiography, and the performance proceeds without requiring a verbal reconciliation scene.

E07's governing formulation is therefore:

> **Commitment is not the absence of fear or contradiction. It is the decision about which uncertainty one is willing to expose to reality. Momoka tries to end the test before it can become a verdict; Mine accepts fear as the price of keeping the test open; Nina moves from demanding that Momoka carry her proof project toward taking a higher-cost risk in her own life. The band becomes more real not because its members finally want the same thing, but because disagreement can begin limiting individual overreach without automatically ending participation.**

# 4. Scene-by-scene close reading

## 4.1 00:00-00:59 - Commerce before identity

E07 opens with the group already doing something that looks professional before it has solved the most basic symbolic question of professional identity.

Merchandise is being sold. T-shirts and towels exist. A temporary `新川崎（仮）` identity remains visible. Tomo objects:

> `バンドの名前も決まってないのに`
>
> `なんで作ったの`

The response is economic rather than aesthetic:

> `少しでも活動の足しになればと思って`

The band can monetize activity before it can name itself.

That order matters because E06 introduced follower counts and industry visibility as external metrics. E07 makes the next layer tangible: an independent group needs small-scale revenue, merch labor, a booth, attention, and repeat attendance. The public-facing surface is not evidence that the internal collective is settled.

Rupa receives direct positive attention from fans. People say they are supporting the group and will attend again. The sequence provides current evidence for Rupa's ease in a public-facing role, but V2 should not convert visible popularity into a universal charisma law. The narrow point is that different members already contribute different forms of social capital.

The group then returns immediately to the unresolved name question.

## 4.2 02:31-03:26 - A live opportunity exposes unequal commitment speeds

Momoka explains that a person who helped her when she was starting out has invited her to a live in Suwa. The schedule requires travel, advance rehearsal, the performance itself, and immediate return.

Momoka explicitly gives Nina an exit:

> `忙しいから 私１人で行くでもいいよ`
>
> `特に仁菜は 勉強もあるんだろうし`

Nina's answer is immediate:

> `行きます`

This continues the E05-E06 shift. Public performance is no longer something Momoka must drag out of Nina. Nina chooses the opportunity even when school is explicitly placed on the other side of the decision.

The important prospective caution is that `行きます` is not yet a career choice. It is another local allocation of time. E07 later shows that the cumulative allocations have already harmed the university plan.

Rupa supports experience accumulation. Tomo agrees conditionally through Rupa. The five-person group's decision structure therefore remains asymmetric: not everybody is independently enthusiastic, and Tomo still uses Rupa as an important relational decision node.

## 4.3 03:27-04:16 - Cold war without relational collapse

The younger members discuss Momoka's response to Nina's E06 professional declaration.

`なんにも` has happened. Momoka has not answered the proposal, and Subaru describes the relationship as a kind of cold war. Momoka has apparently been making needling comments at Nina.

Nina reacts to indirect conflict with her familiar preference:

> `言いたいことがあるなら はっきり言えっての`

Tomo then introduces a boundary that matters for five-person governance:

> `揉めるんなら 私らは抜けるからね`
>
> `時間がないんだから`

This is not yet the mature Tomo abandonment model. It is a plainly stated participation condition. Tomo joined because the group looked capable of real opinion collision, but she does not consent to being indefinitely subordinated to Nina and Momoka's unresolved dyad.

The line also connects to her later family disclosure. `時間がない` is not merely impatience. E07 will show that Tomo has organized her life around an early belief that she cannot afford the normal timetable of school -> graduation -> adult independence -> career experiment.

## 4.4 04:16-05:52 - The failed naming session: preference exposure, not psychological proof

The group attempts to choose a band name.

The source securely establishes proposals including:

- `ウンディーネ`;
- hiragana `ちょこれーと`;
- `ドリームガール`.

The exchange is comic and mutually embarrassing. The names are mocked, repeated, and rejected. By 05:47 Nina concludes:

> `もう 単に一日無駄にしただけじゃ`

This is a nearly ideal held-out test of CP1-P01. A bounded, low-stakes failure - one unsuccessful naming session - becomes a whole-day waste verdict. It does **not** become `our band will fail`, `nobody respects me`, or `my future is ruined`.

That is exactly the checkpoint's graded evaluative-compression model.

V2 should resist a different temptation here. V1 assigned deep symbolic psychology to each proposed name. The source does not provide enough evidence for that level of confidence. The suggestions are useful voice/style data:

- Tomo proposes a stylized foreign/fantasy term;
- Nina insists on a soft hiragana presentation for `ちょこれーと`;
- Subaru offers a polished English-derived phrase.

Those facts may become meaningful if later choices recur. In E07 alone, treating them as transparent windows into each member's hidden ideal band exceeds the evidence.

## 4.5 06:00-08:21 - Nina's sister turns the university plan back into Nina's responsibility

Nina returns to find her older sister waiting.

The scene begins with relational familiarity rather than enforcement. Her sister jokes about a boyfriend or suspicious work, then reacts with genuine surprise when Nina says:

> `バンド`

The band is already a part of Nina's life large enough to require explanation to family.

The mood changes when the sister reveals that the preparatory school has contacted home. Nina's grades have fallen and she has been missing class.

This matters because the university project can no longer be treated as a merely external family script. E01-E06 already established its reactive origin, but E07 makes Nina's own promise explicit. Her sister reminds her:

> `だから 大学は自力で受かる`
>
> `そう言ってこっち来たんじゃないの`

Nina rejected the compromise through which the family might have used the school's desire to avoid the bullying issue to obtain institutional leverage and an easier recommendation. Nina calls such a strategy:

> `ズルだよ`

The sister's account is ethically complicated rather than villainous. She says their father was wrong and suggests that his public identity as a charismatic teacher/author made accepting his own daughter's school refusal difficult. That is **the sister's explanation**, not direct access to the father's interior. She also says she can understand the strategic logic: if the family had left the bullying formally unresolved, the school would owe them, and Nina's grades made recommendation plausible.

The structure strongly parallels E05's Diamond Dust conflict:

- tolerate a present compromise;
- preserve institutional access;
- build leverage;
- reclaim or secure a preferred future later.

Nina and Momoka both resist that logic when the present compromise feels morally contaminating.

But E07 adds a crucial asymmetry. Nina used self-earned university admission as the promised proof that refusing the compromise would not destroy her future. If she now abandons the preparation without acknowledging that earlier bargain, her current choice cannot be reduced to `family control versus authentic self`.

The sister also warns that `家を出る` would mean the possibility of not being able to return. The exact legal/material mechanics are not specified, so V2 should not invent them. What is secure is that Nina understands the family decision as carrying more than ordinary moving-out consequences.

The sister ends with:

> `さすがにあんたの肩持てないよ`

This is not total rejection of Nina. It is bounded disagreement after an explicit acknowledgment that Nina was hurt and the father was wrong.

The scene therefore forces one of the series' hardest ethical distinctions:

> **having been wronged does not automatically validate every later choice made in resistance to the wrong.**

## 4.6 08:21-10:19 - Packing, driving, and the band as logistical organism

Momoka immediately becomes practical. Nina arrives with excessive luggage. Momoka removes unnecessary items and instructs:

> `荷物は 極力少なくするんだよ`

This is unilateral, but it is strongly domain-grounded: shared travel space, equipment burden, and a fixed schedule. It is useful counterevidence against flattening all directive Momoka behavior into paternalism.

Momoka's own competence is then punctured by the manual vehicle. Her driving produces a rapid vote for replacement. Rupa takes over and explains that a previous job involved driving.

The sequence redistributes authority:

- Momoka remains senior in musical/logistical planning;
- Rupa is more competent at this concrete task;
- the group can replace the nominal leader without turning the correction into a status crisis.

That is a small but meaningful governance result.

Subaru proposes a group game because everyone is together. The game itself is designed around finding a statement that applies to all participants. Nina wins with:

> `友達が少ない人`

The joke gives direct low-stakes evidence of a shared self-description: the members recognize themselves as people with relatively few friends. V2 should not inflate one comic game into a complete theory of social failure, but it does support the idea that the band is not composed of people with abundant, frictionless ordinary social networks.

## 4.7 10:23-12:01 - Tomo's withdrawal from school: urgency, family rupture, and perceived absence of a safety net

At the rest stop Nina asks Tomo:

> `なんで学校辞めたの`

Tomo initially asks why Nina wants to know. Nina answers:

> `他に聞く人いないから`

The answer is revealing in itself. Nina is beginning to use the band as a comparison set for life decisions that ordinary peers cannot answer for her.

Tomo's explanation is compact but substantial:

> `親はいないも同然だから`
>
> `卒業したら １人でなんとかしないといけなかったし`

She recalls seeing her mother with a man she did not know:

> `別の生き物みたいだった`

and says:

> `その母を見て 一気にスイッチ入った`

From there she gives a career-timing calculation. Eighteen- and twenty-year-old singers are already common. Starting earlier is advantageous. She came to Tokyo, assembled members, and concluded:

> `学校行ってる時間がもったいなかった`
>
> `それだけ`

The strongest E07 model is not `Tomo left school because ambition mattered more than education`, nor `Tomo left because of trauma`. The source gives a chain:

**family rupture / perceived absence of dependable parents -> expectation of early self-support -> urgency about career timing -> school reclassified as unaffordable time.**

Tomo's `それだけ` closes the explanation. It does not mean there is literally no emotion underneath it; it means the current interaction offers logic rather than emotional elaboration.

Tomo then reports that Rupa had to think similarly when her family died in an accident. This is **peer-reported biographical evidence**, not direct Rupa disclosure. It strengthens the rule that Rupa's calm surface must not be used to infer an unwounded history. It does **not** establish that bereavement caused her current tolerance for uncertainty, her driving competence, her politeness, or any other trait.

Nina answers:

> `私 全然だ`

The line clearly marks downward self-comparison. V1 interpreted it as Nina ranking the legitimacy of suffering. That may be possible, but E07 alone does not prove the stronger model. Nina could be comparing necessity, maturity, preparedness, or material independence. The secure claim is:

> **Tomo and Rupa's histories make Nina newly aware that her own exit from the ordinary route occurred with forms of support and optionality they may not have had.**

That realization will matter when she later decides what risk she is willing to assume.

## 4.8 12:07-13:43 - The first road-trip photograph and rehearsal collision

The group takes a photograph. Subaru manages the pose and asks for smiles. The resulting image is staged; that does not make the shared trip false. E07 continues the series' recurrent separation of constructed presentation from dishonest relationship.

At rehearsal, Tomo immediately criticizes the current state:

> `どこがよ ガッタガタじゃない`

Another member answers that `そっちの２人` are dragging. Tomo rejects the accusation. The exchange remains bounded to timing/playing, and another voice resolves:

> `どっちもだどっちも`

This is the first useful E07 test of Tomo's claim that she wants people who can `ちゃんと意見ぶつけられそう`.

She can in fact receive pushback against a technical judgment without leaving, withdrawing, or converting the criticism into a permanent character verdict. One exchange cannot establish mature conflict tolerance, but it is positive evidence against simulating Tomo as someone whose bluntness is purely one-directional.

Subaru's neck problem is then called out. Embodiment remains ordinary and unglamorous: the group is tired, stiff, carrying equipment, traveling, and still expected to rehearse.

## 4.9 13:08-14:08 - Mine reveals a pre-defeat Momoka social context

Mine arrives and calls Momoka:

> `モモ`

The nickname surprises the younger members.

A sticker Momoka once gave the older musicians is still on a guitar. The source therefore establishes an affectionate, materially persistent connection to Momoka's early Tokyo period.

Subaru says:

This is Subaru's interpretation. The visual evidence does independently show a lighter and more socially familiar Momoka in Mine's presence. V2 can therefore say:

- Mine accesses an older social register in Momoka;
- Momoka is not only the current group's guarded senior / failed professional;

> `ああ`
>
> `それでいい`

The mixed-program level around Momoka's `ありがとう` is notably low relative to Nina's response, but the acoustic evidence must remain cautious because room sound and mix differ. Formally, the scene is not staged as Momoka losing control in a loud explosion. The withdrawal arrives through controlled finality.

For Nina, this is a major CP1-P03 pressure test. The trigger - a meaningful member's announced departure - is clear. Yet Nina does not immediately grab, block, order, or chase Momoka into reversal. She asks one direct question and stops.

That does not falsify the checkpoint prediction after one counter-shaped case. It does establish a new branch that later modeling must preserve.

## 4.12 15:47-17:08 - Mine separates self-judgment from tested judgment

Later, Nina asks Mine whether she has ever wanted to quit. Mine initially calls Nina annoying. She then answers the real question by talking about Momoka:

> `嫌なんだろうね 結論が出るのが`

> `続ければ続けるほど 結果ははっきりしてくるからね`

> `１度目はうまくいかなくても 運が悪かったで済むけど`
>
> `２回やってダメだったら さすがに実力って言われる`

This is one of the series' clearest statements about **epistemic risk**.

Failure is painful not only because an objective is missed. Repeated failure changes the plausible explanation of the miss. The first failure can preserve ambiguity. The second threatens to convert ambiguity into identity-relevant evidence.

Nina asks:

> `うまくいくって 信じられないんですか`

Mine answers:

> `そりゃ信じたいよ`
>
> `でも 信じた上でダメだったら より傷は大きい`
>
> `怖いんだよ`
>
> `音楽が本当に好きだから`

This formulation is more precise than `Momoka lacks confidence`.

Momoka's fear depends on value. Belief increases expected injury because it makes failure more diagnostic. Defensive foreclosure becomes an attempt to reduce the epistemic authority of the next result by preventing the result from existing.

That also explains why concrete music can still activate Momoka in E06. Enjoying arrangement and sound does not require submitting to the external proposition `your work is professionally viable`. The fear appears when artistic activity becomes a test of future legitimacy.

Her model is closer to:

This directly challenges Nina's earlier tendency to treat fear as proof that someone secretly wants the opposite. Here fear is compatible with clear chosen commitment.

# 5. Nina reconstruction delta

## 5.1 Positive desire and correctness no longer form a simple before/after sequence

A weak developmental model would say:

`Nina begins from refusal -> discovers positive musical desire -> stops needing to prove she was right.`

E07 disproves the final arrow.

Nina can now say:

> `でも 楽しくて`
>
> `生まれて初めて 何かに夢中になってて`

while also saying:

> `その歌が間違ってないって 証明したいんです`

Positive attachment and correctness-seeking coexist.

This is important for simulation. Nina does not need to be reduced to a person who secretly has only one motive. Later choices may be simultaneously driven by:

- genuine enjoyment;
- attachment to bandmates;
- artistic attraction;
- anti-capitulation identity;
- desire to validate Momoka;
- desire to validate her past self;
- competitive anger;
- practical ambition.

The weights can change without one motive disappearing.

### New rule

**NINA-E07-R01 — positive desire can coexist with tribunal logic.**

When reconstructing Nina after E07, do not assume that discovering an affirmative want automatically dissolves the need for correctness. The same activity may become both intrinsically desired and instrumentally recruited as proof.

## 5.2 The university plan is now partly Nina's own authored obligation

Earlier episodes made the university route look reactive: a counter-verdict against family and school.

E07 makes the situation harder. Nina apparently came to Kawasaki on the promise that she would enter university through her own effort rather than use the school's strategic accommodation.

That means later resistance to studying is not simply refusal of somebody else's script.

Nina has authored at least part of the obligation herself.

This matters because the mature autonomy model must distinguish:

1. **externally imposed role** - somebody else decides what Nina should become;
2. **defensive self-authored role** - Nina adopts a path to prove the imposer wrong;
3. **positive self-authored desire** - Nina chooses something because she wants it;
4. **obligation created by prior self-authorship** - Nina's own earlier declaration now constrains her present freedom.

E07 is the first episode where all four are visible at once.

### New rule

**NINA-E07-R02 — prior self-authorship can become a later constraint.**

Nina may experience a promise she made in resistance as binding even when a new positive desire emerges. Breaking such a promise is psychologically different from rejecting an externally assigned path.

## 5.3 `ズル` remains a high-salience category, but the source complicates its usefulness

Nina calls the family's proposed recommendation strategy `ズル`.

The moral intuition is coherent. The family would tolerate unresolved institutional wrongdoing to gain leverage and secure a future advantage.

But E07 places this judgment beside Diamond Dust's temporary-compromise strategy and beside Nina's own E06 willingness to use commercial presentation in order to beat Diamond Dust.

The pattern is not simple hypocrisy. It reveals that Nina evaluates tactics differently depending on whether she thinks the compromise corrupts the meaning of the act.

The family wants to convert injury into institutional leverage. Nina experiences that as contamination of the claim that the school was wrong.

By contrast, E06's follower/branding tactics could be framed as instrumental tools serving a project Nina still experiences as hers.

The distinction is unstable, but it is psychologically useful:

> **Nina is less opposed to strategy itself than to strategies she experiences as requiring false assent to a verdict she rejects.**

That is narrower and more predictive than `Nina hates compromise`.

## 5.4 E07 creates a self-directed attachment-threat branch

The checkpoint predicted that threatened relational irreversibility would increase pursuit, interruption, or prescriptive behavior.

E04 strongly supported that branch.

E07 gives a different outcome. Momoka says she will leave after the next live. Nina asks one question, then later seeks explanation from Mine and makes a decision about her own life.

This is not passive acceptance. It is **jurisdictional redirection**.

### New rule

**NINA-E07-R03 — under attachment threat, urgency can be redirected from controlling the other person's choice toward increasing Nina's own commitment.**

The conditions that permit this branch are not fully known. Plausible contributors include:

- E05 deidealization of Momoka;
- E04-E05 learning about jurisdiction;
- the availability of Mine as an alternate source of explanation;
- the existence of a five-person band rather than an exclusive Nina/Momoka dyad;
- Momoka's explicit acknowledgment of Nina's feelings before refusing the project.

These are hypotheses, not yet causal proof.

## 5.5 Nina can accept blunt public correction without converting it into rejection

Tomo's:

> `うざいですよ`
>
> `自分語りは`

is not gentle.

Nina's response:

> `はい`

is unusually compact.

The scene is valuable because it is almost the inverse of E02. Nina is publicly corrected by a socially abrasive peer at the exact moment Nina is emotionally narrating the meaning of the band. She does not infer pity, false friendliness, or total rejection.

She accepts the limit and continues.

### New rule

**NINA-E07-R04 — blunt criticism can be survivable when its target is behavior/content rather than Nina's total personhood and when continued participation is immediately available.**

This converges with CP1-P04's structure even though the scene is not a rupture/repair episode and therefore does not earn a formal P04 confirmation.

It is also early evidence for `GBC-V1-C042`.

## 5.6 `決めた` is self-command rather than interpersonal argument

Nina's repeated `決めた` matters for speech modeling.

Her previous repetition often amplified a conflict or attempted to force a position outward. Here repetition stabilizes an internal decision.

The line is short, plain, and repeated against a large audiovisual environment dominated by fireworks.

### New rule

**NINA-E07-R05 — repetition can function as self-binding, not only affective discharge toward another person.**

This should be preserved separately from angry repetition.

## 5.7 `予備校やめます` is a declared high-cost commitment, not yet a completed irreversible act

V1 correctly recognized the importance of the final line, but reconstruction should be precise about its state.

The utterance is:

- explicit;
- addressed to the band;
- temporally after Nina's solitary decision and performance;
- consequential given the sister scene.

It is not evidence that all follow-through has happened.

### New rule

**NINA-E07-R06 — when Nina resolves a conflict internally, she may announce a high-cost commitment in categorical language before the institutional consequences have been negotiated.**

This resembles earlier body-first/irreversibility behavior in structure, but the mechanism is now more deliberative. The decision follows information gathering rather than immediate panic.

That distinction matters.

# 6. Momoka reconstruction delta

## 6.1 Goal refusal becomes fear of diagnostic exposure

E06 established:

> declared lack of a future target != lack of artistic engagement.

E07 adds:

> professional refusal may protect Momoka from a test she fears will convert uncertainty into a verdict on ability.

This is not a replacement rule. It is a condition on the earlier model.

Momoka remains willing to:

- travel;
- rehearse;
- arrange;
- judge timing;
- play with stronger musicians;
- reconnect with old musical peers;
- perform.

What she resists is the proposition that she should **carry the band into a professional test**.

### New rule

**MOMOKA-E07-R01 — concrete music can remain desirable while professional-scale evaluation becomes aversive because it threatens to turn past failure into evidence about enduring ability.**

This is a high-value distinction for later reconstruction.

## 6.2 `私の作る曲じゃ` is self-verdict, not objective source fact

Momoka says:

> `私の作る曲じゃ プロの世界では通用しない`

The sentence is a statement of belief.

The episode does not independently prove that her songs cannot succeed professionally. Indeed, the surrounding series has already shown audiences reacting to her music and newer collaborators finding value in it.

V2 should therefore store:

- **belief:** strong;
- **objective market truth:** open;
- **behavioral consequence:** strong.

This prevents reconstruction from making Momoka's realism automatically correct merely because it is grounded in experience.

## 6.3 Responsibility language matters: `このバンドを背負って`

Momoka does not say only `I don't want to go pro`.

She says she cannot pursue professional status while **carrying this band**.

That phrase introduces a responsibility dimension.

Possible meanings include:

- she distrusts her songs as the basis for others' careers;
- she fears repeating the Diamond Dust experience with new people;
- she refuses to let Nina's proof project turn her into the guarantor of success;
- she sees professional leadership as a burden she no longer wants.

E07 does not resolve which dominates.

### Current formulation

**MOMOKA-E07-R02 — professional ambition becomes more threatening when Momoka experiences herself as responsible for other members' futures, not merely her own artistic output.**

Confidence: moderate.

## 6.4 Gratitude can become closure language

Momoka tells Nina:

> `嬉しい`
>
> `感謝してる`
>
> `ありがとう`

and still leaves.

This is a useful counterexample to any model in which warmth or gratitude automatically predicts continued engagement.

The sequence is structurally similar to a farewell: Momoka validates the value Nina brought into her life while trying to define that value as something complete enough to end.

This resembles E01's tendency to manufacture closure around objects and performances.

### New rule

**MOMOKA-E07-R03 — positive acknowledgment can be used inside a closure attempt; affection and withdrawal are not opposite states.**

## 6.5 Mine accesses a distinct social register without proving an original essence

`モモ` and the old sticker establish prior intimacy.

Momoka's visible ease around Mine and the older musicians is real. Subaru's `もともとはあんな感じだったのかも` is plausible.

But reconstruction should not use one context to divide Momoka into:

- authentic pre-failure self;
- false post-failure self.

Both are real states conditioned by history and audience.

### New rule

**MOMOKA-E07-R04 — older safe musical ties can evoke a lighter/younger social register without erasing the defensive structures formed after Diamond Dust.**

## 6.6 Mine's diagnosis should remain attributed

Mine says Momoka fears conclusion because repeated failure would look like ability rather than luck.

Mine knew Momoka earlier and the episode gives her interpretive authority, but she is not an omniscient narrator.

The correct model is:

> Mine's interpretation receives strong weight because it predicts Momoka's behavior and fits Momoka's own self-verdict, but it remains an attributed model until Momoka herself confirms or later evidence converges.

That distinction is analytically important because the project has already learned from Subaru and Rupa not to erase epistemic uncertainty simply because a socially perceptive character offers a compelling explanation.
