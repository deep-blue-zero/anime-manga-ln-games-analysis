# Girls Band Cry E04 - V2 Deep Reading

## 1. Episode thesis

Episode 4 relocates the problem of authenticity from the stage to the family.

E03 had already made a simple opposition between performance and truth untenable. Nina delivered one of her most emotionally unguarded performances while wearing an artificial costume chosen for her by someone else; Subaru's socially polished warmth proved compatible with genuine care; and rehearsal demonstrated that constructed form could carry personally real material. E04 makes the problem more difficult because performance is no longer only an expressive tool. It is also the medium through which a family has organized Subaru's future.

Awa Subaru is good at acting. That competence is real. Her grandmother Awa Tendo's love for her is real. Subaru's dislike of an acting career is also real. Tendo's expectation that Subaru will continue her professional lineage is therefore neither a simple lie nor a simple act of cruelty. It is a future built from genuine affection, genuine artistic investment, and a serious misallocation of jurisdiction: the older person has treated the younger person's early admiration as permission to pre-author a life.

Subaru responds with a strategy that is equally mixed. She performs the granddaughter Tendo expects, attends acting school, maintains posture and politeness, and lets her grandmother believe she wants the career. Yet the concealment is not evidence that Subaru is emotionally false. She explains that when she says she wants to become an actress, Tendo gives one of the few smiles Subaru experiences as unconstructed. Subaru's false vocational statement therefore protects something emotionally true: her desire not to wound a person she loves, and her pleasure in being able to make that person genuinely happy.

E04's first governing proposition is therefore:

> **Authenticity is not equivalent to immediate disclosure. A performed role can contain real care, while a truthful demand can become controlling when it claims the right to determine another person's timing.**

This is also the episode in which Nina begins to learn that distinction and immediately mishandles it.

At first Nina treats Subaru's possible departure as an emergency requiring pursuit. A one-week acting commitment becomes, in Nina's language, the possibility that Subaru may disappear from the band altogether. She accuses Momoka of lacking consideration because Momoka is willing to let Subaru manage her own family problem. Subaru reverses the accusation: from her perspective, Nina is the inconsiderate one, because Nina is treating anxiety about the relationship as entitlement to penetrate circumstances Subaru has asked her to leave alone.

Momoka gives Nina a principle:

> `人には触れられたくない事情ってのがあるんだよ`
>
> People have circumstances they do not want others touching.

She then makes the analogy unavoidable: Nina herself does not want strangers pressing into why she came to Kawasaki.

Nina learns the semantic lesson remarkably quickly. After hearing the emotional history behind Tendo's expectation, she reverses her earlier demand that Subaru tell the truth immediately. She physically prevents Subaru from confessing and later repeats Momoka's language back to the group: people have their own circumstances; things are not simple; one has to learn to take account of them.

But this is not mature perspective-taking yet. Nina has changed **what** she thinks Subaru should do without changing the assumption that Nina may decide the timing on Subaru's behalf. Subaru correctly attacks the contradiction. Nina's development therefore has to be modeled as uneven:

> **Nina can acquire a more complex moral principle before she has acquired the jurisdictional restraint required to apply it well.**

That matters for character reconstruction. Nina is not a static absolutist who refuses all nuance. She can update quickly. The danger is that a newly learned nuance can itself become another categorical rule she applies to other people.

Momoka, meanwhile, provides an important counterexample to the E03 model of her as an interventionist who pushes through resistance whenever she thinks she understands the hidden desire. In E04 she repeatedly favors nonintrusion. She tells Nina not to panic over Subaru's departure, accepts Subaru's temporary acting absence, argues that private circumstances should sometimes remain private, and later says that if the consequences of disclosure are as serious as leaving school or returning home, Subaru does not have to force the confession immediately.

The best current Momoka rule is therefore not "she overrides refusal when she thinks she is right." It is narrower:

> **Momoka intervenes aggressively when she classifies an obstacle as fear blocking a desire she believes is already legible; she is much more willing to defer when she classifies the matter as another person's private jurisdiction or a decision with consequences that person must own.**

E04 also gives Subaru her first direct positive-choice language about the band. In E03 she described it as `ささやかな抵抗`, a modest resistance to an imposed acting future. In E04, after the live, she tells Nina:

> `私 意外とこれ本気で好きかもって`
>
> I think I might actually, unexpectedly, really like this.

The `かも` matters: it is still exploratory rather than a final life declaration. But the band is no longer only a hiding place beneath an unwanted role. It is becoming a positive object of attachment in its own right.

Finally, E04 gives the strongest early proof yet of the V1 authenticity hypothesis. Nina uses a romantic improvisation to say what she cannot comfortably say as direct band grievance. Subaru later stages an angry outburst at Nina and Momoka, says `私のことは私が決める / ほっとけよ`, and then marks the outburst as acting: `どう 私の演技`. The performance does not cancel the grievance. It gives the grievance a frame in which it can be transmitted without automatically becoming relational exit.

The episode's larger V2 formulation is therefore:

> **E04 is about jurisdiction over truth. The question is not merely whether a feeling is sincere, but who gets to decide the form, timing, and future into which that sincerity is translated. Acting can imprison Subaru when it is assigned as destiny; the same acting can become hers when she uses it to communicate, protect, vent, or choose.**

## 3. Prospective episode state

E04 may know only E01-E03.

### Nina hypotheses entering E04

1. Nina has a broad tendency toward **evaluative compression**: partial failure can become an overly total local judgment; existential totalization becomes most dangerous when shame, belonging, injury, or identity are implicated.
2. Nina can repair directly when the counterpart names a specific rupture without defining her whole self, leaves a genuine exit, and preserves her agency.
3. Intimacy can trigger an authorship defense: another person's desire for closeness can feel like an attempt to script Nina's role.
4. Nina's politeness can survive high affect; roughness and regionality vary independently by interlocutor and state.
5. Her positive musical desire is behaviorally secure before explicit life-plan language.
6. Threatened irreversibility can produce body-first action before clear relational articulation.
7. Once inhibition breaks, emotional commitment can overshoot sustainable technique.

### Momoka hypotheses entering E04

1. Practical/logistical care is recurrent.
2. When she concedes a misjudgment, Momoka can apologize directly; when she retains the judgment, repair can take the form of presence, action, or reframe.
3. E03 revised her boundary rule: explicit refusal does not necessarily stop her if she classifies the refusal as fear or self-betrayal rather than genuine preference.
4. She values co-creation and is willing to incorporate novice contribution.
5. Her lost-artistic-self identification with Nina is established; a literal `younger self` projection remains unproven.
6. Her former Diamond Dust attention remains emotionally underdetermined.

1. Social warmth is adjustable rather than fake.
2. Acting competence does not imply identification with an acting career.
3. Reciprocal vulnerability allows rougher, less polished affiliative speech.
4. Subaru can name dyadic rupture skillfully, but one successful repair does not yet establish her as the band's stable mediator.
5. The band is meaningful as resistance and enjoyment, but E03's final `バンドやめます / ごめん!` blocks any claim that commitment is secure.
6. The family mechanism behind the unwanted acting destiny remains largely unknown.

# 4. Scene-by-scene close reading

## 4.1 00:00-01:13 - Post-live intoxication and the discovery that Nina experiences ordinary speech as restraint

E04 opens in a guitar shop. Nina looks at the instruments with the afterglow of the previous live still active. Her first line is simply:

> `かっこいい`

She asks whether she could play despite having no experience, explicitly imagining Momoka as the model:

> `できるかな`
>
> `桃香さんみたいに`

Momoka's response is immediate:

> `やめとけ`
>
> `歌がおろそかになるぞ`

The exchange is comic, but it adds two important pieces of reconstruction evidence.

First, Nina's musical attraction is expanding. E03 established voluntary composition labor. E04 begins with instrumental aspiration. She is not merely clinging to Momoka's song or accepting a vocalist role because someone recruited her. She sees a new expressive capability and immediately wants access to it.

Second, Momoka's response is a technical boundary rather than a prohibition on Nina's autonomy. She does not say Nina is incapable of guitar or that the instrument belongs to Momoka's domain. She says Nina has no experience and that splitting attention would harm the singing that currently needs development. This is a small but useful counterexample to reading every Momoka constraint as control.

The more important material is Nina's internal reflection on the live.

She recalls the experience as:

> `自分が思っていることを 思い切り叫ぶ`

and contrasts it with ordinary life:

> `人の顔色を気にして`
>
> `いつも何かが喉につかえながらしゃべっていた`

This materially revises the common surface image of Nina as someone who simply says whatever she thinks. From the outside, Nina can look unusually unfiltered because her anger becomes explicit. From the inside, she describes ordinary speech as chronic monitoring: watching other people's faces, feeling something lodged in her throat, and failing to release the full internal statement.

That distinction is crucial for a reconstructive model.

Nina is not low-monitoring. She may in fact be **high-monitoring until a threshold is crossed**. Her explosive scenes can therefore be modeled as failure or rejection of regulation after accumulated inhibition rather than evidence that inhibition was absent in the first place.

The first live mattered because it gave her a sanctioned structure in which maximal output was the task. For once, volume and intensity did not have to be apologized for in advance.

This also clarifies why music can become attractive so quickly. It is not merely another hobby. It is a social technology that changes what Nina is permitted to do with the material that otherwise remains in her throat.

### Reconstruction delta

Add a new high-confidence distinction:

**NINA-E04-R01 - chronic monitoring / thresholded release.**

In ordinary social conditions Nina may monitor faces and suppress wording more than her argumentative reputation suggests. Once a moral, relational, or performative threshold is crossed, regulation can collapse or be deliberately abandoned, producing unusually direct output.

This rule should be tested against later ordinary-life conversations rather than universalized from one monologue.

## 4.3 02:48-03:29 - Recording: truth is revised, replayed, and worked

The trio is now in a rehearsal/recording room rather than a public performance site.

Momoka stops the take, listens back, asks for changes in how Nina enters the chorus, and the group discusses whether a phrasing is too obvious. The drums are ordered back through another take despite Subaru having an early obligation the next morning.

The important point is not which member owns every micro-critique. The scene's dramatic function is collective revision.

E03 had shown that Nina could enter music through emotional exposure. E04 refuses to romanticize that exposure as sufficient. The band now requires:

- repetition;
- listening back to oneself;
- criticism that does not equal rejection;
- willingness to repeat labor after the emotionally meaningful performance has ended;
- technical decisions that can alter how an emotionally sincere line is delivered.

This is a direct formal extension of E03's authenticity thesis:

> **If authenticity meant lack of mediation, the recording room would corrupt the live. E04 instead treats revision as part of making intention legible.**

The sequence also matters for Nina's conflict model. She accepts correction inside a musical task without converting every critique into a total self-verdict. The episode therefore supplies another counterexample to a global `criticism -> collapse` rule. Domain, relational trust, and the perceived legitimacy of the critic matter.

### Co-creation status

This scene strengthens the existence of collective craft, but it still does not fully prove `conflict becomes composition` in the mature V1 sense. We observe revision and role-specific contribution. We do not yet observe an explicit interpersonal disagreement whose incompatible positions are audibly retained and transformed into the final arrangement.

`GBC-V1-C015` therefore remains open.

## 4.4 03:29-06:22 - Diamond Dust, unfinished history, and Nina's growing fear that sincere groups still end

A cracked smartphone displays Diamond Dust material. Nina and Subaru discuss whether Momoka remains conscious of her former group. The dialogue does not establish jealousy, hostility, or a current competitive project. It establishes attention.

The distinction matters because E04 later gives Momoka a chance to state her own current position. When Nina asks what she thinks of present-day Diamond Dust, Momoka says:

> `別に向こうを恨んでるわけじゃないし`
>
> `今のダイダスは 売れてくれればいいって思ってるよ`

She denies resentment and says she hopes they sell.

Nina immediately asks the question that the episode actually cares about:

> `じゃあ なんでやめたんですか`

Momoka first refuses. She tells Nina not to worry about unnecessary things and to concentrate on singing. Nina keeps pressing until Momoka finally gives the outline of the old band's rise.

The history is strikingly positive.

Momoka describes the original members as unusually skilled from the beginning, full of passion, aligned in what they wanted, and so compatible that she thought the meeting was fate:

> `これは運命だと感じた`
>
> `こんな奇跡ないって 鳥肌が立ったよ`

They drew an overflowing school audience, decided to aim professionally, quit school to remove their retreat, moved to the Tokyo area, worked as an indie act, released CDs, played lives, and reached the edge of a major debut.

Then:

> `で 私が脱退`

The episode still withholds why.

That withholding is analytically important. E04 does **not** yet authorize a conclusion that Momoka left because the others betrayed artistic purity, because she feared success, because the label forced compromise, or because she could not tolerate conflict. Those remain later questions.

What E04 does establish is that the original Diamond Dust was not retroactively meaningless to Momoka. The retained visual material shows a younger Momoka smiling openly inside that earlier group, and Nina comments:

> `こんな顔で笑うんですね`

Nina has already seen Momoka laugh, tease, care, argue, and perform. What surprises her is this particular configuration of visible joy: Momoka inside a group she once believed was miraculous.

For Nina, that history has an uncomfortable implication even before she can formulate it.

A group can be sincere.

A group can feel like fate.

People can sacrifice school and security for it.

And someone can still leave.

The episode does not explicitly state that Nina's later reaction to Subaru is caused by this realization. But the sequencing makes the connection highly plausible: the old-band story immediately precedes the present-band departure scare.

This gives the E02-E03 attachment model a new pressure point. Nina's fear is not only that other people never meant what they said. E04 begins teaching her a more destabilizing possibility: **people can mean it and still change course.**

That possibility is much harder for Nina's binary commitment logic to absorb.

### Momoka reconstruction delta

Momoka's old-band nostalgia is now direct evidence of sincere former investment. It should not yet be upgraded into a stable present desire to reunite, envy the current band, or punish herself for leaving. The cause and current meaning of the split remain open.

### Nina reconstruction delta

Nina's attachment sensitivity is now increasingly tied to continuity. She wants declarations to remain true over time, and evidence that a sincere past bond nevertheless ended creates a threat that later attaches to Subaru's temporary withdrawal.---

## 4.5 06:33-07:52 - `バンドやめます`: attachment turns a departure message into an emergency

After the Diamond Dust history, the episode returns to the present trio with a deliberately mundane image: Nina has dozed off instead of sleeping properly. Then Subaru's message arrives.

The text itself is short. Its effect on Nina is not.

Momoka is comparatively willing to wait. She even produces a comic hypothesis that Subaru may have become involved with a man and warns that over-pursuit could intensify the situation. Nina rejects the wait-and-see posture:

> `この３人でやっていこうって決めたじゃないですか`
>
> `そんな簡単に諦めていいんですか`

The first sentence matters more than the second. Nina is no longer describing the band as Momoka's project that she happens to be trying. She describes the three-person configuration as something **decided**.

E03 established positive musical desire behaviorally. E04 adds positive **relational commitment**. Nina has begun to treat the trio's continuity as a promise whose unilateral withdrawal requires explanation.

That investment also reveals a weakness in her emerging care model. Nina does not merely want to know whether Subaru is safe. She wants the group's prior decision to remain binding. When Momoka hesitates to intervene, Nina recruits her through role assignment:

> `リーダーなんですから よろしくお願いします`

Momoka immediately answers:

> `いつリーダーになった`

The exchange is comic, but it exposes Nina's tendency to create normative structure quickly when uncertainty becomes intolerable. If the trio is a band, someone is leader; if someone is leader, that person must act; if Subaru said she was staying, departure cannot simply be accepted.

This is a new form of the evaluative-compression mechanism developed in E02-E03. A single message is processed not merely as a local disruption but as a possible invalidation of the group's continuity.

The timing after Momoka's Diamond Dust account matters. Nina has just learned that people can call a group destiny, sacrifice heavily for it, and still leave. Subaru's message therefore arrives into a newly destabilized model of commitment. The episode does not state that causal chain explicitly, so V2 should preserve it as a strong sequencing inference rather than a quoted motive.

### Falsification result: attachment alarm is not identical to self-condemnation

E04 gives another reason not to collapse all Nina totalization into one mechanism.

Here the high-stakes compression points outward:

- temporary uncertainty becomes possible permanent departure;
- an individual choice becomes a threat to the group's meaning;
- care becomes pursuit.

Nina does not say that she herself is worthless or doomed. The cognitive structure is similar to E02, but the target differs.

A better rule is:

> **When attachment continuity is threatened, Nina can rapidly promote an ambiguous local event into a possible irreversible relational loss and then act before uncertainty has been metabolized.**

This modestly strengthens the E01 body-first rule while making its trigger more specific.

## 4.6 07:52-10:39 - Subaru before her grandmother: performance as embodied inheritance

Nina and Momoka find Subaru accompanying her grandmother, the famous actress Awa Tendo. The scene immediately answers one of E03's most important questions: Subaru's polished social self changes dramatically by audience, and the change is visible not only in wording but in posture, address, and bodily discipline.

Subaru initially tries to manage Nina and Momoka without exposing the band. When Tendo asks who they are, Subaru says:

> `お友達です 役者仲間の`

The falsehood is efficient. It keeps the two girls inside a category legible to her grandmother's world.

Then Tendo gives a one-word correction:

> `姿勢`

Subaru answers instantly:

> `はい`

Tendo expands the principle:

> `常に姿勢を大切にしなさいって言っているでしょ`
>
> `芝居には普段の言動 立ち居振る舞いがしみ出てくるもの`
>
> `いつも心と体を健全に保っていなくては`

The lesson makes acting an all-day discipline. Ordinary speech, posture, health, and bearing are treated as material that will seep into performance. Subaru has therefore not simply been enrolled in an acting school. Her everyday body has been trained as pre-performance.

The visual evidence makes the inheritance unusually concrete. Beside Tendo, Subaru's carriage is composed and deferential. Her address becomes `おばあさま`. She receives correction with immediate compliance.

But the episode then supplies one of the cleanest code-switches in the V2 corpus so far.

When Nina refuses the improvised exercise:

> `嫌です`

Subaru performs public smoothing:

> `あらニーナさん よく聞こえなかったみたい`

Then, almost immediately and covertly, she turns toward Nina with:

> `人の話聞けこら`
>
> `やって バンドやりたいんでしょ`

The retained frame at approximately 11:25 shows the facial compression and peer-directed roughness corresponding to that aside. The two registers are not separated by hours, locations, or emotional states. They coexist within seconds because the audience changes.

This is decisive for reconstruction.

Subaru's polish cannot be modeled as a stable `true` or `false` personality layer. It is **audience-aware behavioral control**. She can maintain the socially acceptable surface for Tendo while simultaneously transmitting a rough, coercive peer message to Nina underneath it.

### Subaru's threat model

Once Tendo is temporarily out of earshot, Subaru explains the problem more directly.

She has no intention of becoming an actress:

> `役者になるつもりはないんだよね`
>
> `ないよ`

But she believes direct disclosure now would be crushed:

> `今言ったら潰されちゃうって`

She describes Tendo as someone whose surface wording should not be accepted at face value and whose decisions are not spontaneous. Whether Subaru's interpretation is fully fair remains open; E04 later gives Tendo independent evidence that complicates it. But Subaru's behavior is intelligible from her own model: she treats information as something that must be controlled until she has enough leverage to survive the reaction.

That model explains the E03 coda.

When Nina demands to know why Subaru sent the quitting message, Subaru answers:

> `それは気が動転したというか`
>
> `敵を欺くには まず味方からというか`
>
> `とにかく そのくらい本気でごまかさないとダメなの`

The phrase is comic, but the behavior is not trivial. Under threat, Subaru protects the boundary between band life and family life by deceiving even the people inside the band.

This is a necessary correction to any premature `Subaru = ideal mediator` model.

Her social competence is real. Her conflict insight is real. But competence does not guarantee transparent coordination under personal threat.

### Behavioral reconstruction delta

`SUBARU-E03-R02` is strengthened and made more precise:

> **Subaru's social presentation is highly audience-conditioned and can switch rapidly between polished public compliance and rough private affiliation.**

A new rule is added:

> **SUBARU-E04-R02 — Under threat to a compartmentalized life, Subaru may control information unilaterally even at the cost of destabilizing trusted allies.**

That is not simple dishonesty. It is a defense strategy with relational costs.

## 4.7 10:39-12:21 - The acting exercise: fiction becomes a container for true grievance

Tendo invites Nina, Momoka, and Subaru into an etude. The assigned premise is deliberately artificial: one woman confesses love, while an ex-partner attempts to reclaim her.

Nina initially refuses. Subaru pressures her into participating because the band matters to her.

The scene then produces E04's strongest direct test of `GBC-V1-C008`, the authenticity hypothesis.

Nina begins inside the fictional romantic premise:

> `やっぱり 自分の気持ちに嘘をつきたくない`
>
> `私も あなたが好き`

But when Subaru enters as the ex-partner, Nina's lines cease to function only as fictional romance:

> `私は最初に付き合った時から本気だった`
>
> `絶対別れないって思ってたのに`
>
> `どうしてあんな簡単に別れるなんて言ったの`
>
> `あの時私に本気だって言ったのは嘘なの`

The real referent is clear from the episode-local context: Subaru's `バンドやめます` message.

V2 should be careful here. The romantic vocabulary does **not** prove that Nina and Subaru's relationship is literally romantic. The scene's importance lies elsewhere. A fictional romantic role gives Nina access to a form of speech she had not used directly:

- I believed you chose us.
- Your withdrawal hurt me.
- I need to know whether the earlier commitment was sincere.

Subaru recognizes exactly what Nina has done:

> `ひどいよ`
>
> `芝居に格好つけて 言いたいこと言って`

This is not an accusation that Nina's feelings were fake. It is an accusation that Nina used the role as cover to say something real.

That distinction is the episode's central formal insight.

E03 had already suggested that a costume, arrangement, and stage could mediate genuine expression. E04 removes music entirely from the key demonstration. Acting itself becomes the mechanism.

> **A statement can be fictional in surface referent and still be truthful in relational function.**

The performed form does not dilute authenticity. It changes the cost structure of disclosure.

### Subaru as relational technician

Subaru also uses acting strategically here. She knows Nina wants the band. She knows Nina is angry. Rather than demand a conventional apology or explanation before Tendo, she helps create a scene in which the grievance can surface without requiring Nina to state, nakedly, `I was hurt because I already care about this group.`

This strengthens `GBC-V1-C036`: acting is becoming a tool Subaru can appropriate for autonomy and care, not only an inherited demand.

It also strengthens but complicates `GBC-V1-C037`. Subaru can indeed translate emotional pressure into a workable interaction. Yet the same episode shows that she previously destabilized the group with unilateral deception. Her mediation skill should therefore be modeled as **capability**, not constant behavioral virtue.

## 4.8 12:21-14:34 - One week becomes possible disappearance: care, trust, and the right not to be pursued

The trio soon discovers why Tendo appeared. Subaru has been placed in a television-drama role. She describes it as minor and expects it to take roughly a week, but rehearsal will be impossible during that period.

Momoka responds:

> `分かった`

Nina does not.

She argues that Subaru's absence is different from Nina missing practice for study. The dispute quickly reveals that the factual one-week schedule is not Nina's actual concern.

She says:

> `すばるちゃんいなくなっちゃうかもしれないんですよ`

Subaru answers:

> `戻ってくるって言ってるでしょ`
>
> `今はそれを信じてよ`

This is one of E04's most important relationship statements.

Nina thinks care requires intervention against a threatening trajectory. Subaru asks her to recognize **trust and noninterference as forms of care**.

The disagreement is not `caring Nina versus indifferent Subaru`. It is a conflict over what care entitles a person to do.

Nina then accuses Momoka:

> `桃香さんが思いやりがないからです`

Subaru reverses it:

> `私からしたら 思いやりがないのはニーナの方`

Momoka provides the conceptual correction:

> `人には触れられたくない事情ってのがあるんだよ`
>
> `お前だって どうしてこっち来たか聞かれたくないだろ`

This is the strongest counterexample yet to a global `Momoka pushes through resistance` model.

Momoka has been willing to push Nina aggressively when she interprets Nina's refusal as fear blocking a desire she can already see. Here, however, the underlying decision belongs to Subaru, the consequences reach Subaru's family and career, and Momoka explicitly defends a protected private zone.

That suggests Momoka's intervention logic is **jurisdiction-sensitive** rather than simply high-pressure.

> **MOMOKA-E04-R01 — Momoka is more likely to push when she believes the desired action and its primary consequences lie within a domain she understands as jointly actionable; she can defend nonintrusion when the issue belongs to another person's private history or consequential choice.**

The rule is still provisional. But E04 prevents us from reducing her to `correct insight justifies pressure` as a universal behavior.

### Nina's compression under attachment threat

The one-week absence gives the clearest E04 recurrence of evaluative compression.

The local fact is finite: a minor acting job, approximately one week, stated intent to return.

Nina's feared conclusion is permanent:

> Subaru may disappear.

This is not irrational in the sense of being impossible. Subaru's acting family really can exert pressure. But Nina moves from possibility to urgent relational policy faster than the evidence warrants.

Her attachment system therefore appears to weight **irreversibility risk** very heavily. Once a temporary disruption can be imagined as permanent loss, she behaves as though waiting itself may be negligence.

The sequence also clarifies `GBC-V1-C019`. Nina's control problem is not only that she mistakes being affected by others for being controlled by them. E04 shows the inverse: when she fears losing someone, she may treat her own emotional investment as warrant to influence that person's decisions.

The deeper issue is jurisdiction over relational consequence.

## 4.9 14:34-17:17 - Subaru's home: inherited naming, outsourced refusal, and the first direct positive choice of the band

Subaru brings Nina into her home partly because Nina has been demanding access to the problem. The scene is relaxed enough to reveal how much of Subaru's identity has already been narrated by other people.

A film becomes the key object. Subaru explains:

> `このタイトルから取ったんだよ 私の名前`
>
> `これがいいっておばあちゃんが`

The name `Subaru` is itself attached to the role that made Tendo famous.

That fact turns E03's `inherited acting future` into something more literal. The acting script did not begin when Subaru entered school. Her grandmother wrote professional memory into Subaru's name.

Subaru is explicit about what she dislikes:

> `おばあちゃんの七光りで どこ行っても特別扱いなのも嫌だし`
>
> `そもそも 芝居って好きじゃない`

But the scene refuses the easy conclusion that Subaru is merely afraid of an authoritarian elder.

She explains that when she says she intends to become an actress, Tendo smiles differently:

> `その時だけ 本当に笑うんだよね`
>
> `作った笑顔じゃなくて`

This is a difficult sentence inside an episode about constructed expression. Subaru, who is unusually good at performance, believes she can distinguish her grandmother's routine public smile from a rarer unguarded one. That smile has emotional leverage because Subaru loves the person producing it.

Her lie is therefore maintained by multiple forces at once:

- fear of being overruled;
- gratitude and affection;
- dislike of hurting Tendo;
- discomfort with acting as a vocation;
- resentment of inherited status;
- lack of a positive alternative future strong enough to name.

This is much richer than `Subaru is too cowardly to tell the truth`.

### Outsourcing the refusal

Subaru admits that she entered acting school partly hoping the institution would solve the problem for her:

> `役者の学校行けば 才能ないって分かって`
>
> `諦めてくれると思ったんだけどな`

This is an important decision-pattern clue.

Instead of saying `I refuse this`, Subaru hoped an external verdict - lack of talent - would produce the same outcome without requiring her to personally reject her grandmother's dream.

A provisional rule follows:

> **SUBARU-E04-R05 — When direct refusal risks injuring an important relationship, Subaru may try to outsource the `no` to circumstances, institutional judgment, or an externally legible failure.**

This helps distinguish Subaru from Nina.

Nina tends to make refusal maximally visible and then absorb the consequences.

Subaru can preserve surface cooperation while searching for a way to make the unwanted path close itself.

Neither strategy is treated as universally superior.

### Positive band desire becomes explicit

E03 ended with Subaru's band commitment unresolved. E04 changes that status decisively, though not absolutely.

Subaru tells Nina:

> `心配しなくても やめないよ`

Then gives the reason:

> `この前ライブで叩いてて思ったんだ`
>
> `私 意外とこれ本気で好きかもって`

The qualifier `かも` matters. Subaru is not delivering a finished career manifesto. But `本気で好き` is stronger than the E03 description of band activity as `ささやかな抵抗`.

The band is beginning to move from **negative escape** to **positive attachment**.

That strongly strengthens `GBC-V1-C039`.

It also answers Nina's fear more directly than Nina seems able to absorb: Subaru is not returning merely because she promised. She is discovering that she actually likes the activity.

### Repair after Nina's intrusion

The scene also gives ordinary repair evidence. Nina eventually apologizes for her earlier behavior, and Subaru does not convert the conflict into a relationship-ending verdict. Their conversation moves from irritation into explanation and teasing.

This is another important early found-family mechanism: conflict can remain conflict without automatically becoming departure.

The trio still lacks mature stability. But the dyads are beginning to practice survivable disagreement.

## 4.10 17:17-19:36 - The film set: staged faces, material work, and Tendo's independent interiority

The episode then moves to Tendo and Subaru's professional set. The setting is valuable because it literalizes the theme: makeup, role, staging, production schedules, and professional hierarchy surround a family problem about acting.

Nina comments on Tendo's conspicuous makeup. Momoka normalizes it as performance practice and immediately compares it to band staging:

> `あのくらいやらないと 分からないんだよ`
>
> `バンドだって ステージではすごいぞ`

The line further strengthens the E03-E04 authenticity model. The series is not moving toward a romantic ideal of unadorned sincerity. It repeatedly shows that **form has to be made legible to an audience**.

The visual register supports this. Tendo's professional self is highly constructed: makeup, blocking, cameras, production instructions. None of that tells us that the affection she later expresses is false.

Before the confession attempt, Momoka asks whether Subaru really intends to tell Tendo. Nina reports that disclosure may mean leaving the acting school, returning home temporarily, and otherwise materially disrupting Subaru's life while she still wants to continue the band.

Momoka answers:

> `じゃあ 無理に言わなくていいよ`

This is stronger than passive nonintervention. Momoka explicitly says disclosure does not need to be forced when its consequences are that severe.

For her reconstruction, this is a valuable boundary counterexample. She can recognize that `truth now` is not automatically the morally or practically superior action.

### Tendo's independent account

After filming, Tendo tells Nina and Momoka that acting with Subaru has been a long-held dream:

> `ずっと夢だった`
>
> `あの子と同じ作品に出るのがね`

She also confirms that she wants Subaru to become an actress.

Then E04 gives her enough independent history to block a villain reading.

Tendo says her own daughter hated her profession because Tendo was rarely home, behaved importantly, and could not handle ordinary domestic life. Subaru was different:

> `だから嬉しかったな すばるが私の仕事を好きって言ってくれた時は`
>
> `あの子だけは笑ってくれたの`
>
> `私の仕事 あの子だけが好きだって`
>
> `やってみたいって`
>
> `感謝してる`

The program loudness in this sequence is among the quietest of the measured E04 windows, approximately -24.6 LUFS. That number does not prove intimacy by itself, but it corroborates the audiovisual contrast: the episode lowers energy while Tendo supplies the family history that makes the coercive structure emotionally intelligible.

This is major evidence for `GBC-V1-C002`.

Pressure here is not delivered through obvious cruelty. It emerges through:

- gratitude;
- intergenerational hurt;
- pride;
- a dream of continuity;
- professional opportunity;
- genuine love.

Tendo is still exercising substantial influence over Subaru's life. The fact that the motive is sincere does not remove the coercive effect.

Conversely, the coercive effect does not prove malicious intention.

That distinction is central to the series' ethics.

### The meaning of `作った笑顔`

Subaru earlier contrasted Tendo's `本当に笑う` with `作った笑顔`.

E04 does not tell us that the professional smile is a lie. Tendo's own disclosure suggests that the public performer and the private grandmother are not separate moral persons. The better interpretation is that some expressions are more regulated, role-conditioned, or audience-managed than others.

This is exactly the distinction V2 now needs for Subaru as well.

`constructed` describes **form**, not automatically **falsity**.

## 4.11 19:36-21:58 - Nina stops the confession, overlearns nuance, and Subaru turns acting into self-authored catharsis

Subaru approaches Tendo and begins the conversation she has been avoiding.

Her register is careful and deferential:

> `おばあさま`
>
> `あの ちょっと話があるのですが`

Tendo is about to leave for the train. Subaru acknowledges that the timing is bad, then commits anyway:

> `じゃあ言うね`
>
> `あの 私ね…`

Before she can finish, Nina interrupts.

She invents an emergency:

> `すばるちゃん 大変`
>
> `忘れ物だよ すぐ行かなきゃ`

and physically redirects Subaru away.

This is a remarkable reversal.

Earlier Nina had insisted:

> `女優になる気がないなら 早く言ったほうがいい`

Now, after hearing Tendo's history, Nina prevents the disclosure she demanded.

The reversal is developmental evidence, but not simple maturation.

Nina has learned a new truth very quickly: a relationship can contain circumstances that make immediate honesty ethically complicated.

But she has not yet learned a second truth: **recognizing complexity does not give her jurisdiction over Subaru's timing.**

That distinction becomes explicit in the following argument.

### Direct repair survives a less careful conflict

Subaru confronts her:

> `言動不一致も甚だしい`

Nina answers:

> `ごめんね`

This matters because E03's apology occurred under unusually favorable conditions: Subaru named behavior, offered an exit, and reduced asymmetry. E04 shows that Nina's repair capacity is not limited to that exact setup. She can apologize promptly even while the conflict remains active and while Subaru is openly angry.

This strengthens the direct-repair branch.

But the apology is not surrender.

When Subaru reminds Nina that Nina herself condemned lying and demanded early disclosure, Nina agrees:

> `それはそう`

Then immediately defends the reversal:

> `ただ 人にはそれぞれ事情があるんだよ`
>
> `簡単じゃないんだよ`
>
> `そういうところに気を遣えるようにならなきゃ`
>
> `でしょ`

The irony is deliberate. Nina is reciting a version of the lesson Momoka taught her only hours earlier.

Subaru's response is physical and verbal:

> `どの口が言う`

She pinches Nina's cheeks, then snaps:

> `んなこと ニーナと違って`
>
> `こっちはとっくに分かってたっつうの`

Nina has not become nuanced in a stable, mature sense. She has acquired a new principle and immediately universalized it.

This produces an important reconstruction rule:

> **NINA-E04-R03 — Nina can update rapidly when emotionally compelling evidence breaks one of her rules, but principle acquisition can outrun jurisdictional restraint: she may convert the new nuance into another categorical prescription for what someone else should do.**

This is development without smoothness.

### Subaru's own timing criterion

Subaru is still clear that permanent concealment is not the goal:

> `でも いつかは言わなきゃダメ`

Asked when, she answers:

> `やりたいことがちゃんと見つかったら…`

This is one of E04's most important lines.

Subaru's problem is not merely finding the courage to say `I do not want acting`. She wants enough positive self-definition to say what she **does** want.

That directly links her family conflict to the larger early-series structure.

Nina began with refusal before positive aspiration.

Momoka began with a manufactured ending after positive aspiration collapsed.

Subaru has maintained a hidden refusal while waiting for a positive alternative strong enough to bear the consequences of disclosure.

Her E04 `本気で好きかも` makes the band a candidate for that positive alternative, but the episode stops before treating the choice as finalized.

### The performed rant

Subaru then launches into what appears to be a genuine emotional eruption:

> `バンドやめるなって言われたから 覚悟決めたのに`
>
> `直前になって 手のひら返す正論モンスターも`
>
> `それを訳知り顔で見てるやつも`
>
> `私のことは私が決める`
>
> `ほっとけよ`

The line `私のことは私が決める` is the episode's clearest jurisdictional statement.

Nina has tried to decide when Subaru should disclose.

Tendo has implicitly decided what Subaru should become.

Momoka has watched, advised, and sometimes pushed, but in this episode ultimately recognizes the boundary.

Subaru claims the decision itself.

Then she reveals:

> `どう 私の演技`
>
> `すっきりした`

This is not evidence that the grievances were unreal. Their referents are exact and episode-local. The performance gives Subaru control over **how far the grievance commits the relationship**.

She can say the harsh thing, discharge affect, and then re-enter ordinary affiliation:

> `まあ いいよ`
>
> `いずれ自分で何とかしなきゃって思ってたから`
>
> `だからそれまでは`
>
> `ここであんたらのケツを蹴り上げてやる`

The function of acting has now been inverted from the inherited problem.

Tendo's world trains Subaru to perform because a professional identity has been selected for her.

Subaru uses performance here because **she** selects it as the best tool for expressing anger without automatically detonating the relationship.

That strongly strengthens `GBC-V1-C036`.

It also sharpens `GBC-V1-C008` into a practical rule:

> **Authenticity in E04 is not absence of role. It is preservation of recognizable intention and agency through a chosen form.**

### Trio durability

The final argument is staged in the rehearsal room with the members occupying musical positions: Subaru at the drums, Nina around the vocal space, Momoka with guitar. V2 should resist turning that blocking into proof that `conflict becomes composition`; no song is shown converting this exact dispute into arrangement.

But the location matters at a weaker level.

They are already fighting **inside** the shared practice rather than treating conflict as automatic evidence that the practice must end.

Subaru's final commitment is not `you are right`. It is effectively: I am angry, this is my decision, and I am staying for now.

That is meaningful support for `GBC-V1-C010`: found-family stability begins not in harmony but in the capacity for judgment and grievance without immediate departure.

# 5. Character reconstruction observations

## 5.1 Nina Iseri

E04 adds several important corrections to any simple `Nina = brutally honest` model.

### 5.1.1 Chronic monitoring precedes explosive honesty

Nina's opening reflection is direct evidence that ordinary speech often feels inhibited:

- she watches other people's faces;
- something feels caught in her throat;
- the E03 live was unusual because she could shout what she was thinking with full force.

Her confrontational surface should therefore be modeled as **thresholded release against a high baseline of monitoring**, not constant absence of restraint.

### 5.1.2 Attachment continuity is already a high-stakes trigger

The trio matters enough that Subaru's abrupt departure message and later one-week acting absence are interpreted through possible permanent loss.

This is not merely possessiveness. The evidence supports:

- strong commitment to declarations;
- fear that sincere bonds can still end;
- low tolerance for ambiguous withdrawal;
- a tendency to pursue when waiting feels like allowing irreversibility.

The stronger claim that Nina globally wants to own or monopolize people remains too broad at E04.

### 5.1.3 Nina's care can become control

E02-E03 mostly emphasized how Nina experiences other people's approaches as attempted scripting.

E04 demonstrates the inverse.

When she fears losing Subaru, Nina pressures Momoka to intervene, resists Subaru's request to be trusted, and later physically interrupts Subaru's attempted confession.

Nina is therefore not only vulnerable to imposed narratives. She can impose a preferred path when she believes the relationship is endangered.

This materially strengthens and revises `GBC-V1-C019`.

### 5.1.4 Direct repair is now a repeatable capacity

E03 established `ごめんなさい` under favorable conditions.

E04 supplies a rougher test. Subaru angrily calls Nina's contradiction outrageous, and Nina answers `ごめんね` promptly before continuing the argument.

This suggests that apology is becoming an accessible behavior rather than a one-scene exception.

Important limitation: Nina can apologize for the hurt or contradiction while still believing the action she took was justified. Repair does not require complete retraction.

### 5.1.5 She learns fast and overapplies

The movement from `tell her now` to `everyone has circumstances` happens within one episode.

That flexibility contradicts a model of Nina as simply incapable of nuance.

But the speed itself creates a new problem: the newly learned qualification becomes another rule she uses to direct Subaru.

Her development therefore often looks like:

1. hold a categorical principle;
2. encounter emotionally undeniable counterevidence;
3. revise rapidly;
4. overgeneralize the revision;
5. require further relational correction.

That is a much more useful simulation rule than `Nina slowly becomes less stubborn`.

### 5.1.6 Positive music desire is strengthening

E03 established positive musical desire through voluntary labor.

E04 adds:

- fascination with guitar;
- desire to be able to play like Momoka;
- strong language of `this trio` as a binding project;
- emotional alarm at losing a member.

University remains present, but music can no longer be modeled as only a vehicle for proving others wrong or remaining near Momoka.

---

## 5.2 Momoka Kawaragi

E04 is particularly important because it falsifies an overbroad version of Momoka's intervention rule.

### 5.2.1 Craft after catharsis

Momoka treats the band as work immediately after E03's live breakthrough. She stops takes, asks for more nuance, rerecords drums, and rejects the merely obvious.

For reconstruction, this supports a strong split between:

- validating emotional material;
- accepting the first expression of that material as finished craft.

Momoka can believe Nina's feeling is real and still say the performance needs revision.

### 5.2.2 She can defend privacy against pursuit

When Nina wants to force access to Subaru's family problem, Momoka says people have circumstances others should not touch and reminds Nina that Nina herself has protected history.

At the film set, Momoka goes further:

> `じゃあ 無理に言わなくていいよ`

This makes it untenable to model her as someone who invariably believes painful truth should be forced into the open.

Her pressure is conditional and domain-sensitive.

### 5.2.3 Diamond Dust remains affectively alive without becoming a current program

Momoka recounts the original group with language of fate and miracle. Her former joy is visually documented. She also says she hopes the current group succeeds and denies resentment.

E04 therefore establishes:

- old Diamond Dust mattered profoundly;
- Momoka can remember it warmly;
- the split remains unresolved for the viewer;
- present envy/reunion/destruction motives are not yet established.

### 5.2.4 Humor remains a temperature tool

Her implausible romance explanation for Subaru's exit, teasing about leadership, and other light interventions keep high-affect scenes from becoming uniformly solemn.

That function from E01-E03 remains stable.

---

## 5.3 Subaru Awa

E04 is the first episode that makes a substantial reconstruction model of Subaru possible.

### 5.3.1 Performance is an inherited environment

Tendo's `姿勢` correction demonstrates that acting reaches into Subaru's ordinary body. Speech, bearing, health, and conduct are all treated as professional material.

Subaru's competence is therefore trained, not mysterious.

### 5.3.2 Competence and identification separate decisively

Subaru can perform well, manage social surfaces, improvise, and understand acting technique while explicitly saying:

> `そもそも 芝居って好きじゃない`

Skill does not imply vocation.

### 5.3.3 Code-switching is tactical and rapid

Public `ニーナさん` smoothing and private `人の話聞けこら` coexist within seconds.

A future voice model must therefore be audience-sensitive rather than assigning Subaru a single `friendly` or `rough` register.

### 5.3.4 Information control is her high-threat weakness

The false quitting message shows that under threat Subaru may manipulate information even with allies. Her logic is compartmentalization: if the hidden life is endangered, preserve the boundary first and repair the confused allies later.

This can be highly effective socially and still produce unnecessary relational damage.

### 5.3.5 Acting is becoming self-authored technology

The etude helps Nina speak a real grievance.

The final performed rant helps Subaru speak real grievances while controlling whether they constitute a rupture.

Acting is therefore both:

- inherited burden;
- social competence;
- camouflage;
- mediation;
- emotional container;
- potential autonomous tool.

That multi-function model is preferable to `Subaru acts because she is fake` or `Subaru acts because acting is secretly her true calling`.

### 5.3.6 Positive band choice emerges

`私 意外とこれ本気で好きかもって` is the strongest direct evidence yet that band participation is becoming positively chosen.

The `かも` keeps the state provisional.

She has not solved the family problem or announced music as a career. But the band is no longer only rebellion against acting.

### 5.3.7 Disclosure threshold depends on having an affirmative alternative

Subaru says she will need to tell Tendo when she has properly found what she wants to do.

This suggests that she does not want to replace one void with another. A negative `I refuse acting` is psychologically insufficient; she wants a positive self-authored direction capable of surviving the loss of her grandmother's script.

---

## 5.4 Awa Tendo - supporting model only

E04 gives Tendo enough evidence to prevent caricature, not enough for a mature monograph.

Secure observations:

- she is highly attentive to professional discipline;
- she treats ordinary bearing as part of acting craft;
- she has substantial influence over Subaru's opportunities and expectations;
- she named Subaru after a role tied to her own career history;
- she sincerely wants to act with Subaru and wants her to become an actress;
- her daughter rejected important parts of Tendo's professional life;
- Subaru's apparent admiration therefore carries unusually high emotional significance;
- she expresses gratitude for Subaru's childhood affirmation.

Open:

- how knowingly coercive Tendo understands her behavior to be;
- how she would respond to a direct refusal;
- whether Subaru's prediction that she would `crush` the refusal is fully accurate;
- whether Tendo could eventually distinguish continuity of love from continuity of profession.

The episode deliberately gives the audience more sympathy for Tendo without removing Subaru's autonomy problem.

# 7. Speech and voice delta

E04 substantially expands the reconstruction value of partner-specific register.

## 7.1 Nina

Nina's Japanese continues to resist a simple `emotion -> dialect/roughness` mapping.

With Momoka and Subaru she can remain structurally polite or neutral while making aggressive accusations:

- `いいんですか？`
- `桃香さんが思いやりがないからです`
- `ごめんね`
- `人にはそれぞれ事情があるんだよ`

The episode's strongest new speech evidence is instead **metalinguistic**: Nina tells us ordinary speech often involves monitoring faces and speaking around something caught in her throat.

That suggests a voice model with at least three layers:

1. **regulated ordinary social speech** - high monitoring, frequent politeness, inhibition;
2. **argumentative principle speech** - comparatively fluent, declarative, often categorical, not necessarily dialectal;
3. **release states** - live singing, self-talk, street confrontation, or other moments where regulation drops sharply.

E04 contains no major new regional-dialect event strong enough to change the E01-E03 model. That negative evidence matters: high affect with Subaru and Momoka does not automatically produce Kumamoto/Kyushu-coded forms.

## 7.2 Momoka

Momoka remains clipped, colloquial, and practical. E04 adds an important softer authority register.

When warning Nina about privacy, she does not need maximal aggression:

> `人には触れられたくない事情ってのがあるんだよ`

The sentence has the structure of an adult rule offered from experience rather than a threat.

Likewise `じゃあ 無理に言わなくていいよ` is concise but permissive. Momoka's rough/casual speech therefore cannot be equated with coerciveness. Function depends on what the utterance grants or demands.

Her production register is also increasingly distinct: `ストップ`, requests for dynamics, rerecording instructions, and schedule management. Later monographs should separate:

- ordinary casual Momoka;
- teasing Momoka;
- threat/confrontation Momoka;
- producer/arranger Momoka;
- vulnerable autobiographical Momoka.

## 7.3 Subaru

E04 is the first major register-mapping episode for Subaru.

### Grandmother-facing / public mode

Features include:

- `おばあさま`;
- immediate `はい` responses;
- polite introductions;
- posture control;
- socially graceful redirection;
- high audience awareness.

### Peer-private mode

Features include:

- `人の話聞けこら`;
- `やかましい`;
- `どの口が言う`;
- `こっちはとっくに分かってたっつうの`;
- `ほっとけよ`;
- `あんたらのケツを蹴り上げてやる`.

The difference is not simply `fake polite Subaru` versus `real rude Subaru`.

Subaru demonstrably enjoys performance craft enough to use it voluntarily, and her rough peer register itself can be theatrical. The key variable is **audience + purpose + relational safety**.

### Acting reveal as prosodic event

The final rant is especially important because the apparent emotional outburst resolves into:

> `どう 私の演技`
>
> `すっきりした`

The words establish that the performance was knowingly framed. The final-mix levels do not show a simple binary between `acted` and `real`; the grievance sequence is one of the louder dialogue windows of the episode, approximately -19.1 LUFS integrated across the attempted confession/final argument block.

Again, loudness is not sincerity. What matters is that performed structure can carry high affect without destroying the relationship.

## 7.4 Tendo

Tendo's speech is controlled and socially polished. She can issue strong correction (`姿勢`) with minimal lexical force. She also narrates family pain calmly.

E04 therefore gives a useful institutional contrast with Nina:

- Nina often increases explicitness when a conviction must survive pressure;
- Tendo can exert substantial normative force through low-temperature authority.

Do not confuse calmness with low impact.

# 8. Relationship delta

## 8.1 Nina <-> Subaru

E04 converts the dyad from repaired acquaintanceship into a relationship capable of carrying much more conflict.

Key transitions:

- Nina treats Subaru's quitting message as a serious relational injury.
- Acting gives Nina a way to confess that injury indirectly.
- Subaru reveals family history and the positive emergence of band attachment.
- Nina intrudes on Subaru's decision timing.
- Subaru confronts her directly rather than withdrawing.
- Nina apologizes.
- Subaru performs an angry rant, reveals the frame, and recommits provisionally.

The important development is not agreement. It is **increased conflict capacity**.

E02 Nina responded to social discomfort by leaving.

E04 Nina and Subaru can accuse, pinch cheeks, call one another out, and remain inside the relationship.

The dyad is still asymmetrical in some ways: Nina urgently wants continuity; Subaru is more skilled at managing social surfaces. But mutual recognition is increasing.

## 8.2 Subaru <-> Tendo

This relationship should be modeled as a genuine attachment with an autonomy conflict, not as simple oppressor/victim.

Tendo's side:

- pride in Subaru;
- gratitude for childhood affirmation;
- dream of performing together;
- strong professional expectations;
- identity continuity written even into Subaru's name.

Subaru's side:

- love and concern for Tendo's genuine smile;
- fear of direct refusal;
- resentment of inherited status;
- dislike of acting as a vocation;
- strategic compliance;
- plan for eventual disclosure.

The conflict is difficult precisely because both affection and coercive structure are real.

## 8.3 Nina <-> Momoka

E04 shifts their relationship away from simple mentor/pupil alignment.

Nina:

- assigns Momoka leadership responsibility without permission;
- accuses her of lacking consideration;
- pushes against Momoka's nonintervention stance.

Momoka:

- does not retaliate with relationship threat;
- gives Nina an analogy to Nina's own protected history;
- continues working the session;
- later supports non-forced disclosure.

This is early evidence that Nina can contest Momoka's judgment without the dyad automatically becoming existentially unstable.

It also means Momoka's authority is already negotiable even before detailed musical-authority conflict emerges.

## 8.4 Trio

E04 is the first episode where the trio starts behaving like a group that can absorb a member-specific external problem.

They do not solve Subaru's family conflict.

They do:

- pursue the rupture;
- discover the source;
- fight about how much intervention is appropriate;
- adjust rehearsal around a temporary absence;
- witness/participate in disclosure decisions;
- return to the rehearsal room still together.

That strongly supports an early version of `GBC-V1-C010`: durable collectivity is beginning to mean **surviving judgment without automatic exit**.

The final status remains provisional because Subaru's family problem is unresolved and the trio has existed for only a short time.

# 9. Performance, acting, and music

E04 is deliberately lighter on major diegetic musical climax than E03. That absence shifts the performance question into acting and recording.

## 9.1 Recording is performance under revision

The trio's music is now replayable and correctable. Momoka can stop Nina, ask for more chorus dynamics, and ask Subaru to rerecord.

This is important for the series' authenticity model:

> sincerity does not remove the need for craft.

A take can be emotionally real and artistically insufficient.

## 9.2 Acting becomes a parallel performance system

E04 places two performance institutions side by side:

**band**
- rehearsal;
- recording;
- stage presentation;
- makeup/costume when appropriate;
- emotional transmission through arranged form.

**acting**
- posture training;
- improvisation;
- makeup;
- role assignment;
- cameras and production schedule;
- emotional transmission through fictional form.

The episode refuses to declare one authentic and the other artificial.

The conflict is instead over **agency and ownership of performance**.

Subaru does not hate all acting behavior. She hates having acting installed as her destiny.

## 9.3 The etude as relational performance

The romantic scenario is a strong example of dramatic form doing relational work.

Its fictional premise creates enough displacement that Nina can voice real abandonment anxiety.

This supports a general V2 principle:

> **Mediation can increase communicability by changing the social cost of an otherwise intolerable statement.**

## 9.4 Subaru's rant as controlled catharsis

Subaru's final performance goes further.

She uses acting not simply to hide but to regulate commitment. A raw-seeming `ほっとけよ` can be uttered with full expressive force, then reframed as performance without becoming meaningless.

The act allows her to preserve both:

- the truth of the grievance;
- the continuity of the relationship.

This may become important later when evaluating `authenticity` across professionalization. E04 already rejects the idea that emotional truth requires spontaneous unmediated release.

## 9.5 `conflict becomes composition` remains open

E04 strengthens collective craft but does not cross the evidentiary threshold for the mature formal claim.

We see:

- recording revision;
- role differentiation;
- the trio fighting inside rehearsal space;
- continuation after disagreement.

We do **not** yet see a musical arrangement whose formal structure can be traced to incompatible interpersonal positions being preserved and transformed.

`GBC-V1-C015` remains `OPEN` at the E04 boundary.

# 11. Material and institutional analysis

E04 broadens the material world of *Girls Band Cry* beyond rent and band labor into professional acting infrastructure.

## 11.1 Band work has schedules and opportunity costs

Recording requires:

- repeated takes;
- time after other obligations;
- role-specific rerecording;
- accommodation for study and employment;
- adjustment when Subaru loses a week to filming.

The band is becoming a calendar of competing claims on limited time.

That matters because character choice cannot be read only at the level of desire. Nina can want music and still have university obligations. Subaru can want the band and still be captured temporarily by an acting job. Momoka can lead musical work and still have to coordinate around other people's constraints.

## 11.2 Acting is an institution, not merely an art form

Subaru's acting problem is carried by concrete structures:

- an acting school;
- family reputation;
- preferential treatment as Tendo's granddaughter;
- professional casting access;
- television production schedules;
- travel timing;
- a famous relative whose career creates both opportunity and expectation.

The phrase `七光り` is especially important. Subaru does not experience inherited prestige only as advantage. It contaminates feedback: if everyone treats her specially because of Tendo, external success cannot easily tell her whether the result belongs to Subaru herself.

This helps explain why `talent failure` once seemed attractive as an exit. An institutional negative verdict could have been harder for the family to reinterpret as adolescent rebellion.

## 11.3 Opportunity can be coercive without being materially bad

The drama role is objectively useful professional access. The episode does not claim the job is exploitative or artistically worthless.

The problem is that it advances a future Subaru has not chosen.

This distinction matters for `GBC-V1-C002` and the series' institutional ethics:

> **A materially valuable opportunity can still narrow autonomy when its meaning and trajectory are assigned in advance.**

The work itself need not be bad for the pressure to be real.

## 11.4 Family capital and artistic capital intertwine

Tendo's name, role history, and network are forms of artistic capital inherited by Subaru whether she wants them or not.

Subaru's counter-project - the band - is materially weaker but more self-authored.

E04 does not romanticize that asymmetry into `poor authentic band versus corrupt industry`. Momoka herself understands stage makeup, production, and professional discipline. The episode's question is narrower: **who gets to decide what the available infrastructure is for?**

# 12. Retrospective recontextualization

This section may use later-series knowledge only to identify what E04 becomes in hindsight. It does not rewrite the prospective body.

From the full-series perspective, E04 is an early articulation of several questions that later need repeated testing:

1. whether adaptation to a role necessarily constitutes surrender;
2. whether a person's refusal is meaningful before it has a positive alternative;
3. whether care permits intervention into someone else's decision;
4. whether performance can remain authentic after professional mediation;
5. whether the band can survive members whose reasons for staying are not initially identical.

The episode's most durable contribution is probably the jurisdiction question.

`私のことは私が決める` is not simply Subaru's version of Nina's rebellion. It clarifies a limit that Nina herself still violates in E04. Nina is correct that Subaru's concealment cannot continue forever. Subaru is correct that Nina does not own the timing of disclosure. Momoka is correct that the consequences make forced immediacy ethically suspect. Tendo's sincere affection ensures that none of these positions can be reduced to `truth versus lie`.

Later hindsight also makes Subaru's `本気で好きかも` especially important as a state transition. E03 gave the band as resistance. E04 gives the first explicit positive attraction. V2 should preserve the transition rather than back-projecting a mature band identity into Subaru's earlier behavior.

Likewise, E04's acting scenes matter later because they establish that Subaru already possesses a self-authored relation to performance before any mature synthesis is attempted. If later analysis concludes that acting functions as care, mediation, deception, autonomy, or professional competence, those later uses should be routed back through the multi-function baseline established here rather than treated as contradictions.

## 13.1 V1 E04 thesis adjudication

V1's central statement was:

> Telling the truth is not automatically the same thing as acting truthfully. A truth can become cruel when delivered without regard for timing, while a performance can provide the safest available form for saying something real.

V2 **STRENGTHENS but revises** this.

The phrase `a truth can become cruel` is directionally useful but slightly too moralized for the episode's actual evidence. Nina stopping the confession is not clearly proven correct; Subaru is angry precisely because Nina overrides her decision. The stronger V2 formulation is:

> **Truthfulness, timing, form, and jurisdiction are separate variables. A performed form can preserve real intention; a true disclosure can still be mistimed or belong to someone else to decide; understanding another person's circumstances does not automatically confer the right to intervene.**

That formulation better preserves the episode's contradictions.

# 15. Cumulative V2 delta through E04

The first four episodes now support a significantly different character architecture from the most convenient fandom shorthand.

## Nina through E04

Nina is not best modeled as `angry, blunt, honest, stubborn`.

The more predictive model is:

- highly monitored ordinary social behavior;
- strong politeness and upbringing residue;
- unusually high sensitivity to imposed or totalizing judgments;
- graded evaluative compression under perceived failure;
- existential totalization under shame/belonging threat;
- attachment-loss compression under threatened continuity;
- rapid recognition that can precede repair;
- growing direct-apology capacity;
- tendency to confuse relational consequence with jurisdiction when afraid;
- rapid principle revision followed by overapplication;
- positive musical desire emerging behaviorally before being consciously owned;
- thresholded release that can overshoot technical or social limits.

Her central early problem is therefore not `too much authenticity`.

It is difficulty preserving **agency, ambiguity, and proportionality simultaneously** when stakes become personally defining.

## Momoka through E04

Momoka is not best modeled as `laid-back rough older sister who pushes Nina toward music`.

The more predictive model is:

- casual/rough multi-function register;
- practical care through logistics;
- direct apology when she concedes misjudgment;
- teasing as temperature management;
- iteration-oriented approach to failure;
- strong belief in co-creation despite friction;
- willingness to intervene ahead of consent in domains where she believes fear obstructs a real desire;
- meaningful capacity for privacy/nonintrusion when consequences belong to another person's private history;
- producer/arranger standards that separate sincerity from finished craft;
- materially grounded realism;
- affectively alive but incompletely explained Diamond Dust history.

The central E01-E04 Momoka question becomes not `does she care or control?` but:

> **How does she decide which decisions are hers to push, which are jointly actionable, and which belong to someone else?**

## Subaru through E04

Subaru is not best modeled as `fake-friendly actress girl who becomes the mediator`.

The more predictive model is:

- highly trained audience awareness;
- rapid public/private register switching;
- real social competence without guaranteed identification with the role;
- inherited acting identity extending into name, body, school, and opportunity;
- strategic compartmentalization under threat;
- willingness to deceive allies to protect a boundary;

# 18. E04 governing conclusion

Episode 4 does not teach Nina that lying is good, Subaru that acting is secretly her destiny, or Momoka that intervention is wrong.

It teaches the trio something harder to compress into a slogan.

**Truth, sincerity, timing, performance, care, and decision-rights are not the same variable.**

Nina can tell a real grievance through fictional romance. Subaru can perform a real angry grievance and preserve the relationship by controlling its frame. Tendo can sincerely love Subaru while participating in a structure that narrows Subaru's authorship of her future. Momoka can be aggressive elsewhere and still recognize that some truths are not hers to force.

By the end, Nina has learned that circumstances matter - but she has not yet learned that understanding those circumstances does not make the decision hers. Subaru has learned that the band may be something she positively wants - but she has not yet turned that want into a direct refusal of the future chosen for her. Momoka has shown a meaningful limit to her own interventionism - but the conditions under which she honors that limit remain to be tested.

The trio therefore exits E04 more authentic not because they have become more unfiltered, but because their forms of expression are becoming **more self-authored, more legible to one another, and more capable of surviving disagreement without automatic departure**.

That is the state the E01-E04 checkpoint must now freeze.
