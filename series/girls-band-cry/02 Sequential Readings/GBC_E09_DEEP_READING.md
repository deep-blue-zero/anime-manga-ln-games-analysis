# Girls Band Cry E09 - V2 Deep Reading

## 1. Episode thesis

Episode 9 asks whether a group that has survived ideological conflict can survive a more ordinary and professionally necessary injury: being told that its work is not good enough.

E08 ended with a spectacular relational rupture and recommitment. Nina rejected being preserved as Momoka's lost past; Momoka returned to the band's future; Nina named her attachment `告白`. E09 deliberately lowers the temperature. There is no Diamond Dust confrontation, no moving vehicle, no public confession, and no live climax. The episode instead spends much of its running time on air conditioners, sleeping arrangements, a harmless snake, an acoustic guitar, skill gaps, old group messages, and the problem of saying what one actually thinks to people one wants to keep.

That quieter scale produces one of the strongest character-model revisions of the V2 pass so far.

At the E08 checkpoint, Tomo's mature V1 pathology remained deliberately unlicensed. The prospective evidence established that she was blunt, artistically exacting, time-conscious, and willing to leave conflict that consumed time without producing work. It also supplied counterevidence against prematurely treating every criticism as a total verdict: in E07 she could deliver bounded criticism, receive technical pushback, remain, and perform.

E09 now supplies the missing evidence in both directions.

First, Tomo really can convert present inadequacy into permanent judgment. When Nina asks for actionable criticism of her guitar, Tomo eventually says:

> `そんなままごとギター`
>
> `一生かかったってまともにならないわよ`
>
> E09 11:59.210-12:04.050

The technical premise is not fabricated. Nina is not ready for a professional stage. Subaru later agrees. Momoka's later response also does not tell Nina that the playing is stage-ready. Tomo's error is therefore not `she notices a flaw that does not exist`. It is the conversion:

> **present skill gap -> permanent incapacity.**

The `一生` is the diagnostic word. Tomo turns a developmental state into a life sentence.

Second, the episode directly establishes why she has also learned to suppress criticism. The cold open asks Tomo what she thinks of a song Momoka herself calls `ありきたり`. Before Tomo answers, the audiovisual track inserts the remembered messages from her earlier band:

> `センセーの自己満には私 付き合ってらんない`
>
> `やめる`
>
> `ならわたしも`
>
> E09 00:53.960-00:59.760

Tomo then returns to the present and says:

> `いいんじゃないの 別に`
>
> E09 01:04.220-01:06.060

After the OP, Rupa asks why Tomo withheld the criticism she had previously expressed. Tomo again answers that she thought the song was fine.

Later, Rupa gives the causal history with appropriate attribution rather than narrator certainty. Tomo had formed a prior band with musicians who said they wanted to become professionals. Because she believed they shared that seriousness, her demands grew stronger. The group broke apart. Later professional opportunities likewise failed after collisions. Rupa concludes:

> `思ったことを口にするのが怖くなってるんだと思います`
>
> `自分の本気をぶつけると`
>
> `ダメになっちゃうんじゃないかって`
>
> E09 17:49.180-17:57.770

This is still Rupa's interpretation, signaled by `と思います`. But the episode's construction makes it unusually strong: the cold open independently shows criticism being interrupted by remembered collaborator loss, and the ending shows the reverse process.

At 21:50, after Nina has continued practicing and after Rupa has brought Tomo into contact with that continued effort, Tomo finally addresses Momoka directly:

> `桃香`
>
> `この曲`
>
> `今のままだとつまんないと思うんだけど`
>
> E09 21:50.220-21:56.390

Momoka answers:

> `だよな`
>
> E09 22:05.400-22:06.190

The episode therefore begins and ends with almost the same compositional judgment. At the beginning Tomo cannot say it. At the end she can.

The crucial change is not that Tomo becomes nicer, lowers her standards, or discovers that everyone's effort is equally good. Nina remains a weak guitarist. Tomo remains capable of calling her `ヘタクソ`. What changes is the relational meaning of criticism.

The first `一生` judgment tries to close development. The later `ヘタクソ` is delivered into an interaction in which Nina answers:

> `絶対うまくなってやる`
>
> E09 21:12.140-21:14.050

and Tomo answers again:

> `ヘタクソ`
>
> E09 21:21.390-21:22.150

Neither leaves. Rupa says:

> `私 こういうの大好きなんです`
>
> E09 21:27.440-21:30.450

The same harsh surface has moved from terminal forecast toward continuing contact.

Nina's role is equally important. She does not ask Tomo to reassure her. She asks because Tomo can hear what is wrong. Her reason for learning guitar is explicitly relational and collective:

> `桃香さんに頼るんじゃなく`
>
> `力になって 一緒にやっていきたいって`
>
> `もちろん みんなともだけど`
>
> E09 11:35.640-11:44.530

After Tomo's annihilating judgment Nina does temporarily lose motivation. She tells Subaru:

> `やる気 なくなった`
>
> E09 14:14.090-14:15.550

But she does not convert criticism into abandonment. She seeks another evaluation, learns that Subaru substantially agrees with the present technical verdict, asks Momoka directly whether she is stage-ready, receives a skeptical `本気か`, and continues practicing anyway.

That is a more important test than a scene in which Tomo simply apologizes. The episode does not resolve the conflict by deciding that Tomo was technically wrong. It distinguishes three propositions:

1. Nina is not yet at the professional level she wants.
2. Tomo's permanent forecast exceeds the available evidence.
3. A relationship can survive the delivery and rejection of a harsh judgment without requiring either side to pretend the present skill gap is unreal.

Rupa is the member who keeps those propositions from collapsing into one another. She objects to Tomo's universalization:

> `智の物差しで話さないでよ`
>
> E09 12:29.320-12:31.280

but she does not argue that standards are unnecessary. Later she explains that when she saw the original trio perform, what she recognized was not superior technique:

> `この人たち 本気だ`
>
> `うまさとか 内容とか関係ない`
>
> `ただ 本気でステージに立ってるって`
>
> E09 20:32.510-20:41.560

She then asks Tomo whether that seriousness is ultimately what Tomo requires:

> `智ちゃんが求めるものも それだけ`
>
> `ですよね`
>
> E09 20:44.480-20:49.320

The source does not make Rupa omniscient. The phrasing remains question-shaped and earlier interpretation remains `と思います`. Her intervention is better modeled as **re-specifying the criterion**: present excellence and seriousness are not identical variables.

E09's governing formulation is therefore:

> **A serious creative group is not one in which criticism disappears. It is one in which `bad` stops meaning `there is no future`, and truth stops being treated as a weapon that inevitably destroys belonging. Tomo's standards remain real; Nina's current inadequacy remains real; the developmental advance is that criticism, resistance, practice, and continued participation can occupy the same relationship.**

The episode's final compositional exchange adds a second formulation:

> **E09 does not yet prove that interpersonal conflict has become musical structure. It proves something one step earlier and newly necessary: conflict has become compositional permission. Tomo can again tell Momoka that the song is boring, and Momoka can answer `だよな` without the band breaking.**
