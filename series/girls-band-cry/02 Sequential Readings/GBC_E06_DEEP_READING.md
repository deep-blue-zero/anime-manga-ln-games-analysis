# Girls Band Cry E06 - V2 Deep Reading

## 1. Episode thesis

Episode 6 asks what happens when desire acquires a metric.

E05 left Nina with a more adult but more difficult attachment to Momoka. She had learned that the person whose music helped sustain her was neither a spotless martyr nor a simple loser: Momoka protected one form of artistic integrity by breaking Diamond Dust's central relational promise. Nina nevertheless chose her. That deidealization could have made the relationship less dependent on external vindication. E06 instead shows that the need for vindication has not disappeared. It has changed form.

Nina now wants a **goal**.

She no longer needs Momoka to say that current Diamond Dust is false. She wants the world to produce a result that will prove Momoka's music did not lose. The desired verdict begins as `ダイヤモンドダストに勝ちたい`, then attaches itself to follower counts, professionalization, audience acquisition, Budokan, agency attention, and a festival where Diamond Dust itself will appear.

The episode's first major proposition is therefore:

> **Nina can survive the collapse of a morally pure idol without surrendering the need for a public proof that refusal mattered. E06 converts that need from argument into program: if rightness cannot be certified by Momoka's confession, perhaps it can be certified by success.**

That program immediately contaminates the categories Nina had used to judge Diamond Dust. Once the Serbian Night owner tells her that contemporary professionalization depends on numbers, Nina does not merely understand the lesson. She operationalizes it. She proposes cuter presentation, Subaru-centered social-media promotion, and even school-uniform branding. Subaru points out the contradiction directly:

> `それって今のダイヤモンドダストと同じなんじゃないの`
>
> E06 10:52.100-10:55.440

Nina's answer is not denial:

> `わかってる`
>
> E06 10:55.980-10:56.730

This is one of E06's most useful character-model results. Nina's recurrent problem is not simple lack of insight. She can recognize contradiction in real time and continue anyway because the motive on the other side remains stronger:

> `わかってます`
>
> `でも やっぱり私 負けたくないんです`
>
> E06 11:31.640-11:36.560

The model after E06 must therefore separate **recognition** from **regulation**. Nina often understands more than her behavior makes it look as though she understands. The failure lies in weighting, calibration, and stopping conditions.

Tomo and Rupa enter from the opposite side of the same problem.

Tomo already has a metric. A wall declaration says `目指せ！武道館!!`. She composes difficult melodic material, has built a substantial online audience with Rupa, and has received industry interest. Yet when Rupa proposes finding a vocalist who can sing Tomo's difficult melodic line, Tomo refuses. Rupa does not call the refusal cowardly. Instead she accepts it with unnerving literalness: if avoiding loss and pain matters more, then they can stay as two people and keep making songs. She only asks Tomo to remove the Budokan declaration because keeping the dream while refusing the risks required by it is `滑稽です`.

Rupa's first substantial V2 formulation is consequently:

> **Safety is a legitimate choice. What Rupa attacks is not safety but an unacknowledged contradiction between stated ambition and accepted risk.**

This makes Tomo a particularly productive counterpoint to Nina. Nina can convert an unresolved wound into overrapid commitment. Tomo can preserve an explicit ambition while using refusal to keep the relational conditions of pursuit from changing. One moves too quickly from desire into total program; the other risks turning desire into a protected sign that does not have to encounter enough reality to fail.

Subaru occupies a third position. E04 established acting and social construction as tools that can preserve sincerity when self-authored. E06 applies that insight to band formation. Subaru tells Nina that charging at Momoka like a bull does not create conditions in which Momoka will disclose anything. Later she deliberately sequences information: let Momoka hear Tomo and Rupa, let her judge their musicianship, let the five-person sound exist, and only then reveal that the pair want to join and that their ambitions are larger than a one-off session.

This is manipulation in the neutral sense that Subaru alters the conditions of a decision. The episode does not treat all such alteration as equivalent. Subaru's plan does not invent musical compatibility; it exposes Momoka to evidence she can evaluate directly before the symbolic burden of `professional ambition` can trigger refusal. Yet the method also withholds information that Momoka would reasonably consider relevant. E06 therefore keeps the series' authenticity problem difficult:

> **A conversational architecture can make truth encounterable without being identical to full immediate disclosure. Whether that architecture preserves or compromises agency depends on what is withheld, why it is withheld, and whether the person can still refuse once the relevant facts arrive.**

Momoka supplies the episode's most important behavioral test of Subaru's hypothesis. Subaru says there are, metaphorically, `two Momokas`: the person who says she has left competition, and the musician who cannot resist trying to make good music better. V2 must not convert Subaru's interpretation into omniscient narration. But the episode does provide behavioral support for part of it. Momoka says she has no goal. She rejects Nina's attempt to make Diamond Dust her competitive target. Yet when she hears Tomo and Rupa's work, she immediately evaluates arrangement separation, rests, instrumental roles, and practical fit. Once the five play, the source does not show an emotionally disengaged retiree tolerating other people's project. It shows a working musician inside a materially richer ensemble.

The best E06 Momoka formulation is narrower than `she secretly wants the same goal Nina wants`:

> **Declared lack of a future target does not equal low artistic engagement. Momoka can reject an externally imposed victory condition while remaining highly responsive to concrete musical possibility.**

That distinction matters because the five-person band begins to exist musically **before** its members have agreed on what success should mean.

Tomo and Rupa want collaboration because they heard people who seemed to sing only what they wanted and who looked capable of `ちゃんと意見ぶつけられそう` - actually colliding opinions. Nina wants a route toward defeating Diamond Dust. Tomo's wall points to Budokan. Momoka says she has no goal. Subaru wants the band enough to engineer the meeting but does not yet supply a final career doctrine. Rupa openly says that no one can know whether people will work together: close friends can split, terrible compatibility can somehow endure.

Then they play.

Bass and keyboard enter the arrangement. Tomo and Rupa have prepared in advance. Momoka responds technically. Nina experiences the sound as a qualitative expansion. The visual layer surrounds the five with the series' subjective colored performance field. None of this proves that their incompatible goals have been reconciled. It proves something more modest and, at this stage, more important:

> **Musical fit can become evidence for continuing a relationship before the relationship has a shared theory of success.**

That is the episode's ensemble answer to Nina's demand for a goal. Goals matter; institutions make them material; metrics cannot simply be wished away. But a band is not reducible to the metric it adopts. The five-person sound creates a reason to continue negotiating what the metric is for.

E06's governing formulation is therefore:

> **Desire becomes dangerous when it requires a scoreboard to certify the past, but it becomes actionable when people accept the risks, institutions, and relationships required to test it. E06 does not give the new five a common ambition. It gives them a common musical object strong enough to make disagreement about ambition worth having.**

## 4.1 00:13-01:21 - Tomo's difficult melody and Rupa's refusal to flatten it

E06 opens away from the existing trio. Tomo and Rupa are working in a dark apartment around a computer-based composition setup.

Tomo complains:

> `ボカロでメロディー作ると 無駄に歌が難しくなるのよね`
>
> E06 00:42.240-00:46.540

Rupa does not answer by recommending that Tomo simplify the melodic writing. Instead she identifies the same difficulty as part of Tomo's artistic strength:

> `このメロディーラインは 智ちゃんのいいところだと思うから`
>
> E06 00:46.950-00:50.670

and proposes solving around the constraint:

> `これを歌えるボーカルを 探した方がいいかもしれません`
>
> E06 00:51.330-00:54.790

This is the first important Rupa datum. Her supportive behavior is not indiscriminate soothing. She distinguishes the collaborator from the collaborator's constraint. Tomo has written something difficult for a human singer; Rupa does not protect her by saying the problem does not exist. She protects the **artistic specificity** by proposing a change in ensemble architecture rather than a flattening of the work.

Tomo refuses:

> `嫌だって言ったでしょ`

Rupa's response is striking because it is formally polite and semantically severe:

> `そうですか`
>
> `では 仕方ないですね`
>
> `諦めて 今のまま２人で`
>
> `曲を作っていきましょう`
>
> E06 00:59.050-01:07.390

She then says she will take out the trash.

The scene matters because Rupa does not revoke Tomo's right to refuse. She accepts the refusal as a choice with consequences. If Tomo does not want a vocalist, they can remain a two-person composing unit. The pressure comes from **making the chosen future audible**.

Tomo stops her:

> `ルパ`
>
> `ルパは やってみたいの`
>
> E06 01:11.770-01:16.730

This gives the pair reciprocity immediately. Rupa's preference matters to Tomo. Tomo's initial `no` is not the final word once she realizes Rupa has a desire of her own. Rupa then shows her the trio's performance.

A source frame later in the sequence displays the `beni-shouga` profile with a follower count of **22,738**. The pair are therefore not beginning from zero visibility. Before they approach Nina, Momoka, and Subaru, they already have musical work, an audience, and a stalled structural problem.

The safest first Tomo/Rupa formulation is:

> **Tomo protects a creative form by resisting the relational expansion needed to realize it; Rupa protects Tomo's right to make that choice while refusing to pretend that the safer choice leaves the larger ambition unchanged.**

This is more evidence-bound than the V1 shorthand that Tomo simply `needs people capable of surviving her`. E06 has not yet shown the history required for that claim.

## 4.2 03:01-04:28 - Hoarseness, live proof, and the gap between intensity and sustainability

After the opening, the episode returns to the trio in a bath/sauna environment. The immediate concern is Nina's throat.

Momoka asks how it is. Nina says it has improved. Momoka rejects the self-assessment:

> `良くなってねえ`
>
> E06 03:15.060

and warns:

> `まったく ライブのたびにあれじゃ 持たないぞ`

Nina answers with a formulation that makes the recurrent embodiment problem explicit:

> `この痛みが心地よくなってこそ`
>
> `ライブの実感があるってもんよ`
>
> E06 03:36.540 onward

The distinction from E03 matters. E03 showed inhibition flipping into overcommitment until Nina became hoarse. E06 supplies the beginning of an **ideology of the consequence**. Nina treats pain not simply as accidental overuse but as evidence that the live was real enough.

V2 should still resist turning this into a generalized `Nina seeks bodily irreversibility` law. The evidence is specific to live singing and post-performance physical expenditure. But it strengthens a narrower rule:

> **Nina can treat unsustainable expenditure as proof of sincerity or event-reality, while Momoka's professional craft model treats sustainability as part of doing the work seriously.**

That difference is important because E06 will shortly shift from expressive intensity to professional ambition. Becoming professional does not merely mean wanting more stages. It means repeatedly producing without destroying the capacity to continue.

The bath scene also continues Subaru's playful performer framing. `プレアちゃん`, `プレアでーす`, and the insistence that `キャラは大事よ` keep the E04 performance model alive. A constructed public persona is not automatically a false self; it is one of the usable forms through which a performer manages exposure.

## 4.3 04:28-05:22 - Nina mistakes emotionally satisfying reactions for public momentum

Nina reads favorable online reactions to the previous live. The comments praise the old Diamond Dust, say Momoka was central, and compare the trio's performance favorably with the current group.

Nina generalizes:

> `とにかく お客さんみんな 前のダイダスの方が好きなんですよ`

The claim is immediately revealing. E05 ended with Nina making a personally real but more bounded choice: she still loves Momoka after learning Momoka's guilt. E06 shows how quickly that personal truth can be converted into a population claim.

Nina proposes a public declaration:

> `今のダイダスは まがい物です`
>
> `私たちこそが正しいから 応援してください`

Momoka answers with numbers rather than counter-rhetoric. Current Diamond Dust has gained roughly twenty thousand followers; the trio has roughly five hundred.

The corrective is not that follower count determines artistic value. It is that Nina has mistaken **selected approving testimony** for **collective movement**. The people whose comments satisfy her existing interpretation are real people, but they are not automatically representative of the audience landscape.

This matters for later reconstruction because Nina's cognition is again not random. She privileges evidence with high moral salience. A small number of comments saying `Momoka's music was the real one` carry enormous subjective weight because they answer the question Nina is already asking. Momoka counters with a different category of evidence: scale.

E06 therefore begins separating:

- depth of effect on an individual listener;
- prevalence of a reaction;
- audience size;
- commercial reach;
- artistic quality.

Nina initially wants these categories to converge into one verdict. The episode will repeatedly refuse that convergence.

## 4.5 06:11-07:09 - Subaru diagnoses the interactional system, and Nina finally states her personal target

Nina complains that Momoka will not say what she really thinks. Subaru does not first adjudicate Momoka's content. She diagnoses Nina's **method**:

> `ニーナがつっかかりすぎなの`
>
> `闘牛場の牛かって感じだもん`
>
> `ああ言われて 本心話すわけないって`

This is strong evidence for Subaru as an interactional analyst. She is not simply nicer than Nina. She recognizes that conversational conditions change what information becomes available.

Her alternative is:

> `そりゃ 一旦心開くような状況を作って`
>
> `そろりそろりと`

Nina labels the method:

> `嘘つき`

Subaru immediately rejects the equivalence:

> `それは嘘つきじゃない`

The E04 authenticity result therefore generalizes. For Subaru, indirectness can be a **truth-enabling architecture** rather than a falsification. This is not moral permission for any manipulation. The ethical question remains whether the architecture leaves the other person meaningful choice.

Then Subaru asks the more basic question Nina has been using Momoka to avoid:

> `ていうかさ それ以前にニーナはどうしたいの このバンド`

Nina answers:

> `ダイヤモンドダストに勝ちたい`
>
> E06 06:58.320

This is the first explicit personal band goal.

It is not yet the band's goal.

That distinction is essential. Nina has moved from E03's behavioral desire to E05's overt commitment and now to explicit ambition, but the ambition is still organized around an opponent and a verdict. She does not initially say `I want to make this kind of music`, `I want to keep these people together`, or `I want a sustainable career`. She wants **victory over a symbolic rival**.

The episode will spend the rest of its runtime translating that private target into institutions and exposing the costs of doing so.

# 5. Nina - E06 reconstruction update

## 5.1 Desire now has an explicit target, but the target is partly a proof apparatus

E03 established positive music desire through voluntary labor. E04 and E05 made stage desire increasingly overt. E06 removes the remaining ambiguity that Nina is only being carried by Momoka's project.

She researches professionalization on her own, seeks institutional information, argues for audience strategy, visits Budokan, wants Tomo and Rupa to join, and says directly:

> `私 やってみたいんです`

This is stronger than `Nina secretly wants music`.

But V2 should not replace one simplification with another and declare that Nina has now found a clean positive dream. Her ambition is organized by at least three simultaneous motives:

1. **positive musical attraction** - she likes performing and wants the richer band;
2. **relational attachment** - she wants Momoka's music and person to continue rather than disappear into resignation;
3. **retrospective proof** - she wants achievement to demonstrate that Momoka, and by extension Nina's own refusal, was `not wrong`.

These motives can reinforce each other, but they are not interchangeable.

The distinction will matter whenever a future practical choice benefits one motive while harming another.

## 5.2 External metrics are attractive because they promise closure

Nina's rapid attachment to followers, professionalization, Budokan, and beating Diamond Dust is not best modeled as sudden careerism.

The metric solves a psychological problem. E01-E05 repeatedly leave Nina in worlds where no single person has authority to issue the final verdict she wants:

- her family cannot simply erase her experience;
- Momoka cannot make Nina's reading of `Kara no Hako` objectively binding;
- Nina cannot own Diamond Dust because it mattered profoundly to her;
- Momoka will not declare current Diamond Dust illegitimate;
- Momoka's own departure is morally mixed rather than pure.

An external achievement appears to offer what argument cannot:

> `If we reach this stage, if we gain this audience, if we stand beside or above Diamond Dust, then reality itself will say we were not wrong.`

This is why Budokan is immediately translated into `間違ってなかった` rather than merely `we succeeded`.

A later Nina monograph should therefore track not only competitiveness but **verdict-seeking through objective-looking outcomes**.

## 5.3 `わかってる` / `わかってます` establishes insight-control separation

The strongest E06 cognitive datum is not that Nina contradicts herself. It is that she identifies the contradiction explicitly.

When Subaru says the new marketing logic resembles current Diamond Dust:

> `わかってる`

When Momoka says Nina is imposing a private dream on others:

> `わかってます`

Then:

> `でも やっぱり私 負けたくないんです`

The repeated pattern is:

`criticism -> recognition -> motive remains -> action pressure continues`

That is different from:

`criticism -> incomprehension -> repetition`

This distinction improves simulation fidelity. A plausible Nina response to a valid criticism may be genuine acknowledgment without immediate behavioral concession. She can know why a position is problematic and still refuse to let the relevant desire lose the decision.

### New rule candidate: `NINA-R15`

> **When Nina accepts an external criterion as relevant, she tends to operationalize it quickly and aggressively, even when doing so contaminates a prior moral position.**

Status: **NEW / strong E06 support; requires cross-episode replication.**

### New rule candidate: `NINA-R16`

> **Achievement can become retrospective proof: Nina may recruit a future goal to settle an unresolved past verdict about whether refusal was `wrong`.**

Status: **NEW / strong E06 support.**

### Revised rule: insight before regulation

> **Nina's self-awareness can substantially exceed her immediate self-control. `I know` should not be modeled as either empty or as automatic compliance; it often marks a real contradiction she is not yet willing or able to resolve in favor of restraint.**

Status: **STRENGTHEN / EXPAND from E02-E05.**

## 5.4 E06 is another fast-principle-overapplication episode

CP1-P08 receives unusually direct support.

Nina learns a valid descriptive fact:

> professional-scale opportunity is partly mediated by audience numbers.

Within a short interval she translates that into:

- follower maximization;
- cute presentation;
- Subaru image exploitation;
- school-uniform branding;
- professionalization as necessary route to victory.

The lesson is not false. The application outruns calibration.

This resembles E04, where Nina learned that `people have circumstances` and immediately used the new principle to prescribe Subaru's disclosure timing from the opposite direction.

Cross-episode pattern:

`new nuance -> rapid internalization -> categorical implementation -> later boundary correction`

This is more precise than `Nina is stubborn` because it predicts a specific failure mode even during genuine learning.

## 5.5 Nina's relationship to commercial compromise becomes consciously impure

E05 made Nina confront the fact that Diamond Dust's surviving members had a defensible strategy: accept temporary compromise, gain leverage, then recover artistic control.

E06 places Nina herself inside a small-scale version of that problem.

She is willing to consider presentation choices she associates with current Diamond Dust because she believes those choices may produce enough reach to defend the music she values.

The irony is not accidental. But the strongest formulation is not `Nina becomes a hypocrite`.

Hypocrisy would be too thin because she admits the resemblance. The more useful formulation is:

> **Nina is willing to accept tactical contamination once she believes purity would pre-emptively surrender the larger contest.**

What remains unknown is where she will draw a non-negotiable line once the compromises become larger than photos or costumes.

## 5.6 Bodily intensity remains a weakly regulated value signal

Nina's throat is still damaged enough that Momoka rejects Nina's claim that it is better. Nina's response treats pain as part of the lived proof of a live show.

This strengthens the narrow performance-sustainability rule:

> **When Nina experiences an expressive event as identity-validating, she may treat bodily cost as evidence of sufficient commitment rather than as neutral feedback requiring technique adjustment.**

Counterweight:

- she accepts Momoka's musician authority enough to remain in the conversation;
- E06 does not show deliberate self-injury outside singing;
- no general `risk-seeking` trait should be inferred from this alone.

## 5.7 High-affect politeness remains stable

E06 supplies another clean confirmation that anger does not mechanically erase Nina's formal morphology.

She says:

- `じゃあ どうしろって言うんですか`
- `私たちのバンドの目標って 何なんですか`
- `桃香さんの目標を聞いてるんです`
- `私が負けたくないんです`
- `わかってます`
- `でも やっぱり私 負けたくないんです`

The content is confrontational. The morphology remains polite.

By contrast, repetitive `ムカつく` appears as affective discharge, and casual peer exchanges with Subaru can be plain. There is little new regional-speech evidence.

The checkpoint's partner/state/familiarity model remains superior to any `dialect = anger` rule.

## 5.8 Nina can commit quickly to people when they create a route toward a legible direction

Nina is receptive to Tomo and Rupa unusually quickly once their proposal connects to several things she already wants:

- a stronger sound;
- bass and keyboard;
- an existing audience;
- industry experience;
- an explicit large goal.

This is not necessarily evidence of generalized social openness. The proposal is unusually aligned with the direction Nina has just decided matters.

### New rule candidate: `NINA-R18`

> **Nina can move rapidly toward collective commitment when new collaborators create a concrete route toward a goal she already experiences as morally or identity-relevant; speed of commitment may precede full consensus about what the collective goal actually is.**

Status: **NEW / provisional.**

Counterexample requirement: future evidence should distinguish quick commitment to goal-aligned collaborators from ordinary weak-tie social trust.

# 6. Momoka - E06 reconstruction update

## 6.1 `No goal` is not the same thing as `no artistic appetite`

Momoka's `別に 特にないよ` is among the episode's most important statements precisely because later behavior does not permit the viewer to treat it as the whole model.

V2 should not call the line false. There is no need to assume Momoka secretly possesses a fully formulated Budokan/professional goal that she refuses to admit.

Instead the episode distinguishes **future target** from **present artistic drive**.

Momoka can genuinely lack or resist a grand destination while still:

- evaluating arrangement quality;
- noticing part separation;
- noticing effective rests;
- identifying instrumental fit;
- agreeing to a trial;
- playing seriously once the ensemble exists.

### New rule candidate: `MOMOKA-R12`

> **Declared absence of a future goal does not imply low artistic engagement. Concrete craft quality and collaborator fit can activate Momoka immediately even when she resists abstract competitive ambition.**

Status: **NEW / strong E06 support.**

This is the evidence-bound core beneath Subaru's stronger `根っからのミュージシャン` interpretation.

## 6.2 Momoka rejects being conscripted into Nina's proof project

After E05, Momoka has already made the jurisdiction boundary around Diamond Dust explicit. E06 shows a related but distinct boundary.

Nina says `I don't want to lose`. Momoka answers that this is Nina's private matter and tells her not to dump it onto other people.

Momoka is not rejecting the band itself. She continues rehearsing, later tests Tomo/Rupa, and participates in the five-person jam. What she rejects is the transformation:

`Nina needs public vindication`

into

`therefore the band must define itself as a campaign to defeat Diamond Dust`.

### New rule candidate: `MOMOKA-R13`

> **Momoka resists being made the carrier of another person's external-proof project even when she remains committed to the underlying relationship or musical activity.**

Status: **NEW / strong E06 support.**

This is consistent with E04-E05 jurisdiction logic and should be tested against future collective-goal negotiations.

## 6.3 Technical speech remains compact and diagnostic

Momoka's evaluation of Tomo/Rupa is one of the clearest musician/professional registers so far.

She does not praise vaguely. She names:

- overall level;
- separation among upper parts;
- use of rests;
- primary instruments;
- practical suitability for a trial collaboration.

This speech is efficient, domain-specific, and action-oriented. A later voice model should distinguish this register from:

- domestic roughness;
- teasing;
- protective direction;
- genuine threat/boundary enforcement;
- emotionally vulnerable disclosure.

## 6.4 Roughness continues to be multifunctional

E06 provides another strong CP1-P11 confirmation.

Examples include:

- `良くなってねえ` - blunt care / technical correction;
- `だから？` - skeptical challenge;
- `めんどくさいな` - irritation/deflection;
- `蹴るぞ` - comic threat in teasing context;
- `知るか` - serious refusal of Nina's imposed competitive burden;
- `押っかぶせてくんな` - boundary enforcement.

The same rough grammatical/lexical surface cannot be mapped to one emotional state.

## 6.5 Subaru's `two Momokas` should remain a model, not an established split self

Subaru says:

> `多分 桃香さんは２人いるんだよ`

The metaphor is analytically productive but dangerous if naturalized too quickly.

What E06 directly supports:

- Momoka says she has no goal;
- she strongly resists Nina's Diamond Dust victory frame;
- she nevertheless evaluates Tomo/Rupa with professional seriousness;
- concrete ensemble quality draws active participation.

What E06 does **not** directly establish:

- that Momoka secretly has a conscious professional dream identical to Nina's;
- that she experiences two discrete internal selves;
- that all resistance to ambition is merely defensive falsehood;
- that good music will override any future ethical or practical boundary.

The current formulation should therefore be:

> **Subaru has identified a plausible discrepancy between Momoka's explicit future narrative and her immediate artistic behavior. E06 behaviorally supports the discrepancy without proving Subaru's full psychological explanation.**

## 6.6 E06 does not erase E05 guilt

Momoka's resistance to `winning` cannot be detached from E05. The previous episode established that professional competition is entangled with a band she left and a promise she broke.

But E06 should not simply use that history as an all-purpose explanation. The direct current evidence is that Momoka rejects **Nina's** imposed competitive frame. She may have multiple reasons:

- guilt;
- jurisdictional restraint;
- skepticism about metrics;
- resistance to repeating Diamond Dust structures;
- genuine preference for present-scale making;
- fear of renewed commitment.

The relative weights remain open.

# 7. Subaru - E06 reconstruction update

## 7.1 Subaru diagnoses process before content

The `bullring bull` metaphor is the clearest early statement of Subaru's interactional competence.

She tells Nina that charging directly at a person can make the desired truth less accessible. This is not merely a personality contrast where Subaru is `socially smart` and Nina is `socially dumb`.

Subaru is specifically modeling **feedback between method and response**:

`aggressive demand for disclosure -> counterpart closes -> demand becomes evidence that counterpart is hiding -> more pressure`

Her alternative is to create a condition in which openness is possible and then approach carefully.

### Rule: `SUBARU-R10`

> **Subaru often diagnoses interactional process - what a conversational method is doing to the other person's response - rather than limiting herself to which side is substantively correct.**

Status: **STRENGTHEN / strong E06 support.**

## 7.2 Strategic information sequencing expands beyond family-compartment threat

E04 showed Subaru controlling information under severe family pressure. CP1 correctly froze that narrow trigger.

E06 gives a new context. Subaru does not tell Momoka the whole Tomo/Rupa proposal immediately. She wants Momoka to hear the music first. The plan relies on a judgment that abstract knowledge about professional ambition may trigger refusal before Momoka encounters the relevant artistic fact.

This should **not** retroactively broaden CP1-P14. Instead it generates a new rule:

### `SUBARU-R11`

> **Subaru may strategically sequence relevant information outside acute compartment threat when she believes full initial framing would cause premature closure before the decision-maker encounters concrete evidence.**

Status: **NEW / E06-supported.**

Ethical boundary:

- E06 still lets Momoka receive the actual proposal;
- the tactic creates no irreversible commitment before disclosure;
- Momoka retains capacity to refuse.

Future episodes should test whether Subaru respects that distinction consistently.

## 7.3 Her psychological readings are useful hypotheses, not privileged access

`多分 桃香さんは２人いる` and `根っからのミュージシャン` are good examples of Subaru's interpretive style.

She notices contradictions and builds a model that predicts behavior. She also hedges:

- `多分`
- `～んじゃないかなって`
- `わからない`
- `逆らえない気がする`

A reconstructive model should preserve this uncertainty language. Subaru is not merely `the one who understands everyone`. She is a person willing to form social hypotheses, act on them, and accept uncertainty about the result.

### `SUBARU-R12`

> **Subaru converts observed inconsistency into a working social hypothesis and may design action around it, but she often marks the inference as uncertain rather than claiming direct knowledge of another person's interior.**

Status: **NEW / strong E06 support.**

## 7.4 Positive band attachment is now behavioral commitment

Subaru is actively participating in:

- discussion of goals;
- evaluation of professional routes;
- recruitment strategy;
- expansion of the ensemble;
- protecting the possibility of Momoka's assent;
- five-person rehearsal.

None of these actions is necessary merely to resist her grandmother's acting plan.

CP1-P15 therefore receives strong support. The band has become something Subaru works to develop, not just a convenient counter-role.

This still does not establish a permanent career choice.

## 7.5 `嘘つき` remains an identity wound and a tool-category dispute

When Subaru asks Nina not to call the plan lying, E05's stigma-shirt material remains active.

Subaru is comfortable with performance, indirectness, and sequencing. But she resists collapsing all strategic mediation into the moral category `lie`.

Her self-model seems to require distinctions among:

- false statement;
- selective timing;
- persona;
- tact;
- performance;
- information control;
- deception used to avoid responsibility.

The later monograph should recover those distinctions from more episodes rather than treating `Subaru = liar who means well` as a sufficient model.

# 9. Rupa - first V2 reconstruction state

Rupa's E06 introduction is unusually informative because the episode gives her both supportive and adversarial behavior before the larger band absorbs her.

## 9.1 Safety is legitimate if chosen honestly

Rupa's key principle is not `take risks`.

She explicitly says avoiding loss and pain is not necessarily a bad choice.

Her objection is to retaining the Budokan declaration while treating the risks required by growth as categorically unacceptable.

### `RUPA-R01`

> **Rupa treats safety as legitimate when owned as a choice; she applies pressure when a person's stated ambition and accepted risk have become inconsistent.**

Status: **NEW / strong E06 support.**

This is more precise than generic optimism or `never give up`.

## 9.2 Polite calm can carry severe semantic pressure

Rupa's grammar stays controlled:

- `そうですか`
- `では 仕方ないですね`
- `悪い考えではないと思います`
- `剥がしてください`
- `滑稽です`
- `わかるわけないです`
- `合ってると思います`
- `勘です`

There is no need for roughening in order to challenge Tomo strongly.

### `RUPA-R02`

> **Rupa can deliver severe pressure inside stable polite morphology; vocal/social calm should not be inferred to mean low conviction or permissiveness.**

Status: **NEW / strong voice baseline.**

## 9.3 She solves around strengths instead of normalizing them away

The vocalist proposal is the first clear example.

### `RUPA-R03`

> **When a collaborator's difficulty is also part of that collaborator's artistic strength, Rupa may prefer changing the surrounding system rather than asking the person to flatten the strength for convenience.**

Status: **NEW / provisional.**

Future evidence should test whether this generalizes beyond Tomo's music.

## 9.4 Rupa explicitly refuses false certainty

When Tomo asks whether joining the trio will work, Rupa says she cannot know. She offers counterexamples in both directions: close friends can break; badly matched people can persist.

### `RUPA-R04`

> **Rupa can act on bounded intuition while explicitly admitting uncertainty about interpersonal outcomes.**

Status: **NEW / strong E06 support.**

This rule is an important defense against over-inference. A later model should not turn successful Rupa judgments into retrospective certainty she never claimed.

## 9.5 She moderates Tomo without neutralizing Tomo

When Tomo says industry people can only cling to whatever is currently selling, Rupa replies that this is going too far but maintains the underlying point that the proposed forms did not fit what they wanted.

### `RUPA-R05`

> **Rupa may narrow Tomo's harsh external framing while preserving the substantive complaint rather than reflexively taking the opposing side.**

Status: **NEW / provisional.**

This is early direct support for a reciprocal Tomo/Rupa partnership rather than a caretaker/child hierarchy.

## 9.6 Negative-evidence discipline for Rupa

E06 does **not** establish:

- that Rupa is fully healed from prior loss;
- that she never becomes angry;
- that she always knows what other people need;
- that she is emotionally simpler than Tomo;
- that her calmness is effortless;
- that she will always prefer risk to safety;
- that she has no selfish goals.

`GBC-V1-C050` therefore remains an important methodological warning: Rupa is especially vulnerable to being overinterpreted because competent calm creates the illusion of transparent interiority.

# 10. Relationship and ensemble delta

## 10.1 Nina-Momoka: deidealized attachment does not eliminate conscription pressure

E05's `それでも` was real. Nina can love Momoka after losing the pure-martyr narrative.

E06 shows what that development does **not** solve. Nina still wants Momoka's trajectory to validate Nina's own. The demand has moved from:

`Say current Diamond Dust is wrong`

into:

`Join a project that can prove your song did not lose.`

Momoka identifies the imposition directly.

The relationship therefore moves from **ideological validation pressure** toward **goal-jurisdiction pressure**.

Important positive evidence: the disagreement does not terminate participation. Momoka remains. Nina remains. They later play with Tomo and Rupa.

This is strong support for the checkpoint's conflict-capacity model.

## 10.2 Nina-Subaru: the translator now actively constrains Nina's method

Subaru no longer merely explains Nina after a rupture. She tells Nina in advance that the direct method will fail, calls her a `正論モンスター`, and designs another route.

Nina calls the route lying. Subaru demands a distinction.

The pair's relationship now includes an increasingly stable division of labor:

- Nina supplies pressure, explicit desire, and refusal to let issues disappear;
- Subaru supplies pacing, social-model construction, and information sequencing.

This complementarity is useful but should not be romanticized. Subaru's tactics can become ethically contestable; Nina's directness can sometimes be necessary. The series has not assigned one method universal superiority.

## 10.3 Tomo-Rupa: reciprocal dyad under structural tension

E06 strongly rejects a simple `Rupa takes care of difficult Tomo` model.

Evidence of reciprocity:

- Rupa respects Tomo's artistic line rather than overriding it;
- Tomo's refusal matters;
- Rupa's desire also matters enough that Tomo reopens the question;
- Rupa presses Tomo on the Budokan contradiction;
- Tomo asks for Rupa's prediction about whether the group could work;
- Rupa moderates Tomo's rhetoric without dismissing her experience;
- they approach the trio together as an existing creative unit.

The dyad is already mutual before the five-person group forms.

## 10.4 Tomo/Rupa -> trio: recruitment is based on conflict capacity

Their attraction to `好きなことしか歌ってない` and `ちゃんと意見ぶつけられそう` is one of the episode's clearest ensemble statements.

The trio's roughness is legible from outside as a feature rather than a defect.

That matters for C009/C010. Complementarity in *Girls Band Cry* is not only different technical roles. It includes different tolerances and methods of disagreement.

## 10.5 Momoka-Tomo/Rupa: musicianship precedes trust

Momoka's first substantial relation to the pair is technical.

She can recognize quality before knowing whether she accepts their broader ambition or personalities. The pair likewise arrive with direct knowledge of Momoka's professional history.

This produces a useful starting asymmetry:

- Tomo/Rupa know `former Diamond Dust Momoka`;
- Momoka initially knows `the gyudon-shop pair` and their music;
- actual relationship has to be built after competence recognition.

## 10.6 Five-person ensemble: shared sound before shared destination

The episode's final group state is deliberately incomplete.

What is secure:

- the five have played together;
- the musical result is experienced positively;
- Tomo/Rupa have proposed joining;
- Nina wants it;
- Subaru has actively enabled it;
- Momoka has not been shown rejecting the concrete musical fit;
- a festival pathway exists.

What is not yet secure:

- a formally settled membership agreement from all five;
- a common definition of professionalization;
- a common reason for Budokan;
- a common relationship to Diamond Dust;
- stable authority allocation among Momoka and Tomo as arrangers/strong musical voices;
- tested five-person conflict repair.

Correct E06 endpoint:

> **musically instantiated five-person possibility / collectively under-negotiated ambition.**

# 11. Music, performance, and authorship

## 11.1 Tomo enters through composition, not personality exposition

The first thing E06 tells us about Tomo is not her biography. It is that she writes a melodic line Rupa considers difficult but characteristic.

This is good source discipline for later synthesis. Tomo's personality should not be reconstructed independently of her compositional behavior.

The artistic problem is already relational:

`preserve the line` requires `find the voice`.

Composition creates a need for other people.

## 11.2 Rupa's first musical philosophy is systemic

Rupa's solution is not `the melody is wrong` or `Tomo must compromise`. It is `change the ensemble so the melody becomes singable`.

That aligns with the series' emerging co-creation thesis:

> different people can preserve each other's specificity by changing the system around the difference rather than erasing the difference.

This is still only one scene and should remain provisional as a general Rupa philosophy.

## 11.3 Metrics enter music through distribution, not through the notes themselves

Follower counts, audience draw, agency attention, social-media images, and festival selection do not tell the band what chord to play.

They alter:

- who hears the chord;
- what resources become available;
- which collaborators become reachable;
- which stages can be accessed;
- whether industry actors pay attention;
- what kinds of visual packaging are incentivized.

E06 therefore strengthens the project's insistence that materiality and form cannot be separated by putting `art` on one side and `commerce` on the other.

Institutional pressure works through the conditions of circulation.

## 11.4 The five-person jam is the strongest differentiated-ensemble evidence so far

E03 established trio differentiation. E06 adds bass and keyboard and makes the arrangement richer without erasing the prior roles.

The five-person field contains:

- Nina's voice;
- Momoka's guitar/experienced arrangement judgment;
- Subaru's drums;
- Rupa's bass;
- Tomo's keyboard and arrangement intervention.

This strongly advances `GBC-V1-C009` and `C033`.

It also gives CP1-P12 a clear confirmation on **differentiated member contribution**.

## 11.5 Strict `conflict becomes composition` remains OPEN

There is a temptation to say E06 finally proves C015 because the band grows directly out of interpersonal conflict and then sounds better.

That is still too broad.

The source directly proves:

1. argument about goals creates pressure to seek a route forward;
2. Tomo/Rupa are attracted to visible opinion collision;
3. the new ensemble has differentiated musical contributions;
4. the five-person result creates a shared object worth continuing.

The source does not yet show in sufficient detail that:

- Nina's `win` position;
- Momoka's `no goal` position;
- Tomo's Budokan ambition;
- Rupa's risk-alignment ethic;
- Subaru's strategic mediation

are audibly preserved as conflicting musical propositions inside the arrangement.

Current status:

> **conflict creates the ensemble conditions; composition integrates differentiated people; formal encoding of the conflict itself remains OPEN.**

## 11.6 The episode makes professional ambition a dramaturgical action

Unlike a generic `we want to make it big` plot beat, E06 attaches each ambition marker to a decision:

- follower number -> marketing proposals;
- professionalization -> agency research;
- Budokan -> retrospective proof;
- festival -> a concrete application path;
- Diamond Dust on festival bill -> abstract rivalry becomes shared space.

The music-industry exposition therefore changes character behavior rather than pausing it.

# 13. Material and institutional layer

## 13.1 Professionalization is presented as infrastructure, not a magical status change

The Serbian Night explanation breaks `going pro` into mechanisms:

- online distribution;
- follower acquisition;
- live draw;
- agency attention;
- promotion;
- tie-ins;
- management/coordination;
- festival exposure.

This strongly confirms CP1-P19 and strengthens `GBC-V1-C006/C014`.

## 13.2 Independent music still depends on market-facing labor

Nina's reaction shows why the earlier purity fantasy is unstable.

If the group wants larger stages, it must engage in some combination of:

- visual presentation;
- audience cultivation;
- social-media activity;
- promotion;
- relationship-building;
- measurable turnout.

The relevant ethical question is therefore not `commerce or no commerce`.

It is:

> **which market-facing acts alter the work or the people beyond what they are willing to authorize, and which simply make circulation possible?**

## 13.3 Tomo/Rupa have already encountered institutional reshaping

Their agency contacts are important because they prevent the pair from functioning as naïve newcomers.

They already know the sequence:

`attention -> discussion -> proposed packaging/form -> mismatch`

This parallels Momoka without making the cases identical.

The safest common structure is:

> **institutional support expands reach but arrives with proposals about legibility and market form.**

Whether those proposals are always exploitative is not established. Rupa explicitly moderates Tomo's harsher claim.

## 13.4 Subaru's image becomes a resource with consent constraints

Nina sees a flattering Subaru photo as a potential follower-acquisition tool. Subaru rejects public posting because of the family/acting compartment.

This is a small but important materialization of E04's jurisdiction theme:

- image value exists;
- promotional utility exists;
- those facts do not eliminate Subaru's authority over exposure.

The band cannot treat a member's body or face as a free marketing asset merely because doing so might help the collective.

## 13.5 Festival is an institutional coordination device

The festival challenge slot converts five different motives into one actionable near-term operation without requiring final agreement on a life plan.

This is how institutions can constrain **and** enable autonomy.

The festival is a selection mechanism controlled by others, but it also supplies:

- a stage;
- an application deadline/path;
- concentrated audiences;
- industry observers;
- a concrete test;
- proximity to Diamond Dust.

The series is therefore not anti-institutional in any simple sense. Institutions create both pressure and possibility.

# 14. Speech, voice, and interaction delta

## 14.1 Nina - formal morphology remains compatible with confrontation

E06 further stabilizes the CP1 speech rule.

Nina can deliver severe disagreement to Momoka while retaining polite forms. Her formal grammar does not mean deference in substance.

A future voice model should therefore avoid converting `desu/masu` into low aggression.

## 14.2 Nina - repetition remains an affective discharge tool

`ムカつくムカつくムカつく` is structurally different from her more reasoned polite challenge.

Repetition can serve as:

- affect discharge;
- insistence;
- self-amplification;
- refusal to let a state be smoothed over.

More episodes are needed to partition those functions reliably.

## 14.3 Nina - `わかってる / わかってます / でも` becomes a diagnostic construction

This sequence deserves later indexing because it externalizes the cognition-regulation split.

A plausible reconstructed Nina may say `I know` sincerely and then continue arguing. The phrase should not be written as sarcastic by default.

## 14.4 Momoka - technical evaluation has its own register

Momoka's arrangement comments are compressed, concrete, and low-sentiment. This is distinct from both her rough joking and emotional avoidance.

A later monograph should preserve her ability to sound professionally specific without becoming ceremonially formal.

## 14.5 Momoka - roughness spans care through boundary enforcement

E06 strongly confirms the multifunctional model. Surface roughness is not enough to infer intensity without context and action consequence.

## 14.6 Subaru - metaphor is a social-diagnostic instrument

`闘牛場の牛`, `正論モンスター`, `桃香さんは２人いる`, and `根っからのミュージシャン` show Subaru compressing interpersonal models into memorable figurative labels.

These labels are not merely jokes. They let her communicate system-level observations quickly.

However, because the labels are models, later analysis should test them instead of adopting them as narrator statements.

## 14.7 Subaru - tact vocabulary is morally self-conscious

She explicitly disputes Nina's classification of careful approach as `嘘` and later asks not to be called `嘘つき` before revealing the plan.

This gives her speech an ethical metalinguistic layer: Subaru does not only perform tact; she argues about what tact should be called.

## 14.8 Tomo - compression is the first major voice signature

Tomo's early speech frequently deletes cushioning that Rupa supplies.

This produces a faster social temperature and a more evaluative surface. But E06 also shows embarrassment, aspiration, and willingness to recruit; the voice cannot be reduced to hostility.

## 14.9 Rupa - politeness does not reduce semantic force

Rupa's speech is perhaps the clearest early example in GBC of stable polite grammar carrying a very sharp proposition.

`剥がしてください / 滑稽です` is more severe semantically than many rougher lines in the episode.

This is crucial for reconstruction: intensity must be inferred from the proposition, relationship, delivery, and consequence, not honorific morphology alone.

## 14.10 Audio caveat

The extracted measurements are final-mix evidence. They can corroborate that the five-person music occupies a denser acoustic state than the surrounding dialogue, and that particular lines occur in quieter or louder program contexts.

They cannot independently establish:

- isolated vocal pitch;
- timbral intent;
- sincerity;
- breath control;
- microscopic prosodic contour.

Those claims require direct listening judgments and, where load-bearing, more targeted audio analysis.
