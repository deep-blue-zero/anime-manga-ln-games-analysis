## What happens

Miura introduces the five to life as an agency band. They pose for publicity photos. Tomo and Rupa finish the last shift at the restaurant where they worked while the contract is formalized. On learning she will receive a monthly salary, Nina starts thinking in terms of what she might buy. She still resents the attention Diamond Dust received at the festival. At the contract party, the other musicians bicker over how to cook cabbage. Watching them, Momoka gets out her phone. She brings a new song and tentative lyrics about the band to the studio.

The band works with an engineer, tries different versions and listens back. Nina is delighted that somebody will soon be able to listen too. While she is fetching Miura, she overhears part of a call. Diamond Dust's side proposes a two-day Club Citta event. Each band would play its own night. Ticket sales and attendance would decide who gets a television drama theme. Nina wants to take the challenge. The others explain why the two acts playing the same room would still be competing under unequal conditions. A majority votes to decline. Miura agrees to convey their decision and explains why she herself was torn.

Work on the release becomes difficult. Momoka worries about what sounds dated. Nina asks whether the song still expresses this band. She also reopens the concert question. To her the chance to sing counts even when the competition is weighted against them. Rupa asks Momoka to decide because of her relationship to Diamond Dust. At a shrine Momoka tells the others what serious composition has brought back for her. Nina asks to share a future with all five. Later, the engineer tells them what he likes in the version they have made, and Miura believes in its prospects. Alone on release day, Nina looks at the screen. It shows **103 plays**.
