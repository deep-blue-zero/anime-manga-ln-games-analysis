# Girls Band Cry E05-E08 - V2 Checkpoint

## 1. Checkpoint purpose and authority

This checkpoint freezes the second prospective tranche of the *Girls Band Cry* V2 reread after Episodes 5 through 8 and before Episode 9 is admitted.

It has four distinct responsibilities.

First, it reconciles the E05-E08 episode-local models into one current E08-boundary reconstruction state. The individual episode readings remain authority for exact Japanese wording, scene chronology, audio measurements, visual evidence, episode-local ambiguity, and claims whose meaning depends on a specific audiovisual sequence. This document is authority for what the four episodes establish **together**.

Second, it closes the first held-out experiment. `GBC_E01-E04_CHECKPOINT.md` froze twenty prospective expectations at the E04 boundary. E05-E08 were then read without altering those predictions. Their episode-by-episode scores produced 36 `CONFIRM`, 10 `PARTIAL`, 0 `CONTRADICT`, and 34 `NON-DIAGNOSTIC` prediction-by-episode events. Those event counts are useful audit data, but they are not the same thing as twenty independent predictions being proved dozens of times. This checkpoint therefore evaluates the twenty frozen CP1 predictions once at the **unique-prediction level** and records whether the E05-E08 evidence strengthens, revises, preserves, or leaves each rule open.

Third, the checkpoint performs the architecture-mandated second-tranche synthesis:

- Diamond Dust and compromise;
- Hina's arrival as a potentially independent subject rather than only Nina's trigger;
- professional versus independent survival models;
- five-member formation;
- Nina/Momoka projection rupture;
- confession and intimacy coding with evidentiary restraint;
- Tomo and Rupa's first prospective reconstruction state and its confidence limits.

Fourth, it freezes a new E09-E11 prospective register. That register is derived only from the E01-E08 corpus state. It may later be confirmed, narrowed, revised, or rejected, but it must not be edited after E09 is opened.

The evidence boundary is strict:

- `GBC_E01-E04_CHECKPOINT.md` is admitted only as the already-frozen first-tranche reconciliation state and prediction register;
- canonical V2 E05-E08 readings are admitted in full;
- the primary-source basis remains Japanese ASS, complete synchronized episode audio, dense evidence frames/contact sheets, and the source-lock rules declared in each episode artifact;
- V1 material enters only through the canonical revision-target framework and the post-prospective adjudications already performed in E05-E08;
- **no E09-E13 evidence is admitted**, including later-series memory that could make an E08 uncertainty appear prematurely settled.

Once canonical, this checkpoint is frozen. Later episodes do not rewrite it. Later evidence belongs in E09-E11 readings, their held-out audit, later ledgers, and the next checkpoint.

## 2. Executive checkpoint thesis

E05-E08 overturn the simplest early account of *Girls Band Cry* without discarding what E01-E04 established.

The first tranche showed a group learning that authenticity is not rawness, that care can become control, that performance can carry sincere intention, that Nina's anger can identify real contradictions without guaranteeing proportional conclusions, and that relationship durability begins with the ability to return after disagreement.

The second tranche asks whether those principles survive once the objects at stake become larger: a beloved band's historical compromise, professional ambition, public metrics, old trauma, family obligations, a new five-member collective, possible abandonment, and love itself.

They survive, but in revised form.

Nina's greatest development is not that she becomes less intense. She becomes capable of directing intensity differently. E05 proves that she can lose moral purity in an idol without losing attachment: after Momoka admits that she preserved one artistic boundary by breaking Diamond Dust's central relational promise, Nina answers `それでも`. E06 then shows that the need for proof has not disappeared; it migrates into a professional program. Nina wants follower counts, Budokan, an agency route, and eventually victory over Diamond Dust because institutional success might certify that Momoka and Nina were `not wrong`.

E07 supplies the crucial counterexample to any single Nina attachment rule. Momoka announces that she will leave. Nina is activated, but she does not immediately force the reversal. She asks `それでいいんですか`, seeks Mine's account, walks away, thinks, and converts the urgency into her own decision. E08 then supplies the opposite branch when departure becomes physically immediate: Nina pursues Momoka and places her body in front of the moving truck. The E04 checkpoint was right that threatened relational irreversibility can produce intervention; it was too narrow if read as one deterministic motor program.

The E08-current Nina model must therefore preserve a branching architecture:

> **Attachment threat increases action pressure, but the direction of action is not fixed. Nina can pursue and interrupt the other person, or she can redirect the pressure into a self-authored commitment. Immediacy, perceived reversibility, available information, and whether the decision can still be made on her own side are candidate moderators.**

Those moderators are not yet fully proven; the existence of both branches is.

Momoka undergoes an even larger reconstruction. E05 reveals that Diamond Dust was not primarily destroyed by a simple purity-versus-sellout dispute. The original band's core promise was relational: remain the original members even into old age. When commercial pressure arrived, the others proposed enduring compromise long enough to gain leverage. They tried to keep Momoka. She left anyway and later allocates blame to herself. This makes guilt inseparable from her artistic refusal.

E06 adds a second distinction: Momoka can deny having a future `goal` while remaining intensely responsive to concrete musical possibility. She evaluates Tomo and Rupa's material technically and enters a richer five-person sound. Declared lack of ambition is not lack of musical appetite.

E07 then supplies the negative belief underneath the withdrawal:

> `私の作る曲じゃ プロの世界では通用しない`

Mine interprets the withdrawal as fear of a conclusion: repeated professional testing might make failure feel like a verdict on ability rather than one bad outcome. This gives the checkpoint a strong **defensive foreclosure** model. Momoka can convert uncertainty into self-authored defeat before an external second verdict acquires greater authority.

E08 finally resolves the most important first-checkpoint uncertainty. Momoka does not merely identify Nina with an abstract forgotten value. She says directly:

> `あの時の仁菜は 私が好きだった私なんだ`
>
> `あの頃の私なんだよ`

Her protection of Nina is therefore partly care for Nina and partly an attempt to preserve an earlier Momoka inside Nina. Nina answers:

> `私はあなたの思い出じゃない`
>
> `あなたの思い出に閉じ込めないでください`

and then:

> `私で逃げるな`

The relationship's second-tranche hinge is not simply `Nina convinces Momoka to keep going`. It is the collapse of a preservation contract. Nina can no longer demand that Momoka remain the pure author who validates Nina's past, and Momoka can no longer demand that Nina remain the pure young singer who validates Momoka's lost past.

That is why the final confession matters. Nina says:

> `やっぱり私 桃香さんが好きです`
>
> `告白です`

**after** deidealization, after explicit rejection of being used as memory, after seeing Momoka afraid, compromised, controlling, guilty, and competitive. The scene is strongly romance-coded in language, staging, and response. It is not yet evidence of a mutually defined conventional dating relationship. The stronger relationship result is that Nina's attachment survives the loss of the symbolic role she originally needed Momoka to play.

Tomo and Rupa prevent this from remaining only a Nina/Momoka dyad story.

Tomo enters with explicit artistic specificity, a Budokan goal, defensive stasis, compressed evaluative speech, a time-scarcity model of career, and a boundary against conflict that does not produce work. Crucially, the prospective evidence does **not** yet justify importing the mature V1 pathology in which present inadequacy becomes permanent judgment and collaborator loss. E07 supplies counterevidence: Tomo can criticize specifically, receive pushback, remain, and perform. Her current high-confidence rule is narrower: she accepts collision when it moves the work and becomes exit-oriented when recurring interpersonal disorder threatens progress.

Rupa enters with a different discipline. E06 directly establishes that she treats safety as legitimate but considers it incoherent to keep an ambition as decoration while refusing all risks required to test it. Her compatibility judgment is explicitly `勘です`, preserving uncertainty. E07 introduces catastrophic family loss only through Tomo's report; no causal claim about Rupa's temperament is warranted. E08 finally gives direct interior evidence in the present: racialized workplace hostility is routine enough to be `日常茶飯事`, Rupa maintains professional composure, and she states `私にもロックは必要ということです`. Calmness is therefore active regulation in at least one demonstrated domain, not evidence of an unwounded or perfectly resolved interior.

The five-person band likewise becomes more real by refusing to become simple. E06 establishes shared sound before shared destination. E07 gives ordinary travel, technical disagreement, public naming, and a live performed while Momoka's exit threat remains active. E08 gives the first exit-level crisis the five survive. Subaru routes information and context; Tomo preserves a limit on endless dyadic disorder; Rupa supplies practical continuity; Nina forces the crisis; Momoka re-enters the future. The band does not become coherent because everybody finally wants the same thing. It becomes more durable because corrective functions are distributed across people who still disagree.

The second-tranche thesis is therefore:

> **E05-E08 transform authenticity from a question of whether a person is pure into a question of whether people can remain agents while being changed by one another. Art may save someone without becoming her property. Protection may be loving and still projective. Strategic compromise may preserve a group without proving the compromise beautiful. Refusal may preserve integrity while breaking promises. A confession may be sincere without settling a social category. And a band becomes more real when disagreement begins to limit individual overreach instead of merely threatening dissolution.**

# 3. Four-episode longitudinal movement

## 3.1 E05 - deidealization, jurisdiction, and the cost of purity

E05 is the tranche's first destruction of binary moral accounting.

Nina enters with an intense claim on the old Diamond Dust. Hina's appearance as current vocalist turns that attachment into crisis. Nina says that old Diamond Dust is `命の次に大事なもの`, calls it treasure, and asks Momoka to condemn the current band. Momoka refuses the jurisdiction Nina is trying to grant herself:

> `お前だけのものじゃないんだよ ダイダスは`

The episode then turns Nina's own E01 grievance back on her. Momoka says:

> `勝手に決めつけるな`

Nina, who cannot tolerate other people deciding her from incomplete knowledge, is doing exactly that to Momoka and Diamond Dust because the symbolic object has become part of her own identity defense.

The Diamond Dust rupture completes the inversion. Momoka had helped found the band around a promise of lifelong original-member continuity. The others proposed enduring label-directed compromise now so they could recover artistic leverage later. They tried to persuade her to remain. She still left. Her line `だから 悪いのは私なんだ` does not prove that every aspect of the conflict was her fault; it proves that she experiences the broken relational promise as her responsibility.

Nina's `それでも` is therefore not a restoration of the idol. It is the first large demonstration that attachment can survive moral complication.

E05 also brings material economy fully into view. Ticket quotas, Momoka's private subsidy of younger members, sales labor, reputation, costume cost, and major-label strategy all demonstrate that there is no costless position outside commerce. Independent music and professional music occupy different scales and control structures, not purity and impurity.

The episode's strongest formal result is narrower than V1's mature `conflict becomes composition` thesis. The fight directly determines costume, public framing, stage manifesto, and the meaning of the live. **Conflict becomes performance form.** It is not yet proven to become musical structure.

## 3.2 E06 - desire acquires a metric

E06 converts unresolved value into program.

Nina asks what the band is for. Momoka says it has no special goal. Nina eventually states her own:

> `ダイヤモンドダストに勝ちたい`

Professionalization research then gives that desire a set of metrics: follower counts, live draw, agency interest, promotion, tie-ins, public visibility, and Budokan. Nina learns quickly that these measures matter and almost immediately begins proposing market-facing tactics she recognizes as analogous to the very Diamond Dust packaging she criticized.

Her `わかってる` and `わかってます / でも` construction is diagnostically important. Nina often possesses insight before she possesses calibration. She can see a contradiction and continue because the motive on the other side still carries greater weight.

Tomo and Rupa arrive as a structural mirror.

Tomo already has a goal - `目指せ！武道館!!` - and substantial musical seriousness, yet resists adding the collaborators required to test the project at larger scale. Rupa does not moralize safety. She says remaining safe and avoiding suffering can be legitimate. But if that is truly the choice, the Budokan declaration should come down. The contradiction she attacks is not caution but a mismatch between stated ambition and accepted risk.

Their recruitment creates the first differentiated five-person sound. Bass, keyboard, drums, guitar, and voice become distinct functions rather than interchangeable bodies. Yet the five still lack one common reason for being together. Nina wants proof and Diamond Dust victory; Tomo has Budokan; Momoka says she has no goal; Subaru wants the band strongly enough to architect the meeting; Rupa thinks the friction may be workable without pretending she knows.

E06 therefore freezes the ensemble as:

> **shared sound before shared destination.**

## 3.3 E07 - fear of conclusion and self-directed commitment

E07 makes the stakes of E06's ambition physical and institutional.

Nina's sister reveals that the university route is not merely an externally imposed parental script. Nina herself had promised that she would get into university by her own effort after refusing the family's strategic accommodation with the school. The route had become evidence that rejecting a false or unjust settlement would not destroy her future.

That distinction matters when Nina begins leaving the route behind. Her final `予備校やめます` is a declared prep-school withdrawal, not yet proof that every university possibility has been permanently abandoned. It is nevertheless a serious redistribution of risk.

Momoka simultaneously announces another withdrawal. The Suwa performance will be her last because she does not believe her songs can work professionally. Mine's interpretation gives that choice its checkpoint significance: Momoka fears the conclusion produced by repeated testing.

The key Nina counterexample occurs here. Momoka's departure is a real attachment-closure threat, but Nina does not immediately execute the E04-style control branch. She asks a question, seeks Mine, thinks alone, and then makes a decision about her own life. This requires a revision to any deterministic `attachment threat -> pursuit` rule.

The five-person band also acquires ordinary time together. Driving, food, games, shared sleeping space, equipment movement, rehearsal, and low-status logistical labor matter because they demonstrate collectivity outside climax scenes. Tomo's `揉めるんなら 私らは抜けるからね` preserves a live exit boundary: she joined a group capable of conflict, not a group entitled to infinite interpersonal disorder.

The onstage exchange gives the first clean participation-preserving correction between Tomo and Nina:

> `うざいですよ`
>
> `自分語りは`
>
> `はい`

The criticism limits Nina's attempt to make the band an extension of her own autobiography. No apology or ideological settlement is required before the performance continues.

## 3.4 E08 - projection rupture and recommitment without purity

E08 turns E07's parallel withdrawals into a direct relational reckoning.

The cold open shows young Momoka advocating the same retreat-cutting ethic Nina now uses. The episode later makes the identification explicit: Nina resembled the Momoka whom Momoka had loved before professional experience transformed her. That explains why Momoka's concern can be both sincere and controlling. She is protecting an actual younger person and trying to protect a lost self-image at the same time.

Nina's `私はあなたの思い出じゃない` rejects the role. `私で逃げるな` returns Momoka's career decision to Momoka even while pressuring her intensely not to choose withdrawal.

The road intervention then reactivates Nina's body-first branch at maximum clarity. This does not cancel E07. It demonstrates that the same attachment system can produce different actions across different configurations of time, reversibility, and immediacy.

Subaru continues to solve process rather than content. She creates a public cover when needed, then privately supplies Momoka with information she has not adequately incorporated: the professional ambition is no longer Nina's fantasy alone. Tomo and Rupa already had professional orientation; Subaru remains; Nina has committed; the band has actual momentum. Subaru does not tell Momoka what she must feel. She changes the information environment in which Momoka is deciding.

Rupa's workplace scene provides the tranche's strongest direct Rupa interior evidence. The customer is openly racializing and demeaning. Rupa remains professionally controlled and later says she needs rock too. The episode therefore demonstrates **regulated anger under material-role constraint**, not a globally tranquil temperament.

The Diamond Dust confrontation completes the anti-purity argument. Momoka explicitly says the members who stayed chose continuation and that their choice cannot simply be denied. She then competes with them. Recognition of legitimacy does not eliminate rivalry.

The final confession should be held at exactly the confidence the source supports:

- Nina says `桃香さんが好きです`;
- she explicitly classifies the statement as `告白`;
- the framing softens and narrows;
- Momoka's response is nonverbal and tearful;
- `Kara no Hako` remains formal audiovisual framing.

This is **strong romance coding**. It is not a canonical declaration that a conventional reciprocal couple relationship has been established.

# 5. Nina checkpoint model after E08

## 5.1 Current architecture: graded compression survives, but proof becomes a larger problem

`NINA-CP1-R01` survives. Nina continues to enlarge partial evidence into broader verdicts, and the scale still depends on stakes.

E07 supplies the clean low-stakes control: failure to settle a band name becomes a wasted day, not a ruined self. E05, E06, and E08 show what happens when the object is attached to identity, belonging, or moral history. Current Diamond Dust threatens not just musical preference but the meaning Nina has built from the song that helped her survive. Professional success becomes not merely desirable but possible proof that `we were not wrong`. Momoka's withdrawal becomes a threat to Nina's interpretation of the past and the future.

The current rule is therefore:

> **Nina's evaluative compression is stake-graded. Ordinary frustration can become local all-or-nothing accounting; shame, injury, belonging, attachment, or proof stakes can recruit identity, permanence, and moral verdict language.**

This should not be reduced to `Nina catastrophizes everything`.

## 5.2 Attachment threat has at least two action branches

The checkpoint's most important Nina revision concerns `NINA-CP1-R02/R03`.

E05 supports pursuit and prescription under symbolic continuity threat. E08 gives the clearest body-first intervention yet: Nina physically blocks Momoka's moving truck when departure becomes immediate.

E07, however, is not noise. Momoka says she is leaving, and Nina remains active, but she does not immediately try to control Momoka's timing. She asks whether Momoka is satisfied, seeks Mine, walks alone, and converts the crisis into her own decision.

The current model is:

> **Threatened attachment continuity increases Nina's urgency to act. That urgency can become other-directed pursuit/interruption when closure is immediate, or self-directed commitment/information-seeking when enough temporal and procedural space remains.**

`Immediacy`, `perceived reversibility`, `available information`, and `whether Nina has an actionable decision on her own side` are candidate moderators, not yet stabilized causal variables.

## 5.3 Insight and regulation are separate variables

E05-E08 repeatedly show that Nina's moral or social insight can be excellent while her behavior remains poorly calibrated.

She understands ticket economics and becomes too extreme in selling. She sees that market metrics matter and proposes tactics she knows resemble current Diamond Dust. She accurately identifies Momoka's projection and avoidance, then tries to convert Momoka into a morally simple enemy through `殴りたければ` / `軽蔑できます` and later risks her body in front of the truck.

This is now a high-confidence reconstruction rule:

> **Do not infer Nina's level of understanding from the proportionality of her behavior. She can recognize the contradiction she is enacting and continue because the competing motive is stronger.**

That distinction is essential for simulation. A plausible Nina can say `わかってます` sincerely and still not stop.

## 5.4 Positive music desire is no longer merely emerging

CP1 froze the claim that positive musical desire appeared behaviorally before career identity. E05-E07 fulfill that prediction repeatedly:

- immediate acceptance of live opportunities;
- ticket and promotional labor;
- research into professionalization;
- desire to perform at Budokan;
- enjoyment and absorption language;
- continued performance despite school conflict;
- willingness to accept larger material cost.

By E08 the stage has changed. Nina has explicitly embraced a professional aim and reorganized material life around the band. The current state is:

> **Music is a positive chosen project. It remains entangled with correctness, proof, attachment, and refusal, but it is no longer adequately modeled as merely reactive participation for Momoka.**

The important remaining question is not `does Nina really want music?` It is `what happens when music cannot continue serving as proof that earlier refusals were correct?`

## 5.5 Deidealization without detachment is now a core relationship capacity

E05 establishes the first clean case. Nina learns that Momoka broke the original Diamond Dust promise and still says `それでも`.

E08 raises the difficulty. Nina learns that Momoka has partly used Nina as a preserved self-image. She rejects the role and then confesses attachment anyway.

The current rule is:

> **Once enough concrete relationship has accumulated, Nina can revise an idealized model downward without automatically detaching from the person.**

This is not equivalent to unconditional acceptance. Nina remains willing to confront, condemn, pressure, and demand change from someone she loves.

## 5.6 Autonomy develops from `do not define me` toward `do not use me as your alibi`

E01 began with `決めつけないでください`. E03-E04 exposed the inverse problem: Nina could resent being authored while authoring Subaru's choices. E05 makes that bidirectional jurisdiction error explicit with Momoka. E08 produces a genuine developmental advance:

> `私はあなたの思い出じゃない`
>
> `私で逃げるな`

Nina now has language for refusing not only inaccurate judgment but **instrumentalization**. She rejects becoming the object through which another person manages her own past or justifies her own retreat.

But this development is incomplete. Nina still claims substantial knowledge of Momoka's actual desire and still applies intense pressure. The current model is not `Nina learns perfect respect for autonomy`. It is:

> **Nina has acquired a sharper distinction between influence and ownership while remaining capable of jurisdictional overreach when attachment stakes are high.**

## 5.7 Speech, register, and affect regulation

# 6. Momoka checkpoint model after E08

## 6.1 Diamond Dust guilt is relational as well as artistic

E05 closes the first checkpoint's largest historical unknown.

The old group's defining promise was not simply `make uncompromised music`. It was to remain the original members across a lifetime. When label pressure arrived, the other members argued for temporary endurance as a strategy for future leverage and tried to persuade Momoka to stay. Momoka left.

The checkpoint model must therefore keep two truths simultaneously:

- leaving can be understood as preserving an immediate artistic boundary;
- leaving also breaks the relationship promise around which the band had organized itself.

Momoka's guilt is not reducible to `I failed commercially` or `I betrayed art`. It is partly `I was the one who broke us`.

## 6.2 Jurisdiction is a real principle, but classification can be biased

E05 gives Momoka unusually explicit jurisdiction language toward current Diamond Dust:

> `私はもうあそこにはいないんだ`
>
> `あとは自由だ`
>
> `私にそれを否定する権利はない`

This is genuine evidence that Momoka distinguishes personal feeling from claimed authority over other people's choices.

E08 prevents that principle from becoming a flattering universal trait. Momoka's intervention in Nina's prep/professional path is partly driven by explicit self-projection. She believes she is protecting Nina, but her picture of `what Nina really wants` is contaminated by what Momoka wants to preserve from her own past.

The current rule is therefore:

> **Momoka reasons in terms of jurisdiction and can sincerely respect another person's right to choose; however, her classification of another person's `real desire` can be distorted when that person activates Momoka's own unresolved history.**

This is more precise than either `Momoka is paternalistic` or `Momoka respects autonomy`.

## 6.3 Defensive foreclosure is now a major professional mechanism

E07 provides direct negative self-belief and Mine provides the strongest attributed interpretation:

> `私の作る曲じゃ プロの世界では通用しない`

and:

> `嫌なんだろうね 結論が出るのが`

The checkpoint formulation is:

> **Momoka may prefer a self-authored negative conclusion to renewed exposure when further testing could turn uncertainty into a verdict she experiences as more authoritative.**

This is not the same as cowardice. Her expectation is grounded in real professional experience. Nor is it the same as accurate prediction. Mine's countermodel demonstrates that experientially grounded realism can become defensive forecasting.

E08 then shows that behavior can outrun the belief. Momoka re-enters competition and challenges Diamond Dust without first verbally retracting the proposition that her songs cannot succeed professionally.

Thus:

> **Negative professional belief can lose behavioral control before it loses verbal endorsement.**

## 6.4 Younger-self projection is now direct fact

`MOMOKA-CP1-R08` required restraint at E04. That restraint is no longer necessary after E08.

Momoka directly identifies Nina as the self she once liked and wanted to preserve. This does not mean every act of care is projection. It means projection is now one proven motive among others.

The canonical E08-boundary rule is:

> **Nina can function for Momoka as both an autonomous younger collaborator and a living representation of a lost earlier artistic self. When those functions conflict, Momoka's protection can become controlling.**

Nina's `私はあなたの思い出じゃない` is therefore not a metaphorical overreading. It directly contests a role Momoka has admitted assigning.

## 6.5 Practical care and protective burden opacity

The first tranche already established logistical care. E05 adds a useful narrow mechanism: Momoka absorbs ticket cost because the younger members do not have adult earning power and initially does not foreground that burden.

The current model should preserve **protective burden opacity**:

> Momoka may reduce another person's immediate material exposure by silently absorbing cost or responsibility, but doing so can also reduce equal information and shared decision-making.

Care and asymmetry can occupy the same action.

## 6.6 Roughness remains multifunctional

E05-E08 make CP1-P11 one of the strongest validated rules.

The same broad rough/casual surface can carry:

- teasing;
- technical evaluation;
- ordinary direction;
- boundary enforcement;
- protective anger;
- withdrawal defense;
- competitive aggression;
- affiliative deflection after vulnerability.

No character reconstruction should infer Momoka's affect from rough morphology alone. Target, stakes, demanded action, follow-through, body state, and whether she remains present are more diagnostic.

## 6.7 Co-creation and concrete music

Momoka remains the most experienced musician and a major arranger/producer, but E06 makes a five-person arrangement possible only by admitting already-developed Tomo/Rupa material and differentiated roles. Concrete sound has leverage over her that abstract destiny language does not.

The current rule is:

> **Momoka can reject a symbolic or professional goal while becoming highly engaged by a concrete musical object that offers real craft problems and possibilities.**

This does not prove that music bypasses consent or that Subaru is entitled to manipulate her into every decision. It explains why `show her the sound first` can expose a desire that abstract argument leaves defensive.

## 6.8 Self-erasure remains an inadequate general explanation

Across E01-E08 Momoka repeatedly relinquishes, subsidizes, withdraws, and allocates blame to herself. Those facts remain important.

But the second tranche supplies more specific motives - relational guilt, jurisdiction, fear of conclusion, projection, protection, realistic cost assessment. `Self-erasure` is therefore still too total as a general causal model.

Current status:

> **relinquishment is stable behavior; self-erasure is an open motive requiring scene-specific proof.**

# 7. Subaru checkpoint model after E08

# 12. Correctness, proof, non-regret, compromise, and jurisdiction

## 12.1 `Not wrong` is becoming a tribunal problem

One of the strongest E05-E08 developments is the migration of correctness language from defensive self-preservation into an external proof project.

Nina begins the series needing her own experience not to be overwritten. By E06-E08, she increasingly wants public outcomes to certify that earlier refusals were correct:

- beating Diamond Dust;
- professional recognition;
- Budokan scale;
- audience/follower metrics;
- Momoka's renewed success.

This is psychologically understandable and epistemically dangerous.

A follower count can measure reach. A festival result can measure a particular competitive outcome. An agency offer can measure market interest. None can settle whether Nina's school refusal was morally correct or whether Momoka's Diamond Dust exit was the uniquely right decision.

The checkpoint therefore preserves a central V2 distinction:

> **achievement can become evidence inside a character's self-story without becoming proof of the moral proposition the character wants it to settle.**

## 12.2 E08 pluralizes `not wrong` instead of awarding it

Diamond Dust members say they do not regret continuing. Nina insists that Momoka was not wrong. Momoka re-enters competition. These positions coexist.

The series is therefore moving away from a tribunal with one winner and one loser toward a more difficult structure:

- a person can be wronged and still make an imprudent choice;
- a person can choose a prudent compromise without becoming morally superior;
- a person can preserve integrity and still hurt others;
- a person can refuse regret without proving that the alternative was false;
- two incompatible survival strategies can each remain intelligible.

This is one of the strongest series-level themes supported by the second tranche.

## 12.3 Compromise is not one moral category

E05-E08 supply at least four distinguishable forms:

1. **strategic commercial accommodation** - Diamond Dust tolerating changes to preserve continuity and future leverage;
2. **material/social tact** - Subaru constructing forms or sequencing information so difficult truths become encounterable;
3. **coercive mutualization** - the school asking Nina to participate in a settlement she experiences as falsely equalizing responsibility;
4. **ordinary pragmatic adjustment** - ticket sales, marketing, live logistics, image strategy, and rehearsal compromise.

The checkpoint therefore rejects any global equation such as:

> `compromise = betrayal`

or:

> `compromise = maturity`.

The correct questions are:

- what fact or value is being surrendered;
- who controls the form;
- who bears the cost;
- whether the account being ratified is experienced as true;
- whether the concession is reversible;
- what future agency it preserves or forecloses.

## 12.4 Jurisdiction remains the best bridge between ethics and character modeling

Across the tranche, many conflicts can be reframed as jurisdiction errors:

- Nina's transformed life does not give her ownership of Diamond Dust;
- Momoka's experience does not automatically give her authority to decide Nina's future;
- Nina's love does not automatically give her authority over Momoka's career;
- Subaru's social insight does not automatically give her the right to make another person's choice;
- a school institution's desire for closure does not automatically give it authority to define Nina's experience;
- audience expectation and market demand shape art without necessarily owning artistic intention.

The E08-current ethical rule is:

> **Accurate recognition, sincere care, personal investment, and even causal influence do not by themselves confer decision-right.**

E08 also proves that characters can understand this principle selectively while violating it elsewhere.

- scalable live activity.

Professional status is not a magical recognition event. It is a network of resources and constraints.

## 15.3 Family and school remain economic institutions

Nina's university path, prep-school attendance, remittance, bankbook, housing, family bargaining, and possible loss of return-home security all change what musical commitment costs.

The checkpoint must therefore reject `Nina simply chooses her dream over school` as too thin. She is renegotiating a material contract that made her earlier escape possible.

## 15.4 Labor conditions shape emotional expression

Rupa's workplace scene is especially important because it shows why public composure cannot be read as low anger. She is in a service role with asymmetric material consequences. Containment is partly labor.

The broader institutional rule is:

> **who can express anger, refuse tact, or leave a situation depends partly on what the person risks losing by doing so.**

## 15.5 Market adaptation can preserve agency and constrain it simultaneously

Diamond Dust's strategy makes this paradox explicit. Professional compromise can:

- preserve the group;
- preserve access to audience and capital;
- create future leverage;
- constrain present artistic choice;
- generate forms some members cannot accept.

The series' industry model is therefore neither romantic anti-market rebellion nor simple professional realism. It is a negotiation over which constraints a person is willing to internalize in order to keep acting at all.
