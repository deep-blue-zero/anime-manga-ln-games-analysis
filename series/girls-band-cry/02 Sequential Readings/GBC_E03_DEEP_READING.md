# Girls Band Cry E03 - V2 Deep Reading

## 1. Episode thesis

Episode 3 is the point where desire becomes harder for Nina to deny because it has already become behavior.

She still says that study is the governing obligation. Her timetable, prep-school work, and university plan remain visible. But before she can admit that music is a positive want, she spends enough time with a composition application that Momoka can infer roughly eight hours of work per day. She adjusts tempo, density, and relative balance; imagines multiple instruments surrounding her; and produces material substantial enough that Momoka later folds part of it into the trio arrangement. Music is no longer only the thing Momoka does or the song that once saved Nina. Nina is beginning to make it.

That shift gives E03 its first governing result:

> **Nina's positive desire for music appears first as sustained voluntary labor, then as bodily recognition inside rehearsal, and only later as something she can consciously own.**

The episode also gives the strongest correction yet to the E02 shame model. Nina still shows all-or-nothing cognition at low stakes: two pages of studying become equivalent, in her own accounting, to having wasted an entire day. Yet low-stakes failure does not automatically become permanent self-condemnation. When Momoka calls her complaint displaced anger, Nina can say `わかってます`; when she recognizes that the music application has disrupted her schedule, she moves toward stricter self-management rather than declaring her future ruined. The catastrophic E02 form therefore requires more than imperfection. The best current model is that Nina has a general tendency toward **evaluative compression**, while existential totalization becomes most dangerous when failure touches shame, belonging, injury, or identity.

The third major result concerns repair. E02 ended with evidence that Nina can recognize care quickly but can route that recognition into self-attack before returning to the person she hurt. E03 shows that this is not a fixed inability. Subaru explicitly names that Nina is avoiding her, offers to leave the band rather than force proximity, and makes clear that she does not want to interfere with Nina's life. Nina answers with direct denial of the hostile interpretation and then a simple `ごめんなさい`. The apology is followed by explanation rather than flight. She eventually tells Subaru the embarrassing mechanism itself: when someone approaches wanting intimacy, an impulse appears that says `お前の思い通りになるか / 私はそんなに簡単じゃない`.

That changes the reconstruction rule:

> **Nina can move from recognition to direct repair when the counterpart makes the rupture explicit without turning it into a total judgment of her, leaves genuine room for refusal, and reduces relational asymmetry enough for Nina to speak without feeling conquered.**

Subaru is therefore not merely the socially smooth foil V1 sometimes made her. E03 demonstrates that her polished warmth is a mode rather than her whole personality. At acting school she is comfortable, socially competent, and already discussing performance craft. In private with Nina she can stop smoothing, name avoidance directly, admit resentment toward the acting future assigned by her grandmother, and describe the band as `ささやかな抵抗` - a modest act of resistance. Her friendliness is constructed in the literal sense that she knows how to construct social situations. E03 gives no warrant to equate that construction with falsity.

Finally, the episode gives the first substantial **formal** support for Momoka's E02 theory that collective creation can produce something unavailable to one person. Nina's solitary digital experiment is incorporated into a physical trio arrangement. Subaru supplies drums; Momoka guitar and arrangement leadership; Nina voice and some compositional material. The rehearsal sequence becomes much denser acoustically than the surrounding dialogue, and the three participants react as though the result has exceeded their expectations. The opening visual fantasy of Nina alone among virtual instruments is later answered by embodied trio performance.

This is stronger than a verbal thesis, but V2 should still resist declaring `conflict becomes composition` fully proven. Momoka remains the dominant arranger, and E03 does not yet show detailed musical negotiation in which incompatible member positions are audibly preserved inside a new arrangement. What E03 proves is a necessary precursor:

> **difference can become shared musical output without requiring prior emotional resolution.**

The episode then complicates every victory it has earned. Nina's conspicuously artificial stage costume helps produce one of her most emotionally unguarded performances, turning the opposition between authenticity and performance inside out. And at approximately 23:35, after the live climax, Subaru sends the visual-only message `バンドやめます / ごめん!` - she is quitting the band. The first successful trio performance therefore does not certify a stable trio.

E03's larger formulation is:

> **A performed form can make truth communicable, a repaired relationship can still remain unstable, and a person can begin choosing a life long before she can say that she wants it.**

## 3. Prospective episode state

At E03's opening, the current V2 model contains several deliberately provisional rules.

### Nina hypotheses entering E03

1. Nina has strong baseline politeness that can survive high affect.
2. Regional/rough speech depends on interlocutor, home identity, and disinhibition rather than anger alone.
3. E02 revised the narrow `premature-definition trigger` into a broader **totalization-under-shame** model.
4. Nina can understand another person's care rapidly after conflict, but E02 showed shame blocking direct return/repair.
5. Nina may interpret visible social accommodation as pity, hierarchy, or falsity.
6. Under threatened irreversibility she may move bodily before she can state a relational desire clearly.
7. University remains principally a counter-verdict rather than a positive vocation.

### Momoka hypotheses entering E03

1. Practical/logistical care is recurrent.
2. She may intervene ahead of complete consent when she believes she sees a workable path.
3. E02 suggested that explicit, reasoned refusal can stop that intervention.
4. When she concedes fault, she can apologize directly; when she retains the judgment, repair may take the form of presence/action/reframing instead.
5. Her theory of creation treats friction and heterogeneous processes as potentially productive.
6. She has begun identifying Nina with a lost artistic value in herself, but the stronger `younger-self projection` remains unproven.

### Subaru hypotheses entering E03

1. Subaru has a high-warmth, polished, socially proactive baseline.
2. E02 gives no evidence that visible effort is insincere.
3. Her acting/family relation, conflict policy, and private self are still largely unknown.
4. The claim that she is the band's mediator is not yet established.

### Highest-value falsification questions

- Can Nina move from recognition into explicit interpersonal repair?
- Does totalization occur under lower stakes, and if so does it retain the same catastrophic form?
- Does Subaru's social polish persist unchanged when she is private, hurt, or discussing her own unwanted future?
- Can Momoka's co-creation theory be demonstrated in actual rehearsal/arrangement rather than only stated?
- Does Momoka continue to respect an explicit `no`, or does her intervention intensify with artistic investment?

# 4. Scene-by-scene close reading

## 4.1 00:00-01:07 - Music becomes behavior before it becomes a declared desire

E03 opens not on Momoka inviting Nina to sing but on Nina already making music by herself.

The composition interface is framed as an activity of adjustment rather than passive play. Nina says:

> `もうちょい賑やか`
>
> `もう少しだけテンポ速い方がいいのか`
>
> `重ねればいいってもんじゃないのか`
>
> `となると こっちはもう少し静かなほうがいいのかな`

She is changing density, tempo, layering, and balance. The sequence visualizes her imagined parts in an abstract cosmic field: guitar, drums, voice, and rhythmic components are separated around Nina as though she is mentally walking through an arrangement.

Then the clock re-enters the image:

> `零時すぎてる`

The joke at 00:52 is revealing:

> `勉強だよ 勉強`

Nina calls the activity study precisely when it has displaced study.

E01 and E02 established that her university project is verbally central. E03 provides a different kind of evidence: **revealed preference through time**. Nina has no social obligation to spend these hours on music. No one is grading the track. Momoka did not assign it. The activity persists past midnight because Nina keeps finding another change she wants to make.

That makes this the first strong V2 evidence that music is becoming a positive object of desire independent of simply preserving Momoka or `Kara no Hako`.

The opening also matters formally. Nina's imagined ensemble is still entirely under her control. Every instrument is a part inside a private digital workspace. Later rehearsal will give her the same expansion with two other people whose timing and decisions she does not control. E03 therefore begins with **solitary plurality** and moves toward embodied plurality.

### Reconstruction result

`GBC-V1-C016` remains true about Nina's starting point, but the character model must now distinguish **declared motivation** from **behavioral attraction**. By E03, her language still resists a musical future while her voluntary labor has already entered it.

## 4.3 02:48-04:36 - Two pages, a wasted day, and ordinary social circulation

Back at prep school, Nina immediately demonstrates that E02's totalization model has a lower-stakes cousin.

She says:

> `ダメダメ`
>
> `昨日から2ページも進んでない`
>
> `こんなんじゃ一日遊んでたのと一緒だよ`

The factual problem is small: two pages of progress. The evaluative conclusion compresses the entire day into failure.

This is significant, but it is not identical to E02's `一生誰とも仲良くなれない` or `一生暗闇`. Nina does not infer that she is permanently incapable of study. She does not convert two pages into a ruined life. The lower-stakes form is better described as **all-or-nothing productivity accounting**.

That provides the first useful falsification of an overbroad shame theory:

> Nina's cognition can compress partial failure into an overly total local judgment without escalating all the way to permanent identity/future collapse.

The difference appears to be stakes. Productivity triggers self-discipline. Belonging and shame can trigger existential totalization.

The scene then moves into Momoka's social/domestic network. Nina arrives with `ごめんください`; Subaru receives her with elongated warmth, `仁菜ちゃん いらっしゃーい`; food again circulates through weak ties. Momoka explains that a school acquaintance working in Chinatown had leftover meat buns. The episode continues to build relationships through practical surplus: food, errands, instruments, and later rehearsal space.

Nina's upbringing remains legible in small behavior. She objects that eating a meat bun in the casual way proposed is bad manners:

> `そんなの行儀悪いですよ`

Momoka's `やっぱりお嬢様だな 仁菜` is teasing, but the important point is that Nina's formal household regulation persists even after leaving home. Her rebellion has not erased embodied upbringing.

When Momoka asks what kind of song the three should make, she admits uncertainty:

> `なんか見えないんだよ この3人って何やれば一番いいのか`

This is important for later co-authorship. Momoka is already the senior musician, but she does **not** begin E03 with a finished answer for what the trio is. She is looking for a form responsive to the actual members.

## 4.4 04:36-07:03 - Obsessive labor, displaced blame, and self-command without collapse

Subaru and Momoka hear Nina's track. The sequence gives the most concrete behavioral evidence of Nina's emerging music interest.

Subaru says it has only been about a week since Nina learned the application. Momoka asks whether Nina built this much already and estimates:

> `毎日8時間以上はやってただろう`

Nina's immediate response is flight back toward the declared future:

> `帰るんです`
>
> `勉強しなきゃ 勉強しなきゃ`

Then she blames Momoka:

> `桃香さんが 余計なもの教えるからいけないんです`

Momoka identifies the displacement:

> `八つ当たりだな`

Nina answers:

> `わかってます`

This is a valuable counterexample to the idea that Nina lacks metacognition. She recognizes, in real time, that the accusation is unfair. Self-knowledge does not immediately make her emotionally graceful, but it prevents V2 from modeling her as simply unaware of contradiction.

Momoka responds with deliberately exaggerated compliance:

> `教えた私が悪かった`
>
> `削除するから 仁菜は歌うだけでいい...`

Nina immediately refuses actual deletion:

> `削除はしなくていいです`

The ambivalence is behaviorally clearer than any declaration. Nina wants the activity not to interfere with study; she does not want the capacity itself removed.

Then comes the self-address:

> `私が悪いんです`
>
> `私がきちんと切り替えてやればいいんです`

Momoka asks whom she is talking to.

> `私にです`

This is E03's best evidence for a self-regulatory style distinct from E02's collapse. Nina externalizes an internal supervisor. She treats the problem as one of proper switching and discipline: band to Momoka, study to Nina, categories separated cleanly.

The model should therefore distinguish at least three negative-evaluation states:

1. **ordinary error correction** - she can admit `わかってます`;
2. **rigid self-management** - `私がきちんと切り替えてやればいい`;
3. **existential totalization** - E02's permanent loneliness/darkness conclusions.

They are related but not interchangeable.

On the walk afterward, Subaru offers to accompany Nina to the station. Nina says she may stop at a convenience store and then walk home. Subaru answers simply `そっか / じゃあ`. She does not force the company.

That is important prospective evidence: Subaru's warmth includes an ability to accept a small boundary without turning it into drama.

## 4.5 07:03-09:14 - Momoka engineers contact; Nina meets Subaru inside the acting institution

Momoka asks Nina to deliver meat buns to Subaru because Momoka has a work shift. Nina resists:

> `自分で... 行けばいいじゃないですか`

but eventually goes.

This is another example of Momoka acting ahead of explicit relational consent. The errand is genuinely practical - Momoka does have work - yet it also places Nina in exactly the situation she has been avoiding. E03 does not need to decide that Momoka consciously designed a therapeutic exercise. What is observable is that her logistics create interpersonal pressure while preserving a plausible practical reason.

Nina gets lost, reinforcing the still-unfamiliar metropolitan environment. At the actors school she encounters Subaru within a domain where Subaru appears competent and socially legible. Subaru is already discussing acting work:

> `うん あの芝居は流れでいいよ`

When she notices Nina, her attention changes immediately:

> `仁菜ちゃん`
>
> `どうしたの`
>
> `道 大丈夫だった`
>
> `学校に連絡してくれれば 迎えに行ったのに`

Nina apologizes for being late, hands over the food, and refuses Subaru's invitations to eat or meet later:

> `私 勉強あるので`
>
> `本当に気にしないでください`

> `ついでだったんで`

> `じゃあ 失礼します`

This is the E01-E02 speech rule again: avoidance does not require Nina to become linguistically rude. In fact, high formality helps her withdraw cleanly. `失礼します` is not evidence of relational warmth here; it is a controlled exit formula.

Subaru's public competence also complicates the simple opposition `performed Subaru` versus `real Subaru`. ## 4.6 09:14-12:55 - Subaru names the rupture; Nina repairs instead of fleeing

Subaru does not let Nina's controlled exit erase what has happened.

She says:

> `やめたほうがいい`
>
> `バンド やめたほうがいい 私`

and then explains why:

> `私もバカじゃないからさ`
>
> `避けられているのは分かっちゃうよ`
>
> `別にあなたの邪魔したいわけじゃないからさ`
>
> `他にもバンドはあるし`

This is the most important relational test in the episode.

Subaru does several things differently from the kind of approach Nina fears:

1. she names the actual behavior - avoidance - rather than defining Nina's personality;
2. she does not demand that Nina like her;
3. she offers herself as the person who can leave;
4. she explicitly denies a wish to obstruct Nina;
5. she makes the band non-exclusive: there are other bands.

In other words, she restores choice precisely where Nina fears intimacy will remove it.

Nina first denies the interpretation:

> `いや 違うんです`
>
> `別にそういうわけじゃなくて`

Then she reaches the action E02 could not yet demonstrate:

> `ごめんなさい`

The measured mixed-program RMS of this cue is approximately -24.8 dBFS. It is not staged as a huge cathartic declaration. The repair begins quietly.

The significance lies in what follows. Nina does not immediately run. She stays in relation long enough for the pair to eat and continue the conversation.

Subaru then gives a principle that is especially revealing given Nina's suspicion of performance:

> `バンドって みんなで一つのもの作らないといけないから`
>
> `表面作ろうって話合わせてると`
>
> `どっかで必ずすれ違う`
>
> `だから 気にいらないところあったら 言ったほうがいいよ`

A band cannot be sustained by constructing a surface and merely matching the other person's words. Dislike should be spoken.

This is not anti-performance in the simple sense. Subaru is an acting student and highly socially skilled. Her distinction is closer to **performance versus falsified maintenance**. One can regulate a social surface without pretending substantive disagreement does not exist.

Nina initially falls back into self-blame:

> `私が悪いんです`

Subaru interrupts:

> `もう そういうのやめれ`

Nina then says:

> `どうせ嫌われがちですから`

The cue is strikingly quiet in the mix, approximately -29.2 dBFS RMS. The content is globalizing, but Subaru again refuses to ratify the total verdict:

> `違う そうじゃない`

This is a crucial environmental variable for Nina's model. E02 showed how bounded criticism can become permanent self-definition. E03 shows that another person can actively block that conversion by refusing the self-condemning premise and asking for the specific mechanism instead.

Nina finally articulates it:

> `ただ 私 距離取るっていうか`
>
> `人見知りだから 気ぃ遣われるじゃないですか`
>
> `ただ 仲良くなろうって寄ってこられると`
>
> `なぜか`
>
> `お前の思い通りになるか`
>
> `私はそんなに簡単じゃない`
>
> `って急に思えてきて`

This is direct primary evidence for a narrow form of `GBC-V1-C019`.

Nina does not say merely that she doubts sincerity. She describes intimacy itself as capable of triggering a conquest frame. The other person's wish to get close can become, in her mind, a plan whose success would mean that Nina has done what the other person wanted.

But E03 also supplies the necessary boundary condition. Nina is able to continue the conversation once Subaru gives her room to refuse. The problem is therefore not `being affected always equals control`. A more evidence-constrained rule is:

> **When an approaching person seems to have already decided the relationship's direction, especially from a position Nina reads as socially smoother or more secure, reciprocity can feel like surrendering authorship. Explicit choice and reciprocal vulnerability reduce the trigger.**

That boundary matters because Subaru then reveals that Nina has misread her position.

Nina assumes Subaru is straightforwardly pursuing an acting dream:

> `役者目指してるんでしょ`
>
> `それって夢を追いかけてるってことじゃないですか`
>
> `胸張って人に言えるじゃないですか`

Subaru answers that Nina has misunderstood. Her grandmother is a famous actress. The family and surrounding institution treat Subaru as the obvious successor:

> `私の後を継ぐのはすばるだって`
>
> `私が口効いてあげるから 役者の学校入れって`

Subaru then states plainly:

> `本当に目指してなんかないよ`
>
> `だよね 自分が嫌になる`
>
> `目指してないのに 何やってんだって`
>
> `でも 周りは誰もそうは見てなくて`
>
> `家柄的に 私が当然目指してるって みんな思っててさ`

Her band activity began as:

> `ささやかな抵抗ってやつかな`

This disclosure is the first strong evidence that Subaru's polished public behavior and private self-authorship are not aligned automatically.

Yet V2 should avoid the opposite overcorrection. Subaru is not demonstrated to be an empty actress trapped entirely in falsity. She appears competent at the school, understands performance craft, enjoys music, had previously joined a light-music club, and can joke even while discussing something painful. The evidence supports **role conflict**, not absence of self.

### Direct-repair falsification result

E02's provisional rule - insight can route into shame before direct repair - survives as an early tendency but fails as a universal law.

E03 establishes a successful repair sequence:

`rupture named -> freedom to refuse -> Nina apologizes -> self-blame challenged -> specific mechanism disclosed -> counterpart discloses vulnerability -> relationship becomes more reciprocal`.

This is a high-value reconstruction rule because it predicts not simply whether Nina apologizes, but **what interpersonal conditions make apology possible**.

## 4.7 12:55-15:18 - Disclosure becomes play, then Momoka escalates the band without consensus

After disclosure, Subaru's relational style changes without becoming solemn.

She insists that Nina see Hachiko and then declares:

> `話した以上 付き合ってもらうよ`
>
> `お前は今日からニーナだ この野郎`

The deliberately aggressive joking register is important. In E02, `仁菜ちゃん` could feel like unearned familiarity. Here the new nickname arrives after conflict, apology, and reciprocal disclosure. Performance/play is no longer simply something Subaru does *at* Nina; it becomes something the relationship can carry.

This is a useful negative constraint for Nina's authenticity model. Nina is not opposed to stylized social play as such. She can tolerate, and eventually participate in, artificial naming and teasing when the relationship underneath it feels earned.

The scene then shifts back to Momoka and the band's next step. Momoka announces a street live and says she has already reserved a weekend slot.

Nina correctly identifies that this differs from the earlier plan:

> `まず 録音して動画あげるって話でしたよね`

Momoka replies that they will still do that, but says this particular trio feels like a live act:

> `なんかライブって感じがするんだよね`
>
> `この3人って`

Her criterion is not polished market readiness. Looking at Nina and Subaru, she says they both carry an inerasable air of unhappiness/dissatisfaction:

> `拭えない不幸感というか`
>
> `不満が滲み出てる`

This is part artistic diagnosis and part unilateral production decision. Momoka is building a form around qualities the younger members did not offer as a conscious concept.

The material situation is improvised. They do not have a bassist. Momoka says the amateur event can be handled with programmed material and shows a loop machine. The band is therefore already technologically mediated. There is no evidence here for an authenticity ideology in which `real rock` means only unprocessed live instrumentation.

Then Nina finally understands what `street live` entails:

> `人前で歌うということですか`

Her panic is immediate. She leaves toward the door.

Nina's fear is not best reduced to ordinary stage fright. The prospective evidence supports several concrete components:

- she has almost no performance experience;
- she knows Momoka is the better-trained singer;
- she has not consented to this escalation;
- public performance removes control over how strangers interpret her;
- the trio itself is not yet stable;
- she cannot retreat into the claim that the result is merely private experimentation.

Momoka follows and stops the exit. Nina says:

> `放してください`
>
> `聞いていないですよ`

The static sequence shows Nina at the doorway and Momoka lunging after her; without full-motion evidence V2 does not specify the exact grip or duration, but the dialogue establishes physical interruption of departure.

Momoka answers Nina's lack-of-experience concern with confidence:

> `大丈夫 いざとなったら 私がなんとかする`

Nina asks why Momoka does not simply sing instead and notes that Momoka is much better. Momoka returns to the reason she recruited Nina:

> `私は仁菜のボーカルが聴きたいんだって`

Nina asks why.

Momoka gives no explanatory theory:

> `歌えば分かる 私を信じろ`

Nina attempts a clean exit again:

> `帰ります`

Momoka raises the stake:

> `じゃあ バンドもやめるでいいんだな`

Nina responds with formal closure:

> `はい ありがとうございました`

This is a particularly useful speech-state repetition. As in E01 and E02, polite language can function as a **severance technology**. The morphology remains proper precisely when Nina is trying to make departure final.

### Care/control falsification result

E02's provisional `explicit reasoned no can stop Momoka` rule does **not** generalize cleanly.

In E02 Nina's refusal was about a life plan Momoka accepted as coherent. In E03 Momoka believes Nina's refusal is fear contradicting an already-observable desire. Her intervention intensifies: the live is booked before consensus, departure is physically interrupted, and band continuation is made contingent on facing the performance.

The improved rule is therefore:

> **Momoka respects refusal when she accepts the refusal as an expression of the person's real preference. When she interprets the refusal as fear, avoidance, or self-betrayal, she becomes substantially more willing to override process and apply pressure.**

This is more dangerous - and more analytically useful - than a simple `Momoka is caring` or `Momoka ignores boundaries` label.

## 4.8 15:18-16:14 - Momoka names fear, Nina names coercion

Nina's objection is exact:

> `ずるいですよ バンドを人質に取るなんて`

Momoka dismisses the logic but does not deny the pressure. Instead she gives a character judgment:

> `ビビるな`
>
> `うまくいこうがいくまいが`
>
> `成功しようが失敗しようが`
>
> `お前はどっちにしろ後悔するんだ`
>
> `そういう性格だからな`

This is a striking inversion of Nina's sensitivity to being defined. Momoka is, in fact, defining her: whatever choice she makes, regret will be available to her because of the way she processes choices.

The difference from E02's harmful total judgments is that Momoka's claim is not that Nina is doomed or defective. It is an argument against waiting for a choice that will eliminate ambivalence.

Momoka's prescription is:

> `だったらすっぱだかになって`
>
> `思いっきり 今感じてることだけ歌えばいいんだよ`

Subaru jokes `はい ニーナの負け`; Momoka tells her to stay out of it, then redirects Nina's anger:

> `その怒りを歌にぶつけろ`
>
> `それがお前の本音だ`

There is real artistic intelligence here and real coercive pressure. V2 should preserve both.

Momoka recognizes that Nina's anger is immediately available expressive energy. She also decides that this makes pushing Nina into performance justified. The episode does not require the analyst to collapse effectiveness into ethical innocence.

## 4.9 16:14-18:20 - Rehearsal: solitary plurality becomes a trio

At the rehearsal studio, Momoka makes the first concrete move from theory to co-authorship:

> `仁菜 この前渡した曲 覚えた`
>
> `一応仁菜が作った曲も ちょっとだけ盛り込んでみたけど`

Nina's composition is not discarded as amateur play. Momoka incorporates part of it into the arrangement.

That matters for `GBC-V1-C033`. Momoka remains the experienced arranger and the person setting the session, but she has already allowed material produced by the novice singer to modify the work. This is an early, limited instance of shared authorship rather than private auteur control.

Nina remains frightened. Momoka deliberately lowers the expectation:

> `練習なんだし どうせうまくいかないんだ`
>
> `どうせ失敗するんだ`

This is almost the inverse of Nina's failure cognition. Nina treats failure as evidence that should be prevented. Momoka reframes rehearsal as a space in which failure is expected and therefore cannot function as final judgment.

The still sequence begins with Nina physically small around the microphone while Subaru and Momoka already occupy their instruments. Then the visual field changes as the trio locks into the song: highlights bloom, individual members receive stylized subjective emphasis, and the framing begins to treat them as one active sound source rather than three people discussing whether a band should exist.

The audio changes just as clearly. The 16:50-18:20 rehearsal segment measures approximately -16.7 LUFS integrated, nearly five LU louder than the earlier dialogue-heavy domestic sequence. The number does not prove transformation, but it confirms that the program's sonic density expands sharply when the ensemble begins.

Afterward Subaru says:

> `私 鳥肌が立っちゃいました`
>
> `すっごい鳥肌立ってる`

Momoka says she also got goosebumps and, looking at Nina again, says:

> `改めていい声だと思ってさ`

Nina asks:

> `本当ですか`

This sequence is important because it gives Nina feedback she did not script for herself. Her private composition application let her control all variables. Rehearsal produces an outcome that other people independently recognize and that depends on their contributions.

### Co-creation falsification result

Momoka's E02 claim that people can make something together that none could make alone is **materially strengthened**.

Evidence now includes:

- Nina creating music independently;
- Momoka incorporating some of Nina's material;
- Subaru supplying a live drum part;
- Momoka supplying guitar and arrangement leadership;
- Nina supplying the vocal center;
- a shared rehearsal result that surprises/affects the members.

However, `GBC-V1-C015 - conflict becomes composition` remains **not yet fully proven**. The episode shows conflict followed by a shared musical result, but it does not yet demonstrate that distinct incompatible positions are formally preserved inside the composition itself. E11-level proof still requires more exact arrangement causality.

## 4.10 18:20-19:25 - Emotional totality exceeds technique

Nina follows the instruction to sing everything she has so intensely that her voice is hoarse by the meal afterward.

Momoka identifies it immediately:

> `酒焼けか`

then corrects the interpretation and warns:

> `喉は大切にな`

The comedy contains an important reconstruction boundary.

Nina's willingness to commit completely is not the same thing as sustainable performance technique. In E03, `give it everything` becomes physically literal enough to damage her short-term voice.

For a future vocal-performance model, this is an early rule:

> **Nina can overspend vocal/physical resources when emotional permission suddenly replaces inhibition.**

That should not yet be generalized to every later performance. The series must show whether she acquires pacing, breath management, or professional discipline.

The material world remains visible. Momoka has again paid for food and rehearsal expenses; Nina and Subaru notice that they have been treated to both the meal and the karaoke/rehearsal space. Momoka's care continues to appear as money and logistics, not only encouragement.

A stranger notices the guitar case and asks whether they are a three-piece. Momoka hands over information about the upcoming performance. Nina suggests distributing their own material only after the person has seen them live. This is a small but important shift: Nina is beginning to imagine the band as something that can have an audience-facing identity rather than only a private relation among the three.

## 4.11 19:25-21:12 - Diamond Dust overhead; artificial costume as an authenticity device

After rehearsal, Nina notices Momoka looking at a large image of her former band. The old professional unit remains literally above the new improvised one in public space.

V2 should resist filling in the full Diamond Dust meaning from later episodes. At the E03 boundary, what is secure is that Momoka's former group remains emotionally salient immediately after the first successful trio rehearsal.

The street-live introduction gives the group a provisional name and announces them as a three-girl band on their first live appearance. Nina then arrives in conservative clothing and asks whether something is wrong with it.

Momoka responds:

> `それでステージ上がるつもりか`
>
> `言ったろ 心から裸になれって`
>
> `着替えてこい`

Subaru explains the strategy:

> `仁菜が絶対嫌がる衣装のほうがいいと思ってさ`

Momoka follows with:

> `余計なこと考えるな`
>
> `本当に思っていることだけを 気持ちだけをぶつけろ`

The resulting costume is conspicuously artificial: oversized bow, frills, stripes, colors, and a stage silhouette completely unlike Nina's preferred serious-student presentation.

This is some of the strongest E03 evidence for `GBC-V1-C008`.

Authenticity is plainly **not** being defined as unmediated self-presentation. The clothes are theatrical and selected by someone else. The backing arrangement uses digital technology because the trio lacks a bassist. The public event has a stage, microphones, amplification, lighting, and a provisional band identity. Yet Momoka's explicit goal is to make Nina stop curating how she appears and transmit what she actually feels.

The paradox should not be made cleaner than the evidence. The tactic is invasive. Nina did not choose the aesthetic. Subaru selected it partly because Nina would hate it. But Nina does walk onto the stage in it, and the performance that follows is one of her least guarded states so far.

The better V2 formulation is:

> **Artificial form can enable authentic transmission when it disrupts a defensive self-presentation - but the ethical question of who gets to impose that form remains separate from whether the tactic works.**

Momoka then defines Nina through contradiction:

> `仁菜は気が弱いくせに 意固地で`
>
> `臆病なのに 自信家で`
>
> `自己矛盾のコンプレックスの塊で`

Nina responds:

> `嬉しくないです`

> `それだけ仁菜は鬱屈して`
>
> `エネルギーが溜まってる`
>
> `それは 紛れもないロックだ`

Nina's reply is very quiet in the mixed program:

> `ロック?`

The word gives her a new category for contradictions she had previously treated as defects. E02's `性格悪い` becomes, provisionally, `rock`: not because every difficult behavior becomes virtuous, but because accumulated contradiction can have expressive value.

## 4.12 21:12-23:35 - First live: public non-guarantee, contradictory lyrics, and immediate instability

Immediately before the performance Momoka says:

> `誰もお前のことを知らないし`
>
> `誰も待ってやしない`
>
> `でも それが面白いんだろ`

This is almost the opposite of Nina's university logic. University is valuable partly because it can produce a legible result that answers existing judges. The street audience has no prior verdict to reverse. Most of the people do not know Nina, and no one is obligated to care.

That makes the live a different form of agency: expression without guaranteed recognition.

The insert-song subtitle layer begins at 21:54. The lyrics combine outward refusal with a desire for contact. Two especially diagnostic poles are:

- `うるさいんだよ / ほっといてくれよ`
- later, an appeal asking whether anyone is there.

The intervening text also acknowledges that an unknown person's hoarse singing may save a life. The song therefore refuses the fantasy that isolation and attachment are mutually exclusive. The wish to be left alone can coexist with the wish for a voice to cross the distance.

That contradiction now belongs to more than Nina. Subaru has just revealed a life built around performed compliance and secret resistance. Momoka is trying to make music again after declaring closure. The trio's first public sound is coherent without the members having become psychologically coherent.

Visually, the performance develops the episode's opening image. Nina's solitary midnight composition visualized separate instrumental worlds around her. The live sequence distributes saturated subjective color across the actual players: Subaru's drums, Momoka's guitar, Nina's voice, wide stage space, and audience-facing images. Private imagined plurality has become shared embodied plurality.

The stills also preserve a crucial public fact: the plaza is not a guaranteed fan environment. The band is small in wide shots; people pass and gather at different distances. The performance is exposure to an audience whose response cannot be commanded.

The program-level integrated loudness of the 21:47-23:10 performance window is approximately -17.3 LUFS. The score/song layer becomes continuous and dense while the visual grammar alternates between objective public-stage space and highly stylized subjective performance imagery.

At the end Nina experiences the performance as extraordinary and uses Momoka's category:

> `なんか...`
>
> `すごい`
>
> `ロックだ`

Then E03 refuses the obvious success ending.

At approximately 23:35.080, before the next-episode preview begins, a visual message from Awa Subaru appears:

> `バンドやめます`
>
> `ごめん!`

No explanatory dialogue is supplied before the episode ends.

That single visual beat is structurally essential. The band has become musically real enough to produce a successful shared experience; it has **not** become socially stable enough to keep all three members inside it.

The ending therefore imposes a strict boundary on every optimistic inference from rehearsal:

- Subaru's disclosure did not solve her family/acting problem;
- direct repair with Nina did not guarantee continued participation;
- a powerful performance did not convert `ささやかな抵抗` into a settled life choice;
- musical fit and relational continuity are separate variables.

This is exactly the kind of distinction the V2 architecture is designed to preserve.

# 5. Character reconstruction observations

## 5.1 Iseri Nina

### Motivational structure

E03 materially changes the answer to `What does Nina want?`

At E01-E02, her explicit future remains reactive: do not lose, prove the dropout does not define her, enter a good university even though she cannot name a particular university or vocation she positively desires.

E03 introduces a competing evidence class: **time allocation without external compulsion**.

Nina spends extraordinary hours learning and using a composition application. She experiments with arrangement variables after midnight and resists deletion even when she blames the application for disrupting study. That is stronger behavioral evidence of positive attraction than an abstract statement that she `likes music` would be.

Current model:

- declared long-term plan: university / educational recovery;
- dominant explicit justification: prevent dropout from becoming defeat;
- emerging intrinsic activity: music-making;
- conflict: positive desire threatens the controllability of the counter-verdict plan.

Confidence: **A/B evidence for attraction to making music; OPEN on career ambition.**

### Cognitive style: evaluative compression has multiple severities

E03 prevents V2 from turning E02's catastrophic totalization into a universal response rule.

Low-stakes example:

> `昨日から2ページも進んでない`
>
> `こんなんじゃ一日遊んでたのと一緒だよ`

This is all-or-nothing accounting, but it stays local.

Mid-level example:

> `私が悪いんです`
>
> `私がきちんと切り替えてやればいいんです`

Here the result is rigid self-management.

High-shame E02 examples produced permanent conclusions about loneliness and darkness.

The working hierarchy is therefore:

**local imperfection -> compressed evaluation -> self-discipline**

with possible escalation under shame/belonging threat into:

**local failure -> global self-judgment -> permanent future prediction**.

This is substantially more useful for simulation than a flat `Nina catastrophizes` trait.

### Metacognition

Nina is repeatedly aware of her own irrationality.

When Momoka says `八つ当たりだな`, Nina responds `わかってます`. In the Subaru conversation she can describe the exact internal sentence that appears when someone tries to become close to her.

This means the model should not predict that explanation alone reliably changes Nina's behavior. She may know a reaction is disproportionate while still feeling compelled by it.

### Intimacy trigger: authorship, not merely falsity

E02 suggested that Nina dislikes visible social effort because she may read it as fake or patronizing. E03 gives Nina's own explanation:

> `仲良くなろうって寄ってこられると`
>
> `お前の思い通りになるか`
>
> `私はそんなに簡単じゃない`

The salient threat is not only `You are being fake.` It is also:

> **You have already decided that we will become close; if I respond, I have enacted your script.**

This should become a high-value reconstruction feature.

Likely trigger variables:

- other person initiates intimacy strongly;
- other person appears socially fluent or difficult to read;
- Nina cannot yet see the other person's vulnerability/stakes;
- acceptance would feel like confirming the other person's interpretation of her;
- Nina has not experienced herself as freely choosing the relation.

Counterexample / release condition:

Subaru gives Nina permission not to like her, offers to leave, and discloses her own unwanted role. Nina then apologizes and remains engaged.

### Repair behavior

E03 establishes the first direct repair sequence.

Nina's apology is short:

> `ごめんなさい`

She does not initially give a polished account of everything she did wrong. But she stays and gradually makes the repair more substantive by disclosing why she withdraws.

The current rule is therefore:

> **Nina's repair may begin compressed and underarticulated, then deepen through continued conversation if the counterpart does not weaponize the admission.**

This is different from a character who repairs through elaborate apology language.

### Agency and coercion sensitivity

Nina correctly identifies Momoka's tactic as leverage:

> `バンドを人質に取るなんて`

Her objection should not be dismissed simply because the rehearsal succeeds. She is attentive to whether another person is using something she values to force a decision.

At the same time, the episode shows why she is difficult to protect through pure deference: she can say `no` to something that her behavior strongly suggests she wants because the feared exposure is immediate.

The future model must keep separate:

- whether Momoka reads Nina accurately;
- whether Nina's refusal reflects fear or stable preference;
- whether overriding that refusal is justified;
- whether the result is beneficial.

These are four different questions.

### Ordinary-life behavior

E03 adds several useful non-crisis anchors:

- can become absorbed enough in a hobby/task to lose track of time;
- treats modest study progress harshly;
- follows household-manners rules even after leaving home;
- accepts food and practical help but notices obligations/indebtedness;
- gets lost in unfamiliar urban space;
- refuses company by inventing/using a plausible independent plan rather than bluntly saying `I don't want you with me`;
- can carry out an errand for someone she is irritated with;
- becomes curious/engaged once another person's private contradiction is legible;
- has little resistance to being playfully dragged into sightseeing **after** trust has increased.

### Embodied behavior

E03 again separates inhibition from explosive commitment.

Before rehearsal Nina holds herself small around the microphone and questions the sequence. Once permitted to sing fully, she commits hard enough to become hoarse afterward.

At the live, the costume removes her preferred visual control and the performance becomes physically expansive.

Working rule:

> Nina often crosses from restraint to overcommitment rather than moving through a moderate middle state.

Confidence: **B**, pending recurrence.

### Negative constraints

Do **not** model E03 Nina as:

- already wanting a professional music career;
- globally incapable of apologizing;
- incapable of understanding her own motives;
- always dialect-heavy when emotional;
- always rude when resisting;
- unable to enjoy stylized/performative social play;
- automatically correct because her resistance is sincere;
- permanently committed to the band after the live.

## 5.2 Kawaragi Momoka

### Producer/arranger cognition

E03 gives Momoka her clearest producer-like behavior so far.

She does not begin with a fixed genre answer. She asks what these three people should do and says she cannot yet see it. She listens to Nina's amateur track, recognizes the labor inside it, and later incorporates some of it. She sees the trio's visible dissatisfaction as a reason to privilege live exposure. She compensates for the missing bassist with programmed material and a loop machine.

This is a practical, adaptive artistic intelligence:

> **use the available people, tools, affect, and constraints to discover the form rather than waiting for ideal conditions.**

### Care/control threshold

E03 materially strengthens the control side of the ledger.

Observable interventions include:

- using an errand to put Nina and Subaru back into contact;
- booking the street live before Nina agrees to that escalation;
- stopping Nina's physical departure;
- making band continuation contingent on performance;
- defining Nina's fear as something to push through;
- participating in the costume strategy designed to destabilize Nina's preferred self-presentation.

The important refinement is **conditional respect for refusal**.

Momoka is capable of stopping when she thinks Nina has made a clear genuine choice. But if Momoka believes the refusal is produced by fear rather than desire, she privileges her reading of Nina over Nina's current stated preference.

This is the beginning of a potentially recurring danger:

> **accurate perception can become a license for paternalism.**

E03 does not settle whether that danger will grow, be corrected, or be reciprocally constrained later.

### Failure philosophy

Momoka repeatedly treats failure as inevitable and therefore non-disqualifying:

> `練習なんだし どうせうまくいかないんだ`
>
> `どうせ失敗するんだ`

This extends E02's learning-through-collision model. Her practical philosophy is not optimistic in the conventional sense. She lowers the authority of failure by normalizing it.

Nina often tries to avoid situations in which failure can issue a verdict. Momoka makes failure part of the process.

### Recognition style

Momoka's praise is often specific and instrumental rather than effusive. After rehearsal she says, in effect, that hearing Nina again confirms the quality of her voice. She also identifies contradictory personality features and converts them into an artistic resource: `鬱屈` becomes stored energy; contradiction becomes `rock`.

This can be deeply affirming because she gives value to traits Nina experiences as defects.

It can also be invasive because Momoka is willing to define Nina in order to mobilize her.

### Material care

Momoka continues to absorb costs: food, rehearsal/karaoke space, practical equipment decisions, performance arrangements. E03 therefore strengthens the idea that her care is not principally verbal reassurance. She spends money, time, knowledge, and organizational effort.

### Speech/voice baseline

Momoka's rough register remains highly stable:

- `ビビるな`
- `お前`
- `黙って`
- `〜だろ`
- `ほれ`

But roughness is functionally diverse.

It can mark:

- teasing;
- command;
- irritation;
- confidence transfer;
- artistic coaching;
- confrontation.

The same lexical roughness should therefore not be translated into a single emotional temperature.

### Negative constraints

Do **not** model E03 Momoka as:

- purely respectful of explicit boundaries;
- maliciously coercive;
- certain from the beginning what the trio should sound like;
- opposed to digital/technological mediation;
- the only author of the new music;
- a person whose accurate read of Nina makes every tactic ethically correct.

## 5.3 Awa Subaru

### Public competence versus chosen future

E03 is the first episode that gives Subaru a private contradiction large enough for modeling.

At acting school she appears competent, socially fluent, and able to talk about `芝居` as craft. Later she states that she does not actually want the acting career everyone assumes she wants.

This gives an important rule:

> **Subaru can become skilled at a role she did not freely choose. Competence does not prove identification with the role.**

That distinction will matter later for any reconstruction of her acting, politeness, or family behavior.

### Social warmth is a mode, not a mask that turns off

Subaru's warm mode includes:

- elongated greetings;
- `仁菜ちゃん`;
- active invitations;
- concern about whether Nina found the location;
- offers to meet/escort;
- easy conversational topics;
- humor.

When hurt, however, she does not become a radically different cold person. She remains casual and controlled while stating the problem directly.

> `避けられているのは分かっちゃうよ`

She then gives Nina an exit.

The private self therefore looks less like `the opposite of the public self` than like a person who can **change the amount of smoothing while preserving the same underlying social fluency**.

### Conflict policy

Subaru explicitly rejects fake agreement as a band strategy:

> `表面作ろうって話合わせてると`
>
> `どっかで必ずすれ違う`

and invites Nina to say what she dislikes.

This is early evidence against a simple `Subaru avoids conflict` model. Her social skill includes the willingness to surface conflict when maintenance requires it.

What remains open is how she behaves when conflict threatens the family role rather than a new peer relationship.

### Self-authorship

The acting future is described as inherited expectation. The band is:

> `ささやかな抵抗`

This is not yet a full positive identity. The visual-only coda `バンドやめます / ごめん!` is decisive counterevidence against treating E03 as Subaru's stable choice of music over acting.

Her band involvement may be meaningful and enjoyable while still lacking enough authority to survive pressure from the dominant family narrative.

### Humor and intimacy

After mutual disclosure, Subaru's language becomes more aggressively playful:

> `お前は今日からニーナだ この野郎`

This is useful reconstruction evidence. Subaru can mark increased intimacy by **de-polishing**: nickname, teasing threat-like wording, shared outing, and mock command.

The result is not less performative. It is a different performance whose function is affiliation rather than public smoothness.

### Musical identity

E03 establishes that Subaru can drum well enough to participate in the first rehearsal and live. It does not yet establish the mature claim that drumming mirrors her relational role. That metaphor remains attractive but under-evidenced.

### Negative constraints

Do **not** model E03 Subaru as:

- secretly hating all acting activity;
- purely fake in public;
- universally conflict-avoidant;
- already committed to the band as her chosen life;
- merely a mediator between Nina and Momoka;
- emotionally transparent simply because she is socially skilled.

# 7. Speech and voice delta

## 7.1 Nina

### Formality as boundary maintenance

E03 strongly confirms that politeness is not synonymous with calm or submission.

Examples include:

> `本当に気にしないでください`
>
> `じゃあ 失礼します`

while avoiding Subaru, and:

> `聞いていないですよ`
>
> `はい ありがとうございました`

while attempting to leave Momoka and the band.

The last formula is especially revealing. `ありがとうございました` sounds socially proper while functioning as a declaration of termination.

Future voice generation should therefore allow Nina to become **more formally complete when she is trying to close a relationship cleanly**.

### Rough content inside controlled morphology

Nina can express resentment while retaining polite syntax:

> `いいですよね そうやって上から言えて`

She can also quote the rougher inner voice:

> `お前の思い通りになるか`

without directly speaking to Subaru in that register.

This distinction between **quoted internal aggression** and **outward morphology** is important for high-fidelity dialogue generation.

### Self-address

`私にです` reveals a literal self-directed disciplinary dialogue. Nina does not merely think `I should study`; she can frame self-regulation as an authority speaking to herself.

This may support future scenarios in which she mutters commands, repeats obligations, or corrects herself verbally.

### Epistemic verification

When Momoka again praises her voice, Nina asks:

> `本当ですか`

E01 established repeated `本当` around her own sincerity. E03 shows the reverse direction: Nina also wants confirmation that positive evaluation is real.

This should be tracked as a broader sensitivity to **whether statements are genuine enough to trust**, not only whether her own statements are believed.

### Vocal-resource behavior

The rehearsal produces temporary hoarseness. The evidence supports intensity/overuse, not a stable raspy baseline. Future voice reconstruction should not mistake the post-rehearsal state for ordinary Nina timbre.

# 9. Music and performance analysis

## 9.1 Nina's first positive musical desire is evidenced by labor

The strongest musical fact in E03 occurs before rehearsal.

Nina has learned enough of a composition application in roughly a week to make a developed experiment and has apparently devoted more than eight hours per day to it. She adjusts arrangement parameters because she keeps hearing alternatives she wants to test.

This matters because it cannot be explained solely as compliance with Momoka. Nina is choosing the labor when no one is watching.

For the longitudinal music ledger, E03 should therefore mark:

> **first secure behavioral evidence of Nina as a maker rather than only listener/fan/vocalist.**

## 9.2 Momoka incorporates Nina instead of merely using her

At rehearsal Momoka explicitly says she has folded a little of Nina's song into the arrangement.

That is the first concrete authorship transfer in the new group. Nina is not merely selected because Momoka likes her voice; something Nina made changes the shared object.

The authority distribution remains unequal:

- Momoka chooses the broader arrangement;
- Momoka sets rehearsal/performance conditions;
- Subaru provides drums;
- Nina provides voice and some source material.

So the correct status is **emergent co-authorship**, not equal authorship.

## 9.3 Rehearsal is the first nonverbal argument that changes Nina's state

Momoka and Nina spend much of E03 arguing about whether Nina should do this. The rehearsal does not produce a syllogism. It creates an experience unavailable to the argument.

The key transformation is not `Nina realizes Momoka was logically right`. It is:

> Nina discovers what her voice and material feel like inside other people's live sound.

Subaru's and Momoka's goosebumps then return that discovery as social evidence.

The rehearsal is therefore an epistemic event: the band can know something by doing that the members could not settle verbally in advance.

## 9.4 Emotional truth and technical sustainability are separate

Nina's hoarse post-rehearsal voice is a productive corrective to romantic performance ideology.

She has been told to sing the anger she actually feels. She does so with enough intensity to strain herself.

The episode therefore separates:

- emotional commitment;
- expressive force;
- technical control;
- sustainable vocal practice.

A future Nina vocal monograph should not equate `more authentic` with `more physically maximal`.

## 9.5 The live is technologically mediated rock

The trio lacks a bassist. Momoka plans around that with programmed sound and a loop machine.

The performance also depends on amplification, costume, lighting, staging, and arranged material.

E03 therefore undermines any reading in which the anime's authenticity ethic requires purist musical means. The meaningful distinction is closer to:

> **Does the constructed form transmit something the participants actually mean, or does it overwrite them?**

That question will become much more important when the series later confronts professional production.

## 9.6 Contradictory lyrics as a relational structure

The live's lyric layer places `leave me alone` beside an eventual call asking whether anyone is there. It also imagines an unknown singer's hoarse voice saving someone.

The contradiction is not resolved into `actually, she wants company` or `actually, she wants isolation`.

The more faithful model is:

> **desire for non-intrusion and desire for recognition can be simultaneous.**

That is now strongly relevant to Nina and prospectively relevant to Subaru and Momoka as well.

# 10. Visual-form analysis

## 10.1 Solitary plurality -> embodied plurality

E03's opening gives Nina a private imagined ensemble. The composition application is literal, but the visual field exceeds the room: virtual guitar, drums, and other parts orbit Nina in an abstract space.

The rehearsal later produces a parallel visual expansion around three actual bodies.

The formal movement is therefore:

`one person controlling imagined parts -> multiple people producing a shared event`.

This is stronger evidence for co-creation than Momoka's dialogue alone.

## 10.2 Study space compresses; music space expands

The prep-school cubicle is repetitive, bounded, and visually standardized. Nina's music-making produces impossible scale and movement. Rehearsal opens the frame around instruments and shared orientation. The public live expands again into plaza, stage, crowd, and saturated subjective imagery.

The episode does not say that study is false or worthless. It shows that Nina experiences these domains with radically different perceptual energy.

## 10.3 Nina/Subaru repair moves from distance to reciprocal framing

At the actors school Nina initially stands apart, backpacked and ready to leave. Subaru's approach is open; Nina repeatedly angles toward exit.

During the later food conversation the two occupy shared table space and the camera can isolate each face without preserving the institutional crowd around Subaru.

The visual grammar tracks the epistemic change: Subaru moves from `socially fluent girl in her institution` to a singular person whose private constraint Nina can see.

## 10.4 Rehearsal begins with physical contraction

Before singing, Nina keeps the microphone close and her posture comparatively contained. Momoka and Subaru already have instrumental stations.

As the sound begins, the visual field becomes brighter and more mobile, with subjective light effects and member-focused shots.

Without full-motion evidence V2 does not claim exact gesture-to-beat synchronization. The static/sequence evidence is sufficient to show a change from hesitant preparation to expanded performance-state imagery.

## 10.5 Costume destroys preferred legibility

Nina's stage costume matters because it is almost maximally unlike the image she has tried to maintain: serious student, controlled, ordinary, potentially able to return to the expected path.

The costume is not `the real Nina`. Its function is precisely that it makes her ordinary self-curation impossible.

This makes the scene an audiovisual argument about authenticity:

> a surface can be false to habitual self-presentation and still facilitate truthful expression.

The ethical problem - Momoka/Subaru choosing the surface for Nina - remains visible rather than disappearing inside the formal success.

## 10.6 Objective public space and subjective performance space coexist

Wide shots show a small band in a public plaza, surrounded by people who may or may not care. Subjective performance shots saturate members in color and impossible visual environments.

The form keeps both scales true:

- objectively, this is a small amateur first live;
- subjectively, it is enormous.

That prevents the anime from requiring external success to validate the intensity of the internal event.

## 10.7 The coda cuts through triumph

The live ends in warm, luminous performance imagery. The visual-only Subaru message at approximately 23:35 abruptly replaces that affective closure with a plain communication interface:

`バンドやめます / ごめん!`

The formal function is severe: the system of ordinary messages and obligations re-enters immediately after transcendence.

Music has created a real event. It has not abolished the lives waiting outside it.

# 11. Material and institutional analysis

E03 continues to make artistic choice inseparable from ordinary infrastructure.

Relevant material facts include:

- Nina's prep-school schedule and study progress;
- the composition application as a low-barrier creative tool;
- smartphones and route navigation;
- Momoka's paid work shift limiting her availability;
- leftover food circulating through school/acquaintance labor networks;
- Subaru's actors school as an institution embedded in family expectation;
- transit between Kawasaki/Shibuya and the time cost Nina explicitly notices;
- rehearsal/karaoke-room expenses;
- Momoka repeatedly paying for younger members;
- lack of a bassist;
- use of programmed sound and a loop machine to compensate;
- an amateur event with bookable performance slots;
- promotional material and audience solicitation;
- the public billboard visibility of Diamond Dust versus the new trio's anonymity.

Several institutional contrasts are especially important.

### Prep school

The institution offers Nina a legible recovery route. It converts time into measurable pages, tests, and eventual credentials. Its virtue is predictability; its danger for Nina is that she can turn measurable progress into punitive self-accounting.

### Actors school

The institution is not shown as intrinsically abusive. Subaru is competent there and has peers. The coercive element lies in **jurisdiction over future identity**: family lineage has made everyone interpret attendance as proof that Subaru wants the role.

### Amateur live infrastructure

The event allows a technically incomplete new group to perform through booking, backing technology, and inexpensive/improvised setup. It is a space where artistic identity can be attempted before professional legitimacy exists.

### Diamond Dust's public image

The billboard creates a scale contrast. Momoka's previous band occupies polished commercial visibility while the new trio is assembling with cheap rehearsal space, no bassist, and an amateur slot.

E03 does not yet turn that contrast into `industry bad / amateur pure`. Momoka uses technology, promotion, and organized performance infrastructure without hesitation. The distinction is not institutional participation versus purity; it is what kinds of authorship and constraint those institutions eventually impose.

# 15. Cumulative delta after E03

The E01-E02 model is now substantially more predictive.

Momoka's E03 model becomes both more impressive and more dangerous.

- materially generous;
- technically adaptive;
- capable of recognizing undeveloped talent;
- willing to incorporate another person's material;
- comfortable with digital/pragmatic solutions;

- unusually good at seeing expressive potential in contradiction.

But she also:

- books ahead of consensus;
- physically interrupts departure;
- uses band continuation as leverage;
- treats her own reading of Nina's `real desire` as grounds to push past current refusal.

The central Momoka question is no longer whether she cares. She clearly does. It is **what she believes care/artistic responsibility entitles her to do**.

E03 transforms Subaru from baseline to emerging model.

**specificity + preserved refusal + reciprocal vulnerability**.

That makes the relation methodologically important beyond shipping or friendship description: it supplies a testable model for what lets Nina remain in relationship after conflict.

## Nina/Momoka

Artistic reciprocity rises at the same time procedural asymmetry becomes more explicit. Nina is more than a fan now; she contributes material. Momoka is more than an admired artist; she is arranger/producer/mentor with coercive capacity.

The relationship is becoming deeper partly because the stakes over who gets to decide are becoming larger.

E03 supplies four major series-level advances:
