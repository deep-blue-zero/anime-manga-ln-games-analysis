# Girls Band Cry E01-E04 - V2 Checkpoint

## 1. Checkpoint purpose and authority

This checkpoint freezes the first four prospective V2 episode readings before Episode 5 is admitted into the analytical state.

It is not a replacement for `GBC_E01_DEEP_READING.md` through `GBC_E04_DEEP_READING.md`. Those remain the episode-level authority for exact scene arguments, Japanese wording, visual evidence, audio measurements, and episode-local ambiguity. This document performs a different job: it reconciles rules that changed across repeated falsification attempts, separates what has stabilized from what is merely emerging, records counterexamples so later character models cannot flatten the first tranche, and freezes a prospective register for E05+.

The evidence boundary is strict:

- E01-E04 canonical V2 deep readings are admitted;
- their underlying Japanese ASS, synchronized audio, frames, and contact sheets remain the primary-source basis through the episode artifacts;
- V1 material is admitted only as the already-defined revision-target set;
- E05-E13 knowledge is excluded from the checkpoint model, including any later-series memory that might make an early ambiguity look falsely settled.

The checkpoint itself is frozen once canonical. Later episodes may confirm, narrow, revise, or reject its rules, but should not silently rewrite this file. Later validation belongs in later checkpoint/ledger state.

### Governing checkpoint responsibilities

Per `GBC_SYNTHESIS_ARCHITECTURE_V2.md`, this first checkpoint must focus on:

- Nina/Momoka formation;
- refusal and correctness;
- Nina's politeness, anger, and dialect shifts;
- Momoka's roughness, care, and avoidance;
- Subaru's performed social competence;
- early reconstruction rules and their counterexamples.

E01-E04 also make two additional dimensions impossible to omit without losing the actual trajectory:

1. **jurisdiction** - who has the right to decide the timing, form, and consequence of a truthful choice; and
2. **mediation** - whether a constructed form such as song, costume, recording, or acting can carry recognizable intention without becoming false.

These are not hindsight imports. They are generated by the first four episodes themselves.

## 2. Executive checkpoint thesis

The first four episodes do not support a simple story in which an unusually honest girl meets a failed musician, gathers a socially performative peer, and learns to express herself.

They support a more conditional system.

Nina begins by defending her reality against premature definition. E01 makes that sensitivity vivid when Momoka compresses a life she barely knows into `easy`, and Nina answers with the demand not to decide her from partial information. E02 then breaks the temptation to make invalidation the whole mechanism: Nina can perform the same totalizing operation against herself. E03 demonstrates a lower-stakes form in which two pages of inadequate progress become a whole wasted day without becoming a ruined life. E04 adds an attachment form in which a one-week absence becomes possible permanent disappearance. The cross-episode model is therefore not one trigger but a graded cognitive policy:

> **Nina tends to compress partial evidence into a larger evaluative verdict. The scope of the verdict depends on stakes: ordinary productivity can produce local all-or-nothing accounting; shame and belonging can produce identity/future totalization; threatened attachment continuity can produce feared relational irreversibility and intervention.**

That cognition is paired with a second correction. Nina is not globally unfiltered. E04 explicitly describes ordinary speech as face-monitoring and throat blockage. Across all four episodes, high affect can coexist with polite morphology. Release is thresholded rather than constant. The live, self-talk, stranger conflict, and selected rough peer moments are therefore not the disappearance of a normally absent filter; they are changes in the regulation system.

The third correction concerns autonomy. E01-E03 make Nina's resistance to other people's scripts obvious. E03 gives the mechanism directly: another person's desire for closeness can feel like an attempt to author the relationship for her. E04 proves the inverse risk. When Subaru's choices threaten Nina's attachment, Nina begins authoring Subaru: she treats a temporary acting commitment as possible disappearance, demands disclosure, and then, after learning a more nuanced principle, attempts to decide that Subaru should *not* disclose yet. Nina's early autonomy problem is therefore bidirectional.

> **She resists being authored, but fear can make her claim authorship over another person's timing.**

Momoka undergoes a parallel narrowing. E01-E03 establish practical care, unilateral intervention, and a willingness to push Nina when Momoka thinks Nina is fleeing a desire she already possesses. E04 supplies the necessary counterexample: Momoka explicitly defends private circumstances and says a disclosure with serious consequences need not be forced. The current rule is not that Momoka is controlling. It is that she makes judgments about jurisdiction.

> **Momoka pushes hardest when she classifies the obstacle as fear blocking an already legible desire in a domain she considers actionable; she is more willing to defer when she classifies the decision as another person's private jurisdiction with consequences that person must own.**

Whether Momoka makes that classification accurately remains open.

Subaru likewise defeats a simple surface/depth model. E02 introduces polished warmth. E03 proves that the warmth is effortful but not therefore false, and that she can become direct, rougher, and personally disclosive after conflict. E04 demonstrates that acting itself is neither the fake side nor the authentic side of Subaru. Acting is inherited discipline and unwanted vocational destiny, but also a self-authored tool for social smoothing, camouflage, mediation, catharsis, and controlled disclosure. The useful axis is not performance versus truth. It is control over the performance.

The first-tranche thesis is therefore:

> **Girls Band Cry E01-E04 organizes authenticity around agency, proportionality, mediation, and jurisdiction rather than around rawness. A sincere feeling can be badly applied; a performed form can transmit something real; care can become control; restraint can be care; and conflict becomes evidence of relationship durability when the participants remain present without requiring immediate agreement.**

The trio's strongest early property is consequently not harmony. It is increasing **conflict capacity**.

## 3. Four-episode longitudinal movement

### E01 - refusal meets manufactured closure

The initial Nina/Momoka dyad is radically asymmetric. Nina already knows Momoka through `Kara no Hako`; Momoka has no equivalent prior knowledge of Nina. Nina depends on a meaning she has made from Momoka's art, while Momoka is trying to make her own musical past finite by naming last performances, relinquishing rights and objects, and planning departure.

The central collision is not simply optimism versus pessimism. It is jurisdiction over meaning and future action. Nina can legitimately testify to what the song did for her without thereby owning Momoka's career. Momoka can legitimately decide whether she continues music without thereby controlling what the song means to Nina.

The first deep rule candidate appears when Momoka forms a total judgment from too little information. Nina escalates into:

> `決めつけないでください`
>
> E01 16:46.910-16:48.420

Yet the same outburst is immediately followed by formal leave-taking. E01 therefore already blocks the equation `anger = loss of politeness`.

E01 also establishes Momoka's first secure repair mechanism. When she believes her own judgment was wrong, she follows Nina and apologizes directly rather than merely softening the mood. This will matter later because E02 shows the countercase: when Momoka still thinks the criticism is correct, repair takes another form.

The episode closes with performance, but it does not yet prove a band, stable reciprocity, or a mature theory of co-authorship. It establishes the possibility that unresolved disagreement can coexist with a shared musical act.

### E02 - the totalizer turns inward

E02 decisively falsifies the narrow idea that Nina's problem is only other people defining her.

During the Subaru/Momoka conflict, a bounded warning about how Nina sees other people expands into:

> `私 一生誰とも仲良くなれないんだと思います`
>
> E02 18:11-18:53

Later, a broken lamp and isolation become:

> `一生暗いままなんだ`
>
> `私 ずっと闇なんだ`
>
> E02 19:57-21:59

The mechanism is now broader: Nina can internalize the same totalizing structure she resents externally. But E02 also supplies counterevidence against a uniformly hostile reading. After leaving the restaurant she quickly recognizes that Subaru and Momoka were trying to think about her. The failure is not perception of care; shame routes the insight into self-attack before direct repair.

Momoka becomes more diagnostically difficult as well. Her care is practical and recurrent: food, housing, cooking, lighting, introductions, scheduling. Some of that care proceeds ahead of consent. Yet a clear reasoned refusal of the band can stop her. E02 therefore opens the care/control question without resolving it.

The episode also gives Momoka's first explicit theory of collective creation: difficult people and accumulated things-to-say can still create together because doing so produces something an individual cannot make alone. This strongly supports co-creation as a worldview but does not yet prove that interpersonal conflict is formally transformed into musical composition.

Finally, the pinky protocol demonstrates that Nina can encode attachment indirectly:

> `中指立てたくなったら 小指立ててください`
>
> E02 09:48-13:30

The gesture matters because Nina still has difficulty stating dependency directly. Relational form can carry content she cannot yet say plainly.

### E03 - repair becomes behavior and desire becomes labor

E03 gives the first clean counterexample to the proposition that Nina's totalization is always existential. Two pages of study progress become, in her accounting, equivalent to wasting the whole day, but the failure does not become `I will never succeed` or `my future is over`. The best model becomes graded evaluative compression rather than a unitary catastrophizing reflex.

More importantly, Subaru gives Nina a repair structure. Subaru names the behavior - Nina is avoiding her - rather than defining Nina's whole personality, and she offers a genuine exit rather than forcing intimacy. Nina answers:

> `ごめんなさい`
>
> E03 09:43.170

She stays. She explains. Then she reveals the underlying anti-intimacy authorship response:

> `お前の思い通りになるか`
>
> E03 10:49.920

This is a major reconstruction shift. Nina can move from recognition into direct interpersonal repair. The question after E03 is no longer whether she *can* apologize; it is what conditions make repair possible and whether apology changes the behavior that produced the rupture.

E03 also turns Nina's musical desire from inference based on fandom into behavioral fact. She voluntarily spends extraordinary time composing, resists deletion of the software, and later hears some of her own material incorporated into the trio arrangement. The first rehearsal gives Momoka's co-creation theory formal support: differentiated contributions combine into shared output before the members have resolved their interpersonal differences.

But the episode also prevents triumphal closure. Momoka uses leverage Nina explicitly identifies as leverage:

> `ずるいですよ バンドを人質に取るなんて`
>
> E03 15:27.370

And Subaru's final quitting message destabilizes the newly formed trio. Successful performance is not proof of stable relationship.

### E04 - truth acquires timing and jurisdiction

E04 moves the performance/authenticity problem into family structure.

Awa Tendo's expectations are coercive, but they are built from real love, professional pride, gratitude, family history, and an early affirmation by Subaru. Subaru's acting skill is real; her dislike of acting as an imposed future is also real. The problem cannot be represented as `fake career versus true band` without discarding evidence.

Subaru's body and language alter sharply under Tendo's gaze. A small correction -

> `姿勢`
>
> E04 09:02.120

- is enough to produce disciplined compliance. Yet within the same public sequence Subaru can smooth outwardly and then direct a covert rough aside to Nina:

> `あらニーナさん よく聞こえなかったみたい`
>
> `人の話聞けこら`
>
> E04 11:25.980 for the rough aside

This is not evidence for a false polite self covering a true rude self. It is evidence for audience-conditioned control of register.

Nina initially interprets Subaru's temporary acting work through threatened continuity. A one-week absence becomes `Subaru may disappear`. She equates care with pursuit and presses into a family problem Subaru has not granted her jurisdiction over. Momoka supplies the counterprinciple:

> `人には触れられたくない事情ってのがあるんだよ`
>
> E04 13:21-14:34

After learning why disclosure is difficult, Nina reverses her earlier position - but then physically prevents Subaru from confessing. She has learned nuance faster than she has learned restraint. Subaru identifies the contradiction; Nina promptly replies:

> `ごめんね`
>
> E04 20:38.880

This is the second independent demonstration of Nina's direct-apology capacity.

Subaru's own transition is equally important. The band began partly as resistance to an unwanted acting future. E04 gives the first direct positive movement beyond resistance:

> `私 意外とこれ本気で好きかもって`
>
> E04 17:01.590

The modal incompleteness remains important. This is emerging positive attachment, not a settled career declaration.

The final rehearsal-room argument then gives the tranche's clearest statement of jurisdiction:

> `私のことは私が決める`
>
> E04 21:40.330

Subaru later marks the apparently raw outburst as chosen acting:

> `どう 私の演技`

The performance does not erase the grievance. It frames the grievance so that anger can be delivered without automatically becoming departure.

E04 therefore closes the tranche by separating four variables that the early characters often conflate:

- truth of feeling;
- directness of expression;
- ownership of the decision;
- wisdom of the timing.

# 4. Nina checkpoint model

## 4.1 Current high-confidence architecture

At the E04 boundary Nina should not be modeled as `angry`, `blunt`, `honest`, or `stubborn` and then elaborated from those adjectives. Those descriptors describe visible surfaces without predicting when the surfaces appear.

The stronger model has five interacting layers.

### Layer A - high-monitoring social baseline

Nina's default social behavior contains more regulation than her conflict scenes suggest.

Evidence across E01-E04 includes:

- strong `desu/masu` morphology;
- repeated thanks, apologies, and leave-taking formulas;
- learned etiquette persisting immediately after anger;
- continued polite morphology in high-affect conflict with Momoka and Subaru;
- E04's direct self-description of monitoring other people's faces and speaking with something caught in her throat.

This means that explosive speech should be modeled as a state transition from a monitored baseline, not as baseline disinhibition.

### Layer B - graded evaluative compression

Nina repeatedly expands partial evidence, but the *scale* of expansion changes with stakes.

| Context | Partial input | Enlarged verdict | Current interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| E01 identity/invalidation | Momoka judges her situation `easy` | another person is deciding Nina from insufficient knowledge | externally triggered anti-totalization response |
| E02 belonging/shame | Momoka warns about Nina's way of viewing people | permanent inability to become close to anyone | existential social totalization |
| E02 practical/shame | broken lamp and dependence | permanent darkness / inability to manage alone | existential competence totalization |
| E03 study/productivity | only two pages progressed | entire day counted as wasted | lower-stakes all-or-nothing accounting |
| E04 attachment | one week of acting work | Subaru may disappear from the band | feared relational irreversibility |

The rule is not `Nina catastrophizes`. It is:

> **NINA-CP1-R01 - When Nina evaluates a shortfall or threat, she has a recurrent tendency to compress a partial fact into a larger verdict. The scope of that verdict increases when shame, belonging, identity, or attachment continuity are implicated.**

**Checkpoint state:** STABILIZED within E01-E04, still open to later narrowing.

**Counterexamples:** E03 productivity failure remains local rather than existential; E04 high affect does not produce self-condemnation; Nina can revise conclusions after new information.

### Layer C - authorship defense and jurisdiction error

E03 gives direct evidence that closeness can activate an internal objection equivalent to `you do not get to decide me so easily`. E04 proves that the same autonomy sensitivity does not automatically make Nina respectful of other people's autonomy.

Under attachment threat she can treat another person's choice as if its consequences for her create jurisdiction over that choice.

> **NINA-CP1-R02 - Nina is highly sensitive to authored roles, but the sensitivity is not symmetrical by default: when she fears losing a relationship, she may convert relational consequence into a claim on the other person's timing or decision.**

**Checkpoint state:** STRONGLY EMERGING.

The rule should not be moralized into `possessive Nina`. The evidence is better described as a jurisdiction failure under attachment.

### Layer D - body-first action under threatened irreversibility

E01 and E04 independently show Nina moving physically before she has fully articulated what she wants:

- taking the guitar and pursuing Momoka when departure is becoming concrete;
- immediately pursuing Subaru after the quitting message;
- physically redirecting Subaru when confession appears to create an irreversible consequence.

> **NINA-CP1-R03 - When Nina imagines a personally defining relational state becoming irreversible, bodily intervention may precede complete verbal formulation.**

**Checkpoint state:** STRONGLY EMERGING / conditionally stabilized.

**Counterexample boundary:** not every disagreement produces physical intervention. The common factor is perceived irreversibility, not anger alone.

### Layer E - repair is a real branch

E02 demonstrated rapid post-conflict recognition but shame-driven self-attack instead of return. E03 and E04 then provide independent direct apologies.

The best current repair rule is:

> **NINA-CP1-R04 - Nina can apologize directly and remain in the relationship when the conflict is kept behavior-specific rather than becoming a total verdict on her, especially when the counterpart preserves room for her agency. Apology does not necessarily imply that she has surrendered the underlying judgment.**

**Checkpoint state:** STABILIZED within the tranche.

This is a major correction to any model in which Nina's stubbornness prevents explicit repair.

## 4.2 Additional emerging Nina rules

### Rapid principle acquisition, slow jurisdictional calibration

E04 shows Nina absorbing Momoka's `people have circumstances` principle almost immediately, then overapplying it to Subaru.

> **NINA-CP1-R05 - Nina can revise a moral/social rule quickly when new evidence changes the problem, but early use of the new rule may remain categorical or prescriptive.**

State: EMERGING; E04 is the cleanest direct example.

### Positive desire appears in behavior before self-description

Nina's declared university plan remains defensive/counter-verdict oriented, while music attracts voluntary labor:

- extended composition work;
- resistance to deleting the tool;
- bodily recognition inside ensemble rehearsal;
- public performance commitment;
- guitar aspiration;
- insistence on continuity of the trio.

> **NINA-CP1-R06 - Nina may enact a positive desire for a substantial period before she is willing or able to define it as a chosen future.**

State: STRONGLY EMERGING.

### Moral/factual modality collapse

E01's repeated `cannot` in a dispute about what Nina believes Momoka ought not do remains diagnostically interesting, but E02-E04 do not provide enough clean repetitions to stabilize it as a general rule.

> **NINA-CP1-R07 - Under high identity stakes, Nina may phrase normative impossibility as factual impossibility.**

State: OPEN / retained hypothesis.

## 4.3 Nina speech and voice checkpoint

The first four episodes support the following reconstruction constraints:

| Dimension | Current rule | State |
|---|---|---|
| baseline politeness | learned politeness is a durable baseline and can survive anger | STABILIZED |
| anger | anger increases directness and repetition but does not automatically erase polite morphology | STABILIZED |

### Negative Nina constraints

Do **not** reconstruct Nina as:

- globally blunt or unfiltered;
- incapable of recognizing care;
- incapable of apologizing;
- uniformly dialectal when angry;
- possessive whenever she fears loss;
- incapable of nuance;
- someone whose every categorical claim comes from the same cognitive mechanism;
- someone who has already chosen music as a settled career by E04.

# 5. Momoka checkpoint model

## 5.1 Practical care is the most stable early policy

Across E01-E04, Momoka repeatedly expresses concern through logistics before sentiment:

- transit/help/charging and immediate practical rescue;
- food, shelter, rent, work, and moving questions;
- groceries, cooking, housing, lighting, and social introduction;
- rehearsal, recording, scheduling, and accommodation of temporary member absence.

> **MOMOKA-CP1-R01 - When Momoka perceives an actionable need, she commonly responds by changing the practical situation before or instead of making a sentimental declaration.**

State: STABILIZED.

The counterconstraint matters: practicality is not automatically kindness. The action still has to be interpreted in context.

## 5.2 Repair depends on whether she concedes the judgment

E01 supplies a direct apology after Momoka accepts that `easy` was a misjudgment. E02 supplies the contrasting branch: when she does not think her criticism is wrong, she does not apologize merely to restore comfort. She remains present, acts, and reframes.

> **MOMOKA-CP1-R02 - If new evidence makes Momoka concede that her own judgment was wrong, she can apologize directly and revise it. If she retains the judgment, repair is more likely to occur through continued presence, practical action, humor, or reframe than through false retraction.**

State: STRONGLY EMERGING / supported by complementary cases.

## 5.3 Roughness has multiple functions

Momoka's clipped/rough register cannot be represented by one aggression parameter.

E01-E04 require at least:

- ordinary casual roughness;
- teasing/affiliative roughness;
- embarrassed deflection;
- genuine threat;
- producer/arranger direction;
- experienced-adult rule giving;
- vulnerable autobiographical speech.

Teasing often regulates emotional temperature, but it is not always avoidance. Short directives can be caring, coercive, technical, or neutral depending on function.

> **MOMOKA-CP1-R03 - Lexical roughness is a register family, not an emotional diagnosis. Interpret it through target, stakes, action demanded, and repair behavior.**

State: STABILIZED as a speech-model constraint.

## 5.4 Intervention is jurisdiction-sensitive

E02 and E03 show unilateral behavior: recruiting Subaru, scheduling possibilities before Nina fully agrees, entering Nina's space during crisis, booking a live, and using band continuity as leverage. E04 then provides explicit nonintrusion.

Momoka's rule therefore requires two decisions:

1. What does she think the person actually wants?
2. Does she think the decision is within a domain where she has standing to act?

> **MOMOKA-CP1-R04 - Momoka is willing to push ahead of consent when she believes fear is obstructing an already legible desire in a domain she treats as actionable. She is more willing to defer when consequences belong to another person's private history or decision-right.**

State: STRONGLY EMERGING.

This is one of the highest-value E05+ falsification targets. A wrong classification could make care become control.

## 5.5 Failure and craft

E02 gives Momoka's explicit collision/repetition philosophy. E03 turns that philosophy into rehearsal. E04 turns catharsis into recording labor: takes can be sincere and still need revision.

> **MOMOKA-CP1-R05 - Momoka treats failure, collision, and revision as normal production inputs rather than as proof that the underlying project or feeling is false.**

State: STABILIZED within the tranche.

This is central to both her practical philosophy and her artistic leadership.

## 5.6 Co-creation is established as belief; conflict-to-composition is not

By E02 Momoka explicitly values making something with other people despite friction. E03 she incorporates Nina's novice material into an arrangement. E04 the trio records under differentiated roles and revision.

> **MOMOKA-CP1-R06 - Momoka values co-creation because differentiated contributors can produce an object no single member could produce alone, and she is willing to incorporate novice contribution rather than merely extract a voice.**

State: STABILIZED as a belief and early practice.

But `GBC-V1-C015 - conflict becomes composition` remains OPEN. The first tranche has not yet shown enough formal musical negotiation to prove that incompatible interpersonal positions are preserved and transformed *inside* arrangement, lyric, instrumental response, or authorship structure.

## 5.7 Relinquishment and Diamond Dust

E01 establishes that Momoka tries to make closure concrete by relinquishing song claims, place, and guitar. E02 supplies a counterexample to a universal self-erasure model: she accepts the guitar back and resumes musical action. E04 supplies sincere old Diamond Dust joy and a non-hostile wish that the current band succeed, but the rupture cause remains withheld.

Checkpoint constraints:

- relinquishment is real;
- universal self-erasure is not established;
- current jealousy is not established;
- the old band mattered profoundly;
- the causal explanation for leaving remains OPEN;
- Nina's resemblance to a forgotten/loved artistic value is direct evidence, but literal `younger self` projection is too strong.

### Negative Momoka constraints

Do **not** reconstruct Momoka as:

- uniformly controlling;
- uniformly nonintrusive;
- emotionally detached because she speaks roughly;
- a pure realist opposed to emotion;
- someone whose generosity is always self-erasure;
- someone whose attention to Diamond Dust proves jealousy;
- a mentor who already knows exactly what the trio should become.

# 6. Subaru checkpoint model

## 6.1 Social polish is genuine competence, not evidence of false feeling

E02 establishes Subaru's high-warmth weak-tie behavior. E03 shows that she notices Nina's avoidance, stops smoothing over it, offers an exit, and invites direct complaint. E04 maps the behavior across audiences in much greater detail.

> **SUBARU-CP1-R01 - Subaru is highly audience-aware and can actively construct a socially workable surface. The constructed quality of that surface does not establish that the warmth or concern inside it is false.**

State: STABILIZED.

## 6.2 Acting competence and vocational identification are separate

Subaru attends acting school, understands craft, performs effectively, and can manipulate register and body with precision. She also explicitly does not want the acting career Tendo assumes.

> **SUBARU-CP1-R02 - Competence in a role does not imply identification with the future that role represents.**

State: STABILIZED strongly.

This is a general reconstruction safeguard, not only an acting observation.

## 6.3 Public/private register switching is functional

The Tendo-facing sequence supplies a clean within-scene contrast between polished public language and rough peer aside. After reciprocal vulnerability, Subaru also uses rougher affiliative language naturally with Nina.

> **SUBARU-CP1-R03 - Subaru changes politeness, posture, lexical roughness, and theatricality according to audience, purpose, and relational safety. No single register should be treated as her uniquely `real` voice.**

State: STABILIZED.

## 6.4 Performance can become self-authored technology

E04 is decisive here.

Subaru uses performance for:

- inherited compliance;
- public smoothing;
- camouflage;
- provoking Nina into articulating grievance through an etude;
- expressing anger in a controlled frame;
- restoring relational continuity without pretending the grievance was unreal.

> **SUBARU-CP1-R04 - Acting becomes autonomous when Subaru controls its purpose and uses it to manage communication or affect, even though acting remains coercive when installed as an inherited destiny.**

State: STRONGLY STABILIZED at the E04 boundary.

## 6.5 Threat can produce information control rather than mediation

Subaru's false quitting message is a necessary counterexample to any idealized `mediator` model.

> **SUBARU-CP1-R05 - Under high threat to compartment boundaries, Subaru may control information unilaterally, including deceiving allies, even though she is otherwise skilled at social repair.**

State: EMERGING; E04 is the primary case.

This means `mediator` is a capability, not a universal policy.

## 6.6 Direct refusal remains difficult when love and obligation overlap

Subaru hoped that external evidence - lack of talent - would make Tendo release the acting future for her. Her problem is not lack of insight. She knows what she does not want. The difficulty is taking ownership of a `no` that will visibly hurt someone she loves and that may carry material/family consequences.

> **SUBARU-CP1-R06 - When refusal threatens a loved person's emotionally invested future, Subaru may prefer circumstances or an alternative path to perform the refusal for her rather than state an unbuffered `no`.**

State: EMERGING.

## 6.7 Band attachment has moved beyond pure resistance

E03 frames band participation as `modest resistance`. E04 adds a direct positive statement that Subaru may seriously like it.

> **SUBARU-CP1-R07 - The band is becoming a positively valued activity rather than merely an instrument for resisting acting, but the commitment remains exploratory at E04.**

State: STRONGLY EMERGING.

### Negative Subaru constraints

Do **not** reconstruct Subaru as:

- fake in public and real only in private;
- secretly committed to acting because she is talented;
- an infallible mediator;
- transparent with allies under stress;
- merely passive in the face of Tendo;
- already committed to music as a permanent career;
- emotionally detached because she can perform through conflict.

# 7. Supporting model: Awa Tendo

Tendo is not yet a full reconstruction target from one episode. She matters because she prevents Subaru's autonomy conflict from collapsing into a simple antagonist model.

Secure E04 evidence:

- strong professional discipline ideology;
- belief that everyday speech/body conduct becomes acting material;
- direct correction through low-temperature authority;
- family history in which her daughter resented the career and absence;
- gratitude toward Subaru as the child who seemed to affirm the work;
- dream of performing together;
- continuity built into Subaru's name and opportunity structure;
- sincere affection coexisting with a future Subaru did not choose.

The key analytical point is structural:

> **Coercive expectation can be carried by love, opportunity, gratitude, and artistic admiration rather than by malice.**

This strongly supports `GBC-V1-C002` and should remain available as a counterexample whenever later analysis is tempted to identify care with noncoercion.

# 8. Relationship checkpoint

## 8.1 Nina <-> Momoka: from symbolic dependence to contested reciprocity

### Entry condition

At E01 entry, Nina's symbolic dependence is enormous and Momoka's interpersonal dependence on Nina is effectively zero. Nina already knows a public artistic object; Momoka does not know Nina.

### E01-E02 transition

The dyad forms through practical rescue, food, housing, disclosure, argument, apology, gift, pursuit, and song. Nina can interrupt Momoka's story about herself. Momoka begins identifying Nina's refusal to bend with an artistic value she had forgotten and loved.

The relationship nevertheless remains asymmetrical:

- Momoka has more adult/material competence;
- Momoka has musical expertise and local knowledge;
- Nina carries disproportionate symbolic investment;
- Momoka often chooses the practical next move.

### E03-E04 transition

Artistic reciprocity increases when Momoka incorporates Nina's material. Procedural conflict also increases. Nina recognizes leverage and names it. E04 she openly accuses Momoka of lacking consideration and attempts to assign her leader responsibility.

Momoka does not convert these challenges into relational exit.

### Checkpoint state

The best current formulation is:

> **Nina/Momoka is moving from fan/idol asymmetry toward negotiated reciprocity, but the negotiation itself is conflictual because Momoka's experience grants real competence without granting unlimited jurisdiction, while Nina's increasing personal importance does not erase her tendency to turn attachment into demands.**

State: STRONGLY EMERGING.

Important counterconstraints:

- do not call the relationship equal by E04;
- do not reduce Momoka to rescuer/mentor;
- do not reduce Nina to fan;
- do not treat all intervention as intimacy;
- do not infer formal romance from symbolic intensity alone.

## 8.2 Nina <-> Subaru: suspicion becomes conflict capacity

The dyad has the clearest first-tranche developmental sequence.

**E02:** Nina sees effort in Subaru's friendliness and interprets it suspiciously. She can recognize care after the conflict but does not repair directly.

**E03:** Subaru names avoidance without totalizing Nina, offers an exit, and states a maintenance-oriented conflict ethic. Nina apologizes and explains her authorship defense. Reciprocal vulnerability permits nickname and rougher play.

**E04:** Subaru's false quitting message activates Nina's attachment alarm. Nina uses acting to express abandonment grievance, later intrudes on Subaru's decision timing, receives direct pushback, apologizes, and remains in the relationship. Subaru's final acted rant conveys real anger without terminating participation.

### Checkpoint state

> **The Nina/Subaru relationship becomes the first clear laboratory for disagreement as maintenance rather than verdict. Its defining early gain is not reduced conflict but greater ability to remain present while conflict becomes more direct.**

This is the strongest first-tranche evidence for the trio's developing conflict capacity.

## 8.3 Subaru <-> Tendo: genuine attachment plus jurisdiction conflict

The relationship should be represented with both sides intact.

Subaru loves Tendo and values the genuine smile her apparent acting ambition produces. Tendo loves Subaru and treats her as artistic/familial continuation. The same attachment therefore sustains concealment and coercive expectation.

The conflict is not `truthful young person versus deceptive adult` or its reverse. It is:

- who owns Subaru's future;
- what childhood affirmation can legitimately authorize;
- whether gratitude creates vocational obligation;
- when truth should be disclosed;
- whether love can survive a refusal that changes the meaning of the past.

## 8.4 Trio: conflict survival before found family

The trio's sequence is:

- E02: proposed collectivity without Nina's agreement;
- E03: first rehearsal and live, followed immediately by apparent member exit;
- E04: external family crisis, deception, pursuit, privacy dispute, disagreement about intervention, direct accusation, apology, and return to rehearsal.

This supports a provisional version of `GBC-V1-C010`.

> **The trio's earliest durable group property is not harmony but repeated return after disagreement.**

Found family remains too mature a label at E04. Conflict survival is the evidence currently available.

# 9. Refusal, correctness, prudence, and jurisdiction

The first four episodes already require a more precise ethical vocabulary than `Nina is right` or `Nina is stubborn`.

## 9.1 Being wronged does not make every inference correct

Nina's school/family account gives serious evidence that she was harmed and then subjected to pressure not to escalate the problem. That history explains why premature judgments and accommodation demands are dangerous for her.

But E02-E04 also show that a justified defense of one's reality does not make every extension proportionate:

- a bounded criticism can become `I will never be close to anyone`;
- temporary practical failure can become permanent darkness;
- a socially effortful peer can be interpreted as pitying;
- a one-week acting commitment can become disappearance;
- understanding another person's circumstances can become an attempt to decide their disclosure timing.

The checkpoint distinction is:

> **Prior injustice can explain a defensive inference without validating the inference.**

## 9.2 Refusal can preserve selfhood and still impose costs

E01 establishes refusal as identity-preserving. Nina left rather than accept an imposed account of the school conflict. Momoka also refuses a musical path she has judged unsustainable. Subaru resists an inherited career through indirect means.

Yet refusal is not free:

- Nina's school/family rupture narrows options and belonging;
- Momoka's Diamond Dust departure has material/professional consequences even before the cause is known;
- Subaru fears family injury and practical disruption if she refuses directly.

V2 should therefore preserve separate axes:

- was the person wronged?
- is the current judgment factually accurate?
- is the chosen action prudent?
- does the action preserve integrity?
- does the person regret it?
- who bears the cost?
- who has jurisdiction to decide?

These axes are already separable by E04.

## 9.3 Correctness does not confer jurisdiction

E04 makes the point explicit. Nina may be correct that Subaru cannot conceal her acting preference indefinitely. That does not give Nina ownership of the disclosure clock. Momoka may correctly see that Nina wants music while Nina verbally denies it; that does not automatically justify every leverage tactic.

The first-tranche ethical model is therefore:

> **Seeing another person accurately is not the same as having the right to act for them.**

This rule should govern later care/control analysis.

# 10. Speech, voice, and social-performance checkpoint

## 10.1 Nina

Nina's voice model must keep morphology, lexicon, dialect, prosody, and relational target separate.

### Stable findings

- `desu/masu` is robust and survives significant anger;
- direct accusation can remain formally polite;
- formal leave-taking may immediately follow an outburst;
- rough self-talk can exceed roughness directed at peers;
- stranger confrontation can become much rougher than Momoka/Subaru conflict;
- regional forms occur in family/home context and can also appear under familiarity/disinhibition;
- high affect does not automatically produce regionality;
- repetition and categorical modality are common when conviction is high;
- apology is part of the available register by E03-E04.

### Reconstruction consequence

A generated Nina line should not become rough or dialectal merely because she is angry. The model should first ask:

1. who is she speaking to?
2. how much social distance remains?
3. is the conflict about principle, shame, embarrassment, or direct threat?
4. is she still maintaining interactional form?
5. is this outward speech, self-talk, or a release state?

## 10.2 Momoka

Momoka's baseline is casual, clipped, and relatively low-ceremony, but this is not synonymous with emotional simplicity.

Her early register families include:

- ordinary casual;
- teasing/affiliative;
- genuine threat;
- practical-care directives;
- producer/arranger correction;
- experienced-rule explanation;
- direct apology;
- vulnerable autobiographical reflection.

The distinction between `rough` and `controlling` is especially important. E04's privacy rule and permissive `do not force it` conclusion are both delivered in concise casual speech.

## 10.3 Subaru

Subaru has the clearest audience-conditioned register map by E04.

### Public/grandmother-facing mode

- deferential address;
- posture discipline;
- immediate compliance cues;
- polished redirection;
- strong monitoring of what can be said before whom.

### Peer-social mode

- warm active topic generation;
- nickname use;
- playful theatricality;
- capacity to smooth weak ties.

### Peer-private / rough affiliative mode

- compressed casual forms;
- insults/rough imperatives in safe relation;
- grievance without automatic exit;
- theatrical exaggeration used knowingly.

### Threat-state mode

- compartmentalization;
- selective disclosure;
- possible unilateral information control.

The checkpoint rule is:

> **Subaru's voice is deliberately adaptive. Fidelity requires modeling audience and purpose, not finding one supposedly authentic register underneath the others.**

# 11. Performance, music, and authenticity

## 11.1 `Kara no Hako` is already a multi-owner relational object without singular ownership

Across E01-E02 the song acquires at least three functions:

1. Momoka's farewell/closure object;
2. Nina's survival command and refusal resource;
3. Nina's river performance as a relational parting gift that preserves connection without forcing band agreement.

This strongly supports `GBC-V1-C011`, `C027`, and `C063` while also limiting them: listener-created meaning is real, but it does not grant the listener control over the creator's future.

## 11.2 E03 establishes co-creation but not the mature conflict-to-composition thesis

The strongest formal evidence so far is that Nina's solitary composition material enters a trio arrangement with Momoka's guitar/arrangement leadership and Subaru's drums. Rehearsal changes Nina's state in a way verbal persuasion did not.

This establishes:

- heterogeneous contribution;
- shared musical output before emotional agreement;
- the body as a site of musical recognition;
- technology and programmed elements as compatible with the series' live-performance authenticity;
- the difference between emotional totality and sustainable vocal technique.

It does **not** yet establish that a specific interpersonal contradiction has been formally translated into arrangement, lyric, instrumental response, or authorship negotiation.

Therefore:

> `GBC-V1-C015 - conflict becomes composition`: **OPEN at checkpoint 1.**

## 11.3 E04 generalizes mediation beyond music

The romantic etude and Subaru's final acted rant prove that the series' performance logic is broader than song.

The checkpoint model distinguishes:

- **imposed performance** - a role whose purpose/future is controlled by someone else;
- **chosen performance** - a constructed form the person selects to make intent communicable;
- **technical mediation** - arrangement, recording, costume, microphone, acting exercise, makeup;
- **sincerity** - whether recognizable intent/affect is actually being carried;
- **jurisdiction** - who controls the form and its consequences.

No one variable determines the others.

A costume can be imposed and still facilitate sincere expression. Acting can be inherited coercion and also chosen catharsis. A direct statement can be sincere and still seize another person's decision-right.

This is the checkpoint's strongest thematic revision of V1 shorthand.

# 12. Material and institutional checkpoint

The first tranche already makes it impossible to treat autonomy as purely psychological.

Material/institutional constraints include:

- relocation and housing;
- rent and part-time work;
- music income and failed independent commercial sustainability;
- school and exam schedules;
- rehearsal-space and recording logistics;
- equipment and technical skill;
- acting school;
- family reputation and inherited professional capital;
- television casting and production schedules;
- member availability.

Three early principles follow.

### 12.1 A choice can be authentic and materially constrained

Momoka's decision to leave music cannot be evaluated without failed sales/income and work saturation. Subaru's acting problem cannot be evaluated without family capital, training, casting, and possible return-home consequences. Nina's university plan cannot be evaluated only as preference because it also functions as a social counter-verdict.

### 12.2 Opportunity can be coercive without being objectively bad

Subaru receives real training and professional opportunity. The coercive element is not that acting is worthless. It is that access and expectation are bundled into a future she did not authorize.

### 12.3 Amateur music is already labor

Composition consumes time. Rehearsal requires scheduling. Performance requires infrastructure. Recording requires takes and correction. Artistic truth does not remove labor.

This materially strengthens `GBC-V1-C014`.
