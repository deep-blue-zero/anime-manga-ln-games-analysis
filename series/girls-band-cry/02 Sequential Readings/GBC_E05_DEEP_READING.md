# Girls Band Cry E05 - V2 Deep Reading

## 1. Episode thesis

Episode 5 is the first held-out episode after the frozen E01-E04 checkpoint, and it immediately attacks one of that checkpoint's most important distinctions: **being transformed by another person's art does not give you jurisdiction over the artist, the work's later custodians, or the future form of the thing that transformed you.**

Nina has good reasons for caring. The Diamond Dust she heard before coming to Kawasaki is not casual entertainment. She calls it `私が命の次に大事なもの` and `宝物`; later she tells Momoka that the old song changed her life. The work is bound to her survival, her self-account, and her conviction that refusing an imposed verdict can matter. Episode 5 does not belittle that reception. It does something harder: it makes the reception completely real and then denies that reality the authority Nina wants it to have.

Momoka's answer is blunt:

> `お前だけのものじゃないんだよ ダイダスは`
>
> E05 12:01.710-12:04.210

That line extends the E01-E04 authenticity model. Art can transmit meaning beyond the creator's control, but transmission does not become ownership. Nina may be correct about what Diamond Dust did **to her** while still being wrong to demand that Momoka condemn the band's present form on Nina's behalf.

The episode then makes Momoka's side equally impure.

The easy story would be that Momoka alone preserved artistic integrity while the other original members surrendered to the label. E05 confirms only half of that story. The old band was asked to change its sound and look for commercial reasons; Momoka refused. But the founding promise that mattered most to the original members was not a particular musical doctrine. It was that the original members would remain together, even as old women, and never quit. When the major-label compromise arrived, the others proposed a strategy: endure it for now, rise far enough to regain leverage, then make the music they wanted. They tried hard to persuade Momoka to stay. She left anyway.

Momoka therefore says:

> `だから 悪いのは私なんだ`
>
> E05 16:07.370-16:09.670

The most precise V2 formulation is not that Momoka discovered she was simply wrong. The source supports a conflict among kinds of integrity:

- she refused an artistic transformation she could not accept;
- the others accepted temporary compromise in order to preserve the group and pursue later autonomy;
- the band's deepest explicit promise was relational continuity;
- Momoka was the member who broke that promise by leaving.

She preserved one boundary by violating another.

This explains why her present position on Diamond Dust is so resistant to Nina's demand for a winner and loser. Momoka says the remaining members are free, that bands change like people, and that she has no right to deny what they do now. E05 gives strong reason to understand that stance as **jurisdictional humility shaped by guilt**, not as proof that she loves the new direction and not as proof that her neutrality is merely fake.

That distinction revises an attractive V1 shortcut. V1 called Momoka's neutrality toward current Diamond Dust "partly a lie." E05 does not directly establish that. What it establishes is that she refuses to convert her unresolved past into authority over people who stayed after she left. Her private aesthetic response remains partly inaccessible.

Nina's most important movement comes after she learns this.

She does **not** obtain the clean validation she wanted. Momoka does not say `I was right and they were wrong`. Instead, Nina learns that Momoka broke the group's central promise and still says:

> `それでも 私は桃香さんが好きです`
>
> `桃香さんの歌が好きだから`
>
> `私の心を動かしたのは あの時のダイヤモンドダストだから`
>
> `桃香さんだから`
>
> E05 16:46.080-17:01.130

This is the first strong early evidence that Nina can **deidealize without deattaching**. She begins the episode wanting the admired artist to certify the moral world Nina built from the art. She ends the central confrontation by choosing Momoka after the artist has failed to provide that certification.

The attachment becomes less pure and therefore more interpersonal.

Episode 5 then refuses to leave the argument in dialogue. Subaru explicitly proposes that anger, joy, and sadness can all be put into a band. Nina is given responsibility for deciding what the upcoming audience should see. The trio's costumes turn their socially damaging labels into chosen stage text:

- Nina: `不登校`;
- Momoka: `脱退`;
- Subaru: `嘘つき`.

Nina tells the other two that even if they dislike the clothes, they will wear them because they will `一緒に裸になってもらいます` - become naked together.

The phrase deliberately recalls E03, when Momoka used exposure rhetoric to push Nina onto a stage. E05 redistributes that authority. Nina now authors the visual frame, but she does not exempt herself. The costume system is not `I expose you`; it is **we expose the accusations attached to all three of us**.

This gives the first-tranche `conflict becomes composition` hypothesis its strongest evidence yet, but with an important limitation. What E05 directly proves is that **conflict becomes performance form**. The argument about school refusal, quitting, lying, compromise, and public judgment is translated into costume, audience address, band identity, and the framing of the live. The episode does not yet supply enough musical-process evidence to say that those incompatible positions themselves have been encoded into harmony, arrangement, rhythm, or instrumental interaction.

The episode's governing formulation is therefore:

> **E05 is about surviving the loss of moral purity without surrendering attachment or agency. Art can save a listener without belonging to that listener; refusal can preserve integrity while breaking a promise; compromise can be strategic without becoming cowardice; and a relationship becomes more real when admiration survives the discovery that the admired person cannot validate every meaning built from her work.**

The trio's answer is not to purify those contradictions. It puts them on shirts and walks onstage.

# 4. Scene-by-scene close reading

## 4.1 00:00-00:43 - The current Diamond Dust enters first as mediated image

E05 opens not with Nina's band but with current Diamond Dust presenting itself through polished media.

The opening lines are formal self-introduction and debut promotion. Nina notices the person speaking and asks who she is. Momoka answers that she is Diamond Dust's new vocalist and says she has never met her. When Subaru asks whether Nina is interested, Nina retreats into:

> `ううん 何でもない`

At this point the prospective text has not established the vocalist's identity for Nina. The later recognition at the concert must therefore be allowed to happen as an episode event rather than imported backward into the opening.

The scene nevertheless establishes the new Diamond Dust as something Nina encounters first through **commercially legible representation**: a group introduction, coordinated image, and debut framing. E05 will later contrast that legibility with Nina's insistence on the old band's private moral meaning.

The opening also quietly stages Momoka's distance. She knows the position has been filled but says she has not met the new singer. Current Diamond Dust is already operating beyond her social jurisdiction.

The episode then passes through the opening sequence before returning to the trio's small material world.

## 4.2 02:16-04:28 - Serbian Night and Nina's new default answer to performance

The trio visits Serbian Night, an old live house where Momoka has history with the owner.

The venue immediately supplies several pieces of material context. It is small; the stage feels cramped to Nina compared with the previous performance environment; Momoka explains that experienced listeners there will show displeasure openly if a band is bad. The owner jokes that the place is close to failing.

The offer to perform is therefore not romanticized as a glamorous opportunity. It means rehearsal, cost, exposure to a more judgmental audience, and a venue with its own economic precarity.

Momoka gives Nina a genuine exit:

> `気が進まないんだったら 無理しなくていいよ`
>
> E05 03:23.690-03:26.320

Nina answers before Momoka has finished laying out the burden:

> `やります`
>
> E05 03:28.240-03:28.950

Momoka's reaction is diagnostic:

> `ちょっとは悩め`
>
> `すっかり好きになっちゃって`
>
> `この前まで散々嫌がってたのに`

This is the cleanest E05 confirmation of CP1's positive-music prediction. E03 inferred desire from voluntary composition labor. E04 showed post-live exhilaration and instrumental curiosity. E05 gives an immediate affirmative choice to perform even when the next stage is framed as work rather than revelation.

The shift should not be overread as a career decision. Nina has not declared that music will replace university or become her profession. What has changed is her **default behavioral orientation toward participation**. Public performance has moved from something Momoka has to drag her toward into something Nina accepts faster than Momoka considers prudent.

The scene also provides a useful counterexample for an overbroad Momoka-control model. Momoka does not manufacture reluctance and then push through it. She explicitly says Nina does not have to perform if she does not want to. The later CP1 rule therefore remains conditional: Momoka's pressure rises when she thinks fear is blocking a legible desire, not merely because an artistic decision exists.

The owner then hands the trio tickets and removes another possible crutch. They are not to advertise the event as `former Diamond Dust member Momoka plus new band`; he says Momoka is now playing with these two. At this moment the performance is being offered as the trio's own event rather than as a nostalgia act.

That protection will not survive the episode's later promotional machinery.

## 4.3 04:28-06:24 - Ticket quota: Nina's purity meets price

The ticket sequence is one of the most valuable material scenes in the first five episodes because it turns an abstract argument about authentic art into an accounting problem.

Nina gives away a ticket for free. Her rationale is coherent at the level of intention:

> `儲けよりお客さんだよ`
>
> `別にお金欲しくてやってるわけじゃないでしょ`
>
> `たくさんの人に来てもらうのが大事でしょ`

She is not trying to enrich herself. She wants listeners.

Subaru corrects the model on two levels.

First, she gives a behavioral-economic argument: people are more reluctant to waste something they paid for than something received free. Second, she reveals the actual transaction Nina has missed:

> `このチケットって`
>
> `私たちが買い取ったんだよ`
>
> `ライブハウスにお金を払って`

The live house takes participation fees or ticket money in advance so that even if the audience is zero, the venue receives minimum income.

Nina's moral response is immediate:

> `そんなのずるいじゃないですか`

Momoka answers:

> `そういう商売なんだよ`

This exchange strongly confirms the materiality model, but it also shows the limits of Nina's first-pass moral classification. Calling the system `unfair` is not meaningless; ticket quotas shift risk onto performers. But the venue is also economically precarious. The owner has just joked that the live house may fail. The institution is not represented as a rich villain extracting money from powerless artists. It is a small venue using a risk-allocation mechanism to survive.

That is characteristic of E05's larger structure: the episode repeatedly introduces a practice that looks like betrayal, then reveals the competing obligation the practice is trying to preserve.

The scene also exposes a new care/control seam in Momoka. Subaru explains that Momoka has been the one absorbing the expense and has not told the younger girls clearly. Subaru says:

> `桃香さんからしたら 子供なんだよ 私たち`

and grounds the asymmetry materially: Nina and Subaru are younger and have not been living as working adults in the same way.

Momoka's protection here is neither E03-style coercive artistic pressure nor E04-style privacy restraint. It is **burden opacity**. She decides that she will carry a material risk without requiring the younger members to understand or consent to the full cost.

That is caring. It is also hierarchically paternalistic in a narrower and better-supported sense than the broad V1 label. Nina and Subaru cannot become equal participants in risk if Momoka silently absorbs the risk before they know it exists.

E05 therefore adds another dimension to `GBC-V1-C030`: control can arise not only from pushing someone into action but from withholding the cost structure of a shared action in order to protect them.

## 4.4 06:24-08:43 - Diamond Dust tickets and Nina's first overcorrection

The same live-house network that exposes the trio to ticket economics then delivers complimentary tickets to Diamond Dust's major-label debut show.

The owner explains that the current members had visited during the day to thank the venue for its earlier support. He also passes along the industry's simplified story of the split: immediately before debut, the band was asked to change toward more sellable music and a more sellable look; Momoka alone rejected the demand.

The report matters because it gives Nina external evidence for the interpretation she already wants. Momoka did not simply drift away. There was a commercial demand and a refusal.

But the information is still explicitly rumor-level:

> `俺もよくは知らないけど`
>
> `噂だと...`

The episode itself will later replace this partial external story with Momoka's first-person account.

Nina and Subaru decide not to hand the tickets to Momoka because they expect the subject to produce conflict. Their choice is a small information-control act, but it is not a clean test of Subaru's severe compartment-threat rule: the secret is not primarily Subaru's own vulnerable boundary. It should therefore remain contextual evidence rather than a CP1 validation point.

The following prep-school scene is much more diagnostic for Nina.

A classmate who received a free ticket earlier now asks for three more. Nina has absorbed the new rule that tickets cost the band money. She does not simply correct the previous misunderstanding calmly. She jumps to:

> `３枚で7500円です`

When the classmate protests that tickets were free before, Nina invents an explanation about a previous campaign ending, says the ordinary price is 3000 yen, and frames 2500 yen as a special concession. The classmate answers:

> `嘘つき`

Subaru's later gloss is:

> `努力`

The important reconstruction result is not that Nina has suddenly become socially sophisticated. Her story is clumsy enough to be recognized as fabrication. The important result is that she has moved from one categorical rule to another:

1. money is not the point, therefore give tickets away;
2. free tickets create cost and low commitment, therefore charge decisively;
3. if the other person dislikes the condition, `嫌なら 買わなくていいです`.

Subaru says exactly what the checkpoint predicted another person might have to say:

> `ニーナも極端すぎるんだよ`
>
> E05 08:11.230-08:13.440

This strongly confirms `GBC-CP1-P08`. Nina can acquire a more complex practical principle very quickly and then operationalize it too categorically before calibration catches up.

It also refines the relationship between honesty and performance. Nina is capable of tactical presentation when the shared project requires it. But E05 does not present the tactic as mature persuasion. The joke works because her first attempt at strategic social speech is still transparently Nina: literal enough to overexplain, morally abrupt enough to make the transaction adversarial, and inflexible enough that Subaru immediately calls her extreme.

The scene therefore supports **learning capacity without social smoothness**.

## 4.5 08:43-11:02 - The arena, Hina, and self-regulation before verbal harm

Diamond Dust's debut show is staged at a scale Serbian Night cannot approach.

The retained frames establish:

- a large hall;
- coordinated audience light sticks;
- major LED and lighting infrastructure;
- professional styling;
- a visually legible four-member commercial identity;
- an audience large enough for Subaru to remark `さすがソールドアウト`.

The contrast matters because it removes a convenient defense of Momoka's path. Current Diamond Dust has not obviously failed. Whatever one thinks of the compromise, it has produced visibility, audience scale, and institutional power that the trio does not possess.

The polished arena is also the setting in which Nina finally recognizes the new singer.

Her first reaction is not analytical. It is rupture:

> `うそ`
>
> `うそでしょ`
>
> `お前が歌うな`

Outside the hall, Subaru asks what is wrong. Nina stops herself before directing the full verbal attack at another person:

> `なんかすごい汚い言葉を言ってしまいそうだから`
>
> `ちょっと待って`
>
> `先に無意味に叫んで 気持ちだけ吐き出しちゃうから`
>
> E05 10:11.850-10:20.780

She then screams. The regulation attempt only partly succeeds; the release leaks into words:

> `…クソが 死ね`
>
> E05 10:28.370-10:30.040

Subaru immediately notices the failure mode:

> `後半 言葉になっちゃってる`

This is unusually useful character-model evidence.

Nina is not simply overwhelmed by anger. She recognizes in advance that her next language may be destructive and deliberately inserts a **nonlexical discharge step** before explanation. That is a form of self-regulation. It is crude, incomplete, and socially conspicuous, but it is intentional.

The new behavioral candidate is:

> **When Nina anticipates that the next available words will be more damaging than she wants, she can attempt to discharge affect physically or nonlexically before returning to explanation; under sufficient activation, the discharge may still leak into lexical aggression.**

This should not be generalized from one scene into a stable coping technique yet. But it is a valuable counterexample to the idea that Nina is unaware of her own escalation until after the fact.

She then tells Subaru the narrow facts E05 actually supplies about the vocalist:

> `私 知ってる`
>
> `昔友達だった子`
>
> `絶交したけど`

The episode does **not** yet establish the complete school history, Hina's motives, who initiated every break, or the moral structure of the earlier conflict. The correct V2 boundary at E05 is:

- Hina and Nina were once friends;
- they are now estranged;
- Nina describes the relationship using `絶交`;
- Hina had pursued auditions seriously enough to reach advanced stages, according to Subaru's research;
- Hina is now Diamond Dust's vocalist.

Nothing more should be imported into the prospective body.

The symbolic collision is nevertheless clear without hidden history. A person from Nina's abandoned life now occupies the vocalist position in the band whose older work helped Nina survive that life. Hina is therefore not merely `a former friend on a stage`. She has entered the specific artistic object Nina treats as `宝物`.

The sequence also produces a sharp audio-form contrast. The Diamond Dust concert segment measures around -16.7 LUFS in the final mix; Nina's recognition/release sequence remains dense at roughly -18.8 LUFS rather than collapsing into the quieter conversational state seen later in the izakaya. The audiovisual text carries arena energy directly into the emotional rupture.

## 4.6 11:16-12:36 - Fandom, entitlement, and the difference between meaning and ownership

Back in rehearsal space, Momoka notices that Nina is refusing to listen.

Nina's emotional claim quickly becomes explicit:

> `よりによってダイヤモンドダストだよ`
>
> `私が命の次に大事なものだよ`
>
> `宝物だよ`

Momoka does not soothe the fandom claim. She attacks its entitlement structure.

She lists Nina's behavior: Nina went to the live on her own initiative, became angry on her own, and is now complaining as if Diamond Dust has violated an obligation to her. Then:

> `そういうのを何て言うか知ってるか`
>
> `厄介って言うんだよ`

Nina answers by increasing the weight of her reception:

> `私はあそこに集まった人よりは`
>
> `ずっと前からダイヤモンドダスト聴いていて`
>
> `愛してるんです`

That argument is psychologically understandable and normatively dangerous.

Nina is effectively saying:

> I loved this earlier, loved it longer, and needed it more; therefore my account of what Diamond Dust should be deserves special authority.

Momoka refuses the inference:

> `それはお前の事情だろ`
>
> `お前だけのものじゃないんだよ ダイダスは`

This is one of the clearest first-five-episode demonstrations of `GBC-V1-C011`.

The old song's impact on Nina is not invalid because Momoka does not share Nina's current conclusion. Art really did transmit beyond the maker's original use. But interpretation and jurisdiction are separate.

The scene therefore yields a precise three-part model:

1. **Reception fact:** Diamond Dust's old work changed Nina's life.
2. **Interpretive legitimacy:** Nina may truthfully explain what the work meant to her.
3. **Jurisdictional limit:** that meaning does not give Nina unilateral authority over current Diamond Dust, Momoka's feelings, or the new vocalist.

The episode protects all three propositions simultaneously.

# 5. Nina - E05 reconstruction update

## 5.1 Positive musical desire is now overt behavior, not hidden attraction

E05 materially raises confidence in Nina's positive investment in music.

Her immediate `やります` at Serbian Night is not coerced. Momoka explicitly gives her the option not to perform. Nina accepts before deliberating enough for Momoka to tell her to think a little.

She then participates in:

- ticket selling;
- audience recruitment;
- live-house rehearsal;
- conflict processing necessary to resume rehearsal;
- performance concept development;
- costume authorship;
- audience strategy;
- pre-song framing;
- the live itself.

The band is no longer merely something Nina does because Momoka once saved her. Momoka remains central, but Nina is taking independent responsibility for continuing the activity.

`GBC-CP1-P07`: **CONFIRM**.

## 5.2 Fast learning, categorical first implementation

The ticket sequence is a near textbook instance of the CP1 overapplication rule.

Nina learns two real facts:

1. free tickets can reduce attendance commitment;
2. the band itself bears the cost of unsold/given-away tickets.

Her first correction is not calibrated persuasion. It is an abrupt pricing regime backed by an improvised campaign story and `嫌なら 買わなくていいです`.

Subaru's `ニーナも極端すぎるんだよ` provides internal confirmation that the implementation is too binary.

Current rule:

> **Nina can revise her stated principle quickly when presented with concrete causal information, but her first implementation often preserves the same all-or-nothing structure under a new rule.**

This is a stronger formulation than `Nina is stubborn`. She can change her mind quickly; what persists is the shape of application.

`GBC-CP1-P08`: **CONFIRM**.

## 5.3 Fandom as jurisdictional overreach

E05 gives the clearest evidence yet that Nina's attachment can become possessive in a specific domain.

Her reasoning is not merely `I dislike the new band`. It is:

- I loved Diamond Dust earlier than these people;
- I love it more intensely;
- the music is bound to my life;
- therefore the current form and Momoka's verdict ought to answer to the meaning I received.

Momoka explicitly rejects the last step.

This sharpens the CP1 bidirectional-jurisdiction model. Nina's own history of being defined by others does not automatically generate respect for other people's interpretive boundaries.

When an external object becomes part of Nina's identity architecture, she can treat changes to that object as if they require her moral consent.

## 5.4 Hina trigger and pre-emptive affect discharge

The Hina recognition sequence adds a new regulation behavior.

Nina knows she is about to say something vicious. Rather than immediately directing it at Subaru or returning to the hall, she announces the problem and asks for time to scream first.

The strategy is imperfect: `クソが 死ね` emerges anyway.

But the self-awareness is important.

Candidate rule:

> **Under sudden high-intensity anger, Nina may attempt to separate affect discharge from relational communication when she predicts her words will become destructive. The separation is incomplete under maximum activation.**

This rule remains `NEW / PROVISIONAL` after one episode.

## 5.5 High-affect politeness remains real

The Momoka argument is one of Nina's angriest sustained exchanges so far, yet much of her speech remains morphologically polite:

- `分かりません`;
- `なんで...なんです`;
- `言ってくださいよ`;
- `ですよね`;
- `嫌です`;
- `好きです`.

The episode simultaneously gives rough speech in a different target/context:

- `お前が歌うな` toward Hina from the audience;
- `クソが 死ね` during deliberate discharge.

That distribution strongly supports the partner/state model rather than `anger causes rough language`.

There is no strong new E05 evidence requiring a fresh claim about regional/Kumamoto forms. The held-out support comes from high-affect politeness, not from dialect.

`GBC-CP1-P06`: **CONFIRM**.

## 5.6 Anger: diagnostic success and inferential overreach in the same argument

Nina is **right** that Momoka's abstract `bands change` answer is insufficient.

If Momoka left specifically because a change was unacceptable, then `change happens` cannot by itself explain the rupture.

Nina's anger finds the concealed variable.

But she then insists that the only coherent answer must be:

> current Diamond Dust is wrong;
> Momoka was right;
> the old band alone is real Diamond Dust.

The eventual history proves that the decision space was more complicated.

This is perhaps the cleanest early evidence for the mature `GBC-V1-C018` formulation:

> **Nina's anger can identify where an account is evasive or incomplete while still generating a false binary about what the missing truth must be.**

Her epistemic strength and her inferential weakness are not opposites. They are part of the same process.

## 5.7 Deidealization without deattachment

The central relational development is not an apology and not a solved argument.

Nina learns something that makes Momoka less usable as proof of uncomplicated righteousness. Instead of withdrawing, she says `それでも` and chooses her.

Candidate rule:

> **When an admired person's imperfection is made concrete rather than dismissive of Nina's lived meaning, Nina can preserve attachment by shifting from idealized correctness to the specific effect the person and her work had on Nina.**

This rule is `NEW / STRONGLY EMERGING` after E05. It will require counterexamples, especially in relationships where the adverse information concerns harm to Nina directly rather than a third-party history.

## 5.8 Exposure becomes self-authored collective form

Nina's costume decision also matters for autonomy.

She does impose the shirts on the others, so the old jurisdiction problem has not vanished. But she places herself under the same exposure rule. The action is therefore different from one-sided control.

The provisional reconstruction result is:

> **Nina is increasingly willing to convert shame into chosen public form when she can author the terms and when the exposure is shared rather than unilateral.**

That is a major step from E01's defensive need to protect the meaning of her past from other people's definitions.

# 6. Momoka - E05 reconstruction update

## 6.1 Protection through burden absorption

E05 adds a distinct form of Momoka care:

> **she may protect younger collaborators by silently absorbing financial risk.**

The ticket quota is a real shared cost, but Nina learns about it after already giving tickets away. Subaru interprets Momoka's omission through age/work asymmetry: Momoka sees the younger two as children relative to her own adult experience.

This is supportive and paternalistic at once.

The best current `care/control` model now has at least three separate mechanisms:

1. **logistical rescue** - food, shelter, practical help;
2. **artistic intervention** - pushing when she believes fear blocks an already-real desire;
3. **protective burden opacity** - absorbing cost or consequence without fully distributing information.

They should not be collapsed into one trait called `controlling`.

## 6.2 Diamond Dust rupture: integrity against integrity

E05 closes CP1's largest historical unknown.

The old group's explicit ideal was continuing with the original members indefinitely. The label's commercial redirection created a conflict between:

- immediate artistic noncompliance;
- relational continuity;
- strategic patience;
- future artistic leverage.

Momoka chose immediate noncompliance and exited despite the others' appeals.

This strongly supports `GBC-V1-C029`:

> **Momoka preserved one form of artistic integrity by breaking the relational promise of original Diamond Dust.**

The phrase `one form` is essential. The episode does not authorize the conclusion that she had no integrity or that the others did. It establishes incompatible integrity claims.

## 6.3 Why Momoka refuses to condemn current Diamond Dust

The new history materially improves the jurisdiction model.

Momoka says:

> `私はもうあそこにはいないんだ`
>
> `あとは自由だ`
>
> `私にそれを否定する権利はない`

After the confession, those lines can be understood as more than abstract liberal tolerance.

Momoka is the member who left despite a founding promise never to leave. The remaining members stayed and pursued the strategy they had argued for. Her refusal to police their present is therefore grounded in **self-limited authority**.

The causal model is now:

> **Because Momoka sees herself as the person who exited the original compact, she is reluctant to convert her old disagreement into continuing ownership over the people who remained.**

This does not mean she has no aesthetic preference. It means she refuses to make that preference sovereign.

## 6.4 Revision to the V1 `neutrality is partly a lie` claim

V1 inferred that Momoka's present neutrality toward Diamond Dust was partly false because guilt prevented her from defending her real view.

E05 V2 should narrow that.

Directly supported:

- Momoka rejected the label-directed change;
- she still avoids giving Nina the condemnation Nina wants;
- she calls the remaining members free;
- she says she lacks the right to deny their path;
- she sees herself as the one who broke the old relational promise.

Not directly supported:

- that she secretly hates the current Diamond Dust;
- that `好きでも嫌いでもない` is knowingly false;
- that guilt is the only reason for her current position.

Current formulation:

> **Momoka's current neutrality is at minimum a sincere jurisdictional stance shaped by guilt and by recognition of the others' agency. Her unresolved personal aesthetic feeling remains open.**

This is a meaningful V2 revision because it refuses to turn subtext into established interior fact.

## 6.5 Roughness remains multifunctional

E05 gives a compact range:

- `うるせえな` as escalating irritation;
- `それはお前の事情だろ` as hard boundary-setting;
- `勝手に決めつけるな` as defensive correction;
- `黙れ` as genuine termination pressure;
- `バカだな` as affiliative reception after Nina's declaration;
- `鼻垂れてるぞ` as intimacy-deflecting teasing.

The same broad rough register can carry threat, correction, humor, and care.

`GBC-CP1-P11`: **CONFIRM**.

## 6.6 Drinking as aftermath, not explanatory master key

Momoka drinks heavily after the disclosure. The episode permits the inference that the Diamond Dust argument is emotionally costly. It does not justify using alcohol as a universal explanation for her avoidance or care style.

The most useful reconstruction note is narrower:

> **After sustained exposure of a guilt-laden history, Momoka may increase alcohol use and become more physically dependent on companions while continuing to deny or deflect direct sentimental labeling.**

This remains contextual rather than a stabilized trait.

# 7. Subaru - E05 reconstruction update

## 7.1 Practical cognition and the economics of audience behavior

Subaru understands the ticket quota before Nina does and explains both the financial structure and a basic audience-commitment heuristic.

This extends her social competence beyond politeness. She is not only good at smoothing interactions; she is often better than Nina at modeling how ordinary people will actually behave.

Her explanation is pragmatic rather than moralizing:

- paid things are less likely to be wasted;
- free things can be discarded without loss;
- giving tickets away transfers cost to the band.

That makes Subaru a practical interpreter between Nina's intention and social consequence.

## 7.2 `嘘` as tool, stigma, and self-knowledge

E05 repeatedly circles Subaru's relationship to lying.

She openly explains that wearing a school uniform is convenient and produces benefits. Nina calls her `嘘つき`. Earlier, Subaru labels Nina's improvised sales deception `努力` rather than treating any tactical presentation as identity corruption.

The stage later turns `嘘つき` into Subaru's own shirt.

This does not mean Subaru is indifferent to truth. E03 explicitly argued that surface agreement can create destructive misalignment, and E04 showed that she values directness inside chosen relationships. E05 clarifies the distinction:

> **Subaru is relatively comfortable with tactical social performance when the stakes are legible and bounded; she is much less comfortable with relational smoothing that prevents necessary conflict from being named.**

That is a useful reconstruction rule because it predicts when `performance` is likely to feel acceptable to her.

## 7.3 Public/private switching remains coherent

During the izakaya fight Subaru can tell the pair bluntly to finish their quarrel, while turning toward surrounding patrons with:

> `皆さん お気になさらず`

She manages public disruption without ceasing to pressure Nina and Momoka privately.

This is direct held-out support for the checkpoint's audience-specific register model.

`GBC-CP1-P13`: **CONFIRM**.

## 7.4 Mediator, not peacemaker

E05 strengthens but also further specifies `GBC-V1-C037`.

Subaru does not try to make Nina and Momoka agree quickly. Her process is:

- identify that rehearsal is impossible in the current state;
- require the fight to be addressed first;
- move them into a context where it can continue;
- manage the public spillover;
- provide missing factual context;
- later convert the emotional result into a performance task.

This is not conventional harmony maintenance. Subaru's function is closer to **conflict sequencing**.

She is willing to let the argument become more explicit if doing so is necessary for the band to become workable again.

## 7.5 Positive band valuation continues independently of acting resistance

The line:

> `怒りも 喜びも 哀しさも`
>
> `全部ぶちこめるのが バンドのいいところ`

is not about escaping her grandmother.

It is a positive theory of what collective musical practice affords.

Combined with her continued voluntary labor, mediation, and costume planning, this is held-out confirmation that the band is becoming positively valued in its own right.

`GBC-CP1-P15`: **CONFIRM**.

## 7.6 The E05 limit on Subaru inference

E05 does **not** strongly test:

- whether Subaru will deceive allies under severe compartment threat;
- whether she can directly refuse Tendo;
- whether acting-based mediation survives maximum family pressure.

Those CP1 predictions remain unscored here rather than being opportunistically confirmed from weaker analogies.

# 8. Hina and Diamond Dust - source-boundary update

E05 is the first episode in the V2 pass to place Hina directly inside Nina's present rather than leaving her as an unnamed or historically implied figure. It is therefore especially important not to let later-series knowledge rush into the gap.

## 8.1 What E05 actually establishes about Hina

At the Diamond Dust concert, Nina recognizes the current vocalist and reacts first with disbelief:

> `うそ`
>
> `うそでしょ`
>
> `お前が歌うな`

After leaving the venue, she tells Subaru only:

> `私 知ってる`
>
> `昔友達だった子`
>
> `絶交したけど`

Subaru adds that Hina had been auditioning and had advanced far in those processes before becoming Diamond Dust's vocalist.

That is the secure E05 state:

- Hina and Nina were formerly friends;
- that friendship ended badly enough for Nina to describe it as `絶交`;
- Hina pursued performance through auditions;
- by E05 she is the vocalist of the current Diamond Dust;
- seeing her in that position produces an unusually violent affective rupture in Nina.

E05 does **not** yet establish the complete school history, the distribution of responsibility in the earlier rupture, Hina's private interpretation of Nina, or the motives with which Hina joined Diamond Dust. Those remain outside the prospective boundary.

Accordingly, `GBC-V1-C052` and `GBC-V1-C053` remain **OPEN**. The episode does not yet justify either a simple-betrayer model or a coherent `strategic accommodation` philosophy for Hina herself. `GBC-V1-C056`, by contrast, is **STRENGTHENED**: the asymmetry of access is already obvious. Nina's reaction is available in detail; Hina's interiority is not.

## 8.2 Hina is a collision of symbolic positions before she is yet a reconstructed person

Even without importing later history, E05 makes the structural violence of Hina's appearance legible.

Diamond Dust is entangled with Nina's account of survival and resistance. Momoka is the creator whose older music Nina has used as evidence that refusal can matter. Hina is a person from the life Nina severed. When Hina appears as the new vocalist of Diamond Dust, these previously separate symbolic systems collapse into one body.

That helps explain why Nina's first response is not an aesthetic judgment of the performance but a jurisdictional command:

> `お前が歌うな`

The sentence is revealing precisely because Nina does not possess the authority it asserts. At E05, she has not established that Hina lacks musical competence, that Diamond Dust lacks the right to choose her, or that Momoka retains veto power over the band she left. Nina's reaction is psychologically intelligible and still jurisdictionally excessive.

This is one of E05's strongest examples of the checkpoint distinction between **meaning** and **control**. Hina's presence can violate the meaning Nina built around Diamond Dust without thereby becoming objectively unauthorized.

## 8.3 Diamond Dust's adaptation is made harder to caricature

The episode gives two different kinds of evidence about the current Diamond Dust.

First, the live-house owner reports an industry account: before major debut, the group was allegedly pushed toward more commercially legible music and appearance, Momoka alone refused, and the others remained.

Second, the audiovisual text shows the result. The current Diamond Dust performs in a much larger venue than Serbian Night, before a sold-out audience, with coordinated light sticks, large LED surfaces, synchronized styling, polished lighting, and a highly legible professional stage identity.

The point is not that scale proves artistic superiority. It is that the choice produced something materially real: reach, audience, visibility, and professional continuity. The episode denies Nina the easy comfort of treating the remaining members as people who merely failed.

Momoka later supplies the missing internal history. The other original members did not simply say `sell out`. They argued for endurance:

> `今は耐えろ`

The strategy, as Momoka recounts it, was to accept the present compromise, rise high enough to gain leverage, and later make what they wanted. Whether that strategy ultimately succeeds is unknown at E05. But it is coherent enough that `GBC-V1-C055` is **STRENGTHENED STRONGLY**: the commercial path is a rival survival strategy, not a disposable foil for indie purity.

The same evidence gives `GBC-V1-C074` **STRENGTHEN / PARTIAL** status. Strategic accommodation may preserve sincere intention, but E05 does not grant enough private access to the current members - and especially not Hina - to claim that every element of the current Diamond Dust presentation is secretly continuous with the old group's artistic intention.

# 9. Nina and Momoka - relationship delta

E05 is the first episode in which Nina's attachment to Momoka survives a direct collapse of idealization.

That is a more consequential development than simply learning why Diamond Dust split.

## 9.1 The relationship reaches its most explicitly fan-like form

Nina's language after the Diamond Dust concert makes the possession problem impossible to leave implicit.

Diamond Dust is:

> `私が命の次に大事なもの`
>
> `宝物`

She defends the authority of her judgment partly through duration and intensity:

> `私はあそこに集まった人よりは`
>
> `ずっと前からダイヤモンドダスト聴いていて`
>
> `愛してるんです`

Momoka refuses the implied hierarchy:

> `それはお前の事情だろ`
>
> `お前だけのものじゃないんだよ ダイダスは`

This is the strongest direct E01-E05 evidence for `GBC-V1-C025`. Nina's attachment is not merely admiration. Under threat, the meaning she received from the work can become a claim on what the work, the band, and the creator are allowed to become.

The important correction is that this entitlement is not evidence that the love is fake. The episode's ethical force depends on both propositions being true at once:

- Nina genuinely loves the music and has been changed by it;
- that genuine transformation does not grant ownership over Diamond Dust or Momoka.

## 9.2 Nina reproduces the very invasion she fears

The izakaya confrontation turns the checkpoint's autonomy model back on Nina.

She insists that Momoka must care, that the old band was Momoka's soul, that Momoka left because she knew she was right, and that Momoka must say the current Diamond Dust is wrong.

Momoka's response is exact:

> `勝手に決めつけるな`

and later:

> `人ん中に土足でズカズカ入ってきやがって`

This is not a minor irony. E01 began with Nina demanding not to be decided from outside. E03 gave direct evidence that unwanted closeness can feel like another person scripting the relationship. E05 now shows Nina assigning Momoka an interior state and demanding the verbal verdict Nina needs from it.

The current relationship rule is therefore stronger than `Nina confuses influence with control`.

> **When an attachment has become part of Nina's identity defense, she may treat the other person's autonomous reinterpretation as a threat to the reality of what that person once gave her. Under that pressure she can demand jurisdiction over the other's present meaning in order to stabilize her own past.**

This materially **STRENGTHENS / REVISES** `GBC-V1-C019`.

## 9.3 Nina's demand is partly about herself without being reducible to herself

Nina's strongest lines make the overlap explicit:

> `私はただ 桃香さんがやってたダイヤモンドダストが好きなんです`
>
> `あの歌で人生が変わったんです`
>
> `だから 負けてほしくないんです`
>
> `納得もしてないのに`
>
> `自分が悪い 向こうが正しいなんて`
>
> `そんなうそ 言ってほしくないんです`

The temptation is to reduce this to projection: Nina needs Momoka to be right because Nina needs herself to be right.

The source supports a narrower, more useful claim. Nina's self-account and Momoka's refusal have become entangled. If Momoka describes her own departure only as error while the current Diamond Dust becomes the successful continuation, Nina experiences pressure on the ethic she drew from Momoka's old songs.

But Nina is also responding to Momoka as a concrete person whose work she loves and whose self-condemnation she does not want to accept. The scene contains self-protection and care simultaneously.

`GBC-V1-C017` is therefore **STRENGTHENED WITH BOUNDARY**: the `I am not wrong` structure remains identity-protective scar tissue, but E05 should not be flattened into `Nina is only using Momoka to defend herself`.

## 9.4 Momoka's confession removes moral purity from the attachment

Momoka's account of the old Diamond Dust is devastating because it denies Nina the clean martyr she is asking for.

The group's foundational promise was relational:

> `ガールズバンドで`
>
> `ばあさんになっても`
>
> `オリジナルメンバーで続けようって`

She says:

> `絶対にやめないって`

and then sharply demotes the importance of a fixed artistic doctrine:

> `音楽性も主張もない`
>
> `目指していたのはそれだけ`

When the label demanded change, the others pleaded with Momoka to endure temporarily. They did not abandon her casually:

> `あいつらは 最後まで必死に説得してくれた`

Momoka concludes:

> `それでもやめたんだ 私は`
>
> `だから 悪いのは私なんだ`

The V2 formulation is now direct enough to stabilize `GBC-V1-C029` strongly:

> **Momoka preserved immediate artistic integrity by breaking the original Diamond Dust's central relational promise.**

That is not the same as saying Momoka's choice was morally wrong. Nor does it prove the others' strategy was prudent or destined to succeed. It means that `integrity` names competing obligations rather than a clean escape from compromise.

## 9.5 `それでも` is the relationship's first major deidealization test

Nina's answer is not `No, you were right after all.`

It is:

> `それでも…`
>
> `それでも 私は桃香さんが好きです`
>
> `桃香さんの歌が好きだから`
>
> `私の心を動かしたのは あの時のダイヤモンドダストだから`
>
> `桃香さんだから`

The repeated `それでも` is structurally important. Nina does not erase Momoka's admission. She keeps it in the sentence and chooses attachment across it.

That creates a new, strongly emerging reconstruction rule:

> **Nina is capable of deidealization without immediate deattachment when the relationship has accumulated enough concrete shared history.**

The basis of attachment shifts. Momoka no longer needs to function only as proof that Nina's moral universe is correct. She can be the imperfect person whose music genuinely changed Nina and whom Nina now knows directly.

This is early major support for `GBC-V1-C021`, though the full V1 development sequence remains premature. It also **STRENGTHENS / REVISES FOR DEVELOPMENT** `GBC-V1-C025` and `GBC-V1-C057`.

The relationship remains asymmetric. Nina's investment is still unusually intense; Momoka remains older, more experienced, and artistically formative. E05 does not dissolve that difference. It makes the attachment more interpersonal because it survives information that damages the idolized version.

## 9.6 Care begins to move back toward Momoka

After the conflict and disclosure, Momoka drinks heavily. Nina and Subaru later support the intoxicated Momoka physically.

The retained visual evidence is enough to establish role reversal at the level of practical care: the older figure who has repeatedly fed, housed, transported, paid for, and organized the younger girls becomes bodily dependent on them for the night.

Subaru suggests Momoka may have been happy. Momoka denies it:

> `違うよ`
>
> `違う`

V2 does not overrule her denial. The stronger claim is behavioral: after revealing guilt and receiving Nina's `それでも`, Momoka allows herself to be physically cared for while resisting a direct sentimental label.

That is useful future evidence for intimacy and care circulation without requiring mind-reading.

# 10. Music, performance, and authorship

E05 advances the band from `shared musical output` toward something more specific: **shared authorship over how conflict is presented publicly**.

It still does not fully prove the stricter V1 thesis that interpersonal conflict becomes musical composition.

## 10.1 Subaru names the band as an affect container

After the central rupture, Subaru gives Nina responsibility for the live costume and frames the band itself as a place capable of taking incompatible emotional matter:

> `怒りも 喜びも 哀しさも`
>
> `全部ぶちこめるのが`
>
> `バンドのいいところ`

This statement is important because Subaru is not describing the band only as resistance to acting or as a social refuge. She articulates a positive affordance of the form: it can hold anger, happiness, and sadness without requiring one emotion to win before performance begins.

That is direct support for the growing ensemble model.

## 10.2 Nina receives real authorship over presentation

E03 gave Nina some compositional input inside a Momoka-led arrangement. E05 gives her a different domain of authority.

The event page prominently advertises:

> `元ダイヤモンドダスト 桃香出演`

The audience is therefore likely to include people arriving through Momoka's prior identity. Subaru makes that consequence explicit and asks Nina to decide what they want those people to see.

Nina's answer is not to imitate old Diamond Dust or to erase biography. She chooses a deliberately confrontational visual system:

- Nina: `不登校`
- Momoka: `脱退`
- Subaru: `嘘つき`

The budget is limited; the concept depends on plain shirts and text rather than expensive stage image construction. Yet the low-cost design is semantically dense. Each member wears a stigmatizing description already attached to her history.

Nina insists:

> `気に入らなくても 着てもらいます`
>
> `一緒に裸になってもらいますから`

This echoes Momoka's E03 rhetoric of emotional nakedness, but authorship has moved. The youngest and least experienced member now determines the public frame through which Momoka's past, Subaru's performance identity, and Nina's school history will be displayed.

That **STRENGTHENS `GBC-V1-C033`**. Shared authorship is not yet equal in every musical domain, but Momoka is no longer the sole author of the group's presentation.

## 10.3 The costume does not claim purity; it stages accusation

The shirts are not declarations that each accusation is a complete self-definition.

They externalize social labels and make them visible before the audience can use them as hidden disqualifications. This is why the sequence belongs to the E01-E05 authenticity model.

The form is unmistakably constructed:

- the labels are selected;
- the shirts are designed;
- the trio chooses when to reveal them;
- the live is promoted;
- the audience has been strategically assembled partly through Momoka's name.

Yet the constructedness does not cancel emotional truth. The performance makes the contradiction public.

This strongly supports `GBC-V1-C008`:

> **recognizable intention can survive - and sometimes require - deliberate form.**

## 10.4 `視界の隅 朽ちる音` is a dramatic action, not a reward after reconciliation

The source title frame at approximately E05 21:30 identifies the performing unit as `新川崎（仮）` and the insert song as `視界の隅 朽ちる音`.

The band enters the song after a live argument whose underlying ethical questions remain unresolved. Nina and Momoka have not agreed on whether Momoka's departure was `right`; Nina has not solved her Hina history; the current Diamond Dust remains autonomous; the material reality of venue economics remains intact.

The live therefore does not arrive because dialogue has produced consensus.

Nina's pre-performance language explicitly brings unresolved affect into the event:

> `思っていることを叫べ`
>
> `何も飾らず叫びたいことだけを叫べ`
>
> `不満も怒りも悲しさも`
>
> `みっともなさも恥ずかしさも`
>
> `布団かぶって叫びたくなる過去も`
>
> `思い出すだけで 全身から汗が噴き出す後悔も`
>
> `全部さらけ出して`

and the transition into performance culminates in:

> `全部を晒して 生きてやる`

This **STRENGTHENS `GBC-V1-C068`**: performance functions as an action upon unresolved conflict.

## 10.5 `爪痕` revises what transmission means

Nina describes Momoka's song as having left a `爪痕` in her:

> `私 桃香さんの歌に胸をえぐられたんです`
>
> `爪痕残されたんです`
>
> `死んでも負けんなって`

Then she proposes:

> `爪痕 つけて行きましょう`

The metaphor is aggressive but analytically precise. Art matters because it can alter another person without the creator controlling the exact resulting interpretation. Nina's entire E05 conflict has demonstrated the danger of confusing that transformation with ownership.

By choosing to leave a mark rather than demanding total interpretive possession, the live reframes fandom into transmission.

That is one of the strongest E05 supports for `GBC-V1-C011`.

## 10.6 Does conflict become composition yet?

Not fully.

E05 provides strong evidence for a weaker but increasingly important proposition:

> **conflict becomes performance form.**

The argument changes:

- the costume concept;
- the public framing;
- Nina's manifesto;
- the emotional meaning of the live;
- who holds presentational authority.

But the available E05 evidence does not yet show, at the level demanded by `GBC-V1-C015`, how opposed positions are preserved in harmonic, rhythmic, melodic, lyrical, or arrangement structure through explicit member negotiation.

`GBC-V1-C015` therefore receives **PARTIAL / REMAIN OPEN**.

`GBC-CP1-P12` likewise receives **PARTIAL**, not CONFIRM.

# 11. Visual form and CG acting

## 11.1 Two performance spaces, two modes of legibility

E05 places Diamond Dust's major-label arena and the trio's small live house in unusually direct comparison.

Diamond Dust is rendered through:

- large LED surfaces;
- cool neon coloration;
- coordinated audience light sticks;
- standardized professional styling;
- large physical distance between performers and much of the audience;
- a highly legible branded identity.

The later `新川崎（仮）` performance is much smaller:

- the audience is physically close;
- warm/gold lighting dominates many retained frames;
- the three stigma shirts remain readable as bodies occupy the stage;
- the group's provisional name itself remains visibly unfinished.

The contrast should not be moralized into `cold corporate falsity versus warm indie truth`.

That would contradict the episode's material argument. The trio also uses ticket quotas, promotion, costume strategy, reputational leverage, and audience targeting. Diamond Dust's path has produced real collective success.

The visual difference is better understood as **scale and mode of legibility**. Diamond Dust has become a professionally standardized object readable at arena scale. The trio makes individual biographical contradiction readable at close range.

## 11.2 Nina's Hina recognition is a bodily rupture before an explanation

The concert sequence gives Nina's recognition of Hina through face, gaze, and abrupt affective destabilization before she supplies Subaru with the relationship label `昔友達だった子`.

Her immediate verbal output - `うそ`, `うそでしょ`, `お前が歌うな` - is fragmentary and jurisdictional rather than explanatory.

Outside, she then attempts a bodily regulation strategy: scream first, explain after.

This supports `GBC-V1-C067` in a new way. E01-E04 had already shown body-first action under threatened irreversibility. E05 shows **body-first affect management**: Nina knows language is about to become destructive and tries to move the activation through nonlexical sound.

## 11.3 The izakaya gives faces priority over ideological abstraction

The central Diamond Dust argument occurs in an ordinary, crowded eating/drinking space rather than in a stylized musical arena.

The retained frame sequence repeatedly closes distance around Nina and Momoka as abstract claims - freedom, change, rightness, lying - become personal accusations. The room also matters because Subaru must manage bystanders. The conflict is simultaneously intimate and socially exposed.

That visual/social arrangement strengthens the episode's claim that ideology cannot be separated from interpersonal consequence.

## 11.4 Momoka becomes physically dependent after disclosing guilt

The intoxication aftermath visually reverses the early care pattern. Momoka, usually the practical adult and transport/logistics provider, is supported by Nina and Subaru.

This is not enough to infer that alcohol simply reveals a hidden `true` Momoka. It is enough to establish an embodied reduction in self-management after the episode's most difficult disclosure and a willingness - whether chosen in advance or not - to be carried by the people she has been carrying.

## 11.5 The stigma shirts turn biography into body surface

`不登校`, `脱退`, and `嘘つき` are not hidden lyrical references. They are attached directly to bodies onstage.

The staging makes a social judgment inseparable from the performer who has survived it, while refusing to let the word exhaust the person. A viewer must see the label and the active body at once.

That is a particularly clear example of CG acting and costume functioning as argument rather than decoration. `GBC-V1-C066` is therefore **STRENGTHENED**.

# 12. Material and institutional layer

E05 is the strongest material-institutional episode of the V2 pass so far.

## 12.1 Ticket quotas make the economics of independent performance explicit

Nina begins with a morally attractive but economically naive position:

> `儲けよりお客さんだよ`
>
> `別にお金欲しくてやってるわけじゃないでしょ`
>
> `たくさんの人に来てもらうのが大事でしょ`

She gives a ticket away because audience matters more than revenue.

Subaru explains the behavioral problem: people are more likely to use something for which they have paid. More importantly, she reveals the cost structure:

> `このチケットって`
>
> `私たちが買い取ったんだよ`

The live house secures income in advance through participation fees or ticket obligations. Nina's response is moral:

> `そんなのずるいじゃないですか`

Momoka's is institutional:

> `そういう商売なんだよ`

The exchange directly strengthens `GBC-V1-C014` and `GBC-CP1-P19`. Artistic expression is constrained by a venue economy before the group reaches anything resembling major-label pressure.

## 12.2 Momoka's generosity contains information asymmetry

Subaru explains that Momoka has been absorbing the ticket cost because she sees Nina and Subaru as younger people who are not yet working adults.

That is care. It also means Momoka decides the distribution of risk without initially giving the others full information.

This creates a new candidate rule:

> **Momoka may protect younger collaborators through burden opacity: she quietly absorbs costs or consequences she considers hers to carry, but the same protection can reduce the others' ability to consent to the actual material risk as equal collaborators.**

This **STRENGTHENS / REVISES `GBC-V1-C030`**.

It does not yet prove the stronger self-erasure thesis of `GBC-V1-C031`. E05 confirms recurrent generosity and unilateral burden absorption; whether the motive is self-erasure remains OPEN.

## 12.3 Nina's ticket lie shows that `purity` can externalize cost

Once Nina learns that tickets must be sold, she overcorrects. She fabricates a campaign explanation and a discount structure when a prep-school acquaintance asks why previously free tickets now cost money.

The joke works because Nina moves from one categorical rule to another:

1. money is not the point, so give tickets away;
2. tickets must be sold, so construct whatever sales explanation gets them sold.

Subaru calls the deception `努力`; later she also tells Nina she is too extreme.

The institutional lesson is not `lying is necessary`. It is that Nina's initial purity had a hidden beneficiary and a hidden payer. If Nina refuses all commercial mediation, Momoka pays.

That is exactly the sort of material consequence the V2 architecture is designed to keep visible.

## 12.4 Major-label and live-house systems are different scales of the same problem

The old Diamond Dust faced pressure to become more sellable before major debut. The current trio faces ticket quotas, audience acquisition, promotion, and budget constraints before a small live-house show.

The pressures differ enormously in scale and in the degree of aesthetic intervention, but neither route exists outside institutions.

This strongly **STRENGTHENS `GBC-V1-C006`**: the series is not an anti-industry purity fantasy.

## 12.5 Reputation becomes an economic resource

The event page advertises Momoka as former Diamond Dust despite the earlier desire not to build the live around that identity. The practical result is significant: the show sells out, and Subaru anticipates that some people from the Diamond Dust audience will attend.

Momoka's past is therefore not only emotional history. It is marketable reputation.

The trio does not control every use of that reputation, but once it becomes part of the event's audience economy they must decide how to answer it onstage. Nina's costume concept is partially a response to that institutional appropriation.

This is an important bridge between the material ledger and the authorship ledger: **promotion changes the interpretive audience before the music begins.**

## 12.6 Costume itself is budget-constrained

The group cannot spend much on outfits. The eventual shirts are therefore not only an aesthetic idea; they are an idea selected inside a financial ceiling.

That makes their apparent `rawness` materially produced. Simplicity here is not evidence of an absence of mediation. It is a creative response to constraint.

Across E05, `authentic indie performance` is therefore impossible to separate from:

- ticket economics;
- personal subsidy;
- persuasion and sales;
- inherited reputation;
- event promotion;
- audience composition;
- costume budget;
- venue survival.

`GBC-V1-C014` is **STRENGTHENED STRONGLY**.

# 13. Speech, voice, and interaction delta

E05 is unusually useful for separating **affect intensity** from **register choice**. It also supplies the first clear evidence that Nina can attempt to regulate destructive speech before it emerges.

## 13.1 Nina - politeness survives one of her most severe Momoka conflicts

The izakaya confrontation is among Nina's highest-stakes arguments so far. She is asking Momoka to issue a verdict about Diamond Dust, artistic compromise, and the meaning of a song Nina ties to her own changed life.

Yet much of the attack remains morphologically polite:

> `今のダイダスは違うって言ってくださいよ`
>
> `自分が間違ってないって思ったから やめたんですよね`
>
> `そんなうそ 言ってほしくないんです`

Even the direct refusal after Momoka's `黙れ` is:

> `嫌です`

This is strong held-out confirmation of `GBC-CP1-P06`.

Nina's register cannot be modeled as `anger rises -> politeness disappears`. High affect can intensify insistence while leaving the grammatical frame polite. Politeness is therefore partly upbringing and interactional armor, not a simple index of calmness.

## 13.2 Nina - rough lexical aggression is partner- and state-specific

The Hina recognition scene gives the counterstate.

Nina first says:

> `お前が歌うな`

Then, outside the venue, she explicitly anticipates that her language may become unacceptable:

> `なんかすごい汚い言葉を言ってしまいそうだから`
>
> `ちょっと待って`
>
> `先に無意味に叫んで 気持ちだけ吐き出しちゃうから`

She tries to convert the activation into nonlexical screaming. It fails partially:

> `…クソが 死ね`

Subaru immediately notices the leakage into words.

This yields a **NEW / PROVISIONAL** voice-regulation rule:

> **When Nina anticipates verbally destructive output, she can attempt to discharge affect nonlexically before explanation; under sufficiently high activation, lexical aggression can leak through the regulation attempt.**

This is important because it shows anticipatory awareness. Nina does not only discover after the fact that she has escalated.

It also reinforces the CP1 dialect/register model: the roughest language in E05 is directed toward the Hina-triggered state, not mechanically toward the person with whom Nina is having the longest or most conceptually intense argument.

## 13.3 Nina - tactical lying appears before tactical fluency

The ticket-selling scene demonstrates that Nina can learn the *idea* of strategic presentation almost immediately.

She invents a campaign, normal price, and special discount. The performance is functional enough to make the social tactic visible, but clumsy enough that the classmate calls her a liar.

Subaru's `努力` and later `ニーナも極端すぎるんだよ` together prevent an overreading.

Nina is not suddenly a sophisticated social manipulator. She has learned that communication can be instrumentally shaped and then applied the lesson with her usual categorical intensity.

This strongly confirms `GBC-CP1-P08` and adds a useful reconstruction distinction:

> **Nina can acquire a social tactic faster than she can calibrate its ordinary range.**

## 13.4 Nina - `それでも` marks a different kind of directness

Nina's attachment statement after Momoka's confession is neither a shouted manifesto nor a defensive argument about rightness:

> `それでも 私は桃香さんが好きです`

The line is important not merely for content but for interactional form. Nina has spent much of the episode demanding that Momoka say something. Here she stops demanding a verdict and states her own position.

That shift from `say what I need you to say` to `this is what I choose` is one of E05's most important speech-level developments.

## 13.5 Momoka - roughness remains multifunctional

E05 offers an unusually clean internal comparison.

During genuine termination pressure:

> `黙れ`

Elsewhere roughness functions as ordinary pushback or irritation. After Nina's deidealizing affirmation, Momoka says:

> `バカだな`
>
> `鼻垂れてるぞ`

The lexical roughness remains, but its interactional function has changed from shutting down intrusion to affiliative deflection and practical attention.

`GBC-CP1-P11`: **CONFIRM**.

For reconstruction, rough morphology should therefore be read alongside:

- whether Momoka stays or leaves;
- whether she continues practical care;
- whether she answers the substantive question;
- whether she redirects to a bodily/practical detail;
- whether the other person is being teased, warned, or genuinely excluded.

## 13.6 Momoka - jurisdiction language is unusually explicit

Her statement about the current Diamond Dust is one of the clearest early formulations of autonomy:

> `私はもうあそこにはいないんだ`
>
> `あとは自由だ`
>
> `私にそれを否定する権利はない`

This does not tell us whether she likes the current sound. It tells us what she thinks her **rights** are after leaving.

That is why V2 must not turn `好きでも嫌いでもない` into a confession of secret approval or disapproval without evidence. Momoka's legal/moral-jurisdiction claim is secure even if her private aesthetic feeling remains unresolved.

## 13.7 Momoka - guilt is delivered as self-allocation of responsibility

The culmination of the Diamond Dust history is:

> `それでもやめたんだ 私は`
>
> `だから 悪いのは私なんだ`

The final-mix measurement for `だから 悪いのは私なんだ` is approximately **-25.8 dB mean volume**, relatively quiet against several surrounding conflict cues. That measurement cannot establish emotion by itself, but it is consistent with the scene's movement from argumentative public reasoning into lower-intensity self-allocation of blame.

The reconstruction point is semantic: Momoka does not claim merely that her decision had costs. She assigns `悪い` to herself because she broke the band's relational promise despite the others' attempts to keep her.

Future analysis should test whether this guilt explains later withdrawal, unilateral burden absorption, and reluctance to condemn others - without assuming one cause explains every behavior.

## 13.8 Subaru - public smoothing and private bluntness remain one coherent system

During the argument Subaru can say to the pair that rehearsal is impossible and the fight needs to be finished first, while turning to the room with:

> `皆さん お気になさらず`

This is direct `GBC-CP1-P13` evidence.

She does not become a different person when the audience changes. She preserves two simultaneous goals:

- keep the band conflict moving toward a usable state;
- contain collateral social disruption.

Her register changes because the task changes.

## 13.9 Subaru - `努力` reveals her pragmatic ethics of social performance

Calling Nina's ticket lie `努力` is comic, but it fits E03-E04 evidence.

Subaru distinguishes between forms of performance by consequence and context rather than treating construction itself as corruption. She is comfortable with bounded tactical presentation, less comfortable with concealed relational disagreement, and increasingly able to use performance deliberately as a tool.

E05 therefore **STRENGTHENS** the model of Subaru as a practical social translator.

## 13.10 Hina - only a public voice surface is available

E05 supplies Hina's polished promotional/stage presence but almost no private interactional evidence.

That is not enough to infer:

- her ordinary register;
- her Nina-specific speech;
- how much the public Diamond Dust persona resembles private behavior;
- whether her professional polish feels effortful or natural;
- her own account of compromise.

The correct E05 Hina voice state is therefore **insufficient private evidence**.

The source does **not** establish that `好きでも嫌いでもない` is knowingly false.

# 18. Cumulative E05 delta and monograph feed-forward

## 18.1 Iseri Nina

E05 materially advances Nina's reconstruction in five directions.

### A. Positive desire is now overt behavior

The immediate `やります` at Serbian Night, willingness to sell tickets, assumption of costume authority, pre-live manifesto, and performance itself move Nina beyond `music as something Momoka pulled her into`.

The career question remains unsettled, but activity-level desire is now high-confidence.

### B. Her central cognitive danger is increasingly jurisdictional

Nina's anger can diagnose real contradiction. E05 proves that diagnostic accuracy does not guarantee legitimate authority.

Her love for Diamond Dust is real; her interpretation is meaningful; the work changed her life. None of that grants her the right to decide:

- who may sing Diamond Dust;
- whether the band may change;
- what Momoka must feel;
- what verdict Momoka must issue about her former band.

This distinction should become central to the Nina monograph.

### C. She is capable of anticipatory regulation, not merely aftermath regret

The scream-first attempt is the first strong evidence that Nina can recognize an impending verbal hazard and deliberately choose a substitute output.

The attempt is incomplete, which makes it more useful than a clean success: future modeling can predict both awareness and failure under sufficient activation.

### D. She can deidealize without detaching

`それでも` is the major new attachment result.

Nina does not need to restore Momoka to moral purity before continuing to love her. This is a potentially foundational developmental transition and should be watched closely rather than immediately generalized.

### E. She adapts quickly but calibrates slowly

Ticket sales give an ordinary-life version of a broader pattern. Nina can understand a new rule and change behavior immediately, but the first implementation may be too categorical.

That pattern is highly useful for reconstruction because it predicts learning without assuming smooth maturity.

## 18.2 Kawaragi Momoka

E05 resolves the largest open historical variable in Momoka's early model.

### A. Diamond Dust guilt is relational, not merely artistic

Momoka's most painful account is not `I compromised too little` or `they compromised too much`.

It is that the original group's central promise was to remain together, the others begged her to endure, and she left.

Her later reluctance to condemn them now has a secure relational context.

### B. Jurisdiction becomes explicit philosophy

`私にそれを否定する権利はない` is one of the clearest Momoka worldview lines so far.

She can dislike, regret, or remain uncertain about a path while still distinguishing her own preference from her right to control people who no longer belong to her decision domain.

### C. Care includes burden opacity

Momoka absorbs ticket costs without initially disclosing the risk. This is both generosity and asymmetric authority.

The mature monograph will need to distinguish:

- protection;
- concealment;
- unilateral burden absorption;
- actual exploitation;
- self-erasure.

E05 establishes the first three more clearly than the fourth or fifth.

### D. Roughness remains an interactional tool, not a single emotion

`黙れ` and `バカだな` provide a nearly ideal internal contrast. Later voice modeling should never map rough vocabulary directly onto hostility without action/context evidence.

She understands ticket incentives, pricing behavior, audience expectations, and public-room management more readily than Nina.

### B. Conflict sequencing

### C. Positive musical philosophy

That distinction should be preserved in her monograph rather than collapsing into `Subaru lies easily`.
