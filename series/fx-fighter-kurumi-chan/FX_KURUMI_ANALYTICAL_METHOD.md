## The sequence of an exposure

- Give significant depicted decisions a chain from perceived situation and alternatives through action or delay to actually depicted change. Distinguish an intended, submitted, confirmed and resulting action only when the artwork or wording supports that distinction. Mark whose frame of reference a chart or balance shows.
- Distinguish denomination, balance, margin or exposure, an open or realized result, and a stated expectation when explicit in the source. Record relevant figures with sign, timing and provenance. Treat an unknown relationship between figures as unknown. Use transparent minimal arithmetic only when both operands and their meanings are established, and label the result as analyst inference.
- Keep pressure, perceived control, ordinary aspirations, family and peer claims, and material consequences in view. Capture visible hesitation or continuation, competing obligations, offers and refusals, and relevant habitual speech and private choices. Preserve discrepancies between a personal account of intent and the action the page shows. Avoid inferring a clinical condition from financial behavior or visual affect.
- Inspect chart and screen composition, repetition, reaction gaps, the delay across a page turn, and shifts between informational and subjective renderings. Evaluate when form builds confidence or makes a change appear inevitable. Preserve the difference between a compressed transition and a causal link. Compare contrary cases, including decisions that look similar but follow different information or constraints.
