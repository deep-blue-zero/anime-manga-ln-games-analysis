# FX Fighter Kurumi-chan — Architecture and role-gap audit at the V01–V10 boundary

This audit is the role-gap review of gate G2 in the retrospective synthesis FXK-SYN-20261123-V01-V10, held after the run FXK-RUN-20261119-V01-V10 read and accepted the ten collected volumes one at a time. It answers the initiation policy's review questions (§8) and responsibilities (§5.1–5.16) for this corpus, decides which anticipated syntheses deserve separate space and who writes them, fixes how the [decision and evidence ledger](../FX_KURUMI_DECISION_AND_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md) carries synthesis records and stays under its size limit, and defines the gates G1–G5 that the [synthesis architecture](../FX_KURUMI_SYNTHESIS_ARCHITECTURE.md) now records in its retrospective amendment. Its companions are the [source-completion and longitudinal reconciliation](FX_KURUMI_SOURCE_COMPLETION_AND_LONGITUDINAL_RECONCILIATION.md) (gate G1) and the [character qualification](FX_KURUMI_CHARACTER_QUALIFICATION.md) (the person half of G2). The [entrypoint](../CURRENT_STATE_AND_CORPUS_MAP.md) holds the authorization; the [analytical method](../FX_KURUMI_ANALYTICAL_METHOD.md) is unchanged.

Governing text read at the cited lines: architecture "From volumes to synthesis"; initiation policy §5.5.1, §5.6, §5.11–5.13, §8, §9, §10, §13; long-series protocol §6, §8, §10–11; handoff contract §3, §7, §9–§11, §15. The owner's analytical objectives for the run are read as a statement of purpose (hypotheses, not evidence). Character reconstruction, the historical-market concordance, supplemental components and any later volume are not authorized and are outside every gate.

The principal integrator wrote this review independently, reconciled it with the financial specialist's and the character specialist's recommendations and the independent reviewer's diagnostics, and then answered the reviewer's challenges (section 10). ## 2. The single decision and evidence ledger

### 2.1 Finding

One ledger still suffices as the canonical home for episodes, claim and forecast history and person-level evidence. Every volume's architecture check reached that conclusion and, from V06, recommended a per-volume index of row ranges for the synthesis stage with no second canonical home (V06–V10 checkpoints §10). The reconciliation audit §8 supplies that index. The retrieval path the architecture requires (figure or word → what a character could know → choice → consequence → claim history) runs through the ledger's row IDs and the readings' observation IDs; splitting the ledger would break it. All three independent roles reached the same conclusion; none proposed a second canonical home.

### 2.2 What the ledger does not do

- It does not carry arguments. A synthesis claim needs evidence from several volumes, its rivals and what would overturn it; the argument belongs in an audit, a study or the integrated synthesis, and the ledger row points to it.
- It does not hold portraits; monographs do (initiation §5.5.1).
- It does not hold a cumulative forecast scoreboard. No cross-volume tally exists. If one is wanted, it is a process record about the run's forecasters, computed only from the checkpoints' recorded literal scores and exclusions, and it belongs in the integrated synthesis's limitations, not in a study of the fiction.

### 2.3 Additions made by the synthesis

1. **Synthesis claim transitions.** Rows with IDs `FXK-SYN-C001`, `FXK-SYN-C002`, … are appended at the end of the existing claim-and-forecast table, with its columns; the table header, its explanatory paragraphs and every earlier row stay byte-identical. Each row names its synthesis ID and the claim's last explicit transition (a ledger row or a reading's §M table) before its disposition, and its last cell names the audit or study section that carries the argument. The FXK-SYN-Cnnn series is global for the project: a later synthesis continues the numbering, and the synthesis ID in each row tells them apart. This G2 transaction writes FXK-SYN-C001 to FXK-SYN-C006, argued in the reconciliation audit §5.6.
2. **Qualification table.** The "Person-level qualification" section gains, at its end, the table "V01–V10 qualification (FXK-SYN-20261123-V01-V10)": subject ID, person, outcome, a link to the record in the qualification audit, the intended monograph path as plain text, and the self-containment and fidelity results ("pending G4"). It states current names, merged identities and current outcomes, so the stale headings and eligibility lines of the append-only entries are read through it. The record stays mutable: G4 adds the results, and a monograph path becomes a link only when an accepted monograph exists.
3. **No new D or F rows.** The synthesis adds no decision episodes and no forecasts; the forecasts facing events after V10 stay frozen as NOT SCOREABLE IN THIS RUN.

### 2.4 Rule for synthesis claim transitions

- **(a) Decided at G2.** A transition of an accepted claim whose evidence on both sides already sits in the accepted readings, and which needs no study's argument with rivals, is argued in the reconciliation audit and written in the G2 ledger transaction, so that the studies start from stabilized states. Written now: FXK-SYN-C001 (FXK-V01-C007, the trade journal's owner), C002 (FXK-V05-C063 as revised by FXK-V06-C051), C003 (FXK-V05-C032, the basis of the chart convention), C004 (the outcome strand of FXK-V02-C002), C005 (FXK-V01-C006 as extended by FXK-V05-C059) and C006 (registration of V01's competing thesis).
- **(b) Argued in G3, written at G3 integration.** Transitions that need a study's argument with rivals, and every new cross-volume claim, are proposed by the study that argues them and written by the principal at G3 integration. Their state is recorded now as OPEN pending G3: FXK-V03-C001 with FXK-V03-C002 and FXK-V04-C001 with FXK-V04-C002 (each pair together, with the V05–V07 evidence cited on both sides; Study B, strand iv) and the agency strand of FXK-V02-C002 (Study C). The consolidated current wordings of the reconciliation audit §5.5 are retrieval statements; a row is written for one only when a study or the integrated synthesis changes its grade or scope.
- **(c) Never at G4.** Semantic validation checks and raises findings; it does not decide a transition. A finding that would change a claim returns to the owning study or to the principal for an argued row.

### 2.5 Ledger size decision

- The ledger must stay under 1,048,576 bytes. At the base it is 859,614 bytes (188,962 bytes under the limit, ANALYST CALCULATION).
- The synthesis's additions across G2–G5 together are capped at 60,000 bytes, leaving at least about 128,000 bytes for later work. Rows are compact (one sentence per cell; IDs and locators rather than quotation); full arguments live in audits and studies.
- No transaction may bring the file above 1,000,000 bytes. A transaction that would do so opens a successor ledger instead (for later volumes or a later synthesis), linked from the entrypoint, with the existing file frozen for V01–V10 and every ID preserved. The route is fixed now so that the limit is never met by improvisation.
- The navigation index lives in the reconciliation audit, points into the ledger by row ID and base line, is not a second home, and is regenerated from IDs when the ledger changes. The size after each synthesis transaction is recorded in that transaction's audit.

### 2.6 What the reconciliation found about retrieval

The reconciliation (sections 5.1, 5.4, 7.3 and 9 of its audit) found no conflicting ledger states, but it found that the claim history and the obligation record are complete only when the ledger is read together with each later reading's §M tables. V02 records its transitions of V01 claims inside the status cells of new rows; from V03 the ledger carries "Transition of" rows for only part of each volume's §M table; from V06 the §M tables end with a blanket "PRESERVE (no material change)" line, and from V08 with a blanket "no bearing" line for carried obligations. Where a later volume did bear on a claim or an obligation, the blanket line left its state stale. This does not argue for a second home; it argues for the row discipline of sections 2.3–2.4, and, for any successor practice, that each claim a volume bears on be named rather than covered by a blanket line.

### 2.7 The claim-history explanatory paragraph

The paragraph that opens the claim-and-forecast history still describes the V01–V05 cover practice. V06–V10 kept it byte-identical under the ledger's rule and recorded the change in checkpoints and the register. The synthesis does not rewrite it either; the reconciliation audit §2 states the exposure regime for all ten volumes, and the errata carry the qualification of earlier rows.

### 5.4 Transfer, receipt and drift

The handoff contract governs every dispatched G3 and G4 context: the transfer record carries the transfer ID, the base commit, the input paths and versions, and the output paths (handoff §3); the receiver's acceptance checks the delivered files and hashes, the boundary and the base against the current target (§9); drift is classified as irrelevant, mechanically reconcilable, semantically material or unresolved, and material drift reopens the affected claims (§10). A context's returned text stays tied to its dispatched snapshot (long-series §11); while PR #695 and the mutable ledger may advance, nothing newer is overwritten with a stale whole file.
