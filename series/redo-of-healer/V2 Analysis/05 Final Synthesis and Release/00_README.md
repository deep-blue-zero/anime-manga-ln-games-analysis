# 1. Purpose

This corpus is designed as a reusable, source-grounded reference for future literary, ethical, political, linguistic, metaphysical, character, and comparative analysis of *Redo of Healer* through Volume 10.

It does not assume that the series can be adequately described as only:

- a revenge fantasy;
- an erotic shock work;
- an overpowered-protagonist fantasy;
- a political fantasy;
- or a redemption narrative.

Instead, V2 follows how the **governing problem changes** as the novels accumulate consequences that the original revenge structure cannot contain.

The mature longitudinal architecture is:

> **nourishing child**  
> **→ bereaved child who seeks heroic power**  
> **→ Healing Hero reduced to a usable object**  
> **→ Keyarga, who treats authorship as domination**  
> **→ avenger who accidentally builds durable relationships**  
> **→ strategist who discovers that killing rulers does not erase systems**  
> **→ builder and sovereign who tries to institutionalize peace**  
> **→ post-revenge Keyaru who recognizes accumulated people as irreplaceable histories**  
> **→ healer forced to decide whether the power to rewrite history can be exercised without making everyone else material for his preferred world**

The series' central development is therefore not a clean movement from evil to good, revenge to forgiveness, or Keyarga back to an innocent pre-trauma Keyaru.

It is a movement from **exclusive authorship** toward an incomplete recognition that other persons possess claims over themselves, their histories, and any future Keyaru proposes to rewrite.

# 2. Master thesis

The V2 corpus defends the following full-series reading:

> **Redo of Healer begins as a fantasy of reversing victimhood through absolute control, develops into an inquiry into whether revenge can create a livable self, and by Volume 10 becomes a story about whether people shaped by violence can use the power to rewrite history not merely to reproduce the relationships trauma created, but to prevent some of the wounds that made those relationships necessary.**

For Keyaru specifically:

> **Keyaru begins as a person whose body and future are authored by others; Keyarga responds by treating authorship as domination; integrated Keyaru gradually learns that the people he loves possess histories and claims that cannot be reduced to his ownership. The second redo matters because he begins, imperfectly, to share claims over what the future should become rather than simply imposing a better version of it.**

This is **not** a claim that Keyaru develops a universal liberal ethic of autonomy. He does not.

He remains:

- possessive;
- paternalistic;
- comfortable with manipulation;
- capable of severe retaliatory violence;
- willing to concentrate political power;
- and inconsistent about respecting the autonomy of outsiders.

The strongest skeptical interpretation therefore remains partly valid: perhaps Keyaru primarily expands the category of people he treats as **“mine”** rather than becoming universally person-respecting.

V2 nevertheless concludes that this explanation is insufficient by itself. Volumes 8–10 introduce changes in kind as well as degree: accumulated persons become irreplaceable; coercion becomes relevant to culpability; bodily action is distinguished from agency; revenge can be relinquished; mercy can be admired; loved people are permitted to formulate futures that could prevent the very suffering that caused them to meet Keyaru.

The most defensible endpoint is therefore:

> **The protected circle becomes larger, but it also becomes internally more person-sensitive.**

# 4. Evidence discipline

Important claims are classified using four epistemic categories.

### TF — Textual fact
Directly established by narration, dialogue, event, or explicit system explanation.

### SI — Strong inference
Not stated in one definitive sentence, but supported by multiple converging textual signals.

### SP — Speculation
Plausible but unresolved by the current ten-volume corpus.

### VJ — Value judgment
An ethical, political, philosophical, or literary evaluation made by the analysis.

Representative examples:

- **TF:** `概念の【回復】` is explicit Volume-6 terminology.
- **SI:** Keyarga functions as a trauma-built defensive identity or “armor.”
- **SP:** literal external human-like “players” control the world-system described by Chronos.
- **VJ:** Freia's later personhood does not morally legitimate the coercive act that created her.

The corpus also follows several governing interpretive rules:

> **Explanation is not exculpation.**

> **Later attachment is not retroactive consent.**

> **Grievance, culpability, proportionality, and punishment motive are separate questions.**

> **Beneficial consequence does not automatically legitimate the method used to produce it.**

> **What a body does and what a person morally authors may diverge.**

These rules are especially important for Keyaru, Freia, Ellen, Setsuna, Ragna, Lapis, Hakuou, Bullet, Falbo, and the Volume-10 compulsion crisis.

### Terminology conventions

- **Keyaru** names the continuity-level person and the integrated late-series analytical subject; **Keyarga** names the deliberately constructed revenge identity/persona when that distinction matters. The corpus does not treat the names as evidence of two medically separate persons.
- **Freia / Flare** and **Ellen / Norn** are written with paired names when the continuity problem itself is under analysis; the pairs are not treated as simple synonyms.
- **Black Wings** and **Red Dragons** name collectives; **Black-Wing** and **Red-Dragon** are attributive forms.
- `賢者の石` is rendered **Philosopher’s Stone** in English prose. **Sword Hero** and **Healing Hero** are the default English role forms.

# 5. Primary-source locator convention

The evidence ledger and specialist documents use reproducible EPUB locators in the following form:

> `V[volume] — [chapter title] — p-XXX.xhtml ¶[normalized paragraph number]`

The paragraph number is the ordinal non-empty `<p>` element after whitespace normalization. It is **not** a publisher print-page number.

This convention makes exact Japanese passages recoverable from the frozen EPUB source set without inventing page citations that the digital editions do not contain.

`10_VOLUME_BY_VOLUME_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md` is the best starting point when exact provenance matters.

# 7. High-confidence V2 conclusions

Across Documents 01–10, the following conclusions are sufficiently established to serve as default working premises for future analysis.

### Keyarga is defensive architecture, not a wholly separate person
The sequence is textual and developmental:

> V4 internal Keyaru/Keyarga conflict → V8 ideal-self `仮面` → V9 `鎧` → V10 behavioral loosening of the armor.

This is not a clinical diagnosis of dissociative identity disorder.

### Nourishment predates revenge
The orchard is not a late sentimental invention. Volume 1 establishes apple farming; Volume 4 explicitly restores the dream of making people happy through food; Volume 9 imagines cooking and an ordinary restaurant; Volume 10 supplies the complete causal autobiography.

### Personhood is more than bodily continuity
Freia, Ellen, the Volume-8 reset discussion, and the Volume-10 crisis collectively establish a working model in which body, autobiographical memory, skill, disposition, accumulated relationship, choice, and lived history can diverge.

### Bullet's deepest failure is jurisdiction
Bullet can understand another person with extraordinary accuracy and may even feel attachment. His defining failure is treating understanding as permission to override that person's authorship.

### Panakeia changes ends more clearly than methods
It is less supremacist, more incorporative, more materially developmental, and more peace-oriented than Jioral. It nevertheless retains concentrated power, political theater, managed truth, religious engineering, exemplary coercion, and founder dependence.

### Healing is a state-selection power
By Volume 6, `概念の【回復】` makes explicit what the earlier abilities already implied: healing is not merely medical repair. At sufficient scale it becomes jurisdiction over the state to which bodies, roles, concepts, time, and eventually the world are returned.

### Volume 8 is the reset/personhood hinge
Reset stops being a costless optimization once present persons are recognized as products of accumulated history.

### Volume 10 does not erase Volume 8
The second world reset occurs only after the current timeline becomes catastrophically unsalvageable. The companions' wishes make the second redo procedurally different from the first, but not democratically equal.

# 8. Claims deliberately left open

V2 does **not** resolve the following merely because a clean answer would make the synthesis easier:

- the exact landing point of the second reset;
- who, besides Guren, will remember the previous timeline and to what degree;
- the exact future relation between Flare and Freia;
- whether an equivalent Ellen can or should emerge without Norn's coerced rewriting;
- how Setsuna's changed tribal history affects her later identity;
- whether Eve can save the Black Wings while remaining recognizably the same Eve;
- Hiseki's exact survival status beyond the very strong death inference;
- Ellen's survival beyond the overwhelmingly implied death report;
- the precise mechanism linking Keyaru's seven Hero kills to seven Hero-marked Demon King candidates;
- why Keyaru's Hero killings matter differently from numerous historical Hero-on-Hero killings;
- the ultimate identity or ontology of the forces behind Chronos's “board” language;
- whether the Demon King system was intentionally designed to corrupt;
- and how much trauma can be prevented before the resulting person becomes substantially different.

The last question is the master unresolved problem of the second redo.

# 10. Content and analytical handling note

The novels contain sexual violence, slavery, coercion, torture, bodily alteration, identity erasure, mass killing, grooming-like dynamics, and other forms of abuse.

The analytical corpus discusses these subjects **clinically and only to the degree necessary for literary, ethical, or character analysis**. It does not reproduce explicit scenes for their own sake.

Particular care is taken not to confuse:

- depiction with endorsement;
- explanation with excuse;
- attachment with consent;
- later flourishing with moral cleansing of origin;
- or interpretive interest with ethical approval.

# 12. Governing endpoint through Volume 10

The title `やり直し` changes meaning across the corpus.

At first, redo is a weapon that gives a victim superior knowledge and a chance to reverse domination.

By Volume 8, redo becomes dangerous because history has generated particular persons Keyaru cannot reproduce merely by recreating their bodies.

By Volume 10, redo becomes a burden of historical authorship: some catastrophes must be prevented, yet preventing them may alter the very people whose lives Keyaru wants to save.

The endpoint is therefore neither simple revenge fulfillment nor clean redemption.

It is the recognition of a harder problem:

> **The first redo is the victim seizing the pen. The second begins only after he discovers that the other people on the page are also authors of lives he does not own.**

That is the organizing claim of the V2 corpus through Volume 10.
