# Politics, Institutions, and Statecraft — V2
## Jioral, Branica, the demon realm, Panakeia, religion, technology, deterrence, and the problem of personal rule

**Series:** 『回復術士のやり直し』 / *Redo of Healer*  
**Primary scope:** Japanese light novels Volumes 1–10  
**Revision status:** V2 specialist synthesis, revised after Phase-1 audit and Phase-2 Japanese-primary re-entry  
**Primary evidence conventions:** Volume / chapter / XHTML locators refer to the frozen source corpus documented in the Phase-2 evidence ledgers.

---

# Executive thesis

The political argument of *Redo of Healer* grows out of a problem the early revenge plot cannot solve.

Keyaru can escape a palace. He can punish individual abusers. He can disguise himself, acquire money, build a household, rescue a village, kill a king, and even remove a Demon King.

None of those acts automatically creates a political order in which the people he cares about can live safely afterward.

The novels therefore move from a relatively private question—

> **How can a person who was turned into a tool make himself impossible to use again?**

—to a public one:

> **What kind of order can prevent entire populations from being processed as tools, enemies, slaves, sacrificial material, or ideological categories?**

Keyaru never answers that question through liberal constitutionalism or a theory of equal citizenship. His answer remains deeply personalist. He trusts known competence more than procedure, known loyalty more than abstract offices, immediate security more than distant succession design, and overwhelming deterrence more than norms that cannot enforce themselves.

His mature political model can still be described as:

> **authoritarian peacebuilding organized around competence, deterrence, managed narrative, cross-species incorporation, selective amnesty, material improvement, and a widened protected circle.**

But V2 requires a sharper qualification than V1 could provide.

Panakeia is not merely “Jioral with a nicer king.” Several institutional logics genuinely change:

- species hierarchy is rejected rather than merely softened;
- former enemies can be incorporated rather than exterminated;
- coexistence is treated as a practical political objective;
- retaliation can be refused as policy;
- settlement policy includes land, irrigation, housing, tax relief, and administrative delegation;
- technology is intentionally made reproducible rather than hoarded as private magic;
- international politics is used to terminate rather than indefinitely reproduce the human-demon war.

At the same time, several Jioral techniques survive in transformed form:

- concentrated monarchy;
- heroic symbolism;
- political theater;
- narrative management;
- selective deception;
- religion designed for social effect;
- exemplary violence;
- personal networks in place of impersonal institutions;
- and a state whose ultimate credibility depends heavily on the fact that Keyaru can personally destroy those who challenge it.

The central V2 political stress test is therefore:

> **Does Panakeia actually replace Jioral’s instrumental logic, or does it redirect many of the same tools toward a larger, less supremacist, more materially protective moral circle?**

The answer through Volume 10 is mixed.

Panakeia is a real change in ends and in some governing practices. It is not yet a convincing solution to the deeper problem of rule after exceptional people disappear.

# I. How to compare the political systems

The late novels contain enough political material that “good ruler versus bad ruler” is no longer an adequate frame.

For each major polity or institutional order, the relevant questions are:

1. **Claimed legitimacy** — Why does this order say it deserves obedience?
2. **Actual governing logic** — What really determines decisions?
3. **Elite selection** — Who rises and why?
4. **Treatment of bodies** — Are persons protected, traded, transformed, sacrificed, or owned?
5. **Species hierarchy** — Who counts as morally and politically equal?
6. **Dissent** — Can opposition exist without being classified as treason, heresy, or enemy action?
7. **Coercive capacity** — How does the order enforce decisions?
8. **Narrative management** — What public story makes power legitimate?
9. **Religion** — Does sacred language restrain power, legitimate it, or operate as social technology?
10. **Material performance** — What happens to food, land, trade, infrastructure, and security?
11. **Succession** — Can the system survive a worse ruler?
12. **Person versus office** — Does the institution constrain the ruler, or does the ruler effectively become the institution?

These questions reveal why the series’ political progression is not simply:

> bad kingdom → better kingdom.

It is a sequence of increasingly complex attempts to solve the problem of **how power becomes durable social order**.

# II. Jioral — heroic civilization as an objectification state

## 1. Claimed legitimacy: humanity’s protector

Jioral legitimates itself through a tightly integrated public story.

It is:

- a kingdom of Heroes;
- humanity’s shield against demons;
- a center of military and magical competence;
- an ally of religious authority;
- and the home of royal figures whose beauty, Hero status, and public benevolence convert hierarchy into spectacle.

Flare is the perfect symbolic product of that system: princess, Magic Hero, saintly public figure, and military asset.

The important point is not simply that the image is hypocritical.

Jioral’s public legitimacy and private violence depend on one another.

The state needs the story of sacred protection because that story determines which bodies may be used in the name of protection.

---

## 2. Governing logic: usefulness outranks personhood

The clearest institutional evidence is Keyaru himself.

**Locator:** V1 — 第零話「ケヤルが目覚めた日」 — `p-014.xhtml`, especially the first-timeline confinement, drugging, and use sequence.

The Healing Hero is valuable precisely because he is useful.

That utility does not protect him. It increases the incentive to control him.

His body becomes:

- medical infrastructure;
- combat support;
- a pain sink;
- a drug-managed resource;
- and a state asset whose preferences matter only insofar as they affect output.

This is the fundamental Jioral grammar:

> **capacity creates a claim by the state upon the person who possesses it.**

The Hero system makes that grammar especially visible, but it is not limited to Heroes.

Demi-human slavery, forced military transformation, hostage coercion, politically manufactured heresy, and the use of conquered populations follow the same logic.

The body is politically meaningful as something that can be allocated.

---

## 3. The royal family as miniature polity

Prohm’s treatment of Flare and Norn is therefore not merely dysfunctional parenting adjacent to politics.

It reproduces the state in miniature.

**Relevant evidence:** V6 material recovered in the Phase-2 backfill; see also Document 04 for Prohm as sovereign instrumentalization.

The daughters are valued according to function:

- Flare’s magic, charisma, beauty, and saintly legitimacy;
- Norn’s intelligence, strategy, administration, and capacity for ugly decisions.

Affection is subordinated to usefulness.

Norn’s insecurity and competition with Flare do not excuse what she becomes, but they show how the family teaches the same lesson the state teaches Keyaru:

> **to be valuable is to be usable.**

That is why the later Panakeia question is not solved merely by replacing Prohm with Keyaru.

If people are still primarily organized as useful extensions of the ruler, the beneficiary has changed more than the governing grammar.

---

## 4. Elite selection: competence without moral restraint

Jioral is not simply incompetent hereditary rule.

One reason it is dangerous is that it successfully identifies and exploits competence.

It makes use of:

- Flare’s magical power and public charisma;
- Norn’s strategic intelligence;
- Bullet’s intelligence expertise;
- Blade’s Hero power;
- researchers capable of turning bodies into military technology;
- officers willing to treat atrocity as administrative necessity.

Volume 10 later confirms that Jioral historically manipulated Hero succession by killing inconvenient Heroes until more useful replacements emerged.

**Locator:** V10 — Bullet’s historical explanation, recovered in Phase-2 Tranche 2 under Hero succession.

This is meritocratic in a grotesquely instrumental sense.

The state cares whether people are capable.

It simply does not accept that capability belongs morally to the capable person.

---

## 5. Propaganda and moral categories

Norn demonstrates Jioral’s strongest nonmilitary weapon.

Her talent is not merely planning atrocities. It is making policy legible as necessity.

The Branica campaign uses:

- fear;
- species categories;
- false narratives;
- hostage logic;
- and public humanitarian justification

so that destructive policy can be presented as defense of human life.

**Locators:** V2 — 第十五話「回復術士は妹姫を追憶する」 — `p-016.xhtml`; V3 — 第十八話「回復術士はノルン姫の蛮行に心を痛める」 — `p-019.xhtml` and subsequent Branica material.

Jioral therefore governs not only through force but through **classification**.

If a population can be made into:

- demon collaborator;
- heretic;
- traitor;
- nonhuman threat;

then violence can be made to appear like protection.

This mechanism survives late in the series in altered form, because Keyaru and Ellen also understand that political order depends on controlling public interpretation.

The difference is not that Panakeia abandons narrative power.

It is what kinds of social order the narrative is being used to construct.

---

## 6. Succession and office

Jioral’s monarchy combines hereditary status with strategic competence, but there is little indication that the office meaningfully constrains the sovereign.

Prohm can instrumentalize:

- family;
- Heroes;
- religion;
- research;
- the army;
- and black power

because those systems are available to royal command.

The state therefore fails the **person-versus-office test**:

> the ruler uses institutions more than institutions bind the ruler.

Panakeia will improve the person at the center dramatically.

Whether it improves this institutional relationship is a harder question.

---

# III. Lanalitta — market predation without heroic disguise

Lanalitta remains useful as a secondary comparison because it shows that formal state ideology is not required for bodies to become commodities.

Its predation is commercial rather than sacred:

- slavery is priced;
- illness changes bargaining value;
- information is sold;
- criminal power operates openly;
- weak protection makes private force politically significant.

Keyaru adapts quickly because incentives are visible.

The contrast with Jioral is therefore revealing.

Lanalitta says, in effect:

> this body has a price.

Jioral says:

> this body has a duty.

Both can erase personhood.

Jioral is politically more sophisticated because it moralizes extraction.

Lanalitta later becomes useful to reconstruction precisely because its elites, especially Aful, understand practical governance and the danger of destroying a state without replacing its functions.

The series repeatedly rewards such pragmatic elites rather than requiring moral purity before incorporation.

---

# IV. Branica — coexistence as a material counterexample

Branica is politically important because it disproves the strongest ideological premise of both Jioral and Faran:

> **human and nonhuman coexistence is impossible or unnatural.**

**Locator:** V3 — 第二話「回復術士は魔王と出会う」 — `p-003.xhtml` ¶51–63, ¶115–116.

Keyaru sees humans and demons:

- living near one another;
- drinking together;
- trading;
- participating in ordinary exchange.

His conclusion is explicit:

> `共存共栄、ブラニッカは頭のいいやり方をしている。`

Coexistence is not presented primarily as sentimental tolerance.

It works through **ordinary interdependence**.

Food, commerce, alcohol, neighborhoods, and mutual usefulness create incentives for peace.

That is politically important because it gives later human-demon diplomacy a material precedent.

---

## 1. Branica is not a utopia

V2 should resist turning Branica into a liberal ideal city.

The blood arrangement and other asymmetries still reveal instrumental relationships between groups.

Coexistence may depend partly on bargains that remain morally uncomfortable.

Its importance is therefore narrower and stronger:

> **Branica proves that violent species separation is not a natural law.**

Once that is true, the human-demon war becomes more clearly political.

It must be produced, narrated, financed, and maintained.

That is why a functioning mixed city is ideologically dangerous to Norn and to later Faran supremacy.

---

## 2. From instrumental coexistence to desired coexistence

Keyaru’s early response should also be preserved chronologically.

In Volume 3 he can recognize coexistence as intelligent while still thinking instrumentally about Eve and the Demon King succession.

The later political project should not be back-projected onto him.

The development is:

> **coexistence as useful observation → coexistence as strategic possibility → coexistence as part of the world Keyaru actively wants to preserve.**

By Volume 9, human-demon peace is no longer merely a convenient route for Eve.

It becomes a geopolitical objective.

---

# V. Hakuou’s Demon King regime — when office and ruler corrupt one another

The demon realm complicates any simple moral geography in which “humans are corrupt, oppressed demons are virtuous.”

Hakuou’s order contains:

- tribal hierarchy;
- persecution of Black Wings;
- succession politics;
- factional fear;
- elite violence;
- and a ruler whose office is connected to black power and destructive compulsion.

**Locator:** V5 — Hakuou defeat/memory sequence — `p-020.xhtml` and surrounding chapters; see Phase-2 Tranche 1 and Document 04.

The crucial political point is that Demon Kingship is both **office** and **metaphysical burden**.

Hakuou is culpable.

He is also not simply a free sovereign whose personal preferences fully explain the regime.

The office places pressure on the office-holder.

That produces a political problem different from Jioral.

Jioral’s institutions are instruments available to a ruler.

Demon Kingship can turn the ruler into an instrument of the deeper system.

> **The sovereign may command the state while also becoming infrastructure for the power embedded in sovereignty.**

This matters enormously for Eve.

Replacing Hakuou with a better person does not abolish the structural danger of the office she inherits.

The deeper mechanics belong in Document 06; politically, the lesson is straightforward:

> **good character cannot by itself neutralize a bad office.**

# VI. Eve’s order — moral legitimacy without administrative omnipotence

Eve is the strongest challenge in the corpus to Keyaru-style personalism because she becomes a sovereign without ceasing to have independent moral commitments.

Her regime is also the clearest demonstration that moral legitimacy does not automatically create governing capacity.

---

## 1. Revolutionary legitimacy

Eve has several claims to authority:

- Black-Wing history and persecution;
- Demon King candidacy;
- victory over Hakuou;
- Caladrius recognition;
- symbolic relationship to the dead Black Wings;
- capacity to unite anti-Hakuou forces.

Yet the coalition formed to overthrow a ruler does not automatically become a coherent postwar state.

The regime inherits:

- tribes with different interests;
- former collaborators;
- old-regime elites;
- revenge expectations;
- anti-human factions;
- assassination risk;
- patronage pressure;
- and a tiny Black-Wing demographic base.

Eve therefore wins sovereignty before she possesses a deep state.

---

## 2. Thin governing class

**Locator:** V9 — 第十話「回復術士は帰還する」 — `p-011.xhtml` approximately ¶71–94.

The Black Wings have lost many of the people who might have supplied political experience.

Eve must rule among stronger tribes without a large administrative cadre of her own.

Carol and Lapis become important precisely because governing requires more than moral authority:

- information;
- mediation;
- institutional memory;
- practical knowledge;
- and people who can translate sovereign intention into action.

Ellen praises Eve for learning not to decide every difficult matter immediately herself.

That detail is politically significant.

Growth as ruler partly means learning that sovereign decisiveness is not identical to good administration.

Consultation becomes competence.

---

## 3. Anti-retaliation as policy

Eve’s strongest political principle is her attempt to stop inherited revenge.

**Locator:** V9 — Epilogue「回復術士は悩む」 — `p-019.xhtml` ¶30–60.

She forbids retaliation against populations aligned with the old order.

The policy matters because the grievance is real.

The Black Wings have not imagined their persecution.

Eve’s refusal therefore means:

> **suffering does not create an unlimited hereditary title to punish another population.**

This is politically more advanced than simple magnanimity after victory.

It tries to prevent the new regime from legitimating itself through a reversed victim/perpetrator hierarchy.

---

## 4. Sovereign intention is not sovereign control

The policy also reveals Eve’s institutional weakness.

**Locators:** V9 — 第十六話「回復術士は変装する」 — `p-017.xhtml` approximately ¶14–24; V9 Epilogue — `p-019.xhtml`.

Retaliatory persecution can continue despite the order.

Local actors may:

- disobey;
- exploit hatred;
- misreport events;
- participate in false-flag dynamics;
- or create crises faster than the center can correctly diagnose them.

Troops moved to suppress one disturbance leave another region exposed.

This is an important institutional lesson:

> **the ruler’s morality matters only partly if the administrative system cannot implement it.**

Eve’s mature political problem is therefore not “learn to be less naive.”

It is:

> **how can an anti-retaliatory moral order become administratively real in a fragmented postwar society?**

---

## 5. Relationship with Keyaru and Panakeia

Keyaru supplies what Eve lacks most dramatically:

- overwhelming personal force;
- technical innovation;
- external alliance;
- crisis intervention;
- Ellen’s administrative intelligence.

But Eve remains politically separate.

That distinction matters.

She is not merely a provincial ruler inside Keyaru’s state.

The human-demon alliance therefore becomes more interesting than a household expansion.

It joins two centers of sovereignty:

- one personalist and deterrence-heavy;
- one morally anti-retaliatory but administratively fragile.

Their relationship provides geopolitical proof that coexistence can be organized without one side formally disappearing into the other.

---

# VII. New Jioral — transitional legitimacy through managed continuity

After Prohm’s fall, the new regime does not immediately erase Jioral.

That is politically rational.

Destroying a ruler is easier than replacing:

- tax collection;
- administration;
- local authority;
- military command;
- public legitimacy;
- and the ordinary expectation that there will still be a state tomorrow.

The transitional order therefore preserves recognizable symbols while changing who controls them.

**Locators:** V6 — 第六話「回復術士は王を目指す」 — `p-007.xhtml` ¶165–172; V6 — Epilogue「ジオラル王国再建」 — `p-017.xhtml`.

Its architecture includes:

- Freia publicly performing Flare;
- Keyaru/Keyarga as the true Healing Hero;
- Ellen as hidden strategist/administrator;
- selective preservation of useful personnel;
- public concentration of guilt in the old king and corrupt elites;
- a liberation narrative attached to the collapse of black power.

This is not open constitutional refounding.

It is **managed regime transition**.

---

## 1. Freia-as-Flare and symbolic continuity

The most ethically disturbing political resource is Freia.

Her public body provides continuity with the royal symbol the population already knows.

Document 03 establishes why her present personhood must be recognized independently of how she was created.

Document 05 adds the political consequence:

> **a person produced through identity violence becomes infrastructure for state legitimacy.**

That fact cannot be neutralized simply because the resulting regime is better.

It is one of the sharpest examples of the series’ ends/means problem.

---

## 2. Why political fiction works

The regime’s narrative works because it solves real coordination problems.

People need:

- a recognizable hero;
- a recognizable princess;
- a comprehensible explanation for catastrophe;
- an identifiable defeated evil;
- confidence that the administrative world has not vanished.

The fiction is therefore not politically trivial.

It reduces uncertainty.

But it also establishes a precedent Panakeia will continue:

> **public truth is something the ruling coalition may manage for stability.**

The late series never cleanly abandons that proposition.

---

# VIII. Panakeia — authoritarian peacebuilding as a deliberate political project

Panakeia is the mature form of Keyaru and Ellen’s political model.

It is not simply a renamed Jioral.

It is also not a clean institutional break.

That ambiguity is deliberate enough to deserve detailed separation.

---

## 1. Why abolish Jioral?

By Volume 9, the Jioral name has become politically expensive.

Foreign states can use:

- war debts;
- atrocities;
- defeated-state weakness;
- and international moral claims

as tools for extracting territory and resources.

Ellen’s solution is to create a new sovereign identity before others can define the old one’s inheritance for it.

The staged coup therefore preserves much of the actual social and administrative substrate while changing the legal-political identity.

This is statecraft through discontinuity.

---

## 2. Monarchy versus republic: competence, legitimacy, succession

**Locator:** V9 — 第四話「回復術士は選択する」 — `p-005.xhtml` ¶1–57.

The debate is much more explicit than the V1 summary could preserve.

Kureha’s objection is fundamentally anti-privilege:

> inherited elite status does not make a person morally or intellectually superior.

Ellen’s defense of monarchy is not a mystical doctrine of kingship.

It is an argument from known competence.

She believes:

- democratic/republican institutions can reward mediocrity;
- short electoral horizons encourage bad incentives;
- she possesses extraordinary administrative capacity now;
- Keyaru possesses extraordinary coercive and symbolic capacity now;
- therefore monarchy can outperform alternatives in the present generation.

Crucially, Ellen also admits the long-run weakness:

> a worse successor can make monarchy catastrophic.

Keyaru chooses the shorter horizon.

He prefers a system he believes he and Ellen can make work during the lives that matter to him over an institutional design optimized for centuries he will never see.

That is one of the clearest expressions of his political psychology.

His trauma makes **known persons** easier to trust than impersonal procedures.

The decision is intelligible.

It does not solve succession.

---

## 3. Keyaru/Ellen division of labor

Panakeia works through distributed personal competence.

Keyaru supplies:

- sovereign prestige;
- Hero legitimacy;
- personal deterrence;
- military innovation;
- willingness to make final coercive decisions.

Ellen supplies:

- administration;
- legal strategy;
- coalition management;
- intelligence;
- institutional memory;
- political planning.

Freia supplies:

- public continuity;
- royal symbolism;
- magical force;
- socially legible legitimacy.

Other members of the household and allied states supply specialized capabilities.

This is a **high-performance court network**.

Its weakness is obvious:

> much of the state’s competence is still attached to irreplaceable people rather than replaceable offices.

Panakeia can therefore be exceptionally effective and institutionally fragile at the same time.

---

# IX. Panakeia at the world conference — lawfare backed by deterrence

The world-conference sequence is the most developed example of Panakeian diplomacy.

**Locator:** V9 — 第八話「回復術士は異議を唱える」 — `p-009.xhtml` ¶62–129.

Ellen constructs a precedent in two stages.

First, she argues that Jioral’s wartime achievements and claims belong to **Jioral**, not Panakeia.

Other states accept that distinction because doing so allows them to deny Panakeia access to spoils.

They then attempt to impose Jioral’s liabilities and reparations on Panakeia.

Keyaru turns their earlier agreement against them:

> if Jioral and Panakeia are separate for benefits, they cannot conveniently become identical for liabilities.

The narration itself treats the first exchange as the precedent making the later maneuver possible.

This is genuine strategic lawfare inside the novel’s international order.

It should not be mistaken for a real-world international-law rule.

---

## 1. Law does not displace force

The argument succeeds within a coercive environment.

Keyaru’s personal ability to retaliate against rulers and states remains in the background.

So the sequence should not be described as Panakeia becoming a rule-of-law state.

A more accurate formulation is:

> **legal reasoning organizes the settlement; overwhelming personalized force guarantees that the reasoning cannot simply be ignored.**

That combination is central to Keyaru’s mature politics.

He prefers predictable bargains.

But predictability remains backed by fear.

---

## 2. Political economy of war

**Locator:** V9 — 第九話「回復術士は世界会議を支配する」 — `p-010.xhtml`.

The conference also explicitly recognizes that peace creates economic losers.

War sustains demand for:

- armies;
- mercenaries;
- weapons;
- medicine;
- lodging;
- food supply;
- associated state revenue and patronage.

That observation matters because it prevents the human-demon war from being reduced to bad theology or evil monarchs.

The conflict has constituencies.

> **A war can persist because institutions and industries learn to live from its continuation.**

Ending it therefore requires changing incentives and alliances, not merely persuading everyone that peace is morally good.

---

# X. Faran and Scoderia — sacred hierarchy as international jurisdiction

Faran is Panakeia’s clearest ideological rival.

**Locators:** V9 — 第七話「回復術士は世界宗教に挑む」 — `p-008.xhtml` approximately ¶65–107; V9 — 第八話「回復術士は異議を唱える」 — `p-009.xhtml`; V9 — 第九話「回復術士は世界会議を支配する」 — `p-010.xhtml`.

The Faran order fuses:

- church;
- state;
- species hierarchy;
- moral classification;
- and war authorization.

Humans are placed at the top of a sacred hierarchy.

Nonhumans can consequently be treated as:

- lesser beings;
- legitimate slaves;
- pollutants;
- enemies of humanity;
- or subjects of holy war.

The Holy Emperor attempts to convert that theology into geopolitical jurisdiction.

States that reject the hierarchy can themselves be classified as enemies of humanity.

This is why Faran is more than “corrupt religion.”

Its political structure is:

> **sacred human supremacy → social hierarchy → legal discrimination → war authorization → claim to define the international moral order.**

---

## 1. Keyaru’s answer is not secularism

Keyaru rejects Faran’s theology because he regards its god as false and its hierarchy as destructive.

He does not conclude that religion should be separated from politics.

**Locators:** V9 — `p-008.xhtml` ¶64–98; V9 — `p-011.xhtml` ¶10–39.

He explicitly recognizes religion as useful for:

- cohesion;
- emotional stability;
- legitimacy;
- public morality;
- political influence.

Panakeia can also offer something Faran cannot:

- visible healing;
- purification;
- Guren;
- supernatural effects that can be presented as genuine miracles.

The result is not religion versus secular reason.

It is **competing uses of sacred legitimacy**.

Faran universalizes hierarchy.

Panakeia considers constructing a miracle-backed religion to support a more pluralistic political order.

The ethical and institutional danger remains:

> rulers are still designing religious narrative partly for political effect.

---

# XI. Aviation — from personal invention to strategic institution

Aircraft are important because they show Keyaru doing something politically different from hoarding unique personal power.

Volume 7 begins with improvisation.

Keyaru combines materials, magic, calculation, and experimentation to solve a military problem.

The immediate result is horrifyingly effective airpower.

Fortifications, mountain geography, and traditional concentrations of troops become newly vulnerable.

**Relevant locators:** V7 — aircraft development and battlefield deployment, especially `p-013.xhtml`, `p-016.xhtml`, and the subsequent diplomacy in `p-017.xhtml`.

The first function of flight is therefore coercive.

It enables:

- surprise;
- bombing;
- high-altitude magical attack;
- strategic mobility;
- psychological deterrence.

---

## 1. Anti-conquest reasoning

Volume 7 also makes Keyaru’s mature use of military superiority more explicit.

**Locator:** V7 — `p-017.xhtml` ¶66–81.

When Freia suggests eliminating the enemy ruler/capital directly, Keyaru rejects the solution because every other state would then rationally fear that it might be next.

The consequence would be balancing war until Jioral conquered everyone.

He instead prefers:

- conference;
- negotiation;
- reassurance;
- visible strength held in reserve.

This is one of the earliest clear statements of the later Panakeia model:

> **peace through credible overwhelming deterrence plus a believable promise not to use it arbitrarily.**

---

## 2. Volume 9 institutionalization

**Locator:** V9 — 第十一話「回復術士は開発する」 — `p-012.xhtml` approximately ¶45–87 and ¶126–132; V9 — 第十二話「回復術士は競争する」 — `p-013.xhtml`.

Later aircraft are designed around:

- realistic material supply;
- operation by nonexceptional mages;
- cost constraints;
- mass production;
- reproducibility.

Casta receives aircraft technology as part of alliance relations.

Keyaru explicitly expects:

- reverse engineering;
- domestic copies;
- diffusion to additional states;
- eventual loss of monopoly.

That is genuine institutionalization.

The invention moves through a sequence:

> **private ingenuity → battlefield advantage → diplomatic deterrent → reproducible platform → alliance export → expected proliferation.**

The long-run danger is equally clear.

A technology that stabilizes Keyaru’s coalition by making it formidable can later destabilize the world once others possess it.

The novels do not resolve that arms-race problem.

---

# XII. Granzbach and Enritta — two tests of institutional war

## 1. Granzbach under Bullet: the state as processed population

Granzbach under Bullet externalizes Bullet’s personal ontology politically.

People become:

- convertible military material;
- black-monster bodies;
- cultic dependents;
- replaceable components of conquest.

Document 04 treats Bullet’s psychology in detail; Document 06 will treat the black system.

Politically, the significance is that Granzbach turns territorial conquest into **population processing**.

Attrition against such a state becomes irrational if conquered civilians can become replacement forces.

This forces Keyaru’s coalition toward:

- scalable anti-black technology;
- alliance;
- intelligence;
- logistics;
- decapitation of the command structure.

The conflict is one reason the later state cannot remain merely a household protected by one strong fighter.

---

## 2. Enritta/Casta: competence outside the household

Casta matters because he demonstrates that institutional competence exists independently of Keyaru.

Enritta’s defense relies on:

- evacuation;
- layered withdrawal;
- terrain;
- water infrastructure;
- organized infantry;
- crest-engraved weapons;
- disciplined command.

The point is politically important.

*Redo* does not finally claim that the world can only be saved by superhuman household members.

An ordinary polity can convert planning, infrastructure, and collective discipline into strategic effectiveness.

That makes Casta a genuine ally rather than a decorative subordinate.

It also strengthens the case for technology transfer: Keyaru has reasons to prefer capable partners who can act without him.

---

# XIII. Volume 10 settlement — peace becomes material administration

The Red Dragon settlement is the late series’ most concrete test of whether reconciliation can become ordinary life.

The point is not merely that former enemies stop fighting.

The novels begin to ask what people actually require after peace.

**Locator:** V10 — 第六話「回復術士は領地を得る」 — `p-007.xhtml` approximately ¶30–147.

The territory contains:

- water;
- forest and animal resources;
- cultivable land;
- existing lord infrastructure;
- space for irrigation and farmland;
- Black-Wing settlers already improving the site.

Hiseki proposes Black-Wing and Red-Dragon cohabitation.

Red-Dragon manpower accelerates construction and settlement.

This is much more politically meaningful than symbolic reconciliation.

> **peace has to become housing, food, water, labor, and land.**

---

## 1. Tax policy and development

**Locator:** V10 — 第十二話「回復術士は黒幕を暴く」 — `p-013.xhtml` approximately ¶51–62.

Keyaru does not immediately maximize extraction from the new territory.

He allows a two-year tax exemption while the material base develops.

That makes the settlement legible as a rudimentary development policy:

> establish productive capacity first; extract revenue later.

The ruler’s legitimacy is therefore being built partly through **performance**:

- safety;
- food;
- infrastructure;
- low initial burden;
- opportunity to rebuild.

---

## 2. Delegation and local administration

**Locator:** V10 — 第十六話「回復術士は結婚する」 — `p-017.xhtml`.

Hiseki becomes deputy/practical governor and intelligence partner.

This matters because Keyaru cannot personally administer every settlement.

The territory becomes a small test of whether he can move from:

> **I protect these people personally**

into:

> **I construct an order in which trusted people can protect and administer others without my constant presence.**

That is institutional movement, though still heavily based on personal trust.

---

## 3. The false Red-Dragon war and epistemic restraint

Volume 10 also complicates Keyaru’s use of force.

He recognizes competing explanations for the Red-Dragon conflict and withholds premature public conclusions because mistaken attribution could lock both sides into retaliation before causality is understood.

That is a meaningful political development.

The state’s information problem becomes morally relevant:

> **a ruler who can punish quickly may need to delay punishment precisely because power makes a mistaken story dangerous.**

The later revelation that memory and causality have been manipulated vindicates the prudential logic, even though the metaphysical mechanism belongs in Document 06.

# XIV. What Panakeia changes — and what it does not

The strongest case that Panakeia is genuinely new rests on several changes.

## 1. Species inclusion

Jioral and Faran classify nonhumans through hierarchy.

Panakeia’s late coalition treats:

- humans;
- demons;
- beast-people;
- tribal allies

as possible members or partners of a shared political order.

This is not merely rhetorical.

It appears in:

- alliance;
- settlement;
- trade;
- diplomacy;
- amnesty;
- shared military technology.

---

## 2. Incorporation rather than automatic extermination

Falbo is the clearest Volume-9 example.

**Locator:** V9 — 第十四話「回復術士は許す」 — `p-015.xhtml` ¶40–115.

His betrayal remains culpable, but hostage coercion changes Keyaru’s judgment.

After Falbo proves that he values his people above himself, Keyaru rescues the tribe and cancels the execution.

This is not universal forgiveness.

It is selective incorporation based on:

- motive;
- usefulness;
- demonstrated loyalty;
- changed incentives.

The method is still instrumental.

It is nevertheless politically different from indiscriminate purge.

---

## 3. Peace as a strategic objective

By Volume 9, peace is not merely the absence of an immediate enemy.

The new bloc attempts to:

- end the human-demon war;
- isolate Faran supremacy;
- create alliances among previously hostile populations;
- make renewed aggression costly;
- and build material interdependence.

Keyaru’s politics have therefore changed from revenge-state logic to **order-maintenance logic**.

---

# XV. What Panakeia preserves from Jioral

The strongest critical reading remains substantial.

## 1. Concentrated personal authority

The monarchy depends on known exceptional people.

There is no mature constitutional mechanism ensuring that a future ruler will be Keyaru-like rather than Prohm-like.

The text itself acknowledges this through Ellen’s succession argument.

---

## 2. Managed public truth

Freia-as-Flare, the staged transition, the political use of heroic identity, and later religious construction all preserve the principle that rulers may manage public narrative for order.

The justification changes.

The technique survives.

---

## 3. Fear as enforcement

Keyaru increasingly prefers deterrence to conquest.

But deterrence remains credible because enemies believe he can personally destroy them.

The peace order therefore contains a permanent threat:

> cooperate within the pluralistic settlement, or encounter overwhelming personal force.

This is not consensual political membership in the liberal sense.

---

## 4. Household loyalty as administrative architecture

Ellen, Freia, Kureha, Setsuna, Guren, Lapis, Eve, Hiseki, Casta, and others occupy different formal positions, but the system repeatedly relies on relational trust around Keyaru.

That can produce excellent coordination.

It also creates a succession problem more severe than a simple royal heir problem.

What happens when the entire founding network is gone?

Who reproduces:

- Ellen’s intelligence;
- Keyaru’s deterrent power;
- Freia’s symbolic capital;
- Eve’s cross-species legitimacy;
- the household’s personal trust?

Panakeia has not yet answered that.

---

# XVI. State as enlarged household — useful model, dangerous model

The phrase **state as enlarged household** remains one of the most useful ways to describe Keyaru’s political development.

His moral world expands roughly through:

> self → companions → household → allied communities → settlements → kingdom → international peace bloc.

Protection scales outward.

So do several household habits:

- trusted specialists;
- strong personal loyalty;
- protection in exchange for incorporation;
- intense punishment for betrayal;
- material care;
- strategic deception of outsiders;
- centralized final authority.

This helps explain why Keyaru becomes a capable ruler without becoming a political theorist.

He already knows how to build a protected sphere.

The state is what happens when the protected sphere becomes too large to remain private.

---

## 1. Charitable reading

The charitable political reading is powerful.

Keyaru has seen institutions fail catastrophically.

He therefore builds an order around things he can verify:

- competent people;
- strong deterrence;
- practical material improvement;
- clear alliances;
- pluralism where coexistence works;
- selective mercy where enemies can be reintegrated;
- ruthless response where threats remain existential.

Given the world he inhabits, procedural restraint without enforcement might simply deliver vulnerable populations back to predators.

Panakeia may therefore be a rational **transitional authoritarian settlement** in a world with extremely weak institutions and superhuman coercive actors.

---

## 2. Critical reading

The critical reading is equally serious.

Keyaru’s answer to institutional betrayal is still:

> **put better people in control and make them stronger than everyone who might exploit them.**

That solves the competence problem more effectively than the legitimacy problem.

A state cannot remain morally dependent on the ruler continuing to possess:

- good judgment;
- correct information;
- selective mercy;
- emotional attachment to protected populations.

The deeper Jioral danger was never only that Prohm was cruel.

It was that too many people had insufficient standing to resist being made useful.

Panakeia improves whose interests count.

It does not yet establish robust impersonal limits on what the sovereign may do to those interests.

# XVIII. Final political synthesis

The late political development of *Redo of Healer* is more serious than the phrase “Keyaru becomes king” suggests.

The novels repeatedly distinguish between:

- defeating a ruler and governing after him;
- moral legitimacy and administrative capacity;
- personal goodness and institutional incentives;
- peace as aspiration and peace as material infrastructure;
- legal argument and the force that makes law meaningful;
- technological advantage and technological diffusion;
- sovereign intention and subordinate compliance;
- coexistence as sentiment and coexistence as ordinary exchange.

Jioral’s defining political crime is not simply authoritarianism.

It is **instrumentalization backed by moral classification**.

People become:

- Hero resources;
- slave resources;
- military resources;
- religious categories;
- political symbols.

Faran universalizes a similar process through sacred human supremacy.

Hakuou shows that even a ruler can become an instrument of the office he inhabits.

Eve attempts something genuinely different: sovereignty that refuses inherited retaliation and preserves political separateness even inside alliance.

Panakeia attempts another answer: concentrated competence used to create a wider protected order in which humans and nonhumans can coexist under overwhelming deterrence.

The strongest positive conclusion is therefore:

> **Panakeia redirects power away from extraction and species supremacy toward protection, incorporation, peace, and material rebuilding.**

The strongest critical conclusion is:

> **it does so without fully solving the problem of personal rule, because too much political competence, truth-management, coercive credibility, and administrative trust remain attached to exceptional founders rather than durable impersonal institutions.**

So the V2 answer to the central stress test is neither that Panakeia is merely Jioral under a kinder king nor that it represents a clean political rupture.

It is better understood as:

> **a genuine change in political ends and protected membership, implemented through many techniques inherited from the authoritarian world it is trying to replace.**

That is why Keyaru’s mature politics deserve the term **authoritarian peacebuilding**.

The peace is real enough to require:

- diplomacy;
- amnesty;
- lawfare;
- food;
- land;
- taxes;
- roads and water;
- technology;
- alliances;
- restrained retaliation.

The authoritarianism is equally real because the final guarantee remains personal:

> **the order works in large part because Keyaru, Ellen, Eve, and their network are competent enough and powerful enough to make it work.**

The unanswered political question after Volume 10 is therefore no longer merely:

> Can Keyaru defeat the people who made the old world unbearable?

It is:

> **Can the order he builds become good enough that it no longer requires someone like Keyaru to stand at its center?**

Through Volume 10, the novels have not answered that.
