# Purpose

This document is the compact retrieval surface for the V2 synthesis.

Its job is not to repeat the full arguments developed in Documents 02–08. It instead provides:

- comparison-ready character formulations;
- matrices for power, autonomy, personhood, violence, truth, legitimacy, love, and state relation;
- compact institutional contrasts;
- the current status of major Volume 10 questions;
- and pointers to the specialist document that owns each claim.

The controlling rule is therefore:

> **Use this document to locate a conclusion; use the specialist document and evidence ledger to defend it.**

The current endpoint is Volume 10. The second redo has begun, but the post-reset world has not yet been shown. Any claim about exact landing point, general memory retention, successful prevention of trauma, or the ultimate higher-world ontology therefore remains unresolved.

# II. Portable character formulations

These formulations are intended for later cross-series comparison. They compress arguments already defended elsewhere.

### Keyaru / Keyarga
> **A formerly objectified healer who regains agency by becoming an author of other people, then gradually discovers that freedom remains incomplete while everyone around him is only material for his authorship.**

**Confidence:** strong V2 synthesis (SI/VJ), grounded primarily in Documents 02 and 07.

### Freia
> **A person created through an indefensible destruction of autobiographical continuity who nevertheless accumulates enough independent history, competence, preference, and compassion to make her present personhood morally real.**

**Confidence:** strong synthesis; “person” is philosophical judgment rather than a single in-world rule.

### Ellen
> **A coerced identity that later learns its origin, selectively reincorporates the strategic capacities of the self it replaced, and consciously chooses to remain itself.**

**Confidence:** strongly source-supported, with the origin-legitimacy problem intentionally unresolved.

### Eve
> **A survivor-sovereign who attempts to convert communal grief into rule without making retaliation the permanent constitution of the new order.**

**Confidence:** strong synthesis; her administrative success remains incomplete.

### Setsuna
> **A companion whose later loyalty becomes meaningful inside a relationship that began under slavery and desperation, making her a test case for real later attachment without retroactive consent.**

### Kureha
> **A justice-oriented Hero whose continuity of duty and comparatively ordinary reciprocity show that corrupted institutions can be reoccupied without adopting Keyaru's permission structure.**

### Ragna
> **A ruler who performs certainty publicly, reveals fear privately, and finally demonstrates that bodily action under compulsion can diverge completely from allegiance and will.**

### Lapis
> **The clearest case in which the series separates a person's conscious moral agency from a body being used as someone else's instrument.**

### Guren
> **A companion whose growing supernatural power increasingly restricts rather than expands freedom, turning the series' power fantasy logic back against itself.**

### Bullet
> **A predator-teacher who understands other people with extraordinary accuracy yet treats that understanding as jurisdiction over them.**

# IV. Autonomy, personhood, and love axes

These axes are designed for comparisons with characters elsewhere in the project.

## 1. Autonomy

> **self-authorship ←→ authorship over others**

- Original first-timeline Keyaru begins near the first pole as a victim whose autonomy is almost erased.
- Keyarga secures self-authorship by moving aggressively toward authorship over others.
- Bullet occupies the extreme authorial side even toward people he claims to love.
- V8–10 Keyaru moves back toward recognition that loved people have claims against his authorship without becoming a principled autonomy universalist.

## 2. Personhood

> **accumulated lived subject ←→ editable / replaceable material**

The strongest series-level pressure toward the first pole comes from Volume 8's rejection of reset replacements. Freia and Ellen complicate the axis because persons can apparently emerge or continue even after severe rewriting; Lapis and Ragna complicate it further because agency can survive even when action is captured.

## 3. Love

> **recognition of separateness ←→ possession / authorship**

Bullet demonstrates that sincere attachment does not guarantee recognition. Keyaru's movement is real precisely where another person's divergence becomes something he can increasingly tolerate rather than something to overwrite.

The unresolved post-V10 test is severe: can Keyaru honor requests that may prevent the exact relationships he personally wants to preserve?

# VII. Truth, legitimacy, and peace axes

## Truth

> **truth as public right ←→ truth as strategic resource**

Few major actors occupy the procedural-public-right pole strongly.

- Norn/Ellen are consummate managers of narrative.
- Keyaru repeatedly uses public fiction and villain performance instrumentally.
- New Jioral and Panakeia both rely on managed narratives.
- Faran subordinates evidence to sacred doctrine.

The series therefore contains much stronger arguments about **effective truth management** than about a right of citizens to unmanipulated public information.

## Legitimacy

Three recurring bases should remain distinct:

1. **performance legitimacy** — the ruler protects, feeds, wins, and administers;
2. **symbolic legitimacy** — royal/Hero/divine imagery makes rule intelligible;
3. **procedural legitimacy** — authority is justified through impersonal rules or public authorization.

Panakeia is strongest in the first two and weak in the third. Kureha's republican intuition matters because it identifies that deficit even without supplying a mature constitutional program.

## Peace

> **ordinary coexistence ←→ deterrence-backed imposed order**

- Branica demonstrates that coexistence can become ordinary.
- Eve attempts to interrupt retaliation normatively.
- Keyaru supplies overwhelming deterrence and institutional conditions.
- the V10 settlement begins translating peace into water, land, food, housing, taxation, and administration.

The mature series therefore treats peace not as a feeling but as a combination of **norm, security structure, and material reproduction**.

# X. Open-question register after Volume 10

This section distinguishes **questions the text genuinely leaves open** from questions V2 can already narrow substantially.

## 1. Reset landing point

**Status: OPEN.**

The world is targeted by `回帰の【回復】`, but the post-reset scene is not shown. Any exact date, age, or chapter-state is speculation.

## 2. Memory retention

**Status: PARTIALLY BOUNDED.**

- Guren expects to remember with certainty.
- Connected others may remember with uncertain probability.
- No complete post-reset memory distribution is shown.

The next volume must establish whether traumatic memory, divine-office status, Hero/Demon King status, or emotional connection changes retention.

## 3. Flare / Freia

**Status: OPEN, with a precise dilemma.**

Freia asks Keyaru to reach Flare rather than destroy her into Freia again.

Questions:

- Can Flare acquire Freia-like capacities for care without autobiographical annihilation?
- Can Freia's memories or dispositions survive inside Flare without simply replacing her?
- If Flare changes enough to prevent Freia's creation, what does it mean to honor Freia's own present personhood?

The ethical problem is not “save Flare or save Freia” in a simple binary. It is whether continuity can be integrated without making one self merely raw material for the other.

## 4. Norn / Ellen

**Status: OPEN.**

Ellen already demonstrates that Norn's competence can be selectively reconstructed without allowing Norn to replace Ellen.

The second redo reverses the problem:

- can Norn receive the conditions that eventually made Ellen capable of love, loyalty, and self-ratification without coercive rewriting?
- can her attachment wound around Flare be addressed without destroying the strategic self?
- if Ellen's memories persist, whose identity has priority?

## 5. Setsuna and the Ice-Wolf village

**Status: OPEN; desired intervention is explicit.**

Setsuna asks that her people be saved before the enslavement that led to meeting Keyaru.

The crucial test is therefore whether Keyaru can genuinely accept a better Setsuna future whose causal path may not reproduce his preferred relationship with her.

## 6. Eve and the Black Wings

**Status: OPEN; Eve's agency claim is explicit.**

Eve wants to save her people herself.

Questions:

- can the Black-Wing catastrophe be prevented before it forms Eve's survivor identity?
- can she become Demon King without the same destructive pressure?
- can the office itself be altered or healed?
- can Eve's sovereignty develop without the same degree of reliance on Keyaru and exceptional advisers?

## 7. Ragna / Hiseki and the false war

**Status: OPEN.**

The immediate future test is whether the Red Dragon false conflict can be prevented before:

- Ragna is pushed into candidate/Hero anomaly;
- Hiseki is forced into the fatal confrontation;
- political marriage becomes a survival mechanism.

A successful prevention would also test whether Ragna and Keyaru's later reciprocity can emerge without the original coercive structure.

## 8. Lapis

**Status: OPEN.**

Can poisoning, Carol's collaboration, and later system capture be prevented without erasing the political competence and relationships Lapis accumulated through those experiences?

Her case is a particularly sharp version of the series' general trauma/identity dilemma because the harm is both bodily and politically constitutive.

## 9. Bullet

**Status: OPEN; no V2 claim that he is redeemable.**

Questions:

- how early would intervention need to occur to alter Bullet's trajectory?
- is his predation primarily dispositional, institutional, developmental, or some combination?
- can Keyaru neutralize him without repeating the same authorship-over-person logic that Bullet embodies?
- can Bullet's intelligence and genuine attachment exist without domination?

The text through Volume 10 does not answer these questions.

## 10. Hero succession

**Status: PARTIALLY KNOWN, mechanism incomplete.**

Known:

- finite slots;
- vacancy and replacement;
- historical state manipulation through repeated killing;
- independent qualification in at least Kureha's case.

Unknown:

- universal selector mechanism;
- why Keyaru's Hero kills appear uniquely linked to V10 anomalies;
- why replacement intersects with Demon King candidates in precisely this crisis.

## 11. Higher causal architecture

**Status: OPEN.**

Confirmed:

- world-maintaining pieces;
- opposing pieces;
- intervention restrictions;
- punishment for divine-beast violation.

Unconfirmed:

- identity of any higher agents;
- whether the black god is ultimate or subordinate;
- whether the architecture is divine, game-like, systemic, metafictional, or something else.

The safest V2 language remains **higher rule architecture**, not literal external players.

## 12. Demon King corruption

**Status: PARTIALLY KNOWN.**

The office/power system is structurally tied to destructive pressure. What remains unknown is whether that is:

- an intentional design feature;
- corruption introduced by an opposing power;
- a cost of the office;
- or a symptom of some larger conflict between systems.

## 13. Trauma prevention and identity continuity

**Status: THE MASTER OPEN QUESTION.**

The second redo asks whether the benefits of preventing trauma can be reconciled with the fact that trauma helped causally produce the very persons Keyaru now values.

Three simplistic answers all fail:

1. **Preserve every trauma because it created the current people.**  
   This treats suffering as necessary property of identity.

2. **Erase every trauma and assume the same people simply reappear.**  
   Volume 8 explicitly rejects that kind of replaceability.

3. **Let Keyaru decide which version of each person is best.**  
   This reproduces the authorship problem the series has spent ten volumes complicating.

The ethically strongest direction implied by Volume 10 is not perfect preservation.

It is:

> **prevent as much unjust harm as possible while treating affected people, when they can participate, as claimants over what is changed rather than merely beneficiaries of Keyaru's preferred correction.**

Whether the second redo can actually realize that principle is unknown.

# XI. High-confidence V2 conclusions versus deliberately open claims

## High-confidence conclusions

- Keyarga functions as a defensive identity architecture; V8's `仮面` and V9's `鎧` make the structure textual rather than purely speculative.
- Keyaru's constructive/nourishing self predates his Hero aspiration and remains longitudinally active.
- Freia and Ellen cannot be reduced to simple amnesia cases; both require multidimensional continuity analysis.
- Volume 8 makes accumulated history part of the series' operative personhood logic.
- Bullet is best understood as accurate psychological recognition without respect for autonomy.
- Panakeia materially changes protected membership and political ends while retaining major authoritarian/personalist techniques.
- `概念の【回復】` is explicitly textual.
- action and agency can separate under V10 bodily compulsion.
- Keyaru's moral development is real but incomplete and particularistic.
- the second redo ends Keyaru's **exclusive** authorship without establishing equal collective control.

## Claims V2 deliberately does not settle

- exact post-reset chronology;
- who besides Guren remembers;
- whether Freia/Flare or Ellen/Norn can be integrated without identity loss;
- whether Keyaru's seven Hero kills have a simple one-to-one replacement mechanism;
- what exactly exists “outside” ordinary world causality;
- whether the Demon King system was deliberately designed to corrupt;
- whether Panakeia can survive its founders;
- whether Keyaru's recognition of persons can generalize beyond his protected relational circle;
- whether the second redo will honor others' claims when those claims threaten the relationships Keyaru wants.

# XII. Final comparative thesis

The V2 corpus does not reduce *Redo of Healer* to the proposition that revenge is wrong, nor does it rehabilitate Keyaru by treating later affection as retroactive consent.

Its strongest ten-volume pattern is more specific.

The series begins with a person whose body, pain, usefulness, and future are authored by other people and institutions. His answer is Keyarga: become too powerful to be material, then turn everyone else into material first.

That solution works tactically.

It also reproduces the governing moral logic of his abusers.

The later volumes become interesting because the people inside Keyaru's protected world increasingly refuse to remain conceptually reducible to what he made, acquired, healed, rewrote, protected, or desired:

- Freia's present personhood creates a claim that cannot be answered merely by condemning Flare.
- Ellen learns the truth of her own construction and still chooses Ellen.
- Eve's love does not dissolve sovereignty.
- Setsuna's best possible future may be one in which the causal path to Keyaru never occurs.
- Kureha asks for an ordinary beginning rather than a better manipulation.
- Ragna and Lapis show that bodies can act against persons.
- Guren shows that more power can mean less freedom.
- Bullet shows that even genuine attachment becomes monstrous when understanding another person is mistaken for jurisdiction over that person.

Politically, the same problem scales upward. Panakeia is genuinely less supremacist, more inclusive, more materially constructive, and more peace-oriented than Jioral. Yet it still depends heavily on exceptional rulers, managed truth, concentrated force, and personal trust.

Metaphysically, healing finally produces the same problem at world scale. If `【回復】` returns something toward an `あるべき姿`, then world-healing inevitably raises a jurisdictional question:

> **Who is entitled to decide what the world—and the people in it—ought to be restored into?**

The first redo answers:

> **Keyaru.**

Volume 8 answers:

> **Not if doing so makes present persons interchangeable with earlier versions.**

Volume 10 does not supply a final solution.

It supplies a better procedure:

> **ask the people whose histories are about to be changed what claims they have over the change.**

That procedure remains unequal, incomplete, and dependent on extraordinary power.

But it marks the current endpoint of the series' central movement:

> **from escaping objecthood through domination toward the still-unfinished recognition that other people are subjects whose histories can limit what even the healer of the world is entitled to rewrite.**
