## Bodies, Heroes, Demon Kings, divine beasts, time, and the problem of selecting an “ought-to-be” world

**Project:** 『回復術士のやり直し』 / *Redo of Healer*  
**Scope:** Japanese light novels Volumes 1–10  
**Revision status:** V2 specialist synthesis  
**Primary home:** healing mechanics, Hero/Demon King systems, black power, divine beasts, temporal regression, and higher-rule uncertainty

---

# Executive thesis

The metaphysics of *Redo of Healer* are organized around a verb that becomes progressively more dangerous as the series clarifies what it can mean: **to restore**.

Keyaru’s power begins with the social appearance of an ordinary support skill, but the novels gradually reveal that `【回復】 / Heal` does not merely accelerate tissue repair. In Volume 6 Keyaru gives the decisive definition himself:

> `【回復】というのはあるべき姿に戻す力`  
> Healing is the power to return something to its `あるべき姿 / arubeki sugata` — the state or form it ought to have.

**Primary locator:** V6 — 第十五話「回復術士は真の勇者になる」 — `p-016.xhtml`, especially the passage containing `あるべき姿` and `概念の【回復】`.

That formulation reorganizes the entire power system retrospectively. Healing is not best understood as “medical magic with unusual side effects.” It is a **state-selection power**. Keyaru identifies or imagines a prior or proper state, obtains enough information and power to specify it, and forces reality toward that state.

Across Volumes 1–10 the operative target expands:

1. damaged tissue;
2. pain, memory, and embodied experience;
3. learned skill;
4. bodily form and function;
5. memory and identity continuity;
6. deliberately degraded function;
7. the **concept** of the Hero;
8. one person’s temporal state;
9. the world’s temporal state.

The metaphysical escalation therefore does not abandon the original class logic. It radicalizes it.

> **The deeper the series goes, the less “healing” means repairing damage and the more it means deciding which possible version of a body, role, person, or world deserves to count as the correct one.**

That is why the power system and the series’ personhood problem cannot finally be separated. Freia, Ellen, Bullet, Eve, the Hero offices, the Demon King office, and the second redo all ask different versions of the same question: **what is the legitimate baseline of restoration?**

Volume 10 adds a second metaphysical problem. The Hero, Demon King, and divine-beast systems are not isolated supernatural categories. Chronos explicitly describes divine beasts as world-maintaining “pieces” operating under rules on a larger “board,” with opposing pieces and prohibited forms of intervention. Yet the novel simultaneously undermines any simple account of those rules: Heroes have killed Heroes before without Keyaru’s catastrophe, Bullet has killed far more Heroes than Keyaru, and Ellen’s “outside the world” causal explanation is explicitly an inference rather than omniscient narration.

The correct V2 conclusion is therefore deliberately double:

> **The series establishes a higher rule architecture with real constraints, offices, resource balancing, and punishments. It does not yet establish who or what ultimately operates that architecture, why Keyaru uniquely destabilizes it, or what exact mechanism connects his Hero killings to the anomalous Demon King candidates.**

V2 must make the system more intelligible **without making it falsely complete**.

# Evidence-status convention

This document uses the V2 project’s epistemic distinctions.

- **TF — Textual fact:** directly established by narration, dialogue, event, or explicit system rule.
- **SI — Strong inference:** supported by several textual facts but not formally stated as a complete rule.
- **SP — Speculation:** plausible but unresolved by Volume 10.
- **VJ — Value judgment / analytical formulation:** interpretive language used to explain significance.

A recurring rule in this document is:

> **Do not promote a character’s hypothesis into a law of the setting merely because the hypothesis is sophisticated or narratively plausible.**

This is particularly important in Volume 10, where Chronos, Ellen, Keyaru, Bullet, and Guren each know different fragments of the world system.

# I. The healing ladder: restoration becomes increasingly abstract

## 1. Ordinary bodily restoration

At its most familiar level, `【回復】` repairs injury and disease. Even here, however, the mechanism differs from ordinary recovery magic. Volume 10 contrasts normal healing magic, which strengthens natural self-repair, with Keyaru’s return-to-proper-state power and explicitly says that in theory there is no wound the latter cannot heal if the relevant state can be restored.

**Primary locator:** V10 — 第十九話「回復術士は敗走する」 — `p-020.xhtml`, early passage defining `回帰の力` and `あるべき姿`.

The important point is conceptual. Ordinary medical magic asks:

> How can the existing organism repair itself?

Keyaru’s deeper healing asks:

> What state should this organism be in, and can I force it back there?

That difference makes later escalation possible.

---

## 2. Pain, memory, and the body as archive

The traumatic peculiarity of the Healing Hero is established from the beginning: healing does not leave the healer epistemically outside the patient. Keyaru receives pain, memory, training, and bodily experience from the person he heals.

This has two consequences.

First, healing is **invasive knowledge**. The patient’s body is not merely an object under treatment; it becomes an archive that can be read.

Second, the same mechanism that destroys first-timeline Keyaru psychologically becomes the foundation of Keyarga’s exceptional competence. His power turns suffered access into appropriable information.

This is one of the series’ fundamental reversals:

> **The ability that makes Keyaru vulnerable to other people’s suffering also gives him unprecedented access to other people’s embodied history.**

The metaphysical system therefore collapses several distinctions that ordinary medicine would preserve:

- healing versus testimony;
- bodily repair versus memory access;
- care versus surveillance;
- experience versus transferable information.

The ethical consequences belong primarily to Document 07, but the rules point is important here: healing acts on **historically accumulated states**, not merely anatomy.

---

## 3. Skill copying: restoration becomes appropriation of embodied history

Because learned skills are stored in the body’s trained history, Keyaru can reproduce them after healing another person. Kureha’s swordsmanship is one of the most important early examples.

This is not simple magical imitation. It follows from the same epistemic mechanism as pain and memory access: to restore the body, Keyaru understands what the body has learned to do.

The metaphysical implication is striking:

> **A skill can be treated as a recoverable state of a person rather than an irreducibly private achievement.**

The world’s power system therefore treats lived training as information that can, under exceptional conditions, be separated from the person who accumulated it.

That matters later when the series distinguishes:

- body;
- memory;
- skill;
- class;
- Hero office;
- personality;
- temporal age.

These components can be split apart and restored differently.

---

## 4. Bodily alteration and `【改良】`: “better” requires a chosen standard

Keyaru’s healing evolves into deliberate bodily redesign. `【改良】 / improvement` can change appearance, optimize function, strengthen performance, and support disguise.

At the rules level, `改良` demonstrates that the healer does not require a single naturally given “healthy” baseline. He can specify a **preferred** state rather than merely recover the previous one.

That makes the phrase `あるべき姿` unstable in a productive way.

There are at least three possible baselines:

1. **historical baseline** — what the body was before injury;
2. **functional baseline** — what would allow the body to perform correctly;
3. **desired baseline** — what the user or target wants the body to become.

The power itself does not transparently solve which one deserves priority.

This is why the same underlying mechanism can look benevolent when an ally asks for bodily enhancement and invasive when imposed without meaningful choice.

Metaphysically, the operation remains continuous.

Normatively, it is not.

---

## 5. Memory and identity rewriting: the target becomes the person’s continuity

Freia and Ellen show that healing can alter far more than morphology.

In Volume 1, Keyaru’s reconstruction of Flare into Freia erases autobiographical memory while intentionally preserving useful knowledge and capacity.

**Primary locator:** V1 — 第十一話「回復術士はフレア王女を壊す」 — `p-012.xhtml`, especially the memory-erasure / retained-knowledge sequence.

The mechanics therefore distinguish:

- body continuity;
- practical knowledge;
- magical capacity;
- autobiographical memory;
- relational framing;
- personality development after the intervention.

The system does not require these to move together.

Ellen later makes the same point from the opposite direction. By Volumes 8–9, she can reconstruct aspects of Norn’s strategic mind without simply restoring Norn’s autobiographical self. She knows that Norn existed, uses traces of Norn’s cognition, and refuses Keyaru’s offer of fuller restoration because she fears the destruction of Ellen.

That means the setting treats identity as **modular enough to manipulate yet coherent enough for a later self to resist further restoration**.

This is one reason the metaphysics document must not say that healing simply “returns the real person.” There may be several historically real states, and restoring one can destroy another.

---

## 6. `【改悪】`: knowledge of proper function becomes knowledge of how to make function fail

`【改悪】 / degradation` is not an unrelated evil twin of healing. It depends upon the same underlying knowledge.

If Keyaru understands how a body should work, he can also impose a state in which it works incorrectly.

This inversion is particularly important against enemies whose supernatural durability makes ordinary damage inefficient. Rather than exceeding their regeneration through raw force, Keyaru attacks the logic of the state being regenerated.

The conceptual progression is:

> restoration requires a model of proper function → a model of proper function reveals failure points → failure can itself be authored.

The same power is therefore capable of repair and sabotage because both depend on privileged access to state structure.

---

## 7. `概念の【回復】`: Volume 6 makes the abstraction explicit

V1 correctly identified “conceptual healing” as a major escalation but treated the label somewhat cautiously. Phase 2 resolves that uncertainty.

The phrase is explicitly textual.

**Primary locator:** V6 — 第十五話「回復術士は真の勇者になる」 — `p-016.xhtml`.

Keyaru states that healing returns something to its `あるべき姿`, then identifies the lost function of the Hero and performs:

> `勇者という概念を【回復】する`

The narration then explicitly calls the operation:

> `概念の【回復】`

This is one of the most important mechanical passages in the corpus.

It establishes that the target of healing can be not merely a body but a **role-concept embodied in the self**.

Modern Heroes possess:

- social prestige;
- extraordinary growth;
- Hero marks;
- class-linked abilities;
- access to Divine Armaments;

but Volume 6 says they have lost or failed to express an original anti-black function. Keyaru restores the Hero toward that more proper systemic form and acquires the power necessary to destroy black power rather than merely damage the body carrying it.

The Hero is therefore simultaneously:

1. a person;
2. a class/status category;
3. a finite world-system slot;
4. a public political symbol;
5. a metaphysical function.

Those levels can diverge.

A person may be publicly called a Hero while failing ethically. A Hero may retain the slot but lack the original systemic function. Keyaru can restore function without becoming ethically heroic in any simple sense.

This is one of the clearest demonstrations that the series separates **metaphysical Heroism from moral heroism**.

---

## 8. Human-state regression: the “proper state” can be earlier than the present body

After restoring true-Hero functionality, Keyaru uses that anti-black capacity together with healing to return Prohm Jioral from his black-transformed state toward human form.

**Primary locator:** V6 — `p-016.xhtml`, especially the sequence following the true-Hero restoration.

The operation matters because it combines two logics:

- purge the foreign/corruptive black state;
- return the remaining person toward an earlier human state.

This is a bridge between conceptual restoration and temporal restoration.

The power is increasingly willing to treat **earlier** as **more proper**.

But the Prohm scene also warns against a moralized reading of metaphysical restoration. Removing black corruption does not make Prohm innocent or morally restored. The novels explicitly preserve his prior culpability.

Thus:

> **restoring the body to an earlier valid state is not the same thing as restoring moral innocence.**

The system can remove a causal influence without rewriting responsibility for the choices that remain attributable to the person.

---

## 9. Individual `【時間回帰】`: Bullet shows that time can be healed locally

Volume 8 makes the temporal logic explicit.

**Primary locator:** V8 — 第二十三話「回復術士は超越する」 — `p-024.xhtml`, especially the sequence ending in `それはまさに【時間回帰】`.

Keyaru defines an earlier valid state for Bullet and regresses approximately twenty years of personal development. The operation strips away the accumulated adult state, including black power and later progression, while Bullet’s autobiographical memory persists.

Phase 2 corrects the vague V1 shorthand “de-aging.” The text presents this as an **individual temporal regression** produced by narrowing the same logic previously used on the world.

Several metaphysical distinctions become explicit:

- chronological body state can regress;
- memory can remain continuous across regression;
- class/level development can be removed;
- the person is not reducible to current biological age;
- world-scale and individual-scale time restoration belong to one family of operations.

This is one of the strongest pieces of evidence that the series’ ontology is **state-based rather than substance-only**.

---

## 10. World regression: history itself becomes the patient

The first redo in Volume 1 uses the Demon King’s heart / `賢者の石` to amplify Keyaru’s ability enough to target the world.

**Primary locator:** V1 — Prologue「回復術士は、やり直す！」 — `p-001.xhtml`, especially the Sage Stone and world-healing sequence.

By Volume 10 the operation is named with even greater conceptual clarity:

> `回帰の【回復】`

with the target explicitly conceptualized as:

> `世界`

**Primary locator:** V10 — Epilogue「回復術士のやり直し」 — `p-022.xhtml`, final reset sequence.

The metaphysical endpoint of healing is therefore not creation ex nihilo. It is **history selection through regression**.

The healer does not make any arbitrary world imaginable. He forces the existing world toward an earlier state.

This limitation matters. Keyaru’s power is extraordinary, but its conceptual grammar remains conservative in the literal sense: it returns.

The philosophical difficulty is that a world does not have one uncontested healthy past.

That problem becomes decisive in Volume 8 and Volume 10, where returning history to an earlier point may save victims while destroying the exact lived histories that made present persons who they are.

# II. Hero system: finite offices, manipulable vacancies, independent qualification

## 1. Ten simultaneous Hero slots

The early novels state that only ten Heroes can exist simultaneously.

**Primary locator:** V1 — 第三話「少年は回復術士に目覚める」 — `p-004.xhtml`.

The royal household also knows that an unusable Hero can be killed so that a new Hero will eventually emerge.

This immediately establishes that Herohood is not only personal talent. It is a **scarce systemic allocation**.

The world appears to maintain a finite number of Hero offices or slots.

---

## 2. Vacancy and ordinary replacement

Volume 5 provides the clearest technical summary.

**Primary locator:** V5 — Hero-system research sequence — `p-004.xhtml`.

The listed rules include:

- only ten Heroes at one time;
- a new Hero appears when one is missing;
- ordinary Hero selection occurs when a person comes of age and receives a class;
- Heroes lack normal level caps;
- after a Hero dies, ordinary replacement averages roughly two years;
- during the vacancy interval, a person compatible with the dead Hero’s mark may be selected through a singularity under appropriate conditions.

This makes succession **slot-based but not trivially deterministic**.

The state can manipulate the creation of a vacancy. It does not therefore control the selector.

---

## 3. Kureha: the state can engineer opportunity without simply appointing the successor

Kureha’s Sword Hero succession is the most important case.

**Primary locators:** V5 — singularity research `p-004.xhtml`; Sword trial `p-006.xhtml`.

Keyaru identifies the vacancy, finds a singularity, and brings Kureha there. That is deliberate intervention.

But Kureha still encounters an independent qualification process. The Sword calls to her, warns that the unqualified will die, subjects her to trial, and finally recognizes her as worthy of the Sword Hero title.

This prevents two oversimplifications.

### Wrong model A: “the government appoints Heroes”

False. Jioral can kill Heroes and manipulate vacancy conditions, but it cannot simply name a person and make the world comply.

### Wrong model B: “Hero succession is random”

Also false. Kureha’s trial directly evaluates qualification.

The best current model is:

> **finite slot → vacancy → replacement window → compatible candidate → systemic qualification/selection**

with the exact universal selector still underexplained.

---

## 4. State manipulation: Jioral historically “rerolls” Hero outcomes

Volume 10 strengthens the institutional side of the system.

Bullet confirms that Jioral historically killed unwilling or inconvenient Heroes repeatedly until more useful replacements appeared.

**Primary locator:** V10 — 第二十話「回復術士は……」 — `p-021.xhtml`.

This reveals a grotesque political exploitation of a metaphysical rule.

Jioral cannot fully control who the world selects, so it manipulates the input condition:

> unwanted Hero → kill → reopen slot → wait for another result.

The metaphysical system is therefore neither sovereignly controlled by the state nor politically insulated from it.

Institutions learn enough of the world’s rules to **game them**.

This pattern recurs elsewhere:

- Keyaru exploits healing assumptions;
- Bullet exploits black-power and Hero systems;
- Ellen exploits legal/institutional precedents;
- Caladrius’s moral trial can be exploited when its assumptions misread suffering.

The world of *Redo of Healer* repeatedly contains systems that are powerful precisely because they are rule-bound—and vulnerable because intelligent actors can learn the rules.

---

## 5. Divine Armaments: Hero-linked, intent-responsive, partly relational artifacts

V1 called Divine Armaments psychologically revealing. Phase 2 makes the mechanical basis firmer.

**Primary locator:** V6 — 第十三話「回復術士は王を拒絶する」 — `p-014.xhtml`.

Keyaru explains that a captured `神装武具` can be contracted by a Hero and shaped through the Hero’s prayer/desire into the weapon that person needs. Freia wishes, and the artifact becomes the same Vanargand form used by Flare in the first timeline.

This supports several rules.

### TF: Armaments are Hero-linked

Their active form is tied to Hero status rather than being universally usable equipment.

### TF: Armaments are intent-responsive

They can take on the form and capabilities the Hero desires.

### TF/SI: Armaments possess quasi-relational responsiveness

Freia reports the weapon itself as communicating its name; later passages describe Divine Armaments as responding to their owner’s call/will.

### SI: Armament form can reveal deep disposition

Freia reproducing Vanargand despite the destruction of Flare’s autobiographical memory strongly suggests continuity at a level deeper than explicit remembered narrative.

That personhood implication belongs to Document 03. Mechanically, the important point is:

> **A Divine Armament is not a fixed artifact with a fixed move list. It is a Hero-system interface whose realized form depends partly on the bearer.**

This makes the Hero office unusually personal: the world supplies the slot and artifact class, while the individual helps determine the artifact’s realized expression.

---

## 6. “True Hero” function and the decay of office from purpose

Volume 6 establishes that modern Herohood is in some sense incomplete.

Black power is said to drive even otherwise moderate Demon Kings toward violence. Heroes originally possess a power that can kill or purge black power, yet ordinary modern Heroes no longer automatically express that function.

Keyaru’s `概念の【回復】` restores himself toward the Hero’s `あるべき姿` and recovers that function.

This yields four analytically distinct meanings of Hero:

| Layer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Public Hero | political/cultural symbol of salvation |
| Hero class/status | scarce high-growth world-system category |
| Hero office/function | metaphysical counterforce to black power |
| Ethical hero | person who acts heroically in a moral sense |

The series refuses to collapse them.

Flare can be a celebrated public Hero while acting monstrously. Keyaru can restore the metaphysical function of the Hero without becoming conventionally virtuous. Kureha can become a legitimate Sword Hero through qualification while retaining a much more recognizably justice-oriented ethic.

The system therefore contains a recurring problem:

> **offices can survive after their purposes are forgotten, distorted, or politically appropriated.**

This is as important metaphysically as it is politically.

# III. Demon King system: sovereignty as office, vessel, heart, and pressure

## 1. Demon King is an office with candidates

Eve’s introduction establishes early that “Demon King” is not the proper name of one immortal being. Candidates from demon tribes can bear marks and inherit the office after the current Demon King falls.

The office carries:

- extraordinary power;
- political sovereignty;
- a mark/candidate structure;
- inherited knowledge or system-state;
- a heart that functions as the `賢者の石` / Philosopher’s Stone;
- exposure to black power and destructive pressure.

The Demon King is therefore both ruler and metaphysical role.

---

## 2. Heart and `賢者の石`: the sovereign body is a cosmic resource

From Volume 1 onward, the Demon King’s heart is identified as the `賢者の石`.

**Primary locator:** V1 — Prologue — `p-001.xhtml`.

The Stone explosively amplifies magical power and makes otherwise impossible operations—most importantly world-scale healing/regression—possible.

Later volumes repeatedly identify the same relation.

**Primary locators:** V5 `p-020.xhtml`; V6 opening summary; V10 `p-020.xhtml`.

This creates an unusually dense political/metaphysical object:

> **the sovereign’s heart is simultaneously life organ, power source, weak point, magical amplifier, and temporal-reset medium.**

The Demon King’s body is therefore never merely private biology. It is structurally exposed to being treated as a strategic resource.

That helps explain why Eve’s personhood and sovereignty cannot be separated from body politics.

---

## 3. Hakuou reveals the black-god layer

The hidden structure becomes much clearer after Keyaru accesses Hakuou’s memories.

**Primary locator:** V5 — 第十九話「回復術士は最後の復讐を誓う」 — `p-020.xhtml`.

The text establishes:

- a `黒い神 / black god` as the source behind Demon King power;
- a demand for sacrificial souls;
- the black god’s desire for a body/vessel in the world;
- pressure upon Demon Kings toward war and bloodshed because war produces sacrificial souls;
- strengthening of the Demon King as a future vessel;
- Hakuou’s attempt to resist by recovering sacrificial souls;
- punitive control through a terminal implanted in him.

This is stronger than saying “Demon King power is corrupting” in a vague moral sense.

The office is embedded in a **sacrificial economy**.

Violence generates souls. Souls feed the hidden power. The ruler is pushed toward behaviors that reproduce the resource stream required by the source of the ruler’s power.

This is one of the series’ most coherent metaphysical-institutional loops.

---

## 4. Corruptive pressure is textual; “designed to corrupt” is too strong

V1 sometimes approached the Demon King office as though its purpose were definitively to corrupt the holder. V2 should be more precise.

**TF:** black power produces real aggressive/destructive pressure.

**TF:** Hakuou notices himself becoming more violent and fears the loss of his prior personality.

**TF:** the black-god system benefits from warfare and attempts to use the Demon King as vessel.

**TF:** Eve later experiences an accelerating destructive impulse.

What remains less secure is the teleological word **designed**.

The source demonstrates a system whose operation has corruptive effects and whose black-god component benefits from those effects. It does not yet provide a fully external blueprint showing who designed the total Hero/Demon King architecture or whether every part shares the black god’s purpose.

The correct V2 phrasing is therefore:

> **The Demon King office is structurally tied to black power that exerts destructive pressure; the black-god layer benefits from violence and seeks embodiment through the ruler. The ultimate design origin of the total office remains unresolved.**

---

## 5. Eve: the office’s pressure persists under a morally different ruler

Eve matters as a control case.

Hakuou’s brutality could otherwise be dismissed as personality plus corruption. Eve begins from a markedly different moral position and consciously opposes retaliatory politics, yet Volume 10 shows the same black/destructive pressure accelerating inside her.

**Primary locator:** V10 — 第十五話「回復術士は再会する」 — `p-016.xhtml`, especially the passages on `黒い衝動` and `破壊衝動`.

The text emphasizes that the rate of deterioration is abnormally fast relative to historical expectation.

That creates two separate questions:

1. **ordinary Demon King pressure:** why does the office tend toward destructive impulse at all?
2. **Volume-10 anomaly:** why is Eve’s progression accelerated so dramatically now?

The first is substantially established.

The second appears linked to the broader system crisis but is not fully explained.

V2 should not merge them.

# IV. Black power: conversion, immortality, external connection, and counter-system

## 1. Black knights are converted persons, not generic undead

Phase 2 substantially strengthens this section.

Hakuou’s memories establish that black-god-contaminated sacrificial souls can be implanted into human or demon bodies to create immortal black monsters/knights.

**Primary locator:** V5 — `p-020.xhtml`.

Volume 6 then makes their material character explicit.

**Primary locator:** V6 — 第四話「回復術士は弓の雨を突き抜ける」 — `p-005.xhtml`.

The underlying material is human. Black miasma grants:

- extreme physical capacity;
- regeneration;
- apparent immortality;

but destroys reason and reduces many subjects to puppet-like operation.

This is not resurrection in a benign sense.

It is **functional continuation through person-destruction**.

The body survives as usable combat capability while the person’s independent cognition is stripped away.

---

## 2. Loss of reason is both horror and weakness

The black-knight system increases physical performance while reducing adaptive cognition.

Volume 6 repeatedly notes that black-corrupted fighters tend toward direct, forceful behavior rather than sophisticated tactical exchange.

This is consistent with a larger pattern in the series:

> **systems that maximize controllability often lose the intelligence produced by independent agency.**

That pattern matters against Bullet as well. Bullet’s success depends on preserving his own cognition while using black power rather than becoming a standard puppet.

The strongest black-power users are often those who can gain system advantages without surrendering personhood completely.

---

## 3. Propagation and disciplinary terror

The black-knight system is scalable in two senses.

First, conversion can produce additional black fighters from existing persons, creating a potentially multiplying military threat.

Second, the possibility of conversion is used politically as terror: obedience is encouraged by the threat that disobedient bodies can be remade into state assets.

This is where metaphysics and institutional violence most clearly meet.

The black system converts:

> citizen / soldier / captive → material → immortal weapon.

Jioral’s ordinary objectification logic thereby receives a supernatural endpoint.

---

## 4. External power connection and regeneration

Later black-power analysis indicates that regeneration is not merely an internal biological property. The transformed body is connected to a larger source/network.

Volume 8’s countermeasure work shows that regeneration consumes soul and can be disrupted by sealing or interfering with the connection/gate that supplies the black system.

The important V2 distinction is:

- **body** = local combat vessel;
- **black power** = corruptive/augmenting state;
- **external connection** = source/replenishment relation;
- **soul** = consumable resource implicated in persistence.

This makes black immortality less like “infinite HP” and more like **networked persistence sustained by external metaphysical infrastructure**.

---

## 5. Countermeasures become institutional rather than Keyaru-exclusive

Early countermeasures rely heavily on Keyaru’s own `改悪`, freezing, and specialized knowledge.

Guren changes that.

Her purification flame can strip or destroy the black layer so that otherwise regenerative enemies can be killed normally.

Volume 6 explicitly links Guren’s flame to Keyaru’s recovery of true-Hero anti-black functionality: repeated observation gives him a concrete model for what the Hero’s lost power should be.

Later, learned crests and anti-black weapons allow the response to scale beyond Keyaru personally.

The counter-system therefore develops:

> personal anomaly → divine-beast aid → recovered Hero function → reproducible anti-black techniques.

This is important because the setting’s metaphysical threats gradually become **technically intelligible and institutionally counterable**, rather than remaining arbitrary boss powers.

---

# V. Divine beasts: world-maintaining power under rule constraint

Divine beasts should be treated individually before generalizing, because each reveals a different part of the system.

---

## 1. Caladrius: life/death authority, trial, and rule vulnerability

Caladrius is initially associated with disease, purification, Black-Wing legitimacy, and devastating plague-like power. Volume 10 later clarifies through Guren that the public description “controls disease” is incomplete; Caladrius’s deeper role concerns life and death.

Caladrius matters for three reasons.

### A. Divine power has cost

Its strongest interventions consume stamina, magic, and life. Extraordinary divine capacity is not costless escalation.

### B. Divine judgment is rule-bound rather than omniscient

The Caladrius trial can misread morally grotesque circumstances if its formal assumptions are satisfied. Keyaru’s ability to pass part of the trial through brutal means demonstrates that sacred systems can be exploited when their criteria mistake the victim’s desire for release from suffering as evidence of successful resolution.

The implication is not that Caladrius is “evil.” It is that **rule-based supernatural judgment is not equivalent to perfect moral understanding**.

### C. Forbidden knowledge already carries sanctions before Chronos

Volume 10 explicitly recalls that when Caladrius disclosed prohibited information, it permanently lost part of its existence/power.

This matters chronologically. Chronos does not suddenly introduce rule-constrained divine beasts as a retcon.

The architecture already existed.

---

## 2. Guren: countermeasure, familiar, and replacement-role being

Guren’s early presentation can make her look like a comedic familiar with unusually strong purification abilities. V2 needs to preserve the deeper metaphysical structure.

She has:

- sacred purification flame;
- transformation and illusion capacity;
- soul-link relation to Keyaru;
- anti-black functionality;
- knowledge restrictions;
- a world-system role that becomes much larger in Volume 10.

Most importantly, Guren’s role is **substitutive**.

In Volume 10 she explains that while divine beasts possess different special functions, she is unusual because she can replace another divine beast when one is destroyed.

**Primary locator:** V10 — 第七話「回復術士は開き直る」 — `p-008.xhtml`, especially Guren’s explanation after Chronos’s warning.

That makes Guren a kind of redundancy mechanism in the world-maintenance architecture.

Her flexibility is not only a combat feature. It is systemic failover.

---

## 3. Chronos: time, prohibited information, and the higher layer

Chronos is the decisive Volume-10 addition.

**Primary locator:** V10 — 第七話「回復術士は開き直る」 — `p-008.xhtml`.

Chronos says:

- divine beasts were prepared as pieces to maintain the world;
- pieces intended to destroy the world also exist;
- the world functions as a board in a game-like conflict;
- pieces have permitted and prohibited forms of intervention;
- disclosure itself can trigger punishment;
- Keyaru must kill no more Heroes;
- if he does, the first “mistake” must be made not to have happened.

Chronos deteriorates physically while disclosing forbidden information, making the cost observable rather than theoretical.

### What Chronos establishes

Chronos strongly establishes that:

1. there is a rule layer above ordinary politics;
2. divine beasts are not sovereign gods but constrained actors;
3. world maintenance is distributed among specialized roles;
4. opposed functions exist;
5. information itself can be regulated by the system;
6. violating intervention rules has consequences.

### What Chronos does not establish

Chronos does **not** establish:

- literal human-like players sitting outside the world;
- a computer simulation;
- readers/authors as diegetic agents;
- the identity of every “destructive piece”;
- that the black god is the ultimate top-level adversary;
- the exact reason Keyaru’s Hero killings differ from Bullet’s.

The metaphor is game-like. The ontology remains unresolved.

## 4. Greater office, less freedom: Guren after Chronos

Chronos transfers remaining power/knowledge to Guren, who inherits time-related capacity and becomes the operative route to the second reset.

But the gain in power tightens her constraints.

After Bullet forces Keyaru to kill another Hero, Guren says the world has worsened and the bindings upon her have become stronger. She can help only once and must act immediately.

**Primary locator:** V10 — Epilogue — `p-022.xhtml`.

This produces one of the most important metaphysical inversions in the series:

> **more power can mean less freedom.**

For Keyaru, greater power originally functions as protection against being used.

For a divine beast, greater office can mean deeper incorporation into rules that limit what the person may do.

The world system therefore complicates Keyaru’s foundational equation:

> power ≠ autonomy by necessity.

Sometimes power is the mechanism through which the system acquires a stronger claim upon its bearer.

This mirrors the Hero and Demon King offices in different ways.

## 2. Chronos’s statements

Chronos provides privileged but incomplete information.

His core claims are:

- the world is maintained through pieces;
- opposing/destructive pieces exist;
- the larger conflict follows rules;
- pieces cannot freely violate those rules;
- Keyaru’s further Hero killing is dangerous;
- the first mistake may need to be erased through redo.

Chronos’s authority matters, but his explanation is incomplete and partly self-interrupted by punishment.

Moreover, later evidence demonstrates that Keyaru’s first interpretation of the warning is too simple.

Therefore:

> **Chronos is a highly informed source, not a complete rules manual.**

## 3. Ellen’s historical counterevidence

Ellen’s most important contribution is falsification.

**Primary locator:** V10 — 第十四話「回復術士は追い詰められる」 — `p-015.xhtml`.

She searches the record and finds many cases of Heroes killing Heroes without analogous world collapse.

This refutes the naive rule:

> Hero kills Hero → balance always breaks catastrophically.

It also demonstrates why the Volume-10 mystery should not be solved simply by repeating Chronos’s warning.

Something about **Keyaru**, **his method**, **his status**, **the replacements**, **the timing**, or **the larger intervention context** is exceptional.

The text does not yet tell us exactly which combination matters.

---

## 4. Bullet’s evidence: the problem is not Hero killing in general

Volume 10 gives even stronger counterevidence through Bullet.

**Primary locator:** V10 — 第二十話「回復術士は……」 — `p-021.xhtml`.

Bullet says that Keyaru has killed seven Heroes, while Bullet himself has killed dozens.

Keyaru therefore revises the problem:

> Hero killing itself may not be the issue. **Keyaru killing Heroes** may be the issue.

This is a strong inference, not a full mechanism.

The text then observes that when Keyaru kills Heroes, the replacement pattern appears anomalous: Demon King candidates acquire the Hero roles.

That leads to the seven-to-seven problem.

---

## 5. Seven Hero deaths and seven marked Demon King candidates

The numerical correlation is unusually suggestive.

**Primary locator:** V10 — `p-021.xhtml`.

### TF

- Keyaru has killed seven Heroes.
- Seven Demon King candidates now possess Hero marks.
- The count matches.

### SI

It is highly likely that Keyaru’s Hero killings are causally relevant to the anomalous replacements.

### SP

The exact mechanism remains unknown.

We do not yet know:

- why replacement preferentially or exclusively reaches Demon King candidates;
- whether Keyaru’s Healing Hero nature is the decisive factor;
- whether the first redo altered his relation to the Hero system;
- whether an external actor selects the candidates;
- whether the anomaly is a side effect of system balancing rather than deliberate targeting;
- whether the black-god system is exploiting vacancies;
- whether some other higher layer is responsible.

The correct V2 formulation is:

> **The seven-to-seven pattern is textual fact as correlation, strong inference as special causality, and speculation as mechanism.**

## 6. Ellen’s “outside the world” causality model

Ellen notices that ordinary causation has a recognizable sequence:

> motive → preparation → action → result.

The candidate catastrophe appears to her as though the world contains the **result** without a legible internal chain producing it.

She therefore proposes that part of the causal work may occur outside ordinary in-world causality, with Keyaru’s Hero killing functioning as the opening or trigger that permits intervention.

**Primary locator:** V10 — 第二十話 / Ellen message — `p-021.xhtml`.

This is one of the most sophisticated in-world hypotheses in the series.

It should still be labeled correctly.

### TF

Ellen formulates the hypothesis.

### TF

Keyaru recognizes it as a hypothesis rather than proven knowledge.

### SI

The system crisis probably involves causal constraints not fully visible from ordinary political/magical action.

### SP

The exact “outside” agent, level, or process remains unknown.

---

## 7. Literal players remain speculation

Chronos uses `ゲーム`, `盤上`, and `駒` language. That is strong game metaphoricity inside the world-system explanation.

But the series does not yet warrant a literal claim such as:

> “The world is canonically a simulation controlled by players.”

Nor does it establish:

> “The readers or author exist diegetically above the world.”

Nor even:

> “The black god is definitively one of exactly two top-level players.”

A disciplined synthesis should say:

> **The text establishes game-like rule architecture and constrained pieces. The ontological identity of whatever exists beyond or above ordinary worldly causality remains unresolved.**

That formulation is strong enough to preserve the revelation without converting metaphor and character theory into false certainty.

# VII. Body compulsion: when the world system authors action but not intention

Volume 10’s most important metaphysical horror is not simply increased destructive power. It is the separation of agency from motor behavior.

Ragna and Lapis remain conscious of:

- who they love;
- what they want;
- what they do not want to do;
- whom they wish to protect.

Yet their bodies execute hostile actions under the anomalous Hero/Demon King candidate compulsion.

**Primary locators:** V10 — 第十八話「回復術士は恋人に手を伸ばす」 `p-019.xhtml`; 第十九話「回復術士は敗走する」 `p-020.xhtml`.

Lapis even modifies behavior at the margin when she can, making attacks easier to evade and asking to be killed rather than forced to harm the people she values.

This mechanism differs from earlier forms of control.

| Earlier mechanism | What is altered |
|---|---|
| drugs | desire, dependence, resistance capacity |
| slavery | legal/material freedom and incentives |
| memory rewriting | autobiographical identity and relational frame |
| black conversion | cognition/reason plus body function |
| conditioning | preference/behavioral architecture |
| Volume-10 compulsion | **motor action despite retained contrary intention** |

The metaphysical system has therefore reached a form of authorship more invasive than persuasion and cleaner than brainwashing:

> **the person can remain present while the body becomes someone else’s sentence.**

This belongs ethically in Document 07, but it matters here because it demonstrates what the higher system can do.

It can impose **behavioral outcome without rewriting subjective allegiance**.

That is one reason Keyaru’s anger shifts toward the world/rule structure rather than toward Ragna or Lapis. For once, the distinction between hostile action and hostile personhood is made mechanically undeniable.

# VIII. Time, memory, and the limits of restoration

## 1. The first reset: unilateral historical continuity

In the first redo, Keyaru targets the world and sends history back roughly four years.

He expects ordinary memory continuity to fail and relies on the depth of traumatic pain/experience to recover awareness across the reset.

The operative structure is unilateral:

- Keyaru has the relevant memory;
- Keyaru chooses the reset;
- Keyaru chooses the revenge program;
- other people re-enter history without consultation.

Metaphysically, the important issue is that **world-state regression and memory-state continuity are separate variables**.

A body/world can return while some information survives.

---

## 2. Bullet: same memory, earlier body

Bullet’s individual `【時間回帰】` makes the separation explicit.

His body, power, level, and class-state regress while memory persists.

Therefore:

> biological continuity ≠ temporal-state continuity ≠ autobiographical continuity.

The person can carry a later mind into an earlier embodied state.

This is the basic model that makes the second reset’s memory question intelligible.

---

## 3. Volume 8: same-shaped persons are not necessarily the same persons

Volume 8 transforms reset from convenient resource into personhood threat.

Keyaru explicitly rejects the idea that an earlier Eve with the same appearance would automatically be the present Eve. The accumulated relationship, choices, growth, and shared time would be gone.

That principle extends to Freia, Setsuna, Kureha, and Guren.

**Primary locator:** V8 — 第六話「回復術士は貢ぎものになる」 — `p-007.xhtml`.

The metaphysical lesson is:

> **state equivalence at the level of body does not guarantee identity equivalence at the level of lived history.**

This places a hard limit on any simplistic equation:

> earlier = healthier = better.

An earlier state can be less traumatized and still fail to be the same accumulated person.

---

## 4. The second reset: memory preservation becomes probabilistic

Guren says that people connected to the event may preserve memory, but the probability is uncertain. She treats her own continuity as the strongest/most certain case.

**Primary locator:** V10 — Epilogue — `p-022.xhtml`.

The correct V2 status is therefore:

- **Guren’s own memory continuity:** strongly/explicitly expected;
- **others’ memory continuity:** possible but probabilistic;
- **universal household memory preservation:** not established.

This uncertainty is crucial.

If memories persist, the second world may contain persons remembering histories that no longer occurred.

If memories do not persist, Keyaru may save lives while losing the exact persons produced by the first redo.

The second redo is therefore not clean restoration. It is an ontological wager.

---

## 5. Exact landing point remains unknown

The Volume-10 endpoint depicts world regression but does not show the post-reset world or specify the exact date to which history settles.

**Primary locator:** V10 — Epilogue — `p-022.xhtml`.

Any exact second-reset landing date is therefore **SP** until later primary text establishes it.

This should remain an explicit open question in Document 09 as well.

# IX. The philosophy of healing: which past deserves jurisdiction over the present?

The metaphysical ladder creates a corresponding ladder of philosophical problems.

## To heal a wound

Which bodily state counts as uninjured?

Usually this is easy because the immediate pre-injury body supplies a stable baseline.

## To improve a body

Does the “proper” state mean natural, stronger, more beautiful, more useful, or desired?

Now the baseline depends on preference.

## To alter memory

If psychological suffering is tied to memory, does removing memory heal the person or destroy the person who remembers?

Freia and Ellen make this question concrete.

## To restore Herohood

Can an office have an original proper function even when the institution built around it has forgotten or corrupted that function?

Volume 6 says yes at least mechanically.

## To purge black power

If behavior was partly driven by a corruptive force, what exactly is restored when the force is removed: body, temperament, capacity for choice, or moral innocence?

The Prohm case shows that moral innocence does not automatically return.

## To regress Bullet

If the body becomes twenty years younger while memory survives, which temporal layer defines the person?

The novels answer operationally: several layers can be separated.

## To reset the world

Which earlier world is “healthy” when the present contains persons whose valuable relationships were formed through events one wishes to prevent?

This is the hardest version.

The first redo assumes the decision is simple:

> history harmed Keyaru → return history → take revenge.

Volume 8 complicates the ontology:

> returning history destroys accumulated persons.

Volume 10 complicates it again:

> the current timeline may be unsalvageable, yet preventing trauma may alter the people one is trying to save.

The second redo therefore cannot be understood as a simple medical operation scaled upward.

It is a choice among incompatible forms of preservation.

# XI. Final metaphysical thesis

V1’s central insight survives V2, but the primary-source re-entry lets it be stated more precisely.

*Redo of Healer* does not possess a conventional magic system in which healing simply grows into stronger healing. It possesses a **restoration ontology**.

Bodies, memories, skills, roles, offices, temporal states, and eventually history itself can be represented as states that may be returned toward an `あるべき姿`.

At the same time, the world is populated by offices that exert claims upon persons:

- Hero;
- Demon King;
- divine beast;
- black knight;
- candidate;
- vessel.

Those offices offer power while threatening autonomy. Hero vacancies can be politically manipulated. Demon King power carries destructive pressure. Divine beasts become more constrained as their world-maintenance roles deepen. Black knights preserve bodily function by destroying independent personhood. Volume-10 candidates retain subjective allegiance while the system commandeers action.

Keyaru is uniquely dangerous to this architecture because his ability does not merely let him become stronger **inside** a role. It lets him alter the state of roles and their embodiments.

That makes the mature metaphysical problem inseparable from the ethical one:

> **If healing means returning reality toward the state it ought to have, the ultimate power of the Healing Hero is not repair. It is jurisdiction over the definition of “ought.”**

In the first redo, Keyaru exercises that jurisdiction almost entirely alone.

By Volume 8 he discovers that returning people to earlier versions can destroy the persons created by shared history.

By Volume 10 the current history becomes unsalvageable, yet the second redo is preceded by disclosure and consultation. Other persons acquire explicit claims over what the restored world should preserve or prevent.

The system remains unequal. The mechanism remains extraordinarily concentrated. The metaphysics remain unresolved.

But the series’ governing question has changed.

It is no longer merely:

> **Can Keyaru restore the world?**

It has become:

> **Who gets to decide which world deserves to be restored?**

That is the point at which the Healing Hero’s class ceases to be only a power fantasy mechanic and becomes the series’ most durable model of personhood, political authority, and historical authorship.
