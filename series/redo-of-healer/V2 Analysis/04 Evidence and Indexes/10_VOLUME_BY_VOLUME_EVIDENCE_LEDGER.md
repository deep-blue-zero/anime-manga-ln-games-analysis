# I. Purpose and method

The specialist synthesis necessarily reads the series with full hindsight. That creates a recurring danger: later revelations can make an earlier volume appear to have said more than it actually did.

This ledger preserves **prospective truth**.

For each volume it records:

1. the volume thesis;
2. the governing question at that stage;
3. decisive narrative and character changes;
4. relationship and institutional changes;
5. metaphysical additions;
6. the ethical problem introduced or revised;
7. important motifs and Japanese terminology;
8. what a reader could responsibly infer at that horizon;
9. how later volumes strengthen, weaken, or overturn that reading;
10. unresolved questions that genuinely remained open then;
11. reproducible Japanese-primary locators for the claims carrying the most analytical weight.

The ledger is not another plot summary. It is the audit path:

> **V2 synthesis claim → ledger entry → volume/chapter/XHTML/paragraph locator → Japanese EPUB**

## Evidence labels

- **TF — Textual fact:** directly stated or enacted in the primary source.
- **SI — Strong inference:** supported by multiple textual signals but not established as a formal in-world rule.
- **SP — Speculation:** plausible but unresolved.
- **VJ — Value judgment / interpretive judgment:** ethical, political, or literary evaluation.

## Locator convention

> **V[volume] — [chapter title] — `p-XXX.xhtml` ¶[normalized paragraph number]**

The paragraph number is the ordinal non-empty `<p>` element in the frozen EPUB extraction. It is not a print page number.

# Volume 1
## The violated healer seizes authorship over bodies and history

### Volume thesis

Keyaru answers being reduced to a state, military, chemical, and sexual resource by turning healing into the capacity to author bodies, memories, identities, and the timeline itself. His liberation is therefore founded on a reversal that already reproduces the objectification he hates.

### Governing question

> **What does a person do with absolute bodily authorship after learning that usefulness without control makes the body a route to enslavement?**

### What the volume establishes at its own horizon

Keyaru is not introduced as someone whose original dream was conquest. He is an apple farmer maintaining the orchard left by his dead parents. Heroism is present as aspiration, but the causal history of that aspiration is not yet fully disclosed.

The first timeline establishes several distinct forms of objectification:

- his Healing Hero ability is treated as state property;
- the pain intrinsic to healing matters only as an obstacle to output;
- drugs are used to produce dependency and compliance;
- his body is treated as sexually accessible material;
- his mind and memories matter only insofar as they affect usefulness.

The first redo is explicitly revenge-authored. Keyaru alone retains the operative historical agenda, intentionally re-enters the route that will place him near his future targets, and converts the Philosopher’s Stone into world regression.

Flare’s transformation into Freia establishes the series’ first major personhood problem. Keyaru erases autobiographical continuity while retaining useful knowledge and magical competence. The result is neither simple amnesia nor creation ex nihilo.

Setsuna introduces a different problem. Keyaru does not erase her memory, but the relationship begins under slavery, illness, tribal danger, desperation, and overwhelming asymmetry. Her later choices are therefore meaningful without making the founding conditions free.

### Keyaru state

Keyarga is emerging as a tactical and retaliatory self organized around one imperative:

> never again allow another person or institution to possess uncontested authorship over him.

At this horizon, Keyarga is not yet framed explicitly as `仮面 / mask` or `鎧 / armor`.

### Institutional and metaphysical additions

- Jioral treats Heroes as military resources beneath sacred public imagery.
- Lanalitta turns bodily vulnerability into market exchange and slavery.
- `【回復】` already accesses pain, memory, skill, bodily structure, and identity-related information.
- The Demon King’s heart is identified as the `賢者の石`, enabling the world reset.
- Names, contracts, levels, and caps all make identity and capacity administratively or magically legible.

### Ethical problem

A genuine grievance does not answer whether Keyaru’s response is proportionate. The volume establishes the basic distinction V2 must retain:

> **explanation of domination is not moral permission to dominate.**

### Motifs and Japanese terms

- `やり直し` — redo as private revenge opportunity;
- `真名` — the true name as control and recognition;
- renaming Keyaru → Keyarga;
- body as writable territory;
- apple orchard as biography, not yet fully articulated telos.

# Volume 2
## Revenge becomes legitimacy warfare

### Volume thesis

Keyarga expands revenge from private bodily retaliation into control over public interpretation. Jioral’s power depends not only on violence but on naming violence as justice, salvation, heresy suppression, or heroic duty.

### Governing question

> **Who controls the meaning of violence when the state, the Hero, and the crowd all claim the language of justice?**

### What the volume establishes at its own horizon

Kureha’s faith in Jioral begins to fracture. She genuinely believed that serving the kingdom meant serving justice; evidence of institutional evil forces her to reconsider what her sword should serve.

Keyaru, however, knows that Kureha’s moral reading of him is false or at least radically incomplete. In private narration he disclaims a principled justice ethic. In public he can still stage retaliation as justice because political language is useful.

This creates one of the series’ recurring formal structures:

> private motive → curated truth → public moral theater.

Norn emerges as a different kind of antagonist from Flare. Flare’s cruelty is intimate and aristocratic; Norn’s danger is administrative. She can turn massacre, contamination, “brainwashing,” and rescue into categories through which state violence becomes publicly intelligible.

The coliseum sequence makes the crowd part of the political mechanism. Keyaru does not merely kill enemies; he reverses the state’s spectacle and makes the public witness a different narrative of legitimacy.

### Keyaru state

He is becoming propagandist, insurgent, and crowd psychologist. His retaliatory code still has boundaries, but those boundaries are increasingly self-authored rather than impartial.

### Relationship and institutional changes

- Kureha becomes morally detached from automatic loyalty to the kingdom while remaining dependent on Keyaru’s curated narrative.
- Freia’s public Flare identity becomes a political weapon.
- Jioral is revealed as an institution that manufactures categories—heretic, contaminated, brainwashed, traitor—to make violence administrable.

### Metaphysical additions

The mismatch between the current Demon King and first-timeline Eve strengthens the idea that “Demon King” is an office or succession category rather than a single immortal identity.

### Ethical problem

The volume distinguishes **truth** from **truthful political procedure**. Keyaru may reveal real crimes through deception. The result can be politically liberating without making the method ethically clean.

### Motifs and Japanese terms

- `正義 / seigi` — sincere problem for Kureha, rhetorical instrument for Keyaru;
- public saint / private abuser;
- crowd contagion;
- execution arena as political stage;
- false identity speaking truths the official state cannot tolerate.

- Volume 3’s Branica gives empirical evidence that Jioral’s human/demon moral map is false.
- Volume 4–9 show that Norn’s strategic mind can survive into Ellen and be redirected toward a different order.
- Volume 9–10 complicate culpability by distinguishing malicious choice, coercion, and compelled action.

### Unresolved at the volume horizon

- How much of Kureha’s trust rests on Keyaru’s deception?
- What exactly motivates Norn beneath state loyalty?
- Is public truth delivered by a false speaker politically sustainable?
- What is the relation between Jioral’s Hero ideology and the demon war?

# Volume 3
## Branica breaks the inherited moral map

### Volume thesis

Branica demonstrates that human/demon coexistence is materially possible, Eve turns the Demon King category into a vulnerable person and political future, and Norn shows how states destroy counterexamples that threaten the stories legitimating war.

### Governing question

> **What happens when ordinary life disproves the categories through which a state justifies violence?**

### What the volume establishes at its own horizon

Branica is not important because it is a perfect society. Its importance is evidentiary. Humans and demons trade, drink, eat, work, and depend upon one another. Coexistence is therefore not an abstract moral hope; it is an observed social practice.

Keyaru initially recognizes this partly through instrumental intelligence. Coexistence is useful, and Eve is useful. His support for Eve’s path remains mixed with manipulation and future knowledge.

Eve is introduced not as the first-timeline Demon King in completed form but as a hunted, hungry, proud, politically vulnerable candidate. This destabilizes the category “Demon King” in the same way Branica destabilizes “demon enemy.”

Norn’s assault on Branica confirms that Jioral cannot tolerate proof that coexistence works. Her rhetoric converts massacre into liberation from demon brainwashing. The deeper enemy is therefore not only prejudice but a state whose legitimacy materially depends on preserving the prejudice.

Blade establishes a separate villain type: appetite and entitlement supported by overwhelming power. Bullet remains a more structurally important unresolved figure because Keyaru’s relation to him involves learning as well as victimization.

Norn’s private wound also becomes visible. Her strategic brilliance is partly bound to frustrated attachment and desire for recognition from Flare. This explains without excusing.

### Keyaru state

Care and possession are becoming entangled. His actions sometimes protect before his narration can reduce that protection to strategy or ownership.

### Relationship and institutional changes

- Eve enters as a non-erased beloved and future sovereign possibility.
- Freia’s anti-Jioral public speech begins functioning as more than obedience.
- Norn is defeated and captured; the resulting identity transition is not yet shown in this volume.
- Demon politics becomes factional rather than species-monolithic.

### Metaphysical additions

- Demon King candidates and succession become central.
- Divine Armaments respond to their wielders.
- Caladrius enters as Black-Wing sacred power.

### Ethical problem

A counterexample to official ideology does not protect itself. Branica exposes the gap between **truth** and the institutional power required to keep truth alive.

### Motifs and Japanese terms

- `共存共栄 / kyōzon kyōei` — coexistence and mutual prosperity;
- food and drink as political evidence;
- salvation rhetoric masking annihilation;
- false saint speaking humane truth;
- future role versus present person.

### Primary-source anchors

| Humans and demons visibly coexist through ordinary exchange | V3 — 第二話「回復術士は魔王と出会う」 — `p-003.xhtml` ¶51–63, ¶115–116 | TF |
| Keyaru explicitly calls Branica’s coexistence model intelligent | V3 — `p-003.xhtml` ¶115–116 | TF |
| Norn’s attack framed through salvation/brainwashing rhetoric | V3 — 第十八話「回復術士はノルン姫の蛮行に心を痛める」 — `p-019.xhtml`; related material `p-021.xhtml` ¶64–80 | TF |

### Retrospective revision

- Volume 9 later confirms Eve as a sovereign whose independent office prevents full enclosure inside Keyaru’s household.
- Freia’s later accumulated history strengthens the claim that she becomes more than a programmed function.
- Ellen’s later self-choice cannot be understood without keeping Norn’s wound and coercive reconstruction simultaneously visible.

### Unresolved at the volume horizon

- What will Caladrius demand from Eve?
- Can Branica survive being a counterexample?
- What remains of Norn after Keyaru’s intervention?
- Does Keyaru value Eve as person, future power, or both?

# Volume 4
## Home, the buried Keyaru, sacred inheritance, and the dark teacher

### Volume thesis

The volume gives Keyaru a temporary experience of productive communal life and makes his buried nourishing self explicit, then destroys that home and redirects grief into sacred and strategic war. It also reveals Bullet as both predator and teacher.

### Governing question

> **Can Keyaru protect himself without becoming the exact negation of the gentle person he once wanted to be?**

### What the volume establishes at its own horizon

The prologue makes Ellen’s origin unambiguously coercive. Norn’s damaged autobiographical continuity is filled through drugs, hypnosis-like conditioning, and explicit `洗脳 / sennō`. Later Ellen personhood cannot be used to sanitize this origin.

The Black-Wing village makes Keyaru’s constructive capacities visible through hunting, cooking, labor, and reciprocal gratitude. When others enjoy what he makes, he experiences efficacy without domination.

The apple/sweets dream is now explicit. Childhood Keyaru imagined preserving the orchard and making harsh life more bearable through food, even without the power to defeat monsters.

The dream confrontation between old Keyaru and Keyarga is the first direct internal argument over the revenge identity. Old Keyaru says he never wanted to become this person; Keyarga answers that the old dream excluded Keyaru’s own safety and happiness.

At this volume horizon, the scene establishes a narrower proposition:

> benevolence without adequate self-protection made the earlier Keyaru vulnerable to exploitation.

Bullet is then explicitly identified as Keyaru’s teacher. His intelligence background, lie detection, psychological conditioning, ally/enemy classification, and contingency logic become part of Keyaru’s inherited survival architecture.

Eve’s Caladrius trial and the Black-Wing massacre transform survivorhood into sacred-political burden. Guren enters as divine beast, household child, purifier, and weapon.

### Keyaru state

Keyarga remains dominant, but the text now gives the old Keyaru a voice rather than leaving him as pure retrospective abstraction.

### Relationship and institutional changes

- Ellen begins as constructed sister-strategist.
- Eve becomes bearer of the Black-Wing dead.
- Guren joins the household under rule-bound divine-beast conditions.
- Bullet becomes contaminating intellectual inheritance, not merely revenge target.

### Metaphysical additions

- Caladrius trials, blessing, disease power, and life expenditure;
- dead retainers carried through Eve;
- divine beasts shaped by relation to their bearer;
- black regenerative transformation;
- early evidence that sacred power is constrained by rules.

### Ethical problem

The volume makes two difficult propositions coexist:

- Keyarga is a morally dangerous solution;
- the original self-sacrificing Keyaru was not a viable safe solution either.

### Motifs and Japanese terms

- `洗脳` — Ellen’s origin mechanism;
- `教師` — Bullet as teacher;
- apples, sweets, cooking, and home;
- handmade objects and grief;
- sacred judgment’s inability to read Keyaru cleanly.

### Primary-source anchors

| Norn is conditioned into Ellen through explicit `洗脳` | V4 — Prologue「回復術士はノルン姫を教育する」 — `p-001.xhtml` ¶74–85 | TF |
| Cooking and childhood dream of making people happy through apples/sweets | V4 — 第八話「回復術士はかつての夢を見る」 — `p-009.xhtml` ¶150–169 | TF |
| Old Keyaru condemns Keyarga; Keyarga argues the old dream excluded his own happiness | V4 — 第二十話「回復術士は夢を見る」 — `p-021.xhtml` ¶3–24 | TF |

### Retrospective revision

- Volumes 8–9 later recast the Volume-4 conflict explicitly through `仮面` and then `鎧`, making Volume 4 the prehistory of the armor model.
- Volume 10 supplies the full causal chain from orchard to bereavement to Hero aspiration to Jioral and revenge.
- Guren later inherits Chronos’s time function.

### Unresolved at the volume horizon

- Can Ellen become more than an imposed identity?
- Is Bullet’s teaching inseparable from Keyaru’s later survival competence?
- What is the common source of Jioral and Demon King black power?
- Can Eve carry the dead without being consumed by them?

# Volume 5
## Defeating the Demon King reveals the office behind the person

### Volume thesis

Victory over Hakuou exposes Demon Kinghood as a metaphysical-political system rather than a single villain, while Eve’s succession turns revenge into the burden of ruling a fractured aftermath.

### Governing question

> **What does it mean to inherit an office whose power, violence, and corruptive pressures exceed the intentions of the person who occupies it?**

### What the volume establishes at its own horizon

Kureha earns the Sword Hero role through an independent qualification trial. Keyaru provides access but does not simply appoint her. This prevents the Hero system from being modeled as wholly state-controlled.

Lapis’s illness is revealed as deliberate poisoning disguised as medicine. Her body becomes political leverage over Carol and an early instance of care, dependency, and coercion being structurally fused.

Hakuou’s memories reveal that the Demon King’s visible political role rests on a deeper black-power source. Black-god-contaminated souls, vessel logic, destructive pressure, and the Demon King heart/Philosopher’s Stone all enter the system model.

Eve insists on seeing those killed by Caladrius. Her moral seriousness lies not in refusing sovereign violence but in refusing to abstract its victims away.

Bullet again wins by objective structure rather than direct combat superiority. He waits for Keyaru to defeat Hakuou, uses Eve as leverage, extracts the Stone through another unit, and escapes with the actual strategic prize.

### Keyaru state

He is no longer merely hunting private abusers. He is managing succession, preserving useful guilty actors, protecting Eve, and encountering the political consequences of regime change.

### Relationship and institutional changes

- Eve becomes Demon King and therefore sovereign counterpart rather than household possession alone.
- Carol becomes hidden adviser after collaboration is contextualized rather than simply erased.
- Kureha morally and metaphysically reoccupies the Sword Hero office after Blade.

### Metaphysical additions

- independent Hero qualification and vacancy succession;
- Demon King heart as `賢者の石`;
- black-god/soul-sacrifice architecture;
- black conversion as reason-destroying military production;
- Caladrius and Guren as counterforces.

### Ethical problem

The volume distinguishes culpable choice from corrupted or coerced agency without eliminating responsibility. Hakuou is neither innocent puppet nor purely autonomous tyrant.

### Motifs and Japanese terms

- `【剣】の試練` — worthiness tested beyond state appointment;
- `賢者の石` — heart, power amplifier, reset resource;
- seeing victims;
- office versus person;
- victory creating political obligation.

- What exactly will Bullet do with the Stone?

# Volume 6
## Healing becomes jurisdiction over roles, concepts, and political order

### Volume thesis

Keyaru becomes publicly and metaphysically heroic while remaining ethically compromised. Healing expands from bodily repair into explicit restoration of concepts and prior states; regime destruction becomes managed reconstruction.

### Governing question

> **If healing returns something to the state it ought to have, who possesses authority to define that state?**

### What the volume establishes at its own horizon

The novel gives its clearest definition of healing:

> `【回復】というのはあるべき姿に戻す力`

Keyaru then restores `勇者という概念` and explicitly calls the act `概念の【回復】`. Conceptual healing is therefore textual, not merely analytical terminology.

The Hero must now be separated into four layers:

- social title;
- power/class category;
- metaphysical anti-black role;
- ethical heroism.

These do not necessarily coincide.

Keyaru uses restored Hero functionality and healing to return the black-transformed king toward an earlier human state. At this horizon, the operation establishes restoration of a prior valid state at a level more abstract than ordinary bodily repair.

The source also models explanation without exculpation. Keyaru recognizes that Prohm and the royal system helped produce Flare and Norn while refusing to treat institutional production as absolution.

New Jioral depends on symbolic continuity. Freia performs Flare publicly, Ellen supplies administration, and Keyaru’s Healing Hero image provides civic legitimacy. Regime transition is therefore inseparable from political theater.

Freia’s Divine Armament becomes Vanargand, reproducing the first-timeline form independently and strengthening the mixed continuity model between Flare and Freia.

### Keyaru state

He is de facto ruler, public savior, and system purifier, but his rejection of Prohm is still grounded primarily in autonomy rather than a universal constitutional ethic.

### Institutional and metaphysical changes

- black knights become centralized terror and conversion machinery;
- the original Hero role counters black power;
- Divine Armaments are Hero-linked and intent-responsive;
- New Jioral begins as continuity management rather than clean founding.

### Ethical problem

Healing becomes potentially paternalistic or invasive because “restoration” depends on a chosen norm. The power to heal concepts is also the power to decide which concept counts as original or proper.

### Motifs and Japanese terms

- `あるべき姿` — the form/state something ought to have;
- `概念の【回復】` — explicit conceptual healing;
- `真の勇者` — metaphysical role versus ethical person;
- hero as civic religion;
- purification and scapegoating.

### Primary-source anchors

|---|---|---|
| Healing defined as return to the state that ought to be | V6 — 第十五話「回復術士は真の勇者になる」 — `p-016.xhtml` ¶13–16 | TF |
| Keyaru restores the Hero concept and names `概念の【回復】` | V6 — `p-016.xhtml` ¶13–41 | TF |
| Healing returns Prohm toward earlier human form | V6 — `p-016.xhtml` ¶52–61 | TF |
| New Jioral publicly uses Healing Hero/Flare symbolism | V6 — 第六話「回復術士は王を目指す」 — `p-007.xhtml` ¶165–172; Epilogue `p-017.xhtml` ¶19–20 | TF use; SI legitimacy-necessity reading |

- Volume 8 explicitly applies the same temporal logic to one person as `【時間回帰】`.
- Volume 10 targets the world with `回帰の【回復】`.
- Volume 9 abolishes New Jioral into Panakeia, revealing reconstruction as provisional.
- Volume 10 suggests Hero deaths have special systemic consequences not visible here.

### Unresolved at the volume horizon

- What selects replacement Heroes?
- How autonomous are Divine Armaments?
- Can New Jioral avoid reproducing Jioral’s instrumental logic?
- What is the full relation among Hero, Demon King, and black systems?

# Volume 7
## Creation, deterrence, and the limits of personal superiority

### Volume thesis

Keyaru moves from exceptional personal power toward technology, deterrence, and diplomacy, while Bullet demonstrates that battlefield strength cannot substitute for strategic control over the problem’s structure.

### Governing question

> **Can overwhelming capability create peace without creating an endless coalition against the person who possesses it?**

### What the volume establishes at its own horizon

The aircraft sequence shows genuine constructive joy. Keyaru calculates, prototypes, tests, names, and iterates. The builder self is therefore broader than cooking: he enjoys producing systems and objects that did not previously exist.

The invention is immediately dual-use. Airpower provides observation, mobility, safe altitude, range extension, surprise, and mass magical destruction. Creative efficacy and coercive scale are not morally separable.

Freia’s aerial attack demonstrates strategic bombing capacity. Yet Keyaru explicitly rejects the simple solution of destroying every hostile ruler. He reasons that decapitation conquest would make all states fear they were next and push Jioral toward permanent war until total dominance.

His preferred structure becomes:

> reassurance and negotiation in public + overwhelming capability in reserve.

Bullet remains the exception Keyaru treats as capable of predicting even his innovations. The conference catastrophe then shows that Bullet can define the objective, trap, timing, and political field beyond the duel itself.

### Keyaru state

He is becoming engineer, deterrence actor, and diplomatic strategist. The volume also forces him to experience genuine strategic defeat.

### Relationship and institutional changes

- Freia becomes strategic magical-airpower asset and diplomat.
- Ellen becomes state-level planner.
- Eve’s young regime reveals purges, factional fragility, and thin institutional capacity.
- Granzbach becomes Bullet’s political platform rather than merely enemy territory.

### Metaphysical additions

- divine materials such as God’s Tear;
- deeper black monsters closer to the source;
- Divine Armament hidden forms and expanded capabilities.

### Ethical problem

Deterrence may reduce war while making peace depend on credible terror. The volume does not resolve whether peace backed by personalized annihilation threats is stable or merely enforced quiet.

### Motifs and Japanese terms

- `飛行機` — invention as both wonder and coercive scale;
- conference and battlefield as competing theaters;
- deterrence replacing conquest;
- Bullet as adversarial model;
- builder identity under strategic pressure.

### Primary-source anchors

| Claim | Locator | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Keyaru’s iterative aircraft invention and enjoyment of creation | V7 — `p-013.xhtml` ¶70–105 | TF |
| Airpower’s observation, altitude, range, and bombardment effects | V7 — `p-016.xhtml` ¶46–115 | TF |
| Keyaru rejects ruler-decapitation conquest because it would produce endless balancing war | V7 — `p-017.xhtml` ¶66–81 | TF |
| Keyaru expects Bullet to anticipate an otherwise unpredictable air attack | V7 — `p-016.xhtml` ¶94–97 | TF |

### Retrospective revision

- Volume 9 turns aircraft into reproducible alliance technology and accepts proliferation.
- Volume 8 shows companions acting without Keyaru and directly challenges Bullet’s doll model.
- Volume 10 makes material settlement, rather than only deterrence, part of the peace project.

### Unresolved at the volume horizon

- Can Jioral/Panakeia institutionalize technology beyond Keyaru?
- What is Bullet’s deeper black objective?
- Can deterrence survive proliferation?
- Can Eve’s regime enforce its own moral commitments?

# Volume 8
## Irreplaceability defeats the logic of the perfect reset

### Volume thesis

Keyaru refuses to replace present companions with same-shaped earlier versions and defeats Bullet’s worldview partly by trusting those companions to act without him. Personhood becomes accumulated lived history rather than body alone.

### Governing question

> **If history can be optimized, what makes the current person morally non-interchangeable with a better earlier version?**

### What the volume establishes at its own horizon

Ellen proposes the cold reset solution: kill Eve, recover the Stone, begin again. Keyaru refuses because reset Eve would not be the Eve who lived, changed, loved, and accumulated history with him. He extends that reasoning to Freia, Setsuna, Kureha, and Guren.

The key proposition is explicit:

> same shape does not equal same person when shared time has been removed.

Keyaru also describes Keyarga as the `理想の自分` and `仮面` produced because he hated his weaker self. This is the first direct identity-performance language in the series.

Bullet’s doll logic becomes the ethical center of the conflict. Bullet treats persons as reproducible, idealizable material. Keyaru counters that the women around him think and act independently.

The battle structure supports the claim. Freia, Kureha, Setsuna, Ellen, Guren, Casta, and others act outside immediate Keyaru control. Victory becomes distributed competence rather than protagonist omnipotence.

Ellen deliberately reconstructs Norn’s strategic capacities because they are needed. The volume therefore already shows Norn-derived strategic capacities coexisting with the Ellen identity rather than requiring immediate restoration of Norn.

Keyaru ultimately uses localized temporal regression against Bullet rather than erasing the world.

### Keyaru state

Trust becomes possible without eliminating possessiveness. He still calls the companions “his,” but “mine” increasingly contains persons whose independent agency matters rather than interchangeable property.

### Institutional and metaphysical changes

- scalable anti-black weapons distribute resistance beyond exceptional individuals;
- black-gate/crest mechanics become actionable;
- personal `【時間回帰】` is established as a narrowed version of world reset.

### Ethical problem

The volume makes attachment a constraint on optimization. Any future reset is therefore no longer morally free.

### Motifs and Japanese terms

- `仮面` — idealized mask;
- `やり直しは利かないな` — redo cannot simply recover this history;
- doll-play versus personhood;
- distributed agency;
- trust as anti-trauma practice.

# Volume 9
## Integration, Panakeia, and post-revenge statecraft

### Volume thesis

Keyaru explicitly identifies Keyarga as armor, Ellen knowingly chooses Ellen over full Norn restoration, and Panakeia attempts to preserve useful capacity while changing the ends and membership of the political order.

### Governing question

> **What should a revenge protagonist—and the state built around him—become once revenge no longer supplies the organizing purpose?**

### What the volume establishes at its own horizon

The identity thesis becomes explicit:

> `ケヤルガは俺にとって鎧だった。`

Keyaru says the merciless self was required for revenge but cannot simply be his permanent mode. He also refuses simple regression to the old village boy.

Ellen reveals that she knows she was Norn. She has reconstructed Norn’s strategic personality from traces, refuses Keyaru’s offer of complete restoration, and fears that full Norn return would destroy Ellen. This is later self-ratification after coercive origin, not free origin.

Cooking becomes an explicit post-revenge positive identity. Keyaru imagines an ordinary restaurant and a life in which producing happiness is not merely preparation for violence.

The monarchy/republic debate separates competence, procedural legitimacy, and succession. Ellen acknowledges monarchy’s fatal long-term risk; Keyaru chooses the nearer horizon because he believes he and Ellen can govern well now.

Panakeia’s founding uses legal discontinuity, symbolic continuity, lawfare, deterrence, and political theater. The world-conference strategy induces states to treat Jioral and Panakeia as distinct when useful, then uses that precedent against inherited liabilities.

Religion becomes political technology. Keyaru rejects Faran’s human supremacy and false sacred hierarchy but not religion as an instrument of cohesion, meaning, and control.

Falbo introduces coercion-sensitive culpability. Keyaru initially insists crime remains crime, then distinguishes hostage-coerced betrayal from malicious betrayal and grants conditional pardon.

Eve’s refusal of retaliatory persecution becomes a sovereign ethic Keyaru recognizes as strength beyond his own instinct.

### Keyaru state

Integrated Keyaru retains Keyarga’s competence, intimidation, and possessiveness while recovering cooking, building, children, retirement, and a future not organized around the revenge list.

### Institutional and metaphysical changes

The volume is politically dominant rather than metaphysically expansive:

- Jioral → Panakeia discontinuity;
- personal monarchy;
- world-conference capture;
- human/demon alliance;
- counterreligious strategy;
- technology export and expected proliferation.

### Ethical problem

Panakeia may be a genuine change in ends while retaining authoritarian means. The central stress test is whether it is a new order or Jioral’s instrumental logic under a more capable, less supremacist, more protective ruler.

### Motifs and Japanese terms

- `鎧` — defensive identity architecture;
- `共和制` / monarchy debate;
- cooking and restaurant;
- state renaming;
- religion as managed narrative;
- forgiveness after revenge.

# Volume 10
## Love, dreams, bodily compulsion, and the second redo

### Volume thesis

Keyaru finally identifies a positive life beyond revenge—orchard, food, family, settlement, ordinary happiness—just as the higher world-system commandeers bodies and makes the current timeline unsalvageable. The second redo begins with consultation and shared claims over history rather than Keyaru’s exclusive revenge authorship.

### Governing question

> **Can history be rewritten to prevent trauma without treating the people formed by that trauma as disposable drafts?**

### What the volume establishes at its own horizon

The Red Dragon conflict is not ordinary rebellion. Ragna’s memory, Hiseki’s obscured recollection, and the later Hero-mark catastrophe show causal manipulation beyond normal factional motive.

Ragna’s `妾 / warawa` is explicitly performed chiefly language; her private `私 / watashi` emerges with vulnerability. The marriage begins under severe political pressure but later develops genuine personal choice. Later reciprocity neither erases nor invalidates the founding asymmetry.

Keyaru admits fear and loneliness. He has made decisions that killed thousands and has hidden uncertainty because followers depend on his performed certainty.

He explicitly relinquishes the necessity of personally killing Bullet. This behaviorally tests the Volume 9 armor thesis: revenge no longer outranks the current life he wants.

Chronos establishes a higher rule architecture:

- divine beasts as world-maintaining `駒`;
- opposing/destructive pieces;
- restricted intervention;
- punishment for prohibited revelation/action;
- a warning against further Hero killing.

But Ellen finds many historical Hero-on-Hero killings without the same result, and Bullet has killed more Heroes than Keyaru. Therefore the simple causal law is inadequate.

The seven Keyaru Hero deaths / seven Hero-marked Demon King candidates correlation is textual; the exact mechanism remains unresolved.

The orchard conversation supplies the complete causal autobiography:

> apples and apple pies → parents killed by monsters → Hero aspiration → Jioral objectification → revenge → question of whether the present self is the self he wanted.

Ellen’s `損切り / songiri` analysis says there is a point where attachment to the current timeline cannot justify losing the last chance to prevent total collapse.

Lapis and Ragna provide the clearest separation between action and agency. Their conscious loyalties remain intact while bodily behavior is commandeered.

Keyaru does not treat them as moral enemies. He directs anger toward the system imposing the action and refuses immediate escape/reset while loved people remain salvageable.

Bullet’s final act preserves his philosophy. He may produce a beneficial outcome, but he does so by threatening Eve and forcing Keyaru to kill him because he believes his understanding overrides Keyaru’s choice.

Ellen is reported and overwhelmingly implied dead, but not corpse-verified by Keyaru. Hiseki is likewise almost certainly killed or fatally defeated, but the exact endpoint is indirect.

The second redo is consultative, not equal. Keyaru still holds exceptional information and makes the operative decision; Guren supplies indispensable temporal power. Yet Freia, Setsuna, Kureha, Eve, and Guren now articulate claims over what history should become.

### Keyaru state

The integrated self is tested under maximal pressure:

- relinquished personal revenge;
- named fear and loneliness;
- positive future desire;
- recognition of compelled action;
- refusal to reduce loved attackers to enemies;
- willingness to reveal hidden history;
- end of exclusive authorship over the redo.

### Relationship and institutional changes

- Red Dragons and Black Wings are to share territory, infrastructure, food production, tax relief, and delegated government.
- Peace becomes material settlement rather than abstract alliance.
- Ragna becomes genuine partner before system compulsion invades action.
- Ellen’s apparent death converts strategic care into self-sacrifice.
- Freia’s request recognizes Flare as a prior suffering person whose trauma should be prevented.

### Metaphysical additions

- Chronos and higher rule architecture;
- Guren succeeding the time role;
- Hero-marked Demon King candidates;
- action–agency separation through bodily compulsion;
- Ellen’s external-causality hypothesis;
- world-scale `回帰の【回復】`;
- uncertain memory retention after reset.

### Ethical problem

The second reset is procedurally better than the first without becoming democratic or morally clean. Preventing trauma may alter or prevent the exact selves produced by that trauma. Consultation recognizes others’ claims without equalizing operative power.

### Motifs and Japanese terms

- `妾` / `私` — public role versus private person;
- `損切り` — cutting losses when one’s own life-history is among the assets;
- `破壊衝動` — Demon King destructive impulse;
- `回帰の【回復】` — the world as healing target;
- `やり直し` transformed from revenge opportunity into burdened historical revision;
- apple orchard as integrated future rather than return to innocence.

# Final audit synthesis

The ten-volume progression can now be stated without erasing chronology:

1. **Volume 1:** the violated body becomes the authoring body.
2. **Volume 2:** private revenge becomes legitimacy warfare.
3. **Volume 3:** ordinary coexistence falsifies the inherited moral map.
4. **Volume 4:** the constructive Keyaru and Bullet’s intellectual inheritance become explicit.
5. **Volume 5:** villain defeat reveals office, succession, and system.
6. **Volume 6:** healing becomes jurisdiction over concepts and political continuity.
7. **Volume 7:** invention and deterrence meet the limits of personal superiority.
8. **Volume 8:** accumulated history makes persons non-interchangeable.
9. **Volume 9:** revenge armor is consciously integrated into post-revenge rule.
10. **Volume 10:** the world itself becomes an author of bodies, forcing a second redo in which others finally acquire acknowledged claims over history.

The ledger’s controlling caution is therefore:

> **Later insight may reinterpret an earlier volume, but it must not erase what the earlier volume actually knew, what its characters actually valued, or which questions were genuinely still open.**
