# I. Purpose and relation to Tranche 1

Tranche 1 established the longitudinal spine of the V2 argument: first-timeline objectification; the Keyaru → Keyarga identity sequence; the apple/nourishment line; Volume 8 irreplaceability; Volume 9 post-revenge identity and politics; Volume 10 bodily compulsion, higher rules, Bullet’s final intervention, and the second redo.

The remaining Phase-2 problem was narrower. Several specialist documents still lacked enough direct Japanese evidence to support the planned expansion without falling back on memory or the earlier analytical conversation.

This tranche therefore backfills the five areas identified at the end of Tranche 1:

1. **Document 03 — personhood and relationships:** exact Ellen origin; Freia continuity; later Setsuna, Kureha, Lapis, Guren, and Ragna/Hiseki material.
2. **Document 04 — antagonists and dark mirrors:** Blade, Prohm, Hakuou, Hiseki, and Faran.
3. **Document 05 — politics and institutions:** Panakeia legal discontinuity, conference mechanics, airpower proliferation, Eve’s administrative constraints, and the Volume-10 settlement.
4. **Document 06 — metaphysics:** Hero succession, Divine Armaments, Demon King heart/Philosopher’s Stone, black knights, divine-beast restrictions, and individual time regression.
5. **Document 08 — Japanese voice and recurring vocabulary:** Keyaru’s cognitive prose rhythm, role-based register shifts, and a compact lexical anchor set.

The locator convention remains:

> **V[volume] — [chapter title] — `p-XXX.xhtml` ¶[normalized paragraph number]**

Paragraph numbers refer to the non-empty `<p>` elements in the frozen EPUB extraction used by this project. They are analytical locators rather than print page numbers.

Evidence labels remain:

- **TF — Textual fact**
- **SI — Strong inference**
- **SP — Speculation**
- **VJ — Value judgment / interpretive judgment**

# II. Personhood and relationship backfill

# 1. Norn → Ellen: the origin is explicitly coercive conditioning

**Locator:** V4 — Prologue「回復術士はノルン姫を教育する」 — `p-001.xhtml` ¶74–85

This scene needs to remain visible throughout the V2 Ellen analysis because later Ellen self-authorship can otherwise accidentally sanitize her origin.

## Verified facts

**TF:** Norn wants to understand or recover who she is after Keyaru has already damaged her autobiographical continuity.

**TF:** Keyaru uses a combination of drugged drink/incense and hypnosis-like conditioning.

**TF:** The narration explicitly describes the result as successful `洗脳 / sennō` — brainwashing/conditioning.

**TF:** Norn comes to “remember” the name **Ellen** and Keyaru as her older brother.

The relevant point is not the later sexualized material in the same scene, which should remain clinically summarized rather than reproduced. The important V2 conclusion is the mechanism of identity production.

## V2 consequence

Ellen’s character must be analyzed through two propositions that are simultaneously true:

> **Ellen originates in an illegitimate coercive reconstruction.**

and

> **The later Ellen becomes a subject capable of knowing that origin, recovering Norn’s strategic capacities, and choosing not to restore Norn completely.**

The second proposition cannot retroactively convert the first into consent.

Conversely, the first proposition cannot by itself prove that every later preference Ellen forms is fictitious.

The interesting personhood problem is precisely that a person produced through coercion can later become capable of reflective self-ratification.

### Epistemic classification

- Ellen’s coerced origin: **TF**.
- Ellen later knowing the Norn history: **TF** from V9.
- Ellen later choosing Ellen over full Norn restoration: **TF** from V9.
- “Ellen becomes a real person”: **VJ/SI**, requiring an argument about continuity, memory, agency, and accumulated history rather than a single textual declaration.

# 2. Freia: continuity survives autobiographical erasure in deeper dispositions

The V1 synthesis correctly resisted both extremes — “Freia is simply Flare with amnesia” and “Freia is an unrelated new being” — but did not display enough evidence for the mixed continuity model.

## 2.1 Vanargand: a deep preference recurs independently

**Locator:** V6 — 第十三話「回復術士は王を拒絶する」 — `p-014.xhtml` ¶120–140

Keyaru gives Freia a captured Divine Armament. The weapon responds to the Hero who holds it and, through the bearer’s wish/prayer, becomes the weapon the bearer wants.

Freia chooses a world-tree-like magical staff and independently names it:

> `ヴァナルガンド`

Keyaru recognizes the resulting form as the same Divine Armament form Flare used in the first timeline.

Freia does not arrive at this through an explicit recovered autobiographical memory. She describes the weapon almost as if the weapon itself communicated the name.

### Implication

This is unusually strong evidence that erasing autobiographical memory did **not** erase every level of continuity.

Something persists in:

- magical disposition;
- aesthetic/functional preference;
- affinity with a particular weapon form;
- perhaps embodied or deeper cognitive structure.

Therefore V2 should model Freia/Flare continuity dimensionally:

| Dimension | Status |
|---|---|
| Body | continuous but altered by Keyaru |
| Magical capacity | substantially continuous |
| Learned knowledge/skills | deliberately retained |
| Autobiographical memory | deliberately erased/disrupted |
| Prior relational identity | deliberately overwritten |
| Deep preference/disposition | at least partly continuous |
| Post-rewrite lived history | genuinely accumulated as Freia |

The result is a **palimpsest**, not a binary replacement problem.

---

## 2.2 Private Freia versus public Flare is explicit in self-understanding

**Locator:** V6 — Epilogue「ジオラル王国再建」 — `p-017.xhtml` around ¶191

The reconstructed political order requires the public continuity of Princess Flare. Visually and ceremonially, Freia can perform that role.

But the narration makes the private identity distinction explicit: the person doing so understands herself as **Freia acting as Princess Flare**, not as Flare simply restored.

### V2 consequence

The later state uses “Flare” as political continuity while the private subject lives as Freia.

This makes Freia simultaneously:

- a person with a new lived self;
- a bearer of remnants of Flare;
- and a state technology through which the public story of Jioral is managed.

Document 03 should analyze the private subject. Document 05 should analyze the political use of the public Flare persona. Document 07 should evaluate the ethics of both the original rewriting and later symbolic use.

## 2.3 Freia independently reads Keyaru’s emotional danger

**Locator:** V8 — 第四話「回復術士は魔王と再会する」 — `p-005.xhtml` approximately ¶35–60

Freia recognizes that Keyaru’s emotional world has become fixed on Bullet and fears that, once the final revenge target disappears, Keyaru may become empty or lose his organizing purpose.

Keyaru is surprised that she sees through him.

### V2 consequence

This scene matters because Freia is not functioning as a passive programmed attachment unit. She observes Keyaru, forms an independent psychological judgment, and worries about a future that is not reducible to immediate obedience.

It does not erase the conditioning that structures the relationship. It does demonstrate later subjectivity operating inside that structure.

## 2.4 Volume 10: Freia acquires a relation to Flare as a prior suffering person

**Locator:** V10 — Epilogue「回復術士のやり直し」 — `p-022.xhtml` ¶108–110

Freia asks that, in the next redo, Keyaru try to become the original Flare’s friend. She has dim, fragmentary access to the sense that Flare was lonely and suffering.

### V2 consequence

This is philosophically stronger than “Freia forgives Flare.”

Freia is a subject who exists because Flare’s prior continuity was violated, yet she can still recognize that prior Flare as someone whose suffering generated a moral claim.

The strongest formulation is:

> **Freia does not demand that history reproduce the violence required to create Freia exactly as she presently exists.**

That gives the second redo a genuinely difficult identity problem: preventing formative violence may change or prevent the exact self who presently asks for the intervention.

# 3. Setsuna: chosen loyalty develops inside a structure of dependence

Tranche 1 established the coercive founding conditions. The later volumes show how much genuine agency develops without eliminating the dependency problem.

## 3.1 Protection becomes self-worth

**Locator:** V7 — Prologue「回復術士は想い人を探す」 — `p-001.xhtml` approximately ¶86–94

Setsuna frames her life around protecting Keyarga and rejects a relationship in which she is only the one being protected.

### Interpretation

This is evidence of genuine chosen loyalty, but it also exposes how enclosed her self-worth has become.

A useful mixed reading is:

> **Setsuna has agency within the relationship, but much of that agency is organized around proving her value to the person who originally became indispensable under conditions of slavery, illness, tribal danger, and overwhelming asymmetry.**

V2 should avoid the two easy errors:

- “She is merely brainwashed and therefore has no subjectivity.”
- “She chose him later, therefore the coercive origin no longer matters.”

---

## 3.2 Species embodiment is part of agency

**Locator:** V9 — 第十五話「回復術士はペットと旅にでる」 — `p-016.xhtml` ¶6–12

Setsuna cannot simply take on the same kind of disguise used by others without losing substantial combat effectiveness because her fighting style depends on Ice-Wolf bodily features.

### V2 consequence

Her body is not merely a cosmetic identity marker.

It is:

- species history;
- combat capacity;
- sensory capability;
- social vulnerability;
- and personal agency.

This makes body alteration ethically relevant beyond appearance.

---

## 3.3 The second-redo request prioritizes tribe over preserving the relationship’s causal origin

**Locator:** V10 — Epilogue — `p-022.xhtml` around ¶106

Setsuna asks that the Ice-Wolf tribe be saved **before** the massacre/slavery path that led her to Keyaru.

### V2 consequence

This is one of the cleanest late tests of whether her loyalty is merely dependency-preservation.

She chooses:

> save my people first, even if that means the history that produced “us” changes.

That choice does not dissolve her love for Keyaru. It demonstrates that her mature values cannot be reduced entirely to reproducing the conditions under which she became his.

The trial warns that only a qualified person may draw the Sword and that an unqualified claimant may die.

Kureha proceeds anyway, grounding her confidence in a life of sword training rather than in Keyaru’s assurance.

The system recognizes her as worthy; the Hero mark appears.

### Evidence status

- Kureha becomes Sword Hero: **TF**.
- The trial independently assesses worthiness: **TF**.
- Keyaru provides access/opportunity rather than directly assigning Hero status: **TF/SI**.

## 4.2 Keyaru himself attributes the recognition to Kureha

**Locator:** V5 — 第六話「回復術士はデートをする」 — `p-007.xhtml` around ¶43–45

Kureha credits Keyaru with enabling her to reach the trial. Keyaru distinguishes that assistance from the decisive recognition: becoming Hero was Kureha’s own accomplishment.

### V2 consequence

This materially sharpens the Hero-system analysis.

Kureha provides an important counterexample to a fully state-controlled Hero ontology.

The V2 interpretive claim that she “morally reclaims” Blade’s office should remain a **VJ**: the metaphysical succession is factual; the moral reclamation is our comparative ethical reading.

### Ethical importance

This scene contains a recurring Keyaru contradiction in unusually clear form:

> **he accurately recognizes coercion and manipulation when others use them, then immediately treats dependency as a strategic resource when he controls it.**

That should be central in Document 07.

Lapis becomes the new Star Rabbit chief and participates in the work of rebuilding political legitimacy after the tribe’s collaboration with the old order.

This substantially changes her narrative function.

She is no longer merely:

**Locator:** V9 — 第十三話「回復術士は愛人と楽しむ」 — `p-014.xhtml` approximately ¶46–67

By Volume 9 Lapis serves around Eve as attendant, guard, intermediary, and information source while also belonging to Keyaru’s intimate network.

Lapis is one of the clearest cases where Keyaru’s “household” becomes a state apparatus.

Her trajectory can be summarized:

That trajectory belongs jointly to Documents 03, 05, and 07, with each document doing a different analytical job.

# 6. Guren: power is structurally coupled to restriction

## 6.1 Rule-bound knowledge exists well before Chronos

**Locator:** V5 — 第十一話「回復術士は魔王の城下町に旅立つ」 — `p-012.xhtml` approximately ¶55–80

Guren recognizes the black miasma and its source but immediately encounters `禁則事項` — prohibited-information rules. She can identify the black power as an enemy but cannot freely disclose the full underlying structure.

She also identifies herself as a `神獣 / shinjū`, a divine beast, and describes divine beasts as beings born when the world requires them.

Her anxiety over how much she is permitted to say establishes that divine-beast restriction is not invented in Volume 10.

---

## 6.2 Caladrius makes the rule structure explicit

**Locator:** V8 — 第五話「回復術士は神鳥から授かる」 — `p-006.xhtml` approximately ¶49–85

Caladrius explains that divine beasts know too much and are bound by world rules. It cannot simply disclose the identity/structure of black power. It can work around the rule enough to teach a countermeasure, but doing so has costs and constraints.

### Retrospective consequence

Volume 10’s Chronos material should be read as an **expansion of a preexisting rule architecture**, not as a late cosmology appearing without preparation.

---

## 6.3 Becoming “more truly” divine reduces ordinary freedom

**Locator:** V10 — 第七話「回復術士は開き直る」 — `p-008.xhtml` approximately ¶134–152

After inheriting the Time role, Guren gains greater power and knowledge but becomes more tightly constrained in how she may intervene.

**Locator:** V10 — Epilogue — `p-022.xhtml` approximately ¶67–69

The restriction intensifies further; Guren can help only within increasingly narrow rule boundaries.

### V2 thesis

The mature formulation can now be stated with strong textual support:

> **For divine beasts, increased metaphysical power can mean decreased personal freedom.**

This is a direct inversion of Keyaru’s foundational equation:

> power = protection against being controlled.

Guren becomes more powerful and simultaneously less free.

That makes her an important metaphysical counterexample to Keyaru’s trauma logic.

# III. Antagonist and dark-mirror backfill

# 7. Blade: opportunistic appetite and entitlement

The Blade material supports the V1 distinction among villain types.

## Verified behavioral pattern

Across the relevant Volume-3 chapters, Blade presents a socially attractive/seductive surface but treats refusal as something power can override. Existing relationships or family claims do not create meaningful boundaries in Blade’s internal permission structure.

The source also indicates a repeated history of victims rather than one uniquely retaliatory target.

### V2 distinction

Blade is best treated as:

> **appetite + entitlement + power**

rather than as a philosophical counterpart to Keyaru in every respect.

This supports a **behavioral** distinction:

- Blade’s sexual predation is opportunistic and appetite-driven.
- Keyaru’s sexualized coercion is more often embedded in revenge, punishment, possession, instrumental control, or incorporation.
- Bullet’s is tied to processing, cultivation, replacement, and the destruction of another person’s authorship.

This distinction must remain clinical and descriptive.

It does **not** establish that Keyaru possesses a principled consent ethic.

# 8. Keyaru’s self-defined “pure love” exposes his incomplete consent concept

**Locator:** V4 — 第二話「回復術士は純愛を楽しむ」 — `p-003.xhtml` around ¶122–128

Keyaru distinguishes Setsuna and Eve from Freia/Ellen by saying he wants the former to come to him through their own will rather than through direct memory alteration/brainwashing. He uses the language of `純愛 / jun’ai` — “pure love.”

At the same time, his internal logic continues to manipulate circumstances, exploit dependency, and classify Freia/Ellen through pet/obedience language.

### V2 consequence

This is valuable because it shows that Keyaru **does possess an internal distinction** between:

- conditioned attachment;
- and attachment he experiences as more genuinely chosen.

But the distinction is ethically incomplete.

He tends to define “chosen” too narrowly as:

> I did not directly rewrite the mind into loving me.

That leaves unaddressed:

- slavery;
- cure dependence;
- political survival;
- information asymmetry;
- fear;
- status;
- engineered circumstances.

Document 07 should therefore say:

> **Keyaru develops a preference for non-programmed affection before he develops anything resembling a robust theory of consent.**

# 9. Prohm Jioral: fatherhood and statecraft share the same instrumental grammar

**Locator:** V6 — 第十三話「回復術士は王を拒絶する」 — `p-014.xhtml` around ¶169–179

Prohm offers Keyaru a place as his right hand. When challenged over his daughters, he treats them as useful pieces whose loss becomes irrelevant once better pieces are available.

**Locator:** V6 — 第十四話「回復術士は王と戦う」 — `p-015.xhtml` early section

Keyaru reflects that growing up under such a father helps explain what Flare and Norn became, while explicitly refusing to treat that explanation as forgiveness.

### V2 consequence

This is one of the cleanest places where the novels themselves model the method V2 should use:

> **causal explanation ≠ moral exculpation**

Prohm also unifies two levels of instrumentalization:

- **family:** daughters as pieces;
- **state:** Heroes, subjects, species, and institutions as pieces.

This is why “Jioral as objectification state” should be demonstrated partly through the royal household rather than treated as an abstract institutional label.

# 10. Hakuou: perpetrator, office-holder, and conscious captive of black power

**Locator:** V5 — 第十九話「回復術士は最後の復讐を誓う」 — `p-020.xhtml` approximately ¶134–149

Keyaru’s access to Hakuou’s memories provides unusually important world-system information.

## Verified findings

**TF:** Hakuou was not always temperamentally identical to the later violent Demon King; earlier he was kinder/timid enough that he consciously performed strength for political survival and species protection.

**TF:** he notices escalating aggression and cruelty and fears the loss of his prior self.

**TF:** he investigates the hidden structure of Demon King power.

**TF:** the black god behind the power seeks sacrificial souls and a vessel/body.

**TF:** the power structure pushes Demon Kings toward war/bloodshed because conflict generates the sacrifices the black god wants.

**TF:** Hakuou resists by recovering sacrificial souls rather than merely submitting.

**TF:** the black god responds by installing a terminal/control mechanism in Hakuou, contributing to his abnormal survivability and domination.

### V2 consequence

Hakuou should not be flattened into either:

> innocent puppet

or

> ordinary tyrant who merely chose evil.

The strongest formulation is:

> **Hakuou is a culpable ruler operating inside an office that exerts genuine corruptive pressure, and he is sufficiently self-aware to fear, investigate, and partially resist that pressure.**

This makes him a warning about **office consuming personhood**, not merely a monster-of-the-volume.

# 11. Hiseki: preserve the contradiction

Hiseki is one of the characters most vulnerable to V2 simplification because his late competence can make earlier predation disappear from analysis, while his earlier predation can make his later political/relational seriousness disappear.

Both are mistakes.

## 11.1 Public role

Across V9–10 Hiseki is:

- a highly capable Red Dragon martial figure;
- strategically perceptive;
- disciplined enough to acknowledge Keyaru’s superiority;
- protective of the tribe;
- mentor/adviser to Ragna;
- and later a practical deputy in the new settlement.

**Locator:** V10 — 第二話「回復術士は求婚する」 — `p-003.xhtml` approximately ¶45–53

Hiseki correctly reasons that if Keyaru intended extermination, Hiseki’s duty would be to warn the tribe and buy time through self-sacrifice.

Keyaru recognizes the judgment as strategically sound.

## 11.2 Private predatory structure

The V9 infiltration material also reveals a species/status hierarchy in Hiseki’s sexual behavior. When he believes the disguised lower-status persona is available to him, his behavior demonstrates coercive entitlement rather than honorable-warrior restraint.

This should be summarized clinically and without reconstructing explicit detail.

## 11.3 Mentor and administrator

By Volume 10 Hiseki treats Ragna as the legitimate future chief while recognizing that she still needs to grow into authority. He supports Black-Wing/Red-Dragon integration, accepts responsibility for policing betrayal from his own side, and becomes Keyaru’s deputy/practical governor.

During the candidate catastrophe he opposes the compelled Ragna and is overwhelmingly implied to die or be fatally defeated.

### V2 conclusion

> **Hiseki is simultaneously a serious protector, mentor, administrator, and species-hierarchical sexual predator.**

V2 should not use one half to cancel the other.

That contradiction is analytically useful because it shows that competence, loyalty, and even sacrifice do not automatically produce a coherent ethic of personhood.

# 12. Faran and the Holy Emperor: sacred hierarchy becomes geopolitical jurisdiction

**Locator:** V9 — 第七話「回復術士は世界宗教に挑む」 — `p-008.xhtml` approximately ¶65–107

Scoderia functions as the center of Faran power. Faran doctrine treats humanity as uniquely chosen/loved and places beast-people, demi-humans, and demons beneath humans.

The church has materially supported or legitimated:

- slavery;
- discrimination;
- war;
- and the designation of inconvenient populations as heretical.

Keyaru’s own village was classified through this machinery for venal/political reasons.

**Locator:** V9 — 第八話「回復術士は異議を唱える」 — `p-009.xhtml` approximately ¶62–67 and ¶119–144

The Holy Emperor is both head of state and religious authority. There is no meaningful church-state separation in the Faran order.

**Locator:** V9 — 第九話「回復術士は世界会議を支配する」 — `p-010.xhtml` approximately ¶30–49

The Holy Emperor attempts to transform a religious species hierarchy into international political jurisdiction by declaring the demon conflict holy war and trying to define Panakeia as an enemy of humanity.

### V2 conclusion

Faran is not merely “corrupt church.”

Its structure is:

> **sacred human supremacy → political hierarchy → legal/social discrimination → holy-war authorization → attempted international jurisdiction.**

Keyaru rejects that ontology while remaining willing to use religion as a different social technology, which is why Document 05 should compare Faran and Panakeia rather than treating them as simple opposites of religion versus secularism.

# IV. Politics and institutional backfill

# 13. Panakeia’s conference strategy: legal discontinuity as in-world lawfare

**Locator:** V9 — 第八話「回復術士は異議を唱える」 — `p-009.xhtml` ¶62–129

This sequence is substantially more rigorous than the compressed V1 paragraph suggested.

## 13.1 International recognition

Keyaru explicitly reads participation under the name **Panakeia**, rather than Jioral, as international recognition of the new polity.

## 13.2 Ellen constructs the precedent

Ellen first asserts Jioral’s enormous wartime contribution and potential claim to spoils.

She then voluntarily disclaims those benefits on the ground that:

> those achievements belong to **Jioral**, not **Panakeia**.

Other states agree because they expect to keep the spoils for themselves.

## 13.3 The same states then seek liabilities

They subsequently demand that Panakeia inherit Jioral’s reparations burden.

Keyaru uses their earlier agreement against them:

> if Jioral and Panakeia are separate for benefits, they cannot conveniently become the same entity for liabilities.

The narration itself describes Ellen’s first argument as creating a precedent that the two entities are distinct.

## 13.4 Force underwrites the argument

This is not liberal legalism detached from coercive power.

Keyaru’s ability to threaten personal retaliation against leadership remains the background condition that makes “pushing through” the argument possible.

### Correct V2 formulation

> **Panakeia uses in-world legal discontinuity as strategic lawfare, but the success of the legal argument is underwritten by overwhelming personalized deterrence.**

Do **not** present this as a statement of real-world international law.

# 14. The world conference exposes political economy, not only diplomacy

**Locator:** V9 — 第九話「回復術士は世界会議を支配する」 — `p-010.xhtml`

Keyaru threatens to create a rival conference including demons if Faran tries to monopolize the definition of humanity and legitimate war.

Prearranged allies, military weight, economic incentives, and defections combine until the Holy Emperor loses control of the room; a majority vote removes him from chairmanship and installs Keyaru.

The first new agenda becomes ending human-demon war.

Crucially, the novel then explicitly notes that peace has losers:

- militaries;
- mercenaries;
- weapons production;
- medicine;
- lodging;
- food supply;
- states or industries dependent on war demand.

### V2 consequence

This is unusually direct textual support for treating *Redo*’s late politics as more than ruler-personality drama.

The story recognizes:

> **war can become an economic system with constituencies that benefit from continuation even when peace is morally preferable.**

That is an important source anchor for Document 05.

# 15. Aviation becomes intentionally reproducible strategic technology

## 15.1 Mass production is planned

**Locator:** V9 — 第十一話「回復術士は開発する」 — `p-012.xhtml` approximately ¶45–87

Keyaru designs later aircraft around:

- materials the human world can actually supply;
- operation by ordinary/skilled mages rather than only exceptional party members;
- compromises driven by mithril cost;
- explicit expectation of `量産 / ryōsan` — mass production.

## 15.2 Export is an alliance condition

**Locator:** V9 — `p-012.xhtml` approximately ¶126–132

Casta receives an aircraft as part of alliance arrangements.

Keyaru expects:

- reverse engineering;
- domestic production;
- other states eventually copying the concept;
- loss of technological monopoly.

He accepts this as inevitable once the idea exists.

## 15.3 The platform continues to iterate

**Locator:** V9 — 第十二話「回復術士は競争する」 — `p-013.xhtml` approximately ¶6–12 and ¶99–125

The design gains runway/wheel logic and more advanced compressed-air/fire propulsion while preserving the criterion that it remain operable and reproducible beyond Keyaru himself.

### V2 conclusion

The development sequence is:

> **personal magical improvisation → battlefield superiority → diplomatic deterrent → reproducible platform → export → expected diffusion.**

This is genuine technological institutionalization.

It also creates a long-run political problem: Keyaru cannot permanently monopolize the capability he introduces.

# 16. Eve’s sovereignty has ordinary administrative limits

The V1 synthesis rightly treated Eve as a sovereign rather than a household appendage, but it underdeveloped how incomplete her institutional control actually is.

## 16.1 Thin governing class

**Locator:** V9 — 第十話「回復術士は帰還する」 — `p-011.xhtml` approximately ¶71–94

The Black Wings are numerically weak and have lost many politically experienced people.

Eve must govern among stronger tribal chiefs while lacking a deep administrative bench.

Carol, separated from an older tribal identity through an assumed role, becomes useful as adviser/teacher. Lapis helps bridge multiple networks.

Ellen praises Eve for learning not to make every major decision immediately; Eve now takes difficult issues back for consultation.

## 16.2 Sovereignty does not mean total compliance

**Locator:** V9 — 第十六話「回復術士は変装する」 — `p-017.xhtml` approximately ¶14–24

The realm experiences unrest and small rebellions. Moving forces to respond to one problem exposes other areas.

**Locator:** V9 — Epilogue「回復術士は悩む」 — `p-019.xhtml`

Eve has forbidden retaliation against the old side, yet persecution still occurs through subordinate disobedience, local hatred, or manipulated/false-flag dynamics.

### V2 consequence

Eve’s mature political problem is not “be a kinder king.”

It is:

> **how does a ruler make a moral order operational when subordinate actors, factional interests, information limits, and weak administrative capacity do not automatically obey sovereign intention?**

That gives her anti-retaliation policy much more political weight.

# 17. Volume 10 settlement: peace becomes land, water, food, tax, housing, and delegation

## 17.1 Material base

# 22. Black knights: conversion, discipline, and networked immortality

## 22.1 Origin

**Locator:** V5 — `p-020.xhtml` approximately ¶146–149

Black-god-contaminated sacrificial souls can be put into human/demon bodies to create immortal black monsters/knights.

## 22.2 Material body and mind destruction

**Locator:** V6 — 第四話「回復術士は弓の雨を突き抜ける」 — `p-005.xhtml` approximately ¶13–23

The underlying material is human. Black miasma grants extreme physical ability and regeneration but destroys reason, reducing the subject to puppet-like use.

**Locator:** V6 — 第二話「回復術士はつかの間の平穏を楽しむ」 — `p-003.xhtml` approximately ¶152–159

## 22.4 State discipline

**Locator:** V6 — 第五話「回復術士は英雄となる」 — `p-006.xhtml` around ¶73–76

The terror of transformation is used as discipline: obey, or risk conversion.

Guren’s sacred flame removes the black immortality layer enough to permit true killing. Volume 8 adds a deeper scalable approach: regeneration consumes soul, while sealing the external connection/gate with the learned crest disrupts replenishment.

Black knights are not simply undead infantry.

They form a system of:

> **human material → black conversion → reason loss → puppet army → immortality/regeneration → disciplinary terror → propagation risk → external power connection.**

That is why they belong in both metaphysics and institutional violence analysis.

The governing question remains Keyaru’s own definition:

# VI. Japanese voice and lexical backfill

# 24. Keyaru’s voice is cognitively distinctive more than dialectally distinctive

The primary text supports the V1 linguistic thesis.

Keyaru’s narration is generally plain, modern masculine light-novel Japanese with stable `俺` self-reference. His most recognizable “voice” is not a flamboyant sentence-ending tic but a repeated reasoning architecture.

A common sequence is:

> **observe → classify → enumerate constraints → infer motive/mechanism → select plan → justify action**

## 24.1 Explicit enumeration

**Locator:** V9 — 第十六話「回復術士は変装する」 — `p-017.xhtml` approximately ¶34–37

Keyaru enumerates several reasons for choosing the black-lizard disguise using a clean `一つ…二つ…三つ…` structure.

This is characteristic: he turns ambiguous situations into decision trees.

## 24.2 Objective-first planning

**Locator:** V9 — 第十七話「回復術士は仲間を増やす」 — `p-018.xhtml` approximately ¶4–15

He restates the objective, predicts hostage behavior, rejects a direct extermination option, and focuses on tracing the command structure.

## 24.3 Hypothesis management

**Locator:** V10 — 第二話「回復術士は求婚する」 — `p-003.xhtml` approximately ¶57–65

Keyaru lays out competing hypotheses behind the Red Dragon conflict and consciously withholds information because premature public disclosure could force retaliation before causality is understood.

## 24.4 Adversarial-model reasoning

**Locator:** V10 — 第十六話「回復術士は結婚する」 — `p-017.xhtml` approximately ¶135–145

When considering memory countermeasures against `【回復】`, Keyaru asks what he himself would design if he were the opponent, then uses even a failed probe as evidence about the structure of the defense.

### V2 conclusion

The best linguistic/cognitive formula remains:

> **observe → classify → calculate → justify → act**

with possession/protection/punishment/use frequently entering at the justification stage.

Late development does not erase this style. It adds greater affective explicitness to it.

# 26. Compact lexical anchor set

The following terms should be directly anchored in Document 08 because translation choice materially affects analysis.

## `仮面 / kamen` — mask

**V8 — `p-007.xhtml`**

Keyaru describes Keyarga as the mask of an idealized self constructed because he hated the weak Keyaru.

## `鎧 / yoroi` — armor

**V9 — `p-007.xhtml`**

Keyaru explicitly calls Keyarga armor that was necessary for revenge and is no longer required in the same way.

The movement `仮面 → 鎧` is analytically important:

- mask emphasizes performed identity;
- armor emphasizes defensive function.

## `あるべき姿 / arubeki sugata` — the state/form that ought to be

**V6 — `p-016.xhtml`**

Keyaru’s own definition of healing.

This is the key metaphysical phrase behind “which prior state counts as healthy?”

## `概念の【回復】` — conceptual healing/restoration

**V6 — `p-016.xhtml`**

Explicit textual term/formulation, not merely V2 terminology.

## `やり直し / yarinaoshi` — doing over / redo

The title word changes ethical weight across the series:

- first redo = revenge opportunity;
- V8 = replacement threatens accumulated personhood;
- V10 = redo becomes necessary only after catastrophic collapse and is preceded by consultation.

## `回帰の【回復】` — regression/return healing

**V10 — Epilogue — `p-022.xhtml` ¶206–208**

World itself becomes the target of healing toward an `あるべき世界`.

## `損切り / songiri` — cutting losses

**V10 — 第十四話「回復術士は追い詰められる」 — `p-015.xhtml`**

Ellen uses financial/strategic loss-cutting language for the point at which preserving the doomed timeline becomes irrational despite attachment to it.

The term captures Ellen’s ability to apply cold strategic vocabulary to a situation in which **her own accumulated life** is one of the assets being sacrificed.

## `破壊衝動 / hakai shōdō` — destructive impulse

**V10 — mid/late volume, especially `p-016.xhtml` and related passages**

Used for the Demon King destructive drive that Eve increasingly experiences.

### V2 caution

Do not claim that late Keyaru suddenly speaks a fundamentally new Japanese dialect or syntax.

The more defensible development is:

> **the strategic voice remains; what changes is the range of feelings he permits himself to name directly — fear, loneliness, peace, home, love, and uncertainty.**
