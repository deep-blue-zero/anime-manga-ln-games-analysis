# Redo of Healer — Recommended Multi-Document Synthesis Architecture
## Definitive analytical corpus through Volume 10

### Purpose

This file defines the recommended division, order, and scope of the final multi-document synthesis.

The goal of splitting the synthesis is not merely to create more words. The division should allow each analytical layer to become deep enough to function independently while still forming one coherent reference corpus.

Recommended total: **10 core Markdown documents**, plus an optional index/README and optional evidence ledger.

File numbering below is intentional. The sequence moves from orientation → narrative progression → protagonist → relationships/personhood → antagonists → politics → metaphysics → ethics → language/genre → final comparative reference.

---

# 03_PERSONHOOD_HOUSEHOLD_AND_RELATIONSHIPS.md
## Freia, Ellen, Eve, Setsuna, Kureha, Ragna, Lapis, Guren, and the problem of loving people one can alter

### Primary purpose

Analyze Keyaru's intimate sphere as a **personhood laboratory**, not as a harem roster.

### Organizing concept

Different relationships reveal different degrees of Keyaru's capacity to recognize another person as separate.

### Major sections

## A. Freia / Flare
Primary issues:
- identity destruction;
- constructed personhood;
- latent Flare memory;
- political fiction;
- genuine later selfhood;
- Volume 10 request that Keyaru befriend/save Flare.

Key question:
> If preventing Flare's trauma means Freia may never be created in the same way, what exactly does Freia value about herself?

## B. Ellen / Norn
Primary issues:
- Norn as institutional cruelty;
- rewriting;
- recovered strategic mind;
- choosing Ellen;
- Panakeia architecture;
- willingness to contemplate reset;
- sending Bullet away;
- apparent death;
- "please be happy."

Key question:
> Can a coerced identity become self-owned through later self-knowledge and choice?

## C. Eve Reese
Primary issues:
- Black-Wing grief;
- sovereignty;
- Demon King system;
- Caladrius;
- love;
- anti-retaliatory politics;
- refusal of inherited persecution;
- compassion versus tactical error;
- desire to save her own people in the next timeline.

Key question:
> Can mercy govern without becoming helplessness?

## D. Setsuna
Primary issues:
- slavery;
- revenge;
- tribe;
- bounded vengeance;
- loyalty;
- possession-language as security;
- next-timeline request to save her people before enslavement.

## E. Kureha

The files should be written in the following order, even though the reader may later consult them independently:

9. `08_NARRATION_LANGUAGE_GENRE_AND_MOTIFS.md`
10. `09_COMPARATIVE_REFERENCE_AND_OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`

This order is preferable because:

> **chronology establishes what happened → Keyaru establishes the psychological center → relationships establish who became irreplaceable → antagonists sharpen the contrast → politics explains what revenge became → metaphysics explains why the world breaks → ethics evaluates the choices → voice/genre explains how the novels construct those meanings → matrices preserve the result for future comparison.**

# Optional eleventh file

If the final corpus becomes extremely long, create:

`10_VOLUME_BY_VOLUME_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md`

This would not be prose synthesis. It would be an audit-style appendix containing, for each volume:

- concise thesis;
- decisive events;
- character turning points;
- institution changes;
- metaphysical additions;
- important motifs;
- what later volumes revised;
- unresolved questions at that point.

This appendix would make it easier to verify that the thematic synthesis has not accidentally flattened chronology.

It is optional because Document 01 may already provide enough volume-level structure. It becomes worthwhile if the synthesis is intended as a long-term scholarly-style reference corpus.
