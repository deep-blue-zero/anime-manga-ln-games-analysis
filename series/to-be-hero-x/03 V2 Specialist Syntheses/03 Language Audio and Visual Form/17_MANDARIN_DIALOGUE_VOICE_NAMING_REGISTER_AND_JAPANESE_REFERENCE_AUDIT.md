# Mandarin Dialogue, Voice, Naming, Register, and Japanese Reference Audit

## 0. Authority, scope, and evidentiary boundary

This is the first Phase-5 synthesis in the *To Be Hero X* V2 corpus. It consolidates the season’s Mandarin dialogue system, character-specific discourse voices, relational address, naming architecture, English-facing house style, and the proper use of the aligned Japanese track.

Its primary linguistic authority is Mandarin: reconstructed hardsub dialogue, visible Chinese text, episode-local verification, and the mature Phase-2 language ledger. The Japanese track is a secondary semantic and timing witness. Official Chinese, English, and Japanese materials govern published naming surfaces only within their own remit. None of those layers is permitted to erase the others.

The word **voice** has two different meanings and they must remain separate:

1. **Discourse voice** — vocabulary, sentence type, self-reference, modality, address, register, metaphor, habitual contrast, and the social position from which a character speaks.
2. **Acoustic voice** — pitch, timbre, breath, tempo, vocal placement, hesitation, laughter, crying, whisper, projection, overlap, and sung performance.

This document is canonical for the first category. It does **not** claim a fresh, uninterrupted audition of all twenty-four episode audio tracks. The source catalog confirms that the verified bundles contain complete synchronized Mandarin audio and paired Japanese captions, but those audio binaries are not all materialized in the present working runtime. Fine acoustic claims therefore remain reserved for directly auditioned evidence, the required E05–E07 audiovisual-form microsequence audit, and Document 19.

The governing thesis is:

> **In *To Be Hero X*, language does not merely express identities that already exist. It assigns roles, converts persons into public functions, carries Trust and Fear through stories, organizes hierarchy and care, and determines whether a bearer can answer the image imposed upon them. Mandarin dialogue repeatedly distinguishes being from becoming, names from addresses, persons from hero offices, reliance from popularity, and public usefulness from ownership. The Japanese track is most valuable when it exposes omission, ambiguity, or localization strategy; it becomes misleading when treated as a substitute for Mandarin lexical authority.**

## 1. Executive finding: the season’s central struggle is who gets to complete the sentence “you are…”

The most consequential speech acts in the season are identity assignments.

Institutions say, in effect:

- you are Nice;
- you are a brand;
- you are an accessory;
- you are a Sacred Maiden;
- you are a jinx;
- you are the hero who never falls;
- you are a silent killer;
- you are a monster;
- you are trash;
- you are a god;
- you are a catastrophic risk.

Characters answer through negation, self-naming, future-directed becoming, relational address, or refusal:

- `你并不是奈斯` — you are not Nice;
- `我其实不是奈斯` — I am actually not Nice;
- `我叫林凌` — my name is Lin Ling;
- `我很清楚自己不想要什么` — I know clearly what I do not want;
- `我要成为…` — I will / want to become…;
- `就是我儿子` — he is my son;
- `我不愿当小丑` — I do not want to be a clown;
- `绝不加班` — absolutely no overtime;
- `准点下班` — clock out on time.

These are not all assertions of a hidden, untouched essence. Lin does not simply recover a pristine pre-Nice self. A-Hu does not reveal that he was eternally a hero in secret. Qīng does not reject every meaning inherited from the dead. The more precise movement is from **unanswerable assignment** to **answerable relation**.

**SP17-01 — The season’s linguistic conflict is not real name versus fake name, but unanswerable identity assignment versus a role, name, or relation the bearer can know, answer, revise, and leave.**

## 2. Source hierarchy: what each language layer can and cannot establish

| Layer | Governs | Cannot establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Mandarin audio / visible Chinese / reconstructed hardsub | source wording, lexical recurrence, address, idiom, modality, written pronoun where visible | official English branding; complete acoustic characterization without audition |
| Aligned Japanese captions | timing, semantic plausibility, omitted object recovery, localization interpretation, occasional speaker guidance | Mandarin politeness, slang, wordplay, pronoun choice, exact source metaphor |
| Official Chinese materials | published Chinese names, hero labels, ranking/promotional surfaces | episode-local prospective knowledge before publication |
| Official English materials | official English hero/person/organization surfaces | Mandarin lexical semantics or relationship grammar |
| Official Japanese materials | official Japanese names and edition-specific adaptations | source-Chinese characters absent from Mandarin evidence |
| Frozen episode readings | what could responsibly be known at the broadcast boundary | later identity equivalence retrojected into the past |
| Phase-2 ledgers / current claim register | mature wording, confidence, revision state | replacement of primary evidence when exact wording is requested |

The hierarchy matters because the same entity can have several legitimate surfaces. `梁龙` is the source person-name Liang Long and the official English hero Dragon Boy. `阿虎` is A-Hu in Mandarin-sensitive analysis, Ahu in official English, and `トラ` in Japanese. `娟姐` is a relational address whose official English surface, Miss J, does not carry the same morphology.

No single language layer is “the correct one” for every analytical responsibility. Mandarin governs what the character says. Official English governs the official English label. Japanese can reveal how another edition interprets the source. The crosswalk exists to preserve equivalence without flattening difference.

## 3. What can be reconstructed as character voice without overclaiming acoustics

A reliable written voice model can recover:

- preferred identity formulas;
- whether a character speaks in declaration, question, command, proverb, technical explanation, or banter;
- the metaphors through which they interpret persons and power;
- recurring modality: `要`, `想`, `愿`, `不愿`, `必须`, `凭什么`;
- formal versus familiar address;
- public-title versus private-name choice;
- whether vulnerability appears as direct admission, negation, projection, or displaced procedure;
- the transition between personal and institutional registers.

It cannot responsibly recover, without direct audition:

- whether a line is low-pitched, breathy, nasal, clipped, tremulous, or projected;
- exact pace and pause structure;
- voice-placement changes between public and private modes;
- laughter, sobbing, swallowed starts, or nonlexical repair not represented in the text;
- singing technique or timbral blend.

The distinction is not cosmetic. Subtitle punctuation may suggest a pause that the performance does not contain. A short Chinese sentence may be delivered slowly or explosively. A character can use formally identical words with very different interactional force.

A discourse-voice profile should therefore be built from recurrent **speech operations**, not from invented vocal adjectives. The following matrix states what this corpus can reconstruct securely before full acoustic audition:

| Speaker / role | Dominant speech operation | Route into vulnerability | Characteristic danger of the register |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lin Ling | workplace complaint, reluctant assent, later self-naming and moral declaration | confession of benefit and complicity | letting the role supply every available first-person sentence |
| Original Nice | heroic assertion, perfection formula, defensive identity equation | collapse from `我是奈斯` into `我是谁` | treating one public name as the only survivable self |
| Xiao Yueqing / Moon | coarse private refusal against goddess-facing presentation | negative knowledge: knowing what she will not accept | having public romance translate boundary into brand content |
| Yang Cheng | shame, admiration, covenant, upright declaration | admitting dependence upon another hero and upon witnesses | confusing deserved inheritance with permanent moral immunity |
| Qīng / Lucky Cyan | burdened hope, personal naming, entrusted wishes, ordinary-person desire | claiming the right to make mistakes | allowing gratitude to become sacred role debt |
| Liu Yuwei / Queen | diagnosis, rule-making, maxim, institutional reform | acknowledgment of another internal voice | converting self-authorship into sovereign authorship over others |
| Luo Li | résumé logic, technical explanation, declaration of war, refusal to surrender appearance | grief that breaks the controlled image | making permanent composure the price of being believed |
| Wang Yi / Ghostblade | compressed operational language and extensive non-answer | indirect written truth, naming the person he loves | allowing silence to become moral insulation or public essence |
| Little Johnny | kinship assertion, ordinary care, corrective address | direct dependence upon family relation | being translated from father into operator/controller |
| Smile | reassurance, pedagogy, continuous public affect | fatigue and family provenance behind the smile | authentic warmth becoming compulsory infrastructure |
| Liang Long / Dragon Boy | pain accounting, repayment, classification, god-making ambition | childhood humiliation converted into doctrine | treating suffering as jurisdiction over those judged weaker |
| A-Hu | bitter self-narration, refusal of clownhood, future-directed becoming | reciprocal belief in Xinya's possibility | accepting singular public credit that erases co-authors |
| X | clipped ordinary labor, identity switching, refusal of fate and overtime | selective disclosure to a skeptical witness | relying upon personal restraint where constitutional restraint is absent |
| Yan Mo | game rules, cultivation, opportunity design, god-making | rarely direct; motive appears through field construction | making another person's constrained choice look fully self-authored |

This matrix is not a script for synthetic imitation. It is a bounded reconstruction of what kinds of sentences each speaker repeatedly makes available, what they avoid saying directly, and how their register participates in the season's ethics. A later acoustic profile may refine or complicate it; it must not be presumed in advance.

This document therefore treats **lexical voice** as an evidentiary category in its own right and refuses to simulate acoustic knowledge from prose.

**SP17-02 — A character’s discourse voice is reconstructable from repeated lexical, modal, address, and rhetorical choices, but acoustic personality must remain separately auditioned rather than inferred from subtitle punctuation.**

## 4. Quote-grade discipline and alignment exceptions

A publication-grade Mandarin quotation requires:

- high-confidence reconstructed text or manual verification;
- consistency with audible dialogue where audition exists;
- proper-noun review against visible text and the crosswalk;
- an explanation when the Japanese witness materially diverges;
- no silent correction of malformed OCR into a cleaner sentence unless the correction is documented.

The preserved alignment exceptions matter. E01 cue 134 pairs a Chinese line about changed eye color with unrelated Japanese text about Trust. The Chinese and image govern. Other E01 and E04 strings contain OCR contamination around remembered names or insults. Japanese and context can establish broad sense without manufacturing a quote that was never securely reconstructed.

Spoken Mandarin `ta` also does not acoustically distinguish `他 / 她 / 它`. When the visible hardsub chooses a written pronoun, that is textual evidence, not phonetic evidence.

This yields a practical rule:

> **Use Japanese to diagnose a gap; do not use it to invent Mandarin wording inside the gap.**

## 5. Identity grammar I: `是 / 就是 / 不是 / 谁`

The season’s strongest recurring grammatical chain uses being, emphatic equivalence, negation, and the identity question:

- `你是谁不重要` — who you are is not important;
- `现在的我就是奈斯` — the present me is Nice;
- `你并不是奈斯` — you are not Nice;
- `我其实不是奈斯` / `我并不是奈斯` — I am actually not Nice;
- `我叫林凌 / 是一个普通人` — my name is Lin Ling / I am an ordinary person;
- `我是奈斯` — I am Nice;
- `我是谁` — who am I?

The opening line `你是谁不重要` is not merely rude management language. It is the grammar of replaceability. The person’s biography becomes irrelevant so long as the role remains publicly continuous.

Lin’s E01 `现在的我就是奈斯` should not be rewritten as pure false consciousness. `现在的我` marks a present-tense appropriation under duress: the current self is taking up the role because action appears impossible otherwise. Later negation does not erase that moment; it makes the appropriation answerable.

Original Nice’s `我是奈斯` is different. It is defensive total equivalence. When the sentence can no longer absorb failure, Fear, and humiliation, it collapses into `我是谁`.

**SP17-03 — `是 / 就是 / 不是 / 谁` forms a season-long identity sequence in which imposed equivalence, private counter-recognition, self-produced refusal, and totalized collapse remain grammatically distinct.**

## 6. Identity grammar II: becoming is not essence

A-Hu’s decisive statement is future-directed:

`我要成为守护新芽的大英雄`

> I want to become the great hero who protects Xinya.

The clause names a telos rather than an exhaustive ontology. He chooses a direction whose legitimacy can continue to be tested by the relation to Xinya.

That is why the contrast with `我是奈斯` matters:

- A-Hu: **becoming toward a relation**;
- Original Nice: **fixed equation under pressure**.

The season does not romanticize every future project. Institutions also cultivate, package, manufacture, and stage heroes. `培育成为神` is becoming language used by Yan Mo’s apotheotic project. The ethical distinction is therefore not future tense versus present tense. It is whether the projected future remains answerable to the person who must live it.

X’s `换个身份` adds a third possibility: identity as presentation that can be entered and left. His refusal of overtime makes switching identities a boundary practice rather than proof that none of the identities is meaningful.

## 7. Naming the ordinary person

`普通人` appears throughout the season as more than an absence of hero status.

Lin names himself as an ordinary person while refusing the conclusion that ordinariness disqualifies heroism. Qīng asks to become `一个能犯错的普通人`—an ordinary person capable of making mistakes. X seeks ordinary life as a practical boundary against total public jurisdiction.

The phrase does not mean anonymity, mediocrity, or political insignificance. It preserves capacities that public hero roles repeatedly threaten:

- fallibility;
- privacy;
- limited obligation;
- non-performance;
- a life not exhausted by public utility;
- the ability to leave.

The Japanese rendering of Qīng’s line as `普通の人になりたい` preserves the wish for ordinariness but loses the explicit right to error contained in `能犯错`. The Mandarin is analytically stronger.

## 8. Person-to-property vocabulary

The first arc establishes a political lexicon that later becomes constitutional critique:

- `个人` — individual person;
- `品牌` — brand;
- `包装` — package/present;
- `人设` — constructed persona or character setting;
- `附属品` — accessory/appendage;
- `枷锁` — shackle/fetter;
- `棋子` — chess piece/pawn;
- `跳板` — stepping stone;
- `经营` — manage, operate, run as a business.

Juān jiě’s transformation of Nice from `个人` into the company’s most important `品牌` supplies the basic conversion. Once the hero is a brand, coerced romance becomes content, rivalry becomes production, enemies become labor, and private refusal becomes brand malfunction.

E24 generalizes this language when X calls heroes `共同经营的棋子`: pieces jointly operated by the Commission and hero offices. The workplace satire and the constitutional indictment are one continuous register.

**SP17-04 — Commercial and constitutional diction belong to one linguistic system: the same verbs that manage creative labor and brands later describe the institutional operation of heroes as pieces.**

## 9. Labor language: the season begins and ends at work

Lin’s early world is built from:

- payment and supplemental payment;
- revisions;
- overtime;
- wage cuts;
- layoffs;
- cheap labor;
- client direction;
- managerial contempt.

X’s endpoint is `绝不加班` and `准点下班`.

The comedy works because cosmic responsibility is translated into ordinary labor rights. The line does not deny responsibility; it denies the inference that exceptional capacity creates infinite availability.

The strongest linguistic contrast between X and Zero is therefore not heroic rhetoric versus villain rhetoric. It is **bounded work** versus **total sovereignty**.

Zero’s vocabulary expands toward god, all, justice, sovereign order, and history. X’s final language contracts toward schedule, ordinary life, identity change, and departure.

**SP17-05 — Ordinary labor language is the season’s sharpest anti-apotheosis register because it converts the right to remain a person into the right for the role’s claim to end.**

## 10. `信赖` is reliance, not merely popularity

The core term `信赖` carries reliance, confidence, expectation, and belief in someone’s capacity. Fame and follower count can amplify it, but they do not exhaust it.

The season differentiates:

- Yòuzi’s reliance upon Yang;
- Yang’s unwillingness to betray entrusted Trust;
- public expectation attached to E-Soul;
- social memory of A-Sheng;
- mass adherence to Zero;
- Trust Value as institutional quantification.

`背负着成千上万人的信赖` gives Trust a double valence: empowerment and burden. `背负` is to bear or carry. The bearer is strengthened by others’ reliance and simultaneously made responsible to it.

Japanese may translate these scenes through belief, trust, admiration, or confidence. The variation can be useful. It must not encourage the English synthesis to alternate among “popularity,” “faith,” and “reputation” as though they were source synonyms.

## 11. `恐惧` is affect, relation, value, contagion, and material intervention

`恐惧` appears as:

- lived fear;
- public terror;
- measurable `恐惧值`;
- infection;
- engulfment;
- research material;
- administered weapon;
- returnable damage in Liang’s local mechanic.

Repeated verbs include `感染` and `吞没`. These verbs make Fear more than a reputation score while resisting a single purely medical interpretation.

The aligned Japanese track often uses `恐怖粒子`, foregrounding particles. Mandarin E18 and related research scenes use `恐惧值`, foregrounding a quantified value. Particle behavior may be locally real, but the Japanese term cannot replace the Mandarin ontology.

**SP17-06 — The Mandarin Fear vocabulary deliberately spans affect, quantification, contagion, research, and violence; a Japanese particle gloss is a secondary explanatory model, not a universal source correction.**

## 12. Apotheosis vocabulary: fans become `信徒`

The late season escalates from social valuation into religion and sovereignty:

- `信徒` — believers, adherents, followers;
- `造神` — make/create a god;
- `培育成为神` — cultivate someone into a god;
- `凌驾于一切之上` — stand above everything;
- `魔王Zero` — Demon King Zero.

`信徒` is stronger than “fans.” `造神` describes active production rather than accidental celebrity growth. `培育` frames godhood as a developmental project subject to institutional design.

The Commission’s founding purpose is to prevent `造神`, yet the same institutional field can describe X through `围剿` and infer sovereign ambition from his refusal of subordination.

Language thus reveals the recurrent danger: anti-theological institutions can reproduce theological totalization by seeing only god or demon, never a private person with extraordinary capacity.

## 13. Governance and violent authorization

The political register develops through:

- `规则` — rules;
- `制定规则` — formulate rules;
- `漏洞` — loophole/gap;
- `约束` — constrain;
- `不破不立` — no rebuilding without breaking;
- `围剿` — encircle and suppress/exterminate;
- `格杀勿论` — kill without exception;
- `宣战` — declare war;
- `篡改历史` — tamper with/falsify history.

Queen’s vocabulary treats rules as designed and revisable. Her later language begins to permit self-binding and rupture of failed structures.

`围剿` and `格杀勿论` mark the point at which governance stops asking for an answer and moves to enemy elimination. These words should not be softened into “monitor,” “contain,” or “neutralize” when the source authorizes something harsher.

The phrase `漏洞` also requires local discipline. It may describe an institutional gap, a rule-field weakness, a strategic opening, or a broader system problem. It is not automatically a universal game-mechanics term.

## 14. Relational address is analytical evidence

Mandarin frequently locates relationship through address morphology:

- `姐` in `娟姐`;
- `叔` in `岩叔`;
- `总` in `尚总 / 扎总`;
- `前辈` in `女王前辈 / 小强前辈`;
- diminutive `小` in `小青 / 小柚子 / 小强`;
- familiar `阿` in `阿生 / 阿虎`;
- elder address `奶奶` in `王奶奶`.

These forms can encode hierarchy, familiarity, deference, affection, debt, or professional distance. They are not decorative particles that can be discarded once an official English name is known.

Official English often must choose one stable surface. “Miss J,” “Zac,” “Queen,” “Pomelo,” and “Ahu” are legitimate localized identities. They should not erase the relational information carried by `姐`, `总`, `前辈`, `小`, or `阿` when the dialogue itself is under analysis.

The source forms can be grouped by the kind of social relation they perform:

| Form | Typical function in this corpus | What a translation risks losing |
|---|---|---|
| `姐` | familiar-respectful senior female address | the mixture of hierarchy and intimacy; false inference of literal sisterhood |
| `叔` | familiar elder male address | the difference between relational familiarity and legal/familial identity |
| `总` | executive or boss-style workplace address | the distinction between office and personal name; power encoded in the address itself |
| `前辈` | professional or generational seniority | lineage, deference, and the speaker's placement within a field of practice |
| `小 + name` | diminutive, familiarity, relative junior status, or lexicalized nickname | whether the form is affectionate, hierarchical, or part of a stable public identity |
| `阿 + name` | familiar personal naming | the domestic or local force that an unmarked romanization may conceal |
| `奶奶 / 爸爸 / 儿子` | kinship or elder relation | the difference between biological, legal, chosen, and normative family standing |
| role-only address | public office or hero identity replacing the person-name | whether the speaker is recognizing the bearer, the office, or only the social function |

The correct analytical practice is not to leave every address untranslated. It is to preserve the source form when the relationship itself is being interpreted, then use the stable English referent once the social information has been made explicit. The crosswalk should carry both surfaces so future retrieval does not mistake an address for a second person.

**SP17-07 — Address forms are part of the relationship ledger: localization may identify the referent correctly while still erasing hierarchy, familiarity, or chosen kinship encoded in the source form.**

## 15. Executive titles are not personal names

`尚总` and `扎总` illustrate a recurring error.

`总` is an executive or boss-style title. It does not create a legal-name field. `尚总` addresses Shang De. `扎总` addresses the FOMO representative later identified in official English as Zac. The exact source-Chinese legal-name characters for Zac remain open.

Likewise, `老板` and `社长` are workplace titles. Official Japanese provides the external name surface `チョン・ヤオジン`, but the project must not back-form Chinese characters absent from episode-primary or official-Chinese evidence.

The crosswalk’s purpose is precisely to prevent role addresses from mutating into false biographical certainty.

## 16. Diminutives and familiar prefixes

`小` and `阿` do different relational work.

`小青` is an intimate or familiar form for Qīng, not a separate hero identity. `小柚子` is a diminutive nickname whose official English form Pomelo captures the fruit semantics but not the diminutive relation. `小强` is both a source-person surface and the basis for Little Johnny, while `小强前辈` adds professional seniority.

`阿生` and `阿虎` carry familiar-person force. English A-Sheng and A-Hu preserve some of the sound, but not every reader will recognize the morphology. Japanese `トラ` preserves A-Hu’s tiger joke while replacing the source personal form with a semantic animal label.

The right analytical response is not to force every source form into English prose. It is to preserve the information when it affects relation, identity, or wordplay.

## 17. Kinship language can create real relation before biology is known

`就是我儿子` gives Little Johnny’s relation to Big Johnny direct linguistic force before the series settles species, mechanism, or biology.

The statement is not a genetic claim. It is a normative one: this being is my son, and that relation creates duties.

The same network depends on father, son, mother, husband, wife, and grandfather language. These terms are not automatically legal findings. They nevertheless organize care, grievance, inheritance, and public misrecognition.

The public controller/summoner narrative later translates father reaching son into hero controlling anomalous creature. The category may be operationally useful while missing the relation that made the intervention possible.

**SP17-08 — Kinship terms can be socially and ethically constitutive without settling biology or law; the source repeatedly gives chosen relation priority over complete classification.**

## 18. Care formulas: language that leaves room for the other person

Several compact formulas recur:

- `没事的 / 有我在` — it is all right / I am here;
- `还记得你是谁` — still remember who you are;
- `深信不疑` — believe deeply, without doubt;
- `我不想在诺诺面前撒谎` — I do not want to lie in front of Nuonuo;
- `当然是我最爱的人` — of course, the person I love most.

Their ethical value depends on use. “I am here” can provide presence or become possessive assurance. “Remember who you are” can restore plural identity or demand a fixed role. The same words are not automatically benevolent outside relation and context.

Qīng’s singing helps Queen hear `内心中的另一个声音`—another voice within herself. The phrase matters because the helper does not supply the answer. It creates conditions for internal plurality.

## 19. Negative freedom: refusal before a complete alternative

Xiao Yueqing’s `我很清楚自己不想要什么` is one of the season’s most exact autonomy statements:

> I know clearly what I do not want.

The sentence refuses the demand that a person produce a complete replacement life before leaving an imposed arrangement.

Qīng’s `我不是什么英雄` and the Japanese `ヒーローはやだって` similarly distinguish categorical denial from reluctance about one assignment. A-Hu’s `我不愿当小丑` rejects an imposed representational role before he has fully defined the hero he will become.

Negative formulations are not analytically deficient. In a world where institutions speak for bearers, knowing the boundary may be the first available form of authorship.

**SP17-09 — Negative freedom has a stable Mandarin grammar in the season: `不想 / 不愿 / 不是什么 / 不要` can establish legitimate refusal before the speaker possesses a complete positive identity plan.**

## 20. Truth, fact, exposure, and authorship

E10 distinguishes `事实` and `真相`; E04 distinguishes factual exposure from self-authored confession.

A story may contain true facts while omitting causal provenance or stripping the affected person of standing. God Eye reveals real substitution. Lin’s confession differs because he supplies his name, chronology, complicity, refusal, and subsequent action.

The language of `本意` and `契机` also matters. Intent and occasion are not the same as consequence. A character can have one original intention while an event becomes the occasion for a much larger public effect.

The mature English synthesis should therefore avoid using “truth” as a magical moral solvent. The source asks who tells, what is omitted, who can answer, and what the disclosure materially permits.

## 21. Performance vocabulary: `演员 / 表演 / 模仿 / 假的`

The E-Soul arc makes a crucial distinction among actor, performance, imitation, and falsity.

`演员` and `表演` do not condemn artifice. Yang’s performance can carry real aspiration and communicate heroism. `模仿` marks copying another bearer’s surface without yet producing an answerable role. `假的` is applied to superficial enhancement in context; it does not automatically render the entire performance morally fake.

Xia Qing’s `另一个你` preserves plurality. Her later question about the `真正的你` is answered through conduct rather than by selecting either stage-Yang or private-Yang as the sole authentic self.

`你摘下面具后 / 仍是魂电` gives the arc’s mature formulation: after taking off the mask, you are still E-Soul. The mask can mediate vocation without exhausting the bearer.

**SP17-10 — Performance vocabulary separates acting, imitation, public role, and falsity; the source does not treat stylization itself as evidence of inauthenticity.**

## 22. Causality and fate grammar

E24 uses a grammar of retrospective correction:

- `我本以为` — I originally thought;
- `但其实` — but in fact;
- `一切的因果` — all causes and consequences;
- `都早已被种下` — had already been sown;
- `把我们送往彼此的世界` — send us into one another’s worlds;
- `违抗命运的安排` — resist the arrangement of fate.

`本以为` explicitly demotes X’s earlier coin-origin story. `种下` is an organic metaphor of antecedence, not a mechanical claim that every result was predetermined.

The Japanese `予定調和 / 巡り合わせ` strengthens the fate and arrangement field. It must not convert the Mandarin into strict determinism.

**SP17-11 — E24’s causality grammar revises subjective origin into already-sown antecedence while preserving chosen resistance; neither the coin nor the Japanese fate vocabulary proves a closed deterministic system.**

## 23. Lin Ling and Original Nice: two opposite failures of the equation

Lin’s register begins in subordinate creative labor: clients, revisions, payment, overtime, and managerial insult. The Nice body gives him public declarative formulas before he possesses secure authorship over them.

His development can be tracked linguistically:

`现在的我就是奈斯`
→ `你并不是奈斯`
→ `我其实不是奈斯`
→ `我叫林凌`
→ ordinary-person and real-hero language.

Original Nice moves in the opposite direction:

aspiration and Smile’s teaching
→ perfection and cheap-smile discipline
→ `我是奈斯`
→ Fear infection
→ `我是谁`.

The distinction is not one bearer being real and the other fake. Lin becomes able to keep person and role distinguishable. Original Nice loses every stable identity outside the role.

## 24. Xiao Yueqing / Moon: public goddess, private coarse refusal

The public `女神` frame idealizes Xiao Yueqing into support labor for Nice.

Her private `老娘` self-reference is deliberately coarse and anti-goddess. It refuses the linguistic decorum expected of the branded romantic figure. `去他的女神，我不装了` rejects both title and compulsory performance.

Her most important statements are not romantic declarations but boundary claims:

- she knows what she does not want;
- she wants freedom;
- she can recognize Lin without granting him Original Nice’s biography.

Official English foregrounds Moon as the hero-facing identity. Current analysis should use Xiao Yueqing when private personhood, consent, or relation is at issue.

## 25. Juān jiě and managerial speech

Juān jiě’s discourse voice merges command, production, sunk-cost motivation, and strategic paradox.

`娟姐` itself locates her through a familiar-respectful senior-woman address. Official English Miss J supplies a stable role surface but loses the exact relation encoded by `姐`.

Her voice is one of the clearest examples of institutional language sounding intimate without ceasing to be managerial.

## 26. Yang Cheng / E-Soul: shame, uprightness, covenant, and burden

Yang’s early register is organized by shame, imitation, gratitude, and the desire to stand `堂堂正正`—uprightly and without shame—before E-Soul.

The veteran’s mask language treats the role as covenant:

- `初心` — original aspiration;
- `不愿辜负的信赖` — Trust one is unwilling to betray;
- `所承载的希望` — hope carried by the role.

Xia Qing speaks in plural-person language: `另一个你`, “another you.” Shang Chao speaks in self-authorship language: `属于你的全新魂电`, an E-Soul of Yang’s own, `用你的方式`, in his own way.

## 27. Qīng / Lucky Cyan: names as incompatible public demands

`青`, `幸运青`, `圣女`, `扫把星`, and `小青` place the same person inside radically different relations:

- personal/source-sensitive identity;
- official hero identity;
- sacred utility;
- hostile causal blame;
- familiar intimacy.

The source refuses to let one name settle the whole person.

Her voice centers on fallibility and the burden of useful power. `能犯错的普通人` is not a wish to become insignificant. It is a demand that public benefit stop functioning as proof that she must never fail.

Later, Qīng can accept entrusted hope without returning to Sacred Maiden captivity. The change is not from social meaning to pure self-creation, but from imposed obligation to a relation she can answer.

## 28. Liu Yuwei / Queen and Bowa: rules, rupture, and competing legitimacy

Queen’s register is unusually explicit about institutions:

- rules;
- order;
- reform;
- overthrow of the Trust Value system;
- loopholes;
- rupture;

`不破不立` does not celebrate destruction as such. It argues that a failed structure may have to be broken before a new one can be established.

Qīng’s `女王前辈` places Queen inside professional lineage rather than isolated sovereignty. `小青` later marks a familiar relation that her public Queen identity cannot fully explain.

Bowa’s self-description through `无所不用其极` is harsher than a generic line about trying hard. The Japanese witness softens it toward relentless effort. Mandarin better preserves the willingness to use every means.

## 29. Luo Li / Loli: résumé language and the order of legibility

Luo Li’s central vocabulary translates image politics into employment and evidence:

- `以貌取人` — judge by appearance;
- `第一印象往往会代替简历` — first impressions often replace the résumé;
- `凭什么要我屈服` — why should I be the one who gives in?;
- `力与美` — strength and beauty;
- `人设` — authored hero persona;
- `宣战` — declaration of war.

Her discourse does not reject beauty or cuteness. It rejects allowing those traits to preempt evidence of competence.

The personal name Luo Li and official English hero surface Loli must remain distinct. Family `莉莉` preserves another private layer. Japanese and English adaptations identify the hero while risking the loss of the source’s person/role distinction.

## 30. Wang Yi / Ghostblade: silence as public noun and private deficit

`默杀` carries silence/killing semantics. The public identity Ghostblade turns an absence of speech into a functional hero surface.

The problem is not that he has no interiority. It is that observation and protection repeatedly substitute for reciprocal speech.

## 34. X and Zero: bounded work versus total role

## 35. Yan Mo, Shang De, and the language of engineered persons

Yan Mo’s characteristic register uses environment, cultivation, games, rules, and god-making. His method is not always direct command. He speaks as someone who believes the field can be arranged until another person’s choices become useful.

Shang De uses value and object vocabulary more brutally: cheap smile, weapon, ornament, perfection, identity as revenge machinery.

Both convert persons into projects, but their discourse differs:

- Shang De devalues the existing person and imposes a product;
- Yan Mo cultivates conditions in which the person appears to author the desired result.

The forthcoming E05–E07 Yan Mo retrospective evidence audit remains necessary before the causal chain is archivally frozen. This document preserves the established lexical model without promoting the stronger claim that he planned every contingency from the beginning.

## 36. Japanese reference function I: semantic completion

The Japanese track is most useful when the Mandarin reconstruction is truncated, euphemistic, or missing an object.

| Episode | Mandarin | Japanese contribution | Controlled use |

| E05 | `自从父母离开我那天起` — since my parents left me | `両親が死んだ` makes death explicit | State that the parents died while noting the Mandarin euphemism |
| E05 | truncated café belief thesis | fuller semantic direction | Paraphrase the thought; do not fabricate missing Chinese wording |

## 37. Japanese reference function II: interpretive sharpening

Some Japanese choices narrow an ambiguous affective field.

E07 Mandarin says Yang once `深深喜欢过他`, which could range across deep liking, affection, or admiration. Japanese `心から敬愛していました` specifies reverential admiration. “Deeply admired” is therefore the best analytical English.

E07 Japanese `ふさわしい場を用意してやれば`—prepare an appropriate setting—sharpens Yan Mo’s `设定了游戏规则`. Together they support the engineered-environment model.

E24 `予定調和 / 巡り合わせ` strengthens the fate and arranged-encounter dimension of X’s retrospective speech. It does not erase Mandarin `种下` or prove strict predestination.

The secondary witness is strongest when it helps select among several plausible English paraphrases without claiming the Mandarin contains the Japanese phrase.

## 38. Japanese reference function III: independent localization

Many Japanese names are not phonetic reproductions of Mandarin. They are edition-specific adaptations:

- `天眼` → `トゥルーアイ` while official English uses God Eye;
- `破坏王` → `キング・デストラクション` while official English uses Wreck;
- `蛊狼` → `マッドウルフ` while official English uses Wolf Girl;
- `不倒` → `スタンド` while official English uses Firm Man;
- `阿虎` → `トラ` while official English uses Ahu;
- `默杀` → `黙殺` while official English uses Ghostblade.

These are not contradictions to solve by choosing the most literal label. They are evidence that hero identities are re-authored across markets.

A current English-facing synthesis should use the official English surface while preserving Mandarin when name semantics or person/role distinction matters. Japanese should be cited as the Japanese edition’s choice, not imported as a universal alias.

## 39. Japanese reference function IV: source flattening or shift

Some Japanese lines change emphasis:

- Qīng’s `能犯错的普通人` becomes a more general wish to be ordinary.
- Bowa’s `无所不用其极` is softened toward relentless effort.
- E18 `恐惧值` becomes particle-oriented `恐怖粒子`.
- Shang De’s `武器 / 花瓶` becomes `マシン / 人形`.
- E11’s Trust line foregrounds self-cultivation more strongly than the Mandarin balance between others and self.

None of these makes the Japanese “wrong.” Each is a translation decision with interpretive consequences.

The audit’s central discipline is to preserve the difference rather than harmonize it away.

**SP17-14 — Japanese localization often preserves dramatic function while shifting metaphor, ontology, or agency; those shifts are comparative evidence, not grounds for silently normalizing the Mandarin source.**

## 40. Speaker labels, alignment, and false certainty

Subtitle alignment can fail in three distinct ways:

1. **Temporal mismatch** — paired cues belong to different moments.
2. **Speaker-label overreach** — the reference assigns a speaker inconsistent with the visible scene.
3. **Semantic overcompletion** — a plausible Japanese line tempts the analyst to manufacture exact Chinese wording.

The E01 alignment exception and E16 masked-handler speaker issue demonstrate the first two. The E09 condition line demonstrates the third risk: the Japanese supplies the missing object, but the Mandarin quote remains only `我有个条件`.

A confident audit records the mismatch. It does not hide it for the sake of a smoother bilingual transcript.

## 44. What an English translation should preserve even when it cannot reproduce form

An English-facing synthesis should preserve at least five distinctions:

1. **Person versus hero role** — Lin Ling/Nice, Wang Yi/Ghostblade, Qīng/Lucky Cyan.
2. **Address versus legal name** — Juān jiě, Yán-shū, Shàng zǒng, Zhā zǒng.
3. **Reliance versus popularity** — `信赖` is not follower count.
4. **Value versus particle** — `恐惧值` is not automatically `恐怖粒子`.
5. **Being versus becoming** — `我是` and `我要成为` are not interchangeable identity claims.

Where English cannot carry morphology compactly, a first source-sensitive mention or a short note is preferable to silent flattening.

A translation or synthesis should use the following priority order when several surfaces compete:

1. preserve the **entity distinction** first: person, hero office, address, organization, epithet, or historical category;
2. preserve the **source relation** next: familiarity, hierarchy, kinship, professional seniority, or hostility;
3. preserve the **recurring thematic term** consistently: `信赖`, `恐惧`, `人设`, `普通人`, `信徒`, `造神`;
4. use the **official English surface** for ordinary reader-facing prose once the above distinctions are secure;
5. add pinyin or the Mandarin form only when it resolves ambiguity, carries wordplay, or supports exact evidence;
6. cite the Japanese surface when the comparison itself matters, not as ornamental multilingual display.

| Source problem | Preferred English strategy | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| one person, several role surfaces | first mention `person / hero identity`, then hold one surface by context | free alternation that makes one bearer look like several people |
| relational address without natural English equivalent | retain the source form once and explain its social force | silently promoting the address to a legal name |
| semantic hero-name adaptation | use official English in public-facing prose; retain Mandarin for wordplay/role analysis | treating the Japanese or literal gloss as the sole “real” name |
| truncated Mandarin with fuller Japanese | paraphrase the combined meaning and label the cross-track support | fabricating a complete Chinese quotation |
| Japanese shift in metaphor or ontology | state both readings and keep Mandarin primary | harmonizing them into a third sentence supported by neither track |
| unresolved minor identity | use a controlled descriptive key and preserve uncertainty | inventing characters, pinyin, or corporate titles from context |

This hierarchy makes English prose readable without turning readability into source erasure.

## 45. What the Japanese track adds to the series-level reading

Used correctly, the Japanese reference adds four kinds of value:

- it confirms phonetic identity across source uncertainty;
- it recovers semantic direction from truncated Mandarin OCR;
- it exposes edition-specific rebranding of hero names;
- it reveals where another localization interprets the same scene differently.

It is especially useful for reconstructing the **space of plausible meanings**. It is not an independent veto over Mandarin.

The Japanese track also demonstrates something thematically apt: localization itself performs the series’ central operation. It takes a person, title, or role and makes it legible to a new public through selection and adaptation. The crosswalk is therefore not merely technical apparatus. It is a record of how hero identities continue to be re-authored outside the diegesis.

Some earlier episode notes and targeted AV records contain local observations. They remain local evidence. They are not aggregated here into simulated full-season acoustic profiles.

A later acoustic claim becomes synthesis-grade only when the evidence record identifies, at minimum:

- episode and source/program timestamp;
- speaker and addressee;
- the directly auditioned audio segment;
- whether the observation concerns pitch, tempo, breath, pause, overlap, nonlexical vocalization, projection, environmental sound, music, or silence;
- what textual or visual evidence is synchronized with it;
- whether the feature recurs or is local to one scene;

## 49. Final synthesis: language must remain answerable

*To Be Hero X* imagines a world in which words can become bodily and political infrastructure.

A name can gather Trust. A title can carry history across bearers. A public description can alter what a body is expected and able to do. An address can create family, hierarchy, debt, or intimacy. A rumor can become Fear. A performance instruction can harden into compulsory identity. A founding myth can organize an entire constitutional order.

The series does not answer this danger by dreaming of a pre-social self beyond all names. Its most successful characters still inherit language from others:

- Yang inherits E-Soul’s covenantal vocabulary;
- Qīng receives the passengers’ entrusted hope;
- Luo Li keeps beauty while changing the order in which competence is read;
- Little Johnny names Big Johnny as son before institutions know what he is;
- A-Hu accepts Xinya’s heroic possibility and changes it into a chosen future;
- Lin can inhabit heroism after refusing total equivalence with Nice;
- X retains extraordinary public responsibility while insisting that work has an end time.

The positive standard is not linguistic purity. It is **answerable naming**.

A comparatively legitimate name or role:

- preserves provenance;
- does not erase prior and private identities;
- permits negation and reinterpretation;
- can survive the bearer’s failure;
- remains open to relational correction;
- does not convert usefulness into ownership;
- and admits a life outside itself.

The final proposition is therefore:

> **A person may be socially named, publicly represented, and materially empowered without becoming the property of the sentence others complete about them. Language remains humane when the named person can answer back.**

**SP17-16 — The strongest linguistic ethic in Season 1 is plural, revisable, bounded address: a person may be socially named without being exhausted by the name.**
