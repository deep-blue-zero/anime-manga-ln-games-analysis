# Yang Cheng, E-Soul, Pomelo, and Inherited Heroism

## 0. Authority, scope, and reading rule

This is the second numbered Phase-3 character/relationship specialist synthesis in the *To Be Hero X* V2 corpus. Its primary purpose is not to retell Episodes 5–7, but to determine what the E-Soul arc establishes about **inheritance, performance, recognition, social legitimacy, engineered choice, and the possibility of betraying a legitimately inherited role after one has become its recognized bearer**.

The central analytical entities must remain distinct:

1. **Yang Cheng (杨澄)** — an orphan rescued by E-Soul, later a low-status suit actor with zero Trust, then a locally recognized rescuer, public claimant, and eventual contemporary E-Soul;
2. **E-Soul (魂电)** — a hero identity older than Yang and larger than any one bearer, with at least a thirty-four-year contemporary lineage and a much deeper historical association visible in the Zero-era public record;
3. **the veteran E-Soul** — a sincere incumbent whose role has become corporate, spectacular, and vulnerable to public dispossession without thereby becoming fraudulent;
4. **Yòuzi / 小柚子** — rendered **Pomelo** in the official English-facing surface, a socially isolated child whose recognition of Yang's performance precedes institutional validation and appears to seed Yang's first meaningful Trust response;
5. **Xia Qing (夏晴)** — Yang's friend and strongest person-centered recognizer, who repeatedly refuses to make role success the condition of her relation to him;
6. **Shang Chao (尚超)** — technical, narrative, and relational infrastructure for Yang's emergence, whose posthumous influence remains comparatively transparent because Yang knows what he inherits from him;
7. **Yan Mo (岩莫; earlier addressed as 岩叔 / Yán-shū)** — the figure who demonstrates how a person's real desire can be made politically usable by designing the field in which that desire acts.

The later E19–E20 material is essential. A specialist synthesis that ended with Yang's victory in E07 would confuse **successful succession** with **permanent ethical legitimacy**. The same contemporary E-Soul later participates in a classified assassination attempt against Ghostblade, Little Johnny, and Big Johnny, explicitly cites ranking advantage, survives, and appears at rank nine. That later conduct does not make E05–E07 fraudulent in retrospect. It reveals a more demanding principle:

> **A hero identity can be legitimately inherited and later ethically betrayed. Inheritance is not a one-time moral coronation. It is a continuing obligation to keep the role answerable to the person, the relationships that formed it, the people who rely on it, and the conduct performed in its name.**

The document therefore rejects two symmetric simplifications:

- “Yang was always the true E-Soul, so later wrongdoing cannot alter the legitimacy of the role”; and
- “Yang later does something morally grave, therefore his earlier rescue, recognition, and inheritance were never genuine.”

Both erase temporality. *To Be Hero X* instead treats hero identity as something whose moral meaning can change because persons remain capable of revision, capture, failure, and betrayal.

### 0.1 Naming and localization

Mandarin/source-sensitive prose uses **Yang Cheng**, **E-Soul**, **Xia Qing**, **Shang Chao**, and **Yòuzi (小柚子)**. The architecture filename retains **POMELO** because that is the official English localization of Yòuzi. “Pomelo” is therefore a localization surface, not evidence that the Mandarin character is addressed by an English fruit-name inside the diegesis.

### 0.2 Evidence boundary

The strongest source spine is:

- **E05** — person recognition before institutional recognition; performance versus imitation; Yòuzi's belief; rescue before power;
- **E06** — co-authored hero identity; technology and capital; public coronation; Xia Qing's person-centered testimony;
- **E07** — grief, counterweight ethics, live Trust referendum, engineered agency, veteran death, succession;
- **E19** — contemporary E-Soul's participation in covert assassination under Yan Mo;
- **E20** — survival, public rank nine, and the sharp separation between ranking legitimacy and moral heroism;
- **E24** — historical E-Soul as a title/bearer category in public Zero history, without proving uninterrupted personal or metaphysical continuity.

Fine-grained vocal timbre, prosody, score, and leitmotif analysis remain outside this specialist's authority unless already secured by the episode evidence.

## 1. Executive thesis: inheritance is a covenant, not a moral possession

Yang Cheng's arc begins with an unusually small heroic act. A powerful masked hero rescues a bloodied child and then asks him:

> `你叫什么` — **What is your name?**

The child answers:

> `杨澄` — **Yang Cheng.**

That opening establishes the standard against which the whole inheritance story should be judged. Physical rescue is followed by recognition of a person. The mask matters, but it does not eliminate the child standing in front of it.

Sixteen years later, Yang is paid to reproduce E-Soul as children's entertainment. His costume is fake in the ordinary material sense. His effects are fake. His institutional status is nonexistent. His Trust Value is zero. He is embarrassed by the job and imagines legitimacy as the day he can finally stand `堂堂正正`—openly, proudly, legitimately—before the hero who once rescued him.

Yòuzi sees something Yang cannot yet name. When cheap spectacle is added to the show, the child rejects the added effects as fake but says of Yang's earlier performance:

> `你从前的表演我不觉得是模仿` — **I didn't think your earlier performance was imitation.**

The distinction becomes the arc's first governing principle:

> **Performance is not the opposite of authenticity. A role can become a truthful practice when a person voluntarily enacts its values and remains capable of answering what the role asks of him.**

Yang then chooses rescue before he possesses hero power, institutional authorization, or reliable public recognition. Only after that choice does Yòuzi call `起来啊魂电`—“Get up, E-Soul”—and Yang's prop identity begins to operate as material herohood. The public role-name is therefore not person-erasure by default. Yòuzi has already seen Yang fail, work, hide, practice, and persist. “E-Soul” is a relational attribution to a known person, not a substitute for knowing him.

E06 expands this into a model of **co-authored inheritance**. Shang Chao refuses both unilateral self-declaration and corporate monopoly. A hero identity is not something Yang can simply seize because he desires it; it requires other people's Trust. But neither must Yang become a replica of the veteran. Shang Chao tells him to become `一个属于你的全新魂电`—a new E-Soul in his own way, belonging to him. The role becomes legitimate through an interaction among conduct, recognition, equipment, public memory, and bearer choice.

E07 then makes that legitimacy dangerous. Shang Chao's death turns grief into vocation. Yang declares that rather than hand the counterweight to someone else, he will `自己成为那个砝码`—become the counterweight himself. Xia Qing tests whether the duel is truly his desire. He says yes. That answer matters. But so does the fact that Yan Mo has designed the public environment, manipulated the signature-move expectation, coerced intermediaries, and arranged circumstances in which both Yang and the veteran experience a narrowing field of acceptable choices.

The succession is therefore neither free in a vacuum nor puppeteered from outside. It is **engineered agency**.

The later E19–E20 material supplies the final complication. Yang's E-Soul is no longer the excluded claimant fighting a closed system. He is an institutional actor operating inside classified violence. He attacks Ghostblade and the Johnny family and states that Ghostblade's death would remove a ranking rival. Then he becomes rank nine.

The mature E-Soul thesis is thus:

> **Inherited heroism is legitimate only as a continuing practice of answerability. Yang becomes E-Soul because the role is relationally recognized, voluntarily enacted, technically sustained, publicly co-authored, and capable of being reinterpreted through him. He later demonstrates that none of those origins supplies permanent moral credit. A covenant can be inherited sincerely and still be violated by the inheritor.**

## 2. E-Soul is older than the man wearing the mask

The title's temporal depth is central.

By E07 Yang states that `魂电这个名号经历了34个年头`—the E-Soul name has existed for thirty-four years. The wording already pushes the identity beyond one biological person. E-Soul has duration as a public category.

E24 complicates the genealogy further by placing an E-Soul bearer/title inside the official history of Zero's defeat, where `电光斩` appears in founding mythology. The current corpus does **not** prove that the E05–E07 veteran is personally continuous with that ancient bearer, nor that the contemporary Yang inherits one unbroken metaphysical essence. The secure conclusion is narrower:

> **E-Soul functions as a trans-bearer heroic identity whose public history exceeds Yang and whose exact continuity rules remain unresolved.**

That matters because it changes the moral question from “who owns a name?” to “under what conditions may a socially consequential office survive its bearer?”

The identity behaves simultaneously as:

- a remembered rescuer;
- a costume and performance vocabulary;
- a corporate intellectual-property asset;
- a public covenant;
- a set of signature abilities and gestures;
- a Trust-bearing social office;
- a career and ranking position;
- a historical symbol;
- and, after succession, a field in which multiple constituencies can disagree about who counts as legitimate.

No one of those dimensions can settle the others automatically.

---

## 3. The child Yang: rescue becomes recognition

The first E-Soul scene is important precisely because it refuses to stop at domination over enemies.

Yang's parents are dead. Adults discuss what to do with the vulnerable child. E-Soul intervenes. The spectacular dimension is conventional: the hero can do what the child cannot. But after violence ends, the hero asks Yang his name.

This gives the later mask a relational origin. Yang does not first encounter E-Soul as merchandise, ranking, or celebrity. He encounters a figure who restores enough safety for his own name to become speakable.

The scene therefore creates a difficult inheritance standard:

> **If Yang later becomes E-Soul, the deepest thing to inherit is not the shape of the armor. It is the practice by which power returns a person to himself rather than making the hero the whole meaning of the encounter.**

The tragedy of the later arc is that the public office can preserve the armor and lose sight of that person-centered standard.

### 3.1 Trauma does not invalidate admiration

Yang's devotion to E-Soul is trauma-linked, but the series does not therefore dismiss it as psychologically false. The rescue mattered. The name mattered. The ideal mattered.

His later desire to stand before E-Soul with legitimacy is partly a wish to become visible to the person who once saw him at his most helpless. That can generate aspiration without determining his entire future.

### 3.2 Recognition before inheritance

The episode's chronology is elegant:

`E-Soul rescues Yang` → `E-Soul asks Yang's name` → sixteen years → `Yang performs E-Soul` → `Yang rescues Yòuzi` → `Yòuzi names him E-Soul` → `Shang Chao names him Yang Cheng`.

The arc is built from alternating person-name and role-name recognition. Neither is sufficient by itself.

---

## 4. Yang the actor: the mask before power

Yang's ordinary job is analytically indispensable. If he had first put on the E-Soul suit after receiving superpowers, the role could be read as a simple costume attached to prior heroic essence. Instead, he performs E-Soul while materially powerless and socially marginal.

The cheap children's show establishes three levels:

1. **material simulation** — plastic suit, staged effects, scripted combat;
2. **embodied practice** — posture, timing, confidence, protective rhetoric, learned movement;
3. **ethical direction** — whether the person wearing the role acts toward protection when no audience guarantee exists.

Yòuzi rejects flashy pseudo-authenticity but not performance itself. This is why `表演` and `模仿` matter. The series does not ask whether Yang is acting. He unquestionably is. It asks whether acting can carry a value deeply enough to become a practice of self-formation.

Yang's shame initially prevents him from recognizing that possibility. He thinks he must first acquire external legitimacy before his performance can mean anything. The zero-Trust audition intensifies that belief.

## 5. Zero Trust: rational metric, unequal gate

The MG audition is one of the season's clearest cases of an institution being simultaneously rational and unjust.

Yang performs well enough to remain competitive, but the decisive institutional line is effectively: the one with zero Trust can be dropped.

In this world, that criterion is not arbitrary. Trust can affect operational capability. A hero organization has legitimate reasons to care whether a prospective bearer can actually produce or sustain the power expected of the role.

But Trust is also socially distributed. Yang has little visibility, low occupational status, no established hero office, and no preexisting audience. The metric therefore rewards the very social recognition that the institution controls access to acquiring.

The loop is:

`low recognition` → `low Trust` → `exclusion from recognized hero labor` → `low visibility` → `continued low Trust`.

That is why `P2-CR-I07` is important: Trust-based gatekeeping can be **rational and unequal at the same time**.

The episode does not need the institution to be malicious. Structural exclusion is more interesting because it can reproduce itself through defensible criteria.

## 6. Pomelo: recognition before belief becomes power

Yòuzi/Pomelo is not important merely because he appears to provide Yang's first useful Trust. His analytical function begins earlier.

He is a critic before he is a believer.

He sees the cheap performance and distinguishes what is technically fake from what is merely performed. He is also willing to articulate the desire Yang has displaced into legitimacy-seeking: perhaps Yang does not merely want E-Soul to notice him; perhaps he wants to become a hero like him.

This makes Yòuzi's later `起来啊魂电` unusually strong evidence.

If an anonymous spectator who knew nothing of Yang called “E-Soul,” the role-name might simply overwrite him. Yòuzi has instead observed:

- Yang's low-status labor;
- his embarrassment;
- the quality of his earlier performance;
- his failed institutional aspiration;
- his willingness to chase the kidnappers;
- his physical helplessness during that rescue.

The name “E-Soul” is therefore attached to a known trajectory of conduct.

### 6.1 Role-address can preserve person recognition

This is an important correction to any legal-name purity model.

Lin Ling needed `林凌` to break a forced equivalence with Nice. Yang does not need Yòuzi to stop saying E-Soul in order for recognition to become healthier. The ethical issue is not whether a hero name is used. It is whether the role-name makes the person's history disappear.

Yòuzi's address does the opposite. It gathers Yang's history into an aspirational form.

### 6.2 One believer is not one point

The episode strongly supports a causal role for Yòuzi's belief in Yang's first meaningful power manifestation. It does not establish a universal arithmetic rule in which one person supplies one Trust point or one unit of power.

The specialist synthesis therefore uses **relational seed** rather than numerical unit.

### 6.3 The child as epistemic outsider

Yòuzi also stands outside the adult institutions that define Yang as zero-value. He can see craft and intention that MG's metric does not register. This does not make children magically more truthful than institutions. It shows that different recognition systems detect different properties.

## 7. Rescue before power: the moral sequence matters

Yang chooses to pursue Yòuzi before he can rely on hero power.

That ordering is the ethical hinge of E05.

He does not receive Trust, discover he is powerful, and then decide to act heroically because the action is now safe. He acts while still vulnerable. The later power response therefore answers conduct rather than producing it from nothing.

The safest mature sequence is:

`practice` → `unrewarded protective choice` → `relational recognition` → `power manifestation` → `broader public legibility`.

This is not a metaphysical proof that moral virtue always precedes Trust. It is the character arc's legitimacy structure.

Yang's later fall in E19 becomes more painful because the series has already shown he can act before reward. The existence of that earlier capacity removes the excuse that he has never known another way.

---

## 8. Veteran E-Soul: the incumbent is not a fraud

A weaker story would make succession easy by revealing the older bearer as wholly corrupt. *To Be Hero X* refuses that convenience.

The veteran's mask speech treats the role as a chosen reminder of `不愿辜负的信赖`—Trust he does not want to betray—and hope he has chosen to carry. In E07 he explicitly rejects the ranking motive:

> `我不是为了排名才成为魂电` — **I did not become E-Soul for rankings.**

His public career has become surrounded by merchandise, anniversaries, scripts, media management, and corporate property claims. That does not prove the underlying covenant was fake.

The incumbent therefore creates a real ethical problem for Yang.

If the old E-Soul were only a hollow brand, Yang's inheritance would look like liberation from fraud. Instead two sincere forms of E-Soul can exist in conflict:

- an older bearer whose public office has accumulated institutional weight and history;
- a younger bearer whose local conduct reactivates the role's ethical promise for a new constituency.

The contest is not truth versus falsehood. It is a conflict over whether a public role can remain revisable when its incumbent is still alive and sincere.

---

## 9. Shang Chao: transparent infrastructure and co-authorship

Shang Chao is one of the strongest arguments in the series against the fantasy of a self-made hero.

Yang's emerging E-Soul identity depends upon:

- technical stabilization of dangerous electricity;
- capital and workspace;
- public strategy;
- evidence gathering;
- coaching;
- interpretive language;
- and material equipment.

Shang Chao does not hide that help from Yang. That transparency matters.

His core formulation is not “you are secretly the true E-Soul.” It is:

> a hero identity cannot be possessed merely because you want it;

and then:

> `用你的方式 / 成为一个属于你的全新魂电` — **in your own way, become a new E-Soul that belongs to you.**

This creates a healthy co-authorship model:

`person acts` → `others recognize` → `resources support` → `role becomes socially legible` → `bearer revises role through continued conduct`.

### 9.1 Technology does not make heroism less real

Yang's power is unstable. Engineering makes it usable.

This is a useful correction to romantic accounts of hero essence. Heroic efficacy can be socially generated and technically mediated at once. The gauntlet or controller is not “cheating” any more than a prosthetic, instrument, communications system, or trained technique would be.

The relevant ethical questions are provenance, control, dependency, and whether the bearer understands the infrastructure shaping his action.

### 9.2 Posthumous inheritance without secret jurisdiction

After Shang Chao's death, his technical work survives him. Yang knowingly carries it.

That is posthumous influence, but it differs sharply from Yan Mo's manipulation. Shang Chao's equipment remains visible as help. Yan Mo's environment hides its authorship.

This distinction will matter throughout the episode:

> **Influence is not coercion merely because it changes what another person can do. The ethical question is whether influence preserves the other's knowledge, refusal, and capacity to answer it.**

## 10. Xia Qing: recognition through and beyond performance

Xia Qing is Yang's most important protection against role totalization because her relation does not require him to successfully remain E-Soul.

In E06 she calls the performed hero `另一个你`—**another you**. That phrase rejects two thin models:

- the hero role is the only real Yang;
- the hero role is a fake shell hiding the only real Yang.

Instead, the performed self can be one real mode among several.

Her later testimony grounds courage and justice in Yang Cheng. Her support survives his humiliation, uncertainty, and inability to immediately face himself. She does not withdraw recognition when the role becomes contested.

This is the basis of `P2-CR-R10`:

> **Xia Qing recognizes Yang through and beyond performance; her recognition does not depend on his successfully maintaining E-Soul.**

### 10.1 The E07 question that protects agency

Before the duel Xia asks:

> `那真的是你想去做的吗` — **Is that really what you want to do?**

Yang answers yes.

This question is indispensable. The later manipulation does not make his desire fictitious. The episode deliberately places person-centered recognition beside environmental engineering so that neither can erase the other.

### 10.2 Support is not authorization

Xia's belief in Yang does not mean every choice he makes as E-Soul is justified. Healthy recognition permits disagreement. She explicitly tells him that stepping back is not the same as cowardly flight when he begins treating thousands of people's Trust as a reason he cannot refuse the duel.

Her relation therefore contains something public Trust often lacks: the ability to say **you remain you even if you do not perform the expected heroic action**.

## 11. From Trust as gift to Trust as debt

E05 treats Trust primarily as recognition arriving after action. E07 reveals its second face.

Yang says:

> `他背负着成千上万人的信赖` — **I carry the Trust of thousands.**

The verb `背负` matters. Trust is weight.

Being relied upon can create legitimate obligations. A doctor, officer, elected official, parent, engineer, or rescue worker cannot always treat commitment as a purely private preference once others reasonably depend on him. Herohood is no exception.

But dependence can become totalizing when it erases the bearer’s capacity to judge whether the expected action remains right.

Yang begins to argue:

`people trust me` → `therefore I cannot withdraw` → `withdrawal would betray them`.

The series does not simply condemn that logic. It asks who controls the conditions under which the duty is being invoked.

When Yan Mo can manufacture the field of expectations, “duty to the public” becomes a lever by which another actor moves the bearer without issuing a direct command.

The problem is therefore not responsibility itself. It is **captured responsibility**.

---

## 12. Grief and the counterweight: vocation after Shang Chao

Shang Chao's funeral transforms the succession conflict.

His father describes justice as a scale whose balance has become unacceptable. Yang then declares:

> `与其把砝码交给别人 / 还不如 / 自己成为那个砝码`
>
> **Rather than hand the counterweight to someone else, I will become that counterweight myself.**

The formulation contains three truths.

First, it is self-authoring. Yang is no longer merely waiting for a recognized hero to validate him.

Second, it is grief-driven. His vocation is inseparable from the death of the person who helped make his emerging identity possible.

Third, it risks self-appointment. A person convinced that the scales are unjust may begin to treat his own judgment as the necessary corrective weight.

The series will later explore this problem at much larger scale through Queen, Zero, Yan Mo, and X. Yang's version is local but structurally related: **when does the refusal to remain passive become a warrant to make oneself the instrument of justice?**

`P2-CR-R07` is therefore relevant. Grief can preserve relation and provenance, but it can also conscript the survivor into role continuation and posthumous obligation.

Yang does not inherit only equipment from Shang Chao. He inherits an unfinished moral field.

---

## 13. Engineered agency: Yan Mo does not need puppets

Yan Mo's most important E07 proposition is not a threat. It is a theory of environment.

The Japanese witness says, in effect: words alone do little, but prepare the appropriate setting and people move remarkably well. The Mandarin reconstruction gives the complementary formulation:

> `一旦设定了游戏规则` — **once the rules of the game are set...**

This is the conceptual bridge from a harmless social trick to political violence.

Yan Mo's method is to control upstream conditions:

- information;
- economic vulnerability;
- publicity;
- public expectations;
- perceived honor;
- signature-move obligation;
- criminal intermediaries;
- institutional fear of reputational loss.

He does not need Yang or the veteran to be mindless. In fact, genuine agency makes the system more effective because each person supplies his own reasons for doing what the designed environment makes salient.

### 13.1 Manipulation does not erase responsibility

Calling the duel engineered must not become a universal excuse.

Yang chooses. The veteran chooses. MG chooses. Yan Mo shapes the field.

Responsibility is therefore distributed rather than annihilated.

This is one of the series' most mature political ideas: coercive power often works not by eliminating choice but by **structuring what choices cost**.

### 13.2 The signature move as compelled authenticity

Yan Mo makes public demand for `电光斩` / Diànguāng Zhǎn part of the veteran's dilemma. MG warns that refusing the iconic move will damage Trust.

The incumbent is therefore commanded by the public image of his own authenticity:

`a real E-Soul uses the move` → `if you do not use it, perhaps you are no longer E-Soul`.

A signature once expressing identity becomes a disciplinary device.

That is a recurring *TBHX* pattern: an image can become coercive precisely because it is not arbitrary. It is built from real history and then turned into a demand for repetition.

## 14. The duel: public recognition as live constitutional referendum

The E-Soul duel is not merely a fight between stronger and weaker bodies.

Spectators' opinions shift during the battle. Trust shifts with them. The relative capacities of the two claimants respond. Public belief therefore does not merely reward the winner after the fact; it participates in producing the conditions of winning.

The fight is a **live metaphysical referendum** over role legitimacy.

This gives public recognition genuine constitutional force. The audience is not just a crowd being manipulated by corporations. Its changing judgment helps determine which bearer can materially sustain the role.

Yet E07 immediately refuses to idealize this as pure democracy.

Public opinion is itself shaped by:

- media framing;
- advertising expenditure;
- institutional access;
- inherited brand history;
- rumor;
- signature expectations;
- and Yan Mo's hidden interventions.

So the referendum is real and mediated.

> **Collective choice can be causally genuine without being procedurally neutral.**

That principle is essential to the full-series ranking system.

---

## 15. The veteran's death and the problem of dispossession

After the veteran dies, the episode reports the associated Trust as `统合 / 統合`—integrated or consolidated—around the survivor.

This is stronger than mere popularity transfer. E07 depicts the social/metaphysical field as capable of concentrating around the remaining claimant.

But several boundaries remain protected:

- there is no demonstrated universal law that every hero title automatically transfers all Trust on bearer death;
- legal title and metaphysical consolidation are not proven identical;
- we do not know how many veteran supporters consciously endorse Yang before the consolidation;
- the exact mathematical transfer remains unestablished.

The veteran's death also makes clear why role succession can be ethically violent even when the successor is sincere.

The public can cease sustaining an incumbent while he still understands himself as legitimately carrying the role. Social office is therefore not private property—but neither is public choice incapable of dispossessing a person.

The veteran is not exposed as a false man. He becomes **obsolete in a social ontology that helps constitute his power**.

That is tragedy, not simply progress.

---

## 16. Yang becomes E-Soul — but not alone

The inheritance that survives E07 is composite.

Yang carries:

- the childhood rescuer's example;
- years of bodily performance;
- Yòuzi's relational recognition;
- Xia Qing's person-centered trust;
- Shang Chao's language and engineering;
- thousands of supporters' Trust;
- the veteran's public history;
- the trauma of Shang Chao's death;
- Yan Mo's hidden manipulation;
- the institutional and commercial infrastructure around the role.

Calling him “self-made” would erase almost everything the arc has shown.

Calling him merely “made by others” would erase his rescue choice, training, judgment, grief, and consent.

The appropriate category is **answerable co-authorship**—with the crucial caveat that answerability remains vulnerable to later capture.

## 17. The E-Soul name as covenant, commons, and property

The role is contested because different institutions recognize different forms of ownership.

### 17.1 Covenant

The veteran defines the mask through Trust he does not want to betray and hope he chooses to bear. This is E-Soul as ethical promise.

### 17.2 Commons

Yòuzi and later supporters treat E-Soul as something the public can recognize in a new person. The role is not exhausted by corporate title documents.

### 17.3 Property

MG and associated interests treat name, image, events, merchandise, and official authorization as controllable assets.

### 17.4 Embodied office

Because Trust alters capacity, the title is also materially instantiated through bearers.

None of these models can simply erase the others. The conflict occurs because E-Soul is all four at once.

The healthier governance question is therefore not “who owns E-Soul absolutely?” but:

> **Which claims over the role are legitimate, contestable, transparent, and compatible with the bearer's continued standing as a person?**

## 18. Performance and authenticity after the succession

The arc decisively rejects the idea that sincerity means absence of performance.

Yang's acting matters because it teaches him how to inhabit a visible form. The veteran's public gestures matter because repeated form can become covenant. Shang Chao's production matters because publics need legible surfaces through which to recognize emerging roles.

The danger begins when the performance ceases to be revisable.

A useful E-Soul continuum is:

`rehearsal` → `chosen enactment` → `relational recognition` → `public office` → `disciplinary expectation` → `possible totalization`.

The same sign can move along that continuum without becoming intrinsically false.

This is why the mask has no single moral meaning.

It can conceal shame.

It can remind a bearer of duty.

It can let a child address courage.

It can become corporate property.

It can become a public demand.

It can survive the bearer.

## 19. E19: inherited legitimacy does not prevent ethical capture

The later ruins operation is the decisive retrospective test of the E-Soul arc.

The armored assailant is presented as E-Soul. Yan Mo's intercut planning places him inside the conspiracy. E-Soul attacks Ghostblade, Little Johnny, and Big Johnny in a classified environment where public visibility is absent.

The contrast with E05 is severe.

In E05 Yang acts before recognition to save a socially vulnerable child.

In E19 the recognized E-Soul uses his power inside a secret operation against socially vulnerable targets whose public classifications make them easier to eliminate.

This is not proof that the earlier Yang has ceased to exist metaphysically. It is proof that **role inheritance did not permanently secure the ethical direction of his conduct**.

### 19.1 Ranking enters the assassination calculus

E-Soul says Ghostblade's death will remove one ranking rival.

The line does not prove ranking ambition is his only motive. It establishes that career advantage is sufficiently real to be spoken as a benefit of covert death.

That marks an extraordinary reversal from the veteran's E07 declaration:

> `我不是为了排名才成为魂电` — I did not become E-Soul for rankings.

The point should not be simplified into “old E-Soul good, young E-Soul bad.” The veteran had his own institutional compromises. The structural irony is sharper:

> **Yang inherits a title partly by opposing the capture of hero meaning by rank and public image; later he participates in violence for which rank is an explicit private incentive.**

### 19.2 The public covenant is disabled by secrecy

E07's duel is visible. The public can react. Trust moves in response.

E19's assassination is classified. The people whose belief constitutes E-Soul have no opportunity to judge the act while it occurs.

Secrecy removes one of the role's accountability channels.

This is a major institutional lesson: a socially constituted office can become especially dangerous when it operates in domains where the constituting public cannot observe its use.

## 20. Ghostblade versus E-Soul: the inherited hero meets the repudiating assassin

E19 deliberately reverses expected moral positions.

Ghostblade is an assassin whose career was built on accepting target stories. He now protects the beings the hidden institution wants dead.

E-Soul is a publicly celebrated hero whose role was built around trust and protective symbolism. He now performs the cleanup violence.

The motion audit makes the contrast bodily:

- Ghostblade extracts Little Johnny from attack paths;
- holds a defensive line;
- shields Big Johnny with his own body;
- refuses to return to Yan Mo's side.

E-Soul applies forward electrical pressure against them.

The series is not saying “assassins are secretly heroes and heroes are secretly villains.” It is demonstrating that **office labels do not remove the need to evaluate present conduct**.

Heroism remains an activity before it is a permanent moral caste.

## 21. E20: rank nine and the failure of public moral accounting

E20 makes the institutional irony explicit.

Ghostblade rejects covert murder, suffers severe injury, and exits MG.

E-Soul participates in covert murder, survives, and appears at:

`9. E-SOUL RANKING`

The ranking system has not necessarily “rewarded murder” knowingly. That is precisely the problem. Public legitimacy is operating on incomplete information.

The public sees:

- a surviving hero;
- demonstrated strength;
- an established role;
- rank advancement.

The audience of the series knows:

- classified assassination;
- willingness to eliminate Ghostblade and the Johnny family;
- hidden faction strategy;
- explicit ranking benefit.

This establishes a distinction the specialist must preserve:

> **Social legitimacy can be causally real and epistemically wrong.**

Trust is not unreal because the public lacks evidence. But the moral conclusions drawn from the metric can be badly distorted by secrecy.

## 22. Does E19 invalidate Yang's earlier inheritance?

No. But it revises what inheritance means.

There are at least four possible bad readings.

### 22.1 “Yang was fake all along”

This contradicts E05–E07 evidence. He chose rescue before power, was recognized by people who knew him, was tested by Xia Qing, and sincerely articulated a justice commitment.

### 22.2 “A legitimate E-Soul cannot do this, so the E19 bearer must not really be Yang”

The source presents contemporary E-Soul as the assailant; absent contrary evidence, moral discomfort cannot be used to invent a separate bearer.

### 22.3 “Yan Mo manipulated him, therefore Yang has no responsibility”

The exact degree of later coercion/manipulation remains open. E07 already taught that engineered context and real agency can coexist.

### 22.4 “The public still trusts him, therefore his conduct remains heroic”

E20 is practically designed to reject this inference.

The correct mature formulation is:

> **Yang's earlier succession was comparatively legitimate. His later conduct is a possible betrayal of the very covenant that made that succession legitimate.**

A role's provenance matters, but provenance does not immunize later action from judgment.

## 23. Xia Qing's absence from the later covert field

The current corpus does not establish enough about Xia Qing's later knowledge, reaction, or relation to Yang during E19–E20.

That absence should be preserved rather than filled with psychological certainty.

Analytically, however, her earlier function becomes instructive. Xia's recognition was valuable because it could survive Yang's failure and oppose his self-destructive reading of public obligation. She represented a relational channel through which he could be seen apart from the role's demand.

The later covert operation is a field almost entirely organized by institutional secrecy, rank, classified knowledge, and Yan Mo's agenda. The specialist can therefore identify a structural absence without inventing a personal event:

> **The E19 setting lacks the reciprocal, person-centered witnesses who earlier made E-Soul answerable to Yang Cheng.**

This is not a causal claim that Xia's presence would have prevented the attack. It is a contrast in accountability ecology.

## 24. Yòuzi/Pomelo after the origin: why the first believer remains important

The later corpus does not give enough secure evidence to write a completed adult/future state for Yòuzi.

His importance is therefore not dependent on continued plot centrality.

He supplies the arc's epistemic origin:

- performance can be more than imitation;
- a socially marginal observer can recognize value before an institution;
- role-address can preserve personhood when grounded in relational knowledge;
- belief can respond to courageous action rather than substitute for it.

This origin becomes an ethical memory against which later E-Soul conduct can be measured.

The question E19 poses is not merely “does the public still call him E-Soul?” It is:

> **Would the kind of recognition that made Yòuzi call this person E-Soul still survive if the hidden act became known?**

The source does not answer. The question is precisely why secrecy matters.

## 25. Historical E-Soul and the danger of inherited myth

E24 places E-Soul inside the official history by which hero society narrates the defeat of Zero. That fact deepens the role's status as historical institution.

But official history is itself shown to be selective. The Commission's founding story turns the first hero's apotheosis into the story of an external Demon King and uses E-Soul's final blow as part of institutional legitimacy.

This creates a further warning:

> **Inherited heroism inherits not only admirable examples but also public histories whose causal framing may be incomplete.**

Yang's E-Soul identity therefore exists inside a lineage whose historical meaning is partly produced by institutions.

The specialist must not infer that contemporary Yang knows the full Zero history, that his power is mechanically identical to the historical bearer's, or that one soul/essence passes continuously across time.

What is secure is the depth of the public office.

## 26. Trust and the E-Soul body

The E-Soul arc is one of the clearest demonstrations that Trust is neither merely applause nor a neutral fuel tank.

### 26.1 Recognition changes operational capacity

Yòuzi's belief appears to catalyze Yang's first meaningful manifestation.

### 26.2 Broader visibility stabilizes and amplifies

Viral rescue footage expands recognition and produces continued bodily consequences.

### 26.3 Public expectation can constrain technique

The veteran's signature move becomes expected conduct. Trust therefore shapes not only magnitude but role-consistent behavior.

### 26.4 Technology mediates social power

Shang Chao's engineering stabilizes current. Heroic capability is therefore a socio-technical system.

### 26.5 Trust does not certify moral legitimacy

E19–E20 decisively separate power/rank from moral heroism.

This is why the full-series mechanics proposition must remain relational and institutional rather than reducing Trust to popularity.

### 28.6 E19 darkness versus public rank card

The covert ship chamber removes public witnesses. E20's rank-nine image restores a clean public surface. The formal cut between hidden violence and visible legitimacy is itself an argument about institutional memory.

## 29. Political economy: no hero stands alone

The E-Soul arc maps a complete production chain:

`children's performance labor` → `audition screening` → `Trust metric` → `corporate IP` → `investment` → `engineering` → `media coverage` → `public referendum` → `ranking` → `classified deployment`.

Each stage changes what E-Soul can mean.

The hero economy is therefore not external to character. It is one of the mechanisms through which character becomes socially consequential.

### 29.1 Hidden labor

Actors, engineers, managers, evidence specialists, promoters, and technicians help create the visible hero.

### 29.2 Capital can enable real agency

Shang Chao's resources help Yang do what he wants. Institutional mediation is not inherently corrupt.

### 29.3 Capital can capture public meaning

MG's property claims and Yan Mo's publicity engineering demonstrate the opposite possibility.

### 29.4 Ranking can become private incentive

E19 shows a public status mechanism entering covert lethal calculation.

The mature political-economy rule is therefore:

> **Hero infrastructure is legitimate when it expands answerable capacity; it becomes capture when the infrastructure acquires unanswerable jurisdiction over what the bearer must do or what the public is allowed to know.**

## 31. Character-state synthesis

### 31.1 Yang Cheng

Yang begins as a person who imagines legitimacy as something another hero or institution must confer. His most important early development is not receiving power but discovering that his practiced role can carry his own protective intention.

He then becomes increasingly public, responsible, and burdened. His inheritance of E-Soul is real but structurally compromised by hidden environmental engineering. Later, as an established hero, he becomes capable of using that inherited legitimacy inside covert violence.

Mature formulation:

> **Yang is neither impostor nor permanently authenticated heir. He is a real bearer whose legitimacy depends on continuing answerability—and whose later conduct demonstrates that a legitimate bearer can violate the inheritance he once made meaningful.**

### 31.2 Veteran E-Soul

The veteran is a sincere bearer whose role has become deeply institutionalized. He is neither merely obsolete nor morally absolute. His death reveals that public office can dispossess a person even when that person retains sincere attachment to its covenant.

### 31.3 Yòuzi / Pomelo

Yòuzi is the first recognizer who distinguishes sincere performance from imitation and whose role-address appears to seed Yang's power. His later status remains open. His lasting function is to establish that socially marginal recognition can precede institutional legibility.

### 31.4 Xia Qing

Xia is the strongest personhood-preserving relation in Yang's cluster. She accepts plurality of self, tests desire, resists the claim that withdrawal equals cowardice, and does not make hero-role success the condition of recognition. Her later relation to the E19 E-Soul remains unresolved.

### 31.5 Shang Chao

Shang Chao models comparatively transparent influence: engineering, capital, interpretation, and encouragement whose authorship is known to the recipient. His death makes his legacy vulnerable to posthumous conscription but does not erase the real support he provided.

### 31.6 Yan Mo

Yan Mo understands people well enough to manipulate them without denying their agency. His power lies in environment design, asymmetric information, pressure, and hidden rules. He is therefore a central figure for understanding how answerable roles become captured without literal mind control.

## 32. The ethics of inheritance

The E-Soul arc yields a more demanding model than either “legacy heroes are good” or “inherited identities are theft.”

A comparatively legitimate inheritance requires:

1. **provenance** — the predecessor and prior history are not erased;
2. **bearer choice** — the successor can answer why he accepts the role;
3. **relational recognition** — people can recognize the person through the role rather than only the image;
4. **revisability** — the successor may reinterpret how the role is lived;
5. **transparent infrastructure** — material and institutional help is not concealed as spontaneous essence;
6. **contestability** — rival claims and criticism can be heard;
7. **survivable refusal** — ideally the bearer can stop without annihilation or total social death;
8. **ongoing accountability** — prior legitimate inheritance does not confer permanent moral immunity.

Yang satisfies several of these conditions by E07 and violates or endangers others later.

The eighth is the decisive addition supplied by E19–E20.

## 37. Final synthesis: the hero after inheritance

Yang Cheng is one of *To Be Hero X*'s strongest arguments against purity as the measure of authentic identity.

He begins as an actor wearing a fake suit. The suit does not make his aspiration fake. His performance does not make his protection fake. His dependence on Yòuzi's recognition does not make his courage fake. Shang Chao's engineering does not make his power fake. Public co-authorship does not make his selfhood fake. Inheriting a name does not make him a replacement in the same way Lin Ling was forcibly substituted for Nice.

But the arc also refuses the opposite fantasy: that once a role has been sincerely chosen, its legitimacy becomes permanent property.

The E-Soul title accumulates history, expectation, capital, skill, equipment, public reliance, grief, and institutional power. Each of those can support the bearer. Each can also narrow his future.

By E07 Yang has good reason to believe the role is his in a meaningful sense. He has acted toward it before recognition; people have recognized him through it; Xia Qing has seen the person across its boundaries; Shang Chao has helped him reinterpret it rather than merely copy it; the public has participated in a real, if manipulated, succession event.

Then E19 asks the harder question:

> **What happens after the authentic becoming?**

The answer is that authenticity is not a vaccine against capture.

The same hero who once acted before reward can later act in secrecy for an institution. The same identity once justified as a covenant of public Trust can be used where the public cannot see. The same ranking system that once excluded Yang for zero Trust can later elevate him while hiding conduct that would matter to the people supplying that Trust.

This does not make Yang's earlier story a lie. It makes it a history.

A mature liberal-personhood reading of E-Soul therefore cannot ask only whether Yang had the right to inherit the role. It must also ask whether the role remains answerable at every later use.

The most durable Specialist-07 proposition is:

> **Heroic inheritance is legitimate when it transmits a practice without owning the person: provenance is preserved, the successor can answer why he accepts it, others can recognize him through rather than instead of himself, infrastructure remains visible, and the role can be revised. But inheritance supplies no permanent moral credit. The bearer must keep earning the covenant in present action, and institutions must preserve the conditions under which hidden conduct can still be judged.**

Yang Cheng becomes E-Soul.

That is not the end of the ethical question.

It is the point at which the ethical question becomes his responsibility.
