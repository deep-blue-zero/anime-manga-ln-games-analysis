# 12. CRD-E20-11 — X has civilian and hero presentations; the credits show a state transition

User-supplied high-resolution frames refine the provisional identity reading.

`VF / high-confidence continuity inference`:

- X appears first in an ordinary black-haired, suited office-worker presentation;
- the sequence isolates his hand/fingers immediately before a snap;
- the snap is followed by an abrupt transformation from the ordinary urban environment into the highly stylized X-saturated city/world-state associated with his power;
- the later white-haired, glasses-wearing presentation is best read as **X's public/hero mode**, not a separate person.

The sequence therefore suggests a dual-state identity structure:

`anonymous civilian X` → `signature gesture / snap` → `X-saturated world-state` → `public hero-form X`

The exact metaphysical scope of the snap and the ontological relation between the two bodies/presentations remain mechanics questions, but the visual grammar strongly favors **voluntary mode switching by one individual**.

This is unusually important because other heroes are continuously trapped inside identities that public belief can reshape. X appears able to preserve a private, socially unmarked self and selectively assume the public X state.

Two recurring audiovisual signatures should be tracked without collapsing them into one mechanic:

- **coin tossing** — recurrent X identity/decision motif; exact mechanical role open;
- **finger snapping** — directly associated in E20 with transition/state change.

This is a major credits event, not passive ranking imagery.

## 12.1 Lin Ling and his manager place the credits after the E20 main-action chronology

The credits also show **Lin Ling and his manager doing publicity work for Nice**.

Because Lin only inherits the Nice identity after Original Nice's jump, this sequence is chronologically later than the E20 ruins incident.

Therefore the credits themselves are an internal chronology bridge:

> **pre-E1 Original Nice crisis → later Lin Ling/Nice publicity → X-era public world**

The montage need not be interpreted as perfectly continuous day-by-day chronology, but it definitely crosses the main episode's temporal boundary.

## 12.2 Consequence for the credits thesis

The public hero system looks continuous precisely because roles survive their bearers.

Original Nice can die and Nice publicity continues through Lin.

The ruins can kill Luo Tong and still become merchandise/news.

X can alter the world-state while the ranking system presents his apex position as stable institutional fact.

The credits' “continuity” is therefore produced continuity, not evidence that nothing changed.

# 15. Credits thesis

Episode 20's main confrontation asks who will control Trust and Fear.

The credits show what control looks like after the fighting stops.

Institutions transform the same event into different usable forms:

- cemetery: private loss;
- FOMO post: marketable self-authorship;
- MG withdrawal headline: organizational narrative;
- hospital: private bodily cost;
- press conference: public reassurance;
- toy/model: commodified monster image;
- ranking screen: quantified legitimacy;
- tournament cancellation: administrative control;
- X headline: mythic continuity;
- Yan Mo/Spotlight: concealed next operation.

The montage's governing proposition is:

> **Society does not merely recover from catastrophe. It processes catastrophe into institutions, images, products, rankings, and future strategies.**

That processing can:

- preserve memory;
- provide public orientation;
- create economic life;
- recognize heroism;

and also:

- obscure responsibility;
- reward wrongdoing;
- capture self-authorship;
- neutralize terror through merchandise;
- allow conspiracies to continue beneath restored normality.

The credits are therefore not a sentimental epilogue.

They are the episode's final political argument.

# Final X dual-state motion resolution

Continuous video confirms the following montage order:

`Lin Ling Nice publicity → black-haired civilian X → coin motif → finger snap → graphic state change → neon X city → white-haired public X`.

This resolves the earlier identity ambiguity at high confidence: the black-haired office worker and white-haired glasses-wearing man are two presentations of X. The exact transformation mechanism remains open because the edit uses abstraction rather than a literal uninterrupted body morph.

The credits therefore bridge not only chronology but **modes of identity**. Lin Ling demonstrates a hero role imposed and maintained through institutional/public continuity; X demonstrates unusually high apparent control over when his hero role is visible at all.
