## 2. Smile is already searching for the pendant before Liang produces it

The continuous sequence begins with Smile alone and visibly checking/searching himself and the surrounding area before Liang Long enters the confrontation.

This strengthens the pendant from a generic prop into an **already-missing private object** whose absence has begun to register psychologically before Liang weaponizes it.

Liang then produces the pendant and makes its family photograph the center of his taunt.

Current bounded formulation:

> **Liang does not merely surprise Smile with an arbitrary family image. He arrives holding the private object Smile is already searching for, proving access to something intimate before making any claim about the daughter.**

The E21 rule still stands: Liang's claims about death, `遗物`, `骨灰`, or the daughter's location remain adversarial assertions. The pendant itself is real evidence of access; the death story is not independently verified at E21.

## 3. The fight is deliberately solicited as damage accumulation

Motion strongly confirms the frame-based E21 claim that Liang is not primarily trying to avoid Smile's punishment.

The fight repeatedly shows Liang:
- enduring high-force strikes;
- returning to engagement after being thrown or driven through the environment;
- inviting more damage;
- asking for only “a little more”;
- explicitly saying `打我呀` — **Hit me**;
- using information about the daughter's supposed ashes as incentive for Smile to continue striking.

This retrospectively strengthens the E21 mechanic from “received punishment appears to be an activation input” toward the later E22 resolution:

> **Liang is knowingly cultivating incoming damage as usable combat capital.**

E22 later supplies the explicit rule that received `伤害` can be returned doubled. The frozen E21 body should remain historically intact, but the current project-level mechanic is now fully consistent with the motion visible here.

## 4. Smile's emotional recovery is mechanically legible

Late in the clip, after Liang's reversal, Smile explicitly returns to the principle that had been under attack:

> only while maintaining a smile can his power be fully released.

The animation then restores the smiling heroic presentation before Smile regains offensive momentum.

This strengthens, without over-solving, the E21 formulation:

> **Smile's ability is gated by maintaining the smile-state.**

The clip still does not isolate whether the decisive variable is facial musculature, genuine positive affect, Trust feedback, or a composite identity condition. It does show that emotional/formal restoration and combat restoration are intentionally synchronized.

The clip creates an especially tight three-way structure:

- **Smile** fights because a private family wound has overridden his public emotional discipline.
- **Liang** intentionally converts the resulting punishment into fuel.
- **Nice** watches from concealment because Shang De has ordered him to exploit whichever survivor emerges.

Thus the E21 ending is not merely “Nice secretly observes two unaware combatants.”

> **It is a contest in which Liang has become aware of the hidden opportunist, while Smile remains focused on the family wound Liang has placed in front of him.**
