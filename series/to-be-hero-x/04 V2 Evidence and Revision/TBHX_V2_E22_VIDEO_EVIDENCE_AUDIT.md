## 1.1 X interrupts Liang's finishing attack and saves Nice

### Result: `STRENGTHEN`

Liang attacks while saying `消失吧`. A snap is audible within the highly stylized impact passage. When the flash/occlusion clears, X is present before/near Nice and says:

- `还好赶上了`
- `差点儿你就没了`

The safe motion-grounded formulation is:

> **X's intervention interrupts the finishing attack in time to save Nice.**

The exact rescue micro-mechanism is deliberately hidden by the visual impact. Do not invent a physical grab or conventional block.

## 1.2 X repeatedly changes his own representational style

### Result: `STRENGTHEN STRONGLY / NEW MECHANICS EVIDENCE`

After complaining about working overtime, X moves through a sequence punctuated by repeated snap sounds. His own presentation is re-rendered through sharply different visual regimes, including ordinary CG/3D, flattened pop/vector imagery, collage, monochrome manga/ink, rough/crayon-like rendering, and other graphic states before returning to ordinary world presentation.

This is stronger than an environmental filter applied around a stable X body.

> **X demonstrates voluntary sovereignty over how he himself is represented.**

This substantially strengthens the E20–E21 dual civilian/public-X interpretation: his unusual self-authorship may extend beyond social costume or body presentation into representational state itself.

Limit:

> The animation makes the style transitions diegetically coincide with X's actions, but E22 does not separately explain whether every style is a distinct material ontology or whether the power and the anime's expressive form are intentionally inseparable.

## 1.3 Liang's defeat is not ordinary teleportation

### Result: `REVISE STRONGLY`

The frame-only working reading described X as imposing a stylized combat state and later relocating Liang into water. Continuous motion supports a much more radical account.

The decisive chain is:

1. Liang remains an embodied combatant in ordinary viewer-facing composition.
2. X's white-gloved hand enters across the viewer-facing image plane and covers/grips Liang's represented figure.
3. Liang disappears from ordinary world-space; the following world shot is empty where he had been.
4. X is shown physically holding a **small crumpled purple paper/cel-like object**.
5. Continuity identifies that object with the Liang image he has just removed from ordinary embodied presentation.
6. X says `边凉快去吧` and throws the crumpled object over the railing.
7. The object falls into the river.
8. At water impact, rendering briefly shifts into halftone/pop-art treatment.
9. Ordinary rendering returns.
10. Liang emerges from the water again as an embodied three-dimensional person, no longer in the prior powered presentation.

Therefore:

> **X temporarily removes/converts Liang from ordinary embodied world-space into a manipulable two-dimensional/cel-like representation, physically handles and crumples that representation as an object, discards it into the river, and Liang subsequently reconstitutes as an embodied figure.**

This sequence cannot responsibly be reduced to "X teleports Liang into the river."

## 1.4 Diegetic metalepsis: almost a fourth-wall break

### Result: `STRENGTHEN STRONGLY`

The sequence crosses boundaries that normal combat treats as formally unavailable:

- embodied diegetic person;
- represented image on the viewer-facing plane;
- paper/cel-like object in X's hands;
- discarded object;
- reconstituted embodied person.

The most useful term is **diegetic metalepsis / representational-layer manipulation**.

It resembles a fourth-wall violation because X's hand appears to act across the image plane through which the audience sees Liang. But E22 does **not** prove that X knows he is fictional, sees the audience, or consciously addresses an extradiegetic viewer.

Safe formulation:

> **X can manipulate the representational layer that normally separates embodied characters from the image through which they are shown.**

## 1.5 Liang's retaliation mechanic is defeated by category change

### Result: `STRENGTHEN GOVERNING THESIS`

Liang's rule presupposes a shared causal exchange:

`damage received → doubled return`.

X does not answer that rule by taking more damage or returning greater damage. He changes the status of the opponent himself.

> **Liang embodies retaliation. X does not win the retaliation loop; he exits its ontology.**

For the decisive beat Liang is no longer functioning simply as one embodied combatant exchanging harm with another. He becomes an object of representational manipulation.

This is why E22's deepest contrast is not scalar power but **causal asymmetry**.

## 1.6 Water returns as Liang's formal bracket

### Result: `STRENGTHEN FORMAL READING`

Young Liang's endurance conditioning is staged underwater. Adult Liang is defeated when X discards his crumpled representation into a river, where he reconstitutes bodily.

Do not infer that X knows Liang's childhood or deliberately selects water as psychological punishment.

The episode-level formal rhyme is nevertheless strong:

> **The adult retaliation machine is returned to the medium in which endurance was first imposed upon the child.**

## 1.7 Finger-snap rule revised again

### Previous states

- E20: snap strongly associated with civilian/public-X state transition.
- E21: dialogue directly links snapping to voluntary identity/presentation change.
- E22 still pass: snap generalized to external state/relocation effect.

### Clip 01 result: `REVISE / STRENGTHEN`

The clip contains snap cues across more than one kind of X operation:

- interruption/rescue;
- repeated changes in X's own rendering/style;
- the later representational manipulation/discard sequence.

Current formulation:

> **The finger snap is a recurrent execution/transition cue for X's power across multiple representational and action domains, not the signature of one fixed ability.**

Still open:

- whether every X effect requires a snap;
- whether the sound/gesture is causal, ritual, interface, or punctuation;
- whether the final crumpling itself is caused by the snap or manually enacted after a state transition.

## 1.8 The crowd's `神` response becomes more consequential

After witnessing an operation the spectators cannot parse, the crowd asks whether anyone clearly saw what happened and then says:

`他就是神`

The E21 Association concern about `造神` is therefore strengthened in a very specific way:

> **God-language emerges not only from overwhelming force but from causal incomprehensibility.**

X appears to act from outside the audience's ordinary model of what a hero can do.

## 1.9 Retrospective Queen implication — current layer only

This clip also strengthens, without rewriting frozen E11/E12 bodies, the retrospective interpretation of Queen's post-X collapse.

Queen's political psychology is organized around rules, order, and causal intelligibility. E11 established that X defeated her by an unexplained mechanism and that he routinely ended matches almost instantly. E22 now demonstrates that X can operate across representational categories unavailable to ordinary rule-governed combat.

Current longitudinal inference:

> **Queen's loss to X plausibly functions as epistemic destabilization as much as competitive defeat: she encounters an event whose causal middle her rule-centered worldview cannot reconstruct.**

E12-local evidence still establishes only that she isolates after defeat amid bottles/screens and later credits Cyan's song with helping her out of the gloom. The causal emphasis above belongs to the later revision layer.

**Result:** Fear progressively occupies the existing Smile person-sign rather than instantly replacing him with an unrelated form.
