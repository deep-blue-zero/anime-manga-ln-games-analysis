## 4. Analytical consequence

Nice's intervention matters because recognition changes what he chooses without making him free, innocent, or omniscient. Smile is a sustaining private belief; Shang De demands a weapon; public love demands permanent perfection. Nice then disobeys a timing instruction using the very Fear technology furnished by his captor. The catastrophe is more precise than “hero tries to help and fails”: he makes a morally compromised protective choice under incomplete knowledge, and Liang's unsuspected redirection turns it against the person he plausibly sought to spare. This supports responsibility differentiated by choice, knowledge and outcome.

Liang's pain mechanics likewise require ethical separation from his formative victimization. Endurance conditions him to classify worth through what one can bear and return, but being formed by coercion does not remove adult responsibility for the humiliation and killing of Smile. Yan Mo cultivates that disposition; he does not thereby author every choice or know every future event.

X's representational intervention contrasts with the compulsory identities surrounding him. He can change the mode through which an opponent participates in the scene and then leave the public role. The late aftermath shows the limit of this power: he recovers an object rather than undoing a friend's death. A formal account should preserve that limit instead of treating an image-layer ability as emotional omnipotence.
