## Analytical consequence

The island ending materially changes the ethical weight of the apparent release. Returning Moon's spatial device remains an act that acknowledges her mobility; it cannot serve as an unqualified completed liberation once the same episode depicts her bloodied violent aftermath. The correction should preserve the earlier distinction between her stated desires and a public couple script while refusing to narrate autonomy as safely achieved. The visible injury leaves death and private fate OPEN. It does not retrospectively erase her first-person speech or turn her into a mere instrument of Lin's development. Responsibility for the violence must be argued from the attack evidence and subsequent disclosures, not inferred from the analytical usefulness of a villain.

The paired nightmare/beach images also change the formal account. A dialogue-derived ending omits the image that retrospectively binds domestic intimacy to danger. The repetition supports dread, threatened continuity and delayed recognition. It does not establish that Lin knew the future, that Moon consented to the danger, or that the earlier nightmare fixes a universal narrative fate mechanism.

The subtitle discrepancy supplies a methodological correction of comparable importance: an OCR collapse can be real while a later repair is itself wrong. A receiving analyst must preserve both findings. Neither old OCR confidence nor new multi-witness packaging makes a line quotation-grade automatically.
