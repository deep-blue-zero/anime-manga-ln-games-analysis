## 5. Mistaken target and differentiated responsibility

The order to eliminate the impostor and the threat to a captive brother establish compulsion. The gunman acts within a narrowed field: his brother's life is made conditional on compliance. That does not erase the earlier kidnapping responsibility or make the later violence harmless. It changes the moral and causal description of participation. “Willing contractor throughout” would erase the coercion shown in the reveal.

The prototype helmet then mediates an identity error. It gives Shang the outline associated with the target while the attacker acts on that outline. Current evidence supports intended Yang/new E-Soul and actual Shang casualty. It does not support “Yan intentionally kills Shang to awaken Yang” as an established fact. Such a theory needs direct evidence for that intention, beyond a later outcome that Yan can exploit.

Yang's interruption is a separate causal question. The formal audit supports visible electrical disruption and a Yang-centered guilt/jealousy recollection. It retains shock, unstable power, divided attention, and other conditions. No source here proves a deliberate choice to let Shang die. Distinguish **felt guilt**, **capacity to act**, **actual action**, and **intentional omission**; none is a synonym for the others. Shang's VIP speech inside Yang's memory is still a remembered other person's voice, not evidence that Yang uttered every line or that the original scene objectively matched its distorted recollection.

## 7. Assistance, expectation engineering, and adaptive strategy

Yan's subsequent aid is real. Technical/tactical help makes Yang more capable, and the publicity campaign gives the veteran an expected performance whose refusal would cost Trust. In this world, agenda-setting is part of the material combat environment: it can make dropping a defense and using a signature move socially necessary. The spectators' excitement need not be fabricated person by person for organized amplification to be consequential.

The best supported strategic account is **environmental authorship with adaptation**. Yan acts on access, threats, technical opportunity, and public expectation, then uses the surviving possibilities. This is compatible with long-term ambition and some foresight. It neither establishes nor logically disproves that he anticipated particular contingencies. The stronger all-stage claim—that he designed the original rescue, knew Pomelo would generate exactly one point, arranged Shang's death as intended, scripted every hesitation, and guaranteed the veteran's exact death—is not warranted.

The veteran's covenant can be sincere while he is institutionally constrained. Yang's desire can be real while its field has been engineered. Aid and coercion can coexist in one operator. Those conjunctions preserve the mature Phase-3/4 argument: authorship concerns the conditions under which persons can know, answer, revise, or refuse a role, not merely whether their final conduct is emotionally sincere.

The post-duel graph and news make succession materially successful for the surviving bearer. That outcome is evidence of local consolidation. It is not evidence that Yang's personhood, moral responsibility, or relational losses have been repaired by an increased metric. Nor does it turn the succession into a universal law applicable to every hero office.
