## 2. Governing proposition

> **In *To Be Hero X*, power is capacity, heroism is accountable intervention, legitimacy is bounded authority, dignity is standing that survives usefulness and danger, and coercion is the conversion of asymmetry into an unanswerable claim over another person's body, role, future, or interpretation. These categories can overlap without collapsing. A person may genuinely protect others while acting illegitimately; an institution may legitimately regulate danger while degrading the bearer; a public may create real power without acquiring ownership of the person empowered. The series' positive standard is therefore not purity, strength, acclaim, or benevolent intention, but power exercised through proportionality, provenance, contestability, non-instrumentalization, revision, and survivable exit.**

## 3. Stabilized findings

1. **Mechanical efficacy, protective effect, moral heroism, institutional legitimacy, and constitutional authority are independent axes.** Success on one does not establish the others.
2. **Power does not certify heroism.** Yang acts heroically before recognized office; Zero possesses maximal social power while becoming a tyrant.
3. **Heroism does not certify rule.** A protective actor does not thereby acquire permanent jurisdiction over the protected person or public.
4. **Dignity survives usefulness, harmlessness, popularity, legal classification, contamination, culpability, and successful self-control.**
5. **Dignity is not immunity from restraint.** Dangerous beings may require proportionate containment while retaining personhood, review, and non-disposability.
6. **Answerability is the core legitimacy criterion.** The affected person must retain standing to know, contest, revise, refuse, and survive the relation or decision.
7. **Coercion extends beyond physical force.** Dependency, substitution, exposure, affective labor, epistemic monopoly, paternalism, gratitude, risk management, and posthumous obligation can all close the field of answer.
8. **Consent is materially conditioned.** Formal assent under bodily, economic, relational, informational, or institutional dependency is not self-interpreting.
9. **Consent is scope-bounded and temporally revisable.** Initial acceptance cannot authorize indefinite later expansion of a hero role.
10. **Survivable exit is the hardest legitimacy test.** A bearer must be able to leave without losing body, livelihood, social personhood, speech, health, or all identity outside the role.
11. **Negative freedom has independent moral force.** Moon, Qīng, Lin, and X can reject a role before presenting a complete substitute life or constitution.
12. **Lin/Nice separates continuity from legitimacy.** Bodily and brand continuity do not transfer memory, relation, consent, or personal obligation cleanly.
13. **Truthful exposure can remain coercive.** God Eye identifies real fraud while seizing interpretive control; Lin's confession restores answerability rather than innocence.
14. **Heroic myth can protect and constrain simultaneously.** Firm Man's invulnerability and public role preserve social value while erasing vulnerability and exit.
15. **Yang establishes rescue before office.** Recognition and institutional succession can later stabilize herohood without becoming its moral origin.
16. **Inherited office legitimacy remains revocable.** Legitimate succession does not immunize later conduct from judgment.
17. **Public benefit does not create public ownership.** Qīng's useful gift and genuine hope do not authorize sacred captivity.
18. **Care becomes more legitimate when it creates a space for fallibility.** Luo's intervention permits Qīng to fail without becoming a theological catastrophe.
19. **Queen's diagnosis is legitimate while sovereign authorship remains dangerous.** Her mature bounded rule is progress because it becomes self-involving, revisable, and open to differentiated care.
20. **Rules can protect while distributing standing unequally.** Bowa's challenge exposes the difference between procedural order and substantive justice.
21. **Paternal love does not create complete jurisdiction.** Luo Tong's concern remains limited by obsolete knowledge of Luo Li's present self.
22. **Later protection can mark real moral change without retroactive repair.** Ghostblade's interposition does not erase assassination, surveillance, or Nuonuo's abandonment wound.
23. **Target abstraction is a technology of violence.** Restoring biography changes the moral adequacy of administrative categories without denying the institutional fact of the mission.
24. **Danger and dignity are independent variables.** Big Johnny can be a real threat and a son whose family and legal standing matter.
25. **Uncertainty does not authorize unlimited research custody.** Legitimate inquiry requires proportionality, review, provenance, and a route out of classification.
26. **Authentic affect can become compulsory infrastructure.** Smile's genuine warmth does not prevent his smile from becoming extracted labor and a corruption channel.
27. **Protection can be sincere and causally harmful.** Original Nice's concern for Smile does not legitimate the hidden Fear injection or erase his causal role.
28. **Suffering does not create jurisdiction.** Liang's childhood coercion explains his pain-worth logic without authorizing provocation, trash classification, or doubled retaliation.
29. **Punitive heroism manufactures the reason for punishment.** Provocation, injury, repayment, and public recognition become a self-authorizing loop.
30. **Public judgment may be locally intelligible and globally inadequate.** The crowd can correctly see a dangerous Smile-form and still lack the distributed causal history needed for a final moral verdict.
31. **A-Hu's local rescue is distributed.** Singular public credit protects X but obscures Xinya, the neighborhood, FOCUS, and the larger causal network.
32. **A valid danger lesson can be taught through an illegitimate method.** A-Hu and the Commission correctly identify X as a constitutional risk while failing procedurally through preventive-assassination logic.
33. **Catastrophic capacity requires reciprocal constraint, not secret execution or bearer ownership.**
34. **Zero is public power becoming unanswerable sovereignty.** Mass Trust creates causal power without moral authorization to kill dissent or make personal justice world law.
35. **Public will is causally powerful and normatively fragmented.** It cannot be treated as one informed democratic subject or one irrational mob.
36. **Institutions convert asymmetry into durable jurisdiction.** Their legitimacy depends on whether they preserve provenance, contestability, private-role plurality, and revision.
37. **Benevolence does not prevent institutional coercion.** Real opportunity, care, hope, and safety can become indefinite role debt.
38. **Hidden labor is a dignity and legitimacy problem.** Erasing co-producers removes witnesses, claimants, credit, and possible checks on the visible bearer.
39. **Rank and tournament can provide procedural and anti-monopoly legitimacy without moral transubstantiation.**
40. **Alliance is operational coordination, not moral legitimacy.** Shared threat or objective does not prove recognition, due process, or justified means.
41. **The minimum ethics of force requires necessity, proportionality, discrimination, self-involvement, review, and termination of emergency authority.**
