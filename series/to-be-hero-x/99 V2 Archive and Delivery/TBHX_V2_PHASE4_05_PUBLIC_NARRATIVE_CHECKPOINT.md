## 2. Governing proposition

> **Public narrative in *To Be Hero X* is a form of causal governance. Stories select events, assign names and roles, distribute credit and blame, organize collective feeling, and feed back into Trust, Fear, bodies, ranks, contracts, markets, and violence. Branding stabilizes those stories into repeatable identities; celebrity turns asymmetrical visibility into labor and obligation; myth compresses contested history into morally actionable form; collective belief gives the result material force without making it true, consensual, democratic, or just. The ethical alternative is not storylessness. It is answerable public meaning: narratives that preserve provenance, plural persons, legitimate privacy, contestability, revision, and a survivable difference between the person and the image.**

## 3. Stabilized findings

1. **Public narrative is causal governance.** It creates socially actionable identities and consequences rather than merely commenting on completed events.
2. **No hero exists outside narration.** Names, testimony, images, performance, institutions, and memory make action socially legible and durable.
3. **Narrative legitimacy is not reducible to truth value.** Factual relation, causal adequacy, authorship, and consequence are independent variables.
4. **The recurrent circuit is:** event → selection → narration → circulation → uptake → material feedback → recontextualization.
5. **The public/private distinction is not lie/truth.** Public accounts can be reparative or selectively true; private accounts can preserve provenance while remaining interested, partial, or coercive.
6. **Collective belief is segmented and mediated.** Fans, local witnesses, platform users, reporters, offices, officials, families, and hostile observers do not form one informed public mind.
7. **Mechanically convergent belief is not democratic consent.** Material effect does not establish coherent intention, procedural standing, or ownership of the bearer.
8. **Media are dual-use causal infrastructure.** The same screen, platform, recorder, or broadcast can manufacture, expose, protect, preserve, target, or recapture.
9. **Visibility is armor and capture.** It makes persons harder to erase and easier to use.
10. **Narrative allocates Trust and Fear.** A story can generate bodily, market, ranking, custody, and violence consequences even when causally incomplete.
11. **Narrative feedback can become self-confirming.** Fear produced by a curse story can create harms later cited as proof of the curse.
12. **Branding is durable expectation architecture.** It stabilizes identity, role, visual grammar, relationships, output, and market value across time.
13. **Brand continuity does not establish person continuity.** Nice can persist as body/office/brand while memory, consent, intimacy, and obligation remain bearer-specific.
14. **Brands author surrounding roles.** Moon and Wreck show that supporting people can be scripted as romance, mobility, rivalry, or proof of the central hero.
15. **Myth can begin from a true act and still totalize.** Firm Man's situational courage becomes a bodily and moral obligation never to fall.
16. **Exposure is not restored authorship.** God Eye reveals real fraud while Lin's confession comparatively restores answerability from inside the event.
17. **Celebrity is concentrated visibility without reciprocal access.** Genuine public feeling can be aggregated into labor and obligation for a bearer who cannot reciprocally know the public.
18. **Affective gifts can become extracted functions.** Hope, reassurance, inspiration, romance, violence, and the smile may be genuinely valuable while becoming compulsory.
19. **Sacralization converts benefit into entitlement.** Qīng's gift does not authorize public ownership of her identity, availability, or infallibility.
20. **Reparative narrative restores provenance.** The crash recorder and guitar recover causal persons and relations rather than merely reversing emotional valence.
21. **Public succession is managed collective authorship.** E-Soul's referendum is real while its agenda, evidence, access, and conflict are institutionally structured.
22. **Political celebrity can amplify and capture authored thought.** Queen's ideas remain hers even as DOS and public history convert them into a marketable movement-symbol.
23. **Self-authored identity is not outside the market.** Luo Li's authentic counter-image can be captured as a repeatable commercial promise.
24. **Negative-space celebrity is productive.** Ghostblade's silence becomes a public and institutional identity that fills absence with socially useful meaning.
25. **Family recognition can be mistranslated into ability.** Little Johnny reaching Big Johnny becomes a controller/summoner narrative that reduces reciprocal kinship.
26. **Authentic affect can become bodily celebrity infrastructure.** Smile's genuine warmth becomes continuous labor and later a Fear-corruption channel.
27. **Visible protection can become moral certification.** Liang's defeat of a real threat is narratively laundered from a catastrophe he helped produce.
28. **Strategic fiction may be protective.** A-Hu's sole-credit account preserves X's privacy while compressing distributed causality and enabling later capture.
29. **Platform scale has no innocent form.** FOMO widens access while repricing identity; FOCUS interrupts hidden abuse while later footage can singularize the event.
30. **God-language forms around causal opacity.** X becomes narratable as deity or sovereign threat because ordinary categories fail.
31. **Founding myth externalizes an internal catastrophe.** Demon King Zero preserves real danger while concealing that hero society made the first god.
32. **Selective truth is often more durable than total fabrication.** The Zero myth works because it preserves enough history to remain actionable.
33. **Myth is a necessary compression technology.** Constitutive, disciplinary, sacral, succession, protective, founding, counter-, and market myths perform different functions.
34. **Compression is legitimate only while provisional.** Coordination must remain open to excluded persons, causes, and later evidence.
35. **Public causal power does not create public sovereignty.** A public may help create a hero or god without acquiring jurisdiction over the bearer.
36. **Celebrity intimacy is real and politically exploitable.** Institutions can aggregate sincere gratitude into claims of continued availability.
37. **Authenticity can become role debt.** A genuine trait remains real even after institutions make its repetition a condition of value.
38. **Scandal, crisis, and grief are narrative markets.** Whoever supplies the first usable explanation can redirect rank, Trust/Fear, policy, and memory.
39. **Privacy is a legitimate narrative good.** Transparency is justified by accountability, not a general right to possess every truth about a bearer.
40. **Official and insurgent memory can both monopolize partial truth.** Yan Mo's countermemory does not become legitimate merely because the Commission suppressed history.
41. **Objects are portable counter-archives.** Recorder, guitar, notebook, pendant, photographs, scarf, and coin keep alternative provenance available.
42. **Provenance is the strongest corrective to causal compression.** It redistributes credit, blame, labor, relation, and standing without requiring complete transparency.
43. **Every public story distributes benefits and burdens.** Praise may create obligation as effectively as denunciation creates stigma.
44. **Recontextualization is public revision rather than historical erasure.** Later knowledge changes judgment without undoing earlier bodily and institutional consequences.
45. **Narrative pluralism is not relativism.** Competing accounts remain subject to source quality, causal adequacy, confidence, and revision.
46. **The X narrative problem is constitutional.** Society needs an account of catastrophic capacity that permits oversight without converting opacity into guilt, power into godhood, or risk into disposability.
