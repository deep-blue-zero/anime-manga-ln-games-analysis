## 4. Paired comparative result

The two specialists expose complementary models of inheritance.

### Yang / E-Soul — inheritance can be legitimate and later betrayed

Yang receives a role through practice, relational recognition, technical support, public co-authorship, grief, and contested succession. The role becomes genuinely his in an important sense. E19 proves that genuine inheritance does not eliminate future ethical failure.

### Qīng / Lucky Cyan — inheritance can burden without owning

Qīng receives survival, music, public meaning, institutional support, and other people's hopes. Her development does not require rejecting all inheritance. It requires distinguishing meanings that preserve her future authorship from meanings that claim jurisdiction over it.

### Joint proposition

> **The series does not oppose inherited identity to authentic identity. It asks whether what is inherited remains answerable. A bearer may sincerely inherit a role and later betray it; a survivor may carry other people's hopes without becoming their instrument. Legitimacy therefore resides in continuing relations of provenance, revision, contestability, and present conduct—not in purity of origin.**
