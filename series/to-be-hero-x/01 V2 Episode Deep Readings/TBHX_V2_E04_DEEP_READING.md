# To Be Hero X V2 — Episode 04 Deep Reading

## 1. Scope and analytical posture

This document reads Episode 4 **prospectively**, with the evidentiary horizon frozen to Episodes 1–4. Full-series memory, the previous V1 analysis, later hero arcs, and knowledge of the eventual ranking tournament are excluded until the dedicated retrospective revision section.

The incoming state from Episode 3 is deliberately narrow:

- Lin has privately moved from E01's `现在的我就是奈斯` ("the me I am now is Nice") to E03's `我其实不是奈斯` ("actually, I'm not Nice"). That is a genuine change in self-understanding, but it has not yet become a public identity claim.
- Trust can materially alter bodies, abilities, and behavioral possibilities according to a stabilized public concept. Xiao Yueqing's wormhole and Firm Man's inability to bend/crawl are the strongest cases so far.
- A genuine heroic act can be captured by public narrative and turned into an involuntary permanent role. Firm Man's situational promise not to fall for one child became civic doctrine and bodily compulsion.
- Intimate recognition can detect or resist identity substitution, but it has not yet been shown to mechanically defeat mass Trust.
- Public narrative and factual truth are not the same as ethical legitimacy. Mad Wolf's fairy tales preserved relational truth while using coercive means; God Eye's E03 exposé appeared factually threatening but had not yet been morally evaluated.
- Lin's top-ten placement at the end of E03 is real, but the goal was institutionally defined by Juān jiě rather than clearly spoken by Lin as the direction of his own heart.
- Episode 2 left the phrase `内心的方向`—the direction of one's heart—open. Episode 3 did not positively resolve it.

Episode 4 is therefore allowed to answer a very specific question:

> **Can Lin cease being Nice without ceasing to be a hero—and, in a world where Trust literally powers heroism, can a self-authored person survive the loss of the socially authored identity that made him powerful?**

The answer is more sophisticated than "truth makes him strong."

# 3. Prospective thesis

Episode 4 supplies the first complete ethical sequence of Lin Ling's hero origin.

The sequence is not:

> public discovers truth -> public forgives Lin -> Trust returns -> Lin becomes a real hero.

The actual order is more demanding:

1. **Lin rejects the Nice identity before he knows what positive identity will replace it.**
2. **The rejection costs him the body, status, recognition, and power that Nice supplied.**
3. **Lin publicly names himself as an ordinary person and admits not only coercion but his own complicity in enjoying the borrowed hero role.**
4. **He chooses to fight anyway, explicitly saying he does not need anyone to cheer for him.**
5. **Only after he continues acting without the guarantee of public approval does the public begin recognizing the earlier deeds as Lin's, chanting his name, and creating Trust around him.**
6. **That new Trust restores heroic power while he remains visibly Lin rather than returning to Nice.**

The episode therefore separates three things that a Trust-based world invites us to collapse:

- **moral legitimacy** — whether the action is worth doing;
- **personal authorship** — whether the actor owns the reason for doing it;
- **sociological legitimacy** — whether other people recognize him as a hero and invest Trust in him.

The decisive E04 claim is that these should occur in that order. Lin acts from self-authored commitment **before** sociological legitimacy returns.

This is why his line at source `00:16:29.620` matters:

> `我不需要谁为我叫好`
>
> "I don't need anyone to cheer for me."

Within a setting where cheering can literally make him stronger, this is not an anti-social declaration. It is the moment he refuses to make recognition the **reason** for action. The crowd's recognition then becomes amplification rather than authorship of the initial moral decision.

That gives E04 a provisional ethical ordering:

> **person -> chosen commitment -> action -> recognition -> Trust/power**

rather than:

> **brand -> public expectation -> role obligation -> compelled action**.

But Episode 4 does **not** solve the series' social-authorship problem. The moment Lin becomes legible as himself:

- strangers circulate his fight on screens and phones;
- other agencies immediately want to sign him;
- his Trust returns to Nice-level;
- his Rank 10 position survives;
- the formal title transition rewrites the Nice billboard into `普通人 / 林凌 / THE COMMONER`.

Lin escapes one socially authored identity and immediately becomes material for another.

The mature E04 prospective thesis is therefore:

> **Lin wins the right to be socially recognized only after acting as himself, but he does not escape social authorship. Episode 4 replaces involuntary substitution with a more ethical order in which self-authorship precedes public amplification—and then warns that even authentic selfhood can be rapidly branded, ranked, circulated, and captured again.**

# 4. Dramatic architecture

## 4.1 Exposure immediately damages Nice because Trust is epistemically fragile

Episode 4 opens with Xiao Yueqing's survival already becoming public crisis. News reports that she has "returned from the dead" and that Nice's Trust has consequently fallen; his newly achieved top-ten position is already unstable.

This is mechanically important. The public does not need definitive courtroom proof that Lin is not Nice. It needs enough narrative discontinuity for confidence to fracture. Trust is causally powerful precisely because it is epistemically vulnerable.

The Nice ecosystem responds defensively. Fans insist that God Eye's claims are a competitor's trap and repeat a crisis-management slogan: do not respond, handle it with silence. Juān jiě likewise proposes refusing to enter the frame of accusation: if Treeman does not acknowledge it, God Eye can be cast as the same malicious actor responsible for the earlier live disruption.

The series is now showing **information strategy as power defense** in literal terms. Silence is not merely PR advice. It is an attempt to protect the metaphysical resource that sustains Nice.

God Eye announces the opposite epistemology:

> `果然人都容易被主观臆断所蒙蔽`
>
> people are easily blinded/deceived by subjective assumptions;

and:

> `那么我就来成为大家的眼睛`
>
> then I will become everyone's eyes.

His self-conception is therefore not simply "villain who reveals secrets." He imagines himself as a corrective organ for a public that cannot see through its own desires.

This is important because E04 ultimately defeats not the importance of truth but **God Eye's theory of what truth proves**.

## 4.2 Juān jiě explicitly names Trust as a shackle

Lin tries to go to Xiao. He cannot.

Juān jiě tells him repeated attempts will end the same way because:

> `因为你的信赖者们不希望你去`
>
> because your believers do not want you to go.

The fan community does not want its newly top-ten Nice to risk the confrontation. The public image that created the body now constrains the body's choices.

Juān jiě makes the causal hierarchy explicit:

> `没有了他们你什么都不是`
>
> without them, you are nothing;

> `是他们创造了你`
>
> they created you;

> `是他们赋予了你完美的外貌和力量`
>
> they gave you the perfect appearance and power.

Then comes the episode's most severe institutional formulation:

> `这是成为英雄 / 成为奈斯 / 必须承受的枷锁`
>
> this is the shackle one must bear to become a hero—to become Nice.

`枷锁` is not a neutral "responsibility." It is a shackle/fetter. Juān jiě understands that Trust both constitutes and imprisons the hero. Her defense is not "you are actually free." Her defense is that captivity is the price of herohood.

That makes E04 a direct continuation of Firm Man rather than a separate identity story. E03 showed the body trapped by a public concept. E04 shows Treeman's manager explicitly acknowledging that model and calling it necessary.

The visual staging reinforces the point: Nice strains and crawls against a condition produced by believers who want him to remain safe and silent. Protective affection becomes coercive force.

The episode's political problem is therefore sharper than "evil corporation controls hero." The **believers themselves** participate in the control, even when their desire is protective rather than malicious.

## 4.3 Treeman's solution is memory erasure

Juān jiě reassures Lin that the company has sent help and, more importantly, a `善后专家`—a cleanup specialist.

The Chinese hardsub names the hero `忘仔`, which this corpus standardizes as **Wàngzǎi**. The paired Japanese witness uses `クラッシャーボーイ` (Crusher Boy). His famous `铁拳` is described as doing more than physical damage: it can destroy the opponent's memory.

Juān jiě immediately identifies the institutional use:

- God Eye and Xiao Yueqing are the only outsiders who know Lin's identity secret;
- if their memories are erased;
- no one will remain who knows he is Lin Ling.

She even frames this as potentially good for Xiao after everything she has experienced.

This is a major institutional escalation. Treeman's defense of the hero image is no longer merely:

- styling;
- training;
- scripted romance;
- denial;
- selective broadcasting.

It includes **altering the memories of inconvenient witnesses**.

The underlying logic is consistent:

> If the public identity is valuable enough, the continuity of the story can take precedence over the continuity of other people's memories.

This is a direct personhood violation. Xiao's recognition of Lin in E02 was ethically important precisely because she could distinguish person from role. The cleanup plan proposes deleting that recognition at the source.

## 4.4 God Eye attacks heroes by exposing discontinuity between image and person

God Eye finds Wàngzǎi's `破绽`—flaw/breach/opening—and exposes the prior fighter underneath the hero image: an unimpressive boxer who allegedly stuffed metal into his gloves to stand out.

The wording is designed to strip away every support:

> without the cheating device;
>
> without the persona/image enhancement;
>
> you are fundamentally only a showy empty shell.

As the evidence circulates, Juān jiě observes that believers will rapidly leave and that Wàngzǎi's power will be exhausted.

God Eye therefore has a coherent anti-hero strategy:

`find discontinuity -> publish discontinuity -> collapse belief -> collapse Trust -> collapse power`.

This is the mirror image of hero production:

`construct legible image -> circulate image -> accumulate belief -> accumulate Trust -> materialize power`.

God Eye is not outside the Trust economy. He is an expert in **reverse-engineering it**.

At this stage his critique contains real force. He is correct that a marketed hero persona can hide morally relevant facts. He is correct that Trust can sustain a false public image. He is correct that institutions have incentives to conceal damaging discontinuity.

What remains unproven is his stronger inference:

> a hero who contains contradiction, deception, weakness, or moral failure is therefore not truly heroic.

E04 will eventually break that inference by giving him a target who openly admits contradiction instead of pretending it is absent.

## 4.5 The Episode 1 manifesto returns as crisis propaganda

Treeman interrupts the hostile feed by broadcasting a hero advertisement. The wording returns almost exactly to the opening of Episode 1:

- `何为英雄` — what is a hero?
- hero as light in darkness;
- hero as savior in crisis;
- hero as the perfect figure in our hearts;
- in this era created by Trust Value;
- `人人开创英雄`;
- `人人也皆可为英雄`.

The goons explain that this is the newest promotional advertisement from Juān jiě's email.

The formal callback is exceptionally important. In E01, the manifesto was revealed to be Lin's advertising presentation. In E04, the same ideal language has become **institutional crisis filler**, deployed to cover a truth-threatening broadcast.

That could make the slogan seem entirely cynical.

The episode refuses that conclusion.

Later, when Lin is stripped of Nice, wounded, mocked, and without public support, he personally repeats:

> `人人皆可为英雄`
>
> everyone can become a hero.

The slogan is therefore neither simply true nor simply false. Its institutional form is advertising. Lin's later use tries to **recover the proposition from the advertisement** by making it costly and personal.

This is one of E04's strongest formal strategies:

> the series takes a commercial slogan, shows how institutions use it to conceal reality, then asks whether one ordinary person can make the same sentence ethically true through action.

## 4.6 Lin begins with negative self-authorship

Juān jiě calls him Nice.

Lin answers:

> `你叫谁呢`
>
> who are you calling?

and then:

> `我並不是奈斯`
>
> I am not Nice.

This is stronger than E03's private self-admission because it is directed at the person most invested in maintaining the substitution.

But E04 deliberately refuses to replace one neat identity statement with another. Lin continues:

> `我其实还不清楚自己到底要什么`
>
> I actually still don't know exactly what I want;

> `只不过 / 我很清楚自己不想要什么`
>
> but I know very clearly what I do **not** want.

This is psychologically and philosophically important. Lin's self-authorship begins as **refusal**, not complete self-knowledge.

He does not suddenly possess a finished heroic philosophy. He knows:

- he does not want to remain Nice;
- he did not originally want to become Nice;
- what he wanted was to become `真正的英雄`—a real/genuine hero.

That means the arc does not equate authenticity with "knowing your true self." A person can become more self-authored simply by establishing a boundary:

> **this role is not mine; I will not continue it.**

The positive content of Lin's heroism comes later, after he has already accepted the cost of that refusal.

## 4.7 Refusal has an ontological price

The fan chorus continues to demand silence: do not respond, ignore him.

Lin says that if becoming a hero means he cannot protect the person he wants to protect, then he would rather not be that hero. Firm Man's earlier recognition returns in memory:

> `不管是谁做你自己就好`
>
> whoever you are, be yourself.

Lin chooses to return to being an ordinary person.

The episode then makes the cost visible.

His Nice presentation collapses in stages:

- the pale/silver Nice hair returns toward Lin's dark hair;
- the coherent Nice costume/power presentation destabilizes;
- he emerges as the visually ordinary Lin in a dark shirt and light trousers;
- nearby people cease recognizing him as Nice;
- his strength falls with the departing Trust.

Lin himself remarks on how rapidly the power is falling; Juān jiě's side notes the volatility of cheap believers who arrive and leave quickly.

This is the point that prevents E04 from becoming sentimental authenticity rhetoric.

Lin's socially authored identity was **real in its consequences**. Rejecting it means losing:

- appearance;
- capability;
- legibility;
- rank security;
- institutional protection;
- the fan relationship that made the power possible.

The episode does not say "the real Lin was secretly stronger all along." He becomes weaker when he becomes himself.

That weakness is necessary to the moral structure of the climax.

## 4.8 The confession is not merely factual disclosure; it is an account of complicity

God Eye turns the public camera on Lin and demands an explanation.

Lin says the real Nice is dead. He then repeatedly gives the sentence that V1 remembered as the core of his arc:

> `我叫林凌`
>
> my name is Lin Ling;

> `是一个普通人`
>
> I am an ordinary person.

The repetition matters. The first three episodes repeatedly asked whether an unknown/ordinary person can become a hero. E04 does not solve that by revealing Lin was secretly exceptional. He claims `普通人` as part of his identity at the moment he has lost the exceptional body.

His confession then becomes unusually self-implicating.

He explains that:

- he was an ordinary office worker;
- he worked behind the scenes making ads and promotional videos for heroes;
- by accident/circumstance he became Nice;
- he inherited Nice's Trust Value;
- he worked to make himself look more like Nice;
- he **enjoyed the sense of accomplishment/pleasure of being a hero**;
- even after someone told him to recognize/face himself, he remained stubbornly attached;
- now he has abandoned the Nice identity.

This is not the speech of a protagonist saying:

> "I was kidnapped and forced into a lie; therefore none of this was mine."

Lin was coerced, but he also desired what the lie gave him. He enjoyed recognition, power, romantic proximity, heroic status, and achievement.

That admission matters ethically because it prevents self-authorship from becoming self-exoneration.

A stronger formulation is:

> **Lin reclaims personhood by taking responsibility for the parts of the false identity that he himself wanted.**

This is one reason his confession can support rather than destroy legitimacy. The public is not merely told "the company fooled you." It is shown a person who acknowledges that he too was seduced by the role.

## 4.9 God Eye confuses punishment with truth

God Eye rejects the confession as another performance or agency strategy. If Lin is admitting fraud, then, in God Eye's model, he should accept punishment rather than continue acting as a hero.

Lin asks him to release Xiao.

God Eye refuses because she is an accomplice and therefore also guilty.

This reveals the punitive structure beneath his epistemology. God Eye's objective is not simply:

> let facts become known.

It is:

> expose falsity -> assign guilt -> strip public legitimacy -> impose punishment.

The target is not only false propositions but **false people**—people whose public selves can be delegitimized by showing impurity underneath.

Lin's answer is not a defense of the lie. He has already surrendered it. His answer is that, even without Trust and even without being Nice, he still has to fight for Xiao.

The damaged OCR on cue 168 prevents exact quotation of one short Chinese phrase, but the Japanese witness and context are clear: his action is for Xiao Yueqing.

This separates the factual and moral questions:

- Was Lin really Nice? **No.**
- Did he participate in deceiving the public? **Yes.**
- Does that make protecting Xiao wrong? **No.**
- Does exposure of his fraud make every act performed while carrying the Nice identity morally unreal? **No.**

God Eye wants one revelation to settle all four questions. The episode refuses.

## 4.10 The public begins reattributing earlier acts from Nice to Lin

At first the spectators ridicule Lin:

- he is crazy;
- he has no real relationship to Xiao;
- he is acting like a hero without ability;
- someone should stop this.

Then someone makes a causal correction:

> if Nice was already dead, then the person who cried so desperately for Xiao at the wedding must have been **this man**.

This is one of the most important public-narrative moments in the episode.

The confession does not simply destroy the Nice story. It permits the audience to **reattribute genuine acts to their actual author**.

E01 contained the inverse mechanism: Lin's invisible advertising labor was interpreted as evidence that "Nice" knew Xiao intimately. The truth of Lin's labor strengthened the wrong identity.

E04 reverses that process. The truth of the substitution lets spectators recover:

- Lin's tears;
- Lin's attachment;
- Lin's willingness to risk himself;
- Lin's prior public behavior

from the Nice brand and assign them to Lin Ling.

This is not just "the public likes honesty." It is **authorship repair**.

## 4.11 God Eye's theory of the hero is perfectionist in a different way than Nice

God Eye concludes that the public has now seen what so-called heroes really are and that there are no true heroes in this era. He assigns responsibility not only to the Union and hero agencies but also to the people watching the screens.

This criticism is not trivial. E01–E03 already support much of it:

- audiences help construct roles;
- institutions manipulate them;
- hero powers can be built on false stories;
- rankings create incentives;
- public desire can imprison the hero.

Yet God Eye's conclusion repeats the structure he attacks.

Nice ideology says:

> a hero is the perfect person in our hearts.

God Eye says, effectively:

> if the perfection is false, the person is not a real hero.

Both systems make heroism dependent on **lack of contradiction**.

The difference is that Nice hides the flaw while God Eye exposes it. Neither initially knows what to do with a flawed person who acts heroically anyway.

That is why God Eye's flaw-detection visual language becomes so important during the fight. Lin is bruised, bleeding, ordinary, strategically inferior, and in some shots literally covered by "bug/flaw" detection overlays.

God Eye can see everything wrong with him.

What he cannot see is why those flaws fail to disqualify the act.

## 4.12 "I don't need anyone to cheer for me" is the ethical hinge

God Eye mocks Lin: who would cheer for a clown who throws amateur punches?

Lin answers:

> `我不需要谁为我叫好`
>
> I don't need anyone to cheer for me.

Then:

> `这个世界 / 人人皆可为英雄`
>
> in this world, everyone can become a hero.

The causal ordering is crucial. Lin says this **before** the crowd's support becomes the source of his renewed power.

If he had first discovered that the crowd liked "Lin Ling," the scene could still be read as exchanging one approval addiction for another.

Instead, he acts while expecting that he may remain weak, mocked, and socially illegible.

That makes his declaration a statement of **moral independence from Trust** without denying Trust's later causal power.

He is not saying:

> people do not matter.

He is saying:

> their approval cannot be the condition under which I decide whether this person should be protected.

This is the strongest distinction yet between **moral legitimacy** and **Trust Value**.

## 4.13 Lin finally states the positive direction of his heart

Episode 2 asked whether Lin had found `内心的方向` and interrupted his answer with the public announcement that Nice would pursue the top ten.

Episode 4 finally supplies Lin's own answer.

He says the moment he truly felt that he wanted to become a hero was:

- not when he became Nice;
- not when he entered the top ten;
- but when there was truly someone in his heart;
- someone he would protect at any cost.

The paired Japanese witness strengthens the life-staking force: someone he wants to protect even at the cost of his life.

The Mandarin `我心里真正有了一个人` is relationally intimate and readily supports romantic coding, especially given Xiao's established position in Lin's life. But the episode's explicit ethical grammar is broader than a formal love confession. The operative category is **a concrete person whose safety matters enough to reorganize Lin's action**.

This is a striking reversal of the public-image problem.

Public hero ideology asks Lin to protect **everyone's idea of Nice**.

Lin's self-authored heroism begins when he wants to protect **one actual person**.

That does not make universal heroism selfish. It means the universal proposition "everyone can become a hero" acquires moral content through particular relation rather than abstract image.

A provisional formulation is:

> **Lin's heroism becomes real when the object of protection stops being the hero image and becomes another person.**

## 4.14 Recognition returns to the ordinary body rather than resurrecting Nice

As Lin continues fighting, the first explicit encouragement arrives:

> `加油啊林凌`
>
> go, Lin Ling.

The name matters.

The crowd is not chanting Nice.

Other spectators call for him to stand. Phone screens and public displays circulate the fight. New observers ask who this hero is, and the answer is:

> `他没有名号就叫林凌`
>
> he has no hero name; he's just called Lin Ling;

and:

> `而且他也不是英雄 / 就是一个普通人`
>
> and he isn't even a hero; he's just an ordinary person.

At the same time, other agencies become interested in signing him.

Visually, the power returns **without restoring the Nice morphology**. Lin remains black-haired and visibly himself. His fist begins to glow; his action accelerates; the crowd chants `林凌`.

The series has therefore established a major mechanical fact:

> **Trust can attach directly to Lin Ling, an ordinary person without an established hero alias, and empower him as Lin Ling.**

This is not merely a psychological confidence boost. Juān jiě's side later explicitly reports that **Lin Ling's Trust Value has surged back to the same level as the former Nice**.

The identity-power mapping has changed.

E01:

`public believes "this person = Nice" -> Lin changes toward Nice`.

E04:

`public recognizes "this person = Lin Ling" -> Lin gains Trust while remaining Lin`.

That is the mechanical core of the episode.

## 4.15 God Eye loses because the existence of a flaw no longer settles the moral question

As Lin becomes stronger, God Eye cannot understand what is happening.

His internal question fragments:

> `真真相 / 到底是 / 什么`
>
> truth... what is it, in the end?

The wording matters because his defeat is epistemic before it is physical.

God Eye's method worked perfectly on Wàngzǎi:

- expose cheating;
- expose persona;
- public belief collapses;
- power collapses.

It fails on Lin because Lin has already **included the discrediting facts in his own self-description**.

There is no hidden perfect image left to puncture.

God Eye detects that Lin is "full of flaws." The public can see that too. Yet they begin trusting him anyway—not because the flaws disappeared, but because his action remains intelligible after the flaws are known.

This suggests a more robust form of Trust than the Nice system offered:

> **Trust based on acknowledged imperfection is less dependent on maintaining a contradiction-free image.**

That is an interpretation, not yet a universal mechanic. E04 proves the existence of such Trust in this local case; it does not prove it is always more stable.

But conceptually it is a major development. The episode proposes that **legibility through truth can survive imperfection**, whereas branding through perfection must continually suppress contradiction.

## 4.16 Trust returns after—not before—moral action

Near the climax, the public explicitly reinterprets Lin:

- he is willing to die protecting Xiao;
- the person at the wedding really was him.

Juān jiě's side then reports:

> `林凌的信赖值竟然飙升回到和之前奈斯一样的水位了`
>
> Lin Ling's Trust Value has surged back to the same level as the previous Nice.

This line is indispensable because it prevents us from treating the climax as purely symbolic.

The new Trust is mechanically real.

Yet the episode has carefully arranged its sequence so that Trust is **responsive** to heroism rather than its initial author.

The public did not tell Lin whom to protect. Indeed, the public image initially prevented him from going.

The public did not grant permission for him to fight as Lin. It initially mocked him.

The public did not give him his name. He supplied it.

Only then does recognition become power.

The most defensible E04 formulation is therefore:

> **Social authorship remains unavoidable, but it becomes less dehumanizing when recognition responds to a person's self-authored action instead of dictating the action the person must perform.**

## 4.17 "I finally found the direction of my heart"

When Lin reaches the apparent Xiao hostage, he apologizes for being late and says:

> `但我终于找到了内心的方向`
>
> but I finally found the direction of my heart.

This explicitly closes the question E02 left open.

The answer is not:

- top ten;
- being Nice;
- having the perfect body;
- satisfying believers;
- being publicly called a hero.

It is the self-authored willingness to protect a concrete person even if hero status is lost.

The episode is careful, however, not to give him an abstract complete life plan. Earlier he said he still did not know exactly what he wanted. `内心的方向` is therefore better understood as **orientation**, not comprehensive destiny.

He knows the direction in which he wants to move, even if the full future remains unwritten.

## 4.18 God Eye's hostage is fake: truth can be pursued through deception

The hostage dissolves.

God Eye admits that he never knew where Xiao was. The entire hostage situation was a trap designed to lure Lin into revealing the truth.

Lin immediately attacks the contradiction:

> you're a fraud-buster who uses a fake;
>
> have you no shame?

God Eye answers:

> `彼此彼此`
>
> same to you / we're even.

This is the episode's final blow against a simple truth/lie binary.

God Eye's central factual accusation was substantially correct. The Nice identity was a substitution. Treeman was hiding it. Lin had deceived the public.

But God Eye himself:

- uses a false hostage;
- threatens a murder he cannot perform as described;
- manipulates audience fear;
- instrumentalizes Xiao's image without knowing where she is;
- uses truth exposure as coercive punishment.

The episode therefore asks a harder ethical question:

> **What is truth for?**

Lin's confession is self-authored disclosure that assumes responsibility and frees him from an identity he no longer accepts.

God Eye's truth campaign is externally authored exposure designed to strip legitimacy and compel submission.

Both reveal facts. They are not ethically equivalent.

This is the strongest E04 correction to the V1 shorthand "truth versus lie":

> **factual truth is necessary but not sufficient; authorship, purpose, coercion, consequence, and respect for persons remain morally relevant.**

# 5. Trust mechanics: what Episode 4 newly establishes

Episode 4 provides the clearest mechanical experiment yet because Lin passes through two distinct identity states within one continuous public crisis.

## 5.1 Trust can constrain action according to what believers want

E03 established that Firm Man's body has difficulty violating the "never fall" concept. E04 strengthens the general model: Nice cannot easily leave because his believers do not want him to confront God Eye.

The constraint is not merely anatomical. It is **teleological/behavioral**: the public's desire about what the hero should do becomes resistance against action.

**Status:** `F-MECH`, high confidence.

## 5.2 Trust withdrawal reverses the Nice body/power state

When Lin rejects Nice and leaves the fan-script, his appearance and power regress toward ordinary Lin.

**Status:** `F-VIS + F-MECH`, high confidence.

This strengthens the reversibility model suggested by Xiao and Firm Man.

## 5.3 Trust can transfer to a person under a different identity without restoring the old image

The crowd begins trusting `林凌`. Lin's power returns while his appearance remains Lin.

**Status:** `F-MECH`, high confidence.

This proves the mechanical system is not inherently tied to inherited hero aliases.

## 5.4 Hero status/title is not required before Trust begins accumulating

An observer explicitly asks what hero Lin is. The answer is that he has no hero name and is not even formally a hero—just an ordinary person. Yet public encouragement and power are already increasing.

**Status:** high locally.

The institutional category "hero" therefore appears to follow rather than necessarily precede the initial social process that creates hero power.

## 5.5 Public attention can reassign Trust based on reinterpretation of past acts

Spectators reinterpret the wedding scene once they know the original Nice was already dead. A formerly Nice-attributed emotional act becomes evidence about Lin.

This suggests Trust is not only generated by new acts. **Historical narrative reassignment can alter the present social identity to which past evidence attaches.**

**Status:** `INF` supported by explicit dialogue and immediate Trust change; medium-high.

## 5.6 Exposure is mechanically anti-heroic only when it destroys the belief structure

God Eye's method works on Wàngzǎi because the exposed material destroys the public basis for his persona. It fails on Lin because Lin does not defend a contradiction-free persona. Exposure no longer removes the premise of Trust; the acknowledged imperfection becomes part of what people are trusting.

This does **not** prove "truth gives power." It proves:

> **Trust can survive known imperfection when the public still finds the person/action worthy of belief.**

That is a more defensible rule.

# 6. Lin Ling: identity, agency, and ordinary heroism

## 6.1 The identity progression is now four-stage

The V2 record must preserve all four states rather than flattening the arc into a single confession:

**E01** — `现在的我就是奈斯`

> The present me is Nice.

This was a meaningful E01 endpoint: Lin had begun acting authentically from within the imposed role and chose to inhabit it.

**E02** — Xiao says `你并不是奈斯` and asks for/uses Lin's real name.

> Private recognition distinguishes person from role.

**E03** — Lin says `我其实不是奈斯` privately.

> He begins internally rejecting total identification.

**E04** — `我並不是奈斯`; then public `我叫林凌 / 是一个普通人`.

> He rejects the role to its manager, accepts the loss of power, publicly names himself, and builds a hero trajectory under his own identity.

This progression matters because it shows selfhood emerging relationally and temporally. E04 is not evidence that E01 "really meant" the opposite. Lin **changed**.

## 6.2 Ordinary is not mediocrity; it is anti-substitution

`普通人` carries special weight because E01 repeatedly marked Lin as:

- ordinary;
- nameless/unknown (`无名之辈`);
- socially unimportant.

By E04, Lin voluntarily names himself an ordinary person on the same public screens that once displayed the perfect Nice image.

The category has therefore changed function.

E01 "ordinary" is an insult used to justify his disposability.

E04 "ordinary" is a claim of identity:

> I do not need to be secretly Nice in order for my action to count.

The final formal card's `THE COMMONER` language reinforces this transformation, though it should be treated as formal/title construction rather than automatically as a registered in-world hero alias.

## 6.3 He does not become authentic by becoming pure

Lin's confession is morally stronger because he admits enjoying the role.

The episode understands that false identities can satisfy real desires:

- recognition;
- power;
- access;
- achievement;
- romantic proximity;
- release from ordinary invisibility.

A less rigorous story could preserve Lin's goodness by saying he never wanted any of it.

E04 instead lets him say, in effect:

> I was pushed into this, and then I liked parts of it enough to cling to it.

His dignity claim therefore includes responsibility.

## 6.4 His positive heroism is relational rather than rank-oriented

Lin's positive direction is a person, not an institutional benchmark.

He says he did not truly want to become a hero when he became Nice or when he reached the top ten. The decisive moment was having someone he wanted to protect at any cost.

The contrast with Juān jiě is exact:

- Juān jiě's direction: top ten, brand preservation, stable believers.
- Lin's direction: protect Xiao even if hero identity and Trust disappear.

The episode does not condemn rank as inherently meaningless. Lin remains Rank 10 afterward. It establishes that **rank cannot generate the moral reason for heroism**.

# 7. Xiao Yueqing: person, object of protection, and restored agency

Episode 4 risks turning Xiao into a passive object in Lin's hero awakening. The fake-hostage device makes that risk explicit: much of the rooftop battle is organized around an image of Xiao who is not even physically present.

The episode partially corrects this in the epilogue by giving Xiao her own narration.

She identifies herself:

> `我叫潇月卿`

and describes the consequence of finally choosing where her heart most wanted to go: an isolated island where no one knows her and no one can disturb her.

The joke is that freedom taken to its literal endpoint becomes excessive isolation. Worse, she departed without the spatial-transfer launcher/device needed to leave.

Lin arrives with the forgotten object and returns it.

This is a small but important relational gesture.

E02's central problem was that public belief had turned Xiao's freedom-oriented wormhole into a Nice-only tether. E04 ends with Lin returning the material mechanism of her movement rather than demanding that she remain attached to him.

The sequence therefore supports a provisional relationship principle:

> **Lin's protective desire is healthiest when it preserves Xiao's ability to move rather than converting protection into possession.**

The counterpressure must remain visible. Lin has just framed his heroism around protecting one person at any cost. That could itself become another script placed upon Xiao if "the person I protect" becomes her permanent relational role.

Episode 4 does not yet resolve that danger. The island epilogue merely gives us encouraging evidence that the relationship can include restoration of her autonomy rather than only rescue.

Her final complaint that she never wants such a long vacation again also punctures romantic grandiosity. The two return to banter after a climax that had threatened to turn Xiao into an abstract reason for male heroism.

# 8. Juān jiě: the manager who knows the cage is a cage

Episode 4 makes Juān jiě more ethically serious and more dangerous because she is not naïve about the system.

She knows:

- believers create the hero;
- believers grant beauty and strength;
- believers can physically restrain the hero;
- becoming Nice entails shackles;
- witnesses can be memory-erased to preserve the identity;
- media silence and replacement broadcasts can protect Trust;
- ranking and attention can be rapidly redirected.

Her ideology is therefore not "the hero is free." It is closer to:

> **herohood is a bargain in which the person receives power/status by accepting the obligations required by the image.**

Lin rejects the bargain because it was never entered under meaningful informed consent and because the obligation now prevents him from protecting someone.

Yet Juān jiě is not simply removed from the story when Lin rejects Nice.

During the fight she joins the calls for Lin to stand. Afterward, the system around her immediately tracks his new Trust and observes that public attention is now focused on Lin.

The important institutional point is not whether she personally experiences moral growth in this episode. It is that the apparatus is **adaptive**.

Treeman can survive the death of Nice the person.

It can survive replacement by Lin-as-Nice.

And E04 suggests it may be able to survive Lin rejecting Nice by converting Lin's authenticity into a new usable public asset.

This is why the ending cannot be read as "truth defeats PR."

PR is already learning how to sell the truth.

# 9. God Eye: truth without personhood

## 9.1 His diagnosis is substantially correct

A fair reading must grant God Eye his strongest points.

He correctly identifies:

- hero images conceal contradictions;
- agencies manipulate public belief;
- Trust can sustain false legitimacy;
- the public often sees what it wants to see;
- exposure can reveal real institutional deception;
- viewers participate in maintaining the system.

The series does not refute these claims by revealing him to be simply wrong about Nice.

## 9.2 His error is converting exposure into total judgment

God Eye's repeated structure is:

> find flaw -> reveal flaw -> declare image false -> declare person unworthy.

That works only if moral legitimacy requires a contradiction-free public self.

Lin breaks the model by saying:

> yes, I lied;
>
> yes, I enjoyed it;
>
> yes, I am ordinary;
>
> yes, I have lost the power;
>
> I am still going to protect her.

The admission does not erase wrongdoing. It prevents wrongdoing from becoming an exhaustive definition of personhood.

## 9.3 His visual epistemology sees bugs, not persons

God Eye's visor/UI repeatedly identifies `破绽` and "bugs." This is a powerful visual metaphor because it turns human beings into systems whose deviations can be detected and disqualifying defects isolated.

Lin becomes the impossible object for that gaze. He is visibly full of flaws; God Eye says as much.

The system returns the right diagnosis and the wrong conclusion.

The body is imperfect.

The action can still be heroic.

## 9.4 His fake hostage reveals an instrumental theory of truth

God Eye knowingly constructs a false Xiao hostage because it is useful for extracting the true Nice secret.

This does not make his factual accusations false. It reveals that his ethic is not "never deceive."

His ethic is closer to:

> deception is permissible if it exposes a deeper deception I judge more important.

That is an ends-oriented information ethic, and it carries the same danger he attributes to agencies: other people become instruments in a narrative operation.

Xiao's image is used without her presence or consent. Lin is coerced. The public is manipulated through a threatened murder.

God Eye therefore reproduces the dehumanizing logic of the hero system while claiming to purify it.

## 9.5 His final question matters

`真相到底是什么`—what, ultimately, is truth?—should not be read as the series becoming relativist.

The episode has been unusually clear about several facts:

- Nice is dead;
- Lin is Lin;
- Treeman deceived the public;
- God Eye used a fake hostage.

The destabilized category is not factual reality. It is God Eye's assumption that **factual exposure automatically determines moral worth**.

E04's answer is not "truth is subjective."

It is:

> **facts are real, but facts about imperfection do not by themselves answer what a person is for, whether an action is worth doing, or whether recognition is deserved.**

# 10. Public narrative, private event, and media circulation

Episode 4 is among the strongest episodes so far for the project's public/private ledger because the same event passes through several narrative states in minutes.

## 10.1 Public state A — Nice under attack

Xiao's survival becomes evidence that the Nice story is unstable. Fans interpret the accusation as hostile competition. Treeman promotes non-response.

## 10.2 Public state B — perfect heroes are frauds

God Eye's Wàngzǎi exposure supplies the model that all hero identities are manufactured shells. His rhetoric tries to generalize from individual exposé to universal delegitimation.

## 10.3 Public state C — Lin is an ordinary fraud

Lin's confession initially produces ridicule and anger. The public now knows the Nice image was false but has not yet developed a positive Lin narrative.

## 10.4 Public state D — past deeds are reauthored

Spectators realize that the wedding tears and Xiao-directed actions belonged to Lin. The historical record changes without the physical past changing.

## 10.5 Public state E — Lin becomes the focus

The fight circulates. People chant his name. Other agencies scout him. The media apparatus that once constructed Nice begins constructing **Lin Ling**.

This is why E04 cannot be summarized as the triumph of private self over public image.

Lin's self is legible to the world only through the same media infrastructure that previously concealed it.

The more precise problem is:

> **Can mediation transmit a person without immediately reducing the person to a new image?**

E04 provides a partial yes—because the public image now includes the disclosed contradiction—but leaves the long-term answer open.

# 11. Visual and formal analysis

## 11.1 City as screen environment

The episode opens in a city saturated by screens carrying the Xiao/Nice crisis. The environment itself appears to think through media surfaces.

This continues E01's opening logic: before we meet heroism as unmediated reality, we meet it as image, advertisement, broadcast, and public interpretation.

## 11.2 Nice physically trapped by belief

The scenes in Treeman space make an abstract mechanic bodily legible. Nice strains, crawls, and fights against an invisible/social constraint while the believer message insists he should remain silent and safe.

The hero body is therefore staged not as sovereign power but as a body **held in place by care**.

That is one of the series' more disturbing inversions: protective fandom can become coercive without becoming hateful.

## 11.3 The E01 advertisement as recursive screen object

The return of `何为英雄` is not a simple recap. It is visibly a media object used to displace another media object.

The series turns its own opening manifesto into a diegetic artifact and then has Lin later reclaim one of its sentences.

Formally:

`opening thesis -> revealed advertisement -> institutional crisis filler -> personal ethical declaration`.

That is a strong example of meaning changing through reuse rather than through new wording.

## 11.4 Nice drains out of Lin before Lin is publicly recognized

The transformation back to black-haired Lin is one of E04's most important visual sequences because it refuses an easy heroic upgrade.

The visual movement is downward in conventional superhero terms:

- less polished;
- less luminous;
- less iconic;
- less recognizable;
- less powerful.

The narrative movement is upward in agency.

The series therefore visually separates **image grandeur** from **personhood**.

## 11.5 Confession remains mediated

Lin's self-authorship does not occur in a private room. It appears on giant screens and livestream surfaces.

This complicates the standard "authentic self versus media" reading. Lin must use mediation to reclaim authorship from mediation.

The difference is not presence versus absence of media. It is **who supplies the identity claim and whether contradiction is allowed to remain visible**.

## 11.6 God Eye's interface and the aesthetics of defect

During the climactic fight, God Eye's perceptual interface marks flaws/bugs while Lin's body becomes increasingly battered.

The episode visually refuses the perfect hero aesthetic at the exact moment the public begins trusting him.

This is the inverse of E01, where increased Trust made Lin look more like the perfect Nice.

E04 makes increased Trust coexist with visible imperfection.

## 11.7 Stylized fight transition and collective attention

The fight shifts through saturated red/cyan passages, high-contrast ink/sketch forms, phone/social-media surfaces, and public reaction shots. These formal changes make the conflict increasingly less about two private bodies and more about **a social act being witnessed into meaning**.

Because direct audio audition is unavailable, this document does not make precise claims about the accompanying song's instrumentation or vocal performance. Historical V1 memory identifies a major reprise, but that belongs to the retrospective/audio verification layer, not the prospective visual fact.

## 11.8 The rank-10 billboard is rewritten, not simply destroyed

The aftermath contains a particularly precise formal transition.

A hero/ranking advertisement associated with Nice and Rank 10 is visually rewritten around Lin. The resulting formal card prominently includes:

- `普通人`;
- `#04 林凌`;
- `THE COMMONER`;
- continuing Rank 10 visual language.

This should **not** automatically be recorded as proof that "The Commoner" is Lin's formally registered diegetic hero name. The `#04` and presentation strongly function as episode/title graphics.

But as formal argument it is unmistakable:

> **the social position survives while the person occupying its image is reauthored.**

Nice is not merely unmasked. The slot is visually rewritten.

## 11.9 Island epilogue: from media saturation to ordinary relational space

After the rooftop crisis, the episode moves to warm natural island imagery and Xiao's first-person narration.

The contrast matters:

- city = screens, rankings, public truth, mass attention;
- island = one person, one mistaken wish, one forgotten object, one relationship.

Lin arrives in a new black-and-white presentation distinct from both the old Nice costume and the plain black-shirt fight state. This strongly suggests an emerging self-authored hero image, but E04 does not yet give enough evidence to define its institutional status or name.

He returns Xiao's spatial-transfer device.

The image is quietly apt: after an episode about who controls identity, Lin ends by handing another person back the means to control where she goes.

# 12. Chinese-language and naming observations

## 12.1 `枷锁` — shackle/fetter

Juān jiě's `必须承受的枷锁` is more severe than a translation such as "burden" or "responsibility." `枷锁` evokes physical restraint and loss of liberty. It should remain a key term in the institutional/personhood synthesis.

## 12.2 `破绽` — flaw/opening/breach

God Eye's `找到了 / 破绽` has both forensic and combat resonance. A `破绽` can be a flaw in a story, weakness in a defense, or opening to exploit. His epistemology is therefore immediately tactical: truth is something found **in order to attack the structure that depends on concealment**.

## 12.3 `人设` — persona/constructed image

In `没了作弊道具没了人设加持`, `人设` is the constructed persona/image/profile that props Wàngzǎi up. This is unusually direct meta-language for the series' concern with hero-image production.

## 12.4 `我並不是奈斯` and `我其实不是奈斯`

E04 contains both a direct present-tense rejection to Juān jiě and the repeated formulation familiar from E03. The distinction is less semantic than pragmatic: E04's line is a confrontation with the manager who insists he **is** Nice.

## 12.5 `普通人`

Lin's self-description `是一个普通人` should remain distinct from `无名之辈`. `普通人` is ordinary person/common person; `无名之辈` adds social namelessness/nonentity. E04 turns ordinary status into a positive identity category without erasing E01's earlier "unknown person" motif.

## 12.6 `我心里真正有了一个人`

Literally, Lin says that there truly came to be "a person in my heart." This is relationally intimate Mandarin and supports a stronger emotional reading than a flat "someone I wanted to protect." The subsequent line makes protection the explicit action. The corpus should preserve both:

- intimate inward relation;
- outward protective commitment.

## 12.7 `内心的方向`

This phrase returns as the payoff to E02. It is better rendered conceptually as the direction/orientation of the heart or inner direction than as a fixed career goal. Lin still does not claim to know his entire future.

## 12.8 `忘仔` — Wàngzǎi

The visible Chinese hardsub identifies the cleanup hero as `忘仔`. V2 standardization should use **Wàngzǎi (忘仔)** on first occurrence. The Japanese reference's `クラッシャーボーイ` and prior English-layer "Blankster" can be recorded as localization witnesses, not primary naming authority.

# 13. Adversarial readings and counterpressure

## 13.1 Counter-reading: the public merely rewards a better brand

A cynical reading can argue that E04 does not free Lin at all. He simply trades Nice for the marketable "ordinary hero" narrative.

Evidence for this reading is strong:

- public circulation creates his rebound;
- agencies immediately want to sign him;
- Juān jiě tracks the new Trust;
- Rank 10 persists;
- the formal card brands him `THE COMMONER`.

This counter-reading should remain active.

What prevents it from exhausting the episode is the sequence: Lin rejects Nice and continues fighting **before** he knows the ordinary-person narrative will become successful. The later branding may recapture the act, but it did not author the initial decision.

**Disposition:** serious counterpressure; does not overturn the self-authorship thesis.

## 13.2 Counter-reading: "protecting Xiao" simply replaces one imposed identity with romantic obsession

Lin's self-definition becomes strongly organized around one person. It is possible that he has not discovered independent hero ethics so much as replaced public dependence with private dependence.

The episode itself offers partial defense:

- he names the value as protection rather than possession;
- the island epilogue restores Xiao's means of movement;
- Xiao retains her own narration and choices.

But the danger remains. Later episodes must test whether Lin can respect Xiao's autonomy if her chosen direction conflicts with his protective desire.

**Disposition:** unresolved and important.

## 13.3 Counter-reading: God Eye is simply correct and Lin's popularity proves the public is fickle

God Eye can plausibly say the public has not become wiser; it has merely discovered a new emotionally attractive story. Indeed, the speed of the Trust rebound supports public volatility.

E04 does not prove mass opinion has become epistemically reliable.

Its stronger claim is narrower:

> a volatile public can nevertheless recognize a morally meaningful act after learning the actor's flaws.

The episode's defense of Lin is therefore not a defense of crowds as trustworthy judges in general.

## 13.4 Counter-reading: confession absolves Lin too easily

Lin participated in a major public deception and enjoyed its benefits. The episode's fast public rebound could be criticized for underweighting harms caused by the fraud.

That criticism is legitimate. E04 does not stage legal accountability, restitution, or a sustained public debate over deception.

What the episode does accomplish is psychological/moral rather than juridical: Lin stops denying authorship of his own choices and gives up the identity even when doing so costs him power.

**Interpretive limit:** E04 establishes moral growth; it does not establish that Lin has discharged every obligation created by the deception.

## 13.5 Counter-reading: "everyone can be a hero" remains an advertisement

The slogan is still media language. Lin himself came from advertising. His climactic use may simply be another effective public performance.

The episode intentionally keeps that possibility alive by showing the speech on screens and immediately generating Trust.

The difference, again, is cost. Lin repeats the slogan when it no longer guarantees benefit and while visibly losing. That makes it more than advertising without making it outside performance.

A useful formulation is:

> **E04 does not oppose performance to sincerity. It asks whether the performance is answerable to a chosen commitment and whether the performer can admit the gap between image and person.**

# 15. Episode 4 revisions to the incoming E01–E03 model

## 15.1 Revision: self-authorship can precede complete self-knowledge

E03 suggested Lin was moving toward reclaiming identity. E04 demonstrates that he does not need a fully defined future to refuse the wrong identity.

**Revision:** from "find true self" to **establish a boundary, accept cost, then discover direction through action**.

## 15.2 Revision: intimate recognition was necessary but not sufficient

Xiao naming Lin in E02 mattered. Firm Man telling him to be himself in E03 mattered. But E04 shows that private recognition does not complete the arc until Lin **publicly authors the claim himself**.

**Revision:** recognition by others can scaffold self-authorship; it cannot substitute for the person's own disclosure/choice.

## 15.3 Revision: Trust is not inherently dehumanizing

E01–E03 emphasized Trust as substitution, relational constraint, and bodily captivity. E04 provides the first strong counterexample: Trust attaches to Lin after his self-authored action and empowers him without visibly rewriting him into another person.

**Revision:** the problem is not Trust as such. The problem is the ordering and content of social authorship.

## 15.4 Revision: truthful exposure is not automatically emancipatory

God Eye reveals real facts but uses coercion, fraud, and a totalizing theory of moral worth.

**Revision:** distinguish factual accuracy from ethical disclosure.

## 15.5 Revision: the public can correct attribution without becoming infallible

The crowd reassigns wedding behavior from Nice to Lin. That is epistemically productive. It happens through the same volatile attention system that previously sustained the false identity.

**Revision:** mass interpretation is neither simply delusion nor simply wisdom; it is a causal field capable of both misattribution and correction.

# 18. Final prospective conclusion

Episode 4 completes the opening Nice arc's first major personhood argument without actually solving the world that created the problem.

Lin begins the series as an invisible advertising worker whose ordinary body can be replaced because the public values the image more than the carrier. He becomes Nice because other people believe he is Nice. He then begins acting genuinely inside that false identity, which makes the false identity harder to abandon. Private recognition from Xiao and Firm Man teaches him that the person and role can be separated, but it is Episode 4 that forces the separation to become costly and public.

The decisive movement is not confession alone.

It is:

> **refusal -> loss -> self-naming -> responsibility -> action without approval -> recognition -> new Trust.**

That sequence gives the series its clearest answer so far to the problem of social authorship.

A human being in this world cannot simply withdraw from public belief. Belief changes bodies, creates power, distributes rank, and makes heroism socially legible. Lin's victory therefore cannot be radical independence from others.

Instead, Episode 4 proposes a more modest and more defensible freedom:

> **the person should get to speak and act before society decides what the person means.**

When that order is reversed, Nice happens: society supplies the identity and the person is forced to inhabit it.

When the order is restored, Lin can say "I am an ordinary person," act for a reason he owns, and then allow recognition to amplify what he has already chosen.

The public still authors something. The institution still ranks something. The market still sees an asset. The screens still turn the person into an image.

So the danger remains.

But the difference is ethically decisive:

> **Lin is no longer powerful because everyone agrees he is somebody else. He becomes powerful after everyone sees that he is Lin Ling—and after he has already decided what he will do even if they never approve.**
