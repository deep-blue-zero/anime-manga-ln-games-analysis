# 1. Executive thesis — sincerity becomes infrastructure

Episode 21 appears at first to be a ranking-tournament setup episode centered on the veteran hero Smile and the new threat Liang Long. Its deeper structure is much more coherent and more unsettling.

The episode asks what happens when a feeling stops being merely a feeling and becomes a **social, economic, institutional, and mechanical dependency**.

Smile teaches Original Nice that a real smile begins inside the person. It is an overflow of genuine energy that moves the facial muscles and can make other people share the same emotion. He rejects the `商业化` / “business” smile and tells Nice to think of something that truly makes him happy before smiling.

That advice is sincere.

But Smile's own life reveals what happens after sincere affect is industrialized by herohood:

- he has spent decades smiling as a public hero;
- X says he has been doing it so long that it must be exhausting;
- Smile says he has forgotten what “tired” feels like;
- his wife once reproached the asymmetry between the smiles he gives other children and the daughter who has **no smiling photograph**;
- Liang Long discovers that breaking Smile's emotional capacity to smile weakens him;
- Smile later states directly that **only while maintaining a smile can his power be fully awakened**.

The episode therefore refuses the easy opposition “authentic smile good / fake smile bad.” Its harder proposition is:

> **A feeling can be authentic and still become compulsory labor once institutions, audiences, identity, and power depend on its continuous performance.**

Original Nice experiences the same extraction from another direction. As a child, he genuinely admired Smile and wanted a similarly radiant, contagious smile. That desire belongs to him. E20 established that Shang De used Fear to engineer Nice's “perfect” public persona, but E21 makes the crucial correction: **the system did not invent Nice's heroic aspiration; it colonized it.** Shang De dismisses Nice's newly natural smile as `廉价的笑容` — a cheap smile — and says he did not raise Nice to make him a hero. He wants `一把能复仇的武器`, a weapon for revenge; the Japanese witness intensifies the instrumental vocabulary with `復讐のマシン`, a revenge machine.

X supplies a third model. In private he is a tired black-haired man in glasses and sleepwear. When Smile asks him to snap his fingers and `换个身份` — change identity — X refuses because he does not work overtime: `绝不加班`. He wants to return to ordinary life and tells whoever wants to be a hero to take the job. E20's visual inference that X voluntarily moves between civilian and public-X presentations therefore becomes direct dialogue-supported evidence in E21.

Yet the Association is terrified of him. Its official explanation says that when Trust Value crosses a threshold, an enormous number of people have become a person's `信徒` — believers — and that person may become a god. The current X must not be allowed to “reach the top” a fourth time.

This creates the episode's central political paradox:

> **Godhood is relational before it is volitional. A bearer may want less power, less herohood, and more ordinary life while collective belief continues to make that bearer structurally dangerous.**

Finally Liang Long represents the opposite desire. He is offered cultivation into a god and asks whether becoming Zero would let him “clean up” all the `垃圾` — trash — that offends him. Where X resists role capture, Smile has lived inside it for decades, and Nice has his aspiration captured by another man, Liang Long desires total power as permission to purify the world of unwanted people.

The strongest E21 thesis is therefore:

> **Episode 21 is about affect becoming infrastructure. A smile becomes pedagogy, brand, labor, family wound, psychological attack surface, and combat condition; Trust becomes belief, mass belief becomes religion, and religion threatens godhood. The episode's tragedy is that hero society cannot use emotion as power without also creating institutions that manufacture it, people who weaponize it, and bearers whose public identity can escape their own wishes.**

A secondary formulation is:

> **The episode stages four relations to heroic identity: X tries to clock out of it; Smile has internalized it so deeply that his body needs it; Original Nice genuinely wants it but is told he exists only as a weapon; Liang Long wants its absolute form because he wants authority to erase others.**

# 2. Episode architecture

E21 is unusually disciplined in how it builds that argument.

## Movement I — accidental hero image and elite recruitment

The cold open first presents Liang Long through strangers' phones. Winter swimmers notice him in a frozen lake, decide he must be desperate for attention, film him for FOMO, and jokingly invent `冬泳英雄` — “winter-swimming hero” / JP `ヒーロー アイススイマー`.

Before any institution recognizes him, platform spectators have already turned him into hero-content.

The interaction becomes violent. Liang Long knocks away a phone, refuses the demanded apology, calls the men `垃圾`, and endures a prolonged group beating. Even the attackers become disturbed by how hard he is to hurt: his body is compared with stone, and greater violence seems only to make him more defiant.

Yan Mo approaches after the beating and offers a different kind of recognition:

`我在寻找一个人选 / 一个可以被培育成为神的人`

> “I am looking for a candidate — someone who can be cultivated into a god.”

Liang Long's answer reveals why he is usable:

`成为了Zero以后 / 我是不是就能清理掉所有碍眼的垃圾`

> “After becoming Zero, can I clean up all the trash that gets in my way?”

Yan Mo asks his name. `梁龙`. He promises to make everyone remember it.

The cold open thus gives Liang Long two paths to public identity:

1. **bottom-up virality** — strangers invent a joke hero name for platform content;
2. **top-down myth engineering** — Yan Mo promises to make Liang Long's real name universally remembered by cultivating him toward godhood.

Both transform a person into public sign. Liang Long prefers the second because it promises not merely attention but authority.

## Movement II — ranking strategy as assassination strategy

After the opening title sequence, the story returns to the post-ruins present. Liang Long tells Yan Mo that their previous plan has been disrupted:

- he was supposed to replace Ghostblade;
- Ghostblade's rank rose rather than fell after the ruins battle;
- Luo Li and Little Johnny entered the top ten after the high-profile incident.

Yan Mo does not regard the newcomers' visibility as an invitation to strike. He regards it as a shield.

Luo Li and Little Johnny are too hot in public attention. Queen and Lucky Cyan attract crowds. X is difficult even to locate. The Japanese witness identifies another top-ten figure, `ミスター・スタンド`, as an Association symbol whose removal would mean making the Association an enemy; the Mandarin OCR drops the stable name here, so that identity should remain crosswalk-open rather than being forcibly reconciled.

Yan Mo therefore chooses the veteran **Smile**: a hero who has occupied the top ten for decades, whose position is deeply accepted, but whom the public no longer actively discusses.

The distinction is important.

E19 established that public visibility can become armor because assassination becomes politically expensive. E21 sharpens that into a **visibility gradient**:

- intense current attention can shield a person physically/politically;
- institutional symbolism can shield a person through organizational retaliation;
- secrecy can shield a person by making location inaccessible;
- deeply rooted but low-salience reputation can leave the image durable while the body becomes covertly reachable.

Yan Mo says shallow negative publicity cannot move Smile. The image is too entrenched. Therefore they need `最致命的武器` — the most decisive/lethal weapon.

That weapon will not be an argument about Smile's public record.

It will be his daughter.

## Movement III — Smile teaches Nice how to smile

The episode then cuts to Original Nice during a commercial/publicity photoshoot. This temporal placement is critical: E20's ruins incident has already happened, but E1's Original-Nice succession has not yet occurred. Original Nice survived the ruins and is back at public work while Shang De says the previous cleanup is still unfinished.

The photographer cannot get a convincing smile from Nice. The smile is stiff, emotionally empty, and visibly performative.

Smile intervenes with a theory of expression.

CN 109–118 and the Japanese witness establish the core sequence:

- a smile comes from energy overflowing from within;
- that energy acts on the facial muscles;
- the resulting expression can infect other people with the same emotion;
- a naturally emerging smile is difficult to fake;
- do not use a `商业化` / business smile;
- before smiling, think of something that truly makes you happy.

Nice remembers his younger self practicing a smile and being teased that it looks as if he has ulterior motives. When asked where he learned it, he answers:

`偶像英雄微笑`

The Japanese witness is explicit:

`僕のヒーローのスマイルからさ`

> “From my hero, Smile.”

He wanted someday to have the same sparkling, contagious smile.

This is one of E21's most important character revisions.

E20 proved that Original Nice's perfection was engineered through Fear. It would be easy to conclude that his public hero identity is therefore wholly artificial.

E21 rejects that simplification.

> **Original Nice had a genuine heroic model before Shang De's instrumental project swallowed him.**

His desire to smile like Smile is not Fear manufacture. It is childhood admiration.

The system does not create the desire and then exploit it. It finds a desire already capable of meaning and builds a machine around it.

## Movement IV — Shang De abolishes Nice's heroic telos

The contrast is immediate.

Shang De interrupts the photoshoot, takes Nice aside, physically grips/frames his face, and tells him to keep a low profile because cleanup from the last incident is incomplete.

Then he destroys the meaning Nice had just recovered:

`我不需要你这种廉价的笑容`

> “I don't need this cheap smile of yours.”

`我需要一把能复仇的武器`

> “I need a weapon for revenge.”

`而不是一个苍白无用的花瓶`

> “Not a pale, useless vase.”

The Japanese witness shifts the object metaphors — `復讐のマシン` / revenge machine and `役立たずの人形` / useless doll — but preserves the fundamental structure: Nice is valuable either as instrument or as decorative object, not as self-authoring person.

Shang De goes further:

`不会真的以为是让你去当英雄的吧`

> “You didn't really think I was doing this to make you a hero, did you?”

This line retrospectively sharpens Original Nice's E1 collapse without reducing it to a single cause. The viewer already knows where Original Nice's story is heading. E21 now reveals that one of his deepest private aspirations — becoming a hero capable of Smile's genuine radiance — is explicitly denied by the man who built his career.

Shang De assigns the actual purpose:

- enter the top ten;
- kill E-Soul in the ranking tournament;
- ultimately defeat Yan Mo;
- first remove/replace the veteran Smile;
- methods do not matter;
- Smile's death is acceptable.

The cruelty is structurally exact:

> **The man who taught Nice how to smile is made the prerequisite victim for Nice's promotion.**

Nice's authentic hero ideal and Shang De's revenge project are therefore placed in direct collision.

## Movement V — the Association explains why X cannot remain X

E21 then moves from private instrumentalization to institutional theory.

An Association official states that the organization does not want the tragedy of the Downfall to recur. The Mandarin reconstruction around CN 165–166 is partially malformed, but CN 166 reads `下达了诛杀令`, which ordinarily means issuing a kill/extermination order. The Japanese witness instead says:

`そこで今のＸに対して 包囲網を敷いた`

> “Therefore, they laid an encirclement network around the current X.”

Because Mandarin is primary but the preceding Mandarin cue is damaged and the multilingual witnesses diverge materially in degree, the exact operational/legal form must remain **OPEN**. What is secure is that the Association has initiated active measures against the current X's continued tenure.

The reason is explicit.

If Trust Value exceeds a certain level:

`绝大多数的人都成为了那个人的信徒`

> an enormous/overwhelming number of people have become that person's **believers**.

Then that person may become a god.

The Japanese witness similarly uses `信者`, believers/adherents.

The official continues:

- a god can create the world;
- a god can destroy it in an instant;
- the world already experienced Zero's disaster;
- preventing the appearance / manufacture of a god is a founding mission of the Association;
- current X must not be allowed to take/retain the top position for a fourth time.

This is the strongest institutional clarification of Trust mechanics so far.

The system is not merely “fans believe in hero -> hero gets stronger.” At extreme saturation, the Association itself understands Trust as a transformation in **political-religious relation**:

`audience / public` → `believers` → possible `god`

The danger is not just high combat output. It is a social field in which one figure becomes the object of overwhelming faith.

This also sharpens E20's “affective homeostasis” interpretation into explicit institutional doctrine. The Association's founding purpose after Zero is not merely to administer rankings. It is to prevent Trust concentration from producing another god.

# 3. Liang Long — violence converted into a theory of purification

Liang Long's characterization is economical but unusually complete for an introductory episode.

His first significant word is not a request for respect, an explanation, or a challenge. It is `垃圾` — trash.

That word becomes his moral category.

The strangers at the lake are clearly behaving badly. They objectify him through cameras, mock him, invent a hero persona for social-media circulation, demand submission after he knocks a phone away, and escalate into group violence.

E21 does not require the viewer to defend them.

But Liang Long's response is not simply self-defense. He develops the grammar:

`垃圾就该被清理掉`

> “Trash should be cleaned up.”

Later, when Yan Mo offers godhood, Liang Long asks whether he will then be able to clear away **all** the trash that offends his eyes.

The important word is `清理` — clean up / clear away. It removes the target from the category of person-to-be-opposed and places it in the category of waste-to-be-disposed-of.

This creates an immediate bridge between personal grievance and exterminatory abstraction.

Yan Mo does not correct it.

He recruits it.

### 3.1 Pain is already part of Liang Long's self-construction

The cold open also establishes a bodily pattern the climax repeats.

Liang Long is beaten by a group. He refuses to stay down. The attackers exhaust themselves and become frightened by his durability. His torso already bears pale geometric/organic markings, and even while bleeding he maintains a strangely calm or contemptuous expression.

The later Smile fight formally rhymes with this scene.

Liang Long again:

- receives repeated blows;
- invites more;
- tells the attacker he needs only “a little more”;
- explicitly says `打我呀` — hit me;
- offers information about the daughter's supposed ashes if Smile keeps striking;
- after sufficient punishment, says `够了 / 不玩了` — enough, the game is over;
- clarifies that “over” means it is now his turn;
- enters a visibly intensified state in which his body markings and blue-violet energy become more pronounced and his eyes/face shift.

At the E21 boundary, the safest mechanics formulation is:

> **Liang Long deliberately uses received punishment as part of his activation sequence.** (`SRC+VIS`, High)

The stronger claim that his power is numerically proportional to pain, injury, kinetic energy, or accumulated damage remains `OPEN`.

The episode nevertheless makes pain functionally central. He does not merely tolerate being hit while waiting for an opening. He solicits the hits, says he needs more, and changes state after a threshold-like sequence.

### 3.2 The cold open and climax are the same scene with roles reversed

The rhyme is ethically important.

At the lake, violent men hit Liang Long because they want to force him into submission. He calls them trash.

Against Smile, Liang Long deliberately turns a grieving father into the violent man. He fabricates or implies the daughter's death, keeps escalating the language from “where is she?” to `遗物` / relic to “ashes,” and invites Smile to hit him.

Liang Long therefore does not simply survive cruelty.

He learns how to **manufacture the conditions under which another person will become cruel enough to feed his advantage**.

His power and his psychology have the same structure:

> provoke → receive violence → convert violence into superiority → reclassify the attacker as trash.

That is a much darker survival model than ordinary resilience.

# 4. Yan Mo — god-making as cultivation

E20 located Yan Mo inside inherited Zero history and a resurrection-oriented project. E21 adds a second, crucial register: **cultivation**.

His cold-open phrase is not merely “I can make you powerful.” It is:

`一个可以被培育成为神的人`

> someone who can be **cultivated** into a god.

`培育` is developmental language. It can be used for raising, cultivating, breeding, or fostering something over time.

This means Yan Mo's project is not limited to recovering a historical secret. He is also searching for bodies/people who can be grown toward a god-state.

Liang Long interprets the offer through Zero: `成为了Zero以后`.

At E21, this supports a strong but bounded revision:

> **Yan Mo's Zero project includes reproducibility — the idea that a god/Zero-like condition can be recreated or cultivated in a new bearer.**

It does **not** yet settle whether Yan Mo's “resurrection” language refers to literal resurrection of a specific dead person, recreation of Zero as a role/state, restoration of a lost power regime, or some combination.

The old formulation should therefore be narrowed rather than discarded.

### 4.1 Yan Mo is not simply building the strongest fighter

His current strategy demonstrates that he understands the hero system sociologically.

He does not tell Liang Long to challenge the strongest visible target first. He maps:

- attention;
- institutional symbolism;
- crowd density;
- discoverability;
- entrenched reputation;
- scandal resistance;
- ranking opportunity.

He chooses Smile because Smile's **image is strong but attention is weak**.

That is the exact kind of distinction the series has been teaching since E1: public belief is not a single number detached from circulation. It has history, salience, recency, narrative, and institutional protection.

Yan Mo is therefore a god-maker who thinks like a media strategist.

# 5. Original Nice — authentic desire under manufactured perfection

E21 may be the most important Original Nice episode yet because it refuses to let “engineered” mean “empty.”

E20 established that Shang De used Fear to manufacture the conditions of Original Nice's perfection.

E21 establishes all of the following simultaneously:

1. Nice's photographed smile is currently stiff and emotionally unconvincing.
2. Smile can coach him toward a more natural expression.
3. Nice already admired Smile as a child.
4. He wanted a similarly radiant/infectious smile long before this photoshoot.
5. The memory of that desire helps him produce the expression the photographer could not mechanically demand.
6. Shang De immediately devalues the expression and redirects Nice toward killing.

The resulting character thesis is:

> **Original Nice is not a fake hero whose inner emptiness is concealed by Fear. He is a person with a real heroic aspiration whose performance, body, career, and value have been externally engineered around that aspiration until he can no longer own what it means.**

That is a much more tragic construction.

### 5.1 Smile is a chosen hero before Shang De is a controlling patron

Nice calls Smile `我的英雄` in the Japanese witness — “my hero.”

This gives Nice a self-authored lineage.

Shang De gives him an imposed lineage of revenge.

The episode places those two inheritances face to face:

- Smile: “think of what genuinely makes you happy.”
- Shang De: “I do not need your cheap smile.”
- Smile: expression transmits genuine emotion to others.
- Shang De: become a weapon/machine that kills my enemies.
- Nice's childhood: I want to smile like him.
- Shang De's present: replace him, and his death is acceptable.

E21 therefore reframes Original Nice's crisis as a battle over **who gets to define the purpose of his hero identity**.

### 5.2 Shang De's face-grip is not incidental staging

Visually, Shang De does not merely issue instructions across a desk. He physically takes Nice's face/head in his hands while speaking about use, revenge, and the purpose for which Nice was “raised up.”

The composition makes the control literal.

The part of Nice that Smile has just helped him self-author — his face, his smile, the visible surface of heroic affect — is physically framed by the man who claims ownership over its purpose.

The image is the episode's clearest visual statement of **identity capture**.

# 6. Smile — authentic affect hardened into compulsory hero labor

Smile enters as a seemingly simple answer to Nice's problem: stop forcing the brand and recover genuine emotion.

E21 then spends the rest of the episode showing why Smile's own answer is unstable.

### 6.1 His pedagogy is sincere

Nothing in the source suggests Smile is cynically selling authenticity. His advice works because it reaches Nice's actual memory.

The key idea is not “move your mouth this way.” It is:

`在笑之前先想 / 什么值得你真正开心`

> Before smiling, think about what is truly worth being happy about.

This is a theory of expression as inward-to-outward causality.

### 6.2 His public role has become bodily habit

When Smile visits X, X remarks that Smile has been doing hero work and smiling for decades. Smile says he has forgotten what feeling tired is like.

Later, when Liang Long threatens his daughter, Liang is amazed that even Smile's threat is delivered with a smile:

`原来你连恐吓都是笑着的`

> “So even when you threaten someone, you're smiling.”

At this point the smile is no longer merely spontaneous joy. It is a deeply trained bodily identity.

The episode does not call Smile hypocritical. It shows how a genuine affective practice can become habitual to the point that it survives contexts where the underlying emotion is fear, rage, or grief.

### 6.3 His family exposes the cost of distributed warmth

The pendant story is devastating because it does not require a melodramatic accusation.

Smile says his wife bought matching pendants for him and their daughter. She put the daughter's photo inside his because he was always smiling at other people's children.

Then she chose a photograph in which the daughter is not smiling.

Why?

`因为你女儿沒有一张照片是帶着笑容的`

> “Because there isn't a single photograph of your daughter with a smile.”

The series does not tell us exactly why the daughter never smiles in photographs. It does not prove abuse, hatred, or simple paternal neglect. The marital/family present is also unresolved; Smile's lonely-man joking does not establish death or divorce.

What the episode does establish is an **asymmetry recognized inside the family**:

> the man known for giving smiles to the public does not possess a smiling image of his own daughter.

This turns Smile's gift into a family wound without declaring the gift false.

### 6.4 His power makes the emotional labor literal

Liang Long's psychological attack works by stripping away the expression.

When Smile believes his daughter may be harmed or dead, the fight shifts. Liang explicitly says:

`更何况你失去了微笑 / 就是个垃圾`

> “Besides, Smile without a smile is just trash.”

After Liang's activated counteroffensive knocks him down, Smile recovers and states:

`只有保持微笑 / 我的力量才能被全部唤醒`

> “Only by maintaining a smile can my power be fully awakened.”

The Japanese witness is even more numerically explicit:

`笑顔でいれば 私は100%パワーを発揮できる`

> “If I keep smiling, I can exert 100% of my power.”

This is a confirmed local mechanic.

But E21 does **not** establish whether:

- facial configuration alone is sufficient;
- genuine positive emotion is required;
- Trust responds dynamically to the visible smile;
- the ability has an internal psychological gate;
- the smile condition was created by public expectation or existed beforehand.

The episode deliberately places two truths in tension:

1. Smile teaches that genuine smiles cannot be reduced to business performance.
2. Smile's own body now requires continuous smiling for maximum combat output.

The veteran hero has become the limiting case of his own pedagogy.

> **What begins as authentic expression can, after decades of recognition, harden into an obligation the body must reproduce even when the self is hurting.**

# 7. X — the god-risk who wants to clock out

The private X/Smile scene is one of the most consequential mechanics scenes in the episode precisely because it is played as domestic comedy.

Smile arrives disguised in a yellow helmet with beer and skewers. He says he is protecting X's identity. X makes him change his shoes.

The supposedly god-adjacent current X is presented in pajamas, glasses, messy private space, and late-night annoyance.

### 7.1 E20's identity-switch inference is now directly verbalized

Smile asks:

`还是打个响指换个身份跟我聊吧`

> “Why don't you snap your fingers, change identity, and talk with me?”

# 22. E21 mature synthesis

Episode 21 deepens *To Be Hero X*'s central idea that public belief does not merely describe people. It reorganizes their bodies, opportunities, obligations, and danger.

The episode's most revealing irony is that **every major actor is talking about authenticity while building systems that make authenticity impossible to leave alone**.

Smile believes a real smile must come from real joy.

But decades of herohood make smiling his power condition.

Original Nice's real joy comes from having admired Smile.

But Shang De declares the smile cheap and demands revenge machinery.

X has a real desire for ordinary life.

But the public's Trust may make him a god whether or not he wants the office.

Liang Long has a real hatred of humiliating, violent people.

But Yan Mo cultivates that hatred into a theory that unwanted human beings are trash to be cleaned away.

The Association has a real reason to fear concentrated Trust after Zero.

But that reason can make the institution treat a reluctant bearer as the object of coercive containment or possibly lethal action.

So E21 does not resolve the series by choosing sincerity over performance.

It makes a more difficult claim:

> **Sincerity does not protect a person from instrumentalization. In a world where emotion creates power, the most sincere part of a person may be exactly what institutions learn to extract.**

The ethical question therefore shifts from:

> Is the hero image true?

To:

> **Who has authority over the relationship between the image, the feeling, the body, and the public that believes in it?**

That is why the episode's smallest joke — X refusing to transform because he does not work overtime — may be its most serious answer.

A person needs a boundary at which the role stops.

Smile's tragedy is that his boundary has almost disappeared.

Nice's tragedy is that Shang De never intended to give him one.

Liang Long's danger is that he does not want one.

And the Association's fear is that mass belief may erase the boundary even when X himself tries to keep it.
