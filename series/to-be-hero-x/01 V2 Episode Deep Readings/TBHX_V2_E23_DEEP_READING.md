# 1. Executive thesis — a lie can become causal without becoming true

Episode 23 is titled **`LIE / 撒谎`**, and virtually every layer of the episode asks the same question:

> **What happens to the distinction between truth and falsehood in a world where belief itself can alter reality?**

The answer is not that truth ceases to matter.

The episode instead distinguishes several kinds of falsehood according to **who authors them, who bears their cost, and whether the people transformed by them can answer back**.

A-Hu (`阿虎`; JP `トラ`) begins as a dog forced to inhabit somebody else's fiction. His circus trainer could no longer realize the fantasy of handling a tiger, so a dog is trained and named as a tiger-substitute. A-Hu becomes a clown, a spectacle, and an object of children's praise. Their praise makes him stronger, but he despises the children and despises himself. His body is already demonstrating the world's Trust metaphysics while his mind experiences the same process as humiliation:

> **people believe in an image he knows was imposed upon him, and their belief makes the image more real.**

Xinya (`新芽`; JP `シンヤー`) later tells another fiction about him: A-Hu is the strongest land creature, a future hero who can protect the neighborhood. She dresses him, stages propaganda, adds CG/BGM/text to a fake hero debut video, and explicitly understands that each new person's Trust moves him closer to herohood.

On the surface, she is lying too.

But the ethical structure has changed.

She first takes A-Hu into her life as **family**, shares danger with him, fights beside him, trusts him before public certification, and ultimately gives him something his circus identity never had: a role he can choose to inhabit.

A-Hu's decisive realization is therefore not merely:

> Xinya believes in me.

It is:

> **I have believed her from the beginning.**

The recognition becomes reciprocal.

E23 consequently offers one of the clearest formulations yet of a rule already emerging across TBHX:

> **External Trust can make a role available, but the bearer must be able to receive that recognition as a self he can inhabit.**

This is not evidence for a separate numerical “self-Trust stat.” It is evidence for **receptivity to recognition**.

The episode then places that generative fiction against predatory deception.

Tree Man Group uses contracts elderly residents cannot understand, intimidation, and a famous hero's reputation as a guarantee. Original Nice does not merely appear in advertising: his voice is tied to the operation, he prioritizes recovering the residents' documents over helping people in danger, and he later says he cannot allow another mission to fail.

The same social mechanism—**people believe because a hero-image tells them to**—can therefore:

- create a protector;
- dispossess a neighborhood;
- create actual power from false causal attribution;
- and destroy a bearer whose own identity depends on remaining the believed image.

The strongest E23 thesis is:

> **In TBHX, lies are not the opposite of reality; they are technologies capable of manufacturing reality through Trust. Their moral difference lies in relation and accountability. Xinya's fiction becomes an invitation A-Hu can eventually choose; Tree Man and Shang De's fiction turns people into assets; public media creates a simplified hero story that becomes mechanically real whether or not it is causally accurate.**

And Original Nice becomes the episode's negative mirror.

During the fight he insists:

> `我是奈斯`  
> **I am Nice.**

By the credits he asks:

> `我是谁`  
> **Who am I?**

The perfect hero persona that Fear once helped construct can no longer sustain the person trapped inside it.

# 2. Episode architecture

E23 moves through six tightly linked movements.

## Movement I — the dog who was made into a tiger

A-Hu narrates his circus history.

He is a dog whose name and performance identity derive from a substitute fantasy: the trainer associated with his naming/performance had been injured by a real tiger and uses a dog in place of the tiger. A-Hu is dressed, staged, and trained for spectacle.

He calls the situation something like a joke, which is appropriate because he is the circus's clown.

Children laugh, cheer, and praise him.

He notices something extraordinary:

> their praise makes him stronger.

Yet he dislikes the children and dislikes himself.

The contradiction establishes the episode immediately:

> **Trust can be efficacious without being experienced as recognition.**

People can strengthen an image while alienating the bearer.

## Movement II — Xinya adopts the being before she invents the hero

A-Hu's aggression eventually leaves him confined and facing euthanasia if he will not accept treatment.

Xinya enters, chooses him despite warnings, produces the money needed to take him, places a pink neckcloth/collar on him, and declares:

> `我们就是一家人啦`  
> **Now we're family.**

Only afterward does she introduce the neighborhood's mutual-aid project and heroic ambition.

The order matters.

`family → community → hero`

not:

`heroic usefulness → conditional acceptance`.

This continues a pattern already visible in the Big Johnny material: **relational recognition precedes institutional classification**.

## Movement III — local hero production from below

Xinya introduces A-Hu to a long-established neighborhood whose residents have lived through mutual aid for years.

The district is under pressure from people trying to obtain its homes/property.

A-Hu discovers that Xinya's father had been connected to the Hero Association. The Japanese witness identifies him as an Association employee. Xinya therefore possesses more than naïve enthusiasm: she has inherited some practical knowledge about how Trust creates heroes.

She tells A-Hu:

> every person who trusts you brings you another step closer to becoming a hero.

Her early campaign is almost comically artificial:

- homemade costume;
- exaggerated claims that A-Hu can throw knives and breathe fire;
- staged performance;
- a fake “hero debut battle” manufactured with CG, music, and captions.

The public mocks the result.

But the episode does not present the fabrication as simply irrelevant. Xinya is attempting to **bootstrap a social fact** in a world where enough belief can make that fact material.

## Movement IV — corporate Trust capture

Tree Man Group enters from the opposite direction.

Its real-estate project is already being advertised while the actual land remains unsecured. Elderly residents are approached with contracts they cannot understand and a famous hero is used as guarantor.

Original Nice's public image therefore functions as **financial/legal confidence collateral**.

The people do not need to understand the contract because they believe the hero.

Xinya responds by contacting FOCUS.

Media becomes a counter-power: if private coercion relies on hidden asymmetry, visibility can make the coercion more expensive.

## Movement V — hidden X, compromised Nice, and A-Hu's actual awakening

During the neighborhood conflict, A-Hu repeatedly observes something he cannot explain.

Attackers disappear.

He notices the same ordinary-looking man near a vending machine.

He remembers hearing a finger snap.

Continuous motion now resolves the effect more strongly: X and the attackers are selectively transposed into **two-dimensional surface-bound states**. A-Hu remains volumetric while X and the attackers move as flat graphic figures across the pavement, then across a vertical shutter/wall plane.

Because A-Hu grew up around circus magic, the episode gives special epistemic weight to his conclusion:

> he has seen countless tricks; **this is real magic**.

The ordinary man is the civilian presentation of X.

Original Nice then arrives in the same crisis and embodies the opposite relation to herohood. He refuses aid because recovering a stack of important documents has priority. A-Hu recognizes Nice's voice as the same voice associated with the earlier instruction that the coercive operation must not produce fatalities.

Nice apologizes but says this mission cannot fail again.

A-Hu is beaten down and confronts the shame of his own hero fantasy.

Then memory reverses the meaning of the “lie.”

Xinya believed he could become a hero.

Her father had told her that as long as she believed in A-Hu, A-Hu possessed enormous/infinite possibility and could someday become the hero who protects her.

A-Hu realizes:

> `从一开始 / 我就对她的话深信不疑`  
> **From the beginning, I believed what she said completely.**

He then rejects the clown identity and chooses the hero role:

> `我不愿当小丑`  
> **I don't want to be a clown.**

> `我要成为守护新芽的大英雄`  
> **I want to become the great hero who protects Xinya.**

The hero fiction becomes self-authored.

## Movement VI — the public lie becomes a ranking fact; Nice loses himself

FOCUS converts the crisis into a hero story.

A-Hu becomes a public hero and is later asked whether he accomplished the feat by himself.

He answers:

> `没错 / 是我`  
> **That's right. Me.**

That is knowingly incomplete at minimum. X secretly intervened, and Xinya's action made the entire resistance possible.

Yet the simplified narrative creates real social recognition.

By the story-bearing credits, Shang De states that the dog has rapidly become **rank 8**, displacing/surpassing Nice.

Original Nice, meanwhile, directly confirms that he has been infected by Fear.

His identity then collapses into questions:

> What am I afraid of?  
> Afraid of no longer being a hero?  
> Why did I become a hero?  
> Who am I?  
> Why am I here?

The episode therefore ends with two manufactured hero identities moving in opposite directions:

> **A-Hu knowingly enters a fiction until it becomes inhabitable. Nice discovers that the fiction called “Nice” is no longer inhabitable at all.**

# 3. A-Hu — a dog named Tiger, a clown who hates applause

A-Hu is one of the most important ontology tests TBHX has introduced.

He is unequivocally a dog, but the episode grants him:

- reflective first-person interiority;
- moral judgment;
- shame;
- memory;
- resentment;
- strategic reasoning;
- an understanding of performance;
- loyalty;
- and the capacity to choose an ethical telos.

The hero system later recognizes him as a ranked hero.

This means herohood is not merely a human profession with supernatural accessories.

> **The Trust ontology can recognize a non-human moral agent as a hero.**

That matters after Big Johnny. E17–E20 tested whether an extraterrestrial being could be recognized as family/person before institutions classified him. E23 extends the same pressure through an ordinary animal.

## 3.1 The name itself is a performance

`阿虎` contains `虎` — tiger.

The Japanese version renders the name as `トラ`, literally “Tiger.”

A-Hu is a dog carrying a tiger-sign because a human performer needed a substitute for a tiger.

His identity begins as somebody else's workaround.

The circus therefore gives E23 a compact model of hero society:

> **a public role is designed; a body is put inside it; spectators respond to the role; the body's capacities begin adapting to that response.**

A-Hu has lived this before ever becoming an official hero.

## 3.2 Praise without recognition

A-Hu says children's praise makes him stronger.

But he hates them.

Why?

Because praise is not automatically recognition.

The children love:

- the trick;
- the costume;
- the strongest-animal image;
- the clown.

They do not necessarily know the interior being who experiences himself as fraudulent.

Thus E23 distinguishes two things that TBHX frequently lets overlap:

**Trust as metaphysical input**  
versus  
**recognition as ethical relation**.

The first can occur without the second.

That is why A-Hu can grow stronger and more self-loathing at the same time.

# 4. Xinya — family before usefulness

Xinya's first decisive act is not branding.

It is adoption.

She is told A-Hu is dangerous and difficult to treat. She chooses him anyway.

She puts the pink neckcloth on him and says they are now family.

That moment restructures his entire social ontology.

The circus relation was:

> **perform because you are useful.**

Xinya's relation begins:

> **you are with me; therefore we will figure out what we can become.**

This does not make Xinya non-manipulative. She is absolutely willing to push A-Hu into costumes, publicity, and exaggerated claims he finds embarrassing.

But unlike the circus handler or Shang De, she is not outside the risk structure.

She:

- lives in the threatened neighborhood;
- personally confronts the coercive agents;
- fights;
- gets injured;
- and believes the future she is selling.

That difference—**shared exposure to consequences**—is crucial to E23's ethics of storytelling.

# 5. Xinya's father and inherited Trust literacy

In the secret base, A-Hu notices evidence that Xinya's father was connected to the Association; the Japanese witness calls him an Association employee.

Xinya's later childhood memory explains why she understands Trust so naturally.

She says A-Hu is stronger because he can do anything.

Her father responds that as long as Xinya believes in A-Hu, A-Hu has enormous/infinite possibility and may someday become the hero who protects her.

This makes Xinya's hero-making project more sophisticated than childish wish fulfillment.

> **She is attempting to operationalize inherited knowledge of Trust.**

Yet she applies that knowledge in a radically local form.

The Association organizes Trust through rankings, celebrities, tournaments, media, and anti-apotheosis controls.

Xinya starts with:

> **one relationship.**

That becomes a major E23 correction to any model that treats Trust only as mass popularity.

# 6. One believer and the `01` visual

During A-Hu's awakening, the episode isolates a cracked block/counter marked:

> **`01`**

As A-Hu accepts Xinya's belief and chooses the hero role, the `01` object lights cyan.

The safest reading is formal rather than mathematical:

> **the episode visually associates a single unit / one believer with the activation of A-Hu's hero potential.**

This aligns with:

- Xinya's “one person who trusts you” formulation;
- her father's “as long as Xinya believes” formulation;
- A-Hu's realization that he believes her belief.

But E23 does **not** justify a universal equation such as:

`1 believer = N units of power`.

Nor does it justify inventing an independent “self-Trust” stat.

The stronger project-level model is:

> **Trust is relationally available at extremely small social scale, and the bearer's ability to inhabit the recognized identity can determine whether that Trust becomes usable.**

This also strengthens E18's observation that external Trust can become ineffective when a bearer no longer believes in the self it is trying to sustain.

# 7. The neighborhood — small-scale legitimacy before ranking legitimacy

Xinya's neighborhood is important because it gives TBHX an institution that is neither hero corporation nor central Association.

The residents' mutual-aid organization is informal/local, built from long-term cohabitation and reciprocal support.

Its legitimacy emerges from:

- memory;
- familiarity;
- practical care;
- shared place;
- mutual vulnerability.

Xinya's project is therefore not simply:

> make my dog famous.

It is:

> **create enough protective capacity that a community too weak to command elite attention can defend itself.**

She explicitly doubts that high-status flying heroes have time for small neighborhood disputes.

The series does not entirely prove that belief wrong—X secretly helps—but her political diagnosis is understandable:

> **central prestige does not guarantee local availability.**

# 8. Tree Man Group — dispossession by borrowed hero credibility

Tree Man Group's operation is the episode's clearest predatory use of Trust.

The neighborhood is pressured to surrender property.

When blunt intimidation proves insufficient, the operation becomes more sophisticated:

- residents are approached individually;
- elderly people receive contracts they cannot understand;
- property deeds are surrendered;
- a famous hero is presented as guarantor;
- development advertising is already running before the land has actually been secured.

This is not merely “a bad company.”

It is a political-economic use of hero ontology:

> **the company borrows a hero's Trust to lower the target's epistemic defenses.**

The residents do not need to trust Tree Man.

They need to trust the hero standing behind Tree Man.

Hero reputation therefore becomes transferable financial capital.

# 9. Original Nice — from hero image to corporate enforcement instrument

E21–E22 established that Original Nice's authentic heroic aspiration predates Shang De's Fear engineering.

E23 shows how far that aspiration has been captured.

Nice appears in Tree Man advertising.

More importantly, A-Hu later recognizes Nice's voice in connection with the operation's instruction:

> proceed, but do not cause fatalities.

Nice's role should therefore not be softened to accidental celebrity endorsement.

At E23's current evidentiary boundary:

> **Original Nice is operationally complicit in the Tree Man property-seizure mission.**

He still maintains a limit: no deaths.

That limit matters morally, but it does not make the operation legitimate.

It shows instead how compromised systems preserve self-image through thresholds:

> coercion is acceptable;  
> fraud is acceptable;  
> violence is acceptable;  
> **just do not cross the line that would force the actor to recognize himself as a killer.**

This echoes the institutional euphemisms surrounding cleanup operations in E19.

## 9.1 “The documents are more important”

When A-Hu/Xinya need help, Nice says he would like to help but is looking for a stack of important documents and must prioritize them.

In context those documents are tied to the property operation.

The reversal is severe:

> **the hero prioritizes paperwork enabling dispossession over the people endangered by the dispossession.**

That is not generic hypocrisy.

It is what happens when a heroic persona becomes organizational labor.

## 9.2 “This mission cannot fail again”

Nice apologizes, but says he cannot permit another failure.

This line is essential after E22.

Smile's death did not free Nice from Shang De.

It left him more psychologically vulnerable to failure.

His retaliation against Liang was personal. E23 shows that once the grief episode passes, he returns to work under the same manager and performs another morally compromised mission.

That is not proof that Smile meant nothing.

It is evidence that **grief alone does not provide an exit structure**.

The civilian X appears around the conflict in the ordinary black-haired/glasses presentation already established in E20–E22.

A-Hu observes a recurring pattern:

- dangerous men appear;
- something impossible happens;
- he remembers hearing a finger snap;
- the same ordinary man is nearby around a vending machine;
- attackers disappear;
- later they are impossibly embedded in a wall.

E23 gives A-Hu a uniquely appropriate epistemic response.

He grew up in a circus.

He has watched magic tricks all his life.

Therefore when he concludes that:

> **real magic exists**

the conclusion functions as in-world validation that what X is doing cannot be dismissed as ordinary stagecraft.

This strengthens E22's representational-ontology evidence from another angle.

E22 showed the viewer that X can manipulate the representational state of Liang.

E23 gives an **in-world performer** enough evidence to conclude that X's effects violate the ordinary trick/reality distinction.

## 10.2 X helps without taking the hero-image

That makes them almost inverse cases of role sovereignty.

# 11. A-Hu's crisis — “I am only a clown”

Nice's intervention destroys A-Hu's newly forming confidence.

A-Hu tells himself:

> `我真是太傻了`  
> **I really am a fool.**

> `明明就是一个小丑`  
> **I'm obviously just a clown.**

He recognizes the humiliating possibility that Xinya's hero project was merely another costume.

This is where E23 most directly returns to the title.

Was “A-Hu the hero” simply another lie like “A-Hu the tiger”?

The answer depends on authorship.

The tiger/clown identity was imposed.

The hero identity is something A-Hu can reject or choose.

That freedom is what allows the same mechanism—performance becoming reality—to have a different ethical meaning.

# 12. Reciprocal belief — A-Hu does not merely receive Trust

A-Hu asks:

> When did I start believing her?

The memories answer the question.

Xinya believed that A-Hu could protect her.

Her father told her that belief itself mattered.

A-Hu finally realizes:

> `从一开始 / 我就对她的话深信不疑`

> **From the very beginning, I believed her words completely.**

This is one of the strongest relationship statements in the series.

Xinya does not simply project a hero identity onto a passive body.

A-Hu has been quietly accepting her world all along.

Thus:

> **She believes in him; he believes in her belief.**

The social loop closes.

Herohood becomes reciprocal recognition rather than unilateral audience authorship.

This provides a useful contrast with Original Nice.

Public audiences believe in Nice.

But the person inside Nice increasingly cannot inhabit what they believe.

A-Hu begins with almost no audience.

But the one relationship he does have becomes psychologically inhabitable.

That is why small-scale Trust can succeed where mass-scale Trust can become a cage.

# 13. “I don't want to be a clown” — chosen telos

A-Hu's declaration is not:

> I really was the strongest all along.

It is future-directed:

> `我不愿当小丑`  
> I don't want to be a clown.

> `我要成为守护新芽的大英雄`  
> I want to become the great hero who protects Xinya.

This matters.

The hero identity is not validated by discovering that Xinya's earlier claims were literally accurate.

It is validated by A-Hu **choosing to make them accurate**.

The lie becomes promise.

That may be E23's most constructive statement:

> **A fiction becomes ethically generative when the subject can convert it into a promise he chooses to answer.**

# 14. A-Hu and Nice — two manufactured heroes inside the same crisis

The episode places A-Hu and Original Nice in direct thematic opposition, but the final motion audit corrects an important frame-only overstatement: **A-Hu does not defeat Nice in a conventional duel.**

### A-Hu

- named as a tiger though he is a dog;
- trained as a clown;
- later advertised as the strongest land creature;
- given a fake debut video;
- eventually chooses the heroic identity;
- immediately demonstrates new capability by defeating a Fear-created attacker.

### Original Nice

- genuinely admired heroism as a child;
- had his aspiration captured by Shang De;
- had a perfect public persona engineered through Fear;
- is now performing corporate coercion because failure is intolerable;
- is splashed by dark Fear residue when A-Hu destroys the Fear-created attacker;
- then enters escalating contamination panic.

The contrast is therefore not “new hero beats old hero.” It is more precise:

> **A-Hu becomes more able to inhabit a chosen identity at the same moment Nice becomes less able to inhabit an imposed fixed one.**

A-Hu's role can be revised through his relationship with Xinya.

Nice's role is policed through Shang De, public perfection, ranking, and Fear.

The crisis places both transformations beside one another without requiring A-Hu to physically defeat Nice.

# 15. Nice's Fear infection is confirmed — and E23 supplies the strongest visible exposure event

E22 left Nice's own Fear contamination unresolved. E23 closes the fact of infection and substantially narrows the likely exposure route.

After A-Hu's awakening, he attacks and defeats a **Fear-created thug**, not Nice. During the attacker's destruction, dark Fear residue/droplets visibly splash onto Nice's arm/armor.

Only after that contact does Nice's contamination panic escalate:

> `走开`  
> **Get away.**

> `不要碰我`  
> **Don't touch me.**

Then he tries to stabilize himself through identity assertion:

> `我是奈斯`  
> **I am Nice.**

> `恐惧侵蚀不了我`  
> **Fear cannot erode/corrode me.**

The body immediately contradicts the claim. Dark purple/black Fear lesions/effects spread across his arms/hands; his eye rendering becomes black/purple and unstable; panic overwhelms the declarative persona.

He then says:

> `我不想变成那个样子死去啊`  
> **I don't want to become like that and die.**

The referent is strongly tied to the recent spectacle of Fear-corrupted Smile's death.

During the story-bearing credits, Nice directly tells Shang De:

> `我好像被恐惧感染了`  
> **I seem to have been infected by Fear.**

Current causal formulation:

> **Nice's Fear infection is confirmed. E23's visible splash of Fear residue immediately before symptoms is the strongest source-grounded exposure candidate. E22 supplies the psychological trauma/template that gives the contamination its Smile-shaped terror.**

Do not overstate this into proof that no earlier subclinical exposure was possible.

## 15.1 Fear attacks the axis that made Nice

Nice's fear soon becomes explicit:

> `害怕不再是英雄`  
> **Afraid of no longer being a hero.**

This is a remarkable recursion.

Shang De used Fear to construct Nice's perfect hero persona.

Now Fear attacks Nice through the terror of losing that persona.

> **Fear both manufactured and destabilizes the same identity.**

E23 sharpens the mechanism by showing two layers acting together:

- **material/contact layer:** visible Fear contamination;
- **psychological identity layer:** Smile's death and terror of ceasing to be Nice supply the content through which infection is experienced.

# 16. “I am Nice” → “Who am I?”

The episode contains a devastating linguistic reversal.

During the fight:

> `我是奈斯`  
> **I am Nice.**

The name is used like an incantation or defensive assertion.

If he is Nice, Fear cannot destroy him.

By the end:

> `我是谁`  
> **Who am I?**

Then:

> `为什么会在这里`  
> **Why am I here?**

The identity claim has inverted into an identity question.

This is the endpoint of the “perfect image” problem.

Original Nice has spent so long treating the public role as proof of self that when the role becomes unstable, he does not have an obvious private identity to retreat into.

That places him opposite X.

X can apparently leave X and become an ordinary man.

Original Nice does not know who remains if Nice disappears.

# 17. Shang De and conditional human worth

Nice asks Shang De for help with his Fear infection.

Shang De responds through performance failure.

The property development has already been advertised, yet the land is still not secured.

Nice asks for one final chance and says he can reach the top ten.

Shang De tells him that the dog Nice helped make famous has already become **rank 8** and that Nice is now:

> `连一条狗都不如`  
> **not even as good as a dog / below a dog.**

This insult matters especially because earlier Nice recoils from A-Hu with disgust.

The species hierarchy reverses.

The being treated as dirty/inferior becomes the higher-ranked hero.

And Shang De uses that fact not to reconsider what herohood means, but to humiliate Nice further.

The managerial relationship is therefore fundamentally conditional:
