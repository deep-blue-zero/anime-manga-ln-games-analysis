# 1. Governing thesis: injury that demands to be returned

Episode 22 is structured around a single recursive problem:

> **What happens when a person or system treats injury as something that must be returned?**

Liang Long makes the proposition literal. His ability takes **damage received** and returns it at double force. Crucially, he states that this includes **Fear**.

But the episode embeds the same structure at every level:

- Yan Mo teaches that pain is mandatory preparation for godhood.
- Young Liang is taught to endure until failure, then called `垃圾` — trash.
- Adult Liang repeats that dehumanizing vocabulary against everyone who obstructs him.
- Shang De turns his own grievance into a revenge project and tries to make Original Nice its weapon.
- Nice injects Fear into Liang; Liang returns it into Smile at amplified magnitude.
- Fear converts Smile's defining relationship to smiling into terror of ridicule.
- spectators see only fragments of the battle and return confusion as condemnation.
- the crowd's condemnation bears directly upon a hero whose "perfection" depends on public belief.
- Liang treats Nice's strike as permission to retaliate.
- the crowd treats Liang's victim-position as permission to intensify its abuse of Nice.

The result is a social and mechanical positive-feedback loop:

`hurt → return → amplification → reinterpretation → further hurt`.

X enters as the episode's great exception.

He does not defeat Liang by suffering more, striking harder, or returning the same violence at greater magnitude. He imposes a radically asymmetric state on the fight and then, with a snap, removes Liang from the exchange.

That makes the deepest E22 contrast:

> **Liang embodies retaliation. X embodies interruption.**

And between those poles sits Original Nice, whose moral question is whether he will repeat the revenge program Shang De installed around him or recover the heroic ethic he genuinely learned from Smile.

# 2. Cold open: Yan Mo turns pain into initiation

E21 established that Yan Mo was cultivating Liang Long as a possible god/Zero-like figure. E22 supplies the doctrine beneath that cultivation.

Yan Mo tells the younger Liang that the Hero Association was founded in response to the `曙光陨落事件` — the Downfall event — when heroes collectively eliminated the being remembered publicly as a monster.

Yan Mo rejects that public simplification.

His account of Zero is not:

> Zero was secretly innocent.

It is more tragic and structurally revealing:

> **Zero carried something no individual should have been required to carry.**

Yan Mo names the negative affect contained within collective adoration:

- `嫉妒` — jealousy;
- `猜疑` — suspicion;
- `恐惧` — fear.

The important proposition is that praise and worship do not produce a purely positive social field. Their opposite affects can be folded into the same public relation and deposited onto the bearer.

Zero became godlike, but not perfect. He could not bear the pain of what the collective relation placed upon him.

Yan Mo's conclusion is chilling:

> `感受疼痛是必经的历练`  
> **Experiencing pain is a necessary trial before becoming a god.**

This reveals why Liang's psychology matters to Yan Mo.

Yan Mo is not merely searching for someone strong enough to become Zero. He is selecting for a person who can be conditioned to regard extreme suffering as proof of eligibility.

The doctrine therefore turns trauma into credential.

## 2.1 A perverse answer to the Zero problem

The Association and Yan Mo now have opposing lessons from the same catastrophe.

### Association lesson

Concentrated public Trust can produce a god. Therefore prevent `造神`.

### Yan Mo lesson

Zero failed because the bearer could not withstand the affective burden of godhood. Therefore cultivate a bearer whose relationship to pain has already been transformed.

The disagreement is not over whether godhood is dangerous.

Both sides know it is.

They disagree over whether the danger should be **prevented** or **trained through**.

That puts Liang's power, history, and personality at the center of an institutional experiment in political theology.

# 3. Liang Long: the victim who learned the grammar of his abuser

E21 showed adult Liang using a purification vocabulary:

- obstructive people are `垃圾`;
- trash should be `清理掉`.

E22 finally gives that language a psychological history.

In a memory sequence, a young boy strongly identifiable as Liang is held or forced under water in a tank/pool-like training environment while adults observe. A woman's voice urges him onward:

- `还没有到极限` — not at the limit yet;
- `再忍耐一下` — endure a little longer;
- `你可以的` — you can do it;
- then, after failure:
- `我对你很失望` — I'm disappointed in you;
- `没用的垃圾` — useless trash.

The woman's relationship to Liang is **not named at the E22 boundary**. This document therefore does not identify her as mother, coach, relative, institutional caretaker, or researcher.

The psychological pattern, however, is much safer.

Adult Liang has internalized the exact evaluative grammar once used against him.

> **He survives dehumanization by becoming the person who gets to decide who counts as humanly worthwhile.**

The child learns:

`endure pain successfully → deserve recognition`  
`fail to endure → become trash`.

The adult universalizes it:

`withstand me / prove usefulness → perhaps deserve existence`  
`obstruct me / fail my test → trash to be cleaned away`.

This is not merely "trauma made him evil." The more specific mechanism is **identification with the evaluator**.

Liang rejects the helpless position not by rejecting the moral system that produced it, but by claiming its authoritative side.

That makes his power almost too perfect a bodily metaphor:

> **Whatever is done to him must be returned, multiplied.**

The central fight is not only a ranking contest. It triangulates three models of what a hero does with suffering.

### Smile

Smile's E21 ethic begins with inward authenticity. A real smile should express genuine happiness rather than perform marketable affect.

His contradiction is that his own body has made smiling compulsory for maximum power.

### Liang

Liang converts pain into entitlement to retaliation.

### Original Nice

Nice is trapped between two pedagogies:

- Smile: preserve genuine conviction beneath hero-image.
- Shang De: you are not here to become a hero; you are a revenge weapon.

E22 forces Nice to act rather than merely remember those alternatives.

> `然后消灭他`

> `你前进的垫脚石`

This is institutional predation disguised as ranking mobility.

The opponent does not matter as a person. Winner and loser are only stages in a career-production pipeline.

Shang De's worldview is therefore structurally close to Liang's even though the men belong to opposing conspiratorial networks.

Liang calls people trash.

Shang De calls them stepping stones.

Both convert persons into disposable functions.

# 9. Liang's ability is substantially resolved

E21 left the relevant input variable open between pain, injury, damage, kinetic force, and other possibilities.

E22 gives Liang's own explicit definition:

> `我的能力是无论什么伤害 / 都能加倍奉还 / 也包括 / 恐惧`

The bounded translation is:

> **Whatever damage I take, I can return it doubled — and that includes Fear.**

This resolves the broad input class from "received punishment" to **damage/harm (`伤害`)**.

It also makes one of the most important mechanics statements in the series:

> **Fear can count as damage inside a power system.**

Fear is therefore not only:

- emotion;
- value;
- field;
- particle-like JP ontology;
- transformation pressure;
- social atmosphere.

For Liang's ability it is an injurious input that can be processed and returned.

## 9.1 What remains open

Do not yet invent a full equation.

E22 does not establish:

- exact storage duration;
- whether every hit is returned separately or accumulated;
- upper ceiling;
- whether doubling is mathematically exact;
- whether returned damage must preserve the original modality;
- whether it must target the original source;
- whether transferred Fear is literally doubled in measurable quantity;
- physiological cost;
- whether some inputs can overload him.

The in-world rule is clear. Its engineering specification is not.

# 11. Fear-corrupted Smile: the smile survives as a nightmare

Smile's transformation is one of the episode's strongest formal sequences.

His body becomes dark/black with neon red and purple accents. His ordinary face is obscured. What remains most legible is a bright, jagged, graphic **smile**.

This is not random monster design.

E21 established:

- smiling is his public sign;
- smiling is his ethical pedagogy;
- smiling is required for full output;
- smiling is also a source of family contradiction.

Fear does not erase this governing structure.

It **inverts** it.

The corrupted Smile demands:

> `刚才是谁在嘲笑我`  
> Who was mocking me just now?

Then:

> `不许笑`  
> Don't laugh.

> `不许嘲笑我`  
> Don't mock me.

The man whose hero identity is built on smiling now experiences other people's laughter as persecution.

The distinction between:

- **smiling with someone**
- and **being laughed at**

collapses.

That is the precise Fear corruption.

## 11.1 Strengthening E19's affective-inversion rule

DJ Shindig established that Fear can reverse the affective direction of an existing ability:

- excitation → grief/depression.

Smile provides a second, more character-specific example:

- authentic/public smile → terror of ridicule and coercive anti-laughter.

This strengthens the cumulative mechanic:

> **Fear often corrupts a bearer along the axis already organizing that bearer's identity or power rather than replacing the power with unrelated aggression.**

That is still not a universal mathematical law, but it is now a repeated pattern.

# 15. Smile's death turns Nice's faith into revenge

Clip 03 resolves the Nice/Liang encounter much more sharply than the still-frame reading.

Liang does not merely incapacitate Fear-corrupted Smile. After the crowd encourages him to get back up, Liang re-enters the fight, calls Smile `垃圾`, and delivers a terminal counterattack.

The continuous sequence shows Smile's corrupted body catastrophically break apart. The aftermath is not an intact unconscious body: it is a **dismembered torso/remains and a scattered pile of dark Fear-stained body matter/gibs** on the pavement.

Nice sees this.

His response is prolonged shock: widened eyes, frozen posture, and visual fixation on the remains. Liang then walks toward him through the aftermath.

Nice is the person who initiates the subsequent physical fight.

This is an important correction to the earlier possibility that his strike might be primarily defensive or protective. Smile is already dead. Nice launches himself at Liang and lands the first clearly aggressive blow, drawing blood, then continues attacking.

The action is best understood as **grief-driven retaliation / vengeance**.

That creates one of the bitterest reversals in Original Nice's arc.

Minutes earlier, Nice's continuing `信仰` in Smile had helped him disobey Shang De's literal wait-for-the-winner order and choose Liang for the Fear injection, plausibly in an attempt to spare Smile.

But once that attempt accidentally contributes to Smile's death, Nice reacts by becoming exactly what Shang De told him to become:

> `我需要一把能复仇的武器`
>
> **I need a weapon for revenge.**

Shang De does not need to issue a fresh command.

> **The revenge identity reasserts itself through Nice's grief.**

Nice therefore tries to honor the person who taught him an authentic heroic smile by acting through the retaliatory grammar that person's teaching opposed.

That is not hypocrisy. It is tragedy: **faith collapses into vengeance at the moment of bereavement.**

Liang immediately recognizes what Nice has given him:

> `谢谢你给了我还手的理由`
>
> **Thank you for giving me a reason to hit back.**

The wording is exact for Liang's moral economy. Nice's revenge supplies Liang with a socially and psychologically legible justification for further revenge.

The loop is now:

`Liang is hurt as a child`
→ `Liang returns dehumanization outward`
→ `Liang kills Smile`
→ `Nice avenges Smile`
→ `Liang claims the right to avenge Nice's attack`.

This is E22's governing recursion made bodily.

# 34. Current provisional synthesis

Episode 22 turns the series' emotional mechanics into a theory of social repetition.

Liang is the purest case because his power states the rule openly:

> hurt me, and I return it doubled.

But virtually everyone around him is living some version of that equation.

Young Liang is called trash; adult Liang cleans up trash.

Shang De is wounded by the past; Nice must become his revenge weapon.

Nice receives public perfection; he becomes terrified of failing public perfection.

Smile receives Fear; his smile becomes fear of laughter.

The public receives partial images; it returns moral certainty.

Yan Mo receives the Zero catastrophe; he responds not by abandoning godhood but by trying to engineer a bearer who can withstand more pain.

The episode therefore asks whether suffering necessarily reproduces itself.

X's intervention offers the first formal answer that does not depend on moral purity:

> **No — but only if someone can break the exchange rather than win it.**

That is why X's almost comic annoyance matters. He has overwhelming power without Liang's emotional need to make the other person suffer equivalently.

The irony is that this very asymmetry makes the public call him a god, feeding the next dangerous feedback loop.

E22 thus ends by solving one recursion and opening another.

→ `Nice retaliates`
→ `X stops the retaliatory escalation`
→ `Nice collapses into grief`.

Nice's revenge attack is therefore not evidence that the Smile attachment was superficial. It is produced by the depth of that attachment.

## 38.5 X as witness rather than comforter

X remains near Nice during the grief sequence but is not shown giving a speech, embracing him, or otherwise forcing an emotional interpretation onto the moment.

His clearest concrete act is recovering the pendant.

The safest formulation is:

> **X witnesses Nice's grief and preserves the intimate object from the death site.**

Whether that act is consciously intended as comfort, respect, evidence preservation, or simple curiosity is not verbalized.

## 38.6 Final E22 object-ethics contrast

Liang uses the pendant to turn private attachment into an attack surface.

X later removes the same pendant from the remains without weaponizing it.

Thus the object participates in E22's broader ethical contrast:

> **One actor converts relation into leverage; another interrupts violence and leaves relation materially recoverable.**

This should not be inflated into proof that X is morally pure. It is a local formal contrast supported by the object's trajectory.
