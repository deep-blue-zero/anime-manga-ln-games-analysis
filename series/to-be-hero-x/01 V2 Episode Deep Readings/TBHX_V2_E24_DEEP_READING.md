# 1. Executive thesis — the founding lie and the right to remain a person

Episode 24 reveals that hero society was founded not by defeating an external demon, but by **narratively expelling its own founding contradiction**.

Zero was the world's first hero. He gathered effectively limitless Trust, became a god worshipped by everyone, and began reconstructing the world according to his own justice. When one hero defied him, Zero killed that hero. Then he killed more. Public Trust converted into Fear.

The Hero Association's founding myth transformed this internal catastrophe into a cleaner story:

> a Demon King called Zero terrorized the world; righteous heroes united; E-Soul delivered the final blow; the Association arose to protect humanity from excessive Trust and future disaster.

That public story preserves the catastrophe while concealing its most politically dangerous fact:

> **the Demon King was a hero produced by the same social relation the new system would continue to organize.**

The Association therefore begins with a structurally useful lie. It externalizes the danger. Instead of admitting that heroism, mass Trust, and self-authorized justice can become tyranny, it creates an exceptional monster outside herohood and builds legitimacy around the heroes who defeated him.

E24 then shows the institution reproducing the very logic it claims to contain.

The Association, Yan Mo, Micky, FOMO interests, and hero offices secretly coordinate to eliminate X before the tournament. Their rationale is not that X has committed tyranny. It is that even a good person might change; the human heart contains uncontrollable Fear; X has hidden his identity and refused institutional management; therefore the risk must be removed before it matures.

The system that fears a hero placing his own judgment above all others decides—secretly and preemptively—that its own judgment may decide whether he is allowed to continue existing.

The proverb used for Zero consequently turns back upon Zero's slayers:

`屠龙者 / 终成恶龙`

> **The dragon-slayer becomes the evil dragon.**

X's answer is not to deny that he could become dangerous. Nor does he simply accept Association custody. His counter-practice is much smaller, stranger, and more radical:

> **preserve the ability to stop being only X.**

He works an ordinary office job. His coworkers prepare the server and network infrastructure for the tournament while unknowingly sitting beside the tournament's reigning apex hero. They say that only X makes them feel safe. At 19:00, he clocks out.

At the episode's end, after revealing that he once mistook a coin for the invitation that began his hero path and that everyone may be pieces arranged by fate, A-Hu asks what he intends to do.

X answers:

`当然是违抗命运的安排`

> **Of course, defy fate's arrangement—**

and then:

`准点下班`

> **clock out on time.**

The joke is the thesis.

Zero became total because godhood consumed the distinction between a person's justice and the world's law. The Association becomes total when protection consumes the distinction between public safety and institutional domination. X resists totalization by preserving a banal boundary: herohood does not own every hour, every name, every appearance, or every possible version of him.

The strongest E24 formulation is therefore:

> **Hero society is founded by hiding that Trust can make a hero sovereign. The Association responds by trying to make sovereignty institutional instead. X's freedom lies neither in apotheosis nor submission, but in representational and role sovereignty—the power to remain a person whose hero identity does not consume the whole self.**

A second governing thesis is:

> **Trust and Fear are not merely energies inside individual bodies. They are historical and narrative forces that topple causal dominoes, carry people into one another's worlds, and make public stories materially consequential. Controlling history is therefore part of controlling power.**

# 2. Episode architecture

E24 has seven large movements.

## Movement I — public society metabolizes the preceding tragedies

News, commentary, corporate messaging, and arbitration absorb the ruins crisis, Smile's death, Original Nice's death, Lin Ling's succession, and Liang Long's violence into a stable tournament narrative.

## Movement II — the public god sits unnoticed inside ordinary labor

Civilian X works among office employees preparing technical infrastructure for tomorrow's tournament. His coworkers praise X without recognizing him. He leaves at the end of his shift.

## Movement III — A-Hu joins the anti-X logic, then confronts its object

A FOMO representative frames the tournament as an X encirclement designed to prevent deification. A-Hu proposes pre-tournament assassination, follows X, and threatens exposure if X refuses to withdraw—yet reveals that he has told no one X's identity.

## Movement IV — institutions attempt preventive murder

The Association and competing hero-industry actors share intelligence and launch an illegal pre-tournament assault. Yan Mo's E22 discovery pays off as X's hidden civilian identity and workplace. FOMO visibility and ranking ambition become part of the operation.

## Movement V — X explains managed hero society

After escaping the first assault, X buys A-Hu a drink and explains that the Association and hero offices cooperate, constrain one another, and jointly manage heroes as pieces. A-Hu's status as a dog-turned-hero becomes proof of manufacture.

## Movement VI — X reveals the forbidden history

X takes A-Hu into a highly stylized historical/world-state and reveals Zero's origin, fall, the Association's falsification, Yan Mo's childhood relation to Zero, and the institutional genealogy of towers, rankings, offices, and FOMO.

## Movement VII — dominoes, fate, tournament

Trust and Fear become a chain of dominoes connecting every prior arc. X reinterprets his coin and his hero path through causality/fate. The top ten gather. Asked what he will do, he declares defiance through the mundane freedom to clock out.

# 3. The opening: catastrophe becomes administration

The first minutes are not a neutral recap. They show how hero society converts private catastrophe into public continuity.

The public discourse mentions:

- the ruins incident before the prior tournament;
- the death of Xiao Yueqing / Original Nice;
- the Association's unreleased investigation;
- Shang De's appeal for rationality and rumor control;
- Tree Man Group's arrangement with Lin Ling;
- a promise to obtain justice for Xiao Yueqing;
- Liang Long's Association arbitration;
- the characterization of Smile as a hero swallowed by Fear;
- Liang's approval to compete at rank three;
- Lin Ling's formal contract;
- the clearing of Tree Man's substitution/fraud scandal;
- the finalization of the top-ten lineup one day before the tournament;
- X's possible fourth consecutive victory.

The sequence performs institutional laundering.

## 3.1 Original Nice's death becomes Tree Man's moral asset

Original Nice's collapse was produced through:

- Shang De's Fear engineering;
- compulsory perfection;
- covert violence;
- Smile's death;
- identity disintegration;
- an institution that valued him as a revenge instrument.

Yet Shang De appears publicly as the sober custodian of truth. He tells the public not to believe or spread rumors. Tree Man promises justice for Xiao Yueqing.

The organization implicated in making the conditions of his destruction can now use his death as a claim to moral seriousness.

> **The dead bearer becomes reputational capital for the institution that captured his hero identity.**

## 3.2 Lin Ling's fraud becomes succession legitimacy

Lin Ling was installed as Nice through substitution and concealment. E24 says Tree Man has formally contracted him and that this has cleared the earlier fraud scandal.

The institution does not undo the substitution.

It legalizes and narratively regularizes it.

> **What began as identity fraud becomes legitimate succession once the organization controls the contract and the story.**

This is a crucial public/private divergence. The public Nice identity continues. The person who first bore it is dead. The substitute is now official. Institutional continuity survives by changing which body is authorized to inhabit the image.

## 3.3 Smile's murder becomes Liang's qualification

The public formulation is that Liang defeated a hero consumed by Fear. This is not entirely false: Smile was Fear-corrupted and dangerous.

It excludes:

- Shang De's plan;
- Nice's injection;
- Liang's redirected Fear;
- Smile's private wound;
- Liang's deliberate killing blow;
- Nice's grief and retaliation.

The Association arbitrates Liang's conduct and admits him at rank three.

The result is structurally similar to Yan Mo's E19 story about A-Sheng:

> **once the victim is narratively stabilized as a monster or corrupted threat, killing can be converted into heroic legitimacy.**

Liang's violence is not hidden. Its causal context is compressed into a story that makes the violence administratively usable.

# 4. Civilian X maintains the infrastructure of his own spectacle

E24's office sequence is one of the episode's most elegant pieces of dramatic irony.

The employees are preparing for the first day of the tournament. They are told to complete server and network-line allocation because access will surge.

X is sitting among them in his civilian presentation.

The public apex hero is therefore also an ordinary employee helping maintain the technical infrastructure through which the public will consume hero competition.

He is simultaneously:

- the object of the spectacle;
- a worker supporting the spectacle's delivery;
- an anonymous person excluded from the public image;
- the individual whose accumulated Trust terrifies the Association.

His coworkers discuss Fear and Liang. One concludes:

`反正我只相信X`

> **I only trust X.**

`有他在我就安心了`

> **As long as he's there, I feel safe.**

X is there.

But the social relation does not recognize him.

This is almost a perfect demonstration of the difference between **Trust in an image** and **knowledge of a person**.

The coworkers' belief contributes to the public X relation while the actual man remains socially ordinary beside them.

At 19:00 he leaves.

The boss and coworkers are expected to continue working late to prepare the tournament. X refuses the total claim of both office labor and hero labor.

This opening is necessary for the final line. “Clock out on time” is not random comedy added after the cosmology. It is a repeated behavior by which X preserves a boundary against roles that would otherwise become total.

# 5. A-Hu and the anti-X imagination

The FOMO representative provisionally tracked as Mr. Azha / Aja describes the objective as:

`围剿X`

> an encirclement/hunt against X,

with the purpose of preventing his deification.

A-Hu immediately notices the contradiction. If mass attention and competition strengthen hero identity, then the ranking tournament may be counterproductive.

The representative answers that the heroes will stop at nothing to become number one.

The anti-X plan therefore uses the competitive structure that may itself intensify X's public centrality.

## 5.1 A-Hu proposes assassination

A-Hu says the solution is to eliminate X secretly before the tournament because X exposed his real identity to him.

This is a serious moral regression from the E23 hero awakening.

A-Hu's chosen telos was:

> become the great hero who protects Xinya.

E24 shows how quickly protection can be translated into preventive violence when a powerful institution defines someone as an existential risk.

A-Hu's reasoning mirrors the Association's:

- X could become a demon;
- prevention must occur before the transformation;
- hidden civilian identity creates an opportunity;
- killing can be justified as protection of everyone else.

Yet A-Hu's behavior is more conflicted than the assassination proposal suggests.

He follows X, confronts him openly, and first demands withdrawal. His threat is to expose X's identity if X refuses.

More importantly, when the attackers arrive, A-Hu says:

`你的身份我和谁都没提过`

> **I haven't told anyone your identity.**

He has preserved the secret even while threatening to use it.

This does not erase his willingness to consider assassination. It shows that A-Hu has not fully converted X into an abstract target category. He still recognizes the civilian person strongly enough to hesitate before betrayal.

The coalition visibly joins actors who normally compete:

Ordinary competition is tolerated so long as every actor remains inside the shared management system. X's refusal to join an office, reveal his identity, or accept Association custody threatens not one organization but the architecture connecting them.

## 7.1 Autonomy is interpreted as domination

The Association official's reasoning is revealing:

- X concealed his identity from the beginning;
- he joined no office;
- he kept escaping the Association's system;
- therefore he apparently wants to stand above everything.

The institution cannot imagine unregulated autonomy as a stable category.

A person with great power must be either:

- managed within the system;

> **The Association mistakes refusal of institutional ownership for a desire for sovereign ownership.**

## 7.2 Precaution becomes a death sentence

The official concedes that X may originally be good.

That does not matter.

The human heart contains uncontrolled fear and desire. Risk must therefore be eliminated tonight, even at cost.

This is preventive violence against a future possibility rather than punishment for an established act.

It echoes Yan Mo's `祸根` logic from E19:

> survival itself becomes the continued existence of future calamity.

And it reproduces Zero's central error:

> one authority decides that its own judgment of the future authorizes irreversible violence in the present.

# 11. Protective lies versus founding lies

A-Hu says his lie was for Xinya. He also says the Association was founded to protect everyone.

E24 does not reject either statement outright.

The Association really was created after a catastrophic hero-god threatened the world. Protection is not an invented motive.

The episode instead asks what happens when protective purpose authorizes control over truth.

## A-Hu's lie

- conceals an anonymous helper;
- protects a relationship;
- gives A-Hu an identity he has chosen to inhabit;
- does not erase the helper from his private memory;
- is corrected relationally at the hospital through acknowledgment of Xinya and community.

## The Association's lie

- erases Zero's origin as a hero;
- externalizes systemic risk into an exceptional Demon King;
- legitimizes the Association as savior;
- prevents public recognition that herohood itself produced the catastrophe;
- supports continuing control of ranking, history, and apotheosis risk.

The distinction is not simply private lie good / institutional lie bad.

It is:

> **Does the lie preserve another person's agency and relation, or does it monopolize causal interpretation so that an institution can decide what kinds of persons and futures are permitted?**

X says it is time to reveal the truth and takes A-Hu into a highly stylized world-state or representational chamber.

Before the Association existed, Zero was the first hero.

He gathered:

`万人敬仰的神`

When one hero defies his will, Zero kills that hero. Then he kills more.

> **Infinite Trust creates capacity and sovereignty, not infallibility.**

This is the central constitutional problem of TBHX.

- public affect can reverse rapidly;
- the same bearer-image can become the object of the opposite affect;
- accumulated Trust does not vanish harmlessly when legitimacy collapses;
- it can become catastrophic Fear.

The Zero event is the largest known Trust→Fear conversion in the setting.

# 14. The Association's founding myth: selective truth, not total fabrication

The public version says:

- a Demon King called Zero terrified the world;
- heroes united against him;
- E-Soul used `电光斩` / Electric Light Slash as the final blow;
- Zero was defeated;
- a monument commemorates friends, family, and heroes killed in the Downfall;
- excessive Trust can catalyze disaster;
- the Association was founded;
- a new hero age began.

Much of this may be factually true at the event level.

The lie is in classification and causal framing.

The Association changes:

> **first hero who became god and tyrant**

into:

> **Demon King defeated by heroes.**

This preserves the lesson “excessive Trust is dangerous” while hiding the lesson “herohood itself can generate the danger.”

The myth therefore accomplishes several governance goals:

1. **Externalization** — evil belongs to the Demon King, not the hero system.
2. **Legitimation** — heroes become the founders/saviors of the new order.
3. **Continuity** — the public can continue trusting heroes without confronting heroism's origin in catastrophe.
4. **Memory management** — Zero is buried as a person and retained as a cautionary monster.
5. **Institutional monopoly** — the Association alone keeps the fuller causal archive.

This is not merely propaganda added after politics.

In a world where collective memory carries Trust and Fear, historical narrative is part of the power system.
