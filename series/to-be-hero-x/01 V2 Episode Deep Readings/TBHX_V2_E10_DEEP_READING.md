# To Be Hero X V2 — Episode 10 Deep Reading

## 1. Scope and analytical posture

Episode 10 is read prospectively from the frozen E01–E09 state. It completes the first major Lucky Cyan movement begun in E08, but it is not allowed to borrow later explanations of Fear, Liu Zhen, Micky, Queen, the Hero Association, the catastrophic air disaster, or the broader chronology. The V1 corpus is held outside the episode-local reading until the explicit revision section.

E09 ended with a partial rather than complete liberation. Qīng escaped the orphanage, learned that meaningful exit requires material self-support, and then discovered that the Sacred Maiden's social meaning followed her into public space. She nevertheless began to re-author the public identity that emerged around her: DOS recruitment became a means to search for Luo, and the concert stage became a place to restore Luo to the provenance of her music and heroic aspiration.

E10 tests whether that re-authorship survives professionalization and scandal.

The answer is deliberately mixed.

DOS does not simply fabricate a false Lucky Cyan. It takes traits that are genuinely Qīng's—music, independence, courage, her guitar, her public visibility—and curates them into an audience-tested hero image. The first scene openly calls this an `人设`, an image/persona strategy, and instructs her to discard the old weak Sacred Maiden presentation. The same institution then tells her never to mention her past.

The episode's central conflict is therefore not **truth versus lies** in the simple sense. Many of the facts circulating about Qīng are true:

- she survived the air disaster;
- she performed as the Sacred Maiden;
- people sought luck from her;
- the orphanage subsequently suffered;
- people around her have experienced misfortune.

What is false or unsupported is the causal and moral interpretation built around those facts: that her survival and good fortune are purchased by stealing fortune from others, that the dead were sacrificed for her, and that every later misfortune is evidence of her parasitic luck.

The Mandarin script makes the distinction unusually explicit.

Micky asks:

> `他们现在传的难道不是事实吗`
>
> **Aren't the things they are spreading facts?**

Qīng answers that the public has distorted the intention and meaning of what she did. Later, when Queen proposes the last surviving evidentiary route into the air disaster, Qīng says:

> `这是属于我的真相`
>
> `必须由我自己去拿回来`
>
> **This is my truth. I have to take it back myself.**

The lexical movement from `事实` (*facts*) to `真相` (*truth; the underlying truth of what happened*) is the organizing language of the episode.

The strongest prospective thesis is:

> **E10 distinguishes truth from interpretation and inheritance from extraction. Qīng's scandal is not solved by pretending the circulating facts are false. She recovers fuller provenance: people on the doomed plane actively entrusted unfinished wishes to the surviving child; Luo's parents gave her the same song they had given Luo; and the "curse" narrative erased the intentions of both the dead and the living. Her self-authorship therefore consists not in becoming self-created, but in reclaiming the right to know, interpret, and choose what inherited meanings will obligate her.**

A second proposition is equally important:

> **The episode literalizes narrative feedback. A zero-sum rumor about luck produces fear; Fear becomes physical; the resulting violence and social breakdown create new misfortune that appears to confirm the rumor. Moral panic manufactures its own evidence.**

And the ending requires one more restraint:

> **Qīng is not simply liberated by the positive narrative that replaces the negative one. "She steals others' fortune" becomes "she carries the hopes of all the dead." The second account is dramatically better grounded in recovered human intention and is one Qīng chooses to uncover, but it can still convert a person into public emotional infrastructure.**

E10 resolves the immediate arc while preserving the series' larger problem of social authorship.

# 3. DOS opens by making hero-image construction explicit

E10 begins not with Qīng's private self but with the professionalization of her image.

Micky presents clothing, equipment, and a reworked guitar:

> `这些是为你准备的新服装和新装备`
>
> These are the new clothes and equipment prepared for you.

> `我们还重新加工了一下你的吉他`
>
> We also reworked your guitar.

Then the language becomes unmistakably strategic:

> `为你打造的新人设还满意吗`
>
> **Are you satisfied with the new persona/image we built for you?**

The Japanese witness uses `イメージ戦略`—image strategy.

The next lines explain the market logic:

> `现在的受众更期待独立`
>
> Today's audience expects independence...

and the Japanese witness completes the intended profile as a strong, independent female image. DOS wants Qīng to:

> `摒弃掉原来柔弱...`
>
> discard the former weak/passive image,

and reconstruct herself publicly as someone who loves music and transmits luck through songs.

**Evidence:** `F-DLG/F-INST`, High.

This is one of the clearest institutional self-descriptions in the series so far. Hero-image construction is not merely something critical viewers infer from advertising. DOS openly treats the public self as a strategic product calibrated to audience demand.

But the episode also complicates the usual "fake persona" critique.

The new image is made from real pieces of Qīng:

- she really loves music;
- Luo really gave her the guitar;
- she really has become more independent;
- she really wants to become capable of helping others;
- her public performances really matter to her.

The institutional move is therefore not primarily invention.

It is **curation**.

> **A marketable persona can be composed of authentic traits while still becoming alienating if an institution decides which truths are allowed to travel with those traits.**

That second half arrives immediately.

DOS tells Qīng:

> `千万别再提及你从前的经历`
>
> **Absolutely do not bring up your past experiences again.**

Micky justifies the caution with:

> `这个世界 / 人言可畏`
>
> **In this world, what people say can be terrifying.**

The institution has understood the reputational risk before Qīng fully does.

This makes E10 a development of E09's provenance-resistance thesis:

> **Qīng's new image can contain authentic material while excluding the history that makes the material hers.**

Her guitar becomes a weapon and a brand object, but DOS would prefer the audience not know the orphanage, Luo, the air disaster, or the unstable history beneath the polished image.

That is **curated authenticity**.

# 4. The guitar: relation becomes infrastructure, then weapon

The guitar is one of the most analytically dense objects in the Lucky Cyan arc.

In E08 it is associated with Luo's ordinary companionship and musical selfhood.

In E09 it becomes:

- a memory object;
- a portable relationship;
- an instrument of subsistence;
- the medium through which Qīng acquires public attention;
- the object she uses while naming Luo as part of her origin.

In E10 DOS technologically refits it:

> `配备了强化输出的最新技术`
>
> equipped with the latest output-enhancing technology,

and:

> `也是一把你的专属武器`
>
> it is also your dedicated/personal weapon.

Qīng immediately objects from a point of inexperience:

> `可我一点战斗经验都没有`
>
> **But I don't have any combat experience at all.**

The institutional answer is that she will be transformed into a "real hero" as efficiently as possible.

The object therefore carries three layers simultaneously:

1. **private relational inheritance** — Luo's guitar;
2. **public cultural production** — Lucky Cyan's musical instrument;
3. **institutional combat technology** — hero weapon.

Later in the episode, its combat form is not primarily punitive. Qīng learns to use it to identify and purify the Fear material affecting people.

This matters because the instrument has not simply been stolen from its relational origin. Its institutional augmentation allows Qīng to perform a form of rescue consistent with her emerging ethics.

The episode therefore resists a binary reading in which institutional transformation necessarily destroys authenticity.

A better claim is:

> **Institutions can add capabilities to a relational object without owning the meaning of the relationship that made the object significant.**

The ethical struggle is over who determines the object's purpose and story.

# 5. Qīng returns to the orphanage because public success has not resolved private obligation

After a successful performance, Qīng asks her driver to take her somewhere else.

The request itself matters because it violates the smooth logic of professional scheduling. She is using the mobility supplied by her new institutional position for a private purpose.

At the orphanage, the director greets her bitterly:

> `大英雄突然良心发现懂得念旧了`
>
> roughly: has the great hero suddenly discovered a conscience and remembered the past?

He tells her that after she left, people stopped coming, material conditions worsened, and:

> `这些年的苦日子都拜你所赐`
>
> **These years of hardship are all thanks to you.**

The structure is familiar from E08–E09:

- Qīng's usefulness benefited the institution;
- she exercised exit;
- the institution lost the benefit;
- the loss is narrated as a wrong committed by Qīng.

This is a crucial distinction between **causal consequence** and **moral responsibility**.

Her departure may really have contributed to a decline in donations/visitors/resources. That does not by itself establish that she was morally obligated to remain in a role she rejected.

Qīng's response is not retaliatory. She offers help:

> `如果可以的话 / 我愿意尽自己所能帮助福利院`
>
> **If possible, I am willing to do everything I can to help the orphanage.**

The Japanese witness makes the practical form more explicit: she can consult the agency and potentially arrange a charity concert or similar assistance.

This is an important example of **chosen obligation after exit**.

She does not say:
- I owe you my life;
- I must return to being the Sacred Maiden;
- you were entitled to keep me.

She says, effectively:
- I am now capable of helping;
- I choose to help in another way.

That is exactly the kind of distinction the V2 personhood framework has been developing.

The director rejects it:

> `别假惺惺的了`
>
> Stop pretending / don't be hypocritical.

and later:

> `福利院也不需要你的施舍`
>
> **The orphanage doesn't need your charity.**

He is no longer merely seeking resources.

He wants recognition of his grievance and, more deeply, recognition of a lost claim over Qīng.

# 6. Luo's absence becomes the opening for a zero-sum ontology of luck

Qīng asks about Luo and learns:

> `洛不在了`
>
> Luo is gone/not here.

Within the E10 boundary, Qīng has already used DOS resources to search for him, so her inability to locate him is a real failure of her new institutional capacity.

The director then attacks the conceptual foundation of her self-understanding:

> `你是不愿相信自己的幸运会给别人带来恶果吧`
>
> **You are unwilling to believe that your luck brings bad consequences to other people, aren't you?**

He tells her to forget Luo because Luo will only become a burden.

The argument is more dangerous than ordinary insult because it connects three true emotional facts:

- Qīng cares about Luo;
- Luo has suffered;
- Qīng possesses extraordinary luck.

The unsupported step is the proposed causal relation between them.

After Qīng leaves, the director gives Luo an even harsher formulation.

He identifies Qīng as:

> `和你父母在同一架飞机上的唯一幸存者`
>
> **the only survivor from the plane your parents were on.**

Then:

> `她所获得的幸运都是有代价的`
>
> **The luck she obtained has a price.**

The price is:

> `他人的不幸`
>
> **other people's misfortune.**

The Japanese witness states the zero-sum proposition just as clearly.

This is the conceptual engine of the episode's scandal:

> **good fortune is not merely unequal; it is stolen.**

The director does not simply tell Luo that Qīng survived while his parents died. He supplies a moral-metaphysical interpretation in which her survival and power are implicated in their deaths.

That interpretation is devastating because it converts survivor asymmetry into culpability.

# 7. The director's betrayal is not that he never cared; it is that entrusted guardianship becomes weaponized information

E10 later reconstructs the director's relationship with Liu Zhen.

Liu had originally wanted Qīng's identity hidden:

> `我最早希望她能隐姓埋名`
>
> **At first I hoped she could live under an obscured/hidden identity.**

He therefore entrusted her to the director.

The Japanese witness makes the original purpose especially clear: he wanted the child to live peacefully without the crash identity defining her.

Liu asks one further favor:

> within the director's control, prevent Qīng's background from leaking.

This retrospectively changes the ethical status of the director's disclosure to Luo.

The man who was entrusted with protecting the child's provenance from public exploitation uses that provenance privately as a weapon in a moment of resentment.

The correct E10 revision is therefore:

> **The director's original care was real, but by E10 he has converted entrusted guardianship into grievance-backed ownership and betrayal.**

This is stronger and more precise than calling him a hypocrite.

His earlier moral goods remain part of the tragedy:

- he did run an institution that cared for children;
- he did offer Qīng opportunities;
- he did once share a protective arrangement with Liu;
- he did dream of helping more people.

The same history later becomes his entitlement narrative:

> I helped you;
> I invested hope in you;
> your success was built here;
> your departure damaged us;
> therefore your independence is betrayal.

That is why the episode's moral critique is stronger if his care is not erased.

# 8. Rumor works by converting correlation into a moral law

The public backlash does not require a fabricated event.

People begin repeating the zero-sum theory in ordinary examples:

- if one person gets a fortunate job, someone else remains without work or food;
- if someone finds money, another person must have lost it;
- if Qīng rises, perhaps someone else must fall.

The commentary then folds in a conspicuous recent ranking result involving Queen and an unknown opponent. The relevant prospective fact is only that Queen unexpectedly lost a ranking match to someone publicly treated as insignificant.

**Do not identify the opponent from later knowledge.**

The insinuation is that Lucky Cyan's existence or proximity somehow explains the misfortune.

The rhetoric then expands to anyone connected with her.

This is a classic attribution cascade:

1. identify a salient beneficiary;
2. identify unrelated losses around her;
3. narrate the losses as the hidden payment for the benefit;
4. treat each new bad event as confirmation of the rule.

The episode deliberately makes the logic intuitive before making it monstrous.

Zero-sum luck sounds plausible because ordinary resources can be zero-sum. A found wallet generally belonged to somebody. A competitive job has one winner and many losers.

But the public extrapolates from ordinary distribution to a universal metaphysical law.

That inferential leap is never demonstrated.

The difference between **plausible story** and **established mechanism** is precisely what the episode later recovers.

# 9. Moral panic becomes materially self-confirming

The social reaction escalates.

Public anxiety and anger are described as spreading:

> `这种不安和疯狂的情绪正在蔓延和扩散`
>
> **This anxious and crazed emotion is spreading.**

The orphanage then experiences food poisoning, and the entire District 70 becomes associated with Qīng's supposedly contaminating fortune. Other events—illness, digestive trouble, financial problems, injuries—are narratively swept into the same causal basket.

The director gives the grievance its most personal form:

> `要不是你`
>
> **If it weren't for you...**

and blames Qīng for the destruction of his dream.

The episode then formalizes this phrase visually and mechanically.

People repeat variants of:

> `不都是你的错`
>
> isn't it all your fault?

> `要不是你`
>
> if it weren't for you...

As these grievances accumulate, black-violet material appears on or around people. Bodies and behavior become distorted. Fear is no longer only an internal psychological state or a public mood.

It becomes an observable causal force.

This produces one of E10's strongest system-level insights:

> **The rumor does not need to have been true in order to create evidence that looks like proof of the rumor.**

The sequence can be stated as:

> **zero-sum narrative -> fear and resentment -> Fear manifestation -> violence/disaster -> new misfortune -> apparent confirmation of the zero-sum narrative**

This is a self-validating feedback loop.

The series' metaphysics therefore makes social epistemology physically dangerous.

A false causal interpretation can generate real consequences, and those consequences can then be cited as evidence that the interpretation was correct.

This is more sophisticated than "belief makes things real." It is:

> **belief can alter the environment in ways that corrupt later attempts to infer what was originally true.**

# 10. Qīng is a trigger, not the origin of District 70's Fear

The private Liu Zhen/Micky discussion supplies an essential causal limit.

One speaker notes that Fear has broken out in District 70.

The next line states:

> `只是小青给了它们一个契机`
>
> **Qīng merely gave them an opportunity/trigger.**

The following explanation emphasizes that people are prone to superstition around luck and are easily moved by fear when misfortune strikes.

This wording should block a common overstatement.

E10 does **not** establish:

> Qīng created Fear.

It supports:

> **the public controversy around Qīng supplied a socially potent occasion through which existing human fear could aggregate and manifest.**

That is a much more important system claim because it treats Fear as a latent social capacity rather than a personal emanation belonging to one hero.

The episode also makes clear that Liu Zhen and Micky know more about Fear than the public institutions have yet acknowledged.

They say:

> `有些事就瞒不住了`
>
> **Some things will no longer be possible to keep hidden.**

and describe the crisis opportunistically:

> `幸运有时候会奖励给我们这些有准备的人`
>
> **Sometimes luck rewards those of us who are prepared.**

The imagery places them together in a private observational/strategic frame.

The safe prospective conclusion is:

> **Liu Zhen and Micky possessed prior non-public knowledge of Fear and recognize strategic value in its emergence.**

The episode does **not** yet prove that:
- they caused the outbreak;
- they engineered the rumors;
- they created Fear;
- they are responsible for a specific experimental program.

Those propositions remain open.

> `信赖值今天又跌出新低`

The earlier Qīng arc showed that accumulated Trust can survive private doubts. E10 shows that sufficiently powerful narrative reinterpretation can reverse that accumulation quickly.

This is not a simple popularity meter.

The public is revising what Qīng **is understood to be**.

# 12. `事实` and `真相`: the episode's central epistemological distinction

Qīng proposes holding a press conference and clarifying the facts.

Micky stops her:

> `事实`
>
> **Facts?**

Qīng says she wants to explain her background and what happened at the orphanage.

Micky asks:

> `他们现在传的难道不是事实吗`
>
> **Aren't the things they're spreading facts?**

He lists:

- she is the air-disaster survivor;
- she did act as the Sacred Maiden;
- she did distribute/mediate luck in the orphanage setting.

Qīng answers:

> `但他们歪曲了我做这些事的本意`
>
> **But they distorted my original intention in doing those things.**

> `所以我才要去扭转他们的想法`
>
> **That's why I have to change their way of thinking about it.**

The Japanese witness makes Micky's counterpoint even more explicit: people see facts, fill in causal stories through imagination, and then take the result as truth.

This exchange should be treated as one of the main language passages of the entire V2 corpus so far.

The episode refuses two bad epistemologies.

### Bad epistemology 1: "the facts are false"

Qīng cannot simply deny the underlying events.

### Bad epistemology 2: "facts speak for themselves"

Micky understands that facts never arrive socially without interpretation.

The problem is therefore:

> **Who gets to construct the causal and moral relationship among true events?**

That question links E10 directly to the larger TBHX concern with public authorship.

A list of facts can be accurate and still produce a false story when:

- relevant context is missing;
- temporal sequence is collapsed;
- intentions are erased;
- correlation is treated as cause;
- the testimony of absent/dead actors is excluded;
- a social identity supplies the explanatory template before evidence is interpreted.

This is why the episode's later move from `事实` to `真相` matters.

`真相` is not "my feelings are more true than facts."

It is the effort to recover the **underlying event structure and human intentions that make the facts intelligible**.

# 13. Qīng cannot yet explain the metaphysical origin of her luck

Micky presses the hardest unresolved question:

> `你一出生就拥有幸运的能力吗`
>
> Did you possess the power of luck from birth?

The Japanese witness frames the challenge as whether Qīng can actually explain why she has luck.

She cannot.

This is important because the episode does not allow the protagonist to win simply by asserting subjective sincerity.

Qīng knows:

- what she intended;
- that she did not want to hurt others;
- that the zero-sum accusation feels wrong.

But she does **not** yet possess a complete ontology of her own power.

That limitation keeps the source epistemically disciplined.

The later flight recording will disprove or undermine specific accusations about the crash and restore erased human intentions. It will not constitute a scientific proof of the universal mechanics of luck.

The V2 artifact must preserve this boundary.

# 14. Queen recognizes Qīng outside the luck function

Queen enters as the person who identifies the remaining evidentiary route.

Qīng addresses her:

> `女王前辈`
>
> **Queen-senior / senior Queen.**

This is the first episode-primary source stabilization of Queen within the V2 prospective corpus and adds an explicit senior/junior relationship marker.

Qīng apologizes:

> `没能给你带来好运气`
>
> **I couldn't bring you good luck.**

The apology is revealing. Even after E08–E09, Qīng still partially measures her relational value through the luck she can provide.

Queen rejects that framing:

> `恰恰相反`
>
> **Quite the opposite.**

> `是你的歌声给了我力量`
>
> **It was your singing that gave me strength.**

> `帶我走出了阴霾`
>
> **It brought me out of the gloom.**

This is one of the healthiest recognition scenes in the project.

- your luck changed my ranking;

That recognition matters because music is an expressive action with provenance:
- Luo;
- `My Color`;
- Qīng's own performance;
- the public-facing career she has partially chosen.

Queen therefore recognizes something Qīng **does and communicates**, not merely a metaphysical benefit Qīng passively emits.

The E10 relational principle is:

> **Healthy recognition can weaken a coercive identity by valuing the person through a different causal channel than the one around which the public myth is organized.**

Queen's relationship is still only partially visible at this boundary, but this moment is strong evidence for it.

# 15. `这是属于我的真相`: truth recovery as an act of agency

Queen explains that public attention has converged on the air disaster and that Qīng's origin may contain an answer.

She knows that one surviving cabin recording exists:

> `当年有一份机舱内仅存的录音资料`
>
> There was one surviving piece of recorded cabin material from that time.

It remains with the orphanage director:

> `还保存在福利院的院长那里`

Qīng immediately says:

> `我现在就去把它找出来`
>
> **I'll go find it now.**

Queen warns that District 70 has effectively become hell, and the Japanese witness indicates that given Qīng's current weakened Trust, another hero could be sent.

Qīng refuses substitution.

Her wording is the ethical center of the episode:

> `这是属于我的真相`
>
> **This is the truth that belongs to me / my truth.**

> `必须由我自己去拿回来`
>
> **I have to take it back myself.**

**Evidence:** `F-DLG`, High.

The possessive `我的` should not be read as relativism.

Qīng is not claiming that truth changes according to who speaks.

She is claiming a **right and responsibility of retrieval** over facts and history that have constituted her public identity while being withheld from her.

The distinction can be expressed as:

> **Truth is not hers because she can invent it. It is hers because other people have been using her history to define her while she has been denied access to that history.**

This is an unusually strong formulation of epistemic personhood.

A person's identity can be violated not only by false statements but by being excluded from the evidence through which everybody else interprets her life.

# 16. Micky's support is aligned interest, not altruism

The institution's interests temporarily align with Qīng's:

- Qīng wants the truth for personal, relational, and moral reasons;
- DOS wants a reputational reversal;
- both therefore benefit from retrieving the recording.

This is a useful refinement of the E09 category **resource-dependent consent**.

Not every institutional action must be classified as:
- coercion, or
- benevolence.

A third category is:

> **interest alignment under unequal power.**

The same act can serve Qīng's self-authorship and DOS's brand recovery at once.

The ethical evaluation therefore depends on whether the institution lets her purpose remain actionable after the crisis, not on whether it also benefits.

# 17. Fear makes collective interpretation corporeal

Returning to District 70, Qīng notices strange material attached to people.

The episode's dialogue eventually names the outbreak as Fear, though the Association's formal term comes only at the end.

The victims' repeated grievances convert social accusation into embodied threat.

Visually, the black/violet material does several things:

- adheres to bodies;
- distorts silhouettes;
- intensifies aggressive behavior;
- produces grotesque physical transformations;
- creates a common visual grammar across otherwise unrelated resentments.

This is important because the episode is not merely saying "fear makes people irrational."

It creates a system in which a social emotion becomes **interpersonally transmissible material power**.

The public story about Qīng therefore has two kinds of causality:

1. **ordinary social causality** — rumors change behavior, demand, ticket sales, donations, and relationships;
2. **metaphysical social causality** — fear aggregates into physical force capable of producing additional harm.

Trust and Fear are thus increasingly symmetrical as social forces without yet being established as mechanically identical opposites.

# 18. Lucky Cyan's combat ethic: purification rather than punishment

The object of attack is not the person as enemy.

> **Qīng's heroic force is restorative in aim: isolate the harmful causal layer, remove it, preserve the person.**

This should not be universalized into a series-wide statement yet. But it is a strong local contrast with:
- spectacle combat for ranking;
- coercive violence;
- destructive hero/villain role expectations.

Her first major combat sequence makes the distinction between **defeating a person** and **freeing a person from a harmful condition** unusually legible.

This strengthens the V1 intuition that Qīng's power is most coherent when it heals without possessing.

They actively say:

- carry this;
- remember;
- tell the person I love;

That distinction is the moral reversal of the episode.

# 24. Inheritance is not extraction

E08–E09 produced a strong critique of hope becoming extractive.

E10 requires that thesis to be revised rather than merely strengthened.

The flight recorder demonstrates a form of hope that is not imposed by an institution or audience on an unwilling recipient.

The passengers voluntarily give it.

That produces an essential distinction:

### Extractive hope
Another person or institution says:
- your gift helps us;
- therefore you owe us its continued use;
- therefore your refusal is betrayal.

### Entrusted hope
A person facing the end of his or her own agency says:
- I cannot continue;
- if you survive, I hope you will carry something I value;
- the gift is made toward your continued life rather than against it.

The difference is not whether obligation exists emotionally.

Qīng may indeed feel responsible to the dead.

The difference is the **direction of agency**.

The passengers' wishes are oriented toward preserving the child's life and future.

The director's later demand is oriented toward controlling what that future must become.

The better V2 principle after E10 is therefore:

> **Inheritance becomes extractive when the inherited meaning is used to deny the inheritor revision, refusal, or personhood. Inheritance can instead be relationally generative when it is freely entrusted and carried through the survivor's continuing authorship.**

This is a major revision to any simpler "hope is a burden" reading.

# 25. Luo's parents connect Qīng and Luo before they meet

Near the end of the recorder sequence comes a decisive line:

> `晚安了洛`
>
> **Good night, Luo.**

The Japanese witness clearly identifies Luo's father and mother as the speakers around this moment.

This establishes that the adults who:
- had a child of similar age;
- sang a familiar family song;
- wished that child health;
- urged Qīng to live,

were Luo's parents.

That discovery radically changes the Qīng/Luo relationship without reducing it to destiny.

They were connected before meeting, but neither knew how.

Luo's parents gave Qīng something they had already given Luo:
- a song;
- tenderness;
- a vision of continued life.

Qīng later meets Luo and receives the song again through him.

The relationship is therefore not founded on metaphysical replacement.

Qīng is not "Luo's substitute child."
Luo is not "the owner" of what his parents gave her.

Instead, both are heirs to the same relational act from different sides.

That is a much healthier form of shared inheritance than the zero-sum story.

# 26. `My Color` is a circular relational inheritance

After hearing the recording, Qīng recognizes the melody:

> `我好像在哪里听过`
>
> **I feel like I've heard it somewhere.**

The episode then returns to the earlier memory of Luo calling it:

> `我的原创曲`
>
> **my original composition,**

> `《MyColor》`

and explaining that the melody had always seemed imprinted in his mind.

Qīng had also found it strangely familiar.

E10 now supplies the missing provenance.

Luo's parents sang the song to Luo.
They sang it again to Qīng during the crash.
Years later Luo reproduces the melody from childhood memory and calls it his composition.
Qīng recognizes it without knowing why.

This does not make Luo a fraud.

The episode's point is not copyright ownership.

The song has become a **relational loop**.

It moves:

> parents -> Luo  
> parents -> Qīng  
> Luo -> Qīng again  
> Qīng -> public

Each transmission changes context without erasing the previous link.

This gives `My Color` unusual thematic force.

A "color" can be one's own while still having history.

The title does not require self-creation.

It supports the E10 personhood principle:

> **What is mine can have come from others without belonging to them more than it belongs to me.**

That is an elegant answer to the series' recurrent anxiety about inheritance.

The ability to return to triviality is evidence that neither is fully reduced to the trauma.

The narrative polarity changes.

# 29. The positive myth is better—but it is still a mythic compression

The episode strongly prefers the recovered story to the curse story, and for good reason.

The recording supplies evidence that:
- the crash had ordinary catastrophic causes already underway;
- passengers explicitly wanted Qīng to live;
- Luo's parents consciously gave her care and a song;
- the zero-sum accusation erased the dead people's own intentions.

But the final public formulation still compresses many individual people into:

> **"all the victims' hope carried by Lucky Cyan."**

That is meaningful, but it is not the same thing as a complete historical account.

This matters because E08's problem can return in positive form.

The Sacred Maiden had to be pure because everyone needed her blessing.

Now Lucky Cyan may be expected to embody:
- survival;
- collective hope;
- the wishes of the dead;
- inspirational positivity.

The episode does not yet show whether she can fail under that new symbolic burden.

The correct prospective ending is therefore not:

> Qīng finally discovers her true destiny and is free.

It is:

> **Qīng successfully defeats a predatory interpretation of her history by recovering better provenance and choosing to act on it. The public then converts that richer history into a new positive hero myth whose future demands remain unresolved.**

This preserves both the victory and the danger.

# 36. Hero-image/personhood model through Episode 10

The cumulative model now contains ten local cases:

1. **E01 — overwrite:** public belief imposes an already-valued identity on Lin.
2. **E02 — alignment/override:** collective belief can follow or redirect a person's desire.
3. **E03 — essentialization:** situational virtue becomes permanent obligation.
4. **E04 — responsive amplification:** self-authored action precedes Trust attaching to the disclosed person.
5. **E05 — voluntary role mediation:** an inherited performed identity can be authentic when chosen and enacted before power.
6. **E06 — contested co-authorship:** a successful identity becomes social property contested by institutions/publics.
7. **E07 — authored conditions:** choices remain real while hidden actors design the field in which those choices become likely or costly.
8. **E08 — sacralized utility / fallibility capture:** real social benefit becomes compulsory purity and availability.
9. **E09 — chosen re-authoring under persistent mythology:** the person exits direct ownership but accumulated public meaning follows; she begins deciding what the new public role will serve.
10. **E10 — provenance reclamation / contextual self-authorship:** the person recovers suppressed history and uses it to contest the causal meaning imposed on true facts, while accepting freely entrusted inheritance without conceding ownership of her future.

The strongest cumulative personhood principle after E10 is:

> **Personhood does not require being self-created. It requires enough agency to know the histories that formed you, distinguish inheritance from ownership, distinguish factual history from imposed causal interpretation, and decide how inherited obligations will be carried or revised.**

E10 also introduces a useful epistemic condition of freedom:

> **A person cannot meaningfully author an identity if everyone else has access to decisive evidence about her past while she does not.**

# 45. Final prospective synthesis

Episode 10 begins with professionals deciding what Lucky Cyan should look like and ends with society deciding what Lucky Cyan means.

Between those two acts of authorship, Qīng makes a more difficult claim:

> **the history being used to define me is also history I have the right to know.**

That is why `事实` and `真相` matter so much.

The scandal is powerful precisely because it is not made of pure inventions. Qīng really survived. She really was the Sacred Maiden. People really did seek luck from her. The orphanage really did suffer after she left. Luo's parents really died on the same plane from which she emerged alive.

The curse story makes those facts emotionally legible by giving them a single zero-sum cause:

> her fortune is their misfortune.

Once that story circulates, *To Be Hero X* gives it metaphysical teeth. Fear grows. People become violent. New disasters occur. The world starts producing the kind of evidence the rumor predicted.

Qīng cannot solve that by saying "none of this happened."

She has to recover missing causality.

The recording does not turn her into a metaphysician who suddenly knows everything about luck. Instead, it restores something more ethically important: the intentions of people who had been turned into passive victims in somebody else's story.

They did not say:
> she stole our lives.

They said:
> live.

Luo's parents did not become anonymous fuel for Lucky Cyan. They remained parents thinking about Luo, singing the song they knew from home, and giving tenderness to another frightened child because their own child was unreachable.

That makes Qīng's survival an inheritance before it is a public symbol.

And inheritance is where the episode most decisively advances the series' personhood argument.

A person is never fully self-created. Qīng's music comes from Luo; Luo's melody comes from his parents; the same melody reaches Qīng before she consciously meets him; her hero career uses equipment built by DOS; Queen gives her the clue that makes truth recovery possible; the dead leave wishes she later carries.

None of those relationships automatically owns her.

The ethical question is whether what comes from others becomes:
- a gift she can interpret,
- an obligation she chooses,
- a resource she can use,

or:
- a debt other people use to control her.

That is the line between entrusted inheritance and extraction.

The episode's resolution is therefore real but not absolute.

Qīng defeats the most poisonous interpretation of her life.
She purifies people instead of punishing them.
She finds Luo.
She learns why `My Color` felt familiar.
She retrieves the historical evidence herself.
Her Trust returns.
She reaches Rank 7.

Yet the public immediately constructs another sacred story:

> Lucky Cyan carries the hopes of all the dead.

That story is kinder, better evidenced, and much closer to what the dead themselves actually said.

It is still a story capable of becoming a demand.

The E10 freeze should therefore end on the same question the Qīng arc has been refining since E08, now in its strongest form:

> **Can Qīng carry inherited hope without becoming owned by it—and can society value what she carries while still allowing her to remain a person who can fail, refuse, change, and live for reasons that are not public property?**

That question remains open entering Episode 11.
