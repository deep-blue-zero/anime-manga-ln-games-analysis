# Character, Relationship, and Recognition Ledger

## Lin Ling ↔ Xiao Yueqing

### E01 baseline

- Lin knew/admired Xiao Yueqing before becoming Nice.
- His admiration is explicitly inspirational: dream, self-improvement, belief, protection.
- He truthfully says she is not *his* girlfriend.
- Public audience recodes his admiration as a romantic Nice/Xiao Yueqing origin story.
- Xiao Yueqing is Original Nice's long-term cohabiting partner and knows him better than most people.

### Recognition problem

Lin's relation to her is asymmetrical: he knows a mediated celebrity and advertising subject; she has intimate knowledge of the person he is replacing. She is therefore positioned to test the difference between **publicly convincing identity** and **relational recognition**.

**Romantic-status rule after E01:** do not call Lin/Xiao Yueqing mutually romantic. Lin's devotion is clear; reciprocity toward Lin as Lin is not established.

## Lin Ling ↔ Juān jiě

A coercive producer/carrier relationship.

- Juān jiě sees Lin first as witness/material.
- She disciplines affect: no apology, no self-doubt.
- She understands and exploits Trust embodiment.
- Lin eventually accepts the role she imposed.

Open question: whether their relationship can develop into mutual recognition or remains fundamentally instrumental.

## Lin Ling ↔ Original Nice

They barely interact before Nice dies, but Nice becomes the structure through which Lin's life is rewritten.

E01 does not support admiration, rivalry, hatred, or inheritance-by-consent. This is **nonconsensual succession** from Lin's perspective and unavailable consent from Nice.

## Lin Ling ↔ Former boss

- Employer dismisses Lin's hero dream and individuality.
- Employer scapegoats/fires him.
- Later, public false narrative says Lin died because of Nice/Treeman's conduct.
- Boss's contempt becomes Lin's internalized low-point voice.

This is a recognition relationship defined by **misrecognition of potential**: the boss sees ordinary status as destiny.

## Juān jiě ↔ Original Nice

She knows of his instability and calls his death a choice. Her exact emotional bond is unclear. Do not infer indifference solely from operational speed after his death.

## Xiao Yueqing ↔ Original Nice

Confirmed long-term cohabitation and public romantic partnership. Their private emotional quality is not yet known.

## Recognition motif — E01

The episode distinguishes at least four modes of being “known”:

1. **public recognition** — audiences know “Nice”;
2. **professional recognition** — Lin knows Xiao Yueqing's ad history through labor;
3. **intimate recognition** — Xiao Yueqing knows Nice closely enough to threaten substitution;
4. **self-recognition** — Lin must decide what the Nice role means to his own desire.

Later character ledgers should test which mode is strongest under Trust mechanics.


# Episode 02 relationship update

## Lin Ling ↔ Xiao Yueqing

E02 moves the pair from asymmetric parasocial familiarity toward **private mutual recognition**.

- Xiao immediately recognizes that Lin is not Original Nice and refuses to keep pretending in private.
- She asks his real name and deliberately uses `林凌`.
- Lin's advertising labor reveals that his prior knowledge of her was not only fandom; he helped construct some of her public-facing media.
- Xiao's earlier public image genuinely encouraged Lin during a low point, creating reciprocal influence before reciprocal acquaintance.
- Their public “romance” remains imposed, but a chosen private attachment grows inside it.
- Lin eventually asks whether they might continue some version of the relationship voluntarily; this is different from accepting the contractual couple script.
- Xiao sincerely says her days with Lin were happier than the previous years, yet still chooses departure/freedom.

**Status after E02:** mutual affection/attachment is strongly supported; permanent romance is not yet established. Most importantly, the episode permits **care plus separation**.

## Xiao Yueqing ↔ Original Nice

Xiao says the long-running public relationship was `就是份工作 / ただのビジネス` from her perspective. Do not infer a three-year private romance from public couple branding.

## Original Nice ↔ Wreck

A major new relational history:

- once close friends / co-aspiring heroes;
- King accepts the “nemesis” role to help Nice gain Trust and treats that contribution as a substitute route to his own hero dream;
- Nice later stops replying/withdraws;
- King experiences the loss as collapse of both friendship and his derivative hero aspiration.

This is the first clear case where hero-image production consumes **another person's life project**, not only the central hero's.

## Lin Ling ↔ Wreck

Lin inherits the nemesis relation institutionally, even though the relational history belongs to Original Nice. He takes responsibility for confronting King because the Nice role has made that history operationally his; King nevertheless recognizes him as a different person.

## Xiao Yueqing ↔ Juān jiě

Managerial relationship is openly coercive: Juān jiě prioritizes brand/couple continuity over Xiao's contractual exit, freedom of movement, and refusal of marriage content.

## True Eye ↔ public / Spotlight

Public Trust collapses after his E01 failure, but his private investigation becomes more accurate. Spotlight exploits that epistemic/social isolation by offering a Fear-based identity with greater power.

## Recognition motif — E02 revision

E02 now gives three distinct channels capable of resisting mass-authored identity:

1. **intimate/private proximity** — Xiao recognizes Lin is not Nice;
2. **embodied relational memory** — King recognizes the wrong opponent/body-history despite appearance;
3. **forensic/media analysis** — True Eye detects anomalies through recorded data.

Public belief is powerful, but it is not omniscient.

# Episode 04 update

## Lin ↔ Xiao Yueqing

- Lin identifies the decisive moment of wanting to become a hero as the emergence of a person in his heart whom he would protect at any cost; E04 strongly codes Xiao as that person.
- This is not merely rank aspiration or celebrity admiration. It is a concrete relational commitment that survives loss of Nice identity and Trust.
- Counterpressure: defining Xiao as "the person I protect" could become another imposed relational role. E04 partially resists this danger by ending with Lin returning Xiao's spatial-transfer device, restoring her means of movement rather than fixing her in place.

**Relationship state:** `reciprocal recognition + strong protective/romantic coding; formal relationship label unresolved`.

## Lin ↔ Juān jiě

E04 turns their relationship into explicit conflict over authorship. Juān jiě says believers created Nice and that the hero must bear their shackles. Lin answers by rejecting Nice before he knows the full positive future. She remains inside the system that tracks and can potentially monetize his new Trust.

**Relationship state:** `managerial dependency -> authorship conflict -> adaptive post-confession coexistence unresolved`.

## Lin ↔ Firm Man (retrospective carry-forward inside E04)

Firm Man's `不管是谁做你自己就好` returns as memory at the exact moment Lin accepts becoming ordinary again. This makes Firm Man's E03 recognition causally important to Lin's E04 self-authorship without implying Firm Man knew every factual detail of the substitution.

## Lin ↔ public

E04 establishes a new relational category: public recognition can move from **misrecognition** (Lin-as-Nice) to **name-based recognition** (Lin-as-Lin). The public is still volatile and commodifying, but it becomes capable of returning authorship of prior acts to the person.

## God Eye ↔ Lin

God Eye forces disclosure but cannot recognize a flawed person as heroic. Lin refuses the perfect-image standard by admitting imperfection and acting anyway. Their conflict is therefore epistemic and ethical, not merely physical.

E-Soul rescued Yang after his parents' deaths and asked his name. Sixteen years later, Yang's adult identity remains organized around this recognition. His stated goal is initially to stand `堂堂正正` before E-Soul, not to become a hero himself.

The anniversary speech and roadside rescue begin converting the relation from **rescued child -> admirer seeking rescuer's gaze** into **value inheritance -> independent action**. Autonomy is emerging, not complete.

Yòuzi is a socially isolated child who becomes Yang's most important local recognizer. He criticizes fake effects, insists the earlier performance was not imitation, identifies Yang's suppressed hero desire, and later believes in/calls to `魂电` during the rescue.

**Relationship state:** `critical young audience -> mutual recognition -> rescued child / first believer`.

Yang has a longstanding unspoken romantic attachment and is ashamed to let Xia see his suit-actor work. Xia cares about Yòuzi, seeks help from the masked performer because her brother connected with him, and independently invites Yang to a Lucky Cyan concert after his failed audition.

E05 supports care and social closeness but not confirmed reciprocal romance.

**Relationship state:** `longtime classmate + Yang crush + available ordinary intimacy blocked by Yang's shame`.

## Yang Cheng ↔ Shang Chao

Shang is introduced as hero-business oriented and institutionally legible. He appears within MG selection and ends the episode by recognizing the masked rescuer as `杨澄`.

**Relationship state:** `social/institutional contrast -> direct recognition; motive unresolved`.

## Yang Cheng ↔ Yan Mo

Yan Mo functions as employer/mentor and encourages Yang to show Xia his real self. He also articulates a pragmatic theory of belief via the café “love power spot.”

**Relationship state:** `ordinary-life mentor/employer; deeper agenda unresolved`.

## Recognition-model revision

E02–E04 emphasized recognition that names the person beneath an imposed identity. E05 adds **recognition of authenticity within performance**. Yòuzi can call Yang “E-Soul” without the episode treating the act as automatically equivalent to Lin's earlier erasure under Nice.

# Phase 2 stabilization — canonical relationship and recognition model

## P2 authority rule

This section is the current **Phase-2 authority** for full-series relationship and recognition analysis.

The earlier episode entries remain intact because relationship meaning changes through delayed evidence. A later disclosure may strengthen, revise, or reject an earlier interpretation without erasing what the earlier episode allowed a viewer to know.

The stabilized ledger distinguishes:

- **recognition** — responding to another as a person with a history, perspective, and capacity to answer;
- **classification** — assigning a socially usable category;
- **projection** — treating another as the carrier of one's own need, ideal, fear, or unfinished project;
- **testimony** — carrying evidence of another person's conduct into a social field that may not believe them;
- **care** — action directed toward another's good, which may still become coercive if it excludes their answer;
- **relationship infrastructure** — the material and social conditions through which recognition can persist;
- **protected uncertainty** — relations the source leaves incomplete.

The governing proposition is:

> **Relationships in To Be Hero X are not merely private refuge from public power. They are one of the primary sites where identities are created, recognized, distorted, repaired, inherited, and made causally effective.**

A relation can resist public capture. It can also become the first instrument of capture.

## P2-RR-01 — recognition is not the same as accurate identification

A person may know someone's legal name and still fail to recognize them as a person.

Conversely, a role-name can mediate genuine recognition.

Examples:

- Xiao Yueqing's use of `林凌` protects a personhood channel beneath Nice.
- Yang Cheng can be genuinely recognized through E-Soul when the role expresses his prior conduct.
- Xinya initially misdescribes A-Hu as a hero but recognizes him first as family and later receives his reciprocal answer.
- The public can identify Nice correctly as a brand while knowing almost nothing about Original Nice.
- Wang Yi can know Nuonuo's preferences through surveillance while remaining outside reciprocal recognition.

The stabilized distinction is:

> **Identification answers “which entity is this?” Recognition answers “can this being answer me as a historically situated person?”**

## P2-RR-02 — recognition is reciprocal answerability, not benevolent perception

The series repeatedly presents one person who “sees” another.

That perception becomes ethically significant only when the recognized person can answer, correct, refuse, or revise the account.

Wolf Girl remembers Firm Man's vulnerability. Her memory is corrective because it restores traits the public image excludes. Her later coercive plan is not automatically justified by that insight.

The orphanage director sees Qing's real capacity to inspire hope. His care becomes possession because Qing's answer—she does not want the sacred role—is treated as a failure to understand her own value.

Shang De knows Original Nice's genuine heroic aspiration well enough to weaponize it.

Recognition without answerability can therefore become **high-resolution instrumentalization**.

The Phase-2 rule is:

> **The more accurately someone understands another person's wound or desire, the more dangerous that knowledge becomes when the other person has no authority over how it is used.**

## P2-RR-03 — full-series recognition modes

| Mode | Description | Representative cases | Personhood effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Substitutive recognition** | Person is treated as interchangeable carrier of an existing role | Lin installed as Nice; brand continuity | Erases provenance and makes role more real than carrier |
| **Private counter-recognition** | Intimate/embodied familiarity detects what mass image excludes | Xiao→Lin; Wreck→Original Nice discontinuity; Wolf Girl→Firm Man | Preserves person-level truth against public myth |
| **Role-mediated recognition** | Chosen or inherited role becomes a truthful medium of action | Yang Cheng/E-Soul; A-Hu as Xinya's hero | Can enlarge personhood when role remains revisable |
| **Sacralizing recognition** | Person is loved as indispensable blessing, hope, or ideal | Qing/Sacred Maiden; Smile/public children | Positive valuation totalizes function and suppresses fallibility |
| **Conditional managerial recognition** | Person is valued according to usefulness, rank, performance, or strategic fit | Shang De→Nice; managers→heroes | Makes continuity of value conditional on role performance |
| **Testimonial recognition** | One person carries evidence of another's conduct to skeptical publics/institutions | Nuonuo→Luo Li; Xia Qing→Yang; Wolf Girl→Firm Man | Corrects social misrecognition when testimony remains credible |
| **De-totalizing recognition** | Relation reveals that a person is more than the function assigned to them | Luo→Qing; Xinya↔A-Hu; Little Johnny↔Big Johnny | Expands available identity and preserves fallibility |
| **Projective recognition** | Other is read through recognizer's dream, grievance, or unfinished self | orphanage director→Qing; Yan Mo→Liang/Zero; Shang De→Nice | Apparent care/support becomes possession |
| **Surveillant recognition** | Detailed knowledge is gathered without reciprocal access | Wang Yi→Nuonuo | Can be motivated by love while denying consent and mutuality |
| **Adversarial recognition** | Opponents understand one another enough to challenge, test, or disclose | A-Hu↔X; Queen↔Bowa; Lin↔God Eye | Can produce truth or escalation; not equivalent to friendship |
| **Posthumous recognition** | Death reclassifies prior acts and reveals relationships | Shang Chao/Yang; Smile/Nice/X; A-Sheng/Johnnies | Restores provenance but can also convert grief into obligation |
| **Public recognition** | Crowd validates role/image at scale | Lin, Yang, A-Hu, X, Zero | Mechanically powerful but causally coarse and vulnerable to totalization |

## P2-RR-04 — personhood-preserving recognition has seven properties

A comparatively person-compatible relation does not need to satisfy every property perfectly, but the full-series positive model contains seven recurring elements.

### 1. Reciprocity

Both parties can affect the meaning of the relation.

Xinya believes in A-Hu; A-Hu later realizes he has believed her words and chooses the protective telos.

### 2. Revisability

The relationship can change when one person's needs change.

Zhang Lan's desire for a complete family changes. Wang Yi's failure is not that her earlier quiet compatibility was false; it is that his relational model cannot update.

### 3. Fallibility

A person can fail, disappoint, grieve, or become contradictory without losing recognition.

Qing's wish to be able to make mistakes is the clearest statement.

### 4. Provenance

The relation preserves who acted, cared, suffered, or taught.

Smile's pendant preserves private family provenance after public Smile is destroyed.

### 5. Consent and boundary

Concern does not authorize unlimited access.

Luo Tong's statement that unaccepted pursuit cannot be forced becomes a direct relational rule.

### 6. Non-totalization

The person is not reduced to one gift, wound, role, or relation.

Little Johnny's fatherhood to Big Johnny is built from time and care rather than monster classification.

### 7. Survivable separation

The person can leave, change role, or refuse without the relation requiring annihilation.

This is the least secure condition across the series and one reason X's off-duty boundary is so important.

## P2-RR-05 — private recognition can preserve personhood without being sufficient to save it

The series repeatedly gives a character someone who sees them.

That relation matters. It does not automatically overcome institutions, Fear, public expectation, or the character's own choices.

### Nice and Smile

Smile is Original Nice's sustaining `信仰`. Nice's attachment is strong enough to alter his execution of Shang De's order. Yet Nice still uses Shang De's Fear weapon; Smile dies; grief drives retaliation; Nice returns to institutional dependence and later collapses.

Private recognition remains causally real but insufficient.

### Qing and Luo

Luo helps Qing experience a self outside sacred utility. Their relationship enables escape and later public self-authorship, but it does not remove DOS, public need, Fear, or the crash's unresolved politics.

### Wang Yi and Nuonuo

Paternal love motivates observation and protection. Without reciprocal communication it becomes surveillance and absence.

### X and Smile

Their friendship humanizes X and makes the pendant recovery legible as memorial action. It does not prevent Smile's death or settle X's future political danger.

The stabilized principle is:

> **Recognition is a condition of personhood, not an omnipotent rescue mechanism.**

## P2-RR-06 — recognition can begin before public legibility

Several of the series' healthiest relationships recognize a person before institutions or mass audiences can classify them correctly.

- Wolf Girl knows Firm Man before the monument.
- Luo knows Qing before Lucky Cyan.
- A-Sheng and Xiao Qiang recognize Da Qiang as family before the village recognizes personhood.
- Xinya treats A-Hu as family before the Association certifies him.
- Nuonuo recognizes Luo Li's technical competence and witnessed heroism before formal hero legitimacy.
- Xia Qing and Shang Chao recognize Yang's conduct before mass Trust stabilizes E-Soul.

The sequence is often:

`private/relational recognition`
→ `self-relation`
→ `public legibility`
→ `institutional certification`.

This gives relationships a constitutive role in hero formation.

But it does not mean private relations always possess superior truth. The director's private conviction about Qing is also the basis of coercive capture.

## P2-RR-07 — chosen roles become legitimate through relational reciprocity

The series rejects a purity model in which any socially authored identity is false.

Yang Cheng's E-Soul and A-Hu's hero role are both inherited or invented through relationships.

They become comparatively legitimate because:

- the person acts before reward;
- a relation recognizes conduct rather than merely surface;
- the person can answer the offered identity;
- the role becomes a chosen commitment;
- the person remains legible through and beyond it.

The key difference from Lin's initial Nice substitution is not simply that Yang and A-Hu “wanted it.”

It is that the role is grounded in a relationship where their answer changes what the role means.

> **Relational authorship becomes legitimate when it is an invitation capable of receiving revision, not a script whose success requires the subject's replacement.**

## P2-RR-08 — public and private recognition can correct one another

The series does not always privilege private knowledge.

Private actors can be:

- possessive;
- misinformed;
- traumatized;
- self-interested;
- or unable to see changes in the person they love.

Public evidence can correct private misrecognition.

Examples:

- Nuonuo's witnessed testimony changes Luo Tong's view of Luo Li.
- Lin's public confession reattributes acts previously credited to Original Nice.
- FOCUS visibility constrains covert coercion.
- public recognition of A-Hu makes his chosen herohood socially consequential.
- Xia Qing's testimony publicly grounds E-Soul in Yang's prior conduct.

The mature model is bidirectional:

> **private relation preserves fine-grained provenance; public scrutiny can contest private domination. Neither scale is automatically innocent.**

## P2-RR-09 — testimony is one of the series' central recognition acts

A character often cannot make themselves socially legible alone.

Another person becomes a witness.

| Witness | Subject | Testimony function |
|---|---|---|
| Xiao Yueqing | Lin | distinguishes Lin from Original Nice and restores the real name privately |

| A-Hu | X | preserves civilian anonymity while later receiving forbidden history |
| X | Zero history | offers counter-testimony to the Association's founding narrative |

Testimony can fail, be disbelieved, or become another narrative capture. But the series consistently treats social recognition as something persons often **carry for one another**.

## P2-RR-10 — guardianship is legitimate only when protection does not become authorship monopoly

Several relationships begin in rescue.

- Liu Zhen rescues Qing.
- The orphanage cares for children.
- A-Sheng protects Da Qiang.
- Wang Yi saves Zhang Lan.
- Luo Tong tries to keep Luo Li safe.
- X intervenes for Nice and others.
- Xinya adopts A-Hu.

Rescue creates responsibility. It does not create ownership of the rescued person's future.

The orphanage director crosses the line by making Qing's continued sacred role necessary to his own helper identity.

Luo Tong's concern is more revisable: he redirects Luo Li toward safer institutional work and later responds to testimony, even while disagreement remains.

A-Sheng's protection of Da Qiang preserves the child's subjecthood against capture and allows chosen kinship to develop.

The Phase-2 guardianship test is:

> **Does protection enlarge the protected person's future options, or make continued dependence proof that the protector's role was justified?**

## P2-RR-11 — the series distinguishes protection from presence and comfort

Fatherhood/guardianship arcs repeatedly separate these functions.

### Wang Yi

Provides lethal protection and careful observation. Fails at legible presence and reciprocal comfort.

### Luo Tong

Provides real presence and institutional safety concern. Misses the scale of Luo Li's competence and ambition.

### Little Johnny

Provides verbal, time-oriented, chosen fatherhood to Big Johnny.

### A-Sheng

Provides ordinary domestic care, grief teaching, and protection from research capture.

### Xinya

Provides family belonging before hero utility.

The stabilized relationship principle is:

> **Protection can save a body while leaving the person relationally abandoned. Presence, communication, and the ability to receive the other's answer remain distinct goods.**

## P2-RR-12 — silence creates space for projection

Ghostblade's silence has mechanical and relational consequences.

Others write motives into him.

Wang Yi initially treats shared quiet as proof of perfect compatibility.

Zhang Lan eventually needs an explicit answer.

Nuonuo experiences absence without a usable paternal account.

Little Johnny's talkativeness exposes how easily silence becomes a surface for social authorship.

The source does not say speech is morally superior in every context. It shows:

> **when a relationship requires decision, consent, or revision, silence can no longer function as neutral privacy; it becomes a condition others must interpret without the person's accountable participation.**

## P2-RR-13 — grief can preserve recognition or convert it into obligation

Grief is one of the series' strongest relationship transitions.

### Constructive/preserving possibilities

- A-Sheng teaches Xiao Qiang that grief should not be denied.
- X recovers Smile's pendant, preserving private provenance.
- Nice's grief confirms Smile's real place in his inner life.
- Qing's art/song preserve Luo without making Luo only a wound.

### Destructive/obligating possibilities

- Nice's grief becomes retaliation.
- Xiao Qiang's grief becomes a vow of vengeance.
- Yan Mo's memory of Zero becomes a god-restoration political project.
- Shang De's grievance turns Nice into a revenge weapon.
- Liang's childhood pain becomes a right to return dehumanization.

Grief does not have one ethical direction.

The criterion is:

> **Does grief keep the dead/absent person recognizable, or recruit the survivor into a role the relationship can no longer revise?**

## P2-RR-14 — instrumental recognition is often emotionally real

The most dangerous relationships are not necessarily fake.

Shang De knows and exploits Nice's aspiration.

The orphanage director genuinely wants to help children and believes in Qing's social value.

Yan Mo may genuinely admire Zero and believe he is correcting history.

Managers may care about heroes while also treating them as assets.

This matters because coercion is not limited to cynical actors.

> **Care becomes instrumental when another person's flourishing is defined so narrowly that only continued usefulness to the recognizer's project counts as being well.**

The series' institutional relationships are therefore ethically mixed rather than divided into sincere care versus exploitation.

## P2-RR-15 — rivalries are relationships of mutual authorship

Rivals in TBHX do not merely measure power.

They help define the identity under contest.

### Yang Cheng / veteran E-Soul

Each becomes part of the other's claim to E-Soul. Public recognition and hidden manipulation turn the duel into a succession referendum.

### Queen / Bowa

Each exposes a different relation to authorship, rules, institutional legitimacy, and public desire.

### Nice / Wreck

The nemesis script conceals friendship and supporting labor; Wreck's embodied familiarity later challenges substitution.

### X / Association

The Association defines X as unmanageable future sovereign. X defines the system as joint management of pieces. Their competing interpretations shape the political conflict.

### X / Zero

Not an interpersonal relation in present time, but a philosophical rivalry over whether world-level power becomes total judgment or preserves exit.

Rivalry therefore becomes:

> **a site where each actor tries to determine what kind of person the other is allowed to be.**

## P2-RR-16 — adversarial trust is a mature relationship form

X and A-Hu end Season 1 in neither friendship nor simple opposition.

A-Hu:

- considers assassinating X;
- confronts him;
- preserves his private identity;
- challenges his withdrawal;
- and receives forbidden history.

X:

- recognizes A-Hu's institutional capture;
- challenges his public lie;
- buys him a drink;
- and trusts him with dangerous knowledge.

The relation is best stabilized as **adversarial trust**:

> **each preserves the other's capacity to answer while refusing premature agreement.**

This is one of the strongest counter-models to both hero worship and institutional ownership.

## P2-RR-18 — recognition failures that recur across arcs

### Reduction to function

Qing as luck, Smile as smile, Ghostblade as silence, Nice as perfection, A-Hu as dog/hero symbol.

### Replacement

Lin as carrier of Nice; Yang as possible replacement E-Soul; bearers treated as interchangeable offices.

### Projection

Director's missed hero dream, Shang De's revenge, Yan Mo's Zero project, public desire for ideal heroes.

### Conditional worth

Managers value people while useful, successful, ranked, or emotionally legible.

### Non-revisability

Relationships fail when one party's changed need cannot alter the relation.

### Incommunicability

Silence, private grief, hidden identity, and institutional scripts prevent accountable answer.

### Posthumous obligation

Death fixes the survivor into revenge, succession, or memorial labor.

### Public override

Mass recognition makes private testimony unbelievable or turns the relation itself into content.

These are not separate pathologies. They converge when one person's account of another becomes socially and materially powerful while the represented person cannot answer it.

## P2-RR-19 — resolved earlier relationship hypotheses

**Disposition:** `REJECT`.

**Disposition:** `REJECT`.

**Disposition:** `REJECT`.

Their current state is adversarial trust, not established friendship or allegiance.

## P2 relationship synthesis

The stabilized full-series model is:

> **Recognition is the social act through which another being becomes more than a function, image, wound, or use—but recognition remains legitimate only when the recognized person can answer, revise, and survive the relation.**

Relationships are constitutive.

They can:

- create heroes before institutions do;
- preserve names and provenance;
- produce testimony;
- make grief bearable;
- sustain chosen identity;
- and resist public totalization.

They can also:

- project unfinished dreams;
- script compulsory roles;
- convert love into surveillance;
- make support into debt;
- turn grief into revenge;
- and provide institutions with the intimate knowledge needed to control a bearer.

The positive full-series standard is therefore not isolation or pure self-authorship.

It is **answerable interdependence**:

> **persons are shaped by others, yet no relationship becomes legitimate merely because it is loving, accurate, protective, or causally powerful. It must preserve reciprocity, fallibility, provenance, dissent, and the possibility that the other person may become someone the relation did not originally imagine.**
