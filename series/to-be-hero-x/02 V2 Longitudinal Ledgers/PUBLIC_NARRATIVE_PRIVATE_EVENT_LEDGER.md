# Public Narrative / Private Event Ledger

## Purpose

Track the difference among what happened, what institutions say happened, what media presents, and what audiences choose to believe. A public story is not automatically false; the ledger records compression, omission, reframing, and causal consequence.

## E01 pattern statement

The first episode does **not** establish that public narratives are lies. It establishes that public narratives are *usable social reductions*. They take heterogeneous causes—labor, grief, concealment, sincerity, technology, institutional strategy—and compress them into a story audiences can recognize.

In a Trust-value setting, that reduction is causally important because legibility can become belief and belief can become power.

## Adversarial control

Later episodes must include cases where the public account substantially matches private reality. If every public story is presumed false in advance, this ledger would become confirmation bias rather than analysis.

## E02 pattern update

E02 strengthens the distinction between **falsehood** and **dehumanization**. Some lies imprison: the perfect-couple story and nemesis script reduce people to brand components. Other lies or staged representations can reveal care or restore agency: Xiao's fake injury/death creates a path out of the contract and allows Lin to redirect Trust toward her chosen freedom.

The ethical variable is therefore not “truth good / fiction bad” in isolation. The episode increasingly asks **who authors the narrative, who can refuse it, and whose desire it makes causally effective**.

## E03 pattern statement

E03 sharpens the ledger from “public story hides private story” to a harder proposition:

> A public myth can become so authoritative that accurate relational memory is treated as falsehood, while a hostile exposé can be factually accurate yet still threaten personhood by taking control of disclosure.

The relevant axis is therefore not simply true/false. Track **who authors the narrative, whose options it preserves, and what material effects it produces**.

# Phase 2 stabilization — canonical public narrative / private event model

## P2 authority rule

This section is the **current full-series Phase-2 authority** for the relation among events, public stories, institutional framing, private knowledge, and material consequences.

Earlier entries remain the episode-by-episode record of what could be known prospectively. The Phase-2 model does not replace them with a single hidden “true story.” It stabilizes how the series itself treats narration as a causal layer.

## P2-PN-01 — the public/private distinction is not truth versus falsehood

Across the series, public narratives can be:
- factually false;
- factually true but causally incomplete;
- strategically staged yet protective;
- accurate but coercive;
- reparative without being exhaustive;
- or institutionally necessary while morally deforming.

Private accounts can likewise be:
- better informed;
- emotionally sincere;
- self-serving;
- conspiratorial;
- incomplete;
- or unavailable to the people whose lives they explain.

The stable distinction is therefore not:

`public = lie / private = truth`.

It is:

> **which causes, persons, relations, and options become visible in a story—and what that story then allows institutions and audiences to do.**

## P2-PN-02 — canonical causal sequence of public narrative

The series repeatedly uses the following structure:

1. **Event** — heterogeneous persons and causes act.
2. **Selection** — institutions, witnesses, media, or participants choose salient fragments.
3. **Narration** — fragments are organized into recognizable roles and causes.
4. **Circulation** — platforms, agencies, broadcasts, phones, rankings, or rumor distribute the story.
5. **Uptake** — audiences believe, contest, reinterpret, or emotionally invest in it.
6. **Feedback** — Trust/Fear, institutional policy, ranking, bodily power, contracts, violence, or identity change follow.
7. **Recontextualization** — later evidence changes the meaning of earlier facts without making the earlier public observation wholly imaginary.

This is why public narrative is not downstream commentary. It is part of the world's causal machinery.

## P2-PN-03 — narrative operation typology

| Operation | Definition | Representative cases | Primary risk or use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Concealment** | suppress a fact/person to preserve continuity or safety | Original Nice's death; Qing's survival/history; X's civilian identity | can protect vulnerable persons or preserve coercive institutions |
| **Substitution** | attribute one person's body/actions to another public identity | Lin as Nice | makes brand continuity real while erasing bearer discontinuity |
| **Causal compression** | retain visible facts while omitting distributed causes | Firm Man; A-Hu sole-credit story; rankings after ruins | creates legibility at cost of provenance |
| **Essentialization** | turn one trait/act into the whole being | Firm Man, Ghostblade, Big Johnny, Smile | stabilizes image and power while narrowing personhood |

## P2-PN-06 — “the public” is segmented, not a single mind

The series repeatedly presents different publics:
- fans;
- local witnesses;
- children;
- residents;
- platform users;
- journalists;
- hero-office constituencies;
- institutional officials;
- family members;
- conspiracy participants.

They do not share identical information or interests.

Examples:
- villagers can fear city heroes while children admire Vortex;
- Smile's durable reputation prevents ordinary scandal but leaves family relation vulnerable;
- spectators correctly see Nice strike Liang while lacking the cause that gives the act meaning;
- FOCUS can expose coercion even while later media compresses the event into A-Hu's singular hero story.

**Stabilized rule:** do not write “the public believes” without specifying which audience, what evidence it has, and through which medium it encounters the event.

## P2-PN-07 — visibility is both armor and capture

Visibility can:
- make covert assassination politically expensive;
- expose hidden coercion;
- permit counter-messaging;
- provide witnesses;
- preserve memory;
- and reallocate Trust.

It can also:
- freeze a role;
- create service entitlement;
- turn grief into labor;
- intensify competition;
- expose a private person to worship or extermination;
- and make a simplification mechanically real.

The correct full-series formulation is:

> **Visibility changes the geometry of possible violence. It is neither inherently liberating nor inherently oppressive.**

## P2-PN-08 — media institutions have distinct functions

### Hero offices

Produce and defend role continuity, contracts, publicity, succession, and usable scandal narratives.

### FOMO

Democratizes circulation and discovers emergent public images, while converting attention into competitive hero production and platform labor.

### FOCUS / investigative visibility

Can make covert action costly in real time, not merely report it afterward.

### Association

Defines official history, rankings, eligibility, arbitration, tournament order, secrecy, and threat categories.

### Personal devices / crowds

Generate immediate images and moral stories from partial views while events are still unfolding.

### Objects and traces

The notebook, pendant, flight recorder, photograph, guitar, and other material witnesses preserve private provenance against public compression.

These channels cannot be treated as one generic “media.”

## P2-PN-09 — counter-narrative is ethically necessary but never neutral

The series repeatedly shows that silence allows dominant narratives to harden.

Counter-narrative can:
- defend due process;
- restore hidden persons;
- reattribute action;
- reveal institutional violence;
- recover the agency of the dead;
- or disclose forbidden history.

But exposure can also:
- take control of a person's disclosure;
- become competitive content;
- produce another simplified myth;
- or function as strategic agenda setting.

The ethical question is not whether to narrate, but:

> **whether the new story increases the affected person's capacity to answer, revise, and remain connected to provenance.**

## P2-PN-10 — provenance is the principal corrective to causal compression

The series' strongest corrective acts do not simply negate a public fact. They restore:
- who contributed;
- who chose;
- who suffered;
- what chronology preceded the image;
- what relationship explains the action;
- and which institution arranged the visible outcome.

Examples:
- Lin reclaims acts performed under Nice;
- Qing restores passengers' active wishes and Luo's contribution;
- Ghostblade's notebook discloses paternal interiority;
- Smile's pendant preserves family relation beneath the public grin;
- X restores Zero's origin as first hero without absolving his tyranny;
- E24's dominoes place every hero inside antecedent causes.

**Phase-2 principle:** **causal truth is often provenance-rich rather than simply fact-correct.**

## P2-PN-11 — privacy can be a legitimate narrative good

The series does not endorse universal transparency.

Protective opacity includes:
- Xiao's staged death enabling exit;
- Liu Zhen hiding a vulnerable child from malicious public speculation;
- A-Hu concealing anonymous civilian X;
- X preserving a civilian identity separate from public godhood;
- intimate objects/relations remaining outside mass circulation.

Opacity becomes coercive when it protects institutional ownership, murder, fraud, or monopoly over causal interpretation.

The criterion is not transparency versus secrecy in isolation.

It is:

> **whose options the secrecy preserves, whose responsibility it hides, and who controls the terms of later disclosure.**

## P2-PN-12 — the founding lie is political legitimation and possible affective containment

The Association's Demon King Zero story is the full-series limit case.

It preserves real elements:
- catastrophic violence;
- god-risk;
- coalition resistance;
- need for restraint.

It suppresses the causal classification:
- Zero was the first hero;
- public Trust produced the god;
- hero worship generated the catastrophe from inside the system.

In a world where memory and belief can become power, historical control may also function as affective/metaphysical containment.

Phase-2 disposition:

> **The founding lie is rationalizable without becoming innocent.**

It reduces one danger while monopolizing the historical terms through which citizens can judge the institution built afterward.

## P2-PN-13 — narrative legitimacy test

When evaluating a public story in later synthesis, test:

1. **Factual relation** — which claims are true, false, staged, or unverified?
2. **Causal completeness** — what antecedents and contributors are omitted?
3. **Provenance** — whose relationship/history makes the visible event intelligible?
4. **Authorship** — who controls wording, timing, and circulation?
5. **Answerability** — can affected persons challenge or revise the story?
6. **Consent** — who agreed to be made legible in this way?
7. **Reversibility** — can the story change without destroying the bearer?
8. **Burden distribution** — who gains Trust, money, rank, safety, or legitimacy, and who absorbs danger/labor?
9. **Privacy** — does disclosure protect accountability or erase a legitimate private boundary?
10. **Material feedback** — what Trust/Fear, bodily, institutional, legal, or economic consequences follow?
11. **Public segmentation** — which audience actually receives the story?
12. **Temporal position** — is this contemporaneous reporting, retrospective history, strategic myth, or prospective paratext?
