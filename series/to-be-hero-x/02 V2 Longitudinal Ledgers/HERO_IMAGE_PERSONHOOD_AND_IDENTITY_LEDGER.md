# Hero Image, Personhood, and Identity Ledger

## Episode 1 identity chain: Lin Ling / Nice

### HI-001 — Lin begins as an image worker, not an image owner

Lin's labor helps produce hero/celebrity advertising. His ideas and memory have public utility, but he has little public recognition himself.

**Evidence:** opening office sequence; ad-count test.

### HI-002 — Institutional replacement explicitly deprioritizes personal identity

Juān jiě's `你是谁不重要` and later statement that nobody cares whether Lin Ling lives or dies establish the institution's functional logic.

**Class:** `F-DLG/F-INST`, high.

### HI-003 — Public identity can become bodily identity

Believers calling him Nice causes Trust to change his body. “Image” is therefore not merely costume or reputation.

**Class:** `F-MECH`, high.

### HI-004 — Lin's original self remains causally active inside the replacement

His advertising memory answers the live-show test; his prior admiration for Xiao Yueqing grounds sincere speech; his private dream drives the climax.

**Class:** `F-DLG + INF`, high.

### HI-005 — E01 ends with role adoption, not identity refusal

`现在的我就是奈斯` is the canonical E01 identity endpoint. This must not be overwritten by later remembered developments.

**Class:** `F-DLG`, high.

### HI-006 — Role adoption may be either authorship or capture

Two live interpretations remain:

- **Authorship reading:** Lin takes an imposed role and begins defining it through his own values.
- **Capture reading:** the institution succeeds when Lin internalizes the identity that made his original person dispensable.

**Class:** `ALT`, both medium.

## Original Nice

Episode 1 provides too little person-level evidence for a moral profile. His public image is “perfect”; his private act is suicide; his prior destabilizing incident is unnamed. Any conclusion that he is already morally fake is prohibited at this stage.

## Xiao Yueqing

She is simultaneously public brand-partner, Lin's admired figure, and Original Nice's intimate cohabiting partner. Her function tests whether mass-authored identity can survive person-to-person recognition.

## First identity principle

**E01 provisional principle:** a hero image can be socially transferable while personal memory/desire remains locally irreducible. Whether those layers can coexist long-term is the central open question.


# Episode 02 identity update

Juān jiě: `奈斯现在不仅仅是个人 / 他更是公司最重要的品牌` and must continue at any cost.

**Class:** `F-DLG + INF`, high.

**Class:** `F-DLG/F-MECH`, high.

### HI-010 — Public hero images author supporting roles as well as central roles

Wreck's “nemesis” identity is constructed partly to make Nice more compelling. Social authorship therefore distributes identity across a hero's surrounding cast, including antagonists.

**Class:** `F-DLG/F-INST`, high.

### HI-011 — Real-name exchange creates a private personhood channel

Xiao asks Lin's actual name, says it sounds better than Nice, and addresses him `林凌` before sleep. This does not yet constitute public reclamation, but it establishes a relational space where the brand name is not necessary.

**Class:** `F-DLG`, high.

### HI-012 — Qishi / True Eye is explicitly overwritten by God Eye

Spotlight's transformation declares `从今以后世界上没有了启士`; only God Eye remains. This parallels Treeman's functional erasure of Lin but arises through a different institutional/ideological channel.

**Class:** `F-DLG/F-INST`, high.

## Second identity principle

**E02 provisional principle:** social authorship becomes ethically dangerous when it fixes a person's direction, relationships, or function against their ability to refuse. The same Trust mechanism can become less coercive when collective expectation is redirected toward an already-owned desire, as with Xiao's travel freedom.

This is stronger than a generic “public image is bad” thesis: **alignment and authorship matter more than mere publicity**.

# Episode 03 update

## HI-010 — Situational virtue becomes permanent essence

Firm Man's origin clarifies a new person/image mechanism. His statement “for that girl, I cannot fall” is a situational act of courage. Public Trust and professional production convert it into the identity “the hero who never falls.”

**Implication:** hero-image captivity can begin from an authentic virtue rather than a fabricated persona.

**Confidence:** High.

## HI-011 — Public image can make firsthand relational truth socially illegible

Wolf Girl remembers Firm Man as someone who cries, laughs, needs encouragement, and is gentle. Other children reject this because it contradicts the established hero image.

**Implication:** the hero image can erase the subject not only from public knowledge but from the social credibility of people who actually knew the subject.

**Confidence:** High.

## HI-012 — Firm Man's liberation is loss of image, recovery of bodily options

The public “myth” collapses, but Firm Man gains the ability/desire to lie down and leave herohood.

**Implication:** loss of symbolic identity can increase personhood/autonomy.

**Confidence:** High as narrative; Medium-High mechanically.

## HI-013 — Burden transfer from Firm Man to Nice

Nice catches the fallen monument and becomes rank 10 while Firm Man exits. The visual/institutional succession makes Lin inherit the burden of top-tier public expectation.

**Status:** `INT grounded in F-VIS/F-INST`  
**Confidence:** High.

## HI-014 — Lin's identity formula changes

Sequential identity markers now read:

- E01: `现在的我就是奈斯` — “the present me is Nice.”
- E02: Xiao says `你并不是奈斯` and privately names Lin Ling.
- E03: Lin himself says `我其实不是奈斯` — “I actually am not Nice.”

This is a genuine prospective revision. Do not flatten these stages into a single later confession.

Lin does not purify himself by confession. He admits enjoying the hero role and stubbornly clinging to it. Personhood restoration therefore includes responsibility for desire, not only victimhood.

### ID-04C — new capture risk

The public/industry immediately begins producing a new Lin story. Agencies want to sign him; Rank 10 remains; formal graphics render him as `普通人 / THE COMMONER`. The image/person problem therefore survives in a new form.

**Leading unresolved question:** can Lin remain answerable to himself once "ordinary Lin" becomes a valuable public archetype?

## ID-05A — revision of authenticity model

E04 could tempt a legal-name model of authenticity. E05 rejects that simplification. The core distinction becomes **imposed substitution versus voluntarily inhabited role**. A hero image may function as practice, aspiration, or covenant rather than erasure.

## ID-05B — unresolved capture risk

E05 has not shown Yang revising/refusing E-Soul once others depend on the role. The voluntary role may still become a future cage. Carry forward the criterion: **can the person answer back to, alter, or leave the role?**

## E06 identity principle

E06 refines the working ethical test:

> A role is comparatively person-compatible when the carrier is recognizable **through and without it**, may reinterpret it, and has conduct that precedes its social reward.

The missing condition remains **exit**: E06 has not yet proved Yang can relinquish E-Soul once the role is publicly invested.

# Episode 07 update

Yáng Chéng says that he is no longer alone and carries the Trust of thousands. This is not yet proof of capture, but it marks the point where a voluntarily inhabited role begins supplying reasons he believes he **cannot** withdraw.

## HI-026 — Incumbent authenticity does not guarantee continuity

Veteran E-Soul explicitly refuses ranking as the purpose of his identity. His sincerity is preserved, yet sincerity alone cannot secure the social office once Trust migrates.

## HI-027 — Yáng Chéng's self-authorship survives direct testing

Xià Qíng asks whether the duel is genuinely what he wants; he answers yes. This blocks a total-puppet reading even as the environment is later revealed to be engineered.

## HI-028 — E-Soul becomes a contested social office

Before the duel, the public recognizes old/new E-Soul simultaneously. The hero identity functions as a socially constituted office or inheritance rather than simple private property.

## HI-029 — Death resolves contested identity through Trust consolidation

The veteran dies and E-Soul Trust is reported integrated/consolidated into the surviving Yáng Chéng. This is the first explicit local case where a contested identity's public power resolves through death.

## E07 identity principle

> **The person can authentically choose a role and still become constrained by the constituencies, institutions, and hidden architects who gain stakes in that role. The key personhood question is no longer only “did you choose?” but “who designed the conditions in which this choice became obligatory?”**
# Episode 08 update — sacred image and the right to fallibility

## HI-029 — Qīng precedes “Sacred Maiden”

Liú names the child `青` before the orphanage constructs the sacred persona. Preserve the temporal order: person/name first, public sacred role later.

## HI-030 — Sacred image can be built from true capacities and still reduce the person

Qīng is genuinely lucky and the orphanage's beneficiaries genuinely experience hope. The role becomes dehumanizing when her real capacity is totalized into what she must continuously provide.

**Principle:** falsehood is not necessary for image capture.

## HI-031 — Fallibility is a personhood requirement

Qīng's `我想做一个能犯错的普通人` adds a new identity criterion:

> personhood requires not only a name or self-authored role but the right to violate expectations, make mistakes, and remain recognizable.

**Class:** `F-DLG + INT`, high.

## HI-032 — Loved metaphysical identity can dehumanize like feared metaphysical identity

Qīng is reduced to blessing/luck; Luo is reduced to jinx/misfortune. Their social valuations are opposite, but both are read as causal functions before ordinary children.

## HI-033 — White costume as image discipline

Dialogue explicitly connects a white sacred presentation to reassuring people. The costume is therefore not neutral styling: it is identity technology designed around audience affect.

## HI-034 — Director's hero dream becomes projective possession

His closing monologue links the wish to help more people and his missed hero dream to refusal to let Qīng leave. A helper's self-concept becomes dependent on the beneficiary continuing to embody the desired role.

Working term: **projective altruism / benevolent instrumentalization**.

## HI-035 — Luo provides a counter-image rather than a replacement image

Luo does not tell Qīng what public identity she should adopt. He helps her experience a state in which luck can be imperfect and ordinary companionship survives. This is personhood-expanding recognition rather than substitution.

## E08 identity principle

> **A socially authored role is comparatively person-compatible only if the carrier may fail, disappoint expectations, revise presentation, and remain a person rather than a broken version of the role.**

# Episode 09 update

## HI-035 — Leaving the institution does not erase the socially distributed role

Qīng physically escapes the orphanage, changes setting, clothing, and mode of performance, yet members of the public independently reconstruct the Sacred Maiden category around her.

**Principle:** identity capture can become decentralized. Once a role is socially distributed, no single institution is necessary to reproduce it.

## HI-036 — Material independence is part of meaningful exit

Qīng's `自食其力` line establishes that personhood cannot be protected by abstract permission alone. She needs enough resources to survive outside the role before she can return for Luo on her own terms.

**Personhood criterion added:** **survivable exit**.

## HI-037 — Lucky Cyan represents partial self-authorship, not clean liberation

Unlike Sacred Maiden, Lucky Cyan is linked to choices Qīng actually makes: public music, a condition attached to DOS recruitment, Luo's hero aspiration, and an explicit concert acknowledgment of the person who shaped her.

Yet the public continues to define her through luck and mass need.

**Interpretation:** a socially authored identity can become more humane without becoming purely private or autonomous.

## HI-038 — Provenance can protect personhood inside a public role

Qīng does not reject the visible hero/singer identity in her concert speech. Instead, she refuses to let the public treat it as self-originating by naming the absent friend's contributions.

**Working principle:** public identity is less erasing when it remains answerable to the relationships and histories from which the person actually emerged.

## HI-039 — The right to fail now needs a right to choose what obligation means

E08: Qīng wants to be an ordinary person who can make mistakes.

E09: public recognition returns anyway. Her response is not complete withdrawal but a move toward choosing what her new visibility will serve.

**Prospective synthesis:** personhood requires both **fallibility** and **authorship over obligation**.

# E13 identity case — Luo Li and counter-image authorship

## HI-E13-01 — appearance capture

Luo Li's body is not ignored; it is over-read. Observers use cuteness, apparent youth, and small stature to infer weakness or non-heroic suitability even after competence is demonstrated.

**Class:** `F-DLG/F-VIS/INT`, High.

## HI-E13-02 — refusal of assimilation precedes positive reclamation

Luo Li briefly considers short hair / tomboy presentation, then rejects changing herself merely to satisfy the stereotype: `凭什么要我屈服` and `为什么一定要为此而改变自己`.

The current dignity claim is **refusal of self-erasure**, not yet a fully mature declaration that she positively embraces “cute” as her hero identity.

## HI-E13-03 — `英雄人设` as self-authored counter-image

Luo Li explicitly decides to design a battle suit and create a new hero persona in order to break prejudice. This is the first especially clear V2 case where image construction itself functions as resistance rather than institutional substitution.

> **Constructed image is not automatically false identity. Authorship, purpose, evidence, and revisability matter.**

## HI-E13-04 — the helmet controls reading order

The suit retains long pink hair, small stature, and bright design, but its faceplate temporarily prevents facial cuteness from becoming the first category. The suit therefore mediates **which evidence arrives first** rather than simply replacing Luo Li with another person.

## HI-E13-05 — Nuonuo supplies an institutional analogue

Nuonuo's line that first impression can replace the résumé makes appearance capture explicit beyond Luo Li's subjective frustration. Her appearance once displaced evidence of technical qualification.

## E13 personhood category

**counter-image authorship under appearance capture**

### Cumulative proposition

> **A hero image can preserve personhood when the person authors it as a revisable way of making real capacities legible; it becomes capture when the image substitutes for or owns the person.**

# E17 identity update — chosen kinship before public legitimacy

## HI-E17-01 — family recognition precedes species/public classification

Xiao Qiang responds to an extraterrestrial baby not by solving species ontology but by claiming `我的儿子` and naming him Da Qiang. The commitment becomes durable through care, discipline, defense, and future imagination.

The giant transformation makes public-safety concern legitimate but does not retroactively erase the extensive relational evidence established first. E17 therefore rejects the cheap dignity strategy of proving the stigmatized being harmless.

## HI-E17-03 — A-Sheng cannot inhabit the absolute public healer image

Public material gives `治愈万物`; private history gives the wife he cannot save. A-Sheng abandons herohood not because he abandons care but because the heroic identity loses existential credibility after intimate failure.

- internally inhabitable self-relation.

This is the first case in the V2 sequence where a small-scale relationship explicitly authorizes duties toward a being *before* the mass public or an institution grants a stable person-category.

# Episode 18 — accurate reduction, commodified coping, and hidden relation

## HI-E18-01 — Big Johnny is reduced through accurate but incomplete categories

The public calls him a monster because his transformed body is dangerous. Reporters then redescribe Little Johnny's ability to calm him as a possible summoner/control power.

Both descriptions contain observable evidence. Neither is adequate to the relation established first:

> **Big Johnny is a son and reciprocal protector before he becomes a monster-category or ability-object.**

E18 therefore sharpens the identity problem from fabrication to **accurate reduction**: a partial truth becomes socially exhaustive because it is useful to institutions and audiences.

## HI-E18-02 — danger and personhood are independent questions

Little Johnny does not preserve Big Johnny's dignity by denying danger. He reaches him through family recognition while acknowledging the transformed body as a real threat.

**Stabilized implication:**

> **Danger can justify containment without settling whether the dangerous being is a person.**

This becomes a central full-series safeguard against sentimental personhood arguments that require harmlessness.

## HI-E18-03 — Little Johnny's private grief practice becomes public emotional labor

A-Sheng taught Little Johnny to cry and then smile so that hope could continue. FOMO later standardizes the visible smile into mascot labor:

`appearance → smile`
`promotion → smile`
`fan meeting → smile`

The public receives cheerfulness. The bearer carries the history of bereavement that made the expression possible.

**Identity category:** **coping practice captured as market persona**.

## HI-E18-04 — successful branding can become a cage even when it fits the bearer

Little Johnny genuinely is cute, socially skilled, and capable of making people smile. His complaint is not that the mascot image is wholly false. It is that the image narrows the permitted range of his work and turns a partial truth into his assigned function.

This distinguishes:
- **misrepresentation** — the image is factually wrong;
- **capture** — the image is partly right but claims too much of the person.

## HI-E18-05 — A-Sheng's hidden labor disappears beneath Vortex's singular legend

Little Johnny alleges that Vortex depended on A-Sheng's support. Whether every detail is complete, the episode deliberately contrasts the public rank-one apex image with hidden support labor.

**Identity consequence:** singular hero mythology can erase the persons whose work made the apex identity sustainable.

# Episode 20 — engineered affective image and relationally answerable power

## HI-E20-01 — Original Nice's perfection is technically manufactured

Shang De states that Original Nice's “perfect persona” was constructed through Fear. The episode therefore confirms that a polished public identity can be materially engineered rather than merely advertised.

E21 later prevents the opposite overcorrection: the underlying desire to become a radiant hero was genuine. The full identity is therefore neither simply authentic nor simply fake.

**Current model:** **authentic aspiration colonized by engineered perfection**.

## HI-E20-02 — Luo Li's self-authored image is immediately recaptured as platform slogan

Luo Li built her armor/persona to make competence legible without erasing the appearance she valued. After the ruins, FOMO markets essentially the same claim—do not be defined by appearance; be yourself—as platform branding.

> **The system recognizes resistance by learning how to circulate and own its language.**

Her claim remains authentic. Ownership of its public meaning becomes contested.

## HI-E20-03 — grief breaks polished hero legibility without erasing agency

Luo Li's rage after finding Luo Tong protecting Nuonuo is rendered through looser line, facial distortion, shadow, tears, and feral choreography. Big Johnny transforms physically under family threat; Luo Li transforms formally and behaviorally.

The sequence does not prove a common supernatural mechanism. It does show that:

> **emotionally truthful rupture can make a hero look less socially legible than an engineered “perfect” image.**

The episode does not romanticize the resulting excess; Queen and Cyan are needed to restore boundaries and relation.

## HI-E20-04 — market representation can package both mascot and monster

The credits turn cute Big Johnny and catastrophic Big Johnny into alternate products. The image economy does not solve the monster/person conflict. It makes both forms sellable.

## HI-E20-05 — X appears to retain voluntary control over public legibility

The credits show an ordinary black-haired office-worker presentation and a public X presentation connected through a deliberate world-state transition.

At the E20 boundary this is a high-confidence inference; E21 later confirms voluntary identity switching directly.

**Identity category:** **role sovereignty / voluntary public legibility**.

This becomes strong evidence for:

It also shows that X can exercise higher-order authority over another person's representational state without the episode proving that he owns or rewrites the person's interior self.

Zero's sequence collapses distinctions among:

- clock-out boundaries;

Lin's earlier substitution is formally legalized through contract and public continuity. Original Nice is dead; Lin is now officially authorized to inhabit the role.

It does not retroactively erase the coercion through which the succession began.

- visibility;
- role surfaces;
- succession;
- ranking opportunities;

The coalition discovers X's civilian identity and workplace, invades his private sphere, and interprets autonomy as an attempt to stand above everyone.

The private boundary is not proven to be a complete constitutional safeguard. It is nevertheless a real behavioral form of self-limitation that the institution cannot recognize.

Across E22–E24, X and other subjects move through:
- embodied 3D states;
- planar 2D states;
- cel/image-object states;
- multiple graphic regimes;
- shared history-space;
- common tournament rendering.

Subject continuity survives those transformations.

**Formal personhood proposition:**

> **representation is causally real but does not exhaust the person represented.**


# Phase 2 stabilization — canonical personhood and identity model

## P2 authority rule

This section is the **current full-series Phase-2 authority** for hero image, personhood, and identity.

The episode entries above remain part of the project's prospective intellectual history. Where an earlier local formulation is narrower or unresolved, use the Phase-2 rule below while preserving the earlier entry as evidence of what was knowable at that broadcast boundary.

## P2-HI-01 — personhood is not identical to public legibility

The series repeatedly separates being a person from being socially recognized as one.

- Big Johnny is family before the public has an adequate category for him.
- A-Hu possesses reflective interiority before humans can hear his speech.
- Lin's personal memory survives inside the Nice morphology.
- Ghostblade's silence makes his interiority less legible without eliminating it.
- X is a full private person while coworkers know only the public abstraction X.

Stabilized proposition:

> **Public recognition can amplify or suppress access to personhood, but it does not create the whole subject from nothing.**

## P2-HI-02 — hero images are social technologies, not simply lies

A hero image can be:
- imposed substitution;
- inherited office;
- chosen covenant;
- counter-image;
- sacred service identity;
- emotional labor;
- public threat category;
- engineered perfection;
- or self-authored aspiration.

The ethically decisive question is not whether the image is constructed. Every public identity is constructed to some degree.

The correct questions are:

1. Who authors it?
2. What prior person/history does it preserve or suppress?
3. Can the bearer answer back to it?
4. Can the bearer revise it?
5. Can the bearer fail without becoming ontological waste?
6. Can the bearer leave it and survive?
7. Who gains power or profit from its circulation?
8. What obligations does the image impose on surrounding people?

## P2-HI-03 — mechanical reality does not settle moral legitimacy

Trust can make a public role bodily and mechanically real.

That does not prove:
- the bearer consented;
- the role expresses the whole person;
- the institution owns the role ethically;
- the public narrative is causally adequate;
- the resulting obligation is legitimate.

Lin's Nice body, A-Hu's heroization, E-Soul succession, Xiao Yueqing's mobility constraint, and Zero's godhood all demonstrate that **social efficacy and moral legitimacy must be analyzed separately**.

## P2-HI-04 — the bearer must remain answerable, not merely visible

The most stable criterion across the series is not “authenticity” in the sense of a hidden pure self.

It is **answerability**:

> Can the bearer respond to the role, interpret it, accept responsibility for it, alter it, refuse it, or leave it?

This allows several person-compatible identities that are openly performed or inherited:
- Yang Cheng as a new E-Soul in his own way;
- Lucky Cyan as a revised public identity with restored provenance;
- Luo Li's self-authored hero image;
- A-Hu's fabricated hero role converted into chosen protection;
- Lin's public reclamation of `林凌` while retaining heroic action.

It excludes identities where the person is treated only as replaceable substrate:
- Original Nice as revenge machine;
- Lin under `你是谁不重要`;
- Xiao Yueqing as Nice's accessory;
- Ghostblade as assassin-tool;
- Smile's face as compulsory output condition;
- Zero as unquestionable sovereign.

## P2-HI-05 — fallibility is a core personhood right

Qing's desire to become an ordinary person capable of mistakes supplies a full-series criterion:

> **A person-compatible role must permit failure, ambiguity, emotional interruption, and revision without converting the bearer into a broken or disposable version of the image.**

This criterion explains:
- Firm Man's liberation through falling;
- Qing's rejection of sacred utility;
- Queen's growth through defeat and revision;
- Nice's collapse under permanent perfection;
- Smile's vulnerability when the smile cannot be maintained;
- Liang's dehumanization of those who fail endurance tests.

## P2-HI-06 — provenance is part of identity without becoming ownership

Persons are not self-created. Their identities arise through relationships, inheritance, institutions, memories, and prior causes.

Personhood requires access to that history and the ability to interpret it.

Healthy provenance:
- Qing names Luo and the crash victims as part of her becoming;
- Yang inherits E-Soul while being told to revise it;
- A-Hu accepts Xinya's belief without becoming her property;
- X recognizes that the coin was not the sole origin of his path;
- Smile remains Nice's private faith even when the public persona is engineered.

Unhealthy provenance becomes ownership:
- the orphanage director treats Qing as fulfillment of his missed hero dream;
- Shang De treats Nice as a revenge instrument;
- institutions treat succession history as authorization to own bearers;
- public myths erase supporting persons while claiming the apex hero as self-originating.

## P2-HI-07 — material exit is required for meaningful refusal

The right to say no is inadequate when leaving means social death, poverty, physical collapse, or continued mechanical capture.

The series supplies several exit tests:
- Firm Man can finally lie down and leave herohood;
- Qing needs material independence to escape the orphanage role;
- Xiao Yueqing's staged death frees her from contractual/public capture;
- Ghostblade leaves MG but must still negotiate fatherhood and private presence;
- X maintains employment, home, secrecy, and clock-out boundaries as an exit from continuous hero legibility;
- Original Nice lacks a viable identity outside the role and collapses toward `我是谁`.

**Stabilized criterion:** **survivable exit**.

## P2-HI-08 — reciprocal recognition is stronger than unilateral observation

Recognition protects personhood when the recognized subject can answer and revise the relation.

Positive cases:
- Xiao privately asks Lin's name;
- Xia Qing and Pomelo recognize Yang through and without the mask;
- Qing and Luo share inheritance without replacing one another;
- Queen is changed by Qing without being assigned a new identity;
- Xinya and A-Hu believe one another;
- Little Johnny reaches Big Johnny through reciprocal family recognition.

Limited cases:
- Ghostblade studies Nuonuo but remains unable to establish reciprocal recognition;
- public fans know hero images while lacking private relational history;
- the Association knows X as risk while refusing to recognize the person who wants boundaries.

## P2-HI-09 — danger, guilt, and dignity must remain analytically separable

The series does not require morally serious personhood analysis to deny harm.

- Big Johnny is dangerous and a person.
- Liang is traumatized and culpable.
- Original Nice is captured and operationally complicit.
- X may represent real catastrophic risk while retaining rights against preventive murder.
- Zero begins as hero and becomes tyrant; restoring his origin does not absolve him.

This prevents two opposite errors:
- **dehumanization through danger** — threat becomes total ontology;
- **exculpation through personhood** — biography is used to erase responsibility.

## P2-HI-10 — support and relational influence do not automatically erase authorship

The series repeatedly rejects the fantasy of a wholly self-made subject.

Queen, Qing, Yang, A-Hu, Little Johnny, Luo Li, Lin, and X are all shaped by others.

Authorship survives influence when:
- the person can acknowledge the cause;
- interpret its meaning;
- accept or refuse the obligation;
- and remain the deliberating agent.

This is why Qing's song can help Queen without owning Queen, and Xinya's belief can help constitute A-Hu without reducing him to her invention.

## P2-HI-11 — institutions create offices whose continuity can outrank bearer continuity

Nice and E-Soul demonstrate that a hero identity can function like a social office:
- bearers can be replaced;
- Trust can migrate;
- institutional authorization can regularize succession;
- surrounding relationships and enemies can be scripted around the office;
- the role can outlive the body.

The personhood danger is greatest when continuity of the office becomes a reason to treat the current bearer as disposable substrate.

## P2-HI-12 — totalization is the terminal identity failure

The series' strongest negative identity form is not ordinary performance. It is **totalization**:

> the public role becomes the exhaustive person, the exhaustive source of obligation, and eventually the authority to determine other people's reality.

Zero is the terminal case:
`hero = god = judge = sovereign = world order`.

Original Nice approaches psychological totalization:
`if I am no longer Nice, who am I?`

Smile approaches affective totalization:
`if the smile stops, the hero body stops functioning`.

X's current counter-practice is anti-totalization:
`hero role + private person + right to clock out`.

This is a behavioral ethic, not proof that X can never become dangerous.

## Phase-2 dispositions of earlier simplifications

### “The legal/private name is the authentic self; the hero name is false”

**Disposition:** `REJECT`.

Yang Cheng can be authentically E-Soul; Lucky Cyan and Luo Li can inhabit constructed public roles; X can be X without X exhausting the person.

### “Constructed image means fake identity”

**Disposition:** `REJECT`.

Construction is universal. Authorship, answerability, provenance, revisability, and exit determine compatibility.

### “The private self is pure and the public self is corrupt”

**Disposition:** `REJECT`.

Private persons can be complicit, violent, self-deceiving, or punitive. Public roles can express genuine courage and chosen commitment.

### “Self-authorship means independence from all influence”

**Disposition:** `REJECT`.

The series treats relational causation as unavoidable. The issue is whether influence becomes ownership.

### “Support diminishes authorship”

**Disposition:** `REJECT`.

Queen, A-Hu, Qing, Yang, and others become more fully authors by acknowledging relation rather than pretending self-creation.

### “A dangerous being must first prove harmlessness to receive dignity”

**Disposition:** `REJECT`.

## Phase-2 personhood synthesis

The full-series personhood thesis is:

> **A hero image becomes legitimate not when it is “true” in the sense of natural or unperformed, but when the bearer remains an answerable person who can know its provenance, revise its meaning, fail within it, accept responsibility for its effects, and leave it without annihilation.**

The opposite of personhood is not publicity.

It is **totalization**:

> a partial image becomes the whole socially available being, then becomes the whole source of obligation, and finally becomes the authority to decide what other people are allowed to be.

That is why the series can defend both public heroism and ordinary privacy.

The final ethical distinction is:

> **representation may be real, socially necessary, and even empowering without being entitled to own the person represented.**
