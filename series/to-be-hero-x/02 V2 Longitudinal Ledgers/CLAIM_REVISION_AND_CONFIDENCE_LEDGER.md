# To Be Hero X V2 — Claim Revision and Confidence Ledger

# Part I — Phase 2 stabilization: canonical claim state

## 1. Responsibility

This ledger is the current corpus-wide routing authority for **what the V2 project presently claims, how strongly it claims it, what earlier formulations were changed, and which propositions must remain open**.

It is distinct from three neighboring artifact classes:

1. **Frozen episode deep readings** preserve what a careful viewer could responsibly infer at each original broadcast boundary.
2. **Topical longitudinal ledgers** remain the detailed current authorities for mechanics, chronology, personhood, public narrative, institutions, relationships, language, and form.
3. **`V1_TO_V2_REVISION_LEDGER.md`** evaluates the earlier V1 corpus and will be stabilized separately after this document.

This document does not replace those homes. It reconciles them.

Its core task is to prevent four common failures:

- allowing a provisional episode hypothesis to survive after later evidence has changed it;
- allowing a secure local mechanic to be generalized into a universal law;
- allowing an attractive thematic synthesis to be presented as source fact;
- allowing uncertainty to disappear merely because a later document wants a cleaner argument.

### Governing rule

> **A mature claim is not whatever appears latest or sounds most comprehensive. It is the narrowest formulation that survives the strongest currently available evidence and counterevidence.**

The corpus therefore preserves both intellectual history and current authority:

- Part I records the stabilized Phase-2 claim state.
- Part II preserves the episode-by-episode Phase-1 claim history, including claims that were later revised or rejected.

Frozen episode bodies are never silently rewritten to make the project appear retrospectively omniscient.

---

## 2. Authority order for claim adjudication

When two artifacts appear to conflict, use this order:

1. **Mandarin audiovisual source** and synchronized episode evidence.
2. **Completed motion/video audits** for claims depending on continuity, timing, movement, causation, or object identity.
3. **`TBHX_V2_TARGETED_AV_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md`** where a post-freeze clip/still reinspection has explicitly changed the claim state.
4. **Stabilized Phase-2 topical ledger section** for the relevant semantic responsibility.
5. **Frozen episode deep reading** for prospective or episode-local interpretation.
6. **Narrow E18–E24 revision ledgers and episode handoffs** as transition history.
7. **V1 historical material** as a hypothesis source only.

A later date does not override a stronger source class. A broad synthesis does not override a bounded mechanic. A screenshot does not override continuous motion when motion is the disputed issue.

## 3. Claim classes

Every mature proposition should be read through both its **evidence class** and its **scope**.

| Code | Class | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `F-DLG` | Dialogue fact | Directly supported by verified Mandarin dialogue or visible Chinese text. |
| `F-VIS` | Visual fact | Directly visible in frame evidence. |
| `F-MOTION` | Motion fact | Depends on continuous video, ordering, contact, trajectory, or sustained action. |
| `F-AUD` | Audio fact | Directly supported by inspected sound, vocal delivery, music, silence, or mix. |
| `F-MECH` | Mechanic fact | A source-demonstrated causal rule, effect, constraint, or exception. |
| `F-INST` | Institutional fact | Explicit policy, ranking action, corporate practice, research procedure, or governance behavior. |
| `F-CHRON` | Chronology fact | Printed year, explicit temporal relation, or motion-secure sequence ordering. |
| `INF` | Inference | Best explanation of multiple facts, but not directly stated. |
| `INT` | Interpretation | Literary, ethical, political, relational, or formal judgment. |
| `REV` | Revision | Current proposition explicitly changes an earlier claim. |
| `ALT` | Alternative | Serious competing account that remains evidentially viable. |
| `UNR` | Unresolved | The source does not discriminate among important alternatives. |
| `SRC` | Source-control claim | Governs what a source, subtitle, credit, preview, or paratext can legitimately establish. |

### Scope qualifiers

A claim may also be:

- **local** — secure for one character, ability, event, or episode;
- **cross-case** — supported by several independent cases;
- **full-series** — stabilized across E01–E24;
- **historical** — true of an earlier project state but no longer current authority;
- **prospective** — valid only at a frozen episode boundary;
- **paratextual** — applies to credits/opening/preview material without automatic chronology status.

The most frequent overreach in this corpus has been turning **local** into **universal** without an intermediate cross-case test.

## 4. Confidence scale

Confidence attaches to the exact wording of a proposition, not to the general topic.

| Level | Standard |
|---|---|
| **High** | Direct source fact, motion-secure event, or convergent multi-source proposition whose serious alternatives do not threaten the core wording. |
| **Medium-High** | Strong inference or interpretation supported across multiple facts/cases, with bounded alternatives that affect mechanism or scope rather than the central direction. |
| **Medium** | Plausible and useful, but at least one serious alternative remains capable of changing the explanation materially. |
| **Low** | Hypothesis retained for testing; not suitable as a premise in specialist synthesis without explicit qualification. |
| **Open** | The source does not currently justify ranking one major alternative above the others. |

### Confidence discipline

- “High” does not mean exhaustive. A high-confidence event can coexist with an open mechanism.
- A precise claim may be low-confidence even where a broader claim is high-confidence.
- Repetition in several documents does not itself raise confidence if all documents inherit the same unverified assumption.
- Official institutional dialogue can be high-confidence evidence of **institutional doctrine** without becoming omniscient world fact.
- A character's sincere belief is high-confidence evidence of that belief, not automatically of the event believed.

## 5. Revision vocabulary

Current claim transitions use the project-wide vocabulary below.

| Transition | Meaning | Earlier TBHX labels routed here |
|---|---|---|
| `PRESERVE` | Earlier formulation survives substantially unchanged. | `CONFIRMED`, `LOCKED`, `RESOLVE` where no wording change is needed. |
| `STRENGTHEN` | Core direction survives and gains confidence, magnitude, cross-case support, or better source evidence. | `STRENGTHENED`, `CONFIRM FORM`, `PARTIAL RESOLVE` when additive. |
| `REVISE` | Core insight survives but wording, causal mechanism, scope, chronology, or ethical emphasis changes materially. | `NARROWED`, `CORRECTED`, `REWORDED`, `COMPLEXIFIED`. |
| `DOWNGRADE` | Evidence is less discriminating than previously believed; claim remains possible but loses authority. | confidence reductions and source-control quarantines. |
| `REJECT` | Earlier proposition no longer survives the source. | `OVERTURNED`, `CONTRADICTED`, explicit negative-claim rejection. |
| `OPEN` | Major alternatives remain unresolved. | `UNRESOLVED`, `DO NOT PROMOTE`, protected questions. |

A transition changes the **claim**, not the reasonableness of the earlier prospective reading. An E01 hypothesis can be rejected in Phase 2 while remaining a fair E01-boundary inference.

## 6. Maturity levels and synthesis permissions

| Maturity level | Use |
|---|---|
| **Canonical fact** | May be stated directly, with normal scope qualifiers. |
| **Canonical bounded mechanic** | May support argument only within the demonstrated bearer/material/context unless cross-case generalization is separately established. |
| **Canonical inference** | May be used in specialist synthesis if identified as inference and counterpressure remains visible. |
| **Canonical interpretation** | May organize a literary argument, but must route to factual premises and acknowledge competing readings where material. |
| **Protected open question** | May be discussed as uncertainty; may not be silently resolved for narrative neatness. |
| **Rejected/superseded claim** | May appear only in revision history, adversarial analysis, or V1/V2 intellectual history. |

### No-laundering rule

A claim cannot become factual merely by moving through the sequence:

`episode interpretation → longitudinal ledger → specialist synthesis → full-series synthesis`.

Its evidence class remains what the source supports.

# 10. Confidence failure modes and audit rules

## 10.1 Character belief laundering

Do not convert a character's sincere explanation into world fact without independent support. Examples include Spotlight's Fear doctrine, Association accounts, Liang's manipulative assertions, and X's statement about Zero persistence.

## 10.2 Institutional doctrine laundering

A Commission rule or research classification is evidence of governance and knowledge state. It is not automatically a complete mechanic.

## 10.3 Montage causation inflation

Montage can establish thematic, biographical, or distributed-causal structure without proving that every adjacent image is a direct causal link.

## 10.4 Color/mechanics inflation

Color recurrence supports formal analysis only after local context is established. It cannot create a mechanic absent dialogue, motion, or repeated causal evidence.

## 10.5 Public consensus inflation

“The public” must be specified. A local crowd, fan platform, institutional broadcast audience, neighborhood, and ranking electorate are not interchangeable.

## 10.6 Survivor motive inflation

Grief, guilt, love, or protection can explain conduct without justifying it or proving one exclusive motive.

## 10.7 Benevolence inflation

Good intentions, successful rescue, free service, or sincere care do not prove legitimacy.

## 10.8 Late-reveal erasure

Later private history revises earlier interpretation but does not erase the causal effects of earlier public stories or invalidate the reasonableness of prospective readings.

## 10.9 Motion substitution

Contact sheets and stills cannot settle trajectory, duration, contact, order, or causation where continuous video is available.

## 10.10 Acoustic invention

The presence of complete audio does not authorize claims about timbre, score, mix, vocal delivery, or silence unless the relevant sequence was actually auditioned.

## 10.11 Cross-lingual authority inversion

Japanese and English sources are witnesses for alignment/localization. They do not override Mandarin lexical, modal, address, or register distinctions.

## 10.12 Naming retrojection

Later identity equivalences guide current synthesis but cannot be inserted into frozen episode bodies before the broadcast/source establishes them.
