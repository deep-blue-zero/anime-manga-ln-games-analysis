# Chinese Language, Voice, and Naming Ledger

## 1. Phase-2 T03A status and evidentiary boundary

This ledger is now the stabilized full-series authority for **Mandarin lexical choice, written dialogue structure, register, self-reference, address, naming, and translation-sensitive formulation** through Episode 24.

It does **not** claim that every vocal-performance dimension has been re-auditioned in one continuous Phase-2 pass. Complete Mandarin audio exists in the episode bundles, and episode readings sometimes contain directly verified audio observations, but the present stabilization is primarily text/register based. Claims about pitch contour, timbre, breath, tempo, vocal placement, sung technique, or acoustic recurrence remain bounded to previously verified local evidence or deferred to the later Mandarin-voice and sound specialist phases.

The source hierarchy is:

1. Mandarin audio and visible Chinese hardsub where directly checked;
2. reconstructed Chinese ASS/dialogue index;
3. frozen episode deep readings and their source locators;
4. aligned Japanese captions as a secondary semantic/timing witness;
5. official multilingual sources for name equivalence only;
6. V1 terminology as historical hypothesis, never current linguistic authority by itself.

The companion `TBHX_NAME_LOCALIZATION_CROSSWALK.md` resolves current identity surfaces. Historical episode sections below remain preserved under their original prospective boundaries and may therefore contain superseded provisional forms.

## 2. Governing full-series language thesis

> **In *To Be Hero X*, language does not merely describe identity. It assigns roles, converts persons into public functions, carries Trust and Fear through stories, and creates or breaks the conditions under which a person can answer the image imposed on them.**

The decisive linguistic conflict is not “real name versus fake name.” Hero names can mediate genuine vocation, inheritance, family, courage, and public service. Legal or personal names can also be ignored, weaponized, or made socially irrelevant. The more reliable distinction is between:

- a name or role the bearer can answer, revise, inhabit plurally, and leave;
- and a name or role whose grammar permits everyone else to speak for the bearer while making the bearer’s own reply unintelligible.

Across the season, identity is repeatedly negotiated through verbs of **being**, **becoming**, **naming**, **believing**, **managing**, **using**, **protecting**, **remembering**, and **leaving**. The season’s language begins with workplace and branding vocabulary, expands into sacred and institutional vocabulary, and ends by joining mythic causality to the ordinary labor boundary `准点下班`—clocking out on time.

## 3. Full-series identity grammar

### 3.1 The chain of equations and refusals

The most important repeated structure uses `是 / 就是 / 不是 / 谁`:

- `你是谁不重要` — **who you are is not important**;
- `现在的我就是奈斯` — **the present me is Nice**;
- `你并不是奈斯` — **you are not Nice**;
- `我其实不是奈斯` / `我并不是奈斯` — **I am actually not Nice**;
- `我叫林凌 / 是一个普通人` — **my name is Lin Ling / I am an ordinary person**;
- `我是奈斯` — **I am Nice**;
- `我是谁` — **who am I?**

This sequence is not a simple journey from false name to true essence. E01’s `现在的我就是奈斯` is a desperate but partly agentic appropriation of an imposed role. Xiao Yueqing’s `你并不是奈斯` is private counter-recognition. Lin’s later negative and positive self-naming restores answerability. Original Nice’s E23 movement from `我是奈斯` to `我是谁` shows what happens when fixed identity assertion can no longer absorb bodily fear, humiliation, and failure.

The governing transition is therefore:

`externally irrelevant person → imposed equivalence → private negation → self-produced negation → public self-naming → role-compatible agency`

for Lin, versus:

`perfect-role assertion → contamination → compulsive repetition → question without stable answer`

for Original Nice.

### 3.2 Becoming versus fixed identity

A-Hu’s E23 declaration uses future-directed becoming:

`我要成为守护新芽的大英雄`

> **I want to become the great hero who protects Xinya.**

Original Nice answers pressure with the fixed present-tense equation `我是奈斯`. The contrast matters. A-Hu names a purpose whose meaning can still be tested by relation; Nice defends a completed identity that cannot tolerate revision.

The series does not endorse becoming merely because it is future-oriented. Institutions also “cultivate,” “package,” and “produce” heroes. The ethical question is who can answer the project and whether the projected future remains revisable.

### 3.3 X and switchable presentation

E21 verbalizes `换个身份`, changing identity/presentation, while X refuses through `绝不加班` and later `准点下班`. The vocabulary refuses to treat herohood as a total metaphysical essence. X can occupy a public presentation, a civilian/office-worker presentation, and extraordinary world-state authority without allowing any one of them to abolish the others.

The personal name remains unknown. The absence is not proof of deficient personhood: X’s strongest private boundary is expressed through work-time language rather than legal-name disclosure.

## 4. Person-to-property and production vocabulary

The first arc establishes a durable lexicon of conversion:

- `个人` — individual person;
- `品牌` — brand;
- `包装` — package/present;
- `人设` — constructed persona/character setting;
- `附属品` — accessory/appendage;
- `枷锁` — shackle/fetter;
- `经营` — manage/operate, including business force;
- `棋子` — chess piece/pawn;
- `跳板` — stepping stone;
- `廉价劳动力` — cheap labor;
- `结款 / 补款 / 修改 / 加班 / 减薪 / 裁员` — payment, supplemental payment, revisions, overtime, wage cuts, layoffs.

The language is not decorative satire around a supernatural hero setting. Public belief has material effects, so advertising, media scripting, relationship contracts, image cleanup, research classification, and ranking management are literal inputs into bodies and power.

The strongest transformation is Juān jiě’s movement from Nice as `个人` to Nice as the company’s most important `品牌`. Once that grammar is accepted, coercion can be redescribed as continuity management, relationships as content, enemies as production labor, and private refusal as brand malfunction.

E24 generalizes the same model when X calls heroes `共同经营的棋子`: pieces jointly operated by the Commission and hero offices. The shift from local workplace vocabulary to constitutional critique is one continuous linguistic system.

## 5. Trust, Fear, theology, and contagion

### 5.1 Preserve `信赖` and `恐惧`

The setting’s governing pair is `信赖 / 信赖值` and `恐惧 / 恐惧值`. They should not be flattened into generic popularity/dislike, fame/infamy, positive/negative sentiment, or fan count.

`信赖` carries trust, reliance, confidence, and belief in a bearer’s expected capacity. `恐惧` carries fear and can become bodily, contagious, researchable, weaponized, and narratively transmissible. The numeric suffix `值` marks institutional quantification without exhausting the underlying social relation.

### 5.2 Theological escalation

The later season adds:

- `信徒` — believers/followers;
- `造神` — manufacture/create a god;
- `培育成为神` — cultivate someone into a god;
- `凌驾于一切之上` — stand above everything;
- `魔王Zero` — Demon King Zero.

These terms show that apotheosis is not simply “very high popularity.” It is a political-theological transition in which public expectation, institutional organization, and bearer receptivity can produce sovereignty.

The Commission’s founding purpose is described as preventing `造神`. Yet E24 also shows how anti-apotheosis institutions can speak in exterminatory terms—`围剿X`—and construct a founding demon category by `篡改历史`.

### 5.3 Fear as contagion and engulfment

Repeated verbs include:

- `感染` — infect;
- `吞没` — swallow/engulf;
- `被恐惧吞没` — be consumed by Fear;
- `我好像被恐惧感染了` — I seem to have been infected by Fear.

These forms make Fear more than an external reputation score. They also resist a purely medical reading: “infection” can describe material mechanics, social transmission, and the bearer’s loss of interpretive control at once.

### 5.4 Chinese/Japanese mechanics divergence

E18’s Mandarin `恐惧值` and Japanese `恐怖粒子` are not interchangeable technical terms. The former foregrounds a quantified value; the latter foregrounds particles. The Japanese witness may preserve a localization-specific explanatory model, but it cannot silently rewrite the Mandarin source ontology.

## 6. Recognition, care, and relational address

Mandarin social relation is often carried by address rather than exposition:

- `姐` in `娟姐`;
- `叔` in `岩叔`;
- `总` in `尚总 / 扎总`;
- `前辈` in `女王前辈 / 小强前辈`;
- diminutive `小` in `小青 / 小柚子 / 小强`;
- familiar prefix `阿` in `阿生 / 阿虎`;
- elder address `奶奶` in `王奶奶`;
- family nouns such as father, son, mother, husband, wife.

These forms do not merely supply atmosphere. They can locate a person inside care, hierarchy, debt, inheritance, or coercion. Converting them all into bare official names destroys information.

Several repeated care formulations deserve preservation:

- `没事的 / 有我在` — **it’s all right / I’m here**;
- `还记得你是谁` — **still remember who you are**;
- `深信不疑` — believe deeply/without doubt;
- `就是我儿子` — **he is my son**, followed later by clarification that the relation is not biological;
- `我不想在诺诺面前撒谎` — **I do not want to lie in front of Nuonuo**;
- `当然是我最爱的人` — **of course, the person I love most**.

The season repeatedly rejects the assumption that accurate legal identification is sufficient recognition. A hero name may be the correct relational name; a family relation may be chosen rather than biological; a private name may be known while the person is still instrumentalized.

## 7. Institutional, legal, and violent register

The language of governance develops through:

- `规则` — rules;
- `制定规则` — formulate rules;
- `漏洞` — loophole/gap;
- `约束` — constrain;
- `围剿` — encircle and suppress/exterminate;
- `格杀勿论` — kill without exception / summary lethal authorization;
- `宣战` — declare war;
- `组织` — organization, sometimes deliberately unnamed;
- `机密` / confidentiality-zone vocabulary;
- `篡改历史` — falsify/tamper with history.

Queen’s `不破不立` and rule-making language treats institutions as revisable structures. By contrast, `围剿X` and `格杀勿论` reveal moments when governance abandons answerability and moves toward enemy elimination.

The word `漏洞` is especially important because it can describe exploitable system weakness without resolving whether the “system” is metaphysical, institutional, representational, or all three. Avoid turning it into a universal game-mechanics term unless the local context supports that level of generality.

## 8. Ordinary labor language as anti-apotheosis

The season begins with Lin’s exploited creative labor and ends with X’s insistence on leaving work on time.

`准点下班` is funny because it is literal office language spoken at metaphysical scale. Its deeper force does not require treating the line as a hidden doctrinal slogan. It preserves a bounded relation between role and life:

- work has an end time;
- public need is not automatically infinite jurisdiction;
- exceptional capacity does not abolish privacy;
- refusing overtime is not the same as refusing responsibility altogether.

This ordinary register is the strongest linguistic difference between X and Zero. Zero’s vocabulary is total—god, all, sovereignty, destiny. X’s final vocabulary is bounded, mundane, and reversible.

## 9. Causality, fate, and retrospective correction

E24 uses a grammar of revised belief:

- `我本以为` — I originally thought;
- `但其实` — but in fact;
- `一切的因果` — all causes and consequences;
- `都早已被种下` — had already been sown;
- `把我们送往彼此的世界` — send/carry us into one another’s worlds;
- `违抗命运的安排` — resist the arrangement of fate.

This is not equivalent to proving strict predestination. `本以为 … 但其实` corrects X’s earlier causal story; `种下` is an organic metaphor of antecedent causes whose later growth still requires conditions and response. The paired Japanese `予定調和 / 巡り合わせ` strengthens a fate/arrangement reading but cannot by itself convert the Mandarin into deterministic closure.

The coin is therefore linguistically demoted from ultimate origin to remembered threshold. X once thought his hero path began there; he now says prior causes had already been planted.

## 10. Character and role register profiles

These profiles concern lexical choice, address, self-reference, and discourse position. They are not substitutes for a later full acoustic voice study.

### Lin Ling / Nice

Lin begins in subordinate workplace language shaped by clients, revisions, payment, overtime, and managerial contempt. The Nice role gives him declarative hero formulas but initially removes the right to his own name. His mature register combines ordinary self-naming with moral assertion: he can say he is Lin Ling, ordinary, and still committed to being a real hero.

Original Nice shows the inverse movement. His language becomes increasingly identity-defensive and repetitive—perfect persona, cheap smile, `我是奈斯`—before collapsing into `我是谁`.

### Xiao Yueqing / Moon

The public `女神` frame idealizes her. Her private `老娘` self-reference is coarse, assertive, and anti-goddess. Her decisive freedom language is negative and boundary-based: `我很清楚自己不想要什么`. The source does not require her to produce an immediately complete positive life plan before refusal becomes legitimate.

### Juān jiě / Miss J

Her register merges production, command, sunk-cost motivation, and strategic paradox: person to brand, sacrifice to obligation, imperfection to higher-value perfection. Even apparently supportive statements often place the continuation of the project before the bearer’s independent answer.

### Yang Cheng / E-Soul

His early language is organized by shame, imitation, debt to a rescuer, and the desire to stand `堂堂正正` before E-Soul. The arc’s linguistic development moves from role-seeking recognition toward action that can precede institutional validation. Self-belief is important, but the source does not define it as a second numeric Trust meter.

### Qīng / Lucky Cyan

Her language moves among sacred/public titles, burdened luck, personal naming, and familiar diminutives. `青`, `幸运青`, `圣女`, `扫把星`, and `小青` are not interchangeable: they place her inside different public, hostile, intimate, and professional relations.

### Liu Yuwei / Queen

Her register is unusually explicit about rules, institutions, reform, and strategic rupture. `不破不立` does not mean destruction is inherently virtuous; it argues that some structures cannot be renewed without first being broken. `女王前辈` also positions her through professional lineage rather than isolated supremacy.

### Luo Li / Loli

Her central vocabulary concerns appearance, résumé substitution, first impressions, cute-image reduction, force, beauty, surrender, and declaration of war. `以貌取人` and `第一印象往往会代替简历` translate image politics into hiring and institutional legibility. Family diminutive `莉莉` preserves a private identity distinct from the official English hero surface Loli.

### Wang Yi / Ghostblade

His private and public naming split is crucial. The hero identity `默杀` carries silence/killing connotations; the personal name Wang Yi carries fatherhood, marriage, memory, and answerability. His most important intimate statements concern truth before Nuonuo and naming the person he loves most. Silence in his arc is formal and relational, but broad claims about vocal timbre or acoustic suppression remain deferred.

### Little Johnny, Big Johnny, A-Sheng, and Vortex

This network depends heavily on chosen kinship and familiar address. `小强 / 大强` are not merely raw inputs that English “corrects” into Johnny names. `就是我儿子` gives the father-son relation direct linguistic reality before biological qualification. A-Sheng’s `阿` form and Vortex’s role name likewise distinguish intimate person from public hero system.

### Smile, Liang Long / Dragon Boy, and Nice

Their language turns performance technique into psychological command: smile, perfection, cheapness, fear, pain, garbage, cleanup. Smile’s imposed smile and Nice’s perfect image reveal how a professional instruction can become a condition of continued selfhood. Liang Long’s punitive language converts injury into doubled repayment and treats Fear as the only damage that counts.

### A-Hu / Ahu

The source name, clown role, media epithet, and later hero identity are distinct. His key movement is from `我不愿当小丑` to `我要成为…`. After awakening, his human speech becomes outwardly audible; Xinya’s reaction confirms a change in communicative state rather than merely a viewer convention.

### X and Zero

Zero’s vocabulary moves toward godhood, totality, sovereignty, history, and fate. X speaks in clipped, often ordinary work language, uses identity as switchable presentation, and refuses to let cosmic obligation abolish clock time. Their contrast is linguistic as well as metaphysical: total role versus bounded role plurality.

## 12. Translation and alignment discipline

### 12.1 Mandarin governs lexical interpretation

Japanese captions may diagnose timing, missing OCR, or broad semantic plausibility. They do not establish Mandarin politeness, slang, pronoun selection, address hierarchy, modality, or wordplay unless the Chinese layer independently supports those features.

### 12.2 Spoken `ta` and written pronouns

Mandarin speech does not acoustically distinguish `他 / 她 / 它`. Where the hardsub’s written pronoun matters, classify the evidence as textual rather than phonetic.

### 12.3 Quote-grade threshold

A publication-grade Chinese quotation requires:

- high-confidence reconstructed text or manual verification;
- consistency with the audible line where audition is available;
- proper-noun check against visible text or crosswalk;
- explanation of any material Japanese divergence.

The alignment-exception records below remain active. Plausible OCR is not automatically quotation-grade.

## 13. What T03A resolves

- Naming is a causal and ethical layer, not decorative metadata.
- Personal names, hero names, group brands, addresses, titles, epithets, and historical categories must remain distinguishable.
- The season’s identity grammar is organized by repeated equations, negations, self-naming, and becoming formulas.
- `信赖` and `恐惧` are not safely reducible to popularity and dislike.
- Commercial/production vocabulary and constitutional vocabulary belong to one continuous system of person-management.
- A-Hu’s E23 speech is newly outwardly audible/legible.
- The official English surfaces Commission, Dragon Boy, Ahu, Smile, Treeman Group, Zac, and The Johnnies are now routed without displacing Mandarin-primary semantics.
- X’s personal name remains unresolved; his personhood cannot be equated with legal-name disclosure.

#### E01 lexical anchors

##### CL-001 — `人人开创英雄`

Preserve the hardsub wording `开创`, despite its markedness. Do not silently normalize to `创造`. Paired JP `つくり出し` supports the broad semantics “bring/create heroes.”

The former boss's dismissive definition of Lin. The phrase returns as internalized voice during the fight. It defines ordinariness as social closure.

Advertising/PR language: package the substitution seamlessly. Strong evidence that media craft is an intentional input to Trust.

##### CL-007 — `女神`

Lin calls Xiao Yueqing his `女神`. In modern colloquial Chinese this can mean an admired idealized woman/celebrity and should not be mechanically inflated into literal worship or reciprocal romance.

The setting chooses trust/reliance and fear, not generic fame/hate. Preserve terminology in later synthesis.

##### CL-010 — `现在的我就是奈斯`

Identity formula emphasizing present self (`现在的我`) and emphatic equation (`就是`). Do not weaken to “I can act as Nice.”
