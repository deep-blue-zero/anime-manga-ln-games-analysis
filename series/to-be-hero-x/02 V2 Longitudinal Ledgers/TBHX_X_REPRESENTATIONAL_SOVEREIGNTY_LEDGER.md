## 4. Role sovereignty and power sovereignty

X's representational power has a character analogue.

He can apparently decide:
- when to be publicly legible as X;
- how he is rendered;
- when to remain an ordinary worker;
- whether to claim credit;
- when to leave the hero role.

Preferred character term:

> **role sovereignty**.

E24 makes role sovereignty politically central. The Association interprets the unowned civilian self as an attempt to stand above all. Yet the civilian self may be the boundary that prevents X's public god-role from becoming total.

This is `INT`, not a proven mechanics gate.
