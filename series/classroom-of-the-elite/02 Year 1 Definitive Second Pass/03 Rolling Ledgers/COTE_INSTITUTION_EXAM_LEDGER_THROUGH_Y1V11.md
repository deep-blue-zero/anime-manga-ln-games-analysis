# Institution and examination ledger through Y1V11

## Event Selection Exam and procedural capture

Volume 11 adds the **Event Selection Exam / 選抜種目試験**, a deliberately broad test of class-wide portfolios rather than a single standardized capacity. Classes prepare candidate events, narrow the portfolio, select participants, and designate a `司令塔` with formally defined intervention powers (`Y1V11-E027–E045`).

### What the examination measures

The test makes several dimensions of `実力` simultaneously relevant:

- possessed individual skill;
- knowledge of classmates;
- event design;
- information security;
- delegation;
- substitution depth;
- motivation and emotional regulation;
- commander intervention;
- and the ability to make distributed capacities socially usable.

Horikita’s polity performs well because leadership becomes coordination rather than personal omniscience. Sudō’s development defeats stale opponent data. Kōenji demonstrates that extraordinary capacity cannot be counted as collective power when its holder refuses mobilization.

### Institutional authorship above the exam

Tsukishiro changes the category of institutional analysis. The exam can be sophisticated and still cease to measure what it claims to measure if the communications/record layer is captured. In chess, Ayanokōji’s intended command is delayed and replaced; Sakayanagi notices the inconsistency; the substituted command remains the official event history (`Y1V11-E140–E145`).

This adds a new distinction:

> **displayed result is not identical to recorded result.**

A fair educational environment therefore requires not only good rules but trustworthy infrastructure and administrators.

## Governing question

> What does “merit” mean when an institution can alter the record through which merit becomes officially legible?

# Institution and examination ledger through Volume 10

## `クラス内投票 / Class Vote`

### Formal design

- three internal praise votes and three internal criticism votes per student;
- one external praise vote for a student in another class;
- praise and criticism cancel;
- ballots remain anonymous;
- class top receives a non-transferable `プロテクトポイント`;
- class bottom is expelled;
- ties rerun until unique top/bottom;
- twenty million private points can cancel the expulsion (`Y1V10-E013–E019`).

### Governing institutional fact

The extra exam exists because the first year had produced **no expellees**. Expulsion is therefore not merely a risk of failure; it is the output the institution wants the mechanism to generate (`E015`).

### Political consequence

The exam rewards organizational forms that can coordinate anonymous votes, external reputation, private capital, hidden bargains, or sovereign command. It measures a class polity as much as an individual student.

### Developmental contradiction

The same volume that invites irreversible pruning gives explicit evidence that student growth is hard to predict (`E006–E007`). This makes “future usefulness” a politically powerful but epistemically uncertain criterion.

### Four constitutional responses

- Horikita polity: public accountable triage.
- Ichinose polity: universal rescue plus equal-risk lottery fallback.
- Sakayanagi polity: sovereign pruning through information asymmetry.
- Ryūen polity: attempted removal of former ruler followed by voluntary subordinate rescue.

### Protection Point

Ayanokōji receives the new formal survival asset after Sakayanagi directs A-class external praise to him (`E088`). The system therefore converts reputational politics into future immunity from one institutional expulsion event.

### Institutional capture

Sakayanagi reports an adult order to expel Ayanokōji and a harsher earlier exam form allowing outside criticism; Tsukishiro then demonstrates control over surveillance and administrative position (`E111–E122`). The school can no longer be treated as a neutral experimental container at the local boundary.

## Governing question

> When an institution deliberately creates a scarcity of continued membership, does competent allocation of the victim demonstrate leadership—or merely reveal which theory of human disposability each polity is willing to enforce?

# Institution and examination ledger through Y1V08
## Mixed Training Camp delta

### Formal design

The school divides students by sex, requires cross-class small groups, combines them across three years, and scores morality, mental discipline, order, initiative, academics, and physical performance (`Y1V08-E006–E014`, `E121`). Group membership cannot be changed after formation (`E011`).

### Concentrated liability

Every small group must designate a responsible leader; failure to do so expels everyone. Leaders receive doubled rewards but bear expulsion risk, and may name one school-approved co-responsible student (`E015–E018`). Leadership is therefore not simply recognition; it is a priced concentration of reward and danger.

### Rescue market

An expulsion costs the class points and can be cancelled only through twenty million private points plus 300 additional class points (`E020–E021`). Hirata's pooling proposal treats reward as collective insurance, while Chabashira's intervention reframes points as individual self-protection (`E023–E026`). The institution does not merely permit rescue; it determines which communities can afford mercy.

### Temporary polity

Coerced cohabitation creates real cross-class and cross-year relationships. The mixed group develops through cooking, waiting, confession, labor exchange, and final handshakes (`E064`, `E088–E105`, `E120`, `E123`). The school creates the container but does not fully author what emerges inside it.

### Hidden political use

Nagumo's network organizes the women’s-side trap, finances a sacrificial leader, and manufactures an institutionally actionable causal record through testimony (`E093–E095`, `E130–E142`). Ayanokōji's reconstruction indicates that strategy preceded and helped create the rule field (`E138`).

### Institutional judgment

The Mixed Training Camp is not best described as a neutral measure of cohesion. It is simultaneously:

- developmental enclosure;
- risk-allocation market;
- leader-selection mechanism;
- priced rescue system;
- information partition;
- and political technology available to actors with prior access.

## Examination-family note

Christmas functions as a **counter-examination**. It reveals what formal tests cannot determine:

- whom students choose without point incentives;
- how they respond to affection;
- whether protection permits exit;
- and whether a person can value another without converting the relation into measurable utility.
