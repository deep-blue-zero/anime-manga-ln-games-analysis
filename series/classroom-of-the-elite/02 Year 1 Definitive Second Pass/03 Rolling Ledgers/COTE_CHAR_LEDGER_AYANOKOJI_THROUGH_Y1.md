# 綾小路清隆 / Ayanokōji Kiyotaka
## Canonical Year 1 state through *First File*

Year 1 ends with three propositions that must remain separate.

### 1. Ordinary life is a genuine self-chosen project

V11.5 supplies Ayanokōji's strongest local statement of self-authorship: ordinary three-year school life is the first goal he says he chose for himself (`Y1V11.5-E021–E026`). *First File* then strengthens that proposition through low-stakes experience rather than declaration. He describes ANHS as the place where he can live `学生らしい生活`, calls ordinary study and exercise the source of the greatest fulfillment, finds an irrelevant phone conversation intrinsically interesting, and directly thinks `オレは早く恵に会いたい` (`Y1FF-E059–E073`).

The Year 1 lock therefore rejects the model in which ordinary adolescence is merely a camouflage layer over an unchanged strategic core. Strategic concealment remains real, but so does intrinsically valued ordinary experience.

### 2. Developmental authorship is also becoming self-chosen

Manabu's legacy question does not merely make Ayanokōji want to be remembered. It helps him imagine cultivating students across all four classes and then seeing what developed people do against one another (`Y1V11.5-E087–E105`). This develops the V11 movement from `やるべきこと` to `やりたいこと`: the boy escaping an imposed educational project discovers that human development may itself be one of the first projects he wants to author.

That is a real form of liberation from White Room assignment. It is not automatically an ethical improvement. The Hirata intervention remains the control case: Ayanokōji can use coercive pressure when he believes functional restoration is necessary. The Year 1 endpoint therefore distinguishes **self-chosen purpose** from **legitimate jurisdiction over another person**.

### 3. Reciprocal authorship is the unresolved ethical constraint

V11.5 gives multiple countermodels to one-way development. Horikita receives authority over the Kushida problem even though Ayanokōji prefers the efficient expulsion answer; Keisei describes a class in which followers should challenge the leader; Hiyori can criticize Ryūen; Manabu refuses to prescribe Suzune's final form; and Kei explicitly answers Ayanokōji's confession for herself.

*First File* adds an epistemic reason for the same restraint. Ayanokōji's own weak ratings are accurate descriptions of the performance he allowed the institution to observe (`Y1FF-E004–E011`). The file is not useless; it is incomplete. The same principle applies to the psychological models Ayanokōji constructs of other people. Superior predictive knowledge cannot by itself prove that the model exhausts the person.

## Relationship and interiority corrections at the boundary

### Kei

The final lock rejects both poles of the common simplification.

- **Rejected:** Kei is only a tool/textbook. V11.5 contains an explicit mutual confession and affirmative embrace (`Y1V11.5-E135–E138`); *First File* adds direct present-tense desire, enjoyment of purposeless conversation, and reluctance to end contact (`Y1FF-E063–E073`).
- **Also rejected:** the relationship proves completed conventional love or permanent irreplaceability. Ayanokōji still uses `教科書`, connects romance to learning and Kei's dependency problem, frames `掛け替えのない存在` as a hoped-for future, and closes with `祈ろう` rather than certainty (`Y1V11.5-E139–E141`).

The strongest Year 1 formulation is: **explicit reciprocal romance exists inside an asymmetrical hidden curriculum whose future emotional meaning remains unresolved.**

### Ichinose

Ayanokōji gives Ichinose a one-year horizon and occupies a position she can describe as trustworthy even across class rivalry (`Y1V11.5-E079–E083`). Yet `介錯` preserves his willingness to conceptualize himself as the person who may terminate a failed developmental trajectory. *First File* then reveals Ichinose's private attachment and inability to complete the category beyond `大切な友達`, while showing her deliberately return to class competition (`Y1FF-E074–E082`). Care, attraction, competitive structure, and terminal judgment remain unresolved together.

### Horikita

By the boundary, Ayanokōji is no longer working with a person who can be described merely as an unaware proxy. She detects his authorship, challenges him, receives domains of decision he would resolve differently, and develops a self-conception that integrates rather than erases Manabu's influence. His interest in her development is increasingly intrinsic, but her independence is precisely what makes that interest ethically testable.

## The final Year 1 contradiction

> **Ayanokōji has learned to choose purposes for himself, but one of the purposes he most wants is to cultivate other people. Year 1 ends before he proves that he can reliably distinguish creating conditions for another person's growth from possessing jurisdiction over the person who grows.**

*First File* adds the epistemic corollary:

> **A useful model of another person is not the person.**

That principle applies to ANHS ratings, to guidebook summaries, to Ayanokōji's strategic cognition, and to the reader's own character theories.

# 綾小路清隆 / Ayanokōji Kiyotaka

## Current state through Volume 11

Volume 11 makes Ayanokōji’s developmental authorship explicitly **self-chosen**. The move from `やるべきこと` to `やりたいこと`, followed by `あいつを変えてみようと思う`, matters because the project of changing Horikita is no longer merely defensive necessity or accidental by-product (`Y1V11-E080–E081`). That is genuine self-authorship—and precisely why the ethical question becomes more difficult.

## Volume 11 delta

- **Self-authored purpose:** he consciously wants to cultivate Horikita rather than merely use her as visible cover.
- **Recognition of limits:** he explicitly ranks himself poorly in broad information gathering and ordinary knowledge of classmates (`Y1V11-E047–E048`). His strength is not universal social omniscience.
- **Lower-coercion teaching remains available:** he teaches Horikita chess in a way that leaves the act of learning with her, then delays commander intervention because he wants to see what her growth produces (`Y1V11-E137–E139`).
- **Coercive developmental authorship remains available:** with Hirata he knowingly deploys an accusation he does not literally accept, applies pressure until the defensive structure breaks, and privately justifies the action because the class needs `平田というパーツ` (`Y1V11-E095–E104`).
- **Ordinary relational desire is still real:** from the same Hirata episode comes his wish for a friend before whom he could show tears (`Y1V11-E104`).
- **Gift-giving exposes particularity:** the Kei birthday episode shows that generic popularity rankings cannot substitute for knowledge of one person; he recognizes his own miscalibration (`Y1V11-E054–E061`).
- **Desired rivalry becomes intrinsically valuable:** the Sakayanagi contest is not only strategic. He arranges the chance to fight her and values the encounter itself.
- **Institutional scale exceeds his own hidden sovereignty:** Tsukishiro demonstrates procedural authorship—delaying, replacing, and officially preserving a command Ayanokōji did not issue (`Y1V11-E140–E145`).
- **Human warmth closes the volume:** the slow walk with Sakayanagi and the return of `人肌のぬくもり` provide purposeless shared time after the contest (`Y1V11-E147–E149`).

## Current contradiction

> Ayanokōji’s liberation is becoming genuinely self-authored, but one of the first purposes he freely chooses is the authorship of another person’s development. Volume 11 proves he can scaffold growth without owning every step—and also proves he still chooses coercive pressure when he judges a person functionally necessary.

## Current comparative identifiers

- **Relation to power:** developmental environmental sovereignty now confronted by higher-order procedural sovereignty.
- **Relation to autonomy:** increasingly capable of leaving space for another person’s generative action; still willing to manufacture pressure and classify people by systemic function.
- **Relation to ordinary life:** gifts, tears, rivalry, touch, and purposeless shared time possess intrinsic weight.
- **Relation to truth/record:** no longer only an author of interpretation; he becomes the victim of an institution that authors the official record against him.
- **Principal virtue:** unusually precise perception of how environments enable growth.
- **Principal failure mode:** treating insight into another person’s development as sufficient jurisdiction to shape that person from above.

# 綾小路清隆 / Ayanokōji Kiyotaka
## Current state through Volume 10

Volume 10 advances Ayanokōji from **information-environment authorship** into the politics of **disposability allocation**. He rarely occupies the visible office that judges who should leave; instead he alters information, feasible options, rescue markets, and cross-class exchanges so that other actors make consequential choices inside environments he partially designed.

## Changes since Y1V09

- **He accepts pruning as a legitimate strategic response to imposed scarcity.** His internal description of the Class Vote as a chance to remove an unnecessary student is among Year 1’s clearest expressions of forward-looking utility judgment (`Y1V10-E020–E021`).
- **The text simultaneously supplies counterevidence to his confidence.** Sudō’s improvement and Chabashira’s admission that development is hard to predict establish an epistemic limit on irreversible future-value judgments (`E006–E007`).
- **He protects selectively rather than universally.** Kei is explicitly classified as important to the class and outside his expected sacrifice set (`E028`).
- **He supplies the hidden Yamauchi/Sakayanagi information chain but does not author Horikita’s public governing principle.** Horikita independently asks Manabu for courage, chooses accountable deliberation, names Yamauchi, and accepts visible liability (`E046–E054`).
- **His relationship with Ryūen now contains an irreducibly personal residue.** He admits both the strategic desire to build debt with Ichinose and that he simply did not want Ryūen expelled (`E099`).
- **His protection of Kei becomes ethically darker.** Manabe’s possible expulsion is privately valuable because it may ease Kei’s anxiety and increase her trust in him (`E093`).
- **He creates an autonomy-enhancing alternative for Ichinose while instrumentalizing the event.** The B–D exchange lets her refuse Nagumo’s unwanted dating condition, yet he later calls the event useful for building trust with her (`E096–E098`, `E101`).
- **He becomes institutionally protected.** Sakayanagi’s A-class praise produces 42 votes and a Protection Point, making him formally harder to expel (`E088`).
- **His subjective time remains evidence of ordinary-life development.** He experiences the school year as moving with a density comparable to many previous years (`E029`).
- **Tsukishiro exposes the next scale of conflict: rule and evidence control by adults.** Ayanokōji refuses physical retaliation not because he lacks capacity but because a selectively recorded attack on an acting chairman could be converted into administrative defeat (`E116–E122`).
- **Under that threat he chooses rivalry rather than pure concealment.** He formally asks Sakayanagi for a direct contest in the next exam (`E112`).

## Current contradiction

> Ayanokōji increasingly values people for reasons he cannot completely reduce to strategy, yet his most natural form of care remains architectural: he protects selected people by controlling who knows what, which options exist, and who bears the displaced cost.

## Current comparative identifiers

- **Relation to power:** hidden sovereignty shifting from information control to allocation of survival options.
- **Relation to autonomy:** capable of expanding another person’s feasible choices, but also capable of treating third-party harm as relationship-management material.
- **Relation to institutions:** no longer merely gaming school rules; he must now defend himself against adults who may author the rules and evidence environment against him.
- **Relation to ordinary life:** subjective time, rivalry, friendship, and selective attachment possess increasing intrinsic weight.
- **Principal virtue:** extraordinary ability to preserve chosen people under complex scarcity.
- **Principal failure mode:** private jurisdiction over costs that other people do not know are being incurred in their name.
- **Strongest unresolved question:** can he protect people without making himself the hidden sovereign of the conditions under which protection is possible?

# 綾小路清隆 / Ayanokōji Kiyotaka
## Current state through Volume 9

Volume 9 pushes Ayanokōji from institutional authorship toward **information-environment authorship**. He rescues Ichinose by deliberately worsening the rumor ecology until the school must regulate it, while separately offering her a humane but paternalistic path toward confession and self-forgiveness.

## Changes since Y1V08

- **He authors reputational causality.** Kushida supplies private information, Kiriyama posts the prepared rumors, Hashimoto is fed a controlled explanation, and institutional liability is deliberately increased (`Y1V09-E072–E086`).
- **His intervention is ethically double.** He provides nonjudgmental presence and tells Ichinose that every human being has a right to forgiveness, but privately calls the process `一之瀬帆波の心を壊す作業` and decides pressure must continue until she confronts the wound (`E087–E092`).
- **White Room memory becomes autobiographical empathy.** He recalls children who collapsed because they had no one to receive their weakness (`E090`).
- **His contingency against Kushida remains armed.** The half-points agreement is also an information-gathering operation designed to improve the eventual expulsion plan (`E081–E083`).
- **He can use genuine social moments instrumentally without making them unreal.** Kei’s Valentine exchange is authentic and simultaneously staged as counter-surveillance evidence (`E052–E053`, `E085`).
- **His ordinary affect continues to become concrete.** He wants Valentine chocolate, is pleased by Hirata’s praise, and recognizes socially busy days as part of the ordinary life he sought (`E048`, `E028`, `E037`).

## Current contradiction

> He articulates a principle that no person should be permanently denied by their worst act while continuing to reserve for himself the power to decide when other people must be pressured, exposed, or removed for developmental and strategic ends.

# 綾小路清隆 / Ayanokōji Kiyotaka
## Current state through Volume 8

Volume 8 places Ayanokōji beside a student who has scaled environmental authorship into school-wide political power. Nagumo does not merely solve a test: he uses institutional access and a year-wide network to make the test serve a prior strategy. Ayanokōji recognizes the structure, selectively assists the temporary group, withholds decisive information from Manabu, and answers Kei's insecurity with an unrestricted private promise of protection.

## Changes since Y1V07.5

- **His protection hierarchy becomes explicit.** Kei, Hirata, and Horikita are designated protection priorities, while Keisei, Akito, Haruka, and Airi are people he wants to remain but does not guarantee (`Y1V08-E033–E034`).
- **His nonintervention is selective rather than passive.** He declines to design a class-wide solution (`E027`, `E035`), but supplies Kei with instructions (`E031`), assists the mixed group (`E088`, `E105`, `E123`), recruits an upper-year information channel through Asahina (`E092–E095`), and reconstructs Nagumo's operation (`E138–E142`).
- **He withholds politically important information.** He predicts that Manabu may lose despite superior visible performance (`E074–E075`), lies to Tachibana about being on Manabu's side (`E108`), and does not convert his partial knowledge into an accountable warning structure. The text does not establish that he possessed a certain cost-free intervention.
- **He recognizes institutional authorship.** `E138–E142` expand the project from authored environments to authored rules: a strategy can precede and shape the examination that will later appear to measure everyone neutrally.
- **He offers Kei categorical survival protection.** The promise to prevent her expulsion `どんな手を使っても` combines care, operational dependence, and private sovereignty (`E143`).
- **His ordinary and embodied range remains visible.** He finds Ichinose's company non-unpleasant (`E053`), sustains private naming and information exchange with Kei (`E068–E069`), experiences embarrassment in the bath hierarchy (`E071`), and gives practical help without demanding public credit (`E088`, `E105`).

## Current contradiction

> He recognizes that rule authorship can weaponize another person's trust, yet answers relational vulnerability by making himself the unrestricted private author of Kei's safety.

# 綾小路清隆 / Ayanokōji Kiyotaka
## Current state through Volume 7.5

Volume 7.5 gives Ayanokōji the clearest ordinary romantic opportunity of Year 1 and shows why he cannot inhabit it simply. Satō offers transparent affection and conventional adolescent experience; Ayanokōji refuses partly because he does not reciprocate and partly because choosing her would destabilize Kei, the person who has become operationally indispensable to his hidden life.

## Changes since Y1V07

- **The question of irreplaceability becomes explicit.** Before the date he asks whether another person can become `かけがえのない存在` (`Y1V07.5-E001–E002`).
- **His preference profile becomes concrete.** Cinema is becoming a genuine hobby; he prefers first-hand experience, Beethoven, Mozart, rain sounds, birdsong, and perhaps energetic girls, though he suppresses these answers while performing normality (`Y1V07.5-E020–E023`, `E053–E055`, `E059`).
- **He wants ordinary romance without possessing the intuitive structure required to sustain it.** He enjoys the date, wants a girlfriend, and acknowledges sexual curiosity, but cannot imagine the future Satō offers (`Y1V07.5-E052–E067`).
- **The Satō refusal is ethically and strategically mixed.** He refuses promptly rather than using her for experience, but he also treats her as a possible replacement for Kei and evaluates both girls through operational criteria (`Y1V07.5-E061–E068`).
- **The White Room is recognized as an internal system.** He states that even after leaving it he remains inside it and still defaults to insurance and final-victory logic (`Y1V07.5-E069–E070`).
- **Practical concern for Kei is genuine but poorly communicated.** He buys medicine before later conflict, observes her health indirectly, and underestimates her strength; secrecy and risk management leave her believing he did not care (`Y1V07.5-E019`, `E097–E098`).
- **He moves the Kei relationship toward negotiated partnership.** He names her a trusted partner, accepts her objection to unilateral definition, receives her promise of reciprocal rescue, shares private given-name use, and requests continued help under explicit terms (`Y1V07.5-E082–E086`, `E96–E99`).
- **Protection remains structurally possessive.** He acknowledges that he prepared the transfer from Hirata to himself as Kei’s host and continues to understand relational security through exclusivity and necessity (`Y1V07.5-E093–E095`).
- **He now calls Kei indispensable rather than irreplaceable.** The ending grants personal exception and functional necessity while leaving emotional singularity unresolved (`Y1V07.5-E100`).
- **His political horizon expands.** He studies Nagumo, Kiriyama, inter-year exams, and the 800-million-point plan while continuing to preserve Ryūen as a strategic possibility (`Y1V07.5-E036–E045`, `E76–E81`).

## Current contradiction

> He is beginning to value experiences and people that possess no immediate strategic score, but he still translates intimacy into replacement value, curriculum, and controlled protection.

## Current comparative identifiers

- **Relation to power:** hidden environmental sovereignty expanding into political brokerage and relational selection.
- **Relation to autonomy:** capable of ethical restraint toward Satō; still structures Kei’s options through superior information and protection.
- **Relation to truth:** increasingly honest about the White Room’s internal persistence, but withholds that truth from the people closest to him.
- **Relation to ordinary life:** specific tastes, hobbies, gifts, and dating now possess intrinsic interest.
- **Relation to intimacy:** desires it, studies it, and cannot yet separate it from utility.
- **Principal virtue:** practical concern and unusual ability to preserve selected people under complex danger.
- **Principal failure mode:** defines another person’s significance through how well she fits the system he has built.
- **Strongest unresolved question:** can a person become important to him even after ceasing to be necessary?
