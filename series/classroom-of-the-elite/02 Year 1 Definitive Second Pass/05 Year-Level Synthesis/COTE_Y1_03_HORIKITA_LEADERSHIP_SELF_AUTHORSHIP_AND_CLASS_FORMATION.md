# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ』
## Year 1 — Horikita Suzune
### Leadership, self-authorship, and class formation
### From solitary excellence to distributed, correctable leadership

> **CANONICAL YEAR-1 SPECIALIST SNAPSHOT**
>
> This document is the primary analytical home for Horikita Suzune's Year 1 psychology, leadership formation, relation to Manabu's inheritance, class-building practice, and emerging political self-authorship. It synthesizes the fourteen canonical Japanese novel deep readings, the *First File* paratext audit, the final Year 1 evidence lock, the `THROUGH_Y1` character, relationship, institution, and class-polity ledgers, the Japanese terminology index, and the frozen `Y1_01–Y1_02` specialist layer.
>
> It does not import Year 2, Volume 0, *Second List*, Year 3, adaptation material, wikis, or later-franchise interpretation. Questions Year 1 leaves open—especially the long-term Kushida problem, student-council expansion, and whether Class D can survive without Ayanokōji's hidden constitution—remain open here.

---

# 0. Scope, primary home, and interpretive controls

Horikita is frequently reduced to one of three roles:

- Ayanokōji's visible proxy;
- Manabu's imitative younger sister;
- or the stern class leader who gradually becomes kinder.

Each captures part of Year 1. None is adequate as a total account.

She does receive crucial cues, protection, environmental pressure, and hidden strategic support from Ayanokōji. She does organize much of her early identity around a fantasy of becoming the person she believes Manabu would approve. She does become more capable of recognizing the value, vulnerability, and developmental possibilities of other people.

But her Year 1 arc is not reducible to external authorship, sibling imitation, or moral softening.

The more exact question is:

> **Can individual excellence become leadership without turning weaker or different people into defects to be corrected or discarded?**

The answer requires five controls.

## 0.1 Influence is not the opposite of self-authorship

No human self in the series is uninfluenced. Horikita's identity is formed through:

- Manabu's approval and rejection;
- family memory;
- school placement;
- Ayanokōji's interventions;
- Kushida's hostility;
- Sudō's need and growth;
- Hirata's social legitimacy;
- class defeat;
- bodily vulnerability;
- and institutional pressure.

The relevant question is not whether those forces caused her development. They plainly did.

The question is whether she can turn inherited purposes into judgments she can:

- understand;
- own;
- defend publicly;
- revise under criticism;
- and sometimes direct against the expectations of the people who shaped her.

That is the standard used here for self-authorship.

## 0.2 Effective leadership is not automatically legitimate leadership

Horikita becomes increasingly effective during Year 1. Effectiveness alone does not settle whether she is right.

The Class Vote is the clearest example. Her intervention breaks anonymous coalition politics and prevents a conspiratorial faction from selecting its target without public scrutiny. It also culminates in naming a student for expulsion under conditions where future value is uncertain.

The analysis therefore separates:

- effectiveness;
- procedural accountability;
- substantive justice;
- developmental wisdom;
- and institutional coercion.

A decision may be more governable without being morally infallible.

## 0.3 Kindness is not the same as nonjudgment

Horikita does not mature by ceasing to judge.

By the end of Year 1 she is willing to make more consequential judgments than she was at the beginning. The difference is that she increasingly asks:

- what evidence supports the judgment;
- what future development may change it;
- who bears the cost;
- whether affected people can answer back;
- and whether she will publicly accept responsibility.

Her development is therefore not severity becoming softness. It is severity becoming more relational, developmental, and answerable.

## 0.4 Development must be separated from revelation

Year 1 reveals that Horikita was once more expressive, that her imitation of Manabu intensified over time, and that her long hair was connected to his old test of whether she would erase her own preference for his approval (`Y1V11-E077–E079`, `Y1V11.5-E090–E093`).

Those are revelations about earlier formation.

Her growing capacity to teach, rely on others, delegate, own public decisions, detect manipulation, preserve difference, and lead a heterogeneous class is development.

The two should not be collapsed into a story where a hidden “true Horikita” simply returns.

## 0.5 *First File* records a state; it does not define an essence

The guidebook's July profile gives Horikita:

- academics A;
- judgment B-;
- cooperation E;
- physical ability B+;
- intelligence A- (`Y1FF-E012`).

Its prose interprets her D-class placement through inability to consider others and later notes that she gradually learns the difference between solitude and true isolation (`Y1FF-E013–E014`).

Those records are useful because the cooperation E is credible as an early visible state. It is not a timeless verdict. Treating it as her permanent nature would erase the very development the guidebook retrospectively acknowledges.

# 1. Executive thesis

Horikita begins Year 1 with a plausible but politically impoverished theory of excellence.

People differ in ability.

Weak performance creates collective costs.

A school organized around rank and advancement cannot pretend those differences are unreal.

Students who refuse to improve may burden students who take the institution seriously.

None of those observations is wholly false.

Her error lies in moving too quickly from current measurable weakness to total political worth.

The early Horikita treats the class as a damaged inventory:

- strong members should be protected;
- weak members should be corrected;
- and those who remain useless may be discarded.

This is narrow meritocracy not because she believes ability matters, but because she treats ability as:

- presently visible;
- individually possessed;
- relatively stable;
- hierarchically comparable;
- and separable from the relationships that make it socially usable.

Year 1 systematically breaks that model.

Sudō is academically weak, volatile, and dangerous to the class. He is also an exceptional athlete whose discipline, restraint, and leadership can develop.

Ike appears unserious in the classroom and becomes practically important on the island.

Hirata is not the class's strongest strategist, but his voluntary legitimacy and emotional accessibility are indispensable.

Kushida possesses social capacities Horikita conspicuously lacks, even while her private hostility creates a severe security threat.

Kei's authority over social atmosphere cannot be represented by grades.

Kōenji possesses extraordinary ability that the class cannot simply requisition.

Ayanokōji's most important capacities are invisible precisely because he authors what others are allowed to measure.

Horikita's leadership arc is therefore not the discovery that weak people are secretly equal in every respect.

It is the discovery that:

> **a leader never encounters ability as a complete, static, privately owned quantity. Ability develops, changes value with environment, becomes usable through relationships, and remains partly voluntary.**

This changes what leadership means.

At first, Horikita tries to lead by being the best solver in the room.

By the end of Year 1 she is increasingly capable of:

- translating knowledge into forms other people can use;
- recognizing that domain experts know things she does not;
- distributing authority;
- coordinating unequal capacities;
- making criteria public;
- accepting correction;
- distinguishing knowledge of talent from ownership of talent;
- and preserving the possibility that another person may become more valuable than current measurement predicts.

Her mature Year 1 model is therefore best described as **distributed, correctable developmental leadership**.

“Distributed” means the class's strength does not reside only in her.

“Correctable” means leadership must permit dissent, new information, and reversal.

“Developmental” means a current rating is evidence, not destiny.

This is also a story of self-authorship.

Horikita begins by trying to become the person she imagines Manabu wants. Her grades, demeanor, hairstyle, and emotional austerity are organized around that borrowed endpoint. The crucial correction of Volume 11.5 is that liberation does not require declaring the resulting self unreal. She calls the imitation-organized identity a `偽者`, then immediately recognizes the years lived that way as `紛れもない私` (`Y1V11.5-E003–E005`).

Her endpoint is integration, not purification.

Manabu remains causally important.

Ayanokōji remains causally important.

Sudō, Kushida, Hirata, and the class remain causally important.

Independence emerges when those influences become material for judgments whose governing purpose is now hers.

That is why the strongest Year 1 formulation is:

> **Horikita becomes independent not by escaping influence, but by turning influence into a political and ethical practice she can publicly own, defend, revise, and sometimes use to resist the people who taught her.**

The unresolved problem is structural.

Her visible leadership grows inside a class whose hidden security, information, and contingency systems remain substantially controlled by Ayanokōji. She is increasingly capable of producing answers he did not specify. She does not yet possess the full causal map of the polity she is leading.

The final Year 1 question is therefore:

> **Can a class built through both public leadership and invisible authorship become genuinely self-governing before the hidden author withdraws, transfers allegiance, or reveals the scale of his power?**

# 2. Initial Horikita — excellence as self-sufficiency

Horikita's opening posture is often remembered simply as arrogance.

Arrogance is present, but the posture is more systematic than vanity.

She believes:

1. ability is principally individual;
2. disciplined people should not be forced to carry undisciplined people;
3. friendship created by pressure is not genuine trust (`Y1V01-E009`);
4. sociality without utility is largely distraction;
5. and dependence is evidence that one has failed to become strong enough.

This worldview makes her unusually well adapted to some dimensions of ANHS.

She studies.

She plans.

She understands incentives.

She accepts that actions have consequences.

She does not need external praise from classmates.

But the same worldview makes her badly adapted to collective action.

She confuses the ability to solve a problem with the ability to make a group capable of solving it.

She can identify an academic deficit and still fail to teach the student who has it.

She can identify irresponsibility and still fail to create a coalition capable of changing it.

She can see that a classmate is politically dangerous and still lack the social access required to contain that danger.

She can be right about a weakness and wrong about what follows from the weakness.

The early class offers a brutal institutional mirror.

Horikita believes she should have been placed in Class A because her academic and physical profile resembles what the school claims to reward. The school places her in Class D.

The placement does not prove the institution possesses omniscient insight. Year 1 repeatedly warns against treating institutional sorting as total truth.

But the placement identifies something real: her capacities are not yet socially generative.

She can perform.

She cannot yet make a polity perform.

That distinction is the starting point of her leadership arc.

## 2.1 `孤高` and `孤立`

Manabu's early distinction between proud solitude and isolation gives the problem its Japanese vocabulary (`Y1V01-E017`).

`孤高 / kokō` can imply principled elevation or dignified independence.

`孤立 / koritsu` is separation without effective relation.

Horikita imagines herself closer to the first category.

The novel initially places her closer to the second.

Her isolation is not proof that she is secretly weak in every domain. It is proof that individual strength does not automatically create political capacity.

## 2.2 The class as defective inventory

Her early use of defect language matters because it reveals how she conceptualizes people.

A defect is a deviation from the required specification.

If the specification is fixed and the object cannot change, disposal may look rational.

Volume 1 begins revising that ontology when she recognizes that a defective product may be repaired or changed (`Y1V01-E030`).

This is not yet a full philosophy of human development.

It is the first crack in static classification.

The significance is not that she stops noticing defects.

It is that defect ceases to prove finality.

# 3. Manabu — imitation, rejection, and the borrowed endpoint

Manabu is not merely Horikita's admired older brother.

He is the specification around which she constructs herself.

She interprets his excellence as evidence of what a worthy Horikita should be. She then organizes:

- grades;
- bodily discipline;
- emotional restraint;
- social distance;
- ambition;
- and appearance

around a model she believes will close the distance between them.

That makes the sibling relationship both aspirational and deforming.

## 3.1 The early violence must remain ethically visible

The Volume 1 confrontation is sometimes retrospectively softened because Manabu later recognizes her growth.

That is a mistake.

His early physical intimidation and assault are abusive. Later explanation does not transform violence into legitimate pedagogy.

The fact that he wanted her to stop imitating him does not mean the method respected her personhood.

This matters because Horikita's story is centrally concerned with the difference between producing development and having authority to impose the developmental method.

Manabu's conduct belongs inside the same ethical problem that later surrounds Ayanokōji.

A person may correctly identify another's stagnation and still violate her while trying to force change.

## 3.2 Rejection intensifies imitation

Manabu's coldness does not free Suzune from his model.

It increases its jurisdiction.

Because approval remains scarce, she interprets every failure to receive it as evidence that she has not yet reproduced him adequately. Volume 11 later reveals that her imitation intensified over time and that his own coldness contributed to her dependency (`Y1V11-E077–E079`).

This is why surface achievement cannot solve the relationship.

More grades cannot answer a problem whose governing standard is borrowed.

More severity cannot answer a problem produced by the fantasy that severity is what he wants.

## 3.3 Manabu as horizon problem

The final sibling correction is not that Manabu turns out to have been the perfect endpoint after all.

He tells her that treating him as the endpoint narrowed the horizon of a person who may possess capacities he does not (`Y1V11.5-E090–E093`).

This is the decisive succession principle:

> inheritance should transmit vocabulary, standards, and memory without transmitting a compulsory final form.

Horikita can inherit:

- discipline;
- seriousness;
- public responsibility;
- concern for the school;
- and the demand to be strong and kind

without reproducing Manabu's personality.

That is succession without imitation.

# 4. Pedagogy — from ranking learners to translating knowledge

Horikita's first study group is one of the best miniature models of her whole Year 1 arc.

She possesses the relevant academic knowledge.

She wants the class to avoid failure.

She is willing to spend time teaching.

Yet the first effort collapses because she assumes the learners should already possess the foundations from which her explanations begin (`Y1V01-E014`).

She treats misunderstanding as evidence of laziness or inadequacy rather than as information about the teaching environment.

The failure reveals a critical distinction:

> **knowing a subject is not the same as knowing how to make the subject usable by another person.**

That distinction later scales into leadership.

## 4.1 Humiliation is informationally poor

Humiliation may produce compliance.

It often produces concealment instead.

A student who expects contempt has reason to hide confusion, avoid asking questions, or withdraw from the relationship. Horikita's initial method therefore degrades the very information she needs to teach effectively.

This is an early example of how hierarchical certainty can make a leader less knowledgeable.

## 4.2 Basketball research as epistemic humility

Her correction is concrete.

She researches Sudō's basketball world, apologizes, and redesigns instruction around a domain whose structure he already understands (`Y1V01-E019–E020`).

The important act is not mere niceness.

It is translation.

She accepts that the learner's existing capacities are part of the route by which new knowledge must travel.

That is a first movement from:

> people should adapt to my correct explanation

toward:

> my explanation must account for the person who is supposed to use it.

## 4.3 Risking her own score

Horikita later lowers her English result and accepts personal risk to influence the institutional threshold (`Y1V01-E026`).

This is politically important.

She is no longer merely demanding that weak classmates bear the cost of their weakness. She puts her own standing into the coalition's survival mechanism.

The class is not yet a community she loves.

It has already become a project whose costs she is willing to share.

## 4.4 Pedagogy as leadership prototype

The study-group sequence establishes the Year 1 leadership template in compressed form:

1. superior knowledge creates an initial claim to authority;
2. authority fails when it cannot cross the gap between unequal people;
3. failure provides information about the leader's model;
4. the leader learns the follower's domain;
5. instruction becomes reciprocal adaptation;
6. and the leader assumes part of the risk.

Horikita never abandons standards.

She learns that standards become politically meaningful only through an environment in which people can approach them.

# 5. Volume 2 — truth, evidence, and autonomous operationalization

The Sudō case expands Horikita's leadership problem from teaching into public adjudication.

Sudō may be telling the truth.

Truth is insufficient without:

- evidence;
- credibility;
- witnesses;
- institutional procedure;
- and a narrative another actor can act upon.

Horikita initially retains a punitive intuition. Sudō's ordinary aggression has created the reputation that now makes him vulnerable, and she is willing to imagine the unjust consequence as a lesson.

That reasoning contains a real causal observation and an illegitimate conclusion.

Past conduct can explain why a person is disbelieved.

It does not make a false accusation just.

A leader is not entitled to use institutional injustice as an educational instrument merely because suffering may teach prudence.

## 5.1 From abstract logic to institutional proof

Horikita's arguments are not enough to win the hearing.

The case teaches her that correct reasoning must be made procedurally legible.

The distinction between what happened and what can be established becomes part of her political education.

## 5.2 Ayanokōji seeds; Horikita operationalizes

Ayanokōji gives her a cue path involving cameras, corridors, and the possibility that one lie may have to be ended by another (`Y1V02-E024`, `Y1V02-E044`).

It would be inaccurate to describe the resulting operation as wholly hers.

It would also be inaccurate to describe her as an empty puppet.

She independently:

- reconstructs the cue;
- verifies relevant conditions;
- recruits Ichinose and Kanzaki;
- accepts financial and reputational risk;
- and becomes publicly answerable for the trap.

The best formulation is **seeded discovery with autonomous operationalization**.

Ayanokōji authors part of the epistemic environment.

Horikita turns that environment into public action.

This distinction will remain central throughout Year 1.

## 5.3 Sudō as trajectory

At the end of the incident, Horikita tells Sudō that he made progress and can continue to grow (`Y1V02-E041`).

This is a meaningful revision of the early merit model.

The unit of judgment is no longer only present performance.

Trajectory enters.

A student may be dangerous now and valuable later.

That proposition does not erase the need for consequences.

It makes final disposal epistemically harder to justify.

## 5.4 Detecting hidden authorship

Horikita reconstructs Ayanokōji's guidance and accuses him of having rolled her in his palm (`Y1V02-E044`).

This is the beginning of an important counter-development.

The more he teaches her through hidden cues, the more capable she becomes of identifying the teaching environment itself.

Her growth therefore does not simply make her a better instrument.

It increases the cost of instrumentalizing her invisibly.

# 6. Volume 3 — formal leadership and the collapse of solitary competence

The island examination gives Horikita a formal office before she has a complete political practice.

She becomes leader because she combines:

- responsibility;
- academic competence;
- strategic seriousness;
- and lower social visibility than figures such as Hirata (`Y1V03-E008`).

The selection is rational.

It also exposes the difference between formal designation and collective leadership.

Hirata continues to organize service and morale.

Ike's practical knowledge matters.

Kushida remains socially accessible.

Ayanokōji controls decisive hidden information.

Horikita holds the card.

She does not hold the entire class.

## 6.1 Class B as countermodel

Horikita observes Class B's differentiated roles and cohesion as an almost seamless solidarity (`Y1V03-E021`).

The comparison matters because Ichinose's class does not succeed by eliminating unequal capacities.

It turns them into a coordinated structure.

Horikita sees a class polity whose authority is relational rather than merely positional.

## 6.2 The body refutes self-sufficiency

Illness makes Horikita's body a political fact.

She conceals weakness because she associates dependence with failure and fears losing control of the examination.

The concealment increases class risk.

Her body demonstrates what her ideology resists:

> no amount of individual excellence abolishes embodied contingency.

A leader who cannot disclose incapacity prevents the group from reallocating around it.

Vulnerability hidden in the name of strength becomes a collective liability.

## 6.3 The keycard and relational failure

The theft is not merely a tactical defeat.

Horikita later concludes that knowing how to rely on someone could have prevented the failure and admits that she lacks a person close enough to support her in that way (`Y1V03-E042`).

This is one of the year’s clearest self-diagnoses.

She does not need generic sociability.

She needs trustworthy interdependence.

## 6.4 `仲間` and the proposition to fight as two

Ayanokōji tells her that if she cannot fight alone, they can fight as two (`Y1V03-E043`). She calls him `仲間 / nakama` while still trying to preserve agency and distance (`Y1V03-E044`).

The term matters because it names a category her initial worldview lacked.

A companion is not simply:

- a friend produced by social pressure;
- a subordinate;
- or an expert hired for utility.

A companion is someone whose presence changes what one can survive without dissolving the self into dependence.

## 6.5 Development inside an authored environment

The ethical difficulty is that Ayanokōji helps produce the crisis from which she learns.

He manipulates the conditions around the leader card, permits her collapse to advance the strategy, supports her body, accepts losses, and later gives her public credit (`Y1V03-E034–E047`).

The result is not wholly false.

Horikita did labor, observe, endure, and change.

The causal history is false.

Her public legitimacy is therefore produced through a mixture of:

- genuine effort;
- actual class coordination;
- hidden intervention;
- and controlled attribution.

This becomes the founding ambiguity of `POLITY-HORIKITA`.

## 6.6 Public credit creates real office

False causal attribution does not make every consequence unreal.

Classmates now see Horikita as a strategist.

Rivals target her.

Teachers and leaders treat her as a political actor.

She acquires opportunities and responsibilities because the public story exists.

The public office is real even when its origin is partly authored by someone else.

Her Year 1 task is to become capable enough that the office ceases to be merely borrowed.

# 7. Volumes 4 and 4.5 — public role, hidden constitution, and ordinary dependence

Volume 4 enlarges the gap between Horikita's visible status and the class's hidden causal architecture.

Ryūen correctly infers that she did not act alone on the island and begins searching for the hidden collaborator (`Y1V04-E017`). Ayanokōji actively performs Horikita's superiority and his own uselessness under surveillance (`Y1V04-E029`).

Horikita is therefore both leader and decoy.

Her reputation protects the hidden strategist.

The hidden strategist also prevents her from knowing the polity she appears to govern.

## 7.1 The emerging shadow constitution

By this point the class has at least two overlapping governments.

The visible layer includes:

- Horikita's strategic reputation;
- Hirata's voluntary social authority;
- Kushida's broad accessibility;
- and formal class procedures.

The hidden layer increasingly includes:

- Ayanokōji's information;
- Kei's privately controlled network;
- covert protection decisions;
- and contingencies unknown to the public leaders.

This is not merely a tactical arrangement.

It is a constitutional problem.

A class cannot hold hidden power accountable when it does not know the power exists.

## 7.2 The bottle episode as political miniature

Volume 4.5's bottle incident is comic, but analytically important.

Horikita exhausts her own attempts, indirectly asks for help, avoids surveillance to preserve dignity, accepts assistance through a medium that lowers embarrassment, and finally says `……ありがとう` (`Y1V04.5-E029–E033`).

The scene provides a low-stakes version of a central political lesson:

> autonomy does not require never needing help; it requires access to help that does not erase dignity or convert need into ownership.

Airi's chat response is particularly instructive. Horikita can receive assistance without having to perform a humiliating public confession.

The form of the relationship changes the cost of dependence.

## 7.3 Ordinary leisure as educational environment

Horikita's admission that the day was unexpectedly enjoyable matters because it expands value beyond advancement.

The class cannot become a community if every interaction is justified only by points, rank, or correction.

Ordinary time creates forms of knowledge unavailable in formal examinations:

- who can be trusted with embarrassment;
- who notices discomfort;
- who respects privacy;
- and who can help without turning help into a debt.

This is counter-curriculum for Horikita as much as it is for Ayanokōji.

# 8. Volume 5 — relational coordination and the first self-authored leadership turn

The sports festival is the point at which Horikita's acceptance of interdependence becomes a theory of **coordination**.

Before the festival, she has already learned that other people matter. Her initial plan nevertheless treats that truth at aggregate scale. Strong athletes should be concentrated where they can maximize class performance; weaker students can absorb unfavorable matchups; compensation may repair some individual loss afterward.

The plan is not irrational.

It is also not neutral.

Shinohara's objection exposes the distributive structure. A weaker student who is used as a `捨て駒 / expendable piece` loses more than the satisfaction of winning. She may lose private points, academic advantages, and the opportunity to experience herself as a meaningful participant (`Y1V05-E015–E018`). Horikita's plan recognizes difference but still treats the stronger actor's objective as the natural standard for everyone else.

## 8.1 The three-legged race: strength must enter a relationship

Ayanokōji's three-legged-race lesson is deliberately simple. He overpaces Horikita until she experiences the position she normally assigns to weaker partners. He then tells her to watch the other person and, when necessary, yield initiative (`Y1V05-E020`).

The lesson should not be flattened into “the strong must always slow down.”

Its political content is:

> **An ability that cannot cross the relation between people is not yet socially usable ability.**

The faster runner remains faster. The stronger student remains stronger. But collective performance depends upon timing, communication, adjustment, and the willingness to let another person's limit alter one's method.

This is one of the cleanest low-coercion developmental episodes in the year. The goal is explicit, the demonstration is bounded, and Ayanokōji eventually leaves the next step to her. Horikita's later choices therefore cannot be treated as mechanically produced obedience.

## 8.2 Sudō is her developmental mirror

Sudō's festival crisis reveals the resemblance between them.

Both possess conspicuous ability.

Both want recognition.

Both interpret superior performance as a claim to authority.

Both initially struggle to adapt their strength to people who cannot match them.

The principal difference is social presentation. Horikita's pride is disciplined and academically legible. Sudō's is embodied, volatile, and publicly disruptive.

When Sudō admits that he wanted classmates to recognize his value, Horikita can answer because she recognizes the same structure in herself. Her audience was largely Manabu; his audience is the class. She rejects the claim that birth or inherited judgment fixes identity:

> `自分自身が決めるもの`

What one becomes is something one decides for oneself (`Y1V05-E044–E048`).

The intervention succeeds because she does not speak as an untouched superior. She admits her own hunger for recognition and the failure of her earlier treatment of classmates (`Y1V05-E052`). The lesson becomes shared diagnosis rather than correction imposed from above.

## 8.3 The promise is bilateral

Horikita asks Sudō to lend her his strength and offers hers in return. Sudō experiences this as recognition of `存在意義` beyond basketball (`Y1V05-E053–E054`).

The relation is not equal in every dimension.

- Sudō's attraction gives her unusual motivational power.
- Horikita possesses greater academic and institutional competence.
- She still sees his athletic capacity as class infrastructure.

Yet the form differs substantially from simple command.

She accepts an ongoing obligation to pull him back when he loses direction. He accepts a behavioral covenant against unjustified violence. Each person's strength is supposed to compensate for a weakness in the other.

Horikita's refusal to classify the new relation simply as friendship or romance is analytically useful. She calls him `仲間`—a companion or member of a shared side—while recognizing that the bond contains political and personal dimensions not exhausted by either intimacy or utility.

## 8.4 Giving up the relay

Her willingness to surrender the personally important anchor position matters because it tests whether she can subordinate her Manabu-directed desire to class viability.

Earlier she had concealed the private motive. Once she admits that she wants to run against her brother, Sudō voluntarily yields (`Y1V05-E024`). Later circumstances require her to give up that opportunity anyway.

This is not self-erasure.

She has finally made the desire public enough that other people can understand what is being sacrificed. A private fixation becomes a political fact rather than an invisible command.

## 8.5 First self-authored leadership turn, not completed leadership

Volume 5 should not be described as the moment Horikita simply becomes “the leader.”

She still:

- depends on Ayanokōji's diagnosis;
- remains strongly oriented toward Manabu;
- misreads Kushida;
- and has not yet acquired stable classwide legitimacy.

What the volume does establish is more precise:

> **Horikita begins to understand that leadership is not the right of the strongest person to set the pace. It is responsibility for making unequal people capable of moving together.**

This is her first credible self-authored leadership turn because the actual covenant with Sudō exceeds Ayanokōji's instrumental language. He imagines Sudō as a future `武器 / weapon`; Horikita creates a relation in which Sudō can later disagree with her, develop independent expertise, and receive credit for his own effort.

That excess is the beginning of generative leadership.

---

# 9. Paper Shuffle: integration becomes a political principle

Paper Shuffle is the point where Horikita's developmental insight becomes a governing policy rather than a series of local corrections.

The examination requires a class to coordinate:

- question-writing;
- teaching;
- pair assignments;
- academic specialization;
- secrecy;
- sabotage prevention;
- and the management of unequal risk.

The class cannot succeed through one exceptional solver. It needs a system.

Sudō's development becomes retrospective evidence against disposal. Horikita knows that he once appeared to be one of the clearest liabilities in the room. His later contribution means that an early judgment of low value would have destroyed capacity the class had not yet developed.

This does not prove that every dangerous person can or should be preserved indefinitely.

It creates an epistemic presumption:

> **current weakness is not enough to establish a developmental ceiling.**

That presumption brings Horikita into direct conflict with Ayanokōji over Kushida.

Ayanokōji's preferred response is contingency and removal. Kushida is not merely underperforming. She is a deliberate internal threat with access to genuine secrets, social networks, and reputational leverage. The case against preservation is therefore much stronger than it was with Sudō.

Horikita nevertheless resists treating removal as the first answer. Her reasoning is partly principled and partly personal.

She has learned that:

- social capacity can be real even when the person is morally compromised;
- the class cannot easily replace Kushida's accessibility and network knowledge;
- and expulsion destroys every unrealized future as well as every current threat.

The central question becomes:

> **Is Kushida a person to be defeated, contained, rehabilitated, integrated, or eventually removed?**

Horikita's strongest independent action in Volume 6 is operational, not sentimental. She obtains exclusive control over question submission and builds a countermeasure Ayanokōji did not know she possessed. His admission `見くびっていた` is important because it marks a genuine correction to his model of her (`Y1V06-E055–E060`).

Her wager with Kushida also reveals a more mature view of jurisdiction. She does not casually place Ayanokōji at risk without his choice. She recognizes that leadership over a class does not grant ownership of another person's stakes.

The concluding `互いに信じることから始めましょう` is frequently overstated. It does not create trust. Kushida accepts only behavioral noninterference and explicitly refuses cooperation.

The correct classification is:

> **covenant without reconciliation.**

Horikita creates a rule for coexistence where no shared feeling exists.

That is political progress. Institutions often begin where affection fails.

It is also fragile. The covenant rests on unresolved hostility, asymmetric knowledge, and risks borne by classmates who have not authorized Horikita's rehabilitation project.

Paper Shuffle therefore establishes both her strongest emerging principle and its most dangerous application:

> **integration can preserve capacities a static system would destroy, but a leader's belief in integration can itself become a form of unilateral authorship over everyone who must live with the risk.**

# 10. Volumes 7–9: continuity, autonomous judgment, and inter-polity boundaries

The next three stages do not provide one dramatic Horikita breakthrough. They test whether earlier development persists when she is not at the center of the visible crisis.

## 10.1 Sudō's restraint becomes durable

Volume 7 shows that Sudō's development survives beyond the scene that produced it. He can regulate anger under provocation and act with a stronger sense of class consequence. Horikita's influence matters, but the result belongs increasingly to him.

This is an essential distinction for developmental leadership.

A leader has not truly developed another person if the capacity disappears whenever the leader is absent.

Sudō's improved conduct therefore becomes evidence that Horikita's best interventions can produce agency rather than obedience.

## 10.2 The class begins to function beyond her direct control

The class's social and operational infrastructure becomes increasingly distributed.

Hirata, Kei, Kushida, Keisei, Sudō, and other members possess distinct forms of authority. Ayanokōji can imagine the class continuing without his direct public presence. Horikita begins to occupy one node in a broader system rather than the sole point from which all useful action must originate.

This changes the meaning of class formation.

A mature class is not a group in which the leader personally touches every causal chain.

It is a group whose members can:

- act on local knowledge;
- coordinate laterally;
- preserve norms;
- and improve without constant command.

## 10.3 Refusing the student council

Volume 7.5 provides one of Horikita's clearest acts of self-authorship.

Manabu approves her joining the student council. Ayanokōji argues that the position would be politically useful. She refuses.

The choice may be strategically wrong. That is not the point.

For much of her life, Manabu's approval functioned as a near-final score. Here it does not settle the question. Ayanokōji's superior strategic prediction also does not settle it.

Horikita compares the role to her own goals and says no.

This is autonomy in a stronger sense than mere stubbornness because the decision is made against the two figures with the greatest symbolic and strategic authority over her.

It also demonstrates why independence and correctness must remain separate. A self-authored mistake is still self-authored.

## 10.4 Kushida as a chosen risk

Volume 8 makes the Kushida project unmistakably Horikita's.

She accepts formal female-side leadership and openly compensates for her accessibility deficit by recruiting Kushida. This is distributed leadership in a politically dangerous form: she uses the capacity of someone who may want her destroyed.

Her request `あなたに信頼されたい` goes beyond tactical peace. She wants a relationship in which Kushida can stop treating knowledge of her hidden self as permanent threat (`Y1V08-E114–E119`).

Kushida's answer is one of the strongest counterarguments to Horikita's developmental optimism. Secret knowledge cannot be returned. The person who knows can promise restraint, but the vulnerability remains like a knife at the neck.

Horikita's desire for trust is sincere.

Sincerity does not remove the structural coercion created by knowledge asymmetry.

She continues anyway, without assurance that Ayanokōji will protect the experiment.

This is independent judgment. It is also a leader imposing risk.

## 10.5 Respecting another polity's private sphere

Volume 9 shows growth in a different register.

Horikita wants to help Ichinose, but accepts that an allied rival retains information she may legitimately withhold. Their alliance does not create ownership of the whole truth (`Y1V09-E033–E036`).

This is a meaningful contrast with her early relation to classmates.

She no longer assumes that concern or strategic partnership entitles her to complete access.

She receives Ayanokōji's argument for nonintervention as input rather than command. The decision about whether her class should act remains politically hers.

This is an early form of inter-polity respect:

> cooperation without annexation.

The lesson becomes more explicit in Volume 11.5 when she and Ichinose end the alliance without converting rivalry into personal hostility.

# 11. The Class Vote: accountable judgment under manufactured scarcity

Volume 10 is Horikita's most severe Year 1 leadership test because the institution removes the possibility of a clean outcome.

The class must identify someone for expulsion.

The exam does not ask:

> who deserves punishment for a specified wrongdoing?

It asks the class to convert peers into a rank order of continuation.

This is manufactured disposability.

Horikita's first mature act is not selecting Yamauchi.

It is naming the examination's political defect:

> `こんなもの、試験とは呼べないわ`

Anonymous bloc politics can protect popular people and destroy unpopular people without producing a reasoned judgment (`Y1V10-E050`). The examination invites coalition behavior while obscuring responsibility.

Horikita responds by making the decision public.

She names Yamauchi and explains the criteria:

- academics;
- physical contribution;
- discipline;
- evidence of growth;
- contribution to the class;
- and involvement in the betrayal coalition (`Y1V10-E051–E055`).

This is not neutral measurement. It is political judgment.

The importance lies in visible authorship.

She cannot hide behind:

- anonymous votes;
- a technical score;
- Ayanokōji's authority;
- or the claim that the school made the choice for her.

She creates a proposition others can contest.

Hirata contests the class's moral jurisdiction more radically. Sudō contests the particular sacrifice through friendship. Horikita forces Sudō to confront substitution: protecting Yamauchi means exposing someone else (`Y1V10-E058–E059`).

The confrontation demonstrates that leadership sometimes consists in refusing morally comforting vagueness.

But it does not settle whether the final judgment is just.

## 11.1 Why Yamauchi is not an objectively proven least valuable person

The Class Vote occurs under developmental uncertainty.

Chabashira acknowledges that student growth can overturn prior expectations. Sudō is already the year's central example (`Y1V10-E006–E007`).

Horikita's criteria therefore estimate future contribution; they do not discover it with certainty.

Yamauchi's betrayal materially worsens his case. But even then, the judgment does not become a metaphysical proof of low human worth.

The strongest defensible formulation is:

> Horikita selects the person she can most publicly justify sacrificing under the institution's forced conditions.

That is different from:

> Horikita proves who deserves to disappear.

## 11.2 Public liability as constitutional development

Horikita's leadership becomes more legitimate because she accepts reciprocal exposure.

Once she names Yamauchi, classmates can:

- oppose her;
- resent her;
- judge her motives;
- test her criteria;
- and remember the decision as hers.

Ayanokōji's hidden information remains important. He routes the conspiracy toward her and treats her action as part of a larger strategy (`Y1V10-E049`, `Y1V10-E089`).

Yet the text does not show him writing her speech or supplying her governing principle.

Her public political self therefore cannot be reduced to his ventriloquism.

## 11.3 Hirata as constitutional opposite

Hirata's breakdown clarifies the moral stakes.

He rejects the legitimacy of selecting a classmate and offers himself. His ethics begin from anti-sacrifice and care.

Horikita accepts that the institution has forced a choice and insists that refusing to name the trade does not eliminate it.

Neither position is simply correct.

Hirata's refusal protects equal membership but becomes psychologically and operationally unsustainable under the exam.

Horikita's judgment preserves class function but accepts a sovereignty over another person's future that the class may not be morally entitled to hold.

Their conflict is therefore constitutional:

- **Hirata:** a community should not rank members for sacrifice.
- **Horikita:** a leader must own the choice when the institution makes nonchoice impossible.

Year 1 does not resolve the dispute. It makes it visible.

## 11.4 Asking Manabu for courage

Before the speech, Horikita asks Manabu:

> `私に───勇気をください`

She does not ask for the answer (`Y1V10-E045`).

The request is one of her most important acts of vulnerability because it distinguishes counsel from authorship.

She wants emotional support sufficient to make her own decision.

Manabu's answer—`おまえはおまえらしく成長すればいい`—redirects her away from becoming either him or Ayanokōji (`Y1V10-E047–E048`).

The moment converts his authority.

He no longer specifies the action.

He helps create the conditions under which she can act.

This is a healthier form of developmental influence than imitation.

---

# 12. Volume 11: distributed and generative leadership becomes explicit

The Event Selection Exam is the strongest evidence that Horikita's leadership has changed in kind rather than merely degree.

The examination requires the class to select events, train participants, allocate specialists, manage uncertainty, and construct a portfolio broad enough to survive an opponent's choices.

No individual can embody the whole solution.

Horikita states the governing principle:

> `全員が協力し合う必要`

Everyone needs to cooperate (`Y1V11-E050–E053`).

The line is not new as a generic teamwork slogan. Its significance comes from who says it and how she operationalizes it.

## 12.1 The notebook as political technology

Horikita's planning notebook represents a changed relation to knowledge.

Early Horikita treated the leader's own high ability as the primary source of authority.

The notebook instead maps:

- who knows what;
- who can train whom;
- which capacities are visible;
- which require testing;
- and where the class lacks coverage.

Leadership becomes information architecture.

But unlike Ayanokōji's private architecture, Horikita's is oriented toward roles that other people can occupy visibly.

She does not need to be the best basketball judge, archer, typist, or chess player.

She needs to create the conditions under which those abilities become class capacity.

## 12.2 Sudō becomes collaborator and domain authority

Horikita turns to Sudō for athletic judgment.

This is a major reversal of Volume 1.

She once researched basketball so she could translate her academic teaching into his language. By Volume 11, she recognizes that his expertise is not merely a pedagogical aid for reaching him. It is knowledge she herself lacks.

Sudō is no longer only a developmental project.

He is a collaborator whose judgment improves hers.

This is generative leadership in reciprocal form:

- she helped create conditions for his growth;
- his growth now expands the quality of her leadership;
- and the class gains from the feedback loop.

## 12.3 Kōenji establishes the autonomy boundary

Kōenji tells her:

> `その才能を君のために使うかどうかを決めるのは私自身だ`

Whether he uses his talent for her is his decision (`Y1V11-E062–E066`).

The line is arrogant and constitutionally exact.

Recognizing ability does not create a property right over it.

A class may suffer when a member refuses contribution. The school may create duties and incentives. The leader may persuade, bargain, criticize, or design around refusal.

But the mere fact that a capacity would benefit the group does not prove that the leader owns its use.

This is one of the most important limits on Horikita's collective logic.

Her mature task is not simply to aggregate all available ability.

It is to build collective action among people whose capacities remain partly voluntary and whose reasons may not align.

## 12.4 She reads the person reading her

After Ayanokōji's hidden coordination, Horikita tells him that he had read how she would act (`Y1V11-E085–E087`).

The moment matters because detection is a form of political defense.

Ayanokōji's greatest advantage is not only superior calculation. It is the ability to design another person's information environment without appearing as the designer.

Horikita increasingly notices the seams.

She cannot yet prevent or fully reconstruct every intervention. But she can:

- identify patterned influence;
- confront him;
- demand fuller disclosure;
- and create tests that move hidden ability into an auditable form.

This is the beginning of reciprocal authorship.

The developer is becoming visible to the developed person.

## 12.5 Chess and healthy pedagogy

The chess preparation offers a cleaner teaching model than the island crisis.

Ayanokōji teaches explicitly.

Horikita knows she is being taught.

The skill can be practiced and tested.

He delays intervention because she creates a position worth seeing through (`Y1V11-E137–E139`).

Her value is not that she becomes Ayanokōji's equal overnight.

It is that she generates a new option from instruction.

This is a more reciprocal developmental structure:

1. knowledge is disclosed;
2. purpose is shared;
3. the learner acts;
4. the teacher can be surprised by the result.

The contrast with hidden environmental authorship is crucial. Development does not require concealing the curriculum.

## 12.6 Procedural capture limits leadership

Tsukishiro's intervention prevents the volume from ending in a simple celebration of distributed ability.

Horikita can build the class position.

Ayanokōji can produce the stronger move.

Sakayanagi can recognize the abnormality.

The official result can still be corrupted if an administrator controls the communication channel.

This adds an institutional limit to leadership:

> a class can develop real capacity and still lose political reality when the record is captured.

Horikita's leadership therefore depends not only on classmates. It depends on institutional integrity she cannot personally guarantee.

# 13. Volume 11.5: integrated self-authorship and a corrigible class

Volume 11.5 completes Horikita's Year 1 movement by separating self-authorship from the fantasy of an uninfluenced true self.

## 13.1 `偽者` and `紛れもない私`

Before the mirror, Horikita names the self organized around Manabu a `偽者` (`Y1V11.5-E003`).

The label includes more than hair.

It reaches into:

- personality;
- grades;
- appearance;
- discipline;
- and self-conception.

A simplistic liberation narrative would say:

> the false self is discarded; the authentic self appears.

The narration immediately refuses that answer.

The years lived in that form are also `紛れもない私` (`Y1V11.5-E004`).

They produced:

- real knowledge;
- real habits;
- real damage;
- real competence;
- real relationships;
- and real responsibility.

Horikita can reject the **purpose** that governed those years without declaring the years unreal.

This is integrated self-authorship.

The self is not discovered intact beneath social causation.

It is reauthored from materials already lived.

## 13.2 The haircut as reorientation, not essence

The haircut is therefore neither a cosmetic “development marker” nor the recovery of a naturally short-haired true identity.

It is a chosen reorientation.

Manabu reveals that the long-hair preference tested whether she would erase her own color for him (`Y1V11.5-E093`). Horikita later treats both long- and short-haired forms as parts of her history and leaves open growing it again (`Y1V11.5-E095`).

The visual change matters because it is not made into a permanent ontological answer.

She can choose again.

That reversibility is part of authorship.

## 13.3 Succession without imitation

Horikita tells Manabu she will stop chasing him and find her own path while learning with and leading classmates (`Y1V11.5-E090`).

Manabu responds that her potential may exceed his and that making him the endpoint restricted her horizon (`Y1V11.5-E091`).

His final injunction is:

> `他者に強くあれ。そして優しくあれ`

Be strong toward or for others. And be kind (`Y1V11.5-E092`).

He transfers a moral vocabulary, not a personality template.

This is succession without reproduction.

She can inherit:

- seriousness;
- public responsibility;
- the importance of strength;
- and the obligation to orient strength toward others

without inheriting his exact social form.

## 13.4 Corrigibility becomes constitutional

Keisei promises to support Horikita when she is right and challenge her when she is wrong. Ayanokōji identifies the arrangement as proper for the class (`Y1V11.5-E011–E012`).

This is a major endpoint because it supplies a criterion more important than admiration.

A healthy polity does not merely trust the leader.

It creates relations through which:

- errors can be named;
- dissent does not equal betrayal;
- expertise can flow upward;
- and support remains conditional on judgment.

Horikita's leadership is becoming corrigible.

## 13.5 Rivalry without hostility

Horikita ends the alliance with Ichinose because continued partnership would constrain the pursuit of rank. Ichinose accepts the change, and the two become explicit rivals without personal enmity (`Y1V11.5-E059–E063`).

The scene is political maturity.

It distinguishes:

- respect from permanent alignment;
- concern from ownership;
- and rivalry from moral hatred.

This is especially important in a series where class competition repeatedly pressures relationships into instrumental categories.

## 13.6 Hidden ability becomes an auditable wager

Horikita confronts Ayanokōji about hidden matchmaking and manipulation, asks him to stop concealing his contribution, and creates a single-subject academic wager (`Y1V11.5-E064–E068`).

She cannot force immediate full disclosure. He argues that abrupt exposure would destabilize the information environment.

Her response is politically sophisticated: make one bounded claim measurable.

The wager converts:

- suspicion;
- asymmetrical knowledge;
- and private inference

into a contest with a defined object.

It is not complete transparency.

It is an accountability mechanism.

## 13.7 Kushida jurisdiction is transferred, not resolved

Ayanokōji reveals the half-private-point contract and earlier expulsion plan. Horikita rejects both automatic preservation and automatic removal. Future contribution and necessity remain relevant (`Y1V11.5-E101–E102`).

He then yields Kushida's future to her judgment even though he still sees expulsion as efficient (`Y1V11.5-E103`).

The significance is not that Horikita has proven rehabilitation correct.

It is that she receives genuine jurisdiction to produce an answer Ayanokōji did not specify.

The transfer is also ethically ambiguous. He wants to see what she will do; her independence remains inside his developing interest in cultivating people.

Even his surrender can become part of a curriculum.

That is why the final Ayanokōji–Horikita relation remains both more reciprocal and still asymmetric.

# 14. Horikita and Manabu: from specification to inheritance

The sibling relation is the emotional and philosophical backbone of Horikita's Year 1 arc.

## 14.1 Early relation: recognition as external verdict

At the beginning, Manabu's recognition functions like institutional promotion.

If he acknowledges her, then her self becomes validated.

This makes his distance devastating because it does not register merely as familial rejection. It appears as evidence that she has failed the standard she built herself around.

The relation therefore encourages:

- imitation;
- hyperdiscipline;
- emotional suppression;
- and an inability to distinguish what she wants from what would produce his approval.

## 14.2 Manabu's failure

Manabu is not only a wise corrective figure.

His violence and emotional distance materially shape the wound. His use of rejection as pedagogy creates an environment in which Suzune must infer the goal without secure relational support.

The series later dignifies much of his intention, but intention should not erase method.

He wants her to develop independently. His conduct helps produce dependency on his imagined judgment.

That paradox is part of the relationship's seriousness.

## 14.3 Volume 10: counsel without prescription

When Horikita asks for courage, Manabu does not give her the Yamauchi answer. He tells her to grow as herself.

This is the first fully mature form of his guidance:

- he recognizes the difficulty;
- offers emotional authorization;
- and refuses to replace her judgment.

## 14.4 Volume 11.5: recognition through release

The farewell works because Manabu stops being the endpoint.

He can embrace her after she declares separation.

Recognition is no longer:

> you have become what I wanted.

It is:

> you have become capable of pursuing a path I do not prescribe.

The relation therefore reaches maturity through loss of control.

Manabu's greatest positive inheritance is not excellence alone.

It is the idea that strength should be directed toward others and joined to kindness.

Horikita's future leadership must interpret that principle rather than merely obey it.

## 14.5 Why grief matters

After Manabu departs, Horikita asks to be alone. Ayanokōji follows and tells her it is acceptable to cry, overriding the request (`Y1V11.5-E097`). She later interprets the episode positively because the person present was specifically Ayanokōji (`Y1V11.5-E099`).

The scene should not be generalized into blanket permission to violate boundaries.

Its importance for Horikita lies elsewhere.

The leader who once equated control with strength can allow grief to become bodily visible. Crying does not undo succession. It confirms that separation matters.

Her self-authorship is not emotional detachment from Manabu.

It is the capacity to remain changed by him without remaining governed by him.

# 15. Horikita and Ayanokōji: developmental reciprocity under hidden sovereignty

The Ayanokōji–Horikita relation is the most difficult structure in her Year 1 development because it contains real reciprocity and enduring hidden sovereignty.

## 15.1 Why they become usable to one another

Horikita supplies Ayanokōji with:

- a visible strategic actor;
- public legitimacy;
- analytical competence;
- access to class leadership;
- and a person capable of converting some of his hidden interventions into public policy.

Ayanokōji supplies Horikita with:

- information;
- protection;
- tactical insight;
- pressure;
- an alternative model of ability;
- and environments that accelerate her development.

Their early relation is asymmetric, but not empty. Volume 2 identifies a low-pressure silence in which Ayanokōji feels near an unperformed self (`Y1V02-E005`). Horikita can also question him more directly than most classmates.

## 15.2 She is not merely his puppet

The puppet reading fails for several reasons.

Horikita:

- redesigns teaching after failure;
- accepts personal score risk;
- operationalizes the Volume 2 solution;
- produces an independent Paper Shuffle counter;
- refuses the student council despite his recommendation;
- chooses the Kushida project against his efficient preference;
- creates the public Class Vote argument;
- delegates and coordinates the Event Selection Exam;
- detects his hidden influence;
- and creates the academic wager.

These are not trivial variations inside a fully specified plan.

They show a person generating policy.

## 15.3 Ayanokōji's influence remains deeper than she knows

The opposite reduction also fails.

Ayanokōji:

- controls what she discovers;
- protects the class through hidden contingencies;
- shapes the island environment;
- assigns public credit;
- routes politically decisive information;
- preserves expulsion plans she does not share;
- and privately decides that changing her is something he wants to do.

By Volume 11, Horikita's independence becomes one of his self-chosen projects.

That creates the central ethical tension:

> **Can her self-authorship remain hers when another person treats the emergence of that self as an object of deliberate cultivation?**

## 15.4 Detection changes the relation

Horikita's increasing ability to identify his influence matters because hidden authorship is strongest when it is invisible.

When she can say:

> you knew how I would act;

she converts an unexplained causal force into a relationship that can be contested.

The Volume 11.5 wager develops this further. She cannot make him disclose everything, but she can define a test.

The relation therefore moves from:

> hidden developer / visible instrument

through:

> hidden developer / reasoning learner

toward:

> **developmental partners with severe information asymmetry and growing capacity for challenge.**

## 15.5 The best evidence of reciprocity is divergence

A relationship becomes more reciprocal when the developed person can produce an answer the developer did not specify.

Horikita's Year 1 divergences include:

- student-council refusal;
- Kushida preservation as an open project;
- public accountability under the Class Vote;
- and the demand that Ayanokōji become more auditable.

The final test remains open:

> can she create a class order that survives without his hidden correction—and can he accept an outcome her leadership authors that he would not choose?

# 16. Horikita and Sudō: the relationship that changes her theory of ability

Sudō is not merely Horikita's successful rehabilitation case.

He is the person through whom her theory of leadership changes most concretely.

## 16.1 He begins as the strongest case for exclusion

Sudō is:

- academically endangered;
- angry;
- difficult to teach;
- socially abrasive;
- and capable of imposing classwide consequences.

From Horikita's initial perspective, he is a burden whose physical talent does not compensate for immediate institutional risk.

## 16.2 Teaching requires her to enter his world

The first study-group correction forces Horikita to research basketball, use his analogies, and apologize. This is the first time she learns that a leader's superior knowledge may be unusable until translated.

## 16.3 The sports festival creates mutuality

The three-legged race and later conversation reveal that both possess strengths the other lacks.

Sudō needs:

- discipline;
- academic structure;
- and a broader sense of responsibility.

Horikita needs:

- embodied adaptation;
- domain humility;
- relational responsiveness;
- and the recognition that following another person's rhythm can be strength.

Their developmental relation becomes bilateral.

## 16.4 His later resistance validates the development

During the Class Vote, Sudō opposes Horikita's Yamauchi judgment.

This is not proof that he is right.

It is evidence that he has not become a compliant product.

The person she helped develop can now:

- invoke friendship;
- demand reasons;
- and resist her leadership.

That is a healthier outcome than obedience.

Horikita also explicitly returns authorship of Sudō's improvement to Sudō: `彼自身の努力の結果` (`Y1V10-E006–E007`).

She does not claim him as proof of her own greatness.

## 16.5 By Volume 11 he becomes domain authority

When she relies on his athletic judgment, his capacity changes the quality of her planning.

The relation now has a complete feedback structure:

> judgment → pedagogy → effort → development → dissent → expertise → shared leadership.

Sudō is therefore the clearest Year 1 proof that Horikita can become a **generative** leader: someone whose leadership increases the future agency and usefulness of another person rather than merely extracting current performance.

# 17. Horikita and Kushida: anti-disposability, structural coercion, and the limits of rehabilitation

Kushida is the hardest test of Horikita's Year 1 philosophy because she exposes the point at which developmental generosity may become political irresponsibility.

Sudō was dangerous through volatility, poor judgment, and weak academics. Kushida is dangerous through deliberate agency.

She possesses:

- intimate information gathered under trust;
- broad social access;
- high public credibility;
- a need for recognition organized around superiority;
- willingness to sabotage collective outcomes;
- and a personal objective of removing Horikita.

The analogy between Sudō and Kushida is therefore morally suggestive but structurally incomplete.

## 17.1 Why Horikita refuses immediate removal

Horikita's position grows from several Year 1 experiences.

First, present liability can conceal future value. Sudō proves that an early judgment of uselessness may destroy a capacity the class could have developed.

Second, social competence is real ability even when motive is compromised. Kushida's public warmth is performed, but the coordination, memory, trust access, and emotional labor are not imaginary.

Third, leadership that treats expulsion as the default answer risks becoming a pruning regime rather than an educational one. Horikita explicitly rejects `切り捨てる` as the first response and says she cannot simply abandon Kushida (`Y1V06-E020`, `Y1V06-E061`).

Fourth, the conflict implicates Horikita personally. Kushida's hostility is directed at her because she knows the hidden self. Horikita is unwilling to solve a conflict partly caused by her presence solely by removing the other person.

These are serious reasons.

They do not settle the question.

## 17.2 Kushida's knife objection

Volume 8 gives Kushida the strongest answer to Horikita's trust project.

Knowledge cannot be returned.

Horikita may promise never to expose Kushida. Kushida still lives in a world where Horikita *could*. The relationship therefore remains structurally unequal, like a knife held at the neck even if the holder promises restraint (`Y1V08-E115–E117`).

This objection matters beyond Kushida.

It establishes that benevolent intention cannot erase asymmetric power. Horikita's sincerity does not make Kushida irrational for experiencing the knowledge as coercive.

It also complicates the language of rehabilitation. Horikita cannot offer a neutral environment in which Kushida chooses trust from a position of security. The proposed relationship begins inside an irreversible vulnerability.

## 17.3 Covenant without reconciliation

The Paper Shuffle wager is nevertheless political progress.

Horikita limits what she is entitled to stake. She refuses to include Ayanokōji without his consent; he must join voluntarily (`Y1V06-E039–E040`). Manabu's witness role creates an enforcement structure. Kushida's defeat produces a noninterference commitment rather than affection.

Horikita offers a handshake.

Kushida refuses.

The result is not friendship. It is **covenant without reconciliation**.

That distinction should be preserved because mature politics often begins before mutual feeling. People who do not trust one another can still establish behavioral limits.

## 17.4 The strongest objection: unconsented collective risk

Horikita's project is not merely private.

Kushida's continued presence exposes classmates to:

- secret disclosure;
- exam sabotage;
- reputational harm;
- political fracture;
- and potential expulsion.

Those classmates do not receive a complete account of the risk or a vote on whether Horikita may pursue rehabilitation.

The strongest criticism is therefore:

> **Horikita may be universalizing a personal anti-disposability commitment while making the class bear costs it did not authorize.**

That is not a minor caution. It is the central ethical limit on her Year 1 leadership.

## 17.5 Jurisdiction, not resolution

Volume 11.5 does not vindicate Horikita's policy.

Ayanokōji still regards expulsion as efficient. He transfers future jurisdiction over Kushida to Horikita because he wants to see what she will do with the obstacle (`Y1V11.5-E101–E105`).

What becomes canonical at the Year 1 boundary is not:

> Horikita will save Kushida.

It is:

> **Horikita is now recognized as the political author of the answer.**

That is a major gain in independence and no guarantee of wisdom.

Her future legitimacy will depend upon whether she can make the decision accountable to the people whose security is implicated, revise the project when evidence changes, and accept that anti-disposability cannot mean infinite tolerance of deliberate harm.

# 18. Horikita and Hirata: complementary legitimacy and constitutional opposition

Horikita and Hirata represent two different answers to the class's original fragmentation.

Horikita begins from standards, performance, diagnosis, and strategic correction.

Hirata begins from accessibility, voluntary trust, mediation, and preservation of belonging.

Neither possesses a complete leadership model.

## 18.1 What Hirata has that Horikita lacks

Hirata can:

- make participation feel voluntary;
- translate technical burdens into emotionally inhabitable goals;
- notice social discomfort;
- distribute attention without humiliation;
- and accumulate legitimacy through repeated care.

Early Horikita can identify what the class should do but cannot make classmates want to do it with her.

This is why `POLITY-HORIKITA` is never built by Horikita alone. Hirata's social authority is constitutive rather than decorative.

## 18.2 What Horikita has that Hirata lacks

Horikita can eventually do what Hirata cannot during the Class Vote:

- accept that incompatibility may not have a consensus solution;
- formulate criteria;
- make a public judgment;
- and remain answerable for naming a target.

Hirata's refusal to rank classmates has moral force. It also leaves him operationally paralyzed inside a rule that requires loss.

Horikita's action resolves the immediate political problem. It does not solve Hirata's moral objection.

## 18.3 The Class Vote as constitutional collision

Hirata's `堀北……ちょっと黙れよ` is not merely a personal outburst. It marks collision between two theories of legitimate membership.

Hirata argues that classmates lack the moral right to decide another person's worth.

Horikita argues that refusing to decide does not remove the institutional demand; it merely leaves the decision to hidden blocs and manipulation.

The strongest synthesis preserves both:

> **Hirata is morally right that forced choice does not automatically create legitimate jurisdiction. Horikita is politically right that a class cannot escape responsibility by allowing anonymous power to decide instead.**

## 18.4 A mature polity needs both capacities

Horikita's Year 1 development should not culminate in replacing Hirata.

The class needs:

- her willingness to judge and disclose reasons;
- his capacity to preserve human connection after judgment;
- her strategic organization;
- his social information;
- her public accountability;
- and his reminder that membership is not reducible to utility.

Their tension is therefore constitutional rather than redundant.

A healthy `POLITY-HORIKITA` would institutionalize the disagreement rather than resolve it by making one person sovereign.

# 19. Other relational tests of Horikita's leadership

Horikita's growth is distributed across several relationships. Each tests a different aspect of the leadership model.

## 19.1 Ichinose — alliance without ownership

Ichinose offers an early countermodel of socially accepted leadership. Her authority rests on trust, accessibility, distributed participation, and broad legitimacy.

Horikita initially sees the effectiveness before fully understanding the mechanism.

By Volume 9, she can accept that friendship and temporary alliance do not entitle her to Ichinose's private information (`Y1V09-E033–E036`). By Volume 11.5, she can end the alliance because its strategic rationale has expired while preserving respect (`Y1V11.5-E059–E063`).

This is political separation without moral annihilation.

The relation teaches that boundaries are compatible with care.

## 19.2 Kōenji — ability does not create ownership

Kōenji is the hardest case for collective obligation.

His capacity is extraordinary. His willingness to make it available is low.

When he tells Horikita that he alone decides whether his talent will be used for her, he states a principle her leadership must absorb (`Y1V11-E060`).

A class may legitimately impose some shared rules. It cannot treat possession of talent as automatic transfer of jurisdiction.

Kōenji therefore forces her to distinguish:

- knowing what someone can do;
- valuing what someone can do;
- persuading someone to contribute;
- and owning the use of that capacity.

## 19.3 Keisei — dissent as participation

Keisei's Year 1 development provides one of the clearest constitutional correctives.

At graduation he promises to support Horikita when he believes she is right and challenge her when he believes she is wrong. Ayanokōji recognizes this as proper class structure (`Y1V11.5-E011`).

The promise matters because loyalty is not defined as obedience.

A leader becomes more legitimate when disagreement can remain a form of membership.

## 19.4 Kei and Kushida — social intelligence outside Horikita's style

Kei and Kushida each possess forms of social access Horikita lacks.

- Kushida can make people speak, remember networks, and convert warmth into participation.
- Kei can read atmosphere, status, resentment, and female-group response from inside the hierarchy.

Horikita's development requires more than learning to imitate their behavior. She must create a class structure in which unlike social capacities can function without being reduced to her register or privately monopolized by Ayanokōji.

## 19.5 Ayanokōji Group and lateral institutions

Friendship groups, study networks, sports teams, and informal alliances become class infrastructure without being designed by Horikita.

This is a sign of successful class formation.

A polity is stronger when every useful relation does not pass through the visible leader.

It is also harder to govern, because lateral networks can conceal blocs, loyalties, and information the leader does not possess. Horikita's mature task is therefore not to centralize every relation. It is to build procedures by which decentralized relations can remain compatible with collective responsibility.

# 20. `POLITY-HORIKITA`: class formation as dynamic developmental pluralism

The stable polity identifier is necessary because the class letter changes while the student community persists.

At the Year 1 boundary, the class finishes with **347 class points**. This leaves it last in the cohort after the April reassignment, but it also represents the largest increase from a zero-point starting condition.

Neither fact should be ignored.

The polity remains institutionally behind.

It has also changed dramatically.

## 20.1 The opening class is not a political community

Volume 1 Class D is a co-location of incompatible projects:

- Horikita wants Class A and sees weaker peers as burdens.
- Hirata wants cohesion.
- Kushida wants universal recognition.
- Sudō wants basketball and dignity.
- Kōenji recognizes almost no collective claim.
- Ayanokōji wants ordinary peace and concealment.
- many classmates principally want comfort, points, or social life.

Shared punishment creates common pressure before shared purpose exists.

The class begins as an institutional category, not a self-governing polity.

## 20.2 Coalition precedes constitution

The early study group, Sudō rescue, island camp, and Paper Shuffle create temporary coalitions.

Different capacities begin connecting:

- Horikita translates and plans;
- Hirata legitimates and mediates;
- Kushida recruits and communicates;
- Sudō supplies embodied capacity;
- Ike supplies contextual knowledge;
- Keisei supplies academic specialization;
- Kei supplies social information;
- Ayanokōji supplies concealed synthesis.

The coalition becomes constitutional only when recurrent expectations develop:

- who may decide;
- who may dissent;
- who bears risk;
- how members are protected;
- and whether failure leads to development or disposal.

## 20.3 Horikita's visible constitution

By the Year 1 endpoint, Horikita increasingly supplies:

- explicit criteria;
- visible responsibility;
- developmental presumption;
- delegation to domain experts;
- willingness to integrate heterogeneous members;
- and a route for dissent to become information.

This is **dynamic developmental pluralism**.

“Dynamic” means the portfolio changes.

“Developmental” means present weakness does not fix future value.

“Pluralism” means capacities remain different rather than converging on a single model of excellence.

## 20.4 Ayanokōji's shadow constitution

The visible constitution is not the whole polity.

Ayanokōji controls or heavily influences:

- hidden information;
- covert social networks;
- emergency protection;
- counterintelligence;
- expulsion contingencies;
- cross-class transactions;
- and the causal conditions behind several visible decisions.

This shadow constitution can save the class from threats Horikita does not see.

It also prevents full self-government.

The class cannot consent to, contest, or replace a power it cannot identify.

## 20.5 The aggregation problem

*First File* describes the class as containing distinctive students with explosive potential but insufficient cohesion; leadership must aggregate individualists (`Y1FF-E025`).

That is the compact Year 1 diagnosis.

The class's problem is not absence of ability.

It is conversion:

- possessed ability into expressed ability;
- expressed ability into recognized ability;
- recognized ability into voluntary participation;
- and participation into coordinated institutional result.

Horikita becomes valuable because she increasingly operates at that conversion layer.

## 20.6 The succession problem

A polity becomes mature when it can survive the absence of its most powerful hidden or visible actor.

Year 1 does not establish that `POLITY-HORIKITA` can survive Ayanokōji's withdrawal.

It does establish that the class possesses more independent structure than before:

- Sudō's restraint persists;
- Keisei can teach and dissent;
- Hirata can return to social function;
- Kei and Kushida possess independent networks;
- Horikita can generate strategies he did not predict;
- and classmates can act laterally.

The forward-facing constitutional question is therefore exact:

> **Can Horikita convert a class partly built through hidden sovereignty into a polity whose causal structure is visible enough to govern itself?**

# 21. *First File*: the rating and the person

*First File* is useful for Horikita precisely because it places a standardized early profile beside a year whose narrative demonstrates the limits of static profiles.

## 21.1 July visibility state

The guidebook records:

- academics A;
- judgment B-;
- cooperation E;
- physical B+;
- intelligence A- (`Y1FF-E012`).

The editorial prose treats her academic and athletic capacity as compatible with A-class quality while locating her D-class placement in inability to consider others (`Y1FF-E013`). It later notes her growing understanding of the difference between solitude and true isolation (`Y1FF-E014`).

This is not a contradiction.

It is an archive of development.

## 21.2 Cooperation E is historically meaningful

The E rating should not be dismissed as institutional stupidity.

Early Horikita really is poor at:

- translating knowledge;
- generating voluntary participation;
- reading emotional cost;
- asking for help;
- and making heterogeneous capacities work together.

The rating captures an important state.

It becomes misleading only when treated as essence.

## 21.3 Hypothetical OAA and bounded legibility

The retrospective OAA reconstruction gives Horikita 89 academics, 79 physical ability, 57 adaptability, 34 social contribution, and 69 overall (`Y1FF-E043`).

These figures are useful as a model of what a later institutional framework might have seen.

They do not answer:

- how she changes another person's trajectory;
- whether she owns a public decision;
- whether dissent can correct her;
- or how much of class success belongs to hidden intervention.

The rating is a model of performance under selected axes, not a complete theory of leadership.

## 21.4 Official foregrounding and interpretive limit

The guidebook's V11.5 summary foregrounds her pursuit of growth, desire to verify Ayanokōji, and movement toward an independent path (`Y1FF-E052`).

That official emphasis strengthens the Year 1 reading.

It does not license the simplified claim that she has escaped all influence.

The novels give the more precise formulation: independence through integration, public responsibility, and increasing corrigibility.

# 22. Horikita's Year 1 leadership model

Her development can be summarized as movement through five leadership forms.

## 22.1 Scalar leadership

The leader is the person with the highest visible competence.

Others are measured against the leader's standard.

Weakness implies burden.

This is the Volume 1 starting point.

## 22.2 Translational leadership

The leader learns that knowledge must be converted into a form another person can use.

Teaching Sudō through basketball analogy is the prototype.

## 22.3 Developmental leadership

The leader treats current weakness as a state that may change rather than a final identity.

Sudō's trajectory and the anti-discard principle emerge here.

## 22.4 Generative leadership

The leader increases the usability and agency of capacities she does not personally possess.

By Volume 11, Horikita relies on Sudō's domain judgment, maps the whole class, trains new competence, and coordinates unlike strengths.

## 22.5 Corrigible leadership

The leader creates conditions under which her own judgment can be challenged.

This appears through:

- Keisei's promised dissent;
- the Ayanokōji wager;
- public criteria in the Class Vote;
- respect for Ichinose's boundaries;
- and recognition that Kōenji owns the use of his talent.

Corrigibility is the mature Year 1 addition because generative leadership without correction can become paternalism.

The final model is therefore:

> **A leader does not merely possess the best answer. A leader builds a structure in which incomplete knowledge can be corrected, heterogeneous abilities can become voluntarily usable, and people retain enough authorship to exceed the role initially assigned to them.**

# 23. Self-authorship: imitation, influence, integration, and divergence

Horikita's self-authorship cannot be measured by the absence of external cause.

Such absence does not exist.

A more useful test contains four stages.

## 23.1 Borrowed purpose

Early excellence is organized around Manabu's imagined standard.

The self acts, but the endpoint is externally specified.

## 23.2 Destabilization

Institutional failure, social isolation, Ayanokōji's challenges, bodily vulnerability, and classmates' capacities make the borrowed model insufficient.

## 23.3 Integration

Horikita accepts that the imitative years are `紛れもない私`. She does not need to amputate history to claim the future (`Y1V11.5-E003–E005`).

## 23.4 Divergence

She makes decisions that Manabu or Ayanokōji would not automatically choose:

- refusing the student council;
- pursuing Kushida coexistence;
- ending the Ichinose alliance;
- choosing public criteria during the Class Vote;
- and demanding an auditable test of Ayanokōji.

Divergence is not proof of correctness.

It is evidence that influence no longer functions as command.

The endpoint can therefore be stated precisely:

> **Horikita's self-authorship is integrated rather than original, relational rather than isolated, and political rather than merely psychological. She becomes able to convert influence into judgments for which she accepts ownership.**

# 24. Japanese voice, bodily presentation, and the hair motif

Horikita's development is carried not only by plot decisions but by register, bodily vulnerability, and visual continuity.

## 24.1 Polished judgment

Her speech is usually complete, controlled, and sharply evaluative. She often uses polished feminine forms rather than rough aggression.

This creates an illusion of total certainty.

Volume 2's private uncertainty and Volume 3's illness demonstrate why register cannot be treated as transparent access to emotional state. A composed sentence may coexist with fear, loneliness, or physical collapse.

## 24.2 Small linguistic breaches

Several ordinary expressions matter because they are difficult for her established self.

- `仲間` admits another person into a support category.
- `……ありがとう` accepts dependence without converting it into humiliation.
- `今日は意外と楽しかったわ` records experience defeating expectation.
- `私に───勇気をください` asks for support rather than specification.

The arc does not replace her register with generic warmth.

Development occurs inside the same voice through changes in what the voice can admit.

## 24.3 The body as political evidence

The island illness, bottle incident, sports coordination, injury, exhaustion, and final grief all challenge the fantasy of self-sufficient intellect.

Her body:

- requires assistance;
- creates class risk when concealed;
- limits performance;
- and eventually makes grief visible.

The body therefore functions as a counterargument to leadership imagined as pure will.

## 24.4 Hair as history, not essence

Long hair carries the history of Manabu-directed imitation.

Short hair marks a chosen reorientation.

But Volume 11.5 prevents either style from becoming a metaphysical true self. Manabu's original preference was itself part of a test, and Horikita retains attachment to the long-haired self and may grow it again (`Y1V11.5-E090–E095`).

The visual meaning is therefore:

> **she owns the right to decide what the inherited symbol means next.**

That is more durable than treating one hairstyle as authenticity and the other as falsity.

# 25. Ethics of Horikita's leadership

Horikita's Year 1 virtues and failures arise from the same seriousness about development.

## 25.1 Principal virtue — she resists static disposability

She learns to see:

- trajectory;
- context;
- domain-specific strength;
- and unrealized capacity.

This lets her preserve and develop people a purely static system would discard.

Sudō is the strongest successful case.

## 25.2 Principal political strength — she accepts visible liability

Unlike a hidden sovereign, Horikita increasingly states reasons in public and allows the result to affect her legitimacy.

This does not make her right.

It makes the exercise of power more contestable.

## 25.3 Principal ethical danger — rehabilitation can become jurisdiction

Her belief in development may tempt her to decide that other people must live inside the experiment she finds morally meaningful.

The Kushida project is the clearest case.

A leader cannot infer from “this person may change” that everyone else must accept unlimited risk while waiting.

## 25.4 Principal epistemic danger — visible office can conceal hidden cause

Horikita's public authority is partly founded on outcomes whose full causal architecture she does not know.

A leader can sincerely own a judgment while remaining dependent on unseen information selection.

This is why the Ayanokōji problem is constitutional, not merely relational.

## 25.5 Principal corrective — public reasons plus revisability

The strongest Year 1 standard for her leadership is not benevolence or success alone.

It is:

1. gather relevant information;
2. disclose the governing criteria where possible;
3. identify who bears the cost;
4. permit affected people and knowledgeable peers to answer;
5. distinguish present danger from permanent identity;
6. remain willing to revise;
7. and accept public responsibility for the decision.

Horikita reaches this standard inconsistently.

She moves decisively toward it.

# 26. Strong counterarguments and their limits

## 26.1 Counterargument: Horikita remains Ayanokōji's puppet

The argument has substantial evidence.

Ayanokōji authors discovery paths, controls information, assigns credit, routes decisive intelligence, preserves hidden contingencies, and privately makes her development his project.

### Response

“Puppet” becomes inadequate when the supposed instrument:

- generates strategies the controller did not know;
- refuses recommended office;
- pursues a policy the controller opposes;
- detects manipulation;
- creates measurable obligations;
- and supplies classwide coordination the controller admits he lacks.

The more accurate endpoint is **independent agency inside enduring hidden sovereignty**.

## 26.2 Counterargument: the Kushida project is reckless moral vanity

This is the strongest criticism of Horikita.

Kushida is a deliberate security threat, and classmates bear the risk without informed consent.

### Response

Year 1 does not refute the objection.

It explains why Horikita resists removal and establishes her jurisdiction, not the eventual wisdom of her answer. Any synthesis that declares her vindicated at this boundary would exceed the source.

## 26.3 Counterargument: public accountability does not make the Yamauchi judgment just

Correct.

The school manufactures scarcity, growth is uncertain, and Yamauchi's expulsion remains irreversible.

### Response

The developmental advance is procedural and political, not proof of objective human ranking. Horikita replaces anonymous bloc power with stated reasons and accepts liability. Hirata's moral objection remains active.

## 26.4 Counterargument: the class finishes last, so her leadership failed

The year-end 347 points are a real institutional result.

The class remains behind.

### Response

Institutional rank and constitutional development are separable. The class also makes the cohort's largest increase from zero and develops substantially greater coordination. The correct conclusion is not that the result does not matter; it is that point rank alone cannot tell us whether leadership became more generative or legitimate.

## 26.5 Counterargument: Sudō improves mainly because he is attracted to her

Romantic motivation is real and complicates the relation.

### Response

The development later includes restraint without immediate reward, academic effort, dissent during the Class Vote, and domain authority in Volume 11. Horikita explicitly credits his own effort rather than claiming him as her product (`Y1V10-E007`). Attraction helps begin the process; it does not exhaust the later capacity.

## 26.6 Counterargument: refusing the student council proves stubbornness, not independence

The refusal may be strategically inferior.

### Response

Independence is not identical to optimal choice. The significance is that Manabu's approval and Ayanokōji's recommendation no longer determine the answer. The decision becomes available for later revision because it is hers.

## 26.7 Counterargument: the haircut still follows Manabu's hidden preference

True in causal part. Manabu eventually reveals that he preferred short hair.

### Response

The text anticipates the objection. Horikita does not treat short hair as her permanent authentic form, retains attachment to the long-haired self, and may change again. The act matters because she reclaims the symbol's future meaning, not because the external world never influenced it.

## 26.8 Counterargument: First File proves she simply has low social ability

It proves that her early visible cooperation was extremely low under the guidebook's metric.

### Response

The same archive acknowledges development and describes the class's later aggregation problem. A frozen rating becomes evidence of trajectory when later conduct exceeds it.

## 26.9 Counterargument: Manabu's harshness was necessary

Rejected.

His diagnosis may be partly correct. His violence remains abusive. The later sibling reconciliation does not authorize the method retrospectively.

## 26.10 Counterargument: because Ayanokōji's interventions help her, the methods are justified

Rejected.

Developmental benefit is real. Consent and legitimate jurisdiction remain separate questions. Horikita's eventual strength does not retroactively give another person unlimited right to manufacture her crisis.

# 29. Canonical Year 1 profile

## 29.1 Governing identity

**Integrated self-author becoming a distributed, correctable developmental leader.**

## 29.2 Principal strength

She increasingly converts differentiated capacities into collective possibility without requiring herself to possess every capacity.

## 29.3 Principal epistemic virtue

She learns that judgment must account for context, trajectory, domain knowledge, and the limits of the observer's model.

## 29.4 Principal political virtue

She becomes willing to expose criteria and accept visible responsibility.

## 29.5 Principal ethical virtue

She resists treating current weakness or one dangerous identity-state as automatic proof of permanent disposability.

## 29.6 Principal failure mode

She may turn a personally meaningful rehabilitation or developmental project into an unconsented burden for the class.

## 29.7 Principal structural vulnerability

Her public leadership remains supported by hidden information and protection systems she cannot fully inspect.

## 29.8 Principal relational need

People who can challenge her without abandoning the shared project.

## 29.9 Principal developmental achievement

She moves from wanting others to meet her standard toward building a class in which unlike people can become useful without becoming copies of her.

## 29.10 Governing Year 1 question

> **Can individual excellence become leadership without turning weaker or different people into defects to be corrected, owned, or discarded?**

Year 1's answer is provisional but substantial:

> **Yes—when leadership becomes translational, developmental, distributed, public, and correctable. But the answer remains ethically unstable whenever the leader claims unilateral authority to decide whose rehabilitation others must finance or endure.**

# 31. Final synthesis

Horikita enters ANHS believing that excellence should make other people unnecessary.

Her grades, discipline, physical competence, and strategic seriousness support the illusion. The institution initially seems to punish her only by surrounding her with classmates who fail to meet the standard she considers obvious.

Year 1 reveals that the surrounding people are not obstacles placed around the real story of her excellence.

They are the medium through which excellence acquires political meaning.

Sudō teaches that current liability can become future capacity and that development belongs to the person developed rather than to the leader who helped initiate it.

Ike teaches that context can make dismissed knowledge decisive.

Hirata teaches that voluntary legitimacy and emotional accessibility are forms of ability.

Kushida teaches that social capacity can coexist with hostility and that benevolent intent cannot erase structural coercion.

Kōenji teaches that recognizing talent does not create ownership of its use.

Keisei teaches that dissent can be loyal participation.

Ichinose teaches that cooperation does not abolish another polity's private sphere and that separation need not become betrayal.

Manabu teaches—belatedly and imperfectly—that inheritance should expand rather than prescribe the self.

Ayanokōji teaches her to see contextual ability, dependence, coordination, and hidden structure. He also becomes the strongest threat to the legitimacy of her independence because he repeatedly authors the environment in which that independence develops.

The resulting Horikita is not gentle in any simple sense.

She remains ambitious.

She remains capable of severe judgment.

She can name a classmate for expulsion.

She can pursue a risky rehabilitation project.

She can prioritize the polity over individual comfort.

Her development lies elsewhere.

She increasingly understands that judgment must be:

- informed by context;
- sensitive to trajectory;
- exposed to dissent;
- distributed across domain knowledge;
- public enough to contest;
- and revisable when the model proves incomplete.

That is why her Year 1 endpoint is not merely “independent leader.”

It is **integrated self-author and corrigible generative leader**.

She becomes independent not because Manabu and Ayanokōji cease to matter, but because their influence no longer supplies an unquestionable endpoint. She can inherit, challenge, refuse, reinterpret, and diverge.

She becomes generative not because she personally acquires every ability, but because people around her become more capable, more usable, and sometimes more willing to oppose her.

She becomes corrigible not because she stops believing in her judgment, but because leadership increasingly includes structures through which judgment can be answered.

The final contradiction remains unresolved.

`POLITY-HORIKITA` is becoming a real class constitution while much of its security still belongs to a hidden sovereign. Horikita can increasingly govern what she can see. She does not yet possess a complete account of what has been done in her name or what contingencies remain outside her authority.

Year 1 therefore ends with a constitutional problem larger than one character:

> **Can a polity become self-governing when its visible leader has matured inside an environment whose decisive hidden author still controls information, protection, and the conditions of crisis?**

Horikita's development makes an affirmative answer imaginable.

It does not yet make the answer secure.
