# 1. Executive thesis

Year 1 begins with an apparently simple meritocratic deception:

> students are being evaluated even when they do not know the criteria.

By the end of the year, that premise has become much harder.

The books do not discover that merit is unreal.

They repeatedly insist that human beings differ in consequential ways:

- intelligence;
- athletic ability;
- judgment;
- discipline;
- courage;
- social skill;
- strategic inference;
- leadership;
- adaptability;
- and the capacity to make other people more capable.

The destabilization occurs elsewhere.

A person can possess ability without expressing it.

A person can express ability without being recognized.

A true statement can fail because it is not credible.

A capacity can be real but useless in the present ecology.

A brilliant person can remain politically unavailable because no one can coordinate with him.

A class can contain strong individuals without possessing collective strength.

Trust can become political infrastructure and also an attack surface.

A true wrongdoing can be converted into a totalizing identity through reputation authorship.

An institution can force people to treat continuation itself as scarce even when developmental potential remains unknown.

A superior performance can disappear if a higher authority controls the official record.

And a standardized file can accurately describe what was visible at a given time while still failing as a total account of the person measured.

The completed Year 1 therefore rejects both naïve meritocracy and naïve anti-meritocracy.

Its more adequate claim is:

> **Ability is ecological, developmental, relational, political, institutional, and only partially legible.**

A capacity becomes socially consequential through several separable stages:

1. **possession** — the capacity actually exists;
2. **development** — it can change through environment, practice, relationship, and choice;
3. **expression** — the person displays or withholds it;
4. **legibility** — another observer or metric recognizes what was displayed;
5. **coordination** — a group makes the capacity usable alongside unlike capacities;
6. **recording** — the institution truthfully preserves what occurred.

*First File* adds the final Year 1 warning:

7. **model limitation** — even a useful and accurate record remains a model of the person rather than the person in full.

This progression changes the meaning of Ayanokōji.

He begins as someone who authors what other people are allowed to see of **him**.

He then increasingly authors:

- what other people can verify;
- what environments they encounter;
- what alternatives appear available;
- which relationships become necessary;
- when strength becomes visible;
- which futures receive protection;
- what reputations become socially actionable;
- who survives institutional scarcity;
- and which developmental projects he personally wants to undertake.

Volume 11 makes developmental authorship explicitly self-chosen.

Volume 11.5 then changes the ethical problem. Ayanokōji's ordinary school life is explicitly the first goal he has set for himself, yet Manabu's challenge awakens a competing self-chosen desire: to develop students and see what developed people become when they compete. At the same time, Horikita's sibling closure, peer-to-peer development, Hiyori's correction of Ryūen, Keisei's promised dissent, Ichinose's retained class agency, and Kei's explicit answer to the confession all demonstrate that development does not have to be a one-way relation between designer and subject.

The year therefore moves from **developmental authorship** to **reciprocal authorship**.

Self-authorship alone is not enough.

A person can freely choose a project that still overreaches another person's autonomy.

The ethical test becomes:

> **Can Ayanokōji create conditions for growth while leaving other people authority over what their growth is for?**

*First File* adds the epistemic version of the same test.

Ayanokōji's own official file is an obvious demonstration that a record can be factually accurate about demonstrated output and radically incomplete about latent capacity. The person who most clearly exceeds the file written about him is also the person most tempted to treat his models of other people as actionable maps.

The final Year 1 problem is therefore not merely development versus control.

It is the transition from understanding to jurisdiction.

The locked Year 1 thesis is:

> **Year 1 examines developmental authorship: the power to make other people's capacities visible, usable, challenged, preserved, or transformed—and the recurring danger that the person or institution capable of cultivating another human being will mistake understanding for jurisdiction. By the end of the year, the problem becomes reciprocal and epistemic: self-authorship is insufficient unless other people retain authorship over themselves, and no rating, strategy, institutional record, or psychological model is entitled to become the whole person.**

The final architectural progression is:

> **authored visibility → authored legibility → authored environment → authored dependency → counter-curriculum → controlled exposure → distributed integration under hidden contingency → sovereignty over futurity → negotiated indispensability → institutional authorship → authored reputation → manufactured disposability → developmental authorship under procedural capture → reciprocal authorship → the file and the person**

The sequence should not be mistaken for fifteen disconnected motifs.

It is an enlargement of one governing question:

> **Who gets to define what another person is, what capacities count, what development is for, and what happens when the resulting model becomes powerful enough to govern the person's future?**

# 2. Year 1 has five major movements

The fourteen numbered/decimal novels plus *First File* form five large movements. The decimal volumes are not detachable side stories; each interrupts the exam-centered progression to test whether development can exist outside the institution's declared objective.

## Movement I — Learning that ability must become legible
### Volumes 1–2

The first two volumes establish the school as an epistemic institution.

The problem is not merely whether a student possesses ability or tells the truth.

The problem is whether the institution and surrounding social world can recognize what is there.

### Volume 1
The institution hides the evaluative criteria. Students experience freedom before learning that freedom has already been converted into data. Ayanokōji responds by authoring the visible version of himself.

### Volume 2
The institution openly adjudicates a conflict, but event truth is insufficient. Evidence, credibility, reputation, and presentation determine whether truth becomes actionable.

Together these volumes establish:

> **What exists and what a system can recognize are different categories.**

---

## Movement II — From interpreting environments to authoring them
### Volumes 3–4, with Volume 4.5 as counter-curriculum

The island examination broadens ability from individual possession to ecological usefulness.

The zodiac examination then makes trust itself a resource.

Ayanokōji's method escalates from controlling his own visibility to shaping the conditions under which other people discover options, fail, depend, and choose.

Volume 4.5 interrupts this escalation by asking what development looks like when nobody assigned the purpose.

Ordinary summer life creates a domain in which Ayanokōji can manipulate, protect privacy, preserve refusal, or simply decline to optimize.

This matters because later coercion cannot be explained as though he possesses no alternative repertoire.

The movement establishes:

> **An environment can reveal ability, develop ability, and also be authored to make another person's choices more predictable.**

---

## Movement III — Public ability, distributed integration, fear, and relational aftermath
### Volumes 5–7.5

The sports festival makes bodies publicly measurable.

Paper Shuffle makes heterogeneous cooperation academically necessary.

The Ryūen conflict turns fear, ownership, protection, and loyalty into explicit problems.

Christmas then examines what remains after crisis when Ayanokōji confronts ordinary romance, friendship, gifts, attraction, naming, and the possibility that another person could matter beyond tactical indispensability.

This movement produces two durable claims.

First:

> **Ability becomes increasingly social and distributed.**

The strongest person is not necessarily the best leader. The smartest student is not necessarily the best teacher. The most athletic individual is not necessarily the most useful teammate. A hidden operative is not equivalent to a trusted partner.

Second:

> **Ayanokōji's relationships become harder to classify as merely instrumental even while instrumentality remains structurally powerful.**

That unresolved mixture becomes one of the year’s principal engines.

---

## Movement IV — Power over rules, reputation, continuation, and the record
### Volumes 8–11

The scale moves upward.

Nagumo demonstrates that decisive power may belong to the person who can shape the examination rather than merely win within it.

Volume 9 makes reputation itself an authored environment.

The Class Vote forces each class to state—explicitly or implicitly—who deserves continuation under manufactured scarcity.

The Event Selection Exam seems to restore clean competition, but Tsukishiro reveals a higher sovereignty: an administrator who owns the communication channel can author the official result.

By Volume 11 the series has separated:

- capacity;
- visibility;
- legibility;
- coordination;
- and record.

Ayanokōji also crosses a personal threshold. Development is no longer merely something he happens to induce. He decides that changing Horikita is something he **wants** to do.

The movement therefore ends with **developmental authorship under procedural capture**: the protagonist chooses to become a developer at the same moment the institution demonstrates that higher-level authorship can falsify even legitimate performance.

---

## Movement V — Reciprocal authorship, legacy, and the limits of the file
### Volume 11.5 + *First File*

Volume 11.5 closes the narrative year without another special examination.

Transition itself becomes the mechanism.

Manabu graduates. Suzune integrates rather than purges the self built around her brother. Ayanokōji identifies ordinary life as his first self-chosen goal, then discovers that the idea of cultivating people across the four classes excites him. Kei and Ayanokōji enter an explicit reciprocal romantic relationship that remains entangled with developmental framing. Ichinose receives a future horizon without surrendering class agency. Hiyori corrects Ryūen. Keisei promises support and dissent. Sudō's development induces Ike's, which induces Shinohara's.

Development becomes visibly **horizontal and reciprocal**, not only vertical.

Then *First File* turns the completed year into an archive.

The guidebook supplies timelines, ratings, class summaries, hypothetical OAA values, editorial compression, and official retrospective profiles. These are useful precisely because they demonstrate the difference between a person and a usable model of that person.

Ayanokōji's deliberately mediocre file is the decisive case. Horikita's July cooperation E becomes a valid photograph of a state she later outgrows. Kei's social survival competence is poorly represented by conventional ratings. Hiyori's quiet inference exceeds a single formal judgment grade. Ichinose's strong social metrics cannot by themselves produce adversarial victory. Kōenji's high capacities cannot be requisitioned merely because they exist.

The two final bonus stories then return from files to lived experience: strategically irrelevant conversation becomes interesting, Ayanokōji simply wants to see Kei, and Ichinose experiences a feeling she cannot fit cleanly into the category of friendship while preserving her class responsibility.

The movement therefore closes Year 1 with a two-part correction:

> **development should become reciprocal rather than proprietary; knowledge should remain corrigible rather than totalizing.**

# 4. Volume 1 — hidden judgment and authored visibility

**Primary source artifact:** `COTE_Y1_V01_DEEP_READING.md`

Volume 1 establishes the school’s original political asymmetry:

> students act in an environment whose evaluative meaning is known more fully by the institution than by the people being evaluated.

The initial 100,000-point allocation makes the school look radically permissive. The zero-point reveal reframes the same period as surveillance and judgment.

The important second-pass correction is that Ayanokōji does not simply resist being measured.

He authors the visible version of himself.

His fifty-point entrance scores, restrained physical displays, reluctance to claim credit, and delegation of visible action create an interface through which the school and classmates encounter an intentionally partial person.

That makes Volume 1 the first authorship volume.

The object of authorship is **visibility**.

At the same time, Horikita's first transformation establishes the developmental counterclaim that will survive the year.

Her initial political logic is dispositional:

- low performers are burdens;
- the class should prioritize those already able to contribute;
- continued weakness is treated as evidence of stable inferiority.

The Sudō crisis destabilizes that judgment.

By the end of the volume, she is already moving toward the possibility that a "defective product" can change.

The year therefore begins with two opposed but entangled insights:

1. people differ materially in ability;
2. current measurable weakness does not prove a fixed developmental ceiling.

That tension will become brutal in Volume 10.

# 5. Volume 2 — truth, credibility, and authored legibility

**Primary source artifact:** `COTE_Y1_V02_DEEP_READING.md`

Volume 2 advances the institutional problem from hidden criteria to visible adjudication.

Sudō's account can be true and still lose.

Sakura can witness the relevant event and still possess insufficient proof.

The school must act through:

- admissible evidence;
- probative weight;
- credibility;
- reputation;
- and procedurally legible representations.

This creates a major Year 1 principle:

> **Truth is not self-executing.**

A true fact that cannot be made credible may have less institutional force than a false narrative supported by injuries, corroboration, and prior reputation.

Sakura's personal arc reproduces the same architecture.

Her school self and `雫 / Shizuku` persona are not best understood as truth versus falsity.

They are different technologies of visibility.

The camera can:

- author how she appears;
- document events;
- expose her to unwanted attention;
- or become part of a bluff that changes other people's choices.

Ayanokōji's strategic escalation is therefore epistemic.

He increasingly controls:

- which premises Horikita encounters;
- what Class C believes may have been recorded;
- and what options become rational under uncertainty.

The move from Volume 1 to Volume 2 is:

> **controlling what others see → controlling what others can establish as believable.**

# 6. Volume 3 — ecological ability and environmental authorship

**Primary source artifact:** `COTE_Y1_V03_DEEP_READING.md`

The deserted-island exam destroys the illusion that school ability can be represented by one axis.

Ike's camping experience becomes valuable.

Hirata's social translation becomes valuable.

Horikita's individual excellence becomes less valuable when illness and isolation make it socially unavailable.

Ryūen's willingness to sabotage becomes strategically valuable even while damaging trust.

Ayanokōji's synthesis becomes decisive because he can perceive the changing marginal value of unlike capacities.

The second-pass concept of **ecological ability** is crucial.

> An ability does not have one fixed value. Its value changes with environment, scarcity, complementary capacities, social acceptance, and institutional design.

The island is also the first volume where Ayanokōji clearly moves beyond interpreting a system into **authoring another person's developmental environment**.

His interventions exist on a spectrum.

### Ike
He suppresses his own near-solution so Ike can discover that his knowledge matters.

This is relatively low-coercion scaffolding.

### Sakura
He makes room for interaction, recognizes progress, and allows ordinary friendship to produce strategic insight.

This is relational development.

### Horikita
He controls information, exposure, pressure, attribution, and the conditions of failure.

This is coercive developmental authorship.

The famous tool language matters because it demonstrates that victory-conditioned instrumentality is genuinely part of him.

But the same volume also demonstrates:

- interest in friendship;
- appreciation of other people's strengths;
- desire to preserve school life;
- and willingness to let another student keep credit.

The proper conclusion is not that one of these selves is fake.

It is that Year 1 will increasingly ask what happens when genuine ordinary desire and a trained instrumental worldview coexist in the same person.

# 7. Volume 4 — trust as capital and the manufacture of dependency

**Primary source artifact:** `COTE_Y1_V04_DEEP_READING.md`

The zodiac examination turns mixed-class interaction into a market in belief.

The strongest strategies depend on different theories of trust.

- Katsuragi protects through silence.
- Ichinose produces cooperative legitimacy.
- Ryūen centralizes information and coercion.
- Ayanokōji uses identity manipulation and hidden coordination.

The decisive Year 1 development, however, is not the exam result.

It is Kei.

Her social hierarchy, trauma, false relationship with Hirata, and fear of unprotected status reveal a form of ability that earlier scalar models would miss.

She can:

- read female-group atmosphere;
- influence social behavior;
- distribute information;
- and make a social environment intelligible from inside.

Ayanokōji recognizes this capacity.

He then obtains it through a method that makes Volume 4 one of the year's most important ethical pivots.

He does not merely offer protection to an already endangered student.

He contributes to the conditions that discredit alternative protectors and make his protection appear indispensable.

That is why "authored dependency" is the correct architectural label.

The year’s governing problem becomes harder here:

> **A relationship can produce real safety and real future agency even when its origin is coercive.**

The presence of benefit does not erase the method.

The cruelty of the method does not make every later benefit unreal.

Year 1 repeatedly refuses clean moral bookkeeping.

# 8. Volume 4.5 — ordinary life as counter-curriculum

**Primary source artifact:** `COTE_Y1_V04_5_DEEP_READING.md`

Volume 4.5 performs a formal experiment.

It temporarily removes the large special-exam objective and asks:

> What do these people become when nobody has clearly defined what success is?

The answer matters especially for Ayanokōji.

Summer produces:

- gifts;
- rejection;
- embarrassment;
- bodily inconvenience;
- friendship;
- ice cream;
- leisure;
- requests for help;
- privacy decisions;
- and situations where information could be weaponized but sometimes is not.

The strongest concept is **counter-curriculum**.

The school still surrounds everything.

Private points, surveillance, class status, the student council, and expulsion remain real.

But ordinary experience is partially underdetermined.

The meaning of:

- buying a birthday gift;
- enjoying food;
- helping in an elevator;
- preserving someone's right to refuse;
- or enjoying the pool

is not assigned in advance by a formal score.

This matters because Ayanokōji describes himself as in some sense newly born and still fluid.

Ordinary school life is therefore not simply recreation.

It is an educational environment competing with the White Room and with Ayanokōji's own strategic habits.

Most importantly, Volume 4.5 proves that he possesses multiple intervention modes.

He can:

- coerce;
- manipulate;
- scaffold;
- protect privacy;
- preserve refusal;
- or deliberately decline to optimize.

That later makes his coercive choices harder to excuse as mere incapacity.

# 9. Volume 5 — public strength and relational coordination

**Primary source artifact:** `COTE_Y1_V05_DEEP_READING.md`

The sports festival makes ability visible in a more direct way than previous examinations.

Bodies perform in public.

Placements are scored.

Yet the volume immediately demonstrates that public excellence is not enough.

Sudō is an extraordinary athlete and a poor early leader.

Horikita is an extraordinary student who initially fails to coordinate with weaker partners.

Kōenji is extraordinarily capable and often politically unavailable.

Hirata is less spectacular but more socially usable.

Ryūen can lose local contests while dominating the political environment.

Sakayanagi can be physically unable to participate while remaining a major political actor.

The three-legged race therefore becomes a miniature theory of Year 1.

> **Strength becomes socially useful when it can adapt its expression to another person.**

Horikita and Sudō each confront the gap between being strong and being usable by a collective.

Their lunch conversation matters because it creates reciprocal developmental commitment rather than a one-way hierarchy of competent teacher and defective student.

Ayanokōji, meanwhile, demonstrates both sides of developmental authorship.

He can teach through demonstration and leave the next decision to Horikita.

He can also permit defeat because he believes suffering will become future strength.

The final relay introduces another important thread.

Ayanokōji's exposure is controlled, but the desire to race Manabu appears to exceed strategic necessity.

For a brief period, performance is intrinsically meaningful.

The White Room challenge at the end then makes defeat itself into a possible form of liberation.

The protagonist conditioned to win begins to imagine a worthy loss as proof that his creator's developmental model is not absolute.

# 10. Volume 6 — distributed integration and the politics of removal

**Primary source artifact:** `COTE_Y1_V06_DEEP_READING.md`

Paper Shuffle turns class performance into a network problem.

Students must:

- generate questions;
- engineer pairings;
- teach one another;
- distribute academic labor;
- manage secrecy;
- anticipate sabotage;
- and protect vulnerable pairs.

Keisei's realization is central.

The sports festival taught him that academic superiority can look exactly like athletic inferiority when the environment changes.

No one individual contains every capacity the school can demand.

The political answer is therefore not universal excellence.

It is **distributed integration**.

This makes the Horikita–Kushida dispute more important than a personal feud.

Ayanokōji's answer to internal danger is removal.

Horikita increasingly asks whether future value is being destroyed by treating current threat as complete identity.

Her Sudō experience becomes the precedent.

That does not prove she is right.

Kushida represents a qualitatively different security problem because she can weaponize genuine confidential information and deliberately sabotage the class.

The significance is that Horikita now possesses a governing presumption distinct from Ayanokōji's.

> Ayanokōji asks what contingency eliminates the threat.
>
> Horikita asks what developmental future disappears if removal becomes the first solution.

At the same time, Ayanokōji demonstrates a less interventionist developmental mode with Airi.

He can withhold authorship so another person must take the social step herself.

This is a crucial Year 1 correction.

He is not locked into crisis engineering as the only way he knows how to help.

# 11. Volume 7 — ownership, fear, and sovereignty over futurity

**Primary source artifact:** `COTE_Y1_V07_DEEP_READING.md`

Volume 7 makes ownership explicit.

Atsuomi's claim over Kiyotaka is literal in its logic: the child is a produced asset whose creator determines his proper destination.

Chabashira's false paternal threat converts enrollment into leverage.

Ryūen governs through the belief that future retaliation makes resistance unsafe.

Ayanokōji rejects all three forms when directed toward himself.

The volume then forces the reader to compare those rejections with his relationship to Kei.

His promise of protection is real.

The dependency beneath that promise is also partly authored by him.

That is why the rooftop cannot be reduced to a rescue scene or a demonstration of power.

Kei receives substantial reason to believe Ayanokōji manipulated and abandoned her.

Naming him would offer immediate relief.

She refuses.

The second-pass reading correctly treats this as an act that exceeds the simplest parasite model.

Ayanokōji authored the test conditions.

He did not fully author the answer.

This is one of the most important distinctions in the entire Year 1 method:

> **A developed person may exceed the developer's script.**

Ryūen's defeat also begins changing the meaning of his own rule.

Fear is broken as an exclusive foundation.

The later willingness of followers to preserve him will create the possibility of authority sustained by something more voluntary.

Finally, Ayanokōji gives one of his clearest accounts of what the White Room lacked.

It did not simply lack comfort.

It lacked the ordinary social world through which freedom becomes experientially intelligible.

The "irrelevant" scenes of snow, books, meals, friends, movies, and Christmas preparation are therefore the positive content of his rebellion.

# 12. Volume 7.5 — negotiated indispensability and the problem of irreplaceability

**Primary source artifact:** `COTE_Y1_V07_5_DEEP_READING.md`

Volume 7.5 asks whether Ayanokōji can inhabit ordinary intimacy without converting intimacy into strategic selection.

The volume begins with the idea of `かけがえのない存在`—an irreplaceable existence.

It ends with Kei described as `必要不可欠な存在`—an indispensable existence.

The distinction matters.

An indispensable person may be necessary because a system depends upon them.

An irreplaceable person has singular personal value that no substitute can reproduce.

Ayanokōji wants to understand the latter.

By Christmas, he can clearly identify the former.

Satō provides the most ordinary romantic route presently available.

He likes much of what the date offers.

He wants ordinary romance in the abstract.

Yet when actual choice becomes necessary, he preserves the relationship better adapted to the abnormal world he inhabits.

The volume therefore crystallizes a major contradiction:

> **He wants ordinary life, but repeatedly selects relationships compatible with strategic life.**

This does not make his care for Kei unreal.

The medicine episode is particularly important because it shows concern that has no audience and no immediate exam purpose.

But practical care remains easier for him than reciprocal emotional communication.

Volume 7.5 therefore moves dependency from unilateral manufacture toward **negotiated partnership**, without pretending the relationship's coercive origins have disappeared.

# 13. Volume 8 — institutional authorship and the blind spot of exemplary leadership

**Primary source artifact:** `COTE_Y1_V08_DEEP_READING.md`

The Mixed Training Camp enlarges the battlefield.

Nagumo demonstrates a form of power beyond superior interpretation of rules.

He can influence the rules themselves.

The crucial reconstruction is:

> he did not see an examination and devise a strategy; he devised the strategy and helped create the examination.

That is **institutional authorship**.

The official ranking can therefore conceal the real political result.

Manabu wins the visible contest.

Nagumo attacks the infrastructure around him.

Tachibana's vulnerability forces an enormous rescue expenditure and reveals that Manabu's exemplary conduct did not eliminate the relational blind spots created by emotional distance.

The volume also expands the meaning of trust.

Trust can be:

- accumulated moral credibility;
- access to another person's defenses;
- a one-use strategic asset;
- or the infrastructure of genuine temporary community.

The cross-class mixed group matters because not all relational development is commanded.

Students who begin as strangers or enemies can become a real temporary community through:

- labor;
- embarrassment;
- disclosure;
- injury;
- mutual aid;
- and ordinary coexistence.

This is another case where the result exceeds any one strategist's authorship.

Ayanokōji's own protection hierarchy becomes explicit.

He does not promise equal rescue.

He privately identifies a small group whose futures receive exceptional guarantees.

This makes protection itself a form of private sovereignty.

# 14. Volume 9 — reputation, forgiveness, and authorship of moral identity

**Primary source artifact:** `COTE_Y1_V09_DEEP_READING.md`

Volume 9 is not resolved by proving Ichinose innocent.

She committed the relevant theft.

The central question is what a true wrongdoing is allowed to mean.

Sakayanagi's attack converts event into identity.

One wrong act becomes a totalizing category through which later kindness can be reinterpreted as fraud.

This is **authored reputation**.

The year’s truth/evidence thread therefore matures again.

The relevant political power is not only deciding what others see or can prove.

It is deciding:

- which truth is selected;
- what context accompanies it;
- what rumors surround it;
- and whether the target is allowed a future beyond it.

Ayanokōji's response is architecturally revealing.

He does not simply restore a clean information environment.

He saturates the environment with additional rumors until institutional nonintervention becomes too costly.

That strategy protects Ichinose from an asymmetric attack.

It also injures uninvolved students and weaponizes secrets.

At the interpersonal level, the same ambiguity returns.

Ayanokōji can offer a humane proposition—that people have the right to forgiveness—while simultaneously conceptualizing the remaining work as breaking Ichinose's heart so she must confront what she buried.

Again:

> **He attempts to free another person from someone else's authorship by assuming major authorship over the conditions of that freedom.**

Year 1's ethical problem is now unmistakable.

# 15. Volume 10 — manufactured disposability and accountable judgment

**Primary source artifact:** `COTE_Y1_V10_DEEP_READING.md`

The Class Vote transforms the school's old threat of expulsion into an explicit institutional demand.

Someone must become disposable.

The volume is therefore not merely a popularity contest.

It is a forced constitutional crisis.

Each class reveals a different answer.

### Hirata
He rejects the legitimacy of selecting a classmate for sacrifice and finally offers himself.

### Horikita
She accepts that a judgment must be made and that leadership requires publicly owning the reason.

### Ichinose
She tries collective financial rescue and holds a lottery as an egalitarian fallback.

### Sakayanagi
She uses the examination as sovereign pruning.

### Kōenji
He argues from future capacity rather than sentiment.

### Ayanokōji
He evaluates removal through future value, strategic utility, and private protection priorities.

The placement of Sudō's growth immediately before this examination is crucial.

The series has already shown that early institutional weakness can radically underestimate later usefulness.

Chabashira explicitly admits that student growth can overturn predictions.

The exam nevertheless forces a final decision under developmental uncertainty.

That produces one of Year 1's hardest questions:

> **Who has enough knowledge to terminate another person's developmental future?**

Horikita's development is not that she discovers an objectively correct human ranking.

It is that she makes her judgment public and accepts political liability for it.

The distinction from Ayanokōji is now sharper.

He shapes information and contingencies from behind the scenes.

She increasingly creates visible reasons other people can contest.

That does not automatically make her judgment morally correct.

It makes her governable.

The ending then changes the meaning of institutional neutrality.

Tsukishiro's arrival reveals that the school may itself be procedurally captured.

The institution that manufactures a harsh choice may also be susceptible to adult actors trying to manufacture a particular outcome.

# 16. Volume 11 — developmental authorship and the capture of the record

**Primary source artifact:** `COTE_Y1_V11_DEEP_READING.md`

Volume 11 makes the Year 1 development/control thesis explicit at three levels.

## 16.1 Class level: distributed ability

The Event Selection Exam requires a class to identify, train, allocate, and combine unlike capacities.

Horikita's leadership becomes increasingly generative.

She does not need to possess every skill.

She needs to make other people's skills usable.

Sudō's basketball authority, Akito's archery, Professor's typing, chess preparation, and the problem of Kōenji all reinforce the same distinction:

> **possessed ability is not identical to collective capacity.**

Kōenji can be extraordinarily capable and politically unavailable.

That is not the same thing as being weak.

## 16.2 Individual level: Ayanokōji chooses developmental authorship

The most important Ayanokōji development is not another hidden victory.

He distinguishes what he *should* do from what he *wants* to do and decides that he wants to change Horikita seriously rather than incidentally.

That is one of his clearest acts of self-authorship.

The purpose is not assigned by the White Room.

But self-chosen purpose does not automatically generate ethical jurisdiction.

The Hirata intervention demonstrates the danger.

Ayanokōji applies extreme blame because he wants the class to recover the socially useful "part" represented by Hirata.

The outcome is productive.

The method remains coercive.

This is the Year 1 thesis in concentrated form:

> **development can be authentic without making the developer's method legitimate.**

## 16.3 Institutional level: record authorship

Tsukishiro exposes the highest institutional layer reached before the narrative transition of Volume 11.5.

Ayanokōji can make the stronger chess move.

Sakayanagi can recognize that something is wrong.

Horikita can create the board state from which the winning line becomes available.

None of that controls the official outcome if the acting chairman can alter the communication channel.

This makes record integrity part of `実力`.

Ability becomes socially real only when the environment:

- allows it to develop;
- allows it to be expressed;
- makes it usable;
- and honestly records what occurred.

Volume 11 therefore changes the year-level political hierarchy.

The most powerful actor may not be:

- the strongest student;
- the best strategist;
- the most trusted leader;
- or the person who authors the exam.

It may be the actor who controls the record by which everyone else's actions become institutionally real.

# 17. Volume 11.5 — reciprocal authorship, legacy, and hidden curriculum

**Primary source artifact:** `COTE_Y1_V11_5_DEEP_READING.md`

Volume 11.5 is structurally indispensable because it refuses to end Year 1 with another scored contest.

The formal examinations stop.

What remains is transition:

- graduation;
- sibling separation;
- succession;
- ordinary dates and conversations;
- class reorganization;
- decisions about who gets to decide another person's future;
- and the question of what Ayanokōji wants to leave behind.

The volume therefore tests whether the capacities and relationships produced under Year 1 pressure can survive when no examination tells the characters what the next objective is.

## 17.1 Horikita: integration instead of purification

The prologue's mirror and haircut imagery initially appears to invite an authentic-self reading.

Suzune describes the personality, appearance, academic effort, and hairstyle organized around her imagined version of Manabu's preference as a `偽者`.

But the text immediately refuses the conclusion that liberation means discovering a pure original self underneath.

Those years are also `紛れもない私`.

They happened to her.

They produced capacities, habits, injuries, ambitions, and relationships that belong to her history even if she now rejects the purpose that organized them.

Her Year 1 endpoint is therefore **integrated self-authorship**.

Influence does not disappear.

It loses sovereign authority.

Manabu completes that transition by rejecting imitation as the endpoint of development. His mature injunction—`他者に強くあれ。そして優しくあれ`—does not give Suzune a template to copy. It gives her a tension to solve for herself: strength without domination and kindness without helplessness.

The haircut is accordingly not a permanent essence marker. Suzune retains attachment to the long-haired self and may grow it again. The symbol works because it becomes history rather than destiny.

## 17.2 Development becomes horizontal

Earlier Year 1 development often appears vertically:

- Horikita tutors Sudō;
- Ayanokōji designs conditions around Horikita;
- Ayanokōji coerces Kei;
- Ayanokōji pressures Hirata;
- Manabu challenges Suzune.

Volume 11.5 repeatedly gives us another model.

Sudō's improvement makes Ike afraid of remaining behind.

Ike's determination moves Shinohara.

Keisei promises Horikita that he will support her when she is right and oppose her when she is wrong.

Hiyori's concern becomes corrective agency toward Ryūen.

Kanzaki's dissent attempts to strengthen Ichinose's polity.

The developmental relation becomes a network rather than a line.

This is the first major answer to the Year 1 authorship problem:

> **growth need not require a superior designer standing outside the process.**

People can develop because peers change, disagree, model alternatives, and create reciprocal obligations.

## 17.3 Manabu's legacy challenge: freedom must leave a trace

Ayanokōji can now articulate ordinary school life as his own project.

He tells Chairman Sakayanagi:

> `生まれて初めて、オレは自分で目標を立て、そしてそれを実行しようとしてるんです`

The phrase `生まれて初めて` matters.

Ordinary high school is not merely the absence of White Room command. It is explicitly the first goal Ayanokōji claims to have set for himself.

Manabu does not reject that goal.

He asks whether invisibility is enough.

What will Ayanokōji leave in other people's memories?

The question transforms negative liberty into legacy. Escaping someone else's project establishes freedom from authorship; it does not yet answer what a self-authored life should produce.

Ayanokōji's answer is startling because it returns him to development from the opposite direction.

He imagines:

> `生徒たちを育成し、成長させること`

He thinks about developing students across the four classes and then seeing what developed people become when they compete.

The thought excites him.

He is frightened by the excitement because it conflicts with the peaceful ordinary life he thought he wanted.

The year therefore refuses a simple liberation arc.

The boy escaping an imposed developmental experiment begins to discover that **development itself may be the first experiment he wants to author**.

This is not equivalent to the White Room. Nobody assigns the project to him.

That difference is exactly what makes the ethical problem sharper:

> **a self-chosen project can still become another person's constraint.**

## 17.4 Kushida jurisdiction: yielding a decision without ceasing to be a developer

Ayanokōji tells Horikita about his prior plan to remove Kushida and still regards expulsion as the efficient answer.

Horikita refuses both naïve preservation and immediate pruning.

She will not casually discard a classmate, but she does not promise unconditional protection if later circumstances make Kushida the least defensible person to retain.

Ayanokōji then yields future jurisdiction over Kushida to Horikita.

The motive remains partly developer-coded: he wants to see what Horikita will do with the obstacle.

Yet the surrender is real.

He could continue the elimination project privately.

He chooses not to.

This becomes one of the clearest local examples of **reciprocal authorship**: superior confidence in one's own answer does not automatically require unilateral execution.

## 17.5 Ichinose: future orientation without political absorption

The rain conversation gives Ichinose a one-year horizon.

Ayanokōji becomes someone she can regard as trustworthy despite not being an ally.

The promise is real care in the sense that it restores a future toward which she can move.

But his interior language remains disturbing.

If her class collapses, he imagines personally performing the `介錯`—the finishing or mercy blow.

Care, attraction, developmental concern, and sovereign judgment therefore coexist.

The volume refuses to tell us that one cancels the others.

This is important for the year-level architecture because Ichinose is not simply rescued into Ayanokōji's orbit. Her class remains her own political project.

## 17.6 Kei: reciprocal romance inside a concealed curriculum

The confession is real and reciprocal.

Ayanokōji says he likes Kei.

Kei answers that she has come to like him.

They embrace.

Whatever strategic preparation precedes the confession, the relationship is not created by silently assigning her a role without an answer.

At the same time, Ayanokōji's interior narration immediately translates romance into pedagogy.

He does not understand love, so he will learn it through Kei.

He calls her a `教科書`.

He also interprets the relationship as part of curing what he understands as her host-dependency.

The romance therefore becomes a **hidden curriculum**.

That phrase is not an accusation that the confession is false.

It describes the coexistence of two structures:

- reciprocal adolescent attachment;
- and asymmetric developmental purpose known more fully by one participant.

The same narration reaches toward a counterpossibility. Ayanokōji hopes Kei may become `掛け替えのない存在` and ends with `祈ろう`—a prayer that, while holding someone important, he is genuinely smiling and truly means to cherish her.

The prayer matters because it is not certainty.

Year 1 therefore locks the relationship as:

> **explicit reciprocal romance + continuing developmental asymmetry + unresolved irreplaceability.**

## 17.7 Matsushita: hidden ability becomes a class-wide epistemic problem

Matsushita prevents Ayanokōji's concealment from becoming a protagonist-exclusive metaphysics.

She has also deliberately underperformed to avoid visibility, dependency, expectation, and inconvenience.

Her social observation and inference are real.

Yet Ayanokōji notices her surveillance, recognizes the concealment, and supplies a bounded false model that leaves her believing she gained greater psychological advantage than she actually did.

The important point is not merely that another hidden strong student exists.

It is that **the class contains more latent capacity than its public file reveals**.

The epistemic problem of Year 1 is therefore distributed across the student body.

## 17.8 The institutional correction: ANHS contains internal checks

Volume 11 demonstrated administrative record capture.

Volume 11.5 prevents that discovery from flattening ANHS into one sovereign will.

Chairman Sakayanagi, Mashima, and Chabashira can act as partial institutional checks against Tsukishiro.

The school therefore has something closer to a constitutional problem than a single moral identity.

Rules, teachers, councils, administrators, and oversight can diverge.

Institutional integrity depends partly on whether actors with authority are willing to resist capture.

The school remains capable of coercive design.

But it is not analytically adequate to say simply that “the school” wants one thing.

# 18. *First File* — the file and the person

**Primary source artifact:** `COTE_Y1_FIRST_FILE_PARATEXT_AUDIT.md`

*First File* is not Volume 12.

It is a mixed official paratext encountered after the narrative Year 1 sequence. Its analytical value depends on not collapsing its source types.

The guidebook contains four distinct evidentiary layers:

- `FF-DATA` — calendars, school-system information, point states, and other documentary records;
- `FF-RATING` — dated ability evaluations and explicitly hypothetical retrospective OAA modeling;
- `FF-EDITORIAL` — official character summaries, relationship descriptions, class-state framing, and retrospective compression;
- `FF-FICTION` — two exclusive final stories with narrative interiority.

The first three layers tell us how the completed year can be filed.

The fourth returns us to experiences that resist filing.

## 18.1 The archive is itself an authorship system

The guidebook converts a messy year into a retrievable model.

It selects:

- dates;
- score lines;
- categories;
- relationship arrows;
- class summaries;
- ability axes;
- volume summaries;
- and memorable labels.

This is useful.

It is also an act of authorship.

An archive decides what receives a field and what disappears because no convenient field exists.

That makes *First File* formally continuous with Year 1's preoccupation with records, evidence, reputation, and institutional legibility.

The danger is not that the archive is false.

The danger is the inference:

> **recorded = complete.**

The guidebook repeatedly gives us reasons to reject that inference.

## 18.2 Ayanokōji's file: accurate output, incomplete person

Ayanokōji's dated profile records mediocre visible ability.

The same official retrospective framing acknowledges that his entrance-exam performance was deliberately controlled.

This creates the cleanest Year 1 demonstration of measurement scope.

The rating can be accurate about demonstrated output while radically incomplete about latent capacity.

Ayanokōji therefore does not prove that measurement is meaningless.

He proves that a measurement has a more bounded object than readers often assume.

It measures something like:

> **capacity as made visible under a particular observation regime.**

That distinction should govern the specialist treatment of every later Year 1 metric.

## 18.3 Frozen visibility states and developmental obsolescence

Horikita's July cooperation E is not simply an error.

It is a photograph of a developmental state.

By the Year 1 endpoint she has become a leader whose strength increasingly depends on gathering information, coordinating unlike capacities, accepting dissent, revising judgments, and publicly owning consequential decisions.

The rating becomes meaningful precisely because the person can outgrow it.

The same logic applies differently elsewhere.

Kushida's high cooperation does not prove prosocial intention.

Kei's weak conventional file cannot explain her social survival competence.

Kōenji's strong capacities do not make him collectively requisitionable.

Hiyori's quiet judgment exceeds what a single judgment grade can capture.

Ichinose's high social metrics represent genuine political capacity without guaranteeing adversarial success.

The final lesson is not anti-measurement.

It is anti-totalization.

## 18.4 The hypothetical OAA and the rhetoric of false precision

The guidebook's “if OAA had existed” material is explicitly retrospective and counterfactual.

It can illuminate which dimensions the later institutional model would have made visible.

It cannot be smuggled backward as though Year 1 students actually operated inside that measurement regime.

The final lock therefore activates `OAA_LEGIBILITY` only at `Y1FF` as paratext.

This matters especially for extreme-looking values.

Ayanokōji's low modeled score is not evidence that the guidebook forgot his ability.

It dramatizes the consequences of scoring observable school performance rather than hidden total capacity.

Again:

> **the file can be correct about its object and incomplete about the person.**

## 18.5 The four class constitutions as four information regimes

*First File* sharpens the four-polity comparison.

### Horikita
Needs increasingly accurate local knowledge of heterogeneous people so ability can be developed and coordinated. The risk is hidden dependency on Ayanokōji's information and intervention.

### Ryūen
Builds actionable models of fear, incentives, weakness, and desire. The risk is converting psychological accuracy into authorization for coercion.

### Ichinose
Builds order through trust and social transparency. The risk is that norms optimized for reciprocal good faith become exploitable by actors who do not reciprocate.

### Sakayanagi
Builds a sophisticated centralized map of talent and assigns people to roles. The risk is that the better the sovereign's model becomes, the less incentive the polity has to generate independent model-builders and succession capacity.

The political problem is no longer simply which leader has the best information.

It is:

> **What institutional arrangement uses knowledge about people without converting knowledge into possession?**

## 18.6 Bonus fiction: formal rebuttal to total legibility

The guidebook closes by leaving the filing system and returning to ordinary experience.

Ayanokōji values `学生らしい生活`.

He chooses to enter `恋愛という世界` rather than merely solve it from outside.

A strategically irrelevant phone conversation becomes intrinsically interesting.

He is clumsy about the social timing of ending it.

He directly thinks:

> `オレは早く恵に会いたい`

The importance is not that utility disappears from his psychology.

It is that utility no longer exhausts the evidence.

Ichinose's bonus story provides the complementary case. Ayanokōji is a `大切な友達`, then something beyond friendship that she cannot finish naming. The feeling restores warmth and will, but she explicitly remembers that their classes remain rivals and returns herself to the coming struggle.

These are small events compared with the year's exams.

That is exactly why they matter.

They demonstrate values whose significance depends on being lived rather than scored.

## 18.7 The epistemic form of reciprocal authorship

V11.5 establishes reciprocal authorship as an ethical problem:

> another person must retain authority over what their development is for.

*First File* adds the epistemic condition:

> the developer must also accept that their model of the other person is incomplete.

This is particularly important for Ayanokōji.

The boy whose own file is obviously inadequate to him is also unusually confident in his ability to model other people's fear, dependency, incentives, growth, and likely choices.

The final Year 1 danger is therefore not merely that he misunderstands people.

It is that **understanding something true about a person can become a claim of jurisdiction over the whole person**.

# 19. The progression of `実力 / jitsuryoku`

Year 1 steadily decomposes what initially looks like a single concept.

The title's `実力` cannot responsibly be reduced to test scores, combat power, strategy, or a hidden “true stat.”

By the *First File* boundary, at least seven layers must be distinguished.

## 19.1 Possessed ability

What a person can actually do.

Year 1 never denies this layer. Ayanokōji's concealed capacity, Kōenji's extraordinary performance, Sakayanagi's strategic cognition, Horikita's academics, Sudō's athleticism, and Hiyori's inference all depend on real differences.

## 19.2 Developmental ability

What can change through effort, environment, relationship, and opportunity.

Sudō is the clearest recurring example, but the year increasingly treats development as widespread rather than exceptional.

Current output does not establish a fixed ceiling.

## 19.3 Expressed ability

What the person chooses or manages to display under the current conditions.

Ayanokōji deliberately suppresses this layer. Matsushita demonstrates that he is not unique in doing so.

A system can therefore record output faithfully and still miss capacity the subject withheld.

## 19.4 Legible ability

What another observer, institution, or metric can infer from expression.

Volume 2 makes this epistemic distinction explicit through evidence and credibility. *First File* later turns it into literal profiles.

Legibility is not identical to possession.

## 19.5 Ecological and socially usable ability

A capacity's value changes with the environment and with whether other people can use it.

Ike's camping experience, Kei's social position, Hiyori's quiet judgment, Kōenji's refusal, and Horikita's illness all show that ability has context-dependent marginal value.

## 19.6 Distributed and generative ability

A class is not simply the sum of individual strengths.

It needs coordination.

A generative leader does more than contribute personally; the leader identifies, develops, allocates, and links capacities that the leader may not possess.

This is Horikita's major Year 1 move.

## 19.7 Recorded ability

A performance only becomes an institutional result if the system records what actually occurred.

Tsukishiro's chess interference makes record integrity part of the architecture of merit.

## 19.8 Modeled ability and the file/person boundary

*First File* adds a final distinction.

A metric or profile can be useful, accurate, and still narrower than the person.

The final Year 1 formulation is therefore:

> **`実力` is not merely what a person can do. It is what can be developed, expressed, recognized, coordinated, and preserved as a truthful result—while every measurement remains a model rather than the person in full.**

This formulation rejects two symmetrical errors.

The first is scalar reductionism:

> one score tells us who the person really is.

The second is romantic anti-measurement:

> because no score captures the whole person, scores reveal nothing real.

Year 1 supports neither.

# 20. The progression of Ayanokōji's authorship

Ayanokōji's Year 1 power can be tracked by the changing object of authorship.

## V1 — visibility
He authors what others can observe of him.

## V2 — legibility
He authors which facts become believable and which routes of discovery others encounter.

## V3 — environment
He increasingly authors the conditions under which other people's capacities become useful or fail.

## V4 — dependency
He engineers a relationship in which protection becomes indispensable after alternatives are made to fail.

## V4.5 — nonoptimization becomes possible
He demonstrates that he can preserve refusal, protect privacy, or decline intervention. This matters ethically because later coercion is not his only available repertoire.

## V5 — exposure
He decides when strength should become visible and when defeat itself may be educationally useful.

## V6 — hidden contingency
He constructs systems in which other people's authentic behavior becomes predictable within contingencies they do not fully know.

## V7 — futurity
Protection becomes a claim over future threat environments. His resistance to being owned coexists with his willingness to structure another person's future.

## V7.5 — negotiated indispensability
He confronts the possibility that a relationship can acquire value beyond utility while retaining abnormal strategic architecture.

## V8 — selective sovereignty inside larger institutional authorship
Nagumo reveals that rule-making can dominate rule-following. Ayanokōji increasingly chooses which people and futures fall inside his private protection boundary.

## V9 — information environment
He manipulates the rumor ecology through which moral identity becomes actionable.

## V10 — survival allocation
He privately reallocates protection and expulsion risk inside an institutionally manufactured scarcity regime.

## V11 — chosen developmental purpose
He consciously decides that changing Horikita is something he wants rather than merely something useful.

## V11.5 — self-authorship and reciprocal jurisdiction
Ordinary life is explicitly his first self-chosen goal. Manabu's challenge then awakens a desire to leave a developmental trace. At the same time, Ayanokōji yields the Kushida decision, encounters peer-to-peer development, and enters a relationship requiring an explicit answer from Kei.

## Y1FF — model authorship becomes the epistemic problem
His own official file proves that a usable model can be radically incomplete. The ethical question becomes whether the strategist who exceeds other people's models can accept that other people also exceed his.

The completed progression is not simply “Ayanokōji manipulates more people.”

It is:

> **self-concealment → environmental authorship → relational authorship → institutional contestation → developmental authorship → the problem of reciprocal and epistemically humble authorship.**

The crucial distinction for downstream analysis is:

> **self-authorship is not the same as legitimate authorship over others.**

A self-chosen developmental project may be psychologically liberating for Ayanokōji while remaining ethically dangerous for the people inside it.

# 21. Horikita's architectural progression

Horikita's Year 1 development is best understood as movement from **scalar judgment** toward **corrigible generative leadership**.

## V1 — isolated competence

She begins by treating current weakness as strong evidence of disposability and sees leadership largely through superior individual capacity.

## V2 — public operational responsibility

She must work inside an institutional dispute where truth, credibility, and representation matter.

## V3 — interdependence

The island exam makes personal excellence insufficient. Other people's contextual capacities become necessary.

## V5 — relational adaptation

Her partnership with Sudō demonstrates that stronger performance may require adapting one's expression to another person rather than demanding that the weaker participant simply catch up.

## V6 — integration as governing presumption

Her disagreement with Ayanokōji over difficult classmates becomes political. She increasingly prefers attempting integration before removal even when risk remains real.

## V8 — autonomous strategic disagreement

She can disagree with Ayanokōji's preferred path rather than serving merely as his public interface.

## V10 — accountable public judgment

The Class Vote forces her to make a consequential judgment in public and accept responsibility for the decision rather than hiding behind an invisible sovereign.

## V11 — distributed operational leadership

She maps, trains, allocates, and coordinates capacities Ayanokōji admits he cannot manage in the same class-wide way.

Her strength becomes generative.

## V11.5 — integrated self-authorship

The Manabu relationship closes without requiring either imitation or purification.

The years of imitation remain `紛れもない私`, but they no longer dictate the future self.

She accepts influence as history rather than command.

Her class leadership also becomes more explicitly corrigible. Keisei's promised dissent, the Kushida jurisdiction decision, and the academic wager with Ayanokōji all place her inside structures where disagreement can remain legitimate.

## Y1FF — the file becomes an archival photograph rather than a verdict

The guidebook's low cooperation rating preserves what early Horikita looked like to a standardized evaluator.

The completed year demonstrates why the rating cannot be treated as essence.

Her mature value is not that she eventually acquires a perfect file on everyone.

It is that she becomes **more correctable when the file is wrong or incomplete**.

The Year 1 endpoint is therefore not “Horikita becomes kind.”

She remains willing to judge.

The development lies in making judgment increasingly:

- public;
- information-seeking;
- revisable;
- distributed;
- accountable;
- and open to capacities that current metrics or first impressions understate.

# 22. The four first-year class polities

The completed Year 1 corpus supports four stable polity identifiers whose constitutional logic matters more than their changing class letters.

## 22.1 `POLITY-HORIKITA`
### Developmental pluralism with increasing internal accountability

Core strengths:

- willingness to develop heterogeneous members;
- increasing use of specialized capacities;
- public responsibility for judgment;
- growing tolerance for dissent;
- increasingly generative rather than purely personal leadership.

Core risks:

- dependence on hidden Ayanokōji information and intervention;
- incomplete knowledge of latent capacity;
- unresolved difficulty integrating high-risk actors such as Kushida;
- danger that public constitutional growth rests on an unseen private sovereign.

Year-end correction:

The class's most important development is not point rank. It is that more of its operating intelligence is becoming distributed and contestable.

## 22.2 `POLITY-RYUEN`
### Authoritarian mobilization with increasingly voluntary loyalty

Core strengths:

- rapid centralized decision-making;
- strong modeling of incentives, fear, and weakness;
- tactical adaptability;
- high capacity to impose coordinated action.

Core risks:

- coercion suppresses corrective feedback;
- class competence can become overly dependent on the ruler;
- psychological accuracy becomes a temptation to treat understanding as authorization;
- ethical conversion does not automatically follow from defeat or renewed loyalty.

Year-end correction:

Followers increasingly choose Ryūen after his defeat, which raises the legitimacy of his authority sociologically. It does **not** prove moral reform. `勝つための悪逆` remains part of the local record.

## 22.3 `POLITY-ICHINOSE`
### Solidaristic legitimacy under adversarial pressure

Core strengths:

- extraordinary trust;
- social transparency;
- willingness to bear costs for members;
- high internal legitimacy;
- strong relational infrastructure.

Core risks:

- reciprocal norms are vulnerable to nonreciprocal adversaries;
- solidarity does not automatically convert into exam advantage;
- self-blame can distort causal analysis;
- defense mechanisms lag behind social cohesion.

Year-end correction:

The problem is not that kindness is unreal or politically worthless. *First File*'s strong social framing supports the opposite. The challenge is adding adversarial resilience **without destroying the trust that constitutes the polity's real strength**.

## 22.4 `POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN`
### Aristocratic optimization with centralized sovereignty and fiduciary risk

Core strengths:

- sophisticated talent recognition;
- role allocation;
- strong strategic intelligence;
- effective use of elite specialists.

Core risks:

- knowledge remains concentrated near the sovereign;
- succession and independent model-building are weak points;
- private rivalry can impose class-wide costs not chosen by the polity;
- the more accurate the sovereign's talent map becomes, the easier it is to mistake the map for entitlement to allocate persons.

## 22.5 The four-polity comparison

The year-end point totals remain real institutional outcomes:

- Sakayanagi polity: **1131**;
- Ichinose polity: **550**;
- Ryūen polity: **508**;
- Horikita polity: **347**.

They do not constitute a complete ranking of constitutional quality.

Year 1 instead leaves four different questions:

- Can developmental pluralism escape dependence on a hidden sovereign?
- Can authoritarian coordination generate legitimacy without reproducing coercive domination?
- Can solidaristic legitimacy acquire adversarial security without destroying itself?
- Can aristocratic optimization decentralize knowledge and survive the sovereign's private priorities?

These are the proper handoff questions for `Y1_05`, not “which class is objectively best?”

# 23. The school as changing political actor

## V1 — concealed evaluation

The institution knows the behavioral criteria before the students do.

## V2 — adjudication

## V3 — designed ecology

The school constructs environments in which contextual capacities become newly valuable.

## V4 — incentivized betrayal

The examination makes trust strategically extractable.

## V5 — public metricization

Bodies and performance become publicly comparable.

## V6 — engineered interdependence

Rules force students to expose academic weakness and rely on one another.

## V8 — institutional authorship

Nagumo shows that power over examination design may dominate excellence within the examination.

## V9 — information governance

The institution becomes a possible regulator of an engineered rumor environment.

## V10 — manufactured disposability

The school deliberately creates a situation in which continuation becomes scarce and students must participate in judgments about who remains.

## V11 — record sovereignty

Tsukishiro demonstrates that communication infrastructure and official records can be captured.

## V11.5 — internal constitutional conflict

The school is no longer analytically reducible to one will. Chairman Sakayanagi, Mashima, Chabashira, Tsukishiro, teachers, and student-council authority occupy different positions. Institutional integrity can depend on internal checks and adult courage.

## Y1FF — institutional legibility made visible as archive

The guidebook literalizes the school's classificatory imagination through ratings, timelines, point totals, and hypothetical OAA modeling.

This makes the final institution question more precise:

> **What is the legitimate scope of an institution's model of a person?**

The year does not answer that measurement is inherently illegitimate.

It shows that measurement becomes dangerous when bounded information is converted into total judgment, especially when that judgment controls continuation, opportunity, or identity.

# 25. Ordinary life as a rival theory of education

The White Room question is not exhausted by pedagogy.

Year 1 repeatedly suggests that ordinary life itself teaches capacities and values excluded or radically underprovided by an optimized developmental regime.

Ayanokōji seeks or encounters:

- friendship;
- ordinary conversation;
- romance;
- entertainment;
- food preferences;
- seasonal experience;
- voluntary companionship;
- names and nicknames;
- unassigned time;
- separation and grief;
- awkwardness;
- and relationships whose value is not fixed by an external objective.

These experiences sometimes produce strategic insight.

Reducing them to that function would repeat the instrumental logic they challenge.

Their deeper significance is that they can become **ends rather than merely means**.

V11.5 makes this explicit by giving Ayanokōji the language of self-chosen goal.

*First File* then makes the positive content concrete. He finds irrelevant conversation interesting. He is bad at a mundane social transition. He wants to see Kei.

Ordinary life is therefore not best described as a cure whose success has already been proven.

It is a **counter-curriculum**: an educational environment whose values cannot be fully specified in advance by the institution or strategist.

This creates one of the year’s strongest ethical tests:

> **Does the other person get to decide what development is for?**

And *First File* adds:

> **Can the observer accept that the other's value may exceed the categories through which the observer understands them?**

# 26. Development versus revelation

The completed Year 1 architecture requires strict separation of two processes.

## 26.1 Development

The person genuinely changes.

Examples include:

- Sudō's increasing discipline and socially usable capacity;
- Horikita's movement toward distributed, accountable leadership;
- Airi's increasing social participation;
- Ike's late-year decision to improve after seeing Sudō;
- the increasing voluntariness surrounding Ryūen's authority;
- Hirata's crisis and partial reintegration;
- Ayanokōji's growing ability to articulate self-chosen desire, ordinary attachment, and legacy questions.

Development does not require total moral conversion.

Ryūen can develop politically without becoming ethically benign.

Ayanokōji can develop self-authorship without becoming safe as a developer of others.

## 26.2 Revelation

The reader, institution, or another character learns something that was already true.

Examples include:

- Kushida's recognition economy;
- Kei's trauma-organized survival structure;
- aspects of Ayanokōji's prior ability;
- Sakayanagi's earlier knowledge;
- Hirata's childhood history;
- Matsushita's deliberate concealment.

## 26.3 Mixed cases

Some revelations change the environment and thereby create later development.

Some ratings record genuine states that later become obsolete.

Some relationships reveal capacities while also changing the people involved.

The final Year 1 snapshot must therefore avoid two retrospective errors:

1. treating every later-revealed fact as though it was available to all earlier actors;
2. treating every difference between early and late description as proof of development when it may instead be improved knowledge.

*First File* makes this especially important because retrospective summaries naturally compress the road into the destination.

The specialist corpus must restore the road.

# 27. The governing Year 1 tension at the final boundary

The completed Year 1 question is:

> **Can human beings be developed without being possessed—conceptually, relationally, politically, or institutionally—by the person or system capable of developing them?**

The novels repeatedly refuse simplistic answers.

Ayanokōji's understanding can save people.

It can also justify invasion.

Horikita's public accountability can make judgment contestable.

It can also expose classmates to consequential decisions they did not authorize.

Ichinose's trust can produce real solidarity.

It can also create exploitable expectations of reciprocity.

Ryūen's psychological insight can produce extraordinary coordination.

It can also turn accurate models of fear and desire into coercive jurisdiction.

Sakayanagi's elite judgment can identify real differences.

It can also convert classmates into pieces inside a sovereign's private theory.

The school's examinations can create environments in which latent ability appears.

They can also manufacture crises that force students to classify other people as liabilities.

*First File* can preserve useful information about the entire year.

It can also tempt the reader to confuse the retrievable file with the lived person.

The year therefore does not oppose merit and kindness, genius and ordinary people, manipulation and sincerity, or individualism and teamwork as clean binaries.

Its harder problem is **jurisdiction**.

> **Even when someone understands something true about another person's capacities, what gives the observer the right to decide the other's environment, relationships, risks, identity, or future?**

V11.5 adds reciprocity to that question.

*First File* adds epistemic humility.

The final Year 1 answer is not a rule but a constraint:

> **understanding should remain corrigible, and development should preserve the developed person's authorship.**

# 28. What Year 1 does not settle

The final evidence lock closes ingestion, not interpretation of later outcomes.

The following questions must remain open at the Year 1 boundary.

## 28.1 Ayanokōji's four-class developmental thought

He imagines developing students across the four classes and is excited by the possibility.

Year 1 does not establish whether this becomes a durable operational program, what form it takes, or whether he can accept outcomes he did not design.

## 28.2 Ordinary life and return to the White Room

Ayanokōji treats ordinary school life as self-chosen and currently expects eventual return to the White Room.

Year 1 does not establish whether that expectation is accurate or what later circumstances do to it.

## 28.3 Kei and irreplaceability

The romance is explicit and reciprocal.

The developmental asymmetry is also real.

Year 1 does not prove that Kei has already become `掛け替えのない存在` in the stable sense Ayanokōji imagines.

## 28.4 Horikita and Kushida

Ayanokōji yields jurisdiction over the problem to Horikita.

Year 1 does not establish whether integration succeeds, what it costs, or whether later circumstances force removal.

## 28.5 Ichinose's one-year horizon

Ayanokōji promises a future meeting and retains `介錯` language if her class collapses.

Ichinose's bonus fiction preserves independent class agency.

Year 1 does not establish what the future meeting means or where the relationship goes.

## 28.6 Ryūen's legitimacy

His authority has acquired more voluntary support.

Year 1 does not establish durable ethical conversion or whether voluntary loyalty can correct coercive governance.

## 28.7 Sakayanagi's polity and succession

Her class remains extraordinarily strong and highly centralized.

Year 1 does not establish whether knowledge decentralizes or how private rivalry affects long-term fiduciary responsibility.

## 28.8 Tsukishiro and White Room testimony

Year 1 establishes that Tsukishiro can interfere with school procedure and offers detailed White Room claims.

Where the volume itself withholds verification, those claims remain testimony rather than settled world fact.

## 28.9 Institutional checks

V11.5 shows adults capable of resistance to capture.

Year 1 does not establish how durable or effective those checks are.

## 28.10 The limits of Ayanokōji's person-models

*First File* gives the project a strong reason to reject totalizing files.

Year 1 does not yet establish whether Ayanokōji himself can consistently accept the incompleteness of his models of other people.

These questions are **handoff questions**, not invitations to import later answers into this document.

# 30. Canonical Year 1 synthesis

Year 1 transforms a seemingly simple school-meritocracy premise into a theory of human, relational, and institutional authorship.

The school begins by saying:

> your conduct has value even when you do not know it is being measured.

The year then discovers that measurement alone cannot solve the problem of ability.

A person can possess ability without expressing it.

A person can express ability without being believed.

A person can be believed without being useful in the current environment.

A person can be useful without being integrated.

A person can be integrated through coercion.

A person can grow inside an environment another person designed.

A person can exceed the designer's script.

A person can be protected by someone who simultaneously constrains autonomy.

A person can be socially imprisoned by a true fact made total.

A person can lose the right to continue because an institution manufactures scarcity.

A person can perform better and still lose if someone else owns the record.

A person can freely choose to become a developer and still overreach the people being developed.

A person's official file can contain true information and still fail as an account of the person as a whole.

The series therefore insists on real differences while refusing to let difference become destiny.

Ayanokōji stands at the center because he embodies both the promise and danger of developmental knowledge.

He can recognize contextual strengths that institutions miss.

He can preserve people a static model might discard.

He can create conditions in which others discover capacities.

He can restore future orientation.

He can also withhold information, manufacture dependency, engineer emotional pressure, allocate protection privately, override boundaries, and treat development itself as something he can design.

His Year 1 freedom is real.

That is why his danger is real.

If he were merely executing White Room programming, the ethical problem would be external control.

By V11.5 he is beginning to choose developmental authorship for himself.

The question becomes what a free Ayanokōji chooses to do with other people's freedom.

Horikita provides the year’s strongest internal counter-movement.

She begins with a narrow model:

- current weakness strongly implies low value;
- superior ability authorizes judgment;
- dependence is failure;
- and the ideal self is another person's image.

She ends Year 1 without abandoning judgment, standards, or ambition.

Instead she becomes more capable of making judgment:

- information-seeking;
- public;
- revisable;
- distributed;
- corrigible;
- and open to development whose endpoint she does not fully know in advance.

Her relationship with Manabu ends not by erasing his influence but by converting influence into history she can author onward.

That difference is crucial.

The four class polities generalize the same question politically.

Ryūen asks what happens when accurate models of people become coercive power.

Ichinose asks whether trust can remain politically viable under adversarial conditions.

Sakayanagi asks whether elite judgment and centralized talent allocation can avoid sovereign overreach and succession weakness.

Horikita asks whether heterogeneous ability can be developed and coordinated without requiring the leader to possess or perfectly model every strength.

The school then generalizes the problem institutionally.

Its examinations can reveal and develop capacities.

Its point systems can record useful differences.

Its rules can create collective challenges no individual would encounter alone.

The same institution can hide criteria, incentivize betrayal, manufacture disposability, and permit the official record to become a site of capture.

V11.5 complicates the picture further by showing internal actors willing to resist capture.

*First File* then literalizes the classificatory impulse.

The archive is valuable because it makes Year 1 retrievable.

It is dangerous only when retrieval becomes ontology.

The final Year 1 lesson is therefore not that people should never be measured, developed, judged, protected, or led.

It is that all such acts require limits.

> **A measurement should know what it measured.**
>
> **A leader should remain correctable by the people and information the leader does not possess.**
>
> **A developer should distinguish creating conditions from owning outcomes.**
>
> **A protector should distinguish care from jurisdiction.**
>
> **A strategist should remember that an accurate model is still not the whole person.**

The year’s mature theory of `実力` follows from those limits.

Real ability is not only the capacity to win.

It includes the capacity to develop, express, recognize, coordinate, and truthfully preserve human difference without pretending that a current score, role, weakness, or psychological model determines the whole future person.

The final architectural proposition is therefore:

> **The central problem of Year 1 is not who is strongest inside a system. It is who authors visibility, environments, relationships, development, and records—and whether that authorship remains accountable to people who exceed every model made of them.**

That proposition is now frozen as the canonical Year 1 architectural boundary.

# 31. Downstream constraints for the specialist corpus

Because this document is the canonical `Y1_01` snapshot, later Year 1 specialist documents should inherit the following constraints unless they explicitly demonstrate a source-grounded reason to refine them.

1. **Ordinary life is real self-authorship.** It cannot be reduced to camouflage merely because strategic cognition persists.
2. **Self-authorship and reciprocal authorship are distinct.** A self-chosen developmental project can still violate another person's autonomy.
3. **Horikita's endpoint is integration, not purification.** Manabu's influence becomes authored history rather than something that must be erased.
4. **Kei's Year 1 relationship is explicitly reciprocal romance with unresolved developmental asymmetry and irreplaceability.** Neither “only a tool” nor “completed humanization” is adequate.
5. **Ichinose's solidarity is genuine capacity.** Her year-end problem includes adversarial security and result conversion rather than a simple defect of kindness.
6. **Ryūen's increased voluntary support is not identical to ethical reform.**
7. **Sakayanagi's elite allocation is effective and constitutionally risky.** Private priority and centralized knowledge must remain visible.
8. **`実力` must distinguish possession, development, expression, legibility, coordination, recording, and model limitation.**
9. **Institutional measurement can be accurate about a bounded object without exhausting the person.**
10. **ANHS must not be treated as one undifferentiated actor.** Internal institutional conflict and oversight matter.
11. **Tsukishiro's detailed White Room statements remain epistemically tiered where Year 1 itself withholds verification.**
12. **`OAA_LEGIBILITY` enters Year 1 only through *First File* as explicitly retrospective/counterfactual paratext.**
13. **No Year 2, Volume 0, *Second List*, Year 3, adaptation, wiki, or later-franchise answer may silently settle a Year 1 boundary question.**

These are synthesis guardrails, not conclusions that later specialist documents must repeat verbatim.

Their purpose is to prevent downstream analysis from collapsing distinctions already secured by the final evidence lock.
