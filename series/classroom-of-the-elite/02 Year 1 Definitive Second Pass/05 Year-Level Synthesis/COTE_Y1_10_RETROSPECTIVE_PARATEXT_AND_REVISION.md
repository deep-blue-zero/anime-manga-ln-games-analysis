# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ』
## Year 1 — Retrospective Paratext and Revision
### What the second pass preserves, strengthens, revises, downgrades, rejects, and leaves open after *First File*

> **CANONICAL YEAR-1 REVISION AUTHORITY**
>
> This document is the primary analytical home for the transition from the historical first-pass Year 1 reading to the Japanese-primary V2 corpus, for the evidentiary role of *First File*, and for routing important Year 1 claims through `PRESERVE`, `STRENGTHEN`, `REVISE`, `DOWNGRADE`, `REJECT`, and `OPEN`.
>
> It is **not** a substitute for the source-local volume readings, the *First File* audit, or the specialist documents `Y1_01–Y1_09`. Its function is to preserve the history of interpretation: what earlier readings got right, what the second pass made more precise, what later Year 1 evidence actually changed, and which questions remain genuinely unresolved at the boundary.
>
> It does **not** use Year 2, Volume 0, *Second List*, Year 3, adaptation material, wikis, or later-franchise knowledge to settle a Year 1 claim.

# 0. Scope, responsibility, and revision discipline

A retrospective document can easily become methodologically destructive.

Once the analyst knows the endpoint, there is a temptation to rewrite every earlier ambiguity as if the later answer had always been obvious. Once an official guidebook supplies ratings and summaries, there is a temptation to treat the official file as a superior narrator. Once a second pass is more rigorous than the first, there is a temptation to describe the first pass only as error.

This document rejects all three temptations.

The governing problem is:

> **How can a mature corpus correct itself without destroying the historical states through which its understanding developed?**

Six revision states are used.

## 0.1 `PRESERVE`

The earlier claim survives materially intact. New evidence may add detail, but the proposition itself remains a current Year 1 authority.

`PRESERVE` does not mean the first wording was perfect. It means the conceptual result remains sound.

## 0.2 `STRENGTHEN`

The earlier claim was directionally correct, and later Year 1 evidence makes it more secure, more explicit, or more important than the first pass could responsibly state.

## 0.3 `REVISE`

The earlier claim captured part of the phenomenon but used a model too simple for the complete Year 1 record. The current formulation retains what was true while changing the explanatory structure.

## 0.4 `DOWNGRADE`

The earlier proposition remains possible or locally useful, but the completed Year 1 corpus gives it less explanatory authority than it previously held. A downgraded claim must not continue functioning as the default interpretation.

## 0.5 `REJECT`

The completed Year 1 record contradicts the claim, or shows that the claim exceeded the authority of its evidence. Rejection applies to the proposition, not to the historical artifact in which the proposition appeared.

## 0.6 `OPEN`

The Year 1 corpus does not settle the issue. Later-year evidence may eventually answer it, but later knowledge cannot be imported backward into this snapshot.

These states describe **claim transitions**, not grades assigned to documents or authors.

The historical first pass remains valuable because it records what could be seen before the V2 infrastructure existed. The second pass is better not because it knows later franchise answers, but because it reads the same Year 1 boundary with stricter Japanese-primary sourcing, deterministic evidence, longitudinal ledgers, visual analysis, explicit authority tiers, and adversarial correction.

# 1. Executive thesis — the second pass refines more than it overturns

The strongest result of the revision audit is that the historical first pass was **substantially right at the macro level and insufficiently precise at several decisive micro and mid-level questions**.

The durable first-pass insight was:

> **Year 1 is about the conflict between human development and human control.**

That proposition survives.

The V2 corpus makes it more exact.

Year 1 repeatedly presents people and institutions capable of seeing something true about another person:

- Horikita correctly sees Sudō's academic weakness;
- Ayanokōji correctly sees Kei's dependency structure;
- Sakayanagi correctly sees Ichinose's concealed wrongdoing;
- Ryūen correctly sees fear as a political resource;
- Nagumo correctly sees that the class system can trap high-performing individuals;
- teachers correctly see behaviors their students do not yet interpret as evaluative data;
- *First File* correctly records many visible performances and retrospective states.

The ethical problem begins when truth about another person becomes a claim of jurisdiction over that person's future.

The V2 Year 1 thesis is therefore not a repudiation of the first pass. It is its maturation:

> **Year 1 increasingly treats development as an authorship problem: seeing and cultivating real capacities never by itself grants the right to decide another person's purpose. Its endpoint therefore joins reciprocal self-determination to an epistemic limit—every score, strategy, record, and psychological model must remain smaller than the person it describes.**

The principal revisions occur around five axes.

First, **Ayanokōji's ordinary life becomes positive rather than merely negative**. It is not only escape from the White Room. By V11.5 and *First File*, he can name student life as a goal he chose and ordinary conversation as intrinsically interesting (`Y1V11.5-E084–E089`, `Y1FF-E059–E073`).

Second, **Horikita's independence becomes integration rather than purification**. Her Manabu-shaped self is not discarded as unreal. She incorporates that history into a self she can now author (`Y1V11.5-E090–E095`).

Third, **Kei becomes impossible to reduce either to “tool” or “proof of completed humanization.”** Mutual romance is explicit, while the hidden curriculum, dependency history, `教科書`, and hoped-for `掛け替えのない存在` remain active (`Y1V11.5-E124–E140`, `Y1FF-E062–E072`).

Fourth, **ability becomes a chain of conversion rather than a hidden scalar essence**. Possession, development, expression, legibility, ecological fit, social usability, distribution, generation, political power, recording, and model limitation must be distinguished. *First File* intensifies this argument because its measurements are useful precisely when treated as bounded objects (`Y1FF-E004–E007`, `Y1FF-E030–E048`).

Fifth, **the White Room and ANHS comparison becomes more discriminating**. Year 1 supports structural comparison—authored environments, measurement, scarcity, control, developmental purpose—but rejects both simple equivalence and simple opposition. ANHS contains plural authorities, ordinary-life surplus, contestation, dissent, and possible oversight; Tsukishiro demonstrates how vulnerable those protections are to capture (`Y1V07-E022–E041`, `Y1V10-E109–E111`, `Y1V11-E138–E145`, `Y1V11.5-E017–E024`).

The second pass therefore does not replace one Year 1 with another.

It converts a strong thematic reading into an evidence-routed historical corpus.

The historical first-pass material survives in two useful forms.

Its exporter metadata, however, warns that it did not prove absolute completeness of the conversation boundary. It is therefore **historical provenance**, not a canonical evidentiary artifact.

The second is the handoff text:

`Pasted text.txt`

That file is especially valuable because it states what the first-pass Year 1 corpus believed important enough to carry into Year 2. It preserves not only conclusions, but **open questions**. In archival terms, it is a handoff snapshot of the first-pass model.

Its major inherited commitments included:

- the development-versus-control thesis;
- Ayanokōji as a contested rather than solved mastermind;
- ordinary life as genuine rather than pure camouflage;
- Kei as a relationship mixing attraction, protection, dependency, utility, and experimentation;
- Horikita as an emerging independent leader rather than a stable puppet;
- Kushida as both genuine social capacity and genuine internal threat;
- Ichinose's solidarity as politically meaningful but vulnerable;
- Ryūen's post-defeat rule as potentially more voluntarily legitimate without established moral reform;
- Sakayanagi's natural-genius ideology and class-duty conflict;
- Nagumo's individualist meritocracy as possible mobility and possible patronage;
- the four class constitutional models;
- and the central question of whether Ayanokōji can cultivate people without reproducing the White Room.

These are not trivial anticipations. A large proportion of the mature V2 architecture is already latent in them.

What the first pass lacked was the current ability to say, with controlled authority:

- which wording was direct Japanese textual fact;
- which was strong inference;
- which was a provisional synthesis;
- where illustrations changed the reading;
- where a guidebook rating contradicted its own editorial prose;
- where the same Japanese term changed meaning over time;
- and where a later Year 1 source strengthened rather than merely repeated an earlier claim.

The revision project therefore treats the first pass as **historical_legacy**, not as something to delete.

A mature corpus should be able to answer both:

> What do we think now?

and:

> What did we think then, and why did the transition occur?

# 3. The Year 1 authority stack

Revision requires a hierarchy of source authority, but the hierarchy is **question-relative** rather than absolute.

## 3.1 Japanese novel scene evidence

The fourteen Japanese novels are strongest for:

- what occurs;
- what a viewpoint character experiences;
- dialogue and register;
- causal uncertainty;
- emotional contradiction;
- the order in which knowledge becomes available;
- and development unfolding through time.

They remain the governing literary evidence.

## 3.2 Canonical illustrations

Illustrations establish visual facts and editorial emphasis:

- bodily distance;
- visible injury;
- pose;
- clothing;
- embodied hierarchy;
- which scene receives visual memorialization.

They do not independently settle hidden motive. V11.5's final Ayanokōji–Kei image, for example, strengthens the romantic presentation while the adjacent prose explicitly preserves uncertainty (`Y1V11.5-E139–E151`).

## 3.3 Final evidence lock and specialist corpus

The final lock does not become a new primary source. It determines which Year 1 claims have successfully survived reconciliation and which evidence set supports them.

`Y1_01–Y1_09` then provide canonical topical homes so that revision does not become repetition.

## 3.4 Historical first-pass artifacts

The transcript and handoff are authoritative only for **what the earlier analytical state claimed**. They cannot defeat a later source-grounded correction.

This distinction is essential. A historical claim can be faithfully preserved and currently rejected at the same time.

# 4. First File is four source types, not one narrator

*First File* is most useful when the corpus refuses to treat “official guidebook” as a single epistemic category.

The audit separates four layers.

## 4.1 `FF-DATA`

This includes calendars, school-system records, point standings, and other documentary claims.

It is strong evidence for what the official archive records.

It is not automatically strong evidence for hidden motive or total personal capacity.

For example, the year-end class-point table can establish official standing (`Y1FF-E058`). It cannot establish which class constitution is ethically or developmentally superior.

## 4.2 `FF-RATING`

Ratings are institutional measurements or retrospective simulations of such measurements.

The July profiles make visible what the guidebook considers relevant axes (`Y1FF-E004`, `Y1FF-E012`, `Y1FF-E015`, `Y1FF-E030`, `Y1FF-E038`). The hypothetical OAA then turns Year 1 into a counterfactual measurable object (`Y1FF-E041–E048`).

The key wording is that the OAA exercise is hypothetical. It asks, in effect, what the later system would have shown if applied to an earlier state.

That makes the ratings valuable.

It also prevents them from becoming secret Year 1 infrastructure.

## 4.3 `FF-EDITORIAL`

Editorial summaries can corroborate and compress.

They tell us, for example, that Ayanokōji's visible 50-point behavior affected how the file rated him (`Y1FF-E005`), that Horikita's initial cooperation deficit was central to her placement (`Y1FF-E013–E014`), that Ichinose's class combines strong cohesion with insufficient result conversion (`Y1FF-E036–E037`), and that Sakayanagi's class combines talent and allocation with strategy monopolization and incomplete unity (`Y1FF-E039–E040`).

These are official framings.

They are not omniscient replacements for scenes.

A retrospective paragraph may remove ambiguity because compression is its job. The novel's unresolved causal texture remains analytically prior when the question is precisely about that ambiguity.

## 4.4 `FF-FICTION`

The two bonus stories have a different status.

They are canonical narrative fiction with interior access.

Their strongest contributions are therefore not numerical. They concern:

- Ayanokōji's anticipation of school returning;
- student-like life as a personally fulfilling goal;
- willingness to enter romance without optimizing every response;
- enjoyment of strategically irrelevant conversation;
- direct desire to see Kei;
- Ichinose's warm attachment to Ayanokōji;
- her inability to name the next relational category;
- and her deliberate return to class competition despite that attachment (`Y1FF-E059–E082`).

This is why *First File* cannot be summarized as “the ratings book.”

Its own final fiction demonstrates the limits of its documentary forms.

# 5. The governing paratext principle — the file is real information, not total personhood

The guidebook's deepest contribution is epistemic rather than encyclopedic.

Ayanokōji is the clearest case.

The rating can accurately record:

- the scores he produced;
- the degree of cooperation he displayed;
- the physical output available to ordinary observers;
- and the communication behavior the school saw.

The same guidebook can then explain that his academic mediocrity was partly authored by deliberate 50-point scoring (`Y1FF-E004–E007`).

Nothing requires choosing between:

> the file is false

and:

> the file reveals the true Ayanokōji.

The better statement is:

> **The file accurately measures a bounded, partially authored object.**

That principle generalizes.

Horikita's early cooperation E records a real initial state. It does not become a permanent identity after a year of leadership development (`Y1FF-E012–E014`).

Kushida's cooperation A records genuine social function. It does not certify moral trustworthiness (`Y1FF-E015` and the Year 1 Kushida record).

Hiyori's judgment E sits beside editorial recognition of perceptive inference and hard class-protective decisions (`Y1FF-E030–E032`).

Kōenji's capacities can be real while remaining unavailable to the class because he refuses requisition (`Y1V11-E071–E088`).

Sakayanagi's physical score can record bodily limitation while becoming politically misleading if converted into a claim about leadership or total merit (`Y1FF-E038–E048`).

The guidebook therefore strengthens rather than dissolves the V2 distinction among:

> possession → development → expression → legibility → ecological fit → social usability → distribution → generation → political power → recording → model limitation.

A measurement can be valid at one link and invalid as a total theory.

# 6. Retrospective compression is not the same as retrospective revelation

Three operations must remain separate.

## 6.1 Revelation

A revelation supplies information that was previously unavailable or materially uncertain.

Within Year 1, Ayanokōji's controlled displays, Kei's concealed trauma, Sakayanagi's past knowledge, and Matsushita's deliberate underperformance all increase what the reader can know about already-existing conditions.

## 6.2 Development

Development changes the person or relationship across time.

Sudō's discipline, Horikita's leadership, Hirata's crisis recovery, Ryūen's altered follower relation, Ayanokōji's ordinary-life experience, and the Ayanokōji–Kei relationship all include real development.

They cannot be rewritten as “the person was always secretly this way.”

## 6.3 Retrospective compression

A guidebook summary takes an already narrated sequence and reorganizes it into a short official description.

The Activity Report does this across the first year (`Y1FF-E051–E055`).

Compression can reveal what the publication chooses to foreground. It can also remove:

- hesitation;
- contradictory motives;
- failed alternatives;
- temporal sequence;
- and epistemic uncertainty.

Therefore:

> **A retrospective summary may tell us how the completed year is officially packaged without proving that the summary exhausts how the year was narratively constructed.**

This distinction will become even more important in later-year retrospective work, but `Y1_10` freezes it here without importing later sources.

# 7. The first-pass/V2 relation — correction without humiliation

The historical first pass should not be caricatured as an adaptation-shaped misreading that V2 had to rescue.

Its strongest propositions remain visible in the current corpus.

It already recognized that:

- ANHS is a social-engineering institution rather than merely a setting for clever tests;
- Ayanokōji's competence is developmentally abnormal;
- Horikita's arc concerns leadership rather than simply “becoming nicer”;
- Kushida's public identity is a form of real social power;
- Hirata's care is both virtue and institutional burden;
- Ichinose tries to turn altruism into political leadership;
- Kei's bond with Ayanokōji cannot be separated from trauma and dependency;
- and Ayanokōji increasingly treats people and environments developmentally.

The Japanese V2 pass adds three kinds of resolution.

First, **linguistic resolution**. The Japanese makes Ayanokōji's casual `オレ`, Horikita's controlled feminine judgment, Kushida's register split, address terms, and recurring concepts such as `実力`, `自由`, `信頼`, `不良品`, and `成長` more traceable.

Second, **evidentiary resolution**. Claims now route to volume-local IDs and deterministic locators rather than remaining conversational conclusions.

Third, **revision resolution**. The corpus can say that an older claim was right in scope but wrong in mechanism, or right at one boundary but not another.

That is the purpose of the six-state vocabulary.

# 8. The final Year 1 revision sequence

The second pass can be described as a chain of increasingly precise corrections to the first-pass model.

The historical reading saw:

> development versus control.

The V2 volume sequence specifies the changing medium of that conflict:

> **1. authored visibility; 2. authored legibility; 3. authored environment; 4. authored dependency; 5. counter-curriculum; 6. controlled exposure; 7. distributed integration under hidden contingency; 8. sovereignty over futurity; 9. negotiated indispensability; 10. institutional authorship; 11. authored reputation; 12. manufactured disposability; 13. developmental authorship under procedural capture; 14. reciprocal authorship; 15. the file and the person.**

The last two stages matter most to this document.

`reciprocal authorship` corrects the assumption that liberation is complete once Ayanokōji begins choosing purposes for himself.

`the file and the person` corrects the assumption that superior knowledge—whether institutional or psychological—can ever be allowed to become total jurisdiction.

The revision architecture is therefore not merely:

> old claim → new claim.

It is:

> **historical claim → source-local test → later Year 1 pressure → paratextual audit → final boundary formulation → explicit remaining uncertainty.**

# 9. Governing Year 1 thesis — `PRESERVE`, then `REVISE` toward authorship

## 9.1 Historical claim

The first-pass governing statement was:

> **Year 1 is about the conflict between human development and human control.**

## 9.2 Revision state: `PRESERVE`

The claim survives because every major Year 1 layer continues to instantiate it.

The school develops students through concealed rules, scarcity, collective consequence, examinations, rankings, and the threat of removal (`Y1V01-E005–E013`, `Y1V08-E128–E142`, `Y1V10-E013–E019`).

Horikita develops classmates by moving from static exclusion toward differentiated use and training (`Y1V01-E014`, `Y1V01-E018–E020`, `Y1V05-E017–E019`, `Y1V05-E047–E064`, `Y1V11-E071–E088`).

Ayanokōji repeatedly turns accurate perception into environmental intervention: Sudō's survival, Horikita's island condition, Kei's dependency, Ichinose's confession, Hirata's recovery, and class-wide contingency management are not separate moral problems. They are variants of developmental authorship.

## 9.3 Current formulation: `REVISE`

The original thesis is now too symmetrical.

“Development versus control” can imply two opposed forces. Year 1 more often shows **development occurring through control**.

The mature question is therefore not whether control prevents growth. It is whether growth produced under another person's authorship becomes sufficiently self-owned that the subject can answer back.

This is why the final Year 1 thesis adds:

- authorship;
- jurisdiction;
- reciprocity;
- and model limitation.

The first pass is preserved as the conceptual seed. `Y1_01–Y1_10` give the seed a more exact political and ethical grammar.

# 10. Ayanokōji — from “hidden mastermind versus humanization” to layered self-authorship

Ayanokōji receives the largest revision because the first pass was already resisting an overly blank protagonist model, while the V2 Japanese text makes that resistance much more secure.

## 10.1 Ordinary adolescent desire

### Historical claim

Ayanokōji's interest in friendship, romance, sexuality, food, books, leisure, and ordinary student experience is genuine enough that he should not be reduced to an emotionless manipulator performing a schoolboy role.

### Revision state: `PRESERVE` and `STRENGTHEN`

The claim is present from Volume 1. His narration worries about friendship and social ranking, notices women physically, and imagines romantic participation before these experiences have any obvious strategic value (`Y1V01-E003–E010`). The `.5` volumes repeatedly add low-pressure social desire, sexual and romantic curiosity, cinema, shopping, gifts, food, snow, and private conversation (`Y1V04.5-E001–E017`, `Y1V07.5-E063–E100`). Volume 9 identifies busy social life as part of the daily life he wanted to obtain. V11.5 calls ordinary school years a self-selected goal, and *First File* describes student-like life, purposeless conversation, and direct desire to see Kei (`Y1V11.5-E084–E089`, `Y1FF-E059–E073`).

The V2 correction is therefore not “Ayanokōji became human.”

It is:

> **ordinary desire was present early and becomes increasingly integrated into a life he can positively choose.**

The stronger claim that the ordinary narrator is merely camouflage is `REJECT`.

## 10.2 Humanization

### Historical claim

Friendship, romance, rivalry, and ordinary life may humanize Ayanokōji.

### Revision state: `REVISE`

The word remains useful only if separated into several operations.

He acquires:

- new experiences;
- new attachments;
- broader emotional vocabulary;
- desires not assigned by the White Room;
- concern for particular people;
- and the wish to leave something behind.

These are genuine forms of human development.

They do not automatically produce ethical restraint.

The same person who increasingly enjoys ordinary relation still authors crises, delays rescue, retains asymmetric information, and privately treats other people as developmental media. V11.5 makes the contradiction especially clear: he can want Kei and call her a `教科書`; he can value Horikita's development and privately decide to observe what she becomes; he can seek freedom and become excited by a project of developing the four classes (`Y1V11.5-E103–E104`, `Y1V11.5-E124–E140`).

Therefore:

> **humanization as increased attachment and ordinary experience is `PRESERVE`; humanization as stable ethical reform is `DOWNGRADE` and, when stated as completion, `REJECT`.**

## 10.3 Self-authorship

### Historical claim

Ayanokōji's desire to remain at ANHS may become a positive life commitment rather than mere resistance to his father.

### Revision state: `STRENGTHEN`

V11.5 makes self-chosen purpose explicit. Manabu's legacy question refuses to prescribe the answer and asks what Ayanokōji will leave behind (`Y1V11.5-E088–E089`). Ayanokōji then imagines a developmental project involving all four classes (`Y1V11.5-E103–E104`).

This is a profound revision of the freedom thread.

His life at school is no longer only:

> not the White Room.

It is becoming:

> something he wants for its own content.

But this produces the central V2 warning:

> **self-authorship does not equal legitimate authorship of others.**

That distinction is now binding.

## 10.4 Narratorial reliability

### Historical claim

Ayanokōji is unreliable because he hides ability and motive.

### Revision state: `REVISE`

“Unreliable narrator” is too blunt if it implies constant lying.

The Japanese-primary V2 model is selective incompleteness:

- event report is often reliable;
- tactical structure may be withheld;
- competence is minimized;
- motive may be locally true but incomplete;
- metaphor expresses an active model without exhausting the person described;
- emotional meaning may become legible through behavior before he can confidently name it.

The current formulation is:

> **Ayanokōji often tells the reader true things inside an authored information environment.**

That is more precise than either “transparent narrator” or “everything is a lie.”

## 10.5 Final Ayanokōji routing

`PRESERVE`:

- strategic conditioning is real;
- ordinary desire is real;
- his ability to cultivate others is real;
- his ethical danger lies in converting understanding into jurisdiction.

`STRENGTHEN`:

- ordinary life as intrinsic value;
- self-chosen purpose;
- desire to encounter people or outcomes that exceed his model;
- legacy as an emerging concern.

`REVISE`:

- humanization → layered development without guaranteed restraint;
- hidden solver → developmental author;
- escape → positive self-authorship;
- unreliable narrator → selectively incomplete narrator.

`REJECT`:

- emotionally blank totality;
- ordinary narrator as pure camouflage;
- absence of heterosexual attraction or adolescent desire;
- self-authorship as proof of ethical liberation.

`OPEN`:

- can he accept an outcome he did not design?
- can he value a person who ceases to be useful?
- can development become reciprocal rather than proprietary?
- can another person's freedom frustrate his plan without becoming a defect he tries to repair?

# 11. Horikita — from “independence” to integrated self-authorship

Horikita's first-pass arc was broadly correct: isolated academic meritocrat becomes an emerging leader who must learn that human value cannot be reduced to a single present metric.

The second pass makes two decisive corrections.

## 11.1 Her problem was never low ability

`PRESERVE`.

Volume 1 already establishes the paradox. Horikita possesses high academic and physical capacity while making that ability socially self-defeating through contempt, poor pedagogy, and refusal of differentiated talent (`Y1V01-E014`, `Y1V01-E018–E020`). *First File* retrospectively records high academics and physical ability alongside cooperation E (`Y1FF-E012–E014`).

The point is not that the school secretly misclassified a universally superior student.

It is that **individual excellence and collective usability are distinct forms of capacity**.

## 11.2 Independence from Manabu

### Earlier model

Horikita's liberation could be described as becoming independent of Manabu's influence.

### Revision state: `REJECT` as purification; `STRENGTHEN` as integration

V11.5 explicitly refuses a pristine-self model.

Short-haired Suzune says she is no longer chasing Manabu and will find her own path (`Y1V11.5-E090–E092`). Yet she also treats both long- and short-haired selves as part of her lived history (`Y1V11.5-E093–E095`).

The correct formulation is:

> **self-authorship is the power to reinterpret formative influence, not proof that the influence never belonged to the self.**

The haircut therefore cannot be frozen as a symbolic “return to the true Horikita.” It is a bodily act of re-authorship inside continuity.

## 11.3 Horikita as Ayanokōji's puppet

### Earlier/provisional risk

Because Ayanokōji repeatedly protects the class invisibly, Horikita may be only the visible administrator of his hidden decisions.

### Revision state: `DOWNGRADE`

The shadow constitution remains real. Ayanokōji controls information and contingency systems Horikita cannot fully audit.

But the puppet model becomes increasingly unable to explain:

- Paper Shuffle strategy authored without his prior control;
- her willingness to reject the student-council route despite pressure;
- public liability during the Class Vote;
- the academic wager designed to make his concealed ability auditable;
- the ending of the Ichinose alliance;
- and her acceptance of Kushida jurisdiction despite his different preferred solution (`Y1V06` through `Y1V11.5-E100–E104`).

The current formulation is:

> **Horikita is an increasingly self-authored public leader operating inside a class whose hidden security constitution Ayanokōji still partly controls.**

## 11.4 Leadership theory

### Earlier model

Horikita becomes kinder and more cooperative.

### Revision state: `REVISE`

Kindness is part of the change, but the political development is deeper.

She becomes more capable of:

- gathering information;
- distinguishing forms of ability;
- training and delegating;
- allowing domain specialists to speak;
- making reasons public;
- accepting dissent;
- revising her model;
- and treating current weakness as potentially developmental.

This is **distributed, correctable developmental leadership**.

Her continuing danger is paternalistic confidence: the Kushida project may become another form of unilateral judgment if she exposes classmates to risks they did not authorize.

## 11.5 Final Horikita routing

`PRESERVE`: leadership rather than “becoming nice”; contextual ability; Sudō as developmental counterexample.

`STRENGTHEN`: independent judgment; public accountability; ability to generate answers Ayanokōji did not specify.

`REVISE`: independence → integrated self-authorship; leader competence → distributed/corrigible leadership.

`DOWNGRADE`: puppet thesis; static reading of her early cooperation deficit.

`REJECT`: haircut as recovery of pristine true self; freedom as absence of Manabu/Ayanokōji influence.

`OPEN`: can the class survive without the shadow constitution? Can she preserve Kushida without gambling with others' security? Can she make a consequential decision Ayanokōji actively dislikes and still sustain legitimate leadership?

# 12. Kei — from coerced dependency to reciprocal romance without retrospective purification

Kei is the relationship in which revision discipline matters most because later warmth can easily rewrite earlier coercion.

## 12.1 Coercive origin

`PRESERVE`.

Volume 4 establishes Ayanokōji's use of trauma, social isolation, manufactured threat, sexualized coercion, and exclusive protection to create a dependent relationship. Later development does not alter the moral status of those actions.

The historical first pass was right to keep dependency and survival central.

## 12.2 Kei as “only a tool”

### Earlier strong reduction

Kei remains primarily or wholly an instrument whose apparent intimacy is merely strategic.

### Revision state: `REJECT` as total explanation

The relationship changes.

The rooftop proves that Kei can act against the narrow dependency prediction by refusing betrayal under torture (`Y1V07-E055–E070`). Volume 7.5 gives negotiated naming and increasingly ordinary interpersonal contact. V11.5 contains explicit confession and direct desire to prevent her becoming another boy's girlfriend (`Y1V11.5-E124–E140`). *First File* then contrasts the old user/used world with reciprocal contact, records enjoyment of purposeless conversation, and states direct desire to see her (`Y1FF-E062–E073`).

“Tool” remains part of Ayanokōji's vocabulary and history.

It cannot remain the whole relationship.

## 12.3 Romance as completed humanization

### Opposite reduction

Because mutual confession occurs, the relationship demonstrates that Ayanokōji has conventionally fallen in love and ethically escaped instrumental relation.

### Revision state: `REJECT`

The confession is real.

So are:

- `教科書`;
- dependency-cure framing;
- information asymmetry;
- the unknown future of the relation;
- and `掛け替えのない存在` as hope rather than established fact (`Y1V11.5-E139–E140`).

The mature Year 1 formulation is:

> **explicit reciprocal romance inside an asymmetrical hidden curriculum, with genuine care and unresolved irreplaceability.**

## 12.4 Irreplaceability

`OPEN`.

Year 1 supports present particularity and desire. It does not prove permanent singularity.

This distinction matters because “irreplaceable” is stronger than:

- useful;
- loved now;
- desired now;
- girlfriend;
- protected person;
- or uniquely informative developmental partner.

The corpus therefore refuses to solve the question early.

## 12.5 Final Kei routing

`PRESERVE`: coercive origin, dependency history, genuine later agency.

`STRENGTHEN`: reciprocal contact, present desire, romantic reality.

`REVISE`: protected collaborator → romantic partner inside hidden curriculum.

`DOWNGRADE`: parasite/tool model as total person-description.

`REJECT`: instrument-only endpoint; confession as ethical absolution; later affection as retroactive consent.

`OPEN`: equality, informed reciprocity, irreplaceability, whether romance reduces or intensifies exclusive protector dependency.

# 13. Ichinose — from “too kind” to solidarity plus adversarial architecture

The first-pass risk was to describe Ichinose's central weakness as excessive kindness.

The second pass keeps the empirical vulnerabilities and changes the theory.

## 13.1 Solidarity is capacity

`PRESERVE` and `STRENGTHEN`.

Ichinose's class can pool private points, protect members, maintain trust, and preserve cohesion. *First File* explicitly records strong cohesion, collective savings, no expulsions, and insufficient result conversion (`Y1FF-E036–E037`).

The deficit is therefore not absence of strength.

It is difficulty converting one kind of strength into another under adversarial pressure.

## 13.2 Kindness as the defect

`REVISE`.

The mature formulation separates:

- solidaristic legitimacy;
- adversarial security;
- boundary enforcement;
- strategic deception resistance;
- and result conversion.

A polity can be morally and socially strong while strategically exploitable.

The lesson is not that kindness must be abandoned.

It is that trust requires defensive institutions when opponents can profit from violating it.

## 13.3 Ayanokōji and Ichinose

The first pass correctly identified the one-year promise and `介錯` as ethically unstable.

V11.5 and *First File* strengthen both sides.

Ichinose can regard Ayanokōji as trustworthy while explicitly remaining outside his alliance. Her feelings intensify, but she returns priority to class competition (`Y1FF-E074–E082`). Ayanokōji can offer genuine nonjudgmental recognition while privately retaining a terminal developmental judgment.

The relationship therefore becomes a strong case of:

> **care without political absorption on her side; care mixed with self-assigned jurisdiction on his.**

## 13.4 Final Ichinose routing

`PRESERVE`: solidarity, guilt, vulnerability to adversarial pressure, one-year horizon.

`STRENGTHEN`: class agency survives personal attachment; trust is a political resource.

`REVISE`: “too kind” → solidaristic capacity plus weak adversarial architecture/result conversion.

`REJECT`: kindness itself as proof of incompetence; romantic attachment as surrender of class purpose.

`OPEN`: can solidarity become strategically resilient without reproducing Ryūen or Sakayanagi? What authority, if any, should Ayanokōji have at the one-year boundary?

# 14. Ryūen and Hiyori — voluntary loyalty without moral conversion

## 14.1 Ryūen after defeat

The first-pass insight that Ryūen's leadership changes after fear is `PRESERVE`.

What changes is the moral interpretation.

His followers' desire to restore him demonstrates that fear never fully explains their political attachment. Ishizaki, Albert, Ibuki, and Hiyori provide evidence of loyalty, familiarity, competence recognition, and class interest beyond terror.

This strengthens **sociological legitimacy**.

It does not establish ethical reform.

Ryūen continues to accept coercion, deception, and `勝つための悪逆` as available political means.

Therefore:

> post-defeat legitimacy `STRENGTHEN`; moral rehabilitation `DOWNGRADE` or `REJECT` when stated as completed fact.

## 14.2 Hiyori

Hiyori is a major second-pass strengthening because quiet friendship and corrective loyalty receive more weight.

She identifies Ayanokōji as a reading friend and argues that school life need not consist only of enemies (`Y1V09-E031–E032`). V11.5 places her inside Ryūen's corrective network and gives her ordinary book-centered visual emphasis (`Y1V11.5-E145`). *First File* itself exposes the inadequacy of her formal judgment E by describing perceptive inference and hard class-protective judgment (`Y1FF-E030–E032`).

Hiyori therefore performs two revision functions.

She is evidence that:

1. Ayanokōji can form a meaningful low-pressure relationship without rescue or engineered dependency;
2. loyalty to a leader can include correction rather than obedience.

## 14.3 Final routing

`PRESERVE`: Ryūen's fear politics, strategic competence, post-defeat return.

`STRENGTHEN`: voluntary follower attachment; Hiyori as independent corrective actor.

`REVISE`: authoritarian mobilization now contains elements of voluntary legitimation.

`REJECT`: follower loyalty as proof Ryūen is morally reformed; Hiyori's low rating as proof of poor practical judgment.

`OPEN`: can voluntary loyalty eventually constrain the coercive constitution rather than merely support it?

# 15. Sakayanagi — from natural-genius rival to sovereign with a fiduciary problem

The first pass correctly understood Sakayanagi as both intellectual rival and aristocratic leader.

The V2 corpus expands the political cost of those roles colliding.

## 15.1 Natural genius theory

`PRESERVE`.

Sakayanagi's interest in Ayanokōji is not ordinary school rivalry. His existence bears on her theory of natural and artificial excellence.

## 15.2 Personalization of rivalry

`STRENGTHEN`.

By the end of Year 1, the relationship cannot be described solely as an abstract nature-versus-nurture test. She recognizes him as a particular opponent and person and cooperates against procedural falsification.

## 15.3 Aristocratic class model

The class's strength in talent mapping and role allocation is `PRESERVE`.

The simpler proposition “Sakayanagi's class is simply the strongest polity” is `REVISE`.

*First File* emphasizes deep talent and correct role allocation while also noting strategy monopolization and incomplete unity (`Y1FF-E038–E040`).

This creates a constitutional problem:

> the class may possess excellent pieces and a brilliant sovereign while remaining unusually dependent upon the sovereign's priorities and continued judgment.

## 15.4 Fiduciary risk

`STRENGTHEN`.

Sakayanagi's willingness to prioritize her Ayanokōji interest increasingly creates the possibility that a leader's private meaning may override the interests of the people whose futures she controls.

Year 1 does not yet resolve that risk.

It does make it visible.

## 15.5 Final routing

`PRESERVE`: natural-genius rivalry; aristocratic selection/allocation.

`STRENGTHEN`: personal attachment; class dependence on centralized authorship.

`REVISE`: strongest class → highly capable but succession- and sovereignty-dependent polity.

`OPEN`: can Sakayanagi treat Ayanokōji as a personally meaningful rival without making classmates bear the political cost of that meaning?

# 16. Kushida, Hirata, Sudō, and Matsushita — revisions to what counts as a “defect”

These characters collectively prevent Year 1 from treating a person's visible dysfunction as a stable essence.

## 16.1 Kushida — constructed public self is not unreal self

The first-pass claim that Kushida's public identity is genuine social power is `PRESERVE` and `STRENGTHEN`.

Her kindness is constructed, effortful, approval-dependent, and strategically useful.

It is also an actual ability.

She can:

- remember names;
- lower embarrassment;
- create cross-group communication;
- recruit participation;
- and convert reputation into collective access.

Her private rage does not prove that these competencies are fake.

The V2 correction is to reject a two-self hierarchy in which the cruel register is the “real Kushida” and the social register is merely false. The person contains both, and the ethical problem concerns what she does to protect the social system that supplies her `存在意義`.

*First File* makes the measurement problem visible: cooperation A can be a correct description of public social performance without becoming a certificate of loyalty or goodness (`Y1FF-E015`).

`REJECT`: high cooperation = moral trustworthiness.

`OPEN`: can public and private selves coexist once full mutual knowledge destroys the need to eliminate witnesses?

## 16.2 Hirata — kindness as both virtue and burden

The first pass understood Hirata's universal care as an institutional burden as well as a virtue. `PRESERVE`.

His breakdown demonstrates that a politics organized around preventing all hurt can become incapable of choosing under manufactured scarcity. His recovery does not require abandoning kindness. It requires accepting that care cannot guarantee a world without painful judgment.

V11.5 adds a quieter relational revision: Hirata has never experienced romantic love and fears making another particular person important after the trauma that organized his universalism. His development therefore concerns particular attachment as much as class mediation.

`REVISE`: “kind leader broken by responsibility” → person whose universal care partly protects him from the danger of singular attachment.

`OPEN`: can he choose particular people without interpreting particularity as betrayal of everyone else?

## 16.3 Sudō — one of the healthiest developmental models

Sudō is an important `STRENGTHEN` because his arc demonstrates development that increasingly becomes self-owned.

Volume 1 begins with academic failure, aggression, and a class ready to treat him as expendable. His athletic ability immediately challenges academic totalization. Later development adds study, emotional regulation, team responsibility, and the capacity to disagree with Horikita rather than merely obey her.

The key correction is:

> **a person can begin improving for another person's recognition and still convert the improvement into an internally owned future.**

This is one reason the series cannot simply condemn influence.

Influence becomes healthier when the subject can retain the benefit after the original motivator changes or refuses the desired reward.

## 16.4 Matsushita — concealment is not unique to Ayanokōji

Matsushita's V11.5 perspective is a `STRENGTHEN` of the ability/legibility thesis.

She deliberately limits visible performance, integrates dispersed evidence about the class, and investigates Ayanokōji (`Y1V11.5-E106–E123`).

Her function is not to reveal a second Ayanokōji-scale mastermind.

It is to generalize a principle:

> **self-concealed ability is a social strategy available to ordinary actors, and institutional files may describe chosen presentation rather than maximal capacity.**

This substantially weakens any reading that treats visible performance as transparent access to latent potential.

# 17. Nagumo and Kōenji — rival theories of freedom and merit

## 17.1 Nagumo

The first pass treated Nagumo's `本当の実力主義` as a competing institutional theory rather than authorial truth. `PRESERVE`.

His program identifies a real limitation of class fate: highly capable individuals may be trapped by weaker peers and inherited group position.

The V2 corpus strengthens the counterargument that his reform can become **personalized sovereignty**.

He can:

- build trust and spend it strategically;
- design examinations around preexisting political aims;
- use class-transfer liquidity and private-point networks;
- personalize student-council space;
- and define opportunity through access to his own evaluative center (`Y1V08-E128–E142`, `Y1V09-E004–E017`).

`REVISE`: “individual meritocracy versus class collectivism” → mobility project whose justice depends on whether evaluation remains institutional rather than patronal.

`OPEN`: can individual mobility exist without giving the evaluator sovereign power to decide who deserves mobility?

## 17.2 Kōenji

Kōenji is the strongest Year 1 challenge to the assumption that possessed ability belongs to the collective.

The first pass identified radical freedom and exceptional but unreliable ability. `PRESERVE`.

V11 makes the principle explicit: the fact that talent exists does not authorize Horikita or the class to requisition it; he decides whether to use it (`Y1V11-E071–E088`).

This is not a complete defense of Kōenji's behavior. His refusal can externalize costs onto classmates.

The revision is conceptual:

> **social usability is a separate category from possession because consent and motivation affect whether a capacity can legitimately become class power.**

`OPEN`: when, if ever, does belonging create a moral obligation to make private ability available to others?

# 19. `実力` — from five-stage first-pass model to eleven-layer current model

The first-pass corpus already recognized that `実力` did not mean one scalar thing.

It distinguished approximately:

- possessed ability;
- displayed ability;
- measured ability;
- socially usable ability;
- developmental ability.

That is `PRESERVE`.

The V2 corpus expands the model because later Year 1 examinations and *First File* expose additional conversions.

The frozen Year 1 taxonomy is:

1. **possession** — what capacities exist;
2. **development** — how capacities change;
3. **expression** — what the person actually displays;
4. **legibility** — what another observer or metric can infer;
5. **ecological fit** — what the current environment makes valuable;
6. **social usability** — whether others can access or coordinate the capacity;
7. **distribution** — how unlike capacities become collective power;
8. **generation** — whether an actor can produce an answer outside another actor's predictive model;
9. **political power** — whether the person can change incentives, rules, interpretation, or collective behavior;
10. **recording** — whether the official record preserves the performance honestly;
11. **model limitation** — whether the measurement is remembered as a model rather than the person.

The status is `REVISE`, not because the first five were wrong, but because they were incomplete.

## 19.1 Why *First File* matters here

The hypothetical OAA pages (`Y1FF-E041–E048`) make legibility visible as a designed system.

Ayanokōji's low result demonstrates deliberate expression.

Hiyori's judgment rating demonstrates category mismatch.

Horikita demonstrates temporal instability.

Kei demonstrates undermeasurement of informal survival/social intelligence.

Sakayanagi demonstrates the normative politics of aggregating bodily difference.

The central proposition is now:

> **measurement can be accurate without being exhaustive; the moral danger begins when a bounded metric becomes a total identity or irreversible jurisdiction.**

`REJECT`:

- static rating = total ability;
- cooperation = moral goodness;
- overall score = human worth;
- class points = complete merit;
- hypothetical OAA = actually operating Year 1 system.

# 20. Examination interpretation — from “tests reveal students” to authored environments

The first pass correctly treated examinations as more than plot games. `PRESERVE`.

The V2 corpus strengthens a more specific proposition:

> **an examination is an authored environment that changes which behaviors become rational, visible, profitable, punishable, or necessary.**

The school therefore participates causally in the conduct it later evaluates.

The opening month rewards students with apparent freedom while silently converting behavior into collective economic consequence (`Y1V01-E005–E013`).

The island exam makes ecological knowledge valuable.

The zodiac exam prices information and betrayal.

The Mixed Training Camp turns leaders into concentrated liability while private points can purchase rescue.

The Class Vote does not merely discover disposability; it manufactures the need for one student to become disposable (`Y1V10-E013–E019`).

The Event Selection Exam provides the strongest Year 1 model of distributed ability, then Tsukishiro demonstrates that even valid performance is politically meaningless if the official record can be altered (`Y1V11-E133–E145`).

`REVISE`: tests as neutral revealers → tests as partial measurements of behavior produced inside authored incentives.

This does not mean all results are artificial.

It means the designer shares responsibility for what the design makes rational.

# 21. ANHS and the White Room — structural relation without equivalence

This is one of the most important `REVISE` operations in the corpus.

## 21.1 First-pass proposition

ANHS and the White Room share developmental-control logic:

- isolation;
- artificial conditions;
- measurement;
- adaptation;
- human-capital judgment;
- and suffering justified through future excellence.

`PRESERVE`.

## 21.2 Strong equivalence

ANHS is simply a socially distributed White Room.

`DOWNGRADE` and, when stated without qualification, `REJECT`.

Year 1 supplies morally significant differences.

ANHS contains:

- multiple adults with conflicting purposes;
- student political actors;
- ordinary leisure;
- friendship and romance;
- spaces of purposeless life;
- teachers capable of dissent;
- the possibility of appeal and negotiated rescue;

The White Room, as directly established within Year 1, seeks far greater control over environment, curriculum, purpose, and the developed person's future. Atsuomi's ownership claim makes the logic explicit (`Y1V07-E022–E027`, `Y1V07-E040–E041`).

The current formulation is:

> **ANHS is a plural, coercive developmental institution structurally related to the White Room but morally differentiated by incomplete control, emergent ordinary life, contestation, and the possibility that authority can be made answerable.**

Tsukishiro is important because he shows that those differences are contingent rather than automatic.

# 22. White Room knowledge and Tsukishiro — attribution is part of the claim

The second pass imposes a hard epistemic correction.

## 22.1 Broad White Room structure

Year 1 supports broad claims about:

- Atsuomi's developmental project;
- artificial cultivation of exceptional ability;
- Ayanokōji's childhood deprivation of ordinary life;
- his exceptional result;
- and paternal ownership (`Y1V07-E022–E041`, `Y1FF-E008`).

These are `PRESERVE` / partly `STRENGTHEN` through official paratextual framing.

## 22.2 Tsukishiro's detailed exposition

The stronger claim that every detail Tsukishiro supplies about:

- generations;
- failure patterns;
- the incoming student;
- or White Room history

is settled omniscient fact receives status `REJECT` at the Year 1 boundary.

V11.5 explicitly establishes that Ayanokōji considers Tsukishiro capable of narrating complete fabrication persuasively. The final lock therefore preserves the difference among:

- direct experience;
- observed/corroborating evidence;
- informed testimony;
- self-interested testimony;
- adversarial testimony;
- and official retrospective framing.

*First File*'s `Y1FF-E055` can corroborate that the franchise officially packages a coming White Room threat. It cannot convert every finer adversarial detail into independently verified fact.

This is a fundamental revision rule:

> **Attribution is not a nuisance attached to a proposition. Attribution is part of the proposition's epistemic content.**

# 23. Ethics — beneficial development and legitimate method remain separate

The first pass explicitly warned against treating successful strategy as automatically ethical. `PRESERVE` and `STRENGTHEN`.

The V2 ethics document formalizes the standard through:

- goal authorship;
- epistemic warrant;
- necessity;
- proportionality;
- alternatives;
- informed consent;
- practical refusal capacity;
- legitimate authority;
- risk distribution;
- dependency created;
- transparency;
- reversibility;
- later agency;
- third-party harm;
- and record integrity.

The strongest retrospective correction is simple:

> **development is evidence that a method had causal effect, not evidence that the method was morally authorized.**

This principle blocks several bad revisions.

Kei's later love cannot make Volume 4 consensual.

Horikita's later strength cannot prove every pressure placed on her was necessary.

Ichinose's recovery cannot prove rumor saturation and induced psychological fracture were the only legitimate route.

Hirata's return cannot prove severe blame was morally entitled.

Ryūen's altered leadership cannot prove fear and violence were justified developmental instruments.

The answer-back test becomes the positive criterion:

> **the strongest evidence that development is becoming self-owned is the subject's increasing capacity to disagree with, refuse, correct, or depart from the person who helped produce it.**

Sudō, Horikita, Kei, Keisei, Hiyori, Ichinose, and Kōenji supply different versions of that principle.

`REJECT`: good outcome retroactively justifies coercive origin.

# 24. Japanese narration and formal evidence — revision of how certainty is assigned

The V2 Japanese-primary pass changes less plot than **confidence**.

The first pass already reached many correct macro conclusions. The Japanese makes several mechanisms more audibly precise.

## 24.1 Ayanokōji

`REVISE`: blanket unreliability → selective incompleteness.

His casual `オレ`, comic self-deprecation, sexual noticing, social uncertainty, tactical compression, and clinical metaphors coexist in one narrator rather than representing one “real” voice beneath the others.

## 24.2 Horikita

`STRENGTHEN`: her hostility is not generic bluntness. Controlled feminine language and complete declarative judgments make contempt sound like classification, which reinforces the reading of her early social problem as evaluative rather than merely awkward.

## 24.3 Kushida

`STRENGTHEN`: the public/private register split reveals social labor. The public voice is constructed and genuine as ability at the same time.

## 24.4 Address and names

`REVISE`: name changes are relationship evidence, not automatic romance meters.

`清隆`, `恵`, `ヨースケ`, nicknames, honorific choices, and private/public naming create relational spaces. They do not independently prove equality, consent, or permanent intimacy.

## 24.5 Illustrations

`REVISE`: illustrations are neither decoration nor transparent psychological fact.

They establish editorial emphasis and embodied evidence. Kei's scar, Sakayanagi's cane, Horikita's hair, Hirata's tears, Matsushita's surveillance illustration, and the final Ayanokōji–Kei embrace all matter because the publication chooses to make them visible.

The image can intensify a romantic or painful reading without settling the prose's remaining ambiguity.

# 27. `PRESERVE` — the claims that survived the second pass

The most important preserved claims are not generic plot facts. They are structural insights that the Japanese-primary corpus repeatedly confirms.

## 27.1 Human development and human control are intertwined

The first pass was right to see this as the governing Year 1 problem.

The second pass changes the vocabulary to authorship and jurisdiction, but not the core observation.

## 27.2 Ayanokōji's strategic conditioning is real

The correction away from “emotionless mastermind” must not swing into the opposite error.

He remains capable of:

- ruthless calculation;
- manipulated environments;
- concealed contingencies;
- use of dependency;
- sacrifice;
- and treating other people through functional models.

The ordinary person does not replace the strategic person.

They coexist.

## 27.3 Ordinary desire is also real

This was already visible in the first pass and becomes more secure in Japanese.

The corpus therefore preserves a layered Ayanokōji rather than resolving him by choosing whichever register seems morally convenient.

## 27.4 Horikita's arc concerns leadership

Her development cannot be reduced to warmth, romance, or “opening up.” It concerns the conversion of individual capacity into legitimate collective governance.

## 27.5 Kushida's sociality is power

Her public persona performs actual work. Moral danger and political usefulness coexist.

## 27.6 Kei's relationship cannot be separated from dependency history

Even after the romance becomes explicit, the origin remains causally and ethically constitutive of the Year 1 record.

## 27.7 Class D/Horikita-class is not talentless

The original class problem is fragmentation, concealment, volatility, weak coordination, and context-specific ability.

The year is about integration.

## 27.8 Truth, proof, credibility, and official record are different

Volume 2 establishes the distinction; Volume 11 makes it institutionally decisive.

## 27.9 A good outcome is not enough to justify a method

This remains one of the project's governing ethical rules and should be inherited into later years without revision.

# 29. `REVISE` — the claims whose mechanism changed

The most consequential revisions are conceptual rather than factual.

- **freedom**: escape from paternal authorship → positive self-authorship with reciprocal limits;
- **humanization**: becoming emotionally normal → gaining ordinary attachment while ethical restraint remains incomplete;
- **Horikita independence**: separation from influence → integration and self-directed use of influence;
- **Kei relationship**: dependency/protection → genuine romance inside an undisclosed developmental frame;
- **Ichinose weakness**: kindness → solidarity without enough adversarial architecture;
- **Ryūen legitimacy**: fear → fear plus voluntary loyalty without moral purification;
- **merit**: latent scalar → conversion chain across context, coordination, record, and model limitation;
- **exam**: neutral test → authored environment;
- **ANHS/White Room**: binary comparison → structurally related institutions with different degrees of pluralism and answerability;
- **narratorial unreliability**: global deception → selective information control and self-knowledge limits;
- **reciprocity**: mutual affection → ability to answer back, refuse, revise, and remain independently purposive.

`REVISE` is the category that best describes the V2 project's value. Most of its strongest gains come from replacing a true-but-coarse description with a model that can explain more of the evidence without erasing contradiction.

# 36. Final synthesis — what the revision process reveals about Year 1 itself

The most important consequence of `Y1_10` is methodological, but the method reveals something substantive about *Classroom of the Elite*.

The corpus itself has had to learn the same lesson the novels repeatedly teach.

A first analytical model can be intelligent, useful, predictive, and still incomplete.

A later model can explain more without proving that the earlier observations were false.

An official file can record real information without becoming the whole person.

A person can change without proving that the earlier self was fake.

A narrator can be strategically incomplete without making every sentence a lie.

A leader can understand another person's weakness without acquiring the right to decide that person's future.

A relationship can become genuine without purifying its coercive history.

A measurement can be accurate without being exhaustive.

A developmental intervention can work without being justified.

A self can be formed by another person and still become self-authored.

These propositions converge on the same Year 1 boundary principle:

> **Understanding must remain corrigible.**

That requirement applies to:

- the school evaluating students;
- leaders evaluating classmates;
- Ayanokōji modeling other people;
- characters interpreting themselves;
- official guidebooks summarizing the year;
- and the analytical corpus interpreting the fiction.

The mature Year 1 project therefore does not claim victory by possessing the definitive model of every character.

Its stronger achievement is architectural:

> **it knows which claims are stable, which are bounded, which have been revised, which have been rejected, and which must remain open.**

The strongest final transition remains:

> **development versus control → developmental authorship → reciprocal authorship → the file and the person.**

The first pass supplied the first term.

The V2 sequential readings supplied the middle structure.

V11.5 supplied reciprocal authorship.

*First File* supplied the epistemic warning.

The final evidence lock converted those insights into frozen authority.

`Y1_10` preserves the transitions so that later years can revise them without falsifying the history through which they became knowable.
