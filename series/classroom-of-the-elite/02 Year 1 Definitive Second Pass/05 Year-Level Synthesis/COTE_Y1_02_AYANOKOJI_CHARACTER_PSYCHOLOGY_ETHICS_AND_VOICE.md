# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ』
## Year 1 — Ayanokōji Kiyotaka
### Character, psychology, ethics, and voice
### The person inside the strategist: layered selfhood, asymmetrical empathy, and the ethics of developmental authorship

> **CANONICAL YEAR-1 SPECIALIST SNAPSHOT**
>
> This document is the primary analytical home for Ayanokōji Kiyotaka's Year 1 psychology, philosophy, self-authorship, ethical pattern, and first-person voice. It synthesizes the fourteen canonical Japanese novel deep readings, the *First File* paratext audit, the final Year 1 evidence lock, the `THROUGH_Y1` character and relationship ledgers, the Japanese terminology index, and the frozen `Y1_01` architecture.
>
> It does not import Year 2, Volume 0, *Second List*, Year 3, adaptation material, wikis, or later-franchise interpretation. Claims that Year 1 leaves unresolved remain unresolved here.

# 0. Scope, primary home, and interpretive controls

Ayanokōji is unusually vulnerable to reductive reading because the novels place several apparently incompatible kinds of evidence inside one first-person narrator.

He is:

- socially anxious and strategically dominant;
- sexually curious and capable of sexualized coercion;
- interested in friendship and willing to reduce people to tools;
- protective and possessive;
- capable of privacy-respecting restraint and severe bodily invasion;
- attracted to ordinary peace and excited by adversarial uncertainty;
- resentful of being authored by others and increasingly tempted to author other people;
- emotionally underarticulated without being emotionally empty;
- highly predictive without being socially omniscient;
- and increasingly self-directed without yet becoming reliably reciprocal.

The analytical task is therefore not to identify which side is the hidden “real” Ayanokōji and discard the rest.

The task is to explain how the layers coexist, when one layer governs another, and what changes during Year 1.

This document applies five controls.

## 0.1 No clinical diagnosis

Year 1 supports detailed claims about:

- conditioning;
- emotional compartmentalization;
- cognitive empathy;
- practical care;
- limited autobiographical experience;
- self-concealment;
- strategic instrumentalism;
- relational desire;
- and moral decision-making.

It does not support a responsible clinical diagnosis. Terms such as “psychopath,” “sociopath,” “attachment disorder,” or comparable labels would substitute an external taxonomy for the text's own evidence and falsely imply diagnostic completeness.

## 0.2 First-person narration is evidence, not omniscience

Ayanokōji's narration is often highly accurate about observable events and causal mechanisms. It is less reliable as a complete account of:

- the full extent of his planning;
- the full extent of his competence;
- whether one stated motive exhausts all motives;
- what an emotion means before he has language for it;
- or what his future must become.

The method therefore distinguishes:

1. event report;
2. self-explanation;
3. later behavior;
4. external response;
5. structural consequence;
6. and unresolved inference.

## 0.3 Flat delivery is not affirmative evidence of absent emotion

The text repeatedly gives positive evidence of:

- attraction;
- embarrassment;
- pleasure;
- loneliness;
- curiosity;
- social insecurity;
- excitement;
- protectiveness;
- desire for friendship;
- desire for romance;
- and appreciation of shared time.

Emotionlessness cannot be inferred merely because strategic passages are compressed or because he does not narrate affect in conventional sentimental language.

## 0.4 Care does not automatically prove love or legitimacy

The document distinguishes:

- attraction;
- cognitive understanding;
- practical concern;
- protection;
- investment;
- possessiveness;
- dependency;
- affection;
- and love.

A protective act can be genuine while remaining asymmetrical or coercive. A romantic confession can be reciprocal while the relationship still contains undisclosed developmental purposes. An effective intervention can improve a life without becoming ethically authorized by the improvement.

## 0.5 Later benefit does not retroactively create consent

This control is essential for Kei, Horikita, Ichinose, and Hirata.

Year 1 repeatedly shows that a person may genuinely gain:

- confidence;
- safety;
- status;
- functional recovery;
- social belonging;
- or future agency

through an environment Ayanokōji partly authored.

The gain is real.

It does not make the subject's earlier refusal, ignorance, terror, or lack of meaningful alternatives unreal.

# 1. Executive thesis

The strongest Year 1 reading is not that Ayanokōji “becomes human.”

He is human from the beginning.

Volume 1 already gives an adolescent narrator who:

- desperately wants friends;
- fears social failure;
- notices girls' beauty and bodies;
- wants contact information;
- watches videos late at night;
- lacks common hobbies and games;
- enjoys the language of youth;
- and experiences his dormitory as freedom from a family-controlled life.

What Year 1 changes is not the presence of humanity but the range of meanings he can assign to his own experience.

At first, ordinary desire and strategic conditioning coexist without integration.

He wants peace, but becomes excited by difficult opponents.

He values friendship, but controls what friends know.

He recognizes boundaries, but suspends them when he decides development or reliability requires control.

He understands pain, but cognitive understanding does not reliably prohibit him from using pain.

He dislikes being made into another person's project, but increasingly turns classmates into developmental projects of his own.

The governing Year 1 contradiction is therefore:

> **Ayanokōji's greatest virtue and greatest danger arise from the same capacity: he sees what environments, institutions, and relationships do to people with unusual clarity. That vision lets him preserve, teach, and develop people whom a static system would discard. It also tempts him to treat understanding as jurisdiction—the right to decide what another person should know, suffer, depend upon, or become.**

His empathy is not absent.

It is asymmetrical.

He is highly capable of:

- identifying another person's fear;
- modeling hidden motives;
- anticipating shame;
- selecting practical aid;
- and creating conditions in which growth becomes possible.

He is much less consistent at:

- disclosing his own vulnerability;
- permitting informed refusal;
- accepting correction before harm occurs;
- sharing causal authorship;
- or recognizing that a correct model of another person remains incomplete.

Year 1 therefore does not describe a cold machine slowly acquiring emotions.

It describes a conditioned person whose emotions, ordinary preferences, and relationships increasingly resist being exhausted by his strategic vocabulary.

That resistance produces three linked developments.

First, **ordinary life becomes a positive end**. It moves from the spatial freedom of a private dormitory, through friendship, gifts, food, books, cinema, seasonal experiences, phone calls, and purposeless time, into the first life goal Ayanokōji explicitly says he chose for himself.

Second, **developmental authorship becomes self-chosen**. By Volume 11 he wants to change Horikita. By Volume 11.5 Manabu's legacy question awakens a wider desire to cultivate students across the four classes and see what developed people become.

Third, **reciprocal authorship becomes the ethical limit**. The year supplies countermodels in which development is not proprietary: Horikita challenges him, Keisei promises dissent, Hiyori corrects Ryūen, Manabu releases Suzune from imitation, Ichinose preserves class agency, and Kei answers the confession for herself.

*First File* adds the epistemic corollary. Ayanokōji's official profile can accurately record the output he permitted the institution to see while remaining radically incomplete about the person. The same warning applies to his own predictive models.

The locked Year 1 thesis is:

> **Ayanokōji is neither an emotionless mastermind wearing a schoolboy mask nor an ordinary boy whose coldness disappears once he forms attachments. He is an unfinished, layered person whose strategic conditioning is real, whose ordinary desires are real, and whose moral development depends on whether self-authorship can become reciprocal—whether he can care for people without converting superior understanding into ownership of their development.**

# 2. A layered person, not a hidden true self

The governing method separates three layers without treating them as three separate personalities.

## 2.1 The ordinary adolescent layer

This layer includes:

- food and sensory preference;
- friendship;
- sexual attraction;
- embarrassment;
- hobbies;
- gifts;
- dating;
- names and nicknames;
- leisure;
- humor;
- boredom;
- seasonal experience;
- and the wish for a peaceful school life.

The evidence begins before the first special examination. Ayanokōji's panic about making friends and his uncertainty about what counts as friendship are not later humanizing additions. They are part of the initial narrator (`Y1V01-E003–E004`, `Y1V01-E010`).

## 2.2 The relational layer

This layer includes:

- care;
- protectiveness;
- desire to preserve selected people;
- discomfort with loss;
- enjoyment of companionship;
- longing for a friend before whom tears are possible;
- attraction to people who can challenge him;
- and the hope that another person may become irreplaceable.

It becomes increasingly visible through behavior before Ayanokōji can classify it securely.

## 2.3 The White Room / strategic layer

This layer includes:

- authored mediocrity;
- concealment;
- tools logic;
- final-victory reasoning;
- prediction;
- insurance;
- environmental manipulation;
- willingness to impose suffering;
- replaceability analysis;
- and the conversion of people into functions inside a system.

The Volume 3 `本心 / true feelings` monologue gives this layer exceptional local authority. It does not give it exclusive authority over every other scene.

## 2.4 The layers interact rather than alternate cleanly

Ordinary memory produces strategic insight in Volume 3 when Sakura's wish to preserve a moment through photography helps Ayanokōji understand Ibuki's camera.

Relational concern becomes strategic surveillance when he monitors Kei.

Strategic protection becomes ordinary attachment when he does not want Ryūen expelled and admits he would feel lonely without him.

Ordinary romance becomes hidden curriculum when he asks Kei to become his girlfriend while privately understanding the relationship as a way to learn love and address dependency.

The correct model is therefore not:

> ordinary self versus real strategic self.

It is:

> **one person whose different developmental systems assign different meanings to the same event.**

# 3. Selective narratorial unreliability

Ayanokōji's narration should not be classified as globally reliable or globally deceptive.

Its unreliability is selective.

## 3.1 Observable mechanism: usually strong

He is highly reliable when describing:

- rule structures;
- physical movements;
- information asymmetries;
- tactical incentives;
- and the likely consequences of visible choices.

His reconstructions of examinations and opponents are often confirmed by outcome or later explanation.

## 3.2 Competence and planning: systematically underdisclosed

He often describes unusual insight as a stray thought, minimizes advanced knowledge, withholds completed plans, or reveals causal authorship only after the result.

The exact fifty-point entrance scores and mediocre swim time establish that this is not accidental modesty. He authors the data others—including the reader—are initially permitted to possess (`Y1V01-E008`, `Y1V01-E013`).

The first-person form therefore reproduces his in-world politics of legibility. Readers inhabit an information environment whose central narrator controls disclosure.

## 3.3 Motive: accurate fragments, incomplete totals

Ayanokōji's explicit motives should be taken seriously without assuming that one statement exhausts causality.

When he says he helps Sudō because Sudō and Ike are friends, that is direct evidence of friendship motive (`Y1V02-E045` strengthens the same pattern).

When he says the class needs `平田というパーツ`, that is direct evidence of functional motive.

When he later wants a friend before whom he can show tears, that is direct evidence of relational desire.

These statements do not cancel one another. They describe different levels of one intervention.

## 3.4 Emotion: often registered before it is named

Ayanokōji repeatedly experiences an affective response, then treats it as strange, inefficient, or difficult to classify.

Examples include:

- Sakura's growth feeling more important than the formal victory, followed by the question of what kind of person he is (`Y1V02-E038`);
- an unknown feeling of interest toward Ryūen's threat;
- not wanting Ryūen expelled without a fully strategic explanation (`Y1V10-E102` / `何となく龍園を退学させたくなかった`);
- warmth after Hirata asks for given-name intimacy;
- and direct desire to see Kei in *First File* (`Y1FF-E071`).

Behavior and bodily response can therefore precede a stable self-description.

## 3.5 Future forecast: belief, not settled fact

Ayanokōji's conviction in Volume 11.5 that he will return to the White Room and become an instructor is evidence of his current horizon, not proof of destiny (`Y1V11.5-E025`).

The distinction matters because a conditioned person can reproduce the institution's future script even while exercising real choice in the present.

## 3.6 Metaphor: operative model, not complete ontology

`道具`, `武器`, `宿木`, `寄生虫`, `教科書`, `扉`, and pieces within games are ethically revealing metaphors.

They show how he compresses relational complexity into manageable roles.

They should not be treated as magical statements that erase contrary evidence. A person can be modeled as a tool inside one operation and still become a friend, rival, or hoped-for irreplaceable person in another domain.

The metaphor reveals the current grammar of control. It does not necessarily exhaust the relation.

# 4. Identity: product, defect, newborn person, and incomplete file

Year 1 gives Ayanokōji several competing identity languages.

## 4.1 The product

Sakayanagi calls him Atsuomi's `最高傑作 / masterpiece`. Atsuomi calls him property. The White Room is described through the production of ability.

This language locates value in what was manufactured and treats the person as proof of a developmental system.

Ayanokōji rejects the owner's jurisdiction over the product.

He does not reject the efficacy of the production process.

That distinction is crucial. He can acknowledge that the White Room formed his capacity and still deny that formation creates ownership.

## 4.2 The defective product

Chabashira's `不良品` does not mean low capability. She explains that an unusually functional product may be the hardest and most dangerous to handle (`Y1V02-E043`).

The label therefore reveals the institution's problem with him:

> his defect is governability.

Ayanokōji can satisfy requirements while refusing the purpose others want his ability to serve.

## 4.3 The newly born social person

Volume 4.5 describes the ordinary person called Ayanokōji Kiyotaka as `生まれたばかり / newly born` and still fluid. The language does not establish a false school persona covering a complete hidden self.

It establishes developmental incompletion.

The White Room produced capacities, habits, and a victory-conditioned worldview. It did not provide an ordinary autobiographical repertoire for:

- friendship;
- gifts;
- romance;
- unproductive leisure;
- or reciprocal disclosure.

## 4.4 `真っ白` is missing experience, not missing humanity

His description of the ordinary autobiographical field as `真っ白 / blank white` is easily overread.

The text does not say that he lacks desire or feeling. It repeatedly demonstrates the opposite.

The blankness concerns ordinary lived reference points. He lacks memories that tell him what a birthday gift means, how a date should progress, why two people hesitate to end a phone call, or what romantic symbols communicate.

## 4.5 The file

*First File* turns this identity problem into paratext.

His July ratings are mediocre because they record the performance he permitted the institution to see. The file can be accurate about demonstrated output while wrong as a total model of latent capacity.

This becomes the final Year 1 epistemic warning:

> **Ayanokōji is the person who most obviously exceeds the file written about him—and the person most tempted to treat his own models of others as if those models were complete files.**

His ethical development therefore requires more than becoming better at reading people.

It requires learning that accurate reading remains partial.

# 5. The ordinary adolescent layer is structural, not decorative

Ayanokōji's ordinary interiority is sometimes treated as comedy pasted onto a fundamentally mechanical protagonist. Year 1 does not support that hierarchy.

## 5.1 Friendship begins as explicit lack

Volume 1 opens with rehearsal, hesitation, comparison, and fear of social failure.

He does not know how friendship begins. After forming some relationships, he worries that partial inclusion may be worse than isolation because he can now recognize exclusion. He wonders whether he is a `低ランクの友達 / low-ranked friend` in Volume 2.

These are not strategic thoughts.

They establish a person who wants belonging and lacks the ordinary competence required to obtain it naturally.

## 5.2 Attraction and sexual curiosity are continuous

The narration notices Horikita, Kushida, Ichinose, swimsuits, scent, proximity, and bodily embarrassment. Volume 7.5 makes his interest in a girlfriend and sexual experience explicit.

This evidence should be handled carefully.

It proves heterosexual attraction and adolescent curiosity.

It does not prove emotional intimacy, moral maturity, or love.

The sharpest ethical disjunction is that the same person who experiences ordinary sexual desire uses the threat of sexual violation as political communication against Kei in Volume 4. Instrumental intent does not diminish the severity of that act.

## 5.3 Sensory preference becomes autobiographical substance

The soft-serve ice cream line—`これは……美味しいな……`—is analytically important because the pleasure has no strategic purpose (`Y1V04.5`, indexed under `ORDINARY_LIFE_COUNTER_CURRICULUM`).

Likewise:

- cinema becomes a real interest;
- Beethoven, Mozart, rain sounds, and birdsong enter his preference profile;
- books produce low-pressure friendship with Hiyori;
- snow, birthdays, Christmas, and graduation become temporal memories rather than merely calendar events.

These experiences create a personal history not assigned by the White Room.

## 5.4 Social miscalibration is genuine limitation

Ayanokōji can solve examination structures while failing to understand ordinary symbols.

He considers inappropriate gifts, draws “ten friends” and cannot satisfy the task, panics over `好きな人`, and gives Kei a heart-shaped necklace without understanding how intimate it will look.

Volume 11 explicitly has him admit weakness in `情報収集力や統率力` and ignorance of classmates' ordinary preferences.

This is not false modesty.

His high-value diagnostic intelligence differs from broad social memory, everyday maintenance, and public leadership.

## 5.5 Purposeless time becomes intrinsically valuable

The progression is cumulative.

- In Volume 3 he prioritizes school life over Class A.
- In Volume 9 he recognizes socially busy days as `手にしたかった日常 / the ordinary life he wanted to obtain`.
- In Volume 11 he values a slow walk and ordinary conversation after the chess contest.
- In Volume 11.5 he permits explicitly unproductive mall time.
- In *First File* an irrelevant phone conversation is interesting because the interaction itself matters.

The year therefore confirms that ordinary life is not merely recovery time between strategic operations.

It is a rival theory of what life is for.

# 6. The strategic layer: victory-conditioned cognition

The strategic layer is neither fake nor omnipotent.

It is a conditioned system for reducing uncertainty.

## 6.1 Authored mediocrity is the first strategy

Before Ayanokōji authors other people's environments, he authors his own legibility.

Exact fifty-point entrance scores, restrained athletic output, delegated public credit, and low-profile speech create a coherent average student who is easier to leave alone.

Concealment serves several ends at once:

- protection from Atsuomi and institutional attention;
- preservation of ordinary school life;
- avoidance of social responsibility;
- freedom to observe;
- and tactical advantage.

The motive should not be reduced to fear or laziness. The ordinary identity he protects is itself desired.

## 6.2 The final-victory fallback

The Volume 3 tools monologue is the clearest statement of the strategic fallback:

- winning is everything;
- process can be subordinated;
- sacrifice is acceptable;
- and people can become instruments.

Because the text marks the passage as `本心`, it must remain central evidence.

The right qualification is not that the monologue is false.

It is that `本心` identifies a genuine governing doctrine without proving that every other desire is counterfeit.

The doctrine activates most clearly when:

- his continued freedom is threatened;
- decisive victory is at stake;
- or another person is classified as a functional part of a system.

## 6.3 Strategy is environmental rather than merely deductive

Ayanokōji's characteristic power is not simply solving puzzles faster.

He maps the relation among:

- rules;
- information;
- incentives;
- fear;
- status;
- shame;
- institutional procedure;
- and the internal reasons through which another person will choose.

He often avoids direct orders. Instead he shapes the field until another person's authentic motives predictably generate the desired action.

This is why “environmental authorship” is more precise than “mastermind.”

A mastermind label celebrates intelligence while obscuring the ethical object of the intelligence: other people's available worlds.

## 6.4 Insurance is an internalized mode of safety

The White Room layer appears repeatedly as contingency construction.

Ayanokōji does not prefer one plan when several can be prepared. He creates:

- evidence if persuasion fails;
- alternate identities;
- backup communication routes;
- political leverage;
- and later interpretive explanations capable of containing visible exposure.

Insurance is not only tactical style. It is how he experiences security.

This explains why ordinary intimacy is difficult. Intimacy requires accepting that another person's response cannot be fully insured without destroying the reciprocity that makes the response meaningful.

## 6.5 He is not strategically omniscient

Year 1 repeatedly records limits.

He can be wrong about:

- ordinary social symbols;
- the cause of the elevator restarting;
- the exact future behavior of independent actors;
- and broad class-level information that Kei, Kushida, Hirata, or Horikita can gather more naturally.

Matsushita's concealment in Volume 11.5 shows that hidden ability is not exclusively his domain.

His predictive accuracy is exceptional.

It is not total person-knowledge.

# 7. Freedom: escape, ordinary life, self-authorship, and legacy

Freedom is the most continuous philosophical thread in Ayanokōji's Year 1.

## 7.1 Negative freedom: distance from ownership

The dormitory produces the early declaration `オレは自由だ / I am free`.

Freedom initially means:

- physical separation from family;
- privacy;
- reduced surveillance from the origin institution;
- and the ability not to perform a purpose assigned by Atsuomi.

The school is valuable because it interrupts ownership.

## 7.2 The paradox: `自由を守るために自由を捨てる`

Chabashira's coercion in Volume 3 creates the central paradox:

> abandon freedom in order to protect freedom.

To preserve ordinary school life, Ayanokōji must participate in class politics and produce results.

This logic repeats throughout Year 1. Control becomes the means by which he defends a life supposedly defined by escape from control.

The contradiction is not incidental.

It is the mechanism by which White Room logic returns inside freedom.

## 7.3 Positive freedom: choosing what life is for

Volume 11.5 supplies the decisive transition.

Ayanokōji describes ordinary three-year school life as:

> `生まれて初めて`—the first goal he has set for himself.

Freedom is no longer only absence of the owner.

It is the ability to choose an end.

The end includes mundane experiences:

- friends;
- romance;
- learning;
- leisure;
- seasons;
- and the right to continue until graduation.

## 7.4 The legacy problem

Manabu then asks what Ayanokōji will leave behind.

The question changes freedom again.

A completely private ordinary life may protect him from being used, but it leaves no trace beyond his own escape. Ayanokōji begins imagining a legacy made from developed students and four strengthened class polities.

This desire is genuinely self-authored.

It is also ethically dangerous because the content of his freedom becomes other people's development.

## 7.5 Freedom for self versus freedom for others

Year 1 therefore separates two questions.

1. Can Ayanokōji choose a purpose not assigned by the White Room?
2. Can the people inside that purpose retain authority over themselves?

The first question receives a strong affirmative answer by the Year 1 endpoint.

The second remains open.

## 7.6 Desire for defeat as freedom from the creator's claim

Ayanokōji's wish for a genuine defeat is not ordinary self-sabotage.

He does not want to throw a contest. A manufactured loss would remain under his authorship and prove nothing.

He wants an opponent capable of defeating him at full strength because such a defeat would challenge the claim that Atsuomi's product and system are absolute.

This is one of the most revealing desires in Year 1:

> **he wants an outcome he cannot design because only the uncontrollable outcome can free him from the meaning imposed upon his ability.**

The relay with Manabu, the arranged contest with Sakayanagi, and his preservation of Ryūen all participate in this search for an independent other.

# 8. Asymmetrical empathy

The claim that Ayanokōji lacks empathy is too blunt.

Year 1 instead supports a distinction among several forms.

## 8.1 Cognitive empathy: exceptionally strong

He reads:

- Sakura's shame and defensive performance;
- Horikita's recognition wound;
- Kei's learned helplessness and status strategy;
- Hirata's care pathology;
- Ichinose's guilt and inability to seek help;
- Ryūen's relation to fear;
- and Hiyori's quiet political depth.

This understanding is often more accurate than the person's public self-description.

## 8.2 Practical empathy: strong and concrete

He frequently translates understanding into material action.

He:

- buys exam points;
- preserves privacy;
- stays during a confession because Airi asks not to be left alone;
- buys medicine for Kei;
- obtains deterrent evidence;
- provides an alternative to Nagumo's coercive bargain;
- creates institutional defenses against Tsukishiro;
- and gives other people routes through crises they could not build alone.

## 8.3 Affective resonance: present but underarticulated

The text gives bodily and emotional responses:

- loneliness;
- excitement;
- warmth;
- pleasure;
- attraction;
- embarrassment;
- interest;
- and discomfort at loss.

He does not consistently narrate these in mature relational categories.

The problem is not absence so much as limited integration.

## 8.4 Moral restraint: inconsistent

Understanding another person's pain does not reliably create a prohibition against using it.

Volume 4 is the decisive case. Ayanokōji sees Kei's hidden terror and recognizes the strength required to survive it. That recognition occurs inside bodily invasion, delayed rescue, blackmail, and sexualized coercion.

The fact that he understands the wound makes the intervention more—not less—ethically serious.

## 8.5 Reciprocal empathy: the missing dimension

Ayanokōji often knows far more about another person than the person knows about him.

He can require vulnerability without offering comparable disclosure.

He can protect without making the protected person aware of the costs incurred in their name.

He can interpret another person's motives while exempting his own plans from scrutiny.

The Year 1 endpoint therefore suggests:

> **his central relational deficit is not inability to understand another person's inner life; it is difficulty accepting a relation in which the other person can understand, correct, refuse, and alter him in return.**

Horikita, Kei, Manabu, Hiyori, Sakayanagi, and Ryūen matter partly because each becomes capable of some form of answer he did not wholly write.

# 9. Ayanokōji's philosophy of ability and personhood

Ayanokōji is not a simple meritocratic elitist.

Nor is he a consistent egalitarian.

## 9.1 Ability is contextual

From Volume 1 onward, he challenges the idea that academic weakness exhausts a person's value.

He recognizes:

- Ike's camping knowledge;
- Sudō's athletic capacity;
- Kei's control of social atmosphere;
- Kushida's relationship memory;
- Hirata's translation of conflict;
- Hiyori's inference;
- and Horikita's growing ability to coordinate unlike people.

This is one of his genuine virtues.

He often sees capacity where institutional rankings or social prejudice see only defect.

## 9.2 Ability must become usable

Possession alone does not make capacity collective power.

Kōenji is the clearest example. Extraordinary capacity that cannot be reliably mobilized cannot simply be counted as class strength.

Ayanokōji therefore thinks politically rather than metaphysically about ability:

> who can use it, under what conditions, for which goal?

## 9.3 Developmental possibility is real but not equally weighted

Volume 10 gives the harshest formulation.

He acknowledges that every person may develop, then still treats the Class Vote as an opportunity to remove someone with low expected future value.

This reveals the limit of his developmental pluralism.

He does not deny growth.

He discounts uncertain growth under scarcity.

## 9.4 Event is not identity

Volume 9 supplies a more humane personhood principle.

Ayanokōji rejects the claim that a criminal act automatically makes the actor wholly evil and states that every human being has a right to forgiveness.

This principle resists Sakayanagi's conversion of one true wrongdoing into Ichinose's total identity.

## 9.5 The contradiction: forgiveness and pruning

The same character who refuses to reduce Ichinose to her worst act can classify another student as unnecessary for the class.

The positions are not formally identical:

- moral identity asks what a person wholly is;
- Class Vote triage asks whom an institution will permit to continue under forced scarcity.

But the tension remains.

Ayanokōji is more willing to preserve personhood in intimate moral judgment than in collective allocation.

## 9.6 Person-models are useful and dangerous

His models often work.

That success creates the temptation to confuse:

- prediction with comprehension;
- comprehension with consent;
- and consent with jurisdiction.

*First File* supplies the corrective. A model can be accurate and bounded. A rating can record visible output and still miss latent capacity. A psychological map can predict behavior and still fail to possess the whole person.

# 10. Developmental authorship: a typology

Ayanokōji does not have one intervention method.

Year 1 supports at least five.

## 10.1 Mode A — scaffolding without crisis creation

### Ike on the island

Ayanokōji steps back so Ike can discover the usefulness of his own camping experience (`自分自身で確認`).

- crisis created by Ayanokōji: no;
- information withheld: only his own answer;
- refusal available: yes;
- gain: self-recognition and social value;
- dependency created: low;
- subject acts outside design: yes.

This is among the least coercive interventions.

### Airi entering the group

He refuses to make the social decision for her and leaves the act of joining with Airi.

The developmental gain belongs visibly to the subject.

### Horikita learning chess

He teaches, recognizes aptitude, and allows her growing competence to generate the board from which later intervention becomes possible.

## 10.2 Mode B — seeded discovery

### Horikita and the dummy-camera plan

Ayanokōji controls the sequence of ideas but does not dictate the final operation. Horikita synthesizes, verifies, persuades, and accepts risk.

The method is empowering and manipulative at once.

### The past examination

He controls timing so students build study habits before receiving insurance.

The gain is large; the autonomy cost is modest but real.

## 10.3 Mode C — pressure with retained exit capacity

### Horikita in the sports festival

He attacks her self-conception, withholds the answer, and permits class defeat because he predicts `敗北と再生`.

The harm is lower than the Kei operation. Horikita retains more physical and political capacity to reject him.

The underlying logic is continuous: the developer decides pressure is necessary.

## 10.4 Mode D — paternalistic crisis intensification

### Ichinose

Ayanokōji does not create her original guilt or Sakayanagi's attack. He deliberately continues pressure and calls the final phase `一之瀬帆波の心を壊す作業`.

He also becomes `扉 / a door`: a nonjudgmental interface through which confession can become possible.

Support and coercion occupy the same scene.

### Hirata

He knowingly uses an accusation he does not accept literally, applies pressure until Hirata's defensive structure breaks, and justifies the intervention through the class's need for `平田というパーツ`.

The result restores function and deepens intimacy. The route remains coercive.

## 10.5 Mode E — manufactured dependency

### Kei in Volumes 4 and 7

This is the severest Year 1 form.

Ayanokōji:

- reactivates conflict;
- uses intermediaries;
- permits assault;
- withholds rescue;
- invades bodily privacy;
- threatens sexual assault;
- controls evidence;
- discredits alternative protectors;
- withdraws contact;
- predicts later torture;
- delays intervention again;
- and offers himself as the superior protector.

The later relationship produces real safety, agency, and affection.

The origin remains coercive.

## 10.6 Mode F — reciprocal authorship

Year 1 ends by establishing, rather than completing, this mode.

Examples include:

- Horikita receiving jurisdiction over Kushida;
- Horikita converting hidden ability into an auditable wager;
- Kei explicitly answering the confession;
- Keisei promising support and dissent;
- Hiyori correcting Ryūen;
- Manabu giving a legacy question instead of prescribing an answer.

The defining feature is not equality of ability.

It is that the other person retains meaningful authority over:

- interpretation;
- refusal;
- destination;
- and correction.

# 11. Why successful development does not settle the ethics

Ayanokōji's interventions often work.

That success creates the strongest possible defense of his methods—and the strongest temptation to stop asking moral questions.

## 11.1 Consequential defense

His actions:

- prevent expulsions;
- expose sabotage;
- preserve classmates;
- improve leadership;
- restore Hirata;
- help Ichinose confess;
- protect Kei;
- and create a more competitive class.

A purely outcome-based reading can therefore argue that his harshness is justified by superior results.

## 11.2 Epistemic defense

He often knows more than the people affected.

He can see dangers, trajectories, and vulnerabilities they cannot.

This creates a paternalistic argument:

> the person with superior knowledge may be justified in making decisions that the subject would reject under incomplete understanding.

## 11.3 The autonomy objection

The objection is not that outcomes are irrelevant.

It is that persons are not merely sites at which outcomes occur.

When Ayanokōji:

- creates a crisis;
- withholds rescue;
- conceals the plan;
- or makes safety conditional on dependence,

he changes the subject's feasible choices before asking what choice reveals.

## 11.4 The accountability objection

Ayanokōji often remains hidden from the people who bear the cost.

This prevents:

- informed challenge;
- correction;
- public judgment;
- and reciprocal responsibility.

Horikita's visible leadership becomes increasingly important because public judgment can be contested in a way hidden sovereignty cannot.

## 11.5 The model-error objection

Even superior prediction can fail.

Ayanokōji underestimates Kei's refusal on the rooftop, miscalibrates ordinary romantic symbolism, revises Hiyori upward, and encounters concealed ability in Matsushita.

The person may exceed the model.

A system that gives the modeler unilateral jurisdiction magnifies the cost of error.

## 11.6 The stronger Year 1 standard

The mature ethical question is not:

> Did the intervention help?

It is:

> **Did the intervention enlarge the person's future while preserving enough knowledge, refusal, and authorship for the future to remain theirs?**

# 13. Relationships as diagnostic tests of Ayanokōji

This section does not replace `Y1_04_RELATIONSHIPS...`. Its narrower purpose is to ask what each relation reveals about Ayanokōji's psychology and ethical development.

## 13.1 Horikita — from low-pressure familiarity to the test of independent authorship

Horikita is one of Ayanokōji's earliest comfortable relationships because silence does not require continuous social performance. In Volume 2 he describes himself as closer to an unguarded state around her.

The relation then becomes his longest developmental project.

He:

- seeds discoveries;
- routes public credit;
- engineers environmental pressure;
- protects her visible legitimacy;
- teaches her;
- and gradually chooses her growth as something he wants.

The danger is obvious. Horikita can become proof of his ability to cultivate another person.

Year 1 also supplies the correction.

By the endpoint she:

- reconstructs his hidden authorship;
- challenges matchmaking and manipulation;
- demands auditable performance rather than blind trust;
- holds political views he would resolve differently;
- receives jurisdiction over Kushida;
- and integrates Manabu's influence without remaining his imitation.

Ayanokōji's interest in her becomes ethically meaningful precisely because she is becoming less controllable.

The unresolved question is not whether he can make Horikita stronger.

It is whether he can value the strength that produces decisions he would not have chosen.

## 13.2 Kei — the clearest fusion of care, possession, romance, and hidden curriculum

Kei is the most demanding ethical relation in Year 1 because the later reciprocity cannot be separated from the coercive origin.

### Origin

Ayanokōji identifies real social ability and real vulnerability. He then manufactures the conditions under which his protection becomes indispensable.

The relation begins through:

- trauma activation;
- delayed rescue;
- bodily invasion;
- blackmail;
- and a credible sexual threat.

### Development

Kei does not remain a passive product of that operation.

She:

- understands what he did;
- names it as bullying;
- forgives without declaring it right;
- refuses to betray him on the rooftop;
- negotiates partnership terms;
- offers reciprocal rescue;
- ends the Hirata arrangement;
- and explicitly answers the confession.

Ayanokōji authored the field.

He did not wholly author Kei's later dignity or affection.

### Care

His practical concern becomes increasingly particular:

- medicine;
- safety guarantees;
- private names;
- gifts;
- protection from expulsion;
- and direct desire to see her.

These are not erased by the relationship's origin.

### Possession

Protection remains structured through exclusivity and private sovereignty. The promise `どんな手を使っても` gives emotional security and grants Ayanokōji unilateral authority over methods.

### Romance

Volume 11.5 establishes mutual confession. It also retains `教科書`, dependency-cure logic, and irreplaceability as a hoped-for future rather than a completed fact.

The closing `祈ろう / let me pray` matters because it admits uncertainty.

The strongest Year 1 formulation is:

> **Kei is neither merely a tool nor proof of completed romantic humanization. She is a person with whom Ayanokōji enters explicit reciprocal romance while still privately placing the relationship inside a developmental curriculum she does not fully know.**

## 13.3 Ichinose — nonjudgmental presence and terminal jurisdiction

Ichinose reveals Ayanokōji's capacity to articulate humane moral principles.

He separates act from identity and states that every person possesses a right to forgiveness.

He can sit outside a closed door without demanding immediate access and describe himself as `扉 / a door` rather than judge.

At the same time, he:

- saturates the rumor environment;
- uses other students' secrets;
- continues pressure;
- calls the work a breaking of her heart;
- and later retains `介錯 / finishing blow` language.

His support creates a one-year future horizon. It does not eliminate his tendency to imagine himself as final evaluator of whether another person's trajectory should continue.

*First File* supplies Ichinose's side. She experiences him as a precious friend and something not yet nameable, then deliberately returns to class competition.

That preserved class agency is essential. Personal attachment does not make her his dependent project.

## 13.4 Hirata — the ethics of functional repair

Hirata resembles Ayanokōji more than their public styles suggest.

Both understand social systems. Both carry earlier trauma. Both can subordinate direct emotional truth to a protective design.

Hirata's design is universal harmony; Ayanokōji's is controlled outcome.

During Hirata's collapse, Ayanokōji rejects gentle delay and uses concentrated blame to break the structure maintaining dysfunction.

He explicitly denies that benevolence toward Hirata is the motive and classifies him as necessary infrastructure.

Yet the same episode produces:

- the wish for a friend before whom tears can be shown;
- later given-name intimacy;
- and awkward warmth.

The relation demonstrates a recurring pattern:

> Ayanokōji may enter through function and discover personal meaning after the function has already justified intervention.

The ethical risk is that personal meaning then appears to vindicate the route by which access was obtained.

## 13.5 Manabu — recognition without ownership

Manabu becomes important because he recognizes capacity without trying to own it in the same manner as Atsuomi.

The relay gives Ayanokōji a rare field of reciprocal full effort. Strategy temporarily disappears behind the person running beside him.

Manabu later supplies a legacy question rather than a prescribed answer.

He asks what Ayanokōji will leave behind.

That question activates self-authorship without specifying the product.

Manabu therefore offers one of Year 1's strongest countermodels to White Room developmental authority:

> recognition can challenge a person without claiming the right to determine the final form.

## 13.6 Sakayanagi — rivalry, recognition, and the wish to be overcome

Sakayanagi knows that Ayanokōji's visible file is false. She recognizes him as the child produced by an artificial system and turns him into a test case for her theory of natural genius.

The relation is adversarial and unusually reciprocal at the level of cognition.

Ayanokōji wants the contest because she may supply something ordinary classmates cannot: a genuine challenge to the meaning of his manufactured excellence.

The Volume 11 chess result is institutionally corrupted, preventing the contest from resolving either theory cleanly.

The slow walk and `人肌のぬくもり / warmth of human skin` after the match matter because the relation does not end with score. Rivalry creates shared time, recognition, and embodied experience.

The relation should not be forced into romance at the Year 1 boundary. Its importance lies in reciprocal intellectual visibility and the possibility of an uncontrollable outcome.

## 13.7 Ryūen — fear, independent opposition, and relational value through rivalry

Ryūen is initially valuable because he creates uncertainty.

He becomes more than a threat when Ayanokōji:

- preserves him from expulsion;
- admits he did not want him gone;
- says he would feel lonely if Ryūen disappeared;
- and wants him to continue growing.

Competition is one of the relational forms Ayanokōji can inhabit most directly. An opponent may know his capacity without requiring conventional emotional disclosure.

The relation is not ethically symmetrical. Ayanokōji defeats Ryūen through overwhelming physical and strategic closure and later adopts teacher-like language toward him.

But Ryūen's independent will matters precisely because he can generate a future Ayanokōji has not wholly authored.

## 13.8 Hiyori — low-pressure friendship and correction through quiet agency

Hiyori represents one of Ayanokōji's least coercive relationships.

Books create shared interest without institutional necessity. Silence and observation are not treated as social failure. She can ask difficult questions because avoiding them would prevent the relationship from developing.

She also corrects Ryūen in her own way, prompting Ayanokōji to revise his assessment of her political depth.

The relation demonstrates that he can appreciate another person's development without creating the crisis that reveals it.

## 13.9 Sakura — friendship without manufactured dependency

Sakura is an important counterexample to the claim that Ayanokōji can become important only by making himself necessary.

Their friendship develops through:

- adjusted pace;
- listening;
- privacy restraint;
- voluntary proximity;
- and recognition of her initiative.

Her ordinary memory generates strategic insight, but the friendship is not reducible to that utility.

## 13.10 Satō — restraint and the route not taken

Satō offers ordinary romance with relatively little hidden political entanglement.

Ayanokōji enjoys aspects of the date and finds no decisive defect in her. He refuses because he does not reciprocate sufficiently, cannot imagine inhabiting the future she offers, and knows acceptance would destabilize Kei.

The refusal is ethically significant because he does not use Satō merely as a textbook for experience.

It is strategically significant because he chooses the person adapted to his abnormal hidden system over the person who might have offered a more conventional school life.

## 13.11 The Ayanokōji Group — belonging under partial self-disclosure

The group gives him:

- invitations;
- movies;
- nicknames;
- given names;
- and low-pressure companionship.

Its limits are equally important. Members do not know the central structures of his life, ability, or covert politics.

The group therefore establishes genuine friendship without full reciprocity of knowledge.

Year 1 does not settle whether ordinary belonging can survive greater truth.

# 14. Rivalry as a relational language

Rivalry is not merely competition in Ayanokōji's psychology.

It is one of the safest forms of reciprocal recognition available to him.

## 14.1 Why rivalry is easier than ordinary vulnerability

A contest provides:

- rules;
- a defined field;
- mutual commitment;
- visible competence;
- and a legitimate reason to reveal strength.

This allows Ayanokōji to become visible without explaining his childhood or asking directly for emotional acceptance.

## 14.2 Manabu: embodied recognition

The relay strips away strategic purpose. The person beside him becomes the entire field.

The full-speed request is a request to be answered by an equal commitment.

## 14.3 Sakayanagi: epistemic recognition

The chess contest is designed because he wants an opponent who understands the origin problem attached to his ability.

The desired outcome is not necessarily victory. It is a result that means something because the other person is capable of producing it.

## 14.4 Ryūen: uncertainty and growth

Ryūen is preserved because an independent adversary is more valuable than a solved environment.

Ayanokōji's loneliness at the prospect of Ryūen's removal suggests that rivalry has become relational attachment.

## 14.5 The danger of developmental rivalry

Ayanokōji increasingly imagines rivals as people to cultivate.

This preserves their future and risks subordinating them to his private curriculum.

The relational test is whether the rival remains capable of defining the meaning of the contest independently.

# 15. Care, protection, and ownership

Protection is the domain in which Ayanokōji's care is most concrete and most ethically unstable.

## 15.1 Practical protection is genuine

He is capable of making strong guarantees and delivering on them.

For a traumatized person such as Kei, this reliability has enormous value.

## 15.2 Protection creates a hierarchy of lives

By Volume 8 he distinguishes people he hopes will remain from people whose survival he guarantees.

This is private sovereignty.

The hierarchy is not necessarily irrational. No person can protect everyone equally.

The problem is that Ayanokōji often decides:

- who enters the protected category;
- what methods are permitted;
- what third parties may be harmed;
- and what the protected person is allowed to know.

## 15.3 Ownership is structural before it is emotional

Year 1 does not require proof that Ayanokōji consciously feels romantic possessiveness in every relevant scene.

Protection can be possessive at the structural level when:

- safety depends upon exclusive access to him;
- alternatives are discredited;
- the protected person's attachment is managed;
- and he reserves unilateral control of methods.

## 15.4 He rejects ownership over himself

Atsuomi's `おまえは俺の所有物だ` is among the clearest claims Ayanokōji refuses.

This makes his own protector sovereignty more ethically revealing.

He understands why another person's power over his future is intolerable.

He does not yet consistently generalize that understanding to people whose futures he controls for benevolent reasons.

## 15.5 Institutional integrity as self-limitation

Volume 11.5 provides a meaningful counterdevelopment. Against Tsukishiro, Ayanokōji seeks an institutional backstop rather than secret personal favoritism.

He recognizes that fair contest requires procedures capable of constraining power—including his opponent's and potentially his own.

The open question is whether he can apply the same principle to private relationships.

# 16. Sex, embodiment, consent, and intimacy

Ayanokōji's relation to sexuality is one of the clearest places where the ordinary adolescent, relational, and strategic layers cannot be collapsed into one another.

Year 1 supplies evidence for all of the following:

- ordinary heterosexual attraction;
- adolescent curiosity about dating and sexual experience;
- embarrassment around bodies and romantic symbolism;
- awareness that sexual vulnerability can be exploited;
- willingness to use sexualized threat as coercion;
- increasing attention to verbal refusal;
- and physical tenderness whose emotional meaning he cannot yet classify securely.

A responsible synthesis must preserve all of them.

## 16.1 Ordinary sexual desire is real

From Volume 1 onward, Ayanokōji notices girls physically, becomes flustered by proximity, participates in adolescent male sexual talk, and imagines conventional romance.

Volume 4.5 is especially important because his reaction to swimsuit-clad Ichinose is bodily and comic rather than strategically useful. He withdraws because the situation is difficult for him to tolerate comfortably. Volume 7.5 makes the underlying desire explicit: he wants a girlfriend and thinks about eventual sexual experience.

This evidence corrects the claim that he has no sexual desire or merely imitates what other boys are expected to feel.

It does not establish mature intimacy.

Attraction answers:

> What does his body and attention respond to?

It does not answer:

> Can he make another person's consent, meaning, and vulnerability coequal with his own desire?

## 16.2 Sexualized coercion is not ordinary desire intensified

The Volume 4 threat against Kei belongs to a different moral category.

Ayanokōji uses:

- physical restraint;
- trauma knowledge;
- exposure risk;
- informational domination;
- and a direct threat of sexual assault

as instruments for breaking resistance and establishing control.

The fact that the threat serves recruitment rather than sexual gratification does not mitigate it.

Instrumental sexual violence remains sexual violence at the level of what is communicated to the target: her body can be invaded because the person with power judges invasion useful.

This distinction matters because apologetic readings sometimes argue that he “did not really intend” the act in a sexual sense. That misses the mechanism. The coercive force depends upon Kei believing that bodily autonomy can be overridden.

The strategic motive explains the act.

It does not morally downgrade it.

## 16.3 The body becomes an information surface

Ayanokōji often treats embodiment as evidence.

He notices:

- illness;
- injury;
- breathing;
- posture;
- hesitation;
- scars;
- tears;
- physical distance;
- whether touch is accepted;
- and whether another person withdraws.

This observational capacity supports practical care. It allows him to recognize danger and respond to conditions other people miss.

It also makes the body available to strategic reading.

Kei's scar is trauma history, exposure risk, social vulnerability, and leverage. Horikita's fever is illness and a variable inside an exam. Ichinose's willingness to remain in the rain is self-punishment and a point of intervention. Hirata's tears are affect and evidence that a defensive role has collapsed.

The ethical question is therefore not whether he sees bodies accurately.

It is what authority he infers from seeing them.

## 16.4 Reader-facing fanservice and in-world privacy create a formal tension

The light novels repeatedly sexualize female bodies through color art, swimsuits, bath scenes, framing, and commercial illustration. At the same time, the narrative treats voyeuristic recording, scars, sexual threat, and bodily privacy as morally consequential.

Ayanokōji stands inside that tension.

He can notice and enjoy physical beauty without endorsing Operation Delta's covert recording. He can decline to treat the footage as a recreational asset while still participating in a plan whose method violates privacy. He can appreciate Kei, Satō, Ichinose, or Sakayanagi physically while recognizing that attraction does not itself create access.

The corpus therefore does not support a simple opposition between:

- pure respectful protagonist;
- and sexualized surrounding work.

The more difficult truth is that ordinary desire, commercial spectacle, strategic invasion, and emerging consent awareness coexist inside the same formal system.

## 16.5 Later intimacy with Kei shows a changed protocol

Volume 11.5 does not erase the relationship's origin, but it does establish meaningful procedural change.

Before asking Kei to become his girlfriend, Ayanokōji preserves a verbal path for refusal (`Y1V11.5-E131`). Kei answers explicitly and affirmatively (`Y1V11.5-E135`). He declines an immediate kiss and asks before the level of physical closeness depicted in the embrace (`Y1V11.5-E136`).

These details are not trivial.

They demonstrate that he can:

- formulate a request rather than an order;
- wait for another person's answer;
- distinguish one form of intimacy from another;
- and leave an immediate physical escalation unrealized.

The correct inference is **development in protocol**.

The incorrect inference is **retroactive consent**.

Kei consents to the girlfriend relationship at the Year 1 endpoint.

She did not consent to:

- the original recruitment operation;
- the rooftop ordeal;
- the strategic management of her attachment;
- or the full hidden curriculum through which Ayanokōji privately understands the romance.

Later reciprocity and earlier coercion are both facts.

## 16.6 Ichinose: tenderness under explicit awareness of exploitability

The rain and dorm-room scene in Volume 11.5 is ethically important because Ayanokōji knows that Ichinose's emotional and sexual openness could be exploited.

He touches her wet hair, cheek, and lips; she does not withdraw (`Y1V11.5-E081`). The scene contains tenderness and attraction. It also occurs while she is exhausted, distressed, physically cold, and politically vulnerable.

His recognition of exploitability is morally relevant because it shows that he can perceive the asymmetry.

Perception alone is not sufficient moral resolution.

The text prevents the reader from treating the scene as simple romantic rescue by placing `介錯` at its endpoint. The same consciousness capable of gentle touch retains a sovereign model in which he may decide when her developmental trajectory deserves termination.

Embodied closeness does not automatically dissolve jurisdictional thinking.

## 16.7 Year 1 endpoint

By *First File*, Ayanokōji can choose to `恋愛という世界に身を任せる / entrust himself to the world of romance` rather than analyze every step (`Y1FF-E063`). This is a real local surrender of optimization.

The phrase does not mean that all hidden design has vanished.

It means he has become capable of allowing experience to occur without completely predetermining its meaning.

The Year 1 sexual/intimacy profile is therefore:

> **ordinary desire is present; sexualized coercion is a severe ethical fact; later consent practices improve; reciprocal romance becomes explicit; and informed reciprocity remains incomplete because the hidden purposes of the relationship are not fully shared.**

# 17. Two desire systems: peace and intensity

Ayanokōji repeatedly says that he wants peace.

He also repeatedly becomes energized by difficult problems, strong opponents, and environments whose outcomes are not obvious.

The two desires should not be flattened into hypocrisy.

They are competing positive goods.

## 17.1 Peace is not merely an alibi

`平穏な日々 / peaceful days` names a genuine preference.

The school gives him:

- a private room;
- distance from paternal ownership;
- friends;
- unassigned time;
- tastes;
- seasonal memories;
- and the possibility of romance.

Volume 11.5 calls ordinary high-school life the first goal he set himself (`Y1V11.5-E021–E026`). *First File* strengthens the claim through direct experience: study, exercise, purposeless conversation, and wanting to see Kei possess value without being converted into class advantage (`Y1FF-E059–E073`).

A camouflage account cannot explain why these experiences matter inside private narration when no audience needs convincing.

## 17.2 Intensity is also intrinsically attractive

Volume 2 already shows him becoming excited by the growing strategic landscape and recoiling from his own response.

The relay with Manabu, the contest with Sakayanagi, Ryūen's continued growth, and the imagined four-class developmental conflict all provide versions of the same attraction.

He wants an encounter that:

- requires full capacity;
- resists easy prediction;
- produces an answer he cannot manufacture by withholding effort;
- and may defeat the claim that his creator's system is unsurpassable.

This is not reducible to victory hunger.

A completely predictable victory is often less valuable to him than an uncertain contest.

## 17.3 The ordinary world and the contest world educate different capacities

Ordinary life teaches:

- preference;
- patience;
- embarrassment;
- mutual timing;
- gift meaning;
- purposeless companionship;
- and the value of another person's presence apart from function.

Competition teaches:

- full exposure;
- recognition by an equal;
- adaptation;
- strategic consequence;
- and the possibility of an outcome that exceeds his control.

The danger is not intensity itself.

The danger is importing the contest world's developmental grammar into intimate relationships without consent.

Kei becomes a curriculum.

Horikita becomes a developmental project.

Ichinose receives a one-year trial horizon.

Rivals become people whose future strength he wants to cultivate.

## 17.4 Desire for defeat is not self-hatred

Ayanokōji's wish for a genuine defeat is best read as a challenge to authorship.

If another person can defeat him at full strength, then:

- Atsuomi's educational claim is not absolute;
- the White Room's product is not the final measure of human possibility;
- and Ayanokōji can encounter a result he did not design.

The desire is therefore relational and political.

He wants an external will strong enough to become real against him.

That can be understood as an early longing for reciprocal authorship: another person must possess enough independent power to alter his future.

## 17.5 *First File*: rapids become part of ordinary life

The final bonus fiction supplies a useful synthesis. Ayanokōji still wants peaceful days, but he also describes unpredictable rapids as part of what makes school life interesting (`Y1FF-E073`).

The formulation weakens the assumption that he must choose permanently between:

- peace;
- and meaningful difficulty.

A mature ordinary life may include challenge without becoming a totalized war system.

The unresolved question is whether he can accept challenge without manufacturing other people's lives into the challenge he needs.

# 18. Japanese first-person voice

Ayanokōji's voice is not decorative style placed over a plot machine.

It is one of the principal sources for his psychology and one of the principal mechanisms through which the novels reproduce his politics of information.

## 18.1 Casual `オレ` and the distance between interior and public speech

The internal narrator uses casual `オレ`.

His private voice is often:

- loose;
- self-mocking;
- sexually attentive;
- referential;
- rhetorically exaggerated;
- socially anxious;
- and capable of sudden slang or comic phrasing.

His public speech is usually economical and low-risk.

The gap matters.

Classmates often encounter a quiet, mediocre, mildly detached student. The reader encounters someone who rehearses friendship, notices bodies, worries about invitations, makes cultural references, invents jokes, and complains internally about ordinary inconvenience.

The public face is not a completely fabricated personality.

It is a severely bandwidth-limited interface.

## 18.2 Internal rhetorical questions expose unstable self-knowledge

Ayanokōji frequently asks himself questions rather than issuing final diagnoses.

He wonders:

- what friendship is;
- whether he ranks low as a friend;
- why Sakura's growth matters so much;
- whether romantic feeling exists;
- whether refusing Satō was foolish;
- what kind of person he is becoming;
- and whether Kei might someday become irreplaceable.

The interrogative form is important.

It means that the narrator's self-model is often under construction.

A later categorical metaphor should not be projected backward to answer every earlier question, and an earlier uncertainty should not be treated as evidence that no development occurs.

## 18.3 Comic self-talk is positive evidence

The comic voice has no obvious strategic audience.

Examples include:

- panic over how to make friends;
- `救世主平田マン`;
- proposal-like gift ideas;
- drawing “ten friends” and failing the assignment;
- gift miscalibration;
- romantic misunderstanding;
- and embarrassment around dating rituals.

This is not proof that every comic moment reports a deep stable emotion.

It is proof that the consciousness is not written as one continuous machine register.

The humor also prevents strategic mastery from becoming total reader identification. The person who controls an examination may be incompetent at birthday symbolism minutes later.

## 18.4 Strategic compression marks a register shift

When stakes become decisive, the prose changes.

It becomes:

- shorter;
- causal;
- taxonomic;
- contingency-focused;
- and less interested in the texture of another person's experience.

People become:

- capabilities;
- vulnerabilities;
- pieces;
- weapons;
- hosts;
- tools;
- or necessary parts.

The change is not evidence that the strategic voice alone is true.

It is evidence that one conditioned cognitive system has become dominant.

The most ethically consequential transitions are often unmarked. Concern for Kei can become attachment management. Rescue for Ryūen can become trust-building with Ichinose. Hirata's suffering can become recovery of a necessary class part.

The lack of rhetorical alarm tells us that instrumental conversion is normal to Ayanokōji even when it is not morally normal.

## 18.5 Self-correction is one of the strongest linguistic signs of development

The Volume 11 movement:

> `やるべきこと` → `やりたいこと`

is more important than a long abstract declaration.

The correction catches desire entering a sentence initially framed as obligation.

Similarly, Ayanokōji often qualifies or interrupts himself when ordinary meaning exceeds his preferred strategic account.

The voice therefore does not simply hide development.

It records development at the point where his existing grammar fails to contain it.

## 18.6 Negation can be reliable without being exhaustive

`平田を思えばこそ？ 違う。 / Because I care about Hirata? No.` should not be softened.

He explicitly refuses to sentimentalize the coercive intervention.

But the same episode produces his desire for a friend before whom he can show tears.

The correct reading is not that the negation was false.

It is that:

- the immediate decision was functionally motivated;
- the relation nevertheless produced personal recognition;
- and later meaning does not rewrite the motive that authorized the act.

This pattern recurs throughout Year 1.

## 18.7 Flat affect is partly a problem of public embodiment

Satō's observation that he was not smiling is evidence (`Y1V07.5`, indexed as `笑ってない`).

It demonstrates a gap between reported enjoyment and visible expression.

That gap can arise from:

- limited practice;
- compartmentalization;
- restraint;
- uncertainty;
- or shallow feeling in a particular moment.

Year 1 does not license one universal cause.

The important point is that affective display, interior response, and verbal classification are separate channels.

## 18.8 The first-person form makes the reader inhabit authored legibility

Ayanokōji does to the reader what he does to the school.

He supplies:

- enough ordinariness to remain plausible;
- enough analysis to explain outcomes;
- incomplete planning;
- delayed admissions;
- and self-descriptions that may be locally true without being total.

This does not make the novels an arbitrary deception game.

The reader can build a stronger model by comparing:

- what he says;
- what he withholds;
- what he does;
- what other viewpoints reveal;
- and what consequences exceed his explanation.

The form therefore teaches the same epistemic lesson as *First File*:

> a usable account may remain incomplete.

## 18.9 Voice is not the same as moral endorsement

The intimacy of first-person narration can make strategic elegance feel natural.

Because the reasoning is coherent, the reader may experience manipulation first as problem-solving and only later as invasion.

The analytical method must restore what the narrator's compression can remove:

- bodies;
- fear;
- third-party cost;
- refusal;
- and the difference between predicting a person and receiving authority over them.

Ayanokōji's voice is ethically productive precisely because it is persuasive.

# 19. The metaphor system

Ayanokōji's metaphors are not casual ornament.

They are technologies of cognitive compression.

They convert complicated persons and relationships into models that can be acted upon quickly.

## 19.1 Product, masterpiece, defect, and property

The White Room field uses manufacturing language.

- `最高傑作 / masterpiece` praises output.
- `不良品 / defective product` marks governability problems.
- `所有物 / property` claims total jurisdiction.

Ayanokōji rejects property status while continuing to understand himself through the capacities the production system created.

The unresolved identity task is not to discover a pre-manufactured pure self.

It is to decide what to do with a self whose materials were partly authored by coercive institutions.

## 19.2 Tools, weapons, pieces, and parts

`道具`, `武器`, game pieces, and `パーツ` make people legible through function.

The grammar has real advantages.

It helps Ayanokōji identify:

- contextual strengths;
- complementarities;
- hidden capacity;
- and ways a fragmented class can survive.

Its ethical cost is substitution.

The person may disappear behind the role the operation requires.

Hirata becomes a necessary part.

Sudō becomes a weapon Horikita should acquire.

Kei becomes social intelligence.

The model can be accurate and still be morally incomplete.

## 19.3 Parasite and host

Kei initially describes herself through `寄生虫 / parasite` logic. Ayanokōji later calls himself her `宿木 / host`.

These metaphors capture trauma-shaped dependency.

They also lag behind Kei's actual agency.

On the rooftop she chooses dignity over immediate safety. Later she negotiates, challenges, and answers romance for herself.

The metaphor remains operationally useful and descriptively insufficient.

That gap is precisely what Year 1 wants the reader to notice.

## 19.4 The door

`オレは今扉だ / right now, I am a door` is one of his least proprietary metaphors.

A door:

- does not force entry;
- does not determine what the person says after opening;
- and offers transition without claiming to be the destination.

The metaphor is not perfectly nondirective because Ayanokōji continues managing the larger Ichinose environment.

It nevertheless demonstrates that he can imagine a relational role less total than teacher, rescuer, owner, or judge.

## 19.5 The textbook

`教科書 / textbook` is the romance ending's most dangerous metaphor.

It names Kei as an educational medium through which Ayanokōji can learn love, the opposite sex, and perhaps how to alter dependency.

The metaphor should not be softened into mutual learning.

The private curriculum is asymmetrical.

At the same time, the relationship now contains explicit mutual answer, particular desire, purposeless conversation, and a hoped-for `掛け替えのない存在`.

The metaphor therefore does not prove that romance is fake.

It proves that instrumental pedagogy remains active inside romance.

## 19.6 Warmth, touch, and bodily language

`人肌のぬくもり / warmth of human skin` belongs to a different metaphor family.

It does not reduce a person to function.

It registers:

- presence;
- proximity;
- mortality;
- and an experience unavailable in abstract strategic models.

The slow walk with Sakayanagi, Hirata's tears, Kei's embrace, and the reluctance to end a phone call all move toward relation as lived time rather than relation as role.

## 19.7 Models fail most productively when the person exceeds them

Year 1's strongest relational moments are often moments of model failure.

- Kei refuses betrayal despite the parasite forecast.
- Horikita independently defeats Kushida's Paper Shuffle plan.
- Airi enters the group when authorship is withheld.
- Ryūen's followers preserve him after fear alone should no longer explain loyalty.
- Ichinose's class accepts truth without dissolving solidarity.
- Hirata's classmates refuse the hostile identity he tries to perform.

The ethical lesson is not that models are useless.

It is that a person becomes most visible when the model fails—and that failure should produce revision rather than coercive correction of the person back into the model.

Some developments concern what the reader learns was already true.

Others concern capacities, desires, relationships, and purposes that genuinely change during the year.

The distinction is necessary because a retrospective synthesis can easily convert late knowledge into an illusion that the early narrator was already complete.

## 20.1 What is revealed rather than developed

The following are primarily revelations of preexisting structure:

- advanced academic and physical training;
- authored mediocrity;
- the victory-conditioned fallback;
- limited ordinary autobiographical experience;
- White Room origin;
- and the extent to which he can tolerate violence and pressure.

These disclosures reframe earlier scenes.

They do not prove that every later relationship motive was already present in complete form.

## 20.2 What genuinely develops

The following change materially during Year 1:

- the number and quality of ordinary relationships;
- the ability to name friendship;
- sensory and aesthetic preference;
- interest in cinema, music, books, and seasonal experience;
- willingness to reveal selected capacity;
- desire for a worthy contest;
- particular protection and attachment;
- ability to preserve another person's refusal in some contexts;
- explicit self-chosen purpose;
- and the possibility of reciprocal romance.

The development is nonlinear.

Ayanokōji can become more relationally invested and more capable of sophisticated manipulation at the same time.

## 20.3 The sealed-core hypothesis remains a hypothesis

Volume 11.5 has Ayanokōji fear that apparent emotional change may be only plating falling from a sealed core (`Y1V11.5-E105`).

That fear deserves serious weight.

It is not narrator-certified ontology.

The Year 1 corpus gives too much contrary evidence to treat the “sealed core” as settled fact:

- direct friendship desire precedes major strategic escalation;
- ordinary experience accumulates;
- relational loss matters;
- purposeless time gains intrinsic value;
- and he repeatedly encounters responses he did not fully predict.

The statement is best understood as the conditioned person's fear that no experience can finally alter what conditioning made.

Whether the fear is correct belongs to later evidence.

# 21. Strong counterarguments and their limits

A rigorous character synthesis should confront the strongest alternative readings rather than dismissing them as fandom simplifications.

## 21.1 Counterargument: the ordinary adolescent voice is camouflage

The strongest version argues:

- Ayanokōji deliberately constructs mediocrity;
- his first-person narration withholds plans;
- the tools monologue is marked `本心`;
- and ordinary comedy makes the later reveal more surprising.

Therefore, friendship anxiety, attraction, and awkwardness may be reader-facing camouflage rather than genuine interiority.

### Response

This argument correctly identifies strategic concealment.

It overextends concealment into a total explanation.

Private thoughts about:

- loneliness;
- attraction;
- food;
- books;
- gift failure;
- cinema;
- wanting a friend;
- and wanting to see Kei

often have no in-world audience and no tactical need.

The narration can conceal competence while still reporting genuine ordinary desire.

The better conclusion is layered disclosure, not global fabrication.

## 21.2 Counterargument: `本心` makes the tools doctrine the final truth

The strongest version argues that Volume 3 explicitly labels the victory/tools passage his true feelings. All later warmth should therefore be interpreted as useful surface behavior or temporary novelty.

### Response

`本心` gives the passage exceptional local authority.

It establishes that the victory-conditioned doctrine is genuinely part of him.

It does not logically establish:

- that no other genuine motive exists;
- that all future experience is incapable of development;
- or that one activated doctrine is the total ontology of a person.

The same volume contains direct friendship, embarrassment, school-life desire, and concern with Ike and Sakura. The literary achievement lies in refusing to let one register erase the other.

## 21.3 Counterargument: Ayanokōji is simply amoral; psychological nuance obscures responsibility

This argument points to:

- sexualized coercion;
- delayed rescue;
- trauma use;
- rumor saturation;
- classmate pruning;
- and coercive repair.

It warns that speaking of conditioning or underdeveloped reciprocity can become apology.

### Response

The warning is valid.

Explanation must not become exculpation.

The analysis therefore keeps several acts in the category of severe ethical failure even when they produce later benefit.

At the same time, “simply amoral” cannot explain:

- privacy restraint;
- refusal preservation;
- forgiveness language;
- differentiated concern;
- desire for friendship;
- willingness to protect people at cost;
- or discomfort with ownership over himself.

His moral structure is inconsistent, not absent.

## 21.4 Counterargument: outcomes justify the method

The strongest consequentialist defense is substantial.

- Kei becomes safer, socially powerful, and capable of confronting trauma.
- Horikita becomes a better leader.
- Hirata returns to function.
- Ichinose recovers.
- Class D survives.
- Ryūen's continued presence creates future capacity.

Ayanokōji often succeeds where gentler actors fail.

### Response

Success answers whether the intervention worked.

It does not answer:

- who bore the risk;
- whether the subject knew the plan;
- whether refusal was meaningful;
- whether third parties were harmed;
- what less invasive alternatives existed;
- or whether the developer retained unaccountable future power.

A method can be effective and still violate the person whose future it improves.

## 21.5 Counterargument: Kei proves that he already loves conventionally

This reading emphasizes:

- medicine;
- protection;
- gifts;
- private naming;
- mutual confession;
- embrace;
- purposeless conversation;
- and direct desire to see her.

### Response

These are strong evidence of particular care, attraction, attachment, and reciprocal romance.

Year 1 nevertheless retains:

- `教科書`;
- dependency-cure logic;
- the imagined endpoint of the educational role;
- incomplete disclosure;
- and irreplaceability as prayer rather than completed certainty.

The relationship is more than instrumentality and less than securely demonstrated conventional love.

## 21.6 Counterargument: he lacks empathy

The argument draws on his willingness to impose pain and his use of people as functions.

### Response

If empathy means accurate recognition of another person's internal state, he has exceptional cognitive empathy.

If it means concrete response to another person's condition, practical empathy is also strong.

If it means affective resonance, Year 1 provides real but uneven evidence.

If it means treating another person's autonomy as a binding limit, his empathy is morally insufficient.

The problem is not simple absence.

It is asymmetry: he understands more than he reciprocates.

## 21.7 Counterargument: he is socially omniscient

His predictions are frequently correct, and the plot often reveals that he prepared contingencies earlier than the reader knew.

### Response

Year 1 repeatedly records limitations:

- ordinary preference knowledge;
- gift symbolism;
- broad class information gathering;
- public leadership;
- romantic cues;
- and model failure when people exceed trauma or fear predictions.

His strongest intelligence is diagnostic and environmental, not universal.

## 21.8 Counterargument: because he is unreliable, no internal statement can be trusted

### Response

Global skepticism destroys the evidentiary structure of first-person fiction.

The method instead classifies reliability by domain.

- Event mechanics are often strong.
- Planning is underdisclosed.
- Motives may be partial.
- metaphors are operative models;
- emotions may precede labels;
- future forecasts are beliefs.

Selective unreliability produces a more testable interpretation than declaring every line arbitrary.

## 22.1 Principal developmental injury

Without assigning a diagnosis, Year 1 supports a clear developmental deprivation.

Ayanokōji was formed in an environment that supplied:

- skill;
- discipline;
- competition;
- consequence;
- and externally assigned purpose

while withholding much of:

- ordinary social time;
- unproductive preference;
- reciprocal intimacy;
- self-chosen ends;
- and freedom from instrumental evaluation.

His ordinary deficits are therefore not random quirks.

They are the negative space of the curriculum that made him exceptional.

## 22.2 Principal adaptive strength

He can separate immediate appearance from causal structure.

This allows him to:

- see neglected capacity;
- identify hidden coercion;
- preserve people others would discard;
- and build environments in which fragmented abilities become usable.

## 22.3 Principal adaptive danger

The same separation makes it easy to discount present experience in favor of future result.

Pain becomes an investment.

Ignorance becomes a useful condition.

Refusal becomes another variable.

A person becomes the future capacity he expects to produce.

## 22.4 Principal relational need

He wants recognition that does not become ownership.

Manabu, Sakayanagi, Ryūen, Kei, Hiyori, and the Ayanokōji Group provide different partial forms of that need.

The most valuable relation is not necessarily the one that praises him.

It is the relation in which another person remains real enough to answer back.

## 22.5 Principal fear

The explicit fear is recapture by Atsuomi and loss of school freedom.

A deeper Year 1 fear is that ordinary development may never alter the strategic core—that experience is only plating over something sealed.

The text does not settle whether this fear is true.

It shows that the fear itself can become self-fulfilling if he interprets every new relation through the old curriculum.

# 23. Ethical profile: virtue and failure arise from the same capacity

Ayanokōji's principal virtue and principal ethical failure are not opposites.

They arise from the same ability.

## 23.1 Principal virtue — he sees developmental possibility where static systems see defects

He recognizes that:

- Ike's practical knowledge can matter;
- Sakura's hidden courage can become action;
- Sudō can develop;
- Horikita can become a leader rather than a solitary high performer;
- Kei's social ecology is genuine ability;
- Hirata can recover;
- Ryūen can grow after defeat;
- and Ichinose's wrongdoing need not become permanent identity.

This is a major moral achievement in a school organized around classification and expulsion.

## 23.2 Principal ethical failure — he mistakes developmental insight for jurisdiction

Because he sees a possible future, he often assumes authority to design the route toward it.

The route may include:

- suffering;
- deception;
- withheld rescue;
- humiliation;
- bodily invasion;
- rumor;
- or survival allocation.

The future person's likely gratitude can then become a silent justification for overriding the present person's refusal.

## 23.3 Why the virtue is not false

It would be too easy to say that development language is merely self-serving manipulation.

Ayanokōji repeatedly preserves people who are not immediately necessary and expresses genuine interest in what they may become.

He also chooses lower-coercion methods in several cases.

The developmental concern is real.

## 23.4 Why the failure is not accidental

The problem recurs across different contexts and relationships.

It is not one isolated lapse.

His conditioning encourages a sequence:

1. model the person;
2. identify desired future state;
3. determine effective pressure;
4. withhold full information;
5. intervene;
6. judge the outcome.

Reciprocal authorship would interrupt the sequence before step three by asking what future the other person chooses and what methods they authorize.

## 23.5 The mature Year 1 standard

The final ethical standard is not nonintervention.

People often need help, pressure, protection, education, or challenge.

The standard is:

> **Does the intervention enlarge another person's agency while leaving the person enough information, refusal capacity, and corrective authority to make the resulting life their own?**

By this standard Ayanokōji has already demonstrated capability for better practice.

He has not yet made that practice reliable.

# 24. Claim-status audit

## 24.1 Confirmed at the Year 1 boundary

- Ayanokōji deliberately authors mediocre visibility.
- The victory/tools doctrine is a genuine internal structure.
- Ordinary friendship, attraction, pleasure, embarrassment, and leisure are also genuine.
- He possesses exceptional cognitive empathy and inconsistent autonomy restraint.
- His best strategic talent is environmental and informational authorship rather than abstract deduction alone.
- He can distinguish event from identity and articulate forgiveness.
- He can use sexualized coercion and deliberate suffering instrumentally.
- He values selected people beyond immediate utility.
- He wants a worthy contest and can imagine genuine defeat as liberation from his creator's claim.
- Ordinary school life becomes an explicit self-chosen goal.
- Developmental authorship becomes a self-chosen desire.
- Kei and Ayanokōji enter explicit reciprocal romance.
- The romance retains an undisclosed developmental curriculum.
- Ayanokōji's models are powerful and incomplete.

## 24.2 Strengthened by V11.5 and *First File*

- ordinary life is a positive end rather than mere escape;
- purposeless conversation can possess intrinsic value;
- direct present-tense desire for Kei exists;
- development can propagate horizontally without a superior designer;
- Horikita's independence can coexist with acknowledged influence;
- self-authorship does not guarantee reciprocal ethics;
- and institutional files can accurately record bounded visibility without exhausting the person.

# 27. Final synthesis

The governing Year 1 question is:

> **Can a person whose own development was authored by others learn to care for people without reproducing that authorship over them?**

Year 1's answer is neither yes nor no.

It establishes that Ayanokōji can care.

He can:

- desire friendship;
- protect privacy;
- preserve refusal;
- buy medicine;
- make guarantees;
- separate wrongdoing from total identity;
- value tears, touch, books, gifts, walks, phone calls, and seasonal time;
- refuse to use Satō merely for experience;
- and ask Kei for an answer rather than issue an order.

It also establishes that care does not reliably constrain control.

He can:

- manufacture vulnerability;
- delay rescue;
- threaten sexual violation;
- intensify psychological crisis;
- use confidential information;
- privately rank lives for protection;
- allocate survival;
- and retain hidden developmental purposes inside romance.

The central contradiction is not that one set of acts is fake.

It is that the same capacity powers both.

Ayanokōji sees how environments make people.

That insight lets him rescue capacities others would discard.

It also tempts him to believe that the person who understands the environment has earned the right to design it.

Year 1 gives him three forms of liberation.

First, he escapes direct ownership.

Second, he discovers ordinary life as a positive end.

Third, he chooses a purpose for himself.

The third form creates the hardest problem because the purpose he chooses is developmental authorship.

He wants to cultivate people.

The year therefore introduces a fourth freedom that he has not yet mastered:

> **the freedom of other people to become something the developer did not design.**

Horikita, Kei, Ichinose, Ryūen, Hirata, Airi, Hiyori, and the other classes matter because they repeatedly exceed the roles assigned to them.

Their excess is not noise in Ayanokōji's model.

It is evidence of personhood.

*First File* closes the argument by turning the whole year into records and then showing why records are not enough. Ayanokōji's file is accurate about the person he permitted the institution to observe and radically incomplete about the person who existed. His own psychological models possess the same structure: useful, predictive, and bounded.

The final Year 1 judgment is therefore:

> **Ayanokōji has moved beyond being only the White Room's product, but he has not moved beyond White Room grammar whenever he translates care into curriculum, protection into sovereignty, or understanding into jurisdiction. His strongest development is not that he now possesses emotion. It is that friendship, ordinary life, rivalry, and romance have begun generating meanings his strategic vocabulary cannot fully contain. His decisive unresolved task is to let those meanings correct him—and to let the people he understands remain authors of themselves.**
