# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ』
## Year 1 — Relationships, Dependency, Friendship, Romance, and Recognition
### Reciprocity under leverage: whether bonds survive refusal, truth, and role change

> **CANONICAL YEAR-1 SPECIALIST SNAPSHOT**
>
> This document is the primary analytical home for the relationship architecture of *Classroom of the Elite* Year 1. It synthesizes the fourteen canonical Japanese novel deep readings, the *First File* paratext audit, the final Year 1 evidence lock, the canonical `THROUGH_Y1` relationship and character ledgers, the Japanese terminology index, and the frozen `Y1_01–Y1_03` specialist layer.
>
> It does not import Year 2, Volume 0, *Second List*, Year 3, adaptation material, wikis, or later-franchise interpretation. Relationships that Year 1 leaves unresolved remain unresolved here.

# 0. Scope, primary home, and interpretive controls

Year 1 does not offer a clean movement from false relationships to true relationships.

Its major bonds are mixed structures.

A relationship may contain:

- genuine affection;
- strategic utility;
- unequal knowledge;
- protection;
- dependency;
- fear;
- public role;
- private recognition;
- coercive origin;
- voluntary continuation;
- developmental benefit;
- and moral injury

at the same time.

The analytical task is therefore not to decide whether a bond is “real” or “fake” by selecting whichever element seems emotionally dominant. It is to explain how the elements interact, what changes over time, and whether the relationship gives both participants meaningful authorship over what the bond becomes.

This document applies seven controls.

## 0.1 Affection is not consent

A person may later care deeply for someone who earlier violated, deceived, pressured, or endangered her.

The later affection is not thereby false.

It also does not retroactively authorize the earlier method.

This distinction is indispensable for Ayanokōji and Kei, but it also applies to Manabu and Suzune, Ayanokōji and Hirata, and every developmental relationship in which later benefit is used to defend earlier coercion.

## 0.2 Protection is not ownership

Protection can be:

- humane care;
- institutional duty;
- strategic investment;
- emotional attachment;
- reputational insurance;
- exclusive possession;
- or some combination of these.

The decisive questions are:

- who determines the terms of rescue;
- whether the protected person knows the costs;
- whether refusal is possible;
- whether protection must remain exclusive;
- and whether safety increases the person's future freedom or makes the protector indispensable.

## 0.3 Dependency is not the opposite of autonomy

No important Year 1 relationship is free of dependency.

Students depend upon:

- information;
- social status;
- class points;
- emotional reassurance;
- institutional protection;
- practical expertise;
- and other people's willingness to remain.

The relevant distinction is not dependency versus independence. It is between dependency that is:

- acknowledged or concealed;
- plural or monopolized;
- chosen or manufactured;
- contestable or compulsory;
- reversible or catastrophic to leave.

Autonomy is better understood as the ability to participate in dependence without losing the capacity to answer back.

## 0.4 Recognition has several meanings

Year 1 repeatedly separates four forms of recognition.

### Epistemic recognition

Someone accurately sees another person's ability, trauma, motive, or concealed identity.

### Social recognition

A group or institution credits a person with status, office, ability, or legitimacy.

### Relational recognition

Someone treats the other as a particular person rather than a replaceable function.

### Moral recognition

Someone treats the other as a person whose consent, ends, and future remain independently significant.

These forms can diverge.

Ayanokōji may understand another student with extraordinary precision while failing to respect her jurisdiction over the method of intervention. Hirata may morally value everyone while struggling to make discriminating judgments under conflict. Sakayanagi may see Ayanokōji's hidden capacity while also turning him into a test case for her theory. An institutional file may accurately record visible output without recognizing the person beyond that output.

## 0.5 Reciprocity does not require equality of power

Many relationships are inevitably unequal.

A teacher may know more than a learner. A protector may possess more force. A leader may control more information. An older sibling may have shaped a younger one before either can negotiate the relation as equals.

Reciprocity requires something more specific:

- the less powerful person can refuse without annihilating consequences;
- disagreement does not automatically terminate belonging;
- the stronger person can be corrected;
- information asymmetry can become visible and contestable;
- each participant can retain ends not supplied by the other;
- and the relationship can survive the other's development into someone less useful, less dependent, or less predictable.

## 0.6 Role change is the strongest stress test

A relationship is most revealing when its original role becomes unavailable.

Can Kei and Hirata remain connected after the false romance ends?

Can Suzune and Manabu remain siblings after imitation ends?

Can Horikita and Ichinose preserve respect after their alliance expires?

Can Ayanokōji value Kei if she no longer needs his protection?

Can Ryūen's followers remain after he loses the undefeated image that justified fear?

Can the Ayanokōji Group remain friends if the hidden strategist becomes publicly legible?

Role survival is therefore a better test than emotional intensity alone.

## 0.7 Year 1 must remain a Year 1 boundary

The source boundary ends with Volume 11.5 and *First File*.

The analysis may identify trajectories and risks. It may not use later material to declare which relationship eventually succeeds, fails, becomes romantic, becomes autonomous, or proves instrumental.

# 1. Executive thesis

The strongest Year 1 relationship thesis is:

> **Reciprocity is not the absence of asymmetry. It is the capacity of a relationship to make asymmetry answerable—to preserve refusal, contradiction, independent purpose, and the possibility that the other person will become someone the relationship did not design.**

Year 1 begins with characters who often treat relationship as a solution to vulnerability.

Kei needs a host.

Hirata needs universal harmony because he cannot bear another social collapse.

Horikita needs Manabu's recognition to certify her worth.

Kushida needs other people's trust to confirm her superiority and protect the hidden self.

Ryūen treats fear as the universal lever beneath apparent loyalty.

Ichinose's class turns mutual protection into collective identity.

Ayanokōji wants friends and ordinary life, but enters relationships through concealment, utility analysis, environmental authorship, and private protection hierarchies.

None of these structures is wholly irrational.

They are adaptations to danger.

The problem is that adaptation can become jurisdiction.

The protector may decide what the protected person should suffer.

The leader may decide whose future is expendable.

The developer may decide what another person's growth is for.

The admired sibling may become the specification of a self.

The trusted friend may accumulate secrets that make departure unsafe.

The class may call solidarity humane while making one leader's survival structurally indispensable.

Year 1's relational development is therefore not a moral purification from strategy into sincerity.

It is a gradual multiplication of ways to answer back.

Kei refuses betrayal while still afraid.

Horikita reconstructs Ayanokōji's hidden influence and demands auditable tests.

Sudō becomes capable of opposing the leader who helped him.

Kushida refuses the handshake and articulates why knowledge itself remains coercive.

Hiyori refuses Ryūen's rooftop operation without abandoning her class.

Ibuki, Ishizaki, and Albert preserve Ryūen after fear ceases to explain the whole bond.

Kanzaki supports Ichinose without requiring purity.

Keisei defines loyalty as support plus dissent.

Manabu finally recognizes Suzune by releasing her from imitation.

Ichinose treats Ayanokōji as trustworthy without surrendering class rivalry.

Kei answers the confession rather than merely receiving a role.

These acts do not erase the underlying asymmetries.

They alter their meaning.

A relationship becomes more reciprocal when the other person's response is no longer reducible to the strongest actor's model.

This is why Year 1's most important relational distinction is not between utility and affection. Utility and affection coexist throughout the series.

The deeper distinction is between:

> **recognition that enlarges another person's authorship**

and

> **recognition that makes the person easier to govern.**

Ayanokōji's greatest relational strength and greatest danger arise from the same capacity. He sees people. He notices abilities, wounds, lies, social functions, and developmental possibilities that institutions and classmates miss.

That sight can become care.

It can also become possession.

*First File* supplies the final epistemic correction. A useful file remains smaller than the person. The same is true of every psychological model, romantic category, leadership role, class reputation, and theory of dependency in Year 1.

The canonical Year 1 relationship principle is therefore:

> **To recognize a person is not only to understand her. It is to preserve her power to exceed the understanding.**

# 2. A relational test framework

The architecture requires every major relationship to be examined across common dimensions. These dimensions should not be collapsed into one “health score.” A relationship may improve on one axis while deteriorating on another.

## 2.1 Origin

How does the bond begin?

- voluntary attraction;
- institutional assignment;
- shared class membership;
- crisis;
- blackmail;
- sibling formation;
- common interest;
- rivalry;
- or a protective bargain?

Origins matter because later continuity may either reproduce or transform the original structure.

## 2.2 Information symmetry

What does each person know about:

- the other's motives;
- ability;
- trauma;
- hidden plans;
- alternative relationships;
- and the costs of remaining?

Emotional sincerity does not eliminate informational domination.

## 2.3 Power symmetry

Power includes more than physical force.

It includes:

- social credibility;
- class office;
- access to secrets;
- control of evidence;
- points;
- protection;
- public reputation;
- and the ability to define the official story.

## 2.4 Leverage

Can one person impose costs through:

- exposure;
- abandonment;
- expulsion;
- physical force;
- reputational damage;
- withdrawal of protection;
- or loss of class standing?

Leverage may remain unused and still shape choice.

## 2.5 Refusal capacity

Can the person say no?

More importantly:

- does the person know what she is refusing;
- is refusal materially survivable;
- and can the relationship continue after refusal?

## 2.6 Dependency

What needs are concentrated in the bond?

Dependency may involve:

- safety;
- emotional regulation;
- identity;
- public status;
- information;
- ordinary companionship;
- future orientation;
- or access to a valued role.

## 2.7 Protection

Does protection:

- create space for independent growth;
- monopolize the protected person's alternatives;
- conceal its costs;
- preserve exit;
- or require the protector to remain indispensable?

## 2.8 Strategic utility

What function does each person perform?

Utility is not evidence that intimacy is false. The question is whether the person remains valuable after the function changes or disappears.

## 2.9 Emotional disclosure

Who risks being known?

A relationship may contain intense practical cooperation while one participant withholds nearly every fact that would make him vulnerable to ordinary judgment.

## 2.10 Ability to contradict

Can one participant tell the other:

- you are wrong;
- your method is unacceptable;
- I choose a different end;
- or I will not perform the role you assigned?

Contradiction is one of Year 1's clearest measures of relational agency.

## 2.11 Fungibility and irreplaceability

Would another person with the same function be an adequate substitute?

`必要不可欠 / indispensable` is not identical to `かけがえのない / irreplaceable`.

The first may describe a system dependency.

The second describes singular relational value.

## 2.12 Separation and role survival

Can the relationship survive:

- graduation;
- class rivalry;
- the end of a false romance;
- loss of leadership;
- reduction of dependency;
- or greater public knowledge?

A bond that requires one person never to change roles remains structurally fragile even when emotionally intense.

# 3. The Year 1 relational progression

Year 1's relationship architecture develops in five movements parallel to the broader year structure.

## 3.1 Volumes 1–2: friendship as lack, and truth as a relational problem

Ayanokōji enters school explicitly wanting `友達 / friends`, but lacking the practical knowledge required to create ordinary connection (`Y1V01-E003–E004`, `Y1V01-E010`).

Horikita rejects pressured friendship as false and treats self-sufficiency as dignity (`Y1V01-E009`).

Volume 2 then establishes that relationship is part of truth production. Sudō's account cannot become institutionally effective without credibility, advocacy, evidence, and someone willing to believe him. Ayanokōji distinguishes personal trust from merely wanting a story to be true (`Y1V02-E025`, `Y1V02-E045`).

The early principle is:

> truth does not enter the world alone; it travels through relationships of credibility.

## 3.2 Volumes 3–4: companionship, trust, and authored dependency

The island examination makes `仲間 / companions` operationally necessary. Horikita's acceptance of Ayanokōji as a companion marks a break with solitary excellence (`Y1V03-E042–E044`).

The zodiac examination then turns trust into an explicit political resource. Ichinose uses bounded openness to create cooperation. Ryūen centralizes information through coercion. Ayanokōji seeks “eyes and ears” and identifies Kei's social authority as a strategic capacity (`Y1V04-E019–E022`, `Y1V04-E026`).

The relationship with Kei reveals the darkest form of this movement: another person's vulnerability is not merely discovered but placed inside an authored environment so that protection becomes necessary (`Y1V04-E033–E055`).

## 3.3 Volumes 4.5–7.5: counter-curriculum, chosen loyalty, and negotiated partnership

Ordinary summer life demonstrates that relationships can matter without an exam objective. Ayanokōji names friendship with unusual hesitation—`友達……だからな`—and sometimes preserves another person's refusal rather than optimizing it (`Y1V04.5-E036`, `Y1V04.5-E039`, `Y1V04.5-E048`).

The sports festival and Paper Shuffle make development bilateral. Horikita and Sudō learn that each must enter the other's rhythm. Horikita and Kushida create covenant without affection. The Ayanokōji Group develops a low-pressure model of belonging.

Volume 7 then tests relationship under fear. Kei remains terrified and refuses to reveal Ayanokōji's identity (`Y1V07-E061–E064`). Ryūen's followers remain after his defeat (`Y1V07-E101–E105`). Hiyori demonstrates bounded ethical refusal (`Y1V07-E051`).

Volume 7.5 turns crisis aftermath into negotiation. Haruka defines friendship through voluntary attendance and the right to decline—`好きなときに集まって嫌なときに断る` (`Y1V07.5-E035`). Kei can object, negotiate, and offer rescue in return. Ayanokōji refuses Satō's transparent confession rather than accepting a romance he cannot reciprocate sufficiently (`Y1V07.5-E052–E067`, `E082–E100`).

## 3.4 Volumes 8–10: trust as weapon, constitutional relationship, and forced disposability

The Mixed Training Camp shows that compulsory proximity can create real temporary community. It also shows Nagumo treating accumulated trust as attack infrastructure. Trust lowers defenses; betrayal converts that lowered defense into political force (`Y1V08-E088–E105`, `E134–E135`).

Volume 9 makes the ownership of narrative central. Ichinose's class accepts a true wrongdoing without expelling her from membership. Nagumo and Sakayanagi convert trusted access into leverage. Ayanokōji combines nonjudgmental presence with paternalistic control of the information environment (`Y1V09-E033–E036`, `Y1V09-E087–E100`).

The Class Vote forces relationships to confront scarcity. Friendship, leadership, class utility, and the right to continue becoming are no longer separable. Horikita and Hirata become constitutional opponents. Ichinose's class attempts collective rescue. Ryūen's followers help preserve a leader whose fear-based rule has already been broken.

## 3.5 Volumes 11–11.5 and *First File*: tears, reciprocal authorship, romance, and the limits of the model

Volume 11 introduces the desire for `涙を見せられるような友 / a friend before whom one can show tears` (`Y1V11-E105`). Hirata and Ayanokōji move from functional repair toward given-name particularity. Sakayanagi and Ayanokōji's rivalry continues through touch and shared time after the official result is corrupted.

Volume 11.5 makes separation productive rather than annihilating. Manabu recognizes Suzune by releasing her from imitation. Horikita and Ichinose end an alliance without ending respect. Hiyori corrects Ryūen. Keisei defines loyalty as support plus dissent. Ayanokōji gives Ichinose a future horizon while she preserves class agency. Kei answers the confession explicitly (`Y1V11.5-E011`, `E049–E063`, `E079–E105`, `E131–E141`).

*First File* then converts people into profiles, ratings, timelines, and institutional summaries. Its fiction returns to lived relation: purposeless conversation can be enjoyable; ending contact can feel difficult; Ayanokōji can simply want to see Kei; Ichinose can fail to fit him inside `大切な友達 / precious friend` while still returning to class rivalry (`Y1FF-E059–E082`).

The year closes on the proposition that every relationship category is a model.

A useful model may still be incomplete.

# 4. Ayanokōji and Kei — agency inside dependency, romance inside hidden curriculum

Ayanokōji–Kei is the central relationship study of Year 1 because no other bond combines so many apparently incompatible truths.

The relationship is:

- coercively founded;
- strategically useful;
- physically and informationally asymmetric;
- protective;
- emotionally particular;
- increasingly negotiated;
- explicitly romantic by the endpoint;
- and still governed in part by purposes Kei does not know.

Any reading that removes one of these elements becomes easier and less accurate.

## 4.1 Kei before Ayanokōji: social power organized around fear

Kei's public identity is not a false surface hiding an otherwise powerless person.

Her control of atmosphere, status, female-group response, and social circulation is real ability. Ayanokōji recognizes her `場を支配する力 / power to control the social field` before most people understand what she is doing (`Y1V04-E026`).

The public capacity is built around a private survival doctrine.

Kei names herself `寄生虫 / parasite` (`Y1V04-E004`). The metaphor is trauma-authored. She believes survival requires attachment to a stronger host whose status deters renewed victimization.

Hirata supplies the first host.

Their false romance gives her:

- public protection;
- access to class legitimacy;
- social stability;
- and a partner whose reputation discourages attack.

The arrangement also preserves exit as a moral possibility. Hirata does not demand intimacy or private service in exchange. He wants Kei eventually to become capable of leaving the protection structure.

This becomes the first major comparison between two models of protection.

### Hirata's protection

- universal rather than exclusive;
- status-based rather than physically sovereign;
- oriented toward eventual independence;
- morally constrained against retaliation;
- and therefore incapable of giving Kei the exclusive priority she wants during crisis (`Y1V04-E036–E040`).

### Ayanokōji's protection

- exclusive;
- strategically comprehensive;
- backed by evidence and physical force;
- willing to retaliate and manipulate;
- and designed to make his continued presence necessary.

The second model is more effective at eliminating the immediate threat.

It is also more proprietary.

## 4.2 Origin: recognition becomes leverage

Ayanokōji correctly sees two things.

First, Kei has underrecognized social capacity.

Second, her fear of renewed bullying organizes her choices.

Accurate recognition does not lead him to ask how to create safe cooperation.

It leads him to design a recruitment environment.

He:

- reactivates Manabe's hostility;
- prevents Yukimura from intervening;
- uses Hirata as lure;
- selects a private location;
- allows the assault to escalate;
- delays rescue;
- reveals knowledge of Kei's past;
- uses bodily and sexualized coercion;
- examines the scar she protects;
- and offers stronger protection after narrowing alternatives (`Y1V04-E021`, `E033`, `E041–E055`).

The protector compact—she works for him, he protects her—does not emerge from informed negotiation.

It emerges after Ayanokōji has helped produce the urgency that makes the offer rational.

This is manufactured dependency in the strict sense.

The relationship's later warmth cannot be used to describe this origin as secretly consensual.

## 4.3 Volume 7: betrayal, refusal, and the part of Kei he did not author

Ayanokōji later repeats the logic at a higher level.

He severs communication, predicts that abandonment will intensify Kei's loneliness, receives her emergency message, delays intervention, and allows Ryūen's operation to reach the point at which loyalty can be tested.

Kei initially hopes the protection promise will be honored (`Y1V07-E061`).

Ryūen offers immediate relief if she identifies Ayanokōji.

She refuses—`絶対に、言わない / I will never tell` (`Y1V07-E064`).

The refusal must be interpreted with precision.

It does not prove complete independence.

Kei remains:

- afraid;
- attached;
- grateful;
- dependent upon protection;
- and hopeful that Ayanokōji may come.

But dependency does not fully explain the act.

The survival-maximizing path is betrayal. She chooses dignity, attachment, pride, or loyalty instead.

Ayanokōji authored the field in which the decision occurred.

He did not wholly author the answer.

That is the most important correction to the parasite model.

A model that predicts Kei chiefly through survival dependence fails at the moment when she accepts continued suffering rather than use the available exit.

## 4.4 Rescue without innocence

Ayanokōji's arrival is real rescue.

Kei's fear changes when he appears, and his control of the aftermath protects her from renewed external blackmail (`Y1V07-E080`, `E093–E097`).

The rescue is not innocent.

He admits that:

- he betrayed her;
- he delayed intervention;
- the ordeal deepened reliance;
- refusal confirmed loyalty;
- and betrayal would also have generated guilt leverage (`Y1V07-E093–E095`).

He calls himself her new `宿木 / host`.

The relation therefore becomes emotionally stronger through an event whose additional suffering purchased information and control.

This is exactly why consequence cannot settle legitimacy.

A good outcome and a coercive method can both be true.

## 4.5 From coerced asset to negotiating partner

The later Year 1 relationship changes substantially.

Kei is not merely sent instructions.

She:

- reads Ayanokōji accurately;
- asks why he hides ability;
- objects to how he treats her;
- negotiates continued cooperation;
- uses private names;
- questions his relationships;
- offers intelligence rather than only obedience;
- and says `あたしが助けてあげる / I will help or save you` (`Y1V07.5-E082–E100`).

The offer of reciprocal rescue is especially important.

Kei no longer imagines herself only as protected object. She wants a role in which Ayanokōji may also need her.

That does not equalize physical or informational power.

It changes the directionality of care.

The relationship can now include:

- her judgment;
- her jealousy;
- her irritation;
- her social expertise;
- and her claim that he owes her explanation.

Reciprocity appears as the right to become inconvenient.

## 4.6 `信頼できるパートナー`: partnership named before it is fully negotiated

Ayanokōji publicly calls Kei a `信頼できるパートナー / trusted partner` (`Y1V07.5-E089`).

The phrase is positive and still structurally revealing.

He gives the relationship a public category before fully consulting her. Kei then has to negotiate what that category means.

The pattern recurs across Year 1. Ayanokōji often recognizes a relationship first by placing it inside a functional model:

- tool;
- host;
- partner;
- indispensable person;
- textbook.

The model may be increasingly warm.

The act of classification remains his.

The relationship becomes more reciprocal when Kei can contest the category rather than simply inhabit it.

## 4.7 Hirata exit: dependency transfer and successful role survival

Kei's false romance with Hirata approaches termination because the original protective function is no longer the only available basis of safety.

This creates two opposite readings.

### Optimistic reading

Kei can leave a role she once needed. Hirata's protection succeeds precisely because it does not require permanent dependence. Their ethical trust can survive the end of romantic appearance.

### Critical reading

The dependency is not eliminated. It is transferred toward a more powerful and more exclusive protector.

Both readings are supported.

The transfer is therefore developmental and dangerous.

Kei gains greater agency inside the new bond while the new bond concentrates more power in one person.

## 4.8 Ordinary intimacy matters because it is unnecessary

Later evidence adds forms of care that do not require class strategy.

Ayanokōji notices Kei's health, obtains medicine, remembers birthdays, exchanges gifts, uses private names, and chooses contact whose immediate tactical value is negligible.

*First File* is especially important because its fiction shows:

- conversation remaining interesting without useful output;
- reluctance to end the interaction;
- willingness to `恋愛という世界に身を任せる / entrust himself to the world of romance`;
- and direct desire to see Kei (`Y1FF-E059–E073`).

Instrumentality is no longer sufficient to explain the relationship.

That does not mean instrumentality has disappeared.

## 4.9 The confession: reciprocal action, unequal curriculum

Before asking Kei to become his girlfriend, Ayanokōji preserves a verbal route for refusal (`Y1V11.5-E131`).

Kei explicitly answers yes (`Y1V11.5-E135`).

The relationship therefore becomes romantic through reciprocal speech, not unilateral declaration alone.

Ayanokōji also declines an immediate kiss and asks before the depicted embrace (`Y1V11.5-E136`). This is meaningful procedural improvement relative to Volume 4.

The improvement does not erase the hidden curriculum.

His interior narration still frames the relationship through:

- `教科書 / textbook`;
- learning what romance is;
- addressing Kei's dependency;
- and hoping she may become `掛け替えのない存在 / an irreplaceable person` (`Y1V11.5-E138–E141`).

Kei consents to being his girlfriend.

She does not possess full information about the developmental purposes through which he also understands the relationship.

The canonical Year 1 formulation is therefore:

> **explicit reciprocal romance inside an asymmetrical hidden curriculum.**

## 4.10 Indispensable is not yet irreplaceable

Volume 7.5 begins with `かけがえのない存在 / irreplaceable existence` and ends by naming Kei `必要不可欠な存在 / indispensable existence` (`Y1V07.5-E001`, `E100`).

The distinction is central.

An indispensable person is necessary to a system.

An irreplaceable person possesses singular value that cannot be reproduced by substituting another capable actor.

By the end of Year 1, Ayanokōji demonstrates:

- practical particularity;
- direct desire;
- willingness to enter romance;
- strong protection;
- and hope for future irreplaceability.

He does not yet establish settled irreplaceability.

The closing `祈ろう / let me pray` keeps the future open.

## 4.11 The decisive future test at the Year 1 boundary

The relationship's strongest unanswered question is not whether Kei's feelings are real.

Year 1 gives ample evidence that they are.

The harder question is:

> **Can the bond remain meaningful if Kei becomes capable of leaving it safely?**

A relationship that values her independence only as a successful product of Ayanokōji's curriculum would remain proprietary.

A reciprocal relationship would allow:

- reduced dependency;
- greater knowledge of him;
- disagreement over methods;
- refusal of protection terms;
- and a future whose meaning she helps author.

Year 1 reaches the beginning of that test, not its completion.

# 5. Ayanokōji and Horikita — familiarity, developmental partnership, and hidden sovereignty

The Ayanokōji–Horikita relationship is Year 1's longest experiment in whether development can become reciprocal before the developer relinquishes hidden control.

## 5.1 Origin: low-pressure proximity

Their early connection is not founded on romance, rescue, or confession.

It is founded on proximity and unusually tolerable silence.

Ayanokōji experiences himself as closer to an unperformed state around Horikita because neither requires continuous social display (`Y1V02-E005`).

Horikita can also question him with less ceremonial distance than most classmates.

The ordinary compatibility matters. It prevents the relationship from being reduced to strategy even though strategy quickly becomes central.

## 5.2 Functional exchange

Horikita provides:

- visible competence;
- public office;
- class legitimacy;
- analytical conversation;
- and an actor capable of turning some hidden designs into public decisions.

Ayanokōji provides:

- information;
- protection;
- strategic models;
- hidden contingencies;
- and developmental pressure.

The exchange is useful to both.

It is not informationally equal.

Ayanokōji knows far more about the causal environment and often decides what Horikita is allowed to discover.

## 5.3 Companionship and attribution

On the island Horikita calls him `仲間 / companion` and accepts a two-person fight (`Y1V03-E042–E044`).

Ayanokōji simultaneously routes public credit toward her.

The attribution produces real political office. Classmates increasingly treat Horikita as the visible strategist.

But the office rests partly on a false causal history.

This creates the relationship's constitutive problem:

> the visible leader gains authentic capacity inside a record whose decisive hidden author she cannot fully see.

## 5.4 She is not merely his proxy

Horikita repeatedly produces answers Ayanokōji did not dictate.

She:

- redesigns teaching after failure;
- accepts score risk;
- operationalizes the Volume 2 solution;
- constructs the Paper Shuffle counterstrategy;
- refuses student council membership despite his recommendation;
- chooses to preserve the Kushida problem rather than accept his efficient solution;
- creates the public Class Vote argument;
- coordinates the Event Selection Exam;
- and later creates an academic wager.

The relationship would be less reciprocal if her development produced only better obedience.

Its strongest evidence of progress is divergence.

## 5.5 Detection makes hidden authorship contestable

Horikita becomes increasingly capable of identifying when Ayanokōji has predicted or routed her choices.

The question “you knew I would act this way” converts invisible environment into an object of dispute.

By Volume 11 she detects his influence and teaches him that the person being read may also read the reader (`Y1V11-E080–E087`).

By Volume 11.5 she creates a wager that does not require complete trust. She defines a public test through which hidden ability can become partially auditable (`Y1V11.5-E090–E105`).

Reciprocity increases not because information becomes equal, but because asymmetry becomes nameable.

## 5.6 Development chosen as desire

Volume 11 gives the relationship a more personal and more dangerous form.

Ayanokōji decides that seriously changing Horikita is something he wants.

The purpose is self-chosen rather than assigned by the White Room.

Self-authorship does not establish moral jurisdiction.

The relation now asks whether Ayanokōji can want Horikita's growth without treating her eventual form as his work.

## 5.7 Horikita's independent ends

Horikita's Year 1 endpoint protects her from being interpreted solely as his developmental product.

She has ends he does not wholly share:

- public accountability;
- attempted Kushida integration;
- leadership through distributed ability;
- and a growing demand that hidden excellence become answerable to collective structures.

She also integrates Manabu's influence without remaining his imitation.

A person capable of integrating one powerful developer may become capable of resisting another.

## 5.8 Ordinary friendship remains under the political structure

Books, teasing, shared transit, and moments of silence provide a low-pressure layer beneath class politics. Horikita lends Ayanokōji Chandler and notices his inward happiness after friendship-group interaction (`Y1V07-E016`, `E021`).

These scenes matter because neither person is only a political function to the other.

The relationship nevertheless remains limited in vulnerability. Horikita knows little of his childhood, and Ayanokōji withholds most of the life that would allow her to judge him as more than an exceptional classmate.

## 5.9 Endpoint

The best Year 1 classification is:

> **developmental partnership under hidden sovereignty.**

Horikita can now:

- contradict;
- generate policy;
- expose some manipulation;
- preserve independent ends;
- and make herself less predictable.

Ayanokōji still controls:

- crucial information;
- protection infrastructure;
- attribution;
- and many contingencies.

The decisive test is whether he can accept a Horikita-authored outcome he would not choose—and whether the class can survive when her public leadership no longer receives invisible correction.

# 6. Ayanokōji and Ichinose — trust across rivalry and the danger of terminal judgment

Ayanokōji–Ichinose is a relationship in which humane recognition and paternalistic authorship are unusually close together.

## 6.1 Trust does not require alliance

By Volume 11.5 Ichinose describes Ayanokōji as:

`味方じゃない……だけど信頼できる人`

> not an ally ... but someone I can trust

(`Y1V11.5-E079–E083`).

The distinction is politically mature.

Their classes remain rivals. Personal trust does not erase constitutional opposition.

This makes the relationship a strong counterexample to the idea that intimacy requires absorption into one side.

## 6.2 Nonjudgmental presence

During the rumor crisis, Ayanokōji offers one of Year 1's strongest moral propositions: an act does not exhaust a person, and a person retains a right to forgiveness.

He can wait outside a closed door rather than demand immediate access. The `扉 / door` metaphor presents him as an available passage rather than a compulsory judge.

This is genuine care.

## 6.3 The information environment is still his

He also:

- saturates the rumor field;
- uses secrets belonging to other students;
- engineers institutional intervention;
- maintains pressure;
- and conceptualizes part of the process as breaking Ichinose's heart (`Y1V09-E087–E100`).

He does not merely stand beside her while she decides.

He authors much of the environment in which decision becomes unavoidable.

## 6.4 Future horizon and `介錯`

The one-year promise gives Ichinose time. It treats her development as unfinished rather than demanding immediate resolution.

The same interior sequence retains `介錯 / finishing or mercy blow` language (`Y1V11.5-E082–E083`).

Ayanokōji still imagines himself as possible terminal evaluator of whether her trajectory should continue in its present form.

The relation therefore combines:

- hope;
- patience;
- trust;
- attraction;
- and retained jurisdiction.

## 6.5 Ichinose's side in *First File*

Ichinose can call him `大切な友達 / precious friend`, then fails to complete the phrase `とても、私にとって重要な…… / very important to me...` (`Y1FF-E074–E082`).

The unfinished sentence is evidence of category strain.

She experiences attachment stronger than the available friendship label comfortably contains.

She also returns herself to class competition.

The relation does not become the sole author of her future.

## 6.6 Endpoint

The strongest Year 1 formulation is:

> **private attachment and genuine trust across rivalry, inside a relationship where Ayanokōji still retains a stronger model of Ichinose and a private theory of terminal intervention.**

Reciprocity depends on whether Ichinose's class commitments, judgments, and future self can remain independently authoritative rather than becoming evidence inside his developmental evaluation.

# 7. Ayanokōji and Hirata — functional repair becoming particular friendship

Ayanokōji and Hirata begin as complementary class functions.

Hirata supplies visible social legitimacy.

Ayanokōji supplies hidden diagnosis and contingency.

The relation becomes personal through a crisis whose method remains ethically troubling.

## 7.1 Earlier trust

Hirata sets privacy boundaries before disclosing Kei's history and entrusts the problem to Ayanokōji with a request not to make her unhappy (`Y1V04-E035–E040`).

This is not blind trust. Hirata senses something unsettling and still decides Ayanokōji may possess a capacity he lacks.

The delegation foreshadows their later relation: Hirata repeatedly turns to someone whose methods he would not endorse because that person can act where his universal care becomes paralyzed.

## 7.2 Constitutional difference

Hirata's governing instinct is to preserve everyone.

Ayanokōji accepts pruning, hierarchy of protection, and functional necessity.

During the Class Vote, Hirata rejects the class's jurisdiction to rank human worth. Ayanokōji treats the survival of class infrastructure as a problem requiring intervention.

Their relation therefore contains a genuine moral disagreement, not only personality contrast.

## 7.3 Coercive repair

After Hirata collapses, Ayanokōji rejects gentle delay and uses concentrated blame to destroy the self-conception sustaining paralysis (`Y1V11-E095–E105`).

He explicitly frames Hirata as a necessary `パーツ / part` of the class.

The intervention restores function.

It does not become legitimate simply because function returns.

Hirata did not meaningfully consent to the method.

## 7.4 Personal meaning after function

The same arc produces evidence that exceeds infrastructure.

Ayanokōji wants `涙を見せられるような友 / a friend before whom one can show tears` (`Y1V11-E105`). Hirata later requests given-name intimacy, and Ayanokōji experiences awkward warmth (`Y1V11.5-E044–E048`).

A recurring pattern appears:

> Ayanokōji enters through function and discovers personal value after function has already justified access.

The discovery is real.

It does not retroactively authorize the route.

## 7.5 Endpoint

The Year 1 relationship is best described as:

> **particular friendship emerging from coercive functional repair.**

Hirata has enough moral independence to remain a countermodel to Ayanokōji. The unanswered question is whether future friendship can include full knowledge of how often Ayanokōji treats people as class infrastructure.

# 8. Ayanokōji and Hiyori — low-pressure friendship without manufactured need

Hiyori is one of the most important positive counterexamples in the relationship corpus.

Ayanokōji does not need to create a crisis to become significant to her.

## 8.1 Shared interest

Books provide a relation whose immediate value is intrinsic.

Hiyori carries mysteries hoping to encounter someone with matching taste. Ayanokōji openly says that he likes them. She becomes visibly animated and invites him to lunch (`Y1V07-E017–E020`).

The encounter does not begin with:

- blackmail;
- rescue;
- class office;
- or a hidden developmental plan.

## 8.2 Silence is not failure

Both can inhabit quiet without treating it as social deficiency.

This is especially valuable for Ayanokōji because many ordinary interactions require performance he does not yet understand. Hiyori's pace makes preference communicable without demanding spectacle.

## 8.3 Cross-class relation

Class rivalry remains real.

It does not exhaust the bond.

Hiyori can care about Ayanokōji and remain part of Ryūen's polity. Ayanokōji can value conversation without converting her immediately into an intelligence asset.

## 8.4 Hiyori's corrective agency

By Volume 11.5 Hiyori asks difficult questions and corrects Ryūen in her own register (`Y1V11.5-E049–E057`). Ayanokōji revises his estimate of her political depth.

This is recognition without crisis authorship. He learns something because she develops and acts independently, not because he designed the ordeal that exposed it.

## 8.5 Endpoint

The relationship remains comparatively low-dependency, low-leverage, and high in voluntary contact.

It is not necessarily the deepest bond in Year 1.

It is one of the healthiest approximations of ordinary reciprocal friendship available to Ayanokōji because neither person's survival, status, or identity depends upon making the other indispensable.

# 9. The Ayanokōji Group, Sakura, and Satō — ordinary relation, partial disclosure, and refusal rights

The ordinary-life relationships matter because they demonstrate that Ayanokōji is capable of bonds not founded on manufactured dependency.

## 9.1 The group as counter-institution

The Ayanokōji Group supplies:

- invitations;
- food;
- films;
- nicknames;
- ordinary conversation;
- and recurring time without a formal class objective.

Haruka's formulation is one of Year 1's clearest reciprocity rules:

`好きなときに集まって嫌なときに断る`

> gather when you want; decline when you do not

(`Y1V07.5-E035`).

The right to decline does not threaten membership.

That makes the group a small counter-institution to ANHS, where refusal often carries classwide cost.

## 9.2 Partial self-disclosure

The friendship is genuine and epistemically limited.

Members do not know:

- Ayanokōji's true ability;
- his White Room history;
- his covert class interventions;
- or the extent of his relationships with Kei, Ryūen, Sakayanagi, and others.

He receives belonging without becoming fully knowable.

Year 1 therefore leaves open whether friendship can survive greater truth.

## 9.3 Sakura: adjusted pace rather than engineered need

Sakura develops through listening, privacy restraint, adjusted social pace, and voluntary proximity.

Her increasing participation in conversation is not treated as a test she must pass (`Y1V07-E007`, `E010`).

She matters because she contradicts the claim that Ayanokōji can become relationally important only through rescue monopoly.

He can help by making room.

## 9.4 Satō: transparent romance and ethical refusal

Satō offers the most conventional romantic possibility in Year 1.

Her confession—`私と付き合って / go out with me`—is direct (`Y1V07.5-E052–E067`).

Ayanokōji enjoys parts of the date and finds no decisive defect in her.

He refuses.

The refusal is important for two reasons.

First, he does not accept merely to acquire experience.

Second, he chooses against the route most compatible with ordinary school romance because he cannot reciprocate sufficiently and because acceptance would affect Kei.

The scene demonstrates restraint and reveals preference.

Ayanokōji is not compelled toward the least entangled relationship. He selects the bond already integrated into his abnormal hidden life.

## 9.5 Endpoint

The ordinary relationships establish three principles.

1. Friendship can be real without full disclosure.
2. Low-pressure contact can develop people without authored crisis.
3. Ethical refusal may preserve another person's dignity better than using available affection instrumentally.

Their unresolved question is whether partial selfhood can support durable intimacy once the concealed structures become harder to hide.

# 10. Rivalry as recognition — Manabu, Sakayanagi, and Ryūen

Rivalry is one of Ayanokōji's safest forms of reciprocal visibility.

A contest provides:

- rules;
- a defined field;
- mutual commitment;
- and a legitimate reason to reveal competence without narrating vulnerability.

The three major rival relations each recognize a different aspect of him.

## 10.1 Manabu: embodied recognition

The relay allows full effort without requiring autobiographical confession. Manabu asks Ayanokōji to answer through the body.

Later, he gives a legacy question—what will you leave behind?—rather than a prescribed identity (`Y1V11.5-E087–E105`).

Manabu's recognition is developmental without being proprietorial. He challenges Ayanokōji and leaves the answer open.

This makes the relation a countermodel to Atsuomi's authorship.

## 10.2 Sakayanagi: epistemic recognition

Sakayanagi knows that Ayanokōji's visible file is incomplete. She sees the artificially produced child and treats him as a test of her theory of natural genius.

The relation is highly reciprocal at the level of cognition and still objectifying in its own way. She recognizes him as exceptional and also converts him into evidence for an argument.

The Volume 11 chess match matters because both want an outcome capable of meaning something. Tsukishiro's intervention corrupts the record and prevents the contest from settling either theory.

The private replay, slow walk, touch, and `人肌のぬくもり / warmth of human skin` allow recognition to continue after the official score (`Y1V11-E127–E149`).

Romantic coding is possible.

Year 1 does not require it for the relationship's significance.

The bond already provides something rare: another person who knows the public mediocrity is authored and who can confront him on the level of origin and excellence.

## 10.3 Ryūen: uncertainty and adversarial attachment

Ryūen initially matters because he creates a problem Ayanokōji cannot solve through passive concealment alone.

After the rooftop, Ayanokōji preserves him from expulsion, says he did not want him gone, and later recognizes that his disappearance would produce loneliness (`Y1V07-E099–E106`, `Y1V10-E102`).

The attachment is not gentle.

Ayanokōji defeats him through overwhelming closure and adopts teacher-like language about continued growth.

But Ryūen retains independent will. He can surprise, resist, rebuild, and define victory through a philosophy not supplied by Ayanokōji.

The relation therefore combines:

- strategic utility;
- worthy-opponent desire;
- developmental authorship;
- and genuine relational value through unpredictability.

## 10.4 The danger of cultivating rivals

Ayanokōji increasingly wants strong opponents to continue developing.

This preserves futures that a pure pruning logic might eliminate.

It also risks making rivalry another private curriculum.

The reciprocal test is whether the rival gets to define:

- what victory means;
- what development is for;
- and whether participation in Ayanokōji's preferred contest is itself desirable.

# 11. Horikita and Manabu — recognition that matures by surrendering specification

Horikita–Manabu is Year 1's clearest familial study of the difference between influence and authorship.

## 11.1 The sibling as external standard

Suzune does not merely admire Manabu.

She treats his recognition as proof that she has become worthy.

This reorganizes:

- academic ambition;
- emotional restraint;
- social distance;
- bodily presentation;
- and future purpose

around an imagined specification.

The relationship is therefore highly asymmetric before any explicit command is given. Manabu's judgment governs because Suzune has made it the standard of selfhood.

## 11.2 Rejection does not create independence

Manabu's coldness and early violence do not free Suzune from imitation.

They intensify it.

Scarce approval acquires greater value. She must infer what form would finally deserve recognition.

The method is self-defeating: he wants her to become independent while making his inaccessible judgment more powerful.

The later quality of the relationship should not be used to recode the early violence as necessary pedagogy.

## 11.3 Counsel without answer

During the Class Vote, Suzune asks for courage.

Manabu does not tell her whom to name or how to govern. He tells her to grow as herself (`Y1V10-E045–E048`).

The relation begins to change because support no longer replaces judgment.

## 11.4 Recognition through release

Volume 11.5 completes the Year 1 movement.

Suzune rejects imitation as governing purpose while acknowledging the resulting years as `紛れもない私 / unmistakably me` (`Y1V11.5-E003–E005`).

Manabu recognizes her after she declares separation.

The mature relation says:

> I shaped you, but you do not owe me repetition.

This is inheritance rather than specification.

## 11.5 Separation test

Graduation is the strongest possible role change. Manabu leaves the institution and cannot remain Suzune's daily measuring device.

The relationship survives as:

- grief;
- memory;
- principle;
- and a released future.

It therefore passes a separation test that several other Year 1 bonds have not yet faced.

Its endpoint is not freedom from influence.

It is influence whose final interpretation belongs to Suzune.

# 12. Horikita and Sudō — development validated by dissent

Horikita–Sudō is the strongest Year 1 model of developmental relationship becoming genuinely reciprocal.

## 12.1 Origin in judgment

Sudō initially appears to confirm Horikita's narrow meritocracy.

He is:

- academically weak;
- volatile;
- difficult to teach;
- and capable of imposing classwide costs.

She can identify the deficit and initially cannot translate her knowledge into a form he can use.

## 12.2 Translation creates relation

Horikita researches basketball, changes analogies, apologizes, and risks her own score.

The pedagogical correction is relational rather than merely technical. She must enter his world instead of demanding that he enter hers at full speed.

## 12.3 The sports festival creates bilateral development

The three-legged race requires coordination at the level of body and rhythm. Neither person's strength is sufficient alone.

Sudō needs discipline and broader responsibility.

Horikita needs domain humility, embodied adaptation, and the ability to follow another person's pace.

Their later promise is bilateral rather than teacher-to-defective-student (`Y1V05-E047–E055`, `Y1V05-E063–E064`).

## 12.4 Dissent is proof of success

During the Class Vote, Sudō opposes Horikita's judgment of Yamauchi.

The disagreement does not prove Sudō correct.

It proves he has not become a compliant product.

He can invoke friendship, demand reasons, and remain part of the class after resistance.

Horikita also returns authorship of his improvement to him—`彼自身の努力の結果 / the result of his own effort` (`Y1V10-E006–E007`).

## 12.5 Domain authority

By Volume 11 Horikita relies on Sudō's athletic judgment. He is no longer simply a person she has improved. His expertise changes the quality of her leadership (`Y1V11-E034–E043`).

The relationship's progression is:

> judgment → translation → mutual adaptation → effort → dissent → expertise → distributed leadership.

This is one of Year 1's best approximations of reciprocal authorship. The developer's success is measured partly by the developed person's ability to resist her.

# 13. Horikita and Kushida — covenant without reconciliation

Horikita–Kushida is the hardest non-romantic relationship in Year 1 because it tests whether respect for developmental possibility can become a form of coercion.

## 13.1 The bond begins with irreversible knowledge

Kushida wants the hidden self to remain unknown.

Horikita's mere knowledge creates leverage even if she promises silence.

Kushida's knife analogy is therefore structurally serious: a weapon need not be used to shape the choices of the person who knows it is present (`Y1V08-E114–E117`).

Benevolent intention does not eliminate asymmetric vulnerability.

## 13.2 Horikita's anti-disposability principle

Horikita resists immediate removal because:

- current liability may conceal future value;
- social capacity is real even when motives are compromised;
- expulsion as first solution contradicts an educational model;
- and the conflict implicates her personally.

The reasons are coherent.

Kushida is not another Sudō.

Her danger is deliberate, informed, and networked.

## 13.3 Wager and covenant

The Paper Shuffle wager creates enforceable limits. Horikita refuses to stake Ayanokōji without his consent, Manabu witnesses the agreement, and defeat creates a noninterference rule (`Y1V06-E039–E061`).

Horikita offers a handshake.

Kushida refuses.

The result is not trust.

It is covenant without reconciliation.

This is politically important. People who do not like or trust one another may still create behavioral boundaries.

## 13.4 Refusal remains constitutive

Kushida's refusal protects the analysis from converting Horikita's sincerity into mutuality.

Horikita wants a future relationship.

Kushida does not accept the offered category.

That difference remains real at the Year 1 boundary.

## 13.5 Collective risk

Horikita's project exposes classmates to:

- secret disclosure;
- sabotage;
- reputational harm;
- and exam loss.

They do not possess full information about the risk or give informed authorization for her rehabilitation experiment.

The relationship therefore contains a public ethical problem:

> a private commitment to save one person may impose unconsented vulnerability on many others.

## 13.6 Jurisdiction rather than resolution

Volume 11.5 transfers future Kushida jurisdiction toward Horikita because Ayanokōji wants to see what she will produce (`Y1V11.5-E101–E105`).

This confirms independence from his preferred solution.

It does not confirm wisdom or successful integration.

The relationship remains:

> **self-authored by Horikita, structurally coercive from Kushida's perspective, politically risky for the class, and unresolved.**

# 14. Horikita, Hirata, and Ichinose — constitutional relationships rather than private intimacy

Not every important relationship in Year 1 is primarily interpersonal. Some bonds organize competing theories of membership and leadership.

## 14.1 Horikita and Hirata: judgment versus unconditional belonging

Hirata possesses legitimacy Horikita initially lacks:

- emotional accessibility;
- voluntary trust;
- social information;
- and the capacity to make cooperation feel inhabitable.

Horikita eventually possesses something Hirata cannot supply during the Class Vote:

- willingness to formulate criteria;
- make a public decision;
- and accept liability.

Their collision is constitutional.

Hirata is morally right that a forced vote does not automatically give classmates legitimate jurisdiction over another person's worth.

Horikita is politically right that refusing public judgment leaves the decision to hidden blocs and manipulation.

A mature class needs both:

- her answerability;
- and his refusal to let utility exhaust membership.

The relationship should not be resolved by making either theory sovereign.

## 14.2 Horikita and Ichinose: alliance without possession

Ichinose provides an early countermodel of leadership through trust and accessibility.

Horikita gradually learns that cooperation does not create entitlement to another polity's secrets (`Y1V09-E033–E036`).

By Volume 11.5 the strategic alliance can end while respect remains (`Y1V11.5-E059–E063`).

This is one of Year 1's clearest role-survival successes:

> the relationship survives the end of its political function.

Competition does not require personal annihilation.

# 15. Ryūen and his followers — fear, correction, and voluntary preservation

Ryūen's class begins as the year’s clearest challenge to trust-based relationship.

He centralizes information, punishes defiance, and treats fear as the universal political lever.

The Year 1 development is not that the class becomes democratic.

It is that fear ceases to explain the whole bond.

## 15.1 Ibuki: antagonistic loyalty and moral boundary

Ibuki challenges Ryūen more openly than most followers.

During the rooftop operation she refuses active participation in the water torture and objects to escalating violence (`Y1V07-E058`, `E081`).

She remains present and complicit.

The refusal is partial rather than cleansing.

After Ryūen's defeat, she waits, returns the phone, cites his own doctrine of repeated defeat, and calls withdrawal after one loss pathetic (`Y1V07-E101`).

Her loyalty is not gentle obedience. It is antagonistic insistence that he remain answerable to the strength he demanded from others.

## 15.2 Ishizaki and Albert: followers who choose continuation

Ishizaki and Albert wait for Ryūen and threaten to leave if he leaves (`Y1V07-E104–E105`).

This is politically significant because his undefeated authority has collapsed.

They remain when fear should have weakened.

The choice suggests:

- belief in his strategic promise;
- gratitude;
- identity invested in the class order;
- or personal loyalty not reducible to coercion.

## 15.3 Hiyori: corrective friendship inside hierarchy

Hiyori's relation to Ryūen is quieter and more constitutionally promising.

She questions whether the hunt for X is necessary, refuses participation, and offers help if a genuine need arises (`Y1V07-E051`).

By Volume 11.5 she can correct him without adopting his style (`Y1V11.5-E053–E056`).

Her loyalty is compatible with bounded refusal.

That is a stronger form of participation than simple fear or compliance.

## 15.4 Ryūen's protection of followers

Ryūen also tries to absorb sole responsibility for the rooftop and keep followers outside punishment (`Y1V07-E098`, `E104`).

His leadership is not only exploitation. He is willing to concentrate risk upon himself when the system he built fails.

This contributes to sociological legitimacy without establishing moral legitimacy.

## 15.5 Endpoint

The polity's relational progression is:

> coercive centralization → catastrophic defeat → follower refusal to abandon → authority partially reconstituted through voluntary preservation.

Ryūen remains authoritarian.

The followers become more than governed objects.

The relationship system is more durable precisely because members can object, preserve, and sometimes redirect the leader.

# 16. Ichinose, Kanzaki, and Class B — solidarity beyond purity

Ichinose's class offers the strongest Year 1 model of trust as collective infrastructure.

Its strength and danger both arise from emotional centrality.

## 16.1 Membership does not require innocence

The rumor crisis reveals a real wrongdoing.

Classmates do not preserve Ichinose by proving the act never occurred. They accept the truth without withdrawing membership (`Y1V09-E087–E100`).

This is mature recognition:

> event is not total identity.

The class can judge the act and continue the relationship.

## 16.2 Kanzaki's loyalty

Kanzaki acts to protect Ichinose without requiring a purity proof.

His loyalty is longitudinal rather than transactional. He trusts the pattern of the person and class rather than treating one revelation as totalizing.

This makes him more than a subordinate follower.

He is a potential internal critic whose continued membership can carry disagreement.

Year 1 only begins to expose how necessary that function may become.

## 16.3 Collective rescue

During the Class Vote, Class B attempts to preserve every member through pooled points and shared sacrifice. Ichinose's `私は───皆を守るんだ / I will protect everyone` expresses the class constitution at its most intense (`Y1V10-E089–E105`).

The attempt is morally serious.

It also reveals dependence on Ichinose as symbolic center. A polity organized around universal preservation may struggle when adversaries exploit reciprocity or when the leader's emotional collapse threatens collective direction.

## 16.4 Solidarity and centralization

Class B's relationships are not naïve because they are warm.

Trust produces real capacities:

- information sharing;
- pooled resources;
- loyalty under scandal;
- and collective rescue.

The unresolved question is whether solidarity can remain distributed enough to survive failure or dissent without making Ichinose the single emotional object everyone must preserve.

# 17. Additional countermodels — care without monopoly, community without permanence

Several secondary relationships carry disproportionate theoretical weight.

## 17.1 Chairman Sakayanagi and Ayanokōji: autonomy-respecting protection

Chairman Sakayanagi refuses Atsuomi's demand for forced removal and protects Ayanokōji's continued enrollment unless he chooses to leave (`Y1V07-E030–E032`).

The protection is bounded by school rules. It does not grant immunity from legitimate expulsion.

This is one of Year 1's strongest models of protection that enlarges choice rather than monopolizing it.

## 17.2 Manabu and Tachibana: reciprocal care distorted by self-denial

Tachibana conceals danger to avoid burdening Manabu. Manabu refuses to reduce her value to the score and spends enormous resources on rescue (`Y1V08-E107–E109`, `E132–E142`).

The care is reciprocal.

Communication is distorted by exemplary self-sacrifice. Each person protects the other partly by withholding the information required for shared decision.

## 17.3 Hirata and Mii-chan: presence without theory

When others recommend distance during Hirata's collapse, Mii-chan insists that suffering is precisely when help is required—`苦しい時、辛い時だからこそ、助けてあげなきゃいけない` (`Y1V11-E089–E094`).

She does not possess Ayanokōji's psychological model or power.

Her intervention is relational presence rather than developmental design.

It cannot solve every problem. It provides a humane counterweight to abandoning a person because contact has become difficult.

## 17.4 Mixed-camp community: compulsory origin, voluntary remainder

The training camp forces students from hostile classes into shared living. Labor, disclosure, embarrassment, injury, and assistance produce a temporary community (`Y1V08-E088–E105`).

The origin is compulsory.

The resulting handshakes and reluctance to separate are not therefore fake.

The episode demonstrates that institutionally designed proximity can generate authentic relation without the institution owning every meaning participants make from it.

## 17.5 Kei and Satō: care under jealousy

Kei helps Satō navigate the date even while experiencing jealousy and possessing information Satō lacks (`Y1V07.5-E010–E015`).

The relationship tests whether counterfeit expertise can become ethical responsibility.

Kei does not simply sabotage a rival. She takes Satō's trust seriously.

This is another small sign that her relational capacities exceed the survival logic through which she initially understands herself.

# 18. Sakayanagi, Kamuro, Katsuragi, and Ayanokōji — recognition under aristocratic control

Sakayanagi's relationships are organized by unequal knowledge and selective recognition.

She sees capacities others miss.

She also assumes unusual authority over how those capacities should be used.

## 18.1 Sakayanagi and Ayanokōji

The relation provides reciprocal intellectual visibility.

Sakayanagi knows the public file is false. Ayanokōji recognizes that she may supply a genuine test of the system that produced him.

Their chess match is therefore not only competition. It is a struggle over whose theory of ability can survive contact with the other.

Tsukishiro's intervention corrupts the official result, forcing the relationship to continue beyond score. The private replay, slow walk, and bodily warmth give the rivalry a human remainder.

The relation remains asymmetric in origin knowledge and in the costs imposed on classmates by the rivalry.

Recognition can itself become proprietary when another person is valued principally as proof of a theory.

## 18.2 Sakayanagi and Kamuro

Kamuro's relation begins through blackmail.

Sakayanagi obtains leverage from wrongdoing and converts it into service. Over time, stable role, behavioral improvement, familiarity, and usefulness emerge.

This is a coerced developmental relation.

The later benefit does not make the founding blackmail consensual.

The relationship is important because it resembles Ayanokōji's tendency to acquire people through vulnerability, while differing in style. Sakayanagi makes hierarchy public within the private relation; Ayanokōji often conceals how thoroughly he authored the need.

## 18.3 Sakayanagi and Katsuragi

Katsuragi represents internal opposition rather than intimate reciprocity.

He possesses an alternative governing philosophy inside the same class, and Sakayanagi's consolidation requires reducing his political independence.

The relation tests whether a high-capacity polity can tolerate more than one legitimate center.

Year 1 increasingly suggests that Sakayanagi's strategic brilliance is also a succession and fiduciary risk. A class whose intelligence is centralized may perform exceptionally while becoming dependent upon the sovereign's private priorities.

## 18.4 Recognition and use

Sakayanagi is one of the characters most capable of seeing hidden ability.

Her ethical weakness is that recognition easily becomes allocation:

> I know what you are, therefore I know what place you should occupy.

Year 1 does not establish whether her rivalry with Ayanokōji can become a relationship in which either person matters beyond the function of testing the other.

# 19. Manabu, Tachibana, and Nagumo — trust, burden, and betrayal

The senior-year relationships provide a compressed political study of trust.

## 19.1 Manabu and Tachibana: reciprocal care distorted by self-denial

Tachibana conceals danger because she does not want to burden Manabu. Manabu refuses to reduce her value to the Mixed Training Camp score and accepts major class cost to save her.

The care is reciprocal.

Communication is poor.

Both participants act from exemplary self-denial:

- she must not become a burden;
- he must bear the cost without allowing her to become disposable.

The relationship demonstrates that devotion can be genuine while still producing avoidable crisis when each person protects the other from relevant information (`Y1V08-E107–E109`, `Y1V08-E132–E137`).

## 19.2 Manabu and Nagumo: trust as a one-use political asset

Nagumo's prior promise-keeping creates credibility.

That credibility becomes the condition of decisive betrayal.

The relationship shows that trust is not only moral capital. It is access.

A person who has behaved reliably can use the accumulated expectation of reliability to make one deviation unusually powerful.

Nagumo does not merely defeat Manabu inside an exam. He authors the field in which Manabu's relational commitments become liabilities.

## 19.3 The senior contrast

Manabu's mature positive relation to Suzune and Ayanokōji is challenge without specification.

Nagumo's political relation to others increasingly treats relationships as channels through which institutional design can govern behavior.

The contrast anticipates a Year 1 principle:

> **Trust becomes reciprocal only when access created by trust does not become unilateral jurisdiction over the other person's future.**

# 20. Protection, ownership, and the hierarchy of lives

Protection is one of Year 1's most morally ambiguous relationship forms.

It can preserve agency.

It can also create a private government over another person's life.

## 20.1 Four protection models

### Hirata — universal and exit-oriented

Hirata protects through social legitimacy, mediation, and inclusion. He refuses exclusive violence on Kei's behalf. His limitation is that he cannot guarantee first priority.

His ethical strength is that protection succeeds when the protected person no longer needs the role.

### Ayanokōji — particular and sovereignty-oriented

Ayanokōji can promise more complete operational safety. He can deter, expose, injure, manipulate records, and exploit institutional rules.

His protection is effective because he privately claims authority over methods.

`どんな手を使っても / by any means` is emotionally reassuring and constitutionally alarming. It asks the protected person to trust an undisclosed executive power.

### Ichinose — solidaristic and collective

Ichinose protects by pooling trust, points, and membership. The individual is not saved through exclusive ownership by one protector but through a class promise that no member is disposable.

The weakness is adversarial exposure and overdependence on the leader who symbolizes the promise.

### Manabu — fiduciary and office-bound

Manabu protects Tachibana through the resources of a political community. He accepts responsibility and cost publicly.

The relation still suffers because Tachibana hides vulnerability to avoid burdening the leader.

## 20.2 The protection hierarchy

Ayanokōji eventually identifies selected people whose futures receive exceptional guarantees.

This reveals that protection is not distributed equally.

A private hierarchy of lives emerges:

- some people receive near-absolute protection;
- some receive conditional preservation because they are valuable;
- some are allowed to face risk for developmental effect;
- and some remain candidates for removal.

The emotional fact that protection becomes particular is evidence of attachment.

The political fact that one person decides the hierarchy without public accountability is evidence of sovereignty.

## 20.3 The exit criterion

The cleanest comparative question is:

> **Does protection make the protected person more capable of living without the protector?**

Hirata's model explicitly aims at exit.

Horikita's work with Sudō increasingly produces independent expertise and dissent.

Ayanokōji's relation with Kei begins by narrowing alternatives. It later contains dependency-cure language, but the cure is privately designed by the person whose importance the cure may need to reduce.

That contradiction remains unresolved.

# 21. Dependency — neither intimacy nor pathology by itself

Year 1 uses dependency language aggressively, especially `寄生虫 / parasite` and `宿木 / host`.

Those metaphors should be treated as character models rather than omniscient diagnoses.

## 21.1 Kei's dependency is an adaptation

Kei's public dominance, boyfriend strategy, social control, and rapid attachment to stronger protectors are survival technologies built after severe bullying.

Dependency is not proof that she lacks agency.

The rooftop demonstrates the opposite. She remains afraid and chooses against the most obvious survival route.

## 21.2 Dependency can create capability

Some forms of reliance enlarge a person's future options.

Sudō relies on Horikita's academic structure and becomes more capable of opposing her.

Horikita relies on Hirata's social legitimacy and Sudō's athletic expertise while becoming a stronger leader.

The Ayanokōji Group gives members ordinary belonging that makes social participation easier.

Class B pools resources so that individual survival does not depend entirely on private wealth.

These are dependencies, but not necessarily degradations.

## 21.3 Manufactured dependency

Dependency becomes morally dangerous when the protector:

- creates or intensifies the threat;
- discredits alternatives;
- withholds relevant information;
- or makes safety conditional on continued role performance.

The Volume 4 Kei operation is the clearest case.

The relationship's later benefits do not erase the founding fact that one person helped create the need he then satisfied.

## 21.4 Mutual dependency is not automatically reciprocal

Two people can need one another for different reasons while retaining severe inequality.

Ayanokōji needs Kei's social network and increasingly values her ordinary presence.

Kei needs protection, recognition, and a private bond.

That mutual need does not equal mutual knowledge or equal exit capacity.

The correct question is not whether both need the relation.

It is whether both can alter its terms without becoming unsafe.

---

# 22. Friendship — voluntary return, tolerated failure, and ordinary time

Year 1's strongest evidence of friendship often appears outside special examinations.

The test is not grand sacrifice alone.

It is whether people keep returning when no immediate reward requires it.

## 22.1 Voluntary contact

The Ayanokōji Group, Hiyori's book conversations, Sakura's adjusted pace, and parts of Kei's *First File* call matter because contact is not wholly compelled by institutional crisis.

Ordinary time reveals relational value more cleanly than high-stakes rescue because utility is lower.

## 22.2 Failure without expulsion from the bond

A mature friendship must permit:

- awkwardness;
- mistakes;
- disagreement;
- and temporary inability to perform a role.

Sudō's relationship with Horikita survives his failure and later dissent.

Hirata's friends continue attempting contact during collapse.

Class B's solidarity survives Ichinose's disclosed wrongdoing.

The Ayanokōji Group has not yet been tested by the revelation of his largest hidden facts.

## 22.3 Direct support remains difficult

Ayanokōji is often more comfortable providing practical help than requesting emotional support.

Hirata's given-name request and Ayanokōji's wish for a friend before whom tears could be shown indicate a desire for reciprocal vulnerability. Year 1 does not show him fully inhabiting it.

This makes friendship a developmental frontier rather than an achieved condition.

## 22.4 Partial selves can form real friendships

The series does not require total autobiographical disclosure before friendship becomes real.

Every person presents selectively.

The problem begins when withheld information materially changes the other person's safety, consent, or understanding of the relationship's actual terms.

This distinction protects two truths:

- the Ayanokōji Group is genuinely meaningful;
- the group's knowledge of Ayanokōji is radically incomplete.

---

# 23. Romance — mutual choice without completed equality

Year 1's romantic architecture is plural rather than route-like.

Different relationships isolate different parts of romance.

## 23.1 Satō: ordinary courtship and honest refusal

Satō supplies recognizable dating structure:

- preparation;
- nervousness;
- a gift;
- direct interest;
- desire for more time;
- and confession.

Ayanokōji's refusal demonstrates that attraction and enjoyment are insufficient for reciprocal romance.

## 23.2 Kei: romance with history

Kei supplies:

- private names;
- jealousy;
- protection;
- gifts;
- operational partnership;
- bodily intimacy;
- confession;
- and explicit mutual answer.

She also brings a history of coercion, dependency, secrecy, and hidden curriculum.

The relationship is therefore more intimate and less procedurally clean than the Satō route.

## 23.3 Ichinose: unnamed significance

Ichinose's feeling exceeds ordinary friendship language at the endpoint. Year 1 does not establish mutual romance.

The restraint matters because a category problem in one person's interiority is not the same as a relationship jointly defined as romantic.

## 23.4 Sakayanagi: intimate rivalry without required romance

The chess rivalry receives bodily warmth and unusually private recognition. The text permits romantic interpretation without making it necessary to explain the relation.

## 23.5 Irreplaceability and indispensability

Volume 7.5 distinguishes the relation Ayanokōji already has from the relation he wants to understand.

An **indispensable** person is necessary to a system.

An **irreplaceable** person possesses singular value not exhausted by function.

Kei is clearly indispensable before Volume 11.5.

By the end of Year 1, Ayanokōji hopes she may become irreplaceable and prays that his own emotional response is genuine. *First File* strengthens present desire but does not prove permanent singularity.

That distinction should remain active.

## 23.6 Mutual confession is real and incomplete

Mutual confession establishes romance.

It does not establish:

- complete information symmetry;
- equal power;
- permanent commitment;
- or ethical resolution of the relationship's origin.

Year 1 is sophisticated precisely because it permits the romance to be real without making it morally simple.

# 24. Recognition — person, role, event, and file

Recognition is the deepest common structure across Year 1 relationships.

People want to be seen.

They also fear being reduced to what another person sees.

## 24.1 Role recognition

Many relationships begin by recognizing a function:

- leader;
- protector;
- witness;
- social interface;
- rival;
- girlfriend;
- host;
- tool;
- successor;
- or class representative.

Role recognition can be accurate and useful.

It becomes dehumanizing when the relation cannot survive the role's end.

## 24.2 Event is not identity

Ichinose's theft, Sudō's violence, Sakura's social anxiety, Hirata's collapse, Kei's dependency, and Kushida's performed friendliness all test whether one event or adaptation becomes total identity.

The strongest Year 1 relationships preserve the possibility of revision.

Horikita sees Sudō's trajectory.

Class B accepts Ichinose after truth.

Hirata does not treat Kei's survival strategy as moral essence.

The most dangerous relationships weaponize a true fact into permanent definition.

## 24.3 Being known can create vulnerability

Kushida's knife metaphor makes the point directly.

Knowledge creates power even when unused.

Kei's scar, Ichinose's theft, Ayanokōji's ability, and Ryūen's fear all become relational leverage because another person knows.

Recognition therefore requires self-limitation by the observer.

The ethical observer must not treat access as entitlement.

## 24.4 *First File*: model limitation

The guidebook converts the year into profiles, ratings, timelines, summaries, and hypothetical OAA metrics.

It is useful because it shows what a relationship can do wrong.

A file selects variables.

A relationship also selects variables.

Ayanokōji can model Kei's survival system accurately and still fail to exhaust her dignity. Horikita can understand Kushida's value and still misjudge the coercive force of irreversible knowledge. Sakayanagi can see hidden capacity and still turn the person into a test case.

The Year 1 epistemic principle is:

> **Recognition becomes reciprocal only when the recognized person retains the right to correct the model.**

# 25. Names, language, and private relational worlds

Japanese address terms and naming changes carry relationship information that English summary can flatten.

## 25.1 Given names as negotiated access

Kei's shift toward `Kiyotaka` and Ayanokōji's reciprocal `Kei` create a private linguistic world while public surnames remain in place (`Y1V07.5-E096`).

The intimacy is real.

It is also compartmentalized.

Private naming does not mean public acknowledgment.

## 25.2 `仲間`

Horikita's use of `仲間 / companion, comrade` on the island marks a category she previously resisted (`Y1V03-E044`). It is not necessarily friendship in the warm sense. It is recognition that self-sufficiency has failed and another person occupies a legitimate place beside her.

## 25.3 `大切な友達` and the unfinished category

Ichinose's *First File* story begins with `大切な友達` and then strains beyond it. The unfinished phrase preserves uncertainty rather than delivering a route label.

## 25.4 `使う者と使われる者`

The *First File* Kei story explicitly names the old user/used structure. The phrase matters because the text itself recognizes relational change without pretending the old structure never existed.

## 25.5 `教科書`

Ayanokōji's textbook metaphor is ethically double.

It admits ignorance and willingness to learn.

It also casts Kei as an object through which knowledge will be acquired.

The metaphor becomes less dangerous only if the “textbook” can rewrite the lesson, refuse the reading, and become a co-author rather than instructional material.

## 25.6 `掛け替えのない存在`

Irreplaceability is not merely romantic intensity.

It is the idea that a person cannot be substituted without changing the meaning of the life or relationship itself.

Year 1 leaves this as aspiration rather than settled achievement.

# 26. Separation and role change — the strongest stress test

A relationship often becomes most legible when its original role ends.

## 26.1 Kei and Hirata

The false couple role approaches termination. Hirata's care survives because he wanted the role to become unnecessary.

## 26.2 Horikita and Manabu

Graduation ends daily proximity and external specification. The relation survives as inheritance because separation is explicitly recognized.

## 26.3 Temporary Mixed Training Camp community

Compulsory proximity among Keisei, Hashimoto, Ishizaki, Albert, and Ayanokōji produces a real but time-bounded community. Handshakes and reluctance to separate show that temporary institutional grouping can generate genuine relation without requiring permanent merger.

## 26.4 Ayanokōji and Satō

The possibility of romance ends through refusal. The refusal preserves Satō's personhood better than acceptance for instrumental learning would have.

## 26.5 Ayanokōji and Kei

The major role-change test remains ahead.

The relation has already changed from:

> coerced collaborator → protected partner → private indispensable person → girlfriend.

The unresolved question is what remains if:

- Kei no longer needs protection;
- the relationship becomes public;
- class interests diverge;
- or Ayanokōji's hidden origin becomes known.

## 26.6 Relationship durability standard

The strongest relationship is not necessarily the one that never changes.

It is the one capable of surviving change without requiring the person to remain trapped in the role through which the relationship began.

---

# 27. Information asymmetry as relational government

Information is one of Year 1's most powerful forms of relationship control.

## 27.1 Secrets create private law

Kei's past, Kushida's hidden self, Ichinose's theft, Ayanokōji's ability, and the hidden causes of exam outcomes create obligations outside formal school law.

The person who knows can:

- threaten disclosure;
- offer silence;
- demand service;
- change reputation;
- or define what counts as safe behavior.

## 27.2 Surveillance blurs care and control

School phones permit location visibility among friends (`Y1V02-E008`). Information can support rescue, coordination, or ordinary contact.

It can also make friendship infrastructure available for surveillance.

The same ambiguity appears in Kei's intelligence work, Ryūen's network, and Ayanokōji's monitoring.

## 27.3 Hidden curriculum

The most consequential asymmetry occurs when one person does not know that the relationship itself has been assigned a developmental purpose.

Kei knows she is Ayanokōji's girlfriend.

She does not fully know the textbook and dependency-cure framework through which he interprets the romance.

Horikita knows Ayanokōji helps her.

She does not fully know that her development has become a self-chosen project within his emerging legacy ambition.

Ichinose knows he supports her.

She does not know the full extent of his rumor architecture or retained terminal judgment.

## 27.4 The consent threshold

Perfect information symmetry is impossible.

The relevant question is whether the withheld information would materially change a reasonable person's decision to enter, continue, or define the relationship.

Year 1 repeatedly approaches that threshold and often crosses it.

That is why sincere choice can be real without being fully autonomous.

# 29. Strong counterarguments and their limits

## 29.1 Counterargument: every Ayanokōji relationship is manipulation

This view correctly identifies pervasive concealment and environmental authorship.

It becomes too strong when it treats every ordinary desire, pleasure, attachment, refusal, or surprise as strategically produced.

Year 1 gives positive evidence that:

- he wants friends before the major class strategy develops;
- he enjoys ordinary group time;
- Hiyori's friendship does not require crisis;
- he refuses to use Satō merely for experience;
- Kei produces choices his minimum survival model does not predict;
- and *First File* gives direct desire to see Kei.

The relationships remain manipulated in part.

They are not exhausted by manipulation.

## 29.2 Counterargument: successful outcomes justify coercive methods

Kei becomes stronger, Hirata returns, Horikita develops, and Ichinose regains forward motion.

Those outcomes are real.

They do not answer:

- whether refusal was possible;
- whether less coercive methods existed;
- whether third parties absorbed hidden costs;
- whether the developer's model could have been wrong;
- or whether later gratitude is being used to erase earlier violation.

Development is evidence of effectiveness, not automatic evidence of legitimacy.

## 29.3 Counterargument: Kei is either still a tool or already conventional love

The first claim cannot explain mutual confession, ordinary desire, reciprocal decisions, and the explicit contrast with the older user/used structure.

The second cannot explain the coercive origin, unequal information, protection sovereignty, `教科書`, dependency-cure purpose, and the fact that irreplaceability remains a prayer.

The locked formulation must retain both romance and asymmetry.

## 29.4 Counterargument: Horikita is either a puppet or fully independent

Her independent policies, dissent, detection, and public accountability refute the puppet thesis.

Ayanokōji's control of information, contingency, attribution, and developmental purpose refutes full autonomy.

The correct category is reciprocal development under hidden sovereignty.

## 29.5 Counterargument: Ichinose's attachment makes her politically dependent on Ayanokōji

Her feelings are real and may exceed ordinary friendship.

*First File* explicitly preserves her class rivalry and renewed commitment. Personal attachment has not yet absorbed public agency.

The stronger concern is Ayanokōji's private terminal jurisdiction, not proof that Ichinose has surrendered herself.

## 29.6 Counterargument: Ryūen's follower loyalty proves coercion is a successful foundation

Fear can produce coordination.

The voluntary preservation that emerges after fear sovereignty breaks suggests that durable authority requires more than fear. Hiyori's corrective agency and the followers' independent choices are precisely what Ryūen's original model underexplains.

Sociological legitimacy is not identical to ethical legitimacy.

## 29.7 Counterargument: friendship requires full disclosure

Complete disclosure is neither possible nor always desirable.

The relevant distinction is whether withheld information changes the other's safety, consent, or understanding of the bond's material terms.

The Ayanokōji Group can be genuinely meaningful while remaining epistemically fragile.

## 29.8 Counterargument: reciprocity is impossible under major power disparity

Power disparity makes reciprocity difficult, not logically impossible.

Horikita can contradict Ayanokōji. Sudō can oppose Horikita. Kei can answer the confession and decide secrecy. Kanzaki can criticize Ichinose. Hiyori can refuse Ryūen.

The question is whether those acts can occur without catastrophic punishment and whether they materially alter the relationship.

## 29.9 Counterargument: intense dependency is evidence of deeper love

Dependency may intensify attention and fear of loss.

It does not by itself establish recognition of the other's independent good.

Love becomes more ethically serious when the beloved can become less dependent without becoming less valuable.

# 30. Claim-status audit

## 30.1 Confirmed at the Year 1 boundary

- Relationships routinely combine sincere feeling with strategic asymmetry.
- Protection and ownership are structurally distinct.
- Trust is both moral infrastructure and attack surface.
- A developed person can exceed the developer's script.
- Ordinary-life contact provides evidence unavailable in crisis-only analysis.
- Rivalry can function as recognition and substitute intimacy.
- Full reciprocity does not require equal power, but it does require meaningful answerability.
- Class loyalty can survive disagreement and disclosed wrongdoing.

## 30.2 Strengthened by Volume 11.5 and *First File*

- Ayanokōji–Kei is explicitly romantic and mutually answered.
- The relationship remains shaped by hidden developmental framing.
- Ayanokōji's desire for ordinary contact is real rather than merely instrumental.
- Horikita–Manabu reaches mature separation rather than simple reconciliation.
- Horikita–Ayanokōji becomes more reciprocal because she detects and tests his influence.
- Ichinose's private attachment coexists with retained class agency.
- Hiyori has meaningful corrective authority inside Ryūen's polity.
- A file, rating, or psychological model cannot exhaust the person.

## 30.3 Corrected

### Earlier formulation: Kei moves from tool to partner

Correction: the relation does not leave instrumentality behind in a linear way. Explicit romance and ordinary desire emerge while hidden curriculum, protection asymmetry, and dependency history remain active.

### Earlier formulation: Horikita becomes independent from Ayanokōji

Correction: she becomes increasingly self-authored and capable of divergence while remaining inside a class whose shadow constitution he substantially controls.

### Earlier formulation: Class B trust is naïve softness

Correction: trust is real political capacity capable of surviving truth, pooling material resources, and sustaining internal dissent. Its vulnerability lies in adversarial security and symbolic centralization.

### Earlier formulation: Ryūen rules only through fear

Correction: fear is foundational but insufficient to explain later voluntary preservation and Hiyori's corrective role.

### Earlier formulation: ordinary friendship requires the complete self

Correction: partial selves can form genuine bonds. The ethical problem arises when concealed facts materially alter consent, safety, or the relationship's actual terms.

## 30.4 Weakened or rejected

- Kei is only a tool at the Year 1 endpoint.
- The Kei confession alone proves completed conventional love.
- Horikita is merely Ayanokōji's puppet.
- Manabu's harshness was legitimate because it eventually produced growth.
- Ichinose's attachment erases her class agency.
- Hiyori is politically passive because she is quiet.
- Protection is more ethical merely because it is more effective.
- Cooperation metrics are equivalent to moral loyalty or relational goodness.

## 30.5 Unresolved

- Whether Kei can leave the relationship safely and remain singularly valued.
- Whether Ayanokōji can disclose enough of the hidden curriculum for romance to become more fully informed.
- Whether Horikita's leadership can survive without Ayanokōji's shadow constitution.
- Whether Kushida coexistence can become voluntary rather than structurally coerced.
- Whether Ayanokōji Group friendship survives greater truth.
- Whether Ichinose's bond with Ayanokōji becomes romantic, political, adversarial, or some unstable combination.
- Whether Ryūen's increased legitimacy becomes moral development.
- Whether Sakayanagi and Ayanokōji can recognize one another beyond test-case rivalry.
- Whether Class B can distribute authority without losing its solidaristic identity.

# 31. Canonical Year 1 relationship principles

## 31.1 Governing principle

> **Reciprocity is the capacity to answer back under conditions of difference.**

## 31.2 Protection principle

> Protection becomes less proprietary when it enlarges safe exit, informed disagreement, and independent capability.

## 31.3 Dependency principle

> Dependency is not inherently intimacy, weakness, or pathology; its ethical quality depends on how it was created, what it enables, and whether it can diminish without destroying belonging.

## 31.4 Friendship principle

> Friendship is most visible in voluntary return, tolerated failure, ordinary time, and belonging not conditioned upon constant utility.

## 31.5 Romance principle

> Mutual confession establishes romance but does not by itself resolve unequal knowledge, coercive history, dependence, or irreplaceability.

## 31.6 Recognition principle

> To recognize a person is to see something true while preserving the person's right to exceed and correct the model.

## 31.7 Separation principle

> A durable relationship can survive the end of the role through which it began.

## 31.8 Political principle

> Dissent is one of the strongest forms of relational membership when disagreement does not trigger expulsion from the bond or polity.

# 33. Final synthesis

Year 1 begins with students who often encounter one another as functions inside an institution.

A classmate is:

- a source of points;
- an academic liability;
- a witness;
- a public leader;
- a social interface;
- a protector;
- a protected asset;
- a rival;
- a holder of secrets;
- or a candidate for expulsion.

The series does not imagine that mature relationship requires all function to disappear.

People remain useful to one another.

The problem is whether usefulness becomes the complete theory of the person.

Ayanokōji is the central danger because he is exceptionally good at seeing relational structure.

He can identify:

- what someone fears;
- what role stabilizes her;
- what information will move him;
- what environment will reveal growth;
- and what protection will become indispensable.

That knowledge lets him help people whom static measurement would discard.

It also tempts him to treat a working model as a private jurisdiction over another person's future.

Kei is the strongest test.

Their relation begins through manufactured dependency and becomes a bond containing loyalty, ordinary care, names, gifts, mutual operational trust, explicit romance, and direct desire. The later relation is not fake. The founding violation is not erased. The endpoint is therefore neither conversion from instrument to person nor proof that love has conquered strategy.

It is a relationship in which a person once acquired as infrastructure increasingly answers back—and in which the stronger participant still interprets romance through a curriculum the other does not fully know.

Horikita provides a different test.

She begins inside Ayanokōji's hidden environment and becomes increasingly capable of identifying, challenging, and diverging from his authorship. Her relation with Sudō demonstrates what healthier development can produce: not obedience, but dissent, expertise, and differentiated interdependence. Her relation with Kushida demonstrates the opposite danger: a principled refusal of disposability may become unilateral jurisdiction when the class bears risks it did not authorize.

The wider cast supplies alternative relational constitutions.

Hirata protects by hoping the protected person can leave.

Ichinose creates belonging that survives truth.

Kanzaki criticizes from inside loyalty.

Hiyori shows that quiet friendship and corrective agency do not require domination.

Ryūen discovers that fear cannot fully explain why people remain.

Manabu's mature recognition releases rather than specifies.

Sakayanagi shows how seeing hidden ability can still become aristocratic allocation.

The Ayanokōji Group proves that partial selves can share genuine ordinary life.

These relationships produce the Year 1 answer:

> **A relationship is not reciprocal merely because both people feel strongly, need one another, or benefit from the bond. It becomes reciprocal when each person can affect what the relationship means; when refusal does not automatically destroy safety or belonging; when development produces independent authorship rather than obedient performance; when protection makes exit more possible rather than less; and when the person remains valuable after the role through which the relationship began has changed or ended.**

That standard does not require equal power.

It requires answerability.

The Year 1 endpoint is therefore hopeful without being resolved.

Kei can answer the confession.

Horikita can demand a wager.

Sudō can oppose his mentor.

Hiyori can refuse her leader.

Kanzaki can dissent from solidarity.

Ichinose can care across rivalry without abandoning her class.

Suzune can love Manabu and stop becoming him.

These acts do not eliminate asymmetry.

They establish that asymmetry has begun to encounter people capable of answering back.

*First File* adds the final restraint.

No file is the person.

No rating is the person.

No strategic model is the person.

No role—tool, host, leader, girlfriend, rival, witness, protector, successor—is the person in full.

The ethical future of every major Year 1 relationship depends upon whether the participants can preserve that gap:

> **the space in which another person can become someone the relationship did not already know how to contain.**
