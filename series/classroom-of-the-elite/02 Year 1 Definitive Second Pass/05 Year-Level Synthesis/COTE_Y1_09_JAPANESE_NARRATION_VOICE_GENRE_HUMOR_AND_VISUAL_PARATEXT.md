# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ』
## Year 1 — Japanese Narration, Voice, Genre, Humor, and Visual Paratext
### How the novels make hidden interiors audible while keeping every person larger than the voice or image used to represent them

> **CANONICAL YEAR-1 SPECIALIST SNAPSHOT**
>
> This document is the primary analytical home for the Japanese first-person form, character register, pronouns and address terms, humor, genre modulation, illustration, embodiment, dossier aesthetics, and the formal relation between public performance and private structure across *Classroom of the Elite* Year 1. It synthesizes the fourteen canonical Japanese novel deep readings, the *First File* paratext audit, the final Year 1 evidence lock, the Japanese terminology and passage index, and the frozen `Y1_01–Y1_08` specialist layer.
>
> It does not import Year 2, Volume 0, *Second List*, Year 3, adaptation performances, wikis, or later-franchise interpretation. Voice claims are made only where repeated Year 1 evidence supports them. Illustrations are treated as primary visual paratext, not as replacements for the prose or as transparent photographs of interior state.

---

# 0. Scope, primary home, and interpretive controls

Year 1 continually asks the reader to decide what kind of person Ayanokōji Kiyotaka is while denying the reader a single stable linguistic answer.

His narration can sound:

- socially anxious;
- sexually curious;
- self-mocking;
- bored;
- affectionate;
- legally analytical;
- tactically compressed;
- clinically coercive;
- emotionally uncertain;
- and metaphysically estranged from his own life

within the same volume and sometimes within the same scene.

The rest of the cast is similarly constructed through voice rather than merely described by plot function. Horikita’s polished feminine certainty can conceal fear without becoming false. Kei’s assertive `あたし` is both a social weapon and an authentic acquired competence. Kushida’s cushioning public language and contemptuous private language belong to one organized person rather than two simple selves. Hirata’s inclusive `僕` is a political technology until crisis tears it open. Ryūen’s roughness converts local domination into universal theory. Sakayanagi’s refined politeness makes predation more—not less—violent. Nagumo’s casual `～ッス` lets hierarchical power wear the rhythm of social ease.

The illustrations add a second representational system. They may:

- disclose bodily information the dialogue conceals;
- privilege one relationship over another;
- turn institutional power into posture and spatial hierarchy;
- restore pain to a strategically abstract scene;
- or complicate the prose ethically by eroticizing precisely the vulnerability the narration identifies as coercive.

The analytical problem is therefore not only **what happens**.

It is:

> **How do Japanese voice, narrative perspective, genre convention, comic rhythm, and editorial image teach the reader what kind of event is occurring—and where do those forms disclose, distort, aestheticize, or resist the character’s public role?**

This document applies ten controls.

## 0.1 Japanese voice is evidence, not a personality horoscope

Pronouns, sentence endings, politeness, contractions, lexical choices, rhythm, hesitation, and address terms carry characterization.

They do not operate as deterministic codes.

A feminine ending such as `～わ` does not by itself prove warmth, softness, or sincerity. Horikita and Sakayanagi use controlled feminine language for very different forms of judgment. `僕` does not automatically mean kindness; it matters because Hirata repeatedly combines it with inclusive requests and nonexclusive care. Rough masculine speech does not establish cognitive simplicity; Ryūen’s strategic reasoning remains sophisticated.

Voice claims therefore require repeated evidence across ordinary conversation, stress, conflict, public leadership, and private speech.

## 0.2 Ayanokōji’s first-person narration is neither transparent confession nor wholesale deception

His event reporting is often accurate.

His account becomes less complete around:

- advance planning;
- the scale of his competence;
- emotional investment;
- responsibility for another person’s crisis;
- possessiveness;
- and the possibility that an ordinary desire matters independently of strategy.

The correct response is selective analysis, not the claim that “everything he says is a lie.” Separate:

- event report;
- causal reconstruction;
- self-explanation;
- metaphor;
- later behavior;
- other characters’ observations;
- and formal placement.

## 0.3 Flat public delivery is not evidence of an empty interior

Ayanokōji’s ordinary spoken economy contrasts with an internally active voice that worries about friendship status, notices bodies, enjoys food and cinema, fears social failure, and wants romance. His limited outward expressiveness is a fact about presentation. It is not proof of absent desire (`Y1V01-E003–E010`; `Y1V03-E002–E003`; `Y1V07.5-E063–E069`).

## 0.4 Humor is evidence but not exoneration

Comic narration can reveal:

- adolescent ignorance;
- embodied helplessness;
- social belonging;
- pressure relief;
- and the survival of ordinary life.

It can also soften reader judgment, eroticize vulnerability, or reset tone after abuse.

A scene becoming funny does not make its underlying power relation harmless. Conversely, humor should not be dismissed as filler merely because the same narrator can later become frightening.

## 0.5 Genre modes overlap rather than replace one another

Year 1 moves among:

- school comedy;
- social romance;
- examination puzzle;
- procedural mystery;
- survival fiction;
- psychological thriller;
- sports drama;
- institutional political fiction;
- family conspiracy;
- and graduation narrative.

These are not sealed compartments. A Valentine scene can also be a surveillance operation. A sports relay can be personal liberation, public spectacle, institutional metric, and strategic exposure. A Christmas date can be romance, social education, security analysis, and ideological aftermath.

## 0.6 Address changes are relational evidence, not automatic romantic proof

A shift from surname to given name can mark:

- intimacy;
- secrecy;
- redefinition of role;
- self-authorship;
- or a private operational world.

It does not by itself settle love, equality, or consent. `清隆` and `恵` matter because they occur inside a relationship with prior coercion, increasing negotiation, and hidden public/private rules (`Y1V07.5-E093–E100`).

## 0.7 Illustrations are primary paratext, not prose fact

An illustration can establish:

- composition;
- bodily distance;
- pose;
- clothing;
- visible injury;
- editorial emphasis;
- and which scene the publication chooses to make memorable.

It cannot by itself establish an exact thought, motive, or emotion not supported by the prose.

## 0.8 Editorial emphasis is analytically meaningful even when it is commercial

Covers, swimsuit images, color front matter, romantic compositions, and character-database inserts reveal how the publication markets the work and organizes reader attention.

“Fanservice” is therefore not a reason to exclude an image from analysis. It is a description of one function the image performs. The question remains what other narrative and ethical work the same image does.

## 0.9 The file/person distinction applies to voice and image

A voice profile is a useful model.

An illustration is a selected view.

A dossier is an institutional representation.

None becomes the whole person. *First File* makes that limit explicit by placing quantified character pages, timeline compression, casual-clothes archives, editorial summaries, and intimate bonus fiction inside one object (`Y1FF-E001–E058`; `Y1FF-E059–E082`).

## 0.10 The Year 1 boundary remains binding

Later performances, later name changes, Volume 0 disclosure, adaptation voice acting, and later guidebooks may eventually revise the current picture. They are not used here to settle what the Year 1 novels themselves make audible or visible.

# 1. Executive thesis

The strongest Year 1 formal thesis is:

> **The novels make public performance and private structure simultaneously visible without granting either one final authority. Ayanokōji’s `オレ` narration lets comic adolescence, strategic conditioning, care, desire, and coercion coexist in one voice; other characters’ registers make their political and relational methods audible; genre changes alter what counts as relevant evidence; and illustrations turn hidden structures into bodies while sometimes introducing their own commercial distortions. The reader receives privileged access, but never a complete file.**

This first-person structure is crucial to the series’ ethical effect.

A third-person account could simply label Ayanokōji:

- traumatized;
- manipulative;
- lonely;
- brilliant;
- emotionally repressed;
- or morally compromised.

The actual narration makes the reader inhabit transitions among these states.

The same internal voice that worries whether he is a low-ranked friend can later reduce another person’s options with procedural precision (`Y1V02-E004`; `Y1V02-E027–E030`). The same narrator who notices Kushida’s body and fantasizes about ordinary romance can classify Kei as an instrument or educational medium (`Y1V03-E003`; `Y1V07.5-E073–E077`; `Y1V11.5-E127–E140`). The same person who calls Hirata a necessary `パーツ` later wants a friend before whom tears can be shown (`Y1V11-E089–E101`). The narration does not resolve the contradiction by informing the reader which layer is “real.”

All are real.

What changes is:

- which layer controls action;
- which layer he can name;
- which layer he uses to explain himself;
- and which layer another person forces him to confront.

The cast’s voices perform parallel work.

Horikita’s development does not require abandoning her complete sentences, formal feminine endings, or judgment-oriented diction. Her language changes by allowing gratitude, request, uncertainty, and self-revision into the established register (`Y1V04.5-E011–E016`; `Y1V10-E042–E052`; `Y1V11.5-E001–E016`).

Kei’s public gyaru fluency is not exposed as “fake” and discarded. It remains a real social capacity even when fear fragments her speech. Her later ability to challenge Ayanokōji and demand `誠意` shows autonomy developing within the same `あたし` voice (`Y1V04-E001–E013`; `Y1V07-E055–E070`; `Y1V07.5-E093–E100`).

Kushida’s public cushioning is both performance and actual skill. Hirata’s gentleness is both ethical commitment and defensive role. Sakayanagi’s elegance is both personhood and political weapon. Kōenji’s theatrical self-mythologizing is both comic style and refusal of ordinary claims over him.

Genre then decides what becomes visible.

The examination volumes make action legible through rules, inference, risk, and retrospective reconstruction. The `.5` volumes allow food, embarrassment, gifts, films, clothing, bodies, and purposeless conversation to matter without immediate class-point conversion. The White Room material shifts the work toward conspiracy and developmental horror. The romance scenes do not replace the political story; they reveal how strategic grammar enters intimacy.

The visual system completes—and complicates—the design.

- Kei’s scar turns private history into bodily record.
- Sakayanagi’s cane separates physical limitation from political weakness.
- Ayanokōji’s trained body contradicts his public statistics.
- Horikita’s hair carries imitation, integration, and self-authorship.
- Hirata’s tears dismantle the visual role of universal reassurance.
- the database inserts and *First File* pages turn students into legible profiles;
- the bonus fiction and ordinary-life images show persons exceeding those profiles.

The final Year 1 proposition is therefore not that voice and image reveal the hidden truth behind performance.

It is:

> **Voice and image produce competing partial truths. Literary maturity lies in reading their relation without treating any one register, metaphor, illustration, score, or genre convention as the whole person.**

# 2. The first-person form as an authored interface

Ayanokōji controls what most characters know about him.

The narration gives the reader more access than the characters receive, but not unlimited access. This creates three concentric information environments.

## 2.1 The public Ayanokōji

Classmates encounter:

- short answers;
- mild awkwardness;
- mediocre scores;
- limited initiative;
- and a body and expression designed to avoid strong conclusions.

His public performance is not pure silence. It is an authored interface whose principal achievement is preventing other people from asking the right questions (`Y1V01-E008`, `Y1V01-E013`, `Y1V05-E062–E073`; `Y1FF-E004–E008`).

## 2.2 The narrated Ayanokōji

The reader receives:

- ordinary desire;
- self-deprecation;
- sexual noticing;
- social anxiety;
- strategic modeling;
- memories and comparisons;
- and some explicit statements of governing belief.

This is vastly more than classmates receive.

It is still selected.

Plans are often disclosed only after they have worked. Emotional investment is frequently named through utility or uncertainty. The narrator can admit a fact while minimizing its significance. He can describe another person accurately while concealing how much of the surrounding environment he created.

## 2.3 The structurally reconstructed Ayanokōji

The fullest Year 1 account emerges only by combining:

- his narration;
- other-character prologues;
- external observations;
- later reveal chapters;
- illustration;
- and consequences he does not fully explain.

Volume 4 is the clearest early example. Kei’s monologue lets the reader know the survival structure before Ayanokōji turns that structure into a target. His accuracy therefore becomes ethically disturbing rather than simply impressive (`Y1V04-E001–E013`; `Y1V04-E032–E061`).

Volume 9 uses a similar architecture. Ichinose defines her own wrongdoing before the rumor operation is reconstructed. The later causal reveal prevents the reader from rewriting Ayanokōji as a passive source of comfort (`Y1V09-E001–E013`; `Y1V09-E076–E110`).

The formal pattern is:

> **subjective interior → apparent event → strategic intervention → retrospective reconstruction → unresolved remainder**

That remainder is essential. The reveal tells us what he did. It does not always tell us everything the action meant to him.

# 3. Alternate perspectives as limits on narratorial sovereignty

Ayanokōji’s first-person voice is the dominant formal instrument of Year 1, but the novels repeatedly prevent dominance from becoming monopoly.

Prologues, short perspective transfers, interior monologues, reported memories, and scenes organized around another person’s perception do more than fill factual gaps. They allow other characters to appear as subjects before—or after—Ayanokōji’s strategic explanation converts them into functions.

This is one of the corpus’s strongest formal safeguards against the protagonist’s own politics of legibility.

## 3.1 Sakura before “the witness”

Volume 2 lets Sakura articulate the difficulty of living through a divided public self before the disciplinary case turns her into evidentiary infrastructure. Her school hesitancy and her `雫 / Shizuku` persona are not merely clues for Ayanokōji to decode. They are ways of negotiating visibility, shame, desire, and control (`Y1V02-E003–E005`, `Y1V02-E037–E040`).

The perspective design therefore prevents institutional usefulness from exhausting her narrative identity.

## 3.2 Kei before “eyes and ears”

Volume 4’s opening monologue changes the moral order of the book. The reader encounters Kei’s hierarchy, fear, host logic, scar, and survival doctrine from inside before Ayanokōji’s recruitment plan claims explanatory superiority (`Y1V04-E001–E004`).

Without this choice, the volume could look like a strategist discovering a valuable social asset. With it, the reader knows that the “asset” already contains history, self-explanation, and a survival intelligence not authored by him.

The rooftop later repeats the correction. Kei’s refusal to name Ayanokōji receives enough independent interior space to show where she exceeds the dependency model he built (`Y1V07-E060–E064`).

## 3.3 Sudō and Hirata: public problems become private histories

Sudō’s sports-festival monologue makes his rough self-condemnation audible before the class treats him as a failed athletic resource. `クズ` has become an identity inherited from family judgment and reapplied to himself (`Y1V05-E001–E002`).

Volume 10’s flash-forward prologue performs a parallel operation for Hirata. `堀北……ちょっと黙れよ` is heard before its cause is reconstructed, allowing register collapse to become the organizing mystery of the Class Vote (`Y1V10-E005`, `Y1V10-E063–E084`).

## 3.4 Kushida, Ryūen, and Ichinose: doctrines in their own voices

Kushida’s private perspective prevents her hatred from functioning as a simple “true face” twist. Public competence, recognition hunger, secret accumulation, pleasure in sociability, and resentment belong to one structure (`Y1V06-E001–E004`, `Y1V06-E031–E038`).

Ryūen’s interior passages make fear an explicit theory of governance rather than uncontrolled temper (`Y1V07-E001–E005`).

Ichinose’s confession gives her wrongdoing and shame a self-authored form before Sakayanagi’s reputational framing can become the final story (`Y1V09-E076–E100`).

## 3.5 Sakayanagi and Matsushita: Ayanokōji becomes the observed object

Sakayanagi’s childhood memory of the White Room places Ayanokōji behind glass, literally organized by another person’s gaze (`Y1V11-E001–E013`). Volume 11.5’s Matsushita perspective then turns the apparently ordinary classmate into a counterintelligence observer who tails him, builds hypotheses, and makes her own incomplete file (`Y1V11.5-E106–E116`).

The pattern is formally exact:

> **The narrator most skilled at observing others is repeatedly returned to the position of the observed.**

Alternate perspective does not abolish first-person privilege. It makes the privilege answerable by demonstrating that no narrator possesses final jurisdiction over everyone else’s interior life.

# 4. Ayanokōji’s Year 1 voice: one `オレ`, several operative modes

Ayanokōji’s casual `オレ` remains stable across Year 1.

The stable pronoun is important because the internal modes do not arrive as named alternate personalities. The same self speaks them.

## 4.1 Social-comic mode

This mode includes:

- rehearsed friendship openings;
- anxiety about whether another student counts him as a friend;
- exaggerated failure scenarios;
- attraction and sexual curiosity;
- ignorance of common games and ordinary youth culture;
- jokes about proposals, dates, and social rank;
- and bodily retreat when proximity becomes too embarrassing.

Volume 1 establishes the baseline before the strategic plot has acquired full dominance (`Y1V01-E003–E010`, `Y1V01-E031`). Volume 2 continues it through friendship insecurity and ordinary intrusion boundaries (`Y1V02-E004–E006`). Volume 4’s cruise opening adds adolescent sexual comedy immediately before the narration turns clinical. Volume 4.5 makes the ordinary mode the principal surface: gifts, ice cream, invitations, swimsuits, embarrassment, friendship, and bodily absurdity matter without being reducible to exam strategy (`Y1V04.5-E001–E020`, `Y1V04.5-E039–E067`).

Volume 7.5 is especially important because the narration can admit wanting a girlfriend, romantic experience, sex, music, cinema, animals, snow, and ordinary conversation while remaining unable to convert those desires into stable relational knowledge (`Y1V07.5-E001–E015`, `Y1V07.5-E063–E077`).

## 4.2 Observational mode

Ayanokōji notices:

- status cues;
- bodily tension;
- who speaks first;
- who avoids eye contact;
- how a group responds to one person;
- and which detail does not fit the visible story.

This is not always manipulative. It is the basic perceptual habit through which he understands both ordinary social scenes and strategic systems.

The danger begins when observation becomes a presumption of jurisdiction.

## 4.3 Strategic-compression mode

When a problem becomes operational, sentence structure and lexical field tighten.

The narration moves toward:

- constraints;
- resources;
- timing;
- information;
- probability;
- leverage;
- fallback;
- and victory conditions.

Volume 4’s decision that he needs “eyes and ears,” Volume 8’s reconstruction of Nagumo’s resource operation, and Volume 9’s rumor counteroperation all use this causal compression (`Y1V04-E021–E031`; `Y1V08-E095–E142`; `Y1V09-E076–E110`).

The mode is aesthetically attractive because it makes complicated systems suddenly legible. That pleasure is part of the series’ design.

It also risks making people look like variables because the prose itself becomes cleanest when their agency has been reduced to predictable inputs.

## 4.4 Clinical-coercive mode

The most disturbing register appears when he converts:

- trauma;
- bodily vulnerability;
- fear;
- shame;
- social isolation;
- or emotional collapse

into material for intervention.

The tone often lacks the rhetorical alarm that an external moral narrator would supply.

That absence is characterization.

The reader must supply the distinction between:

- an accurate diagnosis;
- an effective method;
- and a legitimate method.

Volume 4, the rooftop delay, the rumor campaign, and the Hirata confrontation are the strongest Year 1 cases (`Y1V04-E032–E061`; `Y1V07-E052–E096`; `Y1V09-E076–E110`; `Y1V11-E089–E101`).

## 4.5 Philosophical-self-modeling mode

At moments of crisis, Ayanokōji names himself through abstractions:

- freedom;
- defective product;
- tools;
- victory;
- White Room;
- textbook;
- prayer;
- and the wish to become someone capable of irreplaceable attachment.

These formulations are unusually important because they are attempts to explain his own continuity.

They are not automatically final truth.

The metaphor may accurately reveal the grammar currently available to him while underdescribing the person or relationship to which it is applied.

# 5. Selective narratorial unreliability

The useful Year 1 model is not “reliable” versus “unreliable.”

It is a field of varying reliability.

| Domain | General Year 1 reliability | Recurring limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Physical event report | high | he may omit prior preparation or delayed intervention |
| Rule and incentive analysis | high | he may treat an institution’s stated mechanism as more complete than its political purpose |
| Other people’s likely behavior | often very high | accurate prediction can be mistaken for moral title over the predicted person |
| His own competence | deliberately low in public, selectively disclosed internally | scale and preparation are minimized until revelation is useful |
| Immediate attraction or embarrassment | comparatively direct | he may not know what long-term relational category the feeling belongs to |
| Motive | mixed | several motives may be true while one strategically safe explanation is foregrounded |
| Emotional attachment | underarticulated | behavior, memory, protectiveness, bodily response, and other observers may precede his label |
| Metaphor | high as self-model evidence | low as exhaustive description of the other person |
| Counterfactual claim | limited | “perhaps I would have…” remains hypothesis, not established alternate fact |

## 5.1 Omission of planning

The series repeatedly lets the reader encounter effects before causes.

Horikita reconstructs that the dummy-camera solution was seeded rather than spontaneously invented (`Y1V02-E027–E030`, `Y1V02-E044`). Volume 3 reveals the full island intervention after the public victory. Volume 4.5 reveals Operation Delta after the pool episode. Volume 9 rewinds the rumor campaign.

The narrator does not usually falsify the immediate scene.

He withholds the causal architecture.

## 5.2 Minimization of emotional stake

Ayanokōji can say he helped because classmates were his first friends and then immediately recoil toward defect language (`Y1V02-E045–E046`). He can preserve Ryūen through strategic explanation while also admitting he somehow did not want him expelled (`Y1V10-E079–E091`). He can call Kei indispensable, later confess, and still describe her as a textbook and irreplaceability as an aspiration rather than fact (`Y1V07.5-E093–E100`; `Y1V11.5-E127–E140`).

The safest interpretation is layered motive, not secret purity or secret emptiness.

## 5.3 Behavior before vocabulary

Emotion often becomes visible through:

- gifts;
- medicine;
- remembering;
- discomfort at loss;
- choice of proximity;
- or the desire to extend time

before Ayanokōji can name it with confidence.

The narration’s lexical delay is itself part of his psychology.

## 5.4 External correction

Satō notices that he enjoyed the date without smiling (`Y1V07.5-E064`). Kei challenges the tool logic directly (`Y1V07.5-E071–E077`). Horikita reconstructs his authorship (`Y1V02-E044`; `Y1V11-E072–E088`). Sakayanagi notices when the official chess play no longer feels like him (`Y1V11-E133–E149`).

The first-person form therefore remains permeable to other people’s readings.

# 6. Humor and the ordinary adolescent layer

Humor is one of the strongest formal arguments against the idea that Ayanokōji’s school self is only camouflage.

An internal joke has no class audience to deceive.

When he:

- panics over friendship rank;
- imagines romantic gifts that accidentally resemble a proposal;
- notices that a date is socially exhausting despite being enjoyable;
- becomes trapped by a water bottle;
- reacts to swimsuits;
- or fails to understand ordinary conversational expectations,

we are not watching a master strategist perform mediocrity for observers.

We are watching someone encounter domains in which strategic mastery does not supply ordinary fluency (`Y1V01-E003–E010`; `Y1V04.5-E001–E020`; `Y1V07.5-E063–E069`; `Y1V11-E055–E071`).

## 6.1 Humor as de-specialization

The comic body is politically useful because it refuses to let competence become total identity.

Horikita can be intellectually formidable and still require help with a bottle. Ayanokōji can repeat the same mistake. A broken elevator ignores Ibuki’s hostility and Ayanokōji’s planning. Heat, fatigue, bathing, clothing, and attraction place ordinary limits on everyone (`Y1V04.5-E021–E038`; `Y1V08-E053–E069`).

## 6.2 Humor as social participation

Many jokes depend on shared references, teasing, embarrassment, and the right to be mildly ridiculous around other people.

This is one of the ordinary competencies the White Room did not teach.

Ayanokōji’s participation in male sexual banter, dating confusion, gift exchange, cinema, and winter outings is therefore developmental even when no strategic lesson follows.

## 6.3 Humor as tonal reset

The series sometimes places bonus manga, chibi imagery, or romantic comedy after severe coercion (`Y1V04-E065–E068`; `Y1V07.5-E110–E111`).

This can restore the cast to ordinary life.

It can also encourage the reader to move past harm faster than the affected character plausibly can.

The tonal reset should be analyzed, not simply accepted as moral closure.

# 7. Strategic pleasure and the aesthetics of compression

Ayanokōji’s strategy scenes are pleasurable because they make disorder intelligible.

The prose often performs three operations:

1. reveal that the visible game is not the real game;
2. identify the hidden variable;
3. compress a large field of people and incentives into one causal explanation.

This is the same mechanism through which he acquires power inside the plot.

The reader’s pleasure and the protagonist’s power are therefore formally aligned.

## 7.1 The ethical danger of elegance

A strategy can be beautiful as a construction while morally objectionable as an intervention.

The Volume 4 phone operation is formally elegant because identity, device, visible user, true user, and favored person are separated into layers. The same volume’s private operation against Kei is also carefully engineered. Formal precision does not erase the difference between solving an exam and manufacturing dependence.

## 7.2 Reconstruction as reader capture

Later reveal chapters make the reader feel that apparently random events always possessed a hidden order.

That structure can encourage the fantasy that Ayanokōji’s model is total.

Year 1 repeatedly inserts counterevidence:

- Horikita creates a Paper Shuffle counter he did not know about (`Y1V06-E051–E067`);
- Kei refuses betrayal under conditions that exceed the parasite model (`Y1V07-E055–E070`);
- other students infer him from partial evidence;
- Tsukishiro controls infrastructure he cannot simply out-think;
- and ordinary relationships produce desires he cannot classify.

The best formal reading preserves the power of reconstruction without granting omniscience.

# 8. Metaphor systems: how Ayanokōji makes people conceptually manageable

Ayanokōji’s metaphors are not ornamental language.

They are operative models.

## 8.1 `道具 / tool`

The Volume 3 tools passage is explicitly marked as `本心 / true inner position`. It gives the strategic worldview exceptional local authority: people and events are subordinated to final victory (`Y1V03-E050–E054`).

The same volume contains friendship, interest in Sakura’s growth, and appreciation of contextual ability.

The tools passage is therefore governing truth, not exhaustive truth.

## 8.2 `宿木 / host tree`

The host metaphor explains Kei’s survival structure and Ayanokōji’s deliberate transfer of protection dependency from Hirata to himself (`Y1V07-E097–E106`; `Y1V07.5-E093–E100`).

Its limitation becomes visible when Kei acts from a value not reducible to preserving a host.

The narration’s metaphor lags behind the agency the scene displays.

## 8.3 `パーツ / part`

Calling Hirata a necessary part accurately describes class function and falsely tempts the reader to treat restoration of function as the whole moral objective (`Y1V11-E089–E101`).

The same sequence produces the desire for `涙を見せられるような友 / a friend before whom one can show tears`.

Function and particular relation coexist.

## 8.4 `教科書 / textbook`

At the confession endpoint, Kei becomes an educational medium through which Ayanokōji hopes to learn love (`Y1V11.5-E127–E140`).

The metaphor is not equivalent to “fake romance.”

It identifies a hidden curriculum inside a reciprocal romantic act.

The ethical question is whether Kei is permitted to become more than the lesson for which he selected her.

## 8.5 `介錯 / finishing or mercy blow`

Ayanokōji’s promise to Ichinose includes a shadow vocabulary in which he may eventually judge when another leader’s path should end (`Y1V11.5-E085–E100`).

The intimate promise and terminal metaphor belong to the same internal voice.

## 8.6 `扉`, blankness, fluidity, and birth

Doors, blankness, fluidity, and being newly born describe an incomplete autobiographical self rather than emotional nonexistence (`Y1V04.5-E001–E020`; `Y1FF-E059–E070`).

These metaphors explain why ordinary experience can matter so intensely: it supplies forms through which a life becomes narratable.

## 8.7 `掛け替えのない` and `必要不可欠`

The lexical difference between irreplaceable and indispensable prevents a functional relation from being mistaken for singular attachment (`Y1V07.5-E001–E015`, `Y1V07.5-E093–E100`; `Y1V11.5-E127–E140`).

The narration’s uncertainty is precise rather than evasive.

## 8.8 Metaphor as self-protection

Instrumental language often makes relational preservation cognitively safe.

If a person is useful, preserving them does not require confessing attachment.

If a romance is a textbook, entering it does not require certainty that love already exists.

The metaphor can therefore disclose motive while simultaneously defending against another motive.

# 9. Self-correction, modality, and the language of unfinished development

Some of the most important Year 1 changes occur at the level of one modal shift rather than one dramatic confession.

## 9.1 `やるべきこと` → `やりたいこと`

In Volume 11, Ayanokōji distinguishes what he ought to do from what he wants to do and chooses Horikita’s development as a personally desired project (`Y1V11-E072–E088`).

The shift marks self-authorship.

It does not automatically legitimate authorship over Horikita.

## 9.2 `生まれて初めて`

Volume 11.5 describes ordinary school life as the first goal he has chosen himself (`Y1V11.5-E017–E040`).

The phrase gives autobiographical weight to something easily dismissed as camouflage.

## 9.3 Interrogative uncertainty

When he wonders whether romantic feeling may never have existed, the grammar remains a question (`Y1V07.5-E076–E077`).

It should not be converted into a definitive declaration of emotional incapacity.

## 9.4 `祈ろう / let me pray, hope`

The final romance narration does not announce that Kei is already irreplaceable.

It expresses aspiration toward a future self and future relationship (`Y1V11.5-E127–E140`).

The modal humility is one of the clearest signs that Ayanokōji knows his current model is incomplete.

# 10. Horikita Suzune: polished judgment becoming corrigible speech

Horikita’s voice is among the most stable in Year 1.

She generally uses:

- complete sentences;
- controlled feminine endings such as `～わ`, `～よ`, and `～かしら`;
- explicit evaluation;
- relatively little vulgarity;
- and a preference for stating judgments as finished propositions.

Her cruelty therefore sounds polished rather than explosive.

That form matters because one of her principal weaknesses is premature closure. The sentence can sound complete before the model of the person is complete.

## 10.1 Early voice: judgment as distance

In Volumes 1–2, Horikita speaks as though accurate classification should settle political value. Her language converts weakness into defect, solitude into superiority, and social interaction into distraction. Even apology remains formally precise because she has no easy idiom for relational vulnerability (`Y1V01-E009`, `Y1V01-E014–E020`; `Y1V02-E040–E044`).

## 10.2 Development within continuity

She does not become colloquial, effusive, or socially indiscriminate.

Instead, the content admitted into the register expands:

- thanks;
- recognition of trajectory;
- requests for help;
- acknowledgment of personal motive;
- and acceptance that other people possess information she lacks.

Her hesitant `……ありがとう` in Volume 4.5 matters precisely because gratitude remains linguistically difficult. `勇気をください / give me courage` in Volume 10 is a request from the person whose self-image was built around needing no one (`Y1V04.5-E011–E016`; `Y1V10-E042–E052`).

## 10.3 Public accountability

By the Class Vote, her directness becomes politically useful because she states reasons and accepts liability. The same linguistic severity that once excluded people can create contestable judgment when joined to public responsibility (`Y1V10-E053–E078`).

## 10.4 `偽者` and `紛れもない私`

The Volume 11.5 prologue uses falsity and authenticity without treating them as mutually exclusive. The self built through imitation is still `紛れもない私 / unmistakably me` (`Y1V11.5-E001–E016`).

This is a mature linguistic formulation of self-authorship:

> constructed influence remains real biography, but no longer determines the only permissible future.

## 10.5 Voice and hair

The haircut does not reveal a hidden pure self beneath false language.

It visually accompanies a verbal integration. Horikita can retain Manabu’s influence, name imitation as imitation, and still choose what the inherited self becomes.

# 11. Karuizawa Kei: social fluency, fractured fear, and the private `あたし`

Kei’s voice is not best divided into “fake gyaru” and “true victim.”

Her public `あたし` is an acquired social technology that became genuinely effective.

She can:

- interrupt;
- mock;
- establish atmosphere;
- recruit compliant friends;
- make a demand look socially normal;
- and translate covert action into ordinary girl-group behavior.

The persona began as survival. It became ability (`Y1V04-E001–E013`; `Y1V04.5-E039–E067`).

## 11.1 Public confidence as embodied speech

Kei’s ordinary public language is rapid, direct, emotionally legible, and status-conscious. It assumes the right to speak before others and to define whether a situation is embarrassing, fashionable, acceptable, or socially dangerous.

This voice is not merely verbal. Clothing, posture, facial expression, phone use, and physical proximity support it.

## 11.2 Fear as loss of syntax

Under severe threat, fluency breaks into:

- repetition;
- incomplete clauses;
- denial;
- pleading;
- and phonetic fracture.

Her rooftop refusal—`何度聞かれても……ぜっ『た』いに、言わない`—matters because certainty is built inside bodily collapse rather than announced from composure (`Y1V07-E055–E070`).

The broken `絶対` makes agency audible as effort.

## 11.3 `最後まで格好つけたい`

Kei does not narrate the refusal through grand moral vocabulary. She wants to remain “cool” or admirable to the end.

That modest self-description protects the act from saintly abstraction. Her agency is personal before it is ideological.

## 11.4 Private directness with Ayanokōji

Kei’s private speech becomes one of the few recurring voices capable of directly accusing him of:

- inconsistency;
- cruelty;
- evasiveness;
- or treating people as tools.

By Volume 7.5 she demands reasons and `誠意 / sincerity, good faith` rather than merely receiving instructions (`Y1V07.5-E071–E100`).

This does not equal information symmetry.

It does show that the relationship increasingly contains correction.

## 11.5 Given-name intimacy

The private use of `清隆` and `恵` creates a hidden speech community inside the public surname system (`Y1V07.5-E096`).

The change has several simultaneous meanings:

- the Hirata fiction is ending;
- Ayanokōji becomes the principal private protector;
- operational distance decreases;
- emotional possibility increases;
- and public concealment remains.

The comedic heart-arrow image correctly registers the change as emotionally forceful, even though the surrounding conversation frames it practically (`Y1V07.5-E110–E111`).

## 11.6 Voice after confession

Kei’s answer to the Volume 11.5 confession matters because she speaks the relationship into existence. She is not visually or verbally reduced to a passive reward. The hidden curriculum remains, but the romantic answer is hers (`Y1V11.5-E127–E140`).

# 12. Kushida Kikyō: cushioning as power and register as evidence control

Kushida’s voice is the most explicit demonstration that social warmth can be both sincere experience and strategic capacity.

## 12.1 Public cushioning

Her public language uses:

- soft questions;
- invitations;
- diminutives;
- responsive backchannels;
- inclusive wording;
- and pressure reduction.

A phrase such as `ゆっくりでいいよ / take your time` does not merely sound kind. It modifies the communicative environment so an anxious person can continue (`Y1V01-E015–E016`; `Y1V02-E017–E023`).

That is real social intelligence.

## 12.2 Private contempt

The private voice becomes blunt, resentful, competitive, and threatening. It exposes the recognition economy beneath the public helper role.

The existence of the private register does not prove every public pleasure false. Kushida genuinely likes being socially desired. The public performance is one mechanism through which that real desire is satisfied (`Y1V06-E001–E011`).

## 12.3 Voice under recording

Volume 9 gives the most technical demonstration. Kushida’s language becomes unnaturally controlled when she knows the phone is recording. Once the apparent recording ends, the voice loosens—only for a second recorder to reveal that the relaxed register was still being evaluated (`Y1V09-E054–E075`).

This scene makes voice itself part of evidence production.

The question is no longer only what she said.

It is:

- which version of her expected preservation;
- what audience she imagined;
- and which voice the record will authorize as authentic.

## 12.4 The limits of the mask metaphor

Kushida’s public and private voices are not two persons competing for ontological priority.

Both belong to a single system organized around:

- being needed;
- retaining secrets;
- preventing exposure;
- and punishing anyone who threatens the hierarchy of recognition.

The literary achievement lies in making social competence morally ambivalent without treating competence itself as unreal.

# 13. Hirata Yōsuke: `僕`, nonexclusive care, and register collapse

Hirata’s ordinary `僕` is gentle, explanatory, and inclusive.

His leadership language typically:

- asks rather than commands;
- offers exit;
- names other people’s likely feelings;
- translates conflict into solvable form;
- and distributes care through class membership rather than exclusive possession.

This is not decorative niceness. It is political method (`Y1V01-E025`; `Y1V03-E014–E015`; `Y1V04-E014–E020`).

## 13.1 Nonexclusive care

Hirata’s refusal to retaliate against Kei’s bullies is linguistically calm. He does not humiliate her for wanting revenge. He states that revenge violates the kind of protection he can offer.

Similarly, his description of Kei as one important classmate rather than an exclusively privileged girlfriend makes the constitutional structure of his care audible.

## 13.2 The defensive role inside the voice

The polished register protects other people and protects Hirata from particular attachment.

If everyone is equally important, no one person can become the site of catastrophic loss. Volume 11.5 makes this logic explicit through `大切な人 / an important person` and his fear of repeating the past (`Y1V11.5-E041–E058`).

## 13.3 Register collapse in the Class Vote

`堀北……ちょっと黙れよ` is powerful because it violates his established voice. It is not extreme profanity. Its force comes from discontinuity (`Y1V10-E053–E078`).

The buried coercive self returns through roughness.

His subsequent attempt to make classmates vote against him is verbal self-erasure: he tries to perform a hated identity strongly enough that accumulated trust will no longer protect him.

The class refuses the newly spoken file.

## 13.4 Tears and restored asking

In Volume 11, withdrawal reduces speech almost to absence. The later disclosure and tears coincide with restored ability to:

- ask;
- apologize;
- accept help;
- and permit particular relation (`Y1V11-E089–E101`).

The voice’s recovery is not return to the unchanged ideal-boy role. It is the first possibility of a less defensive version of the same care.

# 14. Sudō, Ike, and Keisei: roughness, practical speech, and evaluative precision

## 14.1 Sudō’s `俺`

Sudō’s rough `俺`, contractions, imperatives, and insults make bodily aggression audible. His speech initially acts before reflection.

Development is heard not through replacement by polite speech but through the addition of:

- apology;
- request;
- acknowledgment;
- self-analysis;
- and domain-specific authority.

His Volume 5 monologue grants him narrative authority over his own family shame, athletic history, and self-contempt rather than leaving him as the object of Horikita’s developmental project (`Y1V05-E001–E013`).

The voice remains blunt while the person becomes more capable of relation.

## 14.2 Ike’s ordinary social intelligence

Ike’s voice is often comic and unserious, but Year 1 repeatedly shows practical social judgment unavailable to more prestigious students.

His camping language becomes authoritative when the ecology changes (`Y1V03-E010–E013`). In Volume 7.5 he protects Shinohara through a face-saving explanation rather than demanding public admission of guilt (`Y1V07.5-E031–E034`).

The comic register does not cancel competence.

## 14.3 Keisei’s evaluative register

Keisei speaks precisely and analytically. His direct request to be called `啓誠` is therefore significant: self-definition enters a voice normally devoted to evaluation (`Y1V06-E034–E050`).

The given-name request does not dissolve seriousness. It creates a personal identity within it.

His Year 1 endpoint—support Horikita when she is right and oppose her when she is wrong—turns evaluative speech into constitutional dissent (`Y1V11.5-E011–E012`).

# 15. Ryūen Kakeru: proprietary roughness and totalizing political rhetoric

Ryūen’s speech is rough, amused, invasive, and bodily.

He treats:

- space;
- phones;
- information;
- fear;
- and subordinate action

as things he may enter, seize, or define.

The register makes authority personal.

## 15.1 Local success becomes universal law

His monologues repeatedly move from one successful domination to a total theory of humanity.

The lexical cluster `恐怖 / fear`, `愉悦 / pleasure`, `暴力 / violence`, `勝利 / victory`, and `実力 / ability` converts memory into doctrine (`Y1V07-E001–E019`).

This is one of his principal intellectual errors. A model that works powerfully under one environment is treated as a universal anthropology.

## 15.2 Vulgarity is not simplicity

Ryūen’s speech is often more sophisticated than his roughness suggests. He reads incentives, institutional blind spots, and emotional leverage with high accuracy.

The literary form refuses the class-coded assumption that refinement equals intelligence.

## 15.3 `勝つための悪逆`

The phrase is ethically important because he does not euphemize his method. It is wickedness for the sake of winning (`Y1V11-E109–E121`).

The reader should not become more celebratory than the character’s own vocabulary.

## 15.4 Post-defeat voice

The loss of theatrical dominance does not eliminate his speech style. What changes is the political environment around it. Followers begin answering him from loyalty, irritation, judgment, and voluntary investment rather than fear alone.

The same ruler becomes more sociologically legitimate without becoming linguistically democratic.

---

# 16. Ichinose Honami: inclusive warmth, apology, and the recovery of declarative force

Ichinose’s voice combines colloquial warmth with public-command fluency.

She often speaks through questions whose functions are double:

- genuine invitation;
- and diagnostic observation.

The language makes participation feel voluntary while steadily organizing the room (`Y1V02-E012–E023`; `Y1V03-E021–E023`; `Y1V04-E014–E020`).

## 16.1 Warmth as leadership technology

Ichinose’s politeness is not lack of authority. It is one source of authority.

Because people feel seen rather than ordered, they permit her to coordinate information and resources.

## 16.2 Apology as overresponsibility

Under attack, she remains apologetic and relational. She worries about the burden her past imposes on classmates and makes it easier to protect them than to allow protection of herself (`Y1V09-E001–E025`).

The voice’s virtue becomes pathology when consideration removes her own claim to support.

## 16.3 `これで私の懺悔は終わり！`

Her public confession becomes development when it ends with a boundary rather than an endless apology (`Y1V09-E064–E075`).

The declarative force matters.

She does not say that the wrongdoing never occurred. She refuses to let confession remain permanent social ownership.

## 16.4 Political opposition and relational trust

`味方じゃない……だけど信頼できる人 / not an ally, but someone I can trust` gives Year 1 one of its strongest relational categories (`Y1V11.5-E085–E100`).

The language refuses the genre expectation that rival classes must eliminate private trust or that private feeling must dissolve political independence.

# 17. Sakayanagi Arisu: refined politeness as predatory force

Sakayanagi’s speech is controlled, feminine, playful, and often formally polite.

The content can be extraordinarily severe.

This incongruity is central to her effect.

## 17.1 Civility does not moderate domination

Terms such as:

- `実験体 / experimental subject`;
- `調教中 / being conditioned`;
- criminal identity;
- expulsion wagers;
- and natural/artificial genius

arrive without loss of composure (`Y1V09-E026–E053`; `Y1V10-E053–E078`; `Y1V11-E001–E015`).

The polished surface increases the violence by making it sound settled, civilized, and intellectually inevitable.

## 17.2 Ideology in a serene voice

The Volume 11 prologue lets hereditarian absolutism sound almost tranquil. That is a formal warning: ideological extremity does not require theatrical rage.

## 17.3 Personal speech at the boundary of control

After the private chess replay, Sakayanagi’s admission that she wanted one more second in the same space becomes striking because it departs from the ordinary strategic indirection (`Y1V11-E133–E149`).

The intimate statement does not erase rivalry.

It reveals that the rivalry has become a relationship whose value is not exhausted by proving a theory.

# 18. Hiyori and Kōenji: two forms of low-compliance voice

## 18.1 Shiina Hiyori

Hiyori’s register is calm, understated, and observational.

The quietness contrasts sharply with Ryūen’s theatrical command. It makes her perceptiveness easy to underestimate, a problem *First File* later reproduces when low formal judgment coexists with editorial recognition of inference and quick thinking (`Y1V06-E071`; `Y1FF-E030–E032`).

Her book conversations with Ayanokōji are important because the language does not need crisis, leverage, or status performance to sustain contact. Shared cultural interest becomes a low-pressure relational mode (`Y1V07.5-E016–E030`; `Y1V11.5-E059–E070`).

## 18.2 Kōenji Rokusuke

Kōenji speaks theatrically, with amused superiority, self-mythologizing formality, and the assumption that the scene exists partly for his performance.

His voice is openly noncompliant.

Where Ayanokōji hides ungovernability, Kōenji aestheticizes it.

`非常識 / outside ordinary common sense` becomes more than eccentricity. It names a person who does not share the premises through which others predict obligation, embarrassment, fear, or collective claim (`Y1V07-E097–E106`).

His declaration that only he decides whether his talent will be used for the class is the clearest linguistic separation of possessed ability from collective jurisdiction (`Y1V11-E072–E088`).

# 19. Manabu, Nagumo, Tsukishiro, and teacherly authority

## 19.1 Horikita Manabu: role before relation

Manabu’s speech is terse, authoritative, and institutionally abstract.

Words such as `見本 / example` and `模範 / model` sound like evaluation criteria because he learned to narrate himself as an office before narrating himself as a friend or brother (`Y1V08-E001–E019`).

His later language becomes more personally liberating not by becoming casual, but by dismantling imitation:

- Suzune should grow as herself;
- Ayanokōji should become memorable rather than merely survive;
- succession should not require reproduction of the predecessor.

## 19.2 Nagumo Miyabi: casualness backed by network power

Nagumo’s `～ッス` toward Manabu performs several positions at once:

- junior;
- teasing acquaintance;
- challenger;
- and institutional threat.

The semi-casual rhythm makes hierarchy look relaxed while the network behind him gives every joke material consequence (`Y1V08-E020–E052`; `Y1V10-E079–E091`; `Y1V11.5-E017–E040`).

His phrase `本当の実力主義 / true meritocracy` remains political self-description, not narrator-certified truth.

## 19.3 Tsukishiro: polite administration and infrastructural violence

Tsukishiro’s threat is not vocal monstrosity.

It is normal adult composure backed by office, surveillance access, communication control, and record sovereignty (`Y1V10-E109–E111`; `Y1V11-E016–E025`, `Y1V11-E133–E145`).

His language naturalizes designed exclusion through phrases such as `自然の摂理 / law of nature`.

The refined bureaucratic surface hides authorship by presenting chosen institutional harm as inevitable environment.

## 19.4 Chabashira Sae: examiner speech and strategic omission

Chabashira’s terse declaratives and contemptuous address make her sound more like an evaluator than a teacher. She often leaves exact wording open enough that attentive students can discover the rule behind the statement (`Y1V01-E011–E029`).

Her later lie to Ayanokōji shows how authentic information can lend credibility to false threat (`Y1V03-E001`, `Y1V07-E020–E040`).

The voice’s authority therefore depends partly upon the institution presumed to stand behind it.

## 19.5 Mashima: plain institutional conscience

Mashima’s voice is less theatrically distinctive, which suits his function. His moral importance lies in making ordinary professional distinction:

- examination risk is not unlimited license;
- a teacher can obey rules without abandoning judgment;
- and accepting personal sanction may be required to protect a student from avoidable injury (`Y1V11.5-E017–E040`).

# 20. Secondary voices and the refusal of functional flattening

Year 1 repeatedly gives smaller characters a linguistic feature that prevents them from becoming pure class functions.

## 20.1 Sakura Airi: keigo as defensive distance

Sakura’s hesitant politeness marks fear of exposure rather than elite formality. Direct speech contains pauses, repetition, and self-devaluation; mediated text permits greater fluency (`Y1V02-E001–E013`; `Y1V04.5-E021–E038`).

Her development is audible when a refusal must be spoken face-to-face rather than typed or privately decided.

## 20.2 Ibuki Mio: roughness with situational manners

Ibuki’s clipped confrontational style does not eliminate curiosity, gratitude, or practical cooperation. Her polite thanks to the fortune-teller and her awkward leisure behavior matter because fictional roughness is too easily mistaken for total social incompetence (`Y1V04.5-E021–E038`; `Y1V07.5-E016–E030`).

## 20.3 Hashimoto Masayoshi: insurance without grandeur

Hashimoto’s internal language is transactional—backup, insurance, future use—without the mythic rhetoric of Ryūen or Sakayanagi (`Y1V09-E054–E075`).

Opportunism sounds reasonable because it is narrated as risk management.

## 20.4 Satō Maya: ordinary directness

Satō’s confession is linguistically valuable because it is comparatively ordinary. She asks, discloses, cries, and leaves without turning romance into class strategy (`Y1V07.5-E063–E070`).

Her ability to notice that Ayanokōji enjoyed himself without smiling gives the ordinary romantic participant perceptual authority over the hidden strategist.

## 20.5 Mii-chan: humane directness

Mii-chan’s language around Hirata rejects the strategic recommendation to leave him alone: precisely when someone suffers, help is required (`Y1V11-E072–E088`).

The simplicity is not analytical inferiority. It articulates a moral countermodel the more sophisticated characters risk losing.

## 20.6 Matsushita Chiaki: controlled observation from the apparent background

Volume 11.5 elevates Matsushita through a voice capable of inference without public performance. Her surveillance and questioning show that ordinary-looking classmates can construct models of hidden causation (`Y1V11.5-E101–E116`).

The visual decision to illustrate her reinforces the correction.

# 21. Address terms, names, titles, and relational geography

Names in Year 1 do not merely identify speakers.

They distribute intimacy, hierarchy, publicity, and role.

## 21.1 The surname baseline

Most school relationships remain surname-based:

- `堀北`;
- `軽井沢`;
- `平田`;
- `一之瀬`;
- `龍園`;
- `坂柳`.

This maintains ordinary school distance even when informational intimacy becomes extreme.

Volume 4 produces the sharpest mismatch: Ayanokōji knows Kei’s deepest trauma while still addressing her through the surname (`Y1V04-E032–E061`).

Information has collapsed distance faster than relationship has.

## 21.2 Given names as private jurisdiction

`清隆` and `恵` create a private relation whose existence must be concealed publicly (`Y1V07.5-E093–E100`).

The shift does not erase asymmetry. It creates a linguistic space in which asymmetry can be negotiated more personally.

## 21.3 Naming and self-authorship

Keisei’s chosen given-name usage is not romantic. It is a claim about which self the friendship group should recognize (`Y1V06-E034–E050`).

Horikita’s relation to `兄 / older brother`, Manabu’s office/title identity, and the later ability to speak as siblings rather than model and imitator similarly demonstrate that address can preserve or loosen role.

## 21.4 Titles and institutional speech

`生徒会長 / student-council president`, teacher address, senpai/kōhai hierarchy, and formal class labels carry power independent of personal feeling.

Nagumo’s casualization of hierarchical speech does not abolish office. It makes office socially mobile.

## 21.5 Address change is necessary but insufficient evidence

A name change can mark intimacy while leaving:

- hidden plans;
- unequal knowledge;
- coercive history;
- or strategic utility

intact.

The correct method is to read naming beside refusal capacity, information symmetry, ordinary interaction, and role survival.

# 22. Voice under stress, contradiction, and role change

The strongest voice evidence often appears when a familiar register fails.

## 22.1 Fragmentation

Kei's broken phrases on the rooftop show the body interrupting social fluency. The refusal becomes more credible, not less, because it is produced under collapse rather than polished after the fact (`Y1V07-E052–E076`).

## 22.2 Roughness emerging from civility

Hirata's command to Horikita reveals a coercive self normally contained by inclusive speech (`Y1V10-E005`, `Y1V10-E063`).

## 22.3 Vulnerability entering polished speech

Horikita does not need a different dialect to become vulnerable. Requests, hesitation, tears, and admitted dependence alter the function of her established voice (`Y1V05-E024`; `Y1V10-E036–E040`; `Y1V11.5-E090–E095`).

## 22.4 Declarative recovery

Ichinose's confession ends when she claims the authority to end it. The stronger voice is not louder. It is less apologetically available to other people's judgment (`Y1V09-E065–E080`).

## 22.5 Calm becoming personal

Sakayanagi's late desire for one more second and Manabu's graduation recognition matter because they emerge from speakers who usually subordinate personal feeling to theory or role (`Y1V11-E133–E149`; `Y1V11.5-E084–E105`).

## 22.6 Persona becoming explicit

Sotomura's abandonment of a habitual speaking style under pressure reveals that even comic voice has social cost (`Y1V08-E094–E102`).

Stress does not reveal one pure voice beneath all performance. It reveals which parts of the social interface can no longer be maintained and what appears when the interface breaks.

# 23. Year 1 as a system of genres

*Classroom of the Elite* Year 1 does not belong to one stable genre.

Its literary power comes partly from switching among genres that make different aspects of the same person visible.

## 23.1 School comedy

The early classroom, pool, shopping, gift, bottle, elevator, movie, bath, nickname, and dating scenes establish adolescence as lived experience. Without them, strategic competence would become the only available meaning of character.

## 23.2 Examination puzzle

Rules, hidden criteria, point values, leader identities, evidence conditions, and scoring systems invite the reader to solve alongside or behind the narrator. The genre rewards exact wording and causal reconstruction.

## 23.3 Psychological thriller

The Kei operation, Ryūen hunt, Kushida conflict, Ichinose rumor crisis, Hirata collapse, and Tsukishiro capture of the record turn social knowledge into danger.

## 23.4 Political and institutional fiction

Class constitutions, student-council struggle, teacher discretion, point liquidity, expulsion, and administrative control enlarge the story beyond individual cleverness.

## 23.5 Romance and relational drama

Satou's date, Kei's jealousy, Ichinose's unclassified attachment, Hirata's naming request, Hiyori's book friendship, and the final confession expose capacities that exam logic cannot score adequately.

## 23.6 White Room conspiracy

The father, Sakayanagi's memory, Tsukishiro, and the contested history of manufactured genius create an external sovereignty plot. The genre threatens to explain Ayanokōji completely through origin.

## 23.7 Paratextual dossier fiction

Profile pages and *First File* encourage the reader to become an evaluator. Bonus fiction then shows what the file cannot hold.

The genres do not simply alternate for variety. They check one another.

School comedy prevents conspiracy from owning the whole person.

Political fiction prevents romance from privatizing institutional harm.

Romance prevents ability discourse from reducing attachment to function.

Thriller prevents ordinary life from being treated as secure by default.

Dossier form prevents anti-institutional sentiment from denying that bounded measurement can be useful.

The Year 1 formal achievement is not seamless unity. It is productive interference among modes.

# 24. Volume-by-volume genre conversion

The sequence of volumes creates a deliberate escalation and correction.

## Volume 1 - comedy becomes institutional thriller

Spending, leisure, and social behavior are retrospectively converted into evidence. The "dreamlike school" chapter becomes the prehistory of judgment (`Y1V01-E003–E013`, `Y1V01-E031`).

## Volume 2 - adjudication becomes epistemic thriller

A school fight becomes a problem of evidence, credibility, camera performance, and who can make truth actionable (`Y1V02-E021–E044`).

## Volume 3 - survival adventure becomes authorship drama

The island changes the value of bodies and practical knowledge, then reveals a hidden narrator controlling environment and credit (`Y1V03-E018–E056`).

## Volume 4 - social puzzle becomes coercive psychological thriller

The zodiac exam's abstract information game culminates in an operation on a traumatized body. The genre shift prevents trust from remaining a clean strategic resource (`Y1V04-E032–E067`).

## Volume 4.5 - comedy becomes ethical counter-curriculum

Leisure creates friendship and preference, but the voyeurism operation exposes how comedy can coexist with privacy violation (`Y1V04.5-E034–E068`).

## Volume 5 - sports narrative becomes political spectacle

Public bodies, leadership failure, sabotage, and relay rivalry make athletic competition a test of social usability rather than raw speed (`Y1V05-E017–E020`, `Y1V05-E071–E077`).

## Volume 6 - academic puzzle becomes network fiction

Question design, paired risk, study groups, names, rumors, and evidence traps make intelligence collective and relational (`Y1V06-E028–E071`).

## Volume 7 - confrontation becomes ownership drama

The father and rooftop place parental, political, and protective sovereignty inside one volume. Violence is inseparable from the question of who controls the future (`Y1V07-E022–E100`).

## Volume 7.5 - romantic comedy becomes relational audit

The date and Christmas structure ask whether ordinary romance can survive strategic life and whether indispensable function can become irreplaceable value (`Y1V07.5-E001–E111`).

## Volume 8 - school camp becomes institutional political fiction

Nested groups and bath comedy coexist with a rule-authored attack on Tachibana. Casual atmosphere becomes an attack surface (`Y1V08-E001–E150`).

## Volume 9 - rumor thriller becomes confession drama

Information saturation gives way to a person reclaiming authorship over the meaning of a true wrongdoing (`Y1V09-E033–E110`).

## Volume 10 - anonymous voting becomes tragedy of membership

The exam's numerical procedure produces bodily collapse, self-sacrifice, and a forced theory of who may continue (`Y1V10-E005–E122`).

## Volume 11 - competition becomes record-corruption thriller

A distributed class portfolio and long-promised chess match culminate in administrative substitution of the official move (`Y1V11-E015–E149`).

## Volume 11.5 - epilogue becomes self-authorship and romance

Graduation, hair, names, books, rain, surveillance, and confession close the year by moving from institutionally scored action to the meanings people choose afterward (`Y1V11.5-E084–E151`).

## First File - archive becomes rebuttal to archival totality

Profiles and ratings culminate in bonus fiction about purposeless conversation, hesitation, and unclassified attachment (`Y1FF-E001–E082`).

This progression explains why Year 1 cannot be reduced to a series of puzzles. The puzzle genre repeatedly produces a human remainder that another genre must recover.

# 25. The .5 volumes as counter-curriculum and formal decompression

Volumes 4.5 and 7.5 are structurally indispensable because they test character outside the largest formal objectives.

## 25.1 They change the evidentiary environment

Without a class-point crisis dominating every scene, the novels can observe:

- who calls whom;
- who buys gifts;
- what food tastes good;
- who enjoys movies;
- who is embarrassed;
- who can refuse;
- who seeks company;
- and which relations remain when immediate strategic necessity is reduced.

## 25.2 They do not eliminate strategy

Ayanokōji still gathers information, uses cover stories, manages Kei, and evaluates institutional actors. Counter-curriculum means underdetermination, not innocence.

## 25.3 They produce ordinary-life voice

The narrator's comic and sensory registers receive more space. Milk soft serve, snow, cinema, music preference, a double date, gloves, medicine, and Christmas create ends that do not need class-point justification (`Y1V04.5-E061–E062`; `Y1V07.5-E001–E024`, `Y1V07.5-E093–E100`).

## 25.4 They test role survival

Kei and Hirata after the false romance, Ayanokōji and Kei after the rooftop, Ibuki after defeat, and the Ayanokōji Group outside exam pressure reveal whether relationships can continue after their original function changes.

The .5 volumes therefore do not interrupt the serious story. They ask whether the serious story has left room for lives not exhausted by crisis.

# 26. A taxonomy of Year 1 humor

Humor performs more analytical work than relief alone.

## 26.1 Social incompetence humor

Ayanokōji's failed openings, misread romantic situations, and uncertainty about ordinary customs make his exceptional competence locally useless.

## 26.2 Bodily farce

The bottle, elevator, bath, pool, changing clothes, illness, and physical accidents democratize vulnerability. Strategy cannot command a stuck hand, heat, or embarrassment out of existence (`Y1V04.5-E034`; `Y1V08-E148`).

## 26.3 Persona humor

Kōenji's self-theater, Sotomura's stylized speech, Kei's rapid reactions, and chibi visual inserts make public identity conspicuously constructed.

## 26.4 Romantic misdirection

Cupid Kei, proposal-like gifts, legendary dating spots, the wrong necklace, and mock confessions let the text make emotional stakes visible before direct admission (`Y1V07.5-E101–E111`; `Y1V11-E035–E044`; `Y1V11.5-E121–E126`).

## 26.5 Genre self-undercutting

Bonus manga and comic scenes often follow severe material. Volume 4's bonus manga re-registers a cast just subjected to coercion and distrust (`Y1V04-E068`). The juxtaposition can restore plurality, but it can also aestheticize or prematurely discharge discomfort.

## 26.6 Political comedy

Informal banter can expose hierarchy more sharply than speeches. Nagumo's casualness, Ryūen's amused provocation, and the bath's ranking logic make domination socially legible through jokes.

## 26.7 Humor as evidence of desire

Private jokes about romance, sex, food, movies, and social rank show what the narrator notices when no strategic audience exists.

The critical limit is equally important:

> Humor can reveal humanity without morally neutralizing harm.

A scene remains coercive even if the publication later makes the participants funny.

# 27. Sexual comedy, fanservice, and ethical friction

Year 1's visual and comic economy repeatedly asks the reader to enjoy access that the story sometimes condemns when characters seek it without permission.

The tension is clearest in the pool material.

The prose takes seriously:

- Airi's wish to reduce visibility;
- Kei's need to hide a scar;
- Ayanokōji's concealment of his trained body;
- unauthorized changing-room recording;
- and the social consequences of bodily exposure.

The illustrations simultaneously foreground swimsuits, cleavage, thighs, blush, proximity, and reader-facing display (`Y1V01-E033–E035`; `Y1V04.5-E047`, `Y1V04.5-E059–E068`).

This is not a reason to discard visual evidence.

It is evidence of a split formal position.

## 27.1 The narrative condemns unauthorized intra-story capture

Operation Delta is criminalized in the prose, and destruction of the durable recording matters.

## 27.2 The publication commodifies selected visual access

The same bodies are offered to the reader through authorized editorial framing.

## 27.3 Authorization does not end the ethical question

Fictional characters do not literally consent, but the work can still represent their discomfort sympathetically while marketing the image that produces reader attention.

## 27.4 Sexualization can compete with theme

The coercion illustration in Volume 4 gives an ethically severe scene erotic charge. The image can increase memorability while weakening attention to consent collapse (`Y1V04-E067`).

## 27.5 Sexual noticing remains character evidence

The existence of fanservice does not make Ayanokōji's attraction fictitious or irrelevant. It requires separating character interiority from reader-facing commercial emphasis.

The correct critical judgment is not puritanical denial or automatic exoneration. It is to preserve the contradiction as part of the work's form.

# 28. Visual paratext as a second system of legibility

The illustrations do not merely decorate scenes already complete in prose.

They decide:

- who receives a face;
- which private moment becomes iconic;
- which body becomes evidence;
- what relation is visually paired;
- and which political cost is restored from abstraction.

The visual layer performs four recurring functions.

## 28.1 Confirmation

An illustration can confirm visible posture, injury, clothing, or physical proximity.

## 28.2 Reweighting

A scene that occupies little prose may receive full-page visual emphasis, increasing its place in reader memory. Hiyori's library scene and Matsushita's surveillance are important examples (`Y1V11.5-E145`, `Y1V11.5-E150`).

## 28.3 Counterpoint

An image can present warmth or intimacy beside narration that remains uncertain or strategic. The Kei Valentine image and final embrace work this way (`Y1V09-E106`; `Y1V11.5-E151`).

## 28.4 Ethical restoration

Voting, points, and strategy can abstract people into pieces. Illustrations of Yamauchi's fear, Tachibana's grief, Hirata's exhausted body, and Ichinose in the rain restore bodily cost (`Y1V08-E150`; `Y1V10-E120`; `Y1V11-E107`; `Y1V11.5-E147`).

The visual program therefore participates in the same politics of legibility as the school. It can reveal what metrics miss while creating its own selective archive.

# 29. Dossier aesthetics and the reader as evaluator

Student-database inserts appear before *First File* formalizes the year as an archive.

These pages package characters through:

- standardized categories;
- letter grades;
- short descriptors;
- and a clean visual hierarchy.

The form is attractive because it promises mastery.

The reader can compare.

The person becomes portable information.

Volume 2's profiles, Volume 3's dossier pages, and Volume 4's inserts repeatedly coexist with plots demonstrating what the boxes miss (`Y1V02-E047`; `Y1V03-E055`; `Y1V04-E066`).

- Ayanokōji authors mediocrity.
- Kei's social-field control resists conventional scoring.
- Hirata's usefulness conceals trauma and a false romance.
- Kushida's cooperation rating cannot establish moral trustworthiness.
- Sakayanagi's physical score cannot measure political authority.
- Kōenji's capacity cannot be assumed available to the group.

The dossier aesthetic therefore has a double effect.

It gives the reader real information.

It also makes the desire for total legibility aesthetically pleasurable.

*First File* intensifies this effect by organizing the completed year through ratings, timelines, class summaries, point totals, relationship diagrams, and selected images. Its formal power depends on compression. Its epistemic limit is the same.

# 30. Embodied motifs: hair, scar, cane, trained body, clothing, and posture

Year 1 repeatedly stores history in the body.

## 30.1 Horikita's hair

Long hair initially belongs to imitation and the desire for Manabu's approval. Cutting it in Volume 11.5 does not reveal a pristine true self. Her language and the embracing illustration frame both long- and short-haired forms as part of lived history (`Y1V11.5-E003`, `Y1V11.5-E090–E095`, `Y1V11.5-E148–E149`).

Hair becomes biography rather than essence.

## 30.2 Kei's scar

The scar is a private record the institutional file does not possess. Exposure becomes confirmation, invasion, and seizure of evidence. Clothing, swimsuit choice, and fashion status organize how the body can remain socially legible without revealing the trauma underneath (`Y1V04-E002`, `Y1V04-E052`, `Y1V04-E065–E067`; `Y1V04.5-E059–E060`).

## 30.3 Sakayanagi's cane and seated authority

The cane marks real physical limitation. Repeated seated compositions prevent that limitation from becoming visual helplessness. She can command rooms, classes, and arguments without the body required by the sports-festival ideal (`Y1V05-E006`, `Y1V05-E074`; `Y1V08-E143–E144`; `Y1V11.5-E142`).

## 30.4 Ayanokōji's trained body

His body repeatedly contradicts the ordinary student interface: evading Manabu, controlled swimming, rash guard concealment, relay parity, fighting capacity, and others' accidental recognition (`Y1V01-E008`, `Y1V01-E034`; `Y1V04.5-E047`; `Y1V05-E076`).

The body is evidence he must manage.

## 30.5 Sudō's athletic embodiment

Sudō's body is first treated as threat and liability, then as expertise, leadership resource, and teachable domain. The visual pairing with Horikita during their developmental confrontation makes unlike capacity relational rather than merely rankable (`Y1V05-E017–E020`, `Y1V05-E075`; `Y1V11-E055–E068`).

## 30.6 Sakura's visibility management

Glasses, guarded posture, the Shizuku presentation, testimony, and later social participation show one body reorganized through different interfaces (`Y1V02-E001`, `Y1V02-E048`).

## 30.7 Manabu's posture

Rigid upright presentation makes discipline visible. The contrast with Nagumo's relaxed lean in Volume 8 turns posture into political theory (`Y1V08-E145`).

## 30.8 Clothing and class performance

Uniforms create institutional sameness; fashion, winter clothing, swimsuits, casual clothes, and grooming restore distinction. Kei's fashionable legitimacy, Hirata's maintained appearance, Kōenji's theatrical self-display, and *First File*'s casual-clothes archive all make clothing part of social authorship (`Y1V04-E065`; `Y1FF-E049`).

The body in Year 1 is neither transparent truth nor irrelevant surface. It is a contested record.

# 31. Illustration and narration as counterpoint

The strongest visual moments do not simply repeat what the narrator has already explained.

They create tension between what is said, what is shown, and what remains unresolved.

## 31.1 Warm image, strategic context

The Volume 9 Valentine illustration presents genuine adolescent playfulness while the meeting also functions as counter-surveillance theater. Neither layer cancels the other (`Y1V09-E106`).

## 31.2 Intimate image, uncertain narration

The final Ayanokōji-Kei embrace is visually romantic. The adjacent narration remains uncertain about irreplaceability and describes Kei through textbook language. The correct reading preserves both evidence streams (`Y1V11.5-E124–E140`, `Y1V11.5-E151`).

## 31.3 Vulnerable body, instrumental narration

Hirata's bench illustration makes exhaustion and tears visible while Ayanokōji describes the class's need for the part called Hirata (`Y1V11-E098–E107`). The image does not erase instrumentality. It prevents the person from disappearing inside it.

## 31.4 Public role, hidden causal structure

Early Horikita images foreground her as visible leader while Ayanokōji remains behind or turned away. The composition anticipates the shadow constitution without literally revealing it (`Y1V01-E033–E035`; `Y1V03-E056`).

## 31.5 Political abstraction, restored person

Yamauchi's distress, Tachibana's grief, Ichinose's rain-soaked stillness, and Suzune's tears make institutional consequences bodily (`Y1V08-E150`; `Y1V10-E120`; `Y1V11.5-E147`, `Y1V11.5-E149`).

## 31.6 Ordinary pleasure receives serious visual weight

Hiyori with books receives the kind of full-page emphasis often reserved for confrontation. The image is formal evidence that ordinary cultural pleasure belongs to the serious story (`Y1V11.5-E145`).

The paratext therefore acts as a second narrator with its own limits. It can challenge the prose's abstractions and can also create commercial simplifications of its own.

# 32. *First File* — archive, dossier, and formal rebuttal

*First File* is the culminating Year 1 paratext because it turns the completed year into an object that can be browsed.

It offers:

- profiles;
- ratings;
- point totals;
- timelines;
- relationship summaries;
- class descriptions;
- selected illustrations;
- and bonus fiction.

The structure stages the central problem of the series in miniature.

## 32.1 The documentary layer makes the year manageable

Students become comparable. Events become chronological. Class constitutions become short summaries. Ratings make visible output portable.

This is useful.

A bounded file can preserve facts more reliably than memory or rumor.

## 32.2 The file also creates an illusion of completion

The clean page, compact profile, and numeric rating encourage the reader to believe the person has been captured. Ayanokōji's mediocre visible ratings demonstrate the obvious failure, but subtler cases matter equally:

- Hiyori's formal judgment score cannot exhaust her practical restraint;
- Kushida's cooperation cannot certify trustworthiness;
- Kei's social contribution is only partly legible;
- Sakayanagi's physical aggregate distorts political capacity;
- Kōenji's score cannot determine whether his ability is available to anyone else (`Y1FF-E004–E024`, `Y1FF-E038–E048`).

## 32.3 The casual-clothes archive changes the visual register

Selected off-duty images re-present characters outside uniform and formal examination context. The archive still curates them, but it acknowledges that institutional presentation is not the only visible self (`Y1FF-E049`).

## 32.4 Bonus fiction restores what metrics cannot improve by measuring

The Ayanokōji-Kei story gives purposeless conversation, reluctance to end contact, anticipation of school reopening, and direct desire to see another person. The Ichinose story gives a political and emotional category she cannot finish naming.

These facts would not become more true if converted into a higher score.

## 32.5 The formal sequence matters

The guidebook first offers mastery, then ends with remainder.

The reader is allowed to enjoy the file and is then shown why the file must remain smaller than the person (`Y1FF-E059–E082`).

*First File* therefore does not reject paratextual classification. It teaches an ethics of classification:

> record what the file can support, state the date and object, and do not mistake compression for possession.

# 33. Formal strengths

Year 1's narration and paratext produce several major literary strengths.

## 33.1 Viewpoint control

The first-person narrator gives enough information to make events intelligible while preserving uncertainty about planning, motive, and emotional meaning. The reader experiences the same problem faced by classmates: Ayanokōji is visible and partially inaccessible.

## 33.2 Dramatic irony through register

Readers can hear that a polished voice is under strain or that a casual voice carries institutional power before another character fully recognizes it. Horikita, Hirata, Kushida, Nagumo, and Tsukishiro benefit especially from this technique.

## 33.3 Recontextualization without total erasure

Later information changes earlier scenes while leaving the earlier experience historically real. Kei's public confidence, Hirata's civility, Kushida's warmth, and Ayanokōji's comedy all remain genuine functions after private structures are revealed.

## 33.4 Examination mechanics become character form

Rules do not sit outside psychology. They decide which voices become authoritative, which bodies become evidence, and which relationships can survive. The form of an exam is also the form of a social drama.

## 33.5 Genre oscillation preserves plurality

Comedy, romance, thriller, political fiction, and conspiracy continually interrupt totalizing explanations. The work is strongest when no single genre owns the person.

## 33.6 Visual emphasis restores embodiment

Illustrations return bodies to plots that might otherwise become pure logic. Scars, tears, canes, posture, rain, hair, gifts, and clothing keep strategy socially and physically costly.

## 33.7 Lexical recurrence creates longitudinal thought

Words such as freedom, defect, tool, host, trust, ownership, growth, friend, and irreplaceability accumulate changing meanings rather than functioning as one-time symbols.

## 33.8 Naming gives relational development a precise formal marker

Surnames, nicknames, given names, and titles create visible thresholds that translation can easily flatten.

The overall strength is epistemic discipline. The novels offer interpretation while repeatedly exposing its limits.

# 34. Critical reservations

The same formal system also creates recurring problems.

## 34.1 First-person mastery can compress the ensemble

Ayanokōji's ability to explain motives and contingencies can make other characters appear fully readable through his model. The best volumes resist this by giving them independent POV, refusal, surprise, or visual weight. The risk never disappears.

## 34.2 Gender generalization sometimes exceeds local evidence

The narration can turn contextual observations about bodies, attraction, or social groups into broad claims about men and women. These claims are character evidence and may also be reductive.

## 34.3 Fanservice conflicts with privacy ethics

The work condemns voyeurism and coercive exposure while commercial illustration invites sexualized spectatorship. The tension can deepen the analysis but can also undercut victims' control over how scenes are remembered.

## 34.4 Politeness can aestheticize domination

Sakayanagi and Tsukishiro are compelling partly because severe ideas arrive through elegant or civil speech. The aesthetic pleasure of composure can make coercion feel cleaner than it is.

## 34.5 Humor can discharge ethical discomfort too quickly

Bonus manga, chibi images, bath comedy, and post-crisis banter can restore character plurality. They can also move the reader away from harm before responsibility has been fully considered.

## 34.6 Dossier form encourages power-scaling habits

Profile pages are designed to be compared. Readers may reproduce the institution's scalar gaze even when the novels demonstrate ecological, developmental, and political ability.

## 34.7 Illustration selection can overprivilege romantic interpretation

Physical proximity and heroine-focused covers can make relational centrality look more settled than the prose permits. Editorial emphasis is evidence of marketing and framing, not automatic proof of canonical reciprocity.

## 34.8 The narrator's emotional uncertainty can become an all-purpose ambiguity machine

When every severe or affectionate statement is treated as potentially incomplete, interpretation risks becoming unfalsifiable. This document therefore preserves direct declarations, harmful conduct, and explicit metaphors as real evidence even when they are not exhaustive.

The appropriate critical stance is neither total suspicion nor naive trust. It is bounded attribution.

# 37. Final synthesis

Year 1 is a story about legibility told through forms that repeatedly frustrate complete legibility.

The school observes conduct, assigns class rank, prices outcomes, and later imagines ratings capable of placing students on common axes.

Class leaders build models of classmates.

Strategists control what others can know.

Rumors assign identities.

Files compress biography.

Illustrations select what the reader will remember.

The first-person narrator appears to offer privileged access to the person at the center of the system.

None of these media is useless.

All of them are partial.

Ayanokōji's `オレ` voice gives the reader embarrassment, desire, attraction, boredom, humor, and curiosity that his public surface suppresses. It also gives tools, hosts, parts, games, doors, and textbooks through which he reduces unpredictable people to workable structures. His ordinary voice is not fake. His strategic voice is not a detachable monster. His Year 1 self is the coexistence of both, plus the growing evidence that neither vocabulary can fully contain what he is becoming.

The other characters make that formal problem social.

Horikita's polished judgment becomes accountable without losing discipline.

Kei's `あたし` voice remains socially forceful even after trauma becomes visible.

Kushida's kindness is performed and often genuinely useful.

Hirata's civility protects people and protects him from particular risk.
