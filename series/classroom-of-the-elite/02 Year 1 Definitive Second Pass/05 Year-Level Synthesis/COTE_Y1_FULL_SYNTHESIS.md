# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ』
## Year 1 — Full Synthesis
### Developmental authorship, answerable asymmetry, and the person beyond the file

> **CANONICAL YEAR-1 CONTINUOUS SYNTHESIS**
>
> This document integrates the completed Japanese-primary Year 1 second pass: fourteen numbered and decimal light novels, the *First File* paratext audit, the frozen specialist sequence `Y1_01–Y1_11`, the 1,419-record evidence router `Y1_12`, and the 283-entry exact-language router `Y1_13`.
>
> It is not a substitute for those artifacts. Its purpose is continuous reading: to show how the year’s narrative architecture, characters, relationships, class polities, measurements, institutions, ethics, and formal choices become one literary argument.
>
> No Year 2, Volume 0, *Second List*, Year 3, adaptation, wiki, or later-franchise answer is used to settle a Year 1 question. Every conclusion is frozen at the boundary after Volume 11.5 and *First File*.

# 0. Scope, method, and the meaning of a full synthesis

A full synthesis can fail in two opposite ways.

It can become a long plot summary, repeating each examination and revelation without explaining why the sequence changes the meaning of the whole. Or it can become so abstract that the characters and events disappear into a theory the fiction never actually states.

The Year 1 V2 corpus was built to avoid both failures. The volume readings preserve local ambiguity and source order. The specialist documents isolate different governing questions. The evidence and Japanese-language indexes preserve the route back to the original text. This document moves in the other direction: it asks what the complete Year 1 boundary permits us to say when those layers are read together.

The argument is necessarily synthetic, but it remains bounded in four ways.

First, it distinguishes **development** from **revelation**. Sudō genuinely changes; Ayanokōji’s concealed academic and physical capacity is largely revealed. Horikita both changes and becomes more fully legible. Kei’s trauma is revealed while her capacity to refuse and negotiate develops. Later knowledge cannot be treated as though it had always been available to the reader or to the characters.

Second, it distinguishes **capacity** from **legibility**. A student can possess ability without displaying it, display it without receiving credit, receive credit without making the ability collectively usable, or perform decisively only to have the official record corrupted. The title term `実力` therefore cannot be reduced to a single score or hidden power level. Its earliest explicit Year 1 formulation and the broader ideology of `実力至上主義` are preserved as `JY1-002` and `JY1-003`.

Third, it distinguishes **understanding** from **jurisdiction**. A person may correctly diagnose another’s weakness, fear, talent, or developmental possibility. That knowledge does not by itself grant the right to decide the other person’s purpose, environment, relationships, suffering, or future.

Fourth, it distinguishes **the file from the person**. A score, dossier, narrator, psychological model, illustration, reputation, or analytical synthesis can be accurate about its bounded object. None is entitled to become the whole human being.

The specialist corpus supplies primary homes for the detailed arguments. `Y1_01` governs chronology; `Y1_02` and `Y1_03` govern Ayanokōji and Horikita; `Y1_04` governs relationships; `Y1_05` class politics; `Y1_06` ability and measurement; `Y1_07` institutions; `Y1_08` ethics; `Y1_09` voice and form; `Y1_10` retrospective revision; `Y1_11` comparison and handoff; `Y1_12` and `Y1_13` retrieval. This synthesis cross-connects those findings rather than reproducing every specialist proof at full length.

Its central question is simple to state and difficult to answer:

> **What happens when people and institutions become increasingly capable of seeing, measuring, protecting, coordinating, and developing other human beings?**

Year 1’s answer is that capacity can produce growth, rescue, friendship, and political order. It can also produce more sophisticated forms of ownership.

# 1. The mature Year 1 thesis — developmental authorship and answerable asymmetry

*Classroom of the Elite* begins with a meritocratic provocation. Students enter an elite school, receive extraordinary material freedom, and are later informed that their conduct was being judged. The apparent lesson is familiar: freedom has consequences, ability is broader than academic performance, and the students who fail to understand the system will fall behind.

That lesson is real but radically incomplete.

The school does not merely observe a preexisting natural order. It authors the environment in which conduct becomes evidence. It withholds criteria, assigns collective consequences, changes the ecology of useful skills, prices rescue, rewards betrayal, forces interdependence, and eventually manufactures a situation in which one student must become disposable. What the institution later calls merit is partly a response to conditions the institution designed.

Ayanokōji performs the same operation at the interpersonal level. He does not merely discover what people are. He controls what they see, what alternatives appear available, what pressures they experience, what information reaches them, when protection arrives, and which part of the outcome becomes publicly legible. His exceptional ability is therefore not only strategic calculation. It is **environmental authorship**.

Horikita’s development supplies a competing model. She begins by treating current weakness as a stable property of defective people. She gradually learns that a leader’s task is not simply to identify the strongest individuals or issue superior judgments. It is to make unlike capacities socially usable, to rely on expertise she does not possess, to expose judgment to dissent, and to accept that people may develop beyond the leader’s prediction.

The four classes generalize the same problem. Each becomes a political technology for converting unequal capacities into collective action:

- Horikita develops and coordinates;
- Ryūen centralizes intelligence and coercive mobilization;
- Ichinose converts trust and membership into common infrastructure;
- Sakayanagi allocates talent through sovereign strategic judgment.

None is simply right or wrong. Each sees something true. Each becomes dangerous when its truth is converted into unanswerable jurisdiction.

The mature Year 1 thesis is therefore:

> **Year 1 is a study of developmental authorship: the power to create environments in which capacities become visible, usable, challenged, preserved, or transformed—and the recurring danger that the person or institution capable of cultivating another human being will mistake understanding for ownership.**

The year does not oppose all asymmetry. Teachers know more than students. leaders make decisions. protectors may be stronger than the protected. friends and lovers rarely possess identical information, status, or need. A class cannot coordinate without differentiated roles.

The relevant distinction is between **answerable and unanswerable asymmetry**.

An asymmetry becomes more legitimate when the less powerful participant can understand the relevant structure, refuse where refusal is possible, contest the interpretation, demand reasons, survive disagreement, alter the relationship’s meaning, and become someone the stronger actor did not design. This is why the ability to “answer back” becomes a hidden common denominator across leadership, intimacy, measurement, and institutional power.

That standard also reframes freedom. Freedom is not the absence of influence. No one in Year 1 is unformed by family, institution, friendship, rivalry, trauma, desire, or class position. Freedom is the preservation of enough authorship to respond to those influences—to accept, reject, reinterpret, combine, or exceed them.

Ayanokōji’s earliest direct sense of `自由` arises through distance from family and private dormitory space (`JY1-004`; `Y1V01-E006–E007`). Volume 3 gives the paradoxical formulation `自由を守るために自由を捨てる`: he must surrender nonparticipation to protect the ordinary school life he wants (`JY1-027`; `Y1V03-E031`). By Volume 11.5, freedom has become positive purpose. He can identify school life as something chosen `生まれて初めて`—for the first time in his life (`JY1-256`; `Y1V11.5-E021–E022`). Yet the purpose he begins to choose is partly the development of other people. His self-authorship therefore creates the year’s hardest ethical question:

> **Can someone escape being another person’s developmental project without turning freedom into the right to design projects for everyone else?**

The endpoint is neither triumph nor failure. Ordinary life, friendship, romance, rivalry, and other people’s unexpected answers have begun to generate meanings that Ayanokōji’s inherited strategic vocabulary cannot fully contain. Year 1 makes reciprocal authorship imaginable. It does not establish that he has mastered it.

**Primary evidence route:** `Y1_01`, `Y1_02`, `Y1_03`, `Y1_05`, `Y1_08`; `Y1V01-E001–E030`, `Y1V03-E031`, `Y1V11-E133–E145`, `Y1V11.5-E021–E022`, `Y1V11.5-E087–E105`; `JY1-002–JY1-004`, `JY1-027`, `JY1-251`, `JY1-256`, `JY1-262`.

# 2. Freedom becomes evidence — Volumes 1 and 2

The opening month is often remembered as the school’s first trick: students receive 100,000 private points, spend freely, neglect obligations, and then discover that their behavior has reduced the class allocation to zero. Its deeper importance is epistemic.

Only the school knows that freedom is functioning as an examination. The students believe they are living ordinary school life; the institution has already translated the same conduct into data. The asymmetry is not merely that administrators possess more information. They possess the authority to decide what the information means and to attach collective consequences after the fact.

This creates the basic Year 1 distinction between **conduct and institutional legibility**. The school may observe real irresponsibility. It still creates an unfairly one-sided evaluative field by withholding the operative criteria. The students’ apparent freedom is already enclosed within an authored purpose (`Y1V01-E005`, `Y1V01-E011`).

Ayanokōji responds through authored visibility. His exact fifty-point entrance scores, mediocre swimming time, controlled physical displays, reluctance to accept credit, and use of more socially credible intermediaries produce a deliberately partial person (`Y1V01-E008`, `Y1V01-E013`, `Y1V01-E022–E029`). He does not simply hide strength. He gives the institution and class a coherent false-normal interface.

That distinction matters because later files and ratings are not necessarily inaccurate about what they observe. They may correctly record the performance he chose to display. The failure begins only when visible output is treated as total capacity.

Horikita initially makes the corresponding error in the opposite direction. She sees real academic and behavioral weaknesses and converts them into claims about stable human worth. `不良品`, the language of defective products, turns difference into disposability (`JY1-005`). Her first development is not the abandonment of standards. It is the introduction of temporality. A defective product may be repaired; a weak student may change (`修理・変化`, `JY1-006`; `Y1V01-E030`).

Sudō becomes the earliest proof that merit cannot be frozen at the entrance state. His low academic performance, volatility, and poor reputation are real. So are his athletic capacity, loyalty, and later discipline. The series does not ask the reader to deny his defects. It asks whether an early measurement is entitled to terminate the future in which those defects might be transformed.

Volume 2 expands the problem from hidden evaluation to public adjudication. Sudō may be telling the truth about the fight and still lose because truth is not self-executing. The school must act through evidence, credibility, reputation, and procedural form. The Japanese distinction between `証拠能力` and `証明力`—capacity to count as evidence and strength of proof—makes the point unusually precise (`JY1-015–JY1-016`; `Y1V02-E021`).

Sakura’s role carries the same architecture into identity. Her `偽りの仮面` is not simply a false self. The paradox `私じゃなくなって、私になる`—ceasing to be herself in order to become herself—shows that performance can disclose aspects of a person unavailable in the ordinary social role (`JY1-013–JY1-014`; `Y1V02-E001–E002`). The camera can author appearance, preserve truth, invite predation, or become a strategic threat. Visibility is never neutral.

Ayanokōji’s strategy in the hearing therefore concerns legibility more than brute proof. He shapes what the opposing class thinks may have been recorded, what Horikita is able to reconstruct, and which resolution becomes rational under uncertainty. Horikita later recognizes that she was `手のひらで転がした`—moved in the palm of his hand (`JY1-023`; `Y1V02-E044`). Her insight is both accusation and education: she has begun to perceive the hidden author behind the visible process.

The first two volumes establish three propositions that govern the year.

First, real ability and real truth exist.

Second, neither has political force merely by existing. They must become legible in a form that a class, relationship, or institution will recognize.

Third, the actor who controls legibility gains power over the person being represented.

This is why the bus scene, the zero-point reveal, the fifty-point scores, Sakura’s camera, and the Sudō hearing belong to one architecture. Each asks who controls the conversion from life into evidence.

**Primary evidence route:** `Y1_01 §§4–5`, `Y1_06`, `Y1_07`, `Y1_09`; `Y1V01-E001–E035`, `Y1V02-E001–E048`; `JY1-001–JY1-025`.

# 3. Ability becomes ecological; trust becomes capital — Volumes 3 and 4

The deserted-island examination appears to broaden the school’s meritocracy by changing the environment. Academic ranking becomes less decisive. Camping knowledge, physical stamina, social coordination, information security, resource management, and willingness to endure discomfort all acquire new value.

The important concept is not that the island reveals a truer universal ranking. It reveals **ecological ability**. Ike’s practical knowledge matters because the environment has changed. Hirata’s accessibility matters because collective labor requires voluntary cooperation. Horikita’s academic competence becomes less useful when illness and isolation make it unavailable. Kōenji’s extraordinary body remains politically limited when he withdraws from collective use. Ryūen’s aggression can create strategic effects while destroying the trust needed for delegation.

Ayanokōji excels because he can perceive these shifting values and integrate capacities without making himself publicly central. He allows Ike to confirm an answer for himself (`自分自身で確認`, `JY1-031`; `Y1V03-E010–E011`). That is one of the year’s healthier developmental interventions: the environment is scaffolded, but the discovery remains Ike’s.

His treatment of Horikita is more coercive. He withholds information, controls attribution, permits pressure and failure, and produces the circumstances under which she must confront interdependence. Her eventual recognition of him as `仲間` marks genuine relational movement (`JY1-034`; `Y1V03-E044`), but the fact of development does not settle his authority to engineer the route.

Volume 3 also contains the year’s most severe early statement of strategic conditioning. Ayanokōji marks the tools-and-victory passage as `本心`, his genuine inner position, and uses `道具` to subordinate people to final victory (`JY1-035–JY1-036`; `Y1V03-E045`). The second pass must not explain this away as theatrical darkness. It is real evidence of White Room grammar.

It is not the only evidence in the volume.

The same Ayanokōji wants friendship, notices other people’s capacities, protects his school life, and allows Horikita to receive public credit. The contradiction is not that one voice must be false. The strategic and ordinary layers coexist.

Volume 4 turns this contradiction into a relationship.

The zodiac examination makes trust, identity, and betrayal formally profitable. Katsuragi protects through secrecy. Ichinose creates voluntary cooperation. Ryūen centralizes information and pressure. Ayanokōji uses identity inference and hidden coordination. The examination does not merely measure who can think. It creates an economy in which the value of trust depends on whether another actor will honor the same norm.

Kei is the decisive case because her abilities and vulnerabilities are inseparable from social structure. She can control female-group atmosphere, distribute information, read hierarchy, and occupy a visible status position—`場を支配する力`, the power to govern the social space (`JY1-042`; `Y1V04-E026`). Her trauma has also taught her to understand survival through protector dependency, the `寄生虫` model (`JY1-040`; `Y1V04-E004`).

Ayanokōji accurately recognizes both the capacity and the wound. He then manufactures an environment in which alternative protectors appear unreliable and his own protection becomes indispensable. The operation combines diagnosis, danger, evidence, rescue, secrecy, and future role assignment inside one hidden actor (`Y1V04-E033–E045`).

This is the year’s clearest transition from environmental authorship to **authored dependency**.

It also establishes the ethical complexity that later romance cannot dissolve. Kei receives real protection and later gains real agency. Ayanokōji’s method remains coercive, including sexualized threat. The future value of the relationship does not retroactively create consent.

The island and zodiac volumes therefore make one shared claim:

> **Ability and relationship are not possessions stored inside isolated individuals. They are functions of environments, expectations, complementary capacities, reputations, and available alternatives.**

That insight is intellectually powerful. It is also dangerous. The person who can design the environment can influence which version of another person becomes possible.

**Primary evidence route:** `Y1_01 §§6–7`, `Y1_02 §§9–10`, `Y1_04`, `Y1_06`; `Y1V03-E001–E056`, `Y1V04-E001–E068`; `JY1-026–JY1-047`.

# 4. Counter-curriculum: life without an assigned score

Volume 4.5 is structurally indispensable because it interrupts escalation.

After the island and zodiac examinations, Ayanokōji has demonstrated increasing control over environments, information, and dependency. A weaker series could treat the summer interlude as narrative downtime before the next contest. Year 1 instead uses ordinary life to test whether institutional and strategic optimization will become its only theory of development.

The decimal volume asks a simple question with large consequences:

> What do these students become when no formal examination has already defined what success means?

The answer is not that school power disappears. The campus remains enclosed. Private points still determine access. Surveillance cameras remain present. The student council, class hierarchy, and threat of future expulsion do not vanish. Yet the meanings of gifts, food, friendship, leisure, embarrassment, privacy, and bodily inconvenience are not fully assigned in advance by institutional scoring.

Ayanokōji's difficulty buying a birthday present is revealing precisely because the task has no strategic optimum. He has never chosen such a gift. His imagined solutions—roses, even a ring—are comically miscalibrated because ordinary relational judgment cannot be reduced to the kind of explicit objective function at which he excels. Pure leisure with male classmates is new to him. The experience is educational without being a lesson designed by a superior authority (`Y1V04.5-E015–E024`).

The stalled elevator with Ibuki supplies another countermodel. Ayanokōji gives her an apparently useful task partly to redirect panic, but the elevator's movement is ultimately caused by remote staff rather than the students' command. The episode punctures the fantasy of total strategic authorship. Ayanokōji's intervention can still be practically helpful even when he is not the true cause of resolution (`Y1V04.5-E011–E013`).

Horikita's predicament with the bottle and elevator surveillance exposes the moral ambiguity of observation. Cameras can protect, document, deter, humiliate, and permanently preserve vulnerability. Ayanokōji uses his body to shield what the camera would otherwise record. The value of the act lies partly in what does **not** become a file (`Y1V04.5-E025–E033`).

This is a crucial institutional principle:

> Not every true event deserves permanent legibility.

Year 1's interest in evidence never becomes a demand for total surveillance. Privacy is not merely the concealment of wrongdoing. It protects dignity, experimentation, and the ability to live without every awkward moment becoming a durable record.

The pool episode prevents Volume 4.5 from becoming a simple proof of Ayanokōji's ethical improvement. He recruits Kei into an operation she explicitly rejects, tells her she has no right of refusal, later uses `命令だ`—this is an order—and physically pushes her into the water when she refuses to expose her body or swim. Her later retaliation and laughter do not erase the compromised refusal (`Y1V04.5-E044–E060`). The scene demonstrates that ordinary settings do not automatically produce ordinary ethics. Ayanokōji can carry strategic command into leisure.

The visual package intensifies the contradiction. The prose condemns covert sexual recording while the illustrations commercially foreground swimsuit bodies. The work itself participates in the gaze it asks the reader to criticize (`Y1V04.5-E062–E068`). This does not cancel the privacy argument. It makes the form ethically divided.

Counter-curriculum is therefore not a purified space outside power. It is a zone of partial underdetermination.

Ayanokōji can manipulate, protect privacy, scaffold, preserve refusal, or decline to optimize. Because the volume displays this wider repertoire, later coercive choices become more morally attributable. He is not simply incapable of another mode.

Ordinary life also gives positive content to freedom. The White Room problem is not only excessive training. It is the elimination of activities whose meaning is not supplied by developmental utility. A birthday gift, an ice cream, a film, a casual walk, or an embarrassing conversation can matter without being a means to victory. These experiences teach Ayanokōji that life contains ends that do not need strategic justification.

That lesson will remain fragile. He repeatedly wants ordinary life while selecting relationships and projects adapted to strategic life. But Volume 4.5 establishes that freedom cannot be defined only as escape from an external controller. It also requires access to time and relationships whose value the subject is allowed to discover rather than receive as an assignment.

# 5. Public strength and distributed integration

Volumes 5 and 6 make ability public, embodied, and relational.

The sports festival seems, at first, to provide the cleanest possible meritocratic environment. Students run, jump, coordinate, and compete in visible events. Rankings and points appear to translate performance directly into result. Yet the volume immediately complicates that appearance.

Sudō is the strongest athlete in Class D and initially a poor leader. His superiority makes him impatient with weaker teammates. He interprets others' limitations as insufficient effort and treats his own capacity as though it should automatically authorize command. Hirata possesses less spectacular physical ability but greater legitimacy. Horikita possesses discipline and intelligence but cannot initially adapt her pace to a partner. Kōenji possesses extraordinary capacity while refusing collective availability. Sakayanagi's physical disability limits participation without reducing strategic power.

The three-legged practice supplies a compact theory of leadership. Ayanokōji overpaces Horikita until she experiences the failure produced by treating partnership as unilateral command. He then teaches her to watch the other person and sometimes yield initiative (`Y1V05-E020`). The lesson is not sentimental equality. Partners remain differently capable. Coordination requires the stronger participant to alter the expression of strength so that the pair can move.

Sudō's development follows the same logic at a larger scale. He must learn that athletic excellence does not make other people extensions of his body. Hirata and Keisei challenge his legitimacy when self-display becomes more important than the class. His violence against Hirata reveals how quickly unanswerable expertise becomes domination (`Y1V05-E017`, `Y1V05-E035`).

Horikita's relation to Sudō is more productive because it eventually becomes reciprocal. She discloses the personal motive behind wanting the final relay anchor—recognition from Manabu—and Sudō voluntarily yields. Later, injury forces Horikita to sacrifice the chance to run beside her brother for class viability (`Y1V05-E024`, `Y1V05-E055`). Each begins to treat the other as a person with reasons rather than a resource to be positioned.

Ayanokōji's relay with Manabu is equally important because it briefly detaches performance from strategy. He runs at full speed in an open world, contrasting the experience with the sterile enclosure of prior training. The narration explicitly discards strategy and intellect in favor of embodied contest. He is exposed, but the exposure is not wholly instrumental. For a moment, ability is intrinsically meaningful because another person can call it forth (`Y1V05-E056–E061`, `Y1V05-E076`).

This matters for his relation to defeat. The White Room taught victory as survival and proof. Manabu becomes the kind of opponent through whom a loss might demonstrate that Atsuomi's model is not absolute. Ayanokōji's desire for a worthy defeat is therefore not simple competitiveness. It imagines freedom through evidence that his creator's developmental theory can be exceeded.

Paper Shuffle then converts ability from public spectacle into distributed infrastructure.

Academic weakness becomes relational risk because paired students share consequences. The class must create questions, engineer pairings, teach, conceal information, anticipate sabotage, and manage students whose current performance differs radically. Horikita and Hirata publicly organize the system; Keisei becomes a teacher; Kushida's access to secrets becomes both useful and dangerous; Ayanokōji designs contingencies behind the visible order (`Y1V06-E012`, `Y1V06-E029`, `Y1V06-E040–E060`).

Keisei's reflection after the sports festival is one of the year's clearest anti-scalar insights. His academic superiority did not prevent him from becoming useless in an athletic ecology. He can no longer treat academic weakness as a morally distinct form of inferiority (`Y1V06-E031`; `JY1-098–JY1-101`).

This produces the political idea of **distributed integration**.

A class does not need every student to be equally good at every domain. It needs structures that identify unlike capacities, create teaching relations, distribute risk, and allow specialists to contribute without claiming total authority. The problem is constitutional rather than merely individual.

Kushida makes this problem difficult. Her social skill is real. Her ability to remember, reassure, connect, and gather information is class infrastructure. Her willingness to weaponize confidential knowledge and pursue expulsion is also real. Ayanokōji's preferred answer is removal. Horikita increasingly resists immediate disposal because Sudō has taught her that present danger or weakness does not always define future value (`Y1V05-E023`, `Y1V05-E051`, `Y1V06-E036–E044`).

Horikita's position is principled and potentially reckless.

Kushida is not merely an underdeveloped student whose future can be inferred from Sudō. She knowingly converts trust into leverage. The classmates whose secrets she holds bear risks they did not authorize. Horikita's anti-disposability principle can become unilateral developmental vanity if she assumes the right to gamble with other people's security in order to prove someone can be integrated.

Year 1 does not settle the dispute. It clarifies the competing presumptions:

> Ayanokōji asks which contingency neutralizes the threat.
>
> Horikita asks which developmental future disappears if expulsion becomes the first answer.

Both can be rational. Neither possesses complete knowledge.

Paper Shuffle also demonstrates a healthier Ayanokōji intervention with Airi. He withholds direct social rescue so that she must take a step herself. The action is not abandonment; it is a refusal to author the entire movement. By contrast, his hidden submission authority and contingency planning turn other people's authentic choices into predictable operational material.

The class is therefore developing under two constitutions.

The visible constitution is increasingly led by Horikita, Hirata, Keisei, Kushida, Kei, Sudō, and others through public roles and distributed labor. The shadow constitution remains Ayanokōji's control of information, insurance, exposure, and final contingency.

The question is no longer whether Class D can win.

It is whether collective capacity can become self-governing rather than permanently dependent on an invisible sovereign.

# 6. Fear, ownership, and the rooftop

Volume 7 makes the logic of ownership explicit at three scales.

Atsuomi claims ownership over Ayanokōji because the White Room authored his development. Chabashira converts a fabricated paternal threat into leverage over continued enrollment. Ryūen governs through the expectation that resistance will bring future retaliation. In each case, another person's future is made conditional upon submission to an authority that claims superior knowledge or power.

Atsuomi's statement—`おまえは俺の所有物だ`—is the purest form. The child is treated as a produced asset. Because the father built the environment, he claims title over the result (`Y1V07-E022–E041`; `JY1-113–JY1-118`). Ayanokōji rejects the claim not by denying that the environment shaped him, but by asserting that causation does not settle rightful authority over his future.

The volume then forces the reader to compare that rejection with his treatment of Kei.

Ryūen's rooftop operation is abusive domination. Cold water, restraint, threats of filming, isolation, and calculated reenactment of helplessness are used for the pleasure and strategic value of breaking her. Ibuki draws a partial moral line but remains complicit. The camera is disabled after prior experiments with surveillance and punishment, creating a space in which official law is neutralized by reciprocal secrecy (`Y1V07-E051–E058`, `Y1V07-E081`; `Y1V07-E113`).

Kei is given substantial reason to believe Ayanokōji has used and abandoned her. Naming him could end the immediate ordeal. She refuses.

That refusal is crucial because it exceeds the simplest parasite model. Ayanokōji authored many of the conditions. He did not fully author the answer. Kei preserves his identity not merely because a host mechanically controls her, but because she chooses to retain a meaning for herself inside the betrayal she perceives. The choice does not retroactively make the test legitimate. It proves that the subject can exceed the designer's model (`Y1V07-E056–E074`, `Y1V07-E084–E086`).

Ayanokōji's arrival divides ethical judgment into two layers.

The physical force he uses against Ryūen and his followers is justified defensive violence. Kei remains in danger. The attackers continue to threaten. Ayanokōji targets the immediate aggressors, establishes overwhelming control, and ends the operation.

The broader architecture is not thereby justified.

He predicted the danger, delayed intervention, controlled the post-conflict witness structure through Manabu, and treated the ordeal as an opportunity to sever fear and dependency. The fight is rescue. The delayed-rescue environment is coercive developmental authorship (`Y1V07-E070–E099`, `Y1V07-E116`).

This distinction matters because strategic admiration can easily launder the method. Ayanokōji's composure, physical superiority, and foresight make the scene exhilarating. The literary form encourages awe. Ethical analysis must still ask why Kei was required to bear so much risk for a developmental outcome he selected.

His treatment of Ryūen has a similar duality. Once superiority is obvious, he continues controlled strikes to close every route of resistance and produce fear. He reports no pleasure, anger, or triumph; the violence is procedural rather than passionate (`Y1V07-E091`). That absence of sadism reduces one kind of wrong and intensifies another. Ryūen becomes an object inside a designed lesson.

Yet the result again exceeds the script.

Ryūen's followers later seek to preserve him. Ishizaki, Albert, Ibuki, Hiyori, and others do not respond as people simply liberated from a tyrant. Their loyalty reveals that fear was never the whole constitution. Ryūen himself recognizes the defeat as unlike earlier losses and begins to confront the incompleteness of his fear theory (`Y1V07-E100–E104`; `Y1V07.5-E025`).

Sociological legitimacy can therefore rise without moral reform. Followers may voluntarily choose a coercive leader because he offers competence, recognition, shared struggle, or a political identity. Voluntary loyalty does not automatically constrain domination.

Hiyori supplies the clearest internal countermodel. She refuses the rooftop operation, questions whether finding X is necessary, and later remains loyal enough to correct Ryūen. Her friendship is neither obedience nor exit. It attempts to make authority answerable from within (`Y1V07-E018–E020`, `Y1V07-E051`).

The volume also gives positive content to Ayanokōji's rebellion. Snow, books, malls, movies, friends, and Christmas preparation are precisely the kinds of experience the White Room excluded. He does not merely want to avoid his father. He wants a social world in which activities can matter without being assigned by a creator (`Y1V07-E005`, `Y1V07-E015–E020`, `Y1V07-E036`, `Y1V07-E070`).

The rooftop therefore concentrates the entire Year 1 problem:

> Ayanokōji fights ownership while exercising private sovereignty.
>
> He protects people while controlling the conditions of protection.
>
> He creates real development while exceeding legitimate jurisdiction.
>
> The people he treats as models repeatedly produce answers he did not fully predict.

# 7. Christmas, negotiated indispensability, and the problem of irreplaceability

Volume 7.5 begins with a question the rooftop cannot answer.

Can another person become `かけがえのない存在`—an irreplaceable existence—for Ayanokōji? (`Y1V07.5-E001`; `JY1-122`).

The Christmas volume is not a pause after the “real” conflict. It examines what remains once immediate violence ends and relationships must continue in ordinary time.

Satō offers the most conventional romantic route available to Ayanokōji. She is attracted after the relay, prepares carefully, arrives early, carries a gift, and attempts direct intimacy. Ayanokōji enjoys much of the date. He wants romance in the abstract. He also recognizes that accepting Satō without sufficient feeling would use her as experience. His rejection is one of his more ethically restrained choices because he refuses to create a relationship merely to study it (`Y1V07.5-E034`, `Y1V07.5-E049–E080`).

The decision reveals a recurring contradiction:

> He wants ordinary life, but he repeatedly selects relationships compatible with strategic life.

Kei is already entangled with secrecy, danger, information, protection, and class politics. That does not make the attraction false. It means the relationship fits the abnormal environment through which Ayanokōji knows how to become close.

Kei's role in the double date is also more than jealousy. She helps Satō partly from genuine friendship and responsibility toward another person's trust. She gives practical advice despite limited romantic experience. Her social intelligence allows her to mediate an ordinary situation Ayanokōji cannot optimize alone (`Y1V07.5-E011–E012`).

The post-date confrontation becomes a renegotiation.

Kei demands the real reason for Satō's rejection. She objects that Ayanokōji has called her a partner without consultation. She asks for `誠意`—sincerity or good faith—and insists that the relationship's terms become speakable. Ayanokōji recognizes her judgment, asks rather than simply commands continued help, and accepts a protection bargain (`Y1V07.5-E071`, `Y1V07.5-E088–E099`; `JY1-159–JY1-166`).

This does not produce equality. He still knows far more about the White Room, the origins of their dependency, and his long-term purposes. But the relationship moves from unilateral manufacture toward negotiated partnership.

The medicine episode is especially important because it reveals practical care with no public audience and no immediate examination payoff. Ayanokōji buys medicine before knowing Kei is healthy. He withholds delivery when he sees she does not need it. The receipt later proves concern, while his indirect style has allowed her to believe he did not care (`Y1V07.5-E020`, `Y1V07.5-E097–E098`).

Care is real. Communication remains underdeveloped.

The naming shift creates a private relational world. Kei drops Hirata's given name while retaining Kiyotaka; Ayanokōji reciprocates with `恵`. Address does not prove romance or equality by itself, but it marks a change in who may speak which version of the person (`Y1V07.5-E096`; `JY1-142`).

The volume closes not with irreplaceability but with `必要不可欠な存在`—an indispensable existence (`JY1-144`). The difference is exact.

An indispensable person may be necessary because a system depends upon what they do. An irreplaceable person has singular value that cannot be reproduced by another functionally equivalent actor. Ayanokōji can already identify Kei as indispensable to his operations and emotional world. He does not yet know whether he can value her beyond role (`Y1V07.5-E100`; `JY1-166–JY1-168`).

The distinction prevents two errors.

The first is that Kei is “only a tool.” Her rooftop refusal, social judgment, demands for sincerity, gift, concern, naming, and willingness to help save Ayanokōji exceed simple instrumentality.

The second is that Christmas completes Ayanokōji's romantic humanization. The original dependency remains. Information remains asymmetric. Ayanokōji still imagines relationships developmentally and functionally. The opening question is deliberately left open.

Volume 7.5 also broadens the relational field. Hiyori's book friendship requires little pressure or leverage. Ike protects Shinohara through face-saving social intelligence. Haruka defines the Ayanokōji Group through voluntary attendance and absence of coercive obligation. Airi's gloves express attentive care without requiring immediate confession. These are horizontal relationships in which development can occur without a superior designer specifying the result (`Y1V07.5-E023`, `Y1V07.5-E032–E035`, `Y1V07.5-E109`).

The Christmas volume therefore gives Year 1 a comparative standard:

> A relationship becomes healthier not when all asymmetry disappears, but when the stronger or more informed person becomes answerable—when refusal, contradiction, renegotiation, and independent purpose remain possible.

# 8. Rule authorship and the politics above the classes

The Mixed Training Camp expands the scale of political analysis.

Earlier examinations asked students to discover or exploit rules written by the school. Nagumo demonstrates a higher form of power: the capacity to influence the rules themselves. Ayanokōji eventually reconstructs that Nagumo did not encounter an examination and then derive a strategy. He possessed a political objective and helped author an examination suited to it (`Y1V08-E072–E074`, `Y1V08-E138`).

This is institutional authorship.

The distinction changes what victory means. Manabu can outperform Nagumo in the visible contest and still lose politically because Nagumo attacks the infrastructure around him. Tachibana's vulnerability forces Manabu and Class A to spend an enormous amount of collective value on rescue. The official result can therefore record one winner while the political encounter reveals another (`Y1V08-E124–E138`).

Manabu's decision to rescue Tachibana is neither obviously irrational nor easily justified by aggregate utility. It reveals that exemplary leadership contains particular loyalties and relational debts. His public distance has also prevented him from fully recognizing the emotional and political cost borne by a subordinate who organized much of her life around his office. The same leader admired for impartial excellence has a blind spot produced by emotional reserve.

Nagumo's meritocratic rhetoric is likewise double-edged. He identifies a genuine problem in the class system: exceptional individuals can be trapped by collective assignment. He promises mobility and recognition. Yet when advancement depends upon access to Nagumo's attention, personal recognition becomes patronage. “True meritocracy” can become a system in which the evaluator is sovereign over opportunity (`Y1V07.5-E042`, `Y1V08-E128–E138`; `JY1-175–JY1-180`).

Volume 8 also gives trust a more complex political life.

Trust can be accumulated moral credibility. It can be a temporary community formed through shared labor. It can be access to another person's defenses. It can be a one-use asset spent in betrayal. The mixed male group begins with cross-class hostility, status anxiety, and practical inconvenience. Through injury, cleaning, disclosure, embarrassment, and mutual help, it becomes a genuine temporary community. Keisei's tears make shame common knowledge; Ishizaki takes his task; Albert reassures him; Ayanokōji and Hashimoto help conceal weakness (`Y1V08-E042`, `Y1V08-E088–E105`, `Y1V08-E123`).

No mastermind fully designs that result.

The group shows how relationships can emerge from an authored environment without remaining reducible to the author's purpose. The school creates the group. Students make the meaning.

Ayanokōji's own protection hierarchy becomes explicit. He names Kei, Hirata, and Horikita as priority futures while excluding friends he nevertheless wants to keep. He promises to prevent Kei's expulsion by any means (`Y1V08-E031–E034`, `Y1V08-E143`). Protection is particular rather than universal.

Particularity is not inherently unethical. Human beings do care more about some people than others. The problem is private sovereignty. Ayanokōji makes life-shaping guarantees and allocates intervention without public standards, reciprocal knowledge, or institutional accountability. His protection can be morally serious and politically unanswerable at the same time.

The Mixed Training Camp therefore establishes three levels of power:

1. performance inside rules;
2. strategy exploiting rules;
3. authority capable of authoring rules.

Year 1 will add a fourth in Volume 11: authority capable of authoring the record.

# 9. Reputation, forgiveness, and the authorship of moral identity

Volume 9 returns to truth, but the relevant issue is no longer whether Ichinose committed the act.

She did.

As a child under conditions of poverty, maternal overwork, hospitalization, and a younger sister's self-denial, she stole an expensive hair clip. Her mother discovered the theft, struck her, returned the item with her, apologized, and forced the moral reality of the act into view. Ichinose's later shame is not based on false accusation (`Y1V09-E066–E069`; `JY1-199–JY1-205`).

Sakayanagi's attack works by converting a true event into a total identity.

The initial rumor cluster mixes violence, compensated dating, theft, and drugs. Coherence matters less than saturation. Repetition creates an atmosphere in which any concealed past can make all present goodness appear fraudulent. Once the theft becomes public, Sakayanagi extrapolates from a single wrongdoing to a future betrayal narrative. Ichinose's kindness can be reframed as compensation, manipulation, or hypocrisy (`Y1V09-E021`, `Y1V09-E058`, `Y1V09-E067–E069`).

This is authored reputation.

A reputation is not simply what others happen to think. It can be produced by selecting a fact, stripping context, combining it with heterogeneous allegations, and circulating it until the target must answer inside a frame another person built.

Ayanokōji's counterstrategy does not restore a pure information environment. He introduces additional rumors across classes, raising the institutional cost of nonintervention and making the problem too broad to ignore (`Y1V09-E058–E065`, `Y1V09-E078–E091`). The move protects Ichinose from asymmetrical attack. It also harms uninvolved students and weaponizes secrets.

The strategy exposes a recurring Ayanokōji pattern:

> He frees a person from someone else's authorship by assuming authorship over the conditions of freedom.

His direct response to Ichinose is humane. When she asks whether a criminal self can be allowed to exist, he says nobody possesses the right to deny her and that human beings have a right to forgiveness (`Y1V09-E092`; `JY1-211`). The claim does not erase wrongdoing. It rejects permanent identity foreclosure.

Forgiveness here is not innocence. It is the right to remain temporally open.

A person who did wrong can acknowledge the act, accept its moral reality, and still refuse the proposition that every later good action is counterfeit. Ichinose declares her penance finished without declaring the theft good. She resumes public leadership through ownership of the story rather than successful concealment (`Y1V09-E067–E075`, `Y1V09-E108`).

Ayanokōji's internal language again complicates the rescue. He conceptualizes the task partly as breaking Ichinose's heart so that she must confront what she buried. The outcome restores agency. The method remains paternalistic because he decides the pressure and developmental endpoint largely without her knowledge.

Ichinose's class is not exposed as politically worthless kindness. Its solidarity survives. Classmates do not abandon her when the truth becomes known. Trust functions as institutional capacity. The vulnerability lies elsewhere: the polity is weak against deniable bad faith and has difficulty converting social legitimacy into competitive result.

Hiyori provides a useful contrast during the related Class C rumor conflict. She stops a coerced confession because force would not make the statement proof. She is willing to fight to protect a friend but distinguishes loyalty from fabricated evidence (`Y1V09-E032`, `Y1V09-E063`). Her position joins ethical restraint to adversarial seriousness.

Volume 9 therefore refines the Year 1 truth model:

> Truth can liberate when the subject gains authorship of context.
>
> Truth can imprison when another actor converts one event into total identity.
>
> More information does not automatically produce justice; the distribution, framing, and authority of information matter.

# 10. The school manufactures a disposable person

The Class Vote is the moral center of Year 1 because it makes expulsion an explicit institutional objective.

Chabashira announces the examination after the unprecedented absence of first-year expulsions. The school does not first discover a student whose conduct independently requires removal. It decides that a cohort without expulsion is anomalous and constructs a procedure that must produce a victim (`Y1V10-E013–E018`; `JY1-215–JY1-219`).

The vote measures real things:

- popularity;
- resentment;
- coalition organization;
- current usefulness;
- external reputation;
- social visibility;
- and access to private-point capital.

It does not measure total human worth or developmental ceiling.

The institution nevertheless attaches irreversible consequence. The unique bottom student is expelled; the top student receives a Protection Point worth approximately the same as the twenty-million-point rescue price. Continuation and protection become explicitly monetized (`Y1V10-E014–E018`; `JY1-217–JY1-220`).

Each class reveals its constitution under forced scarcity.

Hirata rejects the legitimacy of choosing a classmate for sacrifice. His universal care becomes psychologically incapacitating because the exam does not permit simple refusal. He eventually asks classmates to target him and performs hostility in an attempt to become the victim (`Y1V10-E037`, `Y1V10-E083`, `Y1V10-E115`). His moral insight is substantial: the school is making students do the institution's violence to one another. His procedure cannot stop the demand.

Horikita accepts that judgment must occur and moves it into public reason. She names Yamauchi, explains the strategic betrayal, and accepts the possibility that criticism will redirect toward her. This improves answerability. It does not prove she knows the full developmental value of every student (`Y1V10-E046–E067`).

The distinction between procedural and substantive legitimacy is essential.

A publicly defended judgment is more contestable than anonymous manipulation. It can still be wrong.

Sudō's presence makes the epistemic limit impossible to ignore. The series has already shown a student whose early academic and behavioral state badly underestimated his future value. Chabashira admits that student growth can overturn prediction. The Class Vote nonetheless forces final judgment under uncertainty.

Ichinose's class attempts collective financial rescue. When a full rescue appears impossible, the fallback lottery distributes danger without pretending the class knows who objectively deserves disappearance. The lottery is not a claim that all students are equally useful. It is epistemic humility: the class refuses to convert uncertainty into false moral expertise (`Y1V10-E041–E045`, `Y1V10-E099–E103`).

Sakayanagi uses the exam as sovereign pruning. She manipulates Yamauchi as an experimental subject, hides the true A-class target behind a decoy, and removes Yahiko. Her polity's strength—centralized superior information—becomes a fiduciary danger because followers cannot audit the sovereign's private purpose (`Y1V10-E078–E085`).

Ryūen's class reveals the afterlife of authority. Followers work to preserve him even after fear has been broken as the exclusive basis of rule. Ayanokōji, Ishizaki, Ibuki, Hiyori, and Ichinose participate in a rescue architecture that converts points and cross-class praise into survival (`Y1V10-E094–E103`). Ryūen's continued existence is politically chosen by others, but the process remains partly authored by Ayanokōji.

Ayanokōji's own role is characteristically hidden. He redirects votes, protects selected people, uses external praise, and allows Yamauchi's expulsion while minimizing public attribution. He receives forty-two praise votes and a Protection Point (`Y1V10-E068–E088`). His judgment is often strategically stronger than the class's. It is also less publicly answerable than Horikita's.

The examination therefore asks a question that Year 1 cannot solve:

> Who possesses enough knowledge to terminate another person's developmental future?

The school's answer is procedural: the class will decide under the given rules.

Hirata's answer is moral refusal.

Horikita's answer is accountable judgment.

Ichinose's answer is solidarity and, failing that, nonpretended randomness.

Sakayanagi's answer is sovereign expertise.

Ayanokōji's answer is private calculation of future value.

None removes the institutional authorship of scarcity.

The Class Vote consequently exposes the darkest side of ANHS's developmental claim. The school can create environments in which students grow. It can also decide that growth has had enough time and require peers to convert one person into waste.

# 11. Distributed ability and the capture of the record

The Event Selection Exam appears to offer a more defensible meritocratic form.

Each class creates a portfolio of possible events, trains participants and reserves, predicts opponents, and relies upon a commander to allocate people under uncertainty. The exam treats the class not as an average score but as a distributed system (`Y1V11-E027–E035`; `JY1-229–JY1-235`).

This is the strongest Year 1 expression of collective `実力`.

Sudō possesses basketball authority. Akito possesses archery experience. Professor possesses typing speed. Horikita develops chess capacity. Keisei contributes academic organization. Ayanokōji possesses extraordinary hidden range. Kōenji's ability remains privately owned and politically unavailable. A class's strength depends upon identification, training, allocation, motivation, and willingness—not merely possession.

Horikita's leadership becomes generative. She does not need to be the strongest participant in every event. She increasingly creates structures in which other people's capacities become usable. Ayanokōji delegates event assignment rather than scripting every choice, allowing her judgment to become causally real (`Y1V11-E034`, `Y1V11-E057–E067`).

Kōenji states the autonomy limit directly: whether he uses his talent for Horikita is his decision. Membership can create obligations, but knowledge of capacity does not create class ownership over the body or skill (`Y1V11-E079–E086`; `JY1-239`).

The exam also marks Ayanokōji's conscious adoption of developmental authorship. He distinguishes what he should do from what he wants to do and decides that he wants to change Horikita seriously. This is self-authored purpose. It is not assigned by the White Room (`Y1V11-E119–E126`; `JY1-247–JY1-251`).

The Hirata intervention shows why self-authorship is not enough. Ayanokōji uses extreme blame to force Hirata out of collapse because the class needs the socially functional “part” he represents. Hirata later recovers, accepts that Horikita's Class Vote judgment may be one form of rightness, and begins a more particular friendship with Ayanokōji (`Y1V11-E094–E106`). The recovery is real. The method remains coercive.

The chess match then reveals a level of power above capacity, development, and coordination.

Ayanokōji identifies and transmits a move he considers winning. Horikita receives a different instruction approximately thirty seconds later. The substituted command produces Sakayanagi's official victory and removes Ayanokōji's Protection Point. When the two replay the position privately, they reconstruct the interference and Sakayanagi acknowledges that without it she would have lost (`Y1V11-E136–E145`; `Y1V11.5-E031`; `JY1-249–JY1-252`).

Tsukishiro controls the communication channel through which performance becomes record.

This is record sovereignty.

Ayanokōji can possess greater ability. Horikita can build the board state. Sakayanagi can recognize the anomaly. None controls the official result if the administrator responsible for preserving the event can replace the decisive action.

Record integrity is therefore not clerical housekeeping. It is constitutive of meritocratic legitimacy.

A system cannot credibly reward ability if superior performance can be severed from the official record by the authority that owns the transmission mechanism. The problem is more fundamental than unfair scoring. It concerns who decides what happened in institutional reality.

Volume 11 consequently completes the Year 1 chain:

> ability must exist;
> it must develop;
> it must be expressed;
> it must become legible;
> it must be coordinated;
> and the resulting performance must be honestly recorded.

A failure at any layer can make real capacity politically unreal.

# 12. Farewell, legacy, and reciprocal authorship

Volume 11.5 closes the year by changing the direction of development.

Earlier volumes often place a stronger observer above a subject: Ayanokōji diagnoses Kei, Horikita judges weaker classmates, Manabu judges Suzune, the school measures students, Sakayanagi classifies rivals. The farewell volume increasingly presents development as something that can move horizontally, be refused, or return to correct the person who initiated it.

Horikita's hair supplies the clearest symbolic case.

She initially calls the self built around Manabu a `偽者`, a false or counterfeit self. The text immediately rejects a simple purification story. The years lived in that form are also `紛れもない私`—unmistakably me (`Y1V11.5-E003–E005`; `JY1-254–JY1-255`).

Her short haircut does not uncover a pristine authentic identity beneath influence. It reclaims authorship over an identity whose earlier meaning had been organized around borrowed approval. She can acknowledge that imitation shaped her and still decide that the shaped self belongs to her history.

Manabu's final recognition is powerful because it becomes anti-imitative. He reveals that the long-hair preference was an old test of whether Suzune would erase her own color to satisfy him. He embraces the short-haired sister who declares that she will find her own path, learn with classmates, and lead them (`Y1V11.5-E090–E095`, `Y1V11.5-E148`).

His earlier physical violence remains abuse. Later accurate diagnosis and recognition do not authorize the method retroactively. What changes at the boundary is that he stops specifying what she should become.

Keisei's promise to challenge Horikita extends the same principle into class government. A leader becomes stronger when correction is institutionalized rather than treated as betrayal. Ayanokōji calls the structure proper for the class (`Y1V11.5-E010–E011`).

Hiyori similarly wants someone Ryūen recognizes to restrain reckless methods. Friendship becomes corrective agency rather than unconditional loyalty (`Y1V11.5-E055`).

Manabu's question to Ayanokōji—what will you leave behind?—turns ordinary school life from private consumption into legacy. Ayanokōji has treated peaceful student life as the first goal chosen `生まれて初めて`, for the first time in his life. Manabu asks whether freedom can also create effects that remain in other people (`Y1V11.5-E021–E022`, `Y1V11.5-E084–E089`; `JY1-256`, `JY1-261`).

The prompt awakens Ayanokōji's vision of developing students across all four classes and making developed students compete. The thought excites him (`Y1V11.5-E104`; `JY1-262`).

This is the Year 1 endpoint's most important ambiguity.

The desire is not assigned by Atsuomi. It is evidence of self-authorship. Yet the chosen project resembles a more plural, social version of the White Room: identify potential, shape environments, cultivate capacity, observe competition.

Freedom may initially increase the sophistication of control.

Ayanokōji also transfers Kushida jurisdiction to Horikita despite believing expulsion efficient because he wants to see how Horikita handles the obstacle (`Y1V11.5-E101–E103`). The decision gives Horikita real political authorship. It also treats Kushida and the class as material inside Horikita's developmental test.

Ichinose's farewell material preserves independent agency. She describes Ayanokōji as `味方じゃない……だけど信頼できる人`—not an ally, but someone she can trust. Her feelings deepen, yet she recommits to rival-class competition. Ayanokōji's private `介錯`—kaishaku, the image of administering a final merciful end if she fails—reveals concern entangled with terminal judgment (`Y1V11.5-E060–E083`; `JY1-259–JY1-260`).

The one-year promise is therefore neither pure romance nor pure manipulation. It gives Ichinose a future horizon and places Ayanokōji in a troubling position as possible judge of what the horizon means.

Matsushita broadens the epistemic problem. She integrates clues from the relay, arithmetic, relationships, and Protection Point behavior, then tests Ayanokōji directly. Her own concealed ability demonstrates that strategic underexpression is not unique to him. It also shows that his low-information interface is becoming harder to sustain (`Y1V11.5-E109–E123`, `Y1V11.5-E150`).

The institutional response to Tsukishiro changes as well. Ayanokōji seeks contact with Chairman Sakayanagi, rejects personal favoritism, and frames his request around institutional integrity. The chairman recommends Chabashira and Mashima as an oversight network; Mashima agrees that teachers cannot permit authorities to alter tests or results dishonestly (`Y1V11.5-E017–E035`).

For once, Ayanokōji does not answer institutional capture only through private control. He attempts to activate plural oversight.

The school is not redeemed. Its safeguards remain contingent and vulnerable. But ANHS contains actors capable of resisting abuse from within. That incompleteness distinguishes it from the White Room's aspiration to total authorship.

# 13. Romance as a hidden curriculum

The confession to Kei is both ordinary and structurally abnormal.

Ayanokōji experiences uncertainty, rehearses probabilities, checks himself in a mirror, prepares the room, worries about response, and finally states `オレは軽井沢恵が好きだ`. Kei accepts and explicitly says she has come to like him (`Y1V11.5-E124–E136`).

The confession is not fake.

His uncertainty after speaking does not prove deception. It reveals limited self-knowledge. He has chosen an action before possessing a complete theory of what the feeling means.

That is, in one sense, ordinary adolescence.

In another, the relationship remains an experiment. Ayanokōji deliberately produces jealousy to test Kei's feelings. He says he will learn romance through her, treats the relationship as relevant to changing what he sees as host dependency, calls her a `教科書`—textbook—and imagines the role may end when the learning is complete (`Y1V11.5-E126`, `Y1V11.5-E129–E140`; `JY1-263`).

The hidden curriculum is asymmetric because Kei does not possess equal knowledge of the pedagogical design.

Yet the relationship is not reducible to curriculum. Ayanokōji does not merely predict Kei's answer; he asks for it. She reciprocates for herself. The final image presents an embrace while the adjacent prose preserves uncertainty. Ayanokōji imagines an alternative future in which she becomes `掛け替えのない存在`, an irreplaceable existence, and says `祈ろう`—let me hope or pray—even while another part of him believes the future impossible (`Y1V11.5-E134–E151`; `JY1-264–JY1-265`).

Prayer is different from prediction.

A prediction asserts control through a model. Prayer acknowledges an outcome the subject cannot guarantee.

This is not yet a stable ethic of reciprocal love. It is the first time the language of irreplaceability appears as a desired future rather than a solved classification.

The correct Year 1 formulation is therefore:

> **explicit reciprocal romance inside an asymmetrical hidden curriculum, with genuine care and unresolved irreplaceability.**

The formulation preserves four truths at once:

1. the coercive origin remains morally binding;
2. Kei's later agency is authentic;
3. Ayanokōji's affection and ordinary desire are real;
4. information and developmental purpose remain unequal.

The relationship may become more reciprocal without its origin becoming innocent. It may also become more intimate without becoming fully answerable.

*First File* strengthens the ordinary side of the bond. In bonus fiction, Ayanokōji places Kei explicitly beyond friendship, waits for her contact, mishandles the emotional rhythm of ending a call, finds strategically irrelevant conversation interesting, feels reluctant to separate, and wants tomorrow to arrive because he wants to see her (`Y1FF-E059–E072`; `JY1-273–JY1-278`).

These passages substantially weaken any instrument-only reading.

They do not settle permanent irreplaceability. A desire to see someone is evidence of particular attachment, not proof that every functional model has dissolved.

The relationship ends Year 1 as the clearest test of reciprocal authorship. Can Kei know enough to revise the terms? Can she maintain friendships and purposes not supplied by Ayanokōji? Can he accept development that makes her less dependent on him? Can romance survive if the hidden curriculum becomes explicit? Can he value the person who exceeds the lesson?

Those questions must remain open at the Year 1 boundary.

# 14. *First File*: the archive and the person

*First File* changes the form of the corpus.

The novels present development through time, partial viewpoint, dialogue, contradiction, and scene. The guidebook reorganizes the year into calendars, profiles, ratings, point tables, summaries, casual-clothes images, hypothetical OAA values, and bonus fiction. It turns lived experience into an archive.

The archive is useful because it allows comparison. It is dangerous because its form suggests completeness.

The guidebook must be separated into four question-relative layers.

`FF-DATA` records institutional information such as calendars and point standings. `FF-RATING` presents measurements and retrospective simulations. `FF-EDITORIAL` supplies official summaries. `FF-FICTION` presents canonical narrative interiority. None is simply “the official truth” for every question.

Ayanokōji's rating page is the clearest demonstration. His visible performance can be accurately recorded as mediocre because he intentionally produced mediocre output. The file fails only when the reader converts visible output into total latent capacity (`Y1FF-E004–E007`; `JY1-266–JY1-267`).

Horikita's early cooperation deficit can be accurate as a July state and false as a permanent identity. Sudō's early file can underpredict a trajectory the institution had not yet allowed to unfold. Kushida's cooperation can measure genuine public function while saying little about moral trustworthiness. Sakayanagi's composite can translate physical disability into lower aggregate “ability” because the weighting system contains normative choices.

The hypothetical `もしもOAAがあったら`—if OAA had existed—makes the retrospective status explicit (`Y1FF-E041–E048`; `JY1-268–JY1-270`). The exercise is valuable because it shows what the later measurement model would have made legible. It does not create a secret OAA operating inside earlier volumes.

The distinction between a correct score and an exhaustive person is the final Year 1 epistemic lesson.

A metric can be reliable within a bounded domain and still become oppressive when the domain is forgotten. A psychological model can explain dependency and still fail to predict refusal. A reputation can contain a true wrongdoing and still falsely totalize identity. A narrator can understand mechanisms better than motives. An institutional record can preserve an outcome and still be corrupt.

The bonus fiction formally resists dossier totalization. Ayanokōji values `学生らしい生活`, student-like life, as a source of fulfillment; he enters `恋愛という世界に身を任せる`, allows himself to be carried into the world of romance; he finds conversation without strategic utility interesting; he wants to see Kei (`Y1FF-E059–E073`; `JY1-273–JY1-278`). Ichinose privately reaches beyond `大切な友達` toward a relational category she cannot name, then reasserts that their classes are rivals and that a new struggle begins (`Y1FF-E077–E082`; `JY1-279–JY1-283`).

The fiction restores temporal uncertainty, desire, and self-contradiction after the profiles compress people into readable objects.

*First File* therefore does not refute measurement. It supplies the rule by which measurement should be used:

> **The file is evidence about the person. The file is not the person.**

Ayanokōji most obviously exceeds the file written about him. He is also the person most tempted to treat his own models of others as complete enough to govern them.

The guidebook's final correction is thus ethical as well as epistemic:

> To recognize a person is not only to understand her. It is to preserve her power to exceed the understanding.

# 15. Ayanokōji: freedom without relinquished sovereignty

The most persistent misreading of Ayanokōji is that Year 1 asks whether he is secretly human.

It does not.

He is human from the beginning. He wants friends, fears social failure, notices bodies and beauty, enjoys food and books, lacks ordinary hobbies, seeks privacy, experiences attraction, wants peaceful days, and later values cinema, gifts, snow, phone calls, and romantic time. The first-person `オレ` voice contains adolescent insecurity alongside severe strategic compression (`Y1V01-E003–E007`, `Y1V02-E004–E006`, `Y1V04.5-E015`, `Y1V07.5-E023`, `Y1FF-E059–E073`).

The Year 1 problem is not emotional absence. It is integration.

Ordinary desire, relational attachment, and White Room conditioning coexist without reliably correcting one another.

Ayanokōji wants peace and becomes excited by difficult opponents. He values friendship and withholds the causal truth of his actions from friends. He recognizes that boundaries exist and suspends them when he believes development, protection, or reliability requires control. He understands pain and can use pain as an instrument. He rejects being another person's project and increasingly turns other people into projects.

His greatest virtue and greatest danger arise from the same capacity: he sees what environments do to people.

He notices Ike's contextual competence before the class does. He recognizes that Sudō's current failure need not define his ceiling. He sees that Sakura needs room to act for herself. He identifies Kei's social ability beneath the public persona. He understands the trauma structures beneath Hirata's universalism and Ichinose's shame. He recognizes the strategic limitations of each class constitution. He can build environments in which capacities become visible and useful.

That is an extraordinary form of cognitive empathy.

It is not the same as moral recognition.

Ayanokōji often knows what another person fears, wants, or might become. The ethical failure occurs when accurate understanding becomes a warrant for unilateral action. He decides what information they should receive, how much pressure they should endure, when rescue should occur, what relationship should become necessary, or which future should be protected.

His empathy is therefore asymmetrical across several dimensions.

He possesses exceptional **cognitive empathy**: the ability to model another person's interior structure.

He often possesses strong **practical care**: medicine, protection, information, rescue, study support, privacy shielding, and institutional defense.

He possesses real but underarticulated **affective resonance**: warmth at names, desire to see Kei, interest in Sakura's growth, curiosity about snow, pleasure in books and films, concern for friends.

He possesses inconsistent **moral restraint**: he can preserve refusal and privacy, but he can also manufacture danger, use sexualized coercion, intensify crisis, and distribute risks to uninformed third parties.

He possesses limited **reciprocal vulnerability**: other people rarely know enough to interpret or refuse the relationship on equal terms.

This layered model avoids both flattening directions.

The tool language is not fake. Ayanokōji marks the Volume 3 passage as `本心`, and the fallback criterion of final victory is genuine (`Y1V03-E045`; `JY1-035–JY1-038`). The ordinary voice is not fake either. It appears before anyone could plausibly be the intended audience for a social performance and is strengthened by *First File* interiority.

His narration is selectively incomplete rather than globally deceptive. He is generally reliable about mechanisms, immediate observations, incentives, and physical events. He systematically underdiscloses advance planning, the scale of his competence, and his causal responsibility for environments. His stated motives are often accurate but partial. Emotion frequently appears in conduct before he can classify it.

The metaphors `道具`, `パーツ`, `寄生虫`, `宿木`, `扉`, and `教科書` should therefore be read as operative models, not final ontologies (`JY1-036`, `JY1-040`, `JY1-118`, `JY1-196`, `JY1-247`, `JY1-263`). They reveal how he organizes action. They do not prove that the people so described are exhausted by the category.

The narrative repeatedly lets subjects exceed the model.

Kei's rooftop refusal exceeds parasite logic. Horikita's strategies exceed puppet logic. Ryūen's followers exceed fear logic. Hirata's chosen friendship exceeds “useful part” logic. Ichinose retains rival-class agency despite trust. Matsushita exposes the limits of his concealment model. Hiyori creates friendship without dependency.

Ayanokōji's Year 1 development occurs when he begins to notice these excesses.

Ordinary life becomes a positive goal rather than merely absence of Atsuomi. He describes his school life as something chosen for the first time and worth defending through legitimate institutional support (`Y1V11.5-E017–E024`; `JY1-256`, `JY1-273–JY1-278`).

Developmental authorship also becomes a positive goal. He wants to change Horikita and later imagines cultivating students across four classes.

The two self-chosen goals are not automatically compatible.

A life devoted to creating strong rivals and developed students can reproduce the White Room's experimental grammar even if the environment is more social and the purpose is freely chosen. The White Room's deepest legacy inside Ayanokōji is not simply emotional coldness. It is the assumption that human development becomes most intelligible when someone superior designs the conditions.

His decisive Year 1 ethical task is therefore not to acquire feelings. It is to let feelings, relationships, institutions, and other people's answers limit the jurisdiction of his models.

Can he care without converting care into curriculum?

Can he protect without monopolizing protection?

Can he teach without needing the learner's development to validate his theory?

Can he accept a refusal that frustrates his design?

Can he value a person after she becomes less useful, less dependent, or less predictable?

Can he accept an outcome he did not author?

Year 1 leaves each question open.

The strongest endpoint judgment is:

> **Ayanokōji has moved beyond being only the White Room's product, but he has not moved beyond White Room grammar whenever he translates care into curriculum, protection into sovereignty, or understanding into jurisdiction. His growth lies not in newly possessing emotion, but in encountering meanings his strategic vocabulary cannot fully contain. His next development depends on whether he allows those meanings—and the people who produce them—to correct him.**

# 16. Horikita: from scalar merit to corrigible leadership

Horikita's Year 1 arc is frequently misdescribed in three ways.

She is not simply Ayanokōji's public puppet.

She is not simply Manabu's imitative younger sister.

She is not simply a severe girl who learns kindness.

Each description names part of the truth. None captures her political development.

Horikita begins with isolated competence and a scalar theory of merit. She is academically strong, disciplined, observant, and socially unavailable. She assumes that a class should be organized around those already capable of contributing and that weaker members are burdens. Her early pedagogy fails because she treats inability as insufficient seriousness rather than a different starting point.

Sudō changes the theory.

His trajectory supplies a persistent counterexample to static judgment. He moves from academic liability and impulsive violence toward disciplined study, athletic expertise, loyalty, public leadership, and the capacity to disagree with Horikita. His development is not a gift she owns. She eventually returns authorship to him by recognizing that his effort is his own (`Y1V01-E014–E020`, `Y1V05-E017–E035`, `Y1V10-E064–E067`; `JY1-006`, `JY1-020–JY1-021`).

The relationship teaches Horikita that strength becomes socially useful through translation and adaptation. A stronger participant may need to slow down, explain differently, reveal reasons, or accept expertise from another domain. Leadership is not the right to make everyone imitate the leader's mode of competence.

The island forces interdependence. Horikita's illness and isolation make individual excellence operationally unavailable. The lesson is genuine despite the coercive authorship of the environment. Volume 5 makes the insight embodied. Paper Shuffle makes it institutional. By Volume 11, she is increasingly able to identify, train, allocate, and rely upon abilities she does not possess.

Her Year 1 leadership can therefore be described as **distributed, generative, and corrigible**.

It is **distributed** because class capacity resides across Sudō, Hirata, Kei, Kushida, Keisei, Ike, Akito, Airi, Professor, Kōenji, Ayanokōji, and others.

It is **generative** because leadership can increase the usability of other people's capacities rather than merely display its own.

It is **corrigible** because mature authority must permit dissent, new information, and revision.

Keisei's promise to challenge her at the end of the year is not a threat to leadership. It is evidence that the class may be developing a political structure in which loyalty includes correction (`Y1V11.5-E010–E011`).

Horikita's growing independence is visible in decisions that diverge from Ayanokōji and Manabu.

She authors the Paper Shuffle counterstrategy and secures exclusive submission authority. She refuses to drag Ayanokōji into the Kushida wager until he chooses to join. She refuses the student council despite approval from both men. She treats the Ichinose crisis as her leadership decision. She publicly names Yamauchi during the Class Vote. She later accepts the possibility of joining the student council on terms whose timing remains hers (`Y1V06-E039–E061`, `Y1V07.5-E045–E046`, `Y1V09-E036`, `Y1V10-E056–E060`, `Y1V11.5-E100`).

These choices do not make her fully autonomous from Ayanokōji.

He controls hidden information, protection, attribution, and contingencies. He knows more about her development than she knows about his purpose. By Volume 11, he explicitly chooses her development as a project. The class therefore operates under a shadow constitution:

> Horikita increasingly governs the visible polity; Ayanokōji governs much of the invisible security infrastructure.

The shadow constitution protects the class and limits its self-government.

Horikita's Class Vote decision represents her clearest development in public authority. She names a target, states reasons, accepts risk, and makes the judgment contestable. The moment should not be romanticized. Yamauchi's betrayal is real, but Horikita's evaluation of his future is not omniscient. The institution has manufactured the demand for a victim. Public accountability improves the procedure without proving the justice of the outcome.

Her Kushida policy is the harder constitutional test.

Horikita's anti-disposability presumption is morally serious. She has learned from Sudō that current danger or weakness does not determine permanent value. Kushida's social ability is substantial. Yet Kushida is not simply immature. She knowingly weaponizes confidential information, sabotages, and seeks expulsion. Horikita's integration project imposes risks on classmates who did not authorize the experiment.

The policy can therefore be both principled and hubristic.

It becomes legitimate only if the class gains meaningful safeguards, Kushida retains the ability to choose a different relationship, and Horikita remains answerable for the costs.

Manabu's role clarifies the difference between influence and authorship.

Suzune shaped herself around his recognition. Hair, grades, personality, and self-conception became organized around imitation. The solution is not to discover a self untouched by him. `偽者` is revised by `紛れもない私`: the imitative self is still part of her lived history. Independence means converting influence into material she can own, interpret, revise, and redirect (`Y1V11.5-E003–E005`; `JY1-254–JY1-255`).

Manabu's final recognition becomes legitimate when it stops specifying her future. He embraces the sister who chooses a different form.

Horikita's endpoint is therefore **integrated self-authorship**, not purity.

She has not escaped influence. No person does. She has begun turning influence into publicly owned judgment.

The unresolved question is political:

> Can her distributed, correctable leadership become the actual sovereign structure of the class, or will every serious crisis continue to depend upon an invisible actor who cannot be questioned because most classmates do not know he is governing?

Year 1 makes the first possibility imaginable. It does not yet make it secure.

# 17. Relationships as tests of answerability

Year 1's relationships cannot be reduced to affection.

A relationship may contain love, friendship, loyalty, trust, protection, dependency, rivalry, or mentorship while remaining deeply unequal in information and power. The decisive comparative question is not whether asymmetry exists. It is whether asymmetry becomes answerable.

A relationship is more reciprocal when the less powerful or less informed person can:

- understand the material conditions;
- refuse without catastrophic retaliation;
- contradict the stronger party;
- retain purposes not supplied by the bond;
- form alternative relationships;
- change roles;
- become less useful or less dependent;
- and still remain recognized as the same particular person.

## Kei: reciprocity inside a concealed curriculum

Kei's relationship with Ayanokōji contains the full Year 1 sequence:

> manufactured dependency → autonomous rooftop refusal → negotiated partnership → explicit reciprocal romance

Each stage is real. None erases the previous one.

Her refusal on the rooftop demonstrates agency that exceeds the model. Her demands for sincerity and consultation in Volume 7.5 make the partnership more answerable. Her acceptance of the confession is a personal answer. *First File* confirms that Ayanokōji wants ordinary contact with her.

The relationship remains limited by hidden information and developmental purpose. The strongest Year 1 judgment is not “healthy” or “fake.” It is structurally mixed.

## Ichinose: trust without political absorption

Ichinose offers a different model. She can trust Ayanokōji, draw emotional strength from him, and develop feelings beyond ordinary friendship while retaining class loyalty and rival purpose. `味方じゃない……だけど信頼できる人` names the distinction exactly: not an ally, but someone trustworthy (`Y1V11.5-E079`; `JY1-259`).

Her agency is not disproved by dependence during crisis. She refuses to reveal strategic truths to future opponents. She survives reputational exposure with class solidarity intact. She re-enters competition.

The danger lies in Ayanokōji's private `介錯` model and one-year promise (`JY1-260`). He imagines himself as possible terminal judge of her trajectory. Trust can therefore coexist with external developmental jurisdiction.

## Hirata: universal care and the recovery of particularity

Hirata's social accessibility is one of Class D's most important capacities. He creates safety by making himself available to everyone. The same universalism functions as a defense against particular judgment. If every classmate must be protected equally, he never has to name whom he especially wants, resents, fears losing, or cannot save.

The Class Vote breaks that structure. His collapse is not proof that kindness was fake. It reveals the trauma and impossibility carried by universal care.

Ayanokōji initially restores him because the class needs the `平田というパーツ`, the Hirata part. Later, Hirata asks for chosen given-name intimacy—`清隆くん` and `洋介`—and Ayanokōji experiences awkward warmth (`Y1V11.5-E048`). The relationship begins to move from functional need toward particular friendship.

The ethical question is whether Ayanokōji can value Yōsuke when he is not functioning as class infrastructure, and whether Hirata can choose particular bonds without experiencing them as betrayal of everyone else.

## Hiyori: low-pressure friendship and loyal correction

Hiyori is one of the clearest countermodels to authored dependency.

She approaches through books, conversation, and shared interest. She does not need rescue, subordinate herself as an operative, or abandon rival-class loyalty. She names Ayanokōji a reading friend and argues that school life need not consist only of enemies (`Y1V07-E018–E020`, `Y1V09-E031`).

Her relation to Ryūen is similarly instructive. She remains loyal enough to preserve the polity while refusing some methods and seeking influence to restrain him. Loyalty becomes correction rather than obedience.

## The Ayanokōji Group: friendship with a hidden person

Haruka defines the group through voluntary attendance and absence of compulsory utility. Ayanokōji values the low-pressure structure because he need not force himself to match an assigned role (`Y1V06-E046`, `Y1V07.5-E035`).

The friendship is real and epistemically incomplete. The group knows an authored version of Ayanokōji. They do not know the scale of his power, the environments he has designed, or the protection hierarchy that excludes them. The key future test is whether friendship can survive increased legibility.

Concealment does not make a bond automatically false. People are entitled to privacy. The question is whether the withheld facts are so causally central that others cannot understand the risks or meaning of the relationship.

## Horikita and Sudō: development that returns ownership

Sudō's development is comparatively healthy because he becomes capable of disagreeing with the mentor. Horikita influences him, but he acquires domain authority, self-discipline, and independent judgment. She eventually credits his work to him.

The ability to answer back is evidence that development has not merely produced a more useful subordinate.

## Manabu and Suzune: recognition after specification ends

Manabu's early violence and emotional withholding are not legitimate pedagogy. His later recognition matters because he releases Suzune from the requirement to copy him. The relationship becomes more ethical when love no longer specifies form.

## Ryūen and his followers: voluntary loyalty without constitutional restraint

Ishizaki, Albert, Ibuki, and Hiyori increasingly choose Ryūen after fear is broken as the exclusive basis of authority. Their choice gives his rule sociological legitimacy. It does not establish robust answerability. Followers can want a ruler without possessing institutions capable of limiting him.

## Names as relational geography

Year 1 uses address terms to map permission and role.

`Kiyopon`, `Kiyotaka`, `Kei`, `Yōsuke`, and other shifts do not independently prove romance or equality. They create local worlds in which different versions of a person become speakable. A private name can mark trust while also intensifying secrecy. A given name can be chosen intimacy or assigned leverage.

The relationship corpus therefore yields a general rule:

> **Recognition is not complete when one person correctly understands another. Recognition becomes moral when the understood person retains the power to exceed, reject, and revise the understanding.**

# 18. Four class constitutions and four incomplete theories of collective power

The first-year classes are not merely ranks with distinctive leaders.

Across Year 1, each becomes a political technology for converting unequal individual capacities into collective action. The class letters change. The constitutional tendencies are more stable.

The four polities should not be arranged into a simple moral ladder. Each contains a genuine capacity and a characteristic danger. Their virtues become politically destructive when the source of strength becomes unanswerable.

## 18.1 Horikita's developmental pluralism

`POLITY-HORIKITA` begins with the least coherent population and develops the most explicit theory of heterogeneous capacity.

The class contains academic specialists, athletes, social brokers, practical experts, emotionally accessible leaders, hidden strategists, reluctant geniuses, and students whose usefulness changes sharply across environments. Its strength lies in explosive potential and role diversity. Its initial weakness is that those capacities do not naturally form a collective (`Y1FF-E025`).

Horikita's maturing leadership attempts to solve the conversion problem through development and distribution. Sudō is not discarded. Ike's practical knowledge is recognized. Keisei teaches. Kei reads social atmosphere. Hirata supplies legitimacy. Event Selection requires portfolio allocation rather than one universal metric.

The polity's constitutional ideal is developmental pluralism:

> unequal people can remain members because a class can identify, cultivate, and coordinate unlike capacities without pretending they are the same.

Its danger is hidden sovereignty.

Ayanokōji controls intelligence, insurance, protection, attribution, and final contingency. Public leadership becomes more legitimate while decisive causation remains partly invisible. The class can experience real development inside a political order it does not fully understand.

The Year 1 test is whether Horikita can convert the shadow constitution into transparent, answerable government without losing the protection it supplied.

## 18.2 Ryūen's authoritarian mobilization

`POLITY-RYUEN` centralizes information and decision around a leader willing to use fear, retaliation, deception, and violence.

The advantages are substantial:

- rapid mobilization;
- adversarial clarity;
- willingness to exploit procedural gaps;
- strong offensive coordination;
- and refusal to sentimentalize conflict.

Ryūen sees that institutions and opponents often respond not to moral ideals but to incentives, pain, and credible threat. His class can act with speed because uncertainty is concentrated at the top.

The cost is epistemic and constitutional. Fear suppresses feedback. Followers learn to execute rather than challenge. Critical information becomes a bottleneck. The ruler's mistakes are harder to correct because dissent is dangerous.

The rooftop defeat breaks fear as an exclusive foundation. Followers later choose to preserve Ryūen. Hiyori corrects him; Ishizaki and Albert display loyalty; Ibuki resists some methods. Authority becomes more sociologically legitimate (`Y1V07-E100–E106`, `Y1V10-E094–E103`, `Y1V11-E109–E127`).

Sociological legitimacy is not moral legitimacy.

Ryūen continues to recognize `勝つための悪逆`—wrongdoing for victory—as constitutionally available (`JY1-249`). Voluntary followers may strengthen authoritarian rule without limiting it.

The Year 1 test is whether loyalty can become a source of constraint rather than only a deeper resource for the ruler.

## 18.3 Ichinose's solidaristic trust

`POLITY-ICHINOSE` converts trust into infrastructure.

It pools points, shares information, protects members, sustains legitimacy, and survives the public exposure of its leader's wrongdoing. Kanzaki can criticize from within without automatically becoming an enemy. The class demonstrates that solidarity is not a sentimental absence of strategy. It is a political capacity (`Y1V03-E021`, `Y1V09-E066–E075`, `Y1V10-E041–E045`, `Y1V11-E071`).

Its vulnerability is adversarial security.

The polity tends to assume that others will recognize reciprocity, visible good faith, or the moral cost of harming members. Ryūen exploits that reluctance during Event Selection. Sakayanagi weaponizes shame and rumor. The class has difficulty converting high social capacity into competitive results.

The responsible conclusion is not that Ichinose must become Ryūen.

A polity can develop defensive intelligence, redundancy, and internal dissent without abandoning solidarity. Kanzaki's role suggests one path: loyal opposition that challenges result conversion while preserving shared legitimacy.

The Year 1 test is whether trust can defend itself without destroying the relational infrastructure that makes it strong.

## 18.4 Sakayanagi's aristocratic optimization

`POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` possesses the strongest visible talent pool and the clearest centralized allocation.

Sakayanagi identifies capacity, assigns roles, builds information networks, uses decoys, and accepts sacrifice. Her class can convert elite judgment into efficient action. The system is epistemically powerful because the sovereign often does know more than followers.

That strength produces the fiduciary problem.

When one person possesses the best model of everyone else, interpretation becomes monopolized. Followers cannot easily distinguish class interest from the ruler's personal rivalry. The Class Vote demonstrates sovereign pruning. The Event Selection conflict makes Ayanokōji increasingly central to Sakayanagi's purpose. The class bears costs generated by an objective it did not collectively choose (`Y1V10-E078–E085`, `Y1V11-E087–E100`, `Y1V11-E128–E145`).

The polity's danger is not incompetent hierarchy. It is competent unanswerable hierarchy.

The Year 1 test is whether superior judgment can remain fiduciary—used for the members' independent interests—when the sovereign's private theory becomes politically consuming.

## 18.5 How the four polities distribute risk

The Class Vote and Event Selection Exam reveal a useful comparison.

Horikita increasingly makes risk public, reasoned, and distributable.

Ichinose mutualizes risk through solidarity and pooled resources.

Ryūen concentrates and weaponizes risk, often placing it under the leader's command.

Sakayanagi allocates risk from above through superior information.

None is universally optimal.

Public reason can become majoritarian sacrifice. Mutualization can be exploited. Concentration can become domination. Sovereign allocation can become private rule.

This is why competitive result must be separated from legitimacy.

A class can win while being procedurally opaque. A leader can be loved while morally coercive. A polity can be ethically admirable while strategically vulnerable. A ruler can possess the best information while violating fiduciary duty.

At least six dimensions must remain distinct:

- competitive effectiveness;
- sociological legitimacy;
- procedural legitimacy;
- epistemic legitimacy;
- moral legitimacy;
- and developmental legitimacy.

The class-point table records one of those dimensions. It cannot decide the whole constitution.

## 18.6 Kōenji and Nagumo as constitutional disruptions

Kōenji challenges every polity from inside the problem of membership. He possesses extraordinary ability and refuses the inference that membership grants collective title over its use. His autonomy exposes a limit in developmental pluralism: the class can recognize a capacity and still be unable to make it socially available.

Nagumo challenges the class system from above. He argues that exceptional individuals should be able to move beyond inherited collective outcomes. The problem is that individual mobility can become personal patronage when one evaluator controls recognition and opportunity.

Together they produce a dilemma:

> Collective membership can become imprisonment.
>
> Individual meritocracy can become sovereign allocation.

Year 1 does not choose between them. It asks what institutional structure could preserve mobility without making access dependent upon one ruler, and preserve solidarity without claiming ownership over private capacity.

# 19. `実力`, points, and the legitimacy of measurement

The title places `実力` at the center of the series, but Year 1 persistently resists a single English equivalent. The founding lexical anchors are `JY1-002` and `JY1-003`.

“Ability,” “actual capability,” “demonstrated competence,” and “merit” each capture part of the word. The narrative's deeper work is to separate capacities that institutional discourse tends to collapse.

## 19.1 Possession

A capacity may actually exist.

Ayanokōji's academic and physical abilities exist before they are publicly demonstrated. Kōenji's capacity exists even when he refuses to use it. Sakayanagi's strategic ability exists despite physical limitation.

## 19.2 Development

Capacity changes through time, practice, environment, relationship, and choice.

Sudō is the clearest counterexample to static ranking. Horikita, Airi, Hirata, Ryūen, and the class polities also develop. A dated score cannot become permanent identity.

## 19.3 Expression

The person may display, suppress, distort, or strategically curate what exists.

Ayanokōji's fifty-point scores and mediocre athletic output make expression an authored variable. Matsushita shows that concealment is not unique to him.

## 19.4 Legibility

An observer or metric must infer from expression.

Sudō's truth can be institutionally weak. Sakura's witness can lack probative force. Kei's social capacity can escape academic metrics. Kushida's public sociality can become highly legible while private risk remains concealed.

## 19.5 Ecological fit

The value of capacity changes with environment.

Ike on the island, Sudō at the sports festival, Keisei in academic coordination, and Hiyori in low-pressure strategic correction demonstrate that no ability has one fixed marginal value.

## 19.6 Social usability

Capacity must be accessible to other people.

Kōenji is the extreme case. Sudō before relational adaptation and Horikita before leadership development also possess more ability than the class can use.

## 19.7 Distribution

Collective strength emerges from combining unlike capacities.

Paper Shuffle and Event Selection make this explicit. A class is a portfolio, not an average individual.

## 19.8 Generation

Some actors produce new answers outside another person's model.

Horikita's independent strategies, Kei's rooftop refusal, Hiyori's correction, and Ryūen's return are generative because they exceed prediction rather than merely execute a supplied role.

## 19.9 Political power

Ability can alter rules, incentives, interpretation, or collective action.

Nagumo's institutional influence, Kushida's information network, Ichinose's legitimacy, Ryūen's coercion, and Sakayanagi's role allocation all belong here.

## 19.10 Recording

Performance must enter the official account intact.

Tsukishiro's chess interference demonstrates that a superior move can become institutionally unreal.

## 19.11 Model limitation

The resulting score or file remains a bounded representation.

*First File* supplies the final warning. `能力評価`, `協調性`, and hypothetical OAA categories are useful only if their object and time remain visible (`JY1-266–JY1-270`).

The mature Year 1 formulation is therefore:

> **`実力` is not merely what a person can do. It is what can be developed, expressed, recognized, coordinated, politically converted, honestly recorded, and still remembered as smaller than the person.**

## 19.12 Examinations author the evidence they produce

The school's tests do not merely reveal students.

The opening month creates hidden-rule socialization. The hearing creates a credibility contest. The island changes ecology. The zodiac exam prices betrayal. The sports festival publicly metricizes bodies. Paper Shuffle engineers dependency. The training camp concentrates risk in leaders. The rumor crisis turns reputation into an unofficial rating system. The Class Vote manufactures disposability. Event Selection measures distributed capacity. Tsukishiro turns the final contest into a demonstration of record capture.

Rules decide what becomes rational, visible, punishable, and profitable.

An exam that rewards betrayal cannot treat betrayal as pure evidence of pre-existing character. An exam that forces sacrifice cannot describe the victim as though the class independently discovered disposability. An institution shares responsibility for the behavior its incentive structure produces.

## 19.13 Points are political capacity

Private points and class points create real agency.

They can purchase information, compensate labor, buy an examination point, finance rescue, preserve autonomy, pool risk, and potentially enable class mobility. They also create unequal access to mercy.

A twenty-million-point option can be formally available to everyone and materially available only to actors with wealth, organization, or allies. Equal price does not produce equal capability.

Ichinose's distinction is the moral limit: private points can be lost and regained; a lost classmate cannot simply be restored (`Y1V10-E041–E045`; `JY1-221–JY1-224`).

Points can price institutional options. They cannot establish human worth.

## 19.14 Competing meritocracies

Manabu and Nagumo represent different answers to the unit of merit.

Manabu's order retains class membership, collective responsibility, and developmental time. Its risk is that belonging traps exceptional individuals.

Nagumo emphasizes individual mobility and direct recognition. Its risk is that recognition becomes patronage under the evaluator.

Year 1 therefore does not oppose meritocracy to kindness. It asks:

- what should be measured;
- over what time horizon;
- at what unit—individual, pair, group, class;
- by whom;
- with what opportunity to display capacity;
- and with what consequence.

## 19.15 A positive standard for legitimate measurement

The critique yields a constructive standard.

Measurement becomes more legitimate when it preserves:

- domain specificity;
- temporal specificity;
- criteria relevant to the consequence;
- meaningful opportunity to display capacity;
- developmental humility;
- proportional stakes;
- correction and appeal;
- attention to unequal access;
- multiple forms of evidence;
- and truthful recording.

The more irreversible the consequence, the stronger the requirements.

A score can be accurate and still too narrow for expulsion. A ranking can be useful and still unable to decide moral worth. A file can support judgment and still fail to authorize ownership.

# 20. ANHS, the White Room, and developmental institutions

Advanced Nurturing High School is neither the humane opposite of the White Room nor merely the same prison with classmates.

The structural comparison is real.

Both institutions enclose students, author environments, monitor conduct, classify performance, and attach consequences. Both can make developmental purpose more important than the subject's immediate preference. Both can turn human beings into data and outcomes.

The difference lies in the degree and distribution of authorship.

The White Room, as Year 1 directly establishes it, attempts total developmental control. It restricts ordinary life, privacy, leisure, environmental choice, and goals not supplied by the program. It produces real academic, martial, and survival capacities. Atsuomi converts causal authorship into property: because the institution made the child, the creator claims the future.

ANHS is more plural, marketized, and contested.

It provides privacy, friendship, leisure, romance, rivalry, student politics, multiple teachers, class constitutions, consumer choice, and ordinary time. These are not simply softer incentives. They allow meanings to emerge that no single authority fully designed.

The school remains coercive. It hides criteria, surveils, creates scarcity, rewards betrayal, prices survival, and can be captured by adult power. Its ethical advantage lies less in benevolence than in incompleteness.

No actor completely owns the student.

## 20.1 White Room evidence tiers

Year 1 requires disciplined attribution.

Direct Ayanokōji memory and self-report establish deprivation, training, victory-centered survival logic, and the absence of ordinary life.

Sakayanagi and Chairman Sakayanagi provide informed corroboration of the artificial-genius project and Ayanokōji's exceptional status.

Atsuomi provides self-interested testimony.

Tsukishiro provides adversarial testimony whose detailed claims cannot be promoted automatically into fact because Ayanokōji explicitly recognizes his capacity to make fabrication persuasive.

*First File* provides official but bounded framing.

Later material may answer the open questions. It cannot be imported backward into Year 1.

## 20.2 Chairman Sakayanagi: bounded refuge

Chairman Sakayanagi admits Ayanokōji irregularly and promises to protect his continued enrollment unless Ayanokōji chooses to leave. He does not offer immunity from legitimate school rules (`Y1V07-E030–E032`).

This is a comparatively autonomy-respecting form of protection. It preserves refuge without claiming personal ownership.

## 20.3 Chabashira: institutional knowledge converted into leverage

Chabashira uses a fabricated paternal threat to force Ayanokōji into class advancement. Her knowledge of his vulnerability becomes coercive power. She reproduces White Room grammar on a smaller scale: continued freedom becomes conditional upon serving an adult's unresolved ambition.

Her later growth does not erase the abuse of authority.

## 20.4 Mashima: institutional conscience

Mashima provides the strongest adult countermodel. He distinguishes normal examination risk from deliberately manufactured serious harm. He overrides a random judo selection to prevent injury and accepts punishment as the cost of teacher responsibility (`Y1V11.5-E034–E035`).

Legitimate authority here protects the vulnerable person by making the authority-bearer absorb risk.

## 20.5 Student government

Manabu treats office as restraint, continuity, and example. Nagumo treats council influence, points, recognition, and exam design as instruments for institutional transformation.

The student council is not decorative. It can help author school culture and examinations. This makes its legitimacy a constitutional matter, not merely a popularity contest.

## 20.6 Tsukishiro: procedural capture

Tsukishiro represents a qualitatively different threat from a harsh examiner.

He controls surveillance, communication, administration, and the official record. Dummy footage and selective recording make retaliation institutionally self-destructive. The chess intervention demonstrates the capacity to alter what becomes official reality (`Y1V10-E109–E111`, `Y1V11-E136–E145`).

Procedural capture occurs when formal authority is used to make the system's safeguards serve a private objective.

## 20.7 Surveillance is a chain, not a single act

Year 1 distinguishes:

> observation → documentation → interpretation → institutional record → official consequence

A camera can deter without recording. Footage can show injury without showing initiation. Partial evidence can mislead. A watched person can curate behavior. A blind spot can become strategic space. A record can be replaced.

The school is not legitimate merely because cameras exist. Legitimacy depends on how observation becomes consequence and whether that process can be challenged.

## 20.8 Ordinary life as institutional surplus

ANHS authorizes an enclosed environment but does not fully control what life inside it means.

Students turn space into friendship, meals, books, films, attraction, gifts, boredom, rivalry, dissent, and private purpose. This surplus is the strongest difference from the White Room.

The White Room attempts to define every activity by developmental utility. ANHS often does the same through points and examinations, but ordinary life repeatedly escapes conversion.

That escape is not trivial. It is the positive substance of Ayanokōji's freedom.

## 20.9 The White Room inside Ayanokōji

Institutional escape does not end institutional conditioning.

Ayanokōji carries White Room grammar whenever he authors environments, controls information, selects pressure, treats people as functions, or assumes jurisdiction because his model is more accurate.

His self-chosen purposes are freer than Atsuomi's assigned purpose. They are not automatically more ethical.

The institutional problem becomes personal:

> Can a person leave a total institution without reproducing totalizing habits inside relationships?

Year 1's answer is incomplete. It shows both repetition and resistance.

# 21. Ethics: when does help become rule over another person?

Year 1 repeatedly produces ethically difficult cases because coercive interventions often work.

Ayanokōji preserves Sudō's enrollment. Horikita becomes more interdependent. Kei becomes capable of refusing betrayal and later enters a reciprocal romance. Hirata returns to class function. Ichinose confronts shame. Ryūen's fear monopoly is broken. The class becomes stronger.

If outcome were the only moral criterion, strategic brilliance would settle the question.

The series provides too much contrary evidence for that conclusion.

Beneficial outcomes do not establish rightful authority over the method.

A responsible intervention audit must ask at least fifteen separate questions:

1. **Who authored the goal?**
2. **How strong was the intervener's knowledge?**
3. **Was the danger imminent?**
4. **Was the method necessary?**
5. **Was it proportionate?**
6. **Were less coercive alternatives available?**
7. **Was consent informed?**
8. **Was refusal practically survivable?**
9. **Did the intervener possess legitimate authority?**
10. **Who bore the risk?**
11. **Did the act create dependency?**
12. **Could the decision be questioned or reviewed?**
13. **Was repair possible?**
14. **Were third parties harmed?**
15. **Did the resulting person gain greater ability to answer back?**

These questions prevent several distinctions from collapsing:

> explanation is not justification;
>
> effectiveness is not legitimacy;
>
> sincere choice is not always informed autonomy;
>
> protection is not ownership;
>
> dependency is not love;
>
> forgiveness is not innocence;
>
> development is not retroactive consent;
>
> rule compliance is not justice;
>
> survival value is not human worth;
>
> recognition is not jurisdiction.

## 21.1 A spectrum of intervention

Ayanokōji's actions occupy a wide ethical range.

Buying one examination point for Sudō is comparatively legitimate. The danger is clear, the intervention is proportionate, the goal—continued enrollment—is compatible with Sudō's interest, and the act does not require manufacturing the crisis.

With Ike on the island, Ayanokōji preserves discovery rather than taking credit. The method increases independent confidence.

With Sakura, he initially protects refusal and urges self-authored testimony. The method becomes less legitimate when he later trades her email address without consent.

His island treatment of Horikita imposes pressure and risk for developmental effect. The result is significant. The subject does not meaningfully choose the lesson.

His sports-festival instruction alternates between low-coercion demonstration and willingness to permit suffering because he predicts future strength.

His Kei intervention in Volume 4 is among the least legitimate acts: danger is partly authored, alternatives are discredited, bodily and sexualized threat is used, refusal is compromised, and dependency is a design objective.

The rooftop fight itself is justified defense. The delayed-rescue architecture is not justified merely because Kei later becomes stronger.

The rumor strategy protects Ichinose and harms uninvolved people. The Hirata intervention restores function through extreme blame. The Class Vote operations preserve some futures by contributing to another's expulsion.

The range matters because it defeats the claim that Ayanokōji simply lacks access to noncoercive action. He can scaffold, ask, wait, preserve privacy, or activate oversight. His more coercive choices remain choices.

## 21.2 Protection and ownership

Protection is a genuine moral good in Year 1.

Chairman Sakayanagi protects refuge. Mashima protects bodily safety. Hirata's false relationship protects Kei's social position. Ayanokōji protects students from expulsion, violence, exposure, and reputational destruction.

Protection becomes more legitimate when it:

- responds to danger the protector did not manufacture;
- expands future options;
- distributes support rather than monopolizing it;
- communicates material conditions honestly;
- permits refusal;
- and aims to make exclusive protection less necessary.

It becomes proprietary when the protector:

- creates or preserves danger;
- discredits every alternative;
- conceals costs;
- demands loyalty as the price of safety;
- or needs the protected person to remain dependent.

Atsuomi's property claim makes the fallacy explicit. Producing capacity, safety, or opportunity does not create ownership of the person who benefits.

## 21.3 Violence

Year 1 distinguishes several forms of violence.

**Defensive force** responds to immediate threat and aims to terminate it.

**Dominative violence** uses pain to establish hierarchy or break agency.

**Disciplinary violence** claims educational purpose.

**Sexualized coercion** uses bodily vulnerability and threatened violation.

**Institutional violence** exposes people to danger or expulsion through rules.

**Reputational violence** converts social information into identity foreclosure.

**Protection-withdrawal violence** makes safety conditional upon submission.

Ayanokōji's rooftop force belongs primarily to defense. Ryūen's treatment of Kei belongs to domination and torture. Manabu's early violence against Suzune remains abuse despite later recognition. Ayanokōji's Volume 4 coercion remains sexualized abuse despite later romance. The Class Vote represents institutionalized disposability.

The absence of anger or sadistic pleasure can reduce one dimension of culpability. It does not make violence ethically neutral. Procedural violence may be more controlled and more totalizing precisely because emotion does not interrupt it.

## 21.4 Expulsion

A defensible expulsion process would require:

- sufficiently serious and specific conduct;
- knowable rules;
- reliable evidence;
- opportunity to answer;
- correction or appeal;
- consideration of lesser sanctions;
- proportionality;
- and humility about developmental uncertainty.

The Class Vote fails at its foundation because the institution wants an expulsion before identifying conduct that warrants it.

Horikita improves accountability by making reasons public. Ichinose refuses false certainty through lottery. Hirata exposes the moral injury. Sakayanagi demonstrates sovereign pruning. None removes the school's authorship of the victim-producing demand.

## 21.5 The answer-back test

The strongest positive ethic in Year 1 is the answer-back test.

Development is healthier when the subject becomes more capable of:

- naming independent goals;
- refusing the helper;
- correcting the diagnosis;
- forming alternative relationships;
- demanding reasons;
- surviving disagreement;
- and exceeding the future imagined for her.

Sudō's ability to disagree with Horikita is evidence of healthy development.

Kei's rooftop refusal and later demands are evidence that the relationship has become more reciprocal.

Horikita's divergence from Ayanokōji is evidence that the scaffold did not fully determine the result.

Hiyori's correction of Ryūen and Keisei's promised dissent are political versions of the same principle.

The Year 1 ethical conclusion is therefore demanding rather than comforting:

> **Coercion can produce authentic growth. The growth does not belong morally to the person who engineered it. Legitimate help must leave the helped person more capable of contradicting, leaving, correcting, and exceeding the helper.**

# 22. Truth, proof, reputation, surveillance, and the authored record

Year 1 repeatedly distinguishes what happened from what can be proven and from what the institution records.

The Sudō hearing establishes the basic architecture. Event truth does not automatically produce official judgment. Evidence must qualify, carry sufficient probative force, and survive credibility contests.

Sakura’s photograph, real or threatened, changes behavior because actors believe it may exist. Surveillance operates through anticipation as much as through an archive.

The island exam makes leader identity an information-security problem. The zodiac exam turns identity inference and betrayal into formal strategy. Kushida converts confidential truth into leverage. Ryūen uses real injury and resentment to make a fabricated account experientially convincing—`嘘を真実に変えた`, turning a lie into truth (`JY1-037`).

Volume 9 shows the reverse: a true event can become false as a total identity. Ichinose’s theft is not fabricated. The reputational structure lies by treating the event as exhaustive.

Rumor therefore functions as an unofficial distributed rating system. It decides what a person’s acts will mean before any formal adjudication occurs.

The school’s response to Ayanokōji’s rumor saturation reveals that information governance is driven partly by institutional cost. Administrators intervene when reputational disorder threatens system legitimacy, not simply when truth deserves protection.

The chess interference completes the progression. Tsukishiro does not merely misinterpret evidence. He alters the communication entering the official result. This is why record power is more fundamental than reputation alone.

A useful sequence is:

> **event → witness → evidence → credibility → interpretation → record → consequence**

Different actors may control different transitions.

Ayanokōji often controls what becomes knowable.

Class leaders control how information circulates.

The school controls admissibility and consequence.

Tsukishiro demonstrates that administration may control whether the event survives into the record at all.

The ethical demand is not total transparency. Privacy matters. A person need not surrender every fact in order to be recognized as legitimate. Ayanokōji’s concealment, Sakura’s plural presentation, Kei’s trauma, and Ichinose’s shame all show why visibility can injure.

The demand is **bounded, trustworthy conversion**. When an institution uses a record to impose consequences, the record must preserve what it claims to represent and remain open to correction.

The same principle governs reading. First-person access is not the event itself. Illustration is not motive. Editorial summary is not scene. A useful interpretation must keep the layers distinct.

# 23. Ordinary life as counter-curriculum

The White Room plot can make ordinary school scenes appear secondary: pauses between examinations, romance, and conspiracy.

Year 1’s formal structure argues the opposite.

The White Room’s deepest deprivation is not simply harsh training. It is the exclusion of experiences whose meaning is not assigned in advance by developmental utility.

ANHS provides meals, books, films, gifts, dates, nicknames, seasonal weather, clothes, boredom, friendship, private conversation, leisure, and embarrassment. These experiences sometimes produce strategic benefits. Reducing them to those benefits would repeat the instrumental logic they challenge.

Counter-curriculum names the space in which a person can discover ends that are not merely means.

The .5 volumes are its principal formal carriers.

Volume 4.5 gives Ayanokōji friendship, food, privacy choices, and enjoyment without a major examination objective. Volume 7.5 tests ordinary intimacy after the rooftop. Volume 11.5 gives graduation, legacy, hair, confession, and the transition from a year survived to a life chosen.

The ordinary is not free from power. Points buy gifts. surveillance remains possible. class status structures interaction. romance carries hidden curricula. Yet the meaning of enjoying ice cream, walking with a friend, choosing a haircut, reading quietly, or wanting to see someone is not fully determined by the institution.

Ayanokōji’s sensory and relational preferences become autobiographical substance. He is no longer blank merely because he lacks a conventional childhood. `学生らしい生活` becomes a positive project, not just an alibi for avoiding Atsuomi (`JY1-273`).

Ordinary life also distributes authorship. A date can go awkwardly. A friend can refuse. A group conversation can matter without producing a strategic solution. A person may become valuable through shared time rather than crisis or performance.

This is why ordinary scenes are politically significant.

They create reasons to resist total institutions.

A person who has tasted ends outside assigned utility has something to lose when another authority claims the future is already written.

The counter-curriculum does not automatically make Ayanokōji ethical. It gives ethical correction somewhere to come from.

# 24. Narration, voice, humor, genre, and the body

Year 1's formal design is part of its argument about knowledge.

The reader receives privileged access to Ayanokōji and still does not receive a complete person.

## 24.1 Selective first-person incompleteness

Ayanokōji's `オレ` narration is not globally false. If every ordinary thought were a lie, the text would become analytically empty. The more precise model is selective incompleteness.

He is usually reliable about:

- immediate events;
- institutional mechanics;
- incentives;
- physical observation;
- and the strategic possibilities he chooses to disclose.

He is less complete about:

- advance planning;
- the scale of his competence;
- his responsibility for conditions;
- emotional investment;
- possessiveness;
- and motives that contain ordinary and strategic components at once.

The narrative makes the reader inhabit an authored information environment similar to the one Ayanokōji constructs for classmates. We know far more than they do and remain dependent on a narrator who controls causal revelation.

Alternate viewpoints, outcomes, illustrations, and *First File* limit his narratorial sovereignty.

## 24.2 Character register as political evidence

Horikita's formal, complete, judgment-oriented language changes not by becoming casual but by admitting gratitude, request, uncertainty, and revision. Development occurs within register.

Kei's `あたし`, directness, gyaru-coded rhythm, and social fluency remain competencies after trauma becomes visible. Recovery does not require linguistic purification.

Kushida's cushioning public language is both performance and genuine social skill. Her private contempt does not make every public act unreal. The two registers belong to one person organizing recognition differently.

Hirata's gentle `僕` and inclusive requests create safety while protecting him from particular choice. His Class Vote breakdown is a register collapse.

Ryūen's rough language makes domination audible without implying intellectual simplicity.

Sakayanagi's refined politeness intensifies hierarchy and predation because aggression appears as sovereign composure.

Hiyori's restraint, Kōenji's theatrical self-mythology, Manabu's office-bearing speech, Nagumo's casual hierarchy, and Tsukishiro's administrative menace each make political method audible before the full strategy is visible.

## 24.3 Genre changes what counts as evidence

Year 1 moves among school comedy, procedural mystery, survival fiction, psychological thriller, sports drama, political fiction, romance, family conspiracy, and graduation narrative.

Each genre trains attention differently.

The examination puzzle foregrounds rules and inference.

The thriller makes concealed authorship frightening.

Sports drama makes embodied capacity public.

Romance exposes strategic grammar inside intimacy.

Institutional fiction asks who authors rules and records.

The `.5` volumes formalize decompression. They allow food, clothes, gifts, films, bodily inconvenience, dates, and seasonal time to become evidence of personhood rather than filler.

## 24.4 Humor

Humor establishes that Ayanokōji's interior life is not exhausted by mastery.

He misreads gift conventions, worries about social ranking, fails at ordinary conversation, experiences bodily embarrassment, and cannot use strategic intelligence to solve every adolescent situation. Ensemble comedy makes the class feel like a social world rather than a game board.

Comic framing is not exoneration.

A scene can be funny and coercive. Fanservice can humanize bodily awkwardness and organize a troubling gaze. The pool material demonstrates that the work may condemn voyeurism while commercially reproducing it.

## 24.5 Bodies as records

Kei's scar records trauma beyond her social role.

Sakayanagi's cane separates physical limitation from political capacity.

Ayanokōji's trained body contradicts authored mediocrity.

Horikita's hair records imitation and later integrated authorship.

Sudō's body moves from liability to expertise.

Sakura's glasses, posture, and Shizuku presentation mediate visibility.

Hirata's tears make role collapse bodily visible.

Illustrations do not transparently reveal motive. They establish composition, distance, posture, injury, clothing, and editorial emphasis. The final Ayanokōji–Kei embrace strengthens romantic presentation while the prose preserves uncertainty. The body and narration remain in counterpoint.

## 24.6 Dossier aesthetics

*First File* completes the formal system by inviting the reader to become evaluator.

Profiles, ratings, timelines, and OAA simulations make people sortable. Bonus fiction then restores purposeless conversation and unnamed desire.

The formal lesson matches the ethical one:

> Voice can disclose without exhausting.
>
> A body can record without explaining itself.
>
> An illustration can emphasize without settling.
>
> A file can measure without owning.

The reader must use every form of evidence without allowing any form to become the whole person.

# 25. Retrospective revision — understanding must remain corrigible

The first-pass Year 1 analysis was not broadly overturned. Its strongest proposition—development versus control—survives.

The second pass makes the mechanism more precise.

Development versus control becomes **developmental authorship**.

Developmental authorship becomes **reciprocal authorship** when the developed person’s answer can alter the relationship and the developer.

*First File* adds the epistemic warning: every record remains smaller than the person.

The revision vocabulary preserves this history.

`PRESERVE` marks claims that survive materially intact.

`STRENGTHEN` marks claims made more explicit by later Year 1 evidence.

`REVISE` retains part of an earlier insight while changing its explanatory model.

`DOWNGRADE` leaves a proposition possible but removes default authority.

`REJECT` records contradiction or evidentiary overreach.

`OPEN` preserves genuine uncertainty.

Several major transitions define the mature corpus.

Ayanokōji’s ordinary desire is preserved and strengthened. “Humanization” is revised because emotion was not absent and moral reform remains incomplete.

Horikita’s leadership arc is preserved. “Puppet” is downgraded because she generates independent judgments, while the shadow constitution remains real.

Kei’s dependency history is preserved. “Only a tool” and “completed romantic humanization” are rejected as total accounts.

Ichinose’s solidarity is strengthened; “kindness as the defect” is revised into adversarial-security and result-conversion problems.

Ryūen’s voluntary legitimacy is strengthened; moral reform is rejected.

ANHS/White Room structural comparison is preserved; simple equivalence is downgraded.

The hypothetical OAA is preserved as bounded retrospective modeling; its use as secret Year 1 omniscience is rejected.

The corpus itself must obey the same rule it derives from the fiction.

A mature interpretation is not the model that claims to possess every character. It is the model that records its authority, preserves counterevidence, and knows which conclusions remain open.

# 26. Strong counterarguments and critical limits

A mature synthesis should survive the strongest evidence against its preferred interpretation. Year 1 supports the developmental-authorship thesis, but several counterarguments deserve serious treatment.

## 26.1 The series may be a strategic school thriller, not a theory of personhood

Many structures analyzed here are genre mechanics. Special examinations create suspense. Hidden competence produces power-fantasy pleasure. Romance and fanservice serve commercial expectations. The language of class constitutions, jurisdiction, and developmental authorship may appear to impose political philosophy on a work primarily designed around games and reveals.

This objection is strongest when analysis detaches itself from textual recurrence.

The response is not that every rule or flirtation carries a hidden philosophical message. It is that the same problems recur across independent layers: institutional design, Ayanokōji’s interventions, Horikita’s pedagogy, Kei’s dependency, class politics, expulsion, White Room ownership, record corruption, Japanese metaphors, and *First File* dossier form. The pattern is not produced by one isolated scene.

Genre pleasure and serious structure can coexist. The work can invite admiration of effective manipulation while also supplying evidence of its human cost. Indeed, the tension between those responses is part of its form.

The limitation remains. A synthesis should not transform every clever strategy into a moral allegory or treat every recurrence as proof of authorial philosophical system. The claims here are about the architecture supported by Year 1, not about private authorial intent.

## 26.2 Ayanokōji’s methods may be necessary in an extreme environment

ANHS is adversarial. Students face expulsion, violence, blackmail, hostile classes, and adult interference. Ayanokōji often possesses information others lack and acts under time pressure. His interventions save futures that ordinary transparency might destroy.

The consequential defense is substantial.

Kei survives. Horikita develops. Sudō remains enrolled. Ichinose recovers. Hirata returns. Ryūen’s regime changes. The class avoids repeated defeat.

The epistemic defense is also strong. Ayanokōji is frequently more accurate than the people he manipulates. He understands risks and capacities they cannot yet see.

Neither defense creates unlimited authority.

Year 1 itself provides lower-coercion examples. He can scaffold, preserve refusal, buy time, protect privacy, teach directly, or let another person discover the answer. The availability of these modes makes proportionality a real question.

More importantly, an extreme environment may explain secrecy without justifying manufactured danger. The strongest objection concerns cases where Ayanokōji controls the threat, the information, the rescue, and the interpretation at once. Success under those conditions is partly evidence of his authorship, not independent consent to it.

## 26.3 Reciprocity privileges liberal autonomy over loyalty, duty, and collective life

The answer-back standard emphasizes refusal, informed choice, contestation, and independent authorship. This could be criticized as an individualist framework insufficiently attentive to Japanese social obligations, class solidarity, senpai-kōhai relations, loyalty, or the value of dependence.

The objection prevents a simplistic equation of freedom with isolation.

Year 1 does not treat all dependency as pathology. People need teachers, friends, leaders, protectors, and institutions. Ichinose’s polity derives strength from collective obligation. Horikita’s development requires interdependence. Manabu’s legacy matters. A class cannot function if every member recognizes only private preference.

Reciprocity, as used here, is therefore not independence from others. It is answerable interdependence. The test is whether loyalty can include correction, whether duty can be contested, whether protection expands rather than monopolizes agency, and whether collective membership preserves the person’s status as more than material.

The framework remains a normative interpretation, not a culturally neutral fact. Its legitimacy comes from the novels’ own recurring attention to choice, refusal, self-authorship, coercion, and ownership—not from the claim that all relational values reduce to liberal individualism.

## 26.4 Horikita may still be Ayanokōji’s puppet

Ayanokōji controls decisive victories, hides information, creates developmental environments, and protects the class from threats Horikita does not understand. Her public leadership may be a surface produced by his shadow constitution.

This is partly correct. The synthesis does not declare full sovereignty.

The stronger puppet thesis fails because Horikita generates strategies he did not specify, rejects solutions he prefers, chooses the Kushida project, demands an auditable wager, accepts public liability, and integrates Manabu’s influence on her own terms. A puppet does not meaningfully create disagreement for the hidden author to manage.

The accurate Year 1 state is asymmetric co-constitution: real Horikita authorship inside an environment still protected and distorted by Ayanokōji’s hidden power.

## 26.5 Kei is either instrumental or romantic; combining both evades a decision

One reading treats every tender scene as part of Ayanokōji’s experiment. Another treats the confession and *First File* desire as proof that the earlier tool language has been superseded.

Both seek a cleaner ontology than Year 1 provides.

Instrumentality and attachment are not mutually exclusive psychological states. Ayanokōji can use a person’s capacity and become attached to her. Kei can begin in manufactured dependency and later choose the relationship. The founding coercion remains morally real; the later romance remains emotionally real.

The synthesis does make a decision: neither reality cancels the other. The unresolved question is whether the relationship’s future structure will become sufficiently informed and answerable to transform the asymmetry rather than merely deepen it.

## 26.6 Ryūen’s voluntary followers prove authoritarian leadership works

Ryūen’s class gains speed, adaptability, and offensive coherence. Followers preserve him after fear fails. This may seem like evidence that coercive leadership, when competent, produces durable loyalty.

It proves sociological durability, not complete legitimacy.

Followers may value decisiveness, protection, shared struggle, or the leader’s capacity to win. Their voluntary loyalty is important evidence that Ryūen’s fear theory is incomplete. It does not create a mechanism through which they can constrain him.

The key Year 2 test is whether loyalty becomes institutional correction or remains personal reinforcement.

## 26.7 Ichinose’s losses show that trust-based politics is naïve

Ichinose’s class can be exploited. It struggles to convert cohesion into points. Its leader becomes vulnerable to reputation attack and personal leverage.

These are real defects.

The reductive conclusion ignores what trust produces: pooled resources, information sharing, high membership retention, cross-class legitimacy, and survival of public truth. A coercive polity may win an examination and lack these capacities.

The relevant question is adaptation. Can solidarity acquire adversarial competence without destroying the norms that make it valuable?

## 26.8 ANHS is obviously unlike the White Room

ANHS permits friendships, leisure, romance, varied pedagogy, and student politics. Equating it with the White Room risks trivializing a totalizing childhood institution.

The objection is correct against simple equivalence.

The comparison remains useful at the level of authored environments, measurement, scarcity, developmental purpose, and removal. ANHS differs because authority is plural and contestable, ordinary life exceeds curriculum, and no single actor normally controls the whole person.

The mature claim is structural relation without equivalence.

## 26.9 The eleven-layer `実力` taxonomy is analyst-created

The novels do not present a numbered eleven-stage theory. The categories are an analytical model.

That does not make them arbitrary. Each distinction solves a recurring textual problem: Ayanokōji’s concealment, Ike’s ecological value, Kōenji’s unavailable talent, Horikita’s distributed leadership, Tsukishiro’s record corruption, and *First File*’s bounded ratings cannot be explained cleanly if possession, expression, use, and record are treated as one thing.

The taxonomy should remain corrigible. It is a retrieval and comparison tool, not a hidden doctrine the fiction must have consciously encoded.

## 26.10 *First File* is official and may deserve more authority than this synthesis allows

Official ratings and summaries may be intended to clarify character truth. Treating them as merely bounded could appear evasive when they conflict with a preferred literary reading.

The response is source-type discipline.

Documentary data is authoritative for the official record. Ratings are authoritative for the measurement produced. Editorial prose is authoritative for official retrospective framing. Bonus fiction is authoritative narrative evidence. None of these functions is identical to omniscient access to total capacity or motive.

The guidebook itself demonstrates the distinction by pairing Ayanokōji’s low visible rating with acknowledgment of deliberate performance control and by ending dossier form with interior fiction.

## 26.11 Ayanokōji’s ambiguity may be an all-purpose escape from falsification

Whenever he appears caring, one can say care is real. Whenever he appears instrumental, one can say conditioning is real. Whenever motives conflict, one can invoke layered selfhood or selective incompleteness. The model risks explaining everything and therefore nothing.

This is a serious methodological danger.

The answer is to require discriminating predictions. The layered model predicts that observable mechanisms will be reported more reliably than advance planning, that care will often appear behaviorally before explicit naming, that practical protection may coexist with weak informed reciprocity, and that ordinary desire will persist in low-strategy contexts. It also permits rejection: “emotionless” and “fully humanized” are both ruled out because they fail across evidence clusters.

The model should be revised if later evidence shows that ordinary interiority was systematically fabricated, that instrumentality disappears, or that reciprocal disclosure becomes stable. At the Year 1 boundary, it remains the best explanation with explicit limits.

## 26.12 Formal critique: the series sometimes weakens its own ethics

Year 1’s critical force is not perfectly consistent.

Fanservice can eroticize vulnerable bodies and privacy violations. Gender generalizations sometimes exceed local evidence. Ayanokōji’s mastery can compress the ensemble into extensions of his plan. Polite speech can aestheticize domination. Humor may discharge discomfort before consequences receive full weight. Repeated retroactive reveals can turn ambiguity into a promise that the hidden mastermind already knew everything.

These are not external objections to be ignored. They are formal tensions inside the work.

The strongest reading preserves them. *Classroom of the Elite* is often most interesting when its pleasures and criticisms do not align cleanly: when manipulation is thrilling and ethically disturbing, when a romantic image intensifies a bond whose information asymmetry remains unresolved, or when a dossier invites classification while bonus fiction exceeds it.

The synthesis should not make the work more morally coherent than it is.

# 27. Year 1 conclusion: answerability as constrained freedom

Year 1 begins with students believing they have entered a space of freedom.

The school reveals that freedom was already measured.

The remaining volumes progressively enlarge the question of who controls the conversion from life into judgment.

Volume 1 shows hidden criteria and authored visibility.

Volume 2 shows truth dependent upon legibility, credibility, and proof.

Volume 3 makes ability ecological and turns environmental design into developmental power.

Volume 4 makes trust capital and protection a possible form of jurisdiction.

Volume 4.5 establishes ordinary life as counter-curriculum.

Volumes 5 and 6 separate individual excellence from relational coordination and distributed capacity.

Volume 7 makes ownership explicit and places justified defense inside an unjustly authored ordeal.

Volume 7.5 distinguishes indispensability from irreplaceability and moves dependency toward negotiation.

Volume 8 reveals rule authorship above ordinary strategy.

Volume 9 shows that true facts can become reputational prisons.

Volume 10 requires students to manufacture a disposable person.

Volume 11 shows that ability and coordination remain vulnerable to the authority that controls the record.

Volume 11.5 turns self-authorship toward legacy and reciprocal authorship.

*First File* demonstrates that every archive remains smaller than the person.

The year never concludes that ability is unreal.

It concludes that ability cannot be ethically or politically understood apart from the systems that develop, expose, coordinate, reward, record, and model it.

The strongest individual embodiment of that argument is Ayanokōji.

He is exceptionally capable of seeing how people are made. That capacity allows him to preserve futures, recognize hidden abilities, build coalitions, protect vulnerable people, and create development. It also allows him to manufacture dependency, intensify crisis, allocate survival, and treat understanding as a form of private sovereignty.

His ordinary desires are real. His strategic conditioning is real. His Year 1 freedom is real and incomplete.

Self-authorship becomes ethically meaningful only when it ceases to imply authorship over everyone nearby.

Horikita supplies the principal political counter-movement.

She begins by treating present weakness as disposability and ends by developing a distributed, correctable model of leadership. Her independence is not purity from influence. It is the ability to own, revise, and publicly defend judgments shaped by others. The class's future depends on whether this visible government can survive without invisible correction.

Kei supplies the principal relational test.

Her bond with Ayanokōji moves through coercion, refusal, negotiation, and reciprocal confession. Affection becomes genuine without making the origin innocent. Irreplaceability remains a hope rather than a completed fact.

Ichinose demonstrates that solidarity is real political capacity, not naïve kindness, while exposing the need for adversarial resilience.

Ryūen demonstrates that sociological legitimacy can increase without moral reform.

Sakayanagi demonstrates that superior judgment can become fiduciary danger.

Kōenji demonstrates that possession does not create collective availability.

Nagumo demonstrates that individual mobility can become patronage.

Hirata demonstrates that universal care can be both virtue and defense.

Hiyori demonstrates loyalty through correction.

Sudō demonstrates that current institutional weakness does not fix developmental ceiling.

The school itself remains divided.

ANHS creates genuine opportunities for plural development and ordinary life. It also surveils, prices, ranks, coerces, and removes. Its difference from the White Room is not innocence. It is the presence of competing authorities, unassigned meanings, dissent, relationship, and possible oversight.

Tsukishiro demonstrates how fragile those differences can become when procedure is captured.

The final common concept is **answerability**.

A leader is answerable when dissent can correct leadership.

A relationship is answerable when affection does not eliminate refusal.

A measurement is answerable when its object remains bounded and revisable.

A developmental intervention is answerable when success does not erase consent, necessity, and risk.

An institution is answerable when the authorities who create scarcity, surveillance, and records can themselves be challenged.

A psychological model is answerable when the person modeled retains the right to exceed it.

Freedom in Year 1 is therefore not the absence of influence or constraint.

No student is unshaped. No relationship is perfectly equal. No institution is neutral.

Freedom is constrained but meaningful when influence does not become title, when protection does not become ownership, when development does not foreclose refusal, and when the official file does not become the person.

The locked Year 1 conclusion is:

> **A person becomes freer not merely by choosing a purpose, but by entering relationships and institutions in which purposes remain contestable, other people can answer back, and no actor—however capable—possesses final jurisdiction over what another human being is allowed to become.**
