# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ』
## Year 1, Volume 11 — Second-Pass Deep Reading
### Developmental authorship, distributed ability, and the capture of the record

# 0. Scope, source, and provenance

This artifact is the canonical Japanese-primary second-pass reading of Year 1 Volume 11. It inherits the reconciled state through `Y1V10`, but it does not import any answer from Volume 11.5, *First File*, Year 2, Volume 0, *Second List*, Year 3, adaptations, wikis, or retrospective franchise knowledge.

The verified EPUB contains **26 spine items**, **4,490 normalized substantive paragraphs**, **147,601 normalized Japanese characters**, and **24 image resources**. Its source SHA-256 is `a067284074b9209c4712b41d05ce01da17c971bc6d7993afb07cd548524a0a9f`; the deterministic normalized-text SHA-256 is `bccbf87237aae9e75882de75209c4251ee971888a4e44f578993bf3f26326c4d`.

The prose was read completely in spine order before the preserved historical Year 1 transcript was consulted. The illustration layer was then inspected separately. A provenance limitation matters here: the exported historical transcript currently available does **not** preserve the original Volume 11 deep-reading body. It preserves earlier Year 1 hypotheses—especially about Sakayanagi, the White Room, Horikita's independence, and Ayanokōji's desire for a genuine defeat—but not a recoverable one-to-one V11 first pass. The revision audit therefore compares the fresh reading only against what the historical record actually contains; it does not reconstruct a missing analysis from memory.

At this local endpoint, the following remain unresolved and must remain unresolved:

- whether Tsukishiro's administrative capture can be resisted without the school itself becoming another battlefield of falsified records;
- whether Ayanokōji's explicit desire to change Horikita can become compatible with her independent authorship;
- whether Hirata's recovery survives beyond the immediate crisis;
- whether Ryūen's return changes the political basis of his authority;
- whether Ichinose can build adversarial resilience without abandoning solidarity;
- whether Sakayanagi's revised judgment of Ayanokōji settles anything beyond her own theory of him;
- and what Manabu's impending graduation will do to Suzune's still-changing self-conception.

# 1. Executive thesis

Volume 11 is the point at which Year 1's long argument about **human development versus human control** becomes explicit at three different scales.

At the level of the **class**, the Event Selection Exam asks whether heterogeneous students can be converted into collective power. Horikita's most important achievement is not becoming the smartest participant. It is learning to identify, train, allocate, and trust capacities she does not personally possess. Sudō's basketball leadership, Akito's archery, Professor's typing, Horikita's rapidly acquired chess competence, and even the problem of Kōenji all force the class away from a scalar model of merit toward a distributed one.

At the level of the **individual developer**, Ayanokōji crosses a major threshold. Earlier volumes often allowed him to treat other people's growth as something strategically convenient or intellectually interesting. Here, after discussing Suzune with Manabu, he corrects his own thought from what he *should* do at school to what he *wants* to do:

> `いや、やりたいこと。それがふと見えた気がした。`

He then says:

> `あいつを変えてみようと思う。今までのように何となくではなく、本気で`

Developmental authorship becomes a **personally chosen purpose**.

That is genuine self-authorship for Ayanokōji. Nobody in the White Room assigned him this project. Atsuomi did not command him to cultivate Horikita. Yet the fact that the purpose is self-chosen does not make the method ethically legitimate. The Hirata episode makes that danger impossible to ignore. Ayanokōji knowingly applies false and extreme blame until Hirata breaks because the class needs `平田というパーツ`. He obtains a productive outcome through a method the narration itself refuses to call benevolent.

At the level of the **institution**, Tsukishiro reveals the limit of all student competition. Ayanokōji can make the stronger chess decision; Sakayanagi can recognize the inconsistency; Horikita can grow enough to create the favorable board; none of that controls the official result if the administrator who owns the communication infrastructure can replace the command and author the record.

The volume's central proposition is therefore:

> **Ability becomes socially real only when an environment permits it to be developed, coordinated, displayed, and honestly recorded. Volume 11 tests all four—and shows that institutional sovereignty can corrupt the fourth.**

This reframes `実力 / jitsuryoku`. Ability is not merely what a person possesses. It depends upon:

1. what can be developed;
2. what can be made socially usable;
3. what can be coordinated with unlike people;
4. what can be expressed under the current rules;
5. and whether the institution records the expression truthfully.

The result is one of Year 1's strongest anti-scalar arguments. The novel still delights in exceptional individuals, but the final examination repeatedly demonstrates that a person who is supreme in one dimension may still be politically unavailable, socially unintegrated, institutionally misrecorded, or dependent upon an environment someone else controls.

# 2. Position in the Year 1 architecture

Volume 10 asked who has the authority to decide that another student should lose the right to continue. Volume 11 asks a related but distinct question:

> **Once students remain, what makes their abilities collectively real?**

The movement is important.

The Class Vote produced forced scarcity and political triage. The Event Selection Exam returns to competition, but it does not restore a clean meritocracy. Instead, it reveals that class performance depends upon **institutional design, leadership, participation rules, interpersonal trust, preparation, consent, and record integrity**.

Several established threads change state:

### `JITSURYOKU`

Volume 11 gives perhaps the cleanest Year 1 demonstration that possessed ability is not identical to usable ability. Kōenji can solve the hardest arithmetic item while deliberately making himself unusable to the class. Ayanokōji can be superior at chess while admitting he is weak at class-wide information gathering and ordinary social knowledge. Horikita can begin chess as a novice and become useful within a week because motivation, teaching, aptitude, and a concrete social role align.

### `HORIKITA_LEADERSHIP`

Horikita now performs the work Ayanokōji explicitly says he cannot do well: detailed class mapping, persuasion, allocation, and distributed preparation. Her value is no longer merely that she can publicly implement a hidden answer. She is becoming a generator of the class's operating system.

### `AYANOKOJI_AUTHORSHIP`

His developmental role becomes conscious desire. This is simultaneously one of his largest signs of self-authorship and one of the clearest reasons his freedom cannot yet be equated with other people's freedom.

### `HIRATA_CARE_LEADERSHIP`

Hirata's Volume 10 collapse receives its full psychological architecture. His current kindness is neither simple naivety nor effortless virtue. It is an attempt to escape both passive bystanderhood and the authoritarian regime he once imposed on an earlier class.

### `RYUEN_FEAR_LEGITIMACY`

Ryūen returns not merely because followers summon him, but because the examination becomes a test of his own remaining desire. He risks expulsion to learn whether defeat by Ayanokōji still matters enough to make him fight.

### `TRUTH_PROOF_RECORD`

The series advances from disputes over evidence and reputation to direct **record authorship**. Tsukishiro does not merely create a persuasive interpretation of the truth. He substitutes the actionable institutional record.

# 3. Narrative architecture

Volume 11 is unusually symmetrical. The prologue begins with Sakayanagi observing a boy inside an artificial educational environment; the climax places that same pair across a chess board inside another controlled educational environment. Between those points, the novel asks who is entitled to define what development means.

The ten substantive movements are:

1. **`坂柳有栖の独白`** — child Sakayanagi sees the White Room and forms a hereditary theory of genius.
2. **`教師たちの戦い`** — Tsukishiro and the teachers debate harsh experimental rules and the expendability of students.
3. **`１年最後の戦い`** — the Event Selection Exam turns class ability into a portfolio-and-command problem.
4. **`対戦相手`** — Ayanokōji covertly arranges the Sakayanagi match and admits his own social-information limitations.
5. **`クラスに欠けているもの`** — Horikita tries to build collective capacity around students who are unlike herself.
6. **`罠と手料理と頼み事と`** — social intimacy, Kushida's tactical détente, Manabu's reflection on Suzune, and Ayanokōji's self-chosen developmental project intersect.
7. **`男の涙`** — Hirata's trauma structure is exposed and Ayanokōji restores his function through deliberate psychological pressure.
8. **`綾小路対坂柳`** — the final exam begins; Ryūen's unexpected return destabilizes the parallel battle.
9. **`Ｂクラス対Ｄクラス`** — Ryūen demonstrates adversarial asymmetry through norm-breaking coercion.
10. **`勝者と敗者のライン`** — Horikita's chess growth, Tsukishiro's falsification, private replay, and the Ayanokōji–Sakayanagi walk separate official victory from actual encounter.

The volume's titleless architecture therefore runs:

> **origin theory → institutional ideology → distributed test → developmental preparation → coercive repair → visible competition → falsified record → private truth → ordinary companionship**

That last movement matters. The book does not end on chess superiority. It ends on two students walking slowly and talking about ordinary things.

# 4. Sakayanagi's White Room memory: genius, heredity, and the child who becomes an argument

## 4.1 The White Room is introduced as an experiment in authorship

Sakayanagi remembers visiting a remote white facility with her father and learning that its experiment is to:

> `人工的に天才を作る`

The wording is crucial. The institution is not merely a difficult school. Its explicit object is to determine how far controlled education can **author exceptional ability**.

The children are exposed to common curricula despite unequal starting capacities. Those who fail disappear from the active cohort. The structure therefore conflates two questions:

- how much ability can environmental control produce;
- and what happens to children who do not fit the experiment's success criteria.

Sakayanagi's father fears not merely the cruelty of one facility but its possible normalization. If the experiment succeeds and becomes ordinary, he calls that possibility `不幸の始まり`—the beginning of misfortune.

That is one of the most important institutional statements in Year 1. The danger is not only abuse. It is the possibility that an effective model of human production becomes socially legitimate because it appears to work.

## 4.2 Sakayanagi's hereditary thesis is character ideology, not scientific truth

As a child, Sakayanagi rejects the project's premise. Her view is that real genius is decided at birth; education can refine but cannot create the decisive difference. She therefore sees Ayanokōji as the test case through which she can prove that an artificially cultivated student is still not equivalent to natural genius.

The second pass must resist a common interpretive mistake here.

The novel gives her belief extraordinary rhetorical clarity. It does **not** thereby make the belief omniscient narration. She is a child constructing a theory from:

- her own identity;
- what adults tell her;
- a one-way observational visit;
- and an ideological investment in natural ability.

Her later revision of Ayanokōji from `偽りの天才` to genuine genius is a revision **inside her model**. It does not settle the general nature–nurture problem.

## 4.3 Chess begins as relational imitation

Sakayanagi watches the boy dominate chess and decides:

> `チェスを覚えてみよう`

This means their final contest is not merely a convenient intellectual showdown. Chess is part of her autobiographical relation to him. She learns the game because she once watched him play.

The eventual match therefore contains an asymmetry of memory:

- she has carried him as an organizing figure for years;
- he did not remember her.

Her rivalry is already relational before it becomes mutual.

## 4.4 Her father supplies the volume's earliest human counterargument

Sakayanagi's father focuses not on the boy's score but on what the institution withheld:

- parents;
- touch;
- ordinary childhood;
- relational warmth.

The prologue explicitly observes that, apart from talent, people receive many things through contact with human skin. That language returns at the end when Sakayanagi takes Ayanokōji's hand and speaks about `人肌のぬくもり`.

The prologue and epilogue therefore form a deliberate human-temperature frame:

> **white sterile observation → hand-to-hand warmth**

The final gesture does not undo the White Room. It gives the novel another vocabulary for what the White Room could not measure.

# 5. Tsukishiro and the teachers: designed exclusion disguised as nature

The teacher chapter is one of the volume's most politically important sections because it prevents the final examination from being read as a neutral contest designed by an impersonal school.

Mashima objects to the increasing severity of the first-year program. His argument is not that failure should have no consequence. He objects to deliberately designing rules that force expulsion when this cohort has survived without one.

Tsukishiro replies that the school is experimental, that the first years are useful precisely because they are not yet fully shaped by existing custom, and that current students may be sacrificed for future system design.

His most revealing sentence is:

> `適合しなかった者は排除される。社会の仕組み───いえ、自然の摂理ですよ。`

The sentence performs ideological work. The institution designs the filter, defines the desired adaptation, and then redescribes the resulting exclusion as **nature**.

This is an important distinction:

> **The outcome may be real without being natural.**

A student can genuinely fail a rule that an institution artificially created. Calling the failure evidence of natural unfitness hides the authorship of the rule.

The White Room and ANHS are not identical institutions. Volume 11 nevertheless places them in conceptual proximity. Both can:

- define successful adaptation;
- treat children as data;
- remove those who fail;
- and justify the process through a theory of future social benefit.

The morally decisive difference remains plurality. ANHS still contains teachers who object, students who create relationships, multiple ways to succeed, and spaces of ordinary life. Yet Tsukishiro's arrival demonstrates that these protections are institutional and therefore vulnerable to institutional capture.

# 6. The Event Selection Exam: ability as a distributed system

## 6.1 The exam refuses one-dimensional merit

The `選抜種目試験` requires each class to prepare ten events, narrow them to five, and then compete in seven randomly selected events drawn from both classes' proposals. The events may test:

- academics;
- sport;
- games;
- typing;
- archery;
- group coordination;
- specialized prior experience;
- or luck.

The participation rules prevent a class from simply routing every event through the same superstar.

This matters because the examination's apparent subject is `総合力 / overall strength`, but overall strength is produced through **distribution** rather than simple averaging.

A class with one extraordinary student and thirty-eight unusable ones may lose to a class in which many different capacities can be mobilized at the correct moment.

## 6.2 The `司令塔 / command tower` separates leadership from personal performance

The commander cannot simply participate in every event. The commander operates through bounded, predeclared interventions:

- substitutions;
- hints;
- limited direct input;
- timing decisions.

Leadership is therefore formalized as **changing the conditions under which someone else performs**.

This is almost a miniature model of Ayanokōji's own role throughout Year 1.

The distinction also creates the central ethical question of the volume:

> When does helping another person perform become legitimate guidance, and when does it become authorship of the person?

Horikita and Ayanokōji offer different answers.

## 6.3 Randomness is not merely unfairness

Horikita initially dislikes the randomness because preparation can be invalidated by the draw. Ayanokōji sees another effect: randomness creates upset pathways that a strictly deterministic merit ranking would remove.

This is important to the series' larger meritocratic critique.

A perfectly legible system may favor the already dominant. Randomness can be:

- noise;
- injustice;
- but also a source of opportunity outside stable hierarchy.

The novel does not simply endorse chance. It makes chance one more environmental variable that capable groups must absorb.

# 7. Ayanokōji arranges the contest—and admits what he cannot do

## 7.1 The Sakayanagi match is not pure lottery

Before the draw, Ayanokōji contacts Ichinose and Ishizaki. Ichinose will not select his class because she owes him; Ryūen's side wants another opponent. Ayanokōji calls the resulting interaction `出来レース`.

He has constructed the conditions that make A–C highly likely.

This is another case of his characteristic method:

> do not falsify the official random procedure; shape the choices around it until the desired random outcome becomes probable.

The exam will later be corrupted by a far more direct administrator. That contrast matters. Ayanokōji manipulates the **choice environment**; Tsukishiro falsifies the **recorded action**.

The two should not be morally equated, but the structural rhyme is deliberate.

## 7.2 He wants to fight directly

Ayanokōji's desire to face Sakayanagi is not reducible to class strategy. This follows Volume 5's revelation that a genuine defeat could have existential meaning for him. Here he has a formal opportunity to test himself within the school rather than hide behind another student.

The distinction from Volume 5's relay is instructive:

- the relay contained a spontaneous intrinsic escalation against Manabu;
- the Sakayanagi contest is deliberately arranged in advance because he wants the encounter itself.

His ordinary desire to participate and his strategic capacity to create the opportunity are becoming intertwined.

## 7.3 His self-assessed weakness in social information is essential

Ayanokōji says that in `情報収集力や統率力`—information-gathering and command of the class—he ranks near the bottom. He does not know ordinary details such as classmates' favorite foods.

This should be preserved against protagonist-totalization.

He is extraordinarily good at:

- high-value diagnosis;
- strategic modeling;
- crisis reading;
- and selected interpersonal leverage.

He is not automatically best at:

- broad social memory;
- everyday relationship maintenance;
- continuous class-wide information collection;
- or public coordination.

He needs Kei, Hirata, Kushida, and increasingly Horikita because their capacities are genuinely different from his.

# 8. Horikita Suzune: distributed leadership becomes real

Volume 11 is Horikita's strongest leadership volume so far.

That does not mean she suddenly becomes warm, democratic, or perfectly socially skilled. It means she begins solving a problem that the early Horikita barely recognized as a problem: **how to make unlike people usable together without requiring them to become versions of herself.**

## 8.1 `全員が協力し合う必要`

Horikita explicitly states that everyone needs to cooperate.

This is a major reversal from Volume 1, where the weak were liabilities to be cut away from her path to Class A. The new position is not sentimental equality. It is distributed institutional reasoning.

A student may lack a dramatic specialty and still matter because:

- events are uncertain;
- participation is distributed;
- fatigue and matchup matter;
- preparation changes competence;
- and classes need redundancy.

## 8.2 Sudō becomes a collaborator rather than a project

When basketball is considered, Horikita does not simply order Sudō to win. She asks him to build a team capable of winning without consuming every strongest athlete the class may need elsewhere.

That request recognizes a form of knowledge he possesses and she does not.

Sudō's role changes from:

> person Horikita must teach

into:

> person whose judgment Horikita needs.

This is one of the cleanest signs that his development has become socially usable.

## 8.3 Kōenji states the autonomy problem perfectly

Kōenji tells Horikita that even if his talent is real, whether he uses that talent for her is his decision:

> `その才能を君のために使うかどうかを決めるのは私自身だ`

The line is arrogant, but philosophically exact.

Possessing an ability does not automatically place it under collective jurisdiction.

That creates the hard version of socially usable ability:

> **collective value requires not merely capability, but some legitimate route by which the capable person chooses or accepts participation.**

Horikita cannot simply count Kōenji's physical or intellectual potential in the class balance sheet.

## 8.4 The notebook is political technology

Horikita's event notebook is more important than a tactical prop. It is an attempt to convert dozens of individual capacities into a shared map:

- who can do what;
- who can be trained quickly;
- who should be preserved for later events;
- what combinations are viable;
- what probabilities remain.

Ayanokōji explicitly recognizes that she is doing the work he cannot or will not do at the same resolution.

This is the strongest answer so far to the old question of whether she is merely his public puppet.

She is not.

He still possesses more hidden information and greater strategic depth in several domains. But she has become independently necessary to the class's operating capacity.

# 9. Manabu, Suzune, and the shift from imitation to self-authorship

The Manabu material deepens the Volume 10 correction.

He says his disappointment was not primarily about grades. It was about `心の成長`.

Suzune once smiled more. Her imitation of Manabu became increasingly comprehensive:

- preferences;
- clothing;
- food;
- colors;
- demeanor.

Manabu recognizes that his own cold rejection contributed to this dependence. The sibling relationship therefore becomes another example of developmental authorship gone wrong: an admired person can shape another human being even without intending to.

His mature hope is not that Suzune finally becomes his equal copy. It is that she stops chasing the copy entirely.

Ayanokōji then makes the most consequential internal turn of the volume. He first frames Horikita as something he should work on at school, then changes the thought:

> `いや、やりたいこと。`

That correction is psychologically enormous.

Ayanokōji's first large self-chosen project is not a hobby, club, romance, or career. It is **another person's development**.

This is both hopeful and disturbing.

It is hopeful because he has acquired desire independent of Atsuomi's assigned curriculum.

It is disturbing because the desire takes the form:

> I want to change her.

The unresolved ethical task is therefore not whether Ayanokōji can develop personal purposes. Volume 11 proves that he can.

It is whether he can possess a purpose involving another person **without claiming unilateral authorship over what that person should become**.

# 10. Horikita reads the person reading her

The dinner sequence is one of the most important relational reversals between Ayanokōji and Horikita.

Horikita reconstructs his hidden role in the earlier Manabu intervention and tells him:

> `あなたは私がどう行動するかを全部読んでいたのよ`

Ayanokōji's response is not triumph. He recognizes that continuing to manipulate her from behind will become harder.

That is precisely the kind of development his own project requires if it is to produce a person rather than a puppet.

Their relationship now contains:

- asymmetrical knowledge;
- real teaching;
- mutual observation;
- increasing detection of concealed influence;
- and division of labor.

He tells her that she is already doing well. She takes responsibility for what she can contribute. He teaches her chess because the class needs someone able to represent it in a domain she does not yet know.

The chess lesson therefore becomes a small model of a healthier developmental relation:

- explicit goal;
- voluntary participation;
- visible teacher;
- learner effort;
- no manufactured trauma;
- and an outcome the learner herself owns.

It sits in deliberate contrast to the Hirata intervention later in the book.

# 11. Kei's birthday gift: rankings cannot know one person

Ayanokōji remembers Kei's birthday and buys a relatively expensive heart-shaped necklace, combining birthday and White Day obligations.

The episode is comic because he optimizes badly.

He searches an online ranking for what girls supposedly want and selects a highly ranked gift. Kei reacts as though the gesture is socially overcalibrated, while Ayanokōji realizes that he has not actually asked what *this particular person* would like:

> `本当に相手の気持ちを考えたものかと言われると疑問が残る`

This is a small scene with large thematic value.

The school ranks students.
Sakayanagi ranks talent.
Ayanokōji ranks strategic utility.
The online market ranks gifts.

None of those rankings automatically knows a person.

His failure is not absence of care. Remembering the date, spending the money, and trying to choose appropriately are evidence of ordinary concern. The failure is **generic optimization replacing relational knowledge**.

Kei still keeps and tries the necklace. Their relationship therefore continues developing genuine ordinary intimacy inside the unresolved asymmetry established in Volumes 4–7.5.

Humanization and ethical equality remain different questions.

# 12. Kushida: tactical peace without reconciliation

Kushida remains socially destabilized after the Class Vote, but Volume 11 does not yet produce the later fantasy of full integration.

She tells Ayanokōji that the school has become more comfortable than she expected:

> `この学校は思ったよりもずっと居心地の良いものだった`

That changes her incentive structure.

For the immediate future, destroying Horikita or the class would threaten a place she has discovered she wants to keep.

This is not forgiveness.
It is not friendship.
It is not moral purification.

It is **stable cooperation through aligned self-interest**.

This matters because the series increasingly recognizes that institutions do not need identical motives. They need motives that can coexist without making mutual destruction rational.

# 13. Katsuragi, Keisei, and the limits of factional revenge

Keisei proposes an unauthorized attempt to recruit Katsuragi from A class, promising a future class transfer through the twenty-million-point mechanism. Horikita rejects the plan as unrealistic. Keisei proceeds anyway with Ayanokōji.

Katsuragi refuses the poor bargain but leaks predictions about likely A-class events because he resents Sakayanagi.

This looks at first like factional revenge becoming useful intelligence.

Then the limit appears.

Sakayanagi has anticipated the possibility that Katsuragi may intentionally underperform. She threatens consequences against unrelated classmates if he throws an event.

Katsuragi therefore refuses to convert resentment into collective betrayal.

His case demonstrates two things simultaneously:

1. Sakayanagi's rule remains coercive and informationally dominant.
2. Katsuragi's loyalty to classmates constrains how far his personal grievance can govern action.

The political lesson is not that revenge disappears. It is that **membership can impose moral limits on revenge**.

# 14. Hirata Yōsuke: coercive repair and the ethical limit of developmental authorship

The Hirata chapter is the volume's most important ethical test of Ayanokōji.

## 14.1 Mii-chan represents the humane default

Mii-chan insists that precisely because Hirata is suffering, he should not be abandoned:

> `苦しい時、辛い時だからこそ、助けてあげなきゃいけない`

Ayanokōji recognizes the sentiment as correct.

He also predicts that her attempts will initially worsen Hirata's state and allows the process to continue because the deterioration helps create the conditions he wants.

This is the recurring Ayanokōji problem in concentrated form:

> accurate empathy does not reliably produce restraint.

## 14.2 Hirata's past explains both his virtue and his failure

Hirata's childhood friend Sugimura was bullied and attempted suicide. Hirata first experienced the horror of passive bystanderhood.

When bullying later reappeared, Hirata chose the opposite extreme. He became the coercive sovereign of his year:

- he placed himself above everyone;
- everybody else became equally subordinate;
- disputes were punished;
- fear suppressed visible hierarchy.

The result was peace without ordinary happiness.

This history makes his ANHS persona legible as a third attempt:

> not passive bystanderhood; not authoritarian terror; but prosocial integration.

The Class Vote destroys the conditions under which that third model works. It forces him to participate in exactly the sorting and sacrifice he built himself to reject.

## 14.3 Ayanokōji knowingly uses an argument he considers false

Ayanokōji tells Hirata that Yamauchi's expulsion is his fault and presses the logic until Hirata is cornered.

The narration itself rejects the literal claim.

Hirata is not incompetent merely because he cannot save everybody. Trying to protect people is not wrong merely because protection fails.

Ayanokōji nevertheless keeps applying pressure:

> `重圧を与え、すり潰されるまで執拗に追い込む`

Then he states the motive with unusual clarity:

> `平田を思えばこそ？ 違う。`

and:

> `平田というパーツが必要だからだ`

This is not benevolent pedagogy accidentally becoming harsh.

It is **instrumental developmental violence**.

The class needs Hirata functional, so Ayanokōji chooses the method he believes will make him functional.

## 14.4 The outcome is real; the method remains ethically compromised

Hirata finally accepts that a leader may need to rely upon other people and that weakness can move in both directions. He returns to the class, apologizes, and acknowledges that Horikita's choice represented one form of rightness even though it does not erase his objection.

Ayanokōji also ends the encounter with a genuine human desire:

> `涙を見せられるような友を持ちたいとオレも思う`

That line matters precisely because it coexists with `パーツ`.

The same person can:

- want a friend capable of crying before him;
- understand Hirata's pain deeply;
- and still deliberately use that pain to restore a needed class component.

Volume 11 therefore makes one of the project's central distinctions unavoidable:

> **Humanization is not the same as ethical improvement.**

# 15. Ryūen: the return as self-experiment

Ryūen's appearance as D-class commander initially seems like a resurrection of the old authoritarian strategist.

His return is more interesting than that.

After the rooftop defeat, Ryūen had been willing to leave school. Ishizaki and others preserve him because they still want his leadership. In Volume 11, the commander role carries real expulsion risk because he lacks a Protection Point.

That risk becomes a test he performs on himself.

If he truly no longer cares, he can lose and leave.
If defeating Ayanokōji still matters, he will find himself fighting to remain.

The examination therefore becomes a mechanism of **self-knowledge through irreversible stakes**.

That does not purify Ryūen's methods.

He still describes part of his strategy as:

> `勝つための悪逆だ`

and considers or uses:

- intimidation;
- stalking;
- information theft;
- deliberate physical provocation;
- delayed laxatives designed to cause illness on exam day.

The ethical classification must remain clear. These are not merely clever asymmetric tactics. They include deliberate bodily harm and coercion.

What changes is his relation to the school and his followers, not the moral character of every method he employs.

# 16. Hiyori and the politics of quiet agency

Hiyori remains one of the easiest characters to underread because her political acts rarely take the form of command.

She helps orient the class toward the B-class matchup and functions as a bridge through which Ryūen's minimal initial advice becomes actionable collective preparation.

Her role is important because it demonstrates a form of political capacity the school does not easily score:

- listening;
- translating;
- reducing resistance;
- preserving social continuity;
- and making another person's strategy usable without becoming merely that person's mouthpiece.

This is `socially usable ability` in a low-spectacle form.

# 17. Ichinose: solidarity meets adversarial norm-breaking

Ryūen describes B class as strong in average ability and cohesion but lacking a “true leader.” That is **his partisan diagnosis**, not narrator-certified fact.

The exam gives us a more precise failure mechanism.

Ichinose's class is organized around:

- mutual trust;
- predictable cooperation;
- moral restraint;
- and high social cohesion.

Ryūen attacks the assumptions that make those strengths efficient. He uses tactics that a cooperative polity does not expect a peer to consider legitimate.

The correct conclusion is not:

> kindness is weakness.

It is:

> **solidarity without adversarial security is vulnerable to actors willing to violate the norms that solidarity presumes.**

That is a solvable institutional problem. It does not require Ichinose to stop valuing people. It requires her class to develop defenses that do not depend upon opponents sharing its moral constraints.

# 18. Sudō: development invalidates a static opponent model

The basketball event is one of the volume's cleanest demonstrations of longitudinal development.

Sakayanagi's side attempts to exploit the Sudō of earlier volumes:

- prideful;
- easily provoked;
- unable to regulate anger;
- vulnerable to being baited into self-defeating behavior.

The strategy is based on accurate historical information and therefore fails precisely because history has changed.

Ayanokōji's response is:

> `それはいつの情報だ？`

The narration then states directly:

> `須藤は、この１年間で大きな成長を見せてきた`

Sudō remembers that Horikita expects him to win **as leader**, not merely look impressive. He regulates himself and performs the role.

This is crucial to the nature–nurture argument framed by Sakayanagi's prologue.

Sudō does not disprove heredity as a general scientific concept. He does prove something the school's static measurements repeatedly miss:

> **a strategically relevant person at time T2 may not be the same person the opponent correctly measured at T1.**

Development makes old information stale.

# 19. Kōenji: possessed ability is not collective ability

The flash-arithmetic event compresses the Kōenji problem into one scene.

He deliberately leaves many answers blank, then correctly solves the hardest item.

Ayanokōji's judgment is one of the clearest ability formulations in Year 1:

> `制御しきれない力は力としてカウントするものじゃない`

Power that cannot be controlled cannot simply be counted as power.

“Control” here must be handled carefully. The sentence cannot justify treating Kōenji as property. The better reading is institutional:

> **capacity that cannot be reliably mobilized is not available capacity for collective planning.**

Ayanokōji also notes that the class bears some responsibility for its failure to create conditions under which Kōenji would willingly contribute.

This prevents the line from becoming pure authoritarian complaint.

Kōenji exposes the difference among:

- ability possessed;
- ability displayed;
- ability measurable;
- ability socially usable;
- and ability legitimately available to a group.

# 20. Chess: three different results occupy one event

The chess climax is often simplified into “Ayanokōji beats Sakayanagi.” The source is more disciplined than that.

There are **three distinct results**.

## 20.1 Official result

The school records Sakayanagi's class as the winner.

That result is institutionally real. It changes class points and consumes Ayanokōji's Protection Point.

## 20.2 Counterfactual live result

Ayanokōji transmits a different move from the one Horikita receives. Tsukishiro later admits that the instruction was intercepted, delayed, and replaced with an automated false command.

Sakayanagi herself senses that the final command did not fit Ayanokōji's demonstrated quality.

The source therefore strongly supports the inference that the official outcome does **not** accurately represent the strategy Ayanokōji actually chose.

## 20.3 Private replay result

Ayanokōji and Sakayanagi reconstruct the position from immediately before the interference. He plays the intended move and wins.

Even here the novel refuses an absolute dominance claim.

Ayanokōji notes that:

- the replay grants different thinking conditions;
- Horikita's earlier play helped create the favorable position;
- a full game from move one remains untested.

Sakayanagi estimates their chess ability as near parity, with perhaps a slight advantage to Ayanokōji in a crucial game because of his longer experience.

The strongest defensible conclusion is therefore:

> **Ayanokōji likely would have won this particular live game without interference, but Volume 11 does not establish that he is categorically or overwhelmingly superior to Sakayanagi at chess.**

That qualification matters because the novel itself preserves uncertainty.

# 21. Tsukishiro: procedural capture and the authorship of institutional reality

Tsukishiro's operation is not rumor, persuasion, or ordinary cheating.

He controls the infrastructure through which an action becomes institutionally legible.

He can control:

- room access;
- personnel;
- communication delay;
- automated voice playback;
- surveillance interpretation;
- and therefore the official causal record.

Ayanokōji made one decision.
Horikita heard another.
The school enforced the second.

This is `TRUTH_PROOF_RECORD` in its most advanced Year 1 form.

Earlier volumes distinguished:

- what happened;
- what evidence exists;
- what people believe.

Volume 11 adds:

> **what the institution is capable of recording as the actionable event.**

The political implication is severe:

> Fair competition exists only to the extent that the institution governing the record is itself constrained.

That applies beyond chess. If the acting chairman can author records, the students' strategic brilliance cannot alone secure autonomy.

This is why Tsukishiro is not merely “a stronger antagonist.” He changes the scale of sovereignty.

# 22. Sakayanagi after the replay: rivalry becomes human warmth

Sakayanagi's concluding judgment is often quoted for the word `天才`, but the more important movement occurs after the game.

She says:

> `あなたは紛れもなく天才である`

and rejects her earlier characterization of him as false.

Again, this resolves **her judgment of him**, not the universal nature–nurture debate.

Then the contest changes form.

She admits that she prolonged the path to confrontation partly because:

> `１秒でも長く綾小路くんと一緒の空間にいたかった`

She takes his hand and tells him that people learn warmth through touch, that `人肌のぬくもり` is not a bad thing.

They then walk slowly and talk about ordinary topics without strategic function.

This is one of the most important ordinary-life endings of Year 1.

The rivalry begins as a philosophical argument about whether he is a valid product of education.
It ends, locally, as an encounter between two people who can spend purposeless time together.

The scene is intimate and plausibly romantically coded. It is **not** explicit canonical romance at this spoiler boundary.

Its stronger thematic function is to answer the sterile White Room opening with a form of knowledge that no chess result can produce:

> what another person's hand feels like;
> what it means to slow one's pace for someone;
> what time is worth when nothing is being optimized.

# 23. `実力 / jitsuryoku` after Volume 11

Volume 11 substantially expands the ability taxonomy.

## 23.1 Possessed ability

What a person can do under some conditions.

- Kōenji possesses extraordinary physical and intellectual capacity.
- Ayanokōji possesses exceptional strategic and chess ability.
- Sudō possesses elite athletic potential.

## 23.2 Displayed ability

What the person actually chooses or is able to show.

Kōenji deliberately suppresses this.
Ayanokōji has spent much of Year 1 suppressing it.

## 23.3 Measured ability

What the institution records.

Tsukishiro proves that even this layer can be corrupted.

## 23.4 Socially usable ability

What can be coordinated into a shared outcome.

Horikita's notebook, Sudō's team selection, Hiyori's mediation, and the class's event preparation live here.

## 23.5 Developmental ability

What can become usable through time, practice, support, or changed motivation.

Sudō and Horikita's chess training are the clearest V11 cases.

## 23.6 Generative ability

What produces an answer not already contained in another person's model.

Horikita's increasing capacity to detect Ayanokōji's hidden influence points toward this category. She is becoming harder to author precisely because she is becoming better at authoring herself.

## 23.7 Recorded ability

Volume 11 requires a further distinction inside “measured” performance:

> **the difference between what was displayed and what the institution makes officially actionable.**

The chess falsification proves that a record can cease to be an honest measurement of the underlying act.

# 24. The four class constitutions under the Event Selection Exam

## 24.1 `POLITY-HORIKITA`: developmental pluralism

Horikita's class increasingly treats difference as a coordination problem rather than a hierarchy to eliminate.

Its strengths:

- distributed roles;
- growing trust;
- willingness to train weak domains;
- multiple visible leaders;
- capacity to use Sudō, Akito, Professor, Horikita, Hirata, Kei, Kushida and others differently.

Its unresolved weakness:

- Ayanokōji remains the hidden strategic contingency;
- Kōenji remains politically unavailable;
- some information remains asymmetrically controlled.

## 24.2 `POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN`: aristocratic optimization

Sakayanagi's class remains highly efficient because:

- she anticipates betrayal;
- controls specialists;
- creates deterrence;
- selects matchups precisely;
- and understands rivals well.

Its weakness is that extraordinary central control may reduce the need for distributed political agency.

Volume 11 does not yet resolve succession. It does show how much of the class's capacity flows through one sovereign mind.

## 24.3 `POLITY-ICHINOSE`: solidaristic institutionalism without adversarial hardening

B class retains cohesion and competent average performance.

Its weakness in this exam is not kindness itself. It is insufficient security against a rival willing to violate the expected normative field.

The developmental question is whether the class can add hardening without destroying the trust that makes it distinctive.

## 24.4 `POLITY-RYUEN`: authoritarian mobilization becoming voluntary preservation

Ryūen remains willing to use coercive methods. Yet his political basis is no longer identical to the early dictatorship.

Ishizaki and Hiyori help create the conditions of his return. He does not simply impose himself from above.

The polity is moving toward a more complicated form:

> centralized strategic authority sustained by increasingly voluntary follower investment.

# 25. Developmental authorship: four modes in one volume

Volume 11 offers four distinct modes of changing another person.

## 25.1 Explicit teaching — Horikita and chess

- goal disclosed;
- learner consents;
- method visible;
- effort belongs to learner;
- outcome remains learner's.

This is the healthiest model in the volume.

## 25.2 Distributed leadership — Horikita and the class

- leadership defines tasks;
- specialists retain domain knowledge;
- participation involves negotiation;
- no single person must embody every ability.

This is political rather than pedagogical development.

## 25.3 Coercive repair — Ayanokōji and Hirata

- goal privately defined;
- key premises deliberately distorted;
- emotional pressure intentionally escalated;
- target's breakdown is used as means;
- successful recovery does not retroactively create consent.

This is effective and ethically compromised.

## 25.4 Institutional authorship — Tsukishiro

- environment and record controlled from above;
- subject cannot meaningfully consent;
- action is replaced by the administrator's desired outcome.

This is not development in any humane sense. It is domination over legibility itself.

The important point is that Ayanokōji is **not identical** to Tsukishiro. His purposes often preserve other people; he can teach transparently; he can recognize autonomy; he desires their growth rather than mere obedience.

But Volume 11 makes the structural warning sharper:

> **A benevolent or developmental purpose does not by itself legitimate control over another person's emotional environment.**

# 26. Ordinary life as counter-curriculum

Volume 11's most important non-exam scenes are not decorative pauses. They measure what people can become outside formal scoring.

- Ayanokōji gives Kei a clumsy gift and discovers that market rankings do not equal personal knowledge.
- Manabu and Ayanokōji joke about girlfriends and romantic possibilities.
- Horikita cooks and turns a meal into both hospitality and negotiation.
- Sakayanagi walks slowly beside Ayanokōji after their rivalry has been privately resolved.
- The final conversation has no class-point value.

These scenes matter because Ayanokōji's ordinary-life development is increasingly specific.

He is no longer merely saying “I want a normal school life.” He is accumulating:

- tastes;
- social mistakes;
- gifts;
- meals;
- private jokes;
- rivalries enjoyed for their own sake;
- and purposeless time.

The White Room produced extraordinary competence by excluding enormous amounts of supposedly irrelevant human experience.

Volume 11 repeatedly presents the irrelevant as the material from which personhood is built.

# 27. Japanese voice and linguistic texture

## 27.1 Sakayanagi

Her polished register makes the prologue's hereditarian absolutism sound almost serene. This is important: ideological severity does not require rough language.

At the end, the same controlled voice becomes unusually personal. Her admission that she wanted one more second in the same space is direct precisely because her normal manner is so composed.

## 27.2 Ayanokōji

His most important linguistic moment is self-correction:

> `やるべきこと` → `やりたいこと`

That shift turns obligation into desire.

Elsewhere his narration remains capable of brutal dehumanizing compression (`パーツ`) and ordinary adolescent uncertainty (the gift episode) within the same volume.

## 27.3 Horikita

Her language remains formal and judgment-oriented, but the object of judgment has changed. She increasingly speaks about:

- cooperation;
- allocation;
- preparation;
- and what she herself can contribute.

Her conflict with Kōenji exposes that she can no longer assume a class leader simply owns the use of another student's talent.

## 27.4 Hirata

His language moves through three states:

1. withdrawal and minimal response;
2. disclosure of the coercive earlier self;
3. restored ability to ask, apologize, and accept reciprocal dependence.

The tears are not merely visual affect. They coincide with the collapse of the linguistic role that normally makes him reassuring to everybody else.

## 27.5 Ryūen

His blunt phrase `勝つための悪逆` is ethically useful because it refuses euphemism. He knows the method is vicious. The narrative should not be made more celebratory than the character's own wording.

# 28. Visual and embodied analysis

Volume 11's illustrations reinforce the limits of abstract ability by repeatedly returning competence to bodies.

## 28.1 Cover/frontmatter: Horikita and Kōenji

The cover juxtaposes two highly capable students whose abilities have opposite political statuses.

Horikita is becoming a coordinator whose value lies in making other people usable together.
Kōenji advertises extraordinary self-sovereign capacity while refusing ordinary collective obligation.

This is an interpretive visual rhyme rather than explicit narrative statement, but it fits the volume's ability problem unusually well.

## 28.2 Kei and the necklace

The gift illustration gives bodily embarrassment to Ayanokōji's failed optimization. The issue is not the object's monetary value but what it means on a teenage girl's body and inside their ambiguous relationship.

## 28.3 Kōenji carrying Mii-chan

The image punctures any simple equation of Kōenji with pure nonintervention. His stated philosophy remains self-centered, but his body becomes the mechanism that physically removes Mii-chan from an escalating confrontation.

The act should not be converted into proof of hidden altruism; it is evidence that his actual behavior is more varied than his self-description.

## 28.4 Ayanokōji and Hirata on the bench

The illustration makes the “repair” physically intimate without making it gentle. Hirata's exhausted body and tears are part of the cost of the method.

## 28.5 Ayanokōji and Sakayanagi before chess

The face-to-face framing makes the long-promised rivalry finally symmetrical. Neither is represented through a proxy in the image even though Horikita and Hashimoto execute the board physically.

## 28.6 Horikita at chess

The image restores her to the center of the material action. The climax is not only two hidden geniuses “playing through” lesser students. Horikita has learned enough to create a position that matters.

## 28.7 Tsukishiro

His polite adult appearance contrasts with the infrastructural violence of his intervention. The threat is not visual monstrosity. It is normal administrative presence backed by control of systems.

## 28.8 The final walk

The final Ayanokōji–Sakayanagi illustration shifts from adversarial staging to shared movement. The volume closes not on a board but on pace, distance, and time together.

# 30. Critical cautions and rejected simplifications

## 30.1 “The volume proves nurture beats nature.”

Rejected.

It proves that Sakayanagi revises her classification of Ayanokōji. Sudō and Horikita provide strong evidence of development. The general scientific nature–nurture question remains unresolved.

## 30.2 “Ayanokōji decisively crushes Sakayanagi at chess.”

Rejected as overstated.

The private replay supports the inference that his intended move likely wins the interrupted game. Both characters nevertheless treat their overall chess strength as near parity, and the replay conditions differ from the live match.

## 30.3 “Horikita is still only Ayanokōji's puppet.”

Rejected.

He remains a hidden influence, but she performs detailed class-wide coordination he admits he lacks, detects prior manipulation, trains, delegates, and creates a favorable chess position through her own development.

## 30.4 “Hirata recovers because Ayanokōji understands him compassionately.”

Incomplete to the point of distortion.

Ayanokōji understands him, but explicitly denies benevolent motive and describes Hirata as a necessary part. The intervention works through intentional psychological pressure.

## 30.5 “Ryūen's win proves Ichinose is too nice to lead.”

Rejected.

Ryūen's methods exploit a specific security weakness: B class is not hardened against actors willing to violate expected norms. Solidarity and adversarial preparedness are analytically separable.

## 30.6 “Kōenji's unused ability should simply count for the class.”

Rejected.

The volume makes his refusal central. Ability is not collectively usable merely because someone possesses it.

## 30.7 “The official exam result establishes the better player.”

Rejected.

The final command was falsified. Institutional record and underlying action diverge.

## 30.8 “Sakayanagi's final scene canonizes romance.”

Too strong.

It is intimate, affectionate, and plausibly romantic-coded. The local text does not explicitly establish a romantic relationship.

# 34. Cumulative delta through Volume 11

## Ayanokōji Kiyotaka

**Major development:** developmental authorship becomes an explicitly self-chosen purpose. His language moves from obligation to desire: changing Horikita is something he wants to do.

**Major ethical warning:** the Hirata operation proves that he will still manufacture acute psychological pressure when he judges another person's functioning necessary to a larger system. He explicitly describes Hirata as a required part.

**Strengthened:** ordinary adolescence remains real. He remembers Kei's birthday, fails comically at gift calibration, talks about girlfriends, enjoys a desired contest, and values slow purposeless time with Sakayanagi.

**Strengthened:** he is not a universal social superman. He acknowledges deficits in broad information gathering and ordinary knowledge of classmates.

**New institutional problem:** his own environmental authorship encounters an actor capable of greater-scale procedural authorship. Tsukishiro can change not just incentives but the official record.

## Horikita Suzune

**Major development:** leadership becomes distributed capacity-building. She no longer asks simply who is strong; she asks what combination of people can make the class strong.

**Major development:** she catches Ayanokōji's hidden influence and becomes harder to steer without detection.

**Strengthened independence:** Ayanokōji explicitly depends upon her class-wide coordination and acknowledges capacities he lacks.

**New caution:** Ayanokōji's desire to “change” her means her successful independence can still be absorbed into his private developmental narrative unless she produces choices he did not define.

## Hirata Yōsuke

**Major revision:** his care architecture is now fully historical. It arises from both the guilt of passive bystanderhood and the failure of an earlier authoritarian solution.

**Recovered but not resolved:** he re-enters class leadership after breakdown, but the volume does not prove the underlying moral injury has disappeared.

## Sakayanagi Arisu

**Major development:** her childhood hereditary theory becomes explicit and is partially revised through direct encounter.

**Qualified rivalry result:** she loses the private replay but remains near Ayanokōji in chess; the novel refuses a simplistic domination hierarchy.

**Relational development:** rivalry becomes explicitly tied to wanting shared time and bodily warmth. This gives her relation to Ayanokōji a human dimension beyond nature–nurture ideology.

## Ryūen Kakeru

**Major development:** return becomes self-authored through risk. He uses the commander role to test whether he truly still wants the rematch.

**Ethical continuity:** coercive and physically harmful methods remain available to him. Development in motivation does not equal moral reform.

## Ichinose Honami

**Major diagnostic revision:** B class's defeat is best understood through lack of adversarial security rather than “kindness is weakness.”

Its solidarity remains a real capacity. The problem is defending that capacity against actors who reject the norms on which it relies.

## Sudō Ken

**Strongly strengthened:** his basketball event is direct textual evidence that earlier behavioral models have become obsolete through development.

He now converts emotion into leadership rather than allowing provocation to dictate action.

## Kōenji Rokusuke

**Strengthened:** extraordinary ability remains politically unavailable when the holder refuses collective use. His arithmetic scene becomes the clearest V11 expression of the difference between possession and social usability.

## Advanced Nurturing High School

**Major darkening:** fair measurement is shown to be contingent on administrative integrity. A system can accurately design a sophisticated test and still produce a false result if its record infrastructure is captured.

# 36. Final synthesis

Volume 11 begins by asking whether a human being can be made into a genius and ends by asking what even a genius can do when somebody else owns the record.

That movement is the volume in miniature.

Sakayanagi's childhood theory imagines ability as something fundamentally located inside the person before education. The White Room imagines development as something an institution can manufacture through total environmental control. ANHS imagines ability as something it can expose through designed competition. Horikita increasingly treats ability as something that becomes politically real only when unlike people are coordinated. Ayanokōji begins to treat other people's development as something he personally wants to cultivate. Tsukishiro demonstrates that every one of those theories depends upon a prior political condition: **somebody must control the environment in which ability is allowed to count.**

The Event Selection Exam makes that dependence unusually visible.

Sudō is powerful because his athletic ability has become disciplined, social, and leader-like.

Kōenji is powerful but cannot be treated as reliable class power because he refuses the class's jurisdiction over his talent.

Horikita becomes powerful by learning enough about everybody else that she can assemble capacities she does not possess.

Ayanokōji is powerful enough to recognize the winning chess move, yet his own narration admits that he is weak in broad ordinary social knowledge.

Sakayanagi is powerful enough to understand that the received move is wrong, but not powerful enough to prevent an administrator from inserting it.

The novel therefore refuses the fantasy that merit is a property waiting to be measured cleanly.

> **Merit is developmental, relational, institutional, and political.**

The same conclusion applies to human growth.

Volume 11 gives Ayanokōji one of his clearest steps toward a self-authored life. He discovers something he wants to do. That matters. The White Room child is no longer merely trying to escape another person's purpose.

Yet his chosen purpose immediately reproduces the deepest danger of the institution that made him: he wants to shape another human being.

The volume does not resolve that contradiction by making him either secretly benevolent or secretly monstrous.

He teaches Horikita transparently.
He manipulates Hirata coercively.
He gives Kei an awkward gift.
He wants a friend who can cry before him.
He arranges his own desired rivalry.
He recognizes what Horikita can do that he cannot.
He still calls Hirata a part needed for the class to function.

These are all the same person.

Sakayanagi's final gesture supplies the book's most important counter-language.

The White Room asks what controlled education can produce.
The final walk asks what happens when two people simply share time.

The White Room uses glass.
The ending uses touch.

The White Room removes children who fail to fit.
The final exam shows students changing enough to make old classifications obsolete.

Tsukishiro replaces a command so the institution can obtain the desired result.
Horikita learns chess and produces a result he did not simply type into her body.

That contrast establishes the line Year 1 increasingly asks us to preserve:

> **Education can create conditions for growth. It does not thereby acquire ownership of the person who grows.**

The next volume will inherit a Year 1 in which Ayanokōji has become more self-authored, Horikita more independently capable, Hirata more psychologically exposed, Ryūen voluntarily re-engaged, Sakayanagi more personally connected, and the school itself less trustworthy as a neutral arbiter.

The unresolved question entering Volume 11.5 is therefore no longer merely who is strongest.

It is:

> **Who gets to decide what another person's strength is for—and what remains of freedom when development itself becomes somebody's project?**
