# 1. Executive thesis

Volume 11.5 closes the numbered Year 1 sequence by changing the governing problem from **self-authorship** into **reciprocal authorship**.

The volume is full of people trying to decide what part of their development belongs to themselves and what part has been written by somebody else.

Horikita begins in front of a mirror. She calls the personality, appearance, grades, and hairstyle she built around Manabu's imagined preference a `偽者 / false or constructed self`. The easy interpretation would be that she cuts her hair, discards the imitation, and recovers a pure authentic self underneath.

The text immediately refuses that model.

The years she spent imitating the sister she thought Manabu wanted are also `紛れもない私`—unmistakably herself. They were lived. They produced habits, capacities, failures, pain, and attachments. She can reject the governing purpose that organized those years without pretending the years did not belong to her. Her development is therefore **integration rather than purification**.

Ayanokōji reaches a parallel but more dangerous threshold.

During his conversation with Chairman Sakayanagi, he describes an ordinary three-year high-school life as the first goal he has ever set for himself:

> `生まれて初めて、オレは自分で目標を立て、そしてそれを実行しようとしてるんです`

That is one of the strongest Year 1 statements of self-authorship. The White Room may have authored his training, values, capacities, and expected future. Ordinary school life is the first life project he explicitly claims as his own.

Then Manabu gives him a problem that ordinary refuge alone cannot answer:

> What will you leave behind?

Ayanokōji's emerging response is startling. He imagines becoming memorable by **developing students**, allowing the four classes and their leaders to grow, and then making the developed students compete. The thought excites him. He immediately becomes afraid of the excitement, because it does not resemble the peaceful ordinary life he thought he wanted.

The paradox is the volume's center:

> **The boy escaping another person's developmental project begins to discover that development itself may be the first project he wants to author.**

That does not make him equivalent to the White Room. The volume gives several countermodels that complicate the analogy.

- Keisei promises to support Horikita when she is right and challenge her when she is wrong; Ayanokōji calls that proper class structure.
- Ike is moved by Sudō's visible growth, and Shinohara chooses to improve beside him; development propagates horizontally rather than from one superior teacher.
- Hiyori challenges Ryūen's dangerous method because friendship gives her reason to intervene rather than obey.
- Horikita and Ichinose end their alliance by mutual acknowledgment that respect sometimes requires separation.
- Manabu does not tell Suzune to become his successor-copy; he tells her to grow as herself and be strong and kind toward others.
- Ayanokōji transfers the future Kushida decision to Horikita even though he still thinks expulsion is more efficient.
- Kei's romantic answer is explicit and reciprocal. Whatever hidden curriculum Ayanokōji places around the relationship, she is not merely assigned the girlfriend role without answering for herself.

These scenes establish the year-end test:

> **Can Ayanokōji create conditions for growth while leaving other people enough authority to define what their growth is for?**

The volume does not resolve that question.

Its romance ending makes the uncertainty unusually intimate. Ayanokōji tells Kei he likes her. Kei explicitly answers that she has come to like him. They embrace. The illustration gives the moment a recognizably romantic image.

But Ayanokōji's interior narration immediately translates romance into learning. He does not know love, so he will learn it through Kei. He calls her a `教科書 / textbook`, expects himself to acquire knowledge from the relationship, and also treats the relationship as important to curing what he understands as her host-dependency.

That is not proof that the confession is false.

The same passage contains the counterclaim. He imagines a future in which she becomes `掛け替えのない存在`—irreplaceable—and ends not with a declaration that he has already achieved conventional love, but with a **prayer** that while holding someone important he is genuinely smiling and truly intends to cherish her.

The prayer matters because certainty is absent.

Volume 11.5 therefore ends Year 1 not with completed humanization but with a more demanding condition:

> **Ayanokōji has learned to choose goals, people, memories, rivals, and forms of intimacy for himself. The next question is whether self-authorship can become reciprocal without turning every valued person into material inside another curriculum.**

# 2. Position in the Year 1 architecture

Volume 11 ended with **developmental authorship under procedural capture**. Ayanokōji consciously chose Horikita's development as something he wanted, while Tsukishiro proved that an administrator could corrupt the official record of student performance.

Volume 11.5 does not escalate through another special examination. Instead, it asks what survives when the formal contest stops.

The Year 1 progression now becomes:

> **V1 — authored visibility**  
> **V2 — authored legibility**  
> **V3 — authored environment**  
> **V4 — authored dependency**  
> **V4.5 — ordinary-life counter-curriculum**  
> **V5 — controlled exposure**  
> **V6 — distributed integration under hidden contingency**  
> **V7 — sovereignty over futurity**  
> **V7.5 — negotiated indispensability**  
> **V8 — institutional authorship**  
> **V9 — authored reputation / information-environment authorship**  
> **V10 — manufactured disposability / accountable judgment / procedural capture**  
> **V11 — developmental authorship under record capture**  
> **V11.5 — reciprocal authorship, legacy, and hidden curriculum**

Three shifts make Volume 11.5 structurally indispensable.

## 2.1 Development becomes horizontal

Earlier Year 1 development often appeared as one person acting upon another:

- Horikita tutoring Sudō;
- Ayanokōji creating conditions around Horikita;
- Ayanokōji coercing Kei;
- Ayanokōji pressuring Hirata;
- Manabu challenging Suzune.

Volume 11.5 repeatedly shows people changing because peers move first.

Sudō's improvement makes Ike afraid of remaining behind.
Ike's determination moves Shinohara.
Keisei's willingness to disagree makes Horikita's leadership more accountable.
Hiyori's concern creates a path for correcting Ryūen.
Kanzaki's dissent attempts to make Ichinose's polity more resilient.

Development is no longer adequately modeled as a line from developer to developed.

It becomes a **network of reciprocal influence**.

## 2.2 Self-authorship becomes legacy

Ayanokōji can now articulate that ordinary school life is his chosen goal.

Manabu then asks a harder question: what does a self-authored life produce in other people?

Ayanokōji's first answer is not a career, ideology, or romantic future. It is to leave behind developed people and memories.

That moves Year 1 from freedom as escape toward freedom as responsibility for consequences.

## 2.3 Romance becomes hidden curriculum

Volume 7.5 established that Ayanokōji wanted to understand irreplaceability but had reached only indispensability.

Volume 11.5 returns to the problem after the relationship has become mutual.

The result remains split:

- real confession;
- reciprocal answer;
- physical tenderness;
- deliberate jealousy engineering;
- probabilistic preparation;
- pedagogical framing;
- and hope for singular value.

The romance is therefore neither a simple normalizing cure nor a simple sham.

It is the year-end location where ordinary life and developmental control occupy the same relationship.

# 3. Narrative architecture

The novel is built as a sequence of transitions rather than one central contest.

| Movement | Narrative function | Governing question |
|---|---|---|
| **Horikita prologue** | mirror, false/genuine self, haircut | Can a person reject an inherited purpose without rejecting the self produced while pursuing it? |
| **`卒業式`** | class standings, teacher coalition, graduation, Hirata | What survives a completed school year, and who protects the integrity of the next one? |
| **`デートひより`** | books, Ike/Shinohara, Hiyori, jealousy setup | Can ordinary interest and peer influence produce development without formal scoring? |
| **`迷える子羊`** | alliance separation, Ryūen, Ichinose | How should relationships change when former allies become rivals and damaged leaders must keep moving? |
| **`兄から妹へ`** | Manabu farewell, Horikita reconciliation, Kushida jurisdiction | What does inheritance look like when succession must not become imitation? |
| **`松下の疑念`** | hidden ability, Tsukishiro, counterintelligence | How much talent remains invisible, and how reliable is exposition delivered by an adversary? |
| **`動き出す青春`** | confession, mutual romance, textbook/irreplaceability | Can Ayanokōji learn love without reducing the beloved to curriculum? |

The form matters.

Volume 11.5 is a graduation book, but graduation is not merely departure. It is a test of **transmission**.

Manabu leaves Suzune words.
Manabu leaves Ayanokōji a challenge.
Nagumo inherits institutional space from Manabu while rejecting Manabu's constitutional philosophy.
Hirata attempts to inherit a healthier ordinary relational life after crisis.
Hiyori attempts to influence Ryūen without becoming his subordinate.
Ayanokōji gives Ichinose a one-year future horizon.
Ayanokōji and Kei begin a relationship whose meaning neither the visible confession nor the hidden narration can settle alone.

The book therefore closes Year 1 by asking what one person leaves inside another.

# 4. No special examination: transition itself becomes the mechanism

Volume 11.5 contains no new special examination.

That absence is analytically important.

The year has spent thirteen books placing students inside designed tests. The final numbered/decimal volume instead tests what the accumulated year has done to them when no new rule sheet tells them what counts as success.

The relevant outcomes are therefore harder to quantify:

- whether Keisei can fail without hiding the failure;
- whether Horikita can receive disagreement as legitimate participation;
- whether Ike can interpret another person's progress as a reason to grow rather than resent it;
- whether Hiyori can convert friendship into corrective courage;
- whether Ichinose can leave an alliance without converting the other class into moral enemies;
- whether Suzune can say farewell to Manabu without turning him back into the terminal model of her life;
- whether Ayanokōji can choose a relationship;
- and whether chosen intimacy changes the grammar through which he interprets people.

The school still structures everything.

Class points, private points, student-council succession, expulsion, teacher employment, surveillance, and Tsukishiro's authority remain present.

But the volume's most consequential developments cannot be represented cleanly by the school's metrics.

That fact makes the transitional volume a practical demonstration of Year 1's anti-scalar thesis.

# 5. Horikita's prologue: integration instead of the authentic-self fantasy

The mirror prologue is one of the most conceptually careful passages in the Year 1 sequence.

Horikita looks at herself on Manabu's final day and initially uses the language of falsity.

The self she built was organized around imitation:

- hairstyle;
- appearance;
- academic performance;
- personality;
- and the imagined requirements of being a sister worthy of Manabu.

She calls this a `偽者`.

But the narration immediately makes a distinction that should govern every later reading of the haircut.

Those years are not disposable.

They are also `紛れもない私`.

This means the text rejects two simplifications.

First, **the long-haired Horikita is not simply fake**.

Second, **the short-haired Horikita is not a recovered pre-social essence**.

The person who cuts her hair is the person produced by both phases.

She can reject the *purpose* that organized the old self—reaching Manabu through imitation—while retaining the experience, discipline, wounds, and capacities acquired during it.

This is a stronger account of self-authorship than simple authenticity.

> **Self-authorship does not require proving that prior externally shaped selves were unreal. It requires becoming able to decide what their accumulated material will mean next.**

The same structure will later matter for Ayanokōji.

The White Room formed him.
His strategic capacities are real.
His instrumental habits are real.
His ordinary-life desire is also real.

Escaping the White Room cannot mean pretending the White Room did not produce part of him.

Horikita's prologue therefore gives the volume a model of integration that Ayanokōji has not yet achieved.

# 6. Graduation standings and `POLITY-HORIKITA`: leadership becomes corrigible

The graduation sequence records the first-year standings but refuses to treat rank as complete political truth.

The explicit class-point figures are:

- Sakayanagi's class: 1131;
- Ichinose's class: 550;
- Horikita's class: 347;
- Ryūen's class: 508.

The letter assignments change on April 1, but the stable-polity architecture remains necessary because rank does not explain constitutional development.

Horikita's polity has gained **347 points from zero**, the largest annual increase in the cohort. Yet the more important scene is Keisei's failed attempt to recruit Katsuragi.

Keisei admits that he acted unilaterally and failed.

Horikita does not use the failure to reassert hierarchy.

Instead, she separates two things:

- bringing forward an idea;
- executing a class-affecting plan without consultation.

Future proposals remain welcome.
Unilateral implementation does not.

Keisei then articulates his own transformation. Academic excellence still matters to him, but the year has taught him that:

- athletics;
- communication;
- friendship;
- and other capacities

also matter.

He says he increasingly trusts Horikita, but that trust does not mean obedience. He will support her when he thinks she is right and challenge her when he thinks she is wrong.

Ayanokōji explicitly evaluates that structure positively.

This is a large constitutional development.

The emerging class model is not:

> strong leader + loyal followers.

It is closer to:

> **leader + distributed competence + legitimate internal correction.**

That is important because it provides one of the clearest Year 1 alternatives to both Ryūen's authoritarian command and Ayanokōji's hidden sovereignty.

A leader can be strong without being unanswerable.

# 7. Institutional defense after Tsukishiro: fairness requires adult courage

Volume 11 established that Tsukishiro could alter the communication record in the chess match.

Volume 11.5 asks what an appropriate response looks like.

Ayanokōji does **not** call Chairman Sakayanagi and ask to be secretly favored.

He explicitly distinguishes protection of institutional integrity from personal rescue.

His argument is:

- Tsukishiro has already interfered;
- another intervention could produce expulsion;
- a student cannot reliably constrain acting-chairman authority alone;
- therefore he needs adults willing to witness, monitor, and later recognize legitimate defense.

The language resembles security architecture.

He wants a **backstop**.

That is important because the scene models a form of institutional constraint different from Ayanokōji's normal private control.

Instead of monopolizing the solution, he creates oversight.

Mashima becomes the crucial adult because he is willing to protect students even at personal cost.

The judo story makes this concrete. Faced with the possibility that a random student—potentially a girl with no reasonable chance against Albert—could be selected after time expired, Mashima declared the loss rather than permit a dangerous mismatch to proceed. He was financially punished for violating the formal rule.

His defense is straightforward:

> If students are in danger, stop it. If there is cheating, correct it. A teacher cannot teach standards and then abandon them when they become personally costly.

That does not make Mashima infallible.

It establishes **accountable institutional conscience** as a form of power.

The teacher coalition therefore becomes a countermodel to both Tsukishiro and Ayanokōji.

Tsukishiro can manipulate the record because authority is concentrated.
Ayanokōji often protects people through invisible private sovereignty.
Mashima attempts to protect fairness through a role that can be publicly justified as part of teaching itself.

The volume also preserves a danger.

Ayanokōji and Sakayanagi both recognize that excessive righteous intervention could get Mashima removed.

Institutional integrity therefore requires not only moral courage but survivable strategy.

# 8. Sakayanagi and the fiduciary limit of special attachment

The teacher meeting also gives Sakayanagi one of her clearest political self-definitions.

She rejects Mashima's assumption that a class leader must treat cross-class favoritism as illegitimate.

Her argument is radical:

- people have friends, lovers, debts, bargains, threats, and attachments across formal group boundaries;
- special concern is normal in school and society;
- therefore she has the right to prioritize Ayanokōji even across class lines;
- if Class A suffers, the classmates who bear the cost have standing to resent her, not a teacher who demands abstract impartiality.

This is not liberal institutional neutrality.

It is a theory of **private sovereignty with stakeholder liability**.

The strength of the position is that it refuses hypocrisy. Sakayanagi does not pretend leadership eliminates personal attachment.

The weakness is fiduciary.

If one person governs a class, private attachment can redirect collective resources toward ends the governed did not choose.

Her willingness to help remove Tsukishiro is compatible with competitive hostility after the threat is gone. She is not converting into an ally-class.

That distinction preserves her rivalry as both political and personal.

# 9. Manabu and Nagumo: two theories of meritocratic political order

Graduation formalizes the succession from Manabu to Nagumo without reducing it to winner and loser.

The handshake matters.

Nagumo reveals that what he wanted from Manabu was not only defeat.

He wanted **recognition**.

That converts the student-council conflict into a relational problem as well as an ideological one.

Nagumo's `本当の実力主義 / true meritocracy` remains his political claim, not an authorial definition of the series title.

His model emphasizes:

- greater individual mobility;
- results tied more directly to individuals;
- more interaction across years;
- and reduced shelter under fixed class fate.

Manabu defends the class system for almost the opposite reason.

A class gives students who are individually weaker:

- time;
- collective support;
- distributed roles;
- and the possibility of recovery.

Too much individualization risks converting every peer into a direct rival and dissolving the trust produced by shared fate.

The disagreement therefore sharpens Year 1's meritocracy problem.

Nagumo asks:

> Why should stronger individuals be trapped by weaker classmates?

Manabu asks:

> What developmental possibilities disappear if people are evaluated as isolated competitors before the group has time to change them?

Ayanokōji does not settle the dispute from theory.

He wants to experience Nagumo's system before deciding how to respond.

That empirical restraint is important.

It contrasts with the moments when he acts as though his psychological model of another person gives him enough knowledge to author the person's future.

# 10. Hirata: care learns that truth can preserve agency better than delay

Hirata's post-crisis material is quiet but substantial.

He confirms that the relationship with Kei was never romantic from his side. It was a protective arrangement.

He also reveals that he has never experienced romantic love.

Mii-chan's feelings therefore produce a new form of difficulty.

His instinct is delay.

If he cannot reciprocate, he wants to avoid causing pain.

Ayanokōji challenges this as an extension of the same care pathology Volume 10 exposed.

Delaying a truthful answer may create more harm than giving it.

More importantly, rejection does not give Hirata jurisdiction over Mii-chan's later feelings.

She may continue liking him.

This produces a compact ethical principle:

> **Care is not the elimination of another person's painful choices. Sometimes care requires giving the other person truthful information and allowing them to retain authorship over what they do with it.**

Hirata's fear of particular attachment is also clarified.

His equal treatment of everyone is partly trauma management. Creating another `大切な人 / important person` feels dangerous because special attachment creates unequal vulnerability.

His request to shift names—`清隆くん` and `洋介`—is therefore significant precisely because it is small.

He chooses particularity.

The warmth Ayanokōji experiences is not a class-point event.

It is a friendship relation becoming more individuated after a year in which Hirata often treated care as universal obligation.

# 11. Hiyori, books, Ike and Shinohara: reciprocal development without a designer

The Hiyori chapter opens with books rather than strategy.

Hiyori wants rare books placed in the library because shared access allows other students to read and discuss them. The value is cultural and relational, not competitive.

This makes the chapter a clean continuation of the counter-curriculum thread.

The overheard Ike–Shinohara conversation then produces one of the volume's most important developmental formulations.

Ike has watched Sudō improve.

His reaction is mixed:

- admiration;
- fear;
- comparison;
- and anxiety that he may become the next expendable low performer.

He decides to improve academically and physically.

Shinohara initially teases him, then refuses to laugh at sincere effort and decides to improve too.

Ayanokōji identifies the mechanism:

> when one person walks forward, another may begin walking too.

This is **generative development** without a singular developer.

Sudō did not design Ike's fear.
Ike does not design Shinohara's answer.
The school did not assign the conversation as a pedagogical exercise.

Development emerges from observation inside ordinary relationships.

That matters enormously for the later four-class thought.

If Ayanokōji wants to cultivate everyone, he must confront the possibility that the most important development occurs precisely when nobody owns it.

# 12. Hiyori and Ryūen: quiet friendship becomes corrective political agency

Hiyori's interest in Ayanokōji begins in books, but Volume 11.5 refuses to confine her to a low-stakes hobby role.

She reconstructs that Ayanokōji changed Ryūen and risks asking about it because she believes relationships do not deepen if difficult questions are permanently avoided.

Her evaluation of Ryūen is nuanced.

She does not demand procedural innocence.

She accepts that a leader in this environment may sometimes use severe or `必要悪 / necessary evil` tactics.

But she places a limit at reckless bodily danger.

The health-risk operation against Ichinose's class is unacceptable because it is:

- unnecessarily dangerous;
- traceable;
- and capable of producing consequences far beyond the exam.

She wants someone Ryūen recognizes to correct him.

That is not submissive concern.

It is a form of political agency based on relationship.

Ayanokōji then sends a technical critique through that channel.

He tells Ryūen he could have won at least five events more safely.

The important contrast is that Hiyori's objection contains an ethical component, while Ayanokōji's later direct correction is predominantly operational.

The two together could produce restraint.

But they should not be collapsed into the same philosophy.

# 13. Horikita and Ichinose: respect can require ending an alliance

The spring-break meeting formally ends the Horikita–Ichinose alliance.

This is one of the volume's strongest examples of **separation without dehumanization**.

Ichinose remains kind.
Horikita remains respectful.
Neither needs to manufacture betrayal to justify divergence.

The strategic fact is simple.

Their point positions are close enough that continued mutual protection could prevent either class from moving decisively upward.

Horikita therefore states that the alliance should end.

Ichinose accepts.

The handshake converts former alliance into acknowledged rivalry.

This matters because the series often makes separation feel like injury:

- expulsion;
- abandonment;
- betrayal;
- or broken dependency.

Here separation is an act of institutional honesty.

Both classes are allowed to retain identity rather than remain bound by an arrangement whose original rationale has expired.

The scene also sharpens Ichinose's political weakness without reducing her goodness to stupidity.

She recognizes that Ryūen exploited her class's reluctance to expose one another to harm. Horikita wants formal reporting. Ichinose refuses and treats the loss as something her class must learn from while time remains.

This can be criticized as excessive lenience.

It is also a deliberate attempt to make failure educational rather than outsource every correction to administration.

# 14. Horikita tests Ayanokōji: hidden support becomes an auditable wager

Horikita's suspicion of Ayanokōji has matured.

She recognizes that the previous class matchup did not emerge naturally and presses him about independent manipulation.

She also approaches the truth about Sakayanagi's Protection Point behavior but retreats when proof is insufficient.

This is important epistemically.

She no longer accepts either ignorance or intuition as enough.

When she asks whether Ayanokōji truly wants to rise, his private narration confirms that he **has begun to want a higher class position**.

He still refuses immediate full exposure because an abrupt contradiction between his public record and real ability would destabilize the entire year-level information environment.

Horikita's response is excellent from the perspective of reciprocal authorship.

She does not ask for blind trust.

She proposes a test.

She may select a written subject immediately before the contest. Ayanokōji claims he can score 100. The result creates an auditable basis for future obligations.

If she wins, he must show full effort broadly.
If he wins, he receives a future request.

This converts an asymmetric hidden-capability relationship into a **negotiated contract with measurable performance**.

It does not equalize them.

It makes the asymmetry contestable.

# 15. Ryūen: development without ethical conversion

Ayanokōji's conversation with Ryūen is one of the volume's clearest warnings against reading growth as moral reform.

Ryūen is more self-reflective after defeat.

He can admit errors.
He is more interested in rematch than resignation.
He has followers whose attachment is increasingly voluntary.

But his direct lesson from the Ichinose exam is not:

> harming people was wrong.

It is:

> the operation left too much trace and gave opponents too much leverage.

Ayanokōji reinforces this.

He tells Ryūen that underhanded, cowardly, and even criminal methods can be acceptable as winning strategies, but that easily traceable conduct is foolish.

This is operational ethics rather than moral restraint.

He even says, in teacher-like language, `もっと上手く成長しろ龍園 / grow more skillfully, Ryūen`.

Ryūen recognizes the hierarchy immediately:

> since when did you become my teacher?

The line is analytically exact.

Ayanokōji is beginning to inhabit the developer role openly enough that another major rival can name it.

The outcome is not benevolent education.

Ayanokōji expects Ryūen's strategies to become more polished and harder to detect.

That is development.

It is not ethical improvement.

# 16. Ichinose in the rain: future orientation, intimacy, and retained sovereign judgment

The Ichinose sequence is one of the most difficult relational scenes in the volume because nearly every interpretation contains real evidence and becomes false when treated as exclusive.

Ichinose is genuinely distressed.

She has watched:

- Horikita grow;
- Ryūen return;
- Sakayanagi remain formidable;
- and her own class lose ground.

She stands in heavy rain and appears willing to remain there until physical illness follows.

Ayanokōji's intervention is caring and coercive at once.

He rents an extra umbrella.
He worries that illness would worsen her mental condition.
He then folds his own umbrella and makes himself share the rain.

He explicitly knows the mechanism.

Ichinose is unlikely to protect herself, but she will worry about him.

He uses her concern for another person to end her self-punitive isolation.

The method works.

That does not make it nondirective.

When she later refuses to return to her room because being alone will intensify rumination, he again refuses to leave and finally brings her to his room.

There, the relationship becomes emotionally and physically charged.

Ichinose says she may trust Ayanokōji more than anyone else.

He immediately warns her that this trust could be exploited because they are class enemies.

She refuses the word enemy and arrives at a more precise category:

> not an ally, but a person I can trust.

That formulation is one of the volume's strongest relational achievements. It allows cross-class trust without pretending political alignment.

Ayanokōji then refuses easy reassurance.

He does not promise that Ichinose will win.

He tells her to spend another year advancing with her classmates because their solidarity is the distinctive weapon available to them.

The scene becomes intimate. He recognizes that it would be easy to exploit her emotional vulnerability and sexual openness in the moment. He touches her wet hair, cheek, and lips; she does not withdraw.

The narration should not be sanitized.

His awareness of exploitability remains present inside tenderness.

The one-year promise then creates a future horizon:

> meet me one year from today.

He promises that if they survive, he will tell her the words he is currently withholding.

Ichinose answers by promising to fight for Class A.

This is real developmental intervention because it converts an indefinite fear of collapse into a finite horizon toward which she can act.

But the final sentence prevents the reader from converting the scene into simple romantic salvation.

If Ichinose and her class collapse, Ayanokōji thinks:

> `その時の『介錯』はオレがする`

He will perform the `介錯`—the finishing or mercy blow.

The metaphor retains sovereign judgment over her trajectory.

The correct formulation is therefore:

> **Ayanokōji gives Ichinose a future to fight toward while privately preserving the right to judge when that future has failed.**

Care, attraction, pedagogy, rivalry, and domination remain fused.

# 17. Manabu's final lesson to Ayanokōji: freedom must leave a trace

The Manabu farewell contains one of the most important conversations in Year 1.

Manabu asks why Ayanokōji continues hiding his ability.

Ayanokōji's answer remains ordinary school life.

Manabu does not reject that desire.

He asks whether remaining invisible is truly enough.

The question becomes legacy:

> become a student who remains in other students' memories.

The significance is not that fame is morally superior to privacy.

Manabu is challenging the premise that a self-authored life can be defined only by escaping other people's demands.

Ayanokōji entered ANHS to experience what was denied him.

Manabu asks what his presence will **give back** to the world he entered.

This is a transition from negative liberty to positive authorship.

Ayanokōji also recognizes genuine separation affect.

He experiences Manabu's departure as `どこか寂しい / somehow lonely`, wishes they had another year, and wonders whether Manabu could have changed him further.

Again, the sentence is revealing.

Even when the relationship matters personally, Ayanokōji interprets intimacy partly through developmental effect.

Manabu is valued as a person and as someone capable of changing him.

Those meanings coexist.

# 18. Suzune and Manabu: succession without imitation

Suzune arrives with short hair and a different governing claim.

She is no longer trying to catch her brother.

She wants to:

- find her own path;
- walk in front for classmates;
- learn with companions;
- and become a leader in a form not reducible to Manabu's image.

Manabu's answer is one of Year 1's strongest anti-imitation statements.

He tells her that he saw talent in her that might surpass him.

The problem was not lack of potential.

The problem was turning **him** into the endpoint.

If the goal is to become Manabu, then reaching Manabu means development stops.

His mature injunction is:

> `他者に強くあれ。そして優しくあれ`
>
> Be strong toward/for others. And be kind.

The exact English nuance can vary, but the dual demand matters.

Strength without relational concern becomes domination.
Kindness without strength can become helplessness or avoidance.

Manabu then reveals that his old statement about liking long hair was itself a test.

He preferred short hair and wanted to know whether Suzune would erase her own color to satisfy his stated preference.

She did.

That memory makes the haircut more complex, not less.

The short hair is not simply obedience to his actual preference.

Suzune explicitly says she also has attachment to the long-haired self and may grow it again.

The volume therefore closes the sibling arc by refusing a fixed visual symbol.

Hair becomes history rather than essence.

# 19. Ayanokōji and Horikita after the farewell: comfort, boundary violation, and chosen trust

After Manabu leaves, Suzune asks to be alone so she can cry.

Ayanokōji follows her anyway.

He tells her it is acceptable to cry and continues pressing until her controlled defense breaks and she sobs against him.

The outcome is emotionally intimate.

The method is also a boundary override.

Both facts must remain visible.

A common reading would treat the successful catharsis as retrospective proof that he was right to ignore the request.

The source does not require that conclusion.

What it does show is that Suzune later chooses to interpret the event positively precisely because **it was him**.

That is evidence of selective trust.

It is not a general license for him to override future boundaries.

This distinction is central to reciprocal authorship.

> **A good outcome can become part of a trusted relationship without converting the original nonconsensual element into a universally legitimate method.**

The student-council wager follows the same theme more carefully.

If Ayanokōji wins their academic test, his request will be that Horikita join the student council.

But he does not force an immediate answer.

The wager creates obligation inside a mutually accepted structure, while the timing of the decision remains hers.

The contrast between these two interactions shows that Ayanokōji is capable of both boundary override and negotiated influence within the same relationship.

# 20. Kushida jurisdiction: Ayanokōji deliberately yields the decision

Ayanokōji tells Horikita about the private-point contract with Kushida and his earlier plan to remove her.

He still believes expulsion is the efficient solution.

Horikita does not respond with unconditional sentimental protection.

Her position is more demanding.

She will not casually lose a classmate.

But if Kushida eventually becomes the lowest-value contributor or a sacrifice becomes unavoidable, Horikita cannot promise that she will preserve her regardless of consequence.

This is important because it prevents the integration thesis from becoming naïve absolutism.

Horikita is not saying:

> nobody may ever be removed.

She is saying:

> present danger does not by itself settle future judgment.

Ayanokōji then transfers future jurisdiction over Kushida to Horikita.

He does this even though he believes his own answer is more efficient.

Why?

Because he wants to see how Horikita handles the obstacle.

The act remains developer-coded—Horikita's decision becomes something he wants to observe.

But it is also a real surrender of unilateral control.

He could continue the removal project privately.

He chooses not to.

This is one of Volume 11.5's clearest local examples of movement toward **reciprocal authorship**.

# 21. The four-class developmental thought: Ayanokōji discovers a self-authored experiment

After Horikita leaves, Manabu's words remain active.

Ayanokōji thinks about:

- Ichinose;
- Sakayanagi;
- Ryūen;
- Horikita;
- and the coming four-class struggle.

Then he asks what he could do to become someone who remains in other students' memories.

His answer is development:

> `生徒たちを育成し、成長させること`

He imagines making students grow, then making the developed students compete and reach higher.

The thought produces excitement.

This is the most important new Ayanokōji idea in the volume.

It must also be classified carefully.

At the Y1V11.5 boundary it is:

- an emerging thought;
- an emotionally powerful possibility;
- and a developmental desire.

It is **not yet a fully specified operational program**.

The scene matters because his mind immediately begins analyzing class forces without conscious instruction.

Then his affect reverses.

His heart becomes cold.

He remembers that what he supposedly wanted was `平穏な日常 / peaceful ordinary life`.

He tries to reassure himself that his heart has grown during the year.

The reassurance fails.

He wonders whether the apparent change is only plating falling away from a sealed inner core.

This is not proof that the ordinary self is false.

The entire volume contains strong evidence that ordinary attachment is real.

It is proof that Ayanokōji himself does not possess a stable theory of his own transformation.

The correct endpoint is **epistemic uncertainty about the self**.

> **He knows that he can choose. He does not yet know which of his competing desires deserves to define the person he is becoming.**

# 22. Matsushita Chiaki: hidden ability is a class-wide epistemic problem, not a protagonist monopoly

The Matsushita chapter is easy to underestimate because it introduces no immediate special-exam crisis.

Its structural purpose is larger.

Year 1 has repeatedly criticized the assumption that visible performance exhausts ability.

Matsushita proves the problem exists inside ordinary classmates too.

She comes from an affluent, future-conscious family and places real value on graduating from Class A.

She also deliberately suppresses performance.

Her reason is recognizably social:

- excessive visibility creates jealousy;
- success creates dependency and requests;
- standing out creates hassle;
- middle-high performance preserves freedom.

She thinks of herself as roughly top-ten-percent rather than genius-level.

The similarity to Ayanokōji is therefore structural, not scalar.

Both manage their public records.

Their capacities are not remotely established as equivalent.

Matsushita's real strengths are:

- distributed clue integration;
- social steering;
- strategic curiosity;
- and willingness to investigate anomalies.

She notices:

- Kei's unusual attention;
- Ayanokōji's proximity to Horikita, Hirata, and Ichinose;
- the relay;
- Sakayanagi's strange Protection Point intervention;
- and flash mental arithmetic.

She constructs a plausible hidden-capability hypothesis.

Then Ayanokōji notices her tail and demonstrates the information gap.

He gives her a bounded false model:

- yes, he has been holding back;
- no, not by much;
- perhaps seventy or eighty percent has already been shown;
- overall he remains below someone like Hirata.

He also notices that **she** is hiding ability and declines to expose it.

The scene becomes reciprocal surveillance with radically unequal depth.

His mock confession is especially revealing.

Rather than simply tell Matsushita that Satō's romantic reasoning is psychologically plausible, he creates a moment in which Matsushita herself experiences the salience of receiving a confession. He lets her mind complete the causal story.

That is authored legibility returning in miniature at the end of Year 1.

# 23. Tsukishiro and the White Room: exposition becomes an epistemic trap

The Tsukishiro encounter must be handled with unusual source discipline.

He provides apparently detailed White Room information:

- a roughly twenty-year institutional history;
- generational structure;
- high dropout/failure consequences;
- later cohorts with significant talent;
- an extreme fourth generation;
- Ayanokōji as the sole finisher of that environment;
- and one White Room-affiliated student supposedly hidden among the incoming first-year cohort.

These details are tempting because they sound like lore exposition.

The volume itself inserts the warning label.

Ayanokōji explicitly says Tsukishiro is capable of making a **complete fabrication** sound true.

Therefore, at this local boundary:

> **Tsukishiro's White Room specifics are testimony, strategic claims, or unresolved propositions—not omniscient narrator facts.**

This is a direct application of the Year 1 truth/proof architecture.

The person speaking may possess privileged information.

That does not grant the reader a reliable channel.

Ayanokōji accepts Tsukishiro's proposed identification game formally but trusts neither the numbers nor the promise.

His proverb—`井の中の蛙大海を知らず、されど空の深さを知る`—also refines his ability self-model.

He does not claim global supremacy.

He assumes that in the wider world there must be people superior in domains he has not encountered.

His confidence is narrower:

> within the White Room environment he knows, he is certain there was nobody superior to him.

This combination of local confidence and global epistemic humility is more precise than a generic omnipotence claim.

# 24. Kei: reciprocal confession inside a concealed developmental curriculum

The final chapter is one of the most important scenes for distinguishing **event fact** from **interpretive totalization**.

Several facts are direct.

Ayanokōji deliberately creates jealousy data through Hiyori.

He prepares his room and treats confession partly as a probability problem.

He tells Kei he wants to prevent her from becoming another boy's girlfriend.

He says:

> `そうだ、オレは軽井沢恵が好きだ`

Kei explicitly reciprocates:

> `あたしも…好き…になってた`

He does not immediately kiss her.

He asks before moving into the embrace-level intimacy the scene actually depicts.

They hug.

These facts establish a **real reciprocal romantic transition**.

The scene cannot responsibly be described as a unilateral fake relationship.

The difficult material comes immediately afterward.

Ayanokōji does not possess ordinary confidence about what he is feeling.

He asks what expression is on his own face.

Then he interprets romance through the grammar he knows best: learning.

Humans experience pleasure in improvement.

He does not know:

- romance;
- love;
- sexual/relational experience;
- or the later emotions those experiences produce.

He expects to learn.

Kei becomes:

> `１冊の異性という名の教科書`
>
> a textbook called “the opposite sex.”

He also treats the relationship as important to Kei's growth because he believes her current host-dependency would be harmful over the long term.

This creates a hidden curriculum that Kei does not fully share.

She consents to becoming his girlfriend.

She does not consent in any explicit informed sense to being a developmental experiment or educational instrument.

That asymmetry must remain visible.

But the `教科書` line is also not the final line.

Ayanokōji immediately imagines another future.

When the textbook's role is supposedly complete, perhaps Kei will instead have become:

> `掛け替えのない存在`

an irreplaceable existence he will not let go.

He cannot decide whether that future is possible.

One part of him hopes for it.

Another believes it impossible.

The volume ends with prayer rather than proof:

> he hopes that while embracing an important person, he is smiling; he hopes he is a young student genuinely pledging to cherish her.

That ending should govern interpretation.

The novel does **not** ask the reader to choose between:

- “Kei is only a textbook,” or
- “Ayanokōji has now become a conventional loving boyfriend.”

It stages the unstable coexistence of both grammars.

> **He begins romance using education as the language available to him while hoping the relationship will produce a value that education cannot fully instrumentalize.**

That is a much stronger and more text-faithful endpoint.

# 25. Ayanokōji: self-authorship becomes dangerous precisely because it is real

Volume 11.5 provides unusually strong evidence that Ayanokōji's ordinary-life desire is genuine.

He describes ordinary high school as his first self-chosen goal.

He values the year as a source of `掛け替えのない思い出 / irreplaceable memories`.

He experiences Manabu's departure as lonely.

He enjoys purposeless time.

He values books with Hiyori.

He enters a romantic relationship.

He can imagine wanting Kei to become irreplaceable.

The second pass should therefore reject any interpretation in which “normal Ayanokōji” is merely a mask covering the one true strategic core.

At the same time, the volume makes the opposite simplification equally untenable.

He:

- deliberately engineers jealousy;
- uses concern as pressure against Ichinose;
- retains `介錯` judgment over her trajectory;
- tells Ryūen to improve the concealment of severe tactics rather than abandon them;
- overrides Horikita's request for solitude;
- feeds Matsushita a bounded false self-model;
- treats Kei as learning material;
- and becomes excited by the idea of cultivating all four classes.

The strongest formulation is therefore not “human versus monster.”

It is **competing self-authored projects**.

One project seeks:

- peace;
- friendship;
- ordinary time;
- romance;
- memory;
- and experiences the White Room denied.

Another project seeks:

- development;
- stronger rivals;
- controlled experiments;
- comparative outcomes;
- and the pleasure of seeing people reach higher states.

Both now belong to him.

That is precisely why freedom does not solve the ethical problem.

> **Once the choice is genuinely his, he becomes more responsible—not less—for the methods through which he pursues it.**

# 26. Relationship architecture at the Year 1 endpoint

## 26.1 Ayanokōji–Horikita

The relation has moved far beyond hidden strategist and public puppet.

Horikita:

- detects manipulation;
- challenges him;
- creates an auditable wager;
- receives jurisdiction over Kushida;
- and can refuse his preferred solution.

Ayanokōji still influences her, sometimes invasively.

But the relationship increasingly contains **counter-authorship**.

She can change the environment back.

## 26.2 Horikita–Manabu

The relation ends its Year 1 arc through recognition without merger.

Suzune can love and admire Manabu without making resemblance to him the final criterion of self-worth.

The embrace is therefore not approval of successful imitation.

It is approval of departure from imitation.

## 26.3 Ayanokōji–Manabu

Manabu becomes one of the first people Ayanokōji explicitly imagines as someone who could have continued changing him.

His value is developmental and relational.

The farewell converts rivalry/recognition into legacy.

## 26.4 Ayanokōji–Ichinose

Trust becomes more personal and more dangerous.

Ichinose can show weakness to him.

Ayanokōji gives her a future-oriented promise.

He also retains strategic and quasi-sovereign judgment over whether her path succeeds.

The relationship is neither alliance nor simple rivalry.

## 26.5 Ayanokōji–Hiyori

Shared books remain the low-pressure foundation.

The relationship deepens because Hiyori chooses to risk a difficult question and uses trust to intervene in Ryūen's politics.

Ayanokōji revises his estimate of her upward.

## 26.6 Ayanokōji–Kei

The relation crosses into explicit reciprocal romance.

Its origin remains asymmetrical.

Its future is still organized partly through a curriculum Kei cannot fully see.

The key Year 1 question becomes whether negotiated partnership can eventually outgrow the protector/host structure that produced it.

# 27. Class constitutions at the Year 1 endpoint

## `POLITY-HORIKITA`
### Developmental pluralism with increasing internal accountability

The polity now possesses stronger mechanisms of correction:

- Horikita as visible leader;
- Hirata as recovered social stabilizer;
- Keisei as academically strong loyal opposition;
- Sudō as a developmental success capable of generating motivation in others;
- Kei as social infrastructure;
- Kushida as unresolved high-capacity threat/resource;
- and Ayanokōji as hidden power increasingly challenged by Horikita's demands for legibility.

The central question is no longer whether the class can improve.

It is whether the improvement can become **self-sustaining and publicly governable**.

## `POLITY-RYUEN`
### Authoritarian mobilization with increasingly voluntary loyalty

Ryūen is active again and eager for stronger contests.

His capacity to learn from defeat is real.

His ethics remain coercive.

Hiyori's intervention reveals an emerging possibility of internal correction that does not rely on defeating him from outside.

Whether he accepts such correction when it conflicts with victory remains unresolved.

## `POLITY-ICHINOSE`
### Solidaristic legitimacy under a crisis of adversarial adaptation

Ichinose retains extraordinary trust and class attachment.

Kanzaki increasingly functions as loyal opposition.

The ending of the Horikita alliance forces the polity to compete without external moral buffering.

The unresolved task is to make solidarity strategically generative rather than only defensively humane.

## `POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN`
### Aristocratic sovereignty with explicit private-priority risk

Sakayanagi remains highly effective and openly claims the right to privilege Ayanokōji even across class interest.

Her theory is honest about attachment and dangerous for fiduciary governance.

Class A's future therefore depends not only on her ability but on whether her private priorities can remain compatible with collective responsibility.

# 28. `実力 / jitsuryoku` after Volume 11.5

Volume 11.5 adds two important refinements to the Year 1 ability model.

The current sequence is:

1. **possessed ability** — what a person can do;
2. **legible ability** — what others can recognize;
3. **ecological ability** — what becomes valuable in a particular environment;
4. **socially usable ability** — what can be made available to collective action;
5. **distributed ability** — what emerges from coordination among unlike people;
6. **developmental ability** — what a person may become rather than only what they currently are;
7. **generative ability** — the capacity to make other people's capacities more usable or inspire new movement;
8. **recorded ability** — what the institution honestly preserves as result;
9. **self-concealed ability** — capacity deliberately withheld for social autonomy, now shown not to be unique to Ayanokōji.

Matsushita is important because she normalizes strategic underperformance without equating all hidden performers.

Keisei is important because he recognizes that academic ability cannot monopolize class value.

Ike and Shinohara are important because development itself becomes generative.

Horikita is important because leadership increasingly consists of making capacities usable while accepting correction.

Ayanokōji remains exceptional because he can operate across nearly all these layers.

But Volume 11.5 makes the title's challenge less compatible than ever with a simple power ranking.

# 29. Ordinary life and counter-curriculum at the end of Year 1

The volume provides some of the strongest Year 1 evidence that ordinary life is not merely camouflage.

Ayanokōji explicitly calls an ordinary three-year school life his first self-chosen goal.

The year has given him:

- books;
- friends;
- seasonal memory;
- loneliness at separation;
- private jokes;
- names;
- rivalry he enjoys;
- people whose growth interests him;
- and a girlfriend.

But Volume 11.5 also shows why ordinary life cannot simply dissolve the White Room grammar.

He repeatedly converts ordinary experiences into structured learning.

Romance becomes curriculum.
Jealousy becomes data.
Friendship becomes developmental influence.
Rivalry becomes cultivation.
Legacy becomes a possible four-class experiment.

The counter-curriculum has therefore succeeded in giving him new ends.

It has not yet guaranteed that he will pursue those ends through reciprocal methods.

That is the mature Year 1 result.

| Ryūen coaching | greater tactical sophistication | Ryūen may accept/reject | development without moral improvement |
| Matsushita deception | protects Ayanokōji concealment | information environment deliberately authored | strategically understandable, epistemically manipulative |
| Kei confession | reciprocal relationship | explicit mutual romantic answer and less coercive physical protocol | genuine reciprocity inside unequal knowledge |
| Kei hidden curriculum | possible learning for both | pedagogical purpose not fully shared with her | continuing autonomy problem |

The table captures a central second-pass conclusion:

> **Effectiveness and ethical legitimacy must be evaluated separately.**

Year 1 contains many cases where a coercive method works.

That does not prove coercion was necessary.

It also contains cases where voluntary peer development works.

Those scenes matter as counterevidence against any claim that growth in this setting requires hidden authorship.

# 31. Japanese narration, voice, and key lexical distinctions

Volume 11.5 depends heavily on lexical precision.

## 31.1 `偽者` and `紛れもない私`

Horikita's prologue uses the language of falsity and then immediately reabsorbs the constructed self into genuine biography.

The point is not that `偽者` loses meaning.

The imitation was real.

The point is that **constructed does not mean ontologically disposable**.

## 31.2 `生まれて初めて`

Ayanokōji's statement that ordinary school life is the first goal he has set himself gives extraordinary weight to the choice.

It is not casual preference language.

It is autobiographical rupture.

## 31.3 `本当の実力主義`

Nagumo's phrase should remain attributed to him.

`本当の / true, real` is political rhetoric here.

The novel does not certify his design as the authentic meaning of `実力主義`.

## 31.4 `大切な人`

Hirata's fear of creating another important person clarifies why universal care can function as emotional defense.

Particular attachment is not merely warmth; it creates asymmetric risk.

## 31.5 `味方じゃない……だけど信頼できる人`

Ichinose's description of Ayanokōji—“not an ally, but someone I can trust”—is one of the most useful relational categories in the volume.

It refuses the assumption that political opposition cancels interpersonal trust.

## 31.6 `介錯`

The word carries the sense of a finishing/mercy blow rather than ordinary support.

Its presence after the intimate promise deliberately darkens the scene.

Ayanokōji still imagines himself as someone who may judge the terminal point of another leader's path.

## 31.7 `記憶に残る生徒`

Manabu's legacy language redirects Ayanokōji from merely experiencing school toward affecting the memory of others.

It is the verbal trigger for the four-class developmental thought.

## 31.8 `育成し、成長させる`

The verbs are explicitly developmental and active.

Ayanokōji imagines himself causing or cultivating growth, not merely watching it.

## 31.9 `教科書`

Calling Kei a textbook is the sharpest instrumental metaphor in the romance ending.

It should not be softened into “learning together.”

Ayanokōji is describing her as an educational medium for experiences he lacks.

## 31.10 `掛け替えのない存在`

This returns the irreplaceability question from Volume 7.5.

The difference is that it now appears **after** explicit reciprocal confession.

Ayanokōji still cannot say that Kei has already reached that status.

He can imagine and hope for it.

## 31.11 `祈ろう`

The ending verb—“let me pray / hope”—is epistemically crucial.

Ayanokōji does not announce emotional certainty.

He expresses aspiration toward a self he wants to be.

# 33. Motifs and recurring language

## Mirror

The volume opens with Horikita looking at a reflected self and deciding that constructed history still belongs to her.

The motif is identity as self-inspection rather than simple revelation.

## Hair

Hair records imitation, memory, preference, and future openness.

The short haircut is a choice, not an eternal essence.

## Handshake and embrace

The volume repeatedly uses touch to register transitions:

- Manabu/Nagumo handshake;
- Manabu/Suzune embrace;
- Ayanokōji/Suzune grief contact;
- Ayanokōji/Kei embrace.

Touch often appears where institutional categories become insufficient.

## Rain

Rain externalizes Ichinose's suspended, self-punitive state and gives Ayanokōji a field in which care becomes leverage.

## Books

Books remain Hiyori's low-pressure relational medium.

At the ending, the book metaphor becomes darker when Kei is called a `教科書`.

The volume therefore contains two models of reading:

- books as shared culture between equals;
- person as book to be learned from.

That contrast is one of the most elegant motifs in the novel.

## Memory

Graduation turns memory into a criterion of existence.

Manabu asks what remains in other people after departure.

Ayanokōji's answer begins shifting from consumption of experience to production of developmental consequence.

## Names

Hirata's chosen given-name intimacy continues Year 1's use of names as relational self-definition.

## One year

The volume repeatedly thinks in one-year horizons:

- Year 1 completed;
- another school year opening;
- Ichinose's one-year promise;
- class development over the next year;
- Ayanokōji wondering whether he will still remain.

Time becomes developmental horizon rather than merely school calendar.

# 34. Formal and literary strengths

## 34.1 The volume closes a competitive year without needing a climactic exam

This is formally confident.

The book understands that after repeated institutional ordeals, the meaningful question is what those ordeals produced in the people who survived them.

## 34.2 Horikita's hair symbolism is unusually disciplined

The text anticipates the simplistic “return to true self” reading and corrects it internally.

That gives the identity arc greater psychological plausibility.

## 34.3 The romance ending preserves contradiction rather than using romance as cure

A lesser version of the scene could make confession proof that Ayanokōji has become normal.

Instead, the interior narration becomes more disturbing and more hopeful at the same time.

That keeps the character problem alive.

## 34.4 Manabu's farewell unifies the entire year

“Leave something behind” converts:

- hidden ability;
- friendship;
- leadership;
- development;
- class rivalry;
- and ordinary memory

into one future-facing question.

## 34.5 Matsushita functions as epistemic correction rather than power-creep reveal

Her chapter broadens the hidden-ability problem without requiring her to be another secret superhuman.

That is a healthier use of concealment than merely adding another top-tier rival.

## 34.6 The volume uses competing models of growth

Growth comes through:

- imitation and rejection;
- peer example;
- rivalry;
- institutional oversight;
- honest refusal;
- separation;
- coercive pressure;
- romance;
- and self-chosen experiment.

No single model is allowed to monopolize development.

# 35. Critical reservations and interpretive cautions

## 35.1 Successful boundary violation is still boundary violation

The Horikita crying scene is emotionally effective.

That should not become proof that ignoring her explicit request for solitude was inherently justified.

The later positive interpretation belongs to Horikita and matters. It does not erase the method.

## 35.2 Ichinose intimacy risks collapsing political crisis into protagonist-centered emotional rescue

The scene preserves more complexity than a simple rescue because Ichinose retains class identity and the `介錯` line darkens Ayanokōji's role.

Even so, the structure increasingly makes Ayanokōji the privileged witness to major female characters' private breakdowns.

That pattern deserves continued scrutiny.

## 35.3 Ryūen's “growth” is explicitly morally limited

The volume itself helps here: the lesson is about operational cleanliness, not humane reform.

Any synthesis that calls him morally redeemed at this endpoint would exceed the evidence.

## 35.4 Tsukishiro lore is deliberately unreliable

Readers seeking system lore may be tempted to promote his statements into facts.

The volume directly warns against doing so.

## 35.5 Romance creates an informed-consent asymmetry the narrative does not resolve

Kei's romantic choice is real.

The hidden developmental curriculum is also real.

The relationship cannot yet be treated as fully transparent partnership.

## 35.6 Ayanokōji's four-class idea could become narratively totalizing

At this boundary it is only an emerging thought.

If later material makes every rival's development primarily the protagonist's authored project, the series would risk annexing the ensemble's agency.

The Year 1 corpus already contains strong counterevidence—Horikita, Ike/Shinohara, Hiyori, Kanzaki—that development can be distributed and self-authored.

Those counterexamples should remain analytically active.

# 36. Prior-analysis and hypothesis audit

The second pass produces the following revisions at the Y1V11.5 boundary.

## Confirmed

- Ordinary life is genuinely important to Ayanokōji.
- Horikita's Year 1 movement is toward independent leadership rather than better performance as Ayanokōji's proxy.
- Kei's relation to Ayanokōji has developed beyond the original coerced collaborator structure.
- Ichinose's central political problem is adversarial resilience, not falsity of kindness.
- Ryūen's authority is becoming more voluntarily sustained without losing coercive ethics.
- Nagumo represents a competing institutional theory of merit rather than merely a stronger upperclass antagonist.

## Strengthened

- **Ayanokōji self-authorship:** ordinary school life is explicitly the first goal he says he chose himself.
- **Horikita integration:** her identity arc rejects purification and preserves prior selves as lived history.
- **Reciprocal development:** peer correction and horizontal imitation are now direct countermodels to developer-centered growth.
- **Ayanokōji–Horikita reciprocity:** she challenges, tests, and receives jurisdiction rather than merely executing hidden answers.
- **Kei romance:** explicit mutual confession materially strengthens the relationship beyond protected-partner utility.
- **Legacy:** Manabu's farewell converts Year 1 development into a question of what Ayanokōji leaves in other people.

## Corrected

- **“Horikita returns to her true self by cutting her hair.”** Rejected. Both the constructed and current selves are hers.
- **“Ayanokōji's ordinary self is merely a mask.”** Rejected. The ordinary-life goal is explicitly self-chosen and emotionally meaningful.
- **“Ayanokōji has completed humanization by becoming Kei's boyfriend.”** Rejected. Romance is immediately translated into hidden curriculum and remains epistemically unstable.
- **“Kei is only a textbook/tool.”** Rejected as total explanation. The text adds explicit reciprocity, embodied tenderness, irreplaceability as hoped-for future, and a final prayer to cherish her.
- **“Tsukishiro's White Room exposition is canonical fact at this boundary.”** Rejected. His testimony is internally marked as potentially fabricated.
- **“Horikita promises never to expel Kushida.”** Rejected. Her commitment remains conditional on future contribution and necessity.
- **“Ryūen becomes more ethical.”** Not established. He becomes more reflective and technically sophisticated.

## Weakened

- **Horikita as Ayanokōji-dependent developmental project:** strongly weakened. Her end-of-year self-authorship, alliance separation, test wager, and Kushida jurisdiction are real independent actions.
- **Humanization as stable ethical restraint:** weakened. Ayanokōji has more humane options but continues using coercive and instrumental methods.

## Newly foregrounded

- **reciprocal authorship** as the central Year 1 endpoint;
- **legacy** as a motive capable of transforming Ayanokōji's ordinary-life project;
- **hidden curriculum** inside romance;
- **self-concealed ability** as a broader class phenomenon;
- **institutional conscience** through Mashima;
- **separation as autonomy-preserving relation** through Horikita and Ichinose;
- and **the difference between a self-chosen purpose and a legitimate purpose imposed on someone else**.

# 41. Final synthesis

Volume 11.5 closes Year 1 by asking what remains after a person stops living for somebody else's answer.

Horikita provides the first response.

She spent years shaping herself around Manabu.

She does not escape by declaring that life fake and returning to a pure original identity.

She takes ownership of the whole history.

The long-haired imitator was her.
The short-haired student is her.
The future version may look different again.

Self-authorship is not purification.

It is the authority to decide what inherited material will mean next.

Ayanokōji reaches the same threshold under radically different conditions.

The White Room gave him capacities, habits, expectations, and a future role.

ANHS gave him something else:

- friends;
- rivals;
- books;
- seasons;
- embarrassment;
- grief at separation;
- people whose development interests him;
- a romantic relationship;
- and a three-year goal he can explicitly call his own.

That is real freedom.

It is not yet ethical maturity.

Manabu's farewell exposes the difference.

If freedom only means “nobody else decides my purpose,” then Ayanokōji has already made enormous progress.

If freedom also requires acknowledging that **other people possess the same claim to authorship**, the problem is much harder.

His first answer to legacy is to develop people.

That answer is compelling because it is genuinely self-chosen.

It is dangerous for the same reason.

The White Room was also a developmental project.

The decisive question is therefore not whether Ayanokōji becomes a developer.

He already is one.

It is whether he can become a developer who understands that another person's development does not belong to him.

Volume 11.5 supplies evidence on both sides.

He transfers Kushida jurisdiction to Horikita.

He lets Horikita test him rather than demanding trust.

He values Keisei's plan to challenge a leader.

He notices Ike and Shinohara developing through one another without his intervention.

He accepts that Hiyori sees something he initially missed.

He gives Ichinose a future rather than simply declaring her current value.

Kei must answer the confession herself.

Yet he also:

- uses jealousy as data;
- pressures Ichinose through her concern for him;
- retains `介錯` judgment;
- overrides Horikita's request for solitude;
- tutors Ryūen in more effective concealment of severe tactics;
- authors Matsushita's model of him;
- and turns romance into a textbook.

The contradiction therefore survives the entire year.

The final Kei scene is the best possible endpoint because it contains the whole problem in miniature.

The relationship is real.

The curriculum is real.

Her answer is real.

His uncertainty is real.

His instrumental metaphor is real.

His hope for irreplaceability is real.

The prayer is real precisely because he does not know whether he has become the person capable of fulfilling it.

Year 1 therefore does not end with the hidden genius finally revealing his power.

It ends with a person who knows how to control almost every external environment confronting the one environment he cannot yet reliably model:

> **the relationship between his own chosen purposes and the independent purposes of the people he has begun to care about.**

That is the mature Year 1 transition.

The series began by asking what a school means by `実力`.

By Volume 11.5, the harder question is:

> **What is ability for, once the person possessing it becomes free enough to choose—and what limits must that freedom recognize in everyone else?**
