# Year 3 Volume 2 — Other lives and the limits of knowing

## Source, boundary and central claim

This reading covers the complete Japanese digital Y3V02: 3,756 normalized paragraphs across 26 spine items, including the afterword, colophons and all 22 illustration/paratext occurrences. The three external glyph assets were inspected and restored at all 47 occurrences. The [source audit](../04%20Source%20Maps%20and%20Support/COTE_Y3_V02_SOURCE_AND_RECONCILIATION_AUDIT.md) identifies the edition, hashes, chapter map and extraction limitations. Paragraph locators refer to that exact normalization, not inferred print pages. No Y3V03–04 narrative evidence enters this artifact. Years 1–2 and Y3V01 remain dated predecessors; Year 3 remains incomplete.

Below, E001–E066 mean the corresponding **Y3V02** entries in the [evidence and locator ledger](../04%20Source%20Maps%20and%20Support/COTE_Y3_V02_EVIDENCE_AND_LOCATOR_LEDGER.md). Each separates textual observation, attributed interiority or report, institutional rule, and unresolved interpretation. A character's confident explanation is evidence of that explanation; it is not automatically the explanation of the novel's world.

**V2 tests what remains outside the roles through which Kiyotaka understands people.** A classmate can have a university purpose unrelated to his class project. A follower can want participation beyond insurance. A friend can become affectively particular before he chooses or understands the change. An apparently ordinary classmate can exceed his intelligence model. His capacity to direct outcomes remains formidable, but it does not supply exhaustive knowledge of other lives or of himself. Ordinary encounters consequently become developmental evidence rather than connective material between exams.

The strongest revision concerns Hiyori. The text now gives sustained, relationship-specific interior evidence of unchosen happiness, fear of loss, tentative liking and emerging indispensability. Continuing to describe her only as a possible exception would underread this volume. Yet Kiyotaka folds that discovery into the vocabulary of experiment and disassembly. New feeling and retained proprietary habits are simultaneous findings. The ethical question becomes what authority Hiyori will have within the relationship whose particular value he is beginning to recognize.

## Narrative architecture: knowing an other, encountering a remainder

Nanase's opening memory precedes the leisurely social sequence and prevents its apparent peace from becoming the whole reader horizon. Her genuine attachment supplies the material for an operational persona. The final observer conversation returns to this hidden structure; Horikita's encounter with Ishigami then opens another channel into the concealed history. The volume therefore encloses ordinary learning within unequal distributions of knowledge without establishing that every ordinary event was staged. Nanase's recruitment was arranged; Kiyotaka's pleasure at finding a book for Yoshida is not thereby revealed as somebody's operation. E001–005, E055–063.

The chapter sequence moves from fitting into C, to a quiet examination, to knowing another person and seeking knowledge, to Yamamura's courage, mixed fortune, observation, and the beginning of another story. These movements repeatedly unsettle a supposedly completed assessment. Kiyotaka ends the boys' outing with a classmate-sufficiency judgment, but the outing has exposed a limit in his own experience of love. Shiraishi later refuses the explanatory closure of a classmate profile. Hiyori proves neither fully knowable through strategic importance nor negligible when strategically inconvenient. The titles support this pattern; they do not license a universal hidden-plan thesis.

Changes of focalization matter. Kiyotaka's delayed approaches to Hiyori are legible to him as fear of causing pain, whereas the B-side gathering shows her reading them as indifference and preparing to end the friendship. The reader can identify the misunderstanding before either participant repairs it. Kaneda's jealousy and Ryūen's possible exploitation occupy different levels of access. Watanabe's quiet account of Shibata's feelings at the gym is not heard by Kiyotaka. This is an information architecture, not one narrator transparently reporting everybody's mind. E037, E039–044, E050.

The ending changes temporal position as well. Horikita's retrospective voice alludes to an institution and history not yet known by the scene-time Horikita. Her description of an arranged encounter points toward later involvement; it does not deliver that involvement here. Converting the prolepsis into a completed White Room discovery would erase the very gap on which the ending depends. E063.

There is no new class transfer, letter change or expulsion in this volume. The stable polity names keep the institutional population distinct from its current rank and ruler. C's conversation preserves memories of Arisu's achievements and a plurality of attitudes toward Kiyotaka: Nishikawa's suspicion, Yoshida's beginning trust, Kitō's direct question about motive, and Shiraishi's insistence on private life. These are politically meaningful differences, even though none constitutes a binding constitutional check. E012–015, E028.

The exam's initial announcement withholds the detailed rules until the following week while stating that the test begins tomorrow. The announced class-point schedule is +50, +20, 0 and −25; tie treatment is not initially specified. Kiyotaka builds his conduct hypothesis partly from the teacher's wording, then triangulates C, A and D remarks. His estimate of more than 90 percent confidence is an attributed estimate, not a calibrated probability supplied by this analysis. He withholds both some interpretive distinctions and the identity of the A information channel. That channel does not prove Kushida accepted his earlier offer. E011–012, E029–030.

Horikita reaches the relevant hypothesis independently while cleaning Ibuki's spills. Her reasoning is socially situated without being supplied by Kiyotaka. Sudō and Onodera are already studying, showing preparation generated outside the leader's immediate command. All four polities ultimately adopt appropriate conduct. The result therefore refuses a simple hierarchy in which the cleverest deduction mechanically wins: D receives +50; A and B share the second-place award at +10 each; C loses 25 because two students are ill. The official explanation says Hoshinomiya's unauthorized physical contact did not cause a deduction. The students' proposed source of their illness remains a hypothesis. E025, E042, E049, E052.

This is a modest but consequential test of public sovereignty. Kiyotaka could make the sick students bear the performance demand. Instead, he permits absence and prohibits blame; Hashimoto has already conveyed the latter stance. This protects persons from being reduced to a score and demonstrates initiative compatible with the leader's policy. It does not show Kiyotaka unable to govern, voluntarily surrendering all control, or Horikita witnessing a genuine personal collapse. The distinction is between accepting a bounded adverse result and receiving another person's authority over one's purposes.

## Horikita: recovery becomes work, argument and negotiated dependence

Horikita's recovery is neither instantaneous nor simply private. She tells others she is fine while acknowledging otherwise internally; familiar noise and friction at dinner restore a recognizable social world. Her appraisal of Ibuki's earlier kick holds two judgments together: the intervention helped, and its danger remains objectionable. The sequence values peer care without making usefulness a retrospective permission slip for every method. E065, E022.

Her investigation begins by admitting that her prior view of Kei was inadequate. Kei contributes relational knowledge while retaining a limit on what she will disclose; later, the move from given name to surname becomes a choice over how the old intimacy is carried forward. Horikita learns that dating did not grant Kei full access to Kiyotaka's past. The absence of such knowledge is itself significant evidence about the previous relationship, but references to conversations about food and clothes do not disclose their contents. They cannot support invented preferences in a future monograph. E021, E058.

Kushida's confession about the fabricated Yagami middle-school link supplies new knowledge to Horikita and Ibuki. It does not constitute a new event in the underlying history or reveal the White Room to them. Kushida's admission that kindness has become habitual likewise complicates a binary of true hostility and false warmth. Performance can become practiced disposition; voluntary help can be hers while dislike persists. Horikita's project has produced a relationship in which others can offer their own reasons, not merely enact her preservation decision. That does not backdate consent to its original costs. E023–024.

Kōenji exposes a different limit. Horikita's first effort to compel participation fails against his contract and threatened opposition. She later purchases a week of conduct for 500,000 private points, moving beyond her unspoken 300,000 ceiling after an initial 200,000 offer. Compliance, rather than the accuracy of her hypothesis or the class's final result, governs the bargain. The text establishes agreement, not a payment receipt. Sudō's fairness objection survives her use of personal funds: a privilege can reshape collective membership even when its immediate bill is privately paid. Her resourcefulness and the polity's inequality are both real. E017, E026–027.

Sudō's promise does not pretend to replace Kiyotaka's functions wholesale. It offers a particular ally whose reliability has its own history, and Horikita's private gratitude recognizes that difference. The resulting independence is not isolation, invulnerability or freedom from inherited influence. It consists in reasoning, coordinating and accepting support after separation, while still having to govern difficult members and emotional injury. The conduct result supplies a second kind of evidence after V1's loss: not a triumphant reversal of all deficits, but a functioning coalition that reaches the right interpretation and carries out its policy.

## Council procedure and the terms of curiosity

The first-year assault hearing makes institutional behavior visible at a useful scale. Kusanagi of A and Maki of D arrive with different reported recovery periods, disputed testimony and no direct camera coverage of the incident. Horikita and Nanase use silence and threatened penalties to secure attention, hear both boys and reject the claim that A status deserves favorable treatment. A linguistic slip and the lack of objection suggest a third participant, possibly a girl who struck both. This remains a hypothesis: neither identity nor the complete incident is established. Horikita nevertheless resists an easy disposal because future victims could bear its cost. E018–020.

These acts demonstrate reasons and procedural equality more clearly than office possession alone. They only partially approach the inherited council-contestability test, which asks about contestable allocation, exit and independent correction. Refusing rank privilege in a hearing is relevant evidence; it is not a complete constitution. Her willingness to consider Nanase as successor regardless of D affiliation further separates fitness from class prestige, but no appointment or successful succession has occurred.

The final investigation then subjects Horikita herself to authored circumstances. Ishigami arranges the council summons to secure a private encounter and negotiates anonymity before giving the Nanase lead. Horikita reserves a duty to report sufficiently serious matters and would refuse a wager or contract; her acceptance is best-efforts confidentiality, not unconditional secrecy. He creates curiosity, limits what he initially supplies, then withdraws far enough that she asks for investigative help. She is neither a passive puppet nor the sole author of the resulting choice. E059–062.

Her belief in Nanase is now contestable, but suspicion is not established guilt. Ishigami's claim to know Kiyotaka only in a class-related way and his account of Nanase's dislike are addressed statements with unresolved completeness. The ending's arranged-encounter language supports scrutiny of access and incentive design, not omniscient attribution of every consequence to Ishigami.

## Kiyotaka's ordinary, strategic and authorial layers

The boys' outing gives supporting characters futures that do not begin with Kiyotaka's plan. Shimazaki's university preparation and five-hour study routine are his reported project; Yoshida weighs a decent university against romance and leisure. The latter priorities are not evidence of intellectual worthlessness. Kiyotaka can recommend books while concealing the non-transferable conditions of his childhood learning. His satisfaction when a suitable book is found arrives through cooperation, alongside a partial truth about his own competence. E004–005.

Their drinks provide a small theory of preference. Shimazaki dislikes black coffee but uses it for focus; the largest tea bottle is not necessarily the best choice if the extra quantity goes unused. Behavior cannot be mapped directly onto liking, nor quantity onto value. These distinctions will matter when Hiyori chooses one flower. A monograph should record instrumental consumption, reported taste and the immediate situation separately rather than infer a stable preference from one purchase. E007–008.

The rain conversation allows Kiyotaka to admit envy of Yoshida's capacity for love and a gap in the feeling he sought through dating Kei. Yoshida does not press for an exhaustive explanation. This is meaningful voluntary disclosure and acceptance of ordinary conversation as help. It still does not give Yoshida consequential protective or corrective authority over Kiyotaka. The old strict weakness test stays open while the evidence for candid limitation grows. His closing judgment that these boys are adequately understood as classmates shows how readily experience returns to a functional classification. E009–010.

That authorial habit remains active elsewhere. He cultivates A's hatred by allowing Ike to retain a false payment implication; the scene is meant to coordinate A against him and display C's composure. He converts Watanabe's requested gym cover into a possible reintegration environment for D's reform group. He waits for Yamamura's self-expression but directs the resulting wish toward surveillance. These are distinct forms of environmental design. None proves he controls every ensuing emotion or final result. E031, E034–035, E050.

## Hiyori: particularity before a settled theory of love

Kiyotaka's initial avoidance is already a revision to pure strategic indifference. He imagines joining B as an ordinary boy near Hiyori, yet does not regret choosing C for his larger plan. The conflict is between purposes that coexist, not a simple discovery that the strategic choice was false. Fear of her sadness and his own difficulty finding words delay the very conversation that could reduce the hurt. On the B side, Hiyori interprets the delay as evidence that their closeness mattered little to him. Her intended withdrawal belongs to her attempt to live with class conflict; it is not merely a test engineered for his benefit. E028, E037, E040.

At the library, her hiding is visible resistance. Kiyotaka sets himself a limit on pursuit if contact is unwelcome, apologizes for the delay and asks sincerely to speak. Hiyori emerges and the misunderstanding is repaired. The scene establishes a local response to reluctance and a willingness to explain one failure. It does not establish complete informed choice about his history, the class experiment or his later wish to use the relationship for self-discovery. Kaneda's existing library companionship also matters: Hiyori has continued an ordinary life while Kiyotaka was absent. She is not a reserved resource awaiting his return. E043–044.

The interior evidence intensifies when Kiyotaka cannot immediately affirm that her hatred would be irrelevant. He imagines the place beside her occupied by someone else and registers disturbance. Shared reading produces happiness; her category in his mind has changed without a deliberate decision to move it. Natural given-name address participates in this change without being a calculated declaration. The text supports affective particularity and incomplete self-knowledge. It does not establish an actual romantic rival, an already mutual exclusive relationship or a final diagnosis of love. E045.

The single poppy makes this development concrete. Hiyori values one flower because it is beautiful, against a sales logic that would favor more. Kiyotaka pays fewer than 400 points, she accepts the gift and both articulate an ordinary day made happy. His public apology rationale is narrower than his private wish to bring her joy. He then wants to retain the memory. The earlier bottle's lesson about quantity reappears at a different level: value depends on fit and relation, not accumulation. The illustration of her tears and the wrapped flower reinforces the emotional focus without independently proving an intention. E046.

Hashimoto's challenge produces tentative naming: perhaps he likes her; perhaps this is the beginning of a feeling not present toward the others. The uncertainty is part of the evidence, not a reason to disregard it. Hashimoto supplies the possible first-love interpretation and his own claim that another girl can replace the first. That replaceability claim belongs to Hashimoto's worldview and reported experience. It cannot settle Kiyotaka's relation in advance. E047–048.

The closing interior language is deliberately difficult. Hiyori is becoming indispensable, and Kiyotaka wants to experience a feeling whose course he cannot predict. These formulations strongly advance the irreplaceability thread at the level of subjective attachment. In the same movement he calls the experience an experiment and imagines taking both himself and Hiyori apart to understand it. An analyst should neither erase the affection because instrumental language survives nor grant ethical transformation because affection has appeared. Experiment can be self-directed inquiry, but including another person without a demonstrated right to revise the experiment creates a different moral problem.

Accordingly the directional judgment is asymmetric. **Kiyotaka toward Hiyori:** emerging, specifically felt non-substitutability is now supported; durable refusal of substitution under cost remains untested. **Hiyori toward Kiyotaka:** attachment, hurt, chosen repair and expressed happiness are supported; full knowledge of what participation would entail is absent. **The relation as a shared institution:** it is not yet shown to grant both parties authority over its ends. Her place has changed in his experience; her jurisdiction within his project has not comparably been demonstrated.

## Other relationships retain their own unfinished lives

Ichinose independently sees the developmental and romantic structure of the gym proposal, including people Kiyotaka omitted. Her welcome is an act of political understanding, not merely execution of his instructions. During their walk she withdraws an intimate hand gesture under the boundary of friendship. Kiyotaka recognizes attraction and their prior intimacy yet finds that the specific present romantic stirring is elsewhere, with Hiyori. Ichinose notices his happiness and refrains from pressing. She is not shown identifying Hiyori. The friends label organizes what may be asked while leaving a history and asymmetry it cannot simply erase. E053–054.

Haruka's overture likewise has more than one author. She wonders whether she caused the transfer, brings Airi's modest television progress and offers a route back into contact. Kiyotaka's surprise and curiosity about Airi are real; the report also supplies evidence that Airi's life has continued beyond school. His comforting account includes an explicit lie about why he had not asked earlier, and he privately identifies the intelligence value of the repaired relationship. The old group is not reconstituted by the return of a nickname or a possible call. Airi's later success neither cancels the earlier injury nor proves that expulsion was justified. E038.

Yamamura asks for confidence and a distinctive contribution. Kiyotaka's willingness to let her find her own words acknowledges a form of growth that faster prompting could suppress. Morishita's mediation helps the meeting occur but includes embarrassment and unrequested bodily intrusion. Yamamura's hesitant speech can therefore signify active courage relative to her earlier withdrawal. The surveillance assignment realizes part of her stated desire while also making it useful to Kiyotaka. His assurance to himself that the risks are manageable is not a consent statement by Yamamura or Shiraishi. E033–035.

Hashimoto wants an active place after giving up transfer insurance and breaking off his Ryūen contacts, a change Kaneda corroborates. He acknowledges distrust and still wants more than a passive safe graduation. His anticipation of the no-blame policy is productive subordinate initiative. His pressure over Hiyori mixes class concern, friendship and a replaceability theory. None of these observations warrants declaring him morally rehabilitated; together they prevent reducing him to a single survival motive. E036, E039, E047–049.

Newly foregrounded classmates require the same care. Shiraishi's reported solitary weekdays and social weekends may remain part of her ordinary routine despite a covert role; exposure of that role does not prove every earlier detail false. Nishikawa's suspicion and reported habits establish a situated classmate, not an operative. Morishita modulates extravagant teasing under rival observation; her expensive-drink performance and comic mother story require audience-sensitive reading. Utomiya's possible romantic motive at the gym and Akiyama's report of a first-year girl's extraordinary running remain separate uncertainties. No unnamed girl may be identified by convenience. E006–008, E014–015, E028, E041, E051, E066.

Reciprocal influence on Kiyotaka is no longer merely prospective. Yoshida helps him articulate a lack without demanding mastery first. ## Ability, adults, surveillance and competing accounts of protection

The displayed OAA values of 96/81/66/77 and total 80 contrast with the recalled 51/60/37/60 and total 51. Kiyotaka distinguishes newly expressed ability from Sudō's actual acquisition through growth. The interface is an instrument of dated public legibility; it is not a biography, complete ceiling or measure of moral authority. Horikita's independent inference, Hashimoto's anticipatory policy communication and Ichinose's reading of group dynamics demonstrate generative competence in different forms. An unnamed student's reported sustained 20 km/h run supplies no verified identity or developmental origin. E013, E025, E049, E051, E053.

Adult institutions are permeable but not uniformly powerless. Official scoring rejects the intended significance of Hoshinomiya's arm contact, while camera expansion changes expectations of visibility without filling the assault's blind spot. Career preparation points beyond the class ladder toward university and employment futures. Tsukishiro's provision of care shows how extra-school resources can author school participation. These mechanisms should remain distinct from any unproved national political explanation. E002, E011, E018, E042, E052.

Nanase's memory revises the literal death account by showing Eiichirō alive in that hospital scene with prolonged impaired consciousness. It does not establish his condition at every later date. The memory also explains how a sincere affect could support instructed deception. Her original hostility toward Atsuomi and her treatment of Kiyotaka as a key are distinguishable from her present desire to help him by preventing the future harm she expects him to cause. The expectation is hers, not a narrated future certainty. E001–003.

The back-to-back restaurant meeting adds an assigned observer role for Shiraishi and a request by Nanase for unpredictable early expulsion. Shiraishi declines within an ordinary three-year-student condition and describes limited knowledge of others' assignments. Nanase's intermediate affiliation and warning about Shirogane's wishes do not yield a complete command chain. Shiraishi's knowledge of Kiyotaka's location and management of earlier listeners are real operational evidence; the channel, full ability and training origin remain unresolved. Her dislike of Arisu as an obstacle does not prove she caused Arisu's departure. E055–057.

Protection consequently splits into incompatible purposes. Nanase can call expulsion help because she defines the danger as future Kiyotaka and his potential victims. Kiyotaka can call inquiry manageable because he trusts his ability to handle both outcomes. Horikita can insist on further investigation for people not present at the hearing. These claims require identifying the protected party, the predicted harm and who gets to revise the protector's judgment. Benevolent vocabulary alone cannot arbitrate them.

## Japanese form, embodied scenes and literary assessment

The [Japanese passage index](../04%20Source%20Maps%20and%20Support/COTE_Y3_V02_JAPANESE_PASSAGE_INDEX.md) records 35 targeted entries. The recurrence of 凡庸 connects Nanase's recruitment history to the hearing for the reader without giving Horikita that knowledge. 試験 and 特別 are evidentially important distinctions in the announced exam. Kiyotaka's affective language moves through disturbance, happiness, tentative liking and becoming indispensable; the progressive aspect of なくてはならないものになりつつある prevents converting an emerging relation into a completed universal state. 実験対象 and the disassembly image preserve the competing interpretive habit. E002, E011–012, E020, E045–048.

Speech is relational action. Yamamura's pauses can record chosen effort; Morishita's register changes with audience and surveillance; Kushida can describe practiced kindness without renouncing every hostility. Kei's address choice marks a revised boundary. Ishigami mirrors Horikita's reasoning to make a confidential exchange negotiable. These are written-Japanese observations at particular encounters, not claims about an actor's vocal delivery or a universally reproducible voice. A future operational monograph must preserve recipient, purpose and evidential limits.

The [visual audit](../04%20Source%20Maps%20and%20Support/COTE_Y3_V02_ILLUSTRATION_AND_PARATEXT_AUDIT.md) treats poses and objects as embodied evidence while keeping intentions text-dependent. Shiraishi's handshake, Morishita's ear intrusion, Hoshinomiya's arm link, Hiyori's withdrawal behind the shelf and accepted flower, and Ichinose's intimate proximity form a pattern of approach and access. Consent differs among them. A shared visual vocabulary of contact does not make the contacts ethically equivalent. Cover repetition and promotional pages are paratext; advertised bonus material not embedded in this EPUB remains outside the read corpus.

The volume's strongest formal achievement is making low-stakes activity bear high interpretive pressure. Shopping, reading, meals, a gym proposal and a flower expose the inadequacy of score-based or role-based knowledge. The conduct exam strengthens this by producing a correct deduction with an adverse result and a morally significant response to that result. Alternating access to the two sides of Hiyori's misunderstanding gives emotional delay an intelligible cost without forcing an external catastrophe.

There are also reservations. Female vulnerability and bodily embarrassment remain recurrent vehicles for humor or male self-discovery; acknowledging a scene's developmental usefulness does not settle the allocation of embarrassment. Supporting characters receive more individual purposes, yet some interactions return rapidly to Kiyotaka's information economy. The observer reveal risks making ordinary social texture feel retroactively provisional, although the text does not require every detail to be false. The ending's promised larger history and the afterword's relaxed-volume framing pull in different directions. These tensions are properties to track, not grounds for assuming later resolution. E033–035, E038, E055–057, E063–064.

## Reconciliation, inherited questions and provisional synthesis

The V1 snapshot remains correct to its evidence boundary. Its restrained Hiyori finding now requires development, not retrospective erasure: V2 supplies substantially stronger interior evidence. Nanase's hospital memory is revelation of prior state and a correction to any unqualified literal-death account; it is not a newly occurring recovery. Kushida's disclosure is new for its listeners, not new reader history. Horikita's late retrospective narration is a trajectory signal, not a completed discovery. Rumor, narrator probability, reported athletic performance and covert affiliation retain separate confidence limits.

All seventeen rolling homes receive V2 successors because each has a material delta: character histories, class and institutional state, consent and ordinary life, ability and written voice, succession, identities, terminology, longitudinal claims and the handoff questions. Their V1 predecessors stay intact. The forty-two inherited Year 1 identifiers retain their established categories while the new evidence changes several adjudications. The four strict open tests remain open: sufficient voluntary disclosure for informed relational choice; Horikita witnessing a genuine Kiyotaka failure; Hiyori morally redirecting Ryūen; and disclosed weakness carrying consequential protective or corrective authority. Council contestability receives a partial V2 observation rather than full closure.

Compared with the Year 2 boundary, transfer has now yielded a second form of post-separation coordination, not merely the original injury and first recovery. Kei can govern parts of disclosure and address; the old group has a possible repaired successor relation; Airi has reported outside activity; Arisu's actual off-campus conduct remains absent. C's new sovereign accepts a bounded score loss and protects its sick members, while continuing to design other people's informational environments. The imported ruler's competence and the population's capacity to act are becoming distinguishable rather than interchangeable.

The exact-boundary questions are whether Hiyori can revise the relationship's terms at a cost Kiyotaka accepts; whether subjective indispensability survives conflicting ends; whether A sustains distributed coordination under harder conditions; whether Katsuragi's promised constraint is exercised; whether Nanase's protection claim withstands informed challenge; and whether the observer system's identities, sponsors and assignments can be independently separated. These are observation criteria, not retrospective predictions or reconstruction calibration trials.

V2's provisional contribution is therefore a change in the meaning of unfinished development. Kiyotaka's unchosen feeling is no longer merely an analyst's possibility, yet its emergence does not make the other person sovereign within his experiment. Horikita acts independently through relationships rather than after escaping all need for them. Ordinary people acquire histories and purposes that exceed their assigned functions. The year remains open at the point where learning another person's value must still become a practice of answering to that person.
