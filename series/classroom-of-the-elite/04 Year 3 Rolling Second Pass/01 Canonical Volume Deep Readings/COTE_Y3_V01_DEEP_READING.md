# Year 3 Volume 1 — Public sovereignty and the work of separation

## Scope and position

This is the immutable local reading of **Year 3 Volume 1**, based on the complete supplied Japanese EPUB. The analytical boundary is `Y3V01`; the year remains incomplete. The [source audit](../04%20Source%20Maps%20and%20Support/COTE_Y3_V01_SOURCE_AND_RECONCILIATION_AUDIT.md) records the full read, hashes, ten-chapter map, normalization, numerical checks and exclusions. Separately advertised bonus fiction is not embedded and is not claimed as read. Volumes 2–4 were inventoried without reading their narratives.

The [evidence ledger](../04%20Source%20Maps%20and%20Support/COTE_Y3_V01_EVIDENCE_AND_LOCATOR_LEDGER.md) was established before this thesis. References below use its stable `Y3V01-E` identifiers and deterministic original-Japanese locators. Textual events, interior claims, rules, visual facts, inferences and normative judgments remain distinct. Earlier Year 2 judgments retain their own boundary in the [entering register](../03%20Rolling%20Ledgers/COTE_Y3_ENTERING_STATE_AND_HANDOFF_REGISTER.md).

The book performs the architecture’s first actual post-transfer test. It does not restart the cast. Horikita enters with established independent judgment but an unproven ability to sustain a system after its hidden guarantor leaves. Kei enters with peer agency and unresolved romantic grief. Ichinose’s third answer has already exceeded Ayanokōji’s model; its political translation and relational costs now require observation. The Sakayanagi-origin class enters with its academic culture intact and its central governing function vacant.

## Executive thesis: public sovereignty and the work of separation

Volume 1 makes Ayanokōji publicly assessable without yet making him answerable as an author of other people’s conditions. His new classmates demand evidence, argue about criteria and withhold recognition. He wins their mandate through an exceptional raw score, conspicuous enemy targeting and a public explanation of strategy. Yet the funding, alliance and timing of disclosure remain arranged to narrow the conditions under which their consent can be reconsidered. The class comes to see his strength before it acquires a durable means of limiting the purposes to which he will put it. This is an interpretive thesis about the particular arrangement, not a claim that every classmate has become passive (`E009–013`, `E024`, `E040–042`).

The parallel development is recovery through peers. Horikita first loses the ability to inhabit the future she had imagined; Kei is made to suffer the distance between a technically truthful answer and a caring one. Their eventual exchange does something that neither public strength nor exhortation to perform has accomplished. Practical assistance, chosen disclosure, reciprocal recognition and a shared purpose make action possible again. Ayanokōji anticipated that classmates would help Horikita. The source does not show him selecting Kei’s testimony, dictating this encounter or receiving its private knowledge. Their work remains theirs even inside an environment his withdrawal helped create (`E013`, `E044–047`).

The opening therefore supplies two different achievements: imported competence gains a visible mandate, while wounded students begin to rebuild the capacity to act together. Neither achievement closes the year’s harder tests. Public reputation does not establish reciprocal jurisdiction; one recovered evening does not establish durable political independence. The final surveillance scene also prevents the new class’s apparent consolidation from becoming an image of complete informational control.

## Narrative architecture and the distribution of knowledge

The opening plate and seat carry the first argument. Horikita remembers Yamauchi, Sakura and Maezono rather than treating A as an unqualified moral victory. She has moved from pursuing a brother’s standard toward a shared graduation purpose. The familiar adjacency to Ayanokōji appears to preserve the everyday setting in which that purpose can be lived. The reader’s inherited knowledge of the transfer makes her pleasure vulnerable before she knows why. Time, furniture and a class letter become the material form of an assumption about another person’s presence (`E001`).

The following confusion chapter distinguishes verification from comprehension. OAA resolves where he belongs institutionally; it cannot tell the class why he left or what their past with him meant. Multiple students propose explanations from their own concerns. Chabashira’s shock makes adult dependence visible, but her subsequent refusal to permit collective blame reasserts a legitimate institutional boundary. The teacher is neither an omnipotent interpreter nor irrelevant machinery. Hirata’s practical clarity and Kushida’s attention to exposure make the classroom response plural (`E002–003`).

The café scene then gives Horikita an audience instead of the conversation she wants. Ayanokōji’s disclosure about Matsushita makes the asymmetry active: he can reveal a former ally’s hidden contribution while withholding the motive that would let Horikita interpret his own conduct. Her possible exits do not include the private discussion she requests. Her self-accusation about choosing a bad moment should not become the analyst’s excuse for his control of the meeting (`E004–006`).

The fourth chapter (OPF spine 14) rewinds the opening in Ayanokōji’s first person. This is a change of explanatory access, not a later occurrence of the same transfer. Funding, class consent, the alliance and the delayed revelation precede the café rupture in story time even though the reader receives the explanation afterward. His interior qualifies his cruel public language with a developmental aim and confidence in peer recovery. The qualification changes the motive model; it does not make the injury disappear (`E007–015`).

The long crossing-paths chapter lets an exam occupy several lives at once. Horikita’s attention fails; Hirata and Yukimura initiate work; Kushida calculates the danger to her own future; Ryūen’s advisers debate cost and uncertainty. These are separate cognitive centers. Later, the exam chapter initially offers Ryūen’s successful-seeming interpretation while withholding what Shimazaki actually means. The misleading impression is jointly produced by focal access, an angry expression and interrupted dialogue. It should not be explained away as universal narrator dishonesty (`E021–029`, `E036–043`).

The last two chapters separate the main character from important events. Horikita and Kei speak without him. Morishita, Hashimoto and Shiraishi pursue observation on their own initiative and hear Nanase after Horikita has left. The reader receives information no scene shows Ayanokōji possessing. The resulting uncertainty belongs to the current story boundary, not to an invitation to fill in a familiar White Room plot (`E047`, `E054–057`).

## The exam: capacity, score and public meaning

The test draws 100 questions from seven broad subjects and 21 subdivisions. The higher whole-class sum earns two wins; a tied sum gives one each. A short class receives copies of its own lowest score for the missing students, making weakness at the bottom a collective vulnerability. Five ordered representatives then compete against their counterparts for one win each, with no win awarded for a tied duel. The class with more wins receives 100 class points; a tied match splits that award. A 7–0 sweep adds 50 to the winner and deducts 50 from the loser (`spine16:para0067–0098`; `E022`).

The penalty mechanism makes assessment endogenous to strategy. Each class has 100 free penalty units, may buy more for 50,000 private points each before the deadline, and may assign at most 100 to any one opponent. These deductions affect representative contests, not the whole-class score or the later OAA evaluation. Only participating representatives’ penalties become public. This last rule is crucial: Ayanokōji must enter if he wants the magnitude of Ryūen’s targeting to become visible. Staying out would protect a duel outcome while losing the public signal he values.

| Match | Whole-class totals | Representative wins after penalties | Final result |
|---|---|---|---|
| A / D | 2633 / 2712 | D wins four; A’s Mori wins one | D 6–1 |
| B / C | 2327 / 2880 | C wins four; B’s Ishizaki wins one | C 6–1 |

Ichinose buys 25 additional penalties and allocates 25 each to Wang, Yukimura, Kōenji, Hirata and Kushida. Three become representatives. That costs 1.25 million private points, not the much larger hypothetical sums discussed during preparation. Ayanokōji’s advice draws on knowledge of the former class, while Ichinose’s actual intelligence work identifies the consultation environment. The result is complementary production. Giving him all the credit would repeat the explanatory error the text explicitly corrects at `spine19:para0347–0349` (`E038–039`, `E050`).

Ryūen chooses a different objective. He declines to spend heavily in a domain where his class is inferior, names weaker representatives whom a rational opponent has little reason to penalize, and directs every free penalty at Ayanokōji. The move secures a representative win and prevents a sweep. It is a meaningful adaptation from compulsively entering a bad contest. It also recruits the class’s fear and labor into an objective they were not told. Tokitō’s objection concerns the relation between shared work and sovereign purpose, not simply a failure to appreciate cleverness (`E036`).

Ayanokōji’s 100 raw points become zero in the duel against Ishizaki’s 40. The loss is real under the rules. It is not a loss of academic capacity or evidence of an unforeseen collapse. He intentionally trades the extra sweep reward for the public conjunction of exceptional performance and maximal rival caution. Shimazaki recognizes something broader than the initial prediction test: the person may be able to rescue the class. The criterion has changed; the class has not suddenly discovered that zero correctly targeted representatives satisfies the old requirement. A good reading must preserve both the persuasive success and the shift in the terms of evaluation (`E040–042`).

There is a formal reservation here. The sequence makes rival fear help prove the protagonist’s importance, and then his importance explains the rival’s fear. The raw score supplies an independent academic basis, but it cannot independently establish every strategic or ethical claim. The novel risks allowing a spectacular explanation to purchase a general mandate. Its counterweight is that Morishita later names the danger of being used and Kitō still refuses to confuse assent with trust. The reader is given reasons to remain more discriminating than the celebration.

## Ayanokōji: several developments at once

His ordinary participation becomes broader. He can admit an unfamiliar reference, receive Shiraishi’s help, learn from romantic experience, ask a classmate for a second opinion, and become interested in someone from childhood. These are changes in what attracts his attention and which relations he can enter. A fixed description of an affectless machine would miss both the source’s comedy and its tentative movement toward memory (`E007`, `E015–017`, `E030–032`, `E048`, `E051`).

His strategic layer becomes publicly legible. He no longer relies on Horikita’s visible leadership to obscure his own role. The former class’s insider knowledge becomes transferable strategic capital; his new class witnesses a competence it had mainly heard about. His public-sovereignty layer is therefore distinct from merely possessing unusual ability. Authority has to be elicited from a particular audience under particular fears and hopes (`E005`, `E024`, `E038–042`).

His authorial layer also becomes more explicit. He uses the sunk investment to secure time, shapes the shock of departure, allows rivals to acquire selected true information, and offers Kushida alternatives whose different outcomes can all serve his aims. The special capacity is not ordering every move. It is anticipating how other people’s genuine motives will complete an arrangement. The strongest evidence of this capacity should not become an unlimited explanation for every later scene. Nanase’s calls and the peer-care encounter have knowledge boundaries that resist that shortcut (`E013`, `E042`, `E049–050`, `E057`).

His relational layer remains mixed. He values an equal alliance with Ichinose and recognizes her contribution. He grants Hirata a private hearing while refusing Horikita’s request for one. He protects Yamamura’s pace while privately intending to improve a useful tool. He tells Kitō that strength should protect vulnerable classmates while preserving an exception for sufficiently powerful noncontributors. These are not incidental inconsistencies to be tidied into a single moral score. They show how care, hierarchy, audience and strategic purpose change the permissions of each relation (`E012`, `E014`, `E025`, `E052–053`).

His memory of Yuki supplies a particularly narrow development. School life makes the past newly interesting; the former White Room child can begin to resemble a childhood companion rather than discarded educational residue. Yet he immediately predicts that a meeting would produce no new feeling and does not pursue one. The first movement and its foreclosure both belong in the claim. His own prediction about emotion is not an omniscient verdict on what he could ever feel. Neither is the moment evidence that he has accepted obligations toward the people his training left behind (`E048`, `E051`).

## Horikita: impairment, delegation and peer recovery

The book gives Horikita’s distress duration. She cannot simply turn a discovery into the next efficient action. Repetition, self-correction, fatigue and failure to attend make absence an experience rather than a plot notification. Her wish to meet Ayanokōji is inseparable from wanting the relationship to have meant enough for consultation. Reading the whole sequence as a brief failure of professionalism would eliminate the object of her injury (`E004`, `E021`, `E028`).

The strongest counterargument to an independence claim is real: she fails to contribute the strategy she expects of herself and imagines asking the absent person to save her. The answer is not to deny that dependence. It is to distinguish it from the claim that she never made an independent judgment. Year 2’s documented decisions remain decisions she authored. Here, she recognizes impaired judgment and entrusts the exam to Hirata. Delegation does not ensure victory, but it preserves a functioning collective activity when the leader is not ready to supply one (`E023`, `E037`).

Recovery does not arrive through Ibuki’s assault. The kick causes pain and a bleeding hand, and Horikita remains distressed afterward. Kei’s later interpretation that Ibuki may have been waiting out of concern is plausible but does not change what occurred. The source itself lets Horikita call it violence. Treating the action as uncomplicated tough love would substitute an inferred benevolent purpose for the recipient’s actual bodily cost (`E044–045`).

Kei’s approach is different in procedure as well as tone. She asks about adult help, handles the injured wrist carefully and uses a handkerchief for its ordinary purpose. She does not require Horikita to recover in order to deserve assistance. Horikita then encounters a person whose pain and resilience her old social model had underdescribed. The exchange changes the leader’s view of a classmate and the classmate’s ability to smile. It produces knowledge through a relation rather than extracting it as the price of protection (`E045–047`).

Horikita’s romantic hesitation deserves an affirmative but bounded reading. The text supplies remembered bodily pleasure and embarrassment that interrupt an easy denial. It is stronger than an arbitrary reader’s preferred pairing; it is less than a settled declaration by her. Her renewed determination also contains both class purpose and the desire to make Ayanokōji face them. A future inquiry must test whether those purposes can diverge. At this boundary, her statement that she is not alone provides a workable basis for action, and the later council scene corroborates a first improvement in functioning (`E046–047`, `E056`).

## Kei and Ichinose: the contested meaning of reciprocity

Kei’s distress and agency occupy the same day. She attends school despite grief, asks a difficult question, resists a diagnosis of her wish for returned love, unexpectedly tests Ichinose’s claim to help anyone, and later offers care without demanding a romantic substitute. The move toward Horikita does not require being cured first. It supports the inherited distinction between vertical dependence and horizontal capacity more strongly than a sudden display of untroubled composure would (`E033–035`, `E045–047`).

Ichinose’s conduct requires equally careful differentiation. The narrator supports the sincerity of her preference for giving without return. That statement tells us something true about her self-understanding. It does not make her speculation about the cause of Kei’s breakup true. Nor does it make ordinary reciprocity morally inferior. Kei’s response names a recognizable mutual expectation rather than demonstrating an inability to love selflessly (`E034`).

When Kei asks whether that care includes her, Ichinose retracts the universal claim and explains prioritization under finite capacity. The correction is intellectually significant. She no longer promises to save everyone at the cost of failing those she can save. But the scene does not stop at declining a demand. She knowingly presses a false premise about who ended the romance, discloses the meeting to maximize its impact, and uses the distinction between dating and private intimacy as a weapon. Limited capacity can justify some exclusions; it does not explain why the excluded person must receive extra injury. That is the central normative reservation, grounded in the actual sequence rather than a claim that assertive romantic desire is inherently corrupt (`E033–035`).

Her political competence nevertheless grows. Against Amasawa, she identifies the probe and constructs an answer that destabilizes the interrogator’s certainty. The subsequent focal clarification is essential: Ayanokōji did not tell her about the White Room. She deploys a name whose significance she only partly understands. She has become harder to read without becoming fully informed. In the alliance she contributes the social intelligence that makes another person’s strategic inference reliable. The volume therefore supplies real translation of her third answer into political output, accompanied by selective care and continuing informational inequality (`E019–020`, `E039`, `E042`, `E050`).

## Supporting actors, resistance and inherited institutions

Kushida’s two registers survive exposure because the audiences have not become identical. She can want recognition, fear wider disclosure, dislike Horikita and still require her to function as a leader. Her later refusal of a betrayal proposal is credible precisely because the proposal threatens her own Class A future. Ayanokōji offers money and threatens lost secrecy protection, then permits her to disclose the offer because that too can serve him. Her request for time is not acceptance. This is a new test of her self-authored cooperation, not a retroactive erasure of the earlier compact (`E028`, `E049–050`).

Hirata supplies both care and epistemic restraint. He distinguishes a lawful choice from a forced disappearance, combines gratitude with distrust, organizes consultation, and stops the class from treating plausible suspicion as proven treason. These are consequential political acts. Sudō also remains more than blunt dependence: he brings experience of rejection to the opening discussion and continues to care about classmates, while his quick accusation over the penalty result shows that improved judgment is uneven. An exam score of 66 does not make his established developmental history vanish (`E003`, `E014–015`, `E023`, `E037–038`).

Hashimoto’s insurance-seeking explains why the funding hurts. He has correctly diagnosed a governing vacuum and imported competence, but exposure to his chosen solution weakens his ability to trust correction. He misreads Shimazaki and tries to manage the public conversation before understanding it. Morishita’s warning later challenges precisely the tendency to confuse aligned interests with safe dependence. His initiative is real, and so is the possibility that his method of securing a future gives another person too much control over it (`E009`, `E040–042`, `E054`).

Ryūen develops strategic braking while retaining coercion. His decision not to purchase an expensive low-probability win is an actual revision of conduct, even though the intended reputational victory fails. Katsuragi’s advice and Ibuki’s dissent matter without being sovereign. The wall scene makes the aftermath more than a clever loss on a scoreboard: his belief that he can always win later is destabilized. Recalling Sakayanagi opens the question of winning alone. Nothing here yet shows Hiyori changing his moral choice or followers acquiring a protected right to veto it (`E029`, `E036`, `E043`).

The Sakayanagi-origin class also contains capacities that the imported-leader story could otherwise hide. Its quiet study culture predates Ayanokōji. Sanada supplies a way to formulate the recognition test. Shiraishi successfully contests an arbitrary demand before he holds a mandate and later helps with a mundane problem he cannot solve alone. Kitō’s explanation separates betting on Sakayanagi’s competence from devotion to her. These details preserve the polity as a body of people with their own histories, rather than an empty throne waiting for a superior occupant (`E016`, `E024`, `E032`, `E052`).

The new social actors should remain distinct. Nishikawa engineers an outing through rumor, teasing and strategic praise. Shiraishi combines incisive fairness, curiosity, romantic management of Yoshida and an interest in danger. Her modest OAA aggregate does not settle her capacities; the rumors about her past do not settle her biography. Morishita’s teasing can reveal independence and cause unwanted interference at once. Her final wish to observe Horikita without Ayanokōji present is a methodological choice: she wants information his presence would distort (`E016–017`, `E030–032`, `E054–055`).

A useful function does not by itself prove either replaceability or irreplaceability. An actual replacement might perform a task without being emotionally equivalent. Direction also matters: one person’s indispensable attachment does not prove the other person reciprocates it. The volume’s clearest increase in mutuality occurs where Horikita and Kei let another person’s account change their own view. That example must remain available even though neither grants Ayanokōji new authority over them.

The strongest defense of Ayanokōji is not that nothing is manipulated. A model of literal puppetry cannot explain those features. The strongest remaining criticism is that he often chooses the developmental test, withholds consequential information, and treats others’ costly answers as material for his own project. The ethical question is who may revise the project’s end, not whether the targets move willingly at all.

## Who changes Ayanokōji, and what remains unproved

Ichinose supplies information that makes his inference work. Morishita changes what he notices and forces him to explain unsupported-looking deductions. Shiraishi rescues him from an arbitrary demand and solves the timing problem of the teasing. Tsubaki’s earlier conversation becomes a trigger for a new interest in the past. Kitō’s persistence exceeds the anticipated sufficiency of one blow and receives an adapted response. These are real causal contributions by other people (`E016`, `E026–027`, `E032`, `E042`, `E051–053`).

They do not all count as the same kind of reciprocal authorship. Providing a useful input can improve the stronger actor’s project without changing its end. A mundane correction can expose fallibility without giving someone protective authority. An opponent’s unexpected endurance can require tactical adjustment without producing genuine defeat. The strict tests `Y1H-004`, `016`, `026` and `035` therefore remain open: no adequate voluntary disclosure, Horikita-witnessed loss of control, Hiyori-directed moral revision of Ryūen, or consequentially delegated protection of Ayanokōji is established here.

## Ordinary life, ability and institutional permeability

The ordinary scenes have two distinguishable functions. Karaoke and café meetings can be converted into instruments of attraction, recruitment or surveillance. Yet the class’s study habits, Miyake’s continued friendship, Morishita’s cultural references and Kei’s handkerchief cannot be understood adequately as exam tactics. The novel’s alternative to educational ownership appears most clearly when a person’s meaning exceeds the role that made the relationship useful. That surplus does not require an uncontaminated setting outside all strategy (`E015–017`, `E030–032`, `E045–047`).

The book also keeps ability plural. Shiraishi’s aggregate rating underdescribes her situational judgment. Sudō’s score records a performance, not the endpoint of all development. Kōenji’s ordinary exam participation does not show his maximum or resolve the paternal terms inherited from Year 2. The C class’s superior academic environment was not created by the newcomer. And Ayanokōji’s raw 100, adjusted zero and gain in authority are three different outputs of one performance. The ability ledger therefore tracks capacity, expression, measurement, task conversion and generativity separately.

The last scene introduces a further division between public administration and hidden operation. Nanase works competently with Horikita and professes a wish to finish school safely. After Horikita leaves, she addresses Tsukishiro on one phone, discusses surveillance and uncertain other actors, and takes a separate call on another. The general one-phone rule makes the second device concrete evidence of permeability. It does not tell us who authorized it, what the complete mission is, or whether every earlier action was a staged protection exercise. Her estimate of Ishigami’s role stays an estimate (`E056–057`).

The three listeners now know something their apparent class leader is not shown knowing. Nanase detects them and names them; Shiraishi advises against pursuit. The scene opens an intelligence problem through supporting actors rather than supplying a completed explanation from the protagonist. It belongs in the actor-identity and institutional snapshots, with origin, sponsor, instruction, contribution and personal aim in separate columns.

## Literary strengths and critical reservations

The volume is strongest where focal structure makes the reader distinguish a person from a model. Horikita’s self-judgment is not the complete history of her capacity. Ryūen’s confidence is not the completed strategic result. Ichinose’s sincere generosity is not universal care. Shimazaki’s expression is not his intended verdict. The final observers’ knowledge is not automatically Ayanokōji’s. These distinctions give the analytical method something more demanding than a list of impressive feats.

The mirrored harms and recoveries are also effective. The morning encounter makes a truthful distinction emotionally destructive; the later bench makes partial, chosen disclosure restorative. Both scenes concern what another person is allowed to know and receive. Their procedures differ, and that difference gives ethical content to the theme of reciprocity. The unglamorous handkerchief can consequently matter as much as the striking scoreboard.

The chief reservation is the speed with which exceptional performance becomes a broad governing credential. Ayanokōji’s explanation can make the original criterion seem foolish after the fact, while the class’s justified need for rescue makes its revision easy to accept. The novel acknowledges skepticism, but the extent of future correction remains an open burden on the new arrangement. The treatment of unwanted physical interference as comedy also needs careful handling: Morishita’s jokes and Ibuki’s concern cannot make the recipient’s pain disappear. Finally, the new sexual-history rumors risk substituting provocative aura for characterization unless their uncertain status and speaker’s purpose remain explicit.

These reservations do not require the claim that the volume fails to understand its own protagonists. It gives Kei a forceful challenge to reciprocity’s devaluation, lets Morishita articulate tool-consciousness, lets Kitō reserve distrust, and keeps Ayanokōji’s relational curiosity unresolved. They are internal grounds for criticism and for later tests, rather than external demands that every character already possess a settled moral doctrine.
