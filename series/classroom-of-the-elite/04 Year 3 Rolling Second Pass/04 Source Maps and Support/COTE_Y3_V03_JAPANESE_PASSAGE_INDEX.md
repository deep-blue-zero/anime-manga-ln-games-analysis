Kiyotaka changes speech function by audience: public fallibility invites compliance or diagnoses suspicion, while private retrospective explanation reveals withheld intention. His inner language also records surprise and uncertainty, so not every modest statement is a lie. Hashimoto mixes slang, sociable self-exposure, persuasion and cruel strategic taunting; the audience determines which capacity dominates. Morishita stages exaggerated heroic dialogue and practical interruptions, making literal transcription a poor guide to personal history. Yamamura's hesitant form permits unexpectedly exact criticism. Shiraishi's polite support is distinctive because of its disproportionate certainty. These patterns require conditions and counterexamples in later monographs.

Ryūen's aggressive register changes when Hiyori asks him to think about fiction; her politeness does not erase a decision to oppose Kiyotaka. Kei's casual intimacy creates room for reciprocal questions and her own information boundaries. Horikita's clipped command and private inability to name a feeling belong to different tasks. Kōenji's cultivated address makes compliance contingent on personal pleasure. Hirata's silence during the threat cannot be filled with an invented confession of motive. Behavior and scene access must constrain generated speech before stylistic imitation begins.
