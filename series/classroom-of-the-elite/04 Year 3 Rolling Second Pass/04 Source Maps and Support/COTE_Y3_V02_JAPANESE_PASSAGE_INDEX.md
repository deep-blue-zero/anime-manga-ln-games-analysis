## Written-voice contrasts

Ayanokōji’s calculating explanatory register now includes explicit self-questioning and difficulty naming an involuntary feeling. Horikita’s precise procedural reasoning survives fatigue and private incomplete recovery; her later tense steps beyond the scene-time self. Kei’s casual あたし, revised promise and deliberate surname use express chosen limits. Kaneda’s 氏 and restrained form become vehicles for jealousy and fervent comparison. Morishita’s full-name address, invented authority and literal jokes depend on audience: rival observation suppresses her usual ease. Yamamura’s disfluency is assessed against her attempt to initiate speech. Shiraishi’s polite secrecy and back-to-back voice discrimination qualify her friendly surface; Nanase’s respectful register covers a contested expulsion aim. Ichinose’s warmth supports accurate inference and also carries disappointed restraint. None should be modeled through one catchphrase divorced from context.
