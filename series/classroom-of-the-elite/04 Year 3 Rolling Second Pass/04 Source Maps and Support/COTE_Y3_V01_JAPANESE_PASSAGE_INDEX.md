The initial Horikita chapters use first-person access; the opening confusion chapter largely observes her in third person. Chapter 14 rewinds in Ayanokōji’s first person. The gym confrontation moves between Ichinose and Amasawa’s third-person interiors. The exam chapter withholds C-class interpretation while supplying Ryūen’s mistaken account, then returns to Ayanokōji. The final surveillance scene belongs to other observers. These shifts distribute knowledge; they are not interchangeable instances of an omniscient Ayanokōji narrator.

Written Japanese permits analysis of address, hedging, repetition, self-correction, sentence endings and interpersonal permissions. It does not supply vocal pitch, performed timing or an actor’s delivery. Morishita’s repeated full names and mock titles can make her sound ceremonious and intrusive on the page without proving how an adaptation must voice her. The Japanese text also does not establish exact hidden biographies through a single evocative name.
