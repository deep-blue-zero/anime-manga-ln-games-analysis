## Development does not settle the allocation of authority

The central V2 revision is positive evidence of Kiyotaka's particular attachment without equivalent evidence of Hiyori's authority over its experimental use. His happiness and wish to bring her joy matter ethically; the experiment/disassembly language and absent full disclosure matter too. A gift accepted and an apology received are local choices, not blanket consent to another person's inquiry. E043–048.

Nanase's expulsion request must be attributed to her preventive model; predicted evil is not independently established future fact.

All ten consent axes are assessed in the [local reading](../01%20Canonical%20Volume%20Deep%20Readings/COTE_Y3_V02_DEEP_READING.md): information, alternatives, exit, material pressure, institutional pressure, emotional pressure, manufactured scarcity, timing, post-choice ownership and revision. No single aggregate consent score replaces these distinctions. Ishigami's anonymity agreement supplies a concrete revision boundary because Horikita reserves serious-report duties; it does not make the authored encounter fully symmetrical. E060–062.

The strict answerability test remains unmet. Kiyotaka receives emotional and tactical influence, but no fully informed other is shown changing his governing end at a substantial accepted cost. This preserves the test while crediting the volume's new care and affective evidence.
