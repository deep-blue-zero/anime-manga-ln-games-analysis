## Current written-voice model

The source is written Japanese. Voice here means narrated access, address, syntax, rhetorical permission and register; it does not mean measured vocal pitch, performance rhythm or an adaptation actor’s delivery. The [passage index](../04%20Source%20Maps%20and%20Support/COTE_Y3_V01_JAPANESE_PASSAGE_INDEX.md) verifies 29 local keys and links each to its attributed passage.

## Focal and temporal controls

The opening is not one uninterrupted Ayanokōji narration. Horikita supplies the initial first-person frame; the confusion chapter largely follows her in third person; Ayanokōji’s explanatory chapter rewinds the transfer. The gym exchange admits Ichinose’s and Amasawa’s interiors. Exam perspectives precede the C-class interpretation; the final observers possess information no scene passes to Ayanokōji. These distinctions are positive features of the written architecture, not obstacles to be eliminated through a universal unreliability label.

Within a scene, distinguish an observed statement from the proposition it asserts. Ichinose’s White Room claim is an observed bluff; it is not evidence of the disclosure she names. Her statement of giving preference is narrator-confirmed sincerity, but her diagnosis of Kei’s breakup remains her diagnosis. Ayanokōji’s prediction that a Yuki meeting would change no emotion is self-modeling, not authorial certainty about the future.

## Corrections and invariants

The source’s spine17:para0055 appears to misname Shiraishi in a scene otherwise involving Nishikawa before Shiraishi arrives. Preserve the raw text and annotate the attribution instead of making an invisible emendation. Morishita’s invented Yamamura quotation and fabricated claims are jokes, not biographical evidence. Retain verified inline glyphs `櫛`, `梗` and `－` when reproducing search keys.

The Year 2 principles survive: select a social state before inferring register; do not flatten stress into an invariant personality; separate address from intimacy and intimacy from informed consent. A future scenario reconstruction still requires evidence beyond these page-level observations and must not silently use later facts in an earlier voice.
