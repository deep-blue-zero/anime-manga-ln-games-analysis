Survival of activity after a leader leaves does not by itself prove independent reproduction of the leader's entire function. Conversely, a later loss does not erase others' self-owned reasoning. A's multi-part attack, Kitō's rearguard, D's hospitality and B's trained signals warrant credit at their observed scale. The strongest new C finding is that imported leadership can preserve subordinate initiative while engineering the collective preference that sustains its mandate.

All 12 identifiers and prior relations remain inherited. No new Arisu off-campus event, council succession or original-group restoration is invented. Future review should test durability, protected correction and purposes after separation rather than treating every alliance as healing or every dependence as incapacity.
