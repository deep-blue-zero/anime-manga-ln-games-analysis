## Current boundary proposition

Ordinary life remains a source of purposes, knowledge and relations that an assigned developmental program does not exhaust. The opening volume also shows how readily leisure and intimacy can become strategic infrastructure. The correct distinction is the use and ownership of an activity, not whether it happens in a café rather than an examination room.

## Ownership of recovery

Kei is still suffering when she helps Horikita. Her capacity to care cannot be reduced to evidence that Ayanokōji’s breakup curriculum worked. Horikita begins acting because another student’s story changes how she understands her own solitude. Both remain influenced by the absent person, yet the scene contains reciprocal contributions he is not shown specifying. This is actual evidence of horizontal development rather than a requirement that autonomy arise without influence.

Similarly, Miyake can want friendship without reinstating Ayanokōji as the group’s organizing center. Airi’s prior outside-school future and the successor trio remain valid frozen developments; this volume does not supply new off-campus events. Unshown continuations should not be fabricated to make the ordinary-life theme feel complete.

## Limits on the positive reading

Karaoke participation and physical teasing may constrain other people. Ichinose can use a technically truthful romantic distinction to injure Kei. Ayanokōji’s public accommodation of Yamamura remains joined to private tool cultivation. Calling these ordinary scenes morally innocent would lose the most important contrast with the bench exchange.

The inherited model therefore strengthens in a qualified form: ordinary life generates non-assigned meanings and relationships, while strategic conversion remains possible. The next test is whether the peer-created purposes endure and acquire authority when a powerful planner wants a different result. A moment of laughter is evidence of a moment, not a final emancipation.
