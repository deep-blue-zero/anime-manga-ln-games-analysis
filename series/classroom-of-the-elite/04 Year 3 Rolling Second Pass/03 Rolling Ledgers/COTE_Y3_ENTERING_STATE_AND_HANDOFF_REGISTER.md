# Year 3 entering state: the inherited evidence boundary

This immutable entering record was created before any Year 3 narrative reading. Its evidence boundary is Y2SL. It initializes the rolling pass from substantive, frozen through-Year-2 ledgers; it does not reset the characters, fill future outcomes, or make a prospective behavioral prediction. Later Year 3 snapshots may supersede this record for current-state lookup while leaving its inherited judgments intact.

## What enters the new year

Year 2 separates capacity, authorship of purpose, and answerability. Ayanokōji has acquired ordinary attachments and sometimes respects a boundary on intervention, while also extending his ability to arrange information, incentives, and emotionally consequential alternatives. His departure therefore tests the ownership of a developmental project as well as its effectiveness. One successful act of care cannot establish generalized reciprocity; one harmful intervention cannot erase every demonstrated ordinary preference.

Horikita has already demonstrated independent judgment and increasing coordination of differentiated peers. Her class's ability to reproduce judgment after the actual transfer remains unobserved at this entering boundary. A future failure would need to be assessed for its mechanism and recovery, without cancelling all prior growth. Kushida, Hirata, Sudō, Kei, and other peers have purposes and relationships that cannot be treated merely as components of Ayanokōji's legacy.

Kei's broader social agency and grief coexist with a romance that ended without sufficient reciprocal information. Ichinose's third answer exceeded the alternatives Ayanokōji prepared, but surprise does not establish durable self-authored terms or adequate mutual disclosure. Ryūen has developed delegation and chosen loyalty while protected dissent remains unproven. Sakayanagi's individual recognition and departure coexist with a succession failure borne by the class. Kōenji's exceptional autonomy faces externally stated paternal conditions whose actual effect is not yet observed.

The polity identities below persist across class letters and leadership changes. Letters and points are dated institutional attributes, never ethical rankings. Ayanokōji's transfer destination is the Sakayanagi-origin polity. The new first-year cohort in Second List is the younger cohort during his Year 2, not his own earlier endpoint.

| Stable polity | Inherited governing condition | First missing test |
|---|---|---|
| POLITY-HORIKITA | Growing distributed capacity with a history of hidden terminal security | Repeated decisions and recovery after departure without a concealed guarantee |
| POLITY-RYUEN | Coercive sovereignty supplemented by delegation and voluntary loyalty | Followers' standing to alter governing ends or moral limits |
| POLITY-ICHINOSE | Solidarity, emerging loyal opposition, stronger adversarial judgment | Translation of self-authored terms into durable collective practice |
| POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN | Leadership vacuum addressed by importing Ayanokōji with collectively financed transfer | Who now controls decisions, information, dissent, and reproduction of competence |

Institutional origin, present sponsorship, actual instruction, causal contribution, and personal aim remain separate identity claims. Ishigami's connection to Atsuomi does not establish White Room training. Nanase's proposed role remains a hypothesis. Adult observation can supply constraints or distort incentives; neither neutrality nor total capture follows from political genealogy alone.

## Interpretation and reconstruction controls

New evidence must distinguish development, revelation of a prior state, retrospective recontextualization, correction, reversal, relationship-specific expression, continuity, and unresolved uncertainty. Textual events, interior/self-report, institutional rules, visual facts, strong inferences, interpretive theses, and value judgments remain separately classified. Japanese forms of address and focal ownership must be verified at their local source surface.

The through-Year-2 reconstruction protocol remains a derivative authority at Y2SL. Its calibrated scenarios may constrain interpretation, but they cannot supply Year 3 facts. No prediction has been timestamped for comparison with a future observation in this run; consequently no prospective validation ledger is initialized. New Year 3 evidence will require a distinct later-boundary reconstruction artifact if such an extension is undertaken.

This baseline's central interpretive risk is asking only whether Ayanokōji's project succeeds. The read must also recover ordinary purposes, quiet supporting actors, refusal, alternative interpretations, and costs to those whose lives make that success possible. Beneficial coordination is a substantive counterargument; it must be tested against information, alternatives, exit, and the stronger actor's willingness to revise an end.
