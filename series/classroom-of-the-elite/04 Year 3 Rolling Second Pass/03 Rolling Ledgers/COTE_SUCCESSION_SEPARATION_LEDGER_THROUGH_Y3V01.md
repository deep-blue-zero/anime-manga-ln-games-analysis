## The different meanings of survival

Horikita’s first result is not successful autonomous replication of Ayanokōji’s earlier guarantees. Her class loses. Yet it also prepares, reasons under uncertainty and continues; her later ability to act is restored through another student. Calling the loss proof of no independence would confuse correct judgment with guaranteed victory. Calling the recovery proof of a fully self-sustaining polity would outrun one observation.

C supplies the converse case. Its first result is successful, but it relies on imported exceptional strategy. Academic culture and dissent precede the import, and those surviving capacities matter. The proof still missing is whether the constituency can reproduce the missing governing function or constrain it after accepting the new leader. The class has not merely changed a nameplate; neither has it yet demonstrated organic succession.

The peer-care scene is the strongest local development in ownership of recovery. Ayanokōji expected classmates to help, but no evidence gives him authorship of Kei’s chosen history or Horikita’s recognition of her. Causal influence on the setting and exclusive ownership of its products are different claims. Their new bond therefore counts without requiring that it occur outside every influence of the departing person.

## Future admissibility

Later records must state the actual missing function, current substitute, information access, permission to refuse the predecessor’s end, cost bearer and duration of observed performance. No prospective forecasts were registered in this run; these tests are questions, not predictions to be scored retrospectively. A later successful result must not overwrite this volume’s lost exam or the earlier Y2SL uncertainty.
