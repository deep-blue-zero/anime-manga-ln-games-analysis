# Relationships and irreplaceability — Through Year 3 Volume 1

**PROVISIONAL SUPERSESSION NOTICE — This artifact records the state of evidence through Year 3 Volume 1. A later Year 3 snapshot may supersede it for current-state reference. It remains authoritative for what had been established at this source boundary.**

This snapshot carries [Frozen Year 2 primary home](../../03%20Year%202%20Definitive%20Second%20Pass/03%20Rolling%20Ledgers/COTE_RELATIONSHIP_LEDGER_THROUGH_Y2.md). It supersedes those earlier states **only for current reference in this responsibility**; frozen Y2SL evidence and judgments are preserved. Unchanged history remains available through those primary homes.


## Reading the current state

Each relationship is assessed for origin, information symmetry, power symmetry, leverage, refusal, dependency, protection, strategic use, emotional disclosure, contradiction, reciprocity, fungibility, separation and role survival. These dimensions do not combine into a moral score. A successful function may remain replaceable in principle; a unique personal attachment requires different proof. Where substitution has not been tested, the hard irreplaceability result is **unresolved**, not an inference that a person lacks worth.

## Ayanokōji and Horikita

The inherited partnership has become an adversarial class relation. He controls the timetable and refuses a private explanation; she wants consultation and recognition in addition to retained strategic support. Her later disclosure comes from Kei, not him. New information increases her awareness of how much she did not know. She retains the purpose of making him face her and graduating with the class. Attraction is suggested, not fully named. Strategic importance is high; personal irreplaceability and reciprocal corrective authority remain `unresolved` (`E004–006`, `E046–047`).

## Ayanokōji and Kei

Romance is ended; attachment and learning have not vanished. He credits experience with improved interpretation of others. She continues to regard him as indispensable while creating a new peer relation. That is `claimed_irreplaceable_but_unverified` in the direction Kei → Ayanokōji, especially as a claim about what her future requires. It does not change the frozen negative answer about her irreplaceability within his romance-learning model. No new adequate disclosure from him repairs the information asymmetry. Role loss becomes compatible with horizontal agency (`E030`, `E033–035`, `E045–047`).

## Ayanokōji and Ichinose

The relationship now produces an actual alliance result through complementary resources. Her social reach and intelligence contribute to his reading; his knowledge of former classmates improves her targeting. The low-contract, equality-oriented arrangement promises an exit if C objects. Neither that veto nor a personally costly revision of his ends is exercised. Her White Room claim is a bluff, so strategic cooperation must not be mistaken for full personal disclosure. The functional relationship is high-value; hard functional or personal irreplaceability remains `unresolved` (`E011–012`, `E019–020`, `E039`, `E042`, `E050`).

## Horikita and Kei

A sparse prior connection becomes a voluntary exchange of care and history. Kei protects the injured wrist, offers practical help, keeps one detail private and chooses to disclose the larger past. Horikita revises her view of Kei, recognizes unequal knowledge of Ayanokōji, returns the handclasp and shares a future undertaking. Both smile. Information need not be total to be appropriately chosen: the subject decides what to reveal without a newly imposed penalty for refusal. Their new reciprocity is observed; its durability and non-substitutability remain `unresolved` (`E045–047`).

## Horikita and Ibuki; Horikita and Kushida

Familiar antagonism is not a blanket license. Ibuki’s attack injures Horikita; Kei later infers concern from waiting, but this does not cleanse the means. The shared meal network has real ordinary value and is disrupted by distress. Kushida’s demand for functioning leadership combines self-interest, fear and familiarity; it does not certify selfless tenderness or end hostility. The earlier legitimate cooperation remains historical fact while a new betrayal offer tests its continuation (`E028`, `E044–045`, `E049–050`).

## Ayanokōji and Hirata; Miyake and the former group

Hirata’s gratitude and distrust can be stated together, and a private hearing is allowed. His care has become sufficiently bounded to accept a lawful separation and oppose unwarranted accusation. No new decisive test of particular attachment closes `Y1H-034`. Miyake offers continued friendship across classes from his own purpose. That is role survival, not the restoration of the original group. Haruka’s feelings are partly reported, Airi’s new off-campus conduct is absent, and the successor trio retains its separate identity (`E014–015`, `E037`).

## Ayanokōji and Yamamura; Shiraishi and Morishita

Yamamura’s protected pace is now explicitly juxtaposed with Ayanokōji’s intention to cultivate a useful information source. The benefit is actual; full informed agreement with that purpose is not shown. Do not treat Morishita’s invented quotation as her self-report. Shiraishi honors Ayanokōji’s request to release his hand and helps him catch teasing. Morishita resists explanations and privately warns against excessive trust. These are concrete local permissions and constraints, not proof of either woman’s personal irreplaceability or romantic devotion. Their functions are useful; the hard classification remains `unresolved` (`E025`, `E031–032`, `E054–055`).

## Ayanokōji and Kitō; Ryūen and followers

Kitō chooses a force test and accepts cooperation after defeat while saying he does not trust the leader. He retains a future challenge in speech; no enforceable removal mechanism is established. Ayanokōji’s protective demand concerns vulnerable classmates, while his own political purpose is narrower than an unconditional Class A guarantee. Ryūen’s advisers can contest cost and suggest retreat, yet fear still structures membership and no follower is shown changing his moral end. These are differentiated forms of dissent, not equal constitutions (`E029`, `E036`, `E052–053`).

## Unchanged routes and next proof

Hiyori’s earlier participant-mode relation remains valid without a new library encounter here. Sakayanagi–Kamuro/Yamamura recognition, Sudō–Onodera generativity, Satō’s support and earlier rival relationships retain their frozen evidence where this volume adds no scene. Silence is not a reversal. The next stronger test is a relation in which an informed refusal changes the more powerful actor’s chosen end at real cost, or a new bond sustains its purpose beyond a single crisis.
