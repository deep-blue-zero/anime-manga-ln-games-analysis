# Year 3 provisional synthesis — Through Volume 1

**PROVISIONAL SUPERSESSION NOTICE — This artifact records the state of evidence through Year 3 Volume 1. A later Year 3 snapshot may supersede it for current-state reference. It remains authoritative for what had been established at this source boundary.**

## Current Year 3 state

The completed analytical boundary is **Y3V01**. This is a provisional synthesis of the opening volume, not the final-year synthesis. The [local deep reading](../01%20Canonical%20Volume%20Deep%20Readings/COTE_Y3_V01_DEEP_READING.md) remains the immutable source-level argument; the [current map](../COTE_Y3_CURRENT_STATE_AND_CORPUS_MAP.md) selects the live cumulative snapshots. The Japanese EPUB was read completely, including embedded artwork and back matter. Separately linked bonus fiction is excluded, and later volumes have supplied no narrative evidence.

The opening shifts the central question from whether Ayanokōji can become visible to what kind of authority visibility confers. His new class sees exceptional academic performance and an opponent’s maximal caution. Those observations make leadership persuasive. They do not establish that classmates can require disclosure of his larger purpose, revise his timetable or stop a costly developmental intervention. The distinction between capacity, public mandate and answerability now has a concrete institution in which it can be tested.

## Two observed forms of succession

C’s first success uses imported exceptional competence. Its academic environment already works; students already know how to argue, observe, care about their prospects and refuse some demands. The transfer does not create them. Ayanokōji fills the missing strategic center and obtains recognition by a revised public criterion. This is a successful replacement and a continuing question about concentration, not yet reproduction of the former leader’s function by the polity itself.

Horikita’s class instead experiences the absent guarantor as an opponent. Its delegated response loses the exam. That loss makes the independent-system question more empirical, but the class does not cease to possess agency. Hirata and others prepare and interpret the result; Kei and Horikita later restore one another’s ability to act. The first succession evidence is therefore neither simple failure nor completed self-sufficiency. It is a lost contest accompanied by distributed continuity and initial recovery.

These outcomes require different future tests. C needs a costly case of effective correction after its new leader’s value has been established. A needs sustained performance and recovery without an unacknowledged rescue. Winning the next event alone would not settle either constitutional question.

## Cooperation and its moral limits

Ichinose’s social reach becomes measurable strategic infrastructure. She supplies information that Ayanokōji’s interpretation needs, while he supplies knowledge of the former class that her targeting uses. The first alliance result is real complementary production. It extends the Year 2 third answer into political action without converting her into an obedient instrument.

The same volume makes her care selective and her treatment of Kei cruel. Her sincere preference to give does not prove that Kei caused the breakup by wanting reciprocity. Nor does inability to help everyone require additional harm to an excluded person. These observations complicate a favorable agency finding without erasing it. Strategic growth and ethical cost can belong to the same development.

Ryūen supplies another mixed result. He learns to brake in an unfavorable domain, but retains fear as a means of mobilizing the class. His intended reputational victory fails and he questions whether he can win alone. This is a meaningful crisis in the model of sovereign self-sufficiency. It is not yet a follower’s protected right to alter his purpose. Hiyori’s strict moral-redirection test remains unmet.

## A first positive result outside the leader’s direction

The Horikita–Kei exchange is the strongest new evidence that recovery can become another person’s work. It joins practical assistance, a respected disclosure boundary, voluntary history and reciprocal revision of judgment. Their connection still concerns Ayanokōji, and his withdrawal helped create its circumstances. The source nevertheless does not give him authorship of Kei’s particular testimony or knowledge of the completed exchange.

This distinction matters for the architecture’s counter-authorship criterion. Independence need not mean having no cause outside oneself. The more discriminating questions are whether the person appropriates a purpose, can retain meaningful boundaries and changes another participant’s response. The bench scene provides a local positive case. Its future duration remains unobserved.

## The unresolved external frame

Nanase’s ending scene reveals a declared continuing surveillance arrangement, an addressed Tsukishiro contact and two phones despite a general one-phone rule. The reader and three C-class listeners gain specific information. Horikita has left, and Ayanokōji is not shown learning it. Sponsor, complete instruction and training origin remain separate unknowns. A name or an assessing glance does not solve Shiraishi, Ishigami, Tsubaki or the unnamed first-year student.

Ayanokōji’s recollection of Yuki creates another incomplete movement. School relations make a discarded past newly interesting, but he does not act on the wish to meet. The moment belongs to ordinary development and the limits of his current self-model. It does not demonstrate a reunion, a family link to Tsubaki or a finished transformation of feeling.
