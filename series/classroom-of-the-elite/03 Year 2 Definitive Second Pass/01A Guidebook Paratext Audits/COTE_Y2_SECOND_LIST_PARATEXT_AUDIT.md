# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ』
## Year 2 Official Guidebook — *Second List*
### Second-pass paratext audit
### The measured student and the self-authored answer

# 0. Scope, source identity, and admissible boundary

This artifact is the canonical Year-2 audit of *Second List*. It is **not** a seventeenth ordinary volume deep reading, and it is not an omniscient answer key to the preceding fiction. Its analytical position is deliberately late: the Japanese Year-2 novels through V12.5 are already frozen as local-truth artifacts, Volume 0 has already been admitted at its mandated retrospective position after V08, and only now may the guidebook be used to audit the completed school year.

The governing hierarchy is therefore:

1. the Japanese novels remain primary evidence for what happened, how scenes were focalized, and what ambiguity the narrative preserved;
2. *Second List* exclusive fiction is canonical fiction at its own publication position;
3. guidebook factual records are strong official documentary evidence;
4. OAA tables are institutional measurements rather than omniscient measurements of total capacity;
5. retrospective summaries and relationship captions are official editorial framing, useful for franchise emphasis but not allowed to flatten richer novel evidence;
6. Year 3 is outside this artifact's admissible narrative boundary.

The source reacquired for this audit is byte-identical to the previously staged project holding: **119,398,463 bytes**, SHA-256 **`fcc6f15ff674c9833263247816bf23ce67a7b337924fe87901567285331e98b5`**. The EPUB passes ZIP integrity testing. Its OPF contains **251 spine items**, **252 XHTML resources**, and **246 JPEG resources**. The Japanese metadata identifies the official title, creators, KADOKAWA publication, and 2025-03-24 date.

The guidebook is physically mixed. Most of the documentary body is fixed-layout: the authoritative object is the Japanese page image. The two bonus stories are text-bearing XHTML and can therefore receive deterministic paragraph locators. This audit does **not** promote OCR output into source wording.

## 0.2 Four evidence classes

### `SL-DATA`

Chronology, school-system explanation, class points, biographical fields, transfer records, and other explicitly documentary facts.

### `SL-RATING`

OAA values and other quantified institutional evaluations. These are strong evidence for **what the school records at a stated time**, not for a metaphysical or timeless total of a person's ability.

### `SL-EDITORIAL`

Retrospective profiles, class reviews, relationship captions, Activity Report summaries, Check Point pages, and other official compression/framing.

### `SL-FICTION`

The two included stories. These are narrative fiction and receive the highest literary authority within the guidebook.

The Year-3 teaser is inventoried as a physical source surface but excluded from adjudication. It is not smuggled into `SL-EDITORIAL` as usable Year-2 evidence.

# 1. Executive thesis — the file grows more sophisticated, but the person still exceeds it

*First File* ended Year 1 with a problem of **the file and the person**: institutions can record genuine information while still failing to possess the person as such.

*Second List* does not repeal that insight. It makes the problem more explicit.

Year 2 gives the school OAA, a more elaborate formal language for turning students into numerical profiles. The guidebook then repeatedly supplies its own counterexamples to naive numerical realism:

- Ayanokōji's visible OAA rises dramatically as he reveals outputs that were already latent;
- Kōenji's file explicitly says his published values fail to capture true capacity because participation is elective;
- Ryūen's page says leadership and strategic quality exceed what appears numerically;
- Hiyori's formal quick-thinking score under-describes situational leadership and quiet judgment;
- Kei's ordinary academic profile does not explain her social coordination;
- Kushida's elite social metrics remain genuine even when the moral and political use of that capacity changes.

The correct object of OAA is therefore not "the person." It is closer to:

> **institutionally legible performance under a particular observation regime.**

The Year-2 class reviews perform the same operation at polity scale. Each class is converted into points, strengths, weaknesses, and future tasks. But the future-task fields repeatedly identify a problem that the raw point total cannot answer: **what happens when the leader or hidden security structure is removed?**

That question is decisive because V12.5 had already proposed three increasingly strong forms of successful development:

1. authored compliance;
2. succession competence;
3. counter-authorship.

*Second List* strongly supports that hierarchy rather than replacing it.

The guidebook says Ayanokōji prepared Horikita's class to function after his transfer, but it also leaves their post-transfer performance as an unresolved problem. It records the former Sakayanagi class as losing its sovereign and importing Ayanokōji rather than internally generating an equivalent successor. It warns that Ryūen's improved class remains increasingly dependent on Ryūen. It describes Ichinose's class as extraordinarily unified but still in need of distributed individual capacity.

Most strikingly, the late Check Point page does not merely say that Ichinose recovered. Its headline says that **Ichinose took an action exceeding Ayanokōji's prediction**.

The second bonus story provides the interior precursor: Ayanokōji himself says she stood back up **by her own power**, observes that she has grown rather than merely returned to baseline, and ends by recognizing her as someone who creates new discoveries after he believed his analysis was finished.

The Year-2 terminal thesis therefore survives the paratext audit and becomes stronger:

> **Development survives the developer most convincingly when the developed person can produce an answer neither the institution nor the developer's prior model already contains.**

A related paratext thesis follows:

> **A richer file can improve prediction without becoming ownership. The ethical and epistemic error begins when a usable model is mistaken for jurisdiction over the modeled person.**

# 2. *Second List* as archive — chronology is stabilized, causation is not

The foldout annual-events pages are high-value `SL-DATA`. They stabilize the sequence of volumes, special exams, school events, and class-point movement across the second year. The late foldout records the provisional end-of-Year-2 state:

- Horikita class: **1233**
- Ryūen class: **1040–1090**
- former Sakayanagi class: **793**
- Ichinose class: **714**

The value of this table is longitudinal. It lets the reader see Horikita's extraordinary rise, the relative persistence of Sakayanagi's early lead followed by collapse, Ryūen's climb, and Ichinose's limited point conversion.

But the table is not a causal theory.

A point total does not tell us:

- whether leadership was distributed or centralized;
- whether people consented to the strategy that produced it;
- whether the class can reproduce the result after its leader exits;
- whether a low-metric student supplies important social infrastructure;
- whether a high-metric student will make their ability politically available;
- or whether a victory was authored by the visible leader, a hidden actor, or the class collectively.

That distinction matters because *Second List* itself spends many pages explaining precisely those non-equivalent constitutions.

The archive is therefore strongest when used as a **chronological spine and institutional snapshot**, not when mistaken for the completed interpretation.

# 3. OAA — measurement becomes more precise without becoming total

## 3.1 What OAA claims to measure

The School Guide defines OAA as an application available from the school system that makes individual student data numerically visible. Its four axes are:

- `学力` — mainly written-test performance;
- `身体能力` — PE, club, and special-exam physical evaluation;
- `機転思考力` — friend network, communication, applied quick thinking, and broader social adaptability;
- `社会貢献性` — classroom attitude, problem behavior, school contribution, and related conduct.

The composite overall value half-weights social contribution.

This is important because the guidebook does not present an abstract IQ test. OAA is explicitly built from **school-observable proxies**. Some axes are broad, but the mechanism remains institutional.

That makes OAA politically useful and philosophically incomplete at the same time.

## 3.2 Ayanokōji — the same person, three visibility states

The database preserves a particularly clean sequence:

| Snapshot | Academic | Physical | Quick-thinking | Social | Overall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| April | C 51 | C+ 60 | D+ 37 | C+ 60 | C 51 |
| May | A- 81 | B- 61 | D+ 40 | B 68 | B- 62 |
| March | A 87 | B 73 | C 54 | B 70 | B 71 |

The literary importance of this table is not simply that Ayanokōji "improved."

The White Room framing on the same profile makes that reading impossible. The institutional number rises as his displayed output changes. Much of the underlying capacity did not spring into existence between April and May.

This produces a formal Year-2 distinction:

`latent capacity -> chosen demonstration -> institutional observation -> recorded OAA`

The OAA is not false. It is accurate about a narrower object than readers sometimes assume.

That distinction also protects the project from a common analytical error: treating later legibility as proof that the earlier character was less capable, or treating hidden capacity as proof that all later development is fake. Revelation and development can coexist. Ayanokōji's measured output changes dramatically while his relational, ethical, and ordinary-life capacities can still genuinely develop.

## 3.3 Kōenji — voluntary illegibility

Kōenji is an even cleaner institutional counterexample because the guidebook explicitly supplies the caveat.

His published OAA is mediocre in dimensions tied to coordination and social contribution, yet his file says the values do not adequately represent his true capacity because he often refuses serious participation. The island examination demonstrates that enormous ability can become visible when he elects to use it.

Ayanokōji and Kōenji therefore produce opposite strategic routes to the same measurement problem:

- Ayanokōji often manages what the observer is allowed to infer;
- Kōenji often refuses to make capacity politically available at all.

Both cases show that institutional measurement depends on **performed availability**.

## 3.4 Ryūen — political competence that exceeds the axis

Ryūen's page is unusually explicit: the numerical profile does not exhaust the dimensions in which he excels. His low social contribution does not mean he lacks social causality. On the contrary, he can dominate, intimidate, recruit, delegate, and coordinate a class.

The correct analytical distinction is therefore:

- prosocial contribution;
- social influence;
- coercive coordination;
- strategic leadership;
- legitimacy.

Those are not the same variable.

Year 2 makes Ryūen politically more sophisticated without converting him into a benign leader. The guidebook supports that mixed reading rather than a simple redemption narrative.

## 3.5 Hiyori — quiet judgment and institutional under-description

Hiyori's profile gives a relatively modest quick-thinking score while its prose simultaneously records forms of situational leadership, observation, and class-level judgment that the score cannot summarize well.

This is a useful caution for character modeling. A low or middling OAA axis must not be promoted into a total personality claim. The narrative function of Hiyori's judgment is often relationally specific, quiet, and exercised in moments the institution may not weight heavily.

## 3.6 Kei and Kushida — capacity is not morality

Kei's relatively strong quick-thinking reflects real social navigation among girls. Kushida's high quick-thinking/social contribution reflects real memory, networking, interpersonal fluency, and public cooperativeness.

Neither metric answers the moral question.

Kushida is the clearest case: the social capacity is genuine even while the motive and hidden use of intimacy can be destructive. After exposure, the same social infrastructure can be repurposed under a different public/private constitution.

The guidebook therefore strengthens a recurring COTE rule:

> **ability, availability, legitimacy, and goodness must remain separate analytical fields.**

# 4. Official character framing — useful compression, dangerous if promoted above fiction

The profiles are valuable because they show what the franchise itself wants readers to foreground after Year 2. They are especially useful for **emphasis**, **relationship topology**, and **retrospective naming**.

They are not substitutes for the novels.

## 4.1 Ayanokōji — official emphasis on environmental authorship

The Ayanokōji file foregrounds two ideas simultaneously:

1. his public ability becomes increasingly legible during Year 2;
2. he intervenes across multiple classes while preserving the possibility that each can still compete for Class A.

That second emphasis strongly supports the existing "environmental authorship" model. By the late second year he is not merely surviving threats. He is increasingly designing the competitive ecology in which other leaders will be tested.

But the guidebook does not solve the ethical problem this creates. Keeping every class viable is not equivalent to securing informed consent from the people whose relationships, losses, or futures are used to maintain the ecology.

## 4.2 Horikita — leadership is real; independence is not certified in advance

Horikita's file treats her Year-2 leadership growth as genuine. It names the fading of older arrogance and brother-dependence, recognizes her class leadership, and notes the unusual solidarities that develop through sustained engagement with Kushida and Ibuki.

This is meaningful development.

Yet the class review refuses the stronger claim that V12.5 has already proven independence from Ayanokōji. Its future-problem field explicitly asks how the class will maintain A-class position after his transfer, with Kōenji's available power becoming especially important.

That distinction is exactly the one the V12.5 artifact needed:

- **separation has occurred;**
- **succession competence still requires post-separation evidence.**

The guidebook therefore **strengthens** rather than closes the Horikita-independence question.

## 4.3 Kei — the guidebook contains both the reduction and its corrective

Kei's profile uses strong dependency language and an editorial formulation that reduces Ayanokōji's side of the romance toward "romance textbook" functionality.

That is legitimate evidence for official framing.

It is not sufficient evidence for an exhaustive literary conclusion, because the guidebook's own fiction immediately contains phenomena that the reduction does not capture well: remembered preference, indirect attentiveness, ordinary anticipation, secrecy, loneliness, touch, gift-giving, and the desire for public reciprocal romance.

Likewise, the V12.5 summary gives an unusually precise endpoint:

- Kei understands the intended independence logic cognitively;
- she presents strength during the breakup;
- once alone, she cries uncontrollably.

The existing distinction therefore survives intact:

> **behavioral independence is not identical to emotional independence.**

A second distinction becomes clearer:

> **understanding the developer's intended lesson is not the same as freely authoring the curriculum.**

Kei's strength is real. The information asymmetry is also real. Her ability to perform the independence she believes Ayanokōji expects cannot retroactively make the relationship fully reciprocal.

## 4.4 Kushida — stable capacity, changed interface

Kushida's unusually strong OAA social dimensions survive the collapse of her old public persona. The guidebook therefore helps separate three things that are often conflated:

1. interpersonal skill;
2. public trust;
3. prosocial motive.

Year 2 changes the political interface through which the skill operates. It does not reveal that the skill was fake.

That is a `DEVELOPMENT + REVELATION_OF_PRIOR_STATE` pattern rather than a simple reversal.

## 4.5 Ichinose — official romance emphasis is real but not causally exhaustive

The profile openly foregrounds Ichinose's love for Ayanokōji as part of the energy around her later growth.

That emphasis should be retained.

It should not be converted into:

`romantic feeling -> complete cause of strategic growth`

because the guidebook's own other layers show more:

- her class retains extraordinary mutual protection;
- a reform current exists even when weak;
- her friends can act independently to protect her;
- she rebuilds by her own power;
- she becomes capable of producing an answer Ayanokōji does not possess.

Romance is therefore one causal and motivational component inside a larger reconstruction of agency.

## 4.6 Sakayanagi — ability without succession

Sakayanagi's profile and class review make the fiduciary problem stark. Her class can perform at a high level under precise centralized direction. The same structure is brittle when the sovereign exits.

The guidebook does not show an internally generated successor.

Instead, it presents:

`centralized excellence -> private-priority losses -> sovereign withdrawal -> class decline -> imported strategic center`

That does not mean every Sakayanagi-class student lacks agency. It means the **political constitution** did not reproduce its command function internally before the exit.

## 4.7 Ryūen — improved command, persistent dependence

The Ryūen-class review is almost a mirror image.

His rule remains authoritarian, but the class increasingly trusts him and executes strategy coherently. Katsuragi adds an advisory/strategic layer. The polity becomes more effective.

The explicit future problem is dependence on Ryūen.

This means Year 2 has improved the *quality of centralized leadership* without yet proving *distributed succession*. Hiyori's concern about classmates losing opportunities to grow under overdependence is therefore structural rather than merely personal.

# 5. Four class polities at the Year-2 boundary

The guidebook's class-review pages are among its highest-value editorial surfaces because they convert "which class is stronger?" into four different constitutional problems.

## 5.1 Horikita polity — growth under a departing hidden guarantor

The class gains roughly a thousand points across the year and reaches A. Its visible leadership is much stronger. Its members increasingly possess differentiated strengths, and even the trauma of Sakura's expulsion changes the internal competition for survival.

But the guidebook refuses to let the point total answer the succession question.

Its real Year-3 problem is:

> can the class maintain the result after Ayanokōji's departure, and what role will Kōenji's voluntarily withheld power play?

This is not a downgrade of Horikita. It is the proper evidentiary threshold.

## 5.2 Ryūen polity — effective centralization and the growth bottleneck

Ryūen's class becomes better at following strategy, makes fewer self-destructive errors, and rises.

The cost is that the leader can become the bottleneck through which too much agency flows.

The class's future problem is therefore not simply "become nicer." It is:

> can members become more independently capable without losing the strategic coherence Ryūen supplies?

Hiyori is especially important because she can recognize this problem from inside the polity without yet possessing evidence of morally redirecting Ryūen himself.

## 5.3 Ichinose polity — solidarity without enough conversion

Ichinose's class preserves its no-expulsion constitution for a second year. That is not nothing. In a school designed around scarcity and disposability, maintaining solidarity is a substantive institutional achievement.

The failure is conversion:

- relatively little point gain;
- large end-of-year deficit;
- weak urgency among some members;
- limited effectiveness of the internal reform faction.

This does not invalidate V12's evidence that Ichinose can weaponize distributed relational knowledge in a specific exam. It shows that one exceptional display of adversarial competence is not the same as a permanently transformed polity.

*Second List* therefore **qualifies** any overly easy "Ichinose solved her class" conclusion while preserving the stronger result that solidarity and adversarial competence are not mutually exclusive.

## 5.4 Former Sakayanagi polity — the succession-vacuum case

This is the clearest negative control.

The class possesses unusually strong individuals and spent almost two years near the top. Yet the loss of Katsuragi, Kamuro, and finally Sakayanagi exposes how much effective sovereignty had been concentrated.

After Sakayanagi's withdrawal, morale falls and the class imports Ayanokōji.

The guidebook therefore strengthens the V12.5 answer to the aristocratic-optimization question:

> **optimization can produce excellent performance without producing a successor capable of reproducing the optimizing function.**

# 6. Relationship topology — official compression and what it can safely tell us

The relationship-summary pages are useful if read as **officially emphasized relational vectors**, not as complete emotional models.

Their value is strongest where the caption confirms a boundary already established in fiction.

The most diagnostic examples are:

- Kei is an ex-girlfriend but has not fully emotionally moved on;
- Horikita wants Ayanokōji's recognition;
- Sakayanagi combines rivalry and love;
- Ichinose has learned more of Ayanokōji's nature and shares a secret relation;
- Hiyori is a reading companion with faint romantic feeling;
- Ryūen is a rival to defeat;
- Nagumo recognizes ability and wants competition.

These captions help a future character-modeling corpus because they provide a **franchise-sanctioned topology**.

They should not become dialogue-generation rules by themselves. For example, "Horikita wants recognition" does not imply that every disagreement is secretly a bid for approval; the novels have already demonstrated consequential independence. The caption names an attachment vector, not a total motive.

# 7. The late-Year-2 Activity Report — *Second List* directly audits the V12.5 thesis

The V12.5 report is unusually important because it comes after the project has already frozen a high-resolution local reading. We can therefore ask whether official retrospective compression:

- contradicts it;
- merely restates it;
- or independently sharpens the same structure.

The answer is predominantly **STRENGTHEN**.

## 7.1 Kei — cognition, performance, grief

The breakup summary records the precise split the deep reading identified:

`cognitive understanding -> strong outward conduct -> private grief`

Kei recognizes the intended lesson in independence. That matters. She is not a passive object with no model of what is happening.

But recognition of the lesson does not prove that she selected the lesson, possessed the relevant information early enough, or had already completed the emotional transition.

The guidebook therefore strengthens the existing negative answer to reciprocal-information sufficiency.

## 7.2 Ichinose — the third answer becomes official retrospective emphasis

The report records the prepared two-option structure and Ichinose's third response.

The Check Point then goes further: it explicitly labels her action as exceeding Ayanokōji's prediction.

This is unusually high-value editorial corroboration because the phrase names exactly what makes the V12.5 scene diagnostic.

A person can comply with a developer's desired outcome and still remain inside the developer's authored model.

A person who produces an answer the developer did not possess demonstrates something stronger:

> **the modeled subject has become an epistemic source in her own right.**

That is the heart of counter-authorship.

## 7.3 Horikita — preparation is explicit, proof remains future-facing

The Check Point interprets Ayanokōji's final-exam conduct as preparation for the class to continue functioning after his transfer.

This validates the "training wheels / succession test" reading.

But the class-review page still frames post-transfer maintenance as the future problem. The guidebook thus makes the distinction unusually clean:

`developer prepares separation != developed structure has already proved independence after separation`

## 7.4 Sakayanagi — no retroactive rescue of the succession failure

Nothing in the guidebook repairs the missing internal successor.

The former class remains talented, but the political response to sovereign loss is to accept another strategic center. That may be rational. It is not the same achievement as reproducing leadership internally.

# 8. Bonus fiction I — `模擬デート`

## 8.1 Position and authority

`模擬デート` is set shortly before the second-year uninhabited-island examination. It is first-person Kei fiction and therefore outranks the guidebook's compressed romance captions when the question is **Kei's lived experience at that point in time**.

## 8.2 Secrecy is not only protection; it is deprivation

Kei invents a way for the couple to spend time near each other at Keyaki Mall while appearing to be separate. The experiment succeeds operationally: other students do not recognize the date.

Emotionally, it is incomplete.

Her narration specifies what proximity alone cannot provide:

- direct words;
- eye contact;
- hand-to-hand contact;
- public legitimacy.

She describes the experience as enjoyable and lonely at once, then concludes that she wants to date Kiyotaka openly as soon as possible.

This is important because it prevents "secrecy" from being analyzed only as a strategic security choice.

For Kei, secrecy has an experiential cost.

## 8.3 The kitten strap — responsiveness without vulnerable presentation

Kei notices a kitten strap with a ribbon. Later a bag appears outside her room containing precisely that item. She removes her other straps and uses only the gift.

The scene supports at least three claims.

First, Kei's romantic experience contains ordinary joy and preference, not merely dependency.

Second, Ayanokōji can notice or infer a preference and respond behaviorally.

Third, the form of response matters: the gift is delivered indirectly, without a face-to-face moment in which he has to expose emotional intent.

This is characteristic enough to matter for later modeling:

> **Ayanokōji can perform care through accurate action while still minimizing the interpersonal vulnerability of presenting the care as care.**

That is not the same as saying the act is insincere.

It means the **channel** of attachment remains controlled.

## 8.4 What the story changes at the Year-2 boundary

The story does not reopen the negative conclusion about informed reciprocal choice at the breakup.

It changes the historical texture of that conclusion.

Kei had long wanted:

- a more public relation;
- embodied reciprocity;
- ordinary dating;
- direct contact.

The eventual breakup therefore ends not merely a dependency arrangement but a relationship in which she had an ordinary positive future-image of reciprocity.

The correct transition is **STRENGTHEN**, not REVISE:

> the dependency model remains valid, but it is less adequate when treated as the whole romance.

# 9. Bonus fiction II — `あの頃から───`

## 9.1 Position and narrative function

The story occurs on the first holiday after the Survival and Dropout special exam. Ayanokōji narrates.

Its surface plot is small: gym, pop-culture trivia, Ichinose and Amikura, an old accidental message, a private request.

Its analytical importance is large because it supplies a missing intermediate state between the earlier Ichinose crisis and the late-Year-2 counter-authorship result.

## 9.2 Ayanokōji's ordinary weakness is real but not yet protective vulnerability

Ayanokōji researches a pop-culture knowledge gap after getting a question wrong. Ichinose and Amikura can tease or recognize the weakness.

This is useful character evidence. His world-knowledge is not literally complete.

But it does **not** answer the stronger inherited question `Y1H-035`.

He is not voluntarily placing himself in another person's protective authority. He is correcting an information deficit.

Thus:

`ordinary weakness exposure = evidence added`  
`protective vulnerability = still open`

## 9.3 The Ishizaki-message incident — distributed care inside Ichinose's class

Amikura recounts Ichinose's unstable period after the island/confession sequence.

Ichinose accidentally sends Ishizaki a message that was meant for someone else. When the class begins inferring a romantic recipient, she cannot easily invent a lie without risking somebody else's privacy.

She freezes.

Her female classmates then coordinate a solution: they construct a shared cover story in which the message arose from helping another girl's romance. Multiple witnesses make the explanation credible.

This is politically significant.

The class's solidarity is not merely affection flowing upward toward a charismatic leader. It can become **distributed protective competence** when the leader herself cannot author the immediate answer.

That does not solve the class's point-conversion problem.

It does prove that the polity contains relational capacities not reducible to Ichinose's direct command.

## 9.4 Ayanokōji rejects the "I fixed her" narrative

Amikura asks whether he restored Ichinose to normal.

Ayanokōji answers that he did not; Ichinose stood back up by her own power.

This is one of the most important sentences in the entire guidebook.

It constrains a common over-reading of Ayanokōji as omnipotent developer.

His actions matter. His influence matters. He can destabilize, protect, test, withhold, and provoke.

But the story's own narrator refuses total causal ownership of Ichinose's recovery.

That matters ethically and epistemically:

> **influence does not imply authorship of the other person's entire developmental result.**

## 9.5 Amikura introduces social witness into the developer-subject dyad

Amikura then does something the experimental framing tends to hide: she makes Ayanokōji answer to a third party.

She recognizes that Ichinose still sees him as uniquely important and asks him not to hurt her unnecessarily.

Ayanokōji calls the request difficult. That response is revealing. He understands that influence creates practical consequences, but he does not promise an impossible world in which no pain occurs.

Eventually he agrees to make an effort.

The scene introduces a moral structure broader than:

`Ayanokōji <-> Ichinose`

It is:

`Ayanokōji <-> Ichinose <-> Ichinose's friends / class witnesses`

The subject of an experiment has a social world whose members can contest how the experimenter exercises influence.

That is a significant limit on developmental jurisdiction.

## 9.6 Ichinose refuses to compel Amikura's disclosure

When Ichinose returns, she does not force Amikura to tell her what was said. Later she asks Ayanokōji privately.

Ayanokōji protects Amikura's position and replies in a way that affirms the quality of Ichinose's friendship network.

This small sequence matters because it shows Ichinose respecting another person's informational boundary at a moment when curiosity would make forced disclosure easy to rationalize.

The scene is not a grand strategic victory.

It is evidence of relational governance.

## 9.7 The final line — the completed model becomes incomplete again

Ayanokōji closes by recognizing that Ichinose has changed significantly, has grown rather than merely recovered, and is capable of bringing new discoveries to an analysis he believed finished.

This is the strongest fiction-level bridge to V12.5.

The chronology is important:

1. Ayanokōji once believed the analysis could be completed;
2. the person changes enough to make the completed model obsolete;
3. the later V12.5 Ichinose gives an answer beyond his prediction;
4. the guidebook retrospectively headlines that overrun.

Counter-authorship is therefore not a sudden Year-end trick.

It has an earlier developmental substrate:

> **the person repeatedly becomes more than the model that previously described her.**

# 10. Ayanokōji after the guidebook audit — environmental authorship without epistemic ownership

*Second List* makes the strongest version of Ayanokōji's Year-2 role easier to state precisely.

He is increasingly an **environmental author**.

He:

- calibrates displayed ability;
- cultivates Horikita's class while preparing to leave it;
- preserves competitive possibility across multiple classes;
- uses relationships as information-bearing structures;
- anticipates how leaders will respond to pressure;
- and treats development as something that can be architected.

But the guidebook simultaneously supplies three limits.

### Limit 1 — other people can self-recover

Ichinose's recovery is explicitly attributed to her own power.

### Limit 2 — other people can surprise the model

Ichinose becomes a continuing source of new discoveries and later exceeds prediction.

### Limit 3 — influence creates third-party moral claims

Amikura can demand restraint on behalf of a friend. Ayanokōji is not the only person with standing to define what counts as a successful development.

This suggests a refined ethical formulation:

> **The problem is not that Ayanokōji influences people. Human development is relational. The problem is when predictive success is treated as title to define the other person's goal, acceptable pain, timing of disclosure, or meaning of success.**

Counter-authorship is powerful because it breaks that title without requiring the developed person to deny all prior influence.

# 12. Kei after the guidebook audit — autonomy is multidimensional

The guidebook makes it increasingly difficult to discuss Kei with a one-axis "dependent / independent" scale.

At least four dimensions need separate tracking:

1. **social independence** — can she maintain public/class identity beyond the relationship?
2. **behavioral independence** — can she function and choose under separation?
3. **emotional independence** — has the attachment ceased to organize her inner life?
4. **informational autonomy** — did she possess enough truth to make major relationship choices on reciprocal terms?

At the end of V12.5:

- behavioral independence is demonstrated under acute stress;
- emotional independence is not complete;
- informational reciprocity was insufficient.

`模擬デート` adds a fifth dimension:

This richer model should govern later character ledgers.

# 13. Ichinose after the guidebook audit — from solidarity to epistemic independence

The guidebook produces a particularly coherent Ichinose progression:

`relational legitimacy -> destabilization -> self-recovery -> renewed strategic competence -> model-breaking answer`

The key correction is against two reductive stories.

### Reduction B: "romantic obsession alone made her stronger"

The editorial profile foregrounds romance, but the class network, her self-recovery, and her ability to protect/coordinate relationships show broader developmental resources.

It is that she can define a third option inside a situation he believed he had already reduced to two.

That is why the Year-2 terminal result is better described as **counter-authorship** than "awakening."

# 14. Ryūen and Hiyori after the audit — legitimacy grows faster than constitutional distribution

Ryūen's polity becomes more effective and, in some respects, more legitimate because classmates increasingly trust the competence of his leadership rather than obeying only immediate fear.

But dependence remains high.

Hiyori is one of the clearest internal observers of the cost. Her concern is not that Ryūen lacks ability. It is that the class can lose growth opportunities if his competence becomes too complete a substitute for theirs.

The guidebook therefore leaves `Y1H-026` correctly open.

- preserve people;

- influence atmosphere;

What she has not yet been shown to do at this boundary is **morally redirect Ryūen himself in a way that becomes binding on his conduct**.

# 15. Sakayanagi after the audit — the polity can survive physically without reproducing sovereignty

The guidebook supplies no evidence that requires revising the negative V12.5 succession conclusion.

The class remains populated by strong students. It is not destroyed.

But political survival and reproduced sovereignty are different.

Its response to the leader's exit is not:

`internal successor emerges`

but:

`leadership vacuum -> decline/despair -> Ayanokōji imported as new catalytic center`

The Year-2 lesson is therefore not "centralization always fails."

It is narrower and more useful:

> **centralized optimization can be excellent at producing outcomes while being poor at reproducing the capacity that generated them.**

# 18. Character-modeling implications

The guidebook is unusually valuable for later speech/behavioral modeling, but only if its data are assigned the correct function.

## 18.1 Use OAA as observation context, not personality

A character model should not infer:

`low quick-thinking score -> generally slow thinker`

or:

`high social contribution -> morally trustworthy`

Instead record:

- date;
- observation regime;
- displayed behavior;
- participation choice;
- known concealment;
- context in which the measure succeeded or failed.

This is especially important for Ayanokōji, Kōenji, Ryūen, Hiyori, Kei, and Kushida.

## 18.2 Use relationship captions as priors, not scripts

Official relationship labels are good retrieval anchors.

They should be combined with:

- address terms;
- scene-specific power relations;
- current trust;
- public/private audience;
- unresolved grievance;
- whether the character is protecting face;
- whether the character is concealing motive.

## 18.3 Add `model_surprise` as a character-development field

Ichinose makes a strong case for an explicit longitudinal field:

`MODEL_SURPRISE`

Definition:

> evidence that a character generates conduct not contained in the prior predictive model used by another character or by the project itself.

This is more diagnostic than generic "growth" when evaluating self-authorship.

## 18.4 Add `succession_dependency` at class-polity level

For each class, track:

- sovereign/leader concentration;
- distributed competence;
- replacement depth;
- independent initiative;
- crisis performance without leader access;
- willingness to challenge leader;
- whether the class can reproduce strategy after leader exit.

The Year-2 guidebook makes this field mandatory rather than optional.

# 23. Final synthesis — *Second List* as an epistemic limit on its own archive

The guidebook is at its most interesting when it is allowed to be internally plural.

Its tables say:

> students can be measured.

Its profiles say:

> those measurements are useful.

Its caveats say:

> displayed performance and total capacity are not the same.

Its class reviews say:

> outcomes do not prove succession.

Its relationship captions say:

> attachment can be compressed into a retrievable vector.

Its fiction says:

> lived meaning exceeds the vector.

And its late Year-2 checkpoint says:

> Ayanokōji can prepare the board, yet Ichinose can still move beyond his prediction.

That is not a contradiction to be solved by choosing one guidebook layer over another. It is the guidebook's most productive result.

Year 2 increasingly concerns systems that try to make people legible:

- OAA;
- class points;
- rankings;
- White Room developmental models;
- Ayanokōji's predictive models;
- leader-centered class constitutions;
- retrospective guidebook files.

The completed second-year source corpus repeatedly shows that legibility is real without being exhaustive.

The best student is not always the best-recorded student.  
The most coordinated class is not necessarily the most independent class.  
The person who understands the developer's lesson has not necessarily authored it.  
The person shaped by another can still become capable of answering beyond that person's model.

So the Year-2 boundary can now be stated in its strongest form:

> **The ethical alternative to developmental ownership is not a fantasy of influence-free autonomy. It is reciprocal authorship: a condition in which influence remains real, but the developed person retains authority to redefine the goal, surprise the model, refuse the prepared choice, and become a source of new knowledge rather than merely the object of someone else's analysis.**

`Second List` does not close that problem.

It officially confirms that Year 2 has made it the problem that matters.
