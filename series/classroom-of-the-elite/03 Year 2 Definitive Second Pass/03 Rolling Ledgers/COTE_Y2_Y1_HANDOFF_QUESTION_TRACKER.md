## Status semantics for later analysis and reconstruction

The transition category is not merely archival metadata. It controls how the claim may be used downstream.

### `ANSWERED_BY_DEVELOPMENT`

Use the **end-of-Year-2 developed state** when reconstructing a scenario placed at the `Y2SL` boundary. Do not back-project that state into Year 1. A later Year-3 reversal would be a new transition, not proof that the Year-2 adjudication was mistaken.

### `ANSWERED_BY_REVELATION`

The evidence primarily changes what the **reader knows about a state that was already present or concealed**. Retrospective reader-level reconstruction may use the revelation where temporally legitimate, but character knowledge must still obey its own information boundary. Revelation is especially dangerous to back-project into volume-local interpretation.

### `REFRAMED`

The inherited binary or wording is no longer the best analytical question. Downstream work must use the **current Year-2 formulation**, not force an answer to the obsolete binary. Reframed questions are therefore active lenses rather than unresolved yes/no switches.

### `PARTIALLY_ANSWERED`

Some subconditions are demonstrated and others remain unproven. Reconstruction must preserve the demonstrated branch **and** the unresolved limit. A simulator may generalize only from the demonstrated subcase; it may not silently treat partial resolution as a global trait.

### `STILL_OPEN`

Treat closure as a hard abstention. A Year-2-bound reconstruction may generate multiple plausible behaviors, but it must not assert that the corpus has already shown the missing capacity. The four open questions are especially useful as prospective Year-3 tests.
