# Examination Ledger
## Canonical current-state exam architecture through Year 2 / *Second List*

# 0. What this ledger is for

A *Classroom of the Elite* examination is not merely a container in which preexisting ability becomes visible. It is a **temporary rule-world**.

The rule decides:

- what counts as success;
- which capacities become valuable;
- which weaknesses become leverage;
- whether an individual, pair, group, representative, or class is the operative unit;
- whether private points, class points, mobility, protection, or continued enrollment are at stake;
- what information participants receive and what remains hidden;
- whether refusal is possible;
- whether weaker members can be sheltered or stronger members can substitute for them;
- whether development beneath an old rating becomes valuable;
- whether relationships become usable infrastructure;
- and whether an official result can be trusted as evidence of who or what actually produced it.

The governing Year-2 exam question is therefore:

> **What did this rule make strength mean, and who gained the right to impose the consequence?**

This ledger owns exam/event architecture, units of competition, measured capacities, reward/penalty structure, risk allocation, rescue and insurance, information asymmetry, leadership discretion inside a test, scoring/result interpretation, exam-to-polity implications, and what each result can or cannot prove.

It does **not** own the full constitutional history of ANHS, the White Room as an institution, OAA as a complete measurement ontology, the complete ethics of every expulsion, or full character/relationship interpretation. Those route to their canonical topical homes.

# 1. Executive thesis — Year 2 turns tests of ability into tests of the value assigned to ability

The strongest Year-2 exam progression is:

> **measure people → price people → pool and insure risk → force collective sacrifice → convert individual merit into mobility → convert contribution into capital → reward development beneath the rating → ration protection → make relational knowledge itself an exam resource.**

The compact thesis is:

> **An ANHS exam does not merely measure a preexisting student. It authors a temporary world in which some capacities become valuable, some weaknesses become leverage, some relationships become infrastructure, and some people become disposable. Year 2 therefore moves the examination question from “who has more ability?” toward “which rule converts which capacity into points, protection, bargaining power, mobility, expulsion risk, or jurisdiction?”**

This produces five recurring corrections.

## 1.1 Capacity is not task value

A person can possess high capacity and have low marginal value under a particular rule. A person with a weak institutional rating can become highly valuable when the exam multiplies the value of their successful contribution. A person with enormous raw ability can remain politically unusable if they refuse participation.

## 1.2 Score is not causal attribution

A recorded first place can be real while the causal route to it includes hidden intervention, network failure, deception, administrative interference, cross-class signaling, or an external actor changing the field.

## 1.3 Clean procedure is not complete meaning

A vote can be counted perfectly while the resulting unanimity conceals pressure, time scarcity, unequal information, prior commitments, hidden authorship, or radically different reasons for the same ballot.

## 1.4 Individual success is not polity development

A class can win because one exceptional person solved the decisive problem. That does not prove distributed capacity, succession competence, protected dissent, or that the class can reproduce the result when the exceptional person leaves.

## 1.5 Harmful origin is not redeemed by later success

If a preserved student later contributes strongly, that does not retroactively legitimate who was sacrificed to preserve them. If a coerced or manipulated choice later yields growth, the growth remains real and the coercive method remains analytically separate.

# 3. Exam-reading protocol

Every examination should be read through at least fourteen fields.

| Field | Diagnostic question |
|---|---|
| **Object** | What does the exam claim or appear to test? |
| **Unit** | Individual, pair, small group, class, leader, representative, year? |
| **Task-fit** | Which capacities become valuable under this specific design? |
| **Visibility** | What information is public, hidden, delayed, or role-specific? |
| **Choice** | What may participants choose, refuse, trade, or delegate? |
| **Reward** | Private points, class points, protection, transfer, status, autonomy? |
| **Penalty** | Point loss, expulsion, mobility loss, social exposure, leader liability? |
| **Risk distribution** | Is risk symmetric across participants? |
| **Rescue** | Can risk be insured, bought out, transferred, or socially pooled? |
| **Leadership discretion** | Who allocates protection, targets, roles, or sacrifice? |
| **Record integrity** | Is scoring reliable? Can implementation or provenance be captured? |
| **Development sensitivity** | Does the exam reward current growth beneath old ratings? |
| **Polity effect** | Does it test distributed class capacity or merely exceptional actors? |
| **Inference limit** | What does the final result *not* prove? |

The final field is mandatory. A strong exam ledger must be able to say:

> **This result is institutionally real, but it does not prove X.**

# 4. Frozen Year-1 baseline — the exam is already a theory of merit before Year 2

The Year-1 combined institution/exam ledger remains historical authority for complete Year-1 mechanics. The through-Y2 ledger inherits six conclusions.

## 4.1 Environmental tests reveal ability that classrooms hide

The first uninhabited-island examination made camping competence, resource management, practical judgment, information security, leadership designation, and ecological adaptation politically valuable. It established an early anti-reductionist rule:

> **A student who is weak on the school's default academic hierarchy may become central when the environment changes what competence is for.**

## 4.2 Information games test epistemic ability, not merely knowledge possession

Year-1 special exams repeatedly turn hidden roles, leader identities, class plans, rumor, deception, and incomplete information into strategic resources. Year 2 makes this much more explicit.

## 4.3 Group design redistributes individual capacity

Mixed-group and cross-class structures show that a student can become insurance, liability, leader, bargaining asset, or support node depending on the unit of competition. The exam therefore does not simply rank students. It **aggregates students differently**.

## 4.4 Rescue mechanisms create a political economy of mercy

Year 1 already makes continued enrollment purchasable under some conditions. Private-point rescue and Protection Points mean the institution can make mercy formally possible while making access to mercy unequal.

## 4.5 Expulsion may be an intended output

The Year-1 Class Vote was introduced because the year had produced no expellees. Sometimes an exam does not merely create a risk that happens to eliminate someone. Elimination is part of the function.

## 4.6 A sophisticated exam can still be invalidated at the record layer

The Event Selection Exam is a broad portfolio test of class knowledge, event choice, information security, delegation, substitution depth, and commander judgment. Yet Tsukishiro's chess intervention proves:

> **good measurement architecture cannot save an exam whose communication/record layer is captured.**

Year 2 inherits this as a hard fairness condition.

# 5. `Y2V01` — cross-year pairing
## Legibility becomes market value

The first Year-2 special exam arrives immediately after OAA becomes public. The school first makes students more legible, then creates a rule in which other people must **act on the file**.

## 5.1 Formal architecture

`Y2V01-E017–E020` establish:

- one first-year pairs with one second-year;
- five written subjects;
- combined maximum 1,000 points;
- a pair at **500 or below** causes the second-year to be expelled;
- the first-year ordinarily suffers a much lighter private-point penalty;
- deliberate sabotage/extortion is prohibited;
- but the asymmetry still creates an exploitable bargaining structure.

The second-year cannot simply be individually “good enough.” Even a perfect 500 by one partner cannot survive a zero from the other. The test therefore makes **dependence itself** an exam variable.

## 5.2 What the exam actually measures

At first glance: academic ability.

More fully:

- academic ability;
- partner selection;
- bargaining;
- trust;
- class-level triage;
- market timing;
- information use;
- willingness to spend private points;
- cross-year diplomacy;
- ability to distinguish current development from stale OAA.

The exam is therefore a written test embedded inside a political market.

## 5.3 Asymmetric stakes create price

`Y2V01-E018` is decisive. The same combined score has radically different consequences for the two partners. That means first-year academic ability is not only ability. It is **bargaining power created by asymmetric risk**.

Paid recruitment by Sakayanagi and Ryūen helps normalize a market (`Y2V01-E032`). Hōsen can demand millions because the exam makes a second-year's survival dependent on first-year cooperation (`Y2V01-E033–E034`).

> **Measurement plus asymmetric consequence creates a market.**

## 5.4 Horikita's response — insure the weak, do not maximize the elite

Horikita does not pair the strongest with the strongest. She prioritizes protecting weaker classmates (`Y2V01-E021–E023`). Her explicit goals are no expulsion, no ruinous money war, and at least third place (`Y2V01-E026`). Her proposed cross-year arrangement rejects academic exclusion and point hierarchy (`Y2V01-E027–E028`).

Thus the same exam can produce two theories of merit:

- **auction merit** — high ability commands price;
- **collective insurance merit** — high ability is allocated where it prevents terminal loss.

## 5.5 OAA becomes useful and immediately stale

Horikita already judges Sudō's present academics above his historical OAA snapshot (`Y2V01-E023`). The exam therefore dramatizes:

`recorded past performance -> current developmental state -> current task value`.

Those are not one variable.

## 5.6 Result and inference limit

Class exam averages place Sakayanagi first, Ryūen second, Horikita third, and Ichinose fourth (`Y2V01-E060`). Post-exam class points are recorded as:

- Sakayanagi/A position: **1169**
- Ryūen: **565**
- Ichinose: **539**
- Horikita: **283** (`Y2V01-E061`).

Ayanokōji deliberately scores 100 in mathematics, revealing prior latent capacity (`Y2V01-E058–E059`).

The result does **not** prove that the file predicted Ayanokōji's true capacity, the highest-scoring polity has the strongest constitution, paid recruitment is more legitimate than mutual cooperation, or all first-year decisions were governed only by the public exam. The hidden twenty-million-point bounty (`Y2V01-E054`) creates a parallel incentive layer.

> **Boundary lock:** Y2V01 converts public legibility into market structure. It is nominally a written examination but substantively a test of how classes allocate risk when one person's score can determine another person's continued membership.

# 6. `Y2V02–Y2V04` — second uninhabited-island examination
## Ability becomes portfolio, insurance, surveillance, and ecosystem

The island examination is the largest Year-2 test environment. Treat V02–V04 as one exam with changing local epistemic states: V02 establishes architecture, V03 demonstrates attribution failure, and V04 supplies terminal results and collision with administrative sovereignty.

## 6.1 Individual and group incentives are intentionally in tension

The exam draws on academics, physical ability, mental endurance, communication, navigation, information management, coordination, and adaptability. Second-years may form same-year small groups and later expand them up to six (`Y2V02-E039–E040`). Reward distribution depends on class composition (`Y2V02-E041`).

This creates a deliberate tension:

- mixed-class grouping can distribute risk;
- mono-class grouping can concentrate class-level upside.

The exam does not offer one universal “best group.” It offers different risk-return structures to different polities.

## 6.2 Bottom-five expulsion converts endurance into terminal risk

`Y2V02-E042–E043` establish that bottom-five groups face expulsion unless rescue conditions are satisfied and a **six-million-private-point rescue** exists, divided across group size.

The group becomes an insurance object. Larger groups can spread rescue burden, but group size also changes score aggregation, coordination, exposure, and class interest.

`social composition -> performance -> terminal risk -> rescue price`.

## 6.3 Cards make risk tradable

The eight card types are Advance, Extra, Half, Ride-Along, Insurance, Increase, Nullification, and Trial (`Y2V02-E044`). Ownership is visible through OAA and transfer is possible under restrictions (`Y2V02-E045`).

The card layer turns exam advantage into property, information, bargaining material, insurance, and class-specific political capital. Ayanokōji's Trial-card transaction, conditioned on a Half card protecting Kei, is a clean example (`Y2V02-E078`).

## 6.4 Choice remains real inside strategic pressure

Horikita recognizes that class guidance cannot absolutely coerce grouping; final choice belongs to the student (`Y2V02-E046`). Kōenji's bargain is the extreme case: if he wins individually, he receives future noninterference; if he fails, later cooperation becomes owed (`Y2V02-E081–E083`).

> **Collective access to exceptional ability is purchased through bounded future obligation rather than presumed from class membership.**

## 6.5 Surveillance increases data and preserves attribution gaps

V03 proves dense tracking does not create omniscience. GPS/device records can be incomplete, misleading, manipulated, or detached from actor identity (`Y2V03-E016`, `E032`, `E094`). Komiya/Kinoshita shows objective traces may establish place, time, device state, and injury while failing to establish attacker, intention, or causal sequence.

> **Telemetry improves legibility without abolishing inference.**

## 6.6 Record capture can invalidate fairness above the scoring layer

Shiba acknowledges falsified histories (`Y2V03-E096`). Tsukishiro describes fabricating misconduct under low-surveillance conditions (`Y2V03-E098`) and uses the threat of fabricated innocent-group removal as coercion (`Y2V03-E100`).

The exam therefore has at least three fairness layers:

1. rule fairness;
2. scoring fairness;
3. record/provenance fairness.

An exam can pass the first two and fail the third.

## 6.7 Final results — the two-point lesson

V04 records:

- 13 students retire during the exam;
- bottom five groups are all third-years;
- **15 third-years are expelled** and none possess sufficient rescue protection (`Y2V04-E192`);
- Sakayanagi's group finishes third with **261** (`E194`);
- Nagumo finishes second with **325** (`E195`);
- Kōenji finishes first alone with **327** (`E196`);
- no second-year is expelled (`E199`).

The two-point gap is analytically crucial. Ayanokōji judges that if Nagumo had remained on radio, the top result likely reverses and lower-table outcomes may also change (`E198`). The official scores are real. They do **not** prove Kōenji would necessarily defeat an uninterrupted Nagumo system under otherwise identical conditions.

> **result truth != counterfactual proof of superiority.**

## 6.8 Boundary lock

> **The island exam creates a market of grouping, cards, information, insurance, surveillance, and command architecture, then demonstrates that a two-point first place can be causally contingent on a political interruption outside the winner's own performance.**

# 7. `Y2V05` — Unanimous Vote Special Examination
## Procedure becomes constitutional sacrifice

If the island exam maximizes distributed complexity, V05 puts students in one room, makes the ballots work, and makes the result harder—not easier—to interpret.

## 7.1 Formal architecture

The `満場一致特別試験` protects individual ballot privacy through secret voting, anonymity, prohibition of binding vote contracts, prohibition of outside coercion, personal ballot casting, and a physical arrangement reducing direct surveillance. Chabashira says the system prioritizes `生徒個人の意思`.

Yet:

- five questions must be resolved;
- total time is **five hours**;
- failure costs **300 class points**;
- the final question can grant **100 class points** if one student is expelled;
- once YES becomes unanimous, an expulsion must occur;
- Protection Points are disabled.

## 7.2 Central paradox

> **maximum private freedom at ballot level / maximum collective coercion at outcome level**

A dissenter cannot be directly compelled, yet one dissenter can impose enormous collective cost. A majority cannot directly see the dissenter, yet everyone knows someone is blocking closure. The exam manufactures a conflict among privacy, individual autonomy, collective responsibility, time, class-point scarcity, and membership.

## 7.3 Four polities, four constitutional readings

**Ichinose polity:** no-expulsion solidarity becomes constitutive; Kanzaki's strategic dissent is real but insufficient to change the class's definition of acceptable victory.

**Ryūen polity:** false YES ballots expose the genuine dissenter; the exam becomes a tool of managed dissent under authoritarian sovereignty.

**Sakayanagi-origin polity:** Sakayanagi pre-solves the value calculation and rejects the 100-point expulsion path because the strategic cost of member loss exceeds the reward. Fast unanimity is agenda control, not democratic equivalence.

**Horikita polity:** the class enters the expulsion route; Horikita independently preserves Kushida; Ayanokōji then imposes an ability/social-value criterion through which Airi becomes the sacrifice.

## 7.4 Accurate procedure is not complete moral truth

Airi's final 38–0 expulsion vote is institutionally legible. It does not encode who initially wanted expulsion, who yielded to the deadline, why Horikita preserved Kushida, why Haruka resisted, how Ayanokōji constructed the criterion, or how Airi's psychology was used to break the final resistance.

> **An exam can produce a perfectly accurate record that still radically under-describes the act.**

This is Year 2's inverse of the Tsukishiro problem:

- Tsukishiro: the record can be false.
- V05: the record can be accurate and insufficient.

## 7.5 OAA becomes a disposability technology only because the rule creates scarcity

The exam does not say “expel the lowest OAA student.” Ayanokōji uses OAA and social context because the class has entered a rule state where someone must leave. Measurement is not inherently culling. But once the institution manufactures terminal scarcity, legibility can become a tool for ranking who is easiest to lose.

## 7.6 Inference limit

The result proves Horikita can counter-author Ayanokōji's preferred answer, the class can be forced into an irreversible membership choice, institutional privacy does not remove collective coercion, and a clean vote count does not settle legitimacy.

It does **not** prove later Kushida usefulness justifies Airi's expulsion, Airi's final consent makes the preceding choice environment neutral, or the class discovered a timeless objective hierarchy of human value.

> **Boundary lock:** V05 is the clearest Year-2 example of a rule that protects individual choice procedurally while manufacturing collective coercion structurally.

# 8. `Y2V06` — sports festival
## Individual merit becomes class currency and portable mobility

The second sports festival is best classified as a **scored institutional competition** rather than flattened into V05's category. It turns Nagumo's meritocratic ideology into a practical reward system.

## 8.1 Core architecture

Each participant:

- begins with **5 points**;
- must enter **5 different events**;
- may spend points to enter additional events up to **10**.

Individual choices aggregate into class result.

Class stakes:

- first place: **+150 class points**
- fourth place: **–150 class points**

Individual first place by year/gender can choose:

- **2,000,000 private points**
- or a **limited-duration class-transfer ticket**.

## 8.2 What the festival measures

It rewards physical ability, event selection, stamina management, willingness to spend participation points, class portfolio design, coordination, and individual ambition. But it also asks:

> **Should exceptional individual performance return value to the class, or purchase an exit from class fate?**

The transfer-ticket option gives individual merit a portable institutional form.

## 8.3 Result and inference limit

Horikita's class finishes first. Sakayanagi's finishes fourth. Ayanokōji is absent from active competition. The result matters as one of the better Year-2 examples of class performance without direct Ayanokōji execution. It still does not prove full polity independence; one festival is not the post-transfer succession test.

> **Boundary lock:** the sports festival turns individual merit into a convertible asset: personal performance can feed collective class ascent or purchase personal mobility.

# 9. `Y2V07` — cultural festival
## Contribution becomes budget, reputation, and market execution

The cultural festival is another exam-adjacent scored event. Its central resource is not test performance but **economic/social execution under public attention**.

Classes manage budget, rented space, materials, staffing, pricing, queues, publicity, visitor attraction, and revenue. Additional class budget is allocated partly through accumulated contribution. Horikita's class receives the largest Year-2 additional allocation: **39,400 points**.

The chain is:

> **prior contribution → budget → purchasing capacity → attraction → revenue**

The festival rewards hospitality, presentation, marketing, logistics, costume/image management, price setting, queue control, social reputation, public performance, and media attention. It is a strong corrective to an academic-only model of `実力`.

Horikita/Ryūen can publicly stage conflict as attention management while visitor spending remains real.

> **A performed narrative can causally affect a real scored market without making the resulting revenue fictitious.**

> **Boundary lock:** the cultural festival makes reputation and social presentation economically legible and tests whether a class can turn accumulated contribution and public attention into executable collective value.

# 10. `Y2V08` — no formal exam as negative control
## Development continues when the scoreboard recedes

Y2V08 matters partly because it does **not** need a new formal special examination to produce evidence. The school trip and relational environment show cross-class interaction, ordinary preference, social knowledge, rivalry with moral friction, and friendship under no immediate class-point score.

> **If analysis only studies people under formal examination pressure, it will mistake exam-optimized behavior for the whole character.**

A mature exam ledger needs non-exam controls.

# 11. `V00` — retrospective genealogy, not a Year-2 exam

Volume 0 should not be inserted into the Year-2 event list as though it were another exam. Its contribution is retrospective. The White Room gives the reader a maximally designed developmental environment; ANHS gives repeated temporary designed environments.

The resemblance is real: performance thresholds, environmental manipulation, controlled scarcity, observation, development under pressure. The differences remain substantial: ANHS contains plural authorities, peers create unscripted relations, counterforce exists, students produce purposes designers did not intend, and exam environments end rather than constituting the whole world.

> **Designed test environment != total ownership of what participants become inside it.**

# 12. `Y2V09` — cooperative written special examination
## The rating is made valuable by being outgrown

Y2V09 does not merely use OAA to sort stronger from weaker students. It uses OAA to create **different marginal values for the same correct answer**.

## 12.1 Core architecture

Every student must contribute. The strongest academic student cannot answer everything. The weakest student cannot simply be hidden. Correct answers from lower OAA academic categories are worth more. An A-rated academic performer earns **1 point** per correct response, while lower categories can earn several times as much.

The exact full schedule should be retrieved from V09 if needed; this ledger does not invent values not preserved in the current evidence route.

## 12.2 The exam reverses the intuitive hierarchy

A low institutional academic rating ordinarily marks vulnerability. Under this exam:

> **low baseline + successful development = high marginal value**

The rule does not claim weaker students are secretly stronger. It asks:

> **What has the class done with the student since the rating was produced?**

## 12.3 Horikita's polity is structurally advantaged by prior development

The class has normalized peer study and weaker-student development. Sudō is the clearest example. The exam rewards not merely individual academics but **developmental infrastructure**.

## 12.4 OAA becomes its own adversarial test

The same institutional measurement system is used in opposite ways:

- V01: low OAA creates vulnerability and partner-market pressure.
- V09: low OAA can create high marginal value if the class has developed the student.

This is one of the strongest reasons not to treat OAA as ontology. The meaning of the number depends on the rule.

> **Boundary lock:** V09 turns measurement against static merit. The exam gives the greatest marginal value to students whose development makes an old weakness label understate their current contribution.

# 13. `Y2V09.5` — ordinary-life countercontrol

No new formal exam is required. Winter break restores personal choice, leisure, unscored relationship, and ordinary desire. Formal tests reveal performance under designed pressure; ordinary life reveals what people choose when the institution does not prescribe the question.

# 14. `Y2V10` — survival/elimination special examination
## Finite protection forces explicit valuation

V10 makes protection itself a scarce exam resource.

## 14.1 Core architecture

The exam uses a **20-turn attack/defense** structure across the four classes.

An attacking leader selects:

- knowledge category;
- difficulty;
- **5 targets**.

A defending leader protects **5 students**.

A protected student counts as correct. A student with **3 wrong answers** is eliminated. Each eliminated member costs the class **1 point**. If the last-place class has eliminated members, its leader selects one eliminated member for **expulsion**.

## 14.2 Categories broaden the object of measurement

The test includes conventional academics plus entertainment, life knowledge, subculture, and other broad domains. A high academic student may remain vulnerable outside academic categories; culturally broad or socially ordinary students may become unexpectedly resilient.

## 14.3 Protection is simultaneously tactical and symbolic

A protection slot means probability management, confidence, political insulation, and possible survival buffering against the expulsion branch. But:

> **protection != total value**

A leader may protect someone because they are strategically important, socially central, being targeted, covered by contract, or personally important. The same visible action can have different causal meanings.

## 14.4 Four value types diverge

V10 requires separate tracking of:

1. **institutional value**
2. **strategic value**
3. **contractual value**
4. **relational value**

Kamuro's expulsion is the decisive failure case. Sakayanagi treats several vulnerable members as sufficiently equivalent for a lottery-like choice. Only after loss does relational irreplaceability become legible to her. The exam exposes a variable the optimizer failed to include.

## 14.5 Cross-class signaling becomes legitimate exam strategy

Ichinose repeatedly targets Kei. Horikita reads the repeated targeting of a socially central/protectable student as a signal, opening a communication route that helps Horikita avoid last place. The same outward exam action can serve attack, message, alliance, or deterrence. Exam interpretation requires causal context.

## 14.6 Result-level caution

Ichinose's class finishes **second without internal casualties**. Sakayanagi's class falls to last after internal betrayal and Kamuro is expelled.

The result does **not** prove Kamuro was objectively the least valuable member, Ichinose's solidarity is strategically weak, Ryūen's victory mechanism produces distributed development, or finite protection is a complete map of leader attachment.

> **Boundary lock:** V10 converts protection into a forced theory of value. Leaders act on incomplete models of what people contribute, and irreversible loss reveals what the model omitted.

# 15. `Y2V11` — exchange camp
## Lower stakes produce higher diagnostic information

The exchange camp is explicitly **not a special exam**.

- ordinary class-point movement does not define it;
- expulsion is not the expected penalty;
- participation is comparatively voluntary;
- years and classes mix;
- the camp uses **19 games** spanning craft, sport, knowledge, luck, and unfamiliar tasks.

The event is a high-value negative control.

High-stakes exams induce concealment, optimization, coalition behavior, fear, and defensive role performance. Lower stakes permit more diagnostic observation of curiosity, adaptability, spontaneous competence, embarrassment, risk preference, and social adjustment.

> **Lower institutional stakes can produce better character information even when they produce less official ranking value.**

Maezono becomes the harshest example.

# 39. Canonical Year-2 examination synthesis

Year 2 begins by making students more legible. It ends by making their relationships legible enough to score.

Between those points, the school repeatedly changes what “ability” is worth.

Ayanokōji can be mediocre in a public file and perfect in mathematics.

A first-year's academic strength can become a priced survival resource for a second-year.

A protection slot can keep someone safe while failing to explain what that person means to the protector.

A lower-stakes camp can reveal more about adaptable human ability than a harsher exam.

And a year-end test can convert microexpressions, trust history, and knowledge of another person's likely lie into formal expulsion power.

The exam sequence therefore destroys the simplest reading of `実力至上主義`.

“Ability” is never only the thing a person possesses. Inside ANHS it is also:

- what the institution chooses to ask;
- what the rule makes valuable;
- what the person chooses to display;
- what other people can coordinate;
- what the class can insure;
- what the leader can protect;
- what the record can prove;
- what the scorer cannot see;
- and what consequence the institution attaches to the result.

The terminal Year-2 exam principle is:

> **An examination is not a neutral window onto merit. It is an authored conversion system: it converts selected capacities into institutional consequences. The fairest reading of a result therefore requires asking not only who won, but what was made measurable, what was made valuable, what was made risky, who could refuse, who could be rescued, and whose authorship survived the test.**

The strongest unresolved question for the next analytical layer is not another exam result. It is the measurement problem underneath all of them:

> **When the school assigns a number to a person, what exactly has it measured—and when does that number describe performance rather than become a theory of the person?**
