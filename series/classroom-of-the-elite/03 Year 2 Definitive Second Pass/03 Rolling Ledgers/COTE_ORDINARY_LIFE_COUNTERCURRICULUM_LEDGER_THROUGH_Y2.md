Year 1 distributed this responsibility across the full synthesis, the Ayanokōji/relationship/ethics specialists, the Japanese narration/genre specialist, and the source-local decimal-volume readings. Those frozen Year-1 artifacts remain authoritative at the `Y1FF` boundary. This document is the first canonical completed-year **ledger** whose sole semantic responsibility is ordinary life as counter-curriculum.
>
> The ledger does not claim that every leisure scene is liberatory, that school life is outside institutional design, or that “normality” is a moral endpoint. It records a more precise phenomenon: **situations in which value, learning, attachment, preference, failure, or identity can emerge without being fully exhausted by an externally assigned score, developmental objective, class purpose, or strategist’s prior model.**
>
> Year 3 is excluded. Per-volume readings remain immutable.

# Ordinary Life / Counter-Curriculum Ledger
## Canonical state through Year 2 / *Second List*

# 0. Ledger function

The competitive institution in *Classroom of the Elite* continually assigns purposes.

Study produces grades.

Special examinations produce class points, private points, protection, expulsion risk, or mobility.

OAA converts selected behavior into legible performance.

Student-council activity creates status and political jurisdiction.

The White Room retrospectively represents the most extreme form of this logic: nearly every activity exists because someone designed it to produce a developmental result.

Against that architecture, the novels repeatedly give weight to actions whose value cannot be reduced to an assigned output:

- eating something because it tastes good;
- wanting an inefficient object;
- choosing a book;
- making a friend;
- inviting someone without knowing whether they will accept;
- learning through bodily failure;
- giving a gift;
- watching a film;
- dating;
- talking without strategic need;
- sharing a hobby;
- making something badly;
- laughing;
- being embarrassed;
- repairing a friendship;
- remembering another person through domestic habit;
- imagining a future after school;
- or simply spending time with someone because their presence is wanted.

This ledger calls that domain **ordinary life / counter-curriculum**.

The term does not mean “life outside all curriculum.”

By the end of Year 2, ANHS itself sometimes deliberately engineers ordinary-life encounters. Ayanokōji sometimes converts intimacy into curriculum. The cultural festival turns hospitality and spectacle into a scored economic contest. The year-end examination turns accumulated relational knowledge into formal competitive capital.

The stronger question is therefore:

> **Can an authored environment still generate purposes and meanings that its author did not assign, cannot completely score, and cannot legitimately own?**

That is the counter-curriculum problem.

# 1. Executive Year-2 thesis — ordinary life becomes a theory of surplus purpose

The frozen Year-1 synthesis established ordinary life as **life without an assigned score**.

Volume 4.5 was the decisive interruption. After examinations had made strategic optimization look like the dominant theory of development, the summer interlude asked what students become when no formal examination has already declared what success means. A birthday, film, gift, date, casual walk, ice cream, embarrassment, or purposeless conversation could matter without being a means to victory. The Year-1 result was that freedom requires more than escape from an external controller. It requires access to time and relationships whose value the subject can discover rather than merely receive as an assignment.

Year 2 preserves that claim and makes it substantially harder.

The school itself begins to recognize the value of unscripted or low-stakes experience.

The Hokkaidō trip deliberately mixes students so they will know rivals as people rather than only as class positions and OAA profiles.

The exchange camp removes class-point stakes and thereby reveals capacities that higher-stakes measurement often misses.

Festivals turn craft, hospitality, entertainment, logistics, aesthetics, reputation, and ordinary social skill into forms of value.

Ayanokōji increasingly seeks experiences he knows cannot be learned through advance optimization.

Yet the same year shows three dangers.

First, **counter-curriculum can be colonized by hidden curriculum**. Ayanokōji’s romance with Kei contains genuine affection and ordinary experience while he simultaneously conceptualizes the relationship as a `恋愛のカリキュラム`.

Second, **ordinary life can be institutionally instrumentalized**. The school trip creates authentic humanization partly because the school wants that humanization to affect future competition. The cultural festival converts hospitality and spectacle into budget and ranking. Low-stakes knowledge can later become strategic information.

Third, **relational surplus can become a control surface**. V12 demonstrates that accumulated trust, love, memory, microexpression, and personal knowledge can be converted into formal competitive advantage or indirect jurisdiction.

The Year-2 counter-curriculum thesis must therefore be stronger than:

> ordinary life humanizes people.

The more defensible formulation is:

> **Ordinary life matters because it generates surplus purpose: desires, attachments, skills, memories, meanings, failures, and self-conceptions that are not fully specified by the institution or strategist who created the surrounding opportunity. Its freedom lies not in being uncaused or unstructured, but in remaining open to counter-authored meaning.**

The best evidence is not that no one designed the encounter.

It is that the participants can make the encounter mean something the designer did not already possess.

# 2. Counter-curriculum ontology

For this ledger, ordinary-life evidence is strongest when several of the following dimensions are present.

## 2.1 Unassigned purpose

The participant wants an end that is not reducible to:

- class points;
- OAA;
- institutional rank;
- strategic victory;
- another person’s developmental plan.

Example: Ayanokōji wants the yogurt maker because he wants it, even after cost-performance analysis says not to buy it.

## 2.2 Refusal and uncertainty

The interaction permits an answer that is not known in advance.

A casual invitation can be refused.

A friend can be busy.

A date can fail.

An object can be sold out.

The outcome does not have to be reclassified as “success” because information was gained.

## 2.3 Low conversion pressure

The activity does not immediately become:

- a class-point result;
- expulsion risk;
- protection;
- formal authority;
- scored performance.

Low pressure matters because it allows preference to become visible before actors must optimize for survival.

## 2.4 Inefficiency tolerance

The participant may rationally choose something that is not optimal.

The object can cost more than it saves.

The outing can consume time.

The conversation can accomplish nothing measurable.

The choice remains legitimate because its purpose is experiential.

## 2.5 Failure without catastrophe

A person can:

- fall while skiing;
- make something poorly;
- misunderstand a joke;
- be rejected;
- lose a low-stakes game;
- fail to know an ordinary fact

without the failure becoming proof of low human worth.

## 2.6 Reciprocal contingency

The other person contributes meaning not fully supplied by the initiating actor.

Friendship is not a one-person curriculum.

Conversation changes because the other person answers.

## 2.7 Nonfungibility

A relationship, memory, gift, or habit can matter because of **which person** it involves rather than because it supplies a generic function.

Kamuro’s V10 absence teaches Sakayanagi this negatively.

A second bowl prepared by muscle memory after Kei leaves demonstrates it bodily.

## 2.8 Embodied and sensory knowledge

Ordinary life teaches through:

- taste;
- touch;
- embarrassment;
- fatigue;
- balance;
- intimacy;
- physical routine;
- bodily failure.

This knowledge is not merely another academic subject.

## 2.9 Generative activity

Making, drawing, cooking, designing, choosing, joking, or improvising can require the person to produce rather than reproduce an answer.

The V10 blank sketchbook is the clearest negative image.

## 2.10 Role surplus

A student remains more than:

- class representative;
- leader;
- traitor;
- asset;
- OAA profile;
- girlfriend;
- White Room student.

Ordinary life permits characters to occupy identities not exhausted by their political role.

## 2.11 Futurity beyond the institution

The strongest ordinary-life evidence often points beyond ANHS.

Airi can become Shizuku outside the school.

Kitō can want a fashion career.

Sakayanagi can imagine friendship and reunion after withdrawal.

Expulsion or graduation is therefore not ontological deletion.

## 2.12 Counter-authored meaning

This is the highest-order condition.

An encounter can be institutionally created yet still become counter-curricular if participants make something of it that the institution did not fully script.

The school can arrange a trip.

It cannot dictate exactly what Ryūen, Kitō, Yamamura, Watanabe, Horikita, Ibuki, or Ayanokōji will come to mean to one another.

# 3. Frozen Year-1 baseline

Year 1 already establishes five principles that Year 2 must preserve.

## 3.1 Volume 4.5 — counter-curriculum becomes explicit

The Year-1 full synthesis calls Volume 4.5 structurally indispensable because it interrupts escalation.

Its governing question is:

> **What do these students become when no formal examination has already defined what success means?**

The answer is not “nothing happens.”

Different capacities become visible precisely because the school is not presently demanding a score.

Ordinary life shows:

- privacy;
- leisure;
- embarrassment;
- friendship;
- gifts;
- attraction;
- choice;
- low-stakes social help.

The result is an alternative theory of development.

People can change because they live, not only because someone gives them a test.

## 3.2 Ayanokōji possesses a wider repertoire than coercion

Ordinary life matters ethically because it proves he can:

- scaffold;
- wait;
- preserve refusal;
- decline a relationship;
- protect privacy;
- participate without manufacturing catastrophe.

Later coercion therefore cannot be excused as his only available behavioral mode.

## 3.3 Freedom gains positive content

The Year-1 corpus moves Ayanokōji from negative freedom—

> distance from family and direct control

toward positive freedom—

> a school life containing ends he personally chooses.

The importance of ordinary life is therefore not ornamental.

It supplies the **content** of freedom.

## 3.4 ANHS differs from the White Room through surplus meaning

The Year-1 synthesis explicitly rejects simple equivalence between ANHS and the White Room.

ANHS:

- surveils;
- scores;
- ranks;
- prices;
- coerces;
- removes.

But it also permits:

- friendship;
- leisure;
- romance;
- books;
- food;
- private space;
- multiple teachers;
- rival institutions;
- student politics;
- unscripted time.

The difference is not innocence.

It is **plurality of authorship**.

## 3.5 Ordinary life can still become strategically entangled

Year 1 already gives the central contradiction.

Ayanokōji rejects Satō partly because accepting a conventional date without sufficient feeling would use her merely as experience.

Yet his bond with Kei is easier to integrate into strategic life because it already contains secrecy, protection, class politics, and information asymmetry.

The counter-curriculum is therefore never protected by genre alone.

A date can be ordinary or curricular depending on who authors its purpose and what refusal remains possible.

# 4. Year-2 progression — from ordinary surplus to institutionalized counter-curriculum

## 4.1 Y2V01 — ordinary school life becomes worth public cost

The opening Year-2 volume is dominated by OAA, first-year pairing, bounty politics, and physical danger.

Yet the ordinary-life thread is already consequential.

Ayanokōji’s willingness to expose more of his academic capacity cannot be explained merely as competitive escalation. Remaining at ANHS and preserving the school life he has chosen has become valuable enough that he changes his long-standing visibility policy.

This is an early Year-2 pattern:

> **ordinary life can motivate strategic disclosure without being reducible to the strategy used to preserve it.**

The distinction matters later when ordinary attachments increasingly become things he protects, studies, and sometimes instrumentalizes.

## 4.2 Y2V02 — low-stakes relationship pacing remains ethically diagnostic

V02’s larger theme is jurisdiction.

For the counter-curriculum ledger, the relevant point is that romance, cross-year contact, and ordinary conversation continue developing inside a high-stakes institutional field.

Ayanokōji can pace Kei’s relationship progression and Ichinose’s developmental pressure.

This already demonstrates the danger of **curricular contamination**:

ordinary relational time can be genuine while one participant privately assigns it a developmental schedule.

The counter-curriculum criterion is therefore not “romance exists.”

It is whether the people inside the romance retain purposes and timing not owned by the stronger planner.

## 4.3 Y2V03 — ordinary intimacy survives inside survival fiction

The island examination is not ordinary life.

Yet ordinary human knowledge keeps entering it.

Ike’s concern for Shinohara becomes meaningful because he is not merely optimizing a group’s rank. His words matter because a particular person matters.

Ayanokōji’s first kiss with Kei is similarly double.

The sensory novelty and desire are genuine.

The same kiss is also used to regulate her anxiety.

The scene therefore demonstrates a crucial counter-curriculum warning:

> **an act can contain genuine ordinary meaning and strategic use simultaneously.**

The presence of instrumentality does not make the experience unreal.

The presence of authentic feeling does not make the instrumental use disappear.

## 4.4 Y2V04 — sovereignty reaches a limit in plural agency

V04 is still examination-centered, but its terminal result supports the counter-curriculum indirectly.

Tsukishiro cannot wholly author the field.

Independent procedures, allies, rival interests, and actors with their own motives prevent one sovereign from owning every outcome.

Ayanokōji explicitly values things in school that the White Room cannot provide.

The counter-curriculum is therefore already attached to **plural causality**:

people become different when there are enough independent authors in the environment that no one curriculum can totalize experience.

## 4.5 Y2V04.5 — social embeddedness is knowledge no textbook can contain

V04.5 is the Year-2 counter-curriculum centerpiece.

Its governing concept is social embeddedness.

Ayanokōji measures change through **social density** rather than score:

- friends;
- girlfriend;
- acquaintances;
- cross-class ties;
- memories of shared time.

The decisive line is:

> `友人の作り方は、教科書に載せられるものじゃないな`

How to make friends is not something that can simply be written in a textbook.

This is not a claim that friendship is mysterious or causeless.

It is a claim about method.

Friendship emerges through:

- unequal history;
- accidents;
- conflict;
- jokes;
- embarrassment;
- repeated contact;
- attraction;
- disclosure;
- mutual reinterpretation.

The knowledge is produced **between people**.

It is not fully transferable as curriculum.

Sakayanagi’s discussion of *Beneath the Wheel* makes the contrast explicit: elite cultivation without warmth or relation can become destructive even when performance rises.

Ayanokōji’s cruise life then makes the point concrete.

Ishizaki can invite him because he likes him, not because recruitment is strategically required.

Hiyori can share books without the relationship needing crisis.

Satō can matter to Kei as a friend.

The Ayanokōji Group can supply time whose meaning is not class-point conversion.

Yet V04.5 also supplies the strongest contamination case.

The romance with Kei is genuine.

Ayanokōji also names it a `恋愛のカリキュラム`.

The ledger therefore records:

`ORDINARY_AFFECTION`
+
`HIDDEN_CURRICULUM`
=
`MIXED_COUNTERCURRICULUM`

The scene does not become fake.

Its autonomy becomes incomplete.

## 4.6 Y2V05 — ordinary generativity precedes the institution’s culling logic

V05 is remembered for the Unanimous Vote Special Examination and Airi’s expulsion.

Its early culture-festival preparation matters because it shows the class producing value through ordinary social and practical capacities before the exam forces the class to rank whose presence is least necessary.

Students discuss:

- concept;
- staffing;
- costume;
- demand;
- revenue;
- hospitality;
- organization.

Ideas arise from Satō, Matsushita, Mii-chan, Maezono, and others rather than one hidden strategist.

Horikita creates incentives for initiative.

This is **ordinary generativity becoming collective infrastructure**.

The later exam then performs the inverse operation.

A complex person must be made decision-legible under a forced scarcity rule.

Airi becomes the target of a model combining OAA, social network, self-assertion, and predicted protection.

The contrast is important:

> **ordinary life multiplies the dimensions in which a person can matter; culling compresses those dimensions into a decision about who can be lost.**

The ledger therefore treats V05 as one of the clearest demonstrations of why counter-curriculum and ethics must remain connected.

## 4.7 Y2V06 — peer teaching and non-utilitarian relation

V06 is one of the strongest ordinary-life developmental volumes even though it follows catastrophic class injury.

The class begins redistributing the capacity to help.

Sudō asks for limited advice and reaches answers himself.

Onodera teaches Sudō an anger-regulation technique that he later uses under pressure.

Kei repairs social relations.

Hirata mediates without demanding total control.

The important structure is:

> **people teach one another horizontally rather than every developmental answer flowing from Ayanokōji.**

Kei’s domestic conversation supplies the sharpest non-utilitarian lesson.

When she asks whether Ayanokōji would protect her if she had low OAA, weak social status, and little usefulness, he initially answers through conditional logic.

She rejects the frame.

The desired answer is that the relationship itself matters.

He concludes:

> `そこに理屈なんかは不要`

The point is not irrationality as a virtue.

It is that some relational obligations are not generated by a current contribution score.

Ordinary love can make **nonfungibility** legible.

The same volume immediately preserves the contradiction: Kei asks him not to hide things, while he recognizes she knows very little of his decisive life architecture.

Ordinary intimacy therefore teaches a non-utilitarian grammar without automatically producing reciprocal disclosure.

## 4.8 Y2V07 — the cultural festival commercializes ordinary skill

The festival is a boundary case.

Hospitality, costume, cooking, queue management, atmosphere, embarrassment, teacher participation, spectacle, and customer experience look like ordinary social life.

The institution turns them into a market.

Visitor attention becomes revenue.

Past contribution becomes budget.

Class identity becomes performance.

The event proves that ordinary competencies are real forms of ability.

It also demonstrates **institutional capture**:

once the institution discovers a form of social value, it can attach points, budgets, and strategic incentives to it.

Yet the festival does not exhaust its own meanings.

Haruka’s crisis is transformed not by the school’s scoring system but by evidence that Airi has authored a future outside ANHS.

Airi’s Shizuku activity forces Haruka to revise the belief that expulsion automatically means permanent unhappiness.

That is a major counter-curriculum result:

> **the institution can terminate membership without owning the person’s future.**

## 4.9 Y2V08 — ANHS deliberately authors a counter-curriculum

The Hokkaidō trip is the most conceptually difficult ordinary-life case in Year 2 because the school openly **engineers humanizing contact**.

Students privately rank one another through personal evaluation rather than OAA.

OAA consultation is prohibited.

Mixed-class groups are then built so students encounter people they:

- like;
- dislike;
- barely know.

The school explains that ordinary class competition has prevented students from knowing rivals as people.

This means ANHS itself has recognized a problem in its own curriculum.

The institution creates an environment intended to produce relational legibility.

But it cannot fully author what the encounters mean.

Ayanokōji does not know an ordinary bus object.

He encounters innumerable unknowns outside controlled curriculum.

Most importantly, he deliberately refuses to study skiing completely in advance because the experience of learning at the site has value.

He falls.

He concludes that first failing matters.

This is a direct counter-model to total preparation.

> **Some knowledge is damaged if the learner is not allowed to encounter the unknown as unknown.**

The Kitō material gives the political version.

Watanabe learns Kitō’s dream of becoming a fashion designer.

Competition immediately becomes harder.

The rival is now attached to a future.

The school created the encounter.

It did not dictate that the new knowledge would produce moral hesitation.

V08 therefore gives the mature institutional formulation:

> **a counter-curriculum can be institutionally authored without being institutionally owned if the participants retain enough plurality to counter-author the encounter’s meaning.**

## 4.10 Volume 0 — the retrospective negative image

Volume 0 is not ordinary-life evidence about the Year-2 present.

It is the retrospective genealogy that explains why ordinary life matters so much.

The White Room organizes childhood through assigned developmental utility.

Children know measured abilities before they know one another as companions.

Food is initially described through nutritional function.

Conversation has little assigned purpose.

Feeling rarely changes adult behavior.

Yuki’s carrot question is therefore profound.

Kiyotaka first answers preference with biochemical/function information.

He has to be prompted toward:

> What do **I** like?

This introduces a category of self-relation the curriculum did not prioritize.

Yuki’s smile and Shirō’s desire for the outside world further demonstrate that even a common extreme curriculum does not generate a common self.

The White Room can train.

It cannot fully pre-author:

- liking;
- friendship;
- freedom;
- curiosity about the outside;
- what another person comes to mean.

Volume 0 therefore strengthens the Year-1 distinction:

> **the problem with total curriculum is not only severity. It is the attempt to make every activity answer to an assigned developmental end.**

## 4.11 Y2V09 — ordinary development becomes politically consequential

V09’s exam structure famously creates a case where a student with lower old academic OAA can possess greater marginal value under the actual rule after development.

For the counter-curriculum ledger, the important point is **where that development came from**.

Sudō’s study progress is not simply school-imposed remediation.

It has become self-owned.

He later helps weaker classmates.

What begins as personal study becomes generative social capacity.

The volume therefore supports a broader rule:

> **ordinary practice can create forms of value that formal measurement recognizes only later.**

A person can develop between snapshots.

The institution is often downstream from the life that created the new capacity.

## 4.12 Y2V09.5 — desire as counter-authorship

V09.5 is the Year-2 ordinary-life manifesto.

The opening frames ordinary student holidays as precious because the remaining time is finite.

Ayanokōji does not need an extraordinary event.

Friends, a girlfriend, seniors, juniors, chance encounters, and ordinary days are enough to make time valuable.

The yogurt-maker sequence is the cleanest single counter-curriculum object in the corpus.

He begins cost analysis.

The optimization says the purchase is inefficient.

He stops.

The real reason remains:

> he wants it.

When the object is unavailable, he is disappointed.

He does not need to transform the disappointment into strategic gain.

This is a small but radical form of self-authorship:

> **a desire does not need to optimize value to be legitimate.**

The invitation problem is equally important.

Ayanokōji can manipulate large systems but still worries that an ordinary friend may reject an invitation.

The uncertainty cannot be eliminated without changing the meaning of the invitation.

Hiyori provides the strongest positive relational case.

Ayanokōji invites her because **he wants to invite her**.

He recognizes she can refuse.

She shares personal information about her father because she wants him to know.

Neither needs to manufacture a crisis.

The result is the cleanest local model of **participant-mode relation**.

Kiryūin’s future, Ichinose’s wish for a photograph, Sakayanagi’s love, Kei’s time with Satō, Yamamura’s desire not to be disposable, and Hiyori’s wish to graduate alongside people from rival classes all extend the same principle:

> **people generate ends the institution did not assign.**

Desire is not morally pure.

It is politically important because it creates purpose outside the official objective.

## 4.13 Y2V10 — creation cannot be reduced to trained technique

V10 supplies the counter-curriculum’s strongest image.

Ayanokōji opens the sketchbook and colored pencils he bought after entering school.

The White Room taught artistic techniques, including copying.

It did not teach:

> `自分で考えて創作する過程`

the process of thinking and creating for oneself.

He cannot simply command the blank page to contain an original answer.

The distinction is not:

trained = fake  
creative = pure.

It is:

> **technique does not automatically produce self-authored expression.**

The involuntary smile with Horikita makes the same argument in affective form.

The expression appears before he specifies it.

He observes it as something that happened.

The blank page and spontaneous smile therefore form one counter-curricular pair:

- something he cannot generate merely because technique exists;
- something generated in him before deliberate control catches up.

Kamuro’s expulsion gives the relational equivalent.

Sakayanagi’s optimization model does not register friendship’s nonfungibility until the person is irreversibly gone.

A person can become irreplaceable before the optimizer has language for the value.

## 4.14 Y2V11 — low stakes produce high-value knowledge

The exchange camp temporarily reduces several ANHS pressures.

It is not a special examination.

Class points do not move.

Expulsion is not the expected penalty.

Groups mix classes and years.

Activities include:

- craft;
- sport;
- knowledge;
- luck;
- unfamiliar tasks;
- play.

The result is paradoxical:

> **the school learns more about some people by making the information matter less immediately.**

Hiyori’s fine-motor/artistic capacity appears.

Morishita’s playful strategic competence appears.

Hashimoto’s cross-year integration capacity appears.

Ayanokōji discovers pleasure in unfamiliar making.

Yamamura’s social invisibility becomes obvious in repeated ordinary interaction.

This supports one of the strongest counter-curriculum principles:

> **low-consequence environments can be more diagnostic of personhood because people need not optimize every act for institutional survival.**

Nagumo’s final proving field is also comparatively clean because he risks his own resources rather than extracting third-party pooled points.

Competition itself is not anti-ordinary-life.

The issue is who bears the cost and whether participation is answerable.

## 4.15 Y2V12 — relational surplus is captured by formal competition

V12 demonstrates the dark mirror of the entire ledger.

The year-end examination rewards familiarity with:

- personality;
- history;
- microexpression;
- social ties;
- trust;
- hidden motives.

Abilities cultivated through ordinary relation become formal competitive capital.

Ichinose is extraordinarily strong because accumulated relational knowledge is now task-relevant.

Ayanokōji attacks not her abstract intelligence but the trust structure that supports her cognition.

Sakayanagi’s understanding of him becomes the channel through which an unspoken preference can guide her choice.

V12 therefore proves:

> **the institution or strategist can later capture value generated in counter-curricular life.**

That capture does not erase the earlier ordinary meaning.

It shows why ordinary life requires ethical protection.

The better one person knows another, the more capable they become of:

- caring;
- protecting;
- predicting;
- manipulating.

Counter-curriculum is therefore not safe merely because it is intimate.

## 4.16 Y2V12.5 — ordinary life leaves embodied residue after institutional transition

The terminal Year-2 volume repeatedly shows that role endings do not end ordinary meaning.

After Kei leaves, Ayanokōji begins preparing a second bowl of rice through muscle memory before remembering she is no longer there.

The scene is analytically important because it proves the relationship has become embodied routine.

The romance may have failed his explicit curriculum of learning love.

The other person still entered his ordinary body and habit.

Horikita offers care for his heart.

Ayanokōji does not disclose the vulnerability she imagines, but the offer itself establishes a relationship domain not reducible to class strategy.

Sakayanagi’s withdrawal is converted into an expectation of life after school rather than disappearance.

Airi’s outside future already supplies the same ontology.

The Hiyori/Ishizaki material presents a plausible ordinary final-year life Ayanokōji can decline rather than one he is forced to optimize.

Ichinose’s third answer then supplies the strongest terminal counter-curriculum form:

> **the person generated a purpose the developer did not already contain.**

The year closes with class transfer and institutional reconstitution, but ordinary life does not vanish underneath the political board.

It survives as:

- habit;
- friendship;
- unfinished attraction;
- memory;
- food;
- care;
- future plans;
- invitations;
- resentment;
- chosen continuation.

# 5. Character counter-curriculum states

## 5.1 Ayanokōji Kiyotaka — from ordinary life as escape to ordinary life as desired uncertainty

The strongest longitudinal transformation belongs to Ayanokōji.

### Year-1 state

Ordinary school life becomes a self-chosen good.

He wants:

- friendship;
- romance;
- private space;
- ordinary adolescent experience.

But he frequently translates those experiences into:

- observation;
- curriculum;
- strategic utility.

### Year-2 development

The counter-curriculum becomes more specific.

He values:

- finite remaining time;
- inefficient desire;
- unknown experience;
- bodily failure;
- invitations whose answer he cannot guarantee;
- hobbies and books;
- shared time;
- spontaneous affect;
- making rather than merely reproducing;
- people who generate answers beyond his model.

### Year-2 contradiction

His appetite for ordinary uncertainty develops **alongside** greater enjoyment and sophistication in manipulation.

V09.5 says using words to manipulate people can be fun.

V12 shows relation itself becoming control surface.

The mature state is therefore not:

> he becomes normal.

It is:

> **he increasingly wants experiences whose value depends on not controlling them completely while remaining unusually capable of controlling other people’s environments.**

The counter-curriculum’s unresolved question is whether the first desire can constrain the second capacity.

## 5.2 Horikita Suzune — leadership learns the value of non-centralized life

Horikita’s Year-1 development already moves from defective-product thinking toward temporal, developmental judgment.

Year 2 expands the social architecture around her.

She increasingly permits:

- peer initiative;
- bounded expertise;
- adversarial cooperation;
- friendship that does not fit her original self-image;
- ordinary play with Ibuki;
- dependence on people she cannot simply rank.

Her strongest counter-curriculum function is political:

> **a class can become stronger when useful action originates outside the leader’s plan.**

The open boundary remains post-transfer self-sustainment.

Year 2 can establish that Horikita has learned to live and lead inside plural authorship.

It cannot yet establish how the polity performs after the hidden security layer leaves.

## 5.3 Kei Karuizawa — horizontal ordinary life as defense against monopolized dependence

Kei’s relation with Ayanokōji contains a hidden curriculum.

Her ordinary-life counterweight comes from **horizontal ties**.

Satō becomes the clearest case.

The friendship survives disclosure of Kei’s bullying history.

Satō can judge Ayanokōji independently.

Kei can continue:

- studying;
- social function;
- friendship;
- repair efforts

even during romantic distress.

After the breakup, the important question is therefore not whether she can act independently in one scene.

It is whether the broader social world can support a life whose meaning is not organized around the former “host.”

At the Year-2 boundary:

- behavioral resources: stronger;
- horizontal relation: stronger;
- emotional independence: unresolved.

## 5.4 Sudō Ken — development moves from imposed improvement to lived practice

Sudō is one of the strongest examples of counter-curricular development becoming formally valuable later.

His growth passes through:

- academic practice;
- sports;
- social relation;
- anger regulation;
- rejection;
- peer teaching.

By late Year 2 he does not need Ayanokōji to supply every developmental answer.

Onodera can teach him.

He can teach weaker classmates.

Formal measurement then catches up.

This is the opposite of a file creating the person.

Life changes first.

The file updates later.

## 5.5 Hiyori Shiina — hobbies as a low-pressure relation technology

Hiyori’s book relation is one of the most stable counter-curricular forms in the corpus.

Reading supplies:

- shared reference;
- voluntary disclosure;
- cross-class continuity;
- low pressure;
- non-institutional reason to spend time together.

Her craft/fine-motor evidence in V11 similarly demonstrates capacities whose relevance appears because a low-stakes environment gives them somewhere to exist.

Hiyori is therefore a high-value negative control against “important character = important office.”

## 5.6 Ichinose Honami — relational purpose survives competitive capture

Ichinose’s ordinary-life strength is also her vulnerability.

She treats other people as personally significant rather than fungible.

The school trip and year-end exam prove that this relational density creates both:

- moral friction;
- competitive power.

V12 weaponizes the trust relation.

V12.5 then proves the relationship does not remain owned by the attack.

Her third answer turns relation into counter-authorship.

The mature distinction is:

> **relation can be captured without being exhausted.**

## 5.7 Sakayanagi Arisu — optimization discovers irreplaceability too late

Sakayanagi’s ordinary-life countercurriculum is negative evidence first.

Kamuro becomes meaningful before Sakayanagi’s contribution model knows how to represent that meaning.

Loss produces the new category:

> friend.

Later:

- Yamamura requires recognition beyond utility;
- love for Ayanokōji becomes a purpose with political consequences;
- school withdrawal becomes a future beyond institutional rank.

Sakayanagi therefore demonstrates that even a highly sophisticated optimizer can fail to model **relational nonfungibility**.

## 5.8 Ryūen Kakeru — ordinary loyalty exceeds fear without erasing coercion

Ryūen’s political development matters to this ledger because his followers increasingly possess reasons for relation beyond raw fear.

Komiya’s account in V04.5 is diagnostic.

Ryūen’s visit after injury has interpersonal meaning.

Hiyori can dissent.

Katsuragi contributes independent expertise.

Ishizaki’s friendship with Ayanokōji is not merely recruitment.

Yet Ryūen remains coercive.

The counter-curriculum result is not redemption.

It is that ordinary relation can create **voluntary legitimacy** inside an authoritarian polity without automatically democratizing it.

## 5.9 Kushida Kikyō — social skill remains real after the role collapses

Kushida’s public social competence is partly a strategic performance.

Year 2 demonstrates that the capacity survives exposure.

She can still:

- remember;
- connect;
- recruit;
- mediate;
- function publicly.

The new political compact with Horikita permits her to contribute for **her own reason** rather than pretending affection.

This is counter-curricular because continued life after role collapse creates a self-authored purpose the original mask did not contain.

## 5.10 Airi / Haruka / successor Ayanokōji Group — institution is not the whole life

Airi’s expulsion is the strongest standing reminder that ANHS membership is not personhood.

Her Shizuku future changes Haruka’s understanding from outside the school.

The class could judge her least useful under one institutional scarcity rule.

It could not author what she became after removal.

The successor Haruka–Akito–Keisei trio then reconnects without reinstalling Ayanokōji.

The social structure survives through reconstitution rather than restoration.

This is ordinary-life succession.

## 5.11 Amasawa and Yagami — common curriculum, divergent desire

Volume 0 and V04.5 make a key contrast possible.

White Room formation does not produce one desire.

Yagami organizes identity around return, comparison, and defeating Ayanokōji.

Amasawa increasingly wants ordinary school life after White Room return becomes unavailable.

The common curriculum explains capacities and some shared grammar.

It does not own the purposes the students later form.

# 6. Ordinary-life domains and their analytical function

## 6.1 Food

Food repeatedly shifts between:

- nutrition;
- resource;
- preference;
- domestic routine;
- gift;
- shared experience.

Volume 0’s carrot conversation begins the problem.

V09.5’s yogurt maker makes inefficient wanting legitimate.

V12.5’s second rice bowl turns relation into muscle memory.

Food is therefore one of the cleanest transitions from **function** to **meaning**.

## 6.2 Books and cultural interest

Hiyori’s reading relationship demonstrates a mode of contact not dependent on:

- class alliance;
- exam;
- rescue;
- romantic crisis.

Books also allow characters to disclose private background indirectly.

The relationship exists because the object matters to both people.

## 6.3 Romance

Romance is the most unstable ordinary-life domain.

It can provide:

- desire;
- intimacy;
- touch;
- jealousy;
- routine;
- learning;
- care.

It can also become:

- curriculum;
- dependency;
- leverage;
- timetable;
- inferred control.

The correct ledger state is always multidimensional.

## 6.4 Friendship

Friendship is especially important because it can become useful **without usefulness being its condition of existence**.

Examples:

- Ayanokōji Group;
- Kei–Satō;
- Ayanokōji–Hiyori;
- Ayanokōji–Ishizaki;
- Horikita–Ibuki;
- Haruka–Airi;
- successor trio.

The best counter-curricular friendship allows contradiction.

## 6.5 Hobbies and leisure

Leisure matters because it permits:

- preference;
- time not fully optimized;
- low-consequence ignorance;
- identity outside role.

Its value is highest when the character is allowed to do something badly or pointlessly.

## 6.6 Creation and craft

Creation is analytically distinct from technique.

The blank sketchbook captures the difference.

The exchange camp then shows making can reveal:

- patience;
- fine-motor skill;
- pleasure;
- aesthetic preference;
- unfamiliar competence.

## 6.7 Play

Snowball fights, games, playful rivalry, jokes, and low-stakes contests permit relationships to change without formal political declaration.

Play can function as a **low-cost relational laboratory**.

But unlike Ayanokōji’s hidden experiments, its meaning is often mutually visible.

## 6.8 Domestic routine

Domestic life is especially resistant to abstract strategy because it accumulates through repetition.

Meals, rooms, calls, shopping, shared habits, and bodily familiarity create memory without requiring one dramatic event.

The post-breakup rice bowl is the canonical endpoint.

## 6.9 Consumer desire

Consumer choice is not inherently freedom.

ANHS itself is highly marketized.

But V09.5 demonstrates why personal consumption can still be counter-curricular:

> the object can matter for no reason stronger than wanting to use it.

The legitimacy of inefficient preference contrasts with systems that demand all resources maximize externally assigned value.

## 6.10 Future planning

Career dreams matter because they make the rival a person with a life not reducible to the current game.

Kitō’s fashion ambition is the clearest V08 example.

Sakayanagi’s future after withdrawal and Airi’s post-expulsion activity extend the same ontology.

# 7. Counter-curriculum failure modes

Ordinary life is not automatically liberatory.

The ledger tracks six recurring capture mechanisms.

## 7.1 Hidden curriculum

A participant privately defines the relationship as a developmental program without sufficient disclosure.

Primary case:

- Ayanokōji–Kei romance.

## 7.2 Institutional capture

The school identifies an ordinary capacity and attaches:

- score;
- budget;
- rank;
- reward;
- penalty.

Primary cases:

- cultural festival;
- OAA social axes;
- year-end relational exam.

## 7.3 Strategic conversion

Personal knowledge created in relation becomes leverage.

Primary cases:

- V12 trust attack on Ichinose;
- Sakayanagi’s inferred preference;
- broader use of microexpression and history in the year-end exam.

## 7.4 Surveillance saturation

Leisure occurs but remains watched or politically actionable.

Primary case:

- Nagumo’s V04.5 surveillance network.

The person may technically be “off exam” while still living inside an observation regime.

## 7.5 Utility reabsorption

A person’s hobby, friendship, or social role is reinterpreted only as future class value.

This is the ordinary-life analogue of treating OAA as the person.

## 7.6 Total explanation

The analyst commits the same error as the institution when every ordinary act is interpreted as:

- trauma effect;
- strategy;
- developmental curriculum;
- romantic proof;
- hidden genius signal.

Counter-curriculum requires preserving triviality where the text permits triviality.

Sometimes wanting yogurt is wanting yogurt.

# 8. The White Room / ANHS distinction

The mature through-Year-2 comparison is not:

White Room = bad  
ANHS = good.

Both can:

- author environments;
- measure;
- rank;
- set thresholds;
- create incentives;
- remove;
- pressure.

The decisive structural difference is **scope and plurality of authorship**.

## White Room

Its ideal is total curriculum.

The institution tries to define:

- activity;
- performance;
- developmental purpose;
- acceptable continuation.

Ordinary expression has little causal standing.

Other children are initially known as measured capacities more than companions.

## ANHS

ANHS is coercive and artificial.

But it contains:

- private rooms;
- shopping;
- friendship;
- romance;
- hobbies;
- clubs;
- multiple adult authorities;
- rival leaders;
- travel;
- festivals;
- books;
- play;
- dissent;
- cross-class relation;
- futures outside school.

The school sometimes tries to instrumentalize these.

It still does not successfully own all the meanings generated inside them.

The best concise distinction is:

> **The White Room attempts to eliminate surplus purpose. ANHS repeatedly generates more surplus purpose than any one actor can control.**

That does not prove ANHS fair.

It explains why ANHS can become a site of self-authorship in ways the White Room structurally suppresses.

# 9. Counter-curriculum and ability

The ability ledger establishes:

> capacity → development → expression / withholding → observation → recorded model → strategic use.

The ordinary-life ledger supplies the missing generative environments.

Formal exams can underweight:

- craft;
- reading;
- humor;
- social repair;
- dating competence;
- care;
- friendship;
- refusal;
- embodied learning;
- creative initiation;
- cross-class comfort;
- ordinary persistence.

V11 is especially important.

The low-stakes camp reveals high-value capacity precisely because consequence pressure is reduced.

This suggests a broader measurement principle:

> **A system that measures only high-stakes performance may systematically miss capacities that emerge when the subject is not forced to optimize the act for measurement.**

The answer is not to turn all leisure into another exam.

Doing so would destroy part of the information source.

# 10. Counter-curriculum and ethics

The ethics ledger’s answer-back standard applies directly.

An ordinary-life environment is ethically stronger when the participant can:

1. know what the interaction is for;
2. refuse;
3. fail without catastrophic consequence;
4. pursue a purpose the stronger actor did not assign;
5. change the meaning of the activity;
6. preserve privacy;
7. remain valuable if usefulness drops;
8. create relationships outside the organizer;
9. leave;
10. answer the organizer with an unforeseen result.

This is why the Hiyori invitation is cleaner than the Kei romance curriculum.

It is not because Hiyori matters more.

It is because the interaction discloses its immediate purpose and preserves refusal.

# 11. Counter-curriculum and relationship architecture

The relationship ledger’s hierarchy is:

`persistence < role survival < successor structure < counter-authorship`

Ordinary life is where many of those capacities are trained without being called training.

Kei learns that another person can remain after disclosure because Satō remains.

Sudō learns peer reciprocity through Onodera.

Haruka learns that Airi’s future cannot be owned by school expulsion.

Hiyori maintains cross-class friendship despite structural rivalry.

Horikita and Ibuki acquire familiarity that survives political difference.

Ichinose’s relational density eventually enables an answer Ayanokōji did not supply.

Counter-curriculum therefore acts as **social redundancy against monopolized authorship**.

A person with multiple meaningful relationships is harder for one relationship or institution to define completely.

# 12. Counter-curriculum and political formation

Ordinary life is not politically irrelevant.

It changes what a polity can do.

A class with:

- friendship;
- trust;
- distributed competence;
- informal communication;
- leisure relation

has more channels through which action can emerge.

But informal cohesion can also suppress dissent.

Ichinose’s class is the obvious warning.

High relational solidarity can become conformity pressure.

The correct political principle is:

> **ordinary relation strengthens a polity when it produces additional authors, not merely stronger attachment to one consensus.**

Kanzaki/Himeno reform is therefore counter-curricular in political form: the class needs a second cognitive center inside belonging.

# 13. Decimal volumes as formal counter-curriculum

The `.5` volumes require explicit architectural protection.

They are not optional “filler” repositories.

Year 1 V04.5 and V07.5 and Year 2 V04.5, V09.5, and V12.5 repeatedly move the analytical camera toward:

- gifts;
- food;
- dating;
- embarrassment;
- books;
- holidays;
- shopping;
- ordinary rooms;
- friendship;
- bodily routine;
- social memory.

This shift changes what counts as evidence.

A high-stakes exam over-selects:

- strategy;
- leadership;
- stress;
- rule comprehension.

A decimal volume often reveals:

- preference;
- ordinary ignorance;
- nonfungibility;
- future desire;
- relation without immediate crisis.

The corpus must therefore resist exam exceptionalism.

# 14. Ordinary-life evidence taxonomy

Use the following state labels in future retrieval.

## `UNASSIGNED_DESIRE`

The participant wants an end not reducible to formal objective.

## `LOW_STAKES_LEARNING`

Knowledge emerges without major institutional consequence.

## `INEFFICIENCY_ACCEPTED`

The participant permits a nonoptimal choice because experience itself matters.

## `EMBODIED_LEARNING`

Knowledge arrives through body, sensation, practice, failure, intimacy, or routine.

## `RECIPROCAL_UNCERTAINTY`

Another person’s answer cannot be guaranteed without damaging the interaction’s meaning.

## `HORIZONTAL_DEVELOPMENT`

Peers teach, correct, support, or develop one another without a central developer.

## `GENERATIVE_EXPRESSION`

The person makes or creates rather than merely reproduces.

## `NONFUNGIBLE_ATTACHMENT`

A specific person matters beyond generic utility.

## `ROLE_SURPLUS`

The person acts or matters outside formal institutional identity.

## `FUTURITY_OUTSIDE_ANHS`

Meaning survives graduation, withdrawal, or expulsion.

## `INSTITUTIONALLY_AUTHORED_COUNTERCURRICULUM`

The school deliberately creates a low-stakes/humanizing encounter whose meaning remains plural.

## `HIDDEN_CURRICULUM_CONTAMINATION`

An ordinary relationship is privately converted into developmental program.

## `STRATEGIC_CAPTURE`

Ordinary knowledge is later weaponized.

## `COUNTER_AUTHORED_MEANING`

The participant creates an answer not possessed by the organizer.

# 16. Negative controls — scenes that should not be over-promoted as counter-curriculum

Not every non-exam scene qualifies.

## 16.1 Strategic meeting outside formal exam

A café conversation about alliances remains strategic merely because class points are not moving in that moment.

## 16.2 Leisure under hidden coercion

A date whose meaning is privately governed by severe informational asymmetry may contain ordinary elements without becoming a clean autonomy case.

## 16.3 Social competence used as instrument

Hospitality, charm, or friendship network can become scored or exploited.

The capacity remains real.

The scene is no longer pure counter-curriculum.

## 16.4 “Normal teenager” evidence used to erase abnormal formation

Ordinary desire does not prove White Room effects disappeared.

It proves the person cannot be reduced to them.

## 16.5 Low-stakes scene treated as morally innocent

Humor, romance, or food can coexist with manipulation.

Genre softness is not ethical absolution.

# 17. Counter-curriculum as a character-modeling requirement

A character simulator built only from exam scenes will overfit the cast toward crisis behavior.

It will make:

- Ayanokōji too strategic;
- Horikita too formal;
- Kei too reactive;
- Hiyori too passive;
- Sudō too aggressive;
- Ichinose too political;
- Sakayanagi too sovereign;
- Ryūen too domineering.

Ordinary-life evidence supplies:

- idle preferences;
- pacing;
- embarrassment;
- hobby knowledge;
- teasing;
- friendship maintenance;
- boredom;
- domestic routine;
- ordinary refusal;
- consumer behavior;
- low-stakes curiosity.

For behavioral reconstruction, this is not garnish.

It is the distribution needed to model how the person behaves when the plot is not forcing diagnostic extremes.

# 18. Claim transitions — Year 1 → Year 2

## PRESERVE

1. Ordinary life is counter-curriculum when value is not fully supplied by an assigned score.
2. Decimal volumes are analytically central rather than filler.
3. Ayanokōji’s desire for ordinary school life is genuine.
4. ANHS differs from the White Room through plural authorship and surplus meaning, not through innocence.
5. Friendship, leisure, romance, and private purpose supply positive content to freedom.
6. A person can possess ordinary capacities formal measurement underweights.
7. Later strategic utility does not erase earlier ordinary meaning.
8. Ordinary-life evidence should not be dismissed because the same character is highly strategic elsewhere.

## STRENGTHEN

1. **Counter-curriculum can be institutionally authored without being institutionally owned.**
2. **Low-stakes environments can reveal more person-specific information than high-stakes measurement.**
3. **Failure can itself be valuable when it is allowed to remain non-catastrophic.**
4. **Inefficient desire is evidence of self-authored purpose.**
5. **Horizontal peer development is one of the strongest alternatives to monopolized developmental authorship.**
6. **Embodied routine can preserve relational significance even when explicit cognitive taxonomy denies the expected feeling.**
7. **Future life outside ANHS limits institutional claims over personhood.**
8. **Counter-authored meaning is stronger evidence of independence than merely executing the developmental behavior another person hoped to teach.**
9. **Ordinary relation can create the very knowledge later used for care or manipulation; humanization and leverage rise together.**
10. **Generativity is not identical to trained technique.**

## REVISE

1. **“Ordinary life is outside curriculum.”**  
   → Too strong. ANHS can deliberately author ordinary encounters; Ayanokōji can privately convert romance into curriculum. The key question is whether meaning remains open to participant counter-authorship.

2. **“The White Room lacks emotion.”**  
   → Volume 0 shows preference, appetite, curiosity, dislike, Yuki’s smile, and Shirō’s desire. The stronger claim is that the institution suppresses the causal and relational standing of ordinary feeling and attempts to subordinate activity to developmental purpose.

3. **“Ayanokōji becomes normal.”**  
   → Reject normality as endpoint. He gains ordinary desires, uncertainty tolerance, and participant-mode relation while retaining extreme strategic capacities.

4. **“Leisure is non-strategic.”**  
   → Not necessarily. Leisure can be surveilled, scored, or later weaponized.

5. **“The school trip humanizes rivals.”**  
   → Yes, but humanization also increases leverage and future moral friction. It is not a one-directional moral program.

6. **“Kei romance is ordinary life.”**  
   → It contains genuine ordinary life and genuine hidden curriculum simultaneously.

7. **“Expulsion ends the character’s developmental relevance.”**  
   → Airi and Sakayanagi show that institutional exit can open a different future the school does not own.

8. **“Skills revealed in low-stakes life are less serious than exam skills.”**  
   → Low stakes can be more diagnostic for capacities distorted by high consequence.

## DOWNGRADE

1. Exam performance as the default source for personality simulation.
2. OAA as a complete map of ordinary competence.
3. “Filler” classification for decimal volumes.
4. Consumer desire as merely comic irrelevance.
5. Hobby scenes as disposable characterization.
6. Friendship as a strategic asset unless the text makes strategy primary.
7. Every Ayanokōji ordinary act as covert manipulation.
8. Every low-stakes failure as hidden training success.
9. Every romantic act as proof of conventional love.
10. Every school-created ordinary event as fake because the institution arranged it.

## REJECT

1. Counter-curriculum requires complete absence of external structure.
2. Ordinary life is apolitical.
3. A person is autonomous only when independent of all relationships.
4. Training and self-authorship are mutually exclusive.
5. A socially engineered encounter cannot produce genuine relation.
6. Ordinary desire is morally good merely because it is self-owned.
7. ANHS is equivalent to the White Room because both are authored environments.
8. ANHS is ethically vindicated because it permits leisure and friendship.
9. Strategic capture retroactively makes ordinary meaning fake.
10. Successful development proves the developer owned the result.

## OPEN

1. Can Ayanokōji increasingly choose participant-mode relationships when no hidden curriculum is needed?
2. Can ordinary attachment become a durable ethical constraint on his strategic capacity rather than more precise input to it?
3. Can Horikita’s polity sustain ordinary pluralism after the class transfer without recentralizing around another exceptional actor?
4. Can Kei’s horizontal ordinary-life network eventually outweigh the old host-model dependency?
5. Can Ichinose preserve relational density while building enough internal dissent to prevent solidarity from becoming conformity?
6. Can Ryūen’s voluntary follower relation evolve toward protected dissent without losing operational coherence?
7. Can institutional measurement preserve low-stakes diagnostic value without colonizing leisure into another score?
8. Can generativity be measured without turning original expression into a new compulsory performance?
9. How much of Ayanokōji’s ordinary-life development reflects genuine change versus capacities that existed but lacked opportunity?
10. Year-3 answers are inadmissible here.

# 21. Canonical through-Year-2 counter-curriculum synthesis

Year 2 begins with students entering a more explicit measurement regime.

OAA makes the school better at describing what it can see.

Special examinations become more sophisticated.

Adult power becomes more legible.

Ayanokōji becomes better at modeling not only what people can do, but what they are likely to choose.

If that were the complete developmental story, *Classroom of the Elite* would become progressively more deterministic as its characters became better measured.

The ordinary-life record moves in the opposite direction.

The more the year progresses, the more important it becomes that:

- friendship is not fully teachable from a textbook;
- learning can require failing before one knows the answer;
- a person may rationally want an inefficient object;
- an invitation matters partly because it can be refused;
- a hobby can disclose a capacity no formal metric had reason to notice;
- a low-stakes environment can reveal more than a high-stakes one;
- a trained hand can still confront a blank page;
- a smile can arrive before the person decides to perform it;
- a friend can become irreplaceable before the strategist models that value;
- a relationship can remain in bodily routine after its explicit category ends;
- an expelled student can produce a future the school did not own;
- and a developed person can eventually give an answer the developer did not possess.

This does not prove human behavior is uncaused.

The series does not need that metaphysical claim.

The stronger literary claim is enough:

> **No authored environment shown through Year 2 succeeds in making the purposes generated inside human life identical to the purposes assigned by the author.**

The White Room comes closest to total curriculum.

Even there:

- Yuki smiles;
- Shirō wants freedom;
- Kiyotaka forms preferences;
- common conditioning does not produce common desire.

ANHS is far more plural.

That plurality can be exploited.

The school can score it.

Ayanokōji can study it.

V12 can weaponize it.

But capture is not the same as creation, and prediction is not ownership.

The terminal Year-2 principle is therefore:

> **Ordinary life is counter-curriculum not because nobody arranged the room, but because the people inside the room can still generate purposes, attachments, failures, pleasures, and answers that the arranger did not fully assign.**

And the corresponding freedom principle is:

> **The strongest evidence of self-authorship is not mere escape from curriculum. It is the capacity to make something of an inherited environment that its author cannot legitimately claim to have already contained.**

That is the canonical ordinary-life / counter-curriculum boundary through Year 2 / *Second List*.
