# Relationship ledger
## Canonical relationship state through Year 2 / *Second List*

# 0. Ledger function and analytical controls

This ledger is the canonical longitudinal relationship snapshot at the end of the second school year.

Its job is not to collapse relationships into single labels such as “healthy,” “toxic,” “real,” “fake,” “romantic,” or “strategic.” *Classroom of the Elite* repeatedly constructs bonds in which several apparently contradictory structures remain simultaneously true: affection and utility, protection and leverage, trust and information asymmetry, dependency and agency, rivalry and recognition, coercive origin and voluntary continuation, developmental benefit and moral injury, ordinary intimacy and hidden curriculum, refusal and continued attachment, political conflict and personal care.

The Year-1 ledger ended with reciprocity emerging inside asymmetry. Year 2 keeps that result and adds a harder problem:

> **What happens when one participant understands the other well enough to design the conditions under which the other will choose?**

The strongest Year-2 relationship test is therefore not persistence, affection, or even reciprocal benefit. It is **counter-authorship**:

> **Can the less powerful or less informed participant contradict, redefine, survive, or exceed the role the stronger participant assigned—and can the relationship continue without the stronger participant claiming that independent answer as merely the success of his own design?**

## 0.1 Required architectural dimensions

For each major relationship this ledger distinguishes:

1. origin;
2. information symmetry;
3. power symmetry;
4. leverage;
5. refusal capacity;
6. dependency;
7. protection;
8. strategic utility;
9. emotional disclosure;
10. ability to contradict;
11. reciprocity;
12. fungibility / irreplaceability;
13. separation;
14. capacity to survive a change in role.

These dimensions must not be collapsed into one score. A relationship may improve on one axis while worsening on another. Kei can become more behaviorally autonomous while the romance becomes more informationally unequal. Horikita can gain enormous ability to contradict Ayanokōji while remaining excluded from his transfer timetable. Ichinose can remain intensely attached while becoming the strongest counter-author of his developmental model. Hiyori can have less informational access than Kei while participating in a cleaner local interaction because the purpose of the interaction is disclosed and refusal remains intact.

## 0.2 Three Year-2 relationship states that must remain distinct

### A. Authored continuation

The person can perform the behavior the stronger actor hoped to cultivate, but the stronger actor's expected answer still organizes the behavior. Kei after the breakup is the clearest painful case.

### B. Role-surviving / successor relation

The bond or social structure continues after the original organizer or role disappears. The Haruka–Akito–Keisei successor group and Kei's horizontal friendship with Satō are especially strong examples.

### C. Counter-authored relation

The other participant creates an answer the stronger actor did not supply, cannot fully predict, or must now answer in return. Ichinose at the Year-2 boundary is the strongest case.

The diagnostic hierarchy is:

`persistence < role survival < successor structure < counter-authorship`

## 0.3 Relationship evidence does not retroactively legitimate harmful method

The governing ethical rule remains:

> **Later affection, growth, recovery, or social success does not retroactively authorize the method through which the earlier relationship or intervention was created.**

Therefore Kei's genuine love does not retroactively authorize the coercive origin of Ayanokōji's control over her. Kushida's later self-authored cooperation does not make Horikita's V05 preservation consensual at the moment it occurred. Airi's successful life after ANHS does not prove her expulsion was morally correct. Ichinose's counter-authored recovery does not make Ayanokōji's V12 relational attack legitimate. Horikita's future competence, even if later proven, cannot retroactively make nondisclosure about the class transfer informed consent.

# 1. Executive Year-2 relationship thesis

The strongest Year-2 relationship thesis is:

> **Year 1 asked whether unequal relationships could become reciprocal. Year 2 asks whether reciprocity can survive superior modeling. A relationship becomes most answerable not when the stronger participant understands the other perfectly, but when the other retains enough information, refusal, independent purpose, and causal standing to make the stronger participant respond to an answer he did not already own.**

Five movements organize the year.

First, intimacy becomes a source of both care and precision. Ayanokōji becomes more socially embedded, but increased knowledge of Kei, Horikita, Ichinose, Hiyori, Hirata, Sakayanagi, and Ryūen can make intervention more exact. V12 supplies the terminal warning: once attachment, trust, loyalty, or inferred preference is predictable, direct command may become unnecessary. Formal freedom can remain while relational knowledge becomes the control surface.

Second, horizontal relationships become the strongest defense against monopolized dependence. Kei–Satō, Haruka–Akito–Keisei, Sudō–Onodera, Horikita–Ibuki, Kushida's visible class/council participation, Kanzaki–Himeno reform, Hiyori's cross-class ties, and Ryūen's differentiated follower network pluralize dependence rather than abolish it.

Third, contradiction becomes relational competence. The strongest relationships increasingly survive answers such as “you are wrong,” “I hate you and will still cooperate,” “I do not forgive you,” “I will use you too,” and “your victory did not develop the class.”

Fourth, separation becomes an information test before an endurance test. V12.5 makes the decisive question not simply who keeps functioning, but whether the person knew enough to author the meaning of separation before it happened.

Fifth, counter-authorship becomes the strongest form of reciprocity. Ichinose identifies that Ayanokōji's prepared binary is not the answer, generates a third solution he does not possess, predicts his own likely movement into her class, criticizes his unilateral developmental ethics, and claims reciprocal causal standing over him.

# 4. Ayanokōji – Karuizawa Kei
## Romance becomes the strongest negative test of informational reciprocity

Year 1 ended with a deliberately mixed conclusion. The coercive origin remained ethically binding. Kei's trauma-conditioned `寄生虫 / parasite` and `宿主 / host` model remained relevant. Ayanokōji had used danger, secrecy, protection, and superior information to become indispensable. But the rooftop sequence had already shown the host model was incomplete: Kei refused to betray him even when betrayal offered an exit. She later answered his confession herself. Ordinary contact, gifts, conversation, desire, jealousy, and low-stakes interaction showed that utility no longer exhausted the bond.

Year 2 reveals why instrumentality was insufficient without allowing affection to erase asymmetry.

Y2V03 makes genuine desire and strategic emotional regulation occupy the same act: the first kiss is experienced as novel and desirable, while Ayanokōji also uses it to displace Kei's anxiety about Nanase. Y2V04.5 turns the relationship into an increasingly explicit romance curriculum. Y2V06 then reverses teacher and student: Kei asks whether he would expel her if she lacked OAA/social value, rejects his conditional optimization, and teaches him that in such a relationship `そこに理屈なんかは不要`. But the same volume immediately exposes the disclosure deficit when Kei asks him not to hide things and he internally recognizes she knows almost nothing decisive about family, White Room, transfer plans, wider relationships, or the meaning he assigns to romance.

Y2V07 supplies the key horizontal counterweight. Ayanokōji controls the timing of Kei's disclosure of bullying history to Satō, but Satō's answer is hers. She stays. The narration promotes the relation to `親友`. In V09, during severe conflict with Ayanokōji, Kei continues studying, maintaining public function, seeking repair, and consulting Satō; Satō independently judges Ayanokōji's behavior and tells Kei he is wrong. The correct model is therefore **vertical emotional dependency plus growing horizontal autonomy**.

Y2V09.5 makes the curricular asymmetry explicit: Ayanokōji distinguishes romantic form from acquired feeling, identifies the cold-war period as experiment, and deliberately intensifies dependency. Y2V12 then shows separation being authored before Kei can choose its meaning: he asks permission to meet class leaders while internally entering `希望を絶つための準備段階`.

V12.5 resolves the reciprocal-choice question negatively. Ayanokōji confirms that breakup belongs to the relationship's architecture. Kei reconstructs this. She wants to cling and beg, but does not because she believes doing so would betray **his expectation** that she become capable of standing alone. Her refusal of explanation cannot repair the earlier asymmetry because it occurs under acute distress and inside a developmental norm authored by the person leaving.

The canonical result is:

> **No: the romance ends without enough prior information for fully informed reciprocal choice.**

Yet the relationship leaves real non-fungible residue. The next morning, Ayanokōji begins preparing a second bowl of rice by muscle memory before remembering Kei is gone. He also permits the public story that Kei dumped him, protecting her social position. These are real effects of ordinary relation, not proof of conventional love and not repair of the disclosure deficit.

`Second List` calibrates the endpoint through `Y2SL-E049`: Kei is an ex-girlfriend who has not fully emotionally compartmentalized the relationship.

### Boundary profile

- origin: coercive/protective alliance → mutual romance → planned breakup;
- information symmetry: severe and persistent asymmetry;
- power symmetry: Ayanokōji overwhelmingly stronger strategically/informationally; Kei strong socially in domains he lacks;
- leverage: historically high on his side; decreases as Kei's social world pluralizes;
- refusal: real in discrete moments, but constrained by incomplete information and internalized expectation;
- dependency: high vertical emotional dependency; stronger horizontal support than Year 1;
- protection: genuine care plus proprietary/developmental risk;

- emotional disclosure: Kei high, Ayanokōji low/selective;
- contradiction: real but terminal behavior partly organized by his expected answer;
- reciprocity: genuine in ordinary life, structurally incomplete at hidden-purpose level;
- fungibility: operational irreplaceability undermined; embodied ordinary non-fungibility real;
- separation: completed romantically;
- role survival: Kei survives behaviorally/socially; emotional recovery unresolved.

# 5. Ayanokōji – Horikita Suzune
## The strongest judgment reciprocity coexists with radical timetable asymmetry

Year 1 ended with Horikita increasingly able to detect Ayanokōji's hidden influence, challenge him, generate independent judgments, and receive genuine jurisdiction over class decisions. The asymmetry remained that he knew far more about his capability, motives, security architecture, and the future he imagined for her. The relationship became reciprocal in **judgment** before it became reciprocal in **information**.

Year 2 widens that split. Y2V05 is decisive: Horikita rejects Ayanokōji's intended expulsion of Kushida and preserves her. The result is morally severe because Airi bears the terminal cost, but relationally the result is unmistakable: Horikita can make a policy choice whose content he opposes, and that choice remains consequential.

Across V06–V10, dependence shifts from answer dependence to hidden-security dependence. Horikita authors alliances, solves exam architecture, becomes student-council president, recruits Kushida on terms Kushida can own, uses bounded contracts, and accepts distributed expertise. Ayanokōji's direct answer is less necessary. His hidden security remains important.

The relationship also becomes personally richer. V10 supplies the involuntary smile: Horikita becomes associated with purposeless interaction and spontaneous affect rather than only strategy. V12.5 then has Horikita worry about Ayanokōji's heart after the burden of expulsions and offer to support him if he is hurting. He defers rather than entering her protective jurisdiction. A playful “lover candidate” exchange produces a bodily response in Horikita she cannot yet classify. The source does not justify forcing a definitive romance label; it establishes epistemic novelty.

V12–V12.5 finally convert the relationship into a succession test. Horikita asks Ayanokōji to remain beside her and watch over the class at exactly the moment he is already leaving. Thus:

> **Horikita is more capable of standing without Ayanokōji than ever before, but she is denied the information that would let her author the meaning of his departure before it happens.**

`Second List` calibrates this through `Y2SL-E050`, which emphasizes Horikita's desire for Ayanokōji's recognition while leaving post-transfer class performance unresolved.

### Boundary profile

- information symmetry: improved about school action, poor about his history and transfer timetable;
- power: Horikita has formal class/council authority; Ayanokōji retains hidden strategic superiority;
- refusal: high in policy, low regarding an unseen separation timetable;
- dependency: direct answer dependence greatly reduced; hidden-security and personal recognition remain;
- protection: increasingly mutual in aspiration, but he does not grant her protective authority over his weakness;
- emotional disclosure: Horikita increasingly exposed, Ayanokōji selective;
- contradiction: very high;
- reciprocity: high in judgment, incomplete in life-planning information;
- separation: institutional separation initiated;
- role survival: post-transfer relation not yet adjudicable at Y2SL.

# 6. Ayanokōji – Ichinose Honami
## From protected trust to violated trust to counter-authored reciprocity

Year 1 ended with genuine trust across rivalry, a one-year promise, growing private attachment, and Ayanokōji retaining paternalistic `介錯` jurisdiction over the future of Ichinose's class. The unresolved question was what authority trust gave him over timing and meaning of another person's development.

Y2V07–V09 begin moving the answer away from him. Kanzaki, Himeno, and others form an internal reform structure. Ayanokōji helps seed the conversation but reaches an explicit stopping line: `ここから先は第三者であるオレが立ち入る領域じゃない。` By V09 the second-brain structure is no longer reducible to him, and Ichinose's class survives Ryūen's sabotage through adversarial competence that older models did not predict.

Y2V09.5 makes desire more self-owned. Ichinose's photograph project is personal rather than class-functional; camouflage does not make the desire unreal.

V12 then weaponizes the relationship itself. Ayanokōji creates a bargain that protects Ichinose's class from traitor-expulsion risk and gives it material benefit, but she does not know the bargain's actual developmental function. She becomes unknowingly implicated in Maezono's expulsion, and he uses accumulated trust to destabilize the reasoning system that makes her formidable in the exam. He times truth not to enable informed choice but to produce a psychological effect. Protection of the polity and instrumental attack on the person coexist.

V12.5 destroys the `介錯` model. Ayanokōji presents an apparent binary and predicts one answer with overwhelming probability, while wanting a third answer he himself does not possess. Ichinose produces it. She identifies neither option as the true answer and constructs a method for maintaining the four-class balance through her own thought.

She then explicitly criticizes his method and claims reciprocal jurisdiction:

`綾小路くんが私を利用するように、私もあなたを利用する。その権利はあるよね?`

Ayanokōji admits he lacks the right to deny the proposition. The important analytical result is not equality—the information field remains deeply unequal—but counter-authorship:

> **The target of developmental authorship names the method, contests its one-sided legitimacy, and claims causal rights over the developer.**

`Second List` strengthens this through `Y2SL-E054`, the late Check Point framing that Ichinose exceeds Ayanokōji's prediction, and bonus fiction in which Ayanokōji credits her with standing back up by her own power and continuing to generate new discoveries.

### Boundary profile

- information symmetry: much better at immediate relational level, still low about wider biography/institutions;
- power: Ayanokōji retains hidden informational superiority; Ichinose becomes major political/relational counter-author;
- leverage: he weaponizes trust in V12; she later makes her knowledge of him actionable;
- refusal: exceptionally strong at endpoint;
- dependency: intense attachment but not helplessness;
- protection: his protection remains mixed with developmental jurisdiction; her answer refuses passive rescue;
- strategic utility: explicitly reciprocal by endpoint;
- emotional disclosure: Ichinose high; Ayanokōji more exposed because she can model him;
- contradiction: exceptionally high;
- reciprocity: dramatically strengthened;
- separation: old protector/recipient model ruptured; relation continues transformed;
- role survival: yes.

# 7. Ayanokōji – Shiina Hiyori
## The cleanest local participant-mode countermodel

Hiyori matters because the relationship demonstrates what Ayanokōji can look like when he is a **participant rather than an invisible curriculum designer**.

The bond forms through books, quiet observation, and conversational ease. Class rivalry exists but does not own the relation.

Y2V09.5 gives the cleanest evidence. Hiyori reveals that the author of the book she gave Ayanokōji is her father and that she wanted him specifically to know. Ayanokōji later invites her out. He worries that invitation could feel like pity or pressure. When she asks why, he does not claim she secretly needs socialization. He says that **he wanted to invite her** and recognizes her right to refuse.

This is not absence of influence. It is **disclosure of authorship plus preserved refusal**.

Hiyori can also want Ayanokōji to graduate with her while remaining loyal to Ryūen's class. The relationship supports one of Year 2's most generous principles:

> **Competition can govern outcomes without owning affection.**

`Second List` calibrates the relation through `Y2SL-E055`: reading companion with faint affection.

# 8. Ayanokōji – Hirata Yōsuke
## Mutual care grows as Hirata limits the jurisdiction of universal care

Year 1 gave Ayanokōji unusual access to Hirata after a coercive repair sequence. The relation became personal enough for given-name intimacy and emotional access, but the route remained instrumental and class-functional.

Year 2 improves the relation quietly. Hirata becomes capable of trusting Ayanokōji without treating him as morally infallible, doing mediation work Ayanokōji declines to own, accepting that another legitimate actor may make painful decisions, remaining functional under collective injury, and naming `越権行為` when Ayanokōji's representative authority approaches illegitimate class sovereignty.

V12.5 also shows Hirata committing himself to care for Kei and the class while offering help to Ayanokōji if he becomes lost. Care begins to travel in both directions, though disclosure remains asymmetric and Ayanokōji still does not expose the kind of helplessness that would place him under Hirata's protection.

`Y1H-034` remains `PARTIALLY_ANSWERED`: Hirata's care becomes bounded, but the year still lacks a decisive test of his choosing one particular attachment for himself while tolerating exclusion or disappointment elsewhere.

# 9. Ayanokōji – Sakayanagi Arisu
## Rivalry becomes love; recognition becomes a channel of indirect jurisdiction

Volume 0 reveals that Sakayanagi's nature-versus-nurture project begins with childhood observation of Kiyotaka. The relation is therefore not merely school-age fascination but a self-chosen proof project with deep prehistory. This is revelation of prior state, not Year-2 development.

Year 2 makes the affective dimension explicit. Sakayanagi recognizes love and refuses to classify it simply as weakness. `Second List` later compresses the relation through `Y2SL-E053` as both person to defeat and person she loves.

V12 demonstrates the danger of high mutual legibility. Sakayanagi is not ordered to lose. She reconstructs Ayanokōji's preference from a designed clue chain. She can win and faces no direct threat. Yet her understanding of the person she loves is precise enough that his inferred preference becomes a **relational veto**. Her agency remains real; so does his causal responsibility for creating a field in which he expects her agency to complete his desired outcome.

V12.5 separates personal future from fiduciary failure. Sakayanagi owns her withdrawal and creates an out-of-school future rather than treating school defeat as ontological disappearance. But her class pays through withdrawal, a 300-point penalty, leadership vacuum, and lack of internal successor.

Thus:

> **Sakayanagi's love and self-authorship can be personally genuine while her decision remains a fiduciary failure toward the polity that depended on her.**

# 10. Ayanokōji – Ryūen Kakeru
## Rivalry as reciprocal self-authentication

Year 1 ended with Ayanokōji preserving Ryūen for reasons both strategic and more personal than pure utility. Year 2 makes the rivalry increasingly reciprocal.

Ryūen wants a victory that proves something about **himself**, not merely a favorable official result. Ayanokōji wants Ryūen preserved because Ryūen is one of the independent competitive centers required for the four-class ecology. This is mutual strategic objectification and selective attachment.

V12.5 clarifies the standard: Sakayanagi defeats Ryūen on merit and then throws the result after inferring Ayanokōji's preference. Ryūen refuses to treat the official result as the meaningful victory he wanted. A rival's independent strength has become part of the standard by which he evaluates himself.

`Second List` `Y2SL-E056` frames him as fated rival/person to defeat.

The relationship is best classified as:

> **adversarial reciprocity whose value lies partly in the other's capacity to remain difficult to govern.**

# 11. Ayanokōji – Nagumo Miyabi
## From coerced proving field to bounded rivalry

Nagumo's relation to Ayanokōji begins under institutional asymmetry. Nagumo possesses student-council authority and a third-year point/patronage system, but also an epistemic wound: supremacy without counterforce cannot authenticate itself. Ayanokōji becomes the opponent through whom he wants proof.

Year 2 progressively cleans the personal rivalry even while the political system remains ethically mixed. By the exchange camp, the proving contest is bounded, financed by Nagumo's own resources rather than pooled third-year assets, comparatively low in third-party externality, and compatible with later mutual recognition.

`Second List` `Y2SL-E057` records recognition of ability and desire for competition.

The relationship shows that rivalry becomes more reciprocal when the person seeking validation stops making unrelated dependents bear the cost of validation.

# 12. Ayanokōji – Atsuomi Ayanokōji
## The originating authorship relation

Volume 0 makes this relation indispensable to the Year-2 ledger because it changes the genealogy of every later relationship involving hidden curriculum.

Atsuomi's doctrine includes preference engineering: shaping the environment in which another person comes to accept or prefer the outcome the author wants. `V00-RR-035` shows Kiyotaka inferring Atsuomi's paternity early and concealing how much he understands. `V00-RR-054` gives later staged agency a political genealogy. `V00-RR-056` shows Kiyotaka's later proof/separation experiments emerging from a life already shaped by adult proof experiments. `V00-RR-058` clarifies the deep developmental origin of controlled disclosure.

The correct formulation is not “Kiyotaka simply imitates his father.” The source does not establish conscious doctrinal imitation. It establishes a **grammar of jurisdiction** that predates ANHS.

Strategic opacity toward Atsuomi is relational defense. When being fully legible to the powerful adult makes the child easier to govern, opacity protects an interior margin. The cost is that the same method remains active later with people who are not Atsuomi.

V12.5 restores explicit future conflict: Atsuomi retains political plans, while Kiyotaka's four-class school project is not simply the career path Atsuomi assigns. The relation ends Year 2 in **partial separation under future coercive jurisdiction**, not achieved liberation.

# 13. Horikita Suzune – Kushida Kikyō
## Hatred becomes legitimate cooperation without becoming reconciliation

V05 preservation is genuinely Horikita-authored but not Kushida-authored. Kushida wants expulsion rather than salvage, and preserving her transfers the already-mandatory expulsion danger onto someone else, ultimately Airi. No later success erases that moral remainder.

The left-handed handshake preserves hostility rather than pretending it disappeared.

The canonical classification is:

> **adversarial reciprocity / self-authored cooperation without forgiveness or counterfeit friendship.**

# 14. Horikita Suzune – Sudō Ken

> **the learner's development survives when the hoped-for romance no longer structures the future.**

# 15. Horikita Suzune – Ibuki Mio

# 16. Horikita Suzune – Ichinose Honami

The political outcome is painful for Horikita and highly significant for Ichinose, yet the corpus does not require the personal relation to become hatred. # 21. Sakayanagi Arisu – Kamuro Masumi, Yamamura Miki, Hashimoto Masayoshi

# 24. Cross-cutting audit — dependency versus autonomy

Year 2 decisively rejects a one-axis dependency model.

Kei proves why: she can be emotionally dependent on Ayanokōji, behaviorally functional, socially influential, supported by Satō/other girls, capable of public self-protection after breakup, and privately devastated. Therefore `behavioral autonomy != emotional independence` and `dependency != total absence of agency`.

Horikita shows a different split: judgment independence is established before system independence after withdrawal of hidden security is observed. Ichinose shows attachment without passive jurisdiction: intense attachment coexists with criticism, refusal, strategic independence, and reciprocal causal claim.

The correct questions are whether dependence is monopolized or plural, visible or hidden, survivable to leave, corrigible through other relationships, and compatible with contradiction.

# 25. Cross-cutting audit — protection versus ownership

Protection enlarges freedom when the protected person knows relevant conditions, safety increases future options, the protector does not need to remain indispensable, refusal is preserved, and third parties are not silently made to bear the cost.

Protection becomes ownership-like when the protector monopolizes alternatives, hidden danger is used to deepen dependence, protection becomes evidence that the protector should control the person's future, or the protected person's desired answer becomes secondary to the developmental program.

Year 2 broadens the problem beyond Kei: Horikita preserves Kushida nonconsensually, Ayanokōji protects Ichinose's class while attacking Ichinose's cognition, leaders allocate scarce protection in V10, and centralized classes reveal that “being protected by the sovereign” may itself produce succession weakness.

# 26. Cross-cutting audit — information, timing, and consent

Year 2 makes **when truth is told** as important as whether truth is technically told.

Ayanokōji can reveal ability while concealing motive, ask permission while concealing separation, tell Ichinose truth at a moment selected to disable reasoning, allow Kei to live romance without knowing the full curriculum, and withhold transfer plans from Horikita until separation is effectively institutional fact.

Thus:

> **Accurate information delivered too late to affect the decision is not equivalent to informed choice.**

This is why `Y1H-004` remains open globally. Ayanokōji can tell truths. He has not established a general practice of telling enough truth **in time** for another person to possess informed relational jurisdiction.

# 27. Cross-cutting audit — contradiction and refusal

Strong Year-2 contradiction includes Horikita rejecting Ayanokōji's Kushida policy, Kushida rejecting moral reconciliation while cooperating, Ichinose rejecting Ayanokōji's binary, Hiyori criticizing Ryūen's developmental result, Satō criticizing Ayanokōji to Kei, Haruka refusing forgiveness, Kōenji refusing purchased obligation, and Ryūen rejecting the meaning of an unearned official victory.

Incomplete protected contradiction includes Tokitō's unsafe dissent, Yamamura requiring an externally created opening, and Kei refusing explanation only after the information deficit has already shaped the relationship.

The relevant standard is not simply whether someone said no. It is whether the person knew what she was refusing, could survive refusal, and remained a participant whose future still mattered.

# 28. Cross-cutting audit — fungibility and irreplaceability

Strategic indispensability is not relational irreplaceability. A class may need Kushida's social access or Ryūen's irregular intelligence. Horikita may need Ayanokōji's hidden security. These are system dependencies.

Relational value often becomes clearer after role loss: Sakayanagi recognizes Kamuro as friend after loss, Ayanokōji's body repeats domestic routine after Kei is gone, Haruka remains organized by Airi after school removes Airi, and Ryūen experiences Sakayanagi's disappearance as loss of a meaningful proving rival.

The Year-2 rule is:

> **Irreplaceability is best evidenced when value survives role loss rather than when a person is merely useful inside the role.**

Kei remains intentionally dimension-specific. Year 2 strongly undermines operational irreplaceability in Ayanokōji's romance model, yet Kei leaves embodied/ordinary residue that a pure replacement model cannot explain.

# 36. Year-2 boundary conclusion

The Year-1 relationship ledger ended with a question:

> **Can recognition remain reciprocal when affection, protection, strategic asymmetry, and developmental intention coexist—and when one participant may possess a much stronger working model of the other than the other possesses of him?**

Year 2 supplies a more exact answer.

**Sometimes—but accurate recognition is not enough.**

Ayanokōji can understand another person extraordinarily well and still violate relational jurisdiction. Kei demonstrates that affection and behavioral growth do not cure a hidden curriculum. Horikita demonstrates that a person can become capable of consequential disagreement while still being excluded from the timetable that determines separation. Hiyori demonstrates that declared desire plus preserved refusal can create a cleaner participant-mode relation. Kushida demonstrates that cooperation does not require sentimental reconciliation. Satō demonstrates that disclosure can deepen horizontal attachment rather than destroy it. The successor Ayanokōji Group demonstrates that a friendship may survive its central hidden actor only by becoming a different social object. Sakayanagi demonstrates that love can make another person's inferred preference more binding than direct command.

Ichinose gives the strongest terminal answer. She does not escape influence or become informationally equal. She becomes capable of making the person who modeled her **answer to her model of him**.

The canonical Year-2 relationship principle is therefore:

> **To recognize a person reciprocally is not merely to know her accurately, protect her effectively, or help her become stronger. It is to preserve enough information, refusal, independent purpose, and causal standing that she can change what the relationship means—even when the answer she creates is not the one the recognizer prepared.**

And the strongest Year-2 test of relationship maturity is:

> **Does the other person remain an author after being understood?**
